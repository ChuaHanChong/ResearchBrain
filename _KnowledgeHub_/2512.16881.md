# PolaRiS: Scalable Real-to-Sim Evaluations for Generalist Robot Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> PolaRiS offers a framework for scalable, high-fidelity real-to-sim evaluations of generalist robot policies by generating interactive simulation environments from real-world video scans. The approach demonstrates an average Pearson correlation of 0.9 between simulated and real-world policy performance across diverse, unseen environments, surpassing prior evaluation methods.

## Problem

- Generalist robot policies face a bottleneck in scalable and accurate evaluation due to the high cost, time, and reproducibility issues of real-world testing.
- Existing simulated benchmarks exhibit a significant visual and physical domain gap, leading to poor correlation with real-world policy performance and an inability to assess generalization across diverse tasks.
- Prior real-to-sim methods struggled to support complex robot setups like wrist cameras, which are common in modern generalist policies.

## Method

- PolaRiS provides an automated pipeline using 2D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct high-fidelity, interactive simulation environments directly from short real-world monocular video scans.
- The framework enables compositional scene construction, automatically articulates scanned robot models (including wrist cameras), and integrates objects generated via models like TRELLIS into a physics-based simulator.
- A lightweight, policy-agnostic co-training strategy fine-tunes policies with a small dataset of simulated demonstrations to bridge subtle real-to-sim domain gaps, allowing zero-shot evaluation in unseen environments.

## Results

- PolaRiS achieves an average Pearson correlation (r) of 0.9 between simulated and real-world policy performance, with individual environments showing correlations as high as 0.98 against the RoboArena benchmark.
- The framework consistently outperforms traditional simulated benchmarks (e.g., Libero-Score) and video world models (e.g., Ctrl-World) in accurately predicting real-world generalist policy performance.
- Environment creation is highly efficient, requiring less than 20 minutes of human effort and under an hour of total wall time for generating a new, interactive simulation scene.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High visual fidelity, achieved through 2D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, is a critical factor for strong real-to-sim correlation in robot policy evaluation.
> - A simple, lightweight co-training with out-of-distribution simulated data effectively refines policy visual representations, enabling robust performance and high real-to-sim correlation (up to r=0.9) in unseen evaluation environments.
> - The primary source of performance discrepancies between real and simulated environments for generalist policies is the visual gap, rather than the dynamics gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies—models capable of performing a wide array of tasks across diverse environments—represents a significant recent advancement in robotics. Projects like Google's RT-X models, Octo, and Physical Intelligence's π0 and π0.5 policies have showcased impressive generalization capabilities. However, this progress has simultaneously highlighted a critical bottleneck: the scalable and accurate evaluation of these policies.

Traditionally, robot policy evaluation has been challenging due to the stochastic nature of real-world interactions, reproducibility issues, and the high cost (time, labor, hardware) of real-world rollouts. For generalist policies, this problem is exacerbated, as they demand evaluation across an unprecedented variety of tasks, objects, and environments to truly assess their generalization.

**Existing Evaluation Paradigms and Their Limitations:**

- **Real-world Evaluation:** While the gold standard, it is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming for large-scale or frequent benchmarking. Reproducibility is also a significant issue. RoboArena is a recent attempt to standardize real-world evaluation but remains resource-intensive.
- **Traditional Simulated Benchmarks (e.g., Libero, Meta-World, RLBench):** These offer scalability and control but typically sacrifice realism in dynamics, visuals, and scene geometry. The visual and physical "domain gap" between these simulations and the real world often leads to poor correlation with real-world policy performance, making them unreliable indicators for policies trained on real data. Many also lack the diversity needed for generalist policies or require extensive manual effort to create new, diverse scenes.
- **Real-to-Sim Policy Evaluation:** Earlier works like SIMPLER tried to bridge the gap by generating simulated environments from real ones, but often used simpler "green-screening" techniques that limited their applicability (e.g., unable to support wrist cameras common in modern generalist policies).
- **Video World Models (e.g., Ctrl-World):** These represent a more recent approach, predicting future video frames conditioned on actions. While scalable, the paper argues that current open models struggle to reliably model physical interactions and accurately estimate policy performance in diverse scenes, often suffering from hallucinations during object interaction.

PolaRiS directly addresses the limitations of these existing paradigms. It proposes a novel "real-to-sim" framework that generates *high-fidelity, interactive simulation environments directly from real-world video scans*. This allows for evaluations that are both scalable (like simulation) and predictive of real-world performance (unlike traditional simulations), while supporting complex robot setups (like wrist cameras) that prior real-to-sim methods could not. By democratizing the creation of realistic evaluation environments, PolaRiS aims to accelerate the development and rigorous testing of the next generation of robotic foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **develop a scalable real-to-sim framework for high-fidelity simulated robot policy evaluation that accurately reflects real-world performance, especially for generalist robot policies.**

This core objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Evaluation Bottleneck for Generalist Policies:** Recent advancements in generalist robot policies have outpaced the ability to rigorously and scalably evaluate them. Real-world evaluation is too costly and time-consuming, while existing simulated benchmarks lack the necessary realism and diversity.
2. **Bridging the Real-to-Sim Domain Gap:** Traditional simulations often exhibit significant visual and physical discrepancies from the real world, leading to a poor correlation between simulated and real-world policy performance. PolaRiS aims to minimize this gap.
3. **Enabling Rapid and Personalized Environment Creation:** Current high-fidelity simulations often require substantial human effort and expertise to build. The authors seek to automate and simplify the process of creating diverse, realistic simulation environments tailored to specific real-world scenes or tasks.
4. **Facilitating Zero-Shot Evaluation in Unseen Environments:** For generalist policies, it is crucial to evaluate their performance in novel scenarios. PolaRiS aims to allow policies, once adequately prepared, to be evaluated in newly generated simulation environments without requiring task-specific or environment-specific simulation data collection or re-training.
5. **Promoting Community-Driven Benchmarking:** By open-sourcing the tools and environments, PolaRiS intends to foster a shared ecosystem for developing and comparing generalist robot policies, akin to how benchmarks function in other AI domains.
6. **Supporting Complex Robot Setups:** Specifically, addressing the limitation of prior real-to-sim methods that could not handle moving cameras (e.g., wrist cameras), which are prevalent in modern generalist robot systems.

In essence, PolaRiS is motivated by the need for an **efficient, accurate, and flexible evaluation paradigm** that can keep pace with the rapid advancements in generalist robot learning, enabling faster research cycles and more reliable policy development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PolaRiS proposes a two-pronged approach: first, a robust pipeline for generating high-fidelity simulated environments from real-world data, and second, a lightweight co-training strategy to bridge any remaining real-to-sim domain gaps for accurate policy evaluation.

#### 3.1. Environment Generation with PolaRiS

The framework adopts a compositional strategy to construct interactive simulation scenes:

1. **Environment Creation via Reconstruction:**
 - **Data Capture:** Users record short (2-5 minute) monocular videos of real-world scenes. Separate videos are captured for the static background and the robot to enable compositional scene construction. A ChArUco board assists in resolving global orientation and metric scale automatically.
 - **Neural Reconstruction:** The video data is processed using COLMAP for camera pose estimation and then fed into a **2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS)** pipeline. 2DGS represents the scene as a collection of oriented planar Gaussian disks, optimized to reproduce input views. This method is chosen for its superior trade-off between high visual fidelity and accurate mesh geometry compared to other neural rendering techniques.
 - **Geometry Extraction:** A Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) is derived from the splats, from which an explicit mesh representation (M) is extracted using marching cubes.
 - **Integration into Simulator:** These meshes are imported into a physics-based simulator (IsaacSim) for realistic contact dynamics, while the original Gaussian splats provide photometric renders for high-fidelity visuals.
 - **Automation:** The entire process is highly automated, typically completing within 30 minutes on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, requiring minimal human effort (5-10 minutes for data acquisition).

2. **Automatic Articulation of Robots:**
 - For interactive robot evaluation, the robot's mesh and its corresponding Gaussian splats must be articulated.
 - Subsets of Gaussian splat primitives are anchored to the robot's kinematic links. When the robot's joint configuration changes, the positions and covariances of these splats are updated via forward kinematics.
 - This creates an interactive, photorealistic robot model whose visual components (Gaussian splats) accurately follow the physical kinematics of the underlying mesh model. This scanned and articulated robot model can be reused across multiple environments. Crucially, this 3D reconstruction approach allows for rendering arbitrary camera views, including **wrist camera inputs**, which were unsupported by prior 2D background inpainting methods.

3. **Object Creation with Generative Models:**
 - Instead of tedious manual reconstruction, PolaRiS employs the **TRELLIS 3D image-to-3D generative model** to create high-quality, watertight object assets.
 - Users capture multiview images of an object, segment it using SAM2, and input these segmented views into TRELLIS.
 - TRELLIS decodes a structured latent representation into a Gaussian splat for appearance and an untextured mesh for geometry. The appearance is then baked onto the mesh, producing a fully textured object suitable for simulation.

4. **Composing Simulated Evaluation Scenes:**
 - A **Scene Composition GUI** (Figure 3) allows users to easily assemble the reconstructed environment, articulated robot, and generated object assets.
 - All components (environment, robot, objects) are represented as Gaussian primitives, allowing for easy composition and transformation in Euclidean space.
 - Standard physics parameters (contact dynamics, robot arm defaults) are applied, and object masses can be estimated by the user.
 - The assembled scene is exported as a USD file, ready for evaluation. The GUI also facilitates defining task-specific success criteria for scoring rollouts. The total human effort for creating a new environment is less than 20 minutes, with a total wall time under an hour.

#### 3.2. Enabling High Correlation Policy Evaluation with PolaRiS using Co-Training

Despite high visual fidelity, subtle domain gaps (e.g., lighting, shadows) can still affect policy performance. To mitigate this and boost real-to-sim correlation, PolaRiS introduces a lightweight, policy-agnostic co-training scheme:

- **Simulation Data Collection:** A small dataset (D_S) of human teleoperated demonstrations is collected in a *separate set of simulated co-training environments* (E_train_S) from the evaluation environments. This dataset includes simulated visual observations, expert actions, and proprioceptive states.
- **Co-Training Recipe:** A pre-trained generalist policy (π_θ) is then finetuned by mixing batches of this small simulated data (D_S) with its original large pre-training dataset (D_pre). A behavior cloning objective (L_BC) is used.
- **Purpose:** This co-training primarily refines the policy's visual representations, making it more robust to subtle visual differences between simulation and the real world. Crucially, since it's not for acquiring new skills, the co-training data does *not* need to be collected in the target evaluation environments or for the target tasks.
- **Scalability:** Only a few hundred simulated episodes and a few hundred fine-tuning steps (typically < 25 minutes) are sufficient. This allows co-trained policies to be evaluated **zero-shot in unseen PolaRiS environments**, making the evaluation process highly scalable and reusable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PolaRiS centers on demonstrating its ability to provide strong real-to-sim correlation for generalist robot policies in unseen environments.

1. **Strong Real-to-Sim Correlation in Unseen Environments:**
 - PolaRiS consistently achieves high Pearson correlation (𝑟) and low Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) between simulated and real-world policy performance. The average Pearson correlation across diverse real-world environments is **𝑟 = 0.9**.
 - This strong correlation holds not just for average performance but also for individual environments, with the worst-case correlation observed at 𝑟 = 0.81.
 - PolaRiS evaluations also show strong correlation (𝑟 = 0.98) with policy scores from RoboArena, a comprehensive real-world benchmark for generalist policies. This validates PolaRiS as a useful proxy for real-world performance, albeit not a complete replacement due to limitations in simulating complex dynamics (non-rigid bodies, soft-body interactions).

2. **Superiority over Existing Evaluation Methods:**
 - **MSE:** Training/validation set action Mean Squared Error is a poor indicator (low correlation).
 - **Libero-Score:** Despite extensive finetuning (10x more compute than PolaRiS) and evaluation on the largest Libero dataset, Libero-Score showed poor correlation with real-world performance. Policies that performed similarly in Libero spanned the full spectrum of real-world performance, highlighting the domain gap.
 - **Video Model Evaluation (Ctrl-World):** While promising, current open-source video models struggled with reliability in diverse scenes, often exhibiting heavy hallucinations during object interaction, making accurate scoring challenging and leading to clear policy mis-rankings (MMRV: 0.22).
 - **Prior Real-to-Sim Methods (e.g., SIMPLER):** These were inherently limited as they could not support policies utilizing wrist cameras (common in state-of-the-art VLAs), making a direct comparison impossible for the evaluated generalist policies.

3. **Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
 - **Visual Fidelity:** While co-training can alleviate some visual sensitivity, **Gaussian splatting rendering leads to the strongest correlation** compared to ray-tracing or untextured environments. This indicates that high visual fidelity (enabled by 2DGS) is a key factor, though not the sole driver, for robust evaluations. The effectiveness of real-to-sim reconstruction (PolaRiS) compared to hand-designed low-fidelity benchmarks (Libero) is also emphasized.
 - **Co-training Dataset:** The simple co-training recipe is crucial for strong real-to-sim correlation. Evaluating pre-trained policies without co-training ("no-cotrain") yielded insufficient correlation. Importantly, using **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) co-training data (collected in environments/tasks separate from evaluation)** yielded comparable correlations to in-domain data. This finding is critical for scalability, as it means new evaluation environments can be added *without needing to collect new, targeted simulation data* or re-finetune policies for each new scenario.
 - **Fine-tuning Steps:** A small number of finetuning steps (e.g., 1k steps) consistently led to strong real-to-sim correlation, and even a few hundred steps significantly improved results. However, excessive finetuning could lead to overfitting and reduced correlation, indicating an optimal range for this lightweight process.

4. **Hardware-in-the-Loop Experiments (Appendix D.1):** These experiments confirmed that the visual gap between real and simulated environments was the dominant source of error, rather than the dynamics gap. Feeding policies simulated visuals with real dynamics led to a 25% drop in performance, while simulated dynamics with real visuals showed no significant drop. This finding reinforces the paper's focus on high-fidelity visual reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PolaRiS represents a significant step forward in robot learning research, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Democratization and Scalability of Evaluation:** By providing an efficient, low-cost, and rapid method for creating high-fidelity simulated environments, PolaRiS democratizes access to robust evaluation tools. Individual research groups can quickly generate custom benchmarks for their specific policies or deployment scenarios without needing extensive real-world robot fleets or manual simulation engineering expertise. This drastically lowers the barrier to entry for conducting thorough policy evaluations.
2. **Accelerated Research and Development:** The ability to perform scalable and reliable evaluations in simulation allows for much faster iteration cycles for developing generalist robot policies. Researchers can quickly test hypotheses, compare different architectural choices, and refine training strategies with greater confidence in the simulated results correlating to real-world performance. This can significantly accelerate progress in the field.
3. **Standardization and Reproducibility:** PolaRiS fosters the creation of shareable and reproducible evaluation environments. By open-sourcing the tools and environments, it encourages community-driven development of a diverse suite of realistic benchmarks. This is crucial for enabling fair comparisons between different generalist policies and establishing standardized performance metrics, similar to what exists for large language models.
4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap Effectively:** The combination of state-of-the-art neural reconstruction (2D Gaussian Splatting) and a simple, effective co-training strategy provides an unprecedented level of real-to-sim correlation. This moves beyond traditional simulations that often failed to predict real-world performance, making PolaRiS a reliable signal for policy improvement.
5. **Support for Modern Robot Architectures:** Unlike prior real-to-sim methods, PolaRiS natively supports complex sensor setups like wrist cameras, which are integral to many modern generalist robot policies. This broadens the applicability of the framework to the most advanced robotic systems.
6. **Insights into Generalist Policy Robustness:** The ablation studies highlight the importance of visual fidelity and the effectiveness of a lightweight co-training for bridging domain gaps. This provides valuable insights into what makes generalist policies robust and how best to prepare them for deployment in diverse environments.

**Future Directions and Potential Improvements:**
The authors acknowledge several areas for future work, including:
- **Enhanced Rendering:** Exploring richer diffusion-based renderings to further close visual gaps.
- **Advanced System Identification:** Incorporating more sophisticated techniques to better model robot and environment dynamics, potentially improving the physical realism.
- **Hybrid Simulation Techniques:** Merging world modeling with physics-based simulation could lead to even more scalable evaluations, particularly for complex objects and interactions that remain challenging for current physics engines.

In conclusion, PolaRiS addresses a critical need in robot learning by offering a practical, scalable, and high-fidelity solution for evaluating generalist robot policies. Its potential to accelerate research, democratize access to robust benchmarks, and foster community collaboration makes it a highly significant contribution to the field.
