# On the Efficacy of 3D Point Cloud Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> UC San Diego researchers systematically evaluated the effectiveness of 3D point cloud representations in visual reinforcement learning compared to 2D image methods. Their findings indicate that 3D point cloud agents achieve better sample efficiency and performance in tasks demanding 3D spatial relationship reasoning, with PointNet showing notable representational robustness.

## Problem

- 2D visual inputs (RGB, RGB-D) for reinforcement learning can lose crucial 3D spatial information, complicating agent-object and object-object relationship reasoning for 2D convolutional neural networks.
- A systematic comparison between 2D and 3D visual representations in RL was lacking, hindering a comprehensive understanding of when and how 3D-native neural networks offer beneficial inductive biases.
- The field lacked a robust and widely evaluated RL algorithm that effectively leverages 3D inputs, limiting the establishment of best practices for 3D visual RL.

## Method

- Developed a general algorithmic framework for ==3D point cloud RL==, integrating ==observation post-processing== (e.g., filtering, sampling), common 3D visual backbones (==PointNet==, ==3D SparseConvNet==), ==point frame stacking== for temporal information, and ==3D data augmentations==.
- Conducted systematic comparative experiments pitting 3D point cloud agents against ==2D RGB and RGB-D agents== across a synthetic 3D Reacher task, DeepMind Control Suite, and complex robotic manipulation tasks from the ==ManiSkill== benchmark.
- Investigated optimal design choices and architectural ==inductive biases== within 3D point cloud RL, including the effectiveness of various 3D backbones, data augmentation strategies, point cloud post-processing, and multi-frame stacking techniques.

## Results

- 3D point cloud agents demonstrated superior performance and sample efficiency on tasks requiring 3D spatial relationship reasoning (e.g., synthetic 3D Reacher, ManiSkill robotic manipulation tasks) compared to 2D RGB and RGB-D agents.
- On DeepMind Control tasks, 3D point cloud agents achieved comparable sample efficiency to 2D counterparts for planar agent-object reasoning, though 2D RGB agents generally outperformed them in pure 2D locomotion.
- While 2D data augmentations provided significant benefits for image-based agents, 3D data augmentations offered less substantial performance gains for PointNet-based agents, and current 3D multi-frame stacking methods were less effective for inferring temporal dynamics compared to 2D approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D point cloud representations provide a beneficial inductive bias for visual RL tasks requiring 3D spatial relationship reasoning, leading to improved sample efficiency and performance.
> - PointNet exhibits a strong intrinsic representational robustness to input perturbations, which contributes to its strong performance in 3D reasoning tasks and reduces the necessity for extensive data augmentation.
> - Effective point cloud post-processing, such as filtering irrelevant points and prioritizing salient ones (e.g., agent body points), significantly enhances the performance of 3D point cloud agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual reinforcement learning (visual RL) aims to develop agents capable of solving tasks using visual observations. Significant advancements have been made in areas such as robotic manipulation and control, primarily utilizing 2D visual inputs like RGB or RGB-D images. However, objects and agents exist in a 3D environment, and 2D projections can lose crucial spatial information. For instance, object size in a 2D image varies with camera distance, and distinct depths can be encoded by adjacent pixels, complicating 3D spatial reasoning for 2D convolutional neural networks (CNNs). In contrast, 3D representations such as point clouds or 3D volumes inherently preserve 3D spatial information, making object size invariant to camera position and neighboring points spatially proximate.

Recent years have seen progress in 3D understanding neural networks and the application of 3D representations in fields like perception, self-driving, and robotic manipulation. Concurrently, there have been nascent efforts to incorporate 3D representations, including point clouds and 3D volumes, into visual RL. However, the existing literature on 3D visual RL has largely focused on demonstrating the feasibility of using these representations or on specific method ablations, without systematically comparing their efficacy against established 2D methods across diverse tasks. A comprehensive understanding of when and how 3D-native neural networks provide a beneficial inductive bias for visual RL, particularly concerning agent-object and object-object relationship reasoning, has been largely absent. This paper addresses this gap by conducting a systematic investigation into the utility of 3D point clouds in RL and comparing them to 2D image-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the question: "When and how do 3D neural networks that learn features in the 3D-native space provide a beneficial inductive bias for visual RL?" Specifically, the authors focus on 3D point clouds as a common form of 3D representation.

The motivation stems from several observations and unaddressed challenges in the field of visual RL:
- **Limitations of 2D representations for 3D reasoning:** While much prior visual RL work utilizes RGB or RGB-D images, real-world tasks often require agents to reason about spatial relationships in 3D (e.g., between a robot hand and a door handle). 2D representations can struggle with inherent ambiguities (e.g., perspective distortions affecting perceived object size) and the difficulty for 2D CNNs to accurately localize objects or infer precise 3D relationships from depth information.
- **Inherent advantages of 3D representations:** 3D-native representations like point clouds explicitly encode 3D coordinates, making object size invariant to camera position and spatial proximity explicit. This intrinsic 3D information is hypothesized to offer a more suitable inductive bias for tasks requiring 3D spatial reasoning.
- **Lack of systematic comparison:** Despite recent advancements in 3D deep learning and some initial work on 3D visual RL, a comprehensive comparison of 3D versus 2D representations across various RL tasks, particularly with an analysis from the perspective of agent-object/object-object relationship reasoning, has not been conducted. The absence of such a systematic study means that the necessity or specific benefits of 3D neural networks in RL policy/value functions are not widely acknowledged or understood by the broader RL community.
- **Need for robust 3D RL algorithms:** The field currently lacks a comprehensively evaluated RL algorithm that effectively leverages 3D inputs. This research aims to develop and analyze best practices and design choices for 3D point cloud RL to establish a robust baseline.

The authors aim to provide insights and guidance for future work in 3D visual RL by thoroughly comparing 2D and 3D approaches, investigating optimal design choices for 3D point cloud RL, and analyzing the scenarios where 3D representations offer distinct advantages.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic comparative approach, contrasting 3D point cloud-based visual RL with 2D image-based visual RL across various tasks. The methodology includes:

#### 3.1. Minimalist Motivating Example

To isolate and highlight the inductive bias differences, a synthetic "3D Reacher" environment was designed. In this environment, an agent (red sphere) needs to move to a target (green sphere) within a single time step. Both sphere sizes are randomized, and their initial positions are random within a defined distance range. The reward is negative squared Euclidean distance to the target. This task requires accurate 3D spatial relationship reasoning. Performance of 3D point cloud agents, RGB agents, and RGB-D agents (all based on SAC or DrQ) are compared in this environment.

#### 3.2. General Framework for RL from 3D Point Clouds (Algorithm 1)

A general algorithmic framework for 3D point cloud RL is outlined, incorporating key design factors:
- **Observation Post-processing:** Raw point clouds, obtained by back-projecting RGB-D images, often contain irrelevant or redundant points (e.g., distant points, ground points). These points are filtered using depth clipping, coordinate clipping, or segmentation mask filtering. A fixed number of relevant points are then randomly sampled.
- **Point Cloud Visual Backbones:** Two common 3D point cloud architectures are investigated:
 - **PointNet:** A lightweight architecture that processes point features through MLPs and then uses max-pooling to obtain a global feature.
 - **3D SparseConvNet:** A sparse convolution-based architecture that processes point/voxel features, providing a closer inductive bias to 2D CNNs.
- **Point Frame Stacking:** To incorporate temporal information, particularly for tasks requiring velocity inference, multiple point cloud frames are stacked. This is achieved by appending one-hot vectors to point features to indicate their source frame and concatenating point clouds along the number-of-points dimension. This allows lightweight 3D backbones to process multi-frame inputs.
- **Point Cloud Data Augmentations:** Inspired by 2D image-based RL, various augmentations are explored for 3D point clouds:
 - **Random Shift:** Applies uniform random noise to 3D point coordinates.
 - **Random Rotation:** Rotates point clouds around the z-axis by a random angle.
 - **Random Jitter:** Adds small, distinct random noise to individual point coordinates.
 - **Random Color Shift:** Applies torchvision.colorjitter to point RGB features.
 - **Random Dropout:** Randomly removes a percentage of points from the input.

#### 3.3. Base RL Algorithms and Benchmarks

- **Base Algorithms:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Data-Regularized Q (DrQ) are used as the foundational RL algorithms. "SAC" refers to agents without data augmentation, and "DrQ" to agents with 2D or 3D data augmentations.
- **2D CNN Agents:** For RGB and RGB-D inputs, the implementation, network architectures, and data augmentations of DrQ are followed.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **DeepMind Control Suite (DM Control) / PlaNet Benchmark:** 6 continuous control tasks are used. These tasks are primarily 2D locomotion and generally do not heavily involve agent-object/object-object spatial reasoning. The environments were extended to support point cloud learning.
 - **ManiSkill Benchmark:** 4 robotic manipulation tasks (OpenCabinetDoor, OpenCabinetDrawer, PushChair, MoveBucket) are used. These tasks involve significant 3D spatial relationship reasoning between robots and objects.

Detailed hyperparameters, observation specifications (resolution for images, point count and features for point clouds), and network architectures for both CNNs and 3D backbones are provided in the appendix. Specific point cloud post-processing strategies, including targeted downsampling to prioritize points on the agent body or target objects, are also detailed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded distinct findings depending on the nature of the tasks and the architectural choices.

#### 4.1. Efficacy of 3D vs. 2D Visual RL Across Tasks

- **Minimalist Motivating Example (3D Reacher):** 3D point cloud agents demonstrated superior performance and sample efficiency compared to both RGB and RGB-D agents. This is attributed to the explicit 3D coordinate information in point clouds, which facilitates direct spatial relationship reasoning (e.g., coordinate subtraction), an inductive bias not inherent in RGB-D images despite carrying similar information. RGB agents performed least effectively due to 2D projection ambiguities.
- **DM Control Suite (PlaNet Benchmark):** For tasks primarily involving 2D locomotion without substantial 3D spatial reasoning (e.g., Cheetah Run, Walker Walk), 2D RGB-based agents generally outperformed 3D point cloud agents. However, for DM Control tasks involving planar agent-object relationship reasoning (e.g., Ball-in-cup Catch, Reacher Easy), 3D point cloud agents exhibited comparable sample efficiency to their 2D counterparts.
- **ManiSkill Benchmark:** On robotic manipulation tasks demanding extensive 3D spatial relationship reasoning (e.g., OpenCabinetDoor, PushChair), 3D point cloud agents achieved better sample efficiency and overall performance than 2D RGB and RGB-D CNN agents.
- **Data Augmentations:** Data augmentations in 2D images were found to significantly impact agent performance across most tasks. Conversely, data augmentations in 3D point clouds generally offered less substantial benefit for PointNet-based agents.

#### 4.2. Investigating Design Choices and Inductive Biases of 3D Point Cloud RL

- **Performance and Representational Robustness of Different 3D Architectures:**
 - **PointNet vs. 3D SparseConvNet:** Without data augmentations, PointNet significantly outperformed 3D SparseConvNet. However, 3D SparseConvNet benefited considerably from data augmentations, achieving performance similar to PointNet and surpassing 2D CNNs. This suggests that architectures with convolutional inductive biases (like SparseConvNet and 2D CNNs) benefit more from augmentations.
 - **Representational Robustness:** An experiment measuring the relative change in Q-value under random input shifts revealed that PointNet is notably more robust to perturbations in point cloud coordinates than 2D CNNs and 3D SparseConvNet. PointNet exhibited minimal Q-value changes even with substantial 3D shifts, indicating a higher intrinsic representational robustness. This robustness is posited to assist in learning generalized skills like distance inference without memorizing precise object locations.
- **Efficacy of More Point Cloud Data Augmentations:** Beyond random shift and rotation, other augmentations like random jitter, random color shift, and random dropout were explored for PointNet agents. None of these augmentations provided significant performance benefits, reinforcing the observation that PointNet's inherent robustness reduces the need for extensive data augmentation.
- **Impact of Point Cloud Post-processing Strategies:** Experiments on DM Control tasks demonstrated that prioritizing and retaining a higher proportion of points from the agent body (critical for decision-making) while filtering out irrelevant points (e.g., ground points) significantly improved agent performance. This indicates the importance of an effective post-processing strategy to focus computational resources on salient information.
- **Efficacy of Multi-frame Stacking:** While 3D point cloud agents were less effective than image-based agents on DM Control tasks requiring velocity inference (e.g., Cheetah Run), performance between single-frame 2D and 3D agents was comparable. However, when using two stacked frames, the performance boost for point cloud agents was considerably smaller than for image-based agents on these dynamics-heavy tasks. This suggests that the current multi-frame stacking technique for 3D point clouds may not effectively integrate temporal information for inferring agent dynamics, a limitation not observed for tasks where single-frame observations suffice for spatial relationships.

Overall, the findings indicate that 3D point cloud representations, particularly with PointNet as a backbone, offer a beneficial inductive bias for tasks involving 3D spatial relationship reasoning, demonstrating improved sample efficiency and performance. This benefit is linked to PointNet's representational robustness. For tasks reliant on temporal dynamics inference, current 3D multi-frame stacking methods appear less effective than 2D counterparts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes a systematic investigation into the efficacy of 3D point cloud visual representations in reinforcement learning, addressing a gap in the existing literature. The findings provide empirical evidence and insights into when and how 3D-native representations offer advantages over conventional 2D image-based approaches.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that:
- **Improved 3D Spatial Reasoning:** For tasks that fundamentally depend on accurately understanding agent-object and object-object spatial relationships in a 3D environment, such as complex robotic manipulation, 3D point cloud RL consistently exhibits superior sample efficiency and performance compared to 2D image-based methods. This is a critical insight for designing visual RL systems for physical robots operating in 3D spaces.
- **Understanding Inductive Biases:** The analysis of different 3D visual backbones (PointNet vs. 3D SparseConvNet) and their representational robustness clarifies how network architectures contribute to RL performance. The finding of PointNet's intrinsic robustness to input perturbations offers an explanation for its strong performance and reduced reliance on data augmentation, suggesting a valuable inductive bias for generalization.
- **Guidance for 3D Visual RL Design:** The detailed investigation into design choices, including point cloud post-processing and frame stacking, provides practical best practices and insights for researchers developing 3D visual RL algorithms. For instance, the emphasis on prioritizing relevant points in post-processing is a tangible recommendation.

The potential impact of this research is multifaceted:
- **Advancing Robotic Manipulation:** The demonstrated benefits of 3D point clouds in robotic manipulation benchmarks suggest a pathway towards more robust, sample-efficient, and generalizable robot learning, particularly for tasks requiring precise interaction with objects in complex environments.
- **Shifting Research Focus:** By systematically highlighting the strengths and weaknesses of 3D representations, this work may encourage the RL community to reconsider the default use of 2D visual inputs for tasks requiring 3D understanding, potentially leading to increased adoption and further development of 3D visual RL methodologies.
- **Informing Future Architectures:** The analysis of architectural inductive biases and representational robustness can guide the design of future 3D neural networks tailored for RL, potentially focusing on architectures that inherently promote generalization and efficiency.

The authors acknowledge limitations, including the focus on online RL and relatively short-horizon tasks, and identify future work such as extending to demonstration-based RL, longer-horizon tasks, and developing more effective multi-frame point cloud stacking strategies for improved agent dynamics inference. Addressing these limitations could further broaden the applicability and performance of 3D visual RL.
