# Chain-of-Agents: End-to-End Agent Foundation Models via Multi-Agent Distillation and Agentic RL

> [!summary] Summary
> The OPPO AI Agent Team developed Chain-of-Agents (CoA), a paradigm enabling a single large language model (LLM) to perform end-to-end complex problem-solving by simulating multi-agent collaboration. This approach, trained via multi-agent distillation and agentic reinforcement learning, achieves state-of-the-art performance across various agentic tasks while reducing inference cost by 84.6% compared to traditional multi-agent systems.

## Problem

- Traditional Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) suffer from high computational overhead, generalization issues, and a lack of end-to-end data-centric learning, as their LLM backbones are not natively trained for multi-turn, multi-agent workflows.
- Existing Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) models are limited by a rigid 'Think-Action-Observation' workflow, preventing them from natively supporting flexible multi-agent collaboration patterns.
- Neither MAS nor TIR paradigms effectively leverage end-to-end training to learn complex, dynamic multi-agent and multi-tool orchestration within a single, unified LLM.

## Method

- The ==Chain-of-Agents (CoA)== paradigm unifies multi-agent collaboration within a single LLM's decoding process, dynamically activating various role-playing (e.g., Thinking, Plan, Reflection) and tool agents (e.g., Search, Crawl, Code Generate).
- An ==Agentic Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== framework distills expert multi-agent system trajectories into CoA-compatible training data, using ==progressive quality filtering== to ensure high-quality, complex problem-solving patterns.
- ==Agentic Reinforcement Learning== further refines the LLM's multi-tool orchestration policy through outcome-driven rewards, enhancing strategic tool usage and adaptive reasoning on challenging, task-specific queries.

## Results

- Agent Foundation Models (AFMs) achieve new state-of-the-art performance across multi-hop question answering, complex web tasks, mathematical reasoning (up to **78.0%** accuracy with AFM-RL-32B), and code generation benchmarks.
- AFMs demonstrate an **84.6%** reduction in inference cost (token consumption) compared to traditional multi-agent systems while maintaining or exceeding competitive performance.
- The models exhibit strong generalization capabilities, adapting to unseen multi-hop QA datasets and effectively orchestrating novel tool agents when provided with explicit descriptions and invocation schemas.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single LLM can effectively internalize and dynamically orchestrate complex multi-agent workflows, significantly reducing computational overhead and token consumption compared to traditional multi-agent systems.
> - Multi-agent distillation, paired with rigorous data filtering, is a highly effective method for teaching an LLM agentic capabilities and sequential decision-making patterns.
> - Reinforcement learning, with carefully designed reward functions and strategic data sampling, is crucial for optimizing an agent's policy for multi-tool orchestration and achieving robust performance on verifiable tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of three rapidly evolving areas in AI: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR).

**Existing Paradigms and Their Limitations:**

- **Multi-Agent Systems (MAS):** Recent advances in MAS have shown remarkable capabilities in complex problem-solving tasks like deep research and mathematical reasoning. These systems achieve complex problem-solving through collaboration among multiple specialized agents, each potentially equipped with diverse roles and toolsets. However, the authors highlight several crucial limitations of current MAS:
 - **High Computational Overhead:** This stems from redundant communication between agents and the sophisticated workflow designs often requiring manual prompt/workflow engineering.
 - **Generalization Challenges:** MAS often struggle to generalize to new domains and tasks without substantial reconfiguration and expert prompt engineering.
 - **Lack of Data-Centric Learning:** Traditional MAS are typically built upon static LLM backbones that cannot benefit from end-to-end data-centric training on agentic tasks, limiting performance improvement through learning.
 - **LLM Backbone Mismatch:** The LLMs used in MAS are generally not natively trained to support multi-turn, multi-agent, and multi-tool workflows, requiring extensive prompt engineering to simulate such interactions.

- **Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) Models:** This paradigm explicitly incorporates tool usage into the LLM's reasoning process, typically following a "think-action-observation" (ReAct-like) pipeline. TIR models have demonstrated improved end-to-end information seeking and reasoning compared to general LLMs using prompt engineering alone. However, the paper argues that the current TIR paradigm is limited:
 - It supports a rigid, static "Think-Action-Observation" workflow, lacking the flexibility to emulate the diverse roles and collaborative dynamics of multi-agent systems.
 - It cannot train LLMs to natively support multi-agent systems in an end-to-end fashion.

**Bridging the Gap with Chain-of-Agents (CoA):**

The "Chain-of-Agents" (CoA) paradigm is introduced to bridge the gap between these two approaches. CoA aims to leverage the strengths of MAS (multi-role collaboration, diverse toolsets) and TIR (end-to-end trainability, unified model) while mitigating their respective weaknesses. By enabling a single LLM to dynamically activate different "tool agents" and "role-playing agents" to simulate multi-agent collaboration in an end-to-end fashion, CoA aims to overcome the computational inefficiencies and generalization issues of traditional MAS, and the architectural rigidity of current TIR methods. The resulting models, termed "Agent Foundation Models" (AFMs), represent a novel class of LLMs natively capable of intricate, multi-step agentic problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the Chain-of-Agents (CoA) framework stems from the identified limitations of existing multi-agent systems (MAS) and tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) models. The research aims to develop a more efficient, capable, and generalizable approach to complex problem-solving with LLMs.

The **key objectives** are:

1. **Introduce a Novel Reasoning Paradigm:** To propose Chain-of-Agents (CoA) as an LLM reasoning paradigm that enables native, end-to-end complex problem-solving, simulating multi-agent collaboration within a single model. This paradigm aims to dynamically activate different tool agents and role-playing agents.
2. **Develop a Robust Training Framework:** To design and implement a comprehensive training framework that can elicit end-to-end CoA problem-solving abilities in LLMs. This involves:
 - **Multi-Agent Distillation:** A novel method to distill the capabilities and intricate decision-making patterns of state-of-the-art multi-agent systems into CoA-compatible trajectories for supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
 - **Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL):** To further refine and optimize the models' capabilities on verifiable agentic tasks, enhancing their strategic tool usage and adaptive reasoning.
3. **Establish Agent Foundation Models (AFMs):** To train and evaluate models based on the CoA paradigm and tuning framework, demonstrating their superior performance across a diverse range of agentic tasks.
4. **Promote Open Science:** To fully open-source the entire research, including model weights, training and evaluation code, and training data, thereby providing a solid foundation and accelerating future research in agent models and agentic RL.

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the computational overhead, generalization issues, and lack of data-centric learning inherent in existing multi-agent frameworks, while simultaneously enhancing the multi-agent collaboration capabilities beyond the rigid, ReAct-like patterns of current tool-integrated reasoning models. The goal is a unified, highly efficient, and adaptable agent model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the proposed Chain-of-Agents (CoA) paradigm and a two-stage training framework: Agentic Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) followed by Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Chain-of-Agents (CoA) Paradigm

The CoA framework tackles complex queries by orchestrating dynamic module interactions within a single, unified LLM (Mθ). Unlike traditional TIR which adheres to a static "Think-Action-Observation" cycle, CoA supports highly flexible multi-agent system workflows by defining and dynamically activating multiple agents.

- **Role-playing Agents:** These handle high-level reasoning and coordination:
 - **Thinking Agent:** Orchestrates the reasoning pipeline, activates specialized agents, and maintains solution state coherence.
 - **Plan Agent:** Decomposes complex queries into structured task sequences.
 - **Reflection Agent:** Conducts self-critique, fuses knowledge, and resolves inconsistencies.
 - **Verification Agent:** Validates reasoning integrity against correctness criteria.
- **Tool Agents:** These perform domain-specific execution:
 - **Search Agent:** Formulates optimized queries and prioritizes sources.
 - **Crawl Agent:** Extracts content from web pages.
 - **Code Generate Agent:** Generates and executes code snippets in sandbox environments.

The key innovation is that this multi-agent collaboration occurs *within a single decoding (inference) process* of the LLM. The Thinking Agent dynamically coordinates this ecosystem through state transitions, maintaining contextual continuity and significantly reducing token consumption associated with inter-agent communication in traditional MAS. This unified model design also allows for direct optimization through end-to-end training.

#### 3.2. Agentic Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)

The SFT stage aims to distill the capabilities of expert multi-agent systems into an LLM, making it capable of CoA problem-solving.

- **Training Data Generation (Multi-Agent Knowledge Distillation):**
 - The framework generates agentic tasks, similar to Shi et al..
 - A novel multi-agent distillation framework is introduced to transfer capabilities from state-of-the-art multi-agent systems (e.g., OAgents).
 - Expert multi-agent systems solve these tasks, and their successful execution trajectories (sequential reasoning patterns, agent activation sequences, reasoning states, observations) are recorded and converted into CoA-compatible formats. This effectively distills the *sequential decision-making* of expert systems.
 - The trajectories follow the format: `<think> C_cot </think><tools> α_m(α_p) </tools><observation> O_t </observation><reflection> F_t </reflection>...<answer> A_t </answer>`.
- **Progressive Quality Filtering:** A four-stage filtering process ensures only high-quality, non-trivial trajectories are used for SFT:
 1. **Complexity filtering:** Excludes trajectories with fewer than 5 agent-tool interactions.
 2. **Quality filtering:** Removes instances with incorrect answers, redundant tool inputs, or instruction non-compliance (validated via LLMs for QA/search, test cases for code, exact matching for math).
 3. **Reflection enrichment:** Down-samples trajectories lacking self-critical reasoning mechanisms (e.g., self-reflection, self-refinement), up-sampling for math/code tasks.
 4. **Error-correction trajectory upsampling:** Prioritizes trajectories demonstrating initial low credibility but achieving correct answers through iterative re-reasoning (e.g., using a `<double_check>` agent and GRM credibility metrics).
- **Training Objective:** The model is fine-tuned to minimize the cross-entropy loss, with observation masking to prevent environmental noise propagation, establishing a robust "cold start" for subsequent RL.

#### 3.3. Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The RL stage further refines the agent's policy for multi-tool orchestration using outcome-driven rewards, enhancing strategic tool usage and adaptive reasoning.

- **Data Sampling for RL Training:**
 - **Quality Filter for Web Agent:** Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct evaluates question solvability without tools. Queries with a pass rate > 0.3 (trivially solvable or highly contaminated) are excluded, focusing on cases requiring genuine tool engagement.
 - **Strategic Sampling:** Randomly selects challenging queries (pass rate ≤ 0.3) from the remaining set, ensuring RL emphasizes tasks where tool coordination is critical. This data is excluded from the SFT dataset to prevent overlap.
 - **Quality Filter for Code Agent:** A fine-tuned 7B AFM model samples each query 8 times; queries solved correctly in all trials are deemed too simple and discarded to accelerate training.
- **Reward Function Design:**
 - **Web Agent Reward:** `R_web(τ) = score_answer`. A binary reward (0 or 1) based on LLM-as-Judge (Qwen-2.5-72B) assessment of the final prediction's correctness. This focuses on core correctness and mitigates reward hacking.
 - **Code Agent Reward:** `R_code(τ) = score_answer ⋅ score_format`. This reward reflects both answer correctness (passing test cases for code, Math-Verify for math) and format compliance for tool calls (e.g., ````py...````).
- **RL Algorithm:** Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO) is employed, with specific protocols for rollouts, steps, and token limits.

The overall approach effectively integrates distillation from expert MAS into a trainable, single-model agent, which is then further optimized through task-specific reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical studies demonstrate that Agent Foundation Models (AFMs) trained with the Chain-of-Agents paradigm and tuning framework achieve new state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks and exhibit superior efficiency and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

AFMs consistently outperform existing Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) methods across various domains:

- **Multi-hop Question Answering (MHQA):**
 - AFM-RL established new state-of-the-art average performance across 7 MHQA datasets. For comparable Qwen-2.5-3B-base and 7B-base models, improvements ranged from 1.7% to 6.8% over previous best methods.
 - Notably, AFMs showed exceptional generalization on unseen multi-hop QA datasets, despite being trained primarily on NQ and HotpotQA.

- **Complex Web Tasks:**
 - With a Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct backbone, AFM-RL achieved a 55.3% average success rate on **GAIA**, outperforming WebSailor (53.2%) and WebDancer (51.5% with a stronger backbone).
 - AFM-RL set a new state-of-the-art among 32B models on **BrowseComp** with 11.1% success.
 - On **WebWalker**, AFM-RL achieved 63.0%, substantially exceeding WebThinker-RL (46.5%), WebDancer (47.9%), and WebShaper (51.4%).
 - AFM-RL achieved 18.0% on the challenging **HLE** benchmark, surpassing WebThinker-RL (15.8%) and WebDancer (7.2%).
 - AFM (32B) performance on GAIA (55.3%) approached that of GPT-4.1-based systems like OWL (55.8%) and OAgents (58.3%). On HLE (18.0%), it surpassed OWL (12.6%) and other leading reasoning models. Even the 7B AFM model achieved competitive HLE scores (15.6%), nearly matching QwQ-32B's WebThinker-RL.
 - The SFT-only variant (AFM-SFT-32B) also demonstrated superiority over other SFT-trained methods (e.g., WebSailor, WebDancer) on GAIA, WebWalker, and BrowseComp, validating the effectiveness of multi-agent distillation.

- **Mathematical Reasoning:**
 - AFM-RL-7B achieved an average accuracy of 64.3% across five mathematical benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, AMC23, OlympiadBench), a 3.6% improvement over the second-best, SimpleTIR-7B-Multi.
 - AFM-RL-32B achieved an average accuracy of 78.0%, 3.6% higher than ReTool-32B, with absolute improvements of 10.5% on AIME25 and 5.7% on OlympiadBench.
 - SFT contributed significantly (22.0% for 7B, 23.4% for 32B) to establishing CoA capabilities, with RL providing additional substantial gains (20.8% for 7B, 18% for 32B).

- **Code Generation:**
 - RL-enhanced AFMs achieved average accuracy gains of 8.5% (7B) and 13.2% (32B) over their backbone models on LiveCodeBench v4/v5 and CodeContests.
 - AFM outperformed ReTool-32B and Reveal-32B (a code-specialized model) on LiveCodeBench V5, indicating that the distillation and filtering strategies effectively enhance complex programming performance.

#### 4.2. Computational Efficiency

- AFM demonstrated superior efficiency compared to traditional Multi-Agent Systems (OAgents) and other TIR methods (WebThinker).
- It achieved the lowest token consumption (overall and tool call tokens) and the fewest tool calls among compared methods.
- AFM reduced inference cost (token consumption) by **84.6%** compared to traditional multi-agent systems, while maintaining competitive performance. This efficiency gain is attributed to its data construction mechanism, which mitigates redundant token accumulation.

#### 4.3. Generalization on Unseen Agents

- The Code Agent model, trained exclusively with a Python interpreter, successfully orchestrated unseen tool agents (Web Search, Visual Inspector) in a zero-shot setting when *explicit* tool descriptions and invocation schemas were provided in the prompt. This indicated strong generalization for understanding new tools if their usage rules are clearly specified.
- However, web-agent models, trained only on search/crawl tasks, struggled with character-level precision required by tools like Python executor or Visual Inspector, highlighting that explicit, format-constrained training is crucial for robust generalization to novel, strictly-formatted tool interfaces.

#### 4.4. Agentic Test-Time Scaling (TTS)

- Applying test-time scaling strategies (AFM-Bo3 for best-of-3 selection and AFM-Pass@3 for three attempts with one correct answer) significantly boosted AFM performance.
- AFM-Bo3 improved GAIA scores from 55.3 to 57.3 and HLE from 18.0 to 23.0.
- AFM-Pass@3 delivered even more substantial gains, surging to 69.9 on GAIA (a 14.6-point increase) and 33.2 on HLE (over 10 points above AFM-Bo3).
- These gains consistently outpaced other models' Pass@N variants and substantially narrowed the performance gap with proprietary models like Claude-3-7, demonstrating the superior TTS capability of the CoA framework for end-to-end agent models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Chain-of-Agents (CoA) paradigm and the resulting Agent Foundation Models (AFMs) represent a significant step forward in the development of capable and efficient AI agents. Their significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift in Agentic AI:** CoA introduces a novel and highly effective way to integrate multi-agent collaboration capabilities directly within a single large language model. By internalizing the dynamic orchestration and role-playing typically seen in external multi-agent frameworks, CoA offers a unified, end-to-end trainable solution that overcomes the architectural limitations of previous approaches.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Capabilities:** The demonstrated 84.6% reduction in inference cost (token consumption) compared to traditional multi-agent systems, coupled with state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks, highlights a critical breakthrough in computational efficiency without sacrificing capability. This makes complex agentic problem-solving more practical and scalable for real-world applications.
- **Improved Generalization:** The ability of AFMs to generalize effectively across various tasks, including previously unseen ones, and to adapt to new tools (especially when explicit formats are provided) suggests a more robust and adaptable form of AI agent. This paves the way for agents that require less re-engineering for novel tasks and environments.
- **Advancement of Open-Source LLM Agents:** The full open-sourcing of model weights, training code, and datasets is a substantial contribution to the research community. It provides a robust, high-performing baseline and a clear methodology for future research on agent models and agentic reinforcement learning, fostering transparency and accelerating innovation in the field.
- **Practical Applications:** The advancements in web navigation, code generation, and mathematical reasoning capabilities have direct implications for intelligent assistants, automated research tools, software development assistants, and advanced educational platforms. OPPO, as a consumer electronics giant, could integrate these sophisticated agent capabilities into its devices and services, offering users more intelligent and seamless interactions.
- **Bridging the Gap Between Research Areas:** By successfully combining the collaborative power of multi-agent systems with the end-to-end trainability of tool-integrated reasoning, CoA effectively unifies and advances previously disparate research threads, opening new avenues for hybrid AI architectures.
- **Scalability of Agentic RL:** The success of agentic reinforcement learning in refining and optimizing AFM's capabilities, particularly with techniques like DAPO and careful reward function design, further validates RL as a crucial component for developing highly performant and adaptive AI agents. The impressive gains from test-time scaling further underscore the robustness and potential for further optimization of these models.

In conclusion, Chain-of-Agents represents a compelling vision for the future of AI agents, demonstrating how a single LLM can effectively mimic and even surpass the performance of complex multi-agent orchestrations while being significantly more efficient and adaptable.
