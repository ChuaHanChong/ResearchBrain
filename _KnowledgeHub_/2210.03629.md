# ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Princeton University and Google Research introduced ReAct, a paradigm that enables large language models to synergize reasoning and acting by interleaving internal thoughts with external actions. This approach achieved an impressive 71% success rate on the ALFWorld benchmark and 40% on WebShop, significantly outperforming prior methods while notably reducing hallucination errors in knowledge-intensive tasks.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit factual hallucination and error propagation in multi-step reasoning (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) due to a lack of grounding in external information.
- LLMs used for planning and acting in interactive environments typically lack abstract reasoning or a dynamic working memory for complex, long-horizon tasks, leading to purely reactive behaviors.
- The capabilities of reasoning and acting in LLMs have primarily been studied and implemented in isolation, limiting their robustness and adaptability in real-world scenarios.

## Method

- The ==ReAct paradigm== expands the language model's action space to include a 'language space' for generating ==explicit thoughts==, which are interleaved with task-specific actions.
- Leveraging ==few-shot in-context learning== with large language models like PaLM-540B, the system generates sequences of thoughts, actions, and observations to navigate and solve tasks.
- The approach tailors domain-specific action spaces (e.g., Wikipedia API for QA, text-based actions for ALFWorld) and adapts ==thought generation density== based on task characteristics (dense for reasoning, sparse for decision-making).

## Results

- ReAct achieved a **71%** average success rate on ALFWorld and **40%** on WebShop with just one or two few-shot examples, outperforming imitation learning and RL methods requiring 10^3 to 10^5 training instances.
- On knowledge-intensive tasks, ReAct significantly reduced hallucination compared to Chain-of-Thought (**6%** vs. **14%** false positives on HotpotQA), demonstrating superior grounding through external interaction.
- Finetuning smaller PaLM models (8B/62B) with ReAct trajectories drastically improved performance, with finetuned PaLM-62B ReAct surpassing all 540B prompting methods, indicating a more generalizable skill learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving internal reasoning ('thoughts') with external actions creates a dynamic feedback loop, where reasoning guides actions and observations from actions refine subsequent reasoning.
> - Explicit thought processes in language models enhance interpretability, allowing humans to diagnose failures and understand decision-making, which also facilitates human-in-the-loop control.
> - Interacting with external tools (e.g., APIs) allows LLMs to retrieve up-to-date information, significantly reducing hallucination compared to internal-only reasoning and improving fact-groundedness.
