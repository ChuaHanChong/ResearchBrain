# ActiveUMI: Robotic Manipulation with Active Perception from Robot-Free Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai University, Stanford University, and Midea Group developed ActiveUMI, a portable system enabling robots to learn bimanual manipulation skills and active perception from human demonstrations using consumer-grade VR hardware. This method yielded a 70% average success rate on complex tasks and enhanced generalization to novel environments by 40% compared to systems with fixed camera views.

## Problem

- Robot learning faces a data scarcity problem, as collecting large-scale, high-quality, and diverse robotic interaction data is challenging and expensive.
- Existing data collection methods have limitations: in-lab teleoperation is costly and not scalable, human videos suffer from a cross-embodiment gap, and simulation has a sim-to-real gap.
- Current robot data collection systems, including UMI-style approaches, primarily rely on static or wrist-mounted cameras, lacking human-like active egocentric perception through head movements for visual attention and occlusion management.

## Method

- A novel hardware architecture utilizes modified commercial Meta Quest 3s controllers, rigidly mounted on robot grippers, for precise 6-DoF pose tracking and intuitive gripper actuation.
- Integrated active perception captures the operator's head movements via the VR headset's HMD, allowing the robot to learn and replicate dynamic viewpoint control for a dedicated third camera arm.
- The system is designed as a portable, "in-the-wild" solution, with the computational unit and battery contained in a backpack, enabling data collection in diverse unstructured environments with real-time VR visualization.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of 70% across six challenging bimanual tasks, outperforming baselines by 44% compared to a fixed-head camera and 63% compared to a wrist-camera-only setup.
- Demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, maintaining a 56% average success rate in novel environments, a 40% improvement over the fixed-head camera baseline's 16%.
- Showcased high data collection throughput (e.g., 1.49x slower than direct human demonstration for 'Shirt Folding' vs. 2.63x for teleoperation) and superior accuracy with an average Relative Pose Error of 4.0mm, 2.5 times smaller than the original UMI.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active egocentric perception, mirroring human head movements, is crucial for robots to acquire task-critical visual information, manage occlusions, and perform complex manipulation tasks effectively.
> - Consumer-grade VR hardware provides a scalable, low-cost solution for high-fidelity, low-latency, and high-precision data collection, making it feasible to gather diverse datasets "in-the-wild."
> - Mixing ActiveUMI data with even a minimal fraction (1%) of traditional teleoperated data can significantly optimize policy performance and robustness, reducing overall training costs.
