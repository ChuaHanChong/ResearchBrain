# ActiveUMI: Robotic Manipulation with Active Perception from Robot-Free Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai University, Stanford University, and Midea Group developed ActiveUMI, a portable system enabling robots to learn bimanual manipulation skills and active perception from human demonstrations using consumer-grade VR hardware. This method yielded a 70% average success rate on complex tasks and enhanced generalization to novel environments by 40% compared to systems with fixed camera views.

## Problem

- Robot learning faces a data scarcity problem, as collecting large-scale, high-quality, and diverse robotic interaction data is challenging and expensive.
- Existing data collection methods have limitations: in-lab teleoperation is costly and not scalable, human videos suffer from a cross-embodiment gap, and simulation has a sim-to-real gap.
- Current robot data collection systems, including UMI-style approaches, primarily rely on static or wrist-mounted cameras, lacking human-like active egocentric perception through head movements for visual attention and occlusion management.

## Method

- A novel hardware architecture utilizes modified commercial Meta Quest 3s controllers, rigidly mounted on robot grippers, for precise 6-DoF pose tracking and intuitive gripper actuation.
- Integrated active perception captures the operator's head movements via the VR headset's HMD, allowing the robot to learn and replicate dynamic viewpoint control for a dedicated third camera arm.
- The system is designed as a portable, "in-the-wild" solution, with the computational unit and battery contained in a backpack, enabling data collection in diverse unstructured environments with real-time VR visualization.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of 70% across six challenging bimanual tasks, outperforming baselines by 44% compared to a fixed-head camera and 63% compared to a wrist-camera-only setup.
- Demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, maintaining a 56% average success rate in novel environments, a 40% improvement over the fixed-head camera baseline's 16%.
- Showcased high data collection throughput (e.g., 1.49x slower than direct human demonstration for 'Shirt Folding' vs. 2.63x for teleoperation) and superior accuracy with an average Relative Pose Error of 4.0mm, 2.5 times smaller than the original UMI.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active egocentric perception, mirroring human head movements, is crucial for robots to acquire task-critical visual information, manage occlusions, and perform complex manipulation tasks effectively.
> - Consumer-grade VR hardware provides a scalable, low-cost solution for high-fidelity, low-latency, and high-precision data collection, making it feasible to gather diverse datasets "in-the-wild."
> - Mixing ActiveUMI data with even a minimal fraction (1%) of traditional teleoperated data can significantly optimize policy performance and robustness, reducing overall training costs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The "ActiveUMI" paper situates itself at the crucial intersection of robot learning, data collection, and the emerging field of robot foundation models. The overarching goal in modern robotics is to develop generalist policies that can perform a wide array of tasks in diverse, unstructured environments. This ambition is largely inspired by the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), which thrive on massive datasets. However, robot learning faces a significant hurdle: the **data scarcity problem**. Unlike web-scale corpora available for LLMs, collecting large, high-quality, and diverse robotic interaction data is inherently challenging and expensive.

The paper identifies three prevailing sources of robot data, each with limitations:
1. **In-lab teleoperation:** Provides high-fidelity robot-centric data but is costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale to "in-the-wild" diverse environments.
2. **Human videos:** Offers vast amounts of data but suffers from a **cross-embodiment gap**, meaning the kinematics and affordances of a human demonstrator differ significantly from those of a robot.
3. **Simulation:** Can generate limitless data but is plagued by the **sim-to-real gap**, where policies learned in simulation often fail to transfer effectively to real hardware due to physics discrepancies and sensory noise.

To address these limitations, a promising middle ground involves **sensorized hand-held interfaces** that capture action-aligned trajectories, allowing humans to demonstrate tasks more naturally. Previous works such as DexCap, AirExo, DoGlove, Dexop, and particularly the family of Universal Manipulation Interfaces (UMI, DexUMI, FastUMI) fall into this category. The original UMI introduced a simple handheld controller for scalable bimanual data collection, which proved effective in aligning human motion with robot kinematics.

"ActiveUMI" builds directly on this lineage but identifies a critical, unaddressed limitation in prior UMI-style systems: their primary reliance on **wrist-mounted cameras for perception**. While providing an end-effector-centric view, these cameras' viewpoints are constrained by the robot's manipulation needs, not by perceptual objectives. Humans, however, actively move their heads and bodies to manage visual occlusions, gather context, and focus attention for fine manipulation or long-horizon tasks. This human-like **active, egocentric perception** is largely absent in existing robot data collection frameworks.

This paper's core contribution is bridging this "visual gap." By integrating explicit tracking of the operator's head movements via a VR headset, ActiveUMI enables the robot to learn and replicate active viewpoint control. This positions ActiveUMI as a crucial advancement for developing data-efficient and generalizable robot foundation models. It offers a scalable pathway to collect high-quality, human-like demonstration data that captures not just the *what* of manipulation but also the *how* and *where* of visual attention, thereby enhancing the policy's ability to handle complex, real-world challenges like occlusions, deformable objects, and long-horizon tasks. The use of a state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model ($\pi_0$) for policy learning further aligns this work with the frontier of robot foundation model research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind ActiveUMI stems from the pressing need for scalable, high-quality data to train generalist robot policies, especially for emerging robot foundation models, while overcoming the limitations of current data collection paradigms. The paper articulates several key objectives:

1. **Address the "Active Perception Gap" in Robot Data Collection:** The primary motivation is to overcome the limitation of existing teleoperation and demonstration systems that primarily rely on static or wrist-mounted cameras. The authors recognize that human manipulators naturally use active head movements for visual attention, occlusion management, and contextual understanding. The objective is to explicitly capture this human-like active, egocentric perception and integrate it into robot policy learning.

2. **Develop a Portable and Scalable "In-the-Wild" Data Collection System:** Current teleoperation systems are often confined to laboratories, limiting the diversity and scale of collected data. ActiveUMI aims to create a low-cost, high-mobility framework that allows data collection in diverse, unstructured "in-the-wild" environments, thereby increasing the dataset's variability and richness for better generalization.

3. **Ensure Tight Embodiment Alignment with Natural Human Movement:** For human demonstrations to be effectively transferable to robots, the interface must intuitively align human actions with robot kinematics. The objective is to design a system where human movements are directly mirrored by the robot's end-effectors, minimizing the cross-embodiment gap.

4. **Enable Complex Bimanual and Long-Horizon Manipulation Tasks:** The system is specifically designed to handle challenging tasks that require high precision, bimanual coordination, manipulation of deformable or articulated objects, and successful execution over extended time horizons, which are often difficult for systems lacking active perception.

5. **Improve Generalization Capabilities of Learned Policies:** By providing data that includes active perception, the motivation is to train more robust and generalizable policies that can adapt to novel objects, varying object positions, and entirely new environments, moving beyond merely memorizing demonstrations.

6. **Lower the Cost and Increase the Efficiency of Robot Policy Training:** By offering a method for scalable, high-quality data collection that is more efficient than traditional teleoperation and more robot-relevant than human videos, ActiveUMI seeks to reduce the overall cost and time associated with developing capable robot policies and foundation models.

In essence, ActiveUMI aims to create a "universal manipulation interface" that not only records human actions faithfully but also captures the crucial *visual attention* behind those actions, making robot learning more effective, scalable, and adaptable to the complexities of the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ActiveUMI’s methodology is built upon two core principles: (i) tight alignment of the robot’s embodiment with natural human movement, and (ii) enabling active perception by coupling head-ego sensing with wrist-eye control. These principles are realized through a novel hardware architecture, a sophisticated data collection pipeline, and robust calibration techniques.

#### 3.1. Hardware Architecture for Data Collection

The system is designed for high mobility and efficient data capture in diverse "in-the-wild" environments:

- **VR Gripper Controller:** ActiveUMI modifies commercial **Meta Quest 3s controllers**. These controllers provide synchronous, low-latency, and high-precision 6-DoF pose tracking via the headset's inside-out tracking system, which tracks integrated infrared (IR) LEDs. By rigidly mounting these modified controllers directly onto the target robot's existing end-effectors (grippers), the human operator's hand poses precisely mirror the robot's gripper poses. This design offers flexibility, as it can adapt to various robot grippers by simply attaching a modified controller.
- **Gripper Actuation:** A micro-motor integrated into each VR controller drives the open-close motion of the gripper. This allows for intuitive control of the robot's grasp. The design is non-invasive, meaning it attaches an identical copy of the robot's gripper to the operator's controller, rather than replacing the robot's original hardware.
- **Wrist-Mounted Fisheye Cameras:** Each controller is augmented with a fisheye camera. These cameras provide a broad "wrist view" of the immediate operational environment, offering rich visual context to complement the head-mounted perspective.
- **Head-Mounted Display (HMD):** The Meta Quest 3s HMD serves a dual, critical role:
 - **High-Precision Localization Hub:** Its robust SLAM system establishes a stable world coordinate system, concurrently tracking the 6-DoF poses of both the operator's head and the controllers.
 - **Dynamic "Top Camera":** The HMD's front-facing color cameras function as a dynamic, first-person perspective "head camera," intrinsically coupled with the operator’s line of sight.
- **Wearable Device:** To enable true "in-the-wild" operation, the entire computational unit (a small PC and battery) is self-contained in a backpack worn by the operator. This provides complete mobility, liberating the user from stationary workstations.
- **Immersive Data Collection:** For intuitive operator feedback, a 3D model of the robotic arms is rendered within the VR environment. These virtual arms are precisely aligned with the operator's physical controllers, allowing real-time visualization of the robot's intended movements.

#### 3.2. Active Perception for Policy Learning

This is the defining feature of ActiveUMI. Unlike conventional UMI-style systems that rely solely on wrist-mounted cameras, ActiveUMI explicitly captures and utilizes the operator's head movements.

- The real-time 6-DoF pose of the operator’s HMD is recorded as an additional input during data collection.
- This data helps the policy learn the crucial correlation between human visual attention (head movements) and corresponding hand actions.
- During policy deployment on a real robot, the learned policy can then predict a 6-DoF pose for a robot’s head-mounted camera. This allows the robot to actively control its own viewpoint, mimicking the learned human attention patterns. This capability is critical for overcoming visual occlusions, handling deformable objects, and successfully completing complex, long-horizon tasks. The robot setup consists of two 6-DoF arms for bimanual manipulation, and a third 6-DoF arm dedicated to providing the active, mobile viewpoint from its camera feed, simulating the egocentric head camera.

#### 3.3. Calibration Methods for Precise Data Collection

To ensure consistent, high-quality, and precise data, ActiveUMI incorporates several calibration techniques:

- **In-Situ Environment Setup:** Operators can press a button on the controller to reset the 6-DoF zero-point of the world coordinate system. The axes are rendered in VR, allowing intuitive alignment with the physical workspace, ensuring a consistent initial state for each session.
- **Gripper Placeholder:** A physical jig (docking station) for the VR controllers helps establish a consistent starting point. When controllers are placed in the jig, their relative distance and pose are fixed. A designated button press then instantly calibrates the virtual coordinate system to this known physical configuration.
- **Haptic Feedback for Zero-Point Position:** When a gripper approaches within 3cm of the defined zero-point, the controller's motor generates a high-frequency vibration. This tactile cue assists operators in precisely aligning the grippers without relying solely on visual or numerical readouts, improving efficiency and accuracy.

#### 3.4. Policy Learning and Evaluation

For policy learning, ActiveUMI uses $\pi_0$, a state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, as its base. The model is fine-tuned for 50k iterations. All sensor and robot data are collected at 30Hz. The experiments are conducted on a testbed comprising three 6-DoF ARX R5 robotic arms for bimanual tasks and active camera control.

This comprehensive methodology allows ActiveUMI to capture rich, human-like demonstration data, including the critical element of active perception, which is then used to train robust and generalizable robot manipulation policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with ActiveUMI demonstrate its effectiveness in improving robot manipulation policies, particularly through the integration of active perception.

1. **High Success Rate on Challenging In-Domain Tasks:**
 - Policies trained purely on ActiveUMI data achieved an average success rate of **70%** across six challenging bimanual tasks: Block disassembly (precision), Shirt folding (deformable), Rope boxing (long-horizon), Toolbox cleaning (articulated), Bottle placing (robustness to positional variation), and Take Drink from Bag.
 - This indicates that the framework successfully transfers complex human demonstrations to effective visuomotor policies.

2. **Critical Importance of Egocentric Active Perception:**
 - A direct comparison of ActiveUMI (with active head camera) against baselines highlighted the significant impact of active perception:
 - **ActiveUMI (Our Method): 70% average success rate.**
 - **UMI w/ Fixed Head Camera:** 42% average success rate (44% lower than ActiveUMI). This baseline still benefited from a third-person view, but its static nature limited performance.
 - **Wrist-Camera-Only (UMI Baseline):** 26% average success rate (63% lower than ActiveUMI). This configuration, typical of prior UMI-style systems, performed significantly worse, particularly on tasks requiring dynamic visual information.
 - For specific tasks like "Take Drink from Bag," ActiveUMI achieved 80% success, while the wrist-camera-only baseline achieved only 40%. On "Rope Boxing," ActiveUMI reached 70% success compared to 20% for wrist-camera-only.
 - These results strongly support the hypothesis that active viewpoint selection, mirroring human head movements, is crucial for acquiring task-critical information, managing occlusions, and compensating for demonstrator motion noise during data collection.

3. **Optimal Strategy for Mixed Training with Teleoperated Data:**
 - Experiments on the complex "Shirt Folding" task explored the benefit of mixing ActiveUMI data with traditional teleoperated data.
 - Training exclusively on ActiveUMI data achieved an 80% success rate.
 - Mixing ActiveUMI data with 10% teleoperated data increased success to 90%.
 - The optimal strategy involved mixing ActiveUMI data with just **1% teleoperated data**, achieving the highest success rate of **95%**.
 - This finding suggests that ActiveUMI data is highly sample-efficient and can be effectively boosted and fine-tuned with a small fraction of real-world teleoperated demonstrations, significantly lowering the overall cost of developing robust policies.

4. **Strong Generalization Capability to Novel Objects and Scenes:**
 - Policies trained with ActiveUMI data demonstrated strong generalization when tested on novel objects and in new environments:
 - **ActiveUMI:** Retained an average success rate of **56%** in novel environments, a significant portion of its in-domain performance.
 - **UMI w/ Fixed Head Camera:** Performance dropped drastically to a 16% success rate in novel environments.
 - **Wrist-Camera-Only (UMI Baseline):** Performance plummeted to a mere 6% success rate in novel environments.
 - This indicates that active perception allows the policy to be more resilient to visual shifts and environmental changes, validating the quality of "in-the-wild" data collected by ActiveUMI for producing generalizable policies.

5. **High Data Collection Throughput and Accuracy:**
 - **Throughput (Efficiency):** ActiveUMI significantly speeds up data collection compared to conventional teleoperation:
 - For "Rope Boxing," ActiveUMI was 2.06x slower than direct human demonstration, while teleoperation was 3.27x slower.
 - For "Shirt Folding," ActiveUMI was 1.49x slower than human demonstration, compared to 2.63x for teleoperation.
 - This positions ActiveUMI as a practical middle ground, retaining much of the efficiency of natural human motion while being substantially faster than traditional teleoperation.
 - **Accuracy (Relative Pose Error - RPE):** ActiveUMI demonstrated superior accuracy. Its average Relative Pose Error (RPE) was **4.0mm**, which is 2.5 times smaller than the 10.1mm RPE reported for the original UMI system. This improved data quality is attributed to the inherent advantages of the VR tracking system.

In summary, the results conclusively show that ActiveUMI, by integrating active, egocentric perception and a portable design, provides a highly effective, efficient, and accurate framework for collecting diverse, high-quality human demonstration data, leading to significantly more capable and generalizable robot manipulation policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ActiveUMI framework represents a significant step forward in robot learning and data collection, with profound implications for the development and deployment of advanced robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Active Perception Gap:** ActiveUMI directly addresses a critical, often overlooked limitation in current robot learning datasets: the absence of human-like active, egocentric perception. By explicitly capturing the operator's head movements, it enables robots to learn *how to look* – to actively control their viewpoint to gather task-critical information, manage occlusions, and track objects. This is a fundamental capability that is essential for real-world robustness.

2. **Enabling Generalist Robot Foundation Models:** The ability to collect massive, diverse datasets with both action and aligned active visual attention is crucial for training next-generation robot foundation models (like Vision-Language-Action models). ActiveUMI provides a scalable, low-cost pathway to generate the kind of high-fidelity, varied data these models require to learn generalizable skills, thereby accelerating their development and capability.

3. **Improved Performance on Complex Real-World Tasks:** The demonstrated success on challenging bimanual, long-horizon, precision, and deformable object manipulation tasks highlights ActiveUMI's potential. Robots trained with this data can move beyond simple, pre-programmed actions to execute complex sequences that require dynamic adaptation and intelligent visual strategy, making them suitable for a wider range of industrial, service, and potentially domestic applications.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The strong generalization performance to novel objects and environments is a key indicator of ActiveUMI's impact. Policies that can adapt to unseen scenarios are inherently more valuable and practical. This capability reduces the need for extensive retraining for every new object or environment, making robot deployment more efficient and cost-effective.

5. **Cost-Effective and Scalable Data Collection:** By leveraging consumer-grade VR hardware and a portable, "in-the-wild" design, ActiveUMI drastically lowers the barriers to entry for large-scale data collection. It offers a practical middle ground between expensive in-lab teleoperation and less robot-relevant human videos, achieving a much higher throughput and accuracy. The finding that a small amount of teleoperated data can fine-tune policies trained primarily on ActiveUMI data further underscores its efficiency.

6. **Advancing Human-Robot Collaboration and Teleoperation:** The intuitive, immersive VR teleoperation interface, coupled with precise kinematic alignment and haptic feedback, makes data collection more user-friendly and efficient. This could inspire further innovation in human-robot interaction designs, facilitating not only data collection but also real-time human control for tasks beyond current robotic capabilities.

7. **Foundational Research for Future Robotics:** ActiveUMI's contributions lay important groundwork for future robotics research. It provides a robust methodology and empirical evidence for the value of active perception in robot learning, encouraging further exploration into how robots can learn human-like cognitive and perceptual strategies. This paradigm shift could lead to robots that are not only capable but also contextually aware and adaptable, mimicking human intelligence more closely.

In conclusion, ActiveUMI pushes the boundaries of robot learning by addressing the critical need for scalable, high-quality, and perceptually rich data. Its innovative integration of active perception from human demonstrations promises to significantly enhance robot capabilities, making generalizable and highly capable real-world robot policies a more attainable reality.
