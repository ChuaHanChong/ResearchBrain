# Simulation-Ready Cluttered Scene Estimation via Physics-aware Joint Shape and Pose Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign and Meta Reality Labs developed SPARCS, a physics-aware framework that estimates simulation-ready 3D scenes from single RGBD images, jointly optimizing object shapes and poses in cluttered environments. Reconstructions maintain physical stability in simulators like MuJoCo for over one minute, a capability lacking in prior perception-driven methods.

## Problem

- Scene estimation methods often produce geometrically plausible but physically unstable reconstructions unsuitable for direct use in physics simulators.
- Existing physics-aware optimization approaches struggle with computational scalability and robustness when jointly inferring both object shapes and poses from sparse observations, especially in cluttered environments.
- Traditional differentiable simulators often face challenges with non-smooth contact and visibility events, limiting their application in complex, quasistatic cluttered scenes.

## Method

- **SPARCS**, an end-to-end pipeline, initializes scene elements using learning-based methods (SAM2, SAM3D, FoundationPose) from a single RGBD image.
- It employs a physics-constrained numerical optimization approach that jointly infers object shapes and poses using an ==Augmented Lagrangian Method== with a ==globally differentiable contact model (SDRS)==.
- A novel ==structured linear solver== is integrated to efficiently address the high dimensionality and computational cost associated with frictional contacts and joint shape-pose optimization in cluttered scenes.

## Results

- SPARCS produced physically stable scene reconstructions that maintained force equilibrium in MuJoCo simulations for **over one minute**, with low kinetic energy gain (**~10^-2 J**) and drift (**~3 cm**).
- Compared to baseline methods, SPARCS consistently generated simulation-ready scenes, whereas other approaches resulted in severe object penetrations and simulation instability.
- The proposed structured linear solver demonstrated up to an **8.7x** speedup for large scenes (**22 convex hulls**), reducing computation time for the linear solve from approximately **64 seconds to 7 seconds**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating globally differentiable physics models, specifically a shape-differentiable contact model, allows for robust joint shape and pose optimization in cluttered environments.
> - Efficiently solving the large-scale nonlinear program for joint shape-pose inference in cluttered scenes can be achieved by exploiting the structured sparsity of the augmented Lagrangian Hessian.
> - Combining learning-based initializations with subsequent physics-aware optimization can effectively bridge the gap between perceptual accuracy and physical consistency in scene reconstruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Scene estimation is a fundamental problem in robotics and embodied artificial intelligence, crucial for enabling robots to interact with the real world and for facilitating "real-to-sim" transfer, where insights from real-world observations are leveraged to enhance simulated environments. An ideal scene estimator would reconstruct an environment that is not only perceptually accurate but also physically consistent and directly usable within physics simulators for downstream tasks such as motion planning, model predictive control, and policy learning.

Existing scene estimation paradigms include Bayesian inference, deep learning, and numerical optimization. While many approaches assume known object geometries and focus on pose recovery, real-world applications often necessitate inferring both object shapes and poses from sparse observations. Purely perception-driven methods, while robust to occlusion and missing data, frequently produce physically implausible results (e.g., inter-penetrations, unstable configurations) that are not suitable for physics simulators.

Physics-aware numerical optimization offers an advantage by allowing explicit incorporation of physical laws and constraints (e.g., non-penetration, contact consistency, equilibrium). However, prior optimization-based methods have faced several limitations:
- **Computational Cost:** They often involve large-scale nonlinear programming (NLP) with numerous auxiliary variables for contact forces, leading to poor scalability in cluttered scenes.
- **Robustness and Generality:** Many rely on heuristic contact-selection oracles, which can be brittle.
- **Scope Limitation:** Most assume known object geometries, restricting optimization to object poses only. Jointly optimizing shape and pose significantly increases problem dimensionality, making existing techniques computationally challenging.
- **Smoothness Assumptions:** Differentiable simulators often target dynamic trajectories and can struggle with non-smooth contact and visibility events in quasistatic cluttered scenes.

This research addresses these limitations by introducing a "structure-aware" optimization framework. It aims to provide a practical algorithm for joint shape and pose optimization in cluttered environments that explicitly incorporates physical constraints, leading to robust, physically valid, and simulation-ready scene reconstructions from sparse observations. It builds upon recent advancements in shape-differentiable contact models and efficient linear system solvers to achieve scalability and global differentiability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to estimate "simulation-ready" scenes from real-world observations, specifically single RGBD images, for use in downstream robotics tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to overcome specific challenges in the field:

1. **Ensuring Physical Consistency:** A core motivation is to ensure that the estimated object shapes and poses are physically consistent and stable. Current perception-driven methods often produce geometrically plausible but physically unstable scenes, unsuitable for physics simulators. The goal is to enforce non-penetration, contact consistency, and force equilibrium conditions directly within the estimation process.

2. **Addressing Clutter:** Cluttered environments, where multiple objects interact through contact, pose significant challenges for scene estimation. Accurate physical reasoning is essential for manipulating objects in such environments. The research aims to develop a method robust enough to handle complex inter-object contacts and interactions.

3. **Joint Shape and Pose Optimization:** A significant motivation is to jointly infer both the shapes and poses of objects. Many prior optimization-based methods assume known object geometries and optimize only poses. However, in real-world scenarios, object geometries are often unknown or partially observed, making joint inference necessary for comprehensive scene reconstruction. This dramatically increases the dimensionality and complexity of the problem space.

4. **Improving Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** Existing optimization-based approaches, particularly those using monolithic NLP formulations, become computationally prohibitive and scale poorly with scene complexity (i.e., number of interacting objects and shape parameters). The paper aims to develop a structure-aware framework that can efficiently solve the optimization problem, making it tractable for cluttered scenes.

5. **Leveraging Differentiable Physics:** The motivation includes integrating a robust, globally differentiable physics model that can handle arbitrary changes in object geometry and pose. This is crucial for gradient-based optimization in the joint shape-pose space, avoiding issues like vanishing gradients or constraint qualification failures common in non-smooth contact models.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between perception-based scene understanding and physics-based simulation by developing a unified, efficient, and robust optimization framework that can simultaneously recover physically valid shapes and poses of multiple interacting rigid objects from limited visual data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named Simulation-ready Physics-Aware Reconstruction for Cluttered Scenes (SPARCS), is an end-to-end pipeline that combines learning-based initialization with physics-constrained joint shape-pose optimization.

**A. Problem Statement and Representation:**
The problem is formulated as an equality-constrained Nonlinear Program (NLP): `argmin_q,x O(q, x) s.t. C(q, x) = 0`.
- `q` represents the poses (rotations and translations) of all objects.
- `x` represents the shapes of all objects, where each rigid body is modeled as a union of multiple convex hulls. Each convex hull is defined by a set of vertices.
- `O(q, x)` is a differentiable objective function that quantifies perceptual fidelity.
- `C(q, x)` represents globally differentiable physics constraints.

**B. Scene Estimation Pipeline:**
1. **Learning-based Initialization:**
 - From a single RGBD image, SAM2 generates object masks, and SAM3D estimates initial object meshes and point cloud segmentations.
 - FoundationPose refines the initial object poses provided by SAM3D, as SAM3D's pose estimates are noted as inaccurate.
 - For each refined mesh, convex decomposition (using CoACD) is applied to generate the vertices of convex hulls, providing the initial guess for `x`.
2. **Penetration-Free Initialization:** To ensure strict feasibility for the optimization, an initial penetration-free state is created by shrinking colliding object meshes.
3. **Differentiable Objective Function `O(q,x)`:**
 - The objective function is designed to regularize the shapes of the convex hulls based on observed point clouds and initial meshes. It comprises three types of terms, weighted by coefficients `w_1, w_2, w_3`:
 - **Type I:** Penalizes the distance between each convex hull vertex (in world space) and its closest point on the initial SAM3D mesh of the corresponding object.
 - **Type II:** Penalizes the distance between each point in the segmented input point cloud and its closest point on the surface formed by the union of convex hulls of the corresponding object. This is typically assigned a larger weight.
 - **Type III:** Similar to Type II, but penalizes the distance between each vertex of the initial SAM3D mesh and its closest point on the union of convex hull surfaces. This acts as a shape prior for occluded regions.
 - To maintain convergence guarantees typical of ICP-like methods, a heuristic technique is employed: after each optimization subproblem, terms from Type II and Type III that would increase the objective value are selectively deleted until the objective is non-increasing.
4. **Differentiable Texture Refinement (Optional):** After shape and pose optimization, the union of convex hulls is converted to a triangle mesh, UV coordinates are generated (xatlas), and a differentiable renderer (Nvdiffrast) is used to optimize a texture map by minimizing differences with the SAM3D-predicted image.

**C. Optimization in Joint Shape-Pose Space:**
1. **Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM):** The NLP is solved using a structured variant of ALM, with the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) algorithm used for solving each subproblem. This involves iteratively minimizing an augmented Lagrangian function `O(q, x) + λ^T C(q, x) + (ρ/2) ||C(q, x)||^2`.
2. **Physics Constraints (SDRS Contact Model):** The method builds on the separating-plane-based shape-differentiable contact model (SDRS).
 - **Frictionless Case:** The physics constraints are formulated as minimizing the internal and external potential energy (`Ψ = Ψ_g + Ψ_c`).
 - `Ψ_g` (gravitational potential): Based on mass concentrated at convex hull vertices, globally twice-differentiable.
 - `Ψ_c` (collision potential): Introduces separating planes `(n, d)` between each pair of convex hulls. These variables are implicitly minimized out, resulting in a globally twice-differentiable `Ψ_c` that approximates hard collision-free constraints as a complementarity gap `μ` approaches zero. The constraint `C = ∇_q Ψ` enforces force equilibrium.
 - **Frictional Contacts:** Tangential frictional forces `f_∥` are introduced as additional decision variables.
 - Constraints include: orthogonality of normal and tangential forces, friction cone constraints, and tangential force/torque balance for the separating plane (treated as a fictitious zero-mass object).
 - The augmented potential energy is `Ψ(q, x) - Σ <X_ijk, f_∥_ijk,i'j'>`. The constraint `C_bar` is the gradient of this augmented potential with respect to `q`.
3. **Efficient Linear System Solver:** To address the increased dimensionality from friction forces, the method exploits the structured sparsity of the augmented Lagrangian Hessian in the LM subproblems.
 - It uses the Woodbury matrix identity to factor out a rank-limited term associated with `∇C_bar`.
 - Subsequently, it employs a Schur complement solver to decouple the frictional forces between different convex hull pairs, leading to an efficient solution of the linear system, which is a major computational bottleneck.
4. **Separating Plane Aggregation (Regularization):** To encourage smoother object shapes and reduce the number of feasible local minima, a single separating plane is introduced between each pair of *interacting objects* (rather than between all pairs of their constituent convex hulls). This regularizes occluded contact regions by aligning them with a common plane.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SPARCS was conducted on cluttered scenes with up to 5 objects, represented by a total of 22 convex hulls. The experiments focused on the simulation-ready quality, visual fidelity, and computational performance of the method.

**A. Simulation-Ready Quality:**
- **Physical Stability:** SPARCS reconstructions demonstrated robust physical stability. When forwarded into the MuJoCo physics simulator, the estimated object shapes and poses remained in force equilibrium over 1 minute of simulation time. The maximum kinetic energy gain during the first second and the drift distance over the first minute were consistently low (e.g., max kinetic energy ~10^-2 J, max drift distance ~3 cm).
- **Comparison to Baselines:** In contrast, initial shape and pose estimates from SAM3D and FoundationPose consistently contained severe inter-penetrations, leading to simulation instability and failure in MuJoCo (e.g., max kinetic energy ~1-5 J, max drift distance ~30-170 cm). Other recent single-view scene reconstruction methods (Gen3DSR, SceneComplete, MIDI) also failed to produce stable, simulation-ready results, often exhibiting severe penetrations leading to simulator blow-up.

**B. Visual Fidelity:**
- The method achieved comparable PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio) values to the initial SAM3D+FoundationPose estimates when rendering images from the reconstructed scenes and comparing them to the ground truth RGBD image. This indicates that improving physical consistency did not substantially degrade visual accuracy.
- Ablation studies on objective terms (Type I, II, III) revealed that both the main visual matching term (Type II) and shape regularization terms (Type I and III) are necessary. Removing regularization led to unnatural shapes in occluded areas, while removing the primary visual matching term significantly increased point cloud matching errors (average error increased from ~1-2 mm to ~3-6 mm).

**C. Performance:**
- **Convergence:** The algorithm converged within 6-9 ALM (outer) iterations across the evaluated scenarios, with a typical convergence trajectory illustrating a steady decrease in constraint violation and objective value.
- **Computational Cost Breakdown:** The dominant computational cost (approximately 30-50% of wall time) arose from repeated evaluations of the physics constraint and its Jacobian, which involve nested separating-plane optimizations between convex hulls. The linear solve step accounted for another significant portion (around 20-30%).
- **Structured Linear Solver Efficiency:** The proposed structured linear solver (Algorithm 1) demonstrated considerable speedup compared to direct LU factorization. It achieved an average speedup of up to 8.7x for scenes with many parameters (e.g., ~19,932 parameters for 22 convex hulls), reducing the average linear solve time from 63.909 seconds to 7.342 seconds in the largest scenario.

**D. Downstream Applications:**
- The paper demonstrated two robotic manipulation tasks (quasi-static pick-and-place, dynamic pushing/sweeping) in MuJoCo simulations using the reconstructed scenes. These examples illustrated the direct applicability of SPARCS's simulation-ready outputs for complex robotic tasks, highlighting its potential for data collection and policy training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, SPARCS, that addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics and computer vision: generating physically consistent and simulation-ready scene reconstructions from sparse real-world observations, particularly in cluttered environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Advanced Robotic Applications:** By producing scenes directly usable in physics simulators, SPARCS significantly advances the "real-to-sim" paradigm. This is critical for downstream tasks like motion planning, model predictive control, and policy learning, where physically accurate simulations are essential for developing and testing robust robot behaviors. The demonstration of robotic manipulation tasks (pick-and-place, pushing) in reconstructed scenes underscores this impact.

2. **Novel Joint Shape-Pose Optimization:** The work introduces a practical algorithm for numerical optimization in the joint shape-pose space, a capability largely absent in prior physics-aware scene estimation methods that typically assume known geometries or optimize only poses. This joint inference is crucial for comprehensive scene understanding when object shapes are unknown or only partially observed.

3. **Scalability in Cluttered Environments:** SPARCS tackles the computational bottlenecks that previously limited optimization-based methods in cluttered scenes with many interacting objects. By leveraging the shape-differentiable contact model and developing a structure-aware linear system solver, the method achieves computational tractability, scaling favorably with scene complexity (up to 22 convex hulls). This enables applications in more complex and realistic environments than previously feasible.

4. **Robustness through Differentiable Physics:** The integration of a globally differentiable contact model (SDRS) ensures that the optimization process is smooth and robust, even under arbitrary changes in geometry and pose. This reduces issues like vanishing gradients and constraint-qualification failures, contributing to the reliability of the reconstructed scenes.

5. **Reduced Manual Effort for Simulation Setup:** Automating the generation of simulation-ready scenes from raw visual data reduces the significant manual effort typically required to create or adapt virtual environments for robotics research. This could accelerate research cycles in embodied AI and robotics.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotics Research:** Researchers can more easily generate diverse and complex simulated environments from real-world observations, leading to larger datasets for policy learning and more realistic testing grounds for new algorithms.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Physically consistent simulations generated by SPARCS could lead to more effective policies and controllers that transfer better from simulation to real-world robots.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The method opens avenues for further research, including GPU acceleration for greater efficiency, incorporating priors from Vision-Language Models for physical parameters (mass, friction), extending to multi-view inputs, and developing more robust end-to-end image-guided optimization to reduce reliance on initial mesh estimations. The focus on single-view input, while challenging, demonstrates the method's capability in occlusion-heavy scenarios.
