# Object-centric LeJEPA

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich introduce Object-centric LeJEPA, a self-supervised learning framework that uses external object masks from SAM 2 to train visual encoders to learn modular, instance-specific object representations. The method achieved performance comparable to image-level LeJEPA trained on the full COCO dataset, but using only 10% of the data, and demonstrated improved instance-level feature separation (FG-ARI of 0.431 on ADE20k) and tracking (J&F of 0.682 on DAVIS).

## Problem

- Image-level self-supervised learning methods require large datasets and often learn global, context-dependent semantics, hindering the acquisition of modular, instance-specific object representations.
- Previous object-centric learning approaches struggled with instability when jointly learning scene partitioning and object representation, often relying on frozen pre-trained features or heuristic object proposals.
- Existing image-level methods tend to embed instance-specific information in earlier layers, making final-layer representations primarily semantic and less discriminative for individual instances.

## Method

- **Object-centric LeJEPA** decouples scene partitioning from representation learning by using pre-computed, 'hard' object masks generated by an off-the-shelf model (==SAM 2==) during training.
- It extends the ==LeJEPA== framework's ==anti-collapse objective== to operate on variable-sized sets of object representations, ensuring semantic coherence across views, atop a ==Vision Transformer (ViT)== backbone.
- An additional ==instance-separating loss== (a supervised ==contrastive InfoNCE== formulation) is integrated to encourage patches of the same object to cluster together while distinguishing them from other objects or background within a single view.

## Results

- Object-LeJEPA, trained on **10%** of COCO, achieved performance comparable to or exceeding image-level LeJEPA trained on the full COCO dataset across various downstream tasks, demonstrating significant data efficiency.
- The method demonstrated superior instance-level feature separation, achieving an FG-ARI of **0.431** and mean IoU of **0.355** on ADE20k instance clustering, outperforming all COCO-trained baselines and DINOv3.
- It led among COCO-trained models on dense semantic tasks (mIoU of **0.418** on ADE20k semantic segmentation) and object-level tasks (J&F of **0.682** on DAVIS tracking, **0.476** balanced accuracy on NAVI re-identification).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric self-supervised learning can achieve significantly higher data efficiency, learning robust representations with a fraction of the data required by image-level methods.
> - Leveraging external, pre-computed object masks from models like SAM 2 provides stability for training object representations from scratch, avoiding the instability of joint partitioning and representation learning.
> - Combining an object-level alignment loss with an instance-separating contrastive loss effectively integrates both semantic coherence and instance specificity into the learned representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of self-supervised learning (SSL) has developed various methods for training image encoders to produce robust features for downstream tasks without relying on extensive human annotations. A prominent approach involves training image encoders to align representations of different augmented views of the same image, as exemplified by frameworks like LeJEPA. However, traditional image-level SSL methods typically require large datasets and may encounter challenges in compositional generalization. When random augmentations generate views that capture different objects within a scene, an image-level alignment objective can compel the encoder to focus on global, context-dependent semantics, potentially hindering the learning of distinct object identities.

In response to these limitations, a growing line of research explores region- and object-level self-supervised pretraining. This paradigm aims to align features of corresponding regions or objects across views, rather than whole scenes. Previous attempts at object-centric learning often tried to jointly solve the challenges of scene partitioning and object representation learning in an end-to-end manner. This joint approach, however, proved unstable for natural images unless it relied on features extracted from frozen large-scale pre-trained encoders. Methods like Slot Attention, while enabling joint learning, restricted both partitioning and representation to what was expressible within the frozen feature space. Other approaches incorporated object proposals heuristically or via soft assignments, which could lead to "information leakage" across partitions, undermining clean object separation.

This work positions itself by addressing the instability in joint partitioning and representation learning. Instead of attempting to learn scene partitioning in a self-supervised manner, it explicitly takes object masks as given during training. It utilizes readily available, pre-computed, and "hard" object masks generated by off-the-shelf models like SAM 2. This design choice decouples the representation learning from the partitioning task, allowing the encoder to be trained from scratch without the constraints of frozen features. The method extends the LeJEPA framework, whose anti-collapse objective is adapted to operate on variable-sized sets of objects rather than whole images. Furthermore, it integrates an additional instance-separating loss to ensure that distinct objects, even of the same category, remain distinguishable within a scene. By adopting this strategy, the authors aim to achieve greater data efficiency and generate more modular, instance-specific object representations that can generalize effectively across diverse scenes and downstream tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to address the data inefficiency observed in many image-level self-supervised learning methods. Current image-level approaches align representations of entire scenes, which may contain multiple objects. When augmented views capture different parts of a scene with different objects, forcing their representations to align can compel the model to learn context-dependent, global semantics. This limits the model's ability to learn robust, modular representations of individual objects that are independent of their surrounding scene context.

The authors hypothesize that object-centric learning offers a path to greater data efficiency. By aligning representations at the level of individual objects, a model can exploit the compositional structure inherent in scenes. This allows the model to connect different instances of the same object across various scenes, theoretically reducing the need to re-learn the object in each new context and thereby promoting compositional generalization. Such modular representations are also anticipated to advance capabilities in relational reasoning, interpretability, and temporal dynamic modeling.

The key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop an Object-centric Self-Supervised Learning Framework**: To create a method that aligns representations of individual objects across augmented views rather than aligning whole images. This involves adapting existing successful self-supervised learning paradigms to an object level.
2. **Overcome Instability in Joint Partitioning and Representation Learning**: To circumvent the known instability of jointly learning both how to partition a scene into objects and how to represent those objects in a self-supervised manner. The chosen strategy is to externalize the scene partitioning step by utilizing pre-computed, fixed object masks (specifically, proposals from SAM 2) during training. This allows the encoder to be trained from scratch without being constrained by frozen pre-trained features.
3. **Integrate Semantic and Instance-Specific Information**: To ensure that the learned object representations encode both semantic coherence (where similar objects are close in the embedding space) and instance specificity (where distinct instances, even of the same category, remain separable). The authors observe that image-level methods often embed instance-specific information in earlier layers, with final layers becoming largely semantic. This work aims to embed both types of information directly into the final-layer representations.
4. **Extend LeJEPA to the Object Level**: Specifically, to adapt the LeJEPA framework's distributional anti-collapse objective and alignment term to work with variable-sized sets of object representations.
5. **Introduce an Instance-Separating Loss**: To supplement the object-centric LeJEPA loss with a contrastive loss that explicitly encourages patches belonging to the same object instance within a view to cluster together, while distinguishing them from patches of other objects or the background.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to demonstrate that object-centric learning, when combined with reliable, external mask proposals, can yield more data-efficient, modular, and semantically/instance-aware visual representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Object-centric LeJEPA, is designed to learn object representations by combining an object-level alignment loss with an instance-separating loss, leveraging external mask proposals during training. The overall framework utilizes an image encoder (Vision Transformer, ViT) to extract patch features, which are then processed by two specialized heads: a semantic head and an instance head.

**Object Representation Extraction:**
The method relies on pre-computed object masks. For each training image, masks are generated once using the SAM 2.1 Hiera-Large automatic mask generator, prompted with a 16x16 grid of point cues. Masks with a predicted-IoU threshold of 0.8 and a stability-score threshold of 0.92 are retained, and the number of masks per image is capped at 64. Masks are projected onto augmented views, and an object is considered present in a view if its projected mask covers an area of at least 16x16 pixels.

- **Semantic Object Representations (`z`)**: For each identified object mask, a function `f` aggregates the patch features `g(x)` (from the ViT backbone `g`) that fall within its boundaries. This aggregation process involves:
 1. Calculating a weighted mean of independently projected patch features, where weights are derived from the patch-wise average-pooled object mask.
 2. Applying masked cross-attention, which ensures that keys and values from patches outside the current object's mask are excluded.
 3. Adding a residual connection from the weighted mean, followed by a residual MLP, to produce the final semantic object representation `z`.
 These representations `z` are then projected by a three-layer MLP to a lower-dimensional space (`~z`, dimension 64) for the object-centric LeJEPA loss.

- **Instance-level Object Predictions (`y`)**: For each individual image patch `i`, an MLP `ϕ` is applied to its patch feature `g(x)_i`, followed by L2 normalization. This produces an instance-level object prediction `y_i` for that patch.

**Object-centric LeJEPA Loss (`L_ObjectLeJEPA`):**
This loss comprises two terms:
- **Alignment Term (`L_pred`)**: This term encourages representations of the same object to be similar across different augmented views. For an object `k` present in at least two views, the `L_pred` calculates the squared L2 distance between the projected semantic representation `~z_n,v,k` of object `k` in view `v` and the average `μ_n,k` of `~z_n,v,k` across all views where object `k` is present.
- **Regularization Term (`L_SIGReg`)**: To prevent representational collapse, this term regularizes the projected object representations (`~z`) towards an isotropic Gaussian distribution. Unlike image-level LeJEPA, which applies this to representations within a batch of images, Object-centric LeJEPA applies it to all object representations extracted from all images within a batch. The Epps-Pulley test statistic is used for this, scaled by the batch size `B` to maintain stable gradient magnitudes.
The `L_ObjectLeJEPA` is a weighted sum: `L_pred + λ_LeJEPA * L_SIGReg`, where `λ_LeJEPA` is set to 0.05.

**Instance-level Loss (`L_instance`):**
This contrastive loss is designed to foster instance-specific discrimination within a single view. For every patch `i`, it aims to align its instance-level prediction `y_n,v,i` with those of other patches `p` belonging to the same dominant object mask, while simultaneously pushing them away from `y_n,v,a` of other patches `a` that belong to different objects or the background within the same view. A patch is assigned to a mask if it covers at least 50% of the patch area. Background patches act as negatives but are not used as anchors. The loss is computed using a supervised contrastive formulation (InfoNCE) with a temperature `τ = 0.1`.

**Total Training Objective:**
The final loss function is the sum of the two object-centric losses: `L = L_ObjectLeJEPA + L_instance`.

**Training and Inference:**
Models are trained on COCO for 150 epochs using a ViT-Base backbone (patch size 16x16). Training involves AdamW optimizer, a batch size of 256, a cosine learning rate schedule with a linear warm-up, and mixed-precision training. Data augmentation follows the standard LeJEPA pipeline, including 2 global views (256x256) and 8 local views (128x128). Critically, at inference time, object masks are not required; the trained ViT backbone `g` directly produces informative patch features.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted a comparative evaluation across various downstream tasks, benchmarking Object-LeJEPA against image-level LeJEPA, SlotMIM (150 and 800 epochs), and DINOv3 (as an upper bound). Models were trained on subsets of COCO and evaluated on tasks designed to test generalization beyond the pretraining domain.

**Data Efficiency:**
- A central finding was the significant data efficiency of Object-LeJEPA. When trained on only 10% of the COCO dataset, a ViT-B Object-LeJEPA model achieved performance levels comparable to or slightly exceeding those of an image-level LeJEPA model trained on the *entire* COCO dataset across all evaluated downstream tasks (DAVIS tracking, ImageNet-1k classification, ADE20k segmentation, and NAVI re-identification). For instance, it reached 0.648 J&F on DAVIS tracking, surpassing image-level LeJEPA's 0.613 with 10 times less data. This directly supports the hypothesis of improved data efficiency through object-centric learning.

**Instance-Awareness Probing (ADE20k):**
- **K-Means Clustering**: Object-LeJEPA demonstrated superior instance-level feature separation. When clustering frozen patch features into object instances, it achieved an FG-ARI of 0.431 and a mean IoU of 0.355, outperforming all COCO-trained baselines and also DINOv3 (FG-ARI 0.357, mIoU 0.300).
- **Quadratic Probe**: For predicting whether two patches share the same instance, Object-LeJEPA achieved 0.915 accuracy and 0.954 AUC, effectively matching the performance of DINOv3 (0.914 accuracy, 0.957 AUC) and surpassing other baselines. These results indicate that the method successfully encodes object membership information into the final-layer representations.

**Dense Semantic Tasks:**
- **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20k)**: Object-LeJEPA led among the COCO-trained models with a mIoU of 0.418, outperforming SlotMIM (0.409) and image-level LeJEPA (0.339). DINOv3 remained ahead at 0.500.
- **Label Propagation (DAVIS)**: Object-LeJEPA showed improved tracking performance with a J&F mean of 0.682, significantly higher than image-level LeJEPA (0.613) and SlotMIM (0.629), and narrowed the gap to DINOv3 (0.715). This indicates that the patch representations are both temporally stable and object-discriminative.

**Object-level Tasks:**
- **Object Classification (ADE20k)**: Object-LeJEPA, using its native semantic object representations (`z*`), achieved the highest top-1 balanced accuracy among COCO-trained models (0.250), surpassing its own average-pooled patch features (0.212) and other baselines, although still trailing DINOv3 (0.367).
- **Object Re-Identification (NAVI)**: Object-LeJEPA was the strongest performer among the COCO-trained models. Its averaged patch-object features yielded a balanced accuracy of 0.476 (1-shot), notably outperforming its native semantic object embeddings (`z*`, 0.380). This suggests that for re-identification, local information from patch features is more critical than the potentially context-influenced `z*` representations.

**Image-level Task (ImageNet-1k Classification):**
- Despite being object-centric, Object-LeJEPA's globally average-pooled patch features achieved a top-1 balanced accuracy of 0.539, improving over image-level LeJEPA (0.473) and approaching SlotMIM trained for 800 epochs (0.552).

**Loss & Mask Ablation:**
- The object alignment loss (`L_ObjectLeJEPA`) alone already surpassed image-level LeJEPA across tasks.
- The instance separation loss (`L_instance`) specifically boosted instance-discrimination tasks (tracking, re-identification) but was less effective in isolation for semantic tasks.
- Combining both losses generally resulted in the best performance across tasks, indicating their complementary roles.
- Replacing unsupervised SAM masks with ground-truth COCO instance masks during training yielded strikingly similar performance, with neither consistently dominating. This suggests that the quality of SAM-generated masks is sufficient for learning strong representations without requiring costly human annotations.

**Visual Results (Appendix):**
Qualitative assessments on out-of-distribution objects (fish) demonstrated Object-LeJEPA's ability to separate instances into distinct clusters more effectively than other COCO-trained models. Its intra-image similarity maps also exhibited sharper object boundaries, further supporting its instance-separating capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of self-supervised learning by demonstrating a framework for object-centric representation learning that offers several significant advancements and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Data Efficiency**: The most prominent finding is the empirical demonstration that object-centric self-supervised learning can achieve performance comparable to image-level methods with a substantial reduction in training data. Achieving the performance of a full COCO-trained image-level LeJEPA model with only 10% of COCO data highlights a more efficient learning paradigm, which is crucial for resource-constrained environments or domains with limited data availability.

2. **Modular and Disentangled Representations**: By focusing alignment at the object level, the method encourages the learning of representations that are more modular and disentangle object identity from scene context. This leads to improved compositional generalization, where an object's representation is consistent regardless of the scene it appears in. Such representations are valuable for tasks requiring understanding of scene composition and object interactions.

3. **Integration of Semantic and Instance Information**: The approach successfully integrates both semantic coherence and fine-grained instance specificity into the final-layer representations. This resolves the potential tension between these two types of information and simplifies the use of learned features for diverse downstream tasks without the need to identify specific intermediate layers for different granularities of information.

4. **Practicality and Scalability with Off-the-Shelf Masks**: A key practical innovation is the effective use of cheap, readily available mask proposals from models like SAM 2. This sidesteps the complex and unstable problem of jointly learning scene partitioning and object representations in a self-supervised manner. By decoupling these tasks, the method avoids reliance on frozen foundation models or costly human annotations, making it more scalable and accessible. The ablation study confirming that SAM masks are largely comparable to ground-truth masks underscores this practicality.

5. **Broad Applicability to Downstream Tasks**: The improved performance across a diverse set of benchmarks, including instance-aware clustering, semantic segmentation, video object tracking, object re-identification, and image classification, suggests that Object-centric LeJEPA yields robust and versatile features. This broad applicability indicates its potential to benefit a wide range of computer vision applications, particularly those requiring fine-grained object understanding and discrimination.

6. **Advancement in Foundational SSL**: The extension of LeJEPA's principled anti-collapse objective to the object level provides a robust theoretical and empirical framework for future object-centric self-supervised learning research. This could inspire further development of object-level objectives and regularization techniques.

The work lays a foundation for future research in object-centric learning, particularly regarding the integration of dynamic masking or adaptive partitioning mechanisms. It could contribute to advancements in areas such as relational reasoning, where understanding interactions between distinct objects is critical, and interpretability, by providing clearer, object-level explanations for model decisions. Furthermore, its data efficiency holds promise for robotic perception and temporal dynamic modeling in scenarios with limited training data.
