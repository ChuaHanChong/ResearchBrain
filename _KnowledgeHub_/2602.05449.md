# DisCa: Accelerating Video Diffusion Transformers with Distillation-Compatible Learnable Feature Caching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce DisCa, a framework that accelerates large video diffusion transformers by combining a novel distillation-compatible learnable feature caching mechanism with a more stable restricted MeanFlow distillation approach. DisCa achieves an 11.8x acceleration ratio on a state-of-the-art video DiT model while maintaining near-lossless generation quality and improved memory efficiency.

## Problem

- Traditional training-free feature caching methods struggle to capture complex, high-dimensional feature evolution in diffusion models, leading to semantic and detail degradation.
- Applying existing feature caching to step-distilled models, which have sparser sampling trajectories, exacerbates quality loss due to larger prediction errors.
- Aggressive step-distillation methods like MeanFlow often cause numerical instability, training divergence, and generative artifacts when applied to large-scale, complex video generation models.

## Method

- ==Restricted MeanFlow== limits the sampling range of the mean velocity interval during ==distillation==, preventing aggressive compression and enhancing stability for video models.
- A ==Learnable Feature Caching== mechanism replaces heuristic prediction functions with a lightweight neural Predictor, trained to accurately forecast high-dimensional features.
- ==Generative Adversarial Training== is integrated into the Predictor training using a ==Multi-Scale Discriminator== to preserve semantic structure and high-frequency details, mitigating blurriness and loss of fidelity.

## Results

- Restricted MeanFlow achieved a **70.4%** Semantic Score for 20-step generation (R=0.2), outperforming original MeanFlow (**66.6%**) by **3.8** absolute percentage points and mitigating visual artifacts.
- DisCa attained an **11.8x** acceleration ratio on a 129-frame, **704x704** HunyuanVideo DiT model, preserving a **69.3%** Semantic Score and **81.1%** Quality Score with minimal degradation compared to baseline.
- DisCa exhibited high memory efficiency with only **0.43GB** extra VRAM overhead, significantly less than other caching methods like TaylorSeer (**+33.49GB**) and FORA (**+27.39GB**), making it suitable for distributed video generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggressive step-distillation methods, while effective for images, require restriction for stable and high-quality video generation, as shown by Restricted MeanFlow's improved performance.
> - A lightweight neural network can learn to effectively forecast high-dimensional features across timesteps, even with sparse sampling, outperforming handcrafted prediction functions.
> - Incorporating Generative Adversarial Training in feature caching is crucial for preserving fine-grained details and semantic consistency in generated content under high acceleration ratios.
> - A single-tensor cache structure significantly reduces VRAM consumption, which is essential for the practical deployment of high-resolution, long-duration video generation models.
