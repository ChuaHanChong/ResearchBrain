# DisCa: Accelerating Video Diffusion Transformers with Distillation-Compatible Learnable Feature Caching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce DisCa, a framework that accelerates large video diffusion transformers by combining a novel distillation-compatible learnable feature caching mechanism with a more stable restricted MeanFlow distillation approach. DisCa achieves an 11.8x acceleration ratio on a state-of-the-art video DiT model while maintaining near-lossless generation quality and improved memory efficiency.

## Problem

- Traditional training-free feature caching methods struggle to capture complex, high-dimensional feature evolution in diffusion models, leading to semantic and detail degradation.
- Applying existing feature caching to step-distilled models, which have sparser sampling trajectories, exacerbates quality loss due to larger prediction errors.
- Aggressive step-distillation methods like MeanFlow often cause numerical instability, training divergence, and generative artifacts when applied to large-scale, complex video generation models.

## Method

- ==Restricted MeanFlow== limits the sampling range of the mean velocity interval during ==distillation==, preventing aggressive compression and enhancing stability for video models.
- A ==Learnable Feature Caching== mechanism replaces heuristic prediction functions with a lightweight neural Predictor, trained to accurately forecast high-dimensional features.
- ==Generative Adversarial Training== is integrated into the Predictor training using a ==Multi-Scale Discriminator== to preserve semantic structure and high-frequency details, mitigating blurriness and loss of fidelity.

## Results

- Restricted MeanFlow achieved a **70.4%** Semantic Score for 20-step generation (R=0.2), outperforming original MeanFlow (**66.6%**) by **3.8** absolute percentage points and mitigating visual artifacts.
- DisCa attained an **11.8x** acceleration ratio on a 129-frame, **704x704** HunyuanVideo DiT model, preserving a **69.3%** Semantic Score and **81.1%** Quality Score with minimal degradation compared to baseline.
- DisCa exhibited high memory efficiency with only **0.43GB** extra VRAM overhead, significantly less than other caching methods like TaylorSeer (**+33.49GB**) and FORA (**+27.39GB**), making it suitable for distributed video generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggressive step-distillation methods, while effective for images, require restriction for stable and high-quality video generation, as shown by Restricted MeanFlow's improved performance.
> - A lightweight neural network can learn to effectively forecast high-dimensional features across timesteps, even with sparse sampling, outperforming handcrafted prediction functions.
> - Incorporating Generative Adversarial Training in feature caching is crucial for preserving fine-grained details and semantic consistency in generated content under high acceleration ratios.
> - A single-tensor cache structure significantly reduces VRAM consumption, which is essential for the practical deployment of high-resolution, long-duration video generation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements with Diffusion Models (DMs), which are now capable of generating high-quality content across various modalities, including images, video, audio, and text. To enhance the fidelity and complexity of generated outputs, DMs, particularly Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), have rapidly scaled in size. This increase in model scale, however, is accompanied by a substantial rise in computational demands, leading to high inference costs that hinder practical deployment.

Existing acceleration techniques for DMs primarily fall into two categories: Sampling Timestep Reduction and Denoising Network Acceleration. Sampling timestep reduction methods, such as DDIM, DPM-Solver series, Flow Matching, and step distillation techniques (e.g., Consistency Models, Shortcut, and MeanFlow), aim to reduce the number of iterative denoising steps. MeanFlow, a representative step distillation method, shifts the sampling objective from instantaneous to average velocity, demonstrating performance in image generation. However, its application to large-scale video generation models has shown limitations due to numerical errors from Jacobian-vector product (JVP) operations and an aggressive design for highly compressed distillation.

Denoising network acceleration methods focus on improving the computational efficiency of a single denoising pass. These include model compression techniques (e.g., knowledge distillation, pruning, token reduction, quantization) and feature caching. Feature caching methods aim to reuse previously computed features across timesteps, offering a training-free acceleration. Early approaches involved direct reuse or simple interpolation. More advanced techniques, such as TaylorSeer, attempt to forecast future features using Taylor series expansion to reduce cache errors. However, these handcrafted prediction functions often exhibit limitations in fully capturing the high-dimensional feature evolution in DMs, leading to a loss of semantic and fine-grained details.

A recognized challenge in the current landscape is the incompatibility between traditional training-free feature caching and training-aware step distillation. After step distillation, the sampling trajectory points become sparser, leading to significant inter-step differences that cause traditional caching mechanisms to degrade further. This paper addresses this compatibility gap by proposing a novel approach that integrates a learnable feature caching mechanism with a modified, more stable step distillation strategy, specifically tailored for the complexities of large-scale video Diffusion Transformers. The work aims to combine the benefits of both acceleration paradigms to push the boundaries of computational efficiency while maintaining generation quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly accelerate the inference process of large-scale video Diffusion Transformers while preserving or enhancing the quality of generated video content. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations in current acceleration methodologies:

1. **Mitigate Quality Degradation in Traditional Feature Caching:** Current training-free feature caching methods, even those employing advanced forecasting techniques like Taylor series expansion, inherently rely on fixed, handcrafted prediction functions. These functions are limited in their capacity to fully capture the intricate, high-dimensional evolutionary trends of features within diffusion models. This limitation often results in a noticeable loss of high-frequency details and semantic information in the generated content, particularly under high compression ratios. The motivation is to overcome this by introducing a more adaptable, data-driven prediction mechanism.

2. **Address the Challenge of Feature Caching with Step-Distilled Models:** Diffusion models that have undergone step distillation exhibit sparser sampling trajectories. This sparsity means that the differences between features at adjacent timesteps are more pronounced compared to undistilled models that use many inference steps. Consequently, applying traditional feature caching methods to step-distilled models leads to more severe quality degradation, as simple reuse or interpolation of features becomes less accurate. The research is motivated to develop a feature caching mechanism that is compatible with and effective on these highly compressed, distilled models.

3. **Enhance Stability and Quality of Step Distillation for Large Video Models:** While step distillation methods like MeanFlow have shown promise in accelerating image generation, applying them to the more complex domain of large-scale video generation models presents challenges. The inherent complexity of video data, combined with the numerical errors introduced by operations such as Jacobian-vector products in MeanFlow's original formulation, can lead to training instability, divergence, and the production of generative artifacts, especially when targeting highly aggressive compression ratios (e.g., one-step inference). A key motivation is to devise a more conservative and stable distillation strategy specifically for high-quality video generation.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:

- To introduce a **distillation-compatible learnable feature caching mechanism (DisCa)** that utilizes a lightweight neural predictor instead of traditional heuristic methods. This predictor is designed to accurately capture the evolution of high-dimensional features during diffusion sampling, especially in scenarios involving sparse sampling steps due to distillation.
- To propose a **Restricted MeanFlow approach** that improves the stability and quality of step distillation for large-scale video models. This involves pruning overly aggressive compression scenarios in the MeanFlow training process by limiting the sampling range of average velocities.
- To integrate these dual improvements—learnable feature caching and stable step distillation—to achieve significantly higher acceleration ratios (e.g., up to 11.8×) for large-scale video generation models while maintaining high generation quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed DisCa (Distillation-Compatible Learnable Feature Caching), integrates two main components: a novel Restricted MeanFlow approach for stable step distillation and a lightweight learnable neural predictor for feature caching.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Context

The paper first establishes foundational concepts, including Diffusion Models (DMs) and their iterative denoising process, the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, and Flow Matching, which frames diffusion as learning a vector field between noise and data. It also reviews naive feature caching (direct reuse across timesteps), TaylorSeer (forecasting with Taylor series expansion), and MeanFlow (distilling DMs to learn mean velocity fields). MeanFlow's target optimization involves Jacobian-vector product operations, which are noted to introduce numerical errors, particularly in complex models.

#### 3.2. Restricted MeanFlow

The MeanFlow distillation, originally designed for aggressive one-step image generation, defines the mean velocity interval $I = (t - r)$ within the range $$. For large-scale video generation models, this aggressive compression can lead to numerical instability and significant degradation in quality during training due to the complexity of the data and the difficulty of learning highly compressed transformations.

To address this, the paper introduces **Restricted MeanFlow**. This approach modifies the mean velocity interval by introducing a "restrict factor" $R \in (0, 1)$, so that $I = (t - r) \in [0, R]$. By setting $R$ to a value less than 1 (e.g., 0.2 or 0.4), the training process explicitly prunes the overly aggressive, highly compressed scenarios. This limitation on the sampling range for average velocities facilitates a more stable and less prone to divergence distillation process, aiming to preserve high generation quality even with reduced sampling steps. The goal is no longer solely one-step distillation but rather high-quality few-step inference.

#### 3.3. Learnable Feature Caching

Building upon a "cache-then-forecast" paradigm, the paper proposes a lightweight neural predictor to capture and forecast high-dimensional features, moving beyond handcrafted prediction functions.

- **Inference Process**:
 1. During inference, a large-scale Diffusion Model (M) first performs a full computation step for a given timestep $t_i$. The output, representing the denoising network's prediction (e.g., mean velocity $u$), is cached as $C(x_{t_i}) = M_{\theta_M}(x_{t_i}, r_i, t_i, c_{t_i})$.
 2. For the subsequent $N-1$ inference steps, instead of performing a full computation with M, a lightweight neural **Predictor (P)** is utilized. This predictor takes the cached feature $C$, the current noisy latent $x_{t'}$, and corresponding timestep information $(r', t', c_{t'})$ to predict the required denoising output: $u(x_{t'}, t', r') = P_{\theta_P}(C, x_{t'}, r', t', c_{t'})$. The predictor essentially forecasts the feature evolution based on the previously computed cache.

- **Predictor Training**:
 The predictor is trained to emulate the behavior of the large Diffusion Model.
 1. The large DM (M) first performs one sampling step to generate a reference cache $C = M_{\theta_M}(x_t, r, t, c_t)$. Here, $x_t$ is an interpolation between data and noise, and $(t, r)$ are sampled paired timesteps under the Restricted MeanFlow constraint.
 2. A subsequent timestep pair $(t', r')$ is sampled, where $(t', r') = (t - \Delta, r - \Delta)$, with $\Delta$ being a small timestep bias.
 3. The Predictor $P_{\theta_P}$ then computes its prediction $u_{pred} = P_{\theta_P}(C, x_{t'}, r', t', c_{t'})$.
 4. The large DM $M_{\theta_M}$ computes the ground truth $u_{tar} = M_{\theta_M}(x_{t'}, r', t', c_{t'})$.
 5. The primary optimization objective is an L2 loss (Mean Squared Error) between the predictor's output and the ground truth: $L(\theta_P) = E ||u_{pred} - u_{tar}||^2$.

- **Generative Adversarial Training**: To further enhance the predictor's ability to generate high-frequency details and overcome potential semantic loss or blurriness, a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) training scheme is integrated.
 1. A Multi-Scale Discriminator (D) with Spectral Normalization and Hinge Loss is employed.
 2. The discriminator's loss ($L_D$) aims to distinguish between outputs from the large DM (M, treated as real) and the predictor (P, treated as fake).
 3. The predictor's loss ($L_P$) combines the MSE loss with an adversarial term that encourages it to generate outputs that fool the discriminator. The large DM (M) itself acts as the feature extractor (F) for the discriminator in feature space.

- **Predictor Architecture Design**: The predictor P is designed as a small stack of DiT Blocks. This choice leverages the robust processing capabilities of the Decoder-Only architecture of DiTs. The predictor is deliberately kept lightweight, typically constituting less than 4% of the total size of the large-scale DM, ensuring that its computational overhead is minimal during inference.

- **Memory-Efficient Feature Caching**: Unlike multi-layer caching schemes that store features for each layer of the DM, DisCa maintains only a single tensor as the cache throughout the inference procedure. This design choice is enabled by the powerful learning capability of the neural predictor, which can extract sufficient information from a single cached feature. This memory efficiency is critical for deploying high-resolution, long-duration video generation models, especially in distributed parallel computing environments.

In summary, DisCa addresses the limitations of previous methods by making distillation more stable for video and by using a data-driven learnable predictor for efficient feature forecasting, specifically designed to be compatible with the sparse trajectories resulting from distillation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates DisCa using the HunyuanVideo model, a large-scale video Diffusion Transformer, in both text-to-video (T2V) and image-to-video (I2V) generation tasks. Evaluations are conducted using VBench scores (Semantic, Quality, Total), along with PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS for I2V.

#### 4.1. Restricted MeanFlow Performance

- **Quantitative Results (Table 1)**: Experiments on T2V generation at 20 and 10 sampling steps demonstrate that Restricted MeanFlow consistently outperforms the original MeanFlow. For 20 steps, the Restricted MeanFlow (R=0.2) achieved a Semantic score of 70.4%, an improvement of 5.7% over the original MeanFlow's 66.6%. At 10 steps, this semantic score advantage increased to 12.0%, with Restricted MeanFlow (R=0.2) scoring 68.2% compared to MeanFlow's 60.9%. Smaller improvements were also noted in Quality and Total scores. The Semantic score, which is sensitive to distortions and controllability, indicated a notable reduction in artifacts.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 3)**: Visual comparisons showed that the original MeanFlow at both 20 and 10 steps produced noticeable distortions, malformations, and blending issues in generated videos (e.g., "A bottle.", "A bear and a zebra.", "A blue car.", "A cute happy Corgi..."). In contrast, Restricted MeanFlow consistently maintained higher visual quality, demonstrating its effectiveness in stabilizing the distillation process for complex video generation.

#### 4.2. Distillation-Compatible Learnable Cache (DisCa) Performance

- **Quantitative Results (Table 2)**:
 - DisCa demonstrated performance surpassing existing acceleration methods across various acceleration ratios. At a 7.56× acceleration, DisCa (R=0.2, N=2) achieved a Semantic score of 70.8%, which is an improvement compared to the original 50-step model's 66.7% after CFG distillation.
 - At a high acceleration ratio of 11.8× (R=0.2, N=4), DisCa maintained a Semantic score of 69.3%, a Quality score of 81.1%, and a Total score of 78.8%. This represents a minimal degradation compared to the unaccelerated original model (50 steps), with drops of 5.7% in Semantic, 0.5% in Quality, and 1.4% in Total scores, respectively. This performance was also noted to be an improvement over the 50-step CFG distilled model.
 - In contrast, other training-free caching methods (e.g., ∆-DiT, PAB, FORA) showed significant quality degradation (over 20% semantic drop) at comparable high acceleration ratios. TaylorSeer, while performing comparatively better among training-free methods, still incurred a 13.3% semantic loss at high acceleration.
- **Memory Efficiency (Table 2, Figure 7)**: DisCa exhibited memory efficiency, adding only approximately 0.43 GB of VRAM overhead. This contrasts with multi-layer caching methods like TaylorSeer, which required an additional 33.49 GB, highlighting DisCa's suitability for high-resolution, long-duration video generation with VRAM constraints.
- **Actual vs. Theoretical Speedup (Table 7)**: DisCa achieved an actual speedup ratio that was very close to its theoretical FLOPs-based speedup (e.g., 11.8× actual vs. 11.9× theoretical). This fidelity contrasts with multi-layer caching methods, which showed significant discrepancies between theoretical and actual speedups, attributed to overheads from sparse memory accesses in distributed parallel environments.
- **Image-to-Video Task (Table 4, Figure 6)**: Supplementary experiments on the HunyuanVideo 1.5 I2V task confirmed DisCa's generalization. At 4 NFE (inference steps), DisCa (N=2, R=0.2) achieved an LPIPS of 0.1942, PSNR of 19.29, and SSIM of 0.7071, outperforming other methods like TaylorSeer, MagCache, AdaCache, and DCM. User studies also indicated a strong preference for DisCa's outputs at both 4 and 8 steps.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of Restricted MeanFlow (Table 3)**: Training the learnable caching scheme on the original MeanFlow (without the "restrict factor") resulted in the most substantial degradation, with a 5.9% drop in semantic score and significant visual malformation, underscoring the necessity of pruning aggressive distillation scenarios.
- **Importance of Learnable Predictor (Table 3)**: When a simple training-free caching mechanism was used on the distilled model instead of the learnable predictor, a 2.9% semantic score drop and 0.7% quality score drop were observed, indicating that the learnable predictor contributes to quality preservation.
- **Role of GAN Training (Table 3)**: Removing Generative Adversarial Training led to a 1.2% decrease in semantic score, demonstrating its contribution to enhancing the predictor's ability to generate semantically rich and visually detailed outputs.
- **Predictor with/without Distillation (Table 5)**: Comparing the predictor trained directly on multi-step models without distillation versus DisCa (which uses distillation), DisCa consistently showed better performance (e.g., lower LPIPS, higher PSNR/SSIM). This suggests that distillation, by creating smoother trajectories, facilitates more precise predictions by the learnable predictor.
- **Loss Curves (Figure 5)**: The GAN training process showed stable adversarial dynamics, with both discriminator and predictor losses engaging in a long-term interplay to improve generation quality.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Figure 4, 8)

Visualizations consistently showed DisCa's superiority. While other acceleration methods at high ratios often produced blurring, severe malformations, and distortions (e.g., PAB, TaylorSeer, TeaCache), DisCa maintained high visual quality, rich details, explicit structural information, and temporal consistency even at 11.8× acceleration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "DisCa: Accelerating Video Diffusion Transformers with Distillation-Compatible Learnable Feature Caching" offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of generative AI, particularly concerning video generation.

**Enhanced Acceleration Capabilities**: The most direct impact of DisCa is its ability to push the acceleration boundaries for large-scale video Diffusion Transformers. Achieving an 11.8× speedup while preserving near-lossless generation quality represents a notable advancement over previous methods. This level of acceleration can significantly reduce the computational cost and time required for video generation, making high-quality video content creation more accessible and efficient.

**Bridging Training-Free and Training-Aware Acceleration**: DisCa's novel integration of a learnable feature caching mechanism with a modified step distillation strategy addresses a critical compatibility challenge in acceleration research. By enabling training-free and training-aware methods to complement each other effectively, the paper provides a new pathway for developing more comprehensive and powerful acceleration solutions for DMs. This approach moves beyond the limitations of relying solely on either fixed heuristics or aggressive distillation.

**Improved Distillation for Complex Models**: The introduction of **Restricted MeanFlow** is significant for stabilizing the distillation process, particularly for large and complex video generation models. By prudently limiting the compression ratio during distillation, the method mitigates numerical instabilities and quality degradation observed in more aggressive distillation techniques. This conservative scheme ensures that the benefits of step reduction can be realized for high-quality video outputs without introducing severe artifacts.

**Advancements in Feature Caching**: Replacing traditional handcrafted prediction functions in feature caching with a lightweight **learnable neural predictor** is a key innovation. This data-driven approach allows for a more accurate capture of high-dimensional feature evolution, especially in the sparser sampling trajectories characteristic of distilled models. This enhances the fidelity of cached predictions and contributes to overall quality preservation. The use of generative adversarial training further compels the predictor to generate outputs with rich semantic and high-frequency details.

**Practical Deployment Advantages**: The memory-efficient design of DisCa, which maintains only a single tensor cache rather than multi-layer caches, has substantial practical implications. For high-resolution and long-duration video generation, VRAM consumption is a critical constraint. DisCa's minimal additional VRAM overhead (approximately 0.4 GB) makes it a more viable solution for real-world applications and distributed parallel computing environments compared to memory-intensive multi-layer caching schemes. Additionally, the close alignment between theoretical and actual speedups suggests efficient implementation and execution in such environments.

**Future Research Directions**: The findings open new avenues for research into hybrid acceleration techniques for generative models. Future work could explore further optimizations of the predictor architecture, adaptive mechanisms for the restrict factor in MeanFlow, or the application of learnable caching principles to other forms of generative models or modalities beyond video. The robust performance and efficiency of DisCa suggest a promising direction for making advanced generative AI models more scalable and deployable across various industries.
