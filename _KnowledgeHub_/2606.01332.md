# S2M-Trek: From Single to Multi-Sphere Transport via Per-Frame Deep Sets on a Wheel-Legged Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Huazhong University of Science and Technology introduce the Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS) architecture, enabling a wheel-legged robot to dynamically transport multiple free-rolling spheres simultaneously. This method successfully addresses per-frame permutation symmetry, allowing a tactile-only student policy to achieve 75% no-drop transport for five spheres in simulation.

## Problem

- Scaling dynamic loco-manipulation from single objects to multiple identical, free-rolling objects is challenging due to their unordered and identity-free nature.
- Standard neural network encoders fail to correctly represent the 'per-frame product group' (G_frame) symmetry, leading to a representational mismatch and hindering learning.
- Existing curriculum learning approaches with traditional encoders exploit spurious correlations like fixed slot indices, preventing robust generalization to increased object counts.

## Method

- The Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS) architecture processes each history frame's object set independently with a shared MLP and permutation-invariant pooling, ensuring intrinsic G_frame-invariance.
- Teacher policies were trained using PPO in a high-fidelity simulation, employing a multi-criterion curriculum that progressively increases the number of spheres.
- A tactile-only student policy (TACTSET) was developed using DAgger imitation learning, leveraging a G_frame-invariant Boolean union tactile map for real-world feasibility.

## Results

- G_frame-invariant encoders (PFDS and Set Transformer (PF)) successfully reached the five-sphere transport stage, achieving 98-**100%** no-drop rates in simulation.
- G_diag-invariant encoders only progressed to the five-sphere stage when aided by explicit slot-permutation data augmentation, failing without it and confirming their reliance on such an intervention.
- The DAgger-distilled TACTSET student policy, relying solely on tactile input, demonstrated successful transfer of multi-sphere manipulation skills by achieving a **75%** no-drop rate for five spheres in simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The work formally defines and quantifies the 'per-frame product group' (G_frame) symmetry, highlighting a critical architectural gap in standard set encoders for multi-object, time-series data.
> - Architectural enforcement of G_frame-invariance (PFDS) is more fundamental for robust multi-object loco-manipulation than relying solely on data augmentation, which only compensates for G_diag-invariant encoders.
> - Tactile sensing, when processed as a Boolean union of contact footprints, naturally conforms to G_frame symmetry, providing a viable and robust input for real-world multi-object control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robotics, specifically at the intersection of dynamic locomotion, manipulation, and machine learning, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) and neural network architectures for set-based data.

**Legged and Wheel-Legged Robotics:** The paper builds upon advancements in agile and dynamic locomotion for legged and wheel-legged robots. Prior work has established foundations for robust locomotion in various terrains and rapid motor adaptation using massively parallel RL. Recent extensions have enabled these platforms to perform single-object loco-manipulation, such as dribbling a ball or transporting cargo with back-mounted tactile sensors. This paper aims to extend these capabilities to scenarios involving multiple objects.

**Permutation-Invariant Networks:** A core technical component of the work relates to permutation-invariant neural networks. Deep Sets, Set Transformers, and PointNet are established methods for processing unordered sets of data, primarily in single-frame contexts (e.g., point clouds, collections of features). This paper identifies a gap in how these networks handle temporal sequences of unordered sets, where permutations can occur independently at each time step.

**Equivariant Reinforcement Learning and Multi-Object Symmetry:** The broader field of equivariant RL explores how to incorporate symmetries (e.g., spatial, agent-level) into neural network architectures to improve sample efficiency and generalization. While existing methods address spatial symmetries or symmetries among multiple agents, this paper introduces the specific challenge of "temporal product symmetry" that arises when observing identical objects over multiple history frames with independent slot re-assignments.

**Tactile Sensing and Imitation Learning:** The work also connects to research on tactile sensing for contact-rich manipulation and load transport. DAgger, an imitation learning algorithm, is a common technique to transfer policies trained with privileged (e.g., exact object state) observations to real-world deployable sensors, such as tactile arrays. This paper leverages DAgger to enable a tactile-only student policy.

The novelty of this work lies in formally defining and addressing the "per-frame permutation symmetry" problem, which arises when scaling loco-manipulation to multiple identical, free-rolling objects. It proposes an architectural solution (Per-Frame Deep Sets) and demonstrates its effectiveness compared to existing set encoders, while also showing how tactile sensing can naturally conform to this identified symmetry for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable dynamic loco-manipulation of multiple free-rolling spheres simultaneously on the back of a wheel-legged quadruped. This task is to be achieved without relying on external constraints such as fences, grippers, or mechanical stops to contain or identify the objects.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges identified in existing loco-manipulation research:

- **Scaling from Single to Multiple Objects:** While legged robots have demonstrated proficiency in single-object manipulation, directly extending these methods to multiple objects is not straightforward. The authors argue that merely increasing input channels for more objects introduces a fundamental representational problem.
- **Identity-Free, Unordered Object Sets:** When transporting multiple identical, free-rolling objects, their individual identities are not persistent. The objects form an unordered set, and their relative positions or "slot assignments" in an observation tensor can change independently from one time step (history frame) to the next.
- **The Symmetry Mismatch Problem:** This identity-free, per-frame re-assignment introduces a specific type of permutation symmetry. The correct mathematical representation of this is the "per-frame product group" (G_frame = (S_N)^H), where N is the maximum number of object slots and H is the history length. Standard neural network architectures, including history-concatenation set encoders (like the direct application of Deep Sets or Set Transformers to multi-frame histories), typically enforce only a "diagonal subgroup" symmetry (G_diag = {(π..., π) : π ∈ S_N}). This means they assume the same permutation applies across all history frames, which is often not true for free-rolling objects.
- **Quantitative Representational Gap:** The authors quantify this mismatch, showing that the quotient ratio |G_frame|/|G_diag| can be substantial (e.g., approximately 1.7 million for N=5, H=4). This large number of physically equivalent states that are treated as distinct by G_diag encoders represents a significant redundancy in the observation space, potentially hindering learning.
- **Failure Mode in Curriculum Learning:** The authors hypothesize that this symmetry mismatch leads to a "concrete failure mode" in curriculum-based reinforcement learning. They anticipate that standard encoders might exploit spurious correlations (like fixed slot indices) rather than learning the identity-free multi-sphere dynamics, thereby failing to progress to higher object counts.
- **Architectural vs. Data Augmentation Robustness:** The research seeks to determine whether robustness to G_frame invariance is primarily achieved through intrinsic architectural design or via data augmentation (e.g., randomizing slot assignments during training). The hypothesis is that architectural solutions offer a more fundamental and robust approach.
- **Bridging Simulation to Reality:** The final objective is to develop a deployable policy for a physical robot. Since privileged object state observations (used for teacher training) are unavailable in reality, the work aims to distill the learned policy into a student network that relies on real-world feasible sensors, specifically a back-mounted tactile map.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the representational challenges posed by multi-object, identity-free loco-manipulation, demonstrating the impact of appropriate symmetry-aware network architectures for scalable and robust robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of reinforcement learning, novel neural network architecture design, and imitation learning techniques within a high-fidelity simulation environment.

**Robot Platform and Task Definition:**
The study utilizes a self-designed four-wheel-independent-steering quadruped robot. The primary task is to transport `k` identical free-rolling spheres (radius 55 mm) on a flat back plate (22.4 cm × 15.1 cm). The robot has 12 leg joints and 4 hub motors, controlled at 50 Hz. A 16x16 binary tactile array on the back plate provides contact feedback. Episodes terminate if a ball falls off, or the robot falls.

**Curriculum Learning:**
To manage task complexity, a multi-criterion curriculum is employed. The number of active spheres (`k`) is progressively increased from 1 to a maximum of 5. Promotion to the next curriculum level (k to k+1) occurs when a rolling window of recent episodes simultaneously satisfies six criteria: episode-length ratio, support margin, dangerous fraction, edge-overflow fraction, linear velocity tracking error, and angular velocity tracking error. These thresholds are relaxed at higher `k` levels to account for increased difficulty.

**Observation Space:**
- **Proprioception:** A history window of 6 frames (H_prop=6) containing base angular velocity, projected gravity vector, velocity command, joint positions, joint velocities, previous action, and base height (total 258 dimensions).
- **Multi-ball State (Teacher):** A history tensor `X_t` of `H=4` object frames, where each frame contains features for up to `N=5` balls. Each ball's state is a 14-dimensional vector including relative position, linear velocity, orientation (quaternion), angular velocity (all in robot frame), and an activity flag. Inactive slots are zero-masked. Observation noise is applied, and ball state is only observable upon contact. The critic policy receives a denoised version.

**Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS) Architecture:**
PFDS is proposed as a G_frame-invariant encoder. It operates by:
1. **Per-Element Encoding:** Applying a shared MLP `ϕ` to each individual object observation `x_t,i` (feature dimension `d`) within a history frame `t`.
2. **Within-Frame Pooling:** Performing a continuous permutation-invariant aggregation (masked sum-pooling) `Pool` on the set of encoded object features `{ϕ(x_t,i)}` for each history frame `t` independently. This yields a frame embedding `h_t`. This step ensures invariance to permutations within each frame.
3. **Temporal Readout:** Concatenating the frame embeddings `[h_1; h_2; ...; h_H]` and processing them with a temporal readout MLP `ρ` to produce the final policy embedding `z`.
The authors provide Proposition 1 (proof in Section A) formally demonstrating PFDS's G_frame-invariance and Theorem 1 (proof sketch in Section A) establishing its universal approximation capability for G_frame-invariant functions.

**Contrast with Deep Sets, History-Concatenation (DSHC):**
DSHC, a standard application of Deep Sets to multi-frame histories, concatenates features for each slot across time (`y_i = [x_1,i; ...; x_H,i]`) before pooling. The authors prove (Proposition 2) that DSHC is only G_diag-invariant, meaning it is invariant only to permutations that are consistent across all history frames, but not to independent per-frame permutations. This highlights the architectural distinction and the larger symmetry group addressed by PFDS.

**Encoder Baselines for Comparison:**
Eight different encoder architectures are compared, spanning three symmetry levels:
- **G_0 (No Invariance):** FLAT-MLP (flattens `X` into a single vector) and BRANCH-MLP (encodes each slot independently, then concatenates).
- **G_diag (Diagonal Invariance):** DSHC (Deep Sets, History-Concatenation) and Set Transformer (uses ISAB/PMA attention on history-concatenated tokens).
- **G_frame (Per-Frame Invariance, Proposed):** PFDS and Set Transformer (PF) (frame-wise ISAB/PMA attention). PFDS OFF is a variant of PFDS without slot-permutation augmentation during training.

**Teacher Policy Training:**
All teacher policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in Isaac Lab, a GPU-accelerated simulation framework. Training involves 4096 parallel environments for 30,000 iterations. Reward functions include object-balancing terms (support margin, conditional value-at-risk for precarious balls, edge penalties, spacing rewards) and standard locomotion terms (velocity tracking, height, roll/pitch penalties).

**Tactile Student Policy (TACTSET) and Distillation:**
- **Tactile Observation:** The student policy replaces privileged ball-state observations with a 16x16 binary tactile contact map (`τ_t`). This map is generated as the Boolean union of the contact footprints of all active balls. The authors argue that this Boolean union is naturally G_frame-invariant, aligning the student's input with the teacher's invariant representation.
- **Distillation Protocol:** TACTSET is trained via DAgger, an imitation learning algorithm. This involves an initial behavioral cloning phase (400,000 steps) followed by seven rounds of online rollout where the PFDS teacher provides action targets.
- **Student Architecture:** A 3x3 2-D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) processes the stacked tactile maps (`H` × 16 × 16, 64 channels), followed by a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU, hidden size 512) to produce a tactile embedding. This embedding is then combined with proprioception and fed into the policy head.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **No-drop Rate:** The primary metric, defined as the percentage of episodes where the robot completes all 500 steps without any sphere falling off the plate.
- **Strict Rate:** A more stringent metric, requiring the ball to remain within a defined "safe zone" (zero near-edge events) throughout the episode, in addition to not dropping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results provide empirical evidence supporting the architectural and theoretical claims of the paper.

**Curriculum Reachability and Encoder Comparison:**
- **G_0 Encoders Failure:** FLAT-MLP and BRANCH-MLP, which lack any permutation invariance, failed to progress beyond the two-sphere stage (k≤2). They achieved 0-2% no-drop rates at k=5.
- **G_diag Encoders Dependence on Augmentation:** DSHC and Set Transformer (both G_diag invariant) were able to reach the five-sphere stage (k=5) *only when slot-permutation augmentation was enabled during training*. DSHC with augmentation achieved a 98% no-drop rate at k=5. Set Transformer with augmentation achieved 94%.
- **DSHC OFF Failure:** Critically, when slot-permutation augmentation was disabled, DSHC (labeled DSHC OFF) failed to advance past k=2 within the allocated training budget, achieving 0% no-drop at k=5. This supports the hypothesis that G_diag encoders exploit slot indices as a curriculum shortcut without robustly learning identity-free dynamics.
- **G_frame Encoders Robustness:** PFDS and Set Transformer (PF) (both G_frame invariant) successfully reached the five-sphere stage under both training regimes (with and without slot-permutation augmentation). PFDS achieved a 100% no-drop rate at k=5 with augmentation and 98% without augmentation. Set Transformer (PF) achieved 100% no-drop with augmentation.
- **Training Efficiency:** PFDS demonstrated efficient curriculum progression, reaching k=5 in approximately 6,300 iterations with augmentation, making it the fastest among successful encoders.

**2x2 Ablation Study:**
A controlled ablation comparing DSHC and PFDS, with and without slot-permutation augmentation, provided specific evidence for the roles of architectural design and data augmentation:
- **Architectural Pathway:** PFDS, due to its intrinsic G_frame-invariance, successfully reached k=5 even without data augmentation (PFDS OFF, 98% no-drop, ~16.9k iterations to reach k≥3).
- **Data-Augmentation Pathway:** DSHC *required* slot-permutation augmentation to advance past k=2. Without it, DSHC OFF stalled at k=2, confirming that data augmentation can compensate for a lack of intrinsic symmetry in G_diag encoders, but is not sufficient to enable fundamental progress without it.

**Multi-Seed Robustness:**
Five independent training runs of PFDS with different random seeds all consistently reached the maximum curriculum level (k=5) within the 30,000-iteration budget. This indicates the robustness and reliability of the PFDS approach.

**Tactile Student Distillation (TACTSET):**
The DAgger-distilled TACTSET student policy, which operates solely on a 16x16 Boolean union tactile contact map, achieved 75% no-drop transport for five spheres in simulation. While lower than the teacher's 100%, this result indicates successful transfer of the multi-sphere manipulation skill to a real-world deployable sensing modality. The strict rate for TACTSET at k=5 was 19%, suggesting less conservative margin performance compared to the teacher (41%).

In summary, the results demonstrate that enforcing G_frame-invariance through architectural design (PFDS) is crucial for robustly scaling multi-sphere loco-manipulation, especially in the absence of explicit data augmentation. The work also successfully translates this capability to a tactile-sensing student policy for potential physical deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the fields of robotics, reinforcement learning, and neural network design by addressing a specific representational challenge in multi-object manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Formalization of Per-Frame Permutation Symmetry:** The paper formally defines and quantifies the "per-frame product group" (G_frame) symmetry problem, which arises in dynamic multi-object tasks where objects are identical and their observed identities can change over time. This formalization provides a theoretical framework for understanding and addressing similar challenges in future research.
- **Introduction of Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS):** PFDS is a novel, intrinsically G_frame-invariant neural network architecture. By demonstrating its superior performance and robustness compared to G_diag-invariant encoders (like standard Deep Sets or Set Transformers applied to history), the work highlights the importance of aligning an encoder's symmetry group with the underlying physical symmetries of the task.
- **Robustness of Architectural Guarantees:** The 2x2 ablation study provides empirical evidence that architectural symmetry guarantees are more fundamental than data augmentation alone for complex curriculum-based learning tasks involving identity-free objects. PFDS's ability to succeed without explicit slot-permutation augmentation suggests a more robust and generalizable learning process.
- **Advancing Multi-Object Loco-Manipulation:** This work demonstrates a method to scale dynamic loco-manipulation from single objects to multiple free-rolling objects on legged platforms without needing external mechanical constraints. This capability is relevant for various robotic applications, including logistics, search and rescue, or mobile manipulation in unstructured environments.
- **Pathway to Real-World Deployment via Tactile Sensing:** The successful distillation of the privileged teacher policy into TACTSET, which uses a G_frame-invariant Boolean union tactile map, provides a pragmatic path towards deploying such complex multi-object control policies on physical robots. This bridges the gap between simulation-trained capabilities and real-world sensor constraints.
- **Implications for Reinforcement Learning Curriculum Design:** The observed failure mode of G_diag encoders in curriculum learning (exploiting slot indices as a shortcut) offers insights into the design of robust curricula for multi-object tasks, emphasizing the need for permutation-invariant observations or appropriate augmentation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **Heterogeneous Object Sets:** The authors suggest extensions using composed per-type Deep Sets blocks, which could enable manipulation of different types of objects simultaneously.
 - **Physical Deployment:** The ongoing work on real-robot deployment of the tactile student will be a critical validation and could open new avenues for research in real-world multi-object robot control.
 - **Geometric Constraints:** The discussion on the limitations imposed by the back plate size at higher object counts points to future work on adapting control strategies or robot designs for dense packing scenarios.

Overall, this research represents a step towards more capable and intelligent robots that can dynamically interact with and transport multiple arbitrary objects in complex environments, driven by a principled approach to neural network design that accounts for fundamental task symmetries.
