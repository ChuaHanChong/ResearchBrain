# S2M-Trek: From Single to Multi-Sphere Transport via Per-Frame Deep Sets on a Wheel-Legged Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Huazhong University of Science and Technology introduce the Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS) architecture, enabling a wheel-legged robot to dynamically transport multiple free-rolling spheres simultaneously. This method successfully addresses per-frame permutation symmetry, allowing a tactile-only student policy to achieve 75% no-drop transport for five spheres in simulation.

## Problem

- Scaling dynamic loco-manipulation from single objects to multiple identical, free-rolling objects is challenging due to their unordered and identity-free nature.
- Standard neural network encoders fail to correctly represent the 'per-frame product group' (G_frame) symmetry, leading to a representational mismatch and hindering learning.
- Existing curriculum learning approaches with traditional encoders exploit spurious correlations like fixed slot indices, preventing robust generalization to increased object counts.

## Method

- The Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS) architecture processes each history frame's object set independently with a shared MLP and permutation-invariant pooling, ensuring intrinsic G_frame-invariance.
- Teacher policies were trained using PPO in a high-fidelity simulation, employing a multi-criterion curriculum that progressively increases the number of spheres.
- A tactile-only student policy (TACTSET) was developed using DAgger imitation learning, leveraging a G_frame-invariant Boolean union tactile map for real-world feasibility.

## Results

- G_frame-invariant encoders (PFDS and Set Transformer (PF)) successfully reached the five-sphere transport stage, achieving 98-**100%** no-drop rates in simulation.
- G_diag-invariant encoders only progressed to the five-sphere stage when aided by explicit slot-permutation data augmentation, failing without it and confirming their reliance on such an intervention.
- The DAgger-distilled TACTSET student policy, relying solely on tactile input, demonstrated successful transfer of multi-sphere manipulation skills by achieving a **75%** no-drop rate for five spheres in simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The work formally defines and quantifies the 'per-frame product group' (G_frame) symmetry, highlighting a critical architectural gap in standard set encoders for multi-object, time-series data.
> - Architectural enforcement of G_frame-invariance (PFDS) is more fundamental for robust multi-object loco-manipulation than relying solely on data augmentation, which only compensates for G_diag-invariant encoders.
> - Tactile sensing, when processed as a Boolean union of contact footprints, naturally conforms to G_frame symmetry, providing a viable and robust input for real-world multi-object control.
