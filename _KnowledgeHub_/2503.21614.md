# A Survey of Efficient Reasoning for Large Reasoning Models: Language, Multimodality, and Beyond

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative survey by researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and other institutions maps current techniques for enhancing reasoning efficiency in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) across their lifecycle. It systematically categorizes approaches to mitigate verbose and computationally expensive reasoning traces, aiming to optimize "intelligence per token" for practical LRM deployment.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate excessively long, often redundant, and inefficient reasoning traces (Chain-of-Thought), leading to high computational costs for training and inference.
- Existing surveys on LRMs have not specifically addressed the growing challenge of reasoning efficiency, leaving a gap in synthesized knowledge for this critical area.
- The inherent verbosity of LRMs creates a "token economy" problem, impacting memory consumption, inference latency, and overall computational expenses, thus hindering real-world applicability.

## Method

- The survey formally defines ==reasoning efficiency== and identifies common patterns of LRM inefficiency (e.g., redundant content, overthinking) along with unique challenges.
- It systematically categorizes and details methods for improving reasoning efficiency across the entire LRM lifecycle: during inference, with ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)==, through ==reinforcement learning (RL)==, and during pre-training.
- The authors provide a comprehensive ==taxonomy of techniques== within each stage and maintain a real-time GitHub repository to serve as a dynamic community resource.

## Results

- Efficient inference techniques include length budgeting, dynamic switching between models or systems, and parallel search strategies to prune suboptimal reasoning paths.
- Supervised fine-tuning methods enable reasoning chain compression via data engineering or latent-space reasoning, while reinforcement learning approaches use explicit length penalties or implicit preference-modeling biases to guide concise reasoning.
- Pre-training strategies focus on inherent efficiency through latent space reasoning or by adopting subquadratic attention mechanisms and linear recurrent structures to overcome Transformer limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning efficiency, defined as the ratio of solution quality to computational cost, is crucial for maximizing "intelligence per token" in LRMs.
> - Inefficiency in LRMs manifests as redundant content, overthinking simple questions, and incoherent or suboptimal reasoning paths, requiring targeted interventions.
> - Achieving efficient reasoning necessitates addressing challenges such as quantifying the utility of individual reasoning steps, controlling thinking length dynamically, and overcoming architectural bottlenecks of Transformers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the burgeoning field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and, more specifically, the emerging paradigm of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). The introduction delineates a critical distinction: traditional LLMs operate akin to "System 1" thinking (fast, intuitive), while LRMs, by generating explicit intermediate reasoning steps (Chain-of-Thought, CoT), emulate "System 2" thinking (deliberate, analytical). This shift has led to remarkable performance gains in complex tasks like advanced mathematics and formal logic, as exemplified by models such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1.

However, this enhanced reasoning capability comes at a significant cost: LRMs tend to produce excessively long, often redundant, and inefficient reasoning traces. This inefficiency poses substantial challenges for practical applications, including training, inference, and real-world deployment in token-sensitive environments like agent-based systems. The core problem this survey addresses is how to maximize "intelligence per token" in LRMs by making their reasoning processes more efficient.

The authors meticulously situate their work by acknowledging existing surveys on LRMs, which cover topics such as training methodologies (Besta et al., 2025; Zeng et al., 2024a), broader overviews (Li et al., 2025e), long CoT reasoning paradigms (Chen et al., 2025d), and test-time scaling (Ji et al., 2025). They explicitly state that **none of these prior surveys specifically address the growing, yet crucial, challenge of reasoning efficiency in LRMs.** This is the unique and critical gap that the current survey fills. Furthermore, they differentiate their scope from general LLM inference acceleration techniques (e.g., quantization, pruning, distributed inference systems) by focusing specifically on the *efficiency of the reasoning process itself*, rather than merely computational speed-ups. By focusing on this overlooked aspect, the survey provides a timely and much-needed synthesis of techniques and challenges, establishing a foundational reference for an emerging area of research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a comprehensive overview of recent efforts aimed at improving reasoning efficiency in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). This is driven by a critical observation: while LRMs demonstrate powerful reasoning capabilities by generating explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps, they often produce excessively long reasoning traces filled with redundant content, over-analysis, and superficial exploration of multiple paths. This verbosity and inefficiency lead to significant practical problems.

The key motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing the "Token Economy" Challenge**: The authors highlight that the tendency of LRMs to generate excessively long and redundant reasoning sequences introduces significant challenges for training, inference, and real-world deployment, especially where "token economy" (the cost and computational burden associated with each generated token) is critical. This impacts memory consumption, inference latency, and overall computational costs.
- **Bridging the Gap in Existing Literature**: As noted in the previous section, existing surveys on LRMs have not specifically focused on the burgeoning issue of reasoning efficiency. This survey aims to fill that void by offering a targeted and in-depth analysis of this emerging and crucial topic.
- **Maximizing "Intelligence per Token"**: The paper frames its motivation with the adage "Efficiency is the essence of intelligence." It argues that an intelligent model should optimize its use of tokens, balancing cost and performance by skipping redundancy and optimizing the path to a solution, rather than exhaustively exploring every possibility.
- **Providing a Structured Understanding**: The authors aim to identify common patterns of inefficiency in LRM reasoning, categorize and examine various methods proposed across the entire LRM lifecycle (from pre-training to inference), and discuss promising future research directions. This structured approach is intended to serve as a foundational resource for researchers in this rapidly evolving field.

In essence, the survey is motivated by the need to make powerful LRMs not just intelligent, but intelligently efficient, ensuring their scalability, practicality, and broader applicability in real-world AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a systematic and structured approach to survey the landscape of efficient reasoning in Large Reasoning Models. Their methodology is characterized by:

1. **Formal Definition of Reasoning Efficiency**: The survey begins by formally defining reasoning efficiency ($\eta(M)$) from a task-distribution perspective. This metric quantifies efficiency as the ratio of solution quality ($Q(M, D)$) to computational cost ($C(M, D)$) over a distribution of tasks. This provides a clear, quantitative framework for evaluating efficiency.
2. **Identification of Inefficiency Patterns**: Before diving into solutions, the paper meticulously identifies and illustrates common patterns of inefficiency observed in LRMs. These include:
 - **Redundant Content**: Excessive generation of repetitive rephrasing or verbose explanations without advancing core reasoning (e.g., a specific math problem solved by QwQ-32B in Figure 1 and Table 1).
 - **Overthinking Simple Questions**: Models exhibiting unwarranted uncertainty or generating multiple redundant reasoning rounds for straightforward tasks (e.g., QwQ-32B generating 1248 tokens for 3*3 vs. Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct's 30 tokens).
 - **Incoherent and Suboptimal Reasoning**: Prematurely switching reasoning directions, leading to shallow, fragmented, or lengthy traces that reduce solution quality ("underthinking").
3. **Outline of Unique Challenges**: The authors then delineate four unique challenges specific to achieving efficient reasoning in the LRM era:
 - **Quantifying Reasoning Utility**: The difficulty in evaluating the contribution of each intermediate token to the final answer.
 - **Controlling Thinking Length**: The challenge of dynamically "thinking just enough" without being too shallow or too verbose.
 - **Architectural Bottlenecks**: The quadratic complexity of Transformer architectures becoming a limiting factor for long reasoning traces.
 - **Cross-Task Generalization**: The difficulty of a single reasoning strategy or length policy generalizing across diverse task complexities.
4. **Categorization by LRM Lifecycle Stages**: The core of the survey's organization is a systematic review of methods categorized according to the LRM development pipeline:
 - **Efficient Reasoning during Inference (§3)**: Focuses on run-time strategies.
 - **Efficient Reasoning with Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) (§4)**: Examines how fine-tuning can embed efficiency.
 - **Efficient Reasoning with Reinforcement Learning (RL) (§5)**: Explores RL's role in optimizing reasoning length.
 - **Efficient Reasoning during Pre-training (§6)**: Discusses architectural and foundational approaches for inherent efficiency.
5. **Detailed Method Taxonomy**: Within each lifecycle stage, specific techniques are further sub-categorized (e.g., Length Budgeting, System Switch, Model Switch, Parallel Search under Inference). Each technique is briefly explained, and relevant research papers are cited to provide a comprehensive overview.
6. **Forward-Looking Analysis**: The survey concludes by identifying promising future research directions, building upon the limitations and insights derived from the existing literature, encompassing areas like multimodal reasoning, trustworthy reasoning, application building, and evaluation benchmarks.
7. **Community Resource**: The authors also maintain a real-time GitHub repository, serving as a dynamic and up-to-date resource for the research community.

This systematic and detailed approach ensures a thorough coverage of the topic, from problem definition to potential solutions and future avenues, making the survey a robust and valuable contribution to the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically reviews numerous approaches to improve reasoning efficiency across the entire lifecycle of Large Reasoning Models, revealing a diverse landscape of methods and their underlying principles.

#### 4.1. Reasoning Efficiency Definition, Patterns, and Challenges

The paper defines reasoning efficiency as the ratio of solution quality to computational cost. It highlights three key patterns of inefficiency: **redundant content** (e.g., repetitive rephrasing), **overthinking simple questions** (e.g., excessive verification for easy math problems), and **incoherent/suboptimal reasoning** (e.g., superficial exploration of multiple paths). Unique challenges identified include quantifying the utility of individual reasoning steps, controlling thinking length dynamically, architectural bottlenecks of Transformers (quadratic complexity), and the difficulty of cross-task generalization.

#### 4.2. Efficient Reasoning during Inference

This section focuses on strategies to optimize reasoning at runtime:
- **Length Budgeting**: Methods that explicitly constrain the number of tokens or reasoning steps, often through prompting (TALE, Sketch-of-Thought) or special tokens (S1, SafeChain). Difficulty-adaptive approaches like DSC and Dynasor allocate resources based on query complexity.
- **System Switch**: Dynamically toggling between fast, intuitive "System 1" (LLM-like) and slow, deliberate "System 2" (LRM-like) reasoning based on task complexity or confidence (Dualformer, System-1.x, Dyna-Think).
- **Model Switch**: Directing queries to different models (e.g., small fast vs. large accurate) or using specialized decoding heads (BiLD, EAGLE, MEDUSA) or routing modules (Zooter, RouteLLM).
- **Parallel Search**: Generating multiple candidate reasoning paths concurrently and pruning suboptimal ones early using verifiers or confidence scores (SBoN, TreeBoN, STBoN, Self-Calibration).

#### 4.3. Efficient Reasoning with Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)

SFT methods aim to internalize concise reasoning patterns:
- **Reasoning Chain Compression**: Building datasets with condensed reasoning paths (e.g., via best-of-N sampling, external compressors like GPT-4, or perplexity-based step identification) and fine-tuning models to produce shorter, accurate CoTs (C3ot, TokenSkip, SPIRIT-FT). CoT-Valve even allows for extrapolating reasoning length.
- **Latent-Space SFT**: Replacing explicit CoT steps with continuous hidden representations, where reasoning occurs internally without generating full token sequences. Techniques like Coconut, CCoT, and CODI use curriculum learning and self-distillation to transition from explicit to implicit reasoning, reducing token overhead. Token Assorted mixes latent and text tokens, while SoftCoT uses "soft thought tokens."

#### 4.4. Efficient Reasoning with Reinforcement Learning (RL)

RL is employed to guide models towards optimal reasoning length:
- **Length Reward (Explicit)**: Most methods introduce explicit penalties for excessive length alongside correctness rewards. This includes trajectory-level budgets (O1-Pruner, L1, Kimi-1.5), redundancy-aware penalties (Redundancy, VSRM, SmartThinker), and adaptive budget allocation based on problem difficulty (Train Long, Think Short, DAST). Some approaches train models to switch between discrete reasoning modes (SABER, ARM).
- **Implicit Length Bias**: Some RL methods achieve efficiency without direct length rewards by leveraging implicit biases from preference modeling or training dynamics. MRT, for example, uses a meta-RL framework for adaptive computation, while IBPO and Chen et al. (2025e) use preference data to implicitly regulate length. Dr.GRPO identifies and removes length-biasing factors in standard RL algorithms.

#### 4.5. Efficient Reasoning during Pre-training

Pre-training approaches aim for inherent efficiency:
- **Latent Space Pretraining**: Learning to reason with continuous representations rather than discrete tokens. BLT processes raw bytes, LCMs use abstract concepts as processing units, and CoCoMix integrates continuous concept vectors. LTMs probabilistically guide token generation with latent thought vectors.
- **Subquadratic Attention**: Replacing the quadratic complexity of Transformer self-attention with more efficient mechanisms:
 - **Linear Sequence Modeling**: Uses kernel-based approximations (Lightning Attention), retention mechanisms (RetNet), gated linear attention (GLA), state-space models (Mamba2), and linear RNNs (RWKV, HGRN).
 - **Sparse Attention**: Focuses on subsets of tokens or local contexts (Sliding window, StreamingLLM, Longformer, NSA).
- **Linearization**: Converting pre-trained Transformer weights into linear recurrent structures for efficient inference (Liger, LoLCATs, Llamba, LightTransfer).
- **Efficient Reasoning with Subquadratic Attention**: Applying these new architectures to reasoning tasks, such as distilling Mamba models from Transformers for faster CoT (Think Slow Fast) or using hybrid Mamba-MLP-Transformer architectures (Cosmos-Reason1).

Overall, the survey demonstrates a concerted effort across the research community to tackle LRM inefficiency from architectural foundations to fine-tuning and inference-time adaptations, highlighting a clear trend towards more resource-aware AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the future development and deployment of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), addressing a critical, yet previously underserved, area of research. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Enabling Practical LRM Deployment**: The core problem of reasoning inefficiency directly hampers the real-world applicability of LRMs. By synthesizing and categorizing current solutions, this survey provides a roadmap for developing LRMs that are not only powerful but also economically viable and scalable. Reduced computational costs, lower inference latency, and optimized token usage are crucial for integrating LRMs into real-time applications, agentic systems, and resource-constrained environments.
- **Shifting Research Focus to "Intelligence per Token"**: The paper eloquently argues that "Efficiency is the essence of intelligence." This perspective encourages a paradigm shift in LRM research, moving beyond mere performance scaling to prioritize the optimization of resource utilization. It prompts researchers to consider efficiency not as a secondary concern, but as an integral component of advanced AI capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Innovation**: By identifying common patterns of inefficiency, outlining unique challenges, and categorizing existing methodologies, the survey provides a robust foundation for future research. It highlights promising avenues, such as the integration of explicit and implicit reasoning, adaptive curriculum learning, and multimodal latent reasoning, thereby stimulating innovation in these directions. The detailed taxonomy and extensive citations serve as an invaluable resource for new and established researchers alike.
- **Guiding Architectural Design**: The discussion on pre-training and subquadratic attention mechanisms points to the importance of architectural innovations for inherent efficiency. This can inspire the development of new model architectures that are fundamentally more efficient at handling long reasoning sequences, moving beyond the quadratic complexity limitations of traditional Transformers.
- **Improving Trustworthiness and Control**: The section on future directions emphasizes the link between efficiency and trustworthiness. Generating shorter, more focused reasoning traces can inherently improve model safety by reducing exposure to harmful content within intermediate thoughts and enhance reliability by minimizing the accumulation of errors or hallucinations over extended chains. Better control over reasoning length also translates to better control over model behavior.
- **Advancing AI Applications**: The survey's insights are particularly impactful for specific application areas:
 - **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: Efficient reasoning can optimize how LRMs interact with external knowledge bases, making retrieval and synthesis more targeted and less resource-intensive.
 - **Agent-based Systems**: For AI agents, timely and efficient planning and decision-making are paramount. Reducing overthinking and unnecessary deliberation will make agents more responsive and effective.
 - **Tool Learning**: Efficient reasoning can enable LRMs to use external tools more strategically, making fewer, more impactful function calls.
- **Developing Better Evaluation Benchmarks**: The call for more comprehensive benchmarks that assess not just correctness but also the quality and efficiency of the reasoning *process* (beyond simple math problems, considering creativity and over-reasoning vulnerabilities) is crucial for driving progress in the field.

In conclusion, this survey is a timely and critical contribution that illuminates the growing importance of reasoning efficiency in LRMs. It provides a structured understanding of current efforts and lays out a compelling vision for future research, poised to unlock the full potential of these powerful models for a wide range of practical and impactful AI applications.
