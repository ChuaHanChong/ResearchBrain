# ACE-Data-0: Human-Centric Ambient Capture as Embodied Data Engine

> [!summary] Summary
> The Ambient Capture Engine (ACE) and its generated dataset, ACE-Data-0, offer a comprehensive multi-modal resource with synchronized egocentric and exocentric video, full-body and hand motion, object 6-DoF trajectories, audio, and tactile data from human interactions within realistic home settings. This resource supports research on long-horizon embodied tasks, exposing challenges for existing state-of-the-art methods in handling real-world complexities like occlusion and egomotion.

## Problem

- Existing multi-modal datasets provide fragmented experiences, separating observations across viewpoints, modalities, or spatial scales, leading to incomplete data for the full perception-action loop.
- Many physically annotated datasets are collected in controlled laboratory settings, which limits their generalizability to the clutter, occlusions, and diversity of real-world domestic environments.
- Current datasets largely focus on short-horizon, atomic movements, failing to adequately support goal-directed, multi-step activities that require long-term task and scene memory for embodied AI.

## Method

- Developed the ==Ambient Capture Engine (ACE)==, a human-centric system that transforms real home environments into spatially calibrated and temporally synchronized recording studios at both table-scale (fine manipulation) and room-scale (whole-body interaction).
- Acquired **ACE-Data-0**, a large-scale, long-horizon, multi-modal dataset using ACE, featuring synchronized egocentric/exocentric video, full-body/hand motion, object 6-DoF, audio, and tactile signals, along with detailed ground-truth annotations.
- Established a ==hierarchical benchmark== on ACE-Data-0 to systematically evaluate foundational perceptual capabilities (e.g., tactile inference), scene component recovery (e.g., human motion), and embodied interaction (e.g., hand motion) under realistic conditions.

## Results

- ACE-Data-0 comprises over **150** hours of synchronized multi-modal capture, including **17M+** frames and **75,000+** interaction episodes from real home environments, offering comprehensive ground truth for human and object states and tactile signals.
- Benchmarking for tactile inference from vision revealed that state-of-the-art methods achieved only modest Contact IoU and Volumetric IoU, with residual Center-of-Pressure error, highlighting ongoing challenges in accurate pressure localization under occlusion.
- Evaluations for human and hand motion estimation showed that methods struggled with global trajectory recovery in long-horizon, dynamic home environments (e.g., **~100mm** trajectory error for egocentric hand estimation), indicating significant sensitivity to egomotion and accumulated errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Holistic and synchronized capture of diverse multi-modal data in realistic home environments is critical for building embodied AI capable of understanding complex, long-horizon human interactions.
> - Goal-level instructions during data collection foster natural behavioral variation, providing richer training data that accounts for planning, hesitation, and improvisation in multi-step tasks.
> - Current state-of-the-art methods for tactile inference, human motion, and hand motion estimation exhibit significant generalization gaps in real-world, long-duration home environments, particularly concerning occlusions, egomotion, and accurate contact localization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI aims to develop intelligent agents capable of perceiving and acting in the physical world. A significant challenge in this domain is the acquisition of comprehensive and high-fidelity data that accurately reflects human interaction with environments and objects. Existing datasets, while substantial, often present limitations across several key aspects, leading to a "data bottleneck" for learning complex physical skills.

Current multi-modal datasets frequently offer fragmented experiences, separating observations across viewpoints, modalities, or spatial scales. For instance, large-scale egocentric datasets like Ego4D, EPIC-Kitchens, and Ego-Exo4D provide extensive first-person video but often lack ground-truth body and object motion or synchronized third-person perspectives. Conversely, motion-captured human-object interaction (HOI) datasets such as BEHAVE, GRAB, and ARCTIC offer accurate poses but typically omit egocentric views or other crucial modalities like audio and tactile sensing. This fragmentation means that the full perception-action loop, encompassing first-person perception, whole-body motion, dexterous manipulation, object state changes, sound, and touch, is only partially observed in most available data.

Furthermore, many physically annotated datasets, including DexYCB and ContactPose, are captured in controlled laboratory settings. These environments, often sparse and simplified, do not reflect the clutter, occlusions, spatial constraints, and object diversity characteristic of real-world domestic settings. This limits the generalizability of models trained on such data to more complex and naturalistic scenarios.

Another limitation identified in the existing landscape is the prevalence of short-horizon datasets. The majority of HOI clips focus on atomic, seconds-long movements rather than goal-directed, multi-step activities that unfold over minutes or hours. Genuine household tasks, which involve chained sub-tasks, multiple objects, and locomotion across a scene, require models to maintain task and scene memory, a capability that short-horizon datasets do not adequately support.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing the Ambient Capture Engine (ACE) and the ACE-Data-0 dataset. ACE is designed to integrate diverse sensor modalities (egocentric and exocentric video, full-body and hand motion, object 6-DoF trajectories, audio, and tactile signals) into a unified, temporally synchronized, and spatially calibrated stream. By transforming real home environments into recording studios at two complementary scales (table-scale for fine manipulation and room-scale for whole-body interaction), ACE aims to provide a more holistic and naturalistic representation of human activity. The resulting dataset, ACE-Data-0, focuses on long-horizon, goal-level household tasks, allowing for natural behavioral variation and the study of continuous, complex interactions. This approach seeks to provide a foundation for developing embodied AI models that can chain perceptual capabilities, estimate scene state, and master intricate human-object interactions in realistic settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to overcome a fundamental data bottleneck in the field of embodied intelligence. Developing AI models capable of acting effectively in the physical world necessitates data that captures the intricate interplay of multiple sensory inputs and motor outputs as humans pursue goals over extended periods. Existing datasets are noted to be insufficient in providing a comprehensive, synchronized, and environmentally realistic representation of human activities.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Develop a Holistic Human-Centric Data Engine (ACE):** The first objective was to design and implement a capture system that transforms real home environments into spatially calibrated and temporally synchronized recording studios. This system was designed to operate at two complementary scales:
 - **Table-scale:** To resolve fine-grained hand-object manipulation, focusing on local details.
 - **Room-scale:** To capture whole-body motion, locomotion, and interactions distributed across a furnished home environment.
 This dual-scale approach aims to capture a complete spectrum of human physical interaction, from dexterous manipulation to full-body movement within a larger scene, without compromising on sensor density or spatial coverage.

2. **Acquire a Large-Scale, Long-Horizon, Multi-Modal Dataset (ACE-Data-0):** A central objective was to use the ACE system to collect a substantial dataset that overcomes the limitations of existing resources. This dataset was designed to feature:
 - **Synchronized Multiple Modalities:** Including egocentric and multi-view exocentric video, full-body and articulated hand motion, high-fidelity object geometry and 6-DoF trajectories, multi-channel audio, and tactile signals, all aligned into a unified multisensory stream.
 - **Rich Data Annotations:** Providing detailed labels for human and hand poses, object names, 6-DoF poses, bounding boxes, motion trails, and natural language descriptions.
 - **Diverse Scenarios and Interactions:** Covering atomic manipulation, long-horizon chains of household activities, and human-scene interaction (HSI) across multiple environments and participants.
 - **Natural Behavioral Variation:** Achieved by providing goal-level instructions to participants rather than step-by-step scripts, allowing for natural planning, hesitation, and improvisation.

3. **Establish a Hierarchical Benchmark for Embodied AI Research:** The research aimed to create a benchmark that progresses from low-level sensory signals to complex embodied interactions. This hierarchical structure is intended to:
 - **Evaluate Foundational Perceptual Capabilities:** Assess models on tasks such as tactile inference from visual observations and human motion recovery.
 - **Challenge Interaction Understanding:** Evaluate methods for hand-motion reconstruction during human-object interactions from both egocentric and exocentric perspectives.
 - **Expose Gaps in State-of-the-Art Methods:** Identify specific failure modes under realistic conditions like contact, occlusion, egomotion, and long temporal horizons, thus guiding future research directions.

The overarching motivation is to provide a robust and comprehensive data foundation for advanced embodied AI research, enabling the development and evaluation of imitation learning, world models, vision-language-action systems, and other approaches that require a deep understanding of human physical interaction in complex, dynamic, and realistic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the development of the Ambient Capture Engine (ACE) system and its application to collect and annotate the ACE-Data-0 dataset.

#### 3.1. System Overview

ACE is conceptualized as a "human-centric ambient capture as embodied data engine" paradigm. It operates at two distinct yet complementary spatial scales:
- **Table-scale configuration:** Designed for fine-grained dexterous hand-object manipulation, concentrating on close-range cameras, optical motion capture, and tactile gloves.
- **Room-scale configuration:** Spans a fully furnished apartment, using wide-baseline cameras and optical motion capture to cover a full activity area, targeting global motion and distributed interactions.
Both configurations share a common acquisition and annotation pipeline, ensuring temporal synchronization and spatial registration of all sensor streams into a unified spatio-temporal frame.

#### 3.2. Capture System

**Environment Setup:**
- **Table-scale:** Built around a 30 square meter work desk, populated with over 25 interactable object instances from 8+ categories. Instrumentations include 8 close-range exocentric RGB cameras (0.3–0.5m) and a 16-camera OptiTrack optical motion capture system covering the workspace.
- **Room-scale:** A 200 square meter furnished apartment (kitchen, dining, living room, bedroom) with over 25 object instances. It features a ceiling-suspended truss carrying 8 wide-baseline exocentric RGB cameras and 12 OptiTrack cameras, ensuring at least 4-view coverage even with furniture occlusion. The optical motion-capture system covers the entire apartment within a single tracking volume.

**Hardware Setup:**
The sensor suite is comprehensive, designed to capture diverse facets of interaction:
- **Egocentric Camera:** ACE-Ego-Head-V02 Lite (ACE Robotics) with four fisheye cameras (front-left, front-right, rear-left, rear-right), 1088 × 1280 resolution at 20 FPS, onboard IMU, and five OptiTrack markers for 6-DoF pose tracking.
- **Exocentric Camera:** 8 GoPro RGB cameras (1920 × 1080 @ 30 FPS) for table-scale, and 8 ZED One RGB cameras (1920 × 1080 @ 30 FPS) for room-scale.
- **Human Motion:** Motion-capture suit with 41 markers tracked by OptiTrack PrimeX 22 cameras (28 total across both sites, 60 Hz). Articulated hand poses are captured via Manus motion-capture gloves (60 Hz) in room-scale, and RANSAC triangulation of 2D keypoints followed by manual refinement in table-scale.
- **Object Motion:** Interactable objects are 3D scanned or 2D Gaussian Splatting reconstructed meshes, fitted with OptiTrack markers for 6-DoF trajectories at 60 Hz.
- **Audio:** Captured via GoPro exocentric cameras and the ACE-Ego-Head-V02 Lite.
- **Tactile:** Full-palm tactile gloves record contact pressure maps across palm and fingers.
- **Recording Hosts:** NVIDIA Jetson Orin for ZED One streams (GMSL2), and dedicated PCs for OptiTrack (Motive host).

#### 3.3. Data Acquisition Pipeline

**Sensor Synchronization:**
The OptiTrack clock serves as the reference.
- **Exocentric Cameras:** For room-scale, ZED One cameras are aligned by a common frame trigger via GMSL2 to a Jetson Orin. Alignment to OptiTrack is achieved by photographing a monitor displaying the motion-capture host's time (QR codes) at nanosecond resolution, allowing for post-hoc offset fitting (millisecond residuals). For table-scale, GoPros are audio-synchronized, then aligned to egocentric cameras via the QR-code clock.
- **Egocentric Cameras:** Four fisheye views have mutual misalignment under 2ms. For room-scale, an initial "clock glance" to the monitor aligns egocentric cameras to OptiTrack. For table-scale, alignment is via the QR-code clock and registering AprilTag-based tracking poses to optical markers.
- **Other Sensors:** Tactile gloves are synchronized with ACE-Ego-Head using correlated IMU signals from a short, rigid motion pattern.
All fitted offsets are compiled into a per-take table mapping camera frames to 60 Hz motion-capture frames.

**Calibration:**
- **Exocentric Cameras:** An ArUco board with retroreflective markers is used as a calibration target. This target is visible to both RGB cameras and OptiTrack IR cameras. Calibration proceeds by: (1) Estimating marker positions on the board from IR cameras. (2) Using markers to derive board's 6-DoF pose. (3) Fitting each RGB camera against this marker-anchored board trajectory, discarding moving frames or edge detections. Final joint refinement yields reprojection errors below 3px.
- **Egocentric Cameras:** Five markers on the ACE-Ego-Head chassis are tracked by OptiTrack. Hand-eye calibration solves the fixed transformation from each fisheye camera to this rigid body. A joint bundle adjustment refines all four transformations and per-camera time offsets. Final median reprojection error is ~2px. Camera poses for a take are then interpolated from tracked rig poses.
- **Other Calibrations:** Camera intrinsics (factory for exocentric, Kalibr for egocentric) and object geometry (mesh-to-marker set registration) are established.

**Capture Workflow:**
A five-step protocol ensures consistency: (1) Scene preparation (randomized object placement). (2) Participant setup (suit, headset, gloves, T-pose). (3) Task briefing (goal-level instructions). (4) Recording (clock glance, continuous multi-sensor capture). (5) Post-checks (synchronization/tracking quality verification).

**Multi-modal Recording:**
During recording, simultaneous acquisition includes: (i) exocentric RGB, (ii) 4 egocentric fisheye streams with IMU, (iii) full-body and hand motion (60 Hz), (iv) 6-DoF poses of tracked objects (60 Hz), and (v) tactile signals.

#### 3.4. Data Collection

**Task Design:**
Tasks are long-horizon and goal-level, allowing for natural participant behavior (planning, hesitation, improvisation). Three types of tasks are recorded:
- **Atomic HOI tasks:** 1-3 simple household tasks (e.g., pouring, drinking), 3 minutes each, providing self-contained instances.
- **Chains of HOI tasks:** Full range of short tasks combined into continuous 20-30 minute activities, exercising long-horizon planning and state tracking.
- **HSI tasks:** Human-scene interactions (walking, exercising, sitting) with almost no objects, 5 minutes each.

**Capture Settings:**
50 participants completed all tasks over 2-day sessions. Diversity is achieved through goal-level instructions, leading to varied sub-task ordering, object choice, and movement paths.

**Dataset Statistics:**
ACE-Data-0 comprises over 150 hours of synchronized multi-modal capture, 17M+ frames, and 75,000+ interaction episodes. Shortest takes last minutes, significantly longer than typical HOI clips.

**Modalities:**
Each released take bundles: egocentric video (4 views, IMU, 6-DoF poses), exocentric video (all views, intrinsics, world-frame poses), human motion (41-joint skeletons, articulated hand poses, SMPL-X parameters), object motion (6-DoF trajectories, meshes for 50+ instances), audio (multi-channel from GoPro and ego headset), and tactile (hand-shaped pressure grids).

#### 3.5. Annotation

**Annotation Types:**
Five types of annotations are provided:
- **Calibration and synchronization:** Per-camera intrinsics, poses, and time-alignment tables.
- **Human poses:** Full body and articulated hand poses reprojected onto every camera frame (2D mesh overlays), consistent with 3D capture.
- **Tactile labels:** Per-frame tactile readings (pressure maps) aligned with visual streams and object labels, showing timing and spatial distribution of contact.
- **Object annotations:** Scanned/2DGS meshes, per-frame 6-DoF poses, 2D/3D bounding boxes, and motion trails for each object.
- **Audio and language annotations:** Multi-channel audio paired with natural-language descriptions (identifying sound events, task goals, sub-goals). Textual descriptions are generated by Gemini-3.1-pro-preview from ego-view video and manually checked.

**Annotation Pipeline:**
The majority of annotations (human/object states, poses, bounding boxes, motion trails, contact events) are *measured* or *derived* directly from the synchronized and calibrated ground truth data, minimizing manual effort and estimation errors. Textual descriptions are generated via large language models and then human-corrected.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper establishes a perceptually hierarchical benchmark on ACE-Data-0, structured into three levels: low-level signals, scene components, and embodied interaction. This framework is designed to evaluate state-of-the-art methods and identify current limitations in embodied perception within long-horizon, home-scene data. The evaluation uses a held-out test set of 10 hours of capture.

#### 4.1. Low-level Signals: Tactile from Vision

This benchmark assesses the ability to infer tactile signals, specifically full-hand grasp pressure, from egocentric video. This task is challenging due to the indirect nature of visual evidence and frequent occlusion of fingertips in egocentric views.

- **Metrics:** Temporal accuracy (frame-wise contact-state prediction), Contact IoU (C-IoU, spatial overlap of thresholded contact regions), Volumetric IoU (V-IoU, accounts for pressure magnitude), and Center-of-Pressure (CoP) error (spatial distance between predicted and ground-truth pressure centroids).
- **Baselines:** PressureVision, EgoPressureDiff, TouchAnything.
- **Results:**
 - PressureVision demonstrated limited contact prediction, with near-zero C-IoU and V-IoU and the largest CoP error.
 - EgoPressureDiff showed substantial improvement in temporal contact recognition and pressure localization, but its spatial overlap remained limited.
 - TouchAnything consistently achieved the strongest performance across all four metrics, with notable advantages in temporal accuracy and spatial overlap.
- **Interpretation:** Despite improvements, the absolute C-IoU and V-IoU values for TouchAnything remained modest, and residual CoP error indicated ongoing challenges in accurate pressure localization under occlusion. This suggests a significant generalization gap for existing tactile-estimation models in resolving fine-grained pressure distributions from visually ambiguous interactions.

#### 4.2. Scene Components: Human Motion Estimation

This benchmark evaluates the reconstruction of articulated human body pose over time. Challenges include furniture occlusion, diverse household postures, and long durations leading to global trajectory drift.

- **Metrics:** PA-MPJPE (Procrustes-aligned Mean Per-Joint Position Error), PA-PVE (Procrustes-aligned Per-Vertex Error), MPJPE (Mean Per-Joint Position Error), PVE (Per-Vertex Error), and WA-MPJPE (Whole-path-aligned MPJPE for global trajectory). All metrics are in mm, with lower values indicating better performance.
- **Baselines:** 22 methods, covering per-frame, temporal, and scene-aware approaches, and inputs from single-view exocentric, multi-view exocentric, and egocentric videos. Examples include OSX (single-view per-frame), SMPLest-X (single-view temporal), Human3R (single-view scene-aware), MAMMA (multi-view), and EgoEgo (egocentric).
- **Results:**
 - A distinct gap was observed between local pose estimation (PA-MPJPE, PA-PVE) and global trajectory recovery (WA-MPJPE). Many methods achieved strong local pose accuracy but exhibited much higher world-frame trajectory errors, indicating that correct per-frame pose does not guarantee accurate long-term motion paths.
 - Scene-aware methods generally achieved lower trajectory errors compared to temporal methods, suggesting that scene context primarily aids in global body positioning rather than relative joint configuration.
 - Egocentric methods performed comparatively worse across most metrics, attributed to the limited field of view and the need to infer body parts outside the camera's perspective.
 - Multi-view methods did not consistently outperform the strongest single-view methods in this evaluation, which may reflect the effectiveness of recent single-view approaches and the limited range of multi-view baselines available.
- **Interpretation:** The findings highlight that current methods struggle with global trajectory recovery in dynamic, long-horizon home environments, particularly due to accumulated errors over time and persistent occlusions. Scene context provides some benefit for global positioning, but egocentric views present significant challenges for full-body pose estimation.

#### 4.3. Embodied Interaction: Hand Motion Estimation

This benchmark focuses on the accurate recovery of articulated hand motion from video, which is crucial for robot learning. It compares performance from egocentric and exocentric viewpoints on the same interactions.

- **Metrics:** PA-MPJPE, MPJPE, F@5 (fraction of joints with error below 5mm), F@15 (fraction below 15mm), AUC_J (area under PCK curve), and Trajectory error (world-frame wrist trajectory after whole-clip similarity transform).
- **Baselines (Ego-View):** WildHands (per-frame), Dyn-HaMR (video-based, world-space), HaWoR (video-based, world-space).
- **Baselines (Exo-View):** HaMeR, WiLoR, OmniHands (per-frame); HaPTIC (video-based, world-space); HORT (joint hand-object reconstruction).

- **Results (HOI from Ego-View):**
 - The per-frame method (WildHands) generally showed stronger articulation accuracy, while video-based world-space methods (Dyn-HaMR, HaWoR) performed less well on local pose metrics.
 - World-space methods exhibited large trajectory errors (98-102 mm), much greater than local joint errors.
- **Results (HOI from Exo-View):**
 - Exocentric methods achieved comparable articulation accuracy (PA-MPJPE values from 9.1 to 10.8 mm), with WiLoR performing best (9.1 mm PA-MPJPE).
 - HaPTIC obtained a substantially lower trajectory error of 63 mm, while maintaining comparable articulation accuracy.
- **Cross-View Analysis:**
 - Exocentric methods generally achieved better results for both articulation and global trajectory estimation compared to egocentric methods.
 - The largest difference was in trajectory analysis, with exocentric methods achieving 63 mm error compared to approximately 100 mm for egocentric methods. This difference is attributed to the stable camera coordinate system provided by fixed exocentric cameras, reducing uncertainty from egomotion.
- **Interpretation:** Egomotion estimation in egocentric videos is identified as a major source of error for hand trajectory recovery. While egocentric views offer close-range details, they are susceptible to truncation, distortion, and motion blur. Exocentric views provide a stable reference frame but suffer from occlusion. This suggests that combining both viewpoints could mitigate individual failure cases and that providing measured headset motion to egocentric methods could isolate reconstruction errors from egomotion estimation errors.

Overall, the benchmark evaluations expose significant challenges for existing methods under the realistic conditions of ACE-Data-0, particularly concerning contact detection, handling occlusions, managing egomotion, and maintaining accuracy over long temporal horizons.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces ACE-Data-0, a large-scale, multi-modal dataset captured using the Ambient Capture Engine (ACE), which represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI. The work addresses critical limitations in existing datasets by providing a holistic, synchronized, and environmentally realistic data source for human-centric interactions.

The primary significance of ACE-Data-0 lies in its unique combination of features:
- **Comprehensive Multi-modality:** It simultaneously records egocentric and exocentric video, full-body and articulated hand motion, object 6-DoF trajectories, multi-channel audio, and tactile signals. This integrated data stream, where all modalities are temporally aligned and spatially registered, offers a richer understanding of human actions and their physical consequences than previously available fragmented datasets. This synchronized nature is essential for training embodied agents, as every training signal refers to the same physical moment.
- **Realistic Home Environments:** By capturing data in furnished home settings rather than sterile laboratories, ACE-Data-0 exposes models to the complexities of real-world clutter, occlusions, and spatial constraints. This realism is crucial for developing AI systems that can effectively operate in human-centric environments.
- **Long-Horizon, Goal-Oriented Tasks:** The dataset focuses on continuous, goal-level household activities rather than isolated atomic actions. This design encourages the development of models that can perform long-horizon planning, maintain task and scene memory, and adapt to natural behavioral variations, including hesitation and improvisation.
- **Ground-Truth Annotations:** The pipeline derives most annotations (poses, object states, contact) directly from measured physical states rather than relying on estimations from images. This high-fidelity ground truth provides reliable supervision for learning and robust evaluation.

The hierarchical benchmark established on ACE-Data-0 further contributes by systematically evaluating current state-of-the-art methods across low-level signal inference, scene component recovery, and embodied interaction. The results consistently highlight substantial generalization gaps and expose specific failure modes related to contact detection, occlusion, egomotion, and long-duration activity. These diagnostic insights are valuable for directing future research efforts, suggesting the need for models that:
- **Fuse multiple views and modalities:** To leverage complementary information and overcome individual sensor limitations.
- **Enforce physical constraints:** To maintain consistency and realism in predictions.
- **Learn from measured contact and motion supervision:** To improve accuracy in understanding physical interactions.

The potential impact of ACE-Data-0 is broad, offering a scalable foundation for various embodied AI research areas:
- **Imitation Learning:** Providing human demonstrations with rich, synchronized supervision for training robots.
- **World Models:** Enabling the development of models that can learn to predict the dynamics of the physical world and the consequences of actions.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Systems:** Facilitating the creation of agents that can interpret natural language instructions, perceive their environment, and execute corresponding physical actions.
- **General Embodied Perception and Robot Learning:** Advancing the ability of AI to sense, reason, and act in complex, dynamic human environments.

While the dataset has limitations, such as a restricted number of sites, instrumentation of entities, and visible markers, its contributions in addressing the data bottleneck for embodied intelligence by providing comprehensive, high-fidelity, and contextually rich human interaction data are substantial.
