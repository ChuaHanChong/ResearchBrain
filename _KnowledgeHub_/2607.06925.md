# Grounding Spatial Relations in a Compact World Model: Instruction Leakage and a Goal-Free Dynamics Fix

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stony Brook and Westlake Universities characterized "instruction leakage" in goal-conditioned world models, where models transcribe relational information from language goals rather than genuinely perceiving it, and introduced a diagnostic protocol and a "GoalFree" architectural remedy that recovers instruction-independent grounding for spatial relations, preventing control degradation seen in leaked models.

## Problem

- Grounding inter-object spatial relations robustly in compact world models for control and planning tasks.
- The potential for goal-conditioned world models to exploit "instruction leakage," where explicit language goals lead to apparent understanding through transcription rather than genuine visual perception.
- Lack of reliable diagnostic methods and architectural solutions to differentiate true relational understanding from superficial transcription in embodied AI agents.

## Method

- Developed **PrismWM**, a ==JEPA==-based world model architecture that factors state into a global latent, sparse ==metric point anchors==, and a goal embedding.
- Introduced a **"GoalFree"** model variant that removes the language goal from the world model's dynamics, providing it only to the planner's cost function, while explicitly supervising read anchor paths.
- Designed a rigorous evaluation protocol including ==goal-withheld== and ==counterfactual instruction== probes to detect and quantify instruction leakage.

## Results

- Goal-conditioned models (GoalDyn) showed severe leakage, with predicted-anchor accuracy collapsing from **0.895** to **0.262** (chance) when the goal was withheld, and following counterfactual instructions **94.5%** of the time.
- The proposed "GoalFree" remedy recovered genuine grounding, maintaining predicted-anchor accuracy at **0.88** with and without goal instructions, and achieved control success comparable to the no-goal baseline (**0.29 ± 0.03**).
- Genuine relational perception exhibited a sharp boundary, collapsing to chance (**0.31**) at maximal ambiguity, and failed to transfer across different ambiguity levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Apparent high performance in goal-conditioned world models can be a result of "instruction leakage," where the model transcribes information directly from the language goal, independent of the strength of non-instruction inputs.
> - Genuine, instruction-independent grounding of spatial relations is achieved when observed anchors are explicitly supervised and the language goal is kept out of the world model's dynamics.
> - Goal conditioning within the world model's dynamics consistently degrades control performance by creating misleading internal signals, while placing the goal solely in the planner's cost preserves robust control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI, specifically focusing on the development and evaluation of world models for control and planning. World models aim to enable agents to simulate the consequences of their actions and plan to achieve goals, often through learning latent dynamics from sensory inputs. A common approach involves compact world models, such as those based on Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), which learn compact latent representations of the environment.

A key challenge for these models in tasks involving relational goals (e.g., "put X left of Y") is representing and grounding inter-object spatial relations. Existing compact world models sometimes augment a global latent state with sparse, explicit metric reference anchors, and frequently condition their predictions on a language-based goal. The intuition behind this design is that explicit coordinates provide a clear representation for spatial relations that might be difficult to capture solely within an entangled global latent.

The paper critically examines this common practice of conditioning world model dynamics on language goals, particularly when the instruction explicitly names the relation being evaluated. It identifies a specific failure mode, termed "instruction leakage" or "shortcut learning," where the model achieves high performance by transcribing information directly from the instruction rather than genuinely perceiving and grounding the relation in the visual scene. This phenomenon is analogous to shortcut learning observed in natural language inference and visual question answering, where models exploit spurious correlations in the data.

The work aims to provide a diagnostic protocol and an architectural remedy for this leakage, thereby refining the design principles for goal-conditioned world models that aim to genuinely understand and act upon spatial relations. It differentiates itself by not proposing a new architecture, but rather characterizing a failure mode and offering a solution applicable to various structured world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to investigate the efficacy of explicit metric reference anchors in enabling compact world models to represent and act on spatial relations. Specifically, the authors sought to determine when these explicit references genuinely contribute to grounding a relation and when a language-conditioned latent representation might suffice.

A core motivation for this investigation was the observation of a potential confound in existing goal-conditioned world models. The authors hypothesized that models conditioned on a language goal, especially when the goal instruction explicitly names the relation being evaluated, might achieve high apparent accuracy not through genuine perception and grounding, but through a form of instruction transcription. This "instruction leakage" could lead to models that appear to understand spatial relations robustly, while in reality, they are merely copying information from the prompt. This scenario poses a significant challenge for developing reliable and perceptually grounded AI agents.

To explore this, the research set out to:
- **Measure the intuition:** Quantify if and when explicit references help a compact world model represent and act on spatial relations.
- **Identify and characterize the confound:** Precisely define and demonstrate "instruction leakage" in goal-conditioned world models. The authors aimed to establish that this leakage occurs when the scored quantity is transcribable from the instruction, largely independent of the strength of non-instruction inputs.
- **Develop diagnostic tools:** Create a robust protocol to detect instruction leakage, specifically by using goal-withheld and counterfactual instruction probes.
- **Propose and validate a remedy:** Design an architectural modification to mitigate instruction leakage, aiming to recover genuine, instruction-independent grounding of spatial relations.
- **Evaluate impact on control:** Assess how instruction leakage and its proposed fix influence the model's ability to plan and achieve relational goals in a control task.
- **Establish boundaries of grounding:** Determine the limitations of genuine relational perception, such as its robustness to referential ambiguity and transferability across different ambiguity levels.

The overarching motivation was to move beyond anecdotal observations of "grounding" towards a rigorous, quantified understanding of how language goals interact with world model dynamics, ensuring that reported successes reflect true perceptual understanding rather than superficial transcription.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a structured approach involving a custom-designed environment, a specific world model architecture, a ladder of model variants, and a comprehensive evaluation protocol including critical leakage controls and external validations.

#### 3.1. Relational Tabletop Environment

A controlled 2D relational tabletop environment was procedurally generated. Scenes consisted of N=4 colored shapes, with a designated target (slot 0) and anchor (slot 1). Four spatial relations (left, right, above, below) were defined. Referential ambiguity (number of duplicate distractors) was a tunable parameter (a ∈ {0..., 4}), allowing for scenarios where language alone might not suffice to identify referents. The environment supported deterministic kinematics for target object pushes, allowing relations to be changed through actions. Language goals were generated from templates like "move the <target> to the <r> of the <anchor>," which critically named the relation `r`.

#### 3.2. PrismWM World Model Architecture

The core model, termed PrismWM, was based on a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA). It factored the state into three components:
- **Global JEPA latent (z):** A compact, global representation of the scene, produced by a ViT-tiny encoder.
- **Sparse metric point anchors (p):** Explicit coordinates for key referents, read from patch features.
- **Goal embedding (g):** A tokenized embedding of the language goal.

The model components included:
- A ViT-tiny encoder to produce `z` and visual features.
- A referential module to read anchors `p` from features and condition the latent `z` on them.
- An action-conditioned transformer predictor that rolled `(z, p)` forward in time, predicting `(z', p')`.
- Crucially, in goal-conditioned variants, the goal `g` was fed into the dynamics of the predictor.

Training minimized a combination of losses: next-latent prediction, a JEPA regularizer, an anchor (primitive) loss, and a goal loss.

#### 3.3. Model Variants (The Ladder)

Several model variants were trained to systematically investigate the research questions:
- **NoGoal:** A baseline without any goal conditioning.
- **TextGoal:** A model with goal conditioning but no explicit anchor channel.
- **ObjToken:** A baseline using object-token latents instead of point anchors.
- **GoalDyn:** The primary goal-conditioned model, where the goal `g` was fed into the predictor's dynamics, and only predicted anchors were supervised.
- **GoalDyn+Read:** An extension of GoalDyn where both predicted anchors and *observed* (read) anchors were supervised.
- **GoalFree (Proposed Fix):** The key variant where the goal was explicitly removed from the predictor's dynamics. The goal was only supplied to the planner's cost function, and the read path for observed anchors was supervised.

All models were trained per ambiguity level.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol

The evaluation protocol was designed to rigorously separate genuine perception from instruction transcription:
- **Representation Readouts:**
 - **Observed-anchor accuracy:** Relation geometrically read from the observed anchors of the current frame. This was confirmed as a clean, goal-invariant perception measure.
 - **Predicted-anchor accuracy:** Relation read from anchors after a one-step action-conditioned rollout by the model.
 - **Linear probe on global latent:** A linear classifier trained on the latent `z` to predict the relation.
 - Evaluation metrics included balanced accuracy over 512 episodes, with 1000-sample bootstrap 95% intervals.
- **Leakage Controls (The Crux):**
 - **Goal Withheld:** The goal tokens were zeroed, and predicted-anchor accuracy was recomputed on the *same in-distribution frames*. A genuine perceptual readout should be invariant, while leakage would cause a collapse.
 - **Counterfactual Goal:** A goal naming a *different* (false) relation was fed to the model, and it was measured whether the predicted anchors followed the false instruction or the true scene relation. A genuine model would follow the true scene.
 - These controls were run on the training render distribution to avoid under-counting render-sensitive supervised encoders.
- **Control Evaluation:**
 - The tabletop was framed as a control task: planning an 8-step sequence of pushes to achieve a goal relation different from the current one.
 - **Model-Predictive Control (MPC):** The cross-entropy method (CEM) was used. At each step, the real scene was re-encoded, the model's anchors were rolled forward under candidate actions, and a soft relation-satisfaction cost was scored.
 - **Success Metric:** True relation achieved.
 - **Baselines:** A random policy (floor) and an oracle planner using true dynamics (ceiling).
 - **Goal-in-rollout ablation:** Quantified how leakage-inducing goal conditioning within the planning rollout destroyed control signals.

#### 3.5. External Validation and Ablations

- **BabyAI (GoToLocal):** Evaluated leakage on an external gridworld environment where instructions name egocentric left/right relations.
- **Language-Table:** Tested on Google's Language-Table, where instructions name referents ("push the blue cube towards the red moon") rather than the direction/relation, and then augmented the instruction to induce a leak.
- **Action-ablation dose-response:** A within-task ablation where the strength of the action input was scaled (α: 1→0) to test if leakage increases when non-instruction inputs are degraded, thereby refuting a "predictor-competition" hypothesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Apparent Grounding is Instruction Transcription

- **Initial Deception:** A goal-conditioned model (GoalDyn) with supervised anchors initially displayed high predicted-anchor accuracy (≈ 0.90), appearing robust to ambiguity.
- **Leakage Uncovered:** When the language goal was withheld, GoalDyn's predicted-anchor accuracy collapsed from 0.895 to 0.262 (chance level) at medium ambiguity (a=2), with stable results across three seeds (0.900 ± 0.009 given → 0.270 ± 0.034 withheld).
- **Counterfactual Confirmation:** Under a counterfactual goal, GoalDyn's predicted anchors followed the false instruction 94.5% of the time, while following the true scene only 2.3% of the time. This demonstrated that the model was copying the instruction into coordinates rather than perceiving the scene.
- **Control Degradation:** Feeding the goal into the planner's rollout for GoalDyn reduced control success to the random floor (0.047 at a=2).

#### 4.2. Where Genuine Perception Lives, and Where It Breaks

- **Supervised Observed Anchors:** Genuine, goal-independent perception was identified in the observed (read) anchors, but only when explicitly supervised (GoalDyn+Read). This model achieved 0.82 and 0.86 accuracy at ambiguity 0 and 2, respectively, with identical scores whether the goal was present or zeroed.
- **Explicit Anchors vs. Latent:** The explicit anchor channel consistently carried relational information more cleanly than the global latent (e.g., GoalFree at amb2: observed-anchor accuracy 0.83 vs. latent probe 0.54).
- **Limitations of Unsupervised Anchors:** Learned (unsupervised) anchors performed at chance (≈ 0.25) on the geometric readout, which assumed a supervised slot convention.
- **Ambiguity Boundary:** Genuine grounding exhibited a sharp boundary. At maximal ambiguity (a=4, all distractors duplicated), it collapsed to chance (0.31).
- **No Transfer Across Ambiguity:** A model trained at a=2 achieved 0.86 at a=2 but performed at chance (0.26–0.33) at other ambiguity levels (including easier a=0), indicating narrow tuning rather than general relational perception.

#### 4.3. Goal-Free Dynamics Recovers Grounding; Goal-Conditioning Hurts Control

- **Genuine Grounding with GoalFree:** The proposed GoalFree model, which removes the goal from the dynamics and supervises the read path, achieved predicted-anchor accuracy identical with and without the goal (e.g., 0.87 at a=2 for single seed, 0.88 averaged over three seeds), demonstrating instruction-independent grounding. This was a unique contribution of the fix.
- **Goal-Conditioning Hurts Control:** Control success monotonically decreased as the goal gained more influence over the dynamics. Across three seeds, NoGoal (0.30 ± 0.02) and GoalFree (0.29 ± 0.03) outperformed GoalDyn (goal as predictor, 0.14 ± 0.02), which in turn outperformed GoalDyn with the goal directly in the rollout (0.03 ± 0.02, near random floor 0.055).
- **Control: A Tie, Not a Win:** GoalFree's control success tied with the simplest NoGoal baseline, indicating that the fix's primary value was in achieving grounded perception rather than an improvement in control performance over the simplest non-goal baseline.
- **Dynamics Fidelity Bottleneck:** All variants remained significantly below the oracle planner (0.71), attributing the residual gap to limitations in the learned dynamics (e.g., one-step predictors drifting in multi-step rollouts) rather than the representation itself.

#### 4.4. When Does the Leakage Occur? Three Settings and a Dose-Response

- **Characterization:** Leakage is governed by "transcribability" (whether the instruction names the scored quantity) and is largely independent of non-instruction input strength.
- **BabyAI Validation (Leakage Present):** On BabyAI GoToLocal, a goal-conditioned model with a relation-naming instruction achieved 1.00 with the true instruction but also 1.00 with a counterfactual instruction (true scene score 0.00), confirming leakage in an external environment despite easy perceptual information (observation-only model 0.86).
- **Language-Table Validation (No Leak, then Induced Leak):** On Language-Table, where instructions name referents, no leakage was observed (counterfactual cosine ≈ 0). However, augmenting the instruction to name the goal direction *induced* a weak leak (counterfactual cosine from ≈ 0 to 0.17), confirming that transcribability is the key factor.
- **Action-Ablation (Independence of Non-Instruction Strength):** Systematically degrading the action input never increased leakage. With a transcribable instruction, leakage remained high even against a perfect action (0.975 → 0.986). Without transcription, it stayed at chance. This refuted the hypothesis that leakage occurs only when non-instruction inputs are weak.
- **Implications for Design:** Designs conditioning the predictor on language (e.g., FiLM-into-the-predictor) are exposed to this leakage, while designs placing the goal solely in the planner's cost are immune.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the development of intelligent embodied agents and world models:

- **Identifies and Characterizes a Fundamental Confound:** The paper rigorously identifies, defines, and quantifies "instruction leakage" as a critical shortcut learning phenomenon in goal-conditioned world models. This confound can artificially inflate reported performance metrics for relational understanding and simultaneously degrade control. Its characterization, linking leakage directly to the "transcribability" of the scored quantity from the instruction, provides a clear theoretical understanding of this issue.
- **Provides a Practical Diagnostic Protocol:** The introduction of inexpensive and decisive controls (goal-withheld and counterfactual instruction probes) offers a standardized and essential methodology for researchers to detect instruction leakage. This protocol enables the community to differentiate between genuine perceptual grounding and superficial transcription, ensuring more reliable evaluation of world models. The validated positive control further strengthens the robustness of this diagnostic.
- **Proposes an Effective Architectural Remedy:** The "GoalFree" approach—removing the goal from the dynamics and relocating it to the planner's cost function, combined with supervised anchor read paths—provides a simple yet effective architectural fix. This remedy recovers genuine, instruction-independent grounding, demonstrating that the goal's proper placement is crucial for robust relational understanding.
- **Clarifies Impact on Control:** The findings reveal that goal-conditioning in the dynamics monotonically hurts control performance. The fix restores control to a reasonable baseline, emphasizing that the primary value of the proposed solution is robust perception, not necessarily an improvement in control over a simple no-goal baseline, but rather a correction of a detrimental design choice.
- **Guides Future Research and Design Choices:** By establishing clear boundaries for genuine relational perception (e.g., collapse at high ambiguity, lack of transfer across ambiguity levels) and identifying dynamics fidelity as a key remaining bottleneck for control, the work directly informs future research directions. It suggests that efforts should focus on multi-step prediction, action calibration, and robust training across varied ambiguity levels, rather than solely on representational complexity.
- **Broad Applicability and Field-Wide Audit Potential:** The diagnosis, detection protocol, and remedy are applicable to a wide range of goal-conditioned world models where instructions name the scored quantity. This implies a potential for a "field-wide audit" of existing and future models, guiding the community towards more robust and genuinely intelligent designs in embodied AI. It structurally distinguishes between exposed and immune model designs, providing clear guidance for practitioners.
