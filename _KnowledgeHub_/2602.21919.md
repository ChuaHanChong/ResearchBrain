# Learning in the Null Space: Small Singular Values for Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The NESS algorithm introduces a continual learning method that directly utilizes small singular values from previous task inputs to constrain weight updates, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting. This approach consistently demonstrates superior backward transfer rates, often achieving positive or near-zero forgetting on standard benchmarks, while maintaining stable performance across a sequence of tasks.

## Problem

- Catastrophic forgetting prevents models from sequentially learning new tasks without losing performance on prior ones.
- Balancing model plasticity (adapting to new information) with stability (retaining old knowledge) remains a core challenge in continual learning.
- Existing orthogonality-based continual learning methods often rely on complex gradient projection techniques which can be optimizer-dependent and may not ensure strict orthogonality in weight space.

## Method

- The NESS algorithm directly parameterizes weight updates by constructing a fixed orthogonal basis from the singular vectors corresponding to small singular values of previous task inputs.
- Weight updates (ΔW) are defined as U_t V_t, where U_t is the non-trainable orthogonal basis (representing the approximate null space of past tasks) and V_t is a compact, trainable matrix.
- This approach embeds the orthogonality constraint directly into the weight space, allowing new task learning in directions minimally affecting prior knowledge, and incorporates a stability bound via norm regularization on V_t.

## Results

- NESS consistently achieved superior Backward Transfer (BWT) rates, indicating significantly reduced catastrophic forgetting across CIFAR-100, 5-datasets, and MiniImageNet.
- On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), NESS with SGDm obtained a BWT of 0.03%, outperforming orthogonal baselines such as SGP (-1.18%) and competitive with TRGP (0.06%).
- The method demonstrated strong stability, often achieving positive or near-zero BWT (e.g., 0.41% on MiniImageNet), visually confirmed by fewer performance degradations on past tasks compared to other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directions corresponding to small singular values of previous task inputs are approximately orthogonal to those inputs, providing a stable subspace for new learning without interference.
> - Embedding orthogonality directly into the weight update parameterization offers a more robust and optimizer-agnostic solution for catastrophic forgetting compared to gradient projection methods.
> - Utilizing a Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA-style) for weight updates in this null space intrinsically provides parameter efficiency and explicit stability guarantees.
