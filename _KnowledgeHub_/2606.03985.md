# Humanoid-GPT: Scaling Data and Structure for Zero-Shot Motion Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, Galbot Inc., and collaborators developed Humanoid-GPT, a GPT-style Transformer that achieves robust, real-time, zero-shot whole-body motion tracking for humanoid robots. By training on an unprecedented 2-billion-frame motion corpus, the system demonstrates high fidelity on unseen, dynamic tasks and successfully transfers to physical hardware, addressing a long-standing trade-off between tracking agility and generalization.

## Problem

- A persistent trade-off exists between motion tracker agility (tracking dynamic motions) and generalization (performing on unseen movements), hindering robust whole-body control.
- Current humanoid motion tracking systems primarily rely on shallow Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) and relatively small motion datasets, limiting their capacity for universal, online tracking.
- Existing methods require fine-tuning for new tasks, styles, or environments, making general-purpose humanoid behavior execution challenging.

## Method

- A GPT-style ==Transformer== architecture with causal attention was designed to serve as a generalist, online motion tracker, capable of handling sequential data and scaling with increased capacity.
- An extensive motion corpus of 2 billion frames was curated by aggregating, filtering, retargeting, and augmenting diverse existing and in-house motion capture datasets.
- A two-stage training pipeline was implemented: first, training PPO-based motion experts on clusters identified by Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME), then distilling their knowledge into the Transformer using DAgger for zero-shot generalization.

## Results

- Humanoid-GPT-L, trained on 2 billion frames, achieved a **92.58%** tracking success rate and 40.99mm Mean per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE) in simulation, outperforming MLP and TCN baselines that saturated at lower data scales.
- The system demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer to a physical Unitree-G1 humanoid robot, performing real-time teleoperation and tracking complex, unseen dancing sequences without task-specific fine-tuning.
- Engineering optimizations, including ONNX export and TensorRT compilation, enabled an end-to-end inference latency of under 1.5ms on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, ensuring real-time performance on hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent trade-off between motion tracking agility and generalization in humanoid control is not a fundamental limitation but can be overcome through substantial scaling of data and model capacity.
> - Transformer architectures exhibit clear scaling laws in humanoid motion tracking, continuously improving with increased data and model size, unlike MLPs which show saturation and overfitting.
> - A diverse, large-scale, and distribution-balanced motion corpus is crucial for training a robust zero-shot generalist policy, as quantified and managed by the Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME) technique.
