# Humanoid-GPT: Scaling Data and Structure for Zero-Shot Motion Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, Galbot Inc., and collaborators developed Humanoid-GPT, a GPT-style Transformer that achieves robust, real-time, zero-shot whole-body motion tracking for humanoid robots. By training on an unprecedented 2-billion-frame motion corpus, the system demonstrates high fidelity on unseen, dynamic tasks and successfully transfers to physical hardware, addressing a long-standing trade-off between tracking agility and generalization.

## Problem

- A persistent trade-off exists between motion tracker agility (tracking dynamic motions) and generalization (performing on unseen movements), hindering robust whole-body control.
- Current humanoid motion tracking systems primarily rely on shallow Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) and relatively small motion datasets, limiting their capacity for universal, online tracking.
- Existing methods require fine-tuning for new tasks, styles, or environments, making general-purpose humanoid behavior execution challenging.

## Method

- A GPT-style ==Transformer== architecture with causal attention was designed to serve as a generalist, online motion tracker, capable of handling sequential data and scaling with increased capacity.
- An extensive motion corpus of 2 billion frames was curated by aggregating, filtering, retargeting, and augmenting diverse existing and in-house motion capture datasets.
- A two-stage training pipeline was implemented: first, training PPO-based motion experts on clusters identified by Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME), then distilling their knowledge into the Transformer using DAgger for zero-shot generalization.

## Results

- Humanoid-GPT-L, trained on 2 billion frames, achieved a **92.58%** tracking success rate and 40.99mm Mean per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE) in simulation, outperforming MLP and TCN baselines that saturated at lower data scales.
- The system demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer to a physical Unitree-G1 humanoid robot, performing real-time teleoperation and tracking complex, unseen dancing sequences without task-specific fine-tuning.
- Engineering optimizations, including ONNX export and TensorRT compilation, enabled an end-to-end inference latency of under 1.5ms on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, ensuring real-time performance on hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent trade-off between motion tracking agility and generalization in humanoid control is not a fundamental limitation but can be overcome through substantial scaling of data and model capacity.
> - Transformer architectures exhibit clear scaling laws in humanoid motion tracking, continuously improving with increased data and model size, unlike MLPs which show saturation and overfitting.
> - A diverse, large-scale, and distribution-balanced motion corpus is crucial for training a robust zero-shot generalist policy, as quantified and managed by the Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME) technique.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Humanoid-GPT is situated within the broader context of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) for embodied agents, specifically addressing the challenge of robust whole-body behavior execution in unseen tasks, styles, and environments. Historically, significant advancements in domains such as natural language processing and computer vision have been achieved through the scaling of data, model capacity, and training objectives, exemplified by large language models and foundation models like GPT and Segment Anything.

In contrast, humanoid motion tracking has largely relied on shallow Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) trained on relatively small motion corpora. Existing datasets typically comprise millions of frames, which is considerably less than the scale seen in other AI domains. This limitation has historically led to a trade-off between a tracker's agility (ability to track dynamic motions) and its generalization capability (performance on unseen movements). For instance, methods such as BeyondMimic and ASAP demonstrate proficiency in agile motion tracking but exhibit limited zero-shot generalization. Conversely, approaches like TWIST and UniTracker achieve better generalization but may struggle with highly dynamic actions.

More recent efforts have explored various architectural and data scaling strategies. GMT utilizes a Mixture-of-Experts approach, UniTracker employs a CVAE-based teacher-student framework, and SONIC scales MLP controllers to hundreds of millions of frames. HumanPlus introduces a Transformer controller, though trained with standard PPO. However, these methods have generally been constrained either by the size of the motion data, the architectural capacity to scale effectively, or the training methodology.

Humanoid-GPT aims to address these limitations by applying the "science of scaling" to humanoid motion tracking. It reframes tracking as a GPT-style sequence modeling problem, seeking to overcome the agility-generalization trade-off by drastically increasing data scale and model capacity, thereby attempting to establish a new paradigm for general-purpose whole-body control. This approach seeks to unify agile and generalized tracking capabilities within a single, scalable model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a universal, online humanoid motion tracker capable of robust whole-body behaviors under unseen tasks, styles, and environments without requiring fine-tuning. The authors identify a persistent failure mode in current humanoid motion tracking systems: an inherent trade-off between the agility of tracking and its generalization ability. Trackers optimized for agile, in-domain motions often fail on novel styles, while those designed for generalization tend to underfit complex dynamics. The central hypothesis of this work is that this trade-off is not a fundamental constraint but rather a symptom of insufficient data scale, limited model capacity, and suboptimal training design.

To address this, the research is motivated by three decisive questions that arise when considering scaling motion tracking systems by orders of magnitude:
1. **Data Curation:** What constitutes appropriate training data for large-scale motion tracking, and how can vast, potentially noisy datasets be processed effectively? The motivation here is to move beyond limited, studio-constrained datasets and incorporate diverse, physically consistent motions from multiple sources, including video-estimated data.
2. **Model Architecture:** What model structure can efficiently handle online tracking constraints (causality) and demonstrate continuous performance improvement with increasing scale? The authors argue that capacity-limited MLPs and non-causal models are inadequate for this, motivating the adoption of a scalable, causal Transformer architecture.
3. **Training Stability:** What training recipe can remain stable and effective when datasets grow from millions to billions of frames? This involves refining reward components, hyperparameter tuning, and developing strategies to manage motion diversity.

The specific objectives for Humanoid-GPT are:
- To construct a motion corpus of unprecedented scale (billions of frames) by aggregating existing datasets and incorporating large-scale in-house recordings, with a focus on filtering, retargeting, and augmentation.
- To introduce Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME) as a representation learning tool to quantify and manage motion diversity, enabling distribution-balanced sampling for effective training.
- To design and implement a GPT-style Transformer with causal attention, aligning with online tracking constraints and capable of scaling with data and model size.
- To develop a two-stage training pipeline involving the training of multiple PPO-based motion experts on diverse motion clusters, followed by distilling their knowledge into a single generalist Transformer policy using DAgger.
- To systematically analyze how data scale, model capacity, and diversity balance collectively influence zero-shot agile motion tracking performance.
- To demonstrate robust zero-shot generalization and real-time performance on a physical humanoid robot.

Through these objectives, the authors aim to overcome the agility-generalization dilemma and establish a new performance frontier in humanoid control, driven by scalable data and model architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Humanoid-GPT framework is structured as a two-stage pipeline: initially training multiple reinforcement learning (RL) based motion experts, followed by distilling their collective knowledge into a single Transformer-based generalist policy.

#### 3.1. Data Curation and Processing

The foundation of Humanoid-GPT is an extensive motion corpus, constructed through several steps:
- **Aggregation:** The corpus unifies major motion capture datasets including AMASS, LAFAN1, Motion-X++, PHUMA, and MotionMillion. This is supplemented by a large internally captured dataset to enhance real-world coverage.
- **Filtering and Retargeting:** After aggregation, sequences involving explicit object interactions (e.g., sitting, swimming) are filtered out to ensure compatibility with a humanoid’s actuation in a plain environment. All motions are then retargeted to the 29-DoF joint space of the Unitree-G1 humanoid using an established framework.
- **Augmentation:** To increase temporal variability and robustness to motion speed, motion time-warping is applied, uniformly accelerating and decelerating each sequence. This expands the dataset to approximately five times its original size, resulting in a total of 2 billion G1-retargeted motion frames (tokens).

#### 3.2. Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME)

To manage the diversity of the large motion corpus and enable efficient training, the paper introduces Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME):
- **Feature Extraction:** Periodic Autoencoders are trained on different data partitions to extract per-joint periodic amplitudes and frequencies from each motion sequence.
- **Embedding Generation:** For each sequence, the mean and standard deviation of these joint-level harmonic features are aggregated to form a compact HME vector.
- **Clustering:** K-Means clustering is applied to all HME embeddings, using pairwise distances as the similarity metric. This process generates approximately 300 motion clusters, each containing 1,000–2,000 sequences, designed to provide strong intra-cluster consistency while covering the global motion distribution.

#### 3.3. Training Motion Experts

- **PPO-based Experts:** For each motion cluster identified by HME, a separate PPO-based policy (motion expert) is trained. The goal is for each expert to accurately track all sequences within its specific cluster.
- **Policy Formulation:** Each policy, π: G × S → A, maps the input reference joints and humanoid proprioceptive observations to low-level motor actions. The input state (`s_priv`) includes per-joint positions and velocities, root angular velocity, projected gravity, and the previous control action.
- **Reward Function:** A keypoint-level reward function is designed to ensure physically grounded tracking. It includes exponential terms for position, rotation, and linear velocity consistency between the humanoid and the reference motion for critical body parts (e.g., arms, hips, feet, pelvis). Additional penalties for self-contacts and lack of smoothness promote stable motion.
- **Domain Randomization:** During training, various domain randomization strategies are applied, including variations in floor friction, terrain height, external forces, DoF friction, armature scaling, torso center-of-mass shifts, and torso mass perturbations. This aims to enhance robustness and generalization.
- **Expert Selection:** Only experts demonstrating high-fidelity and long-horizon stability are retained to form a diverse library of motion priors.

#### 3.4. Building Zero-Shot Foundational Tracker (Humanoid-GPT)

- **Distillation Framework:** To consolidate the specialized knowledge from hundreds of motion experts into a single, unified policy capable of zero-shot generalization, a distillation stage is employed using the DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) framework.
- **Transformer Architecture:** The generalist tracker, Humanoid-GPT (`G_θ`), is a Transformer-based model with GPT-style causal attention. This architecture inherently supports sequence modeling and aligns with the online tracking constraint of not accessing future observations.
- **Input and Output:** At each timestep `t`, an input token embedding (`e_t`) is formed by concatenating the current proprioceptive state (`s_t`) and the target reference pose (`q_ref_t`). A sequence of `H` such tokens is fed into the Transformer. The model is supervised to predict `H` actions corresponding to the historical sequence of expert outputs in a single pass, using SmoothL1Loss.
- **Inference:** During inference, a queue of `H` historical tokens is maintained as input, and the output from the last position is used as the current control target. This design allows for parallel sequence supervision during training and autoregressive temporal prediction during inference, enabling stable control even with limited historical information.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key DAgger hyperparameters include 32,768 environments, batch size of 32,768, and a learning rate of 1x10^-4 for 200k iterations. The Transformer model scales up to 12 layers and channel dimensions up to 768.

#### 3.5. Engineering Optimization

To meet the real-time requirements of humanoid control, the deployment pipeline is optimized:
- The model is exported to ONNX and compiled using TensorRT for compute graph optimization.
- A C++-based streaming pipeline is developed to minimize communication latency for online teleoperation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a systematic analysis of data and model scaling, diversity, and architectural choices, leading to several findings:

#### 4.1. Data Diversity Analysis

- Using Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME), the diversity of motion datasets is quantified by geometric mean standard deviation (gstd) and logarithmic volume of the covariance ellipsoid.
- The curated large-scale dataset used for Humanoid-GPT training demonstrates significantly higher embedding scale and broader latent coverage compared to prior datasets like AMASS or AMASS+LAFAN1 (approximately 4-5 times increase in log-volume over AMASS). This indicates that the dataset spans a larger and more uniformly distributed region in the motion manifold, providing richer priors for robust zero-shot tracking.
- Ablation studies confirm that a balanced distribution across motion clusters (e.g., ~384 experts) is crucial for optimal performance, avoiding both overly heterogeneous and overly fine-grained clustering.

#### 4.2. Simulation Evaluation and Scaling Laws

- **Performance Metrics:** The model was evaluated in MuJoCo simulation using metrics including Tracking Success Rate (SR), Mean per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE), Mean per-Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE), Root Velocity Error (RootVelErr), and Mean per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE).
- **Scaling Laws:** Transformer-based Humanoid-GPT models exhibit clear scaling laws. Increasing both the motion corpus size (from 2M to 2B frames) and model capacity (from Humanoid-GPT-S with 5.7M parameters to Humanoid-GPT-L with 80.4M parameters) consistently yields substantial improvements in tracking accuracy and stability across all metrics.
 - The largest model, Humanoid-GPT-L, trained on 2B tokens, achieves the best performance (SR: 92.58%, MPJPE: 0.0735, MPJVE: 0.4820, RootVelErr: 0.1785, MPKPE: 40.99mm).
- **Comparison with Baselines:** MLP and TCN-based baselines (GMT, TWIST, Any2Track) show limitations:
 - **Data Saturation:** While larger MLP/TCN models can reach competitive success rates with sufficient data, their performance gains saturate between 200M and 2B tokens, unlike Humanoid-GPT, which continues to improve.
 - **Overfitting:** Larger MLP/TCN models trained on small datasets (e.g., 2M tokens) exhibit worse performance than their smaller counterparts, indicating overfitting. This gap in precision (MPKPE) remains significant even for their best configurations (best baseline TCN-L at 56.15mm vs. Humanoid-GPT-S at 43.25mm), demonstrating the Transformer's superior tracking precision and more efficient scaling.
- **Model Scalability:** The Transformer architecture demonstrates continuous improvement in training loss over 200K training steps, whereas an MLP of comparable parameter size saturates early, confirming the scalability of Humanoid-GPT.
- **History Length:** The Transformer’s performance improves with increased history length, with gains observed up to 64 frames, although 32 frames were adopted as default for computational balance.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** The distilled Humanoid-GPT policy is deployed on a physical Unitree-G1 humanoid. It successfully tracks pre-recorded, unseen, highly dynamic dancing sequences in real-time without any task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Teleoperation:** In an online whole-body teleoperation setting, the humanoid robot accurately follows an actor's live MoCap stream, imitating diverse actions such as squatting, stepping, turning, leaning, and expressive arm motions, while maintaining balance and fluid transitions.
- **Sim-to-Real Consistency:** Quantitative analysis of real-world tracking accuracy (MPJPE/MPJVE) on unseen dancing motions closely matches simulation results. This indicates strong zero-shot transfer from simulation to hardware and maintenance of high-fidelity whole-body tracking under real-world dynamics.
- **Diverse Behaviors:** Additional demonstrations include tracking complex behaviors like playing basketball, collaboratively carrying boxes, rolling over, and standing up, and various dance routines from videos, all without prior training.

#### 4.4. Engineering Optimization

- Through ONNX export, TensorRT compilation, and a C++ streaming pipeline, the system achieves an end-to-end inference latency of under 1.5ms on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. This ensures real-time performance, despite the large model size.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Humanoid-GPT represents a notable advancement in the field of humanoid motion tracking and control, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Overcoming the Agility-Generalization Trade-off:** The work empirically demonstrates that the agility-generalization trade-off, a long-standing challenge in humanoid control, is not fundamental but can be overcome through scaling data and model capacity. This provides a new perspective and direction for future research in embodied AI.
- **Establishing New Performance Frontiers:** Humanoid-GPT sets a new standard for zero-shot motion tracking, exhibiting robust generalization to unseen tasks while simultaneously tracking highly dynamic and complex motions. Its ability to perform complex dance routines and teleoperation tasks without fine-tuning underscores a significant leap in capability compared to prior methods.
- **Validation of the "Science of Scaling" in Robotics:** By leveraging a 2-billion-frame motion corpus and a GPT-style Transformer architecture, the research provides empirical evidence for the efficacy of scaling principles, previously observed in language and vision, within the domain of physically embodied agents. This could accelerate the development of more capable and general-purpose robotic systems.
- **Roadmap for Future Whole-Body Control:** The derivation of scaling laws relating performance to data scale and model capacity offers a concrete roadmap for researchers and developers. This guidance can inform the design of future general-purpose whole-body control systems, predicting performance improvements with increased investment in data and computational resources.
- **Advancement Towards Embodied Foundation Models:** By distilling a diverse library of RL motion experts into a single, generalist policy, Humanoid-GPT takes a step towards the concept of embodied foundation models. Such models could serve as versatile backbones for various robotic tasks, potentially simplifying the development of complex behaviors.
- **Practical Applications in Real-World Robotics:** The demonstrated real-time, zero-shot teleoperation and execution of complex, unseen motions on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree-G1) opens immediate avenues for practical applications. This could include more intuitive human-robot collaboration, dynamic task execution in unstructured environments, and advanced entertainment or assistance robotics without extensive reprogramming or training for each new task.
- **Pioneering Specific Contributions:** The paper positions Humanoid-GPT as the first system to (i) distill a large library of RL motion experts into a single GPT-style tracker, (ii) train on a curated 2B-frame corpus, and (iii) systematically characterize how data scale, model scale, and diversity balance jointly govern zero-shot agile motion tracking on real humanoid hardware.

Future work indicated by the authors, such as incorporating richer modalities (contacts, vision, language) and extending to interactive or multi-agent scenarios, suggests that Humanoid-GPT could serve as a foundational component for increasingly sophisticated and intelligent humanoid behaviors.
