# MoE-ViE: Mixture of Experts Vision Encoder for Efficient Image and Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> MoE-ViE is a fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts vision encoder for CLIP-style image-text pretraining, using narrow-width experts, shared experts, sigmoid routing, a magnitude-aware loss-free balancing rule, and a custom Triton kernel, plus frame-distillation video finetuning; it matches a 1.7x larger dense SOTA encoder at 76% latency.

## Problem

- Dense vision encoder scaling raises compute cost and inference latency, an issue that compounds for high-resolution images and long videos.
- Prior MoE vision encoders use coarse expert topologies matching original MLP width and have not closed the gap to SOTA dense CLIP-style encoders.
- Naive video finetuning of pretrained image encoders causes catastrophic forgetting of image representations, and mixing image data only partially recovers accuracy while limiting video gains.

## Method

- ==Fine-grained MoE architecture==: narrow-width routed experts plus always-active shared experts with ==sigmoid gating== and renormalized top-k selection, applied only to the vision tower.
- ==Magnitude-aware loss-free balancing==: replaces the sign-based bias update of prior loss-free balancing with a z-score-scaled correction for smoother, oscillation-free expert load balancing.
- Robust video finetuning via ==frame-level distillation== against a frozen image-pretrained teacher combined with freezing MoE experts and text-tower MLPs to preserve image knowledge.

## Results

- A custom Triton kernel with grouped GEMM and kernel fusion gives over **2.5x** inference speedup versus a vanilla MoE implementation across batch sizes.
- MoE-ViE-H matches SOTA dense encoder PE-core-G, **1.7x** its activated parameters, on zero-shot benchmarks while running at **76%** of its latency.
- Ablations show the magnitude-aware loss-free method beats prior auxiliary-loss and loss-free balancing, and combined distillation plus freezing gives the best image and video tradeoff.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained expert granularity, not just adding MoE as a drop-in MLP replacement, is what unlocks consistent gains, mirroring findings from LLM MoE scaling.
> - Sparse capacity scaling only pays off in practice when paired with hardware-aware kernel engineering, since naive MoE execution can erase its theoretical latency advantage.
> - Preserving a frozen pretrained skill while adapting to a new modality benefits from freezing the parameters believed to store that skill, not just adding a distillation loss.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision encoders serve as a foundational component in modern vision-language models (VLMs), with their capacity scaling often correlating with enhanced VLM capabilities. However, scaling dense vision encoders typically incurs substantial increases in computational cost and inference latency, an issue that intensifies with high-resolution images or extended video sequences. This challenge highlights the need for alternative scaling mechanisms.

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have emerged as a method to scale model capacity while maintaining a decoupled relationship with compute cost, as only a subset of experts are activated for any given input. This approach has demonstrated success in large language models (LLMs) and has seen some transfer to VLMs. Despite these advancements, the integration of MoE into CLIP-style vision encoders, particularly to achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on core vision benchmarks, has remained an underexplored area. Prior attempts to develop MoE vision encoders have not consistently matched the performance of leading dense models.

This work addresses this gap by systematically investigating MoE designs for vision encoder scaling. It aims to develop MoE-based vision encoders that not only efficiently scale but also attain SOTA performance, thereby extending the proven benefits of MoE from language models to the vision domain and establishing a unified, high-performing encoder for both image and video understanding. The paper builds upon existing contrastive language-image pretraining (CLIP) frameworks, refining them through novel architectural designs, training strategies, and system-level optimizations to overcome the limitations observed in prior MoE vision work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent trade-offs associated with scaling dense vision encoders in modern VLMs. While increased capacity consistently leads to improved performance and broader applicability, it also results in significant rises in compute cost and inference latency, especially problematic for high-resolution imagery and video. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures offer a theoretical solution by decoupling model capacity from active compute, but their practical application to CLIP-style vision encoders at SOTA levels has been limited, with previous MoE vision encoders failing to close the performance gap with dense counterparts. Furthermore, integrating video understanding capabilities into vision encoders often leads to degradation of image-specific knowledge due to representational shifts during finetuning. Practical system-level overheads, such as memory-bound operations, also hinder the real-world efficiency of MoE.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:

- **Systematic MoE Design Exploration:** To conduct a comprehensive study of MoE architecture design within CLIP vision encoders to identify configurations that yield substantial performance gains over both dense and conventional MoE models. This includes investigating the optimal expert topology (e.g., fine-grained experts) for diverse visual features.
- **Enhanced Expert Utilization:** To develop an effective and stable mechanism for balancing expert utilization during training, aiming to improve over existing auxiliary loss-based methods by decoupling utilization control from the main training objective.
- **Inference Latency Mitigation:** To design and implement specialized MoE kernels that address the practical system overheads associated with sparse computations, thereby translating theoretical efficiency gains into tangible reductions in inference latency.
- **Robust Unified Image and Video Encoding:** To devise a training strategy that enables a single vision encoder to achieve high performance on both image and video understanding tasks without compromising pretrained image capabilities during video finetuning. This involves exploring techniques like frame-level distillation and expert freezing.
- **SOTA Performance and Efficiency:** To pretrain a series of Mixture-of-Experts Vision Encoders (MoE-ViE) that consistently outperform their dense counterparts across various scales, ultimately matching or surpassing the zero-shot performance and efficiency of current SOTA dense encoders and other multimodal models on image and video benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors' approach to developing MoE-ViE involved systematic architectural design, specialized training strategies, and kernel-level optimizations.

#### 3.1. MoE Architecture Design

- **Fine-Grained Expert Design:** Instead of simply replacing dense MLP blocks with MoE layers of equivalent expert capacity (conventional MoE), the authors adopted a fine-grained expert design. This involves reducing the hidden width of each individual expert relative to the original dense MLP, which allows for a larger total number of experts within a comparable compute budget. This choice was motivated by the heterogeneous nature of visual features and insights from LLM research. MoE was applied only to the vision tower, keeping the text tower dense.
- **Shared Experts:** The architecture includes a small number of 'm' shared experts that are always active, complementing the 'cN' specialized experts that are conditionally routed. This design aims to provide a persistent pathway for global context, while routed experts handle token-specific transformations. Ablation studies indicated that a single shared expert provided optimal performance without further gains from increasing the number.
- **Sigmoid Gating:** The standard Softmax-based gating in MoE was replaced with a Sigmoid function for top-k selection, followed by renormalization of selected scores. This modification aimed to mitigate expert competition.
- **Expert Width and Activation:** Ablation studies showed that reducing expert width to 1/4 of the dense MLP width consistently improved performance. The number of activated experts (k) was also studied, with k=8 generally providing a favorable balance between capacity and efficiency. The first transformer block was kept dense, as no gains were observed from specializing it.

#### 3.2. Load Balancing

- **Magnitude-Aware Loss-Free Balancing:** To ensure balanced utilization of experts during training without perturbing the main contrastive training objective, the authors proposed a variant of the loss-free balancing approach. This method introduces an extra bias term (`b`) to the router. The bias for each expert is updated based on its observed token load (`t_e`) relative to the mean (`μ_t`) and standard deviation (`σ_t`) of token counts across experts. Specifically, the update rule `b_e = b_e - α * (t_e - μ_t) / σ_t` scales the correction by the z-score, providing a more stable and effective balancing mechanism compared to a constant-magnitude sign function.

#### 3.3. Latency Optimization

- **Specialized Triton Kernel:** Recognizing that naive MoE implementations suffer from fragmented computations, CPU-GPU synchronization overhead, and excessive memory traffic, a custom Triton kernel was developed. This kernel incorporates two main optimizations:
 - **Grouped GEMM:** This technique aggregates all per-expert matrix multiplications into a single batched GPU task, maximizing parallelism and GPU utilization. It employs a 3D grid parallelizing over tokens, output columns, and experts, and handles variable per-expert token counts with a jagged layout.
 - **Kernel Fusion:** This optimization combines multiple sequential operations (e.g., MatMuls and non-linear activations like SwiGLU) into a single GPU pass. This minimizes round-trips to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) by keeping intermediate tensors in registers, significantly reducing memory bandwidth usage. Token gathering and output scattering are also fused into the GEMM operations.

#### 3.4. Robust Video Finetuning

- **Frame-Level Distillation:** To enhance video understanding capabilities while preventing degradation of image-pretrained knowledge during video finetuning, a frame-level distillation approach was introduced. A copy of the image-pretrained model serves as a teacher. For a randomly sampled video frame, the student model's logits are encouraged to align with the teacher's through minimizing cosine distance (`L_d`). This distillation loss (`βL_d`) is added to the contrastive training loss (`L_c`).
- **Expert Freezing:** During video finetuning, MoE experts in the vision tower are frozen. This is based on the premise that MLP layers primarily store acquired knowledge (per-frame visual vocabulary), while attention layers handle feature interactions for video-specific alignment.
- **Text Tower MLP Freezing:** To address potential optimization mismatches arising from the vision tower being distilled and the text tower being updated by contrastive loss, the MLP layers in the text tower are also frozen during video finetuning. This ensures the text tower's alignment established during pretraining is preserved.

#### 3.5. Training Data and Schedule

- **Pretraining:** Models were contrastively pretrained on 3.5 billion image-text pairs (2 billion from MetaCLIP and 1.5 billion proprietary data). A progressive resolution training schedule was employed, increasing image resolution as training progressed for efficiency.
- **Finetuning:** After pretraining, a short warmup phase was conducted using pretraining data augmented with CC12M and TreeOfLife. Video finetuning used curated video-text data with uniformly sampled 8 frames per video.
- **Optimization Details:** LAMB optimizer with weight decay 0.05 and a large batch size of 262,144 was used. InfoNCE loss was the primary objective during pretraining.

The models were developed across three scales (MoE-ViE-B, MoE-ViE-L, MoE-ViE-H) by replacing MLP modules in a standard CLIP vision encoder architecture with the proposed MoE blocks, excluding the first transformer block.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the efficacy and efficiency of MoE-ViE:

- **Superior Performance over Dense and Conventional MoE Models:** MoE-ViE consistently outperformed its dense counterparts across multiple scales under the same compute budget, demonstrating a favorable capacity-to-compute trade-off. The fine-grained MoE design specifically achieved more substantial and consistent improvements compared to conventional MoE architectures.
- **State-of-the-Art on Zero-Shot Benchmarks:** MoE-ViE models achieved state-of-the-art results across various zero-shot image and video benchmarks. The largest model, MoE-ViE-H, matched or exceeded the performance of the SOTA dense encoder PE_core_G, despite PE_core_G being 1.7 times larger in activated parameters. Notably, MoE-ViE-H demonstrated a +0.6% improvement on ImageNet-A and strong performance on fine-grained image classification and OCR benchmarks, suggesting effective expert specialization.
- **Enhanced VLM Alignment:** When aligned with large language models (Llama 3.1 Instruct 8B and Qwen 2.5 VL 7B), MoE-ViE surpassed all compared encoders on image and video benchmarks, including those with up to 5 times more activated parameters. On captioning tasks, MoE-ViE remained highly competitive. Extended alignment training revealed MoE-ViE-H's strong data scaling behavior, enabling it to outperform PE_lang (a model mid-trained on 70M images with 1.7× more active parameters) across image, video, and captioning tasks when trained on 42M samples.
- **Significant Latency Reduction:** The specialized Triton MoE kernel provided a >2.5× speedup in inference latency compared to a vanilla implementation. MoE-ViE-H achieved approximately 76% of the inference latency of the 1.7× larger SOTA dense encoder PE_core_G/14 while delivering comparable accuracy. These efficiency gains were observed to translate directly to downstream VLM applications, where MoE-ViE demonstrated comparable system-level latency to dense models of similar active parameter counts.
- **Effective Load Balancing:** Ablation studies on expert balancing demonstrated that the proposed magnitude-aware, loss-free balancing method (based on z-score) outperformed other auxiliary loss strategies and the original loss-free approach. This indicates a more stable and effective control over expert utilization, contributing to overall model performance.
- **Robust Video Finetuning Strategy:** The combination of frame-level distillation and freezing MoE experts during video finetuning proved effective in mitigating performance degradation on image benchmarks while simultaneously enhancing video understanding capabilities. The ablation showed that individual components (distillation or freezing) provided benefits, but their combination yielded the best overall results. Critically, applying frame-level distillation without freezing the text tower's MLP layers significantly degraded image performance.
- **Demonstrated Expert Specialization:** Both qualitative and quantitative analyses revealed clear and meaningful expert specialization within MoE-ViE. Visualizations showed individual experts activating on distinct, semantically meaningful aspects of images (e.g., dog's ears vs. eyes/nose, plate vs. food), with shared experts maintaining global context. Quantitative analysis, including per-task expert utilization heatmaps and routing entropy, confirmed that experts developed distinct activation profiles across tasks, with deeper layers exhibiting higher specialization and benchmarks with narrower visual domains showing lower entropy, correlating with MoE-ViE's strong performance on fine-grained tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a comprehensive advancement in the field of vision encoders, particularly in the context of scalable multimodal AI systems. The significance and potential impact of MoE-ViE can be summarized as follows:

- **Validation of MoE for Vision at Scale:** MoE-ViE conclusively demonstrates that Mixture-of-Experts architectures can effectively scale CLIP-style vision encoders to achieve and surpass state-of-the-art performance, thereby closing the performance gap previously observed between MoE-based and dense vision models. This establishes MoE as a viable and highly efficient pathway for increasing the capacity of vision encoders, akin to its impact on large language models.
- **Architectural Innovation and Refinement:** The introduction of a fine-grained MoE topology tailored for heterogeneous visual features, coupled with a magnitude-aware, loss-free load balancing strategy, provides significant architectural and algorithmic contributions. These innovations address specific challenges in applying MoE to vision, leading to improved expert specialization and more stable training dynamics.
- **Practical Efficiency and Deployment:** The development of a specialized Triton MoE kernel is a critical engineering contribution. By translating theoretical computational efficiency into practical, hardware-efficient inference, MoE-ViE can significantly reduce the compute cost and inference latency of large-capacity vision models. This makes advanced vision capabilities more accessible and cost-effective for deployment in real-world applications, especially for latency-sensitive tasks.
- **Unified and Robust Multimodal Understanding:** The robust video finetuning strategy, incorporating frame-level distillation and expert freezing, addresses a key challenge in developing generalist vision encoders. It enables MoE-ViE to excel in both image and video understanding tasks simultaneously without performance compromises, fostering the development of more versatile and capable multimodal AI systems.
- **Advancement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The demonstrated superior performance of MoE-ViE when aligned with LLMs underscores its potential to significantly enhance downstream VLM applications, including visual question answering, video understanding, and captioning. By providing a more powerful and efficient visual backbone, MoE-ViE contributes to the development of more intelligent and responsive multimodal agents.
- **Future Research Directions:** This work provides a robust foundation for future research in scalable vision architectures. It encourages further exploration of MoE designs, expert specialization, and efficient implementation techniques for vision tasks, paving the way for even larger and more capable vision models without prohibitive increases in operational costs.

In summary, MoE-ViE represents a substantial step forward in efficient and high-performing vision encoding, with broad implications for the development and deployment of next-generation multimodal AI.
