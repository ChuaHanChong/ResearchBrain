# Self-Improving World Modelling with Latent Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> The SWIRL framework enables Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models to construct robust internal world models by learning from unlabelled state-only sequences. It employs a reciprocal optimization process between a Forward World Model and an Inverse Dynamics Model, achieving up to a 26.4% improvement in visual dynamics prediction and setting new benchmarks for tool-calling without explicit action labels.

## Problem

- Developing comprehensive world models for AI agents typically requires prohibitively expensive and often intractable action-labelled data for complex, open-world tasks.
- The inherent ambiguity in inverse dynamics makes inferring actions from observed state transitions brittle for purely supervised learning.
- Foundation models need robust internal world models for advanced reasoning, planning, and reducing 'hallucinations', but current approaches face significant data bottlenecks.

## Method

- Introduces **SWIRL**, a reciprocal optimization framework that iteratively refines a ==Forward World Model (FWM)== and an ==Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)==.
- Learns from state-only sequences by treating actions as latent variables, using ==GRPO== for continuous refinement.
- Optimizes FWM to generate identifiable future states by maximizing ==Conditional Mutual Information== and IDM to best explain observed transitions by maximizing an ==Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)==.

## Results

- Achieved an average GPT-4o evaluation score of **5.06** on AURORA-BENCH, a **16%** gain over the Liquid-SFT baseline, and a **26.4%** improvement on BYTEMORPH visual dynamics prediction.
- Demonstrated significantly improved temporal consistency in multi-turn visual dynamics prediction on WORLDPREDICTIONBENCH, maintaining higher fidelity with a 14.4% relative improvement over the baseline at T=6.
- Surpassed supervised fine-tuning by +4.03 BLEU on ID-Low and +3.69 BLEU on ID-Medium splits for simulating API execution outcomes on STABLETOOLBENCH, establishing a new state-of-the-art for its model scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A 'virtuous cycle' exists where improvements in the FWM enhance the IDM's ability to provide precise reward signals, which in turn further improves the FWM.
> - The reinforcement learning approach using GRPO is more data-efficient and robust than supervised fine-tuning, especially when dealing with the inherent ambiguities of inverse dynamics.
> - Using separate weights for the FWM and IDM leads to more stable optimization and better peak performance, particularly in long-horizon tasks, by preventing gradient interference between heterogeneous objectives.
