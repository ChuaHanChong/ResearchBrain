# Self-Improving World Modelling with Latent Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> The SWIRL framework enables Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models to construct robust internal world models by learning from unlabelled state-only sequences. It employs a reciprocal optimization process between a Forward World Model and an Inverse Dynamics Model, achieving up to a 26.4% improvement in visual dynamics prediction and setting new benchmarks for tool-calling without explicit action labels.

## Problem

- Developing comprehensive world models for AI agents typically requires prohibitively expensive and often intractable action-labelled data for complex, open-world tasks.
- The inherent ambiguity in inverse dynamics makes inferring actions from observed state transitions brittle for purely supervised learning.
- Foundation models need robust internal world models for advanced reasoning, planning, and reducing 'hallucinations', but current approaches face significant data bottlenecks.

## Method

- Introduces **SWIRL**, a reciprocal optimization framework that iteratively refines a ==Forward World Model (FWM)== and an ==Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)==.
- Learns from state-only sequences by treating actions as latent variables, using ==GRPO== for continuous refinement.
- Optimizes FWM to generate identifiable future states by maximizing ==Conditional Mutual Information== and IDM to best explain observed transitions by maximizing an ==Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)==.

## Results

- Achieved an average GPT-4o evaluation score of **5.06** on AURORA-BENCH, a **16%** gain over the Liquid-SFT baseline, and a **26.4%** improvement on BYTEMORPH visual dynamics prediction.
- Demonstrated significantly improved temporal consistency in multi-turn visual dynamics prediction on WORLDPREDICTIONBENCH, maintaining higher fidelity with a 14.4% relative improvement over the baseline at T=6.
- Surpassed supervised fine-tuning by +4.03 BLEU on ID-Low and +3.69 BLEU on ID-Medium splits for simulating API execution outcomes on STABLETOOLBENCH, establishing a new state-of-the-art for its model scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A 'virtuous cycle' exists where improvements in the FWM enhance the IDM's ability to provide precise reward signals, which in turn further improves the FWM.
> - The reinforcement learning approach using GRPO is more data-efficient and robust than supervised fine-tuning, especially when dealing with the inherent ambiguities of inverse dynamics.
> - Using separate weights for the FWM and IDM leads to more stable optimization and better peak performance, particularly in long-horizon tasks, by preventing gradient interference between heterogeneous objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the development of sophisticated AI agents: enabling LLMs and VLMs to build robust internal models of the world without relying on prohibitively expensive action-labelled data. This work is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: intrinsic world modeling, self-improving learning, and multimodal foundation models.

**Intrinsic World Models:** Recent research has shown that large foundation models, through their vast pre-training, implicitly internalize some form of world knowledge, allowing for reasoning and planning (e.g., Vafa et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025b). Explicitly training models to predict outcomes of actions, such as tool invocations or code execution, has been demonstrated to significantly boost performance in downstream tasks and reduce hallucinations (Guo et al., 2025b; Copet et al., 2025). However, a major bottleneck remains: creating comprehensive world models, especially for open-world tasks, requires vast amounts of data where observed state transitions are densely annotated with specific actions. Such annotation is often impractical and economically unfeasible. Furthermore, the inherent ambiguity in inverse dynamics—where multiple actions could explain a single state transition—makes purely supervised learning for world models brittle. This paper directly tackles this data scarcity problem by proposing a method that learns from *state-only sequences*, treating actions as latent variables.

**Self-Improving Learning:** This paper also draws heavily on the advancements in self-improving learning, where models leverage their own generated signals to enhance their capabilities without external human supervision. This paradigm has seen success in various applications, from LLMs generating high-confidence answers and fine-tuning themselves (Huang et al., 2023) to self-reflection and reinforcement learning loops for analyzing mistakes (Bensal et al., 2025). In the context of multimodal models, a common strategy involves using an "understanding head" as a critic to guide the "generation head" (Mao et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2025; Qiu et al., 2026), recognizing that understanding often precedes flawless generation in unified VLMs. SWIRL contributes to this landscape by offering a theoretically grounded *reciprocal optimization framework* where both a Forward World Model (FWM) and an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) iteratively reinforce each other, ensuring that improvements in one component directly enhance the other. This reciprocal relationship, explicitly using predicted likelihoods as reward signals, differentiates it from many heuristic self-improvement approaches.

By focusing on learning from unlabelled state sequences and formalizing the interaction between FWM and IDM with theoretical guarantees, SWIRL pushes the boundaries of how AI agents can acquire a deeper, more robust understanding of environmental dynamics. It addresses a fundamental limitation of current approaches, paving the way for more autonomous and generalizable AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-improving framework that enables Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to learn robust internal models of environmental dynamics from readily available *state-only sequences*, effectively bypassing the need for costly and often intractable action-labelled trajectories.

The core motivations behind this objective stem from several critical challenges in the current landscape of AI research:

1. **Data Scalability for Open-World Tasks:** Traditional approaches to world modeling heavily rely on execution logs or trajectories where observations are explicitly annotated with actions. While feasible in restricted environments (e.g., tool-calling logs, coding environments), collecting such dense action annotations for complex, open-world scenarios (e.g., general visual dynamics) is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and often impossible at the scale required for foundation models. This data bottleneck severely limits the development of generalist agents capable of understanding and interacting with diverse environments.
2. **Ambiguity of Inverse Dynamics:** The problem of inferring actions from observed state transitions (inverse dynamics) is inherently ambiguous. A single transition between two states can often be explained by multiple valid underlying actions. This ambiguity makes purely supervised learning of inverse dynamics models brittle, especially when ground-truth action data is sparse or noisy, leading to models that may overfit to specific verbalizations rather than capturing the underlying physics or logic.
3. **Enhancing Foundation Model Capabilities:** Robust internal world models are considered essential for advanced AI capabilities such as sophisticated reasoning, effective planning, and reducing "hallucinations" (predictions that contradict environmental regularities). By internalizing how actions lead to state changes, foundation models can simulate possible futures and generate more coherent and physically plausible outputs, thereby unlocking new levels of intelligence and reliability.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes SWIRL (Self-improving World modelling with Iterative RL), aiming to:
- Formalize world modeling into two interdependent components: a Forward World Model (FWM) for predicting next states and an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) for inferring actions.
- Develop an iterative reinforcement learning framework that optimizes these components using only state transitions, treating actions as latent variables.
- Provide theoretical guarantees for the learning process, connecting it to established information-theoretic objectives like Conditional Mutual Information (CMI) and Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO).
- Empirically validate the framework's effectiveness and generalizability across various modalities and tasks, including visual dynamics, textual simulations, web interactions, and tool-calling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SWIRL operates as a reciprocal optimization framework that iteratively refines a Forward World Model (FWM) and an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) using only state-only sequences, treating the intermediate actions as latent variables. The entire process is driven by Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm.

The methodology is structured around two alternating phases:

#### 3.1. Task Formulation and Components

The core idea is to model transitions from a source state `x` to a target state `y` through a latent action `z`.
- **Forward World Modelling (FWM):** `Pθ(y|x, z)` – This model, parameterized by `θ`, predicts the next state `y` given the current state `x` and a specific action `z`.
- **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):** `Qϕ(z|x, y)` – This model, parameterized by `ϕ`, infers the action `z` that likely caused the transition from state `x` to state `y`.
The parameters `θ` and `ϕ` can be either disjoint or shared, allowing for flexibility in model architecture.

#### 3.2. SWIRL's Iterative Optimization Phases

- **Phase I: FWM Optimization (Variational Information Maximisation)**
 - **Goal:** Update the FWM (`Pθ`) to generate future states (`ˆy`) that are consistently *identifiable* by the IDM. This means the generated future state should clearly imply the latent action that supposedly caused it.
 - **Mechanism:** In this phase, the IDM's parameters (`ϕ`) are frozen. The FWM (`Pθ`) acts as a *policy*.
 1. For a given observed state transition `(x, y)` from the unlabelled dataset `D`, the frozen IDM `Qϕ(z|x, y)` is used to infer a latent action `z`.
 2. The FWM `Pθ(·|x, z)` then generates a group of `G` possible next states, `{{ˆy_k}}`, given `x` and the inferred `z`.
 3. Each generated next state `ˆy_k` is evaluated by querying the *frozen* IDM `Qϕ(z|x, ˆy_k)` to see how well it "explains" the original latent action `z`.
 4. The reward for `ˆy_k` is defined as `r_k = log Qϕ(z|x, ˆy_k)`.
 5. GRPO is then applied to update `Pθ`, maximizing the advantage induced by these rewards.
 - **Theoretical Justification:** This phase is proven to be equivalent to maximizing a variational lower bound on the Conditional Mutual Information `I(Z; ˆY |X)`. This objective encourages the FWM to produce distinct and informative outcomes for varied latent actions, enhancing the identifiability of these actions from the generated states.

- **Phase II: IDM Optimization (ELBO Maximisation)**
 - **Goal:** Update the IDM (`Qϕ`) to infer actions (`z`) that *best explain* the observed state transitions `(x, y)` under the learned FWM. This ensures *data fidelity* to the observed dynamics.
 - **Mechanism:** In this phase, the FWM's parameters (`θ`) are frozen. The IDM (`Qϕ`) acts as a *policy*.
 1. For an observed state transition `(x, y)` from the unlabelled dataset `D`, the IDM `Qϕ(·|x, y)` generates a group of `G` candidate actions, `{{ˆz_k}}`.
 2. Each candidate action `ˆz_k` is evaluated by querying the *frozen* FWM `Pθ(y|x, ˆz_k)` to see how likely the observed target state `y` is, given the initial state `x` and the inferred action `ˆz_k`.
 3. The reward for `ˆz_k` is defined as `r_k = log Pθ(y|x, ˆz_k)`.
 4. GRPO is applied to update `Qϕ`, maximizing the advantage from these rewards.
 - **Theoretical Justification:** This phase is proven to be equivalent to maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) of the marginal log-likelihood `log Pθ(Y|X)`. This ensures that the IDM learns to infer actions that are highly plausible given the observed state transitions and the current understanding of forward dynamics.

#### 3.3. Initialisation and Training Details

- **SFT Warm-up:** Before SWIRL, a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) warm-up phase is performed. This is crucial for general-purpose VLMs/LLMs (e.g., Liquid-7B, Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct) to acquire the necessary input-output interface and basic generation/perception capabilities required for the specific environments.
- **Iterative RL:** The two phases are alternated until convergence. Training is typically performed by uniformly sampling videos from large-scale unlabelled corpora like VIDGEN-1M, extracting frame pairs for state transitions.

#### 3.4. Environments and Models

SWIRL's generality is tested across a wide range of environments:
- **Visual Dynamics:** Using Unified Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Liquid for open-world visual dynamics tasks (e.g., image editing, object motion).
- **Synthetic Textual Environments:** Using LLMs like Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct for physics simulations (SCIENCEWORLD) and web interactions (MIND2WEB).
- **Tool Calling:** Using LLMs for simulating API execution outcomes (STABLETOOLBENCH).

This reciprocal, RL-driven optimization, grounded in information-theoretic principles, allows SWIRL to learn consistent world models purely from observational data, addressing the inherent challenges of data annotation and inverse dynamics ambiguity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of SWIRL across a diverse set of environments and benchmarks demonstrates significant improvements in world modeling capabilities for both VLMs and LLMs, particularly without reliance on explicit action labels.

#### 4.1. Visual Dynamics Prediction (VLMs)

- **Single-Turn Prediction (AURORA-BENCH & BYTEMORPH):**
 - **AURORA-BENCH:** SWIRL consistently and significantly outperforms the Liquid-SFT baseline, achieving an average GPT-4o evaluation score of 5.06, a **16% gain** over the SFT baseline (4.36). It also surpasses other strong baselines like bootstrapping and test-time verification. Despite starting with a smaller base model, SWIRL remains competitive with larger, state-of-the-art unified VLMs (e.g., OmniGen2, BAGEL) and substantially outperforms diffusion-based image editing models (e.g., InstructPix2Pix).
 - **BYTEMORPH:** All SWIRL variants substantially increase the average GPT-4o score, with the iterative version showing a **26.4% improvement** (from 43.38 to 53.77) over Liquid-SFT. While camera control performance remains comparable, SWIRL exhibits pronounced improvements in understanding and generating object and human motion and interactions, indicating effective learning of fine-grained dynamics from unlabelled videos.

- **Multi-Turn / Long-Horizon Prediction (WORLDPREDICTIONBENCH):**
 - SWIRL demonstrates significantly improved temporal consistency in long-horizon rollouts compared to Liquid-SFT. While SFT performance rapidly degrades (from 3.09 at T=1 to 0.97 at T=6), SWIRL (ITERATIVE) maintains higher fidelity, achieving a **14.4% relative improvement** over the baseline at T=6 (1.11). This suggests that SWIRL learns more robust physical dynamics, resisting the compounding covariate shift inherent in sequential generation.

#### 4.2. Textual Environments (LLMs)

- **SCIENCEWORLD & MIND2WEB:** For physical and digital simulation environments, both SWIRL and the Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct-SFT baseline achieve near-saturation in semantic accuracy (BERTScore >92) and comparable lexical matching (ROUGE-L). This indicates that for tasks where the base LLM already performs well, SWIRL maintains this high performance.
- **STABLETOOLBENCH:** SWIRL exhibits substantial improvements in generalization for simulating API execution outcomes. It surpasses SFT by **+4.03 BLEU** on ID-Low and **+3.69 BLEU** on ID-Medium splits, establishing a new state-of-the-art for its model scale and outperforming significantly larger open-weight instruction models (e.g., Qwen-2.5-32B, DeepSeek-7B). This highlights SWIRL's ability to internalize complex API dynamics effectively.

#### 4.3. Key Analysis Findings

- **Iterative RL Effectiveness:** Analysis shows a clear "virtuous cycle" where enhancing FWM improves IDM, which in turn provides a more precise reward signal for subsequent FWM updates. Both FWM and IDM training rewards effectively improve and converge with GRPO, validating the iterative self-improvement paradigm.
- **RL vs. SFT Advantages:** SWIRL (RL) significantly outperforms SFT baselines (continual training and data merging) in data efficiency, achieving higher GPT-4o scores with fewer samples and showing performance gains as data scales. This is attributed to GRPO's ability to encourage consistency rather than exact replication, which is more robust to the inherent ambiguity and multi-modality of action verbalizations compared to token-level imitation in SFT.
- **Shared vs. Separate Weights:** Using separate weights for FWM and IDM yields more stable optimization and better peak FWM performance, especially in long-horizon tasks. While shared weights reduce memory, they introduce instability due to gradient interference between the heterogeneous objectives of visual generation (FWM) and linguistic action inference (IDM).
- **IDM Reliability and Latent Actions:** The IDM achieves high action prediction accuracy (GPT score of 6.38), confirming its reliability as a reward mechanism. Importantly, generated latent actions do not degenerate into "reward hacking" artifacts; they remain diverse (>94% unique), descriptive (average length > ground truth), and natural-sounding throughout training, maintaining interpretability.
- **Inference-Time Verification:** Experiments confirm that the FWM can generate high-fidelity transitions in multiple rollouts, validating the GRPO formulation's coverage hypothesis: a larger pool of generations increases the likelihood of capturing valid transitions.

In summary, SWIRL successfully demonstrates a novel, robust, and theoretically sound approach to self-improving world modeling, enabling foundation models to learn complex dynamics from unlabelled data and achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks and modalities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SWIRL framework represents a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly for enhancing the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its primary significance lies in circumventing a major bottleneck in AI development and offering a path toward more autonomous and generalizable intelligent agents.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Research

- **Overcoming the Data Annotation Bottleneck:** The most critical contribution is enabling foundation models to self-improve their internal world models using *unlabelled state-only data*. This eliminates the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and often impractical human-annotated action trajectories, especially for open-world scenarios. This dramatically increases the scalability and accessibility of training sophisticated reasoning agents.
- **Enhanced Intrinsic World Modeling:** SWIRL fosters a deeper, more robust internal understanding of environment and agent dynamics. This intrinsic world model is crucial for advanced cognitive abilities such as complex reasoning, long-horizon planning, and reducing "hallucinations" by grounding model outputs in physical or logical consistency.
- **Theoretical Grounding and Robustness:** The provision of rigorous theoretical guarantees, linking the FWM optimization to Conditional Mutual Information and IDM optimization to the Evidence Lower Bound, provides a strong foundation for the method. This theoretical backing distinguishes SWIRL from many heuristic self-improvement techniques and contributes to the understanding of the underlying learning dynamics.
- **Broad Generalizability:** The empirical validation across diverse modalities (visual, textual), environments (real-world visual dynamics, synthetic physics, web, tool-calling), and model types (VLMs, LLMs) demonstrates the framework's broad applicability. This suggests that SWIRL can be a versatile tool for various AI agent development tasks.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The analysis highlights that SWIRL's reinforcement learning approach is more data-efficient and robust than traditional supervised fine-tuning, especially when dealing with the inherent ambiguities of inverse dynamics. By encouraging consistency rather than exact imitation, it learns more generalizable dynamics.

#### 5.2. Potential Positive Impact

- **Democratization of Advanced AI:** By reducing the need for expensive expert-annotated data, SWIRL can lower the barrier to entry for developing powerful AI agents. This could accelerate research and development in smaller labs or resource-constrained environments.
- **More Capable and Reliable AI Assistants:** The ability of LLMs and VLMs to better understand and simulate physical and digital environments can lead to more effective and trustworthy general-purpose AI assistants, capable of complex tasks, human-like planning, and safer interactions.
- **Accelerated Scientific Discovery and Automation:** Improved world models could enhance AI's utility in scientific simulations, robotics, and complex automation, allowing agents to predict outcomes and plan in intricate scenarios more effectively.
- **Reduced Hallucinations:** A better internal world model means fewer outputs that contradict reality, leading to more factual and reliable generative AI.

#### 5.3. Potential Negative Impact and Societal Consequences (as identified by the authors)

- **Bias Amplification:** SWIRL learns from unlabelled "in-the-wild" data, which inherently contains biases and harmful patterns present in web and video distributions. Without human curation, there is a significant risk that the model could internalize, reinforce, or even amplify these biases. This necessitates the integration of strong safety filters, ethical guidelines, or "constitutional AI" principles within the reward mechanisms to mitigate harm.
- **Misuse of Advanced Generative Capabilities:** Enhancements in visual dynamics prediction (generating future frames) and web HTML interaction capabilities can be misused. This includes:
 - **Deepfakes and Synthetic Media:** Lowering the technical barrier for generating highly realistic fake videos or images, potentially contributing to misinformation, manipulation, and erosion of trust.
 - **Autonomous Malicious Agents:** Creating more capable autonomous web agents that could bypass security measures (e.g., CAPTCHAs), conduct large-scale automated interactions for nefarious purposes (e.g., fraud, exploitation), or engage in other forms of digital harm.

The authors responsibly emphasize the need for robust detection tools for synthetic media and strict access controls and guardrails for autonomous agents operating in real-world environments. This highlights the ongoing ethical imperative to align powerful AI capabilities with societal well-being and to proactively address potential misuse cases.
