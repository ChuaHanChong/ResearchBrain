# Towards Fusing Point Cloud and Visual Representations for Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology developed FPV-Net, a multimodal imitation learning method that integrates point cloud and RGB image representations using Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN) conditioning. This approach enables state-of-the-art performance on challenging robotic manipulation tasks on the RoboCasa benchmark, demonstrating enhanced generalization and robust skill acquisition.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation tasks often require both precise 3D geometric understanding (from point clouds) and rich semantic/texture information (from RGB images), but single modalities are insufficient for consistent high performance.
- Existing multimodal fusion techniques in imitation learning frequently lead to a loss of global contextual information from RGB images when 2D features are assigned to 3D space.
- Prior diffusion policies for 3D robotics sometimes discard critical local geometric features through aggressive pooling, limiting their efficacy for fine-grained manipulation.

## Method

- ==FPV-Net== utilizes a transformer-based diffusion policy for action generation, adopting an EDM-based approach for robust learning.
- It processes RGB images with a language-conditioned FiLM-ResNet to extract both global and local visual tokens, preserving fine-grained details, and processes point clouds using Furthest Point Sampling and k-Nearest Neighbors to capture structured local geometric features.
- The core fusion mechanism involves Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN) conditioning, where rich RGB features modulate the primary point cloud and language representations, balancing geometric precision with semantic context.

## Results

- FPV-Net variants (FPV-MLP and FPV-SUGAR) achieved average success rates of **49.42%** and **50.50%** on the RoboCasa benchmark, substantially outperforming baselines like BC (**28.80%**), 3D Diffusion Policy (**22.75%**), and 3D Diffuser Actor (**5.50%**).
- AdaLN conditioning, specifically using image features to modulate point cloud and language inputs, consistently demonstrated the most effective fusion strategy, outperforming direct concatenation and other conditioning approaches.
- Ablation studies confirmed that incorporating local RGB features significantly improves performance on fine-grained tasks, and finetuning pretrained 3D encoders (SUGAR) further boosts average success rates by **2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligent fusion of complementary modalities, specifically using AdaLN conditioning with RGB features to modulate point cloud representations, significantly outperforms single modalities or simpler concatenation strategies.
> - The preservation and integration of local RGB features alongside global visual context are crucial for achieving high performance in fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Transformer-based diffusion policies, when effectively combined with this multimodal sensor fusion strategy, can learn robust and generalizable robotic manipulation skills from limited demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Towards Fusing Point Cloud and Visual Representations for Imitation Learning" addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of robotic learning, computer vision, and multimodal AI: enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by effectively utilizing rich, complementary sensory information.

**Imitation Learning (IL)** has emerged as a fundamental paradigm in robotic control, allowing robots to acquire intricate behaviors by observing and mimicking expert demonstrations. However, the success of IL hinges significantly on the quality and richness of the input sensory representations.

Historically, **RGB images** have been a ubiquitous input modality due to their ease of acquisition, rich texture, and semantic information crucial for object recognition and contextual reasoning. However, 2D images inherently lack explicit 3D geometric information, making them prone to issues with occlusions, viewpoint variations, and precise spatial reasoning crucial for manipulation. While multi-view cameras can partially mitigate this, they demand more data and computational resources.

In contrast, **3D scene representations**, particularly **point clouds**, offer direct geometric and spatial information, making them highly effective for tasks requiring precise 3D understanding. Yet, point clouds, often derived from depth sensors, can suffer from sparsity, noise, and the loss of fine-grained texture or semantic details during down-sampling processes, which are typically necessary for computational efficiency.

The burgeoning field of **multimodal sensory fusion in IL** seeks to combine the strengths of these disparate modalities while mitigating their individual limitations. Prior approaches have largely focused on two main strategies:
1. **Assigning 2D visual features to 3D point clouds:** Methods like Act3D (Gervet et al., 2023) and 3D Diffuser Actor (Ke et al., 2024) attempt to lift 2D image features (e.g., from ResNets) into 3D space. A key limitation here, as identified by the authors, is the loss of global contextual information from the original images, leading to suboptimal performance in tasks requiring both fine spatial reasoning and high-level semantic understanding.
2. **Combining image observations with language goals:** Much research has explored integrating language instructions with visual inputs, often using Transformer architectures or leveraging large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP to create Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While successful, these approaches typically remain anchored to 2D image features, limiting their efficacy in scenarios demanding detailed 3D spatial reasoning, especially with small datasets.

This paper positions itself by identifying a crucial gap: the effective and balanced fusion of **RGB images and point clouds (alongside language instructions)**, explicitly addressing the shortcomings of current 2D-to-3D feature assignment methods. The authors highlight that a novel, flexible sensor fusion scheme is needed, particularly noting that **adaptive conditioning in Layer-Norm layers (AdaLN)**, a technique successfully used in scalable diffusion models (Peebles & Xie, 2022), has not yet been thoroughly explored for RGB-point cloud fusion in the context of imitation learning.

Furthermore, the work operates within the increasingly popular framework of **diffusion policies** for robot control. Diffusion models have shown remarkable success in generating high-quality samples by learning to reverse a denoising process, and their application to action generation in IL (e.g., Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusion Policy) represents a state-of-the-art approach for robust and generalizable policy learning. By integrating multimodal fusion with a diffusion-based policy, FPV-Net aims to achieve superior performance in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent limitations of relying on single sensory modalities or sub-optimal fusion techniques for complex robotic manipulation. The authors clearly articulate that while point clouds provide essential geometric structures and RGB images offer rich texture and semantic information, existing methods for combining them often fall short. Specifically, assigning 2D image features to point clouds frequently results in the loss of global contextual information from the original images, hindering performance in tasks requiring a holistic understanding of both spatial and semantic cues.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of this paper are threefold:

1. **To systematically demonstrate the limitations of single-modality approaches and naive fusion strategies:** The authors aim to experimentally prove that neither RGB images nor point clouds alone are sufficient for consistently strong performance across diverse and challenging manipulation tasks. Furthermore, they seek to show that simple concatenation-based fusion methods can often lead to performance degradation due to poor alignment between modalities.
2. **To introduce FPV-Net, a novel, diffusion-based multi-modal imitation learning method:** The core objective is to develop a robust architecture that effectively leverages point cloud inputs as a primary modality and visual inputs as a conditional modality, integrated via **Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN) conditioning**. This approach is hypothesized to better balance geometric precision with semantic richness. A critical sub-objective here is to establish the novelty and efficacy of using AdaLN for fusing point cloud and RGB modalities in imitation learning.
3. **To highlight the crucial role of local RGB features in fine-grained robotic manipulation:** The paper aims to demonstrate that incorporating both global and local visual features significantly enhances model performance, particularly for tasks requiring precise interaction with small objects or intricate maneuvers, a detail often overlooked by approaches that only extract global image tokens.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to enable robots to acquire more robust, generalizable, and adaptable policies for manipulation in diverse and realistic environments by intelligently combining all available sensory information—geometric, semantic, and linguistic context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FPV-Net (Fusion of Point Cloud and Visual Representation Network) is designed as a multi-modal transformer-based diffusion policy. Its methodology can be broken down into problem formulation, sensory processing for each modality, and novel fusion strategies.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The agent learns from a dataset of expert trajectories, where each observation `o` is multimodal, comprising a language instruction `x_L`, an RGB image `x_I`, and a point cloud `x_P`. Following the success of Action Chunking, the policy `π(a|o)` predicts a sequence of future actions `a = (a_k..., a_{k+H})` over a prediction horizon `H`.

#### 3.2. Score-Based Diffusion Policies

FPV-Net adopts a continuous-time denoising diffusion model, specifically based on EDM (Karras et al., 2022), to represent the policy. The core idea is to learn a neural network that approximates the score function `∇_a log p_t(a|o)`, which guides the denoising process from Gaussian noise back to the data distribution. During training, noise is added to expert action sequences, and the network is trained to predict the denoised actions using a Score Matching (SM) objective. For inference, the DDIM-solver (Song et al., 2020) is employed to generate new action sequences from random noise in a few discrete denoising steps (4 steps in FPV-Net).

#### 3.3. Image Processing

To extract rich visual representations, FPV-Net utilizes a **FiLM-ResNet architecture** (Perez et al., 2018). Crucially, this ResNet is **conditioned on language instructions** via FiLM layers, allowing the linguistic context to modulate visual feature extraction. Unlike many prior works that extract only a global token, FPV-Net extracts both:
- **Global Token:** Obtained by applying global average pooling over the ResNet feature map.
- **Local Tokens:** Obtained by flattening the feature map into a sequence of tokens, preserving fine-grained spatial details.
These global and local tokens are then concatenated to form a comprehensive visual representation `z_I`, providing a multi-scale understanding.

#### 3.4. Point Cloud Processing

To address the limitations of prior 3D imitation learning methods (e.g., max pooling discarding local features, excessive token generation), FPV-Net processes point clouds `x_P` by constructing a structured representation:
- **Furthest Point Sampling (FPS):** `M = 256` center points are sampled to ensure global geometric coverage.
- **k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN):** For each center point, `K = 32` nearest neighbors are retrieved, forming local point groups.
Each local point group is then encoded into a latent representation using a point cloud encoder `ψ_θ`. The final point cloud embedding `z_P` consists of `M = 256` tokens. Two encoding strategies are explored:
- **Lightweight MLP Encoder:** A simple multi-layer perceptron followed by max pooling for computational efficiency.
- **Pretrained SUGAR Model:** A more sophisticated 12-layer Transformer-based encoder pretrained on large-scale 3D datasets (Objaverse), with the last 2 layers finetuned to reduce computational cost.

#### 3.5. Fusing Multi-Modal Embeddings

Language embeddings `z_L` are obtained using a frozen CLIP model (Radford et al., 2021). The paper explores three strategies for combining `z_I`, `z_P`, and `z_L`:

1. **Concatenation-Based Fusion:** The simplest approach, directly concatenating `z_I`, `z_P`, and `z_L` as input to the transformer policy. This serves as a baseline for multimodal integration.

2. **Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN) Conditioning:** Inspired by DiT models (Peebles & Xie, 2022), this approach treats one modality as a conditioning input to scale and shift the main features within the Transformer's attention mechanism. AdaLN(z | c) = `γ(c) ⊙ (z - μ(z))/σ(z) + β(c)`.
 - **Image and Language as Main Modality:** Here, `z_I` and `z_L` are the primary features, and point cloud embeddings `z_P` are used as the conditioning input `c`.
 - **Point Cloud and Language as Main Modality (FPV-Net's primary choice):** `z_P` and `z_L` are the primary features, and image embeddings `z_I` (specifically, a global CLS token extracted from RGB features via a transformer) serve as the conditioning input `c`. This strategy is hypothesized to preserve geometric precision while leveraging semantic richness.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmark:** **RoboCasa** (Nasiriany et al., 2024), a challenging, large-scale simulation framework for generalist robots in diverse kitchen environments. It features 120 scenes, 2,500+ objects, and 100 tasks (24 evaluated in this work). Crucially, it provides only *one demonstration per scenario*, and training/evaluation datasets use *entirely distinct scenes*, making it a rigorous test of generalization.
- **Training & Evaluation:** Models are trained for 100 epochs with 50 human demonstrations per task, then evaluated over 50 episodes.
- **Baselines:**
 - **BC (Behavioral Cloning):** RoboCasa's BC-Transformer (ResNet-18 + FiLM layers for RGB, CLIP for language).
 - **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3):** Uses MLP-based encoder for single-view point clouds, generates actions with a convolutional network.
 - **3D Diffuser Actor (3DA):** Diffusion-based policy conditioned on 3D scene features (lifted 2D features) and language.
- **FPV-Net Variants:**
 - **PC-only:** Point cloud + language, MLP point encoder.
 - **RGB-only:** RGB views + language, ResNet-18 with FiLM layers.
 - **PC+RGB:** Concatenation of PC-only and RGB-only features.
 - **FPV-MLP:** AdaLN conditioning (RGB on PC+Lang), MLP point encoder, uses local RGB features (8x8 map).
 - **FPV-SUGAR:** AdaLN conditioning (RGB on PC+Lang), pretrained SUGAR point encoder (finetuned last 2 layers), uses local RGB features (4x4 map).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the challenging RoboCasa benchmark yielded several significant findings, addressing the core questions posed by the authors:

**Q1) Is a single modality enough to perform efficiently on challenging environments?**
- **Finding:** No. Table 1 clearly demonstrates that models utilizing both point cloud and RGB modalities consistently outperform those relying on a single modality. FPV-Net's various fusion methods achieve average success rates significantly higher than PC-only or RGB-only baselines.
- **Analysis:** The simple concatenation of point cloud and RGB features (PC+RGB) alone leads to a 10% improvement over individual modalities, underscoring their complementary nature. Tasks like pick-and-place and insertion, which require both precise spatial reasoning and high-level object understanding, particularly benefit from this combined input.

**Q2) How does our method compare with state-of-the-art imitation learning policies?**
- **Finding:** FPV-Net achieves state-of-the-art performance across nearly all tasks, establishing a new benchmark in multimodal imitation learning on RoboCasa.
- **Analysis:** FPV-MLP and FPV-SUGAR (the AdaLN-conditioned FPV-Net variants) yielded average success rates of 49.42% and 50.50% respectively. This is a substantial improvement over baseline methods like BC (28.80%), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) (22.75%), and 3D Diffuser Actor (3DA) (5.50%). The authors attribute DP3's lower performance to its max-pooling step, which discards critical spatial information, contrasting with FPV-Net's point grouping strategy that better captures local geometric features. 3DA's very low performance is tentatively attributed to potentially insufficient training epochs given its complexity.

**Q3) What kinds of fusion types are most powerful?**
- **Finding:** AdaLN conditioning, specifically with **image features as the conditioning input and point cloud + language as the main modality**, proves to be the most powerful fusion strategy.
- **Analysis (Figure 4):** Directly concatenating modalities (PC+RGB) performs better than conditioning RGB on PC, but significantly worse than conditioning on RGB. Conditioning the RGB-based transformer on point cloud features (Cond. on PC) underperforms compared to simple concatenation, possibly due to compressing the entire point cloud into a single, potentially information-losing, vector. Conversely, FPV-Net's approach of treating point cloud and language as the primary modalities and conditioning them with RGB features (Cond. on RGB) consistently yields the best performance. This suggests that the diffusion policy effectively leverages the rich texture and semantic details from RGB data via AdaLN conditioning, while maintaining the geometric precision of point clouds as its main input.

**Additional Ablation Studies:**
- **Ablation on Obtaining Condition Vector:** A transformer-based approach for extracting a global condition token from RGB features for AdaLN conditioning consistently outperforms simple max-pooling, indicating that a more sophisticated aggregation of visual features is beneficial (Figure 5).
- **Ablation on RGB Features:** Incorporating **local RGB features** (e.g., from 4x4 or 8x8 feature maps) significantly enhances performance, particularly for fine-grained manipulation tasks such as interacting with buttons and drawers. This highlights the critical role of preserving fine-grained visual details, which are often lost when only global image tokens are used (Figure 6).
- **Ablation on Finetuning SUGAR:** Finetuning the last two layers of the pretrained SUGAR encoder improved the average success rate by 2% compared to a fully frozen encoder, suggesting that task-specific adaptation of pretrained 3D features is beneficial (Figure 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Towards Fusing Point Cloud and Visual Representations for Imitation Learning" by Donat et al. marks a significant advancement in the field of robotic learning, particularly in enabling generalist robots to operate effectively in complex, unstructured environments.

1. **Advancement in Multimodal Perception for Robotics:** FPV-Net demonstrably pushes the state-of-the-art in multimodal imitation learning. By outperforming existing baselines by a substantial margin on the highly challenging RoboCasa benchmark, the research provides compelling evidence that intelligent integration of complementary sensory data is paramount for achieving robust and generalizable robotic manipulation skills. This success on a benchmark with diverse scenes, novel objects, and single demonstrations per task underscores its potential for real-world application where robots must adapt to unseen scenarios.

2. **Novel and Effective Fusion Strategy (AdaLN Conditioning):** The most impactful contribution is the introduction and validation of Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN) conditioning as a superior mechanism for fusing point cloud and RGB image data in imitation learning. Unlike previous attempts that often compromised one modality for the other, FPV-Net's approach—using RGB features to condition the point cloud-based policy—successfully preserves the geometric precision of 3D data while leveraging the semantic richness of 2D images. This novel fusion scheme offers a flexible and powerful paradigm that could inspire future research in multimodal representation learning across various robotic tasks.

3. **Emphasis on Local Visual Features:** The paper's ablation studies compellingly demonstrate the critical, often underappreciated, role of local RGB features for fine-grained manipulation. By showing that integrating both global and local visual information significantly enhances performance on tasks requiring precise interaction with small objects (e.g., pressing buttons, opening drawers), the authors challenge the common practice of relying solely on global image tokens. This insight has immediate implications for designing more effective visual encoders for robotic systems.

4. **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The success of FPV-Net on RoboCasa, a benchmark specifically designed to test generalization to novel scenes and objects from limited demonstrations, highlights its potential for creating more adaptable robot policies. This improved generalization is crucial for deploying robots in real-world environments, such as homes and kitchens, where variability is immense and pre-programming for every scenario is infeasible.

5. **Practical Implications for Generalist Robots:** The findings pave the way for developing more capable and versatile robots. By enabling robots to robustly interpret both geometric structure and semantic meaning from their surroundings, FPV-Net brings us closer to realizing generalist robots that can perform a wide array of everyday manipulation tasks, understand human instructions, and adapt to unforeseen circumstances in dynamic environments. This has significant potential impact on various applications, from household assistance to industrial automation and hazardous environments.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The work opens several new avenues for exploration. Future research could investigate even more advanced AdaLN conditioning variants, explore dynamic ways to weigh local vs. global features, integrate additional modalities (e.g., haptic feedback), or apply this fusion strategy to more complex hierarchical tasks or long-horizon planning. The demonstrated effectiveness of finetuning pretrained 3D encoders also points towards promising directions for leveraging large-scale foundation models in robotic perception.

In conclusion, FPV-Net offers a sophisticated and empirically validated solution to a long-standing challenge in robot learning. By thoughtfully combining multimodal sensory inputs through a novel conditioning mechanism, the authors have made a substantial contribution towards building more intelligent, adaptive, and general-purpose robotic systems.
