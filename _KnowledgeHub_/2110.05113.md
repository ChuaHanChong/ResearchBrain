# Learning High-Speed Flight in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Zurich and Intel developed an end-to-end learning framework that enables quadrotors to autonomously navigate complex, unknown environments at speeds up to 10 m/s using only onboard sensing and computation. The system, trained entirely in simulation, achieved zero-shot generalization to diverse real-world conditions like dense forests and disaster zones, reducing failure rates by up to 10 times compared to baseline approaches.

## Problem

- Autonomous quadrotors traditionally struggle to fully exploit their agility and fly at high speeds (comparable to human pilots) through complex, unknown environments due to system limitations.
- Traditional modular navigation pipelines (sensing, mapping, planning) introduce significant latency and suffer from compounding errors, limiting achievable speed and robustness in dynamic, cluttered settings.
- Previous end-to-end learning approaches for robotics face challenges with data requirements and the 'sim-to-real gap,' where policies trained in simulation fail to generalize effectively to physical systems.

## Method

- An end-to-end learning approach where a lightweight convolutional neural network directly maps noisy depth images and inertial measurements to short-term, collision-free trajectories.
- Training data is generated in simulation using a 'privileged expert' that has perfect environment knowledge and an unconstrained computational budget to sample optimal collision-free B-spline trajectories.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer is enabled by training solely in simulation, utilizing abstract input representations (depth images), multi-modal trajectory prediction with a Relaxed Winner-Takes-All loss, and Dataset Aggregation (DAgger).

## Results

- The system achieved zero-shot generalization, autonomously navigating diverse real-world environments (e.g., dense forests, disaster zones) at average speeds up to 10 m/s using only onboard computation.
- In simulation, the approach reduced failure rates by up to 10 times compared to state-of-the-art baselines (FastPlanner, Reactive) at high speeds, demonstrating a **60%** success rate at 10 m/s where baselines consistently failed.
- The learned policy exhibited low processing latency of **41.6** ms on an onboard Jetson TX2, enabling a control update rate of approximately 24 Hz, crucial for real-time high-speed flight.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-to-end learning can effectively replace traditional modular sensing-mapping-planning pipelines for high-speed, agile autonomous navigation, significantly reducing latency and enhancing robustness.
> - Privileged learning, coupled with multi-modal output prediction via a Relaxed Winner-Takes-All (R-WTA) loss, is effective for training complex robotic policies in simulation, enabling zero-shot transfer to diverse real-world conditions.
> - Using abstract sensor representations like depth images, rather than raw RGB, is crucial for robust sim-to-real generalization as they are less sensitive to appearance variations and noise.
