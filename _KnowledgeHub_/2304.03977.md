# EMP-SSL: Towards Self-Supervised Learning in One Training Epoch

> [!summary] Summary
> EMP-SSL introduces an approach to dramatically accelerate self-supervised learning by processing numerous overlapping image patches to efficiently learn co-occurrence statistics. This method achieves competitive performance, such as 85.1% accuracy on CIFAR-10, in a single training epoch, significantly reducing computational requirements compared to traditional SSL.

## Problem

- Current self-supervised learning (SSL) methods are computationally inefficient, typically requiring hundreds of training epochs to converge.
- This inefficiency creates substantial computational barriers, limiting the broad application of SSL approaches.
- Traditional SSL relies on a small number of augmented views per image, necessitating many passes through the dataset to accumulate sufficient statistics.

## Method

- EMP-SSL processes a large number of random, ==overlapping patches (20-200)== from each input image, instead of relying on a small number of augmented views.
- The architecture consists of a standard encoder network (ResNet-18) and a two-part objective function: ==Total Coding Rate (TCR)== to prevent collapse and an ==Invariance Loss== to enforce consistency among patch representations.
- The approach avoids complex components like momentum encoders, prediction networks, or non-differentiable operators, resulting in a clean and efficient implementation.

## Results

- EMP-SSL achieved impressive accuracies in a **single training epoch**: **85.1%** on CIFAR-10, **58.5%** on CIFAR-100, **38.1%** on Tiny ImageNet, and **58.5%** on ImageNet-100.
- The method significantly outperformed traditional SSL methods (BYOL, VICReg, SwAV, SimCLR) when all were limited to few training epochs, showing a strong performance gap in early stages.
- EMP-SSL attained over **90%** accuracy on CIFAR-10 in fewer than **10 epochs**, reaching competitive results in 10-30 epochs, compared to hundreds required by conventional methods.
- Representations learned by EMP-SSL showed clearer class separation in t-SNE visualizations and exhibited better transferability to out-of-domain datasets than baseline SSL methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning methods fundamentally learn the co-occurrence statistics of image patches.
> - Processing a large number of overlapping patches from a single image allows for efficient capture of co-occurrence statistics in a single training pass.
> - A balanced loss function combining invariance objectives with total coding rate regularization prevents representation collapse while encouraging meaningful feature learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training deep neural networks without labeled data. However, a significant drawback of current SSL methods is their inefficiency - they typically require hundreds of training epochs to converge to effective representations. This inefficiency creates substantial computational barriers, limiting the broad application of SSL approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper "EMP-SSL: Towards Self-Supervised Learning in One Training Epoch" introduces a novel approach that dramatically accelerates SSL training convergence. By leveraging the extreme multi-patch (EMP) concept, the method achieves impressive performance in just one training epoch, substantially reducing the computational requirements for effective self-supervised learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The EMP-SSL Approach

The core insight behind EMP-SSL is that SSL methods fundamentally learn the co-occurrence statistics of image patches. Traditional SSL approaches rely on a small number of augmented views per image, requiring many passes through the dataset to accumulate sufficient statistics. EMP-SSL takes a radically different approach:

1. Instead of using just 2-8 views per image as in conventional SSL, EMP-SSL extracts and processes a large number of overlapping patches from each image (20-200 patches)
2. These numerous patches provide the model with rich co-occurrence statistics in a single pass
3. By learning from many patches simultaneously, the model efficiently captures the underlying structure of the data without requiring multiple epochs

The approach strikes a balance between computational efficiency and statistical learning, making one-epoch SSL a practical reality.

#### 3.2. Methodology

EMP-SSL employs a simple yet effective architecture consisting of three key components:

1. **Patch Extraction and Augmentation**: For each input image, EMP-SSL extracts a large number (n) of random, overlapping, fixed-size patches. These patches undergo standard data augmentations such as random cropping, color jittering, and Gaussian blur.

2. **Encoder Network**: The augmented patches are processed through a standard encoder network (ResNet-18 in the experiments) to obtain feature representations.

3. **Learning Objective**: EMP-SSL employs a two-part objective function:
 - Total Coding Rate (TCR): Prevents representation collapse by maintaining diversity
 - Invariance Loss: Enforces consistency between representations of different patches from the same image

The final representation of an image is computed as the average of all patch embeddings. This approach avoids complex components such as momentum encoders, prediction networks, or non-differentiable operators, resulting in a clean and efficient implementation.

Mathematically, the invariance loss can be expressed as:

```
L_invariance = (1/n) * Σ_i=1^n ||z_i - z̄||^2
```

where z_i represents the embedding of the i-th patch and z̄ is the mean embedding of all patches from the same image.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across multiple datasets to evaluate EMP-SSL's performance:

1. **One-Epoch Performance**: In just a single training epoch, EMP-SSL achieved impressive accuracies:
 - 85.1% on CIFAR-10
 - 58.5% on CIFAR-100
 - 38.1% on Tiny ImageNet
 - 58.5% on ImageNet-100

2. **Comparison with SOTA Methods**: EMP-SSL significantly outperformed traditional SSL methods (BYOL, VICReg, SwAV, SimCLR) when all were limited to few training epochs. The performance gap was especially pronounced in the earliest stages of training.

3. **Convergence Speed**: While conventional SSL methods require hundreds of epochs to reach their peak performance, EMP-SSL achieved competitive results in just 10-30 epochs. For instance, on CIFAR-10, EMP-SSL attained over 90% accuracy in fewer than 10 epochs.

#### 4.2. Accelerating Training Convergence

The dramatic improvement in convergence speed is EMP-SSL's most significant contribution. The paper demonstrates that with just 10 epochs of training, EMP-SSL already achieves comparable performance to traditional methods trained for hundreds of epochs.

This acceleration stems from two key factors:

1. **Efficient Learning of Co-occurrence Statistics**: By processing many patches simultaneously, EMP-SSL captures the statistical structure of the data more efficiently.

2. **Balanced Loss Function**: The combination of invariance objectives and coding rate regularization prevents representation collapse while encouraging meaningful feature learning.

The plots show that EMP-SSL not only starts from a higher initial accuracy but also maintains a faster learning trajectory throughout training.

#### 4.3. Impact of Multiple Patches

A crucial finding from the ablation studies is the direct relationship between the number of patches used and the model's performance. The authors experimented with varying patch counts (10, 20, 100, and 200) and found:

1. **More Patches = Better Performance**: Increasing the number of patches consistently improved accuracy, especially in the early epochs.

2. **Diminishing Returns**: While going from 10 to 100 patches showed substantial gains, the improvement from 100 to 200 patches was more modest, suggesting a point of diminishing returns.

3. **Batch Size Considerations**: The authors also investigated the impact of batch size, finding that with a sufficient number of patches per image, smaller batch sizes (50-100) could still achieve strong performance. This finding has important implications for training on hardware with limited memory.

#### 4.4. Representational Quality

To assess the quality of the learned representations, the authors visualized the feature spaces using t-SNE projections. These visualizations revealed that even after just a few epochs, EMP-SSL learned meaningful and well-structured representations:

The t-SNE plots show that EMP-SSL (a) produced clearer class separation than BYOL (b), VICReg (c), SwAV (d), SimCLR (e), and ReSSL (f). This visual evidence supports the quantitative findings and demonstrates that EMP-SSL learns semantically meaningful features efficiently.

#### 4.5. Out-of-Domain Transferability

A common concern with efficient learning methods is whether they achieve their speed by overfitting to the training domain. The authors addressed this by testing the transferability of the learned representations to out-of-domain datasets:

1. **Cross-Dataset Transfer**: Models trained on one dataset were evaluated on different datasets without additional fine-tuning.

2. **Results**: EMP-SSL exhibited better transferability than baseline SSL methods, suggesting that it learns more generalizable features rather than dataset-specific patterns.

This finding is particularly important as it indicates that the efficiency of EMP-SSL doesn't come at the cost of generalization. Instead, by learning from diverse patches, the model develops more robust representations that transfer well across domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EMP-SSL represents a significant advancement in self-supervised learning by addressing one of its major limitations: training efficiency. By leveraging multiple patches from each image, the method achieves in a single epoch what traditional approaches require hundreds of epochs to accomplish.

The key contributions of this work include:

1. **Extreme Efficiency**: Achieving competitive performance in just one training epoch, reducing computational requirements by orders of magnitude.

2. **Simplified Architecture**: Using a clean, straightforward approach without complex components like momentum encoders or prediction networks.

3. **Strong Generalization**: Developing representations that transfer well to out-of-domain data, indicating robust feature learning.

4. **Practical Implications**: Opening possibilities for applications in resource-constrained environments, online learning scenarios, and large-scale datasets where training efficiency is crucial.

The success of EMP-SSL suggests a fundamental rethinking of the data efficiency in self-supervised learning paradigms. Rather than requiring many passes through a dataset, the key may be to extract more information from each pass by considering multiple perspectives simultaneously. This insight could guide the development of even more efficient self-supervised learning methods in the future.
