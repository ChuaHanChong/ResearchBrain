# Beyond Instance Slots: Semantically Rich World Models for Physical Interaction Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> SR-WM (Semantically Rich World Model) is a compact 45M-parameter world model built on the LeWM encoder-predictor that binds soft visual entities to five task-conditioned roles - gripper, target, goal, relation, phase - and predicts action-conditioned semantic transitions. The role state conditions flow-matching action generation, stage-aware candidate reranking, and violation-aware suffix repair, lifting average LIBERO closed-loop success from 45.5% to 83.0%.

## Problem

- Latent world models such as DINO-WM and LeWM minimize prediction error, yet low error does not tell a planner whether an action establishes the required task state.
- Object-centric slot states identify which entities are present but keep slots exchangeable, leaving task binding, grasp status and required predicates to the downstream policy.
- Recent object-centric world models lean on persistent segmentation-derived slots or ground-truth masks and poses as state, so they degrade when proposals are missing or corrupted.

## Method

- A ==visual entity encoder== extracts soft entity hypotheses (appearance, geometry, motion, confidence) from ==LeWM== patch tokens; segmentation proposals enter only as an additive attention bias with independent dropout.
- A ==task-conditioned role binder== cross-attends language and proprioception queries over entities into five non-exchangeable state components; a ==gated role transformer== predicts transitions at J sub-horizon boundaries.
- The shared role state conditions a ==flow-matching== head sampling K action chunks, a ==stage-aware semantic selector== trained with shuffled-action hard negatives, and ==violation-aware suffix resampling== that pins the valid prefix.

## Results

- Swapping monolithic LeWM latents for role states raises Candidate 0 success from **45.5%** to **62.8%** and Oracle@4 coverage from **68.0%** to **89.5%** across four LIBERO suites.
- The semantic selector converts coverage into **75.3%** top-1 success, full resampling **78.5%**, and prefix-preserving suffix repair **83.0%**, against **55.8%** for instance slots with a slot selector.
- Patch-only inference keeps **72.6%** rollout success (oracle masks **78.4%**); dropping shuffled negatives collapses the action-discrimination margin to **0.018** and ranking accuracy to **53.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Typing state by functional role rather than instance identity changes what the generator proposes, not merely how a fixed candidate set is reranked afterwards.
> - Segmentation is more useful as a droppable attention bias than as a mandatory state interface; dropout training preserves mask-free, corruption-tolerant deployment.
> - Predicting intermediate sub-horizon transitions localizes the first violation, letting a plan be repaired by suffix resampling instead of being discarded wholesale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of robot learning and artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on visual world models for physical interaction planning. The work addresses limitations in existing approaches by introducing a semantically rich representation for robotic manipulation tasks.

Current visual world models typically predict future observations or latent features based on candidate actions. While these models, such as DINO-WM (Zhou et al. 2024), LeWM (Maes et al. 2026), and RC-aux (Li et al. 2026), have demonstrated efficiency and a degree of alignment with planning, they often lack explicit representations of task-critical predicates. This deficiency means that a plausible predicted future might not be *task-consistent*, potentially involving interactions with incorrect objects, loss of grasp, or premature release, thereby not directly answering whether an action establishes a required state or preserves constraints.

Another area this work builds upon is object-centric manipulation. Methods like Slot Attention (Locatello et al. 2020), FOCUS (Ferraro et al. 2025), VIOLA (Zhu et al. 2023), and Slot-MPC (Spieler, Villar-Corrales, and Behnke 2026) decompose scenes into individual entities, improving robustness and exploration. More recent world models like OA-WAM (Liu et al. 2026), WorldDP (Goswami et al. 2026), MaskWAM (Yu et al. 2026), and MRO-GWM (Kreber, Mack, and Stueckler 2026) also incorporate object information, sometimes utilizing segmentation priors like SAM. However, these approaches primarily focus on object *instance* identity, which does not inherently define an object's *functional role* within a task context. An object's role (e.g., target, goal, distractor) can change depending on the instruction, leaving the critical aspects of task binding and progress implicit for downstream policy decisions.

Furthermore, this research relates to action proposal and semantic progress in robotic control. Diffusion policies (Chi et al. 2025) and nearest-neighbor policies (Pari et al. 2021) generate action candidates. However, simply generating diverse candidates or evaluating them with a value head does not guarantee that the chosen action will lead to the desired semantic transition. This work aims to integrate semantic understanding directly into the action generation and selection process.

The Semantically Rich World Model (SR-WM) distinguishes itself by explicitly integrating functional roles into its world model, moving beyond generic instance slots. It aims to provide a structured, task-conditioned representation that directly informs planning by predicting semantic transitions and supporting robust action selection and repair.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to address a fundamental gap in existing world models for physical interaction planning: while many models excel at predicting future observations or latent features, they often fail to provide the semantically rich information necessary for a robot to determine if a candidate action will successfully achieve task objectives and maintain essential constraints.

The specific problems identified are:
1. **Lack of Task-Consistent Prediction:** Traditional world models predict plausible futures, but these futures might not be aligned with the task's specific semantic requirements. For instance, an action might successfully move an object, but to the wrong location, or fail to establish a stable grasp, leading to task failure despite accurate low-level prediction.
2. **Obscured Entities in Monolithic States:** Monolithic state representations, which do not explicitly delineate individual objects, hinder the model's ability to reason about specific interactions with discrete environmental components.
3. **Insufficient Semantic Context in Instance-Level Slots:** While object-centric representations, such as instance-level object slots, identify the presence of objects, they typically do not specify the *functional role* each object plays within the current task context. An object identified as "carrot" or "container" does not convey whether it is the target to be grasped, the goal to be reached, or merely a distractor. Furthermore, an object's role can dynamically change across different phases of a task or different instructions, a flexibility not inherently captured by fixed instance identities. This places the burden of inferring task binding and progress on subsequent, often separate, policy components.

To bridge this gap, the research defines the following key objectives:
1. **Introduce Task-Conditioned Role Binding:** To develop a mechanism that maps general visual entities to a structured set of five functional roles: gripper, target, goal, relation, and phase. These roles are designed to explicitly encode semantics, geometric relationships, and task progress, providing a high-level, interpretable interface for planning.
2. **Unified Role State for Planning:** To utilize this semantically rich role state as a direct conditioning input for both multi-candidate action generation (via a Flow-Matching head) and an action-conditioned dynamics model. This allows the model to predict fine-grained semantic transitions, including contact, grasp status, predicate establishment, predicate preservation, fixture states, and phase changes.
3. **Enhance Decision-Making with Semantic Information:** To integrate this semantic understanding into a stage-aware selection mechanism that ranks action candidates and a violation-aware suffix resampling strategy that can repair problematic action sequences. The evaluation protocol is designed to systematically assess these contributions across representation quality, action sensitivity, candidate coverage, selector performance, transferability, and system efficiency.

Ultimately, the motivation is to transform basic object-centric prediction into a robust semantic interface, thereby directly linking the visual dynamics of an environment with the higher-level decision-making requirements of physical interaction planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Semantically Rich World Model (SR-WM) is a compact architecture that extends the 15M-parameter LeWM encoder–predictor (Maes et al. 2026). Its core innovation lies in binding soft visual entities to five task-conditioned functional roles and using this structured representation to guide action generation, prediction, and selection.

### 4. From Exchangeable Entities to Task-Conditioned Role States

The SR-WM separates the representation of visual entities from their task-specific functional roles.
- **LeWM Entity Tokens and Soft Visual Entities:** The process begins with a lightweight LeWM visual encoder that processes multimodal observations (RGB history, robot state, language instruction) into patch tokens. Learned entity queries then cross-attend to these multi-view patch tokens to produce *N* soft, task-agnostic entity hypotheses (e_t,n). Each entity comprises appearance, geometry, motion, and confidence features. These entities are "soft," meaning they do not require a one-to-one correspondence with ground-truth segmentation masks.
- **Optional Mask Prior:** Segmentation proposals from SAM-family models (e.g., Grounding DINO + SAM/SAM2) can be optionally used as an additive attention bias during entity encoding. This prior helps focus attention but is not a hard requirement; dropout mechanisms (over complete proposal sets, individual masks, or bias strength) during training prevent over-reliance, allowing for robust performance even without mask-based inference.
- **Task-Conditioned Role Binding:** Language-conditioned role queries (q_r) then attend to these soft visual entities (e_t,n) to construct the task-conditioned predictive state (Z_t). This state consists of five non-exchangeable, separately parameterized functional roles: z_grip (gripper state, including proprioception), z_tgt (task-relevant object or part), z_goal (destination or desired fixture state), z_rel (geometric and symbolic predicates between entities), and z_phase (progress through task stages). This binding mechanism allows the same visual entity to assume different roles based on the instruction or robot state, without retraining the entity encoder.

### 5. Action-Conditioned Role Dynamics

The role state (Z_t) directly conditions both action generation and prediction. For each candidate action chunk (A_k_t), an action encoder maps it to action tokens. A role transformer then predicts future role states (bz_r,k_t,j) at *J* sub-horizon boundaries. This transformer uses inter-role self-attention and action cross-attention, with role-specific gates (sigma(g_r,k_j)) that allow for selective updates, preserving unaffected state components. Predicting intermediate transitions helps localize the onset of any violations.

### 6. Semantic Transition Supervision

At each sub-horizon segment, lightweight heads decode various task-critical semantic transitions from the predicted role states. These include:
1. **Gripper-target status:** Contact and stable grasp.
2. **Predicate establishment:** Satisfaction of the active target-goal spatial predicate.
3. **Predicate preservation:** Maintenance of previously established predicates.
4. **Conjunction satisfaction:** For multi-condition tasks.
5. **Phase transitions:** Identity and validity of progress through task phases (e.g., approach, grasp, transport, release).
6. **Fixture states:** Kinematic states of articulated objects (e.g., drawer displacement, microwave door angle).
Labels for this supervision are derived from simulator states, native predicates, collision information, articulated joint states, or expert annotations during training, not as inference-time inputs.

### 7. Action Discrimination via Hard Negatives

To ensure the model learns sensitivity to causal action order and task-relevant details, training incorporates hard negative examples. These negatives preserve observations and goals but involve manipulations like shuffling action segments, reversing approach-grasp order, substituting trajectories, or perturbing release timing. A margin loss (L_shuf) forces the selector to rank positive chunks higher than these hard negatives.

### 8. Candidate Generation, Selection, and Repair

- **Role-Conditioned Generation:** A Flow-Matching (FM) head, integrated into SR-WM, generates *K* candidate action chunks. Crucially, this FM head is conditioned directly on the semantically rich role state (Z_t) and language instruction, allowing the task structure to influence the generated action distribution.
- **Stage-Aware Semantic Selector:** An MLP-based selector (S_k_omega) evaluates and ranks these *K* candidates. Its input includes the current and predicted phases, contact/grasp confidence, predicate establishment/preservation, fixture validity, and predictive uncertainty. This allows the selector to adapt its criteria based on the current stage of the task.
- **Violation-Aware Suffix Resampling:** If the top-ranked candidate's score falls below a threshold (eta) or predicts a violation at an early segment (j*), the system attempts to repair the trajectory. It anchors the valid prefix (A_k*_1:j*-1) and resamples only the violating suffix (A_k*_j*:H). This is trained using masked action inpainting and implemented during inference by pinning the prefix during ODE steps.

### 9. Training Objective

The overall training objective combines eleven loss terms across three main groups:
- **Representation (L_rep):** Includes the original LeWM prediction loss, role binding loss, and state dynamics loss.
- **Semantic Grounding (L_sem):** Incorporates losses for contact/grasp, predicate establishment, predicate preservation, phase prediction, and fixture state prediction.
- **Policy (L_policy):** Comprises the Flow-Matching action loss, hard-negative ranking loss, and suffix inpainting loss.
Training uses dynamically sampled data from expert demonstrations, simulated rollout deviations, and repair examples, with explicit acceptance criteria for checkpoints based on semantic metrics and validation rollout success.

### 10. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation protocol spanned four LIBERO simulation suites, cross-suite transfer, perception diagnostics, and action-sensitivity analysis, focusing on closed-loop rollout success, pairwise temporal ranking accuracy, and system efficiency.

### 11. End-to-End Performance (Table 1)

- **Action Prior and Coverage:** Conditioning the Flow-Matching (FM) generator on SR-WM roles substantially improved performance compared to using monolithic LeWM latent features. The nominal success rate for Candidate 0 (the unselected best candidate) increased from 45.5% (LeWM latent + FM) to 62.8% (SR-WM role-conditioned FM). The Oracle@4 coverage (indicating if any of the top 4 candidates could succeed) also significantly rose from 68.0% to 89.5%. This indicates that the semantic role structure intrinsically improves both the quality and feasibility of generated action candidates.
- **Closed-Loop Selection and Repair:** The semantic selector converted this improved coverage into a top-1 success rate of 75.3%. Further enhancing this with violation-aware suffix repair boosted the success rate to 83.0%. The selector demonstrated a 78.2% pairwise ranking accuracy, reflecting its ability to differentiate between effective and ineffective actions.

### 12. Role and Mask-Dependence Diagnostics (Table 3)

- **Semantic Role Probing:** Probes confirmed that the learned role states effectively capture physical semantics, exhibiting high accuracy in target/goal binding, relation decoding (e.g., "inside," "grasped"), and phase boundary detection.
- **Segmentation Prior Dependence:** The SR-WM demonstrated robustness to segmentation mask availability. The patch-only SR-WM, operating without any inference-time segmentation masks, achieved a competitive 72.6% rollout success. Even under corrupted mask priors, performance marginally degraded to 70.8%. While oracle masks provided an upper bound of 78.4%, these results validate that SR-WM does not critically rely on external segmentation and can leverage its learned patch-based representations for task-critical geometry.

### 13. Action-Conditioned Dynamics and Sensitivity (Table 4)

The model's ability to capture causal temporal ordering and physical semantics was evaluated against hard negative action chunks.
- The full SR-WM model effectively distinguished positive demonstrations from hard negatives, yielding a shuffled-action gap of 0.157 (higher is better) and a 78.2% pairwise ranking accuracy.
- Ablating semantic heads significantly reduced ranking accuracy to 60.8%. Crucially, removing hard negative training examples collapsed the decision margin to 0.018, resulting in near-random accuracy (53.7%). Similarly, removing relation preservation or phase loss degraded accuracy to 69.4% and 66.8% respectively, confirming the necessity of granular semantic supervision for robust physical interaction validation.

### 14. Semantic Role Structure Ablation (Table 5)

A progressive ablation study demonstrated the incremental value of explicit semantic roles:
- Starting with five unstructured instance slots yielded 55.0% success.
- Adding global instruction context to object slots increased success to 62.0%.
- Explicitly binding target and goal roles boosted success to 68.0%.
- Incorporating relation roles further improved it to 73.0%.
- The full SR-WM architecture, including gripper and phase states, achieved the highest performance at 83.0%. This monotonic improvement supports the claim that explicit, factored semantic role architectures are essential for robust manipulation.

### 15. Cross-Task Generalization (Table 6)

SR-WM exhibited strong generalization capabilities:
- Trained exclusively on the Object suite, it achieved 58.4% zero-shot success on novel spatial configurations and 52.7% on new semantic goals.
- Adapting the Object-pretrained model significantly outperformed training from scratch on target suites (e.g., 74.6% vs. 66.3% on Goal suite, and 58.7% vs. 50.6% on LIBERO-10).
- Joint multi-suite pretraining established an even stronger foundation, enabling adapted models to reach 78.4% on held-out tasks. These results confirm that explicit role representations facilitate robust and data-efficient adaptation across tasks.

### 16. Efficiency Analysis (Table 7)

The complete SR-WM + FM system has a parameter count of 45.4M (compared to the 15M LeWM backbone alone). The paper provides measurements for peak training memory, FM generation latency (30.2 ms), and semantic scoring latency (7.3 ms) on matched hardware (single A100-40GB GPU in BF16 precision), indicating its operational characteristics.

### 17. Qualitative Error Profiling

Despite high overall success, remaining failures were categorized primarily into two areas:
1. **Severe Perceptual Occlusion:** For precise tasks like peg-in-hole, extreme gripper occlusion occasionally caused the soft entity encoder to lose accurate track of the target's geometric center, leading to premature predicate establishment.
2. **Granular Phase Misalignment:** In complex sequential tasks, the discrete nature of supervised phase transitions sometimes lagged continuous control shifts. This could lead to issues like premature release, where the receding-horizon controller lacked fine-grained feedback for correction.

### 18. Significance and Potential Impact

The Semantically Rich World Model (SR-WM) introduces a structured approach to world modeling that enhances robotic physical interaction planning. Its significance stems from several key aspects:

1. **Semantic Interface for Planning:** SR-WM transforms object-centric prediction into a semantic interface. By explicitly binding visual entities to functional roles (gripper, target, goal, relation, phase), it provides high-level, interpretable information crucial for planning-oriented decision-making, moving beyond generic latent or instance-level representations. This direct semantic link is fundamental for robots to understand *what* an action achieves in the context of a task, not just *how* the environment changes visually.

2. **Improved Manipulation Performance:** The model achieves high closed-loop rollout success rates (83.0%) across diverse and challenging manipulation tasks within the LIBERO simulation suites. This performance gain is attributed to the integration of semantic information directly into action generation, selection, and repair mechanisms, leading to more task-consistent and robust behaviors compared to prior world models.

3. **Enhanced Action Generation and Selection:** The role-conditioned Flow-Matching generator produces higher-quality initial action candidates, as evidenced by improved Candidate 0 success and Oracle@K coverage. The stage-aware semantic selector, informed by granular predictions of contact, grasp, predicate establishment, preservation, and phase changes, effectively converts this candidate diversity into reliable top-1 action choices. This demonstrates that semantic grounding can profoundly shape both the generation of feasible actions and the intelligence of the decision-making process.

4. **Robustness and Generalization:** SR-WM exhibits robustness to varying perceptual conditions, performing effectively even without inference-time segmentation masks. Furthermore, its explicit role representations facilitate strong zero-shot cross-task generalization and significantly accelerate data-efficient adaptation to novel tasks. This suggests that the learned semantic roles encapsulate reusable physical semantics, which can lead to more versatile and adaptable robotic systems.

5. **Systematic and Transparent Evaluation:** The research employs a comprehensive evaluation protocol that deliberately disentangles the contributions of representation quality, action sensitivity, candidate coverage, selector performance, and repair strategies. This systematic approach provides clear insights into the strengths of the SR-WM architecture and contributes to more rigorous scientific reporting in robot learning.

The potential impact of SR-WM lies in advancing the capabilities of autonomous robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments. By providing world models that are not only predictive but also semantically *plannable*, this work could enable robots to:
- Understand and execute multi-stage tasks with higher reliability.
- Adapt more quickly to new tasks with limited demonstrations.
- Engage in more intuitive human-robot collaboration through interpretable semantic states.
- Form a foundation for more general-purpose robot intelligence by grounding high-level task descriptions in physical interaction dynamics.

**Limitations and Scope (as stated in the paper):**
- **Ontology Scope:** The five-role ontology (gripper, target, goal, relation, phase) is suitable for many single-arm, goal-directed manipulation tasks but may be insufficient for more complex scenarios such as bimanual interaction, manipulation of deformable objects, tool use, or tasks involving multiple simultaneous targets.
- **Supervision Dependency:** While predicate and phase supervision are readily available and inexpensive in simulation, acquiring such detailed labels or estimations for real-world physical interactions would require careful labeling efforts or advanced perception systems.
- **Perceptual Failure:** While optional segmentation reduces a hard dependency on a particular segmenter, it does not eliminate all forms of perceptual failure (e.g., due to extreme occlusion or challenging lighting conditions). The claim of weak mask dependence is contingent on successful performance in no-mask and corrupted-mask tests.
- **Safety Considerations:** Prefix-preserving repair, while effective for task completion, introduces additional inference calls and is not directly suitable for safety-critical execution without incorporating explicit collision checks and low-level safeguards.
- **Model Complexity:** The full SR-WM system, including the FM action head and added role modules, comprises 45.4M parameters. Therefore, the paper does not make a general "small model" or "real-time" claim without specific supporting evidence from measured totals and matched-hardware results.
- **Oracle@K Interpretation:** Oracle@K serves strictly as an analytical upper bound. A high Oracle@K score coupled with weak top-1 selection indicates an unresolved problem with the selector rather than a deployable solution.
