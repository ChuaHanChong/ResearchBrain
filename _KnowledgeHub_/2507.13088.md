# ZipMPC: Compressed Context-Dependent MPC Cost via Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and Örebro University developed ZipMPC, a framework that enables computationally efficient short-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) to achieve the performance of a high-performance long-horizon MPC. It accomplishes this by learning a context-dependent cost function, leveraging imitation learning and differentiable MPC to compress and integrate long-horizon contextual information. The method demonstrated superior imitation, reduced lap times (e.g., 8.394 ± 0.012 sec vs 9.103 ± 0.043 sec for a kinematic model) with 68.5% to 87.9% less optimization time, and robust generalization in autonomous racing simulations and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Model Predictive Control (MPC) faces a trade-off between computational cost (long prediction horizons are expensive) and control performance (short horizons lead to an 'information gap').
- Designing effective cost functions for short-horizon MPCs is challenging due to limited foresight, often resulting in suboptimal decisions or task failures.
- Existing solutions like explicit MPC struggle with generalization and constraint satisfaction, while automatic tuning methods do not reduce the inherent computational demand of long-horizon MPC.

## Method

- **ZipMPC** uses a neural network to learn a context-dependent cost function for a short-horizon MPC (MPC_N_S) that imitates the optimal behavior of a computationally expensive long-horizon MPC (MPC_N_L).
- It compresses and integrates long-horizon contextual information (e.g., upcoming track curvature in autonomous racing) into the short-horizon MPC's cost function.
- ==Differentiable MPC== is employed to enable end-to-end gradient-based optimization of the neural network weights, minimizing an ==imitation loss== by backpropagating through the MPC solver.

## Results

- **ZipMPC** achieved significantly lower imitation error (RMSE of **0.068 ± 0.009** vs **0.203 ± 0.004** for baseline MPC_N_S) and improved lap times (e.g., **8.394 ± 0.012 sec** vs **9.103 ± 0.043 sec**) compared to baselines in autonomous racing simulations.
- It maintained computational costs comparable to the short-horizon MPC, achieving a **68.5% to 87.9%** reduction in optimization time per step compared to the long-horizon MPC, while yielding competitive performance.
- The method demonstrated robust generalization to unseen tracks in simulation and successful deployment on real racing hardware, where it completed laps in challenging scenarios where standard short-horizon MPC often failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning to embed compressed long-horizon contextual information into a short-horizon MPC's cost function effectively bridges the 'information gap', allowing for more foresightful control decisions without increasing the prediction horizon.
> - Differentiable MPC provides a powerful mechanism for training neural networks to modulate MPC cost parameters, enabling a computationally lightweight controller to mimic the complex behavior of a more powerful reference.
> - The learned cost parameters demonstrate an intuitive correlation with contextual inputs (e.g., lateral deviation cost adjusts with track curvature), indicating the neural network successfully extracts and utilizes environmental cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a widely adopted control strategy for safety-critical applications, including robot locomotion, manipulation, autonomous racing, and biomedical robotics, due to its ability to handle system dynamics and constraints through optimization. However, a significant challenge in MPC deployment, particularly in real-time systems, is its computational burden. This often necessitates the use of short prediction horizons, which are computationally tractable but introduce an "information gap." This gap limits the controller's foresight, making it difficult to design cost functions that effectively reflect long-term objectives and potentially leading to suboptimal performance or even feasibility issues.

Existing approaches attempt to address these challenges in several ways:
- **Approximate explicit MPC (eMPC):** These methods learn closed-form policies, often using neural networks, to approximate MPC solutions, aiming for faster real-time inference. While computationally efficient, they frequently struggle with generalization to unseen environments and may not guarantee constraint satisfaction.
- **Learning the cost-to-go:** This involves learning an additional cost term to compensate for the information missing due to a reduced prediction horizon, effectively mimicking the terminal cost or constraint of a longer-horizon MPC.
- **Automatic cost parameter tuning:** Techniques such as Inverse Optimal Control (IOC), Bayesian Optimization (BO), or Reinforcement Learning (RL) are used to automatically tune MPC cost parameters to achieve desired performance or imitate expert demonstrations. While effective for performance improvement, these methods generally do not alleviate the computational demand of long-horizon MPCs, and when applied to short-horizon MPCs, they may not adequately bridge the information gap without external contextual information.

The current work positions itself at the intersection of these fields, leveraging recent advancements in differentiable MPC. Differentiable MPC integrates optimization solvers as differentiable layers within deep learning frameworks, allowing for gradient-based learning through constrained optimization problems. This capability enables end-to-end learning where the output of an MPC (e.g., control actions) can be optimized with respect to external objectives (e.g., an imitation loss) by backpropagating gradients through the MPC solver itself. This approach has been explored for applications like imitating human behavior and for developing deep learning alternatives to explicit MPC that respect hard constraints. ZipMPC builds upon this foundation to specifically address the trade-off between MPC horizon length, computational cost, and control performance by integrating long-horizon contextual information into a short-horizon MPC's cost function via imitation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for ZipMPC stems from two primary challenges inherent in deploying Model Predictive Control (MPC) in real-world, real-time systems:

1. **Computational Tractability vs. Performance with Prediction Horizon:** MPC's computational complexity generally increases with the length of its prediction horizon. For real-time applications like autonomous robotics, this often forces the adoption of significantly short prediction horizons (MPC_NS) to ensure computational feasibility. However, a short horizon limits the controller's look-ahead capability, creating an "information gap" where crucial future environmental or task-specific details are not considered in the optimization.
2. **Designing Effective Cost Functions for Short Horizons:** When an MPC operates with a long prediction horizon (MPC_NL), it can account for a broader range of future events, simplifying the design of its cost function. An intuitively designed cost function often yields good performance because the controller has ample foresight. In contrast, for short-horizon MPCs, the limited foresight makes designing a cost function that accurately reflects long-term objectives and ensures optimal behavior highly challenging. Suboptimal cost function design can lead to reduced performance, conservative control, or even failure to complete tasks, as the controller may make locally optimal but globally suboptimal decisions.

Recognizing these limitations, the authors aim to combine the advantages of both short and long horizons. The core objective of ZipMPC is to enable a computationally efficient short-horizon MPC to achieve the performance characteristics of a high-performance, but computationally expensive, long-horizon MPC.

Specifically, the key objectives are to:
- **Bridge the Information Gap:** Develop a mechanism to efficiently compress and incorporate long-horizon contextual information (e.g., upcoming track curvature) into the cost function of a short-horizon MPC.
- **Imitate Long-Horizon Behavior:** Learn a context-dependent cost function for a short-horizon MPC that enables it to imitate the optimal control behavior of a well-tuned long-horizon MPC.
- **Maintain Computational Efficiency:** Achieve the desired performance improvements without significantly increasing the computational cost beyond that of a standard short-horizon MPC.
- **Ensure Constraint Satisfaction:** Preserve the inherent constraint satisfaction properties of MPC, which is often a limitation of purely data-driven control policies.
- **Facilitate Generalization:** Develop a method that can generalize the learned control behavior to environments or scenarios not explicitly encountered during the training process.
- **Improve Long-Term Objective Optimization:** Demonstrate that the proposed method optimizes the long-term objective more effectively than existing short-horizon methods.

By achieving these objectives, ZipMPC seeks to overcome the fundamental trade-off between computational tractability and control performance in MPC, particularly for dynamic and safety-critical real-time applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ZipMPC proposes a learning framework that leverages differentiable MPC and neural networks to enable a short-horizon MPC to imitate the behavior of a long-horizon MPC by learning a compressed, context-dependent cost function.

#### 3.1. MPC Preliminaries

The foundation of ZipMPC is a general MPC formulation for discrete-time dynamics:
`x(k + 1) = f(x(k), u(k), ζ(k))`
At each time step `k`, the MPC solves an optimization problem over a prediction horizon `N` to minimize a sum of stage costs `ℓ(x_i, u_i, c_i)` subject to system dynamics and state/input constraints (`X`, `U`). The stage costs are parameterized by `c_i`, and `ζ` represents an environmental context variable. The problem returns optimal state and control trajectories `X*`, `U*`, but only the first optimal control input `u*₀` is applied (receding horizon control). For simplicity and due to recent theoretical advancements, the formulation in this work does not include terminal ingredients, though their inclusion would be straightforward.

#### 3.2. Problem Formulation

The core idea is to learn parameters for a short-horizon MPC (`MPC_N_S`) that minimize an imitation loss (`L`) with respect to the optimal trajectories generated by a long-horizon MPC (`MPC_N_L`).
Let `N_L` be the long (expensive) prediction horizon and `N_S < N_L` be the short (computationally efficient) horizon. The long-horizon MPC uses manually tuned cost parameters `C_M_N_L`. The short-horizon MPC uses learned cost parameters `C_θ_N_S`, where `θ` represents the learnable parameters of a neural network (NN).
The objective is to find `θ*` that minimizes:
`θ* = arg min_θ E_k [L([X_θ_k,N_S, U_θ_k,N_S], [X*_k,N_L, U*_k,N_L])]`
Here, `X_θ_k,N_S` and `U_θ_k,N_S` are the trajectories from the short-horizon MPC with learned costs, and `X*_k,N_L` and `U*_k,N_L` are the reference trajectories from the long-horizon MPC. The expectation `E_k` is over initial states `x(k)` sampled from a feasible region.

#### 3.3. Learning Framework

To bridge the information gap, the short-horizon MPC's cost parameters `C_θ_N_S` are parameterized by a nonlinear function `h_θ` (a neural network) that takes the current state `x(k)` and a sequence of long-horizon contextual information `Z_k,N_L` as input. `Z_k,N_L` ideally contains as much information as provided to the `MPC_N_L`.
The imitation loss `L` is calculated only over the first `N_S` steps of both the learned and reference trajectories, ensuring that the short-horizon MPC anticipates behavior relevant to its horizon length by incorporating the `Z_k,N_L` context.

A critical aspect of the methodology is the use of **differentiable MPC** to enable gradient-based optimization of the NN weights `θ`. The gradients of the imitation loss with respect to `θ` are propagated through the MPC optimization procedure, which is generally a non-differentiable operation. ZipMPC specifically employs the differentiable method proposed by Amos et al., which uses an iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) as an approximation to MPC and differentiates through its fixed point using KKT conditions. This allows for handling general MPC cost formulations and non-convexities.

#### 3.4. Detailed Algorithm (Algorithm 1)

The training process (Algorithm 1) involves:
1. **Initialization:** Initialize NN weights `θ` and expand manually designed cost parameters `c_M` into `C_M_N_L` and `C_M_N_S` for long and short horizons, respectively.
2. **Iterative Training:** For each training iteration:
 - Sample an initial feasible state `x(k)`.
 - The NN `h_θ` takes `x(k)` and the long-horizon context `Z_k,N_L` (e.g., track curvature sequence) to predict correction values `∆C_θ_N_S`.
 - These corrections are added to `C_M_N_S` (serving as a warm start) to obtain the learned cost parameters `C_θ_N_S`.
 - The `MPC_N_S` with `C_θ_N_S` computes its trajectory `[X_θ_k,N_S, U_θ_k,N_S]`.
 - The `MPC_N_L` with `C_M_N_L` computes its reference trajectory `[X*_k,N_L, U*_k,N_L]`.
 - An imitation loss `L` (Mean Squared Error, MSE) is computed between the first `N_D` steps (`N_D <= N_S`) of the learned and reference trajectories. The paper notes manual weighting of state and control variables in the MSE.
 - The NN weights `θ` are updated using an Adam optimizer based on the computed gradients.
The algorithm is executed in mini-batches.

#### 3.5. Application: Autonomous Racing

Autonomous racing is chosen as a demonstration platform due to its real-time computational demands and the challenge of designing effective short-horizon MPC cost functions.
- **System Dynamics:** Two car models are used: a simpler kinematic bicycle model and a more complex Pacejka model.
- **Control Approach:** A Model Predictive Contouring Controller (MPCC) is employed, operating in the Frenet frame (which describes position relative to a reference path using progress `σ`, lateral deviation `d`, and orientation `ϕ`). The MPCC uses a quadratic stage cost `ℓ` with parameters `q_i`, `p_i` and polytopic constraints for track boundaries and state/input limits.
- **Context Variable:** The long-horizon contextual information `Z_k,N_L` is defined as a sequence of upcoming track curvatures.
- **NN Input:** The NN receives `v`, `d`, `ϕ` from `x(k)` and the curvature sequence `Z_k,N_L`. The NN architecture features a convolutional feature extractor for `Z_k,N_L` followed by fully connected layers, outputting `∆Q_θ_N_S` and `∆P_θ_N_S`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ZipMPC was evaluated through extensive simulations and real-world experiments on autonomous racing platforms, comparing it against several baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Imitation Error:** ZipMPC consistently demonstrated superior imitation capabilities compared to `MPC_N_S`, Bayesian Optimization (BO), and explicit MPC (eMPC) baselines. Using the kinematic model, ZipMPC achieved the lowest Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) between its trajectories and those of the `MPC_N_L` reference across various `N_S` and `N_L` combinations. For example, with `N_S=5, N_L=25`, ZipMPC achieved an RMSE of 0.068 ± 0.009, significantly lower than `MPC_N_S` (0.203 ± 0.004), BO (0.150 ± 0.006), and eMPC (0.086 ± 0.009).

- **Lap Time:**
 - **Kinematic Model:** ZipMPC considerably improved lap times over `MPC_N_S`. Its lap times were consistently close to the `MPC_N_L` reference, outperforming BO and eMPC. Notably, the eMPC baseline failed to complete laps in all tested kinematic scenarios. For `N_S=5, N_L=25`, `MPC_N_L` achieved 8.286 ± 0.012 sec, while ZipMPC achieved 8.394 ± 0.012 sec, a substantial improvement over `MPC_N_S` (9.103 ± 0.043 sec) and BO (8.955 ± 0.028 sec).
 - **Pacejka Model:** Similar improvements were observed with the more complex Pacejka model. In scenarios where `MPC_N_S` failed to complete a lap, ZipMPC succeeded, albeit with slightly longer lap times than `MPC_N_L`. For `N_S=6, N_L=45`, `MPC_N_L` achieved 9.89 ± 0.10 sec, `MPC_N_S` failed, but ZipMPC completed laps in 12.82 ± 0.33 sec.
 - **Execution Time Reduction:** ZipMPC maintained computational costs comparable to `MPC_N_S`. For the Pacejka model, ZipMPC achieved a significant reduction in optimization time per MPC step compared to `MPC_N_L`, ranging from 68.5% to 87.9%. This indicates that it effectively combines the performance of a long-horizon controller with the speed of a short-horizon one.

- **Track Generalization:** ZipMPC demonstrated robust generalization capabilities to tracks unseen during training. When trained on a specific track and tested on two new tracks (one longer, one shorter, with varying curvatures), ZipMPC consistently reduced lap times compared to `MPC_N_S` and achieved lap times very close to `MPC_N_L` for the kinematic model. Even with the Pacejka model, ZipMPC significantly improved performance on unseen tracks over `MPC_N_S`, although the gap to `MPC_N_L` was larger.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study in Simulation

- **Context Relevance:** An ablation study investigating the importance of the long-horizon context `Z_k,N_L` revealed that incorporating context provides substantial performance improvements, especially for shorter `N_S` (smaller `N_S/N_L` ratios). As `N_S` approached `N_L`, the performance difference between context-aware and context-free ZipMPC diminished, which aligns with the decreasing need for external information when the prediction horizon is already long.

- **Learned Parameters:** Analysis of the learned cost parameter associated with lateral deviation (`p_d`) showed a strong and intuitive correlation with upcoming track curvature. Positive `p_d` values were learned for right-hand curves, and negative `p_d` values for left-hand curves, indicating that the NN successfully learned to modulate the cost function based on contextual track geometry.

#### 4.3. Real-World Results

ZipMPC was validated on hardware using a miniature racing platform and the Pacejka car model. Models trained solely in simulation were deployed without retraining. The real-world experiments confirmed the simulation findings, illustrating that ZipMPC effectively incorporates contextual information to improve racing performance over `MPC_N_S`. In specific challenging scenarios, `MPC_N_S` failed to complete laps, while ZipMPC successfully did so, demonstrating its practical utility and robustness on physical hardware. Accompanying videos further supported these observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ZipMPC framework presents a significant contribution to the field of Model Predictive Control, particularly for applications requiring real-time, high-performance control on resource-constrained systems. Its core significance lies in addressing the long-standing trade-off between MPC's computational complexity and its control performance.

The potential impacts include:

- **Enhanced Performance in Resource-Constrained Systems:** ZipMPC enables the deployment of short-horizon MPCs that achieve control performance comparable to much more computationally expensive long-horizon MPCs. This makes advanced, optimization-based control accessible to platforms such such as robots, drones, and autonomous vehicles where computational resources are limited.
- **Improved Control Quality and Reliability:** By learning to incorporate long-horizon contextual information into the short-horizon cost function, ZipMPC helps overcome the "information gap" that typically plagues short-horizon MPCs. This leads to more intelligent, foresightful control actions, resulting in better optimization of long-term objectives, reduced lap times in racing, and more robust behavior in dynamic environments.
- **Preservation of MPC Guarantees:** Unlike purely black-box learning control policies (e.g., behavioral cloning with NNs), ZipMPC retains the underlying MPC optimization. This is crucial for maintaining desirable properties such as constraint satisfaction and recursive feasibility, which are critical for safety-critical applications.
- **Robust Generalization:** The method demonstrates strong generalization capabilities to environments and scenarios unseen during training. This is a key advantage for practical applications, as it reduces the need for extensive retraining and enables deployment in varied operating conditions.
- **Fusion of Strengths from Diverse Fields:** ZipMPC effectively combines the computational speed benefits typically associated with explicit MPC or neural network inference with the generalization and constraint-handling strengths of autonomous cost parameter tuning methods. This hybrid approach offers a robust and efficient solution that capitalizes on the advancements in both differentiable optimization and imitation learning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of ZipMPC in autonomous racing, both in simulation and on hardware, highlights its potential for broader application across various domains where dynamic systems require sophisticated, real-time control. It also opens avenues for further research into quantifying theoretical guarantees for such learned MPC approximations, exploring more complex context features, and addressing challenges like sensitivity to training data.

In summary, ZipMPC provides a practical and effective method for deploying advanced MPC on real-time systems by learning to compress and utilize essential contextual information, thereby achieving high performance without incurring prohibitive computational costs. This advancement has the potential to accelerate the adoption of sophisticated model predictive control in a wider range of robotic and autonomous applications.
