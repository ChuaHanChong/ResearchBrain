# Large VLM-based Vision-Language-Action Models for Robotic Manipulation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> The survey presents the first systematic, taxonomy-oriented review of large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, consolidating diverse research and proposing a coherent framework. It identifies key architectural paradigms, integration strategies, and distinctive characteristics while outlining critical future research directions.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation systems struggle with adaptability, generalization to novel tasks/objects, and interpreting natural language instructions in unstructured environments.
- The rapidly expanding field of VLM-based VLA models suffers from inconsistent terminology, fragmented research, and a lack of integrated reviews that comprehensively synthesize advancements at this interdisciplinary intersection.
- Existing research often focuses on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or robotic manipulation in isolation, hindering a holistic understanding of their combined potential and challenges.

## Method

- The authors propose a precise, two-part definition for large VLM-based VLA models and introduce a novel taxonomy, categorizing them into ==monolithic== and ==hierarchical== architectural paradigms.
- The survey conducts an in-depth examination of VLA models' integration with advanced domains like ==Reinforcement Learning==, ==training-free optimization==, ==learning from human videos==, and ==world models==.
- It synthesizes the distinctive architectural traits, operational strengths, and a comprehensive landscape of datasets and benchmarks, concluding with critical open challenges and future research avenues.

## Results

- Established a clear definition and the first systematic, taxonomy-oriented review for large VLM-based VLA models, providing a unified framework for understanding the field.
- Categorized VLA models into two principal architectural paradigms: Monolithic Models (single-system and dual-system) and Hierarchical Models (planner-only and planner+policy), detailing their respective advancements and characteristics.
- Provided a comprehensive overview of how VLA models integrate with advanced domains, synthesized their distinctive characteristics (multimodal fusion, instruction following, generalization), and reviewed crucial datasets and benchmarks, alongside identifying key future research directions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed taxonomy effectively structures the diverse landscape of VLA models, delineating between monolithic (single/dual-system) and hierarchical (planner-only/planner+policy) architectures based on their integration of perception, reasoning, and action generation.
> - VLA models significantly enhance robotic capabilities through sophisticated multimodal fusion, advanced instruction following, and multi-dimensional generalization, moving beyond rigid, task-specific automation.
> - Integration with advanced AI techniques such as Reinforcement Learning, world models, and leveraging human video data are crucial for improving VLA models' efficiency, generalization, and robust skill transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a critical subfield of robotics and embodied AI, demanding not only precise motor control but also sophisticated understanding of visual and semantic cues in complex, dynamic environments. Historically, traditional robotic manipulation approaches have relied on predefined task specifications and rigid control policies. While effective in structured settings, these methods inherently struggle with scalability and generalization when faced with novel objects, ambiguous natural language instructions, or unstructured, unseen environments.

In parallel, the field of artificial intelligence has witnessed a paradigm shift with the advent of Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Pretrained on vast web-scale image-text datasets, VLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in bridging the semantic gap between visual perception and natural language understanding, enabling open-world generalization, hierarchical task planning, knowledge-augmented reasoning, and rich multimodal fusion. The natural progression of this advancement is the integration of these powerful VLMs into robotic systems, leading to the emergence of Large VLM-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This new class of models holds immense potential to overcome the limitations of traditional robotics by empowering robots to interpret high-level human instructions, generalize to novel scenarios, reason about complex relationships, and execute sophisticated manipulation tasks.

This survey meticulously positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing a critical gap identified by the authors. Existing reviews often either focus broadly on VLA architectures or specific VLM advancements, or on robotic manipulation in isolation. The authors argue that there is a lack of a "systematic and principled survey that elucidates the foundations of large VLM-based VLA models, organizes the space of relevant methods, and outlines future directions for this integrated paradigm." By providing "the first systematic, taxonomy-oriented review of large VLM-based VLA models for robotic manipulation," this work serves as a foundational reference. It consolidates fragmented research, resolves inconsistencies in terminology and modeling assumptions across disciplines (robotics, computer vision, natural language processing), and provides a coherent framework for understanding the unique challenges and advancements at this crucial intersection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the inherent limitations of traditional robotic manipulation approaches and the transformative potential of Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in addressing these limitations. Traditional robots struggle with adaptability and generalization in dynamic, unstructured real-world settings, particularly when interpreting natural language instructions for novel tasks or objects. The emergence of VLMs, with their ability to interpret complex visual scenes, understand natural language, and perform sophisticated reasoning, presents an unprecedented opportunity to imbue robots with higher levels of intelligence and autonomy.

However, the rapid growth and interdisciplinary nature of this field have led to several challenges:
- **Inconsistent Terminology:** There is a lack of standardized definitions and modeling assumptions, causing confusion and hindering systematic progress.
- **Fragmented Research Landscape:** Contributions are dispersed across various fields, making it difficult to gain a holistic understanding of the advancements.
- **Lack of Integrated Review:** Existing surveys tend to focus either on VLMs or robotic manipulation separately, without comprehensively synthesizing the unique challenges and innovations arising from their intersection.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this survey are clearly articulated:
1. **Systematic Definition:** To provide a clear and coherent definition of "large VLM-based VLA models" to establish a common understanding for the field.
2. **Taxonomic Organization:** To propose the first systematic, taxonomy-oriented review, delineating two principal architectural paradigms—monolithic and hierarchical models—and their sub-categories. This aims to bring structural clarity to the diverse methodologies.
3. **In-depth Examination:** To conduct a detailed examination of VLA models, covering their integration with advanced domains such as reinforcement learning, training-free optimization, learning from human videos, and world model integration.
4. **Synthesis of Characteristics:** To consolidate the distinctive architectural traits, operational strengths, and the datasets and benchmarks that underpin the development of VLA models.
5. **Identification of Future Directions:** To identify critical open challenges and promising future research avenues, thereby serving as a roadmap for future breakthroughs in the field.
By achieving these objectives, the survey aims to resolve inconsistencies, mitigate research fragmentation, and fill a critical gap in the literature, fostering a deeper understanding and accelerating progress in embodied AI for robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic, taxonomy-oriented review methodology to analyze the rapidly evolving landscape of large VLM-based VLA models for robotic manipulation. Their approach is structured around defining the core subject, categorizing existing solutions, examining cross-cutting themes, and identifying future research trajectories.

**A. Core Definition:** The survey begins by proposing a precise, two-part definition for large VLM-based VLA models:
1. The model leverages a large VLM to understand visual observations and natural language instructions.
2. It performs reasoning processes that directly or indirectly serve robotic action generation.

**B. Principal Architectural Taxonomy:** Based on this definition, the authors introduce a novel and comprehensive taxonomy, dividing VLA models into two principal architectural paradigms:
- **Monolithic Models:** These integrate environmental comprehension (visual perception, linguistic understanding, robot state awareness) and action generation within a unified architecture.
 - **Single-system Models:** Combine all functionalities into a single processing unit, typically utilizing autoregressive or parallel decoding. The survey further categorizes advancements in this area into classical autoregressive decoding, model performance enhancement (e.g., enhanced perception modalities like 3D/4D, tactile, auditory; enhanced reasoning via chain-of-thought; enhanced generalization), and inference efficiency optimization (e.g., architectural, parameter, and decoding accelerations).
 - **Dual-system Models:** Employ a VLM backbone for scene interpretation (System 2) and a separate action expert for action determination (System 1), with information exchange via latent representations. These are sub-categorized into cascade-based methods (where System 2 forwards outputs to System 1 in a serial fashion) and parallel-based methods (where both systems operate concurrently and interact through shared attention or cross-attention mechanisms).
- **Hierarchical Models:** These explicitly decouple planning from policy execution through interpretable intermediate representations.
 - **Planner-Only Models:** Focus on designing the high-level planner component, which generates intermediate representations (subtasks, keypoints, programs) that can be processed by existing off-the-shelf policies.
 - **Planner+Policy Models:** Integrate both a high-level planner and a low-level policy, with the planner producing interpretable intermediate representations (subtasks, keypoints, programs) that guide the execution of the dedicated low-level policy.

**C. Cross-Cutting Examinations:** Beyond architectural classifications, the survey provides an in-depth analysis of:
- **Advanced Domains:** It explores the integration of VLA models with Reinforcement Learning (RL-based optimization), Training-Free methods (for efficiency), Learning from Human Videos (for robust skill transfer), and World Model-based approaches (for predictive reasoning).
- **Distinctive Characteristics:** It synthesizes the unique attributes of VLA models, including their mechanisms for Multimodal Fusion (shared embedding space, token-level integration, comprehensive modal compatibility), Instruction Following (semantic grounding, task decomposition, explicit chain-of-thought reasoning), and Multi-Dimensional Generalization (cross-task, cross-domain data, cross-embodiment, and sim-to-real transfer).
- **Datasets and Benchmarks:** A comprehensive overview of the empirical foundations is presented, categorizing resources into Real-world Robot Datasets, Simulation Datasets and Benchmarks, Human Behavior Datasets, and Embodied Datasets and Benchmarks.

**D. Future Directions:** The survey concludes by identifying critical open challenges and promising future research directions, covering aspects like improved datasets, memory mechanisms, 3D/4D perception, mobile manipulation, multi-agent cooperation, lifelong learning, and model efficiency.

The authors also commit to maintaining a regularly updated project page, underscoring their dedication to providing a dynamic and current resource for the research community. This methodical approach ensures a structured, thorough, and forward-looking analysis of the rapidly evolving VLA field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

This survey consolidates a wide array of research to present a structured understanding of Large VLM-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Its main "findings" are the comprehensive synthesis and organization of the field, rather than experimental results.

**A. Unified Taxonomy and Definitions:** The survey successfully establishes a clear definition for large VLM-based VLA models and introduces a novel, systematic taxonomy. This is a crucial contribution for a nascent and fragmented field.
- **Monolithic Models:** Defined as integrated systems that jointly optimize perception, reasoning, and control. This category is further broken down into:
 - **Single-system models:** Exemplified by RT-2 and OpenVLA, which leverage autoregressive decoding and have seen advancements in multimodal perception (3D, 4D, tactile, auditory), reasoning capabilities (chain-of-thought), generalization (universal action spaces, robust action generation), and inference efficiency (architectural optimization, parameter optimization, parallel decoding).
 - **Dual-system models:** Characterized by a VLM backbone (System 2) for high-level reasoning and an action expert (System 1) for low-level execution. These are realized through cascade-based (e.g., CogACT, GR00T N1, Fast-in-Slow) or parallel-based architectures (e.g., π0, SmolVLA), emphasizing improved real-time performance and task-specific optimization.
- **Hierarchical Models:** Defined by the explicit decoupling of high-level planning from low-level policy execution via interpretable intermediate representations. This category includes:
 - **Planner-Only models:** Where a VLM generates subtasks, keypoints, or programs, relying on external policies for execution (e.g., PaLM-E for subtasks, MoManipVLA for keypoints, Chain-of-Modality for programs).
 - **Planner+Policy models:** Integrate both a VLM-based planner and a low-level policy, where the planner's intermediate outputs guide the policy's actions (e.g., HiRobot for subtasks, HAMSTER for keypoints, VoxPoser for programs).

**B. Integration with Advanced Domains:** The survey identifies and details four key areas where VLA models are being integrated with other advanced AI techniques:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL is crucial for enhancing generalization and task completion, with research focusing on addressing reward sparsity (learned dense rewards, VLM-generated feedback) and sample inefficiency (hybrid offline-online training, RL as a data engine).
- **Training-Free Methods:** These methods aim to improve VLA efficiency without costly retraining, through techniques like dynamic inference (skipping layers, early exits), token-aware compression, caching, and optimized control-time scheduling.
- **Learning from Human Videos:** A significant trend is leveraging human video data to guide robot policy learning, bridging the embodiment gap and transferring task-relevant knowledge to robot agents for robust and versatile manipulation.
- **World Model-based VLA:** Integration of predictive world models enables explicit environment dynamics modeling, supporting predictive reasoning, simulating future states, and enhancing long-horizon planning.

**C. Distinctive Characteristics of VLA Models:** The survey synthesizes core attributes distinguishing VLA models:
- **Multimodal Fusion:** Achieved through shared embedding spaces, token-level integration of diverse modalities (vision, language, proprioception, actions), and comprehensive modal compatibility without requiring architectural redesigns.
- **Instruction Following:** Characterized by semantic grounding of natural language, hierarchical task decomposition into sub-goals, and explicit chain-of-thought reasoning for transparent and reliable execution of complex tasks.
- **Multi-Dimensional Generalization:** VLA models exhibit strong zero-shot and few-shot generalization capabilities across tasks, diverse data domains (web text, simulation, cross-robot embodiments), and embodiments (sim-to-real transfer), surpassing traditional robotic systems.

**D. Landscape of Datasets and Benchmarks:** The survey provides an exhaustive overview of the crucial datasets and benchmarks, categorizing them into:
- **Real-world Robot Datasets:** Emphasizing large-scale, language-annotated demonstrations like RT-1, RT-2, and the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, which are vital for real-world applicability and cross-embodiment generalization.
- **Simulation Datasets and Benchmarks:** Including BEHAVIOR, ALFRED, RLBench, and CALVIN, which offer scalable, safe, and reproducible data for complex instruction following and multi-stage planning, while addressing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Human Behavior Datasets:** Comprising egocentric video corpora (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D) and manipulation-focused datasets (EgoDex), which provide rich semantic and contextual demonstrations for pretraining.
- **Embodied Datasets and Benchmarks:** Such as EmbodiedQA and LoTa-Bench, which evaluate agents' planning and reasoning abilities in interactive 3D environments.

Overall, the survey's main "findings" are its ability to systematically map out the current state of large VLM-based VLA models, identify their strengths, highlight the diverse approaches being taken, and establish a common ground for future exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a significant contribution to the fields of robotics, embodied AI, and multimodal machine learning, with a substantial potential impact on future research and development.

**A. Unifying a Fragmented Field:** Perhaps the most immediate and profound impact of this survey is its systematic attempt to bring coherence to a rapidly evolving and interdisciplinary domain. By clearly defining "large VLM-based VLA models" and proposing a comprehensive, taxonomy-oriented review, the authors provide a much-needed framework for understanding, comparing, and categorizing diverse research efforts. This unification helps resolve inconsistent terminology, mitigates research fragmentation across robotics, computer vision, and natural language processing, and facilitates cross-disciplinary communication. For new researchers entering the field, it serves as an invaluable conceptual roadmap, reducing the learning curve and enabling more targeted contributions.

**B. Accelerating Research and Development:** By consolidating recent advances, detailing methodologies, and synthesizing distinctive characteristics, the survey provides a solid foundation for future work. Researchers can leverage this organized knowledge to identify promising avenues, avoid redundant efforts, and build upon established paradigms more effectively. The detailed analysis of architectural choices, learning strategies, and advanced integration methods (e.g., RL, world models) offers insights into best practices and areas ripe for innovation, thereby accelerating the development of more capable and generalist robotic manipulation systems.

**C. Guiding Dataset and Benchmark Creation:** The extensive review of existing datasets and benchmarks, coupled with an identification of their limitations (e.g., reality gap in simulations, long-tail distribution in real-world data), directly informs future data collection and evaluation efforts. The call for richer metrics, longer-horizon tasks, and more diverse real-world data will guide the creation of more effective benchmarks that can truly assess the practical capabilities and generalization prowess of VLA models. This is crucial for driving empirical progress and ensuring that models are tested against real-world complexities.

**D. Advancing Embodied AI:** The survey underscores the transformative role of large VLMs in embodied AI. By demonstrating how visual, linguistic, and action modalities can be tightly integrated, it paves the way for robots that are not just reactive machines but intelligent agents capable of sophisticated reasoning, flexible instruction following, and broad generalization. This shift moves robotics beyond rigid, task-specific automation toward adaptable, general-purpose systems that can operate in complex, open-ended environments.

**E. Identifying Critical Future Directions:** The explicit articulation of open challenges—such as memory mechanisms for long-term planning, the need for 3D and 4D perception, capabilities for mobile manipulation and multi-agent cooperation, lifelong learning, and model efficiency—provides clear research agendas. These identified directions are critical for unlocking the full potential of VLA models and addressing the practical hurdles for real-world deployment. The emphasis on efficiency, in particular, highlights the need for models that can run on resource-constrained robotic platforms without compromising performance.

In conclusion, this survey is more than just a summary; it is a strategic document that shapes the discourse and trajectory of large VLM-based VLA models for robotic manipulation. Its comprehensive nature, structured taxonomy, and forward-looking perspective will undoubtedly foster deeper understanding, stimulate innovation, and accelerate the realization of truly intelligent and versatile robotic systems. The commitment to maintain a regularly updated project page further reinforces its role as a living, authoritative resource for the scientific community.
