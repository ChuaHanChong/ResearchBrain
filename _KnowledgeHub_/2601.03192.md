# MemRL: Self-Evolving Agents via Runtime Reinforcement Learning on Episodic Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> MemRL introduces a non-parametric approach for AI agents to continuously improve skills after deployment, decoupling a frozen Large Language Model backbone from a dynamic episodic memory. The framework achieved an average of +3.8% relative improvement in Cumulative Success Rate over strong baselines, demonstrating superior transferability of learned knowledge across diverse tasks.

## Problem

- AI agents struggle with continuous skill acquisition post-deployment without experiencing catastrophic forgetting of previously learned knowledge.
- Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) for continuous adaptation is computationally expensive and highly susceptible to catastrophic forgetting.
- Existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems are passive, often retrieving semantically related but low-utility 'noise' from memory, hindering effective learning from environmental feedback.

## Method

- **MemRL** decouples a stable, frozen LLM backbone from a dynamic, plastic external episodic memory to enable continuous learning without weight updates or catastrophic forgetting.
- The system formulates memory retrieval as a Memory-Based Markov Decision Process (M-MDP) where a Retrieval Policy, rather than LLM parameters, is optimized through reinforcement learning.
- It employs an 'Intent-Experience-Utility' triplet memory structure and a two-phase retrieval mechanism that combines semantic recall with value-aware selection to actively filter and utilize high-utility experiences.

## Results

- MemRL achieved an average of **+3.8%** relative improvement in Cumulative Success Rate over state-of-the-art baselines like MemP across various tasks, with gains of **+6.2%** in exploration-intensive environments.
- It demonstrated superior transferability, outperforming MemP by an average of **+2.8%** in Success Rate on held-out, unseen tasks, validating its ability to retain and generalize high-utility procedural knowledge.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of runtime RL and Q-value weighting for filtering noisy memories, and the system exhibited enhanced stability (mean forgetting rate of 0.041) and robust cross-model knowledge transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the stable LLM from an external, plastic memory allows for continuous skill evolution while maintaining foundational stability, addressing the stability-plasticity dilemma.
> - Formulating memory retrieval as a reinforcement learning problem enables agents to actively learn the utility of past experiences, distinguishing valuable strategies from semantically similar noise.
> - The learned Q-values accurately predict task success and capture latent strategic utility, including transferable procedural lessons from 'near-misses', enhancing robustness beyond simple success/failure metrics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas in artificial intelligence: large language models (LLMs), intelligent agents, continuous learning, and memory systems. Its core contribution lies in addressing the long-standing "stability-plasticity dilemma"—the challenge of enabling a system to learn new information (plasticity) without forgetting previously acquired knowledge (stability).

Current AI agents, particularly those powered by LLMs, face significant hurdles in achieving human-like self-evolution. The paper identifies two primary limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Fine-tuning:** While effective for adapting LLMs to specific tasks, fine-tuning is computationally expensive due to the massive number of parameters in LLMs. More critically, it is highly susceptible to "catastrophic forgetting," where learning new tasks erases knowledge from old ones. This prevents continuous, lifelong learning in real-world deployments.
2. **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** RAG systems augment LLMs with external knowledge bases, providing a non-parametric way to incorporate new information without modifying model weights. However, the paper argues that current RAG mechanisms are inherently *passive*. They rely predominantly on semantic similarity for retrieval, which often leads to the retrieval of "noise"—information that is semantically related but not *functionally useful* or high-utility for solving the current problem. This passive approach prevents agents from actively learning from environmental feedback and distinguishing truly valuable strategies from mere informational matches.

MEMRL directly tackles these limitations by introducing "Runtime Continuous Learning," a paradigm emphasized by recent works like Silver & Sutton (2025) as signaling the "era of experience." Unlike traditional Continual Learning or Test-Time Adaptation, which typically involve updating model parameters, MEMRL's defining constraint is to keep the backbone LLM frozen. This ensures stability and computational efficiency, directly avoiding catastrophic forgetting and the high costs of weight updates.

The work builds upon the growing field of "Agentic Memory," which has evolved from static RAG to more dynamic and governable memory structures. While existing agentic memory systems emphasize memory organization and reflection, they often lack a robust mechanism for *value-aware* selection of experiences based on their actual utility in solving tasks. MEMRL fills this gap by drawing inspiration from cognitive theories, specifically constructive episodic simulation and memory reconsolidation, to formulate memory retrieval as a *value-based decision process* akin to reinforcement learning. This is a significant shift from mere semantic matching, allowing the agent to actively learn which past experiences are truly beneficial for achieving success.

In essence, MEMRL represents a pivotal step towards building genuinely self-evolving AI agents that can adapt and improve continuously in dynamic environments without compromising their foundational stability, bridging a critical gap between current LLM capabilities and desired lifelong learning intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind MEMRL is to enable AI agents to achieve "self-evolving ability"—the hallmark of human intelligence—where new skills are mastered through continuous learning from past experiences. The authors identify a critical gap in current AI systems: their struggle to emulate this decoupled self-evolution, where stable reasoning is maintained while new knowledge is assimilated.

The key objectives driving the development of MEMRL are:

1. **Reconcile the Stability-Plasticity Dilemma:** The primary objective is to develop an approach that allows AI agents to continuously acquire new knowledge and adapt (plasticity) without forgetting previously learned skills or corrupting their core capabilities (stability). This is framed as explicitly decoupling the model's stable cognitive reasoning (the frozen LLM backbone) from its dynamic, plastic episodic memory.
2. **Overcome Limitations of Fine-tuning:** To address the computational expense and catastrophic forgetting associated with fine-tuning large language models for continuous adaptation.
3. **Address Passivity and Noise in RAG:** To move beyond the limitations of traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which relies on passive semantic matching. The goal is to enable agents to actively distinguish high-utility strategies from semantically similar but ultimately unhelpful "noise" within their memory.
4. **Enable Runtime Continuous Learning without Weight Updates:** To create an agent that can continuously improve its performance after deployment and rapidly adapt to new tasks, critically, *without modifying the weights of its pre-trained backbone model*. This ensures efficiency and prevents catastrophic forgetting.
5. **Formalize Memory Usage as a Learnable Decision Process:** To transform memory retrieval from a static, similarity-based lookup into an active decision-making process, guided by environmental feedback and learned utility.
6. **Develop a Non-Parametric Framework for Self-Evolution:** To propose MEMRL as a novel, non-parametric approach that uses reinforcement learning principles directly on episodic memory, rather than optimizing model parameters.

In summary, the authors are motivated to create a more robust, efficient, and human-like learning paradigm for AI agents, one that allows for continuous, adaptive improvement in the wild while maintaining the integrity and capabilities of the foundational LLM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MEMRL (Memory Reinforcement Learning) introduces a non-parametric, reinforcement learning-driven framework designed to enable self-evolving agents. Its methodology is fundamentally inspired by human cognition, specifically the constructive episodic simulation that balances cognitive stability and episodic plasticity.

The core approach involves:

1. **Model-Memory Decoupling:** MEMRL explicitly decouples the stable cognitive reasoning capabilities of a pre-trained (and frozen) Large Language Model (LLM) from a dynamic, plastic external episodic memory. This is crucial for avoiding catastrophic forgetting and computational overhead associated with weight updates. The LLM's parameters remain unchanged throughout the agent's runtime learning process.
2. **Problem Formulation as an M-MDP:** The interaction between the frozen LLM and external memory is formalized as a Memory-Based Markov Decision Process (M-MDP). In this framework, the agent's policy for generating an action `a_t` given state `s_t` and memory `M_t` is a marginal probability over possible retrieved memories `m`: `π(a_t|s_t, M_t) = Σ_m μ(m|s_t, M_t) p_LLM(a_t|s_t, m)`. Here, `μ` is the *Retrieval Policy*, which MEMRL optimizes, and `p_LLM` is the fixed *Inference Policy* of the frozen LLM. This shifts learning from LLM parameters to the memory usage policy.
3. **Intent-Experience-Utility Triplet Memory Structure:** To support value-based decision-making, the external memory `M` is structured not merely as key-value pairs, but as a set of triplets `(z_i, e_i, Q_i)`:
- **Intent (z_i):** Represents the user query or current state, typically an embedding of the query.
- **Experience (e_i):** Stores the raw historical experience, such as a successful solution trace or a detailed trajectory.
- **Utility (Q_i):** A learned scalar value that approximates the *expected return* (or utility) of applying experience `e_i` to intents similar to `z_i`. This `Q_i` serves as the critic in the reinforcement learning setup, guiding the retrieval process.
4. **Two-Phase Retrieval Mechanism:** This mechanism transforms retrieval from passive semantic matching into an active, value-aware selection process:
- **Phase A: Similarity-Based Recall:** Given a query `s`, MEMRL first identifies a candidate pool `C(s)` of semantically similar experiences from the memory bank. This filtering is based on the cosine similarity between the query embedding and the stored Intent embeddings, constrained by a sparsity threshold `δ`. This step ensures relevance.
- **Phase B: Value-Aware Selection:** From the candidate pool `C(s)`, MEMRL selects the `top-k2` memories using a composite score. This score balances semantic similarity (exploration) with the learned utility (exploitation): `score(s, z_i, e_i) = (1-λ) * normalized_similarity(s, z_i) + λ * normalized_Q_i`. The weighting factor `λ` allows for controlling the trade-off. This phase actively filters out semantically similar but low-utility "distractor" memories.
5. **Non-Parametric Reinforcement Learning on Memory (Utility-Driven Update):**
- After the LLM generates an action `a` using the retrieved context `m_ctx(s)` and executes it in the environment, a scalar reward `r` is received.
- For the memories that were actually injected into the input context `m_ctx(s)`, their corresponding `Q_values` are updated using a Monte Carlo style rule: `Q_new ← Q_old + α * (r - Q_old)`. Here, `α` is the learning rate. This update drives the `Q_value` towards the empirical expected return, enabling the agent to "remember what works."
- Furthermore, for each sampled trajectory (successful or failed), an LLM summarizes the experience, and this summary is stored as a new triplet `(z, e_new, Q_init)` in the memory bank, continuously expanding the agent's experience base.
6. **Theoretical Stability Analysis:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis demonstrating that under assumptions of a frozen inference policy and a stationary task distribution, the utility estimates converge in expectation to stable, unbiased estimates of memory effectiveness, with bounded variance. The framework is conceptualized as a Generalized Expectation-Maximization (GEM) process, where retrieval (E-step) and utility updates (M-step) monotonically improve a global objective, ensuring global stability and preventing catastrophic forgetting.

This integrated approach allows MEMRL to learn dynamically from interaction, distinguish between relevant and useful memories, and continuously improve its performance without the pitfalls of fine-tuning or the limitations of passive RAG.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MEMRL demonstrates significant advancements in agentic runtime learning and generalization across a diverse suite of benchmarks. The key findings and results are:

1. **Superior Performance in Runtime Learning:**
- MEMRL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including RAG-based methods (RAG, Self-RAG) and agentic memory systems (Mem0, MemP), over 10 epochs of continuous learning.
- It achieves an average of **+3.8% relative improvement in Cumulative Success Rate (CSR)** over MemP, the strongest baseline.
- The gains are particularly pronounced in exploration-intensive environments like ALFWorld and OS tasks (both +6.2% improvement in CSR), where the ability to filter noise and identify high-utility procedural patterns is critical.
- MEMRL exhibits smoother learning curves and superior stability compared to baselines (e.g., MemP, RAG), indicating more robust and consistent adaptation.

2. **Enhanced Transferability of Learned Knowledge:**
- When memory banks trained by MEMRL are frozen and tested on held-out, unseen tasks, MEMRL demonstrates superior transferability.
- It outperforms MemP by an average of **+2.8% in Success Rate** on these transfer tasks.
- The advantage is significant in complex environments such as ALFWorld (+5.8%) and OS tasks (+2.6%), validating that MEMRL effectively filters low-value noise and retains high-utility procedural knowledge that generalizes robustly.

3. **Ablation Studies Confirm Mechanism Efficacy:**
- **Effectiveness of Runtime RL:** Direct comparison against non-RL counterparts (MemP and RAG) shows a clear divergence in performance. The RL-driven value function in MEMRL effectively filters noisy memories, consolidates successful experiences, and leads to a monotonically widening gap in Cumulative Success Rate, confirming the utility of reinforcement learning on memory.
- **Impact of Q-Value Weighting (λ):** Experiments on the `λ` factor (balancing semantic similarity and Q-value) reveal an optimal performance at `λ = 0.5`. Pure semantic retrieval (`λ = 0`) plateaus, while excessive reliance on Q-values (`λ → 1`) introduces volatility, confirming the need for a balanced approach.
- **Cross-Task vs. Single-Task Optimization:** MEMRL's full "Cross-Task Retrieval" significantly outperforms "Single-Task Reflection" (conceptually similar to Reflexion) in structured environments like OS-Agent (+9.0%) and ALFWorld (+5.1%). This indicates effective horizontal transfer of policies across similar tasks. For environments with low internal semantic similarity (e.g., HLE), its performance aligns with single-task methods, suggesting dependence on shared patterns for cross-task generalization.
- **Sensitivity to Retrieval Size:** An inverted-U trajectory of performance across retrieval sizes (`k1`, `k2`) demonstrates that a moderate configuration (e.g., `k1=5, k2=3`) is optimal, maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio in retrieved context. Both too sparse and too dense retrieval degrade performance.

4. **Predictive Power and Insight from the Q Critic:**
- The learned Q-values exhibit a **strong positive correlation (Pearson r = 0.861)** with empirical task success rates, indicating the critic's ability to accurately rank memories by their success likelihood.
- Analysis of memory composition shows that MEMRL retains a small fraction of "failure" memories (∼12%) even in high-Q bins. This suggests that the Q-values capture latent strategic utility beyond binary success/failure, including transferable procedural lessons from "near-misses," providing greater robustness than simple success-replay mechanisms.

5. **Enhanced Stability and Reduced Forgetting:**
- MEMRL achieves a lower mean forgetting rate (0.041) compared to MemP (0.051), demonstrating superior stability in preventing catastrophic forgetting over extended learning.
- Ablation shows that removing z-score normalization and similarity gating causes the forgetting rate to spike (to 0.073), emphasizing the critical role of strict filtering in maintaining stable self-evolution.

6. **Advanced Capabilities: Cross-Model Transfer and Multi-Task Merging:**
- **Cross-Model Transferability:** A memory bank trained by a strong model (Gemini-3-pro) provides substantial zero-shot performance gains when transferred to weaker or different models (e.g., Qwen3-235B improved by >3x), indicating that MEMRL captures model-agnostic procedural knowledge.
- **Multi-Task Memory Merging:** Merging memory banks from distinct domains (OS control, DB management) results in negligible interference. Performance remains stable due to the Phase-A semantic filter, which effectively gates irrelevant cross-task memories, supporting modular capability expansion.

In conclusion, the findings overwhelmingly support MEMRL's effectiveness in enabling robust, stable, and continuously improving AI agents through non-parametric reinforcement learning on episodic memory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MEMRL carries significant implications for the future of AI agents and continuous learning, addressing foundational challenges and paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable autonomous systems.

1. **Fundamental Advancement in Continuous Learning:** MEMRL offers a robust solution to the long-standing "stability-plasticity dilemma" by decoupling the stable LLM backbone from a plastic, evolving memory. This is a critical step towards enabling true lifelong learning for AI agents, allowing them to adapt to new environments and tasks without the risk of catastrophic forgetting, which plagues parametric fine-tuning approaches.
2. **Efficiency and Scalability:** By adopting a non-parametric approach that eschews weight updates, MEMRL drastically reduces the computational expense associated with continuous adaptation. This makes the deployment and maintenance of self-evolving agents more feasible and environmentally friendly, enabling large-scale, real-world applications where frequent fine-tuning is impractical.
3. **Enhanced Intelligence through Value-Aware Retrieval:** The shift from passive semantic matching to active, value-based retrieval is a paradigm change for memory-augmented LLMs. By learning the *utility* of past experiences through environmental feedback, agents can effectively filter noise and prioritize truly helpful information. This makes the agent's decision-making more intelligent, robust, and aligned with task success. The ability to learn from "near-misses" (high-utility failures) further enriches the learning process.
4. **Portability and Modularity of Knowledge:** The demonstration of cross-model memory transferability is particularly impactful. It suggests that MEMRL captures model-agnostic procedural knowledge, allowing trained memory banks to serve as portable knowledge bases. This could enable weaker or specialized LLMs to "inherit" advanced capabilities from stronger, more extensively trained models without direct fine-tuning, democratizing access to complex behaviors. Furthermore, the seamless merging of multi-task memories without interference indicates MEMRL's support for modular knowledge acquisition and expansion.
5. **Broader Applicability and Future Research:** MEMRL's effectiveness across diverse benchmarks (coding, navigation, OS/DB interaction, multidisciplinary reasoning) highlights its broad applicability. It opens numerous avenues for future research, including exploring multi-step utility updates for long-horizon tasks, advanced credit assignment mechanisms, and strategies for managing memory security and multi-agent shared memory.
6. **Shift towards "Era of Experience":** The paper reinforces the idea that interactive experiences generated by agents during their operational lifetime will become a vital source of knowledge, complementing or even surpassing static training datasets. This paradigm shift can lead to AI systems that are not only powerful but also continuously learning and improving through lived experience.

In conclusion, MEMRL represents a significant scientific and engineering contribution that pushes the boundaries of autonomous AI agents. Its focus on stable, efficient, and intelligent self-evolution positions it as a foundational technology for building the next generation of adaptive and continuously learning AI systems that can thrive in complex, dynamic real-world environments. The collaboration with MemTensor also suggests a strong potential for practical implementation and broad societal impact in the near future.
