# STVG-R1: Incentivizing Instance-Level Reasoning and Grounding in Videos via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The STVG-R1 framework by Zhang et al. introduces an object-centric visual prompting paradigm to reformulate spatial-temporal video grounding as an instance ID identification task, followed by reinforcement learning to fine-tune Vision-Language Models. This approach achieves new state-of-the-art results on several STVG benchmarks and demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization across various video understanding tasks, improving metrics like m vIoU by up to 6.8% over prior methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer from "hallucination" and cross-modal misalignment when performing dense prediction tasks like Spatial-Temporal Video Grounding (STVG).
- Existing alignment-based and decoder-based VLM approaches for STVG incur high annotation costs, significant computational overhead, and often exhibit limited generalization due to their reliance on explicit coordinate prediction or iterative decoding steps.
- Prior methods lack explicit, interpretable inputs for instance-level reasoning in dynamic video contexts, making it difficult for VLMs to precisely ground specific objects over time.

## Method

- A novel ==object-centric visual prompting== paradigm is introduced, which pre-processes videos by embedding unique, temporally consistent numeric object identifiers directly onto object centroids within frames.
- The paper develops the first reinforcement learning framework for STVG, utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to fine-tune VLMs. The reward function combines temporal IoU, sparse spatial consistency (object ID match), and output format adherence.
- An automated pipeline based on YOLOv12-x and SAM2 with tracking generates robust visual prompts, converting complex coordinate prediction into a simpler instance ID identification task.

## Results

- STVG-R1 achieved state-of-the-art performance on major STVG benchmarks, including **40.8%** m vIoU on HCSTVG-v2, surpassing the previous SOTA (SpaceVLLM) by an absolute margin of **6.8%**.
- The framework demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization, achieving **47.3%** J&F on multi-object Referring Video Object Segmentation (MeViS) and outperforming LLaVA-ST by +**7.7%** at tIoU@**0.5** on Charades-STA for video temporal grounding.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effect of visual prompts (enhancing spatial localization) and GRPO (improving temporal accuracy and reasoning), with their combined use yielding the most consistent and significant performance gains across metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating dense coordinate prediction into instance-level ID identification, supported by explicit visual prompts, significantly mitigates visual-textual misalignment and VLM hallucination in video grounding tasks.
> - Embedding simple, temporally consistent object identifiers as visual prompts provides effective and interpretable instance-level cues to VLMs, leading to performance improvements even in zero-shot settings without architectural modifications.
> - Applying reinforcement learning with a task-driven reward function, encompassing temporal accuracy, spatial consistency, and response format, enables VLMs to optimize directly for coherent spatial-temporal reasoning in videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Challenges in Vision-Language Models for Dense Prediction

The paper addresses a critical challenge in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in dense prediction tasks like Spatial-Temporal Video Grounding (STVG). STVG requires models to localize a natural language query in both time (temporal segment) and space (bounding box or mask for each frame) within a video. A widespread problem in VLMs, especially when applied to such fine-grained tasks, is "hallucination"—where the model generates outputs that are plausible but misaligned with the visual content, such as temporal segments exceeding video duration or coordinates falling outside frame resolution. This misalignment is exacerbated in dense prediction where consistency across frames is vital.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Prior Approaches

Existing research to mitigate cross-modal misalignment generally falls into two categories:
1. **Alignment-based approaches:** These methods (e.g., LLaVA-ST, SpaceVLLM) attempt to directly enhance the visual-textual alignment within VLMs, often by incorporating auxiliary trainable modules or special query tokens. While these can improve performance, they typically involve significant annotation costs (for large-scale dense prediction data), introduce substantial computational overhead, and exhibit limited generalization dueating their reliance on explicit coordinate prediction. They often struggle with multi-object scenarios and can produce inconsistent or meaningless bounding box predictions across frames.
2. **Decoder-based methods:** These approaches (e.g., those using segmentation tokens and trainable decoders) avoid direct coordinate prediction by having VLMs generate abstract "segmentation tokens," which are then processed by a separate, trainable decoder (e.g., inspired by SAM2). While this can alleviate misalignment issues and improve cross-frame consistency, it often introduces iterative decoding steps, further increasing training complexity and inference time, and limiting generalization due to the implicit nature of the outputs.

The paper highlights that both paradigms suffer from the introduction of additional trainable components and fail to provide explicit, interpretable inputs for instance-level reasoning, especially in dynamic video contexts.

#### 1.3. Related Work: Visual Prompts and Reinforcement Learning in VLMs

The current work draws inspiration from two distinct but converging fields:
- **Visual Prompts:** Recent research has shown the effectiveness of simple, consistent referential cues embedded directly into visual inputs to guide VLMs. Examples like GPT4Scene use consistent object IDs across multi-view images to enhance 3D understanding. This approach offers an interpretable way to provide explicit instance-level information to VLMs without complex architectural changes.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) in VLMs/LLMs:** RL, particularly with task-driven rewards, has proven highly effective in improving the reasoning capabilities and adherence to desired output formats in Large Language Models (LLMs) (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, R1-V, Time-R1). This paradigm, often called "Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward" (RLVR), allows models to optimize directly for task-specific metrics rather than relying solely on token-level supervised fine-tuning (SFT) losses. While RL has seen applications in general VLM tasks and video understanding (e.g., Video-R1, Time-R1), its application to jointly address *spatial-temporal* video grounding, with its intricate spatial and temporal requirements, remains largely unexplored.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two trends, proposing a novel combination to overcome the aforementioned limitations in STVG.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind STVG-R1 are driven by the challenges of visual-textual misalignment, computational overhead, and limited generalization in existing STVG methods:

1. **Reformulate Dense Prediction for Interpretability and Consistency:** The core motivation is to simplify the complex, dense per-frame coordinate prediction problem into a more compact and interpretable instance-level identification task. This aims to inherently mitigate visual-textual misalignment by providing explicit object references.
2. **Introduce an Object-Centric Visual Prompting Paradigm:** To achieve the above, the paper aims to develop a novel method for embedding unique, temporally consistent object identifiers directly into video frames as "visual prompts." This paradigm provides explicit and interpretable input cues for VLMs, simplifying the grounding task.
3. **Develop the First Reinforcement Learning Framework for STVG:** The paper's second major objective is to leverage reinforcement learning for STVG, addressing the shortcomings of traditional supervised fine-tuning. This involves designing a task-driven reward function that can jointly optimize temporal accuracy, spatial consistency, and the structural format of the model's output, thereby enhancing reasoning capabilities and output reliability.
4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance and Strong Generalization:** Ultimately, the work aims to demonstrate that this combined approach (visual prompting + RL) can surpass existing state-of-the-art methods on multiple STVG benchmarks and exhibit robust zero-shot generalization to related but distinct tasks like multi-object referring video object segmentation and video temporal grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The STVG-R1 framework introduces a two-pronged approach: an object-centric visual prompting paradigm to preprocess video inputs, and a reinforcement learning strategy to fine-tune Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for the STVG task.

#### 3.1. STVG-R1 Framework Overview

The core idea is to shift from directly predicting raw per-frame bounding box coordinates to identifying a unique, temporally consistent object ID within a given time segment. Given a video $V = \{I_1, \dots, I_T\}$ and a textual query $q$, the first step involves augmenting each frame $I_t$ with visual prompts $P_t$, resulting in a "prompted video" $\tilde{V} = \{\tilde{I}_1, \dots, \tilde{I}_T\}$. This $\tilde{V}$ and query $q$ are then fed into a VLM, $\pi_\theta$, which is trained to jointly predict the temporal interval $[t_s, t_e]$ and the corresponding object identifier $ı$.

#### 3.2. Object-Centric Prompted Video Construction

This paradigm aims to provide explicit instance-level cues.
- **Data Format:** Each training sample consists of $\{V, q, P, M, A\}$, where $P$ are the visual prompts, $M$ is a segmentation mask database (storing instance IDs with run-length encoded masks per frame), and $A$ is the ground-truth answer (the target object ID). Masks are converted to bounding boxes for IoU calculations. The target object ID $A$ is determined by finding the instance ID with the highest overall IoU consistency across the ground-truth temporal segment through majority voting.
- **Data Generation Pipeline:** An automated pipeline generates the object-centric visual prompts:
 1. **Initial Detection:** The first frame $I_1$ is processed by an off-the-shelf object detector (YOLOv12-x) to obtain candidate bounding boxes across COCO categories.
 2. **Segmentation and Tracking:** These initial detections are used to prompt SAM2 (Segment Anything in Images and Videos), which generates high-quality segmentation masks. These masks are then propagated to subsequent frames via tracking.
 3. **Re-detection and Re-ID:** To handle newly appearing or occluded objects, re-detection is performed at fixed intervals (every 15 frames). New detections are considered new instances only if their overlap with existing tracked objects is consistently low. SAM2 then performs bidirectional tracking (forward and backward) to recover the full trajectory of these newly identified instances.
 4. **Visual Prompt Embedding:** For each tracked mask, a compact numeric marker (its object ID) is overlaid onto its centroid (x,y) coordinates within the frame. These markers serve as explicit visual prompts.
- **Robustness:** The pipeline is designed to be robust, with global detection failures occurring in less than 1% of samples. Mechanisms like periodic re-detection, bidirectional tracking, and majority voting for ID assignment mitigate local missing detections and reinforce identity consistency. Semantic misclassifications by the detector (e.g., fish detected as bird) do not impede the framework as supervision relies on consistent instance IDs, not precise class labels.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning for Enhanced VLMs

STVG-R1 is the first reinforcement learning framework for STVG. Since the task is reformulated as ID identification, RL can directly optimize the VLM policy $\pi_\theta$.
- **Reward Modeling:** The total reward $R(o)$ for a generated output $o$ combines three components:
 1. **Temporal IoU Reward ($r_t(o)$):** Measures the IoU between the predicted temporal interval $[t_s, t_e]$ and the ground-truth segment $[t'_s, t'_e]$.
 2. **Spatial Consistency Reward ($r_s(o)$):** A sparse (0/1) reward that is 1 only if the predicted object ID $ı$ matches the ground-truth ID $ı^*$ AND that object ID appears within the predicted temporal segment $[t_s, t_e]$. This encourages joint spatial-temporal correctness and prevents trivial solutions.
 3. **Format Reward ($r_f(o)$):** A sparse (0/1) reward that is 1 only if the model's response adheres to a predefined reasoning structure, enclosing thought processes within `<think>...</think>` tags and the final answer within `<answer>...</answer>` tags. This promotes interpretable reasoning chains.
 The overall reward is $R(o) = r_t(o) + r_s(o) + r_f(o)$.

#### 3.4. Training Strategies

The policy model $\pi_\theta$ is optimized using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, an effective method for tasks with well-defined evaluation signals.
- **Response Generation:** For a given video-text query, the model generates $n$ candidate responses (e.g., $n=8$).
- **Advantage Calculation:** The advantage $A_i$ for each response $o_i$ is calculated by normalizing its reward $R(o_i)$ against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its group of $n$ responses.
- **Optimization Objective:** The GRPO objective updates $\pi_\theta$ to assign higher probabilities to responses with larger normalized advantages, relative to a previous policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$. It includes a clipping parameter $\epsilon$ to prevent overly aggressive updates and a KL regularization term $\beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{ref})$ to prevent policy drift from a frozen reference policy $\pi_{ref}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed object-centric visual prompting paradigm and the STVG-R1 reinforcement learning framework across a wide range of benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Spatial-Temporal Video Grounding (STVG)

- **Zero-shot with Visual Prompts:** The visual prompting paradigm alone significantly improves the zero-shot performance of various general VLMs. For instance, on HCSTVG-v2, Qwen3-VL-8B shows a remarkable +35.1% increase in vIoU@0.3 (from 22.8% to 57.9%). Even Qwen2.5-VL-7B sees a +2.7% gain on vIoU@0.3. This highlights the ability of the paradigm to leverage full video information and provide clearer object references.
- **State-of-the-Art with STVG-R1 (Fine-tuning):** STVG-R1 establishes new state-of-the-art results on major STVG benchmarks:
 - **HCSTVG-v2:** STVG-R1 achieves 40.8% m vIoU, significantly surpassing the previous SOTA (SpaceVLLM, 34.0%) by an absolute margin of 6.8%. It achieves 67.9% vIoU@0.3 and 38.8% vIoU@0.5, representing gains of 10.9% and 14.1% over SpaceVLLM, respectively.
 - **ST-Align:** STVG-R1 outperforms LLaVA-ST, the previous strongest VLM model, by +0.6% on m vIoU, achieving 23.4%.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Video Spatial Grounding and Referring Video Object Segmentation

- **Video Spatial Grounding (ST-Align):** For tasks focusing purely on spatial localization, the object-centric visual prompting paradigm achieves a notable zero-shot gain of 11.1% on m vIoU. After RL fine-tuning, STVG-R1 further surpasses LLaVA-ST by 13.1% on m vIoU, demonstrating robust spatial grounding capabilities.
- **Multi-Object Referring Video Object Segmentation (MeViS):** Crucially, STVG-R1 exhibits strong zero-shot generalization to this more complex task, achieving a new state-of-the-art of 47.3% on J&F. This is particularly impressive as STVG-R1 was trained only on single-object STVG data, suggesting that the simplified identifier-based formulation facilitates transfer to scenarios with multiple target objects requiring segmentation.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Video Temporal Grounding

- **Out-of-Distribution Benchmarks (Charades-STA, TVGBench):** STVG-R1 achieves the best zero-shot performance on Charades-STA, surpassing LLaVA-ST by +7.7% at tIoU@0.5. Although slightly below the task-specific Time-R1 on TVGBench, it achieves competitive results, indicating strong temporal generalization despite its primary focus on spatial-temporal grounding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Visual Prompt Design:** Red-colored numeric prompts of medium font size (20) proved optimal, balancing visibility and minimal visual interference. Varying prompt size or using letters instead of numbers showed only moderate performance differences.
- **Mask Filtering Thresholds:** A moderate mask filtering threshold (1/3 of maximum mask size per category) improved spatial grounding in zero-shot settings by reducing clutter from semantically non-salient small instances, suggesting a beneficial trade-off.
- **Module Contributions:**
 - Visual prompts primarily enhance spatial localization.
 - GRPO (reinforcement learning) substantially improves temporal accuracy and overall reasoning.
 - The combination of visual prompting and GRPO yields the most consistent and significant gains across all metrics, confirming their synergistic effect.
- **Reward Design:** The sparse 0/1 spatial consistency reward performed better than a continuous or coupled spatio-temporal reward, suggesting that a clear, unequivocal signal for correct instance identification is more effective for RL optimization in this context.

#### 4.5. Impact of Visual Prompt Occlusion

An evaluation on the MME-VideoOCR benchmark, which includes ten OCR-centric tasks, showed negligible negative impact from the visual prompts. While some fine-grained character recognition tasks saw a minimal drop, higher-level tasks like Visual Text QA and Text Grounding even improved, suggesting that the markers can guide attention to relevant regions without significant visual interference.

#### 4.6. Visualizations

Qualitative results confirm that STVG-R1 produces interpretable reasoning chains, first identifying relevant object IDs and then determining temporal boundaries. Complex descriptive expressions are compactly mapped to object IDs, facilitating precise instance-level grounding. The method also demonstrates robustness on diverse video domains like ego-centric and movie videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The STVG-R1 paper presents a significant advancement in the field of video understanding by Vision-Language Models, with several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Mitigation of VLM Hallucinations and Misalignment:** The core innovation—reformulating dense coordinate prediction into instance-level ID identification—directly addresses the persistent problems of visual-textual misalignment and hallucination in VLMs. By providing explicit, interpretable object references, the model is less prone to generating nonsensical or inconsistent spatial outputs.
2. **Novel and Interpretable Paradigm:** The object-centric visual prompting paradigm is a simple yet highly effective method for enriching VLM inputs. It avoids the need for complex, trainable architectural modifications or expensive explicit coordinate alignment, making it a more efficient and generalizable solution. The generated reasoning traces with ID references offer enhanced interpretability, a valuable feature for complex AI systems.
3. **Pioneering RL for STVG:** Introducing the first reinforcement learning framework for spatial-temporal video grounding marks a crucial step. By directly optimizing with task-driven rewards that encompass temporal accuracy, spatial consistency, and structural compliance, STVG-R1 unlocks the potential for VLMs to achieve a deeper, more coherent understanding of video content, moving beyond token-level supervision.
4. **State-of-the-Art Performance and Robust Generalization:** Achieving new SOTA results across multiple challenging STVG benchmarks (HCSTVG-v1/v2, ST-Align) validates the effectiveness of the proposed approach. More importantly, its strong zero-shot generalization to multi-object referring video object segmentation and competitive performance on video temporal grounding tasks demonstrate that the learned capabilities are transferable and robust across diverse video understanding challenges, hinting at a more general-purpose video VLM.
5. **Reduced Computational Overhead and Annotation Cost:** By sidestepping the need for auxiliary trainable decoders or extensive dense annotation for coordinate alignment, STVG-R1 promises a more cost-effective and computationally efficient approach to training robust video grounding models.
6. **Broader Applicability:** The demonstrated zero-shot capabilities in multi-object grounding, video QA, and multi-person video captioning suggest that the object-centric visual prompting paradigm could serve as a foundational component for a wide array of video-based VLM applications, enabling more precise and reliable interaction with dynamic visual content. This work could inspire further research into explicit instance-level representations in other multimodal tasks.

In summary, STVG-R1 offers a powerful and interpretable framework for enhancing VLM capabilities in video understanding. By merging an innovative visual prompting strategy with reinforcement learning, it paves the way for more accurate, consistent, and generalizable solutions to complex spatial-temporal grounding problems, significantly advancing the state of the art in the field.
