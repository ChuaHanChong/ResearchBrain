# Learning Cross-Coupled and Regime Dependent Dynamics for Aerial Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Manchester, Delft University of Technology, International Institute of Information Technology Hyderabad, and Newcastle University developed a structured encoder-decoder framework for adaptive residual dynamics learning in aerial manipulators. This system, which combines an offline-trained history encoder with an online-adapted linear decoder, achieved the lowest trajectory tracking error across various payload and trajectory conditions and demonstrated robust, rapid adaptation to abrupt dynamic shifts caused by payload drops.

## Problem

- Traditional analytical models struggle to capture strong quadrotor-manipulator coupling, where manipulator motion continuously alters the system's inertia distribution and center of mass, leading to complex, configuration-dependent interactions.
- Aerial manipulator dynamics exhibit history-dependent effects due to delayed phenomena such as aerodynamic wake propagation and actuator dynamics, which are not captured by instantaneous state-input models.
- Aerial manipulators operate under regime-dependent non-stationarity, experiencing abrupt and gradual dynamic changes from tasks like payload pickup/release or manipulator reconfiguration, necessitating online adaptation to distribution shifts.

## Method

- A structured encoder-decoder framework is proposed to learn residual dynamics, featuring an offline-trained nonlinear history encoder and an online-adapted linear decoder to separate complex feature extraction from adaptable mapping.
- The history encoder uses alternating temporal and cross-variable attention blocks to capture both delayed effects within each variable's history and interactions between different state and input variables.
- A linear latent decoder is updated recursively online using a Kalman filter-like approach, augmented with consistency-driven covariance inflation, to efficiently adapt to regime-dependent non-stationary dynamics and rapid changes.

## Results

- The full structured encoder achieved the lowest open-loop residual prediction RMSE (**0.18**) compared to simpler architectures like DNN (**0.34**), TCN (**0.26**), and temporal-only encoders (**0.21**), demonstrating its superior ability to capture complex dynamics.
- The proposed full adaptive decoder update with consistency-driven covariance inflation exhibited the lowest RMSE, smallest post-shift error excursion, and fastest, smoothest settling behavior during payload drop scenarios, outperforming non-adaptive and fixed-forgetting Bayesian updates.
- The complete framework achieved the lowest closed-loop trajectory tracking error across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution payload conditions and different tracking scenarios, surpassing nominal models and other offline and online adaptive learning baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling both temporal dependencies and cross-variable coupling through a structured attention-based encoder is crucial for accurately representing the complex, coupled, and history-dependent residual dynamics of aerial manipulators.
> - Separating the nonlinear, history-dependent feature extraction into a fixed encoder from a linear, adaptable mapping in the decoder allows for a powerful representation that is computationally efficient for real-time online adaptation.
> - Implementing consistency-driven covariance inflation within the Bayesian online decoder update enables rapid and stable adaptation to abrupt dynamic shifts by dynamically adjusting the learning rate based on observed prediction inconsistencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Aerial manipulators, which integrate multirotor aerial vehicles with robotic arms, are designed for complex tasks such as inspection, contact interaction, grasping, and payload transport. The reliable execution of these tasks is predicated on accurate dynamic models. However, modeling these systems presents significant challenges due to the inherent complexity of their dynamics.

Traditional modeling approaches, including purely analytical physics-based models, struggle to capture several critical aspects:
1. **Strong Quadrotor–Manipulator Coupling:** The manipulator's motion continuously alters the system's inertia distribution and center of mass, leading to significant, configuration-dependent coupling effects between the aerial platform and the robotic arm. These inertial couplings, combined with aerodynamic interactions such as rotor downwash and manipulator interference, introduce complex dynamics that are difficult to model exhaustively with analytical formulations.
2. **History-Dependent Dynamics:** Residual dynamics often depend on recent state-input trajectories. This is due to delayed effects, including aerodynamic wake propagation and actuator dynamics, which necessitate models that can account for temporal dependencies rather than just instantaneous states.
3. **Regime-Dependent Non-Stationarity:** Aerial manipulators frequently operate under varying conditions. Tasks like payload pickup and release, grasping, contact interaction, and manipulator reconfiguration induce both abrupt and gradual changes in dynamics. These lead to distribution shifts and non-stationary behavior, meaning a model trained on one set of conditions may degrade under others.

Recent research has increasingly explored data-driven and learning-based methods to address these modeling challenges. Approaches such as Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), Gaussian Processes (GPs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs) have demonstrated the capacity to approximate complex quadrotor dynamics. However, a significant limitation of many of these methods is their reliance on offline training on fixed datasets. This makes them susceptible to performance degradation when encountering the time-varying, non-stationary conditions prevalent in aerial manipulation, as they cannot effectively adapt to deployment-time distribution shifts.

To overcome the issue of non-stationary disturbances, several strategies for online model adaptation have been developed:
- **Last-layer adaptation:** Updating only the final layers of a neural network using gradient descent. This approach can be computationally efficient but often suffers from poor sample efficiency and requires careful tuning.
- **Full network retraining/meta-learning:** Periodically retraining the entire network or interpolating between pre-trained models. These methods can be computationally intensive or limited in their ability to extrapolate to unseen dynamics.
- **Bayesian online updates/adaptive control:** Incorporating GP regression or Bayesian last-layer adaptation to update models. While effective for continuous adaptation, these methods are often not specifically designed to simultaneously handle strong quadrotor-manipulator coupling and abrupt, regime-dependent non-stationary dynamics, particularly in scenarios with limited data or rapid transitions.
- **Sequence models (e.g., Transformers):** While capable of exploiting temporal structure and incorporating variable-aware attention for multivariate forecasting, these models are typically trained offline. They generally lack lightweight mechanisms for real-time online adaptation to the specific challenges of coupled and non-stationary dynamics in aerial manipulation.

The present work aims to bridge these gaps by developing a framework that explicitly accounts for cross-coupled, history-dependent, and regime-dependent non-stationary dynamics, enabling robust and efficient online adaptation in real-time aerial manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a structured encoder–decoder framework for adaptive residual dynamics learning in aerial manipulators. This framework is designed to overcome the limitations of existing modeling approaches by simultaneously addressing the complexities of cross-coupled, history-dependent, and non-stationary dynamics.

The motivation for this work stems from several fundamental challenges observed in aerial manipulator systems:

- **Difficulty in Analytical Modeling of Coupling:** Manipulator movements induce configuration-dependent forces on the aerial platform, and conversely, platform motion influences manipulator dynamics through inertial and aerodynamic coupling. These interactions are highly complex and difficult to capture accurately using analytical models alone. The paper illustrates this with a cross-correlation matrix of system states, showing strong off-diagonal dependencies that highlight severe dynamic coupling between the aerial platform and the articulated manipulator (Figure 2A). An effective model must capture these cross-variable interactions rather than treating state dimensions independently.

- **Importance of Temporal Dependencies:** The residual dynamics of aerial manipulators are not solely determined by instantaneous states and inputs. Delayed effects, such as the propagation of aerodynamic wakes, actuator dynamics, and inertial lags, mean that current behavior is influenced by recent state-input trajectories. This necessitates history-based representations. The research supports this by presenting a cross-correlation function that reveals distinct peaks at non-zero lag times, indicating significant history-dependent effects (Figure 2B).

- **Necessity for Online Adaptation to Non-Stationary Dynamics:** Aerial manipulation tasks frequently involve events like payload pickup and release, grasping, contact interactions, and manipulator reconfigurations. These events introduce abrupt dynamic transitions and gradual changes across operating regimes, causing the underlying dynamics to be non-stationary and leading to distribution shifts during deployment. Models trained purely offline cannot adequately cope with these variations. A t-SNE projection of unmodeled dynamics residuals under different payload masses (0.1 kg, 0.2 kg, and 0.3 kg) demonstrates clear clustering and distribution shifts (Figure 2C), providing direct evidence for the need for online adaptation capabilities.

To address these motivations, the research establishes two key objectives:

1. **Nonlinear Residual Representation Learning:** To develop a history-dependent latent encoder for aerial manipulators. This encoder is designed to capture complex cross-variable coupling and temporal dependencies inherent in the residual dynamics. The goal is to learn a nonlinear representation that models coupled interactions across system states while maintaining temporal consistency throughout complex aerial manipulation trajectories.

2. **Online Regime-Adaptive Residual Estimation:** To introduce a linear latent decoder specifically engineered for efficient online adaptation under regime-dependent non-stationary dynamics. The proposed linear-in-parameter structure is intended to facilitate lightweight closed-form Bayesian adaptation. This adaptation mechanism is augmented with consistency-driven covariance inflation, allowing for rapid and stable responses to both transient and slowly varying dynamics changes, while remaining compatible with real-time Model Predictive Control (MPC) operations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a structured encoder-decoder framework for learning residual dynamics, combining offline representation learning with online adaptation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system dynamics are formulated in discrete time as $x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k)$, where $x_k$ is the state and $u_k$ is the control input. A nominal, physics-based model $f_{nom}(\cdot)$ is assumed to capture the dominant rigid-body behavior. The modeling error is represented as an additive residual $f_{\Delta}(H_k)$, leading to the system equation:
$x_{k+1} = f_{nom}(x_k, u_k) + f_{\Delta}(H_k) + \epsilon_k$
Here, $\epsilon_k$ is Gaussian noise, and $f_{\Delta}(\cdot)$ is the unknown residual dynamics. The residual explicitly depends on the recent state-input history $H_k$, defined as a matrix concatenating past state and input vectors over a history length $h$. The one-step residual target is defined as $\delta_{k+1} := x_{k+1} - f_{nom}(x_k, u_k)$.
The core idea is to approximate the residual dynamics as $f_{\Delta}(H_k) \approx \Theta z_k$, where $z_k = \phi(H_k)$ is a nonlinear latent feature extracted by an offline-trained history encoder, and $\Theta$ is a decoder matrix that is adapted online. This structure separates complex nonlinear, history-dependent feature extraction from a linear, adaptable mapping to the residual target.

#### 3.2. Structured History Encoder

The encoder $\phi(H_k)$ is designed to extract a latent feature $z_k$ that captures both temporal dependencies and cross-variable coupling.
1. **History Segmentation and Embedding:** The input history $H_k$ is segmented along the temporal dimension for each state/input channel. Each segment $s_{i,c}$ (i.e., the $i$-th temporal segment of channel $c$) is then embedded into a shared feature space using a learnable projection matrix $W_{emb}$ and an encoding $e_{i,c}$ that represents temporal position and channel identity. This produces an initial feature tensor $Z$.
2. **Alternating Attention Blocks:** The encoder layers apply two types of self-attention in an alternating manner:
 - **Temporal Attention:** Multi-head self-attention is applied independently for each channel across its temporal segments. This mechanism models delayed effects within each variable's history.
 - **Cross-Variable Attention:** Multi-head self-attention is then applied across channels within each temporal segment. This captures interactions and coupling between different state and input variables (e.g., quadrotor states and manipulator joint states).
 Each attention operation is followed by a position-wise feedforward transformation, residual connections, and layer normalization.
3. **Latent Aggregation:** The features from the final encoder layer are aggregated into a single vector $\tilde{z}_k$ using attention-based pooling across both temporal segments and channels.
4. **Projection Network:** A final projection network $f_{proj}$ maps $\tilde{z}_k$ to the latent representation $z_k \in R^{\ell}$, which is then used by the linear decoder.

#### 3.3. Offline Training

The encoder $\phi(\cdot)$ and an initial decoder matrix $\Theta_0$ are learned offline from a dataset of state-input history segments and their corresponding residual targets. The decoder matrix $\Theta$ is structured row-wise, such that each output dimension $j$ of the residual $\delta_{k+1}^{(j)}$ is modeled as a linear combination of the latent features: $\delta_{k+1}^{(j)} = z_k^T \theta_j + \epsilon_k^{(j)}$. The offline training minimizes the empirical prediction error $\sum_{k=1}^N \|\delta_{k+1} - \Theta z_k\|^2_2$, which corresponds to maximum likelihood estimation under Gaussian noise assumptions. The resulting $\Theta_0$ serves as a well-conditioned initialization for online adaptation.

#### 3.4. Online Decoder Update

During deployment, the nonlinear encoder $\phi(\cdot)$ is kept fixed, while only the linear decoder parameters $\Theta$ are adapted online. This adaptation is performed recursively using a Kalman filter-like approach in parameter space.
1. **Recursive Bayesian Update:** For each output dimension $j$, the parameter vector $\theta_{k,j}$ is modeled as a random walk: $\theta_{k,j} = \theta_{k-1,j} + w_{k,j}$, where $w_{k,j}$ is Gaussian process noise. A Gaussian posterior distribution over $\theta_{k,j}$ is maintained. At each step, a prediction of the parameter mean and covariance is made. Upon observing a new residual target $\delta_{k+1}^{(j)}$, the posterior is updated recursively using the new data pair $(z_k, \delta_{k+1}^{(j)})$, yielding updated mean $\mu_{k|k,j}$ and covariance $P_{k|k,j}$. The overall decoder matrix $\Theta_k$ is formed by stacking these posterior mean estimates. This update mechanism has a computational complexity of $O(d\ell^2)$, making it suitable for real-time operation.
2. **Shift Detection via Innovation Consistency:** To handle abrupt dynamics changes, the system monitors the consistency between its predicted and observed residuals. The innovation $e_k = \delta_{k+1} - \hat{\delta}_{k+1|k}$ (difference between observed and predicted residual) is computed. A normalized innovation score $\psi_k = e_k^T S_k^{-1} e_k$ is calculated, representing a Mahalanobis distance. To filter out transient noise, an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) $g_k = \alpha\psi_k + (1-\alpha)g_{k-1}$ is maintained. A sustained increase in $g_k$ indicates a mismatch between the current model and observed dynamics.
3. **Adaptive Covariance Inflation:** To enable rapid adaptation upon detecting a mismatch, the process noise covariance $Q_{k,j}$ is made adaptive. It is defined as $Q_{k,j} = (\frac{1}{\lambda_k} - 1) P_{k-1|k-1,j}$, where $\lambda_k \in (0, 1]$ is an adaptive forgetting factor. This makes the prior covariance $P_{k|k-1,j} = \frac{1}{\lambda_k} P_{k-1|k-1,j}$. The scaling factor $\lambda_k$ is inversely related to the EWMA score $g_{k-1}$, specifically $\lambda_k = \frac{1}{1 + \beta \max(0, g_{k-1} - \eta)}$. If $g_{k-1}$ exceeds a threshold $\eta$, $\lambda_k$ decreases, increasing the prior covariance and thus the Kalman gain, accelerating adaptation. This two-stage process of inflation and Bayesian update allows the estimator to be responsive to abrupt changes while maintaining stability under nominal conditions.

#### 3.5. Model Predictive Control for Tracking

The learned residual model is integrated into a standard nonlinear MPC framework. At each control step, the MPC solves a finite-horizon optimal control problem. For real-time tractability, the current posterior mean of the decoder matrix $\Theta_{k-1}$ and the current latent feature $z_k$ are treated as constant over the prediction horizon. The nominal dynamics $f_{nom}$ are augmented with the estimated residual $\Theta_{k-1}z_k$ to predict future states. The MPC minimizes a cost function (including state tracking errors and control input costs) subject to system dynamics and operational constraints, with the first computed control input applied to the system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the proposed framework involved both open-loop prediction accuracy evaluation and closed-loop trajectory tracking experiments on a real aerial manipulation platform.

#### 4.1. Experimental Scenarios and Setup

Experiments were conducted on an aerial manipulator composed of a Tarot-650 quadrotor frame with a 2R serial-link manipulator. Key components included a CUAV X7+ flight controller, a Jetson Orin Nano Super for onboard computing, and an OptiTrack motion capture system for state feedback. Data were collected from randomized smooth trajectories involving variations in position, velocity, joint angles, and pick-and-drop actions to induce payload changes.
Two trajectory-tracking scenarios were designed to evaluate performance under coupled dynamics and abrupt payload-induced changes (payload drop at t=2 min):
- **Scenario A (Planar switching):** The manipulator tracked a figure-eight trajectory in the xz-plane, released a payload, and immediately transitioned to a figure-eight in the yz-plane.
- **Scenario B (Axis reorientation):** The manipulator tracked a figure-eight trajectory with its major axis along the x-direction, released a payload, and then tracked a figure-eight with its major axis along the y-direction.
Two payload masses (300g and 500g) were used, along with manipulator joint actuation within a range of [−45°, 45°] to induce configuration-dependent coupling. All results were averaged over 10 independent trials.

#### 4.2. Model Validation: Open-Loop Prediction Accuracy

The model's open-loop prediction accuracy was evaluated by computing the residual prediction error (RMSE) in Scenario B, where a 300g payload was dropped at t=2 minutes.

- **Encoder Ablation:** This experiment evaluated different encoder architectures without online decoder adaptation.
 - **DNN (instantaneous state/input):** Achieved the highest RMSE (0.34), indicating its inability to capture history-dependent and coupled effects.
 - **TCN (fixed temporal convolutions):** Improved performance with RMSE of 0.26, demonstrating the benefit of considering trajectory history.
 - **Temporal-only encoder (temporal attention):** Further reduced RMSE to 0.21, suggesting that adaptive temporal attention is more effective than fixed convolutions.
 - **Full structured encoder (temporal + cross-variable attention):** Achieved the lowest RMSE (0.18), indicating that jointly modeling both temporal structure and cross-variable coupling is crucial for accurate residual dynamics.

- **Decoder Ablation:** Using the full structured encoder, this experiment compared various online adaptation strategies for the decoder.
 - **No decoder update:** Showed the largest RMSE and the slowest recovery after the payload drop at t=2.0 min, highlighting the inadequacy of a purely offline model for non-stationary conditions.
 - **Bayesian linear update (no covariance inflation):** Improved performance and reduced steady-state error compared to no update, but exhibited a relatively slow transient adaptation after the shift.
 - **Fixed forgetting (constant $\lambda=0.5$):** Accelerated adaptation speed but resulted in more oscillatory behavior after the shift, illustrating the trade-off of a constant adaptation rate.
 - **Full adaptive decoder update (consistency-driven covariance inflation):** Achieved the lowest RMSE, the smallest post-shift error excursion, and the fastest, smoothest settling behavior. This demonstrated that online adaptation is essential, and consistency-driven covariance inflation is particularly effective for managing abrupt non-stationary changes.
 - **Consistency-driven adaptation visualization:** Analysis of the normalized innovation score ($\psi_k$) and adaptive covariance scaling ($1/\lambda_k$) confirmed this. The payload release at t=2.0 min caused a sharp spike in $\psi_k$, triggering a temporary increase in $1/\lambda_k$, which amplified the adaptation rate. As the model adapted, both $\psi_k$ and the adaptive response decayed, indicating stable and efficient recovery.

#### 4.3. Trajectory Tracking & Disturbance Rejection

Closed-loop trajectory tracking performance, measured by the RMSE of the error signal, was evaluated for scenarios A and B under both in-distribution (300g payload) and out-of-distribution (500g payload) conditions. The performance ranking from worst to best was: Nominal < DNN < Diffusion < Proto-MPC < Active MLP < Ours.
- **Nominal Model:** Performed worst due to its inability to account for unmodeled dynamics like payload-induced changes, coupling effects, and delays.
- **Offline Learning Baselines (DNN, Diffusion):** Showed improvement over the nominal model but were limited by their offline nature, struggling with deployment-time distribution shifts and history-dependent effects without adaptation.
- **Online Adaptive Baselines (Proto-MPC, Active MLP):** Proto-MPC's prototype-based adaptation was restricted to interpolation, performing less effectively with unseen dynamics (e.g., payload release and coupled arm motion). Active MLP's gradient-based adaptation showed better performance but was subject to a speed-stability trade-off.
- **Proposed Method:** Achieved the lowest tracking error across all test conditions (in-distribution and out-of-distribution payloads, both scenarios). This performance is attributed to the combination of:
 - A structured history encoder that captures delayed aerodynamic/actuator effects and cross-variable coupling.
 - Closed-form Bayesian decoder adaptation.
 - Adaptive covariance inflation, which dynamically adjusts the adaptation rate based on observed model inconsistency. This allowed the model to maintain accuracy and rapidly recalibrate after significant dynamic shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for addressing critical challenges in the dynamic modeling of aerial manipulators by integrating structured representation learning with adaptive control principles.

The **significance** of this work lies in its comprehensive approach to handling complex, time-varying, and coupled dynamics. By separating the learning of a nonlinear, history-dependent feature representation (via a fixed encoder) from the online adaptation of a linear mapping to residual dynamics (via a Bayesian decoder), the framework offers both representational power and computational efficiency. The introduction of consistency-driven covariance inflation provides a mechanism for robust and rapid adaptation to abrupt changes, a common characteristic of aerial manipulation tasks.

The **potential impact** of this research is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Performance and Reliability:** The demonstrated improvements in open-loop prediction accuracy and closed-loop trajectory tracking performance suggest that aerial manipulators can operate with greater precision and reliability, even under challenging and changing operating conditions (e.g., varying payloads, reconfiguring manipulator arms, and complex aerodynamic interactions).
- **Real-time Adaptability:** The lightweight nature of the online decoder update, compatible with real-time Model Predictive Control, opens possibilities for more autonomous and robust operation of aerial manipulators in dynamic environments where swift adaptation to unpredicted changes is necessary.
- **Broader Applicability:** The structured learning approach, capable of disentangling complex dynamics into adaptable components, could potentially be extended to other robotic systems facing similar challenges of coupled, history-dependent, and non-stationary dynamics.
- **Foundation for Advanced Control:** By providing a more accurate and adaptive dynamics model, this work lays a foundation for future advancements in areas such as uncertainty-aware control formulations and the tackling of more complex manipulation tasks involving contact-rich interactions, which typically demand high fidelity in dynamics prediction.

In conclusion, this research provides a validated method for learning and adapting residual dynamics in aerial manipulators, contributing to the development of more capable and resilient autonomous aerial systems.
