# Training-Time Action Conditioning for Efficient Real-Time Chunking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Physical Intelligence optimized Real-Time Chunking for Vision-Language-Action models by introducing training-time action conditioning, moving computationally intensive prefix conditioning from inference to training. This approach maintained task performance and execution speed, notably reducing latency from 135ms to 108ms and improving robustness at higher inference delays compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models incur high inference latency, hindering real-time, reactive control necessary for robots in dynamic environments.
- Current Real-Time Chunking (RTC) frameworks rely on inference-time inpainting for inter-chunk continuity, which introduces significant computational overhead via vector-Jacobian products during robot operation.
- This inference-time overhead limits the effectiveness of RTC, especially under higher inference delays, counteracting its purpose of enabling efficient real-time execution.

## Method

- The authors proposed 'training-time action conditioning,' where the VLA model learns to generate action postfixes directly conditioned on known action prefixes by simulating inference delays during training.
- Modifications to a standard diffusion transformer architecture included allowing per-token flow matching timesteps, feeding ground-truth non-noisy prefix actions, and computing loss solely on the postfix actions.
- Inference delay was randomly sampled during training to enhance robustness to variable real-world latencies, designing the solution as a 'drop-in replacement' that requires no changes to existing VLA models or robot runtimes.

## Results

- In simulated environments, training-time RTC outperformed inference-time RTC at inference delays of 2 timesteps or higher, with performance differences widening significantly at greater delays.
- Real-world experiments on complex tasks like box building and espresso making showed training-time RTC matched the task success rates and execution speeds of inference-time RTC using the π₀.₆ VLA.
- The method eliminated inference-time computational overhead, reducing average end-to-end latency from 135ms (inference-time RTC) to 108ms (training-time RTC) on a remote H100 server.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The computational burden of conditioning on previously committed actions can be effectively shifted from inference time to training time, leading to significant efficiency gains during robot operation.
> - Directly teaching the model to handle action prefixes during training results in greater robustness to increased inference delays compared to inference-time methods that rely on linearization.
> - Substantial improvements in real-time robot control efficiency can be achieved through minimal architectural modifications and fine-tuning of existing VLA models, enabling broader practical deployment.
