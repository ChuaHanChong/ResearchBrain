# See, Think, Act: Online Shopper Behavior Simulation with VLM Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> A team including researchers from Amazon and academic institutions introduced a VLM-driven framework to simulate online shopper behavior, integrating visual webpage screenshots alongside text to create more cognitively aligned and realistic action sequences. The multimodal approach achieved 44.57% exact match accuracy, a 6% improvement over text-only methods, demonstrating enhanced fidelity in capturing human decisions influenced by visual cues.

## Problem

- Existing human behavior simulation methods for web interactions rely solely on text-based inputs (HTML, action histories), neglecting the visual context.
- Real human users heavily depend on visual perception for decision-making on image-rich webpages, considering aspects like product layouts and button salience.
- This reliance on text-only input limits the fidelity and cognitive alignment of simulated behavior, failing to capture the full nuances of human interaction.

## Method

- Developed a ==GUI-aware simulation framework== for online shopper behavior that integrates current webpage screenshots alongside simplified HTML and past actions as input to a VLM agent.
- The ==OPeRA dataset== was post-processed and augmented with LLM-synthesized rationales to provide suitable training data for multimodal learning.
- Employed a two-stage training scheme involving ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a ==hierarchical, difficulty-aware reward system== to refine action and rationale generation.

## Results

- The multimodal VLM agent trained with SFT+RL achieved **44.57%** exact match accuracy, outperforming text-only methods by over **6%** (**38.44%**) and image-only methods by nearly **20%** (**24.71%**).
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) significantly improved exact match accuracy and helped calibrate action distributions, increasing the proportion of 'scroll' actions to a more realistic **41.04%** (Text + Image) and **55.00%** (Text-only).
- Fine-tuning via SFT and RL drastically reduced structured output formatting errors to under **0.2%**, while zero-shot Claude-3.5-Sonnet exhibited very low exact match accuracy due to a strong bias towards 'click' actions (over **96%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating visual input is essential for high-fidelity human behavior simulation in visually rich web environments, validating that human users "see" before "acting."
> - Combining Supervised Fine-Tuning with Reinforcement Learning, particularly with a hierarchical and difficulty-aware reward structure, significantly enhances both semantic understanding and sequence-level action precision.
> - Even advanced generalist LLMs exhibit prediction biases and underutilize cross-modal signals without explicit, task-aware fine-tuning for structured interaction tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work "See, Think, Act: Online Shopper Behavior Simulation with VLM Agents" is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of using Large Language Models (LLMs) to simulate human behavior in web-based environments. This domain holds immense promise for applications ranging from digital assistant training and GUI design optimization to large-scale user behavior forecasting [1–12].

Prior research, including significant contributions from some of the current authors, has successfully demonstrated LLMs' potential in simulating human actions on digital platforms, particularly in e-commerce scenarios [13–17]. These earlier approaches largely focused on refining action prediction and reasoning capabilities by using supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on action traces, often augmented with LLM-generated rationales, and by employing reinforcement learning (RL) to align model-generated reasoning with realistic user trajectories. However, a "fundamental limitation" of these methods, as highlighted by the authors, is their exclusive reliance on text-based inputs, such such as HTML content and action histories.

This paper directly addresses this crucial gap. It argues that real human users heavily depend on visual perception when interacting with modern, image-rich webpages, considering aspects like product layouts, button salience, and the visual composition of search results [24–30]. Ignoring this visual modality, the authors contend, hinders the models' ability to faithfully capture the full spectrum of user behavior.

By integrating visual information (webpage screenshots) into the simulation pipeline via Vision-Language Models (VLMs), this research marks a significant step towards creating more "cognitively aligned simulations" that mirror human decision-making. Unlike many existing VLM works that focus on task completion and optimizing for correctness or utility [46–55], this paper leverages VLMs to "enrich the cognitive fidelity of simulated user behavior." The goal is not merely to execute actions more effectively but to generate *more realistic human action sequences* by modeling how visual stimuli shape user decisions in real-world web environments, particularly in online shopping contexts where visual cues are paramount. This work, therefore, represents a critical evolution from text-only LLM agents to multimodal VLM agents in web interaction simulation, pushing the boundaries of realism and applicability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the existing gap between current text-only human behavior simulation methods and the inherently multimodal nature of human decision-making processes in visually rich online environments. Specifically, the authors aim to investigate and demonstrate the benefits of integrating visual information, in the form of webpage screenshots, into the behavior simulation pipeline using Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

The core motivations driving this research can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhancing Simulation Fidelity:** Current LLM-based simulators, despite their advancements, lack visual grounding. Real human shoppers heavily rely on visual perception—such as understanding product layouts, identifying salient buttons, or interpreting the visual hierarchy of search results—when navigating and making decisions online. The absence of this visual input in text-only models leads to a less faithful and less cognitively aligned simulation of human behavior.
- **Capturing Nuances of Human Interaction:** Visual cues convey critical information that text-based HTML alone cannot fully capture. For instance, the aesthetic appeal of a product image, the prominence of a "Buy Now" button, or the clutter of a webpage layout all influence human attention and decision-making. Integrating visual modality is motivated by the desire to capture these subtle but significant nuances.
- **Narrowing the Gap between Synthetic Agents and Real Users:** By enabling agents to "see, think, and act" like humans, the researchers seek to create more realistic synthetic agents. Such agents would be better equipped to mimic authentic human interaction patterns, thereby improving their utility in various applications.
- **Building upon Existing Frameworks:** The research is motivated by the potential to enhance state-of-the-art LLM-based simulation frameworks (like those employing SFT and RL) by incorporating visual perception, thereby pushing the performance boundaries for online shopper behavior simulation.

In essence, the paper is driven by the conviction that for human behavior simulation in web GUI interactions to be truly effective and realistic, it must account for the visual dimension of the user experience.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper focuses on developing a GUI-aware simulation framework for online shopper behavior, leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The approach involves three main components: problem formulation, dataset construction, and sophisticated training schemes.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is formalized as predicting the user’s next-step rationale ($r_t$) and action ($a_t$) in a sequential interaction trajectory. At each time step $t$, the VLM agent observes a comprehensive contextual input:
1. **Past HTML observations ($c_{1...t}$):** A simplified HTML representation, filtering out irrelevant structures (scripts, styling metadata) to retain essential layout and content elements.
2. **Past user actions ($a_{1...t-1}$):** The sequence of actions performed in previous steps.
3. **Current visual observation ($v_t$):** A screenshot of the current webpage, providing GUI-level perception.
The model is trained to output the rationale and action in a structured JSON format for ease of parsing and evaluation.

**Dataset Construction:**
The researchers post-process the publicly available **OPeRA dataset**, which consists of 692 shopping sessions from 51 users, totaling 28,904 real-world ⟨action, observation⟩ pairs, to make it suitable for VLM-based simulation.
- **Action Space Simplification:** The diverse real-world actions are distilled into three primary categories: `input`, `click`, and `scroll`. Consecutive `scroll` actions are merged into a single action, and scroll rationales are generalized due to the dataset's limitation in capturing specific scroll directions.
- **HTML Pruning for Coherence:** To ensure consistency between textual HTML and visual screenshots, the HTML structure is pruned to include only elements visible within the current visual viewport. This reduces noise and aligns the two modalities.
- **Rationale Augmentation:** As the original OPeRA dataset has limited user-written rationales, **Claude-3.5-Sonnet via Amazon Bedrock** is used to synthesize plausible rationale sentences ($r_t$) for each action step. This ensures every interaction has an interpretable reasoning trace for training.

**Training Schemes:**
Two complementary training paradigms are adopted, building upon recent state-of-the-art LLM-based methods:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - The model `f` is trained to jointly generate rationales and corresponding actions.
 - It maximizes the likelihood of the augmented rationale-action trajectories.
 - The input query `q_t` includes the contextual HTML, past actions and rationales, and the current screen observation.
 - This phase establishes a strong foundation by teaching the model explicit reasoning and decision-making patterns based on annotated data.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - This scheme refines the SFT-trained policy using a tailored reward system, adapting from Shop-R1.
 - **Hierarchical Reward Design:**
 - **`R_format`:** A binary reward for adherence to the strict JSON output format, crucial for machine parsability.
 - **`s(r_t | q_t)`:** A self-certainty score for rationale generation, computed as the KL divergence between the model's token-level predictive distribution and a uniform distribution. Higher scores indicate more confident reasoning.
 - **`R_type`:** A base reward for correctly identifying the high-level action type (e.g., 'click', 'input', 'scroll').
 - **`R_subaction`:** Additional rewards for accurately predicting fine-grained subaction components (e.g., specific clickable element names or input text).
 - **Difficulty-Aware Reward Scaling (DARS):** The `R_subaction` reward is amplified by a DARS factor (default 10,000) for complex actions (e.g., those requiring precise element identification or long text input). This encourages the model to tackle harder but more impactful actions.
 - **Total Reward:** The overall reward signal is a sum of these components: `R_total = R_format + s(r_t | q_t) + R_type + DARS × R_subaction`.
 - **Policy Optimization:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used, with training on NVIDIA A100 GPUs using FSDP.

**Model and Evaluation:**
The **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model** serves as the backbone, chosen for its practical experimentation capabilities and multimodal reasoning capacity. Baselines include Zero-shot Prompting, SFT, and SFT+RL (text-only and image-only variants), as well as Zero-shot Claude-3.5-Sonnet. Evaluation metrics include exact match accuracy (requiring perfect alignment of all components), coarse-grained action type accuracy, and F1 scores.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical studies presented in the paper yield several significant findings regarding the effectiveness of multimodal grounding and advanced training schemes in simulating online shopper behavior.

1. **Superiority of Multimodal Grounding:**
 - The most prominent finding is that incorporating both textual and visual inputs (`Text + Image`) consistently and substantially enhances performance for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model, particularly when fine-tuned.
 - Specifically, the `Text + Image` input format under the `SFT + RL` configuration achieves an exact match accuracy of **44.57%**. This represents a notable improvement of **more than 6%** over the `Text-only` input (38.44%) and a substantial lead over `Image-only` input (24.71%) under the same training regime.
 - While visual cues do not provide significant gains for coarse-grained `Action Type` prediction, they lead to clear improvements for **fine-grained subaction prediction**, demonstrating the model's enhanced ability to perform precise grounding and reasoning in visually complex environments.

2. **Impact of Training Schemes (SFT and RL):**
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** provides a significant performance boost across all input formats compared to zero-shot prompting. It effectively distills modality-specific reasoning, enabling both textual and visual inputs to capture task-relevant semantics (e.g., action type F1 scores rise to ~53% for text-only and image-only after SFT).
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, building upon SFT, further refines the model's performance, especially in **exact match accuracy**, which measures sequence-level consistency. For instance, the exact match accuracy for `Text + Image` jumps from 24.16% (SFT) to 44.57% (SFT + RL). This indicates that RL fine-tuning enhances the model's decision precision and helps reduce over-reliance on a single modality. RL consistently pushes action type F1 scores above 57% across modalities, suggesting its primary role in sequence-level alignment without sacrificing semantic understanding.

3. **Comparison with Claude-3.5-Sonnet:**
 - Claude-3.5-Sonnet, a powerful closed-source model, shows similar performance across different zero-shot input modalities, characterized by **extremely low exact match accuracy** but disproportionately high action type accuracy.
 - This discrepancy is attributed to a strong **prediction bias toward the 'click' action** (over 96% of predictions), with the model often defaulting to 'click' regardless of context. It rarely predicts 'scroll' or 'input' actions.
 - This finding suggests that even highly capable generalist models like Claude, while producing well-formed outputs, underutilize cross-modal signals in structured interaction tasks and lack behavioral realism without explicit, task-aware fine-tuning.

4. **Prediction Distribution Analysis (Action Types):**
 - **Zero-shot settings** exhibit a substantial failure rate in producing outputs that conform to the required structured JSON format (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL Text + Image zero-shot has 64.74% incorrect format outputs).
 - **SFT and SFT + RL** dramatically reduce these formatting errors to negligible levels (under 0.2%), highlighting the critical role of task-specific alignment for structured output generation.
 - **Action type bias** changes significantly with training. Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct initially shows a strong preference for 'click' actions after SFT (over 84% for Text + Image, 88% for Text-only). However, RL fine-tuning adjusts this distribution towards a more realistic balance by increasing the proportion of `scroll` actions (reaching 41.04% for Text + Image and 55.00% for Text-only under SFT + RL). This shift indicates that RL helps calibrate action distributions to better match observed user interaction patterns.

In summary, the results strongly validate the hypothesis that multimodal grounding, combined with supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, is essential for achieving high-fidelity and realistic human behavior simulation in visually rich web environments like online shopping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of human behavior simulation, particularly for interactive web environments. By successfully integrating visual perception into VLM agents, the authors have enhanced the realism and fidelity of synthetic shopper behavior, moving beyond the limitations of text-only approaches.

**Significance of Findings:**

- **Bridging the Modality Gap:** The most critical contribution is the empirical demonstration that visual grounding is indispensable for accurate and cognitively aligned simulation of human behavior in visually complex web GUIs. The >6% exact match accuracy gain from multimodal input over text-only inputs validates the intuitive notion that humans "see" and "think" before "acting" online.
- **Refining Simulation Methodologies:** The work highlights the power of combining supervised fine-tuning (for distilling task-specific semantics) with reinforcement learning (for enhancing sequence-level precision and behavioral realism). The hierarchical, difficulty-aware reward structure in RL is shown to be crucial for calibrating action distributions and improving decision accuracy for complex actions.
- **Exposing Limitations of Generalist Models:** The comparison with Claude-3.5-Sonnet underscores that even powerful, general-purpose LLMs require domain-specific fine-tuning to overcome inherent biases and achieve truly realistic behavioral simulation in structured interaction tasks.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**

The development of such realistic online shopper behavior simulators unlocks a broad spectrum of impactful applications across various domains:

1. **Customer Behavior Simulation for UX Testing:** These simulators can serve as scalable and adaptive tools for automated user experience (UX) evaluation. Unlike traditional scripted bots or generic LLM agents, VLM-driven agents can capture the diversity and realism of human interaction patterns, enabling robust stress testing of new website features, layout designs, and checkout flows under varied behavioral scenarios. This can drastically reduce the cost and time associated with manual usability testing.
2. **Personalized Recommender System Evaluation:** High-fidelity, synthetic interaction traces generated by these agents can act as reliable proxies for measuring how different user personas engage with recommendation algorithms. This facilitates the benchmarking of personalization quality across heterogeneous contexts, reducing reliance on costly and time-consuming A/B testing in live environments.
3. **Training of Digital Shopping Assistants:** Simulators that incorporate both reasoning and action generation, grounded in multi-modal cues (HTML context and visual observations), can directly benefit the training of intelligent digital shopping assistants. These assistants can be pre-trained or fine-tuned to exhibit more intuitive, adaptive, and human-aligned shopping behaviors, leading to more effective and user-friendly automated support.
4. **Vision-Language Evaluation of Product Pages:** Integrating VLMs into simulation pipelines enables automated assessment of how effectively product detail pages convey key attributes (e.g., discounts, usability, product variants) through their visual and textual cues. This provides actionable insights for optimizing visual merchandising, page design, and content strategy, leading to improved conversion rates and customer satisfaction.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as identified by the authors):**

The paper also candidly discusses several limitations and proposes future research directions to further enhance behavior simulation:

- **Simulation Prediction Format:** Current models predict exact DOM element names, which doesn't align with human cognition (relying on spatial location and saliency). Future work should explore predicting coarse-grained **spatial regions of interest** (like eye-tracking data) and move towards entirely **HTML-free visual-only simulation**, mirroring human perception.
- **Multi-modal Context Fusion:** Simple concatenation of modalities is sub-optimal. Future research should investigate structured pipelines for visual region detection, semantic classification of interface components, and modular encoding strategies to better integrate visual and textual semantics.
- **Context Compression:** Long interaction histories create memory and compute overhead. Developing techniques like hierarchical memory architectures, learned summarizers, or retrieval-augmented models is crucial for efficient long-context processing in real-world deployments.
- **Personalized Human Behavior Simulation:** Current datasets aggregate behaviors across many users, missing individual idiosyncrasies. The need for large-scale, **longitudinal, user-specific datasets** is emphasized to enable personalized modeling, continual learning, preference drift adaptation, and long-term user-agent co-adaptation.

In conclusion, this work represents a significant stride towards building more faithful and robust human behavior simulators. By leveraging VLMs, the research moves beyond mimicking actions to capturing the nuanced reasoning patterns that drive real-world user interactions, paving the way for scalable evaluation of interactive systems and the development of truly adaptive, user-centric AI.
