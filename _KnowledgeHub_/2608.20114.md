# DECOWAM: Decoupled Whole-Body World-Action Model for Legged Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DECOWAM, whole-body world-action model for legged mobile manipulation, freezes FastWAM backbone and adds trainable residual adapters, privileged-distilled future bottleneck, adversarially separated base/arm latents, and base-velocity ego-motion conditioning. Cuts action MSE 21.7% vs FastWAM with only 25.95M trainable params, best whole-body coordination across 79 closed-loop trials.

## Problem

- Fixed-base VLA/WAM systems assume static camera geometry, never modeling ego-motion from a moving legged base mixed with scene and arm dynamics.
- Prior action heads concatenate arm joints and base velocity into one uniform-rate chunk, ignoring their different control time scales (15-30Hz vs 3-5Hz).
- No existing robot dataset synchronizes moving-camera video, whole-body base/arm state-action, and language for a legged manipulator, blocking joint world-action training.

## Method

- ==Staged parameter-efficient adaptation==: Stage 1 fully fine-tunes ==FastWAM== on ARMDOG, Stage 2 freezes it and trains only ==residual adapters== plus three structural modules (25.95M params).
- ==Action-equivalent future bottleneck== distills a privileged teacher (seeing future VAE latents) into a causal student via reconstruction, distillation, and geometry-preservation losses.
- ==Base-arm factorization== splits action context into 16-D base/arm latents with ==gradient-reversal== cross-heads, and base-velocity tokens condition the video expert on ego-motion explicitly.

## Results

- On the 23-episode ARMDOG replay slice, DECOWAM cut frame MSE **15.03%** and action MSE **21.71%** versus its FastWAM(50k) initialization, with PSNR up **0.222dB**.
- Ablating the future bottleneck or base-velocity conditioning degraded every metric; full model beat adapter-only control by **4.6%** frame MSE and **15.7%** action MSE.
- Across **79** real-robot trials, DECOWAM reached **58.2%** task success (vs FastWAM 57.0%, X-WAM 15.2%) and led whole-body coordination (**44.3%**) and displacement robustness (**30.4%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit factorization of ego-motion, base control, and arm control as separate conditioning interfaces generalizes beyond this backbone to any moving-camera embodied model.
> - Freezing a pretrained world-action prior and adapting only small residual/factorization modules can match or beat full fine-tuning, showing embodiment specialization needn't touch the base model.
> - Video-prediction quality and closed-loop whole-body robustness move together: an explicit ego-motion signal improves both branches rather than trading one off against the other.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in embodied Artificial Intelligence, specifically in the domain of mobile manipulation for legged robots. Existing vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models, such as Aloha, RT-1/2, OpenVLA, PaLM-E, SayCan, π0, RDT-2, and Motus, have demonstrated capabilities on stationary bimanual platforms. These platforms typically feature fixed camera geometries and action spaces primarily restricted to arm trajectories.

Legged mobile manipulators introduce several complexities not adequately addressed by fixed-base models:
- **Dynamic Viewpoint**: The onboard camera moves with the robot's base, causing image streams to contain a mixture of ego-motion and scene motion. Differentiating these components is crucial for accurate perception and control.
- **Multi-rate Action Coupling**: Arm joint trajectories often require high-rate control (e.g., 15–30 Hz), while base velocity commands are typically issued at a lower rate (e.g., 3–5 Hz). Combining these into a single action representation poses a challenge for uniform sampling and modeling.
- **Hierarchical Intent**: Real-world tasks for mobile manipulators often involve interleaving navigation (where to go) with manipulation (how to act), which a monolithic latent representation may struggle to capture simultaneously.

Prior research on legged locomotion has primarily focused on reinforcement learning for base velocity commands without engaging manipulation. Wheeled and home mobile-manipulation systems have produced cascaded policies that separate navigation and manipulation, but few works learn a unified policy, and even fewer ground such a policy in a predictive video model. The paper notes an absence of prior work performing unified whole-body world-action modeling on a legged base with an attached manipulator.

Furthermore, existing robot datasets, including Open-X Embodiment, DROID, BridgeData V2, RoboNet, RLBench, CALVIN, Language Table, ManiSkill2, LIBERO, and Aloha-derived collections, provide coverage for fixed-base or wheeled manipulation. However, they generally lack the synchronized data streams required for this problem: legged-base ego-motion, manipulator actions, visual observations, and language instructions, all within a single training unit. The introduction of the ARMDOG dataset by this work aims to fill this specific data gap.

DECOWAM positions itself by developing an embodiment-aware factorization approach to adapt a pre-trained world-action backbone for legged mobile manipulation, explicitly addressing the challenges of dynamic viewpoints and multi-factor actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a whole-body world-action model that can effectively operate a legged mobile manipulator by predicting future observations and generating control actions, while explicitly distinguishing and managing the distinct factors of robot motion.

The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties in extending existing world-action models, largely designed for static or wheeled platforms, to legged mobile manipulation. These difficulties are:
1. **Complexity of Camera Ego-Motion**: The camera, mounted on a moving base, experiences ego-motion that intertwines with actual scene dynamics and arm movements. Current models often do not explicitly separate these components, which can lead to ambiguity in visual prediction and understanding.
2. **Multimodal and Multi-rate Action Space**: The robot's actions involve diverse modalities (arm joints, gripper state, base velocity) with different semantic meanings and control frequencies. A single, undifferentiated action representation may struggle to capture this complexity and coordinate movements effectively.
3. **Limitations of Monolithic Representations**: A single latent representation attempting to encode both navigation-scale decisions and manipulation-scale corrections for a mobile manipulator can be insufficient for hierarchical task execution.

To address these issues, the authors propose a decoupled modeling paradigm. This paradigm aims to represent the base's movement, the arm's actions, and the camera's ego-motion as distinct and explicit factors within the world-action model.

Specific objectives include:
- Formulating legged mobile manipulation as a decoupled world-action modeling problem where base control, arm manipulation, and camera ego-motion are treated via semantically aligned interfaces.
- Implementing this formulation through parameter-efficient adaptation of a pre-trained world-action backbone (FastWAM), employing mechanisms such as frozen residual adaptation, causal distillation from privileged future latents, adversarial base/arm factorization, and base-velocity-conditioned video prediction.
- Creating and releasing a novel, synchronized real-robot dataset, ARMDOG, specifically designed for legged mobile manipulation, to enable the training and evaluation of such embodiment-complete models.
- Evaluating the DECOWAM model through controlled open-loop replay experiments and closed-loop real-robot deployments to demonstrate improved prediction capabilities, whole-body coordination, and robustness to perturbations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DECOWAM model builds upon the FastWAM architecture, a pre-trained world-action backbone that couples a Wan-2.2 video diffusion backbone with an ActionDiT branch for chunked action prediction. FastWAM consumes the current RGB frame, language context, and a 14-dimensional proprioceptive state, then jointly predicts future RGB frames and a 48-step whole-body action chunk. DECOWAM enhances this by introducing four mechanisms that decouple adaptation in parameter space, future-information usage, action factors, and ego-motion conditioning.

**Problem Formulation:**
The model learns a conditional joint probability distribution $p_{\theta}(x_{1:T}, a_{1:K} | x_0, s_0, \ell)$, where:
- $x_{1:T}$ represents a future video clip (T=8 frames).
- $a_{1:K}$ is an action chunk (K=48 steps), with each action $a_k \in \mathbb{R}^{14}$ semantically decomposed into arm joints (6 DoF), gripper state (1 DoF), base velocity (3 DoF for $v_x, v_y, \omega_z$), and loader-compatible padding (4 DoF).
- $x_0$ is the current RGB observation, $s_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{14}$ is the current whole-body state, and $\ell$ is a natural-language instruction.
During training, future observations supervise the model, but deployment remains causal, relying only on current inputs.

**Staged Parameter-Efficient Adaptation:**
Training proceeds in two stages to optimize for efficiency:
1. **Stage 1 (Domain Alignment)**: All FastWAM parameters ($\Theta$) are fine-tuned on the ARMDOG dataset for 50,000 steps. This initial stage aligns the pre-trained video prior, action expert, and proprioceptive interface to the specific characteristics of moving-camera observations and the quadruped-arm action space. The objective minimizes a sum of video and action losses.
2. **Stage 2 (Structural Adaptation)**: After Stage 1, the parameters $\Theta^{(1)}$ (from the 50k step checkpoint) are frozen. Only a new, smaller set of parameters $\Phi = \{\phi_{adp}, \phi_q, \phi_{ba}, \phi_{ego}\}$ are optimized. This reduces the trainable parameter count from 6020.75 million to 25.95 million.
 - **Residual Adapters ($\phi_{adp}$)**: These are small modules inserted after each block of the frozen WAN backbone. They learn robot-specific corrections by adding a residual branch $h'_l = h_l + \alpha_l W^{(l)}_{up} \sigma(W^{(l)}_{down} LN(h_l))$ (where $W^{(l)}_{down}$ projects to a 128-D bottleneck and $W^{(l)}_{up}$ restores the dimension), thus preserving the pre-trained video prior while introducing specific adaptations.

**Decoupled Conditional Interfaces:**
DECOWAM introduces three decoupled conditional interfaces:
1. **Action-Equivalent Future Bottleneck ($\phi_q$)**: This mechanism distills privileged information about future action-equivalent outcomes from a teacher into a causal student.
 - A teacher embedding $z_t$ observes summaries of current $e_0 = \psi_{vae}(x_0)$ and future $e_{1:T} = \psi_{vae}(x_{1:T})$ visual latents, along with $s_0$.
 - A student embedding $z_s$ observes only $e_0$ and $s_0$.
 - Only the student $z_s$ causally conditions the action expert.
 - The bottleneck is trained with an objective $L_q$ that combines action reconstruction, teacher-student distillation ($||z_s - \text{sg}(z_t)||_2^2$ where sg stops gradients), and geometry preservation. The geometry term encourages trajectories with similar normalized actions to remain close in the privileged latent space.
2. **Base–Arm Factorization ($\phi_{ba}$)**: The action context is explicitly separated into navigation-scale and manipulation-scale information.
 - A pooled representation of the action context is mapped into two 16-D factors: $z_{base} = b_{\phi}(u_a)$ and $z_{arm} = m_{\phi}(u_a)$.
 - These concatenated factors condition the action expert.
 - A disentanglement loss $L_{disent}$ is used: direct prediction heads reconstruct their assigned factor (e.g., $g_b(z_{base})$ predicts base actions), while gradient-reversal cross-heads ($GRL$) are used to suppress information about the opposite factor (e.g., $\tilde{g}_b(GRL(z_{arm}))$ attempts to predict base actions from the arm latent, with gradient reversal enforcing disentanglement).
3. **Ego-motion-Aware Video Conditioning ($\phi_{ego}$)**: To explicitly account for camera motion due to the robot's base, the normalized current base velocity $v_0 = \Pi_{base}(s_0)$ (comprising $v_x, v_y, \omega_z$) is used as a condition for the video expert.
 - Each video token receives the base velocity projected into the WAN hidden dimension: $\tilde{h}_{v_i} = h_{v_i} + \beta B_v v_0$.
 - This provides an explicit explanatory variable for camera-frame motion, without imposing geometric warping.

**Training Objective and Deployment:**
Both the action and video experts utilize conditional flow matching for their respective prediction tasks. The complete Stage 2 objective ($L$) combines the flow matching losses for video ($L_{video}$) and action ($L_{action}$), with the additional losses for the future bottleneck ($L_q$) and base-arm disentanglement ($L_{disent}$).
For inference, the teacher model for the future bottleneck and all auxiliary prediction heads are removed. The model computes $z_s$, $(z_{base}, z_{arm})$, and $v_0$ from current inputs and then samples both flows using only $(x_0, s_0, \ell)$.

**The ARMDOG Dataset:**
ARMDOG is introduced as a real-robot dataset tailored for legged mobile manipulation. It synchronizes 15 Hz RGB-D video, proprioception, IMU, base state, whole-body commands, and language instructions from a wheeled quadruped robot with a 6-DoF arm and 1-DoF gripper. Each episode is converted into a FastWAM-compatible format: RGB video, a structured $T \times 14$ state/action tensor, natural-language instruction text, and a precomputed language embedding.
- The 14-dimensional state/action tensor explicitly contains arm targets ([0:6]), gripper opening, base velocity ($v_x, v_y, \omega_z$ at [7:10]), and loader padding ([10:14]).
- The quality-filtered corpus comprises 1,487 episodes, 343,550 RGB frames (approximately 321.3 minutes) at 15 Hz.
- Task distribution includes Bottle Pick&Place (56%), Place Block (39%), Object Pick&Place (4%), and Climb Slope (1%).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both open-loop replay settings and closed-loop real-robot deployments.

**Open-Loop Replay Protocol:**
Evaluations were performed on a fixed 23-episode slice of the ARMDOG dataset. Metrics included Frame Mean Squared Error (F-MSE), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) for video quality, and Mean Squared Error (A-MSE), Mean Absolute Error (A-MAE), and mean Euclidean error (A-L2) for action prediction.

1. **Comparison to FastWAM Initialization (Table I):**
 - DECOWAM reduced frame MSE by 15.03% and action MSE by 21.71% relative to its 50k-step FastWAM initialization, which was identified as the strongest Stage-1 checkpoint. PSNR increased by 0.222 dB. This indicates that the Stage 2 adaptation improves both future video and action prediction.

2. **Internal Module Ablation (Table II):**
 - Ablations showed that removing any of the decoupled modules (action-equivalent future bottleneck, base-velocity conditioning, or base/arm factorization implied by "w/o decoupled") led to degradation across all reported metrics.
 - Compared to an adapter-only control, the full DECOWAM model achieved a 4.6% reduction in frame MSE, a 15.7% reduction in action MSE, and a 16.1% reduction in action MAE. This suggests that the future bottleneck and explicit ego-motion signal contribute to the shared video-action model's performance beyond just residual adaptation.

3. **Comparison with VLA and WAM References (Tables III & IV, Figure 4):**
 - **VLA Comparison (Action-only)**: While dedicated action-only VLA models like X-VLA achieved the lowest action errors, DECOWAM was competitive, ranking second in A-MSE and A-L2, and within 11.1% of $\pi^{0.5}$'s A-MAE. Crucially, DECOWAM simultaneously predicted an 8-frame future RGB video, providing future awareness that VLA references did not.
 - **WAM Comparison (Joint video/action prediction)**: DECOWAM ranked first across all reported video and action metrics among the compared World-Action Models (FastWAM, Motus, Cosmos, X-WAM). It reduced FastWAM frame/action MSE by 15.03%/21.71%. Qualitatively (Figure 4), DECOWAM demonstrated better preservation of workspace geometry and object layout in future video predictions compared to other WAMs, which often exhibited blur or viewpoint drift.

**Real-Robot Deployment Experiments (79 trials per method):**
Closed-loop evaluations on the physical quadruped-arm platform assessed task progress and whole-body robustness.

1. **Task Progress and Completion Efficiency (Table V(a)):**
 - DECOWAM completed 58.2% of tasks, with a mean completion time of 49 seconds. This was comparable in success rate to the strongest baseline (FastWAM at 57.0%) but significantly faster (16 seconds less).
 - DECOWAM demonstrated the highest cumulative approach and transport completion rates, indicating strengths in mobile-base navigation and manipulation while transporting objects.

2. **Whole-Body Robustness (Table V(b), Figure 5):**
 - DECOWAM tied for the highest base docking success rate (BD-SR: 87.3%) and achieved the highest rates in whole-body coordination (WBCM-SR: 44.3%), base displacement robustness (BDP-SR: 30.4%), and autonomous recovery (AR-SR: 32.9%).
 - Compared to FastWAM, DECOWAM improved WBCM-SR by 10.1 percentage points and BDP-SR by 17.7 percentage points.
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 5) showed DECOWAM maintaining base pose and arm alignment for interaction, where X-VLA and FastWAM exhibited unintended base motion. This corroborates the improved whole-body coordination.

**Parameter-Efficient Adaptation and Deployment (Table VI):**
- DECOWAM reduced the number of trainable parameters during Stage 2 by approximately 232-fold (from 6020.75M for full FastWAM fine-tuning to 25.95M for DECOWAM).
- Despite this reduction in trainable parameters, DECOWAM improved both frame and action MSE.
- The increased evaluator latency was 11.4% (from 1196.6ms to 1333.2ms).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DECOWAM as a whole-body world-action model designed for legged mobile manipulation, addressing several fundamental challenges in embodied AI.

The primary significance lies in its **decoupled modeling paradigm**. By explicitly separating base motion, arm manipulation, and camera ego-motion, DECOWAM offers a structured approach to managing the complex interplay of perception and action in dynamic robotic systems. This factorization directly confronts the limitations of monolithic models on platforms with moving viewpoints and multi-rate action spaces.

The **parameter-efficient adaptation** strategy, which freezes a pre-trained backbone and trains only a small set of residual adapters and specialized modules, represents a practical advancement. This approach allows for substantial performance improvements in both video prediction and action control while significantly reducing the computational cost and data requirements for robot-specific specialization. This has implications for deploying large foundation models on diverse robotic hardware without extensive retraining.

DECOWAM's **empirical performance** demonstrates its impact. In open-loop replay, it improves predictive accuracy for both future video frames and whole-body actions over its FastWAM initialization and outperforms other world-action models. In closed-loop real-robot experiments, DECOWAM exhibits enhanced whole-body coordination, greater robustness to base displacement, and improved recovery capabilities, leading to competitive task completion rates and faster execution. These results suggest that the model's explicit representation of embodiment factors contributes to more coherent and reliable robot behavior in dynamic environments.

The introduction of the **ARMDOG dataset** is a direct contribution to the research community. By providing synchronized, embodiment-complete data for legged mobile manipulation, it fills a critical gap in existing robotic datasets, enabling further research and benchmarking of models addressing this specific problem domain.

The work contributes to the development of **future-aware robotic control**. By simultaneously predicting future visual observations alongside action chunks, DECOWAM explicitly forecasts scene evolution, object motion, and base-induced viewpoint changes. This capability can provide the robot with a richer understanding of the consequences of its actions, potentially leading to more proactive and robust decision-making compared to action-only policies.

In summary, DECOWAM offers a method for integrating complex whole-body control with visual prediction for legged mobile manipulators. Its decoupled architecture, parameter-efficient training, and demonstrated performance on a physical robot contribute to the advancement of mobile manipulation capabilities and the broader field of embodied AI. The approach could inform the design of future robotic systems that require nuanced control under dynamic perceptual conditions.
