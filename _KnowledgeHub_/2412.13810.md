# CAD-Assistant: Tool-Augmented VLLMs as Generic CAD Task Solvers

> [!summary] Summary
> CAD-Assistant presents a tool-augmented Vision and Large Language Model (VLLM) framework that integrates GPT-4o with specialized CAD tools and the FreeCAD software to enable generic CAD task solving. This framework demonstrates robust performance across diverse multimodal inputs, achieving superior results compared to baselines in tasks like autoconstraining and hand-drawn sketch parameterization.

## Problem

- Existing CAD automation solutions are primarily task-specific, focusing on fixed workflows and lacking the generality to support the entire iterative design process.
- Vision and Large Language Models (VLLMs) inherently struggle with precise geometric reasoning, mathematical accuracy, and understanding the complex effects of CAD commands.
- There is a need for adaptable CAD agents capable of processing diverse multimodal inputs and interacting dynamically with evolving design states to provide comprehensive AI assistance.

## Method

- A ==tool-augmented VLLM framework==, CAD-Assistant, was developed using GPT-4o as a high-level planner to generate natural language plans and corresponding Python code actions.
- The framework integrates a comprehensive set of CAD-specific tools, including a hand-drawn image parameterizer, sketch/3D solid recognizers, and a constraint checker, along with direct ==FreeCAD API== access.
- An ==iterative plan-action-execution-feedback loop== allows the VLLM to dynamically refine its strategy based on textual feedback and visual renderings from the CAD environment.

## Results

- CAD-Assistant achieved **0.979** Primitive F1 (PF1) and **0.484** Constraint F1 (CF1) on the SketchGraphs benchmark for autoconstraining, outperforming raw GPT-4o (**0.693** PF1, **0.274** CF1) and supervised methods like Vitruvion.
- It demonstrated strong performance in CAD Question Answering (CQA), with **0.791** Accuracy (2D) and **0.857** Accuracy (3D), representing substantial gains over raw VLLM baselines.
- The system successfully performed real-world tasks such as 3D reverse engineering from scans, visual interpretation of hand-drawn commands, and 3D solid generation from sketches, with an average cost per user request ranging from
0.0299to0.0887.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tool-augmentation effectively overcomes the geometric reasoning shortcomings of VLLMs, enabling them to achieve high precision in complex CAD tasks by offloading specific operations to dedicated tools.
> - Multimodal input, specifically precise 2D renderings of sketches and over-parameterized JSON representations, significantly improves the VLLM's understanding and reasoning about CAD geometry.
> - The training-free nature and reliance on docstrings for tool integration make the framework highly extensible and adaptable to a wide array of CAD commands without requiring extensive retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "CAD-Assistant" is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Computer-Aided Design (CAD) automation, Vision and Large Language Models (VLLMs), and tool-augmented AI agents.

Historically, CAD has been a highly manual process, relying on skilled designers to create, modify, and optimize designs using specialized software. Recent years have seen a significant surge in research aimed at automating parts of the CAD pipeline. This includes areas like 3D reverse-engineering from point clouds or images, CAD generation from various inputs (text, sketches, multi-view images), edge parameterization, and text-guided CAD editing. However, as the authors rightly point out, most existing efforts in CAD automation tend to focus on *fixed workflows* and *task-specific solutions*. This means a model might be excellent at generating a 3D model from a text description but incapable of then applying geometric constraints or modifying the generated design interactively. The development of truly *generic CAD agents* that can handle a diverse range of tasks and adapt to evolving design states remains largely unexplored.

Concurrently, **Vision and Large Language Models (VLLMs)** have emerged as powerful foundational models, demonstrating impressive capabilities in multimodal understanding and reasoning. Their vast pre-training endows them with broad knowledge, which theoretically should extend to domains like design and manufacturing. However, VLLMs suffer from significant limitations when applied directly to CAD tasks. These include weaknesses in precise geometric reasoning, handling mathematical concepts, semantically interpreting complex CAD command effects, recognizing spatial arrangements, and accurately orienting primitives. The inherent unpredictability of high-level CAD operations (e.g., fillet, chamfer) and their complex impact on geometry and topology further limits raw VLLM applicability.

This is where **tool-augmentation** becomes a critical paradigm. Tool-augmentation involves equipping foundational models with external, specialized tools (e.g., calculators, search engines, APIs) that they can strategically call upon to overcome their inherent limitations. This approach has proven effective in various domains, enhancing factual accuracy, providing real-time information, extending domain expertise, and producing more interpretable outputs by making intermediate steps explicit. The current work specifically addresses the *gap* in exploring tool-augmented VLLMs within the CAD domain.

"CAD-Assistant" thus proposes a novel solution by integrating a powerful VLLM (GPT-4o) as a high-level planner with a comprehensive suite of CAD-specific tools. This positions the work as a significant step towards realizing truly **AI-assisted CAD design**, moving beyond specialized single-task models to a more general-purpose, agentic framework capable of interacting with and supporting designers throughout the entire CAD process. It directly tackles the geometric reasoning shortcomings of VLLMs by offloading precise operations to dedicated CAD software (FreeCAD) and specialized modules, while leveraging the VLLM's strength in high-level planning and multimodal understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the CAD-Assistant project is to develop a **general-purpose CAD agent for AI-assisted design**. This overarching goal is motivated by several key challenges and opportunities:

- **Bridging the gap in CAD automation:** Existing CAD automation solutions are often task-specific, focusing on narrow workflows (e.g., generating 3D models from text). The authors identify a critical need for "CAD agents to address generic tasks," which can interact with designers through the entire iterative CAD process rather than just performing isolated operations.
- **Leveraging and mitigating VLLM limitations:** VLLMs hold immense potential for AI-assisted CAD due to their broad pre-training knowledge. However, they inherently struggle with precise geometric reasoning, mathematical accuracy, and understanding the complex, often unpredictable, effects of CAD commands on geometry. The key motivation is to overcome these VLLM weaknesses to unlock their potential in computational design.
- **Enabling multimodal interaction:** Designers interact with CAD systems using various modalities – text, hand-drawn sketches, precise drawings, and even 3D scans. A generic CAD agent must be able to process and respond to these diverse inputs. CAD-Assistant aims to support this multimodal interaction comprehensively.
- **Developing an adaptable and extensible framework:** Traditional CAD automation often relies on models trained on limited datasets of specific CAD operations. The authors are motivated to create a "training-free framework" that is "highly extensible" and can leverage the full range of commands available in open-source CAD software (FreeCAD) by simply providing docstrings, moving beyond the limited operations found in existing datasets.
- **Enhancing geometric reasoning for VLLMs:** The work is motivated to explore how external tools can enhance VLLMs' semantic understanding and reasoning about CAD programs, particularly regarding different parameterization and serialization strategies for geometric data, and the utility of visual renderings.
- **Demonstrating practical utility and outperforming baselines:** A core motivation is to quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrate that tool-augmentation provides significant advantages, outperforming both raw VLLM baselines and even supervised task-specific methods on various CAD benchmarks, while also showcasing new, diverse real-world use cases.

In essence, the project seeks to transform AI-assisted CAD from a collection of specialized tools into a cohesive, intelligent agent capable of understanding, reasoning about, and executing complex, diverse design tasks by strategically combining the high-level cognitive abilities of VLLMs with the precision and robustness of dedicated CAD software and specialized tools.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CAD-Assistant employs a **tool-augmented VLLM framework** designed to act as a generic CAD task solver. Its core methodology revolves around an iterative plan-action-execution-feedback loop, enabling dynamic interaction with CAD software and adaptation to evolving design states.

The framework consists of three main components:

1. **Planner (P):** The brain of CAD-Assistant is a **Vision and Large Language Model (VLLM)**, specifically **GPT-4o**, chosen for its advanced reasoning capabilities. The planner receives the current context, which includes the multimodal user query and feedback from previous actions. Based on this context and the available tool set, it generates a natural language plan (`p_t`) for the current timestep (`t`) and then formulates a corresponding action (`a_t`) as Python code. The iterative nature allows the VLLM to refine its strategy based on the outcomes of prior steps.

2. **Environment (E):** The actions generated by the planner are executed within a **Python interpreter**. This interpreter serves as the primary environment, integrating the open-source **FreeCAD software** via its Python API. This direct programmatic access to FreeCAD is crucial, as it allows CAD-Assistant to execute actual CAD commands, manipulate geometry, and query the current state of the design. After executing an action, the environment provides feedback (`f_t`) and the updated state of the CAD design (`e_t`) back to the planner. This feedback can be textual (e.g., success messages, JSON data of geometries) or visual (e.g., rendered images of the current CAD model).

3. **Tool Set (T):** CAD-Assistant is augmented with a diverse set of **CAD-specific tools** and standard Python libraries. Each tool is defined by its Python method signature and a comprehensive docstring, which explains its functionality and usage to the VLLM planner. Key CAD-specific tools include:
 - **Hand-drawn Image Parameterizer:** A specialized module (based on) that extracts geometric primitives and constraints from hand-drawn sketch images, converting visual input into structured text.
 - **CAD Sketch Recognizer:** This utility generates both a JSON serialization of the sketch's geometries and constraints (with optional over-parameterization) and a visual rendering of the sketch with numeric markers. This enhances the VLLM's "visual grounding" by linking textual descriptions with visual elements.
 - **3D Solid Recognizer:** Similar to the sketch recognizer, this tool provides a JSON summary of 3D model parameters (sketches, extrusions) and multi-view renderings of the 3D solid, offering a comprehensive multimodal representation.
 - **Constraint Checker:** A dedicated function that evaluates the validity and impact of a parametric constraint *before* it is applied to the sketch. This tool is vital for preventing unintended geometric changes.
 - **Cross-section Extract:** A routine to generate 2D cross-section images from 3D meshes (scans) along specified planes, enabling reverse engineering workflows.
 - **FreeCAD API Integration:** Direct access to FreeCAD's `Sketcher` and `Part` modules for adding/manipulating primitives, applying constraints, and performing extrusion operations.
 - **Standard Python Functionality:** The VLLM can use Python for general logical and mathematical operations, such as calculating lengths or angles.

A crucial aspect of CAD-Assistant's methodology is its **training-free nature**. Unlike many prior works that rely on large datasets for fine-tuning task-specific models, CAD-Assistant operates in a zero-shot manner (though few-shot prompting is also explored for further enhancement). Its extensibility is high: new FreeCAD API capabilities can be integrated by simply providing their Python docstrings to the planner, allowing it to interpret and utilize them. The framework's ability to "inspect the evolving state of a design and refine or correct actions based on the current CAD geometry" is a core strength, allowing for robust problem-solving and error correction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CAD-Assistant's effectiveness is evaluated quantitatively on several CAD benchmarks and qualitatively demonstrated through diverse real-world use cases.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluations

1. **Strategies for Effective Geometric Reasoning (Table 2):**
 - **Parametrization Strategy:** A point-based primitive representation (as in) significantly outperforms the implicit parametrization used in the original SGPBench (e.g., 0.748 vs. 0.674 accuracy for JSON serialization). Over-parameterization provides a negligible drop in performance but is considered safer for diverse tasks.
 - **Serialization Strategy:** Schema-embedded formats like **JSON (0.748 Acc)** perform better than tabular formats (e.g., HTML 0.710, Markdown 0.706, CSV 0.703) for textual representations of CAD sketches, contradicting findings in other domains for tabular data.
 - **Rendering-based Reasoning:** Providing the VLLM planner with precise 2D renderings of CAD sketches achieves higher accuracy (0.754) than relying solely on textual descriptions, indicating the importance of visual context. Hand-drawn sketches perform lower (0.616), highlighting the need for dedicated parameterization tools.
 - **Conclusion:** CAD-Assistant is equipped with tools that generate over-parameterized JSON representations and precise renderings, forming a comprehensive multimodal input for the VLLM.

2. **CAD Question Answering (CQA) (Table 3):**
 - CAD-Assistant significantly outperforms VLLM baselines (GPT-4 mini, GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-4o without tools) on both 2D and 3D subsets of SGPBench.
 - For GPT-4o, CAD-Assistant achieves **0.791 Acc** (2D) and **0.857 Acc** (3D), compared to 0.686 Acc (2D) and 0.782 Acc (3D) for the raw GPT-4o baseline.
 - The performance gain is more substantial with more powerful VLLMs, emphasizing the need for pairing tool-augmented frameworks with robust planners.

3. **Autoconstraining (Table 4 & 5):**
 - CAD-Assistant (zero-shot) achieves **0.979 Primitive F1 (PF1)** and **0.484 Constraint F1 (CF1)** on SketchGraphs. This dramatically surpasses the GPT-4o zero-shot baseline (0.693 PF1, 0.274 CF1) and even the supervised task-specific model Vitruvion (0.706 PF1, 0.238 CF1).
 - The high PF1 indicates that CAD-Assistant effectively preserves the integrity of the geometry while applying constraints.
 - **Impact of Tools (Table 5):** Both the multimodal sketch recognizer (MMrecog) and the constraint checker (ConstrCheck) tools contribute to performance gains. The combination is crucial.
 - **Prompting Strategies (Table 5):** While CAD-Assistant excels in a zero-shot setting, incorporating a few (e.g., 5) high-quality demonstrations further boosts performance (0.984 PF1, 0.529 CF1).

4. **Hand-drawn CAD Sketch Parameterization (Table 6):**
 - CAD-Assistant achieves **0.784 parametric accuracy** and a **Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.680**.
 - It matches the accuracy of the task-specific Davinci model (0.789) but significantly reduces the Chamfer Distance, indicating a better reconstruction quality and more accurate constraint application. It also outperforms Vitruvion.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Demonstrations (Figure 5 & Supplementary)

- **Beyond Simplified CAD Commands:** CAD-Assistant can leverage a wide array of FreeCAD API commands (e.g., `Revolution`, `Fillet`) not commonly found in large-scale datasets, simply by interpreting their docstrings. This shows its potential for handling complex operations.
- **Real-world Use Cases:**
 - **Sketch to 3D Solid:** Generates a 3D solid by revolving a hand-drawn sketch around an axis, demonstrating multimodal input processing (image + text) and complex 3D modeling.
 - **3D Scan Reverse Engineering:** Processes a 3D scan and a textual query to extract a cross-section, parameterize it, and extrude it into a 3D CAD model, showcasing sophisticated reverse engineering capabilities.
 - **Visual CAD Design:** Semantically interprets hand-drawn commands (e.g., "extrude," "delete circle") directly on a CAD sketch and executes the corresponding operations, enabling intuitive visual interaction.

#### 4.3. Human Evaluations (Figure 4)

- **Failure Case Analysis (CQA):** Most errors (for misclassified answers) are attributed to incorrect VLLM reasoning or misinterpretation of tool-generated renderings (e.g., confusing a trapezoid with a triangle).
- **Tool-use Validity:** Human annotators found a high validity rate of 98.5% for tool usage in autoconstraining/parameterization trajectories, indicating reliable and correct application of the CAD-specific tools.

#### 4.4. Costs (Table 11)

The average cost per user request using GPT-4o ranges from $0.0299 for CQA to $0.0887 for hand-drawn sketch parameterization, indicating a practical cost for the demonstrated capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CAD-Assistant framework represents a significant advancement towards achieving **generic, AI-assisted CAD design**, with broad implications for both research and industry:

1. **Paradigm Shift in CAD Automation:** By moving beyond task-specific, fixed workflows, CAD-Assistant introduces a truly general-purpose agent capable of addressing diverse CAD tasks. This shifts the paradigm from isolated automation tools to an intelligent assistant that can interactively support designers across the entire design lifecycle, from conceptual sketching to complex 3D modeling and reverse engineering.

2. **Unlocking VLLM Potential in Engineering:** The work effectively demonstrates that tool-augmentation is a powerful strategy to overcome the inherent limitations of VLLMs in geometric reasoning and mathematical precision. By offloading precise operations to specialized CAD tools while leveraging the VLLM's planning and multimodal understanding, CAD-Assistant shows how foundational AI models can be made practical for demanding engineering and design domains. This opens avenues for VLLMs in other precision-critical applications.

3. **Enhanced User Interaction and Accessibility:** The ability to process multimodal inputs – from hand-drawn sketches and textual queries to drawn commands and 3D scans – makes CAD design more intuitive and accessible. Designers can communicate intent in natural ways, reducing the learning curve and accelerating the early stages of design exploration. The visual interpretation of drawn commands is particularly impactful for direct, intuitive manipulation.

4. **Extensibility and Adaptability:** The training-free nature and reliance on docstrings for tool integration make CAD-Assistant highly extensible. This means it can easily adapt to new CAD software features or specialized tools without requiring extensive retraining, a stark contrast to traditional deep learning models. This adaptability is crucial in a rapidly evolving software landscape.

5. **Interpretable and Editable Outputs:** By generating FreeCAD code that is executed directly within the CAD software, CAD-Assistant produces outputs that are not only editable but also highly interpretable. Designers can review, understand, and modify the generated code, retaining control and ensuring design intent is met. This addresses a common challenge with "black box" AI models, fostering trust and collaboration between AI and human designers.

6. **Benchmarking and Future Research:** The paper identifies and adapts an evaluation setting for generic CAD agents, addressing a critical gap in the field. This provides a robust framework for future research to quantitatively assess and compare new methods. The identified failure cases (e.g., VLLM reasoning errors, misinterpretation of renderings) also highlight clear directions for future improvements, such as more advanced planning and verification algorithms.

In conclusion, CAD-Assistant paves the way for a new era of AI-assisted design, where intelligent agents powered by tool-augmented VLLMs can genuinely collaborate with human designers, enhancing creativity, efficiency, and precision across a vast spectrum of CAD workflows.
