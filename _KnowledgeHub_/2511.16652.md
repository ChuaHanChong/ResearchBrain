# Evolution Strategies at the Hyperscale

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, EGGROLL, scales Evolution Strategies to billion-parameter deep learning models through low-rank perturbations and hardware-aware optimizations. It delivers hundredfold speedups and successfully enables pretraining of pure int8 language models, outperforming fp32 Transformers on next-token cross-entropy.

## Problem

- Evolution Strategies (ES) were historically limited to smaller models and population sizes due to the prohibitive memory and computational cost of generating full-rank parameter perturbations.
- Naïve ES requires replicating entire parameter sets for each population member, leading to massive memory footprints and inefficient batched matrix multiplications on GPUs.
- Traditional gradient-based methods struggle with non-differentiable objectives, integer-only computations, and noisy landscapes, limiting their applicability in certain advanced AI problems.

## Method

- EGGROLL (Evolution Guided GeneRal Optimisation via Low-rank Learning) replaces full-rank matrix perturbations with ==low-rank approximations== (E = (1/√r)ABᵀ) to drastically reduce memory requirements.
- A hardware-efficient implementation uses ==counter-based deterministic random number generators== for on-demand noise reconstruction and optimizes ==batched low-rank inference== for higher arithmetic intensity.
- The method employs a ==Gaussian approximate score function== and provides theoretical proofs of its consistency with standard Gaussian ES updates in high dimensions, even for low-rank perturbations.

## Results

- EGGROLL achieved a **hundredfold** increase in training speed for billion-parameter models, reaching up to **91%** of pure batch inference throughput.
- It successfully pretrained an int8 nonlinear recurrent language model ('EGG') to **3.40 bits/byte** next-token cross-entropy, outperforming a standard fp32 Transformer model (**3.58 bits/byte**) on the same dataset.
- The method fine-tuned a 14B parameter RWKV-7 model on math reasoning tasks, improving accuracy from **13%** to **30%** on AIME24 and **7%** to **33%** on AIME25, significantly outperforming GRPO within similar hardware and time constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-rank perturbations effectively resolve the memory and computational bottlenecks of ES, making it feasible for hyperscale models without compromising the ability to perform full-rank updates over time.
> - Rigorous theoretical analysis establishes critical noise scaling rates for stable Gaussian ES convergence in high dimensions and confirms EGGROLL's high-dimensional consistency with true ES gradients.
> - Efficiently scaled ES enables pretraining and fine-tuning of models with pure integer arithmetic and direct optimization of non-differentiable objectives, opening avenues for novel hardware-friendly architectures and complex reward functions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Evolution Strategies at the Hyperscale" directly addresses a critical gap in the application of Evolution Strategies (ES) to modern, large-scale deep learning problems. For years, ES has been recognized as a powerful class of black-box optimization methods with several compelling advantages over traditional gradient-based approaches (e.g., stochastic gradient descent with backpropagation). These advantages include the ability to handle non-differentiable or noisy objectives, robustness to ill-conditioned landscapes, and inherent parallelizability across a population of agents or perturbations.

However, despite these theoretical strengths, ES has historically been limited to smaller models and population sizes. The primary bottleneck has been the prohibitive memory and computational cost associated with generating and evaluating full-rank parameter perturbations for deep neural networks, especially those with billions of parameters. Naïve ES requires replicating the entire parameter set for each population member, leading to massive memory footprints and inefficient batched matrix multiplications due which have poor arithmetic intensity on GPUs.

This work positions itself at the intersection of ES, large language models (LLMs), and hardware-efficient deep learning. It draws inspiration from recent breakthroughs in parameter-efficient fine-tuning for LLMs, particularly Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). LoRA has demonstrated that for gradient-based methods, introducing low-rank adapters can drastically reduce the number of trainable parameters and computational overhead while maintaining performance. EGGROLL extends this successful paradigm to the ES framework, aiming to unlock ES's potential for hyperscale applications.

In the context of LLM training and fine-tuning, backpropagation remains the dominant method. However, there's a growing interest in ES variants due to their ability to optimize non-differentiable objectives, which are common in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and complex reasoning tasks where discrete rewards are prevalent. Prior ES work in LLMs (e.g., zeroth-order methods or ES on LoRA matrices) has typically been limited by small population sizes or extensive rollouts per perturbation. EGGROLL directly tackles this by enabling massively parallel populations, thereby offering a more scalable and potentially robust alternative for LLM optimization.

Overall, EGGROLL represents a significant advancement in black-box optimization, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with ES. It aims to make ES a competitive or even superior choice for training and fine-tuning foundation models, especially when dealing with novel architectures, integer-only computations, or complex, non-differentiable objective functions that challenge traditional gradient-based methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable Evolution Strategies (ES) to scale efficiently to billion-parameter models and beyond**, overcoming the significant practical obstacles that have historically limited their application in modern deep learning.

The key motivations driving this work are rooted in the inherent advantages of ES and the limitations of traditional gradient-based methods for certain cutting-edge AI problems:

- **Exploiting ES Advantages:** ES offers several compelling benefits that are highly desirable for contemporary AI challenges:
 - **Black-box Optimization:** It does not require objective functions to be differentiable, making it suitable for models with discrete parameterizations, non-differentiable components (e.g., symbolic modules, complex rule-based systems), or noisy/outcome-only rewards (e.g., LLM fine-tuning based on qualitative feedback).
 - **Robustness:** ES can be more robust to noisy, ill-conditioned, or discontinuous optimization landscapes, common in deep reinforcement learning or long-range recurrent settings, by smoothing irregularities through population-based exploration.
 - **Parallel Scalability:** Fitness evaluations for different population members are independent, leading to near-linear speedups on large clusters with minimal communication overhead (scalar fitness values). This contrasts with backpropagation, which requires costly gradient aggregation.
 - **Flexibility with Data Types:** ES can directly optimize models using the same low-precision data types used at inference time, avoiding the complexities and overhead of "quantization-aware training" often required by backpropagation for integer-only models.

- **Addressing Current Scaling Bottlenecks in ES:** The main practical impediments to applying ES at scale in deep learning have been:
 - **Memory Costs:** Naïvely, ES requires generating full-rank matrix perturbations, necessitating the replication of entire parameter sets for every population member. This becomes memory-prohibitive for large models.
 - **Computational Inefficiency:** Evaluating these full-rank perturbations on GPUs leads to batched matrix multiplications with low arithmetic intensity. This means a low ratio of arithmetic operations to memory traffic, making computation inefficient and wall-clock time scale poorly with population size.

- **Unlocking New AI Paradigms:** By resolving these scaling issues, the authors aim to:
 - **Train Novel Architectures:** Enable the stable training of highly efficient, hardware-friendly models like nonlinear recurrent networks operating purely in integer datatypes, which are challenging for gradient-based methods.
 - **Advance Foundation Model Capabilities:** Provide a powerful tool for end-to-end training and fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks and verifiable, potentially non-differentiable, rewards.
 - **Facilitate Neurosymbolic AI:** Open avenues for training hybrid neurosymbolic systems where neural networks interface with non-differentiable symbolic modules, bridging a long-standing challenge in AI.

In essence, the motivation is to leverage ES's inherent strengths by eliminating its Achilles' heel – its inability to scale efficiently – thereby broadening the applicability of population-based optimization to the most advanced and computationally demanding AI problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of EGGROLL's methodology lies in its innovative approach to structuring perturbations for Evolution Strategies, coupled with a rigorous theoretical foundation and hardware-efficient implementation.

#### 3.1. Evolution Guided GeneRal Optimisation via Low-rank Learning (EGGROLL)

- **Low-Rank Perturbations:** The central idea is to replace full-rank matrix perturbations (E ∈ R^(m×n)) with low-rank approximations. Instead of sampling E directly, EGGROLL samples two smaller matrices, A ∈ R^(m×r) and B ∈ R^(n×r), with *r ≪ min(m, n)*, and forms the perturbation as E = (1/√r)ABᵀ. This significantly reduces the storage requirement for perturbations from *mn* to *(m+n)r* per layer. The (1/√r) scaling ensures bounded variance.
- **Gaussian Approximate Score Function:** While low-rank matrices technically reside on a manifold and lack a standard Euclidean density, EGGROLL uses a Gaussian approximate score function (Ŝ(E) = -E). This choice is theoretically motivated by proving that the low-rank perturbation E converges in distribution to a Gaussian as rank *r* increases (due to the Central Limit Theorem applying to the sum of independent outer products that form E), and also that EGGROLL's updates linearize consistently with the Gaussian ES update in high dimensions.
- **Overall Update:** Critically, the overall parameter update for the mean matrix M (M ← M + α Σ Eᵢfᵢ) is a weighted average of *N* low-rank matrices. This means the final update matrix is not restricted to be low-rank; its rank is *min(Nr, m, n)*, ensuring that EGGROLL can still perform full-rank updates when the population size and rank are sufficiently large (*Nr > min(m,n)*).

#### 3.2. Hardware-Efficient Implementation

The efficiency gains of EGGROLL stem from smart engineering that addresses the low arithmetic intensity of naive ES:

- **Noise Reconstruction on Demand:** EGGROLL uses a counter-based deterministic random number generator (RNG) to reconstruct noise samples on demand. This eliminates the need to store large perturbation matrices in memory, saving significant VRAM.
- **Batched Low-Rank Inference:** The perturbation is applied as u(M+σE)ᵀ = uMᵀ + (σ/√r)(uB)Aᵀ. The first term (uMᵀ) is a standard, efficient batched matrix multiplication. The second term, involving the low-rank perturbation, is implemented by efficiently batching the (uB) operations (a batch of vector-vector dot products) and (uB)Aᵀ operations (a batch of scalar-vector multiplications for rank-1). This decomposition allows EGGROLL to reuse the efficient general matrix multiplication kernels typically used in batched inference, leading to dramatically higher arithmetic intensity than naive ES.
- **Optimized Update Aggregation:** The summation of perturbations (Σ Eᵢfᵢ) is also optimized. For rank-1, instead of explicitly materializing each Eᵢ, the sum is computed as (diag(F)A)ᵀB, which is an efficient matrix multiplication where F is the vector of fitnesses and A, B are batched perturbation vectors.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides robust theoretical analysis to underpin EGGROLL's efficacy:

- **High-Dimensional Gaussian ES Convergence:** The authors analyze the convergence of general Gaussian ES in high dimensions (*d* → ∞). They identify a critical noise scaling rate (σ_d = o(d⁻¹/²)) under which the ES update provably linearizes and converges to the first-order derivative for a broad class of (possibly discontinuous) objectives. This analysis reveals three distinct regimes (linearization, critical, divergence) and establishes tight conditions for stable ES optimization in large models.
- **EGGROLL's High-Dimensional Consistency:** Extending the analysis, they prove that the EGGROLL update, even with fixed low-rank perturbations (e.g., rank-1), converges to the true ES gradient as dimension *d* grows. This consistency is crucial for explaining EGGROLL's empirical success in high-dimensional neural networks and connects its behavior to concepts like the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK).
- **Rank Convergence Rate:** A rigorous theoretical analysis demonstrates that EGGROLL updates converge to the full-rank Gaussian ES updates at a fast O(r⁻¹) rate as the rank *r* increases. This faster-than-typical Central Limit Theorem rate is attributed to the symmetry of the perturbation distributions, which causes odd-order cumulants to vanish in the Edgeworth expansion, making the convergence controlled by the 4th-order term.

This comprehensive approach—combining a novel architectural idea (low-rank perturbations), intelligent hardware-aware implementation, and strong theoretical grounding—enables EGGROLL to scale Evolution Strategies effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation and theoretical findings of EGGROLL demonstrate its significant advancements in scaling Evolution Strategies for diverse machine learning tasks.

- **Exceptional Speed and Throughput:**
 - EGGROLL achieves a hundredfold increase in training speed for billion-parameter models at large population sizes compared to naive ES.
 - It reaches up to 91% of the throughput of pure batch inference (Figure 2a), effectively making the computation of a large population of unique perturbations nearly as fast as a single batch inference. This directly addresses the arithmetic intensity bottleneck of previous ES methods.

- **Pure Integer Language Model Pretraining (EGG Model):**
 - EGGROLL enables the stable pretraining of "EGG," a novel nonlinear recurrent language model designed to operate purely in int8 integer datatypes, without explicit activation functions (relying on implicit clipping nonlinearity).
 - With sufficiently large population sizes, the int8 EGG model achieves a test next-token cross-entropy of 3.40 bits/byte, outperforming a standard fp32 Transformer model (3.58 bits/byte) trained with backpropagation on the same data and batch size (Figure 2b).
 - This experiment showcases EGGROLL's flexibility by allowing optimization of models that are fundamentally challenging for gradient-based methods due to integer-only arithmetic. It also scales to unprecedented population sizes (over 1 million on a single GPU).

- **Reinforcement Learning Performance:**
 - In tabula rasa and multi-agent RL (MARL) settings across 16 diverse environments, EGGROLL performs competitively with (or even outperforms on 7/16 environments) OpenES, a highly optimized vanilla ES implementation.
 - Crucially, EGGROLL achieves this while delivering substantial wall-clock speedups due to its batched low-rank structure (Figure 4a, Appendix N.4), demonstrating that efficiency does not compromise performance in standard control tasks.

- **Foundation Model Fine-tuning (LLMs on Reasoning Tasks):**
 - **RWKV-7 on Countdown:** EGGROLL fine-tunes an RWKV-7 1.5B model on the Countdown reasoning task, converging to a higher validation accuracy (35%) compared to GRPO (23%) within the same hardware and wall-clock time (Figure 4b).
 - **RWKV-7 on GSM8K:** Similarly, EGGROLL outperforms GRPO on GSM8K fine-tuning (Figure 5a).
 - **Large Scale Math Reasoning:** EGGROLL successfully fine-tuned a 14B parameter RWKV-7 model on complex math reasoning tasks (DeepScaleR dataset), improving accuracy significantly (e.g., 13% to 30% on AIME24, 7% to 33% on AIME25). This was achieved on hardware setups where GRPO was infeasible due to memory constraints (Figure 5b, 13b).
 - **Non-differentiable Objectives:** EGGROLL is shown to be capable of directly optimizing for non-differentiable objectives like pass@k for LLMs, a known limitation of other methods (Figure 10).

- **Fine-tuning Integer Quantized LLMs:**
 - EGGROLL successfully performs model distillation of a non-quantized RWKV-7 model into its int8 quantized version using GSM8K examples.
 - The quantized model progressively recovers its problem-solving capabilities, demonstrating EGGROLL's ability to train models with low-precision weights (Figure 6).

- **Fine-tuning Time Series Foundation Models (High-Frequency Trading):**
 - EGGROLL effectively fine-tunes a pretrained S5 model (a state-space model architecture for sequences) on limit order book data for a high-frequency trading task, directly optimizing for Profit and Loss (PnL).
 - It achieves a remarkable 155% improvement in mean PnL over the baseline pretrained model, showing its applicability beyond natural language to complex structured time series data (Figure 11).

- **Theoretical Validation:**
 - The theoretical analysis rigorously proves that EGGROLL's low-rank updates converge to the true Gaussian ES updates at a fast O(r⁻¹) rate as the rank *r* increases.
 - It establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for the convergence and linearization of general Gaussian ES in high dimensions, providing a deep understanding of its behavior in the context of large neural networks.

These findings collectively highlight EGGROLL as a highly efficient, versatile, and theoretically sound method for scaling Evolution Strategies to the demanding challenges of modern hyperscale AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of EGGROLL marks a pivotal moment for Evolution Strategies (ES) and black-box optimization in the era of hyperscale AI. Its significance and potential impact span several critical areas:

- **Democratization of Evolution Strategies for Large-Scale AI:** EGGROLL effectively solves the long-standing problem of scaling ES to billion-parameter deep learning models. By resolving the computational and memory bottlenecks associated with full-rank perturbations, it makes ES a viable, efficient, and competitive alternative to gradient-based methods. This opens the door for a wider adoption of ES in research and industry, particularly where backpropagation is difficult or impossible.

- **Enabling Novel Architectures and Data Types:**
 - **Pure Integer Training:** The successful pretraining of the EGG model purely in integer (int8) datatypes, without explicit activation functions, demonstrates a groundbreaking capability. This has immense implications for energy-efficient AI hardware and inference-optimized models, as integer arithmetic is significantly cheaper and faster than floating-point. EGGROLL can drive the development of hardware-friendly neural networks from the ground up.
 - **Non-differentiable Components:** EGGROLL empowers the training of models with non-differentiable components, such as neurosymbolic systems or those integrating external modules (e.g., memory, calculators, databases). This could lead to a new generation of hybrid AI systems that combine the strengths of neural networks with symbolic reasoning.

- **Advanced Foundation Model Training and Fine-tuning:**
 - **LLM Alignment with Complex Rewards:** EGGROLL offers a robust method for fine-tuning LLMs on verifiable and potentially non-differentiable reward functions, which are critical for aligning large models with human values and complex reasoning tasks (e.g., RLHF, optimizing for pass@k). This can lead to more capable and safer LLMs.
 - **Broader Applicability:** By outperforming existing ES-inspired LLM fine-tuning methods (like GRPO) and operating efficiently at scales where others fail, EGGROLL can accelerate research into how foundation models are adapted for specialized tasks.
 - **Time Series Foundation Models:** Its successful application to fine-tuning a time series foundation model for high-frequency trading showcases its versatility across different data modalities and complex, real-world optimization problems.

- **Computational Efficiency and Sustainability:** The substantial speedups and improved arithmetic intensity offered by EGGROLL mean that large-population ES can be run more economically. This contributes to more sustainable AI research by reducing the compute resources needed to explore complex models and optimization landscapes.

- **Theoretical Advancement:** The rigorous theoretical analysis of ES in high dimensions, including the identification of critical noise scaling and the proof of consistency for low-rank updates, provides foundational insights. This not only explains EGGROLL's empirical success but also enriches the theoretical understanding of population-based optimization in large neural networks, potentially inspiring further theoretical work.

- **Future Research Directions:** EGGROLL opens up numerous avenues for future research, including:
 - Exploring even more complex, non-differentiable objectives for LLMs and other foundation models.
 - Developing new hardware-optimized AI architectures that fully leverage EGGROLL's integer-only training capabilities.
 - Applying ES to multi-agent systems and complex simulations where robustness and exploration are paramount.
 - Integrating EGGROLL with more sophisticated optimizer states (e.g., momentum for integer weights) to further accelerate convergence.

In conclusion, "Evolution Strategies at the Hyperscale" is a landmark paper that transforms ES from a niche optimization method into a serious contender for training the largest and most complex AI models. By combining elegant theoretical insights with practical engineering, EGGROLL lays a crucial foundation for the next generation of AI systems.
