# Evolution Strategies at the Hyperscale

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, EGGROLL, scales Evolution Strategies to billion-parameter deep learning models through low-rank perturbations and hardware-aware optimizations. It delivers hundredfold speedups and successfully enables pretraining of pure int8 language models, outperforming fp32 Transformers on next-token cross-entropy.

## Problem

- Evolution Strategies (ES) were historically limited to smaller models and population sizes due to the prohibitive memory and computational cost of generating full-rank parameter perturbations.
- Naïve ES requires replicating entire parameter sets for each population member, leading to massive memory footprints and inefficient batched matrix multiplications on GPUs.
- Traditional gradient-based methods struggle with non-differentiable objectives, integer-only computations, and noisy landscapes, limiting their applicability in certain advanced AI problems.

## Method

- EGGROLL (Evolution Guided GeneRal Optimisation via Low-rank Learning) replaces full-rank matrix perturbations with ==low-rank approximations== (E = (1/√r)ABᵀ) to drastically reduce memory requirements.
- A hardware-efficient implementation uses ==counter-based deterministic random number generators== for on-demand noise reconstruction and optimizes ==batched low-rank inference== for higher arithmetic intensity.
- The method employs a ==Gaussian approximate score function== and provides theoretical proofs of its consistency with standard Gaussian ES updates in high dimensions, even for low-rank perturbations.

## Results

- EGGROLL achieved a **hundredfold** increase in training speed for billion-parameter models, reaching up to **91%** of pure batch inference throughput.
- It successfully pretrained an int8 nonlinear recurrent language model ('EGG') to **3.40 bits/byte** next-token cross-entropy, outperforming a standard fp32 Transformer model (**3.58 bits/byte**) on the same dataset.
- The method fine-tuned a 14B parameter RWKV-7 model on math reasoning tasks, improving accuracy from **13%** to **30%** on AIME24 and **7%** to **33%** on AIME25, significantly outperforming GRPO within similar hardware and time constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-rank perturbations effectively resolve the memory and computational bottlenecks of ES, making it feasible for hyperscale models without compromising the ability to perform full-rank updates over time.
> - Rigorous theoretical analysis establishes critical noise scaling rates for stable Gaussian ES convergence in high dimensions and confirms EGGROLL's high-dimensional consistency with true ES gradients.
> - Efficiently scaled ES enables pretraining and fine-tuning of models with pure integer arithmetic and direct optimization of non-differentiable objectives, opening avenues for novel hardware-friendly architectures and complex reward functions.
