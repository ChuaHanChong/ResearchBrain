# Uncertainty-Aware Velocity Correction for Proprioceptive Vehicle Localization using Evidential Mamba

> [!summary] Summary
> Kalyanasundaram et al. at Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt and Technische Universität München developed EVC-Mamba, an architecture that provides uncertainty-aware velocity corrections for proprioceptive vehicle localization in GNSS-denied environments by leveraging onboard sensor data with a Mamba-based model and evidential deep learning. This method reduced maximum position drift to within 10% of dedicated hardware sensors and operated three times more efficiently than Transformer-based approaches.

## Problem

- Unbounded position drift occurs in IMU-based vehicle localization when Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) signals are unavailable or unreliable.
- Existing methods for GNSS-denied localization often require expensive external sensors, dedicated infrastructure, or struggle with environmental dependencies.
- Prior learning-based approaches for inertial odometry either lack principled uncertainty quantification or employ computationally intensive models unsuitable for real-time, on-board vehicle deployment.

## Method

- A learning-based virtual velocity sensor utilizes readily available onboard vehicle sensor data to infer vehicle velocity without external hardware.
- The **EVC-Mamba** architecture combines a ==Mamba-based Selective State Space Model== for efficient temporal dynamics with an ==evidential deep learning== framework to quantify velocity estimation uncertainty.
- Uncertainty-aware velocity estimates are then integrated as virtual measurements into an ==Error-State Extended Kalman Filter (ES-EKF)== to correct IMU drift in real-time.

## Results

- EVC-Mamba achieved high velocity estimation accuracy with MAE values of **0.03 m/s** for longitudinal and **0.006 m/s** for lateral velocity, outperforming a Transformer baseline for lateral velocity by **30%**.
- It reduced maximum position drift to **2.4 ± 1.3 m** during a 5-minute GNSS outage, achieving localization accuracy within **10%** of a dedicated Correvit hardware sensor.
- The system demonstrated real-time capability with **20-24 ms** inference latency (**40 Hz** operation) on edge hardware and was approximately **3x** more computationally efficient than Transformer-based alternatives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured State Space Models (Mamba) enable linear-time sequence modeling, offering a computationally efficient alternative to Transformers for precise velocity estimation from vehicle sensor data.
> - Evidential deep learning provides a principled method for quantifying both aleatoric (data inherent) and epistemic (model uncertainty) in velocity predictions, crucial for robust system integration.
> - Integrating a learning-based virtual velocity sensor, trained with uncertainty awareness, into an Error-State Extended Kalman Filter significantly reduces IMU position drift in GNSS-denied scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Accurate and reliable vehicle localization is a foundational requirement for the safe operation of intelligent vehicles, supporting functions such as path planning, motion control, and automated driving. Conventionally, precise localization is achieved by integrating Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) with Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) signals to counteract the inherent drift of inertial systems. However, environments where GNSS signals are unavailable or unreliable—such as tunnels, urban canyons, or multi-story parking structures—pose a significant challenge. In these "GNSS-denied" environments, inertial systems accumulate unbounded position drift over time due to noise and bias accumulation in Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs).

Existing approaches to address GNSS-denied localization broadly include:
- **Radio Frequency (RF)-based techniques:** These rely on dedicated infrastructure and are susceptible to signal attenuation and multipath effects.
- **Vision-based methods:** Utilizing cameras or LiDARs, these can achieve high accuracy but are sensitive to environmental factors like illumination changes and occlusions.
- **Multi-sensor fusion:** While offering improved robustness, these methods typically increase system complexity and cost due to the integration of multiple external sensors.
- **Proprioceptive inertial navigation:** IMU-based solutions offer a low-cost, infrastructure-free option. However, their primary limitation is the long-term drift.

Recent advancements have seen the application of data-driven and machine learning methods to improve IMU-based dead reckoning. Examples include learning-based IMU calibration (e.g., CalibNet) and direct estimation of vehicle states from onboard sensors (e.g., sideslip angle). While these have shown promise, their effectiveness often remains limited to shorter outage durations or they rely on traditional recurrent networks (like IONet) for sequential modeling, which can be computationally intensive for long sequences. Transformer-based models offer improved long-range reasoning but incur quadratic computational costs, hindering real-time deployment. Structured State Space Models (SSMs) such as Mamba offer a linear-time alternative for sequence modeling, but their application in vehicle state estimation, particularly with uncertainty quantification, has been underexplored.

This work addresses the limitations of existing proprioceptive localization by proposing a learning-based virtual velocity sensor that leverages readily available onboard vehicle data (e.g., wheel speeds, steering angle) to provide continuous drift correction for IMU-based localization in GNSS-denied environments. It integrates a Mamba-based selective state space model for efficient temporal dynamics capture and employs evidential deep learning for principled uncertainty quantification. This combination aims to provide a robust, real-time, and hardware-free solution that surpasses the limitations of traditional kinematic models and addresses the computational challenges of other learning-based sequence models while also providing reliability measures through uncertainty estimates.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reliable and real-time localization system for intelligent vehicles operating in GNSS-denied environments, without requiring expensive external sensors or dedicated infrastructure. The system aims to correct the unbounded position drift inherent in Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) when external GNSS signals are unavailable.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges in current localization practices:
- **Drift in GNSS-denied environments:** Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) suffer from accumulated errors, leading to significant position drift when GNSS corrections are absent, which is critical for safety-of-life applications in autonomous driving.
- **Limitations of existing solutions:**
 - Dedicated external hardware sensors (e.g., Correvit) provide accurate velocity but are costly and increase system complexity.
 - Kinematic models for inferring velocity from onboard sensors rely on simplifying assumptions, limiting their robustness across varying driving conditions.
 - Many learning-based approaches for inertial odometry or state estimation either do not explicitly quantify uncertainty, potentially leading to overconfidence in challenging scenarios, or they use computationally expensive architectures (like Transformers) that hinder real-time deployment on edge hardware.
- **Leveraging readily available data:** Modern vehicles are equipped with a suite of onboard sensors (e.g., wheel speeds, steering angle, brake pressure, lateral acceleration). These proprioceptive measurements contain rich information about vehicle motion that can be exploited to infer velocity without additional hardware.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives and contributions of the proposed EVC-Mamba architecture are:
- **GNSS-Independent Velocity Correction:** To create a learning-based virtual velocity sensor that uses only onboard vehicle sensors to provide corrections for IMU drift, thus removing the need for external sensing hardware or dedicated infrastructure.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Velocity Estimation:** To develop an architecture that not only predicts vehicle velocity but also quantifies its associated uncertainty in a principled manner. This is achieved through a Mamba-based Selective State Space Model for temporal dynamics and an evidential deep learning framework using a Normal-Inverse-Gamma (NIG) distribution. This allows for robust and reliable correction signals by providing a measure of confidence in the velocity estimates.
- **Integration with ES-EKF:** To integrate the uncertainty-aware velocity estimates from EVC-Mamba as a virtual measurement into an Error-State Extended Kalman Filter (ES-EKF). This fusion aims to enhance IMU mechanization and reduce position drift during prolonged GNSS outages.
- **Real-time Capability:** To ensure the proposed architecture is computationally efficient enough for real-time onboard deployment on edge hardware, while maintaining localization accuracy comparable to systems utilizing dedicated external velocity sensors.

In summary, the work aims to provide a cost-effective, robust, and real-time solution for vehicle localization in challenging environments by intelligently processing existing onboard sensor data with state-of-the-art sequence modeling and uncertainty quantification techniques.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed Evidential Velocity Correction using Mamba (EVC-Mamba) framework integrates a learning-based virtual velocity sensor with an Error-State Extended Kalman Filter (ES-EKF) for proprioceptive vehicle localization in GNSS-denied environments.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The objective is to estimate the vehicle's dead-reckoning pose `T_t` (position `p_t` and rotation `R_t`) at each time step `t` in a global coordinate frame, using IMU measurements `u_t = [a_t, ω_t]^T` and onboard sensor data (OSD). The vehicle state is defined as `x_t = [p_t, θ_t, v_t]^T`, where `v_t` is linear velocity and `θ_t` is orientation.
The core of the method is an uncertainty-aware mapping function `G_Θ` that transforms an observation window of `L` time steps of OSD, `O_t ∈ R^(L×m)`, into a velocity estimate `˜v_t` and its associated uncertainty `δ˜v`. This is formulated as an evidential regression model.

**B. Preliminaries (Mamba - Selective State Space Models)**
The EVC-Mamba architecture leverages Mamba, a selective State Space Model (SSM), for efficient temporal modeling. Unlike Transformers with quadratic complexity due to self-attention, Mamba achieves linear-time inference. It maps an input `x_t` to an output `y_t` via a latent state `h_t` using a discrete-time recurrence `h_t = Ah_t-1 + Bx_t, y_t = Ch_t`. A key feature of Mamba is its selective mechanism, which makes the parameters `B`, `C`, and the timescale `Δ` input-dependent (i.e., `B_t = ϕ_B(x_t)`, `C_t = ϕ_C(x_t)`, `Δ_t = ϕ_Δ(x_t)`), allowing for adaptation to input-dependent dynamics. This selectivity is applied independently across feature channels and the temporal dimension.

**C. EVC-Mamba Architecture**
The EVC-Mamba architecture learns the mapping function `G_Θ` by combining a Mamba-based sequence model with evidential velocity regression.

1. **Mamba-based Velocity Estimation:**
 - The task is formulated as a time-series regression problem, taking a sequence of `L` onboard sensor measurements `O_t ∈ R^(L×m)` to infer vehicle velocity.
 - Each onboard measurement `o_t ∈ R^m` is projected into a `d`-dimensional latent space via an up-projection layer `ϕ_u`.
 - These embeddings (`U_enc`) are processed by `n_k` stacked Mamba blocks. Each block applies a depthwise convolution, a gated activation, and the selective SSM (as described in Preliminaries) with a residual connection and Layer Normalization.
 - An output projection layer `ϕ_f` maps the final hidden state to the parameters of a Normal-Inverse-Gamma (NIG) distribution over the velocity likelihood.

2. **Evidential Velocity Regression:**
 - To account for the inherent uncertainty in velocity estimation from onboard sensors, an evidential regression framework is employed.
 - The true velocity `v_t` is assumed to follow a Gaussian distribution `N(μ, σ^2)`. The framework predicts the parameters of a Normal-Inverse-Gamma (NIG) prior over the Gaussian mean `μ` and variance `σ^2`.
 - The NIG distribution `p(v_t | γ, ν, α, β) = NIG(γ, ν, α, β)` is parameterized by `γ` (mean of prediction), `ν` (inverse variance weight), `α` (shape), and `β` (scale).
 - From these NIG parameters, the mean velocity estimate `˜v_t = γ` and total uncertainty `δ˜v` are extracted. `δ˜v` comprises both aleatoric (data inherent) and epistemic (model uncertainty) components.
 - Velocity is modeled independently along each axis (e.g., `v_x, v_y`). The network outputs for `ν` and `β` are transformed using the softplus function to ensure positivity, and `α` is constrained (`α > 1`) for finite predictive variance.
 - The training objective combines a Negative Log Likelihood (NLL) term for data fitting and a regularization term (`L_reg`) to penalize inaccurate uncertainty estimates, with a regularization weight `λ`.

**D. ES-EKF Based IMU Mechanization**
An ES-EKF is used for 3D localization. IMU measurements `u_t = [a_t, ω_t]^T` are bias-corrected and transformed to the vehicle's Center of Gravity (CoG). The state vector `x = [p, v, R_g_v, b_a, b_ω]^T` includes position, velocity, orientation (`R_g_v` from vehicle to global frame), and IMU biases.

1. **A-Priori Prediction:**
 - The nominal state `ˆx_t+1^-` is propagated by integrating high-frequency IMU data.
 - Errors from process noise and model approximations are captured in a linearized error state `δx = [δp, δv, δθ, δb_a, δb_ω]^T`.
 - Only the error-state covariance `P_t+1^-` is propagated during prediction using the linearized error-state transition matrix `F_t` and discrete process noise covariance `Q_t`.

2. **Measurement Update:**
 - The EVC-Mamba velocity estimate `(˜v_t, δ˜v)` is incorporated as a virtual measurement `z_t = [˜v_x, ˜v_y, 0]^T`. A zero vertical velocity constraint is added, valid for ground vehicles.
 - The noise covariance `M_s` for this virtual measurement uses `δ˜v_x`, `δ˜v_y` and a small value `σ^2_z` for the vertical component.
 - The measurement innovation `y_t` is the difference between the virtual measurement and the predicted velocity in the vehicle frame.
 - A Jacobian `H_t` is derived to relate the error state to the measurement innovation.
 - Standard ES-EKF update equations are applied to compute the posterior error state `δx_t` and covariance `P_t` using the Kalman gain `K_t`.
 - Finally, `δx_t` is injected into the nominal state (`ˆx_t = ˆx_t^- ⊕ δx_t`) to correct position, velocity, biases (additively), and orientation (multiplicatively), and the error state is reset.

**D. Dataset**
The method is evaluated on the publicly available Real-world Vehicle State Estimation Dataset (ReV-StED), which contains five hours of driving data. It includes space-time synchronized measurements from a navigation-grade IMU, an external Correvit velocity sensor, and a comprehensive set of onboard vehicle sensors, alongside centimeter-level accurate ground truth from INS and RTK-based positioning. The onboard sensor signals `o_t` are provided at 50 Hz and include speedometer reading, steering wheel position, yaw rate, wheel speeds (front-left, front-right, rear-left, rear-right), brake pressure, and lateral acceleration.

**E. Experimental Setup**
The ReV-StED dataset is split 70:10:20 for training, validation, and testing. The Mamba model uses `n_k = 4` blocks, sequence length `L = 250`, and latent dimension `d = 256`. Training is performed using Adam optimizer for 25 epochs, with a regularization weight `λ = 4` for the evidential loss. ES-EKF process noise parameters are initialized from sensor specifications and refined on the validation set. Evaluation metrics include Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) for velocity estimation, and maximum position drift for localization accuracy.

### 4. Main findings and results

The evaluation of EVC-Mamba focused on vehicle velocity estimation accuracy, the impact of velocity correction on IMU-based localization, and real-time capability.

**A. Vehicle Velocity Estimation Accuracy**
EVC-Mamba demonstrated the highest velocity estimation accuracy among the evaluated methods using onboard sensor data.
- **Comparison:** Against an OSD-Baseline (kinematic model-based) and a Transformer-based architecture, EVC-Mamba achieved MAE values of 0.03 m/s for longitudinal velocity (`v_x`) and 0.006 m/s for lateral velocity (`v_y`).
- **Performance Improvement:** EVC-Mamba matched the Transformer's performance in `v_x` estimation (both 0.03 m/s MAE) but outperformed it in `v_y` estimation by approximately 30% (0.006 m/s MAE vs. 0.008 m/s MAE for Transformer). This indicates an advantage of the Mamba-SSM for sequential velocity estimation, particularly for the more challenging lateral component.

**B. Uncertainty Calibration**
The reliability of EVC-Mamba's uncertainty estimates was assessed using calibration plots.
- **Longitudinal Velocity Uncertainty (`δ˜v_x`):** The calibration plot for `δ˜v_x` closely followed the diagonal, indicating that the predicted uncertainties were well-calibrated, meaning empirical coverage matched the expected confidence level.
- **Lateral Velocity Uncertainty (`δ˜v_y`):** The calibration plot for `δ˜v_y` exhibited mild underconfidence, where the model was slightly less uncertain than the observed errors. This was attributed in part to the prevalence of near-zero lateral velocities in typical driving scenarios.
- **Correlation with Error:** Pearson correlation coefficients of `r = [0.47, 0.45]` for `δ˜v_x` and `δ˜v_y` respectively, confirmed that the predicted uncertainty meaningfully tracked velocity prediction error.

**C. Accuracy of Inertial Navigation with Velocity Correction**
The impact of EVC-Mamba's velocity correction on IMU localization was evaluated across GNSS outage durations of 2, 5, and 10 minutes, using maximum position drift as the primary metric.
- **No Correction Baseline:** Pure IMU dead reckoning (No Correction) exhibited unbounded error growth, with maximum position drift increasing quadratically (e.g., 7.8 m for 2 min, 31.8 m for 5 min, 142 m for 10 min).
- **Drift Reduction:** The introduction of velocity correction substantially reduced drift. Even the OSD-Baseline (kinematic model) cut maximum drift by approximately 80%, leading to near-linear error growth.
- **EVC-Mamba Performance:** EVC-Mamba achieved the lowest drift among all onboard sensor-based velocity correction methods. For a 5-minute outage, it recorded a maximum position drift of 2.4 ± 1.3 m, compared to 2.9 ± 1.5 m for the Transformer baseline and 4.8 ± 2.1 m for the OSD-Baseline. For a 10-minute outage, EVC-Mamba achieved 5.9 ± 2.0 m, significantly lower than the No Correction baseline.
- **Comparison to External Sensor:** The external Correvit sensor, representing a practical upper bound for hardware-based correction, showed maximum drifts of 0.9 ± 0.5 m (2 min), 2.2 ± 1.2 m (5 min), and 5.4 ± 1.7 m (10 min). EVC-Mamba achieved localization accuracy within approximately 10% of this hardware-based upper bound across all evaluated durations. A qualitative trajectory for a 5-minute outage visually confirmed EVC-Mamba's ability to maintain a trajectory close to the ground truth, significantly outperforming the 'No Correction' baseline and approaching the 'Correvit' performance.

**D. Real-Time Capability**
EVC-Mamba's real-time deployment capability was assessed on an NVIDIA Orin edge device.
- **Inference Latency and Frequency:** It demonstrated an inference latency of 20-24 ms, allowing operation at 40 Hz, which is suitable for real-time onboard deployment.
- **Computational Efficiency:** With 22.8 MFLOPs per inference pass, EVC-Mamba was approximately 3 times more computationally efficient than the Transformer baseline. This reduced complexity was achieved while maintaining superior velocity estimation accuracy.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The EVC-Mamba architecture represents a contribution to the field of autonomous vehicle localization, particularly for scenarios where Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) signals are unavailable. The core significance lies in its ability to provide an accurate, uncertainty-aware, and real-time velocity correction without relying on costly external hardware or dedicated infrastructure.

**Key achievements and their impact:**
- **Hardware-Free Localization in GNSS-Denied Environments:** By transforming readily available onboard vehicle sensor data (e.g., steering angle, wheel speeds) into a virtual velocity sensor, EVC-Mamba eliminates the need for expensive external sensors like Correvit. This reduces the overall cost and complexity of localization systems, making advanced autonomous capabilities more accessible for a wider range of vehicles and applications. This is particularly impactful for operations in urban canyons, tunnels, multi-story car parks, or other electromagnetically shielded environments.
- **Enhanced Robustness through Uncertainty Quantification:** The integration of evidential deep learning provides principled, single-pass quantification of both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties in the velocity estimates. This uncertainty information is crucial for downstream components in autonomous driving systems (e.g., path planning, control) to make informed decisions, especially in situations where sensor data might be ambiguous or unreliable. This mechanism enhances the overall robustness and trustworthiness of the localization solution.
- **Superior Accuracy and Efficiency:** EVC-Mamba demonstrates localization accuracy within 10% of a dedicated hardware sensor, significantly outperforming previous learning-based and kinematic model-based approaches that rely solely on onboard sensors. Furthermore, its Mamba-based State Space Model enables linear-time sequence modeling, resulting in a system that is approximately three times more computationally efficient than Transformer-based alternatives. This efficiency allows for real-time deployment (40 Hz) on edge hardware, which is critical for safety-critical applications in autonomous vehicles.
- **Foundation for Future Development:** The successful integration of efficient sequence modeling (Mamba) with principled uncertainty quantification (Evidential Deep Learning) for a practical automotive problem provides a robust framework. This approach could be adapted for other vehicle state estimation tasks where temporal dynamics and reliability are paramount.

**Potential impact areas:**
- **Autonomous Driving:** Enables safer and more reliable operation of intelligent vehicles in complex and challenging environments where GNSS reception is intermittent or absent.
- **Logistics and Industrial Automation:** Facilitates autonomous navigation of vehicles in indoor environments such as warehouses, factories, or container terminals, which are inherently GNSS-denied.
- **Cost Reduction in Automotive Industry:** Offers a cost-effective alternative to high-precision localization systems, potentially accelerating the adoption of autonomous features in mainstream vehicles.
- **Research and Development:** Provides a benchmark and a new direction for exploring efficient, uncertainty-aware learning architectures in automotive perception and state estimation.

Future work outlined by the authors includes exploring transfer learning across different vehicle platforms and online model adaptation during periods of satellite availability. These directions could further enhance the generality and long-term robustness of the EVC-Mamba system, extending its impact across diverse automotive applications.
