# DLAM: Distributional Latent Actions with Temporal Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, Nanjing University, Shanghai Jiaotong University, Xi’an Jiaotong University, Shenzhen University of Advanced Technology, Amap, Alibaba Group, and Chery Auto developed DLAM, which learns distributional latent action representations from action-free videos by modeling visual transitions as diagonal Gaussian distributions. This approach demonstrated improved temporal consistency and achieved an 87.6% success rate on MetaWorld MT50, a 2.6 percentage point gain over deterministic baselines.

## Problem

- Scarcity and high cost of obtaining action-labeled robot demonstration data for VLA model training.
- Previous latent action models (LAMs) learned reconstruction-centric representations that encoded extraneous visual information, making them suboptimal for generating robot actions.
- Existing structured LAMs used deterministic point estimates for transitions, leading to error propagation and compounding in recursively applied temporal relations over longer horizons.

## Method

- A ==relational encoder== is pretrained on action-free videos to predict ==diagonal Gaussian distributions== for latent actions, representing visual transitions with both means and dimension-wise variances.
- ==Temporal constraints==, including normalized composition and reversal, are extended to supervise both the mean and variance of these distributional transitions, incorporating a lightweight shared scalar correlation coefficient for composition.
- The learned mean transition sequences from the frozen **DLAM** encoder are then used as auxiliary targets to enhance the training of a downstream ==flow-matching== VLA robot control policy, such as π₀.

## Results

- DLAM exhibited superior temporal consistency, maintaining lower composition and reversal residuals over increasing temporal spans (up to **10k frames**) compared to deterministic baselines.
- The method achieved higher reconstruction quality, with an average PSNR of **29.14 dB** for direct and **22.40 dB** for cumulative reconstruction, representing gains of **3.45 dB** and **1.17 dB** respectively over ALAM.
- Pretraining with DLAM led to an **87.6%** average success rate on MetaWorld MT50 and a **73.8%** success rate on real-world manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming deterministic latent action baselines by **2.6 to 10 percentage points**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing visual transitions as diagonal Gaussian distributions allows temporal constraints to supervise both the central tendency and variability of latent actions, leading to more robust representations.
> - Formulating correlation-aware composition for distributional latent actions helps mitigate error propagation and significantly improves temporal consistency over longer spans.
> - Learning these distributional latent action priors from action-free videos effectively improves the performance of robot control policies on diverse manipulation tasks without altering the policy backbone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robot learning, particularly through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, relies on extensive datasets for training policies capable of complex manipulation skills. A significant constraint in this domain is the scarcity and high cost associated with obtaining action-labeled robot demonstration data. In contrast, action-free videos are abundantly available and offer a rich source of observations regarding physical changes in environments.

Latent action models (LAMs) emerged as a method to extract transition priors from these action-free videos by encoding observed visual changes into an abstract "latent action" space. Early LAMs primarily focused on reconstruction-based training, where the latent code is learned to predict future observations. However, a limitation of such reconstruction-centric approaches is that the learned latent representations may encode extraneous visual information (e.g., camera motion, background dynamics) that aids prediction but is not directly relevant or optimally structured for generating robot actions. This can lead to representations that are predictive but not necessarily suitable for joint generation with robot policies.

To address this, structured LAMs (e.g., AC-LAM, ALAM, RotVLA) have introduced explicit temporal constraints such as composition, reversal, inverse, or cycle relations. These constraints impose algebraic structure on the latent space, aiming to make the representations more organized and meaningful for control. A common characteristic of these structured methods is their reliance on deterministic point estimates for each transition. This deterministic nature means that each temporal relation operates on a single estimate. Consequently, any residual errors in locally inferred transitions can propagate and compound when these relations are recursively applied over longer temporal horizons, potentially limiting the reliability of long-horizon planning and policy execution.

DLAM (Distributional Latent Actions with Temporal Constraints) is positioned as an advancement in this landscape by addressing the deterministic limitation of prior structured LAMs. Instead of representing transitions as single points, DLAM models them as diagonal Gaussian distributions. This shift allows temporal constraints to supervise not only the mean (center) of the transition but also its dimension-wise variance (dispersion). By incorporating distributional information and refined temporal consistency constraints, DLAM aims to learn more robust, temporally consistent, and control-relevant latent action representations from action-free video data, thereby improving their utility for downstream robot policy learning. The work aligns with the broader push towards "World Action Models" that directly connect representations of visual dynamics with executable actions, specifically focusing on enhancing the transition representation learned from action-free videos without altering the downstream VLA backbone.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective method for learning latent action representations from action-free videos that are suitable for integration into and improvement of downstream robot control policies. The work seeks to overcome specific limitations observed in existing latent action models.

The key objectives and motivations can be detailed as follows:

- **Address the scarcity of action-labeled robot data:** A fundamental motivation for this work, and for latent action models in general, is the high cost and labor involved in collecting large datasets of robot demonstrations with explicit action labels. Action-free videos, which are far more abundant, represent a valuable but under-leveraged resource for learning about physical changes and potential actions in the world. The objective is to extract meaningful action-like priors from this readily available data.
- **Improve the structure and utility of latent action representations:** Previous latent action models, particularly those trained solely on reconstruction objectives, may learn representations that are good for predicting future frames but poorly aligned with the requirements for generating robot actions. The objective is to imbue these latent representations with a structure that is more directly relevant and useful for control.
- **Mitigate error propagation in structured latent action models:** Existing structured latent action models, while improving upon reconstruction-only methods by imposing temporal constraints (e.g., composition, reversal), treat each transition as a deterministic point. This deterministic approach implies that errors in individual transition estimates can accumulate when these relations are applied recursively over longer sequences. A core objective of DLAM is to move beyond deterministic point estimates to distributional representations.
- **Represent visual transitions as distributions:** The central objective is to model each visual transition not as a single latent vector, but as a diagonal Gaussian distribution. This allows for a more nuanced representation of the transition, where the mean captures the observed change and the variance provides an auxiliary signal related to the dispersion of this change.
- **Formulate temporal constraints for distributional transitions:** With distributional representations, the objective is to extend traditional temporal constraints (composition and reversal) to operate on both the mean and the dimension-wise variance of the Gaussian transitions. This includes:
 - **Normalized Composition:** Defining how the means and variances of two adjacent transitions compose to form a direct transition over a longer span. A specific objective is to account for potential dependencies between adjacent transitions that share an intermediate frame, leading to the formulation of a correlation-aware variance composition.
 - **Reversal:** Defining how a transition's distribution changes when its direction is reversed, specifically by negating the mean while preserving the variance.
- **Enhance temporal consistency and reconstruction quality:** By applying these distributional temporal constraints during pretraining, the model is motivated to learn latent representations that exhibit improved consistency in their temporal relations (e.g., composition and reversal residuals) and better preserve information for accurate reconstruction of target frames, both directly and cumulatively over multiple steps.
- **Improve downstream robot policy performance:** Ultimately, the motivation is to demonstrate that these improved latent action representations, learned from action-free videos, can effectively enhance the performance of robot control policies on various manipulation tasks. The objective is to show that freezing the DLAM encoder and using its learned mean transition sequences as auxiliary targets for a VLA policy (e.g., a flow-matching policy like $\pi_0$) leads to superior task success compared to baselines.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need to leverage abundant action-free video data more effectively for robot learning, specifically by developing a more expressive and robust latent action representation that can capture temporal dynamics with greater fidelity and propagate errors more gracefully, leading to improved robot control.

### 3. Methodology and approach

DLAM operates in two main phases: a pretraining phase where distributional latent actions are learned from action-free videos, and a transfer phase where the learned representations enhance downstream robot policy learning.

#### 3.1. Distributional Latent-Action Pretraining

The pretraining phase aims to learn an encoder that maps visual observations to distributional latent actions and a decoder for reconstruction.

- **Input Data:** The model is trained on action-free videos by sampling equally spaced triplets of frames ($O_a, O_b, O_c$) where $b-a = c-b = k$ (a fixed frame gap), and backward pairs ($O_b, O_a$). No action, language, or proprioceptive labels are utilized.
- **Relational Encoder ($E_{\phi}$):** For an ordered pair of frames ($O_i, O_j$), the encoder $E_{\phi}$ predicts $K$ transition tokens. Each token $\kappa$ is represented as a diagonal Gaussian distribution $N(\mu_{j}^{i, \kappa}, \text{diag}((\sigma_{j}^{i, \kappa})^2))$.
 - The encoder outputs the mean $\mu_{j}^{i}$ and raw log-variance $\tilde{\ell}_{j}^{i}$, both of shape $K \times d$ (where $d$ is token width).
 - The raw log-variance is clipped to $[\ell_{min}, \ell_{max}]$ and then exponentiated to obtain the element-wise standard deviation $\sigma_{j}^{i} = \exp(\frac{1}{2} \ell_{j}^{i})$.
 - The predicted standard deviation contributes an auxiliary training signal via temporal constraints and a prior, but is not intended for calibrated uncertainty estimation of unseen futures.
- **Source-Conditioned Decoder ($D_{\omega}$):** This decoder reconstructs the target frame $\hat{O}_{i,j}$ from the source frame $O_i$ and the stacked transition means $\mu_{j}^{i}$. Crucially, only the mean is used for reconstruction, not posterior samples or the variance.

The pretraining objective combines several loss components:

- **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$):** An $\ell_2$ loss is applied between the reconstructed target frame $\hat{O}_{i,j}$ and the actual target frame $O_j$, averaged over pixels and channels. This grounds the latent mean in observed visual change.
- **Prior Loss ($L_{prior}$):** A Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence is used to regularize each forward posterior $q_{j}^{i, \kappa}$ towards a factorized standard Gaussian prior $N(0, I_d)$. This is applied per token and utilizes the free-nats floor.
- **Temporal Constraints on Mean and Variance:** These constraints are applied at the token level for equal-gap triplets and backward pairs.
 - **Normalized Composition:** For a triplet ($O_a, O_b, O_c$), the composition $Z_{a \leadsto c} = \frac{Z_{b}^{a} + Z_{c}^{b}}{\sqrt{2}}$ is defined. The composed mean $\bar{\mu}_{c}^{a} = \frac{\mu_{b}^{a} + \mu_{c}^{b}}{\sqrt{2}}$ is derived directly. The composed variance $(\bar{\sigma}_{c}^{a})^2 = \frac{(\sigma_{b}^{a})^2 + (\sigma_{c}^{b})^2}{2} + \rho \sigma_{b}^{a} \odot \sigma_{c}^{b}$ incorporates a lightweight shared scalar correlation coefficient $\rho$. This $\rho$ is learned (derived from a scalar `r` via $\rho_{max} \tanh(r)$) to account for dependence between adjacent transitions sharing the intermediate frame $O_b$. This defines a pairwise normalized relation, not an associative composition law. The **Composition Loss ($L_{comp}$)** is a mean-and-log-variance discrepancy $D(q_{c}^{a}, \bar{q}_{c}^{a})$ between the directly encoded posterior and the composed posterior.
 - **Reversal:** The reversal operator $R[\cdot]$ negates the mean while preserving the variance of a Gaussian distribution: $R[N(\mu, \text{diag}(\sigma^2))] = N(-\mu, \text{diag}(\sigma^2))$. The **Reversal Loss ($L_{rev}$)** is a mean-and-log-variance discrepancy $D(q_{b}^{a}, R[q_{a}^{b}])$ between the forward and transformed backward posteriors.
- **Total Pretraining Objective ($L_{DLAM}$):** A weighted sum of $L_{rec}, L_{prior}, L_{comp}, L_{rev}$. The weights ($\lambda_s$) are hyperparameters.

#### 3.2. Transfer to World Action Modeling

After pretraining, the distributional latent actions are transferred to a robot control policy:

- **Frozen Encoder:** The pretrained encoder $E_{\phi}$ is frozen, and the decoder is discarded.
- **Latent Target Extraction:** For a given demonstration, the frozen encoder extracts mean transition sequences ($\mu_h^m$) from $H$ consecutive multi-view frame pairs (e.g., $I_t^m, I_{t+1}^m$). Only the mean is used as the downstream latent target; the log-variance is not passed to the policy.
- **Policy Backbone:** The research utilizes the $\pi_0$ policy framework, which consists of a PaliGemma-2B vision-language backbone and a Gemma-300M action expert.
- **Joint Flow Matching:** The policy is trained using joint flow matching to simultaneously generate (denoise) both the view-specific latent action trajectories (using the extracted $\mu_h^m$ as targets) and the executable robot action trajectories. The loss function $L_{transfer} = \lambda_u L_{u}^{FM} + \sum_{m=1}^{M} \lambda_m L_{m}^{FM}$ represents the flow-matching losses for the executable action stream and each latent stream, respectively.
- **Inference:** During inference, the policy generates both streams, but only the robot action stream is executed on the robot.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Action-free pretraining:** Utilized a mixture of 11 robot-video datasets, primarily from Open X-Embodiment and CALVIN. Trained for 57 epochs on 64 AMD MI308X GPUs.
- **Policy transfer:** All variants were transferred to the same $\pi_0$ policy, updating only the policy backbone, action expert, and projection layers.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluated on MetaWorld MT50 (50 diverse manipulation tasks), LIBERO (manipulation generalization suites), and four real-world manipulation tasks using a Piper 6-DoF arm.
- **Baselines:** Compared against reconstruction-only LAM, deterministic ALAM, and various established VLA models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, π_0).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Temporal Consistency:** Scale-normalized composition and reversal residuals ($R_{\mu}^{comp}, R_{\mu}^{rev}$) using a length-aware probe over increasing temporal spans (up to 10k frames), measuring consistency beyond direct supervision.
 - **Reconstruction Quality:** PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS for direct and cumulative reconstruction on held-out transitions.
 - **Downstream Control:** Task success rates.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents findings across three main areas: evaluation of transition representations, reconstruction quality, and downstream policy learning performance. Ablation studies further isolate the contributions of specific DLAM components.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Transition Representations

- **Temporal-span diagnostics:** DLAM demonstrated improved temporal consistency compared to deterministic baselines. On held-out transitions, DLAM maintained consistently lower composition and reversal residuals ($R_{\mu}^{comp}$ and $R_{\mu}^{rev}$) across increasing temporal spans (from 3k to 10k frames). For ALAM, these residuals showed a marked increase with span, whereas DLAM's residuals degraded only mildly. This suggests that DLAM's representations are more robust to longer temporal compositions under the evaluated diagnostic.
- **Latent transition visualization:** Visualizations showed that ALAM maps each transition to a single point, while DLAM predicts a mean and an element-wise standard deviation, illustrating the difference in their representational capacity.

#### 4.2. Direct and Cumulative Reconstruction

- DLAM achieved higher absolute reconstruction quality compared to baselines. Across 3k–5k temporal spans, DLAM's average PSNR values were 29.14 dB for direct reconstruction and 22.40 dB for cumulative reconstruction. This represents an improvement of 3.45 dB and 1.17 dB over ALAM for direct and cumulative reconstruction, respectively.
- Improvements were also observed in perceptual metrics: DLAM reduced LPIPS by 45.6% for direct reconstruction and 26.1% for cumulative composition, alongside consistent gains in SSIM. These results indicate that the learned mean transitions effectively retain information necessary for accurate endpoint reconstruction over the evaluated spans.

#### 4.3. Downstream Policy Learning

DLAM's learned latent means consistently improved policy performance across various robot manipulation benchmarks.

- **MetaWorld MT50:** Under the controlled $\pi_0$ transfer protocol, DLAM achieved an average success rate of 87.6%. This is a 2.6 percentage point improvement over the deterministic ALAM baseline and a 39.7 percentage point improvement over the action-only $\pi_0$ policy. The most substantial tier-level gain over ALAM was observed on Very-Hard tasks, where success increased from 82.0% to 91.3%.
- **LIBERO:** DLAM reached a 99.0% average success rate on LIBERO, showing improvements across all four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). The largest gain was on LIBERO-Long tasks, where it improved the deterministic baseline by 2.7 percentage points.
- **Real-world Manipulation:** On four real-world tasks using a Piper 6-DoF arm (insert cylinder, insert cube, arrange flowers, hang cup), $\pi_0$ + DLAM achieved an average success rate of 73.8%. This compares to 63.8% for $\pi_0$ + ALAM, 53.8% for $\pi_{0.5}$, and 40.0% for $\pi_0$. Specifically, DLAM improved over the deterministic transition baseline by 10 percentage points on each of the four real-world tasks.

These results indicate that pretraining with the proposed constraints on transition means and variances produces mean representations that are effective for policy transfer in both simulation and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

A controlled ablation study analyzed the contribution of different DLAM components:

- **No temporal relations:** Baseline without composition or reversal constraints, but still using a Gaussian encoder and prior.
- **Matched mean-only:** Normalized mean composition and reversal, but fixed $\sigma=1$ and $\rho=0$.
- **Learned variance ($\rho=0$):** Predicted variance is restored, but adjacent transitions are propagated independently ($\rho=0$).
- **Full DLAM:** Includes learned variance and correlation-aware composition (learned $\rho$).

The findings were:
- Normalized mean constraints (comparing "No temporal relations" to "Matched mean-only") contributed substantially to the reconstruction gain, increasing cumulative PSNR from 21.236 dB to 22.109 dB, and improved downstream success from 76.6% to 82.1%.
- Learning the variance (comparing "Matched mean-only" to "Learned variance ($\rho=0$)") further improved downstream success to 85.3% while maintaining comparable reconstruction quality and slightly reducing relation residuals.
- Introducing the shared coupling coefficient for correlation-aware composition (comparing "Learned variance ($\rho=0$)" to "Full DLAM") yielded the best results across all metrics: 22.400 dB PSNR, composition and reversal residuals of 1.1662 and 1.0010 respectively, and 87.6% downstream success.

These results indicate the complementary contributions of normalized mean constraints, learned variance, and correlation-aware composition to both reconstruction quality and downstream control performance.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The introduction of DLAM represents an advancement in learning robust latent action representations from action-free videos, with several potential impacts on the field of robot learning and control.

- **Enhanced Representation of Visual Dynamics:** By extending structured latent actions from deterministic point estimates to diagonal Gaussian distributions, DLAM enables a more expressive representation of visual transitions. This allows temporal constraints to supervise both the central tendency (mean) and the inherent variability (dimension-wise variance) of an action, providing a richer signal during learning. This is a methodological step beyond prior deterministic structured latent action models.
- **Improved Temporal Consistency and Long-Horizon Planning:** The formulation of normalized composition and reversal constraints for distributional transitions, particularly the inclusion of a lightweight shared-correlation coefficient for variance composition, contributes to learning more temporally consistent latent dynamics. The observed lower and less degrading residuals over longer temporal spans suggest that DLAM's representations may be more suitable for recursive composition and thus for planning or reasoning over extended horizons in robot tasks, which is a significant challenge in robot control.
- **More Effective Leveraging of Action-Free Data:** The work directly addresses the fundamental challenge of data scarcity in robot learning by demonstrating an improved method for extracting control-relevant priors from readily available action-free video data. This increases the utility of a vast, inexpensive data source, potentially reducing the reliance on costly, human-labeled demonstrations.
- **Versatile and Non-intrusive Policy Integration:** DLAM’s transfer mechanism is designed to be compatible with existing vision-language-action (VLA) policy architectures. By freezing the pretrained encoder and using only its posterior-mean transition sequences as auxiliary targets for flow-matching policies, DLAM enhances control without requiring modifications to the core policy backbone or its execution interface. This allows for straightforward integration into current and future VLA systems.
- **Demonstrated Practical Efficacy:** The consistent performance improvements across diverse and challenging benchmarks—MetaWorld MT50, LIBERO, and real-world manipulation tasks—underscore the practical effectiveness of DLAM. The observed gains in success rates across these varied settings indicate that the learned distributional latent actions contribute meaningfully to robot task completion.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** DLAM opens avenues for further research into sophisticated representations of visual dynamics. Future work could explore more complex covariance structures beyond diagonal Gaussians, context- or dimension-dependent correlation modeling, and robust composition laws that might generalize even better to arbitrarily long horizons. The variance signal, while not used for calibrated uncertainty in this work, could potentially be explored for uncertainty-aware planning or adaptive control in future systems.
