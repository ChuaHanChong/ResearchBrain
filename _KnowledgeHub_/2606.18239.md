# EBench: Elemental Diagnosis of Generalist Mobile Manipulation Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> EBench provides a unified simulation benchmark that diagnoses generalist mobile manipulation policies using a multi-dimensional evaluation, going beyond aggregate success rates. It reveals specific capability profiles and generalization behaviors of Vision-Language-Action models across tasks involving long-horizon planning, dexterous manipulation, and mobile robot navigation.

## Problem

- Current evaluation of general-purpose manipulation policies relies on aggregate success rates from simulation suites, which are often narrow in scope or lack detailed analysis beyond a single scalar.
- Existing benchmarks fail to provide fine-grained insights into where a model excels or fails, or how its performance changes under varying deployment distributions.
- No single benchmark simultaneously offers a broad task suite encompassing long-horizon, dexterous-and-precise, and mobile manipulation while also allowing for fine-grained, multi-dimensional analysis.

## Method

- Developed **EBench**, an open-source simulation benchmark with 26 diverse manipulation tasks spanning mobile pick-and-place, long-horizon multi-stage, and tabletop dexterous-and-precise categories.
- Tasks are manually annotated along ==five capability dimensions== (scene, atomic skill, temporal horizon, precision, operating mode) to enable fine-grained diagnostic profiling.
- Introduced four controlled generalization dimensions (unseen backgrounds, unseen objects, paraphrased instructions, and their mixture) with asset-level isolation to systematically evaluate robustness to out-of-distribution shifts.

## Results

- Aggregate test success rates for state-of-the-art VLA models ranged narrowly from **24.4%** to **29.5%**, but their underlying capabilities diverged, with 𝜋0.5 showing the highest test SR and InternVLA-A1 exhibiting strong in-distribution performance but weaker generalization.
- Generalization studies revealed a hierarchy of difficulty: background and instruction perturbations were mild (27–**35%** SR), object swaps posed a stronger challenge (21–**29%** SR), and mixed shifts were the most difficult (18–**23%** SR).
- EBench identified five 'universally hard tasks' (e.g., `peg_in_hole`, `flip_cup_collect_cookies`) where all evaluated models achieved ≤ **5%** success rate, representing current frontiers for generalist policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalist manipulation models with similar aggregate success rates exhibit significantly different underlying capability profiles and generalization behaviors when diagnosed across multiple dimensions.
> - Different Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models show varied proficiencies, such as one excelling in mobile manipulation but failing at dexterous tasks, while another demonstrates stronger temporal credit assignment.
> - Large-scale pretraining consistently provides substantial performance gains (9-21 SR points) on EBench, unlike other benchmarks where from-scratch models often perform comparably.
