# LLMs are Greedy Agents: Effects of RL Fine-tuning on Decision-Making Abilities

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind researchers systematically analyze three key failure modes of LLMs in decision-making tasks (greediness, frequency bias, and knowing-doing gap), demonstrating how reinforcement learning fine-tuning with chain-of-thought reasoning can improve exploration and decision-making capabilities across multi-armed bandits, contextual bandits, and game-playing environments.

## Problem

- LLMs exhibit poor exploration and premature commitment to suboptimal actions in decision-making scenarios
- There exists a gap between LLMs' ability to reason about decisions and their actual action choices
- Smaller LLMs tend to copy frequent actions regardless of rewards, showing strong frequency bias

## Method

- Implement ==reinforcement learning fine-tuning== (RLFT) with chain-of-thought reasoning
- Introduce exploration mechanisms like try-all and exploration bonus
- Leverage expert data and increased generation budgets for better decision-making

## Results

- Successfully identified and quantified three specific LLM failure modes in decision-making
- Demonstrated improved exploration and decision-making through RLFT across multiple environments
- Showed that expert data significantly enhances decision-making performance
- Established the importance of both classic RL and LLM-specific exploration techniques

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chain-of-thought reasoning is critical for effective RLFT and decision-making
> - The knowing-doing gap can be narrowed through careful reward shaping
> - Exploration mechanisms significantly improve performance compared to greedy approaches
> - Increased "thinking time" (generation budget) leads to better decisions

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **LLMs as Agents:** This paper directly addresses the growing interest in using Large Language Models (LLMs) as agents capable of decision-making. This is a rapidly evolving area, building upon the successes of LLMs in text generation and language understanding.
- **Limitations of LLMs in Decision-Making:** The paper identifies specific limitations of LLMs in decision-making scenarios, namely, greediness, frequency bias, and the knowing-doing gap. This is an important contribution, as it moves beyond simply applying LLMs to decision-making and instead focuses on understanding their shortcomings.
- **Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning (RLFT):** The study uses RLFT as a method for improving LLMs' decision-making abilities. RLFT is a popular technique in the broader field of LLMs and aligns with other work exploring how to tailor LLMs for specific tasks and improve their reasoning capabilities. It leverages the strengths of RL in learning optimal policies through interaction with an environment.
- **Exploration Strategies:** The paper investigates both classic RL exploration mechanisms (e.g., ε-greedy) and LLM-specific approaches (e.g., self-consistency) for enabling more effective fine-tuning of LLMs for decision-making. This fits into a wider discussion about how to adapt existing RL techniques to the unique characteristics of LLMs.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** The study emphasizes the importance of CoT reasoning for decision-making, further cementing CoT as a crucial ingredient in many successful LLM applications.
- **Connection to Existing Work:** The paper directly references and builds upon previous research, such as the work of Krishnamurthy et al. (2024) and Nie et al. (2024) on exploration in LLMs, and Paglieri et al. (2024) and Ruoss et al. (2024) on the knowing-doing gap. This contextualizes the current work within the ongoing conversation in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To systematically understand why LLMs often perform sub-optimally in simple decision-making scenarios.
- **Motivation:**
 - The increasing interest in using LLMs for agentic applications and decision-making problems.
 - The observed limitations of LLMs in exploration and the knowing-doing gap, hindering their effectiveness as agents.
 - The desire to improve the decision-making abilities of LLMs by addressing their specific failure modes.
 - To bridge the gap between the knowledge present in LLMs and their ability to effectively act on that knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Failure Mode Analysis:** The authors systematically examine three prevalent failure modes in small-to-medium-scale LLMs: greediness, frequency bias, and the knowing-doing gap. They design experiments to isolate and quantify each of these failure modes.
- **Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RLFT):** The core method involves fine-tuning pre-trained Gemma2 models using RL on self-generated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales. The RLFT pipeline aims to enhance the decision-making abilities of LLMs by increasing exploration and narrowing the knowing-doing gap.
- **Environments:** The experiments are conducted in three environments:
 - Multi-armed bandits (MABs): Used to study the exploration-exploitation trade-off.
 - Contextual bandits (CBs): Introduce state representations at every interaction step.
 - Textual Tic-tac-toe: A stateful environment with proper state transitions.
- **Exploration Mechanisms:** The study evaluates a variety of exploration mechanisms, including both "classic" RL approaches (e.g., ε-greedy) and LLM-specific techniques (e.g., self-correction, self-consistency).
- **Ablation Studies:** The paper includes ablation studies to investigate the importance of various components of the approach, such as CoT reasoning, the use of expert data, and the effect of the generation budget (the number of tokens allowed for reasoning).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Greediness:** LLMs exhibit greediness by prematurely committing to a small set of actions, leading to poor exploration of the action space.
- **Frequency Bias:** Smaller LLMs (2B) tend to copy the most frequent actions in the context, regardless of their reward.
- **Knowing-Doing Gap:** LLMs often know how to solve a task (generate correct rationales) but fail to act on that knowledge, prioritizing greedy actions.
- **RLFT Effectiveness:** RL fine-tuning enhances the decision-making abilities of LLMs by increasing exploration and narrowing the knowing-doing gap.
- **Exploration Mechanisms:** Exploration mechanisms like try-all and exploration bonus have significant performance improvements.
- **CoT Importance:** CoT reasoning is critical for effective decision-making and RLFT.
- **Expert Data:** Expert data can significantly improve decision-making performance.
- **Thinking Time:** Increasing the generation budget ("thinking" time) improves performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Understanding LLM Limitations:** The paper provides a valuable systematic analysis of the limitations of LLMs in decision-making, identifying specific failure modes. This contributes to a more nuanced understanding of LLMs beyond their capabilities in text generation.
- **Improving LLM Agents:** The study proposes and evaluates RLFT as a method for improving the decision-making abilities of LLMs. This contributes to the development of more effective LLM agents for various applications.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The findings regarding exploration mechanisms and the importance of CoT and expert data provide valuable guidance for future research in this area. The paper identifies promising avenues for improving LLM agents, such as better exploration strategies, more effective reward shaping, and leveraging expert knowledge.
- **Practical Applications:** The research has potential implications for various practical applications of LLM agents, including:
 - Recommendation systems
 - Robotics
 - Game playing
 - Autonomous navigation
- **Highlighting the Importance of Reward Shaping:** The positive impact of the "exploration bonus" highlights the need to carefully design reward functions to encourage desired behaviors during RLFT.

In conclusion, this research paper makes a significant contribution to the field by systematically analyzing the limitations of LLMs in decision-making and proposing RLFT as a method for improvement. The findings provide valuable insights into the behavior of LLMs and guide future research towards developing more capable and effective LLM agents.
