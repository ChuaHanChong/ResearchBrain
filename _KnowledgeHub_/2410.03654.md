# Learning Humanoid Locomotion over Challenging Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley developed a learning-based control system, combining sequence modeling pre-training with reinforcement learning fine-tuning, that enables a bipedal robot to traverse diverse and difficult terrains previously challenging for humanoids. This single controller, deployed on a Digit humanoid robot, successfully navigates steep streets up to 31% grade, rough hiking trails for over 4 miles, and loose sand without terrain-specific tuning.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion over challenging and unpredictable terrains is difficult due to their inherent instability.
- Existing control methods either struggle with the complexities of natural terrain (model-based) or are limited to gentle terrains (learning-based).
- Developing a single controller capable of robustly navigating diverse and extreme terrains for bipedal robots remained an open challenge.

## Method

- A transformer network serves as the core control system, processing proprioceptive observations to output target joint positions and PD gains.
- A two-stage training methodology is introduced: pre-training with sequence modeling on flat-ground walking data.
- The pre-trained model is then fine-tuned using reinforcement learning in simulation environments with diverse challenging terrains and domain randomization.

## Results

- The Digit humanoid robot successfully traversed diverse real-world terrains, including steep San Francisco streets (up to **31%** grade), over 4 miles of hiking trails, and previously unseen loose beach sand.
- The proposed two-stage approach (HT-2) demonstrated superior performance over prior methods, achieving higher success rates on challenging terrains and improved gait stability and energy efficiency.
- The controller exhibited emergent adaptive behaviors, such as implicitly learning terrain representations and automatically adjusting its gait and posture based on terrain properties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single learning-based controller can effectively handle a wide range of diverse and challenging terrains for bipedal robots.
> - Combining sequence modeling pre-training with reinforcement learning fine-tuning substantially improves sample efficiency and learning stability.
> - The controller can implicitly learn to recognize different terrain types and adapt its gait and posture solely from proprioceptive feedback, without explicit terrain information.
