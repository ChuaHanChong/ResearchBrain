# YOLOE: Real-Time Seeing Anything

> [!summary] Summary
> YOLOE, a real-time object detection and segmentation model from Tsinghua University, unifies text, visual, and prompt-free inputs to identify and segment arbitrary objects with high efficiency. It surpasses YOLO-Worldv2-S by 3.5 AP on LVIS with 3x less training cost and achieves significant speedups while outperforming existing generative prompt-free methods with 53x faster inference.

## Problem

- Existing object detection models, especially real-time ones like YOLO, are limited to predefined categories, lacking adaptability in open-world scenarios.
- Current open-set detection and segmentation methods suffer from high computational costs, complex deployment, or specialization in only one or two prompt types.
- A critical need exists for a single, highly efficient model that integrates detection and segmentation across all diverse open prompt mechanisms (text, visual, and prompt-free) while maintaining real-time performance.

## Method

- **YOLOE** builds upon the efficient YOLO architecture, unifying text, visual, and prompt-free inputs through a ==shared object embedding== and classification head where prompt embeddings act as classification weights.
- For text prompts, a ==Re-parameterizable Region-Text Alignment (RepRTA)== refines textual embeddings during training and collapses into the classification head for ==zero inference overhead==.
- A ==Semantic-Activated Visual Prompt Encoder (SAVPE)== efficiently processes visual cues with decoupled semantic and activation branches, while ==Lazy Region-Prompt Contrast (LRPC)== reformulates prompt-free detection as an efficient ==retrieval problem==, avoiding costly generative language models.

## Results

- YOLOE-v8-S achieves **3.5 AP** higher than YOLO-Worldv2-S on LVIS, with **3x** less training cost and **1.4x** inference speedup on T4 GPU, while YOLOE-v8-L gains **7.6% AP** for rare categories (APr).
- In prompt-free scenarios, YOLOE-v8-L reaches **27.2 AP** and **23.5 APr**, outperforming GenerateU by **0.4 AP** and **3.5 APr** with **6.3x** fewer parameters and **53x** faster inference speed.
- YOLOE demonstrates strong zero-shot instance segmentation, with YOLOE-v8-M/L achieving **20.8** and **23.5 AP_m** on LVIS, surpassing YOLO-Worldv2-M/L by **3.0** and **3.7 AP_m** respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Open-set capabilities can be integrated into real-time architectures like YOLO with minimal overhead by re-parameterizing auxiliary networks into existing inference paths.
> - Decoupling feature extraction and prompt-aware activation in visual prompt processing enables efficient handling of diverse visual cues without complex transformer designs.
> - Prompt-free detection can be efficiently achieved via a 'lazy' retrieval mechanism that filters anchor points with a generic object embedding before contrasting with a large vocabulary, avoiding the high costs of generative language models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object detection and segmentation are cornerstone tasks in computer vision, with pervasive applications ranging from autonomous driving and medical analysis to robotics. Traditional methods, particularly the widely adopted YOLO series, have achieved impressive real-time performance. However, a fundamental limitation of these conventional models is their reliance on predefined object categories, making them inflexible and non-adaptable in open, dynamic real-world scenarios.

The broader research landscape has increasingly shifted towards "open-set" or "open-vocabulary" object detection and segmentation. These advanced methods aim to enable models to identify and segment arbitrary objects, including those not encountered during training, by leveraging diverse forms of user input or "prompts." These prompts typically fall into three categories:
1. **Text Prompts:** Users provide natural language descriptions (e.g., "red car," "person with a hat") to guide object identification. Pioneering works like GLIP, Grounding DINO, and more recently, YOLO-World, have integrated vision-language pre-training to align visual features with textual embeddings, achieving recognition for novel categories.
2. **Visual Prompts:** Users provide visual cues, such as a bounding box around an example object or a point indicating an object, to prompt the model to find similar instances. Methods like OV-DETR, T-Rex2, and DINOv utilize visual encoders or transformer-heavy designs to process these cues.
3. **Prompt-Free Scenario:** In this most challenging setting, the model is expected to autonomously identify all salient objects in an image and provide their category names without explicit user prompts. Existing approaches often rely on large language models (LLMs) to generate descriptions for detected regions, as seen in GenerateU and GRiT.

Despite significant advancements, a critical gap remains: a single, highly efficient model that can seamlessly integrate detection and segmentation across *all three diverse open prompt mechanisms* (text, visual, and prompt-free) while maintaining real-time performance and low computational overhead. Current solutions often suffer from trade-offs:
- **Performance vs. Efficiency:** Many open-set models, especially those involving complex cross-modality fusion or large language models, incur substantial computational demands for training and inference, hindering real-time deployment, particularly on edge devices.
- **Deployment Complexity:** Transformer-heavy designs or reliance on additional external encoders can make models difficult to deploy in resource-constrained environments.
- **Single-Modality Focus:** Most existing works specialize in one or two prompt types, requiring multiple separate models or highly complex unified architectures (like DINO-X, which still has high resource costs).

YOLOE positions itself directly to address this gap. By building upon the highly efficient YOLO architecture, it aims to create a unified, real-time, and easily deployable solution that supports "seeing anything" across text, visual, and prompt-free paradigms, thus setting a new benchmark for practical open-world vision systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **YOLOE (YOLO for Everything)**, a novel, highly efficient, unified, and open object detection and segmentation model. The central aim is to enable YOLOE to perform "real-time seeing anything" across diverse open prompt mechanisms: text, visual cues, and a prompt-free paradigm.

The motivation behind developing YOLOE stems from several critical challenges and demands in modern computer vision applications:

- **Bridging the Gap between Efficiency and Open-Set Capabilities:** Traditional YOLO models are renowned for their speed but are confined to predefined categories. The motivation is to extend this real-time efficiency to open-set scenarios, allowing detection and segmentation of arbitrary objects.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Open-Set Models:** Current open-set detectors and segmenters, while innovative, often grapple with significant drawbacks:
 - **High Computational Cost:** Many state-of-the-art methods rely on resource-intensive operations (e.g., large transformer models, complex cross-modality fusion, or external large language models), leading to slow inference speeds and high training costs. This limits their practicality for real-time applications and deployment on edge devices.
 - **Deployment Complexity:** Architectural complexity, including multiple specialized encoders or heavy dependencies, makes integration into real-world systems cumbersome.
 - **Lack of Unification:** Most models focus on a single type of prompt. While some unified architectures exist (e.g., DINO-X), they typically come with a substantial increase in training and inference overhead. The motivation is to provide a truly unified solution without such performance compromises.
- **Meeting the Demand for Adaptability:** Practical applications increasingly require vision models that can adapt to novel objects and user-defined categories on the fly, without needing extensive retraining. This demands flexibility in how users can interact with and guide the model.
- **Leveraging YOLO's Proven Efficacy:** The YOLO series has consistently demonstrated strong performance and efficiency. The motivation is to build upon this established foundation, integrating cutting-edge open-set capabilities while retaining the core advantages of YOLO.

In essence, YOLOE is motivated by the desire to deliver a practical, high-performance, and versatile object detection and segmentation system that can understand and react to various forms of human intent or operate autonomously in an unconstrained environment, all while adhering to the stringent demands of real-time efficiency and deployability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

YOLOE is architecturally built upon the widely recognized and efficient YOLO models, specifically leveraging the YOLOv8 and YOLO11 architectures. It integrates novel strategies for each prompt mechanism into a unified framework, allowing the model to perform both detection and segmentation.

**Core Architecture:**
YOLOE adopts the standard YOLO architecture components:
- **Backbone and PAN (Path Aggregation Network):** Extract multi-scale image features.
- **Regression Head:** Predicts bounding boxes for detected objects.
- **Segmentation Head:** Produces prototype masks and mask coefficients for instance segmentation (following YOLACT principles).
- **Object Embedding Head:** A modified classification head that outputs a high-dimensional object embedding for each anchor point, rather than a fixed class score.

The core idea for unification is that all prompt mechanisms (text, visual, prompt-free) are processed to generate **normalized prompt embeddings (P)**. These prompt embeddings then serve as classification weights, which are contrasted with the anchor points' **object embeddings (O)** to determine object categories: `Label = O ⋅ P^T`.

**Specific Strategies for Diverse Prompt Mechanisms:**

1. **Re-parameterizable Region-Text Alignment (RepRTA) for Text Prompts:**
 - **Problem:** Traditional cross-modality fusion for text-visual alignment can be computationally expensive, especially with large vocabularies.
 - **Approach:** RepRTA aims to refine pre-trained textual embeddings for better alignment with visual features with zero inference and transferring overhead.
 - **Text Encoding:** Utilizes a pre-trained, lightweight text encoder (MobileCLIP-B(LT)) to obtain initial textual embeddings. For efficiency, all textual embeddings from datasets are pre-cached, allowing the text encoder to be removed during training and inference.
 - **Auxiliary Network:** A lightweight auxiliary network `f_θ` (a single SwiGLU FFN block) refines these pre-cached textual embeddings to `P = f_θ(P_initial)`. This network is trained to enhance visual-semantic alignment with minimal overhead during the training phase.
 - **Zero Overhead Inference:** Crucially, after training, this auxiliary network is seamlessly "re-parameterized" into the classification head's final convolution layer. This means the inference architecture becomes identical to a standard YOLO classification head, introducing no additional computational cost or complexity for deployment or transfer to downstream closed-set tasks.

2. **Semantic-Activated Visual Prompt Encoder (SAVPE) for Visual Prompts:**
 - **Problem:** Prior visual prompt methods often use transformer-heavy designs or external vision encoders, leading to deployment challenges and inefficiency.
 - **Approach:** SAVPE efficiently processes visual cues (formalized as masks) using two decoupled, lightweight branches:
 - **Semantic Branch:** Extracts prompt-agnostic semantic features from the multi-scale PAN features (P3, P4, P5) using a series of convolutions and upsampling. This branch focuses on generating rich, context-aware visual features.
 - **Activation Branch:** Interacts the visual prompt mask with image features (also derived from PAN) to produce grouped "prompt-aware weights" in a low-dimensional space. This branch is designed to be highly efficient, avoiding complex operators. The semantic features are then grouped and weighted by these prompt-aware activations.
 - **Prompt Embedding Aggregation:** The prompt embedding is derived by aggregating the weighted semantic features, ensuring that the visual cue selectively highlights relevant semantic information. This design achieves strong performance with minimal computational complexity.

3. **Lazy Region-Prompt Contrast (LRPC) for Prompt-Free Scenario:**
 - **Problem:** Prompt-free detection typically relies on costly generative language models to produce object names, leading to high memory and latency.
 - **Approach:** LRPC reformulates the prompt-free task as an efficient retrieval problem, avoiding language model dependency.
 - **Specialized Prompt Embedding:** A unique prompt embedding `P_s` is trained specifically to identify *all objects* in an image, treating them as a single, generic category.
 - **Object Filtering:** This `P_s` is used to efficiently filter the anchor points' object embeddings (O), retaining only those corresponding to actual objects (`O' = {o ∈ O | o ⋅ P_s^T > δ}`). This "lazy" approach prunes irrelevant anchor points early.
 - **Built-in Large Vocabulary Retrieval:** Only the filtered object embeddings in `O'` are then contrasted against a pre-compiled, built-in large vocabulary (e.g., 4585 category names from a tag list). This targeted retrieval significantly reduces computational overhead compared to contrasting all anchor points with all vocabulary entries, ensuring high performance and efficiency.

**Training Objective:**
YOLOE's training follows established practices for open-vocabulary detectors and YOLO models:
- **Online Vocabulary:** An online vocabulary is created for each training sample, based on texts associated with the images, serving as positive labels.
- **Label Assignment:** Task-aligned label assignment is used to match predictions to ground truths.
- **Loss Functions:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for classification, IoU loss and Distributed Focal Loss (DFL) for regression, and BCE loss for segmentation masks.

This comprehensive methodology allows YOLOE to achieve its goal of a unified, efficient, and versatile object detection and segmentation model across various prompting paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

YOLOE demonstrates exceptional performance, efficiency, and versatility across diverse open-prompt mechanisms and downstream tasks, significantly outperforming existing methods.

#### 4.1. Text and Visual Prompt Evaluation (Zero-Shot on LVIS)

- **Detection Performance & Efficiency:**
 - **Superiority over YOLO-Worldv2:** YOLOE-v8-S (small scale) surpasses YOLO-Worldv2-S by **3.5 AP** on LVIS, while requiring **3× less training cost** and achieving **1.4× inference speedup** on a T4 GPU (1.3× on iPhone 12).
 - Similar trends are observed across medium (YOLOE-v8-M gains 0.2 AP with 1.3×/1.2× speedup) and large (YOLOE-v8-L gains 0.4 AP with 1.3×/1.2× speedup) scales.
 - YOLOE shows remarkable improvements for rare categories (APr), with YOLOE-v8-S gaining 5.2% and YOLOE-v8-L gaining 7.6% AP_r over YOLO-Worldv2 counterparts.
 - The model exhibits strong generalizability, with YOLOE using YOLO11 architecture also showing competitive AP with significantly higher inference speeds (e.g., YOLOE-11-L achieves comparable AP to YOLO-Worldv2-L but with 1.6× speedup on both T4 and iPhone 12).
- **Visual Prompt Efficacy:**
 - YOLOE-v8-L, when using visual prompts, outperforms T-Rex2 by **3.3 AP_r** and **0.9 AP_c**, despite T-Rex2 using 2× more training data and significantly more powerful GPUs (16 A100 vs. 8 RTX4090).
 - Visual prompts achieve comparable AP_r and AP_c to text prompts for various model scales, highlighting their effectiveness, especially for less frequent objects that are difficult to describe textually.
- **Segmentation Performance:**
 - YOLOE-v8-M/L achieves **20.8 and 23.5 AP_m** respectively on LVIS in a zero-shot manner, significantly outperforming YOLO-Worldv2-M/L (which were fine-tuned on LVIS-Base dataset) by **3.0 and 3.7 AP_m**. This demonstrates YOLOE's strong segmentation capabilities without specific LVIS training data.

#### 4.2. Prompt-Free Evaluation (Zero-Shot on LVIS)

- **Dominant Performance & Efficiency:** YOLOE-v8-L achieves **27.2 AP and 23.5 AP_r**, outperforming GenerateU (Swin-T backbone) by **0.4 AP and 3.5 AP_r**.
- Crucially, YOLOE accomplishes this with **6.3× fewer parameters** and **53× inference speedup** compared to GenerateU, validating its efficient retrieval-based paradigm over generative models.

#### 4.3. Downstream Transfer (COCO Dataset)

- **Strong Transferability (Linear Probing):** With only 10 epochs of linear probing (less than 2% of original training time), YOLOE-11-M/L achieves over 80% of the performance of models trained from scratch, highlighting excellent feature generalization.
- **Enhanced Performance (Full Tuning):** YOLOE further boosts performance under limited training cost. For example, YOLOE-v8-M/L outperforms closed-set YOLOv8-M/L by **0.4 AP_m and 0.6 AP_b**, respectively, with nearly **4× less training epochs** (80 vs. 300 epochs). YOLOE-v8-S also achieves better performance than YOLOv8-S with 3× less training time. These results establish YOLOE as a robust pre-trained foundation for downstream tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **RepRTA's Effectiveness:** The RepRTA strategy yields a notable **2.3% AP enhancement** for text prompts with **zero inference overhead**, proving its efficiency in refining textual embeddings.
- **SAVPE's Superiority:** SAVPE significantly outperforms a simpler "Mask pool" baseline by **1.5 AP** for visual prompts. The decoupled activation and semantic branches effectively model semantic importance within prompt regions.
- **LRPC's Efficiency:** LRPC provides **1.7× / 1.3× inference speedups** for YOLOE-v8-S / L in prompt-free scenarios for the same performance, by intelligently filtering irrelevant anchor points. Different threshold values (δ) allow for flexible performance-efficiency trade-offs.

#### 4.5. Visualization Analyses

Qualitative results demonstrate YOLOE's ability to accurately detect and segment various objects across all four scenarios: zero-shot inference on LVIS, customized text prompts, visual prompts (bounding box, point, handcrafted shape), and prompt-free inference, further validating its efficacy and practical utility.

In summary, YOLOE successfully delivers on its promise of a unified, highly efficient, and versatile object detection and segmentation model, setting new state-of-the-art benchmarks for performance and efficiency in open-set vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

YOLOE represents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision, particularly in the domain of open-set object detection and instance segmentation. Its key contributions and potential impacts are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Technical Significance

- **Unified Multi-Prompt Framework:** YOLOE introduces a novel and highly effective unified architecture capable of handling text, visual, and prompt-free inputs within a single model. This is a substantial leap beyond previous works that often specialized in one or two prompt types or incurred prohibitive costs for unification.
- **Efficiency by Design:** The core innovation lies in achieving this unification and open-set capability *without sacrificing real-time performance or increasing deployment complexity*.
 - **Re-parameterizable Region-Text Alignment (RepRTA):** This strategy elegantly enhances visual-text alignment during training and then collapses into the standard classification head at inference, ensuring **zero overhead** for deployment. This is a crucial design for real-world application.
 - **Semantic-Activated Visual Prompt Encoder (SAVPE):** By decoupling semantic and activation branches and operating in low dimensions, SAVPE provides a lightweight yet powerful mechanism for visual prompt processing, avoiding the computational burdens of transformer-heavy designs or additional encoders.
 - **Lazy Region-Prompt Contrast (LRPC):** Re-framing the prompt-free task as an intelligent retrieval problem, rather than a generative one, dramatically reduces computational costs by eliminating the dependency on large language models and efficiently filtering irrelevant detections.
- **Enhanced YOLO Capabilities:** By building upon the robust YOLO architecture, YOLOE extends its proven speed and accuracy to handle arbitrary, open-set categories and perform instance segmentation, making YOLO a more versatile and powerful tool.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact

- **Real-time Open-World Vision:** YOLOE's ability to "see anything" in real-time under various prompts makes it immensely valuable for applications demanding high adaptability and low latency. This includes:
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Self-driving cars, drones, and robotics can identify unforeseen objects or adapt to new tasks guided by natural language commands or visual examples.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) / Virtual Reality (VR):** Real-time understanding of novel objects in dynamic environments.
 - **Intelligent Surveillance:** Detecting and tracking unusual or user-defined objects in complex scenes.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Identifying specific anomalies or structures with visual prompts from experts, or automatically detecting general abnormalities.
 - **Content Moderation and Image/Video Editing:** Precisely selecting and manipulating arbitrary objects.
- **Edge Device Deployment:** The model's efficiency, low parameter count, and zero inference overhead for text prompts make it highly suitable for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices (e.g., mobile phones, embedded systems), democratizing advanced open-set capabilities.
- **Reduced Development Cost:** By offering a single model for diverse prompting scenarios, YOLOE can streamline development workflows, reduce the need for multiple specialized models, and lower the computational resources required for training and fine-tuning. Its strong transferability to downstream tasks further contributes to cost efficiency.

#### 5.3. Broader Research Implications

- **New Baseline:** YOLOE establishes a strong and efficient baseline for future research in real-time open-prompt-driven vision tasks, encouraging further exploration into optimized architectures for multimodal understanding.
- **Inspiration for Efficient AI:** The innovative strategies within YOLOE (RepRTA's re-parameterization, SAVPE's decoupled design, LRPC's retrieval paradigm) can inspire similar efficiency-focused solutions across other complex AI domains.
- **Bridging Research and Industry:** By making state-of-the-art open-set capabilities highly efficient and deployable, YOLOE bridges the gap between academic research breakthroughs and practical industrial applications.

In conclusion, YOLOE is not just another object detection model; it is a meticulously engineered system that addresses critical limitations in current open-set vision, offering a powerful, efficient, and versatile solution for "seeing anything" in real-world scenarios. Its impact will likely be felt across both the academic research community and various industries relying on advanced computer vision.
