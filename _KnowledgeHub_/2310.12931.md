# Eureka: Human-Level Reward Design via Coding Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> EUREKA, developed by NVIDIA and academic partners, is an algorithm that leverages Large Language Models to automatically design executable reward functions for reinforcement learning agents. It successfully generates human-level reward code that outperforms manually engineered rewards across various robotic tasks, including complex dexterous manipulation like pen spinning, significantly reducing the bottleneck of reward engineering.

## Problem

- Manually designing effective reward functions for reinforcement learning (RL) is time-consuming, prone to sub-optimality, and a major bottleneck in RL research.
- Traditional methods like Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) require expensive expert demonstrations and produce non-interpretable reward functions.
- While Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at high-level planning, they struggle to translate these plans into precise, low-level control policies for complex motor skills.

## Method

- EUREKA leverages a coding LLM (e.g., GPT-4) to generate free-form, executable Python reward code directly from environment source code and a natural language task description.
- It employs an evolutionary search where the LLM iteratively refines reward candidates based on their performance, using GPU-accelerated RL simulations for rapid evaluation.
- A "reward reflection" mechanism provides automated, fine-grained textual feedback to the LLM on individual reward component contributions and policy training dynamics, enabling targeted code mutations.

## Results

- EUREKA autonomously generated reward functions that outperformed expert human-engineered rewards on **83%** of 29 diverse RL tasks, achieving an average normalized improvement of **52%**.
- It enabled the acquisition of multi-cycle dexterous pen spinning on a simulated anthropomorphic hand, a complex task previously considered unfeasible with manual reward engineering.
- EUREKA significantly outperformed other LLM-based reward design methods like L2R due to its ability to generate free-form, executable reward code and its iterative refinement process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating automated feedback through "reward reflection" is crucial for LLMs to effectively refine reward functions, enabling targeted and intelligent code modifications.
> - Large Language Models can autonomously discover novel reward functions that diverge from human intuition yet lead to superior performance.
> - Combining LLM-driven code generation with an evolutionary search and high-speed simulation provides a powerful framework for tackling complex, open-ended problems like reward design in RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has seen remarkable successes in various domains, including robotics, often achieving impressive dexterity. However, a persistent and well-documented bottleneck in RL research is the **design of effective reward functions**. Manual reward engineering is notoriously difficult, time-consuming, and often leads to sub-optimal policies or unintended behaviors. A recent survey cited by the authors highlights that 92% of RL researchers report using manual trial-and-error for reward design, with 89% indicating their designed rewards are sub-optimal. This problem is exacerbated in tasks requiring complex, low-level manipulation or sparse reward settings, where incremental learning signals are crucial but hard to define.

Concurrently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated extraordinary capabilities as high-level semantic planners for sequential decision-making tasks, particularly in robotics. They can interpret natural language instructions and generate high-level plans. However, a significant gap remains: LLMs traditionally struggle to translate these high-level plans into precise, low-level control policies required for complex motor skills like dexterous manipulation. Existing attempts to use LLMs for low-level control often require extensive task-specific prompting or are limited to simpler skills.

EUREKA directly addresses these two critical challenges:
1. **Automating Reward Design:** It aims to automate the tedious and error-prone process of reward engineering, moving beyond manual trial-and-error and parameterized templates.
2. **Bridging LLMs to Low-Level Control:** It leverages the code-writing and in-context learning abilities of state-of-the-art LLMs (like GPT-4) to generate executable reward functions. These functions then serve as the learning signals for RL agents to acquire complex low-level manipulation skills, effectively bridging the gap between LLM's high-level understanding and RL's low-level execution.

Previous attempts at automating reward design have included Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL), which infers rewards from demonstrations but requires expensive expert data and yields non-interpretable "black-box" functions. Evolutionary algorithms have also been used, but typically for optimizing parameters within predefined reward templates, lacking the generality and expressiveness of programmatic reward generation. More recently, LLMs have been explored for reward design, most notably L2R (Yu et al., 2023). While L2R uses LLMs, it relies on a two-stage prompting approach and fixed reward templates/APIs, limiting its expressivity and requiring significant manual effort in defining these templates.

EUREKA distinguishes itself by proposing a **universal reward programming algorithm** that:
- Generates **free-form, executable reward code** (not just parameters or natural language descriptions) directly from LLMs.
- Operates **without any task-specific prompting or pre-defined reward templates**.
- Incorporates an **evolutionary optimization loop** combined with "reward reflection" to iteratively refine and improve the generated rewards, addressing the sub-optimality of single-sample generation.
- Is designed for **interpretability ("white-box" rewards)** and **human compatibility** (e.g., incorporating human feedback for alignment).

By tackling the fundamental problem of reward design with an innovative LLM-centric, evolutionary, and code-generating approach, EUREKA represents a significant advancement in making RL more practical, scalable, and capable of addressing highly complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EUREKA stems from the long-standing and well-recognized challenge of **reward function design in reinforcement learning (RL)**. While RL has shown impressive capabilities in learning complex behaviors, the success often hinges on the laborious and expert-intensive process of manually crafting reward functions. This manual process is widely reported to be prone to sub-optimality and unintended behaviors, limiting the broader applicability of RL.

The paper articulates several key objectives for EUREKA, aiming to address these pain points:

- **Human-Level Reward Generation:** The overarching goal is to develop an algorithm that can autonomously design reward functions capable of achieving performance comparable to, or even surpassing, expert human-engineered rewards. This implies the ability to capture intricate task nuances and provide effective learning signals.
- **Solving Complex Low-Level Manipulation Tasks:** A specific and ambitious objective is to enable RL agents to learn highly complex, low-level manipulation skills, such as dexterous pen spinning, which have historically been challenging to acquire due to the difficulty of designing appropriate rewards. This aims to bridge the gap between LLMs' high-level semantic planning abilities and the precise, continuous control required for fine motor skills.
- **Automation and Generality:** EUREKA seeks to automate the iterative trial-and-error associated with reward design, reducing or eliminating the need for human supervision and task-specific prompt engineering or reward templates. The algorithm should be universal, capable of generating rewards for a diverse range of robot morphologies and tasks without requiring extensive adaptation for each new scenario.
- **Interpretability and Flexibility:** The generated rewards should be "white-box" (executable code), allowing for human inspection and understanding. Furthermore, the system should be flexible enough to incorporate various forms of human input and feedback, promoting safety and alignment of learned behaviors with human intent, similar to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) but in a gradient-free, in-context learning manner.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** The proposed solution should be efficient enough to facilitate rapid experimentation and policy learning. This necessitates leveraging high-performance simulation and computational resources to evaluate reward candidates.

In essence, EUREKA's motivation is to transform reward design from a bottleneck requiring specialized human expertise into an automated, scalable, and generalizable process, thereby unlocking the full potential of RL for complex real-world applications, especially in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EUREKA's methodology is built upon three core algorithmic components, leveraging the advanced capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for code generation, combined with an evolutionary search paradigm:

**1. Environment as Context:**
- **Input:** EUREKA takes the raw environment source code and a natural language task description as its primary inputs. Crucially, it only receives the environment's observation and action variable definitions, specifically excluding any pre-existing reward code, to prevent "cheating" and ensure true zero-shot generation.
- **Automatic Snippet Extraction:** To manage the LLM's context window limitations and prevent leakage of simulator internals, an automatic script extracts only the relevant code snippets that define the environment's state and action variables. This ensures the prompt's generality across different simulators.
- **Zero-Shot Generation:** With this context, EUREKA instructs a coding LLM (e.g., GPT-4) to directly generate executable Python code for the reward function. The instructions are minimal, focusing on generic reward design principles and output formatting (e.g., exposing individual reward components as a dictionary). Remarkably, this enables the LLM to zero-shot generate plausible reward functions even on its first attempt for diverse environments.

**2. Evolutionary Search:**
- **Iterative Refinement:** Recognizing that initial LLM-generated rewards may be sub-optimal or even buggy, EUREKA employs an evolutionary search strategy for iterative improvement.
- **Batch Sampling:** In each iteration, EUREKA samples a batch of `K` (typically 16) independent reward candidates from the LLM. This batch sampling increases the probability of obtaining at least one executable and potentially viable reward function.
- **GPU-Accelerated Evaluation:** To rapidly evaluate these reward candidates, EUREKA integrates with GPU-accelerated distributed reinforcement learning platforms like IsaacGym. This offers significant speedups (up to three orders of magnitude) in policy learning, making the extensive search process computationally feasible. Each candidate reward function is used to train a policy, and its performance is measured against a black-box task fitness function `F`.
- **In-Context Mutation:** The best-performing reward function from the current batch/iteration, along with its "reward reflection" (detailed below), is fed back into the LLM's context window. The LLM then generates `K` new, mutated reward candidates based on this feedback, aiming to refine the promising solutions. This iterative process continues for a specified number of `N` iterations (typically 5).
- **Random Restarts:** To mitigate the risk of converging to local optima, EUREKA performs multiple independent runs (e.g., 5 runs per environment), each with its own evolutionary search, akin to standard global optimization techniques.

**3. Reward Reflection:**
- **Automated Feedback Generation:** This crucial component generates a textual summary of the policy training dynamics for the LLM. Since EUREKA's reward functions are designed to expose their individual components, reward reflection tracks the scalar values of *each reward component* (e.g., "rotation_reward", "angular_velocity_penalty") as well as overall policy metrics (e.g., success rates, episode lengths) at intermediate training checkpoints.
- **Fine-Grained Credit Assignment:** Unlike the holistic task fitness `F`, which provides only a single score, reward reflection offers detailed insights into *why* a reward function performs well or poorly. For example, it can highlight if a component is not being optimized (values are near identical), if its scale is off, or if it leads to unintended behavior.
- **Enabling Targeted Editing:** This detailed feedback empowers the LLM to perform more intricate and targeted reward modifications in subsequent evolutionary steps (e.g., adjusting temperature parameters, re-writing functional forms, adding/removing components). It effectively grounds the in-context reward mutation process.

In summary, EUREKA creates a powerful closed-loop system where an LLM proposes reward code, a high-speed RL simulator evaluates it, and an automated reflection mechanism provides actionable feedback, enabling the LLM to intelligently evolve increasingly effective reward functions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EUREKA demonstrates remarkable capabilities in automated reward design, consistently outperforming existing methods and human experts across a diverse range of tasks.

- **Superior Performance over Human-Engineered Rewards:** In a comprehensive evaluation across 29 open-source RL environments featuring 10 distinct robot morphologies (including quadrupeds, quadcopters, bipeds, manipulators, and dexterous hands), EUREKA autonomously generated reward functions that outperformed expert human-engineered rewards on an impressive 83% of tasks. This led to an average normalized improvement of 52% over human performance. EUREKA excelled especially in high-dimensional dexterous manipulation tasks, where human reward engineering is most challenging.

- **Unlocking Dexterous Pen Spinning:** A groundbreaking achievement is EUREKA's ability to facilitate rapid pen spinning maneuvers on a simulated anthropomorphic Shadow Hand. This highly dynamic and complex task was previously considered unfeasible with manual reward engineering. By combining EUREKA-generated rewards with a curriculum learning approach (pre-training on re-orienting, then fine-tuning on spinning), the system achieved multi-cycle pen spinning, showcasing its potential for acquiring advanced robotic skills.

- **Outperformance of L2R:** When compared to L2R, another LLM-based reward design method, EUREKA significantly outperformed it, particularly on high-dimensional tasks. This superiority is attributed to EUREKA's ability to generate free-form, expressive reward programs from scratch, unlike L2R's reliance on rigid templates and API primitives which limit expressivity.

- **Effectiveness of Evolutionary Search:** The results clearly show that EUREKA's performance consistently improves over successive evolutionary iterations. An ablation study demonstrated that simply increasing the number of initial sampled rewards ("EUREKA w.o. Evolution") did not yield the same level of improvement, underscoring the critical role of the iterative refinement process and the "in-context reward mutation."

- **Novelty of Generated Rewards:** Analysis of the correlation between EUREKA-generated and human-engineered rewards revealed that EUREKA often produces weakly or even negatively correlated reward functions, especially for more challenging tasks. This suggests that EUREKA can discover novel reward design principles that diverge from human intuition yet lead to superior performance.

- **Importance of Reward Reflection:** Ablation studies confirmed that detailed reward reflection is vital for EUREKA's performance. Removing this component, which provides fine-grained feedback on individual reward components, resulted in a significant average performance degradation of 28.6%, particularly pronounced in higher-dimensional tasks. This highlights how reward reflection enables targeted and effective reward editing by the LLM.

- **Gradient-Free RLHF Capabilities:** EUREKA proves adaptable for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):
 - **Improving Human Initializations:** EUREKA can take existing human reward functions as initializations and significantly improve upon them, uniformly outperforming both the initial human rewards and EUREKA's own from-scratch attempts on tested tasks. This suggests a complementary role where humans identify relevant state variables, and EUREKA excels at formulating the reward logic.
 - **Incorporating Textual Feedback:** EUREKA successfully incorporates purely textual human feedback (e.g., "running with natural gait" instead of a "duck walk") to generate more human-aligned and safer behaviors, even if it might slightly reduce raw performance metrics (e.g., forward velocity). User studies confirmed a strong preference for policies trained with human-aligned EUREKA rewards.

These findings collectively establish EUREKA as a robust and highly effective method for automated reward design, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in complex robotic control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EUREKA represents a significant advancement in the field of reinforcement learning and robotics, with the potential for wide-ranging impact:

- **Democratization of Reinforcement Learning:** By automating the notoriously difficult and time-consuming process of reward function design, EUREKA makes RL more accessible to a broader range of users, reducing the reliance on specialized human expertise and extensive trial-and-error. This could accelerate research and application development in domains currently bottlenecked by the reward engineering challenge.

- **Unlocking New Robotic Capabilities:** The demonstration of human-level performance in complex dexterous manipulation tasks, such as pen spinning, for the first time, showcases EUREKA's ability to enable the acquisition of skills previously considered infeasible. This opens avenues for developing highly agile and precise robotic behaviors in various real-world applications, from manufacturing to service robotics.

- **Enhanced Human-AI Collaboration:** EUREKA's ability to incorporate human feedback, both through improving existing human rewards and responding to textual guidance, provides a powerful gradient-free approach to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). This facilitates a more intuitive and effective collaboration between human designers and AI systems, allowing for the generation of more aligned, safe, and robust behaviors without the need for complex model updates. It effectively turns the LLM into an "RL assistant" for reward design.

- **Discovery of Novel Reward Structures:** The finding that EUREKA can generate rewards weakly or negatively correlated with human designs, yet achieve superior performance, highlights its capacity to discover non-intuitive and more optimal reward functions. This could lead to breakthroughs in task-specific solutions that human experts might overlook due to cognitive biases or conventional approaches.

- **Generality and Scalability:** EUREKA's design, which leverages the intrinsic code-writing and in-context learning capabilities of LLMs with minimal task-specific prompting, makes it highly generalizable across diverse robot morphologies and tasks. Its integration with high-performance GPU-accelerated simulation platforms ensures scalability, allowing for rapid iteration and exploration of reward candidates. This indicates its potential applicability to custom simulators and various RL algorithms beyond those tested.

- **Broader Implications for AI Research:** The core principle of combining large language models with evolutionary algorithms for tackling difficult, open-ended search problems extends beyond reward design. This methodology could inspire similar approaches for other challenging aspects of AI, such as architecture search, curriculum generation, or even automated scientific discovery, wherever programmatic solutions can be iteratively improved based on empirical feedback.

In conclusion, EUREKA offers a transformative solution to a fundamental problem in RL, propelling the field closer to realizing truly autonomous and adaptable intelligent agents capable of learning complex skills in the real world.
