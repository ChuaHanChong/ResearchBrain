# Memory Retrieval in Visuomotor Policies for Long-Horizon Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> HALO, a visuomotor policy, improves long-horizon robot control by mitigating spurious correlations and error accumulation in memory retrieval. It achieves this by distilling task-relevant priors from Vision-Language Models through Video Question-Answering and employing top-k sparse attention, resulting in 41% average absolute task success in simulation and 55% in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robots need robust memory to perform diverse, long-horizon tasks in partially observable environments, but existing reactive visuomotor policies lack sufficient retrieval capabilities.
- Direct application of attention-based memory in robotic imitation learning leads to spurious correlations, causing policies to attend to task-irrelevant information.
- Long-horizon closed-loop execution can cause error accumulation, where small prediction errors compound in memory, leading to model drift and cascading failures.

## Method

- HALO, an attention-based visuomotor policy, integrates a memory retrieval mechanism from past observations and actions.
- It distills task-relevant priors from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) through a co-trained Video Question-Answering (VQA) objective to guide the retrieval mechanism.
- Top-k sparse attention is employed to limit the influence of noisy or irrelevant historical information, selecting only the most salient memory entries at each timestep.

## Results

- HALO achieved an average absolute task success of **41%** in simulation and **55%** in real-world scenarios, surpassing the Standard Transformer baseline (**22%** sim, **36%** real) and other prior methods.
- VLM-induced priors through VQA supervision improved average task success by **10%**, while top-k sparsification contributed a **9%** improvement and reduced model drift (Jensen-Shannon Divergence from **0.07** to **0.06**).
- The top-k sparse attention mechanism outperformed alternative sparsification strategies (e.g., Gated Attention) and latent-state compression methods like LSTM or Mamba.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling VLM priors through VQA provides effective supervision for memory retrieval, directing attention to causally relevant information and suppressing spurious correlations.
> - Top-k sparse attention significantly reduces model drift and error accumulation over long horizons by selectively retrieving only the most salient historical entries.
> - Co-training VQA with action prediction is crucial for grounding VLM priors in robotic control, proving more effective than pretraining or omitting VQA supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

General-purpose robots operating in partially observable environments, such such as domestic settings, require memory capabilities to support extended autonomous operation. These robots must recall diverse information, including object locations, human task completion status, or event timings, to execute a wide array of long-horizon tasks. Existing purely reactive visuomotor policies are insufficient for such tasks, necessitating robust memory retrieval mechanisms.

The field of robotics has explored several approaches to memory:
- **Hand-designed or heuristic-based methods:** These approaches often rely on task-specific assumptions and representations (e.g., object-centric memory for spatial information, hand-designed rules for selective storage), which limit their generalizability across diverse manipulation tasks and information types (spatial, relational, numerical, temporal).
- **Learned compact representations:** Methods like recurrent neural networks (RNNs), state-space models (SSMs), or Scene Memory Transformers aim to compress history into latent states. While effective for maintaining memory, they can struggle with selective recall and may discard granular information crucial for low-level action prediction, particularly over very long horizons.
- **Associative retrieval mechanisms:** Transformer architectures, employing attention based on learned query-key similarity, have shown promise in domains like natural language processing for general, modality-agnostic memory retrieval. However, their direct application to long-horizon robotic imitation learning from offline data presents two significant challenges:
 - **Spurious correlations:** Policies may learn to attend to information that correlates with expert behavior in training data but is not causally relevant, leading to performance degradation at test time.
 - **Error accumulation:** In closed-loop execution, small prediction errors compound over time, introducing noise into memory representations. Conditioning on extensive past information can amplify this effect, causing model drift and cascading failures.

This work addresses these specific challenges associated with attention-based memory retrieval in visuomotor policies. It aims to develop a general, learned memory retrieval mechanism that is robust to spurious correlations and error accumulation, thereby enabling reliable long-horizon control in partially observable environments. It leverages advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for knowledge distillation, a concept explored in robotics for improving parametric memory, but applies it to guide non-parametric memory retrieval. Furthermore, it investigates the application of sparse attention mechanisms, previously studied in language and vision for long contexts, to visuomotor control to mitigate the impact of accumulated errors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a visuomotor policy capable of performing long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks in partially observable environments by effectively retrieving diverse information from past observations and actions. The authors introduce HALO (History-Aware visuomotor policy for LOng-horizon robotic imitation learning) to achieve this objective, specifically addressing two fundamental limitations of directly applying attention-based memory retrieval in long-horizon robotic imitation learning:

1. **Mitigating Spurious Correlations in Memory Retrieval:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard attention mechanisms in imitation learning can cause policies to attend to task-irrelevant details that happen to correlate with expert actions in the training data. These spurious correlations degrade performance when tested in new scenarios where such correlations do not hold. Robots need to identify and retrieve *task-relevant* information from their history to make robust decisions.
 - **Objective:** To guide the retrieval mechanism toward causally relevant information and suppress reliance on spurious correlations. The authors hypothesize that Vision-Language Models (VLMs) encode valuable priors about what information in past observations is relevant to a task. While VLM priors are not directly grounded in action, they can provide useful signals for directing memory retrieval.

2. **Reducing Error Accumulation and Model Drift in Long-Horizon Control:**
 - **Motivation:** In closed-loop robot control, small prediction errors by the policy can lead to observations that increasingly deviate from the training distribution. When the policy conditions on an extensive history containing these accumulated errors, the noise can be compounded, leading to degradation of latent representations, model drift, and eventual cascading failures, particularly over long task horizons (e.g., several minutes).
 - **Objective:** To limit the influence of noisy or irrelevant information from accumulated history on decision-making, thereby reducing closed-loop drift and improving robustness for extended task execution. The authors propose that by restricting retrieval to only the most salient parts of the history, the policy can maintain robust performance.

In summary, the motivation is to create a general-purpose, end-to-end learned memory retrieval system that overcomes the practical challenges of deploying attention-based models for long-horizon robotic control, making it more reliable and scalable for complex household tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HALO is a visuomotor policy that incorporates an attention-based memory retrieval mechanism designed for long-horizon robot control. It addresses the identified challenges through two main components: knowledge distillation from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) via Video Question-Answering (VQA) and top-k sparse attention.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Model Architecture

The robot's goal is to learn a closed-loop visuomotor policy, `π_θ(a_t | τ_t, l)`, that predicts a low-level action `a_t` at time `t`, given the interaction history `τ_t` (past observations and actions) and a natural-language task instruction `l`. The policy is trained on a dataset of expert demonstrations `D`.

The model architecture consists of three main components:
- **Modality-specific encoders:** `g_obs_θ` for observations (e.g., RGB images, proprioception) and `g_act_θ` for past actions, mapping them into a shared latent space.
- **Policy backbone (`f_θ`):** A Transformer architecture responsible for memory retrieval over the encoded history (`M_t`) and processing this information along with the current observation embedding (`x_t`) and task instruction (`l`) to produce a latent state `z_t`. The retrieval mechanism within `f_θ` uses a query-key-value formulation, where `q_t` is derived from the current context, and `k_i`, `v_i` from memory elements `m_i`.
- **Task-specific heads:** `h_act_θ` for predicting low-level control commands and `h_text_θ` for predicting text answers for auxiliary VQA supervision.

#### 3.2. Distilling Vision-Language Model Priors via Video Question-Answering (VQA)

To mitigate spurious correlations, HALO distills task-relevant priors from VLMs into the policy through VQA supervision.
- **VQA as Retrieval Supervision:** In addition to the standard imitation learning objective, the policy is co-trained with a VQA objective. For this, `π_vqa_θ` predicts an answer `v` to a text-based question `u`, conditioned on `M_t`, `x_t`, and `u`. This forces the retrieval module to attend to specific historical information necessary to answer task-relevant questions, thereby providing direct supervision on *what* information should be retrieved.
- **Joint Grounding through Imitation Learning:** The policy is trained with a combined objective `L(θ) = L_IL(θ) + λL_VQA(θ)`, where `L_IL` is an `ℓ_1` loss for expert action imitation, and `L_VQA` is a cross-entropy loss for VQA. The parameter `λ` balances these objectives. This joint training ensures that VLM-induced priors guide retrieval toward task-relevant information while grounding it in the context of action prediction.
- **VQA Data Generation Pipeline:** A multi-stage pipeline generates high-quality VQA data from long-horizon trajectories:
 - **Trajectory-to-text summarization:** Combines outputs from specialized vision models (e.g., object detectors, VLMs for activity descriptions) to create textual descriptions of scene elements, their locations, and activities within trajectory segments.
 - **Question and answer synthesis:** A large language model (LLM, specifically GPT-4o-mini) generates memory-based question-answer (Q&A) pairs. To counter bias toward early trajectory information, questions are generated for randomly sampled frame indices. The LLM is also prompted to generate 'N/A' if no task-relevant information is present.
 - **Automatic filtering:** A separate LLM (GPT-4o) evaluates generated Q&A pairs for correctness and task relevance, assigning scores and filtering out low-quality or irrelevant entries.

#### 3.3. Reducing Model Drift with Top-k Attention Sparsification

To address error accumulation over long horizons, HALO explicitly limits the amount of historical information the policy conditions on at each time step.
- **Sparse Attention Mechanism:** Instead of computing attention over all memory elements `M_t`, HALO computes similarity scores `s_i = <q_t, k_i>` between the current query `q_t` and keys `k_i` from memory elements. It then selects the `k` most relevant elements (`I_k = TopK({s_i}, k)`) and computes attention only over this subset. Elements outside `I_k` receive zero weight.
- **Implementation:** A straight-through estimator is used during training to enable backpropagation through the non-differentiable `TopK` operation. This mechanism encourages the policy to learn to select and reinforce useful information while suppressing noisy or irrelevant historical data.

In essence, HALO maintains a complete memory of past observations and actions but uses a guided, selective retrieval mechanism that prioritizes task-relevant information (via VQA distillation) and limits noise (via top-k sparsification).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HALO was conducted on the REMEMBENCH simulation benchmark and five real-world tasks across two robotic platforms (fixed-base manipulator and mobile manipulator). These tasks require diverse memory types (spatial, relational, numerical, event-time) and span long horizons, including human-robot collaborative scenarios.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison to Prior Methods

- **General Superiority:** HALO consistently achieved higher average task success rates compared to various baselines in both simulation and real-world environments.
 - In simulation (Table I), HALO achieved an average absolute task success of 41%, significantly outperforming the Standard Transformer baseline (22%).
 - In real-world tasks (Table II), HALO achieved an average absolute task success of 55%, compared to 36% for the Standard Transformer.
- **Versatility:** HALO demonstrated competitive performance across tasks requiring different types of memory (spatial, relational, numerical, event-time), suggesting its generalizability without task-specific assumptions. This contrasts with baselines like SAM2Act++ (object-centric memory), which excelled in spatial tasks but struggled with others, or ReMemBer (text-based summarization), which often omitted details necessary for control.
- **Reduced Failures in Real-World:** For the ‘Retrieve Object’ task in the real world, HALO reduced manipulation failures by 8% and memory failures by 25% (absolute percentages) compared to full attention, indicating improved robustness and memory retrieval.
- **Superiority over Hand-Designed and Compressed Memory:** HALO showed an absolute improvement of 12% over Hand-Designed Features, suggesting that learned retrieval is more effective than manually specified rules. It also outperformed Scene Memory Transformer (SMT) by 7% and Token Merging by 12%, indicating that selective retrieval from full history is more beneficial than learned compression, which can lose granular details.

#### 4.2. Effects of VLM-Induced Priors and Top-k Sparsification

- **Impact of VLM-induced Priors (VQA Supervision):** Ablation studies (Table I) showed that adding VLM-induced priors through VQA supervision improved average task success by 10% (HALO with VQA: 41% vs. HALO without VQA: 31%). This highlights the role of VQA in guiding the policy to identify and retrieve task-relevant information, particularly for tasks involving object placements or numerical counts.
- **Impact of Top-k Sparsification:** The top-k sparsification mechanism contributed a 9% improvement in performance. By restricting retrieval to the most relevant memory entries, it reduced the incorporation of noisy or irrelevant information, which is crucial for long-horizon tasks. Quantitative analysis (Sec. VII-D) showed that top-k attention reduced the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) between policy and expert action distributions (0.06 for top-k vs. 0.07 for full attention), indicating reduced model drift.

#### 4.3. Comparison to Alternative Sparsification and Memory Mechanisms

- **Alternative Sparsification:** HALO's top-k sparsification (64% success on 'Retrieve Object') outperformed other sparsification methods like Gated Attention (45%), Hierarchical Attention (28%), Window Attention (13%), and Strided Attention (20%) (Table III). This suggests that content-based, flexible retrieval is more effective than fixed access patterns for robot tasks where relevant events can occur at unpredictable times.
- **Non-Associative Memory:** HALO demonstrated significant advantages over models that compress history into latent states, such as LSTM (−20%), Mamba (−14%), and Transformer-XL (−12%) on tasks requiring specific event recall (Table III). This reinforces the benefit of explicit memory with selective retrieval for accessing fine-grained details.

#### 4.4. Effects of Design Choices

- **Number of Retrieved Entries (k):** An optimal value of k=8 for top-k attention yielded the best performance (52% success). Too few entries (k=4, 40%) omitted useful context, while too many (k=12, 48%; k=16, 33%; k=24, 44%) likely incorporated excessive irrelevant information.
- **Co-training vs. Pretraining:** Co-training VQA and action prediction (64% success) significantly outperformed pretraining VQA followed by action fine-tuning (44%) and training without VQA (42%). This indicates that VQA knowledge is more effectively integrated when both objectives are learned simultaneously.
- **VQA Generation Pipeline:** The proposed stage-wise VQA generation pipeline achieved 60% more accurate Q&As and 20% fewer hallucinations compared to single-prompt generation from long videos, highlighting its importance for generating high-quality supervision data for VLMs.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

- **Retrieved Information (Fig. 6):** HALO's retrieval mechanism successfully identified diverse and complementary information (e.g., target object visibility, other potential object locations, past actions, subgoal progress) relevant to decision points, surpassing what typical human-designed features might capture.
- **Spurious Correlations (Fig. 7):** Grad-CAM visualizations showed that HALO, with VQA supervision, focused attention on task-relevant objects (e.g., olive oil bottle), while a standard Transformer without VQA exhibited sensitivity to irrelevant distractors (e.g., tissue roll position).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of HALO represents a notable advancement in the field of long-horizon robot control under partial observability, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Enhanced Generalizability for Visuomotor Policies:** HALO provides a general, learned memory retrieval mechanism that does not rely on task-specific assumptions or hand-designed rules. This is crucial for developing general-purpose robots capable of operating in diverse and unstructured environments like homes, where the variety of tasks and information required can be extensive. By learning what to retrieve directly from data, HALO offers a more scalable and adaptable solution than previous methods.

- **Improved Robustness in Long-Horizon Tasks:** The work directly addresses two critical limitations of attention-based memory in imitation learning: spurious correlations and error accumulation. By distilling VLM priors, HALO guides retrieval towards causally relevant information, leading to more robust decisions. The use of top-k sparse attention effectively mitigates model drift and cascading failures by limiting the influence of accumulated noise in memory over extended task durations. This directly contributes to more reliable and persistent robot operation in complex, real-world scenarios.

- **Effective Integration of Vision-Language Model Priors:** The research demonstrates a concrete and effective method for distilling knowledge from powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to improve robot control. It highlights that while VLM priors are highly informative for identifying task-relevant information, they must be grounded in action through joint imitation learning for successful low-level control. The proposed VQA generation pipeline also offers a practical approach to leverage VLMs for generating high-quality supervision data for memory-dependent tasks, overcoming challenges associated with direct long-video reasoning by state-of-the-art VLMs.

- **Foundation for More Capable Autonomous Agents:** The ability to reliably recall diverse information over long horizons is a fundamental requirement for truly autonomous agents. HALO's contributions lay a stronger foundation for building robots that can perform multi-stage, sequential tasks, track progress, respond to temporal cues, and adapt to dynamic environments. This has implications for applications in home robotics, assisted living, manufacturing, and other domains requiring complex interaction with the environment and memory-dependent decision-making.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies promising avenues for future work, including investigating adaptive strategies for dynamically adjusting the amount of retrieved information (beyond a fixed `k`), broadening the scope of memory to include cross-episodic information (learning from past failures), and exploring speculative retrieval methods to minimize latency associated with large memory access. These directions suggest a clear path towards even more sophisticated and efficient memory systems for robots.

In conclusion, HALO contributes to making robotic systems more intelligent and capable by enabling them to effectively leverage past experiences, moving closer to the vision of general-purpose robots that can seamlessly operate in complex, partially observable real-world settings.
