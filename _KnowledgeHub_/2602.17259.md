# FRAPPE: Infusing World Modeling into Generalist Policies via Multiple Future Representation Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> FRAPPE introduces a training paradigm that integrates world modeling into generalist robotic policies by aligning model features with latent representations from multiple visual foundation models. This approach demonstrates improved generalization across diverse tasks, enhances data efficiency through the use of action-free human egocentric videos, and achieves scalable performance in both simulated and real-world bimanual manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Generalist robotic policies struggle with computational inefficiency and poor generalization when relying on pixel-level reconstruction for world modeling, due to high computational cost and error accumulation.
- Implicit world modeling approaches often suffer from inductive biases by using representations from only a single visual foundation model, limiting their versatility for diverse robotic tasks.
- The development of robust generalist policies is hindered by the high cost of collecting action-annotated robot data and the absence of efficient fine-tuning and inference strategies.

## Method

- FRAPPE infuses implicit world modeling by aligning policy features with latent representations of future observations from multiple diverse Visual Foundation Models (VFMs), avoiding pixel-level reconstruction.
- A two-stage fine-tuning strategy is implemented: an initial full-parameter mid-training phase followed by a parameter-efficient post-training phase utilizing a Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA) architecture.
- The MiPA architecture leverages multiple sets of future prefixes and LoRA modules on a shared, frozen Robotic Diffusion Transformer (RDT) backbone, each dedicated to aligning with a different teacher VFM for parallel world knowledge integration.

## Results

- Achieved the highest average success rates in RoboTwin 2.0 simulation, outperforming state-of-the-art methods with 52.3% in easy settings and demonstrating superior generalization with 25.5% in hard, randomized settings.
- Showcased strong generalization on real-world basic tasks and successfully executed a complex long-horizon dual-arm task with a 20% success rate, where the baseline policy failed.
- Demonstrated significant performance improvements by co-training with large-scale action-free human egocentric videos, enhancing world modeling for novel objects and reducing reliance on robot demonstration data, while maintaining practical inference efficiency with only about 20ms additional latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit world modeling through aligning with latent representations from multiple diverse Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) offers a more robust and semantically rich understanding of future states compared to explicit pixel generation or single-VFM methods.
> - A two-stage fine-tuning strategy effectively adapts pre-trained foundation models to complex world-modeling objectives, addressing distribution shifts and architectural changes; parameter-efficient methods like LoRA, combined with future prefixes in a Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA) architecture, enable scalable integration of diverse world knowledge while keeping the core policy backbone frozen.
> - Action-free human egocentric data can effectively provide a strong inductive prior for world modeling, substantially reducing the need for costly action-annotated robot demonstration data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generalist robotic policies has seen significant advancements, largely driven by the adoption of diffusion generative models. These models, exemplified by works like Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2025) and Robotic Diffusion Transformer (RDT) (Liu et al., 2024, 2026), excel at modeling complex, multi-modal action distributions, leading to improved performance in manipulation and navigation tasks. A crucial development within this landscape is the integration of "world modeling" objectives—where policies learn to predict future environmental dynamics (e.g., future images). This augmentation helps models capture scene dynamics, mitigate overfitting to action histories, and enhance robustness against visual perturbations, as demonstrated by approaches such as Guo et al. (2024) and Hu et al. (2024).

However, the existing research primarily focuses on designing novel architectures for world modeling, often overlooking practical challenges in fine-tuning and inference for real-world deployment. FRAPPE identifies two critical limitations in the current state of the art:

1. **Inefficiency of Pixel-Level Reconstruction:** Policies that explicitly generate future pixels for world modeling tend to over-emphasize redundant visual details. This consumes substantial computational resources without necessarily aiding semantic learning or generalization, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. Furthermore, relying on explicitly predicted future observations during inference can lead to error accumulation, undermining policy robustness (Du et al., 2023; Liang et al., 2024).
2. **Inductive Biases of Single Visual Foundation Models (VFMs):** While some prior work attempts implicit world modeling through representation alignment (Zheng et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025a), these methods often depend on representations learned from a single visual task. Such single-source representations inherently carry inductive biases, limiting their versatility and suitability for the broad range of tasks a generalist robot policy needs to handle.

FRAPPE strategically positions itself as a solution to these challenges. Instead of explicit pixel generation, it champions *implicit* world modeling via future representation alignment. Crucially, it moves beyond single-VFM biases by aligning with *multiple, diverse* pretrained Visual Foundation Models in parallel, thereby harvesting broader "world knowledge." Furthermore, FRAPPE introduces a sophisticated two-stage fine-tuning and inference recipe, which has been a neglected aspect, to optimize efficiency, scalability, and data utilization. By building upon robust base models like RDT, FRAPPE offers an accessible pathway to enhance existing VLA capabilities through a refined approach to future image prediction, addressing both performance and practical deployment hurdles in generalist robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind FRAPPE stems from the recognized importance of infusing "world modeling" capabilities into generalist robotic policies to improve their reasoning, robustness, and generalization across diverse tasks. While existing methods have made strides in integrating world modeling, they fall short in several key areas.

The core motivations for developing FRAPPE are:
- **Overcoming Pixel-Level Reconstruction Limitations:** Current world modeling policies often force models to reconstruct future observations at the pixel level. This approach is computationally expensive, diverts resources from crucial semantic learning, and restricts generalization to novel or out-of-distribution scenarios. Moreover, explicit pixel prediction during inference can lead to compounding errors, destabilizing long-horizon tasks.
- **Addressing Inductive Biases of Single Visual Representations:** Prior attempts at implicit world modeling through representation alignment typically rely on a single visual foundation model (VFM). However, representations from a single VFM inherently carry specific inductive biases, limiting their applicability and generalizability across the vast array of visual information relevant to robotic tasks.
- **Improving Fine-tuning and Inference Efficiency:** There has been a significant oversight in developing efficient fine-tuning and inference strategies tailored for deploying world-modeling-infused generalist policies. The focus has largely been on architectural novelty, leaving practical implementation and scalability as open challenges.
- **Reducing Reliance on Action-Annotated Data:** Training generalist robotic policies typically requires extensive datasets of robot demonstrations with action labels, which are costly and time-consuming to collect. A pressing need exists for methods that can leverage more readily available, cheaper forms of data, such as action-free human videos.

Based on these motivations, FRAPPE sets forth the following key objectives:
1. **Enhance Implicit World Modeling:** To develop a novel training paradigm that builds a robust implicit world model by aligning the policy's features with latent representations of future observations from *multiple* diverse Visual Foundation Models, thereby avoiding the pitfalls of pixel-level reconstruction.
2. **Achieve Scalability and Efficiency:** To introduce a two-stage fine-tuning strategy and a parallel progressive expansion architecture (MiPA) that scales computational workload and the implicit grounding of future observations, leading to improved fine-tuning efficiency and performance.
3. **Improve Generalization:** To enable generalist policies to exhibit strong generalization capabilities in long-horizon and unseen real-world scenarios, surpassing state-of-the-art methods.
4. **Promote Data Efficiency:** To significantly reduce the dependence on expensive, action-annotated robot data by effectively leveraging readily available action-free human egocentric video demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FRAPPE proposes a novel approach to infuse world modeling into generalist policies, built upon the Robotic Diffusion Transformer (RDT) (Liu et al., 2024), a foundational model for bimanual manipulation. The core methodology revolves around Future Representation Alignment via Parallel Progressive Expansion (FRAPPE), implemented through a two-stage fine-tuning strategy and a specialized Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA) architecture.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Robotic Diffusion Transformer (RDT)

FRAPPE begins with RDT, which models the conditional distribution of future action sequences, $p_\theta(a_t|o_t, l)$, by training a denoising Diffusion Transformer (DiT) network, $f_\theta$. This network predicts a clean action chunk $a_t$ from a noisy version $\tilde{a}_t$, conditioned on observation $o_t$ and language instruction $l$. The DiT processes low-dimensional state tokens (including $\tilde{a}_t$ and proprioception) through cross-attention layers, conditioned by high-dimensional visual tokens (from a SigLIP encoder) and language tokens (from a T5 encoder).

#### 3.2. Future Representation Alignment

To introduce world modeling without explicit pixel generation, FRAPPE incorporates a set of learnable tokens, $p \in R^{n \times d}$, termed "future prefixes," which are concatenated with RDT's input. The model is trained to predict future states from these prefixes. Instead of predicting raw pixels, FRAPPE leverages pretrained Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) as "teacher visual encoders" ($\Phi$) to provide supervision. The target for alignment is the latent representation of future observations, $e_{t+h} = \Phi(o_{t+h})$, where $h$ is the future horizon.

The alignment loss for a VFM $\Phi$ is formulated as:
$L_\Phi = \text{cos}(p_t, \text{sg}(e))$, where $\text{cos}(\cdot, \cdot)$ is cosine similarity, and $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator, ensuring the teacher VFM's weights are not updated. This loss encourages the model to learn to predict future states and their dynamics implicitly.

#### 3.3. Parallel Scaling with Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA)

To leverage the diverse "world knowledge" embedded in multiple VFMs and achieve parallel scaling, FRAPPE introduces the Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA) paradigm:
- **Architecture:** Multiple sets of future prefixes and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules (Hu et al., 2022) are constructed. These operate on a *shared and frozen* RDT backbone. Each set (acting as an "expert") is designed to align with a *dedicated* teacher VFM. This parameter-efficient approach only trains the prefixes and LoRAs, minimizing the memory footprint during fine-tuning.
- **Multiple VFMs:** The paper uses $M=3$ teacher encoders: CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), and ViT (Dosovitskiy, 2020), chosen for their diverse representation spaces. The overall alignment loss becomes the sum of individual alignment losses: $L_{align} = \sum_{i=1}^M L_{\Phi_i}$.
- **Output Aggregation:** A lightweight router network computes gating weights, $w = \{w_i\}_{i=1}^M$, for the M experts. The latent action representations from each expert ($z_j$) are then combined via a weighted sum, which passes through a shared MLP head to produce the final action chunk: $a_t = \text{MLP}(\sum_{i=1}^M w_i \cdot z_i)$.
- **Load Balancing:** To prevent mode collapse (where one expert dominates), a load-balancing loss, $L_{balance}$, is incorporated. Additionally, label smoothing is applied to the gating weights $w$ to ensure all experts receive a minimum, non-zero weight, promoting balanced updates.
- **Total Training Objective:** The final objective combines the action loss, alignment loss, and load-balancing loss: $L_{total} = L_{action} + \lambda_1 L_{align} + \lambda_2 L_{balance}$.

#### 3.4. Two-Stage Fine-Tuning Strategy

Empirically, directly applying parallel scaling and world-modeling objectives to a foundation model proves challenging due to significant architectural and objective shifts. FRAPPE addresses this with a two-stage approach:
1. **Mid-Training Phase:**
 - **Goal:** Adapt the RDT model to the world-modeling objective and bridge the distribution gap from pre-training.
 - **Method:** Full-parameter fine-tuning of a *single-stream* model. The newly added future prefix aligns with a "teacher encoder" distilled from multiple VFMs (Theia, Shang et al., 2024), ensuring comprehensive robotic capabilities and a strong initialization.
2. **Post-Training Phase:**
 - **Goal:** Achieve parallel scaling and assign each expert a distinct visual representation from a different encoder for supervision.
 - **Method:** The MiPA architecture is deployed. Only the prefixes and LoRA modules are trainable, keeping the RDT backbone frozen. This stage further refines the model's world-modeling capabilities through parallel alignment with diverse VFMs.

#### 3.5. Inference

During inference, FRAPPE retains the same parallel computational graph established during post-training but without the VFMs for supervision. This allows the model to benefit from the learned multiple alignment objectives and multi-stream forward passes, thereby retaining the scaling advantages at deployment.

#### 3.6. Leveraging Action-Free Human Egocentric Data

A significant aspect of FRAPPE's approach is its ability to incorporate action-free human egocentric data. This drastically reduces the reliance on costly, action-annotated robot teleoperation data. In scenarios with limited teleoperation data, human hand-manipulation videos can be used to improve world-modeling capabilities. For large-scale training, internet egocentric data can serve as a form of continual pre-training for the VLA base model. When training on action-free samples, the action loss $L_{action}$ is omitted, and only the alignment loss $L_{align}$ is optimized.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FRAPPE's effectiveness is rigorously demonstrated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Arts (Table 1):**
 - **Easy Setting:** FRAPPE consistently achieved the highest average success rates across 8 diverse tasks, significantly outperforming the baseline RDT and other state-of-the-art methods like Diffusion Policy (DP), Video Prediction Policy (VPP), $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$. This highlights the substantial performance boost from FRAPPE's training paradigm.
 - **Hard Setting:** Under conditions of extreme domain randomization (e.g., scene clutter, varied textures, lighting), all methods struggled. However, FRAPPE still demonstrated superior generalization, surpassing the most effective baseline ($\pi_{0.5}$) by a clear margin (25.5% vs. 13.3% average success rate). This suggests FRAPPE's ability to learn robust, low-level dynamics rather than spurious correlations.
- **Comparison of Training Paradigms (Table 2):**
 - **Necessity of Mid-Training:** Direct application of post-training without a mid-training phase yielded results even lower than the RDT baseline, underscoring the critical role of mid-training in adapting the model to the world-modeling objective and bridging distribution gaps. Full-parameter fine-tuning during mid-training proved essential for this adaptation.
 - **Efficiency of Post-Training:** While introducing multi-prefixes alone without LoRA led to performance degradation (due to VFM representation mismatches), combining prefixes with LoRA fine-tuning in the post-training stage yielded substantial improvements. This validated the parameter-efficient nature of MiPA, showing that LoRA fine-tuning is sufficient in this stage.
 - The two-stage approach (mid-train (full ft) + post-train (prefix & lora ft)) achieved the highest overall success rate (52.3%), confirming its effectiveness.
- **Inference Efficiency (Table 3):**
 - Utilizing CUDA Graph, FRAPPE's parallel inference incurred only a marginal increase of approximately 20ms in latency compared to the baseline RDT, with memory usage remaining within typical GPU capacities (8.0 GB on an NVIDIA H100).
 - Furthermore, by reducing denoising steps to 3, FRAPPE achieved higher performance than the baseline while *lowering* inference latency, demonstrating its practical efficiency for real-time robotic applications.
- **Verification on Smaller-Scale Policy Model (Figure 3):**
 - FRAPPE consistently improved performance on a significantly smaller model (RDT-130M) across all tasks, validating its versatility.
 - LoRA fine-tuning during post-training on the 130M model achieved performance comparable to full-parameter fine-tuning, demonstrating the high parameter efficiency of the architectural framework.
 - The optimized RDT-130M with FRAPPE training showed performance comparable to the RDT-1B baseline, especially improving generalization on hard tasks, proving FRAPPE's capability to extend the performance boundaries of smaller models.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Generalization and Long-Horizon Tasks (Figure 4 & 5):**
 - **Basic Tasks:** FRAPPE demonstrated strong generalization in real-world basic tasks involving variations in lighting, height, pose, and target objects, particularly excelling in unseen scenarios. This aligns with the simulation findings regarding robust visual generalization.
 - **Long-Horizon Tasks:** For a challenging long-horizon task requiring complex dual-arm coordination, FRAPPE achieved a 20% success rate, while the vanilla RDT failed completely due to difficulties in fine-grained manipulation and action continuity. This highlights FRAPPE's enhanced dynamic modeling and sequential task execution capabilities.
- **Co-Training with Human Egocentric Data (Figure 6):**
 - **Data Pyramid:** FRAPPE's framework effectively integrates data from a hierarchical data pyramid: large-scale action-free human egocentric data (web-scale), task-specific human egocentric data, and task-specific robot data.
 - **Benefits of Web-Scale Data:** Training with large-scale open-sourced egocentric videos (TASTE-Rob) provided a strong inductive prior, leading to consistent performance improvements for easy-to-grasp objects and significantly higher success rates for hard-to-grasp objects, even when robot data was limited. This indicates enhanced world modeling for novel and complex geometries.
 - **Task-Specific Egocentric Data:** Incorporating task-specific human egocentric data enabled the model to learn future prediction and underlying dynamics from more samples, significantly improving spatial generalization, especially when robot data was scarce (e.g., 5 trajectories).
 - **Maximized Performance:** Combining all data types—robot, task-specific human egocentric, and web-scale human egocentric data—led to further maximized performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of FRAPPE's data scaling capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FRAPPE represents a significant advancement in the development of generalist robotic policies, offering solutions to several critical challenges currently limiting the field. Its contributions span novel training paradigms, enhanced generalization capabilities, and improved data and parameter efficiency, paving the way for more robust and scalable robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Novel Training Paradigm for Enhanced World-Awareness

FRAPPE introduces a groundbreaking two-stage fine-tuning strategy that effectively infuses implicit world modeling into VLA models. By aligning model features with the latent representations of *multiple* diverse Visual Foundation Models, FRAPPE moves beyond the limitations of explicit pixel-level reconstruction and the inductive biases inherent in single visual representations. This approach fosters a deeper understanding of scene dynamics, leading to policies that are more "world-aware" without the computational overhead and error accumulation associated with generating future pixels. This paradigm sets a new benchmark for adapting and scaling large foundation models for robotic control.

#### 5.2. Superior Generalization and Robustness

The empirical results, particularly in challenging simulated (RoboTwin Hard setting) and real-world unseen scenarios, demonstrate FRAPPE's exceptional generalization capabilities. Its ability to perform effectively in diverse visual environments, across varying lighting, object poses, and long-horizon tasks, indicates a policy that has learned robust underlying dynamics rather than superficial correlations. This enhanced generalization is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic real-world settings where encountering novel conditions is inevitable.

#### 5.3. Unlocking Data and Parameter Efficiency

One of FRAPPE's most impactful contributions is its innovative approach to data utilization. By effectively leveraging large volumes of *action-free* human egocentric videos—both web-scale and task-specific—FRAPPE drastically reduces the dependence on expensive and time-consuming action-annotated robot teleoperation data. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and scaling capable robotic policies, making advanced robot learning more accessible. Furthermore, the use of LoRA in its Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA) architecture ensures high parameter efficiency during fine-tuning. This allows for effective adaptation and scaling of large models without requiring full-parameter training, making the approach practical for resource-constrained environments and continuous model improvement.

#### 5.4. Scalability and Practical Deployability

The parallel progressive expansion mechanism, combined with the efficient inference strategy (which maintains the parallel computational graph without requiring VFMs online), positions FRAPPE as a highly scalable solution. Its ability to extract diverse world knowledge from multiple VFMs, while maintaining acceptable inference latency and memory footprint, makes it well-suited for deployment on real robotic platforms. The successful application on smaller models (RDT-130M) further underscores its practical utility, suggesting that advanced robotic capabilities can be achieved even with less massive foundation models.

#### 5.5. Future Implications

FRAPPE's work provides a clear roadmap for future research in generalist robotics. It encourages a shift towards implicit world modeling, multi-modal knowledge integration, and creative leveraging of diverse, low-cost data sources. This could accelerate the development of truly autonomous and adaptive robots capable of performing complex tasks in open-world environments, learning continuously from human interactions and readily available visual data, thereby bridging the gap between current robotic capabilities and the demands of general-purpose intelligence.
