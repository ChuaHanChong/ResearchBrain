# FRAPPE: Infusing World Modeling into Generalist Policies via Multiple Future Representation Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> FRAPPE introduces a training paradigm that integrates world modeling into generalist robotic policies by aligning model features with latent representations from multiple visual foundation models. This approach demonstrates improved generalization across diverse tasks, enhances data efficiency through the use of action-free human egocentric videos, and achieves scalable performance in both simulated and real-world bimanual manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Generalist robotic policies struggle with computational inefficiency and poor generalization when relying on pixel-level reconstruction for world modeling, due to high computational cost and error accumulation.
- Implicit world modeling approaches often suffer from inductive biases by using representations from only a single visual foundation model, limiting their versatility for diverse robotic tasks.
- The development of robust generalist policies is hindered by the high cost of collecting action-annotated robot data and the absence of efficient fine-tuning and inference strategies.

## Method

- FRAPPE infuses implicit world modeling by aligning policy features with latent representations of future observations from multiple diverse Visual Foundation Models (VFMs), avoiding pixel-level reconstruction.
- A two-stage fine-tuning strategy is implemented: an initial full-parameter mid-training phase followed by a parameter-efficient post-training phase utilizing a Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA) architecture.
- The MiPA architecture leverages multiple sets of future prefixes and LoRA modules on a shared, frozen Robotic Diffusion Transformer (RDT) backbone, each dedicated to aligning with a different teacher VFM for parallel world knowledge integration.

## Results

- Achieved the highest average success rates in RoboTwin 2.0 simulation, outperforming state-of-the-art methods with 52.3% in easy settings and demonstrating superior generalization with 25.5% in hard, randomized settings.
- Showcased strong generalization on real-world basic tasks and successfully executed a complex long-horizon dual-arm task with a 20% success rate, where the baseline policy failed.
- Demonstrated significant performance improvements by co-training with large-scale action-free human egocentric videos, enhancing world modeling for novel objects and reducing reliance on robot demonstration data, while maintaining practical inference efficiency with only about 20ms additional latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit world modeling through aligning with latent representations from multiple diverse Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) offers a more robust and semantically rich understanding of future states compared to explicit pixel generation or single-VFM methods.
> - A two-stage fine-tuning strategy is effective for incrementally adapting pre-trained foundation models to complex world-modeling objectives, addressing distribution shifts and architectural changes.
> - Parameter-efficient methods like LoRA, when combined with future prefixes in a Mixture-of-Prefix-and-LoRA (MiPA) architecture, enable scalable integration of diverse world knowledge while keeping the core policy backbone frozen.
> - Action-free human egocentric data can effectively provide a strong inductive prior for world modeling, substantially reducing the need for costly action-annotated robot demonstration data.
