# HiCoGen: Hierarchical Compositional Text-to-Image Generation in Diffusion Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> HiCoGen (Hierarchical Compositional Text-to-Image Generation) introduces a Chain of Synthesis paradigm, powered by Large Language Models and Reinforcement Learning, to generate complex images from hierarchical text prompts. It achieves significant improvements in concept coverage (0.7127), attribute accuracy (0.7673), and relationship accuracy (0.8203) on the new HiCoPrompt benchmark, while maintaining high visual appeal.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-image diffusion models struggle with complex prompts, leading to 'concept missing,' 'concept confusion,' and 'overall quality decline' due to a widening semantic gap.
- Traditional diffusion model samplers offer limited exploration space, hindering the effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for fine-tuning generative models.
- Lack of standardized benchmarks for rigorously evaluating compositional and hierarchical reasoning abilities in text-to-image generation models.

## Method

- Introduces the '==Chain of Synthesis== (CoS)' paradigm, which uses an LLM to decompose complex prompts into ordered, subject-centric sub-prompts, then iteratively synthesizes visual content, using previous steps as visual context.
- Develops a GRPO-based Reinforcement Learning framework with a 'Decaying Stochasticity Schedule' to optimize the CoS pipeline and maximize sample diversity during early generation stages.
- Implements a hierarchical reward mechanism, combining global image quality, subject-specific fidelity, and inter-subject relationship accuracy to provide comprehensive supervision for RL.

## Results

- HiCoGen achieved superior performance on the HiCoPrompt benchmark, with concept coverage of **0.7127** (+**0.0835** over best baseline), attribute accuracy of **0.7673** (+**0.075** over best baseline), and relationship accuracy of **0.8203** (+**0.0374** over best baseline).
- Qualitatively, HiCoGen consistently generates images that faithfully represent complex descriptions, mitigating concept confusion and omission compared to strong baselines like FLUX and Qwen-Image.
- The Decaying Stochasticity Schedule enhanced sample diversity (lower SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS scores) and improved RL performance, leading to higher HPSv2 scores (**0.3357**) indicating better alignment with human preferences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex visual generation into iterative, context-aware synthesis steps, inspired by LLM's Chain-of-Thought, effectively bridges the semantic gap for compositional prompts.
> - Sample diversity in diffusion models is maximized by concentrating stochasticity in early generation stages, a theoretically proven insight enabling more effective Reinforcement Learning.
> - Fine-grained hierarchical rewards (global, subject, relationship) are essential for precisely guiding diffusion models to accurately render individual elements and their complex interactions within a scene.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has seen revolutionary advancements with the advent of diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, DALL-E). These models are capable of producing visually stunning and diverse images from simple textual prompts. However, a significant limitation persists when these models are confronted with "complex prompts" involving multiple objects, intricate attributes, and nuanced hierarchical relationships between entities. Existing T2I models often suffer from issues like "concept missing" (omitting parts of the prompt), "concept confusion" (misinterpreting relationships or attributes), and "overall quality decline" when the semantic gap between the text and desired image becomes too wide.

This paper positions itself directly at the forefront of addressing this fundamental challenge in compositional T2I generation. It draws inspiration from the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) which, through techniques like "Chain-of-Thought" prompting, demonstrate robust reasoning by decomposing complex problems into sequential, manageable steps. This contrasts with traditional T2I models that attempt a "monolithic generation" from a single, complex prompt. HiCoGen thus contributes to the emerging trend of integrating LLM-like reasoning paradigms into visual synthesis, moving beyond merely scaling diffusion inference to scaling the *content-level reasoning* within the generative process.

Furthermore, the work also fits within the growing area of applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to fine-tune diffusion models. Recent successes like DDPO and Diffusion-DPO have shown the efficacy of RL in aligning generative models with human preferences. HiCoGen specifically leverages the GRPO framework, building on its success in enhancing visual generation. However, the authors identify a critical bottleneck in applying RL to diffusion models: the limited exploration space offered by standard samplers. Their theoretical contribution of the "Decaying Stochasticity Schedule" directly addresses this, aiming to optimize sample diversity for more effective RL training.

Finally, the introduction of the "HiCoPrompt" benchmark addresses a crucial gap in evaluation. While various T2I benchmarks exist (e.g., DrawBench, T2I-CompBench, LongBench-T2I), many lack the explicit hierarchical and richly attributed compositional structures necessary to rigorously test models on complex prompts. HiCoGen, therefore, not only proposes a novel generative framework but also provides a much-needed tool for standardized and robust evaluation of compositional reasoning in T2I models, pushing the entire field forward.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind HiCoGen stems from the observed limitations of current state-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models when processing complex, long, or hierarchically structured textual prompts. While these models excel at generating high-quality images for simple descriptions, they often fail to accurately represent all specified concepts, misinterpret relationships between objects, or suffer from a general degradation in image quality when faced with intricate instructions. The authors posit that this failure arises from the "semantic gap" between text and image domains, which widens with increasing prompt complexity, making a "monolithic generation" approach insufficient.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the HiCoGen framework are:

1. **To overcome limitations of monolithic generation for complex prompts:** The central aim is to move away from attempting to generate an entire complex scene in a single step, which often leads to concept omission and confusion.
2. **To introduce a "Chain of Synthesis (CoS)" paradigm:** Inspired by Chain-of-Thought in LLMs, the objective is to decompose complex visual synthesis into a series of manageable, iterative steps, where each step builds upon the visual context established by previous steps.
3. **To leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for prompt decomposition:** An objective is to utilize the reasoning capabilities of LLMs to break down complex hierarchical prompts into minimal, subject-centric semantic units and rewrite them for effective generation.
4. **To optimize the CoS pipeline using Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The authors aim to fine-tune the iterative generation process for improved fidelity and accuracy by employing an RL framework.
5. **To enhance exploration in diffusion model RL:** A crucial objective is to address the inherent limitation of standard diffusion samplers in providing sufficient exploration space for effective RL. This involves developing a theoretically grounded sampling mechanism that maximizes sample diversity.
6. **To design a hierarchical reward mechanism for fine-grained supervision:** The goal is to provide comprehensive feedback to the RL algorithm by evaluating generated images not just globally, but also at the subject-specific and inter-subject relationship levels.
7. **To construct a new benchmark for compositional T2I evaluation:** To facilitate rigorous and standardized assessment of models' abilities to handle complex, hierarchical prompts, an objective is to create the "HiCoPrompt" dataset.

In essence, HiCoGen seeks to enable text-to-image models to "reason" visually by constructing complex scenes piece by piece, guided by precise, hierarchical rewards and an optimized sampling strategy, thereby achieving unprecedented accuracy in compositional image generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HiCoGen introduces a novel framework built on the "Chain of Synthesis (CoS)" paradigm, designed to tackle the challenges of hierarchical compositional text-to-image generation. This methodology integrates Large Language Models (LLMs), a specialized Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework with an enhanced sampler, and a multi-level reward system.

#### 3.1. Chain of Synthesis (CoS) Paradigm

The core innovation is CoS, which replaces monolithic image generation with an iterative, part-by-part construction process.
- **Parse & Rewrite LLM:** A complex input prompt `O` is first processed by an LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B). The LLM decomposes `O` into an ordered set of subject-centric sub-prompts `P(i)`. Each `P(i)` further breaks down into fine-grained components (attributes). The LLM also rewrites these sub-prompts to be suitable for individual generative steps, ensuring detailed descriptions for the current subject while higher layers retain only general categorical information.
- **Iterative Visual Construction:** Instead of generating the entire image at once, HiCoGen synthesizes these semantic units iteratively. In each step, a subject-driven generative model (e.g., FLUX with UNO LoRA weights) generates a specific content (`z_0` in the latent domain) based on its sub-prompt. Crucially, the image generated in a previous step provides "visual context" for the subsequent steps. This is achieved by concatenating the latent representation of previously generated contents (`z_0(0), z_0(1)...`) with the current text prompt (`P(i)`) and the noisy latent (`z_t`) as input to the diffusion model. This progressively replaces entangled textual descriptions with concrete visual information, ensuring faithful construction of the final complex scene. The process continues until all concepts in the original prompt are incorporated.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Framework

To optimize the fidelity and accuracy of the CoS pipeline, HiCoGen employs a GRPO-based (Generalized Ratio Policy Optimization) reinforcement learning framework.
- **Advantage Function:** The RL algorithm computes an advantage function for each sampled output, normalized by the mean and standard deviation of rewards within a batch, to stabilize training.
- **Policy Optimization:** The policy model (diffusion model) is optimized by maximizing an objective function that ensures improvements in high-reward trajectories while preventing drastic policy changes.

#### 3.3. Decaying Stochasticity Schedule

A critical challenge for effective RL in diffusion models is the limited exploration space of standard samplers. The authors address this with a novel theoretical insight and an associated schedule:
- **Optimal Stochasticity Allocation:** Through rigorous theoretical analysis of the reverse-time Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) that governs diffusion models, the authors prove that sample diversity (quantified by the trace of the covariance matrix of the final sample `Tr(Cov(z_0))`) is maximized by concentrating stochasticity in the early generation stages. This is based on assumptions of linearizability and increasing contractivity of the SDE dynamics towards the end of the denoising process. Early perturbations have a greater impact on the final output due to less suppression by the system dynamics.
- **Cosine Decay Implementation:** Based on this proof, they propose a "Decaying Stochasticity Schedule." This is implemented as a cosine decay function for the stochasticity term `η(t)`, where `η(t)` is maximized in early diffusion steps (large `t`) and gradually decreases towards zero in later steps (small `t`). This strategy allows for broad exploration during the initial structural formation of the image while preserving fidelity during the final refinement, optimizing the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Reward Mechanism

To provide comprehensive and fine-grained supervision for the RL algorithm, a novel hierarchical reward function `R_total` is defined as a weighted sum of three components:
- **Global Reward (R_global):** Assesses overall image quality and text-image alignment using standard metrics. It combines scores from the CLIP model (`S_clip`) for semantic alignment and HPSv2 (`S_hps`) for human preference.
- **Subject-Specific Reward (R_subject):** Evaluates the fidelity and attributes of individual key subjects. It uses GroundingDino to locate subjects and then assesses similarity between the cropped subject region and a reference image using DINOv2 embeddings. Additionally, powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs, e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) are employed as rule-based reward models to verify specific attributes (e.g., color, pose) of each subject.
- **Relationship Reward (R_relationship):** Focuses on the contextual correctness, interaction, and proportional accuracy between subjects. VLMs are prompted to verify hierarchical relationships, relative proportions, and the naturalness of interactions (e.g., preventing floating objects or stiff interactions).

#### 3.5. HiCoPrompt Dataset

To enable rigorous evaluation, the authors constructed "HiCoPrompt," a new benchmark dataset.
- **Structured Generation:** The dataset is systematically generated using ChatGPT/LLMs. It defines multiple subjects, each with up to three distinct, richly described attributes.
- **Phases of Construction:**
 1. **Subject Generation:** Diverse primary subjects are selected, and ChatGPT generates core quantifiable attributes (wearing, holding, accessory).
 2. **Attribute Rewriting:** LLMs rewrite simple attributes into imaginative, detailed descriptions (e.g., "book" to "an open ancient magic book with a thick dark brown tanned leather cover...").
 3. **Sub-Prompt Reframing:** Multiple subjects with their detailed attributes are combined, and LLMs construct the final prompt by adding necessary prepositions or actions to integrate distinct sentences and define relationships.
- The dataset comprises 3k prompts for testing and 12k for RL training, offering clear hierarchical and compositional structures for robust evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the newly introduced HiCoPrompt dataset demonstrate that HiCoGen significantly advances the state-of-the-art in hierarchical compositional text-to-image generation.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Superiority

HiCoGen was benchmarked against two categories of existing methods:
- **End-to-end Text-to-Image Generative Models:** Including SDXL, Stable-Diffusion-3, FLUX.1-dev, FLUX.1-Krea, and Qwen-Image.
- **Subject-Driven Text-to-Image Generative Models:** Such as MS-Diffusion, OmniGen, and UNO (used in a multi-step fashion for multi-subject prompts).

The results in Table 2 clearly show HiCoGen outperforming all baselines across all evaluation metrics:
- **Concept Coverage (Acc_exist):** HiCoGen achieves 0.7127, significantly higher than Qwen-Image (0.6292), which was previously noted for handling complex prompts. This indicates a superior ability to ensure all specified concepts are present in the generated image.
- **Attribute Accuracy (Acc_attribute):** With a score of 0.7673, HiCoGen demonstrates excellent fidelity in rendering the detailed attributes of subjects, outperforming Qwen-Image (0.6907) and even specialized subject-driven models like UNO (0.6923).
- **Relationship Accuracy (Acc_relationship):** HiCoGen achieves 0.8203, showcasing its effectiveness in correctly interpreting and depicting interactions, proportions, and spatial relationships between subjects, far surpassing other models (e.g., Qwen-Image at 0.7829, UNO at 0.3697).
- **Visual Appeal (CLIP Score & HPSv2):** HiCoGen also achieves the highest CLIP Score (0.3192) and HPSv2 (0.3357), indicating superior overall image quality and better alignment with human preferences compared to all other models. This suggests that improved compositional accuracy does not come at the cost of aesthetic quality.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Demonstrations

The visual results in Figure 4 provide compelling qualitative evidence of HiCoGen's capabilities.
- **Mitigation of Concept Confusion/Omission:** Unlike baseline models (e.g., FLUX, Qwen-Image) which struggle with specific details (e.g., gradient color transitions, extra body parts, misassigned attributes), HiCoGen consistently generates images that faithfully represent complex descriptions.
- **Accurate Multi-Subject Composition:** For prompts involving two or three subjects, HiCoGen significantly reduces issues where attributes are incorrectly applied to different subjects or where subjects interact unnaturally. For example, baseline models might incorrectly assign "floral clothing" to the wrong person or misplace "stacked necklaces," problems HiCoGen largely avoids.
- **Intermediate Results (Supplementary):** Figure 6 in the supplementary material illustrates the Chain of Synthesis process, showing how HiCoGen progressively builds the image by adding subjects and attributes step-by-step, ensuring their coherent integration.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Table 3 presents ablation studies on the hierarchical reward components:
- **Importance of Subject Reward:** Removing the subject reward leads to a significant decrease in `Acc_attribute` (approx. 14% decline), highlighting its crucial role in ensuring individual subject details are accurately rendered.
- **Importance of Relationship Reward:** Removing the relationship reward results in about a 4% reduction in `Acc_relationship`, confirming its effectiveness in governing accurate inter-subject interactions and proportions.
- **Comprehensive Reward System:** The combination of global, subject, and relationship rewards yields the best overall performance, validating the design choice of a multi-level supervision mechanism.

#### 4.4. Discussion on Number of Subjects

The paper discusses (Table 4) that while HiCoGen performs exceptionally well for 1-2 subjects, accuracy metrics (especially `Acc_exist` and `Acc_attribute`) show a decline when the number of subjects increases to 3. Despite this, HiCoGen still substantially outperforms baselines like Qwen-Image and UNO in multi-subject scenarios, indicating its robustness even under increased complexity.

#### 4.5. Impact of Decaying Stochasticity Schedule

Figure 5 demonstrates the effectiveness of the Decaying Stochasticity Schedule.
- **Enhanced Sample Diversity:** Compared to a baseline, samples generated with the proposed schedule exhibit lower similarity (lower SSIM, PSNR, and LPIPS scores) between different samples. This confirms that concentrating stochasticity in early stages indeed maximizes sample diversity.
- **Improved RL Performance:** This enhanced diversity is crucial for effective RL, allowing the algorithm to explore a broader range of possible generation policies, ultimately leading to higher `HPSv2` scores, indicating better alignment with human preferences. This validates the theoretical proof and the practical implementation of the schedule.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HiCoGen framework represents a significant advancement in the field of text-to-image generation, particularly in its ability to handle complex and hierarchically structured prompts, a long-standing challenge for even the most advanced diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Semantic Gap in T2I:** HiCoGen fundamentally addresses the limitation of monolithic generation by introducing the "Chain of Synthesis" paradigm. This iterative, context-aware approach, inspired by LLM reasoning, allows for a more faithful and robust translation of intricate textual descriptions into visual scenes. This paradigm shift could pave the way for T2I models to tackle even more abstract or nuanced compositional tasks.

2. **Principled Reinforcement Learning for Diffusion Models:** The theoretical analysis and subsequent implementation of the "Decaying Stochasticity Schedule" provide a crucial contribution to the application of RL in diffusion models. By proving and demonstrating that early-stage stochasticity maximizes sample diversity, the authors offer a principled method to enhance exploration, making RL-based fine-tuning more effective and efficient for a wider range of generative tasks beyond compositional T2I.

3. **Fine-Grained Controllability and Fidelity:** The hierarchical reward mechanism is a key innovation for achieving precise control over generated content. By providing supervision at global, subject, and relationship levels, HiCoGen ensures not only overall quality but also accurate depiction of individual elements and their interactions. This level of fine-grained control is vital for professional applications requiring high fidelity to specific design parameters.

4. **Enabling New Applications:** The enhanced compositional capabilities of HiCoGen could unlock numerous new applications.
 - **Creative Content Generation:** Artists, designers, and marketers could generate highly specific scenes with complex arrangements of characters, objects, and environments, greatly accelerating content creation workflows.
 - **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Generating detailed and consistent virtual worlds or game assets from textual descriptions, including dynamic interactions between elements.
 - **Product Design and Prototyping:** Visualizing complex product configurations or scenarios based on detailed specifications.
 - **Education and Training:** Creating custom visual aids for complex concepts.
 - **Human-Machine Interaction:** Enabling more intuitive and descriptive interfaces where users can describe intricate visual requirements.
 The collaboration with an automotive R&D company (ZEEKR) hints at potential applications in areas like virtual car configurators, design exploration, or autonomous driving simulation environments.

5. **New Benchmark for Future Research:** The HiCoPrompt dataset is a critical contribution to the research community. By providing a systematically constructed benchmark with explicit hierarchical and compositional structures, it establishes a robust standard for evaluating future T2I models. This will facilitate fair comparisons, drive targeted research efforts, and accelerate progress in compositional text-to-image generation.

In conclusion, HiCoGen represents a substantial step towards more intelligent and controllable generative AI. Its blend of LLM reasoning, theoretically-grounded RL, and fine-grained visual supervision addresses core limitations of current T2I models, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in complex visual synthesis and opening doors for a new generation of sophisticated AI-powered applications.
