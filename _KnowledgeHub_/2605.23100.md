# Four Simple Proprioceptive Estimators for Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed and evaluated four proprioceptive state estimators for legged robots, fusing high-rate IMU data with intermittent foot contacts using factor graph optimization. These estimators achieved reduced translational and vertical drift compared to existing baselines on the GaRLILEO dataset.

## Problem

- Consumer-grade IMUs in legged robots suffer from integration drift over time, compromising state estimation accuracy.
- Legged robots require robust and stable proprioceptive state estimation (pose and velocity) as a fundamental capability for autonomous navigation.
- Many existing complex estimation systems rely on detailed kinematic models and direct joint angle integration, potentially increasing computational overhead and implementation complexity.

## Method

- A sequence of four progressively expressive proprioceptive state estimators was developed, ranging from an ==invariant EKF== to ==fixed-lag smoothers== with evolving IMU biases.
- The estimators fuse high-rate IMU data with intermittent geometric constraints from pre-computed 3D foot points in the body frame, rather than directly using joint angles.
- An event-driven update schedule for foot contacts and ==factor graph optimization== within the ==GTSAM== framework are used to process measurements and handle simultaneous contacts.

## Results

- The GTSAM variants consistently achieved lower accumulated errors, particularly in translational APE and APE_z, significantly outperforming existing baselines (Pronto, MUSE, Holistic Fusion) on the GaRLILEO dataset.
- On non-elevation-change sequences, the FL-Combined estimator (with evolving bias) often delivered the best translational and rotational Absolute Pose Error (APE_t, APE_r).
- In elevation-change environments, filtering variants or FL-Single (with persistent bias) sometimes yielded better APE_z, suggesting a more stable vertical drift suppression in some 3D scenarios despite FL-Combined's horizontal improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incrementally complex and modular estimators, even with simplified input requirements, can achieve competitive proprioceptive odometry performance in legged robots.
> - Fixed-lag smoothers, by incorporating a short history of measurements, generally provide improved trajectory-level accuracy compared to filtering approaches.
> - Allowing IMU bias to evolve can enhance horizontal and relative pose accuracy but may lead to increased vertical drift in certain challenging 3D environments, indicating a performance trade-off.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Proprioceptive state estimation is a fundamental capability for autonomous legged robots. It involves combining internal sensor data, primarily from Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) and leg kinematics, to determine the robot's pose (position and orientation) and velocity relative to its environment. The challenge in this field lies in the inherent limitations of these sensors: consumer-grade IMUs provide high-rate motion information but suffer from integration drift over time due to noise, while foot contacts offer strong geometric constraints but are intermittent.

Existing research has explored various sophisticated approaches to fuse these information sources. Some systems, such as those by Bloesch et al. (2013), Hartley et al. (2020), and Agrawal et al. (2022), integrate joint angles as direct measurements into the state estimation process, leveraging detailed kinematic chain models. This paper positions its work as a deliberately simpler alternative to some of these complex systems. Instead of directly using joint angles, it assumes that forward kinematics are computed by a front end, providing the estimator with a 3D foot point in the body frame. This simplification influences how contact measurement uncertainty is handled, as it is not informed by forward-kinematics Jacobians. Furthermore, the estimators presented here employ an event-driven update schedule for contacts, activating updates only upon touchdown or after a significant time interval, rather than at the high rate of joint angle measurements. This design choice is reported to be beneficial for reducing drift.

The broader objective of robust proprioceptive estimation is to create a reliable foundation upon which exteroceptive sensor information (e.g., from LiDAR or cameras) can be optionally fused. By addressing the core proprioceptive estimation problem with a sequence of incrementally complex yet effective methods, this work aims to provide both strong baselines and tools for further research in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a sequence of four increasingly expressive state estimators for legged robots. These estimators are designed to leverage high-rate inertial information from IMUs and intermittent geometric constraints from foot contacts to effectively mitigate the drift inherent in consumer-grade IMUs.

The motivation behind pursuing this objective is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing IMU Drift with Contact Constraints:** Legged robots inherently produce intermittent foot contacts, which offer precise geometric constraints. The core motivation is to effectively integrate these constraints with noisy IMU data to produce a more accurate and stable state estimate than either sensor could provide independently.
- **Incremental Complexity for Understanding and Reproducibility:** The paper adopts an incremental approach, starting with a fundamental invariant Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) and progressively adding complexity. This strategy is motivated by the desire to facilitate understanding of the contribution of each algorithmic enhancement, enable easier reproduction of results, and provide clear stepping stones for future research in proprioceptive legged odometry.
- **Simplification of Input Requirements:** Unlike some existing, more complex legged estimation systems that directly use joint angles, these estimators assume a front end computes forward kinematics to provide 3D foot points in the body frame. This design choice is motivated by the potential to simplify the estimator's interface and reduce dependencies on detailed kinematic models within the estimator itself, potentially lowering computational overhead or easing integration with diverse robot platforms.
- **Exploring Different State Estimation Paradigms:** The development moves through different state estimation paradigms: from a sequential EKF, to a local graph-based update, and finally to fixed-lag smoothers with varying IMU bias models. This exploration is motivated by a desire to understand the performance implications of handling simultaneous contacts, incorporating a short history into the estimation process, and modeling the evolution of IMU biases.
- **Facilitating Open Science and Research:** A key motivation is to provide open-source implementations of all four estimator variants within the GTSAM framework and as ROS2-compatible packages. This is intended to promote reproducibility, allow for direct comparison with future research, and serve as a accessible tool for the wider robotics community.
- **Quantitative Performance Evaluation:** The research aims to quantitatively evaluate the performance of these estimators against existing baselines and amongst themselves across diverse environments (flat and elevation-changing) to identify their strengths, limitations, and trade-offs, particularly concerning translational, rotational, and vertical drift.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research develops four distinct proprioceptive state estimators, building upon each other in complexity. All estimators share the common goal of combining high-rate IMU data with intermittent foot contact measurements to estimate the robot's floating-base state, which includes attitude, position, velocity, and IMU biases.

**Common Elements and Assumptions:**
- **Floating-Base State:** The floating-base state X consists of (R_N_B, p_N_B, v_N_B), representing body attitude, position, and velocity in the navigation frame.
- **Foot Contact Measurements:** A contact measurement c_B_i is the vector from the body frame to foot i, expressed in the body frame. The estimators assume forward kinematics has been computed externally, providing this 3D foot point in the body frame.
- **Event-Driven Contact Updates:** Contact updates are not performed at every IMU or joint-angle measurement. Instead, they are triggered by touchdown events or when a specified time interval (e.g., 100 ms) has passed since the last update.

**The Four Estimator Variants:**

1. **Invariant EKF (Inv. EKF):**
 - **Foundation:** Based on the contact-aided invariant EKF proposed by Hartley et al. (2020), which itself draws from the invariant filtering paradigm of Barrau and Bonnabel (2017). This framework represents the state on a Lie group (SE_k+2(3)) with local tangent-space errors, allowing for structured error propagation.
 - **State Representation:** The state X augments the base SE_2(3) (attitude, position, velocity) with *k* navigation-frame footholds (f_N_i), resulting in an SE_k+2(3) state. Foothold perturbations are defined as body-frame errors.
 - **IMU-driven Prediction:** A single IMU sample prediction is performed at the IMU rate using a left-linear Lie-group form (X+ = W * phi(X) * U).
 - `W`: Left factor representing gravity increment in the navigation frame.
 - `phi(X)`: Autonomous flow, advancing position via velocity.
 - `U`: Right factor representing the single-sample, zero-order-hold inertial increment in the body frame, built from bias-corrected gyroscope (ω_B) and accelerometer (a_B) readings.
 - The prediction Jacobian is state-independent due to the left-invariant formulation.
 - **Contact Update:** When a foot f_i is in stance, it is treated as a temporary landmark. The contact measurement model relates the body-frame vector (z_B_i) from base to foothold.
 - **Touchdown:** New footholds are initialized from the current robot pose and the measured foot position.
 - **Liftoff:** When a foot lifts off, its corresponding contact episode is marginalized out of the fixed-size state, and its covariance is updated.
 - **Optional Height Prior:** An optional scalar prior on navigation-frame foothold height (f_N_i,z) can be applied, though it was disabled in the reported experiments.

2. **Replacing the Update by a Local Graph (Inv. IEKF):**
 - **Modification:** This variant maintains the same Lie-group state and IMU-driven prediction model as the Inv. EKF. The key change is that the sequential EKF measurement update is replaced by a small batch correction using a local factor graph.
 - **Process:** At each scheduled contact update, the predicted state belief is converted into a nonlinear factor graph. This graph includes a prior factor on the current state (from the filter prediction), and contact factors for all active feet.
 - **Benefit:** Simultaneous contacts are handled symmetrically within a single nonlinear optimization solve (Levenberg–Marquardt), rather than through an arbitrary sequence of EKF updates.

3. **From Local Updates to a Fixed-lag Smoother (FL-Single):**
 - **Extension:** This approach extends the local graph idea by allowing the graph to span a short, recent time window, creating a fixed-lag smoother.
 - **State Representation:** Instead of fixed-size foot slots, the smoother uses explicit navigation-frame landmark variables for contact-episode footholds (f_N_i,e). The window contains:
 - One (R_N_B, p_N_B, v_N_B) base state per contact event.
 - **One shared IMU bias variable** across the entire window.
 - One navigation-frame foothold landmark per active contact episode.
 - **Event-based Timeline and Preintegration:** Base states are created only at contact events. High-rate IMU samples between events are summarized by a single preintegrated IMU factor (Forster et al., 2017), which provides a relative SE_2(3) increment.
 - **Factor Graph Structure:** The graph includes preintegrated inertial factors connecting successive base states, an initial prior on the persistent bias, contact factors between event base states and foothold landmarks, and optional height priors. Old contact episodes falling outside the fixed-lag window are marginalized.

4. **Letting the Bias Evolve (FL-Combined):**
 - **Modification:** This variant is an extension of the fixed-lag smoother, relaxing the assumption of a single persistent IMU bias across the entire window.
 - **State Representation:** Each contact event now includes separate pose, velocity, *and bias variables* (b_j).
 - **Factor Graph Structure:** Successive event states are connected by an *extended preintegrated inertial factor*, which models the evolution of the IMU bias as a random walk, allowing the bias estimate to change over time within the window.

**Implementation Details:**
All four variants are implemented in GTSAM, a C++ library for factor graph optimization, and are available with a ROS2-compatible interface, facilitating reproducibility and further research. The experiments used the calibrated IMU-to-body transform provided with the dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The estimators were evaluated on the GaRLILEO dataset, collected using a Boston Dynamics Spot robot, which includes leg joint encoders, foot contact measurements, an IMU, and reference sensors for ground truth. Only IMU and leg measurements were used for the proprioceptive evaluation. The dataset includes sequences in both non-elevation-change (2D: Atrium, CorriLoop, BridgeLoop, Tunnel) and elevation-change (3D: Downstair, BiCorridor, Upstair, Quad, Overpass, SlopeStair) environments. Performance was assessed using Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for Absolute Pose Error (APE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE), decomposed into translational and rotational components, with a specific focus on APE_z for vertical drift.

**Comparison with Existing Legged-IMU Odometry Baselines:**
The four GTSAM variants were compared against Pronto, MUSE, and Holistic Fusion.
- **Overall Superiority:** The GTSAM variants consistently achieved lower accumulated errors, particularly in translational APE and APE_z, compared to the baselines. This advantage was evident in sequences involving long loops, stair traversal, or slope segments, where drift accumulation is significant.
- **Non-Elevation-Change (2D) Sequences:** On flat environments, the GTSAM variants demonstrated a notable reduction in translational and vertical drift. For instance, on CorriLoop and Atrium, the best GTSAM variants achieved substantially lower translational APE.
- **Elevation-Change (3D) Sequences:** The benefit of the GTSAM variants was more pronounced in 3D environments. They generally achieved the lowest or second-lowest APE_z across Downstair, BiCorridor, Upstair, Quad, Overpass, and SlopeStair, indicating effective suppression of vertical drift amplified by stair/slope traversal. While some baselines maintained competitive rotational RPE, their accumulated trajectory errors, especially vertically, were larger. The SE_K(3)-based invariant filtering representation contributed to this by providing a state-independent prediction Jacobian and a structured contact linearization. The fixed-lag formulations further aided by retaining recent contact episodes and inertial constraints within a sliding window.

**Analysis of the Four GTSAM Variants:**

- **Inv. EKF vs. Inv. IEKF:** These two variants exhibited very similar performance across all metrics. The replacement of the sequential EKF measurement update with a local nonlinear graph (Inv. IEKF) resulted in minor improvements in some rotational metrics but did not fundamentally alter the estimator's behavior or overall accuracy profile, likely due to the nature of the specific contact model and state representation being preserved.

- **Fixed-Lag Variants (FL-Single, FL-Combined) vs. Filtering Variants (Inv. EKF, Inv. IEKF):**
 - The fixed-lag smoothers generally improved trajectory-level accuracy compared to the filtering approaches by incorporating a short history of measurements.

- **FL-Combined (Evolving Bias) Performance:**
 - **Non-Elevation-Change (2D):** FL-Combined often achieved the best translational (APE_t) and rotational (APE_r) APE on sequences like Atrium, BridgeLoop, and Tunnel. It also showed the best RPE_t on most sequences. This indicates that allowing the IMU bias to evolve can improve accumulated pose accuracy when sufficient information from contacts and IMU is available. However, its benefit for APE_z was not uniform; in CorriLoop and BridgeLoop, FL-Single or filtering variants showed lower vertical drift.
 - **Elevation-Change (3D):** FL-Combined was strong in RPE_t and RPE_r. On Overpass and Downstair, it substantially reduced APE_t and APE_z (Overpass) or achieved the lowest APE_t, RPE_t, and RPE_r (Downstair). However, on BiCorridor, Upstair, and SlopeStair, the filtering variants or FL-Single produced better APE_z. This suggests a trade-off: the additional bias degrees of freedom in FL-Combined might, in some 3D scenarios, absorb transient contact-induced vertical impacts into the estimated IMU bias, leading to larger vertical drift, even if horizontal accuracy is improved.

- **FL-Single (Persistent Bias) Performance:**
 - FL-Single often provided a stable balance, improving upon filtering variants in some accumulated metrics while often avoiding the larger APE_z sometimes observed with FL-Combined, particularly on elevation-change sequences. It was noted as a stable middle ground in several cases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a systematic development and evaluation of four proprioceptive state estimators for legged robots, ranging from a basic invariant EKF to a sophisticated fixed-lag smoother with evolving IMU biases. The primary significance lies in demonstrating that these relatively simple and modular approaches, implemented within the GTSAM framework, can achieve competitive proprioceptive odometry performance without relying on complex kinematic models or exteroceptive sensors.

The potential impacts are several:

- **Enhanced Legged Robot Autonomy:** The demonstrated ability to maintain stable long-term proprioceptive odometry, especially with significantly reduced translational and vertical drift, forms a more robust foundation for autonomous legged robot navigation. This improved state estimation can directly benefit higher-level tasks such as motion planning, control, and environment mapping, reducing reliance on external infrastructure or accurate prior maps.
- **Guidance for Estimator Design:** The incremental development and comparative analysis of the four variants provide valuable insights into the trade-offs involved in designing state estimators. Specifically, it highlights that while increasing estimator expressiveness (e.g., allowing IMU bias to evolve) can improve certain aspects of accuracy (e.g., horizontal or relative pose), it does not uniformly benefit all metrics (e.g., vertical drift in challenging 3D environments). This finding offers practical guidance for researchers and developers to select or design estimators tailored to specific performance requirements and operational conditions.
- **Reproducibility and Benchmarking:** The commitment to open-sourcing all four estimator variants in GTSAM and providing ROS2-compatible implementations is a substantial contribution to the robotics community. This facilitates direct comparison of future work, encourages benchmarking on standardized datasets like GaRLILEO, and lowers the barrier to entry for researchers exploring proprioceptive legged odometry and multi-sensor fusion.
- **Foundation for Multi-Sensor Fusion:** By establishing strong proprioceptive baselines, this work creates a solid platform for integrating additional exteroceptive sensor information (radar, LiDAR, camera). A highly accurate and drift-mitigated proprioceptive estimate is crucial for robustly fusing external measurements, especially in challenging environments where exteroceptive data might be sparse or unreliable.
- **Advancements in Factor Graph Optimization:** The successful application of factor graph optimization techniques (GTSAM) to problems in legged robot state estimation further validates its utility and flexibility in handling complex, nonlinear estimation problems with intermittent measurements and varying state representations.
