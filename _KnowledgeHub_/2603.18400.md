# Graph-of-Constraints Model Predictive Control for Reactive Multi-agent Task and Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed Graph-of-Constraints Model Predictive Control (GoC-MPC), a framework that generalizes sequential constraints to a graph-based representation for multi-agent Task and Motion Planning. This approach allows for dynamic agent assignment and parallel task execution, achieving up to 70 times faster computation and higher success rates compared to existing reactive TAMP methods in simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing "sequence-of-constraints" formulations in multi-agent Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) impose a total (sequential) order on task steps, limiting parallelism and overall efficiency.
- Current TAMP methods typically rely on static assignments of agents to specific subtasks, hindering the system's ability to adapt to disturbances or revise suboptimal allocations.
- Limitations of existing frameworks significantly exacerbate the impact of disturbances, often leading to full system halts or resets rather than localized, adaptive recovery.

## Method

- Introduces a Graph-of-Constraints (GoC) model, a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) that represents partially ordered task steps and their dependencies.
- Integrates GoC with a receding-horizon ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) scheme, incorporating a dynamic agent assignment matrix and disjunctive constraints for online optimization of agent-to-task allocations.
- Decomposes the complex multi-agent TAMP problem into three sequential subproblems (waypoints/assignments, agent splines, short receding-horizon control) solved within each MPC cycle for real-time reactivity, including a coordinated backtracking mechanism for disturbance recovery.

## Results

- Achieved **100%** success rates on multi-agent manipulation tasks (Block-Stacking, Pick-and-Pour) in static simulations, outperforming a baseline (ReKep) that achieved **70%** and **60%** respectively.
- Demonstrated significant computational efficiency, running up to 70 times faster (e.g., average time of 0.108s vs. 7.11s for Block-Stacking) and producing shorter total path lengths in simulated environments.
- Maintained robust performance under disturbances with **100%** success and up to 80 times faster maximum execution times compared to the baseline, successfully deploying on a real dual-UR5e manipulator system with comparable performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalizing sequential task constraints to a graph structure inherently allows for partial ordering, enabling significant gains in parallel execution and overall task efficiency for multi-agent systems.
> - Decomposing the combined task and motion planning problem into distinct, sequentially solvable optimization subproblems enables efficient, near real-time reactivity for complex multi-agent scenarios.
> - Integrating dynamic agent assignment and coordinated backtracking within an MPC framework provides robust resilience, allowing multi-agent systems to adapt fluidly to disturbances without complete task resets.
