# Graph-of-Constraints Model Predictive Control for Reactive Multi-agent Task and Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed Graph-of-Constraints Model Predictive Control (GoC-MPC), a framework that generalizes sequential constraints to a graph-based representation for multi-agent Task and Motion Planning. This approach allows for dynamic agent assignment and parallel task execution, achieving up to 70 times faster computation and higher success rates compared to existing reactive TAMP methods in simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing "sequence-of-constraints" formulations in multi-agent Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) impose a total (sequential) order on task steps, limiting parallelism and overall efficiency.
- Current TAMP methods typically rely on static assignments of agents to specific subtasks, hindering the system's ability to adapt to disturbances or revise suboptimal allocations.
- Limitations of existing frameworks significantly exacerbate the impact of disturbances, often leading to full system halts or resets rather than localized, adaptive recovery.

## Method

- Introduces a Graph-of-Constraints (GoC) model, a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) that represents partially ordered task steps and their dependencies.
- Integrates GoC with a receding-horizon ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) scheme, incorporating a dynamic agent assignment matrix and disjunctive constraints for online optimization of agent-to-task allocations.
- Decomposes the complex multi-agent TAMP problem into three sequential subproblems (waypoints/assignments, agent splines, short receding-horizon control) solved within each MPC cycle for real-time reactivity, including a coordinated backtracking mechanism for disturbance recovery.

## Results

- Achieved **100%** success rates on multi-agent manipulation tasks (Block-Stacking, Pick-and-Pour) in static simulations, outperforming a baseline (ReKep) that achieved **70%** and **60%** respectively.
- Demonstrated significant computational efficiency, running up to 70 times faster (e.g., average time of 0.108s vs. 7.11s for Block-Stacking) and producing shorter total path lengths in simulated environments.
- Maintained robust performance under disturbances with **100%** success and up to 80 times faster maximum execution times compared to the baseline, successfully deploying on a real dual-UR5e manipulator system with comparable performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalizing sequential task constraints to a graph structure inherently allows for partial ordering, enabling significant gains in parallel execution and overall task efficiency for multi-agent systems.
> - Decomposing the combined task and motion planning problem into distinct, sequentially solvable optimization subproblems enables efficient, near real-time reactivity for complex multi-agent scenarios.
> - Integrating dynamic agent assignment and coordinated backtracking within an MPC framework provides robust resilience, allowing multi-agent systems to adapt fluidly to disturbances without complete task resets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) within multi-robot systems, specifically focusing on reactive control strategies. TAMP involves integrating high-level symbolic planning with low-level continuous motion planning to enable robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks. As robot teams scale, the complexity of TAMP problems increases significantly, particularly for collaborative manipulation.

A notable family of existing TAMP approaches leverages constrained optimization, often formulating problems as sequences of constraints. When used in a receding-horizon loop, similar to Model Predictive Control (MPC), these optimization-based formulations can extend to reactive execution, allowing for the recomputation of plans during runtime in response to new information or disturbances. Recent advancements in this area have shown that defining constraints using universally applicable geometric primitives, such as spatial keypoints tracked within the robot's workspace, can enable general-purpose manipulation across a diverse range of tasks without requiring precise system models or extensive task-specific training data. Methods like Relational Keypoint constraints (ReKeps) exemplify this approach.

However, the existing frameworks, particularly those based on singular sequences of constraints, present two primary limitations in multi-agent settings. First, they impose a total order on task steps, which can hinder the parallel execution of independent subtasks by different agents. Second, they often assume a static assignment of agents to tasks, limiting the system's ability to adapt when disturbances alter task allocations or when a more optimal assignment becomes available. This work directly addresses these limitations by generalizing the sequence-of-constraints paradigm to a Graph-of-Constraints (GoC) framework, integrated with MPC, thereby enabling dynamic agent coordination and improved disturbance recovery in reactive multi-agent TAMP. It positions itself as a development within the category of reactive TAMP methods that re-solve optimization problems derived from a task skeleton, specifically enhancing their multi-agent capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and robust method for reactive multi-agent Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) that can effectively operate under real-world disturbances. This objective is motivated by several fundamental limitations identified in existing constraint-based TAMP approaches when applied to multi-agent scenarios:

1. **Addressing Total Task Ordering:** Current "sequence-of-constraints" formulations inherently impose a total (sequential) order on the execution of task steps. This strict ordering can prevent multiple agents from performing independent subtasks in parallel, leading to unnecessary delays. For instance, if two agents could simultaneously perform initial setup tasks, a totally-ordered sequence would force one agent to wait for the other, underutilizing resources. The motivation here is to enable the representation and execution of partially ordered tasks, allowing for greater parallelism and efficiency in multi-agent operations.

2. **Enabling Dynamic Agent Assignment:** Existing methods typically rely on static assignments of agents to specific subtasks, or they implicitly treat the entire team as a single "joint-agent." This static assignment lacks flexibility; a suboptimal initial assignment cannot be revised, and the system cannot adapt if an agent becomes incapacitated or if a disturbance makes a current assignment infeasible. The objective is to introduce dynamic agent assignment, allowing the system to optimize agent-to-task allocations online, responding to the current system state and environmental conditions.

3. **Improving Disturbance Recovery:** The aforementioned limitations significantly exacerbate the impact of disturbances. If a single agent encounters a failure or perturbation in a statically assigned, totally ordered task, it can cause the entire team's progress to halt or delay, even if other agents are unaffected. For example, a dropped object might force an entire task stage to reset for all agents. The motivation is to enhance the system's ability to recover from disturbances by allowing for adaptive re-planning, including dynamic reassignments and flexible backtracking, ensuring that unaffected agents can continue progress or adapt their actions efficiently.

4. **Achieving General-Purpose Manipulation from Visual Observations:** Drawing inspiration from recent work, a key motivation is to maintain and extend the generality of task specification. By defining constraints in terms of abstract, universally applicable geometric primitives like 3D keypoints, the method aims to perform a wide range of manipulation tasks without requiring precise environment models or extensive task-specific training data. This enables direct operation from visual observations, increasing the practical applicability of the system.

5. **Achieving Real-Time Reactive Planning:** Given the dynamic nature of multi-agent tasks and the inevitability of real-world disturbances, the ability to recompute plans rapidly is critical. The research aims to develop a computational framework that can decompose and solve the complex multi-agent TAMP problem at real-time speeds, thereby enabling continuous reactive adaptation during execution.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to introduce a robust, flexible, and efficient multi-agent TAMP framework capable of operating in complex, dynamic, and uncertain environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Graph-of-Constraints Model Predictive Control (GoC-MPC), generalizes existing sequence-of-constraints approaches by introducing a Graph-of-Constraints (GoC) model and integrating it with a receding-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme.

**A. Preliminaries**
The method operates on a `d`-dimensional configuration space `X`, composed of an actuated component `X_a` (for robot configurations) and a passive component `X_p` (for scene elements like keypoints). In a multi-agent system with `M` agents, `X_a` is partitioned into `M` single-agent spaces `X_{a_j}`. Constraints are defined as functions `ϕ_i: X → R^{d_i}` where `ϕ_i(x) ≤ 0` indicates satisfaction. Prior methods used "sequences of constraints" `(ϕ_{1:K}, φ_{1:K})` which enforce a total ordering `t_{i-1} ≤ t_i` and typically treat multiple agents as a single entity or assign tasks statically. GoC-MPC directly addresses these limitations.

**B. Graphs of Constraints (GoCs)**
A GoC `G = (V, E, Φ_V, Φ_E)` is defined as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG):
- `V`: A finite set of `N` nodes, representing task steps or waypoints.
- `E`: A set of directed edges `(a, b) ∈ V × V`, representing dependencies between steps.
- `Φ_V: V → Φ`: A mapping from nodes to "waypoint" constraint functions `ϕ_v`.
- `Φ_E: E → Φ`: A mapping from edges to "edge" constraint functions `φ_{ab}`.

This DAG structure inherently supports partially ordered tasks. To enable dynamic agent assignment, constraint functions `ϕ_i` are extended to `ϕ_i: {0, 1}^{K×M} × X → R^{d_i}`, where `K` is the number of subtasks and `M` is the number of agents. An assignment matrix `A ∈ {0, 1}^{K×M}` is introduced, constrained to be row-stochastic (each subtask assigned to exactly one agent). Disjunctive constraints based on `A` are implemented using big-M gating.

The overall objective for a GoC is to find optimal agent assignments `A`, a constraint-satisfying system trajectory `ξ: [0, t_{max}] → X`, and timings `t_{1:N}` for each node. This is formulated as a combined optimization problem:
$$
\min_{A, \xi, t_{1:N}} t_{max} + \int_{0}^{t_{max}} c(\xi(t), \dot{\xi}(t), \ddot{\xi}(t)) dt
$$
subject to:
- Initial state: `ξ(0) = x_0`, `˙ξ(0) = ˙x_0`, `t_0 = 0`.
- Assignment constraint: `∑_{j=0}^{M} A(k, j) = 1` for each subtask `k`.
- Waypoint constraints: `ϕ_v(A, ξ(t_v)) ≤ 0` for all `v ∈ V`.
- Edge constraints: `t_a ≤ t_b` and `φ_{ab}(A, ξ(t)) ≤ 0` for all `t ∈ [t_a, t_b]` for all `(a, b) ∈ E`.
- Dynamical constraints: `˙ξ(t) = h(ξ(t))`.
Here `c` is a cost function (e.g., penalizing acceleration or collisions).

**C. Problem Decomposition**
To solve this complex problem efficiently in real-time, it is decomposed into three subproblems solved sequentially within each MPC cycle:

1. **Waypoints and Assignments Sub-Problem:**
 - **Goal:** Determine optimal waypoint configurations `W ∈ X^N` and agent assignments `A`.
 - **Objective:** Minimize a surrogate cost based on the total geodesic distance `¯c` between waypoints, including from the initial state `x_0` to the topologically first set of nodes `V_{next}`.
 - **Constraints:** `A` is row-stochastic; all waypoint constraints `ϕ_v(A, W(v)) ≤ 0` are satisfied; edge constraints `φ_{ab}(A, W(a)) ≤ 0` and `φ_{ab}(A, W(b)) ≤ 0` are satisfied at waypoints; and `¯h(A, W(a), W(b)) ≤ 0` approximates dynamical constraints between waypoints (e.g., manipulated keypoints move rigidly, others stay fixed).
 - **Solution:** This is a mixed-integer non-linear program (MINLP). It is solved by enumerating possible integer assignments for `A` and then solving the resulting non-linear program (NLP) using Ipopt for each assignment. Warm-starting is used for subsequent iterations.

2. **Agent Splines Problem:**
 - **Goal:** Given `W` and `A`, compute optimal multi-agent paths as a set of agent-specific cubic splines `{ξ_j}`.
 - **Process:** A topologically-ordered breadth-first traversal of the GoC identifies ordered agent-specific waypoints `W_j` for each agent `j`. Each spline `ξ_j` starts from `x_{0_j}` with velocity `˙x_{0_j}` and passes through its respective `W_j` with velocities `V_j` and time-deltas `∆_j`.
 - **Inter-agent Timing Constraints:** Additional "less-than" `E_≤` and "equals" `E_=` constraints are derived from the GoC structure to enforce ordering and synchronization between agent actions.
 - **Objective:** Minimize the total time `∑ ∆_j(i)` plus agent-specific motion costs `c(W_j, V_j, ∆_j)` (e.g., total acceleration, max velocity/acceleration/jerk).
 - **Solution:** This is formulated as a quadratic program (QP) and solved efficiently using MOSEK.

3. **Short Receding-Horizon Problem:**
 - **Goal:** Generate a short `H`-step trajectory for immediate execution that tracks the computed reference splines `ξ*(t)` while satisfying fine-grained dynamical costs.
 - **Objective:** Minimize `∫ ||ξ(t) - ξ*(t)||^2 + c_h(ξ(t)) dt`, where `c_h` includes environment collision and reachability costs.
 - **Constraints:** The trajectory must start from the current state `x`.
 - **Solution:** This is also a QP, solved efficiently using MOSEK.

**D. Graph-of-Constraints MPC (Algorithm 1)**
The complete GoC-MPC algorithm iteratively executes these subproblems.
- **Phase Progression:** The algorithm tracks a set `R` of remaining nodes. If an agent `j` is expected to reach its next node `v_j` within a small time-delta cutoff `τ`, and its associated waypoint constraint `ϕ_{v_j}` is satisfied, `v_j` is removed from `R`.
- **Backtracking:** Before initiating the planning cycle, edge constraints are checked. If an edge constraint `φ_{ab}` is violated for an edge `(a, b)` where `a` is a completed node (not in `R`) and `b` is a remaining node (in `R`), the source node `a` is added back to `R`. This allows for coordinated backtracking across agents if disturbances invalidate previously completed steps.
- A subgraph `G_s` derived from `R` defines the active portion of the task for each planning cycle.

This cyclical process, combining high-level task planning (waypoints, assignments) with intermediate-level motion timing (splines) and low-level reactive control (receding horizon), enables flexible parallelization, robust progression, and efficient recovery from disturbances in multi-agent TAMP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to validate GoC-MPC's efficiency, robustness, and scalability in reactive multi-agent TAMP, comparing it against a relevant baseline.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks:**
 - **Block-Stacking:** Two robots stack three colored blocks into a tower.
 - **Pick-and-Pour:** Two robots coordinate to transfer liquid between two cups.
 - **Cloth-Folding:** Two robots fold a rectangular cloth.
- **Baseline:** ReKep, identified as the sole existing method capable of reactive TAMP from visual keypoints without explicit world models.
- **Environments:** Simulated in Isaac-Sim with OmniGibson (static), and Drake (disturbance, scalability). Real-world experiments used a dual-UR5e manipulator system with a RealSense D455 camera for RGB-D input. Keypoint tracking used SAM2 for objects and a Kanade–Lucas–Tomasi tracker, for cloth corners.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate, Maximum Wall-Clock Execution Time per MPC cycle (Max Time), Average Wall-Clock Execution Time per MPC cycle (Avg. Time), and Total Path Length.

### 5. Main Findings

1. **Static Setting (without external disturbances):**
 - **Performance:** GoC-MPC achieved 100% success rates on both Block-Stacking and Pick-and-Pour tasks (10/10 trials), compared to ReKep's 7/10 and 6/10 success rates, respectively.
 - **Efficiency:** GoC-MPC demonstrated significantly faster computation, with average times of 0.108s for Block-Stacking (approx. 70x faster than ReKep's 7.11s) and 0.216s for Pick-and-Pour (approx. 40x faster than ReKep's 8.49s).
 - **Solution Quality:** GoC-MPC produced shorter total path lengths (2.54m vs. 4.75m for Block-Stacking; 1.94m vs. 3.20m for Pick-and-Pour).
 - **Attribution:** The GoC structure's ability to represent partial orders and facilitate joint optimization (e.g., for a central pouring location) was identified as key to avoiding idle times and failure modes that affected ReKep (e.g., ReKep's total ordering unnecessarily interleafed placements in Block-Stacking, and a failed grasp would reset the entire stage for all agents in Pick-and-Pour).

2. **Robustness to Disturbances (Block-Stacking):**
 - **Success:** Both GoC-MPC and ReKep achieved a 100% success rate (10/10 trials) in the presence of external disturbances (e.g., objects pushed from robot hands).
 - **Efficiency and Path Length:** GoC-MPC was considerably faster (Max Time of 0.161s vs. ReKep's 12.36s; Avg. Time of 0.052s vs. 3.29s), executing approximately 80 times faster in maximum time. It also resulted in shorter total path lengths (6.89m vs. 7.53m), saving an average of 0.64 meters of movement.
 - **Adaptive Behavior:** The ability of GoCs to support agent-independent backtracking was critical. When a disturbance occurred (e.g., an object dropped by one robot), only the affected agent backtracked to an earlier stage, while the other agent's progress was smoothly delayed rather than completely reset, demonstrating coordinated adaptation. The higher times in static settings for GoC-MPC compared to disturbance settings were attributed to different initial task stage configurations for robustness.

3. **Scalability Analysis (Block-Stacking):**
 - **Performance with Scale:** Tested with varying numbers of objects (5, 8, 11) and agents (2, 3, 4). As the number of objects increased, average computation time and path lengths naturally increased but remained sufficiently low for online reactive execution.
 - **Success Rates:** Success rates remained high (mostly 5/5 or 4/5 across trials), indicating the method's ability to handle increased complexity, although some larger problems occasionally reached solver iteration limits.

4. **Real-World Evaluation:**
 - **Comparable Performance:** GoC-MPC successfully deployed on a dual-UR5e manipulator system, demonstrating performance comparable to simulations.
 - **Success Rates:** Achieved 5/5 success for Pick-and-Pour and Cloth-Folding tasks. For Block-Stacking, it achieved 3/5 success, with failures attributed to severe block occlusions during execution, rather than planning failures.
 - **Computational Times:** Maintained low computational times in the real world (e.g., Avg. Time of 0.065s for Pick-and-Pour), confirming its practicality for physical systems.

In summary, the results indicate that GoC-MPC provides a robust, efficient, and scalable solution for reactive multi-agent TAMP. Its core strength lies in the GoC structure, enabling dynamic agent assignments and parallel task execution, which translates to superior performance compared to baselines in terms of success rates, computation time, and path efficiency, particularly in dynamic and multi-agent environments.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the domain of multi-agent Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) by introducing the Graph-of-Constraints (GoC) model and the GoC-MPC framework. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to overcome critical limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Enhanced Flexibility and Generality:** By generalizing sequential constraints to a graph-based structure, GoC-MPC enables the natural representation and execution of partially ordered tasks. This architectural change permits true parallelization of subtasks among multiple agents, leading to more efficient utilization of robotic resources. Furthermore, the framework supports dynamic agent assignment, allowing the system to adapt its task allocation decisions online based on the current state of the environment and agents. This flexibility is a substantial improvement over rigid, statically assigned, or totally-ordered plans.

2. **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** The integration of GoCs with Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides a mechanism for reactive planning and real-time adaptation to disturbances. The ability to coordinate backtracking among agents, where only affected agents re-plan while others adjust their timing, significantly enhances the robustness of multi-robot systems. This capability is crucial for deploying robotic teams in unpredictable real-world settings where failures or external perturbations are common.

3. **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** The problem decomposition strategy employed by GoC-MPC allows for efficient, near real-time computation of complex multi-agent plans. The experimental results demonstrate significant reductions in computation time and total path lengths compared to existing baselines, even as the number of objects and agents increases. This computational efficiency is vital for maintaining reactivity and enabling the deployment of larger, more complex multi-robot teams.

4. **Practical Applicability with Visual Sensing:** The method's reliance on geometric keypoints tracked from visual observations, without requiring explicit world models or extensive task-specific training data, increases its practical applicability. This approach aligns with the growing trend of vision-based robotics, making GoC-MPC adaptable to diverse manipulation tasks in unstructured environments. Its successful validation on real physical robot systems underscores its readiness for practical implementation.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust foundation for future research in multi-agent TAMP. The identified areas for improvement, such as integrating learned perception modules for more robust state estimation and incorporating discrete planning for generating improved initial plan skeletons, point towards promising avenues for further enhancing the intelligence and autonomy of multi-robot systems. This could lead to more sophisticated multi-agent behaviors and even broader applicability of TAMP solutions.

In essence, GoC-MPC offers a framework that moves multi-agent robotics closer to achieving truly adaptive, coordinated, and resilient operation in complex real-world scenarios, thereby expanding the potential for automation in various industrial, logistical, and service domains.
