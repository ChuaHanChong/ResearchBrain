# From Grasps to Dexterity: Large-Scale Grasp Pretraining for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed a hierarchical policy framework that leverages large-scale grasp synthesis datasets to enable dexterous robot manipulation for complex articulated tool-use tasks. This method, which introduces a new DexCraft benchmark and a G2D-Pretrain dataset, achieved a 33.3 percentage point improvement in full-task success over an end-to-end baseline in real-world articulated tool operations.

## Problem

- Large-scale grasp datasets, despite encoding rich priors on hand-object interaction, have been underutilized beyond basic grasp generation and pick-and-place tasks.
- Functional articulated tool use requires complex multi-stage interaction, including acquiring, maintaining stable grasp during contact-rich motion, and adapting grasps, which poses significant challenges for robots.
- Learning complex dexterous skills from scratch through robot demonstrations or reinforcement learning is often data-intensive and computationally costly.

## Method

- Introduced **DexCraft**, a simulation benchmark consisting of six articulated tool-use tasks specifically designed to require fine-grained ==dexterous manipulation== with a LEAP hand.
- Constructed **G2D-Pretrain**, a large-scale dataset of 355,000 physically validated hand-object interaction trajectories, augmented from the ==Dexonomy grasp synthesis dataset==, for ==low-level policy pretraining==.
- Proposed a ==hierarchical policy== that decomposes control into a high-level ==sub-goal prediction policy== (predicting ==16 hand keypoints== via a ==PointNet++== backbone and a ==Gaussian Mixture Model==) and a low-level ==goal-conditioned policy== (a modified ==3D Diffusion Policy (DP3)== with ==Act3D-style cross-attention==) that executes ==behavior cloning== actions to achieve these sub-goals.

## Results

- Hierarchical policies demonstrated higher success rates and greater sample efficiency across various demonstration budgets (**20, 50, 100** demonstrations) in the DexCraft simulation benchmark compared to end-to-end methods.
- Low-level policy pretraining with G2D-Pretrain resulted in higher average success rates across all tasks and demonstration budgets in simulation compared to training from scratch or pretraining with GraspXL.
- In real-world tasks, the method achieved a **33.3 percentage point** improvement in full-task success over the DP3 baseline, demonstrating enhanced grasping, alignment, and triggering capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical policy representations consistently offer substantial performance gains and greater sample efficiency over end-to-end methods for learning dexterous manipulation tasks.
> - Pretraining the low-level policy on large-scale, augmented grasp datasets (G2D-Pretrain) provides effective priors that significantly improve performance and data efficiency on downstream articulated tool-use tasks.
> - A richer sub-goal representation using 16 hand keypoints (representing the full hand skeleton) is more effective for robust low-level execution than simpler, coarser conditioning schemes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation, involving coordinated finger motion and stable contact, is a critical capability for advanced robot autonomy. The broader research landscape has seen the emergence of large-scale robot grasping datasets, which contain millions of physically plausible grasps across various object categories. These datasets have primarily been utilized for grasp prediction models and pick-and-place manipulation tasks. However, their application to longer-horizon, functional tasks, where a robot must not only acquire an object but also actively use it, has received less attention.

Functional tool use presents challenges beyond conventional grasping. Such tasks often require multi-stage interaction, including tool acquisition, maintaining a stable grasp during contact-rich motion, and adapting the grasp as the task progresses. Articulated tool use further complicates this, necessitating both precise object pose maintenance and force application to manipulate specific articulated parts of the tool. While existing benchmarks like Adroit and DexArt have contributed to research on manipulating articulated objects, they do not explicitly focus on the fine-grained, coordinated hand motions inherent to highly dexterous tool operation.

In parallel, hierarchical policy learning has demonstrated promise in various manipulation contexts, particularly with parallel-jaw grippers. These approaches often decompose control into high-level sub-goal prediction and low-level execution. However, applying these frameworks to dexterous hands requires richer sub-goal representations beyond those sufficient for simpler grippers.

This research addresses the gap by investigating how large-scale grasp datasets can be leveraged to achieve robot dexterity in complex downstream tasks, specifically articulated tool use. It seeks to bridge the existing data-rich domain of grasp synthesis with the challenging requirements of functional, contact-rich dexterous manipulation, moving beyond simple grasp generation to the more intricate domain of tool operation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate the utility of large-scale grasp datasets for supporting functional dexterity in articulated tool use. The authors pose the key question: "How can we leverage large-scale grasp datasets to achieve robot dexterity in downstream tasks like articulated tool use?"

The motivations for this work stem from several observations:

- **Underutilization of Grasp Data:** Existing large-scale dexterous grasp datasets, despite encoding rich priors over hand-object interaction, have largely been confined to grasp generation and pick-and-place tasks. The researchers are motivated to explore if these valuable resources can be extended to support more complex, contact-rich manipulation.
- **Challenges of Functional Articulated Tool Use:** Functional tool use, particularly with articulated tools, presents significant challenges for robots. These tasks require not only stable object acquisition but also maintaining contact, coordinated finger movements to operate moving parts, and adapting grasps dynamically. This complexity often exceeds the capabilities addressed by traditional grasp prediction methods.
- **Need for Specialized Benchmarking:** Prior benchmarks for articulated object manipulation do not fully capture the fine-grained, coordinated hand motions necessary for dexterous tool operation. There is a need for a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate dexterity in these contact-rich scenarios.
- **Enhancing Learning Efficiency:** Learning complex dexterous skills from scratch, especially through robot demonstrations or reinforcement learning, can be data-intensive and computationally costly. Leveraging pre-existing large-scale data, such as grasp datasets, could provide beneficial priors that improve the data efficiency and performance of learning algorithms for downstream tasks.
- **Limitations of Current Hierarchical Approaches for Dexterous Hands:** While hierarchical policy learning shows promise, existing sub-goal representations, often designed for simpler grippers, may be insufficient to describe the full state and diverse configurations of high-degrees-of-freedom dexterous hands.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives articulated by the authors are:

1. To introduce **DexCraft**, a simulation benchmark consisting of six articulated tool-use tasks specifically designed to require fine-grained dexterous manipulation.
2. To construct **G2D-Pretrain**, a large-scale hand-object interaction demonstration dataset of 355,000 trajectories, augmented from the Dexonomy grasp synthesis dataset, for low-level policy pretraining.
3. To propose a pipeline that integrates large-scale grasp synthesis data with a hierarchical policy representation to enable data-efficient learning of dexterous tasks.
4. To demonstrate through experiments that this approach can improve success rates on real-world articulated tool-use tasks compared to established baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a hierarchical imitation learning framework augmented with large-scale grasp pretraining.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Policy Representation

The core of the approach is a hierarchical policy that decomposes control into two main components:

- **High-Level Sub-Goal Prediction Policy (π_high):**
 - **Function:** Predicts an explicit 3D representation of the end-effector state at a future sub-goal time step.
 - **Representation:** Unlike prior work that used 4 points for parallel-jaw grippers, this work predicts 16 keypoints, one on each hand link, for the LEAP-hand, to provide a richer description of the hand's configuration, contact modes, and functional finger roles.
 - **Architecture:** Utilizes PointNet++ as the backbone network.
 - **Input:** Current point cloud observation (from depth camera) and current hand keypoints. One-hot vectors are appended to points to distinguish their source.
 - **Output:** The model predicts displacements to the 16 hand keypoints and a mixture logit for each point in the input point cloud. These form a dense Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) over the scene points.
 - **Training:** Trained by minimizing the L2 loss between the predicted sub-goal keypoints and the ground-truth sub-goal keypoints from demonstrations. A negative log-likelihood loss for the GMM, summed over multiple fixed variances, and a uniform-mixture auxiliary loss are used.
 - **Inference:** Samples an anchor point from the GMM and uses its corresponding mean as the predicted 16-keypoint hand sub-goal.

- **Low-Level Goal-Conditioned Policy (π_low):**
 - **Function:** Executes actions to achieve the sub-goal predicted by the high-level policy.
 - **Action Space:** Predicts delta transformations for the end-effector (6-DoF for arm) and delta joint positions for the fingers (16-DoF for hand).
 - **Architecture:** Adapts a modified 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) architecture.
 - **Input:** Point cloud observation, low-dimensional robot state, and the sub-goal hand keypoints predicted by π_high. It incorporates gripper-goal and gripper-scene cross-attention. One-hot labels distinguish scene points, current hand keypoints, and goal hand keypoints. The input is encoded using an Act3D-style cross-attention module.
 - **Training:** Trained to minimize a behavior cloning loss, aiming to match the demonstrated actions.
 - **Inference:** Both π_high and π_low operate sequentially at the same frequency.

- **Task Decomposition:** Demonstrations are empirically divided into three sequential stages: grasping, aligning, and actuating. Sub-goal frames are defined as the last frame of each stage. This hybrid object-centric and grasp-centric decomposition captures transitions in object pose and robot's grasp configuration.

#### 3.2. Augmenting Dexonomy Dataset for Low-Level Policy Pretraining (G2D-Pretrain)

- **Source Data:** The Dexonomy dataset, containing 10.7k objects and 9.5M grasps for a 22-DoF Shadow hand, is used. Dexonomy provides static pre-grasp, grasp, and squeeze poses.
- **Trajectory Augmentation:** To convert static grasp annotations into complete trajectories suitable for policy pretraining, a 3-stage process is implemented:
 1. **Pre-grasp:** Robot hand moves from a random initial pose to approach a randomized pre-grasp pose using motion planning.
 2. **Grasp:** Hand moves from the pre-grasp pose to the grasp pose, and then to the squeeze pose, using interpolation.
 3. **Post-grasp:** After squeezing, the hand translates to another random position via interpolation.
- **Validation:** Trajectories are physically validated in the ManiSkill3 simulator with a floating Shadow hand. Only trajectories where the robot hand is confirmed to be grasping the object at the end are retained.
- **Dataset Size:** This process generates G2D-Pretrain, a dataset of 355,000 successful trajectories, providing diverse hand-object contact patterns and grasp formations.
- **Goal Generation:** Stage-wise goal hand point clouds are constructed from hand point clouds at key transition indices within each trajectory to condition the low-level policy.

#### 3.3. Grasp Pretraining and Downstream Fine-tuning

- **Pretraining:** The low-level policy (π_low) is initially pretrained on the large-scale G2D-Pretrain dataset using the Shadow-hand action space.
- **Fine-tuning:** The pretrained low-level policy is then fine-tuned on demonstration data specific to the downstream tasks, which involve a LEAP hand.
- **Morphology Adaptation:** A deterministic manual adapter facilitates transfer between the 22-DoF Shadow hand (used for pretraining) and the 16-DoF LEAP hand (used for downstream tasks). The 6-DoF end-effector command is copied directly, while LEAP hand joints are semantically mapped to corresponding Shadow hand joints, with unused Shadow hand entries zero-padded. This maintains compatibility with the pretrained action dimension.

#### 3.4. DexCraft Benchmark Design

- **Tasks:** Six articulated tool-use tasks are introduced: Spray bottle, Lighter, Dispenser, Pliers, Stapler, and Pen. Each task requires grasping, lifting to a target pose, and operating the tool's articulated joint.
- **Environment:** Implemented in the ManiSkill3 simulator using an XArm6 robot arm and a LEAP Hand. Articulated assets are from the PartNet-Mobility Dataset.
- **Variations:** Focuses on object pose variation rather than category generalization, using one asset per task with randomly sampled initial and target poses.
- **Observation Space:** Includes robot joint position/velocity, previous joint position target, end-effector pose, observed object point cloud, and hand skeleton points.
- **Action Space:** 22-dimensional vector (6-DoF arm delta end-effector control, 16-DoF hand delta joint position control).
- **Data Collection (DexCraft):** 5 demonstrations per task collected via keyboard teleoperation, then augmented to 100 demonstrations using MimicGen to introduce spatial variations.
- **Success Criteria:** An episode is successful if both the tool's position and orientation match the goal pose, and its articulated joint is triggered beyond a predefined threshold. Stage-wise success (Grasping, Aligning, Triggering) is also evaluated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented its findings through experiments in both simulation using the DexCraft benchmark and real-world robot tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Benefit of Hierarchical Policy Representation:** Hierarchical policies (HP-Scratch and Ours) consistently demonstrated substantial performance gains over end-to-end methods (Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3)). The hierarchical approaches exhibited greater sample efficiency, achieving higher success rates across various demonstration budgets (20, 50, 100 demonstrations). Qualitative analysis suggested that end-to-end policies often struggled with precise object localization and grasp alignment, leading to failures in initial grasping stages, whereas hierarchical policies provided more structured guidance.
- **Effectiveness of Low-Level Policy Pretraining:** Pretraining the low-level policy with G2D-Pretrain (Our method) resulted in higher average success rates across all tasks and demonstration budgets compared to HP-Scratch (hierarchical policy trained without pretraining). The benefits of pretraining were observed to vary across tasks, potentially influenced by task difficulty and demonstration coverage. Pretraining also provided moderate performance improvements for end-to-end policies (DP3-PT vs. DP3), suggesting its utility as an initialization strategy.
- **Comparison of Pretraining Datasets:** G2D-Pretrain outperformed GraspXL for pretraining the low-level policy. The authors attributed this to Dexonomy's explicit coverage of diverse taxonomy-level grasp types, which yielded broader variation in hand configurations and contact patterns. This diversity was considered a better match for the functional contact modes required by articulated tool-use tasks, as opposed to GraspXL's primary focus on varying graspable regions and approach directions.
- **Keypoint Conditioning Scheme:** An ablation study showed that conditioning the low-level policy on 16 hand keypoints (representing the full hand skeleton) was more effective than using 5 coarse fingertip keypoints. This suggests that a richer, more complete hand configuration representation is beneficial for robust low-level execution.
- **High-Level Policy Prediction Quality:** The learned high-level policy's sub-goal predictions achieved performance comparable to, and in some instances surpassed, an oracle baseline that used ground-truth sub-goals. This indicates that the learned policy generates effective sub-goals and can potentially adapt to scene deviations, unlike a fixed sequence of oracle sub-goals.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

- The method was evaluated on three real-world articulated tool-use tasks: syringe, spray bottle, and scissors.
- Our method consistently outperformed the end-to-end DP3 baseline and the hierarchical policy trained from scratch (HP-Scratch) across most task stages (Grasping, Aligning, Triggering).
- It achieved the highest average triggering success rate among the evaluated methods, resulting in a 33.3 percentage point improvement in full-task success over the DP3 baseline.
- These real-world results suggest that the benefits of grasp pretraining extend beyond initial grasp acquisition, enhancing downstream alignment and the actuation of articulated tools in physical deployments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by demonstrating that large-scale grasp synthesis datasets can be effectively leveraged to support complex dexterous manipulation tasks beyond their conventional use in grasp generation or pick-and-place. This finding suggests a broader utility for these valuable data resources.

The introduction of **DexCraft** establishes a new simulation benchmark specifically designed for articulated tool use, emphasizing fine-grained, coordinated finger motions. This benchmark provides a standardized environment for evaluating and advancing research in dexterous manipulation skills that require intricate hand-object interaction.

The creation of **G2D-Pretrain** from the Dexonomy dataset represents a significant contribution of pretraining data. By converting static grasp annotations into dynamic trajectories, this dataset provides a scalable source of physically validated hand-object interaction demonstrations, which can serve as a prior for learning diverse contact-rich behaviors.

The proposed hierarchical policy framework, combined with grasp pretraining, offers a data-efficient approach to learning complex dexterous skills. By providing the low-level controller with rich, pretrained priors on hand-object interaction and guiding it with high-level sub-goals, the method can achieve higher success rates with fewer task-specific demonstrations. This has implications for reducing the expensive and time-consuming process of collecting robot demonstration data.

The empirical results, particularly the substantial improvements in real-world task success, indicate the practical viability of this approach for enabling autonomous visuomotor policies for dexterous manipulation. This work lays a foundation for future research in several areas, including exploring automatic sub-goal discovery to enhance scalability, increasing object diversity within benchmarks to improve generalization, and investigating the integration of higher-quality demonstration data, tactile feedback, and online adaptation for improved robustness in real-world settings. Ultimately, this research moves towards more capable and adaptable robotic systems that can perform intricate tasks requiring dexterous tool manipulation.
