# Long-Horizon Embodied Decision-Making via Multimodal Memory Compression

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed DunphyBench, a new benchmark for evaluating long-horizon, human-centered embodied decision-making, and introduced MeMento, a preference-conditioned multimodal memory compressor. MeMento improves decision accuracy by 15.74% and reduces memory usage by 85.38% compared to baselines, by selectively distilling relevant information from extensive multimodal histories.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI agents struggle with long-horizon decision-making, particularly in accumulating evidence, interpreting implicit human preferences, and managing large volumes of multimodal information effectively.
- Human decision-making in complex, long-duration tasks is prone to fatigue and degraded judgment due to information overload and the need to recall subtle details across multiple observations.
- Current VLM-driven agents face challenges with preference under-specification (interpreting nuanced user intent) and long-horizon grounding (connecting preferences to extensive embodied exploration).

## Method

- Development of ==DunphyBench==, a new benchmark for long-horizon, human-centered embodied decision-making, featuring multiple housing environments and diverse explicit/implicit user preferences.
- Introduction of **MeMento**, a preference-conditioned multimodal memory compressor, which selectively distills relevant information from extensive interaction history into a fixed set of memory tokens.
- **MeMento** employs a ==Perceiver-style module== with learned queries conditioned by user preferences and a ==two-stage training== process to make compressed memory readable by frozen VLMs.

## Results

- The best VLM-driven agent achieved **58.3%** accuracy on DunphyBench, significantly below human performance of **83.3%**, with notable degradation on implicit preferences and increased task complexity.
- MeMento improved decision accuracy by **15.74%** relative to strong baselines while reducing memory usage by **85.38%**, demonstrating superior efficiency and effectiveness in long-horizon memory management.
- MeMento effectively preserved decision-relevant multimodal evidence (Macro-F1 of **0.719**) and generalized its performance, achieving **83%** success with minimal tokens on web-agent tasks, surpassing other memory strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLM-driven agents exhibit a substantial performance gap compared to humans in long-horizon, preference-driven decision-making, particularly with implicit preferences and increasing task complexity.
> - Long-horizon memory management is a major bottleneck; simply scaling context length leads to performance degradation due to irrelevant information, emphasizing the need for selective information distillation.
> - Preference grounding and multimodal evidence are critical for effective memory compression and overall agent performance in complex decision tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent agents capable of performing complex tasks on behalf of human users represents a significant area of research in artificial intelligence. Historically, embodied AI research has often focused on tasks requiring procedural planning or immediate goal completion within relatively short interaction horizons. Existing benchmarks, such as BabyAI, ALFRED, ALFWorld, WebArena, EmbodiedAgentInterface, 3DMem-Bench, EmbodiedBench, Embodied Web Agent, and UserBench, have advanced capabilities in areas like long-term information aggregation, preference reasoning, and multi-modal integration. However, as agents are increasingly envisioned as decision-makers and personal assistants in real-world scenarios, a critical gap emerges: the ability to accumulate evidence over extensive periods, interpret nuanced, implicit human preferences, compare multiple candidates under partial observations, and manage large volumes of multimodal information.

Current large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated advancements in perception, navigation, and action generation within interactive environments. While these models enable agents to interact with the world, their efficacy in long-horizon, preference-driven embodied decision-making, where information overload and subtle user preferences are paramount, remains an active area of investigation. This work addresses this gap by introducing a new benchmark designed specifically for evaluating these advanced capabilities and proposing a mechanism to overcome a identified bottleneck in memory management. It shifts the focus from purely task-execution to sophisticated, human-centered decision support.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that human decision-making processes, especially over long durations and with accumulating information, are susceptible to fatigue and degraded judgment. The paper uses the example of apartment searching, where a human user must remember numerous visual and spatial details, amenities, and trade-offs across multiple virtual tours. This process highlights the need for an automated decision partner that can effectively manage long-horizon memory, systematically compare alternatives, and make informed decisions aligned with user preferences.

Building such an intelligent assistant requires a multidimensional set of capabilities that current agents often lack. Specifically, agents need to:
1. **Autonomously navigate and inspect remote environments:** This involves understanding spatial layouts and interacting with virtual spaces.
2. **Link and aggregate information across modalities:** Textual descriptions alone are often insufficient; visual and other sensory information must be integrated.
3. **Manage large volumes of accumulated evidence over long horizons under limited time and memory budgets:** As interaction histories grow, the challenge of retaining and utilizing relevant information intensifies.
4. **Infer and align with implicit user preferences:** User preferences are often underspecified, indirect, or persona-driven, requiring agents to infer underlying intentions rather than simply checking explicit constraints.

Against this background, the paper outlines two core challenges for long-horizon, preference-driven embodied decision-making:
- **Preference under-specification:** Human preferences range from explicit requirements (e.g., "three bedrooms") to implicit statements (e.g., "I often cook for friends"). Agents must interpret and ground these varied signals.
- **Long-horizon grounding:** Agents need to connect personalized preference signals to accumulated embodied exploration experience across multiple candidate environments over an extended period.

Based on these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **To propose DunphyBench:** A new benchmark designed to evaluate agents on long-horizon human-centered embodied decision-making, requiring navigation through multiple embodied housing environments and alignment with compositional human preferences (both explicit and implicit).
2. **To conduct a comprehensive evaluation:** Assess the capabilities of current state-of-the-art VLM-driven agents on DunphyBench, identify performance gaps relative to human capabilities, and diagnose key bottlenecks.
3. **To address the identified memory management bottleneck:** Motivated by the diagnosis that excessive, unfiltered multimodal history introduces noise and hinders decision quality, the paper aims to design and implement MeMento.
4. **To develop MeMento:** A preference-conditioned multimodal memory compressor that selectively distills decision-relevant information from long-horizon history into a fixed set of memory tokens, thereby improving both decision accuracy and memory efficiency.
5. **To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of MeMento:** Validate its performance on DunphyBench and explore its applicability to other long-horizon decision-making tasks, such as web-based agent interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research introduces a new benchmark, DunphyBench, for long-horizon embodied decision-making and proposes a novel memory compression method, MeMento.

#### 3.1. DunphyBench: A Benchmark for Long-Horizon Human-Centered Embodied Decision-Making

**Task Formulation:** The benchmark instantiates the problem in a house selection task. Each instance involves a set of `N` user preferences and a candidate pool of `1 + 3M` housing environments. One environment is the "golden" (perfectly matching all preferences), while the remaining `3M` are distractors of varying difficulty (easy, medium, hard, each with `M` environments). The agent's goal is to identify the unique golden environment. The design is intended to be generalizable to other real-world decision tasks.

**Environment Representation:** Environments are derived from ProcTHOR, a collection of embodied housing environments. Each environment is converted into a structured feature vector capturing properties like room counts, layout, per-room attributes, object inventories, spatial connectivity, and aesthetic features. A fixed set of deterministic predicates is computed from this representation to enable automatic and objective preference verification.

**Preference Formulation:** To evaluate explicit reasoning and implicit inference separately, DunphyBench includes two independent evaluation tracks:
- **Explicit Track:** Preferences are directly specified as structured constraints (e.g., "at least three bedrooms"), mapping directly to verifiable predicates.
- **Implicit Track:** Preferences are expressed as natural language statements (e.g., "I cook large meals daily"), requiring the model to infer underlying structural needs.

**Preference Taxonomy:** Preferences are organized into seven semantic categories to ensure comprehensive coverage and prevent overfitting: room counts/types, room dimensions/area, windows/lighting, object presence, object counts, spatial connectivity, and materials/finishes.

**Data Generation Pipeline:** A five-step pipeline ensures diverse and verifiable data:
1. **Feature Extraction:** ProcTHOR environments are converted into structured feature vectors and deterministic predicates.
2. **Golden Sampling:** Golden environments are selected for sufficient complexity and diversity, with each instance receiving a unique golden.
3. **Preference Generation:** For the explicit track, preferences are generated from features via an LLM. For the implicit track, predicates are sampled first, then expressed as natural language statements.
4. **Distractor Construction:** Distractors are grouped into easy (<0.3N satisfied), medium (0.3N–0.6N satisfied), and hard (0.6N–0.9N satisfied) tiers based on preference satisfaction, excluding near-perfect matches.
5. **Quality Control:** Ensures the golden environment satisfies all preferences, no distractor does, tiers have equal samples, preferences are balanced, and cross-instance redundancy is reduced. Implicit statements must uniquely imply a single predicate.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Setting and Agent Design

**Backbone Models:** The study evaluates four representative multimodal reasoning models: Qwen3VL-8B-Instruct, Qwen3VL-30B-Thinking, Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it, and GPT-5.4.

**Agent Design Variants:**
- **Memoryless Agent:** Performs one-shot reasoning without maintaining history.
- **In-Context Memory Agent:** Appends external recent memory to the prompt during inference.
 - *Text Memory:* Stores structured textual summaries of experience.
 - *Multimodal Memory:* Extends text memory with visual context (ego-view and map-view images).
**Tools:** Agents can optionally use:
- **Sketchpad:** A persistent workspace for recording intermediate thoughts.
- **Retriever:** A search tool for relevant memory based on queries.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Accuracy (Acc):** Fraction of instances where the agent makes the correct final decision.
- **Normalized Satisfaction Ratio (nSAT):** Measures partial correctness by comparing selected preference satisfaction to gold requirements.
- **Exploration Efficiency (EE):** Balances efficiency and accuracy, defined as $(1 - K/K_{max}) \times \text{nSAT}$, where `K` is exploration steps and `K_max` is the maximum budget.

#### 3.3. MeMento: Preference-Conditioned Multimodal Memory Compression

MeMento aims to compress long multimodal history into a small set of preference-relevant memory tokens, addressing the issue of excessive context introducing noise.

**Core Idea:** A preference-conditioned Perceiver-style module uses a fixed set of learnable queries (memory tokens) to selectively compress information from raw multimodal history.

**Preference and Multimodal Memory Encoding:**
- **Preference Representation:** User requirements (explicit or implicit) are transformed into keywords by a frozen VLM backbone. Their text embeddings form preference tokens, pooled into a single preference embedding $\bar{z}_r$.
- **Multimodal Step Representation:** Each explored step ($x_t$, including ego image, map, observation, action) is encoded into $e_t = f_{enc}(x_t)$. These are augmented with step ID ($p_{time_t}$) and environment ID ($p_{env_k}$) to form $\tilde{e}_t = e_t + p_{time_t} + p_{env_k}$.

**Preference-Conditioned Memory Compression:**
- A set of learned queries $Q_0$ is conditioned by the preference embedding $\bar{z}_r$ via a projection network $\phi(\cdot)$, yielding $Q_r = Q_0 + \phi(\bar{z}_r)$.
- For each environment $E_k$, these conditioned queries $Q_r$ attend over its corresponding step representations $\{\tilde{e}_t : t \in E_k\}$ using cross-attention. A self-attention layer then fuses information across steps to produce environment-level memory tokens $H^{(k)}$.
- These environment-level tokens are concatenated to form compressed global memory tokens $H_{global}$.

**Compressed Memory Injection:**
- The $H_{global}$ tokens are projected into the frozen VLM's embedding space via a trainable adapter $\psi$, resulting in $\tilde{M}$.
- $\tilde{M}$ is then injected as a structured prefix prepended to the current step prompt tokens: $[\tilde{M}; X_{current}]$. The VLM backbone remains frozen, treating $\tilde{M}$ as a soft prompt providing long-horizon context.

**Training MeMento:** A two-stage training process is employed:
1. **Stage 1: Preference-Guide Compression Training:** The compressor is trained to predict step-level preference relevance scores ($s_t = g(\bar{z}_r, \tilde{e}_t)$) using a cross-entropy loss $L_{rel}$ against annotated relevance labels. Additionally, the compressor's cross-attention weights ($\alpha_t$) are aligned with these relevance labels using $L_{attn}$. The total loss is $L_{stage1} = L_{rel} + \lambda L_{attn}$.
2. **Stage 2: Alignment Training:** The compressor and projection adapter are trained to make the compressed memory "readable" by the frozen VLM. This stage uses preference-related QA questions about previously explored environments. The VLM receives $\tilde{M}$ and the question prompt, and the language modeling loss is optimized only on the target answer tokens.

**Inference:** The prediction head from Stage 1 is removed. MeMento encodes and compresses interaction history for each environment, producing global memory tokens that are projected and injected into the VLM prompt for decision-making.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DunphyBench and the MeMento framework yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. DunphyBench Evaluation Results

- **Substantial Performance Gap:** Across all configurations, agent performance remained considerably below human level. The best agent achieved an average accuracy of 58.3%, whereas human annotators achieved 83.3%.
- **Degradation with Task Complexity:** Agent accuracy decreased significantly as task complexity increased. For example, accuracy dropped from 49.1% to 25.0% for explicit preferences and from 26.7% to 16.1% for implicit preferences when moving from "Easy" to "Hard" settings. Increasing the number of distractors (`M`) or requirements (`N`) also led to sharp declines in accuracy, indicating sensitivity to compositional reasoning and cluttered decision spaces.
- **Struggle with Implicit Preferences:** Agents consistently performed worse on tasks involving implicit preferences compared to explicit ones. This highlights the difficulty in inferring underspecified user intent from natural language statements.
- **Beneficial Role of Memory:** Incorporating memory substantially improved performance for some models. For instance, GPT-5.4's accuracy improved from 25.0% (no memory) to 50.0% (text memory) and further to 58.3% (multimodal memory), demonstrating the critical role of effectively aggregating long-horizon historical context.
- **Limited Gains from Tools:** While tools like Sketchpad and Retriever could provide incremental improvements for some models, their gains were modest compared to memory. Tool usage sometimes led to inconsistent results or even performance degradation due to introduced overhead or noise.
- **Challenges in Exploration Efficiency:** Agents struggled to balance efficient exploration with decision accuracy. Some agents took fewer steps but still achieved low accuracy, suggesting insufficient evidence collection. Humans demonstrated a better balance between exploration cost and decision quality.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Multimodal Memory Management Bottleneck

A detailed failure analysis revealed that current agents frequently failed due to ineffective memory management. Common errors included losing track of location (21%) and repeatedly revisiting explored areas within the same environment (13%), indicating poor state tracking and evidence aggregation.

Further analysis on memory budget showed a non-monotonic relationship between history length and performance. Initially, increasing historical context up to 10 turns improved accuracy by 17%. However, beyond a moderate budget (around 15 turns), performance began to decline. This suggested that while additional observations are beneficial, excessive, unfiltered context introduces irrelevant or distracting information, leading to degraded performance. The key takeaway was that effective, selective memory management, rather than merely scaling context, is crucial.

#### 4.3. MeMento Performance and Ablation Studies

- **Improved Accuracy and Memory Efficiency:** MeMento achieved the highest accuracy (52.78%) on DunphyBench among the evaluated memory strategies. This represented a 15.74% relative improvement (7.18% absolute) over the strongest baseline. Simultaneously, MeMento drastically reduced memory usage to approximately 3.8K tokens, an 85.38% reduction compared to the strongest baseline (Multimodal Recent Memory, ~60K tokens).
- **Component Contribution (Ablation Study):**
 - **Preference Grounding:** Removing the preference keywords generation module (MeMento$_P$) caused the largest performance drop (from 52.78% to 15.67%), underscoring the criticality of explicit preference grounding for task alignment.
 - **Multimodal Evidence:** Using only textual memory (MeMento$_T$) resulted in moderate degradation (44.44%), indicating that visual information provides complementary gains.
 - **Hierarchical Compression:** Removing the hierarchical two-stage compression design (MeMento$_U$) also led to moderate degradation (41.67%), highlighting the importance of structured long-horizon aggregation. The results indicate that MeMento's effectiveness stems from the synergy of these components.

#### 4.4. Memory Probing and Generalizability

- **Multimodal Memory Compression Effectiveness:** In a memory probing task designed to recall fine-grained details about previously explored environments, MeMento achieved the best non-oracle Macro-F1 score (0.719), demonstrating its ability to preserve more decision-relevant visual evidence compared to recent-memory or text-based compression baselines. It also had the smallest distance to the oracle upper bound (0.097). Zero and random memory variants performed substantially worse, confirming that MeMento's learned tokens encode useful information.
- **Generalization to Web-Agent Tasks:** MeMento's effectiveness extended beyond embodied environments. When evaluated on 100 web-agent tasks (with explicit requirements), MeMento achieved 83% success using only 512 tokens. This significantly outperformed memory-less agents (34% success), recent multimodal memory agents (69% success with 4.5k tokens), and text summarization agents (72% success with 1k tokens), showcasing its memory efficiency and generalizability to web-based decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of embodied AI, particularly in the domain of long-horizon, human-centered decision-making.

- **Establishment of a New Benchmark:** DunphyBench provides a rigorous and comprehensive platform for evaluating advanced embodied agents. It uniquely focuses on integrating multimodal, multi-source input into coherent knowledge, supporting complex reasoning over long horizons, and interpreting both explicit and implicit human preferences. This addresses a critical gap in existing benchmarks, which often concentrate on procedural planning or short-term goal completion. DunphyBench's design allows for systematic study of challenges related to compositional reasoning and preference inference.
- **Identification of Key Bottleneck:** The empirical evaluation on DunphyBench highlights that long-horizon memory management is a substantial bottleneck for current VLM-driven agents. The finding that simply scaling context can degrade performance by introducing noise is crucial, shifting the focus from mere memory capacity to the *quality* and *relevance* of retained information.
- **Development of an Effective Solution (MeMento):** MeMento offers a practical and effective approach to overcome the identified memory management challenge. By selectively compressing preference-relevant multimodal information into a fixed set of tokens, it enables agents to leverage extensive history without being overwhelmed by irrelevant details. This not only enhances decision accuracy but also dramatically improves memory efficiency, making long-horizon embodied AI more computationally feasible.
- **Advancement Towards Human-Centered AI:** The ability of MeMento to align with human preferences, including implicit ones, and manage complex information over time is a step towards building more capable and reliable intelligent assistants. These agents could act as genuine decision partners, reducing human cognitive load and improving decision quality in real-world scenarios such as apartment searching, disaster response planning, facility inspection, or assistive navigation.
- **Demonstrated Generalizability:** The successful application of MeMento to web-agent tasks suggests its underlying principles of preference-conditioned multimodal memory compression are not restricted to embodied housing environments but can generalize across different domains requiring long-horizon decision-making and efficient context management.

In summary, this work advances the state-of-the-art by providing a robust evaluation framework for complex decision-making and introducing a novel memory compression technique that addresses a fundamental limitation of current AI models, paving the way for more intelligent, efficient, and human-aligned embodied agents.
