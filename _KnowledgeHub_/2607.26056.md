# INTACT: Isomorphic Intent-to-Action Learning for Search-Free World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The INTACT framework enables search-free control from world models by directly mapping latent "intent"—representing desired state changes or future goals—to actions. It achieves high control success rates across diverse tasks with dramatically reduced inference latency compared to traditional search-based methods.

## Problem

- Traditional latent world models exhibit a representation-control asymmetry, learning forward prediction but requiring computationally expensive test-time search to recover actions for desired future states.
- The reliance on iterative, trial-and-error search for action generation at inference time leads to inefficient and slow control deployment, limiting applicability in high-frequency scenarios.
- Latent representations in forward-only world models are optimized for predictive sufficiency, potentially obscuring distinctions crucial for direct, goal-conditioned action generation.

## Method

- An augmented ==Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)== incorporates an "**INTACT** Predictor"—a shared conditional action operator that directly maps a latent state and motion intent to an action.
- The framework employs dual, isomorphic supervisory signals during training: one for local physical transitions (observed latent differences) and another for future goal intents (latent displacement to a stop-gradient goal).
- It supports ==search-free inference== by autoregressively generating action sequences from current and goal observations using the learned INTACT Predictor, eliminating the need for iterative sampling and evaluation.

## Results

- INTACT Direct models achieved a **95.33%** macro average success rate across four tasks in a search-free manner, performing comparably to or exceeding state-of-the-art search-based methods with a **300x** reduction in inference latency (**2.9–5.5 ms**).
- A single shared Goal-displacement INTACT encoder achieved an **89.39%** macro Direct success rate across four heterogeneous tasks, significantly outperforming a matched shared-encoder LeWM baseline (**66.17%**).
- Even with the direct actor disabled, models trained with INTACT supervision demonstrated improved pure CEM performance (e.g., **69.44%** for PushT vs. **42.22%** for LeWM), indicating that the training methodology produces a more usable underlying representation for planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single, shared conditional action operator can effectively interpret and align the action-law semantics of both immediate physical transitions and distant future goal intents without enforcing pointwise latent equality.
> - Jointly training an inverse dynamics component with the forward model explicitly shapes the latent space to retain "controllable distinctions," making it more suitable for direct action generation.
> - Low-budget, local search, when initialized and guided by a learned direct policy (Guarded A mode), can provide robustness benefits over pure direct control or unguided extensive search while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning and representation learning, specifically building upon the paradigm of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) and latent world models. Traditional latent world models, such as PlaNet, Dreamer, DINO-WM, and LeWorldModel (LeWM), learn a forward conditional: given a visual state and an action, they predict the next state in a compressed latent space. However, these models typically recover actions for a desired future state only through computationally expensive test-time search procedures, such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) or the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM). This creates a "representation–control asymmetry," where actions shape the latent space during training, but deployment relies on trial-and-error search in the action space, which is then evaluated by the forward model.

INTACT addresses this asymmetry by introducing a mechanism that directly converts a desired latent change (intent) into an action, eliminating the need for extensive search at inference time. It integrates aspects of inverse dynamics models, which learn to infer actions from state transitions (e.g., SMWM, GC-IDM), and hindsight goal-conditioned imitation learning, where agents learn from trajectories by treating achieved states as goals (e.g., GCSL). However, INTACT distinguishes itself by proposing an end-to-end architecture that unifies these concepts under a single, shared conditional action operator, applied to both immediate physical transitions and future goal intents. Unlike some prior methods that train a separate controller on a frozen world model (e.g., GC-IDM, PRISM), INTACT ensures that action supervision jointly shapes the visual encoder and the world model itself. Furthermore, it aims for a unified multi-task learning setting with a shared encoder across heterogeneous domains, a feature not commonly reported among directly comparable JEPA-derived systems.

The concept of an "action quotient," where perceptually distinct latent states can be functionally equivalent if they lead to the same optimal action, resonates with ideas in bisimulation metrics and action-induced equivalence. However, INTACT defines this equivalence through the conditional action law rather than a pairwise distance metric, preserving richer information for forward prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the INTACT framework are:

1. **Bridging the Representation–Control Asymmetry**: World models typically learn what actions *do* to a scene but not *which* actions *should* be performed to achieve a desired scene change. This disconnect necessitates expensive numerical search at deployment, rendering direct goal-conditioned control inefficient. INTACT aims to provide a direct, amortized mechanism for action generation from latent intent.

2. **Enabling Search-Free Control**: Current model-based control often relies on iterative sampling and evaluation of action sequences. This process, exemplified by CEM or MPPI, can be computationally intensive and time-consuming, hindering deployment in high-frequency control scenarios. A core motivation for INTACT is to facilitate "search-free Direct control" by learning a deployable intent-to-action interface.

3. **Leveraging Intrinsic Supervisory Signals**: Offline action-labeled trajectories inherently contain information about the actions that realize specific state-conditioned motion intents. The paper argues that this "missing supervision" can be explicitly leveraged to train a policy jointly with the world model, organizing latent space in a manner conducive to direct action readout.

4. **Shaping Latent Representations for Controllability**: Forward-only world models compress visual information primarily for predictive sufficiency. This compression may discard or obscure distinctions crucial for direct control. INTACT seeks to "calibrate" the latent bottleneck, ensuring that the latent representation retains "controllable distinctions" needed by the intent-to-action conditional, rather than merely forecasting observations.

5. **Establishing "Intactness" in Representation and Conversion**: The paper defines three complementary notions of "intactness" as motivations for its design:
 - **Representation Intactness**: Ensuring that RGB-to-latent compression preserves information relevant for control.
 - **Conversion Intactness**: Maintaining the relationships among state-conditioned intent families as they are mapped to corresponding action families.
 - **Isomorphic Intactness**: Structurally, by using the same input grammar and shared parameters for both local (physical successor) and goal (future goal) motion-intent calls. Semantically, by ensuring that the supported motion-intent families are isomorphic through the conditional action relation induced by the shared predictor, without requiring pointwise latent equality.

6. **Unified Multi-Task Learning**: A significant objective is to train a single shared visual encoder that can support control across multiple heterogeneous tasks (e.g., PushT, Cube, Reacher, TwoRoom) within a single training run, using task-specific heads. This aims to improve generalization and stability compared to training individual world models per task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

INTACT augments a standard Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with a novel "INTACT Predictor" that directly maps latent intent to actions. The architecture leverages two complementary forms of supervision and a shared operator concept to achieve its objectives.

**Core Architecture Components:**
1. **Shared Visual Encoder (E_theta)**: Maps raw RGB observations to a compact latent state `z`. For multi-task settings, this encoder is shared across all tasks.
2. **Forward Predictor (F_phi)**: A recurrent component that predicts the next latent state `z_t+1` given the current latent state `z_t` and action `a_t`. It is trained with a mean squared error (MSE) loss for latent prediction and a Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) loss for representation stability.
3. **INTACT Predictor (G_eta)**: This is the central contribution. It is a shared, nonlinear conditional action operator (implemented as a three-layer MLP) that predicts a diagonal Gaussian distribution over the next action chunk. Its input grammar is consistently structured: `[z_t; m_t; z_t ⊙ m_t; A_omega(a_t-1)]`, where `m_t` is the motion intent, `z_t ⊙ m_t` is a state-intent bilinear interaction feature, and `A_omega(a_t-1)` is an embedding of the previous action.

**Isomorphic Intent-to-Action Learning:**
The INTACT Predictor `G_eta` is invoked in two distinct but structurally isomorphic ways during training:

1. **Local Motion Intent (Physical Grounding)**:
 - **Intent `m_local_t`**: Defined as the observed latent difference `z_t+1 - z_t`.
 - **Gradient Routing**: The endpoint `z_t+1` is *attached*, meaning its gradients flow back through the encoder `E_theta`, shaping the latent representation to preserve action-recoverable physical changes. This provides physical reachability information.
 - **Loss**: A negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss `L_z+` for predicting the demonstrated action `a_t` given `z_t`, `m_local_t`, and `a_t-1`.

2. **Goal Motion Intent (Deployment Anchor)**:
 - **Intent `m_goal_t`**: Defined as the latent displacement from the current state to a future goal state: `sg(z_g) - z_t`. `sg()` denotes a stop-gradient operation.
 - **Gradient Routing**: The future goal latent `z_g` is treated as a *stop-gradient anchor*. This means `z_g` does not receive gradients from this loss term, ensuring it acts as a fixed reference point for deployment rather than being directly molded by the immediate action. However, `z_t`, `G_eta`, and the forward/physical branches continue to receive gradients. This prevents `m_goal_t` from being forced to become a one-step physical successor.
 - **Loss**: An NLL loss `L_goal` for predicting `a_t` given `z_t`, `m_goal_t`, and `a_t-1`. This resembles a goal-conditioned imitation objective.

**Action Quotient and Shared Operator:**
The framework posits that distinct endpoint conditions `y` and `y'` are conditionally action-equivalent at a fixed state `z` if they induce the same expert action law `p*_E(a | z, y) = p*_E(a | z, y')`. The single, shared INTACT Predictor `G_eta` is designed to interpret both `m_local` and `m_goal` families, aligning their "action-law semantics" without enforcing numerical equality between their latent coordinates. This constitutes the "isomorphic intactness" principle: structural (same input grammar/parameters) and semantic (induced action relation).

**Training Objective:**
- **Single-Task (`L_ST`)**: `L_world + λ_inv * L_z+ + λ_goal * L_goal`
- **Multi-Task (`L_MT`)**: A weighted sum of the single-task objective across all `T` tasks, sharing `E_theta` but using task-specific heads for `F_phi`, action embedding, and `G_eta`.

**Inference (Control Modes):**
At deployment, INTACT primarily supports search-free control:
1. **Direct Control**:
 - The current observation `o_t` and goal observation `o_g` are encoded to `z_t` and `z_g`.
 - A sequence of actions (a plan) is generated autoregressively: `m_k = z_g - ez_k`, `a_k = μ_eta(ez_k, m_k, a_k-1)`, `ez_k+1 = F_phi(ez_k, a_k)`.
 - The mean `μ_eta` from the INTACT Predictor is directly used as the action.
 - No candidate sequences are sampled, and no terminal latent cost is computed. The first action chunk is executed, and the process replans from the next real observation.
2. **Guarded A (Optional Local Verification)**:
 - This mode uses the Direct plan as an initialization.
 - A small, local CEM search (e.g., 128 candidate sequences for 3 iterations with a low initial standard deviation) is performed *around* the Direct plan.
 - The system executes the best action from this verified local search, or the Direct plan itself if no improvement is found. This balances efficiency with robustness.
3. **Pure CEM (Baseline/Ablation)**:
 - Disables the INTACT Predictor. Actions are sampled from an uninformed Gaussian distribution (e.g., unit variance).
 - The forward model `F_phi` is used to roll out these action sequences, and a terminal latent cost function ranks them to select the best action. This serves as a baseline to evaluate the benefit of the learned intent-to-action mapping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several findings from both single-task and multi-task experimental setups, benchmarking against the official LeWM tasks (PushT, OGBench Cube, DMC Reacher, TwoRoom).

#### 4.1. Single-Task Results

- **Search-Free Performance**: One-epoch, zero-search INTACT Direct models achieved a macro average success rate (SR) of 95.33% on the four official LeWM tasks (85.78% PushT, 100.00% Cube, 97.67% Reacher, 97.89% TwoRoom). This performance level is comparable to or exceeds state-of-the-art JEPA controllers that employ extensive search, such as LeWM with CEM 300x30, which achieved 85.75% macro SR with 10 epochs and 9,000 candidate sequences.
- **Computational Efficiency**: INTACT Direct inference latency was measured between 2.9–5.5 ms, representing approximately a 300x reduction compared to the 1.48s for CEM 300x30.
- **Optional Verification**: The Guarded A mode, which uses the Direct plan as a prior and performs a limited local CEM search (384 candidate sequences vs. 9,000 for pure CEM), achieved 96.86% macro SR. This indicated that limited search, when guided by a learned policy, can enhance performance while significantly reducing computational overhead (23.44x fewer sequences). Guarded A improved pure CEM by 16.00 points.
- **Representation Shaping**: Even with the INTACT Predictor (the actor) disabled, actor-disabled pure CEM models trained with INTACT supervision showed improved performance (e.g., 69.44% PushT for Waypoint INTACT) compared to LeWM (42.22%). This suggests that the action likelihoods in INTACT contribute to shaping a more usable underlying representation for planning, even when the learned direct action interface is not used. Frozen probes independently confirmed improved recoverable state and intervention information.
- **Coordinate Choice**: The "goal displacement" intent coordinate (`sg(z_g) - z_t`) consistently outperformed the "waypoint" coordinate across all matched inference interfaces, demonstrating its stability and effectiveness in the converged model.
- **Actor Sharing**: An audit demonstrated that sharing the conditional action operator for both local and goal intents was more effective than using independent actors, even with doubled capacity, supporting the design choice of a unified operator.

#### 4.2. Multi-Task Results

- **Shared Encoder Performance**: At epoch 5 (E5), a single shared Goal-displacement INTACT encoder achieved 89.39% ± 0.77% macro Direct SR across the four tasks. This represents a significant improvement over the matched shared-encoder LeWM, which achieved 66.17% ± 2.67% macro SR with CEM 300x30. INTACT improved all four tasks individually (e.g., PushT by 5.66 points, Reacher by 12.56 points, TwoRoom by 42.44 points) and exceeded the published task-specific LeWM macro score.
- **Complementary Dual Branches**: Comparing Full INTACT to the goal-intent-only ablation showed that adding the attached physical likelihood (inverse dynamics) to the goal-conditioned learning increased macro SR by 8.78 ± 1.63 points. This indicates that the physical grounding from local transitions provides complementary benefits to the goal-conditioned deployment.
- **Separation of Concerns**: The results confirmed that INTACT improves the world representation (as shown by actor-disabled CEM improvements) and supplies a direct control interface (via Direct mode), with low-budget search acting as an optional verifier.

#### 4.3. Mechanism Audits and Diagnostics

- **Family-Level Metrics vs. Pointwise Accuracy**: Across 45 eligible checkpoints, predicted-expert kNN overlap and linear CKA, which assess action-family geometry, correlated more strongly with official Direct SR (Pearson r = 0.954 and 0.897, respectively) than pointwise action R^2 (r = 0.815). This suggests that preserving the overall action-family relation is more critical for robust control than precise single-point action recovery.
- **Gauge Intervention**: A 21,600-episode audit demonstrated that correct pairing of task-local calibration (fitting latent coordinate maps) recovered 68.04% CLEAR Moderate SR, compared to 9.46% for shuffled pairs. This emphasized that valid correspondence between latent spaces is crucial for control, even if the absolute coordinate values differ. Furthermore, while different backbone types could align into a trained INTACT actor, a coordinate gauge could not *create* a deployment map if it was absent from the target actor (e.g., mapping a Full INTACT backbone to a LeWM actor resulted in only 5.08% SR).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The INTACT framework introduces several advancements that could significantly impact the field of model-based reinforcement learning and robotic control:

1. **Enabling Search-Free High-Frequency Control**: By directly amortizing action search through a learned intent-to-action interface, INTACT addresses a major bottleneck in deploying world models for high-frequency real-time control. This can extend the applicability of model-based methods to a wider range of robotics tasks where fast reaction times are critical.

2. **Principled Representation Learning for Control**: INTACT provides a principled approach to shaping latent representations not just for accurate forward prediction, but specifically for direct action generation. By explicitly addressing the representation-control asymmetry and aligning distinct condition families (local physical changes and distant goals) through a shared action-law semantics, the learned latent space becomes inherently more "action-aligned" and readily usable by a controller.

3. **Unified Multi-Task Learning and Generalization**: The successful demonstration of a single shared encoder controlling four heterogeneous tasks within one training run suggests improved learning efficiency and potential for better generalization across related domains. This moves towards more capable and general-purpose world models.

4. **Rethinking the Role of Search**: The work redefines the role of planning-time search from a mandatory, often expensive, process to an optional, guided verification step. This can lead to more robust and efficient control systems that leverage the strengths of both learned policies and local model-based refinement.

5. **Broader Applicability of Family Mappings**: The theoretical concept of aligning distinct condition families through a shared proper conditional operator, without forcing pointwise latent equality, offers a generalizable template. This principle could extend beyond world models to other domains involving learned representations where multiple input conditions require a consistent behavioral output mapping.

In summary, INTACT offers a method for making world models more directly actionable and computationally efficient, enhancing their utility for robotic control by integrating perception, prediction, and policy learning into a cohesive end-to-end framework.
