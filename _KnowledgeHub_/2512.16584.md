# Sketch-in-Latents: Eliciting Unified Reasoning in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Sketch-in-Latents (SkiLa) introduces a unified reasoning paradigm for multimodal large language models (MLLMs), enabling them to dynamically interleave textual thoughts with intrinsic latent visual thoughts. This approach improves performance on vision-centric and general multimodal tasks, achieving superior results compared to baseline MLLMs and competitive proprietary models like GPT-4o, while incurring no additional inference cost.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs are restricted by a 'text-centric bottleneck,' relying solely on linguistic reasoning even for tasks requiring visual imagination, unlike human cognition.
- Existing approaches, such as external toolkits, are limited by predefined actions, while pixel-level image generation within reasoning is computationally expensive and not generalizable.
- MLLMs lack an intrinsic capability to generate and reason with visual thoughts internally, hindering performance on tasks requiring rich spatial intuition or counterfactual visual reasoning.

## Method

- SkiLa enhances MLLMs with a ==hybrid auto-regressive process==, enabling dynamic alternation between generating discrete text tokens and ==continuous latent visual sketch tokens==.
- A dedicated ==sketch module== (encoder and projector) is employed exclusively during training to provide semantic reconstruction targets for these latent visual thoughts, ensuring they are grounded.
- The training objective combines a ==Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss== for latent sketch reconstruction with a standard cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction, optimizing over interleaved text-image reasoning traces.

## Results

- SkiLa consistently outperforms the Qwen2.5-VL 7B baseline and state-of-the-art MLLMs, including GPT-4o and Claude3.7-Sonnet, across various vision-centric (e.g., MMVP, RealWorldQA) and general multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MMStar, MMBench).
- The system demonstrates robust generalization, improving TextVQA performance by **+3.3**, MME by **+3.9%**, and reducing hallucinations on the POPE benchmark by **+0.9**.
- Qualitative analyses reveal emergent capabilities such as spatial imagination (simulating 3D objects from 2D patterns), hallucination mitigation via implicit fine-grained attention, and adaptive unified reasoning depth based on task complexity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can natively generate and reason using continuous latent visual embeddings, providing unlimited visual thinking capabilities without external tools or costly pixel-level synthesis.
> - Enabling unified, interleaved visual-text reasoning within MLLMs leads to more flexible, human-like thought processes that leverage the complementary strengths of both modalities.
> - Effective visual-semantic grounding for internally generated latent visual thoughts can be achieved efficiently through a reconstruction loss during training, with the entire sketch module discarded during inference for zero additional computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) represents a significant frontier in AI, aiming to equip models with the ability to understand and generate content across various modalities, particularly text and vision. Building upon the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, MLLMs have made remarkable progress in visual understanding by translating visual information into linguistic reasoning. However, a fundamental limitation persists in most existing MLLMs: their reasoning processes are predominantly restricted to the linguistic domain. This "text-centric bottleneck" forces models to verbalize visual concepts, even those humans would solve intuitively through mental visualization, leading to shortcomings in tasks requiring rich visual imagination or spatial intuition.

The current research landscape has explored two main directions to address this visual imagination gap:

1. **Tool-driven Visual Exploration and Programmatic Manipulation:** Methods like Visual Sketchpad and DeepEyes equip MLLMs with external tools (e.g., for cropping, drawing, marking regions) or programmatic capabilities to interact with images. While these approaches offer some degree of visual interaction, they are limited by predefined toolkits and fixed action spaces. They do not cultivate an *intrinsic* visual reasoning faculty within the model but rather rely on external, discrete operations, making them less flexible than human visual imagination.

2. **Unified Foundation Models for Text and Image Generation:** Models such as Janus-Pro and Bagel attempt to interleave text and image generation directly within reasoning chains. This direction aims for a more integrated multimodal output. However, these models incur substantial computational costs due to the pixel-level synthesis required for generating detailed images. Furthermore, their effectiveness has largely been validated only in specific, often simplified, downstream tasks (e.g., navigation, maze solving), raising concerns about their generalizability and scalability. Their reasoning is also bounded by their generative fidelity – they can only reason about what they can accurately draw.

"Sketch-in-Latents" (SkiLa) positions itself as a novel paradigm that seeks to overcome the limitations of these existing approaches. Inspired by human cognition, where visual-text interaction occurs in a unified internal space, SkiLa proposes to enable MLLMs to generate *visual thoughts natively* as continuous latent embeddings within their internal reasoning process. This approach is distinct because it:
- **Avoids external toolkits:** Unlike tool-driven methods, SkiLa fosters an intrinsic reasoning capability.
- ** sidesteps pixel-level generation:** Unlike unified foundation models, it generates *latent sketch tokens* (continuous embeddings) instead of costly, high-fidelity images, thus maintaining computational efficiency and generalizability.
- **Leverages existing MLLM architecture:** It builds upon the fact that MLLMs already encode visual and text information in the same feature space, allowing for seamless insertion of visual tokens into the reasoning process.

This work also relates to the nascent field of "latent reasoning," which explores performing reasoning in a continuous embedding space rather than discrete token space to improve LLM efficiency. While some prior MLLM work (e.g., Mirage, LVR) has explored latent visual reasoning, these methods typically focus on visual-only reasoning or single-step inference, often lacking interpretability due to opaque processes. SkiLa differentiates itself by enabling *multi-step, interleaved* visual and textual reasoning, enhancing both capability and potentially interpretability (as it alternates with textual thoughts). By integrating visual and textual thinking within a unified, internal, and efficient framework, SkiLa represents a significant step towards more human-like, flexible, and capable MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind "Sketch-in-Latents" (SkiLa) stems from a critical observation about the limitations of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) when contrasted with human cognition. While MLLMs have demonstrated impressive abilities in visual understanding when coupled with textual reasoning, they consistently struggle in scenarios that necessitate genuine visual imagination. Humans, on the other hand, naturally and seamlessly interleave linguistic reasoning with internal visual imagination, forming a unified thought process within the brain's internal space. This inherent human capability allows for flexible visual-text interactions and counterfactual reasoning without relying on predefined external tools.

This disparity leads to the central problem statement: MLLMs are primarily confined to linguistic reasoning, even when presented with visual stimuli. This "text-centric bottleneck" forces them to describe visual concepts verbally, even when a human would intuitively solve the problem through mental visualization (e.g., spatial arrangement, object localization, jigsaw puzzles). Existing attempts to bridge this gap, such as external toolkits or pixel-level image generation within the reasoning process, have proven to be either restrictive (limited action spaces of tools) or computationally prohibitive and task-specific (costly image generation).

Against this backdrop, the authors pose a fundamental question: **"Can MLLMs transcend their linguistic reasoning framework to generate visual thoughts natively as part of an internal, unified reasoning process?"**

This question directly motivates the paper's primary objective: to enable MLLMs to *internally generate continuous embeddings that natively represent visual thoughts*. The underlying intuition is that since MLLMs already encode both visual and textual information into a shared feature space, visual "tokens" should be seamlessly insertable into the auto-regressive reasoning process currently dominated by text tokens.

The key advantages anticipated from achieving this goal are:
1. **Unlimited Visual Thinking:** Representing visual imaginations as token features allows for a theoretically infinite range of visual thoughts, overcoming the rigidness of predefined tools.
2. **Flexible Human-like Reasoning:** The model can seamlessly form interleaved visual-text reasoning traces, leading to a more natural and adaptable thinking process.

Therefore, the main objective of this work is to introduce **Sketch-in-Latents (SkiLa)**, a novel paradigm designed to expand the auto-regressive capabilities of MLLMs. SkiLa aims to achieve unified multimodal reasoning by enabling MLLMs to dynamically alternate between generating textual thoughts (discrete text tokens) and generating continuous visual embeddings, termed "latent sketch tokens," as intrinsic visual thoughts. A crucial secondary objective is to ensure these latent sketch tokens are semantically grounded, which is addressed through a latent visual semantics reconstruction mechanism during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkiLa introduces a novel reasoning paradigm that empowers MLLMs to jointly reason over discrete text and continuous visual representations within a unified auto-regressive process. This approach is designed to be intrinsic, avoiding dependencies on external tools or costly pixel-level image generation.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The SkiLa architecture builds upon a standard MLLM framework, which typically comprises three core components:
- **Vision Encoder (E_v):** Maps input images (I) into visual tokens (V).
- **Modality Projector (proj):** Aligns the visual tokens (V) into the LLM's embedding space (V_e).
- **LLM Backbone (θ):** An auto-regressive language model that generates responses conditioned on V_e and textual embeddings (T). The authors adopt Qwen2.5-VL 7B as their base MLLM.

The key innovation in SkiLa is the introduction of a **sketch module**, which is utilized exclusively during the training phase. This module consists of:
- **Sketch Encoder (E_s):** Encodes intermediate "sketch images" (I_s) from the training data.
- **Sketch Projector (proj_s):** Projects the features from the sketch encoder into the LLM's embedding space, generating visual semantic reconstruction targets (V_s = proj_s(E_s(I_s))).

#### 3.2. Hybrid Auto-Regressive Process

The LLM backbone in SkiLa is trained to execute a hybrid auto-regressive process, enabling unified internal reasoning by dynamically switching between two modes:
- **Textual Thinking Mode:** This is the standard mode where the model performs next-token prediction to generate discrete text tokens, representing textual thoughts.
- **Visual Sketching Mode:** Activated by the special token `<|sketch start|>` and exited by `<|sketch end|>`, in this mode, the model auto-regressively generates a sequence of *continuous latent sketch visual tokens* (H_sketch = {h1..., hK}). These tokens are trained to reconstruct the visual thought semantics (V_s) obtained from the sketch module.

This dynamic alternation allows for the generation of a unified reasoning trace (S), where each step (s_i) can be either a text token (t_i) or a latent sketch token (h_i).

#### 3.3. Unified Training Objective

The training objective for SkiLa is designed to jointly optimize the model's ability to generate coherent text and meaningful latent visual semantics. This is achieved through supervision over an interleaved reasoning trace that contains both textual thoughts (X_t) and corresponding sketch images (I_s), which serve as ground truth for visual thoughts.

The training loss is composed of two main parts:
1. **Latent Sketch Reconstruction Loss (L_Sketch):** When the model is in visual sketching mode, it auto-regressively generates K latent sketch tokens (H_sketch). This loss ensures these generated tokens semantically approximate the target visual semantics (V_s). A **Mean Squared Error (MSE)** objective is used:
 `L_Sketch = (1/K) * Σ ||h_j - v_s,j||^2`, where `h_j` is a generated latent sketch token and `v_s,j` is a target vector from `V_s`. The parameters of the sketch projector (`proj_s`) are jointly trained via this loss. The sketch encoder (`E_s`) is typically frozen.
 To create `V_s`, the `E_s` extracts patch-level features from `I_s`, and `proj_s` projects these features. Average pooling then compresses these projected features into a fixed-size sequence of K salient vectors (e.g., K=27).

2. **Next-Token Prediction (NTP) Loss (L_NTP):** For all discrete tokens, including special tokens, textual thoughts (X_t), and the final answer (X_A), a standard **cross-entropy loss** is applied. A sketch mask ensures this loss is not applied to the latent sketch tokens within the `<|sketch start|>` and `<|sketch end|>` delimiters.
 `L_NTP = - Σ log p_θ(t_i | V_e, T, s_1:i)`

The final training loss for SkiLa is a weighted sum of these two components: `L_SkiLa = L_NTP + λ_Sketch · L_Sketch`, where `λ_Sketch` is a hyperparameter balancing the two losses.

#### 3.4. Training Data

The training data is curated from **Zebra-CoT**, a large-scale dataset of 182K samples featuring logically coherent interleaved text-image reasoning traces. This dataset is filtered to exclude 3D data and overly complex intermediate sketch images, resulting in a refined training dataset of 101K samples. Each sample is reformatted into an interleaved sequence of textual thoughts and visual sketch targets.

#### 3.5. Inference with Unified Reasoning

Crucially, during inference, the entire sketch module (E_s and proj_s) is discarded. This means SkiLa incurs **zero additional parameter or computational cost** compared to a standard MLLM, making it highly efficient.
Inference proceeds in the same hybrid auto-regressive manner. The model generates text tokens normally. Upon sampling `<|sketch start|>`, it switches to "latent sketching mode," propagating its last hidden state to auto-regressively generate a sequence of latent sketch tokens (H_sketch). This continues until `<|sketch end|>` is sampled or a maximum limit (e.g., twice the training length 2K) is reached. These internally generated latent sketch tokens function as the model's *visual thought*, conditioning subsequent language generation without ever being decoded into pixels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SkiLa's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on a comprehensive suite of benchmarks, highlighting its superior performance on vision-centric tasks and strong generalization capabilities across diverse multimodal benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Vision-Centric and Multimodal Tasks

The primary evaluation (Table 1) reveals that SkiLa consistently surpasses the Qwen2.5-VL 7B baseline and generally outperforms other state-of-the-art MLLMs, including leading proprietary models like GPT-4o and Claude3.7-Sonnet, across various benchmarks:
- **Vision-Centric Benchmarks:** MMVP, RealWorldQA (RWQA), BLINK, V*Bench, HRBench (HR_4K and HR_8K), and MME-RealWorld-Lite. These tasks demand fine-grained visual perception, spatial reasoning, and high-resolution image understanding.
- **General Multimodal Benchmarks:** MMStar and MMBench, which evaluate general-purpose multimodal reasoning in multi-choice settings.

Key observations from these results include:
- **Impact of Visual Semantics Reconstruction:** SkiLa-V (a purely visual reasoning version, trained on the same dataset but without interleaved textual thoughts) provides substantial gains on visually intensive tasks (e.g., BLINK, MMVP, MME-RealWorld-Lite Perception). This indicates that linguistic descriptions often fall short in capturing the richness of visual information, which visual semantics reconstruction effectively addresses by allowing the model to "imagine" visual details.
- **Superiority of Unified Reasoning:** SkiLa, combining both textual and visual thoughts, consistently achieves the best overall performance, outperforming single-modality reasoning approaches and even competitive closed-source models. This validates the hypothesis that a unified paradigm, leveraging the complementary strengths of visual imagination and structured linguistic deliberation, leads to enhanced MLLM capabilities.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Diverse Multi-modal Tasks

Beyond vision-centric tasks, SkiLa demonstrates robust generalization across out-of-domain multimodal benchmarks (Table 2):
- **TextVQA:** SkiLa-V and SkiLa show improved text-grounded reasoning, outperforming the baseline by +3.3 and +1.8, respectively, indicating better integration of textual information within images.
- **MME (Perception and Cognition):** SkiLa achieves the best overall performance, enhancing perceptual and cognitive reasoning by +3.9% on MME.
- **POPE (Hallucination Mitigation):** SkiLa improves factuality and reduces hallucinations by +0.9 on the POPE benchmark.
These results confirm SkiLa's ability to not only excel in its primary reasoning objectives but also to generalize effectively, enhancing overall multimodal understanding and factual consistency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Choice of Sketch Encoder:** Experiments (Table 3) show that powerful, contrastively pre-trained external encoders like **SigLIP2** (adopted as default) yield superior performance compared to architectural consistency with the native QwenViT. This highlights that richer, more general semantic features from robust visual encoders are more critical for reconstructing meaningful latent visual semantics.
- **Impact of Sketch Token Number:** Figure 4 (left) indicates that performance improves with an increasing number of visual tokens up to **27 tokens** (the empirical optimum). Beyond this point, an excessive number of visual tokens can lead to a slight performance drop, suggesting a need to balance visual and textual contributions to reasoning.
- **Latent Sketch Reconstruction Loss Weight (λ_Sketch):** Figure 4 (middle and right) demonstrates that with more sketch tokens (e.g., 27), stronger supervision (higher `λ_Sketch`) is crucial for generating meaningful latent sketch tokens. For fewer tokens (e.g., 9), the model is more robust to variations in `λ_Sketch`. The default `λ_Sketch` is set to 0.5. The authors also found that **Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss** for latent sketch reconstruction consistently outperforms cosine similarity loss, as MSE captures both direction and magnitude of latent features, ensuring more precise visual reconstructions (Table 5 in supplementary).

#### 4.4. Emerging Capabilities (Qualitative Analysis)

Beyond quantitative metrics, SkiLa exhibits remarkable emergent capabilities (Figures 5-7 and supplementary materials):
- **Spatial Imagination:** SkiLa can internally simulate 3D objects from 2D patterns (e.g., jigsaw puzzles), overcoming the inherent ambiguity of textual representations for complex spatial reasoning.
- **Hallucination Mitigation:** By implicitly "zooming in" on key image regions within its latent space, SkiLa acquires fine-grained information, effectively reducing hallucinations on visual details without explicit tool invocation.
- **Adaptive Unified Reasoning:** SkiLa dynamically adjusts its reasoning depth based on task difficulty. For simpler tasks, a single-step unified reasoning may suffice; for challenging cases (e.g., highly overlapping bounding boxes), it performs multiple sequential reasoning steps to discern subtle distinctions.
- **Fine-Grained Visual Grounding, Latent Scene Completion, Cross-Image Visual Alignment, and Multi-Round Visual Refinement:** Further case studies demonstrate SkiLa's ability to focus on subtle attributes, reconstruct missing scene content, align visual correspondences across images, and iteratively refine visual comparisons.

In summary, SkiLa's results underscore the effectiveness of its unified reasoning paradigm, showcasing significant improvements in multimodal understanding, reasoning, and factual consistency across a broad spectrum of tasks, while maintaining computational efficiency at inference time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Sketch-in-Latents" (SkiLa) research represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications for how AI systems process and reason about visual information. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Novel Paradigm for Unified Multimodal Reasoning:** SkiLa introduces a groundbreaking approach that allows MLLMs to perform truly unified reasoning. By enabling the dynamic interleaving of explicit textual thoughts and intrinsic latent visual thoughts, it moves beyond the text-centric limitations of previous models. This hybrid auto-regressive process is a conceptual leap, demonstrating that MLLMs can "think" in modalities beyond mere verbalization.

2. **Bridging the Gap to Human-like Cognition:** One of the most compelling aspects of SkiLa is its inspiration from human cognition. The ability to seamlessly integrate visual imagination with linguistic thought in a unified internal space is a hallmark of human intelligence. By enabling MLLMs to construct visual-text thinking processes in a shared, internal feature space, SkiLa brings AI models closer to emulating a more holistic and flexible form of human reasoning. This could lead to MLLMs that are not just powerful but also more intuitive and adaptable in their problem-solving.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - **Elimination of External Tools:** SkiLa liberates MLLMs from the constraints of predefined, external toolkits. This means models are no longer limited by a fixed set of actions or their ability to generate executable code, offering vastly more flexible and "unlimited visual-thinking manners."
 - **Efficiency without Pixel-Level Generation:** By generating *latent sketch tokens* (continuous embeddings) rather than costly, high-fidelity pixel-level images, SkiLa bypasses the prohibitive computational expense and scalability issues associated with unified foundation models that generate both text and images. Crucially, the sketch module is *discarded during inference*, ensuring zero additional parameter or computational cost, making it practical for real-world applications.
 - **Multi-step, Interleaved Reasoning:** Unlike prior latent reasoning methods for MLLMs that were often limited to single-step or purely visual reasoning, SkiLa enables complex, multi-step reasoning that integrates both visual and textual information dynamically.

4. **Enhanced MLLM Capabilities and Performance:** The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate SkiLa's ability to significantly improve performance on a wide array of challenging tasks. This includes:
 - **Superior Visual-Centric Reasoning:** Excelling in tasks requiring fine-grained visual perception, spatial imagination (e.g., jigsaw puzzles, 3D object simulation from 2D patterns), and high-resolution image understanding.
 - **Strong Generalization:** Consistently improving performance on diverse general multimodal benchmarks, including VQA, cognitive reasoning, and hallucination mitigation. The reduction in hallucinations is particularly impactful, making MLLMs more reliable and factual.
 - **Adaptive Reasoning:** The ability to dynamically adjust the depth and modality of reasoning based on task difficulty showcases a higher level of intelligence and adaptability.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** SkiLa opens several exciting avenues for future research. The authors themselves point to developing models that can autonomously select the optimal reasoning pattern (textual, visual, or joint) in a task-adaptive manner. This work lays the foundation for:
 - More sophisticated internal "mental simulation" capabilities in AI.
 - Better understanding of how visual and linguistic representations interact in deep learning models.
 - Development of more robust and reliable MLLMs for complex real-world scenarios in fields like robotics, healthcare, design, and education, where intricate visual-textual understanding and reasoning are paramount.

In conclusion, "Sketch-in-Latents" does not merely offer an incremental improvement but proposes a transformative shift in how MLLMs perform reasoning. By embedding visual imagination directly into the latent space of the model, SkiLa paves the way for a new generation of MLLMs that are more intelligent, flexible, and capable of truly unified multimodal understanding, bringing us closer to AI systems that can "think" with both words and images.
