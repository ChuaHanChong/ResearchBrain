# Stable Language Guidance for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sun Yat-sen University developed Residual Semantic Steering (RSS), a probabilistic framework that enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' robustness by disentangling physical affordance from semantic intent. This approach improved success rates by up to 29.85 percentage points on language-perturbed benchmarks and facilitated better generalization for novel task compositions.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit "instruction blindness" and rote execution, struggling to robustly interpret varied natural language instructions.
- "Modality collapse" occurs due to manifold sparsity, where limited linguistic training data causes models to overfit to specific instruction phrasings.
- "Modality collapse" also results from prior dominance, where dense visual signals overpower sparse linguistic tokens, leading to actions based on visual affordance over explicit linguistic intent.

## Method

- Proposed **Residual Semantic Steering (RSS)**, a probabilistic framework designed to disentangle physical affordance from semantic execution in VLA models.
- Introduced **Monte Carlo Syntactic Integration (MCSI)**, which uses an ==Oracle Teacher== to generate a dense ==syntactic neighborhood== of instructions, approximating the true semantic policy.
- Implemented **Residual Affordance Steering (RAS)** to isolate and amplify the causal influence of language by subtracting a visually-driven ==affordance prior== from conditional ==action logits==.

## Results

- RSS improved average success rates on destructive instruction overwriting benchmarks by approximately **29.85 percentage points** for π0 and **11.08 percentage points** for π0.5.
- Enhanced robustness across obfuscated instruction reinterpretation variants, with gains of approximately **20.44 percentage points** for π0 and **3.12 percentage points** for π0.5 on challenging conditions.
- Demonstrated improved performance for out-of-distribution semantic transfer and achieved superior results on the overall LIBERO-Plus benchmark, indicating robustness to both linguistic and visual shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models' brittleness to linguistic variations stems from a "modality collapse" phenomenon where sparse language inputs are overshadowed by dense visual priors.
> - Denser linguistic supervision through Monte Carlo approximation of semantic intent can make VLA policies invariant to surface-level syntactic perturbations.
> - Explicitly decoupling visually-driven affordance from linguistic intent, rather than just amplifying language, can effectively suppress visual bias and enhance semantic grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI aims to develop intelligent agents capable of mapping high-level human intent to low-level robotic control actions. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent approach in this domain, leveraging large-scale imitation learning on extensive robot demonstration datasets. Recent advancements have led to the development of sophisticated VLA architectures, including large-scale imitators like RT-1 and RT-2, efficient open-source models such as OpenVLA, and autoregressive models like SpatialVLA and OpenVLA-OFT. Diffusion-based control methods, exemplified by Diffusion Policy, CogACT, and RDT, have also matured, leading to state-of-the-art generalist frameworks like π0, π0.5, and E0, which focus on broad physical generalization.

Despite these architectural innovations and scaling efforts, a significant challenge within VLA research is the brittleness of these models to linguistic perturbations. Existing VLA agents often struggle with "instruction blindness" or exhibit rote execution, failing to robustly interpret and act upon varied natural language instructions. This issue stems from an imbalance in multimodal inputs, where dense, high-frequency visual signals tend to overwhelm sparse linguistic tokens. Previous studies and benchmarks, such as Libero-Plus and RADAR, have highlighted this pervasive problem, demonstrating that models often default to visually probable actions rather than strictly adhering to semantic intent encoded in language. Architecturally, some work, like RDT-1B, has attempted to mitigate this "text overshadowing" through mechanisms like alternating cross-attention.

This paper addresses this specific vulnerability by identifying a "modality collapse" phenomenon where visual priors dominate sparse linguistic signals, causing models to overfit to specific instruction phrasings rather than understanding underlying semantic intent. It positions itself as a methodological advancement aimed at enhancing the robustness and semantic grounding of existing VLA models against diverse linguistic variations. The proposed framework directly confronts the issue of modality imbalance and instruction fragility, aiming to improve the reliability of language-conditioned robotic control, a crucial step towards more generalist and deployable embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and semantic grounding of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly when faced with linguistic perturbations. The authors identify that despite their generalized control capabilities, current VLA models are susceptible to variations in natural language instructions, often failing to consistently execute tasks when instructions deviate from training templates.

The motivation for this work is rooted in two critical observations, which the authors term "modality collapse":
1. **Manifold Sparsity:** VLA models are typically trained on datasets where the linguistic descriptions for a given latent semantic intent (z) cover only a small fraction of its possible syntactic realizations (p(l|z)). This limited exposure causes models to overfit to the surface-level syntactic patterns of the training data, treating specific lexical choices as essential signals rather than variable expressions of the same underlying intent. Consequently, when presented with paraphrased, obfuscated, or slightly altered instructions, the model may fail because it has not learned to generalize across the broader linguistic manifold of the intended action.
2. **Prior Dominance:** In the high-dimensional joint distribution of actions, observations, and language p(a|o, l), the visual input (o) contains dense, high-frequency information (e.g., edges, textures, object proximity). This dense visual signal often dominates the gradient during training, causing the model to develop a "visual affordance prior." This prior leads the agent to frequently default to physically feasible and visually salient actions (e.g., "grasp the nearest object") regardless of the specific linguistic instruction provided. The sparse linguistic signal (l) becomes a weaker factor in determining the action, diminishing the model's reliance on and interpretation of the language.

To address these identified fragilities, the research aims to develop a probabilistic framework, Residual Semantic Steering (RSS), with the following specific objectives:
- To disentangle physical affordance (what is visually possible) from semantic execution (what is linguistically intended).
- To enable the VLA policy to approximate the true semantic posterior π(a|o, z), ensuring invariance to surface-level syntactic perturbations.
- To isolate and amplify the causal influence of language by suppressing the visually driven "affordance prior."
- To maximize the mutual information between action and intent while minimizing the influence of visual distractors.
- To demonstrate empirically that the proposed framework achieves state-of-the-art robustness against various linguistic perturbations, including destructive overwriting, obfuscated reinterpretation, and out-of-distribution semantic transfer.

The overarching motivation is to move beyond VLA models that merely execute rote patterns based on visual cues or memorized instruction templates towards agents that genuinely understand and follow semantic intent conveyed through natural language, thereby enhancing their reliability and generalizability in real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose Residual Semantic Steering (RSS), a probabilistic framework designed to improve the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models against linguistic perturbations by disentangling physical affordance from semantic execution. RSS consists of two main theoretical innovations: Monte Carlo Syntactic Integration and Residual Affordance Steering.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework operates within the context of VLA models trained via imitation learning, maximizing the log-likelihood of actions given observations and natural language instructions. Modern VLA architectures typically use pretrained vision-language model backbones, processing visual, linguistic, and action inputs as unified token sequences for next-token prediction. The goal is to ensure the policy π(a|o, l) is invariant to linguistic noise ϵl, such that π(a|o, l) ≈ π(a|o, l + ϵl).

#### 3.2. Monte Carlo Syntactic Integration (MCSI)

MCSI is designed to address the problem of "manifold sparsity" in linguistic training data.
- **Problem:** A single instruction (l) for a latent intent (z) is often a noisy and sparse representation of the true intent. Training on limited linguistic variations leads to overfitting to specific lexical choices.
- **Approach:** Instead of relying on a single instruction, MCSI approximates the true semantic policy p(a|o, z) by marginalizing over the syntactic nuisance variable. This is achieved through a Monte Carlo approximation.
 - An "Oracle Teacher" (a high-capability Large Language Model, e.g., Qwen2.5-VL for training, ChatGPT-5.2 for evaluation) is used.
 - For an original instruction l_orig, the Oracle Teacher generates a "dense syntactic neighborhood" N(l_orig) = {l_1..., l_K}, effectively sampling from an induced distribution p(l|z).
 - The model is then optimized using an Expected Semantic Loss: L_RSS = E_(o,a)~D [1/K Σ_(k=1)^K -log π_θ(a|o, l_k)].
- **Effect:** This formulation forces the policy encoder to map diverse linguistic inputs (l_k) corresponding to the same intent (z) to a unified region in the latent embedding space. This explicitly minimizes conditional entropy H(A|L) with respect to syntactic variation, making the policy invariant to surface-level changes.

#### 3.3. Residual Affordance Steering (RAS)

RAS is introduced to counteract "prior dominance," where dense visual signals often overpower sparse linguistic signals.
- **Problem:** Standard VLA inference, s(a|o, l), can approximate s(a|o, ∅) (the affordance prior) when language is weak or visual features are overwhelming, leading to actions driven by visual instinct rather than explicit intent.
- **Approach:** The decision process is decomposed into the Affordance Prior s(a|o, ∅) (actions based solely on visual scene geometry) and Semantic Modulation (the shift induced by the instruction).
 - The "unconditional" forward pass s(a|o, ∅) is reinterpreted as the Base Affordance Distribution, capturing physically feasible actions independent of intent.
 - A "Pure Semantic Signal" (∆_sem) is calculated by subtracting this visual prior from the conditional logits: ∆_sem(a, o, l) = s(a|o, l) - s(a|o, ∅). This subtraction is designed to cancel out the visual bias, isolating the causal influence of language.
 - The final "Steered Policy" is then calculated as: ˜π(a|o, l) ∝ exp(s(a|o, ∅) + γ · ∆_sem(a, o, l)), where γ > 1 is the Steering Coefficient.
- **Distinction from CFG:** While mechanically similar to Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), RAS differs conceptually. CFG in generative models typically acts as a quality booster. In RSS, RAS functions as a "Bias Suppressor" in control, mathematically penalizing actions driven solely by visual instinct not confirmed by text.
- **Theoretical Analysis (Proposition 1):** The authors provide a theoretical analysis showing that if the logit function is approximated linearly, RSS with γ > 1 effectively restores the "rank" of language features, orthogonalizing the semantic vector from the visual manifold by artificially amplifying the linguistic contribution.

#### 3.4. Training Setting

- **Baselines:** π0 and π0.5 were adopted as baseline models.
- **Backbone:** A pretrained vision-language model backbone based on Gemma, initialized from large-scale pretraining on diverse robotic datasets, was fine-tuned.
- **Oracle Teachers:** Qwen2.5-VL was used for generating semantic neighborhoods during MCSI training. ChatGPT-5.2 was employed for generating consistent linguistic variations during evaluation.
- **Training Details:** Models were trained for 30,000 steps with a batch size of 32, using a cosine decay learning rate schedule and an exponential moving average (EMA).

#### 3.5. Simulation Experiments

The LIBERO benchmark was used, extended with three categories of controlled instruction variants:
- **Destructive Instruction Overwriting:** Critical semantic components were corrupted or removed (e.g., Blank, Simple, Multi, Rand, Mask with varying probabilities).
- **Obfuscated Instruction Reinterpretation:** Semantically preserved but linguistically challenging descriptions were introduced (e.g., R0-R4, including multiword substitution, distraction, common sense descriptions, reasoning chains, and confusion with distractors).
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Semantic Transfer:** Novel task instructions were created by recombining known object concepts into unseen compositions, evaluating generalization under few-step adaptation.

#### 3.6. Ablation Studies

Ablations were conducted on the steering coefficient (γ) for RAS and the number of denoising steps, primarily under destructive instruction overwriting benchmarks. Further studies investigated the generalization of MCSI by testing with different VLM teachers (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-3.5) for paraphrasing, and using different VLMs (InternVL3, LLaVA-OneVision) for instruction generation during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Residual Semantic Steering (RSS) demonstrated its effectiveness in improving the robustness and semantic grounding of VLA models against various linguistic perturbations.

#### 4.1. Robustness under Destructive Instruction Overwriting

- **Performance:** RSS, particularly with the combined application of Residual Affordance Steering (RAS) and Monte Carlo Syntactic Integration (MCSI), achieved higher average success rates across all variants of destructive overwriting compared to baseline models (π0 and π0.5).
- **Specific Improvements:** The most substantial improvements were observed under conditions where linguistic input was severely degraded, such as "Blank" instructions (no language input), "Simple" generic instructions, and high-ratio "Mask" conditions (e.g., 80% masked words). For instance, with π0, the average success rate increased by approximately 29.85 percentage points with RAS & MCSI. With π0.5, the average success rate increased by approximately 11.08 percentage points.
- **Interpretation:** This indicated that RSS partially mitigated the reliance on brittle instruction patterns, fostering more semantically grounded policy behavior. RAS's role in injecting residual affordance signals helped guide the policy even when language was severely degraded, while MCSI improved robustness to syntactic variations by learning from a dense neighborhood of instructions.

#### 4.2. Robustness under Obfuscated Instruction Reinterpretation

- **Performance:** Models augmented with RAS and MCSI demonstrated improved robustness across most obfuscated instruction variants on the LIBERO-Goal benchmark.
- **Challenging Conditions:** Variants R3 (Reasoning Chain) and R4 (Confusion), which required interpreting implicit reasoning structures or resisting distractor objects, posed the most significant challenges for baseline models. RSS significantly improved performance in these conditions. For π0, RAS & MCSI increased average success rate by approximately 20.44 percentage points. For π0.5, the increase was approximately 3.12 percentage points.
- **Interpretation:** These results suggested that richer vision-language alignment, achieved through RSS, encouraged policies to depend less on superficial lexical cues and more on semantically grounded representations.

#### 4.3. Out-of-Distribution Semantic Transfer

- **Baseline Limitations:** The vanilla models exhibited limited generalization, particularly under low-step adaptation regimes (e.g., 10-steps), and often overfit to single held-out tasks.
- **RSS Performance:** RSS-augmented models achieved more consistent successful execution across both held-out OOD tasks. MCSI was identified as the dominant component in enhancing semantic transfer and reducing reliance on task-specific memorization, consistently achieving stronger gains. RAS and MCSI combined offered additional benefits.
- **Interpretation:** This indicated that RSS facilitated better transfer of learned object semantics to novel task compositions, reflecting improved robustness to compositional distribution shifts.

#### 4.4. LIBERO-Plus Task Performance

- The method demonstrated superior performance on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, outperforming baselines and other state-of-the-art competitors not only in language-perturbed subsets but also across the overall benchmark. This indicated that RSS improved robustness without negatively impacting performance on other visual domain shifts present in Libero-Plus.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Steering Coefficient (γ):** Moderate steering coefficients (e.g., 1.25 to 1.5) consistently improved robustness under semantic-preserving perturbations. However, excessively large coefficients amplified sensitivity to corrupted instructions, leading to performance degradation under destructive perturbations. This suggested that over-strong RAS could lead to over-conditioning on unreliable linguistic signals.
- **Denoising Steps:** Increasing the number of denoising steps did not consistently translate to higher overall performance and sometimes led to slight degradation. This indicated that excessive denoising primarily refined low-level action details without providing additional semantic benefits for language understanding. The overall impact was limited under smaller steering coefficients.
- **Generalization to Different VLM Teachers:** MCSI demonstrated stable performance even when evaluated with instructions paraphrased by different VLMs (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-3.5) not used during its training. The performance drop was minimal compared to baseline models, indicating that MCSI generalized to the underlying semantic intent rather than overfitting to a specific teacher's syntax. Training with different VLM teachers (InternVL3, LLaVA-OneVision) also yielded consistent performance, confirming robustness to the choice of instruction generator.

#### 4.6. Training Loss Curves

Variants equipped with RAS and MCSI achieved lower loss values during training compared to baseline models, suggesting that the proposed components provided a more effective training signal and facilitated smoother optimization.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative rollouts under destructive instruction overwriting showed that vanilla policies exhibited brittle behavior, failing to localize objects or align with affordances when linguistic tokens were removed. MCSI improved robustness, but RAS was crucial for steering the policy towards task-relevant configurations when language was severely degraded. The combination of RAS+MCSI achieved the most reliable performance, consistently executing correct behaviors by jointly mitigating linguistic uncertainty and reinforcing visual affordance alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental limitation in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their vulnerability to linguistic variations and their tendency to prioritize visual affordances over explicit semantic instructions. By identifying and proposing solutions for "modality collapse," characterized by manifold sparsity in language data and the dominance of visual priors, the work contributes to the development of more robust and reliable robotic control systems.

The introduction of Residual Semantic Steering (RSS), comprising Monte Carlo Syntactic Integration (MCSI) and Residual Affordance Steering (RAS), represents a methodological advancement in language guidance for VLA models.
- **Theoretical Impact:** MCSI provides a principled approach to approximate the true semantic posterior by densifying linguistic supervision, which helps models generalize beyond specific instruction phrasings. RAS offers a mechanism to explicitly disentangle and amplify the influence of language by subtracting visual affordance priors, a conceptual shift from standard classifier-free guidance. The theoretical analysis supporting visual bias decoupling reinforces these innovations.
- **Practical Impact:** The empirical results demonstrate that RSS significantly improves VLA models' ability to cope with diverse linguistic perturbations, including masked instructions, synonyms, verbose descriptions, and implicit reasoning chains. This enhanced robustness is critical for deploying robotic systems in real-world environments where natural language commands are inherently varied and imprecise. By fostering stronger semantic grounding, RSS can reduce "instruction blindness" and prevent agents from defaulting to unsafe or unintended actions based solely on visual cues. The improved performance in out-of-distribution semantic transfer further suggests better generalization capabilities for novel task compositions. The validation on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, including various visual domain shifts, indicates the general applicability of the method without sacrificing performance under different environmental conditions.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that Residual Affordance Steering (RAS) can lead to conservative behavior when faced with extremely vague or underspecified instructions. In such scenarios, where the linguistic signal lacks sufficient semantic content, the model may hesitate or become inactive because it is explicitly designed to suppress actions driven solely by the base visual affordance distribution. While this prevents the model from "hallucinating" actions or defaulting to rote visual patterns, it implies a dependency on sufficiently meaningful commands for initiating motion. This characteristic, though a limitation in certain contexts, can also be viewed as a safety feature, preventing autonomous execution of potentially unsafe actions when user intent is unclear.

Overall, this work contributes to advancing the state of the art in embodied AI by providing a framework that enhances the stability and interpretability of language guidance in robotic control. The potential impact includes more dependable human-robot interaction and a foundational step towards generalist robot policies that can robustly interpret and execute complex instructions in varied settings.
