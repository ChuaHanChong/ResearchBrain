# Tactile-based Multimodal Fusion in Embodied Intelligence: A Survey of Vision, Language, and Contact-Driven Paradigms

> [!summary] Summary
> Multimodal tactile fusion survey covering research up to the first quarter of 2026. It proposes a hierarchical taxonomy spanning multimodal datasets (Tactile-Vision, Tactile-Language, Tactile-Vision-Language, Tactile-Vision-Other), three method pillars (perception and recognition, cross-modal generation, interaction and manipulation), and four tactile sensor families, plus a four-stage transduction-to-decoding formalism, an evaluation-metric catalogue, and open challenges toward general-purpose tactile intelligence.

## Problem

- Unimodal tactile perception has sparse spatial coverage and no global semantic context, so touch alone cannot resolve object identity or scene-level reasoning for embodied agents.
- Prior work is fragmented across incompatible datasets, sensor morphologies and task definitions, with no unified theoretical framework linking data, methods, hardware and embodied interaction.
- Earlier surveys cover only single modality pairs or model families, leaving the full pipeline from hardware transduction to high-level semantic reasoning unmapped.

## Method

- **Hierarchical taxonomy** with three pillars: multimodal datasets grouped by ==modality composition== (T-V, T-L, T-V-L, T-V-O), multimodal methods, and tactile sensors grouped by physical embodiment.
- **Four-stage formalism** of the fusion pipeline: ==physical transduction==, ==modality-specific encoders==, a ==cross-modal fusion operator== producing a ==joint representation==, and ==embodied decoding== to class, generated signal, or action.
- Comparative tables: datasets by year, object instances, scale, modality, source and sensor type; methods tagged by encoders and task; metrics listed with formulas and usage.

## Results

- The dataset table catalogues **32** resources across **four** modality groups, ranging from an **18-object** 2016 set to million-scale collections such as VisGel (**3M**) and VinT-6D (**2.1M**).
- The method table spans 2016 to early 2026, showing a shift from CNN encoders (AlexNet, VGG, ResNet) to ViT plus OpenCLIP or Vicuna backbones in tri-modal models.
- Publication counts rise steadily from 2015 and accelerate sharply after 2020, spreading from robotics and haptics venues into vision and multimodal learning communities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-dataset transfer stays weak because sensor hardware and contact protocols dominate tactile statistics; evaluations remain in-distribution, so report sensor-shift results before claiming generality.
> - Pretrained vision and language encoders supply priors that tactile branches cannot learn from scarce contact data, making transfer, not architecture, the main performance driver.
> - No single metric captures tactile realism, semantic correctness and control effectiveness together; generation scores correlate weakly with downstream manipulation utility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile sensing is a fundamental component of embodied intelligence, providing direct feedback on physical interactions such as contact geometry, material properties, and interaction dynamics. While essential, unimodal tactile perception is limited by its sparse spatial coverage and lack of global semantic context. The broader research landscape has seen an increasing exploration of multimodal integration, especially combining tactile signals with vision, language, and other modalities, driven by advancements in deep learning and large language models (LLMs). This integration aims to bridge physical interaction with semantic reasoning, leading to the emergence of "Multimodal Tactile Fusion."

Despite rapid progress, the existing research in multimodal tactile fusion has been fragmented, characterized by disparate datasets, sensing modalities, and tasks, and lacking a unified theoretical framework. Previous surveys in related areas primarily focused on specific modality pairs or model families, rather than providing a holistic view of the field. This paper addresses this fragmentation by offering a comprehensive survey that consolidates the current research landscape of multimodal tactile fusion. It positions itself as an effort to provide a unified system-level view by linking data resources, learning methods, sensing hardware, and embodied interaction within a coherent framework. By doing so, it aims to establish a clear foundation for developing more general, scalable, and robust tactile fusion systems, thereby supporting the advancement of general-purpose tactile intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the recognition of tactile sensing as a crucial modality for embodied intelligence, capable of providing direct and unique feedback that remote sensors cannot replicate. However, the inherent limitations of unimodal tactile perception, such as sparse spatial coverage and absence of global semantic context, highlight the necessity of its integration with other modalities. The recent proliferation of deep learning and large language models has further emphasized the importance of integrating tactile information with vision and language to facilitate a connection between physical interaction and semantic reasoning. Despite the rapid progress in this nascent field, the research has been characterized by fragmentation across diverse datasets, sensing modalities, and tasks, leading to a lack of a unified theoretical framework.

To address these identified gaps, the paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Comprehensive Survey:** To provide a detailed and comprehensive review of multimodal tactile fusion research, encompassing studies published up to the first quarter of 2026.
2. **Hierarchical Taxonomy:** To propose and establish a hierarchical taxonomy that systematically organizes the field into two primary dimensions: multimodal datasets and multimodal methods. This taxonomy aims to offer a structured understanding of the research landscape.
3. **Dataset Review:** To categorize and review available multimodal tactile datasets, classifying them into Tactile-Vision (T-V), Tactile-Language (T-L), Tactile-Vision-Language (T-V-L), and Tactile-Vision-Other (T-V-O) datasets, along with their respective characteristics and utility.
4. **Methodological Structuring:** To structure prior work on multimodal methods into three core pillars: Multimodal Perception and Recognition (focused on object understanding and grasp prediction), Cross-Modal Generation and Transformation (emphasizing bidirectional translation between modalities), and Multimodal Interaction and Manipulation (centering on feedback control and language-guided manipulation).
5. **Hardware Overview:** To summarize representative tactile sensing hardware platforms, detailing their principles, designs, and applications within multimodal fusion systems.
6. **Evaluation Analysis:** To review commonly used evaluation metrics and benchmark settings across various multimodal tactile tasks, highlighting their applicability and limitations.
7. **Challenges and Future Directions:** To identify and discuss the current challenges faced by the field and to outline promising future research directions, aiming to guide subsequent advancements toward general, scalable, and robust tactile fusion systems.
8. **Consolidation and Foundation:** Ultimately, the survey aims to consolidate the existing research landscape, offer a unified perspective on how tactile signals are integrated into multimodal systems, and lay a clear theoretical and practical foundation for future development in embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper presents a systematic survey, and its methodology primarily involves the structured collection, categorization, and analysis of existing literature on multimodal tactile fusion. The authors followed a specific approach to achieve their objectives:

1. **Literature Collection:** Papers were gathered from major academic journals and conferences specializing in robotics, computer vision, haptics, and multimodal learning. The collection period extended up to the first quarter of 2026.
2. **Inclusion Criteria:** The selected works focused on studies that combined tactile sensing with at least one additional modality, specifically vision and/or language, within data-driven or learning-based settings. The scope explicitly included bimodal (Tactile-Vision, Tactile-Language) and trimodal (Tactile-Vision-Language) settings, with an expansion to Tactile-Vision-Other (T-V-O) for modalities like audio or action streams.
3. **Hierarchical Taxonomy Development:** A central aspect of the methodology was the development of a hierarchical taxonomy to organize the fragmented field. This taxonomy is structured along three primary pillars, as depicted in Fig. 1:
 - **Multimodal Datasets:** Categorized based on modality composition, including T-V, T-L, T-V-L, and T-V-O datasets. For each dataset, the survey reviews its sensing setup, scale, source (human, robot, simulation), real environment usage, and supported tasks.
 - **Multimodal Methods:** Organized into three core research categories: Multimodal Perception and Recognition (covering object understanding, attribute/material recognition, grasp prediction, cross-modal retrieval), Multimodal Cross-Modal Generation and Transformation (focusing on vision-tactile and language-tactile generation), and Multimodal Interaction and Manipulation (emphasizing robot manipulation with multimodal perception and language-guided manipulation). The analysis of methods focuses on architectural designs, fusion strategies (e.g., early/late fusion, shared embeddings, cross-attention, contrastive learning), and learning objectives (supervised, RL, transfer learning).
 - **Tactile Sensors:** Platforms were categorized into four groups based on their physical embodiment and deployment: wearable tactile systems, handheld and fingertip sensors, robotic skin and multimodal sensor patches, and gripper-mounted and integrated sensors. This section provides an overview of their characteristics, transduction principles, and integration benefits.
4. **Trend Analysis:** The survey analyzes the publication trends from 2015 to 2026, observing the acceleration of research post-2020 and the expansion of relevant venues from robotics/haptics to broader vision/multimodal communities.
5. **Evaluation Framework Review:** The methodology includes a systematic review of evaluation metrics and benchmark settings used across the diverse tasks. Metrics are grouped by task categories (Perception/Recognition, Generation, Manipulation) to illustrate their specific applications and limitations, addressing accuracy, reconstruction fidelity, semantic consistency, and control effectiveness.
6. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** Based on the comprehensive review, the authors synthesize current limitations and identify key open challenges (e.g., data scalability, modality misalignment, hardware-software integration, benchmark deficiencies). They then propose promising future research directions (e.g., scalable datasets, hierarchical fusion, bio-inspired sensors, end-to-end systems, simulation synergies).

This systematic and structured approach allowed the authors to present a consolidated view of the field, highlight developmental trajectories, and provide a foundational resource for future research in multimodal tactile fusion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically analyzes the landscape of multimodal tactile fusion, presenting key findings across datasets, methods, tactile sensors, and evaluation paradigms.

**A. Evolution and Landscape Overview:**
The field of multimodal tactile fusion has experienced substantial growth, particularly after 2020, expanding from traditional robotics and haptics venues into broader computer vision and multimodal communities. This growth is driven by advances in deep learning, reinforcement learning, and the emergence of large language models and multimodal foundation models, which facilitate the integration of tactile perception with semantic reasoning and action. The survey establishes a unified physical-to-computational pipeline that explicitly connects raw sensor transduction to high-level semantic reasoning.

**B. Multimodal Datasets:**
The paper categorizes datasets into four groups based on their modality composition, reflecting an evolution towards larger, richer, and more complex data:
- **Tactile-Vision (T-V) Datasets:** Initially focused on controlled robotic grasping (e.g., VT, Feeling of Success) with high-resolution sensors like GelSight for object recognition and grasp prediction. More recent datasets (e.g., Touch and Go, Touch in the Wild) have shifted towards less constrained, free-form human interactions in realistic environments, providing large-scale aligned multi-sensor data.
- **Tactile-Language (T-L) Datasets:** Emphasize semantic grounding without relying on visual observations. Examples include PhysiCLeAR (material attributes), TCL3D (3D tactile point clouds with language), and STOLA (open-ended commonsense descriptions). These datasets, though smaller in scale, are crucial for tactile captioning and language-grounded representation learning.
- **Tactile-Vision-Language (T-V-L) Datasets:** Represent the most semantically expressive setting, combining visual, tactile, and natural language annotations. Early datasets (e.g., TVL, TLV, MMWand) established tri-modal alignment in controlled settings. Recent datasets (e.g., Touch100k, VTV150K, ObjTac) offer larger scales, richer semantics (fine-grained phrases, temporal data), and better connect perception, language, and interaction.
- **Tactile-Vision-Other (T-V-O) Datasets:** Extend T-V settings by incorporating additional modalities such as audio, action, force, or proprioception (e.g., ObjectFolder series, TLA, VinT-6D, OmniViTac). These datasets broaden the focus from multisensory perception to embodied perception-action modeling, emphasizing dynamic and physically grounded signals.

**C. Multimodal Methods:**
The survey organizes methods into three core paradigms, highlighting a progression from task-specific solutions to more generalized, transferable frameworks:
- **Multimodal Perception and Recognition:** This paradigm covers object recognition, attribute/material recognition, grasp success/failure prediction, and cross-modal retrieval.
 - **Object Recognition:** Evolved from early T-V fusion (e.g., VT) to deep cross-modal architectures (e.g., VITO-Transformer) and recent T-V-L fusion with fine-grained spatial alignment (e.g., VHTformer, DAMF, ViTaPEs), where language increasingly acts as a semantic scaffold.
 - **Attribute and Material Recognition:** Progressed from shared T-V representations (e.g., ViTac, Touch and Go) to scalable representation learning enabled by foundation models (e.g., UniTouch, MViTac) and strong language grounding (e.g., Octopi, Tactile-VLM, CLTP). Recent work addresses data scarcity and hardware heterogeneity through sensor-agnostic architectures (e.g., AnyTouch).
 - **Grasp Success/Failure Prediction:** Established the importance of tactile feedback for post-contact evaluation (e.g., TFOS, ACVTM). Recent research uses transferable representations (e.g., Touch and Go, UniTouch) and robust alignment for zero-shot generalization and improved robustness across heterogeneous sensors (e.g., ConViTac, TLV-Link).
 - **Cross-Modal Retrieval and Matching:** Serves as a benchmark for evaluating learned representations, ensuring they preserve contact-aware semantics and support robust cross-modal transfer (e.g., CLAF, UniTouch).
- **Cross-Modal Generation and Transformation:** This paradigm focuses on synthesizing signals between modalities.
 - **Vision-Tactile Generation and Translation:** Explores bidirectional synthesis between visual observations and tactile responses (e.g., LVTR, CTAV). Recent methods emphasize scalable, unified representations (e.g., BVT, UVTS, UniTouch) to achieve more general vision-tactile generation.
 - **Language-Tactile Generation and Translation:**
 - **Tactile-to-Language Description:** Translates tactile signals into natural language (e.g., Tactile-VLM, UniTouch). Recent work uses LLMs for evaluation and focuses on grounded, interactive, and open-ended generation (e.g., CLTP, VTV-LLM, SToLa, Octopi-1.5).
 - **Text-Driven Tactile Synthesis:** Generates tactile signals from language prompts, with emerging research showing feasibility by anchoring tactile features in pre-trained vision-language spaces (e.g., UniTouch).
- **Multimodal Interaction and Manipulation:** The most application-oriented branch, tightly coupling perception with physical action.
 - **Robot Manipulation with Multimodal Perception:**
 - **Stable Grasping:** Uses continuous contact feedback for stability (e.g., ACVTM, Visuotactile-RL, TEVG). Recent work moves towards unified multimodal policy learning (e.g., OmniVTLA).
 - **Fine Manipulation and Assembly:** Requires precise perception of continuous contact states (e.g., SFA, VTT, ViTacFormer). Recent research focuses on contact-aware policies and broader multimodal integration (e.g., DexTac, DreamTacVLA, OmniVTA).
 - **Multimodal Manipulation under Language Instructions:** Maps high-level semantic commands to contact-rich physical control, integrating T-V-L-A frameworks (e.g., VTLA, VTLG) for tasks like insertion and grasp generation.

**D. Tactile Sensors:**
The survey provides a taxonomy of tactile sensing platforms, emphasizing their role as the hardware foundation for data acquisition and cross-modal alignment. Categories include wearable tactile systems (e.g., glove-based, OSMO), handheld and fingertip sensors (e.g., GelSight, DIGIT, TacThru, TacUMI), robotic skin and multimodal sensor patches (e.g., thin-film arrays, hydrogel e-skin, piezoresistive sponges), and gripper-mounted and integrated sensors (e.g., LightTact, FVTS, TacUMI). These sensors vary in design, transduction principles, and integration, influencing the quality and richness of tactile signals for fusion.

**E. Evaluation and Comparative Analysis:**
The field currently lacks unified benchmarks and standardized evaluation protocols, with practices remaining highly task-dependent.
- **Metrics:** Diverse metrics are employed, grouped by task. For perception/recognition: accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, mAP, R@k, MRR, cosine similarity. For generation: MSE, PSNR, SSIM, FID, classification accuracy on generated samples, LLM-based semantic scores. For manipulation: success rate, IoU, angle error, goal convergence rate, L1 distance, human normalized score.
- **Benchmarks:** Are largely task-driven and dataset-specific, with prominent datasets (e.g., VisGel, UniTouch, AnyTouch) serving as de facto benchmarks for specific tasks.
- **Cross-Task and Cross-Dataset Comparison:** Generalization remains a challenge due to sensor and dataset biases. However, larger datasets, richer modality combinations (especially with language), and pretraining on foundation models are shown to improve representation quality and transferability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the advancement of embodied intelligence and multimodal learning by providing a critical consolidation and structured analysis of the fragmented field of multimodal tactile fusion.

**A. Significance:**
1. **Unified Framework:** The paper's primary significance lies in its proposal of a hierarchical taxonomy that unifies multimodal tactile fusion across datasets, methods, and hardware. This framework provides a much-needed organizational structure, making it easier for researchers to navigate, categorize, and build upon existing work, thereby fostering a more coherent research direction.
2. **Comprehensive Overview:** By systematically reviewing representative datasets, model architectures, applications, sensing technologies, and evaluation metrics up to Q1 2026, the survey offers a comprehensive snapshot of the field's current state. This detailed overview helps identify key trends, technological shifts (e.g., the rise of LLMs and foundation models), and areas of rapid development.
3. **Identification of Gaps:** The explicit discussion of current challenges, such as data scalability gaps, modality misalignment, hardware-software integration barriers, and benchmark deficiencies, is crucial. These identified bottlenecks highlight critical areas that require concentrated research efforts for the field to progress beyond task-specific solutions.
4. **Foundation for Future Research:** The survey serves as a foundational reference, equipping new and experienced researchers with a structured understanding of the field's complexities and interdependencies. It offers a starting point for developing more general, scalable, and robust tactile fusion systems by outlining clear future directions.

**B. Potential Impact:**
1. **Accelerating Embodied AI Development:** By bridging raw sensor transduction with high-level semantic reasoning, multimodal tactile fusion is positioned as an indispensable component for embodied AI. Addressing the identified challenges can lead to more capable autonomous agents that can accurately perceive and interact with complex, unstructured physical environments.
2. **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation:** The insights into multimodal methods for perception, generation, and interaction, particularly in stable grasping and fine manipulation, can lead to more dexterous and robust robotic systems. This advancement will enable robots to perform intricate tasks requiring precise contact feedback, especially with visually ambiguous or deformable objects.
3. **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** By grounding semantic reasoning in physical contact and enabling language-guided manipulation, multimodal tactile systems can facilitate more intuitive and safer human-robot collaboration. Robots capable of "understanding" touch through language will be more adaptable to human instructions and preferences.
4. **Development of General-Purpose Systems:** The emphasis on scalable multimodal datasets, hierarchical fusion architectures, and simulation synergies is expected to foster the development of general-purpose tactile systems. These systems would exhibit improved transferability across diverse tasks, sensor morphologies, and real-world environments, moving beyond specialized applications.
5. **Innovation in Sensing Hardware:** The review of tactile sensors and the discussion of bio-inspired designs will likely stimulate further innovation in hardware development. Future sensors are anticipated to be more compliant, durable, power-efficient, and capable of multi-physics sensing, catering to the demands of complex multimodal fusion and deployment in varied environments, including underwater settings.
6. **Standardization and Benchmarking:** The clear articulation of benchmark deficiencies is expected to prompt the community to develop more holistic, end-to-end embodied benchmarks. This will facilitate more meaningful comparisons across studies, accelerate progress, and ensure that research outputs are evaluated against practical considerations such as safety and robustness in physical interaction.

In essence, this survey provides a roadmap for the transition of tactile feedback from a supplementary modality to the indispensable physical cornerstone of embodied AI, enabling autonomous agents to achieve robust, contact-aware intelligence in real-world scenarios.
