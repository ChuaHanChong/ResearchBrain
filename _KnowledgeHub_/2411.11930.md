# AtomThink: Multimodal Slow Thinking with Atomic Step Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> AtomThink introduces a framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that employs Self-structured Chain-of-Thought (SCoT) reasoning, enabling adaptive adjustment of reasoning depth and structure based on problem complexity. This approach yields an average accuracy improvement exceeding 10% on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and reduces inference time by 85.3% compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods for MLLMs are either too rigid with fixed templates (structured CoT) or inefficient and prone to 'overthinking' (unstructured CoT).
- Structured CoT approaches limit reasoning diversity and generalization across varied multimodal tasks due to predefined structures.
- Unstructured CoT often consumes excessive tokens and computational resources by generating unnecessarily long reasoning chains for simpler problems.

## Method

- A ==Self-structured Chain-of-Thought (SCoT)== paradigm that decomposes complex reasoning into minimal =='atomic steps'==, allowing MLLMs to dynamically generate and adapt the length and structure of reasoning paths.
- Development of the ==AMATH data engine==, a high-quality multimodal long CoT dataset, generated through dynamic prompting and rigorous filtering, to supervise granular, step-by-step reasoning.
- Implementation of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for atomic step learning and policy-guided inference with a ==Process Reward Model (PRM)== to search for optimal reasoning paths during multi-turn inference.

## Results

- AtomThink achieved an average accuracy improvement of over **10%** (up to **16.6%** with PRM) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and outperformed GPT-4V by **8.5%** on MathVista and **3.2%** on WeMath.
- The framework demonstrated strong cross-domain generalization, showing gains of **+25.3%** on ChartQA, **+12.2%** on TextVQA, and **+20.2%** on ScienceQA, despite its mathematical-centric training.
- AtomThink significantly improved resource efficiency, with **5x** higher data utilization and reductions of **87.8%** in output tokens and **85.3%** in inference time per sample in SCoT mode.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing reasoning into 'atomic steps' enables MLLMs to dynamically scale their 'slow thinking' depth according to problem difficulty, leading to both efficiency and higher accuracy.
> - Training on a high-quality, fine-grained multimodal CoT dataset, even if primarily mathematical, fosters robust reasoning skills that generalize effectively to diverse domains like scientific and general visual question answering.
> - Policy-guided search mechanisms, such as beam search, can further refine the generated reasoning paths, enhancing performance while maintaining significant improvements in inference efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): achieving sophisticated, human-like reasoning capabilities, often referred to as "slow thinking" or System 2 cognition.

The broader research landscape for LLMs and MLLMs has seen significant advancements through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. CoT methods enhance models' ability to tackle complex problems by generating intermediate reasoning steps, making the process more transparent and often more accurate. Existing CoT paradigms generally fall into two categories:

- **Structured CoT:** Approaches like LLaVA-CoT and LlamaV-o1 rely on predefined templates or fixed modules to guide the reasoning process. While this introduces some order, it often constrains the diversity of reasoning actions and limits generalization, especially in diverse multimodal contexts where problems vary greatly in nature.
- **Unstructured CoT:** Models such as OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 employ more free-form reasoning chains, iteratively refining their thoughts. These approaches better approximate human cognitive processes and demonstrate superior generalization. However, they frequently suffer from "overthinking" on simpler problems, leading to inefficient token utilization and unnecessary computational expense.

The concept of "slow thinking" (inspired by Kahneman's System 2 thinking) emphasizes deliberate, sequential reasoning. While advanced models like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have shown emergent slow-thinking capabilities, the authors highlight that their inefficiency and overthinking tendencies are significant limitations.

AtomThink directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel **Self-structured Chain-of-Thought (SCoT)** paradigm. This work positions itself as a solution that unifies the benefits of both structured and unstructured CoT while mitigating their drawbacks. It aims to enable MLLMs to adaptively generate reasoning structures and lengths based on problem complexity, thus achieving efficient "slow thinking" that avoids overthinking for simple tasks and provides deep reasoning for complex ones. This approach represents a significant step towards more flexible, efficient, and human-like multimodal reasoning in AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AtomThink is to significantly enhance the multimodal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs by incorporating an adaptive "slow thinking" mechanism. This mechanism allows models to dynamically adjust their reasoning depth and structure according to the complexity of a given problem.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving this research are:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing CoT Approaches:** The authors identify fundamental shortcomings in current Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods. Structured CoT approaches are too rigid, using fixed templates that limit reasoning diversity and generalization. Unstructured CoT, while more flexible, often "overthinks" simpler problems, leading to inefficient token usage and higher computational costs. The motivation is to create a paradigm that is both flexible and efficient.
2. **Developing Self-structured Chain of Thought (SCoT):** A core objective is to introduce SCoT, a novel reasoning paradigm that decomposes complex reasoning into minimal semantic "atomic steps." Unlike prior methods, SCoT aims for autonomous generation and dynamic length control of reasoning chains, allowing the model to adaptively create appropriate structures for problems of varying complexity.
3. **Building a Comprehensive Slow-Thinking Framework (AtomThink):** The research is motivated by the need for a full-pipeline solution to activate and operationalize self-structured reasoning in MLLMs. This includes objectives to:
 - **Data Engine Development:** Create a high-quality, multimodal long CoT dataset (AMATH) through automated generation and rigorous filtering to provide the necessary supervision for detailed, step-by-step reasoning.
 - **Atomic Step Fine-tuning:** Design a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) strategy that teaches models to learn individual inference steps, enabling granular reasoning.
 - **Policy-Guided Multi-turn Inference:** Implement advanced inference methods, including policy search mechanisms (e.g., beam search), to guide the model in generating optimal reasoning paths.
 - **Atomic Capability Evaluation:** Develop a metric to quantitatively assess a model's utilization rate of individual atomic reasoning steps, offering a fine-grained understanding of its cognitive abilities.
4. **Improving Efficiency and Generalization:** A crucial motivation is to achieve higher data utilization and inference efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, while also demonstrating robust generalization across diverse tasks (mathematical, scientific, and general visual question answering), even when trained predominantly on mathematical data.
5. **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning Research:** By providing a novel framework, a high-quality dataset, and detailed analytical tools, the authors aim to contribute to a deeper understanding of how MLLMs reason, paving the way for more capable and intelligent AI systems.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to enable MLLMs to reason more intelligently and efficiently, mimicking the adaptive and deliberate "slow thinking" observed in human cognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AtomThink framework is a comprehensive, full-pipeline approach designed to enhance multimodal large language models (MLLMs) with self-structured, adaptive "slow thinking." It comprises four key components: a self-structured reasoning mechanism (SCoT), a data engine, an atomic step fine-tuning process, and an atomic capability evaluation method.

#### 3.1. Self-structured Chain-of-Thought (SCoT)

SCoT enables MLLMs to dynamically generate reasoning paths by decomposing complex processes into "atomic steps"—minimal, semantically consistent predictive actions.

- **Multi-round Atomic Step Generation:** The model iteratively predicts one atomic step at a time. Each new prediction is appended to the historical reasoning steps and fed back as contextual input for the next cycle, allowing for the construction of dynamic and flexible thought chains.
- **Anomaly Detection and Thought Restart:** To prevent repetitive or erroneous reasoning loops, the framework incorporates:
 - **Rule-based Filter:** Uses template matching and Jaccard similarity to detect semantic repetition within and between steps. It employs thresholds (e.g., <45% intra-step, <98% inter-step repetition) and controls maximum step/response lengths.
 - **Temperature Accumulation:** If an anomaly is detected, the model re-attempts the step with an incrementally increased temperature, encouraging diverse thinking.
- **Policy Search with Process Reward Model (PRM):** AtomThink expands the search space for predictive actions using atomic steps as the fundamental unit, categorized into path-wise and step-wise strategies.
 - **Path-wise Searching:**
 - **Majority Voting:** Selects the most frequent outcome across multiple parallel reasoning paths.
 - **Best-of-N:** Generates *n* candidate rollouts and selects the one with the highest score, determined by a PRM. The PRM can aggregate scores based on the worst action, the last action (final answer prediction), or the average score across all actions in a chain.
 - **Step-wise Searching:**
 - **Greedy Algorithm:** Selects the locally optimal choice at each step without considering future consequences.
 - **Beam Search:** Explores multiple branches at each action, maintaining a fixed number of top candidates to balance exploration and exploitation. This is used in the main experiments for extended inference.
 - **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** Explores the search space through selection, expansion, simulation, and backpropagation, balancing exploration and exploitation via Upper Confidence Bounds (UCB).

#### 3.2. Data Engine (AMATH Dataset)

To guide MLLMs towards deep reasoning, a high-quality CoT dataset called AMATH is developed.

- **Multimodal CoT Generation:**
 - **Dynamic Prompting:** A large language model (LLM) iteratively constructs state-reasoning paths. Each path node captures the previous stage, current state, and a possible action (continue, verify, conclude), leading to higher-quality, multi-step reasoning.
 - **Short CoT Augmentation:** Existing short CoT annotations from VQA datasets are atomized and augmented by an MLLM to create more detailed, discrete reasoning steps.
- **Bad Case Filtering:** To ensure data quality, especially given machine-generated CoTs:
 - **Difficulty Scoring:** Qwen2-VL-7B is used to sample multiple candidates for each question, and the win rate determines the difficulty level, filtering out very easy questions (difficulty level 0).
 - **Secondary Review:** GPT-4o performs a secondary review focusing on the accuracy of atomic steps and final answers. Professional human annotators conduct sampling inspections to correct or eliminate flawed samples (e.g., calculation errors, image recognition mistakes).
- **AMATH Dataset:** Comprises 20,008 metadata samples, generating 124,000 supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data points from various multimodal reasoning sources (CLEVR, Geometry3K, MAVIS, TabMWP, GeomVerse, MathV360K, GeoQA+, IconQA).

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

To imbue MLLMs with atomic step-wise reasoning abilities, CoTs from the AMATH metadata are dissected into atomic steps. These steps are then incrementally incorporated into historical reasoning sequences using serialized masking to create multiple training samples (AMATH-SFT). This process ensures that the models learn to generate individual inference steps effectively.

#### 3.4. Atomic Capability Evaluation

This novel evaluation strategy provides a fine-grained analysis of MLLM reasoning abilities.

- **Canonical Set of Capabilities:** GPT-4o's reasoning behaviors on the AMATH dataset are clustered (using Kimi-1.5) into 16 distinct categories (e.g., Mathematical Understanding, Operations, Interpretation, Verification). Each cluster represents a specific cognitive ability.
- **Step Utilization Rate:** For a given set of historical reasoning steps, the model generates multiple rollouts. The "utilization rate" for a specific capability is defined as the average probability of reaching a correct answer by continuing to reason based on historical steps that exemplify that capability. This quantifies how effectively the model leverages individual atomic abilities.

By integrating these four components, AtomThink creates a powerful framework that not only enhances MLLM reasoning performance but also offers a detailed understanding of the underlying cognitive processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across a wide range of benchmarks demonstrate the significant efficacy of the AtomThink framework.

#### 4.1. Mathematical Reasoning Performance

AtomThink consistently improves performance on mathematical reasoning tasks:
- **Overall Gains:** Achieves an average accuracy improvement of over 10% (specifically 16.6% with PRM) across MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, and WeMath benchmarks, surpassing baseline MLLMs.
- **State-of-the-Art:** AtomThink-LlamaV, an 11B parameter model, outperforms GPT-4V by 8.5% on MathVista and by 3.2% on WeMath.
- **Sub-problem Emphasis:** Particularly strong gains on WeMath (up to 38% improvement with PRM), indicating AtomThink's effectiveness in decomposing and solving multi-concept problems through single-step reasoning.

#### 4.2. Cross-Domain Generalization

Despite being fine-tuned primarily on mathematical reasoning data, AtomThink-LlamaV exhibits impressive generalization capabilities to other domains:
- **General Tasks:** Achieves significant gains on ChartQA (+25.3%), TextVQA (+12.2%), and DocVQA (+6.4%).
- **Scientific Tasks:** Shows strong improvements on ScienceQA (+20.2%) and AI2D (+11.0%).
- **Mechanism:** This generalization is attributed to the high-quality data fine-tuning and the SCoT mechanism, which forces the model to revisit visual tokens multiple times, potentially reducing recognition bias inherent in single-pass inputs.

#### 4.3. Further Improvement with Process Reward Model (PRM)

Integrating PRM for reasoning path search generally yields additional gains, though with some caveats:
- **Performance Boost:** AtomThink-LlamaV with PRM shows further improvements on MathVista (+1.3%) and ChartQA (increasing gains from 18.7% to 25.3%).
- **Sensitivity:** PRM benefits are not uniform; some tasks like HLE (Humanity's Last Exam) and certain scientific benchmarks show slight performance degradation, possibly due to excessive or ineffective supervision for extremely challenging problems, or reliance on domain-specific knowledge not present in the PRM's training data.

#### 4.4. Scaling Reasoning According to Difficulty

AtomThink demonstrates an adaptive "slow thinking" behavior:
- **Dynamic Length:** The model generates longer reasoning chains (more tokens) and a greater number of atomic steps for more challenging problems (e.g., average tokens increase from 184.5 to 270.5 across benchmarks of increasing difficulty).
- **Reduced Performance Decline:** AtomThink models maintain more stable accuracy across different difficulty levels on MathVision, exhibiting a narrower performance decline margin compared to baselines.

#### 4.5. Autonomous Generation of Diverse Structures

- **Diverse Reasoning Patterns:** Unlike rigid structured CoT, AtomThink's SCoT generates a more diverse range of reasoning structures, categorized into 16 types (e.g., Decision Making, Image Description, Calculation, Data Extraction being predominant).
- **Emergent Behaviors:** The model spontaneously exhibits self-verification behaviors like Fact Verification and Hypothesis Testing in its reasoning process.

#### 4.6. Data Utilization and Reasoning Efficiency

AtomThink significantly improves resource efficiency:
- **Data Utilization:** Achieves 5 times higher data utilization compared to LLaVA-CoT (using only 20k VQA samples versus 100k) while maintaining superior performance.
- **Inference Efficiency:** Reduces output tokens by 87.8% and inference time by 85.3% per sample in SCoT mode, making it highly suitable for resource-constrained deployments. Even with PRM-guided search, reductions of 44.4% in tokens and 33.4% in latency are achieved.

#### 4.7. Scaling Law in Data and Test-time

- **Data Scaling:** Increasing the amount of AMATH-SFT data steadily improves model accuracy.
- **Test-time Scaling:** Increasing the number of candidates in a step-wise Best-of-N search strategy linearly increases reasoning time and significantly improves accuracy (e.g., from 13.9% to 23.3% by increasing candidates to 3), demonstrating that more deliberate "slow thinking" at inference time yields better results.

#### 4.8. Cross-Model Applicability and Ablation Studies

- **Versatility:** AtomThink consistently delivers significant performance gains across various base models (LLaVA-Llama3-8B and EMOVA-8B), validating its general applicability. It also outperforms the `g1` inference strategy on smaller models, indicating its robust enhancement.
- **Component Importance:** Ablation studies confirm that the AMATH-SFT dataset and the SCoT inference paradigm are crucial for performance, with PRM providing further benefits by refining reasoning paths.

#### 4.9. Analysis of Capabilities and PRM Focus

- **Capability Utilization:** AtomThink improves the utilization rate across nearly all atomic capabilities, with pronounced gains in Logical Deduction and Image Description. However, spatial reasoning saw limited benefit, and initial reasoning steps showed higher error rates.
- **PRM Bias:** Even for vision-dominant tasks, a language-based PRM performed comparably to or slightly better than a multimodal PRM, suggesting that current PRMs predominantly rely on textual information for supervisory signals.
- **Case Studies:** Attention maps show AtomThink-LLaVA better focuses on relevant visual features (text regions, geometric elements) compared to baselines. Error correction examples demonstrate SCoT's ability to resolve theorem misapplication, erroneous assumptions, and visual misinterpretation by enforcing explicit, grounded reasoning steps.

In summary, AtomThink significantly advances multimodal reasoning by introducing an adaptive, efficient, and robust "slow thinking" paradigm, demonstrating substantial performance gains, strong generalization, and superior resource efficiency across a diverse range of tasks and models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AtomThink framework represents a substantial advancement in multimodal reasoning, addressing critical limitations of current MLLMs and offering a clear path towards more intelligent and efficient AI systems.

### 6. Significance

1. **Adaptive "Slow Thinking":** The core contribution is the introduction of Self-structured Chain-of-Thought (SCoT), which enables MLLMs to adaptively adjust their reasoning depth and structure based on problem complexity. This paradigm effectively bridges the gap between rigid structured CoT and inefficient unstructured CoT, moving closer to human-like cognitive flexibility.
2. **Comprehensive Framework:** AtomThink provides a full-pipeline solution, encompassing novel data engineering for high-quality, fine-grained reasoning paths (AMATH dataset), an effective supervised fine-tuning strategy, advanced policy-guided inference, and a unique atomic capability evaluation metric. This holistic approach makes the findings robust and applicable.
3. **Enhanced Efficiency:** By generating dynamic and concise reasoning steps for simpler problems and detailed multi-step reasoning for complex ones, AtomThink significantly improves data utilization (5x over SOTA) and inference efficiency (85.3% reduction in inference time). This is crucial for deploying MLLMs in real-world, resource-constrained environments.
4. **Strong Generalization:** The framework demonstrates impressive cross-domain generalization, achieving substantial performance gains on scientific and general visual question answering tasks, even when trained primarily on mathematical data. This suggests that teaching models robust, atomic reasoning skills can transfer broadly.
5. **Granular Analysis of Reasoning:** The atomic capability evaluation metric offers unprecedented fine-grained insights into which specific reasoning abilities (e.g., logical deduction, image description) MLLMs leverage and how effectively. This diagnostic tool is invaluable for understanding and further improving model cognition.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **More Capable MLLMs:** AtomThink paves the way for developing MLLMs that can tackle highly complex, multi-step reasoning problems in scientific research, engineering, and education with greater accuracy and reliability. This could accelerate discoveries and automate tasks requiring deep understanding.
2. **Resource-Efficient AI:** The demonstrated improvements in inference efficiency and data utilization will make advanced MLLMs more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications, including edge devices or scenarios with strict latency requirements.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** The SCoT paradigm and the AMATH dataset will serve as valuable resources for researchers exploring adaptive reasoning, process supervision, and the development of more generalizable slow-thinking models. The atomic capability evaluation can guide future model architectures and training strategies.
4. **Improved Explainability and Trust:** By generating explicit, atomic reasoning steps, AtomThink inherently provides a more interpretable process. This enhanced explainability can foster greater trust in AI systems, particularly in critical applications where understanding "why" an AI makes a decision is as important as the decision itself.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as identified by the authors):**

The authors acknowledge several limitations that also highlight avenues for future research:
- **Dataset Quality:** Despite rigorous filtering, AMATH still faces challenges like redundant expressions and imprecise atomic step granularity due to the high cost of human annotation and limitations of current data generation models. Future work should focus on improving visual reasoning capabilities of generative models for better data synthesis and success rates.
- **Data Engine Bottleneck:** Relying primarily on GPT-4o for data generation creates a performance bottleneck and potential data overlap issues with pre-trained models. Exploring diverse open-source models as data engines could mitigate this.
- **Training Complexity:** The current SFT strategy is data-intensive. Transitioning to more complex training methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which currently presents parameter tuning and computational resource challenges, could further unlock performance and generalization bounds.
- **PRM Limitations:** Current PRMs primarily rely on textual information for supervision, even in multimodal contexts. Future research needs to investigate how to effectively leverage multimodal features to correct the reasoning process in PRMs.
- **Error Accumulation:** Initial steps in the reasoning chain exhibit higher error rates, indicating a need for strategies to improve the quality of early reasoning stages.

In conclusion, AtomThink makes a significant contribution to the field of multimodal AI by introducing an innovative framework for adaptive "slow thinking." Its advancements in efficiency, generalization, and diagnostic capabilities provide a strong foundation for the next generation of intelligent MLLMs, while its identified limitations open exciting new research directions.
