# X-Fusion: Introducing New Modality to Frozen Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-tower architecture enables integration of visual capabilities into frozen Large Language Models by introducing separate modality-specific weights, achieving strong performance on both image-to-text and text-to-image tasks while preserving the LLM's original language capabilities through parameter isolation.

## Problem

- Existing approaches to adding visual capabilities to LLMs either require expensive training from scratch or risk degrading language abilities through fine-tuning
- Cross-modal reasoning and error accumulation challenges in current hybrid approaches combining LLMs with image generation models
- Related dual-tower approaches like LMFusion rely on a less flexible joint attention mechanism, limiting extensibility to additional modalities beyond vision and language

## Method

- **X-Fusion** ==dual-tower architecture== with frozen LLM "text tower" and trainable "vision tower" with ==modality-specific weights==
- ==Feature alignment== at intermediate processing levels between towers while maintaining ==parameter isolation==
- Optional ==X-Fuse operation== to combine text features from both towers

## Results

- Outperforms alternative architectures on both image-to-text and text-to-image tasks while preserving language capabilities
- Successfully handles multiple downstream tasks including visual QA and image editing without task-specific weights
- Demonstrates practical efficiency advantages over training from scratch or fine-tuning approaches

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating understanding-focused data (image-to-text) improves generation quality (text-to-image)
> - Reducing image data noise in image-to-text samples enhances overall performance
> - Feature alignment with pre-trained vision features accelerates convergence for smaller models but has minimal impact on larger ones

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work directly addresses the rapidly evolving field of multimodal learning, specifically the challenge of integrating visual understanding and generation capabilities into Large Language Models (LLMs). The paper acknowledges the significant progress made by LLMs in language processing and generation while highlighting the need for models that can seamlessly handle multiple modalities, such as vision and language.

The paper positions itself within the existing research landscape by referencing several key approaches:

- **Training unified vision-language models from scratch:** This approach, exemplified by models like Chameleon, is computationally expensive and requires vast datasets.
- **Adapting LLMs using "visual instruction tuning":** Models like LLaVA fine-tune the LLM to align text and vision features. However, fine-tuning can degrade the original language capabilities of the LLM.
- **Leveraging pre-trained image generation models (e.g., Stable Diffusion):** Models like DreamLLM combine LLMs with pre-trained image generation models. This approach can suffer from limited cross-modal reasoning and error accumulation.
- **Hybrid training with next-token prediction and diffusion loss:** Transfusion is a recent approach that combines next-token prediction loss for language with diffusion loss for images to achieve state-of-the-art results.

X-Fusion distinguishes itself from these approaches by proposing a novel "dual-tower" architecture that keeps the LLM's parameters frozen, preserving its language capabilities, while introducing separate modality-specific weights to process visual information. This approach offers a practical and efficient way to build unified multimodal models, avoiding the computational cost of training from scratch and the degradation of language abilities caused by fine-tuning. Additionally, it is closely related to other works like LMFusion, which share similar high-level approaches, but employ a different, less flexible joint attention mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind X-Fusion are:

- **To extend pre-trained LLMs for multimodal tasks, specifically integrating visual understanding and generation capabilities.**
- **To retain the original language abilities of the pre-trained LLM while adding new modalities.** This is a key differentiator from methods that fine-tune the LLM, which can lead to catastrophic forgetting or degradation of language performance.
- **To develop a more efficient and scalable approach for unified multimodal model training compared to training from scratch.**
- **To create a flexible architecture that can be easily extended to additional modalities (e.g., audio).**
- **To investigate the impact of training data composition and noise levels on the performance of unified multimodal models.**

The motivation stems from the limitations of existing approaches, including the high computational costs of training from scratch and the degradation of language abilities during fine-tuning. The authors aim to address these limitations by leveraging the power of pre-trained LLMs while introducing new modalities in a way that preserves the LLM's existing knowledge and skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of X-Fusion involves a novel architecture design:

- **Dual-Tower Architecture:** The model consists of two towers: a "text tower" that is the frozen LLM and a "vision tower" with trainable weights. This design allows the model to process both image and text inputs while preserving the language capabilities of the pre-trained LLM.
- **Modality-Specific Weights:** The vision tower has separate vision weights in each layer to process visual information. This allows for the integration of vision-specific information without modifying the LLM's parameters.
- **Feature Alignment at the Intermediate Processing Level:** The text and vision features are aligned not only at the input or output level but also at the intermediate processing level. This allows for more effective cross-modal reasoning.
- **X-Fuse (Optional):** An operation is introduced to fuse text features from both towers to further improve performance.

The methodology also includes a comprehensive set of ablation studies from a data-centric perspective:

- **Effect of Noise Amount:** The authors systematically vary the maximum noise level applied to images in image-to-text samples to investigate the impact on performance.
- **Effect of Data Ratio:** The authors vary the ratio of image-to-text and text-to-image data in the training set to investigate the synergy between visual understanding and generation.
- **Pre-trained Representations for Vision Feature Regularization:** The authors explore whether aligning vision features with a pre-trained encoder (e.g., CLIP) can improve performance.

The authors use LLaMA-3 family models as the pre-trained LLMs and follow the flow matching scheduler from Stable Diffusion 3. The models are trained using AdamW optimizer with a linear warm-up scheduler and DeepSpeed Stage 2 distributed training on H100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The key findings and results of the paper are:

- **The dual-tower architecture consistently outperforms alternative architectures on both image-to-text and text-to-image tasks while maintaining the original language capabilities.**
- **Incorporating understanding-focused data (image-to-text) improves generation quality (text-to-image).**
- **Reducing image data noise in image-to-text samples enhances overall performance for both image understanding and generation.** Training with clean images for image-to-text improves feature representation for understanding.
- **Feature alignment with pre-trained vision features (e.g., CLIP) accelerates convergence for smaller models but has minimal impact on larger ones.**
- **X-Fuse layer could accelerate the convergence when initializing from a pretrained diffusion model.**
- **The model can be fine-tuned to perform multiple downstream tasks, including visual question answering, image editing, localization, and in/out-painting, without the need for task-specific weights.**

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of X-Fusion are:

- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** X-Fusion offers a more efficient and scalable approach for building unified multimodal models compared to training from scratch or fine-tuning, which makes it an efficient method for developing multimodal AI systems.
- **Preservation of Language Abilities:** By freezing the LLM's parameters, X-Fusion preserves the original language capabilities of the pre-trained model, which avoids catastrophic forgetting.
- **Flexibility and Extensibility:** The dual-tower architecture is flexible and can be easily extended to additional modalities (e.g., audio).
- **Insights into Data-Centric Training:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the impact of training data composition and noise levels on the performance of unified multimodal models.
- **Potential Applications:** X-Fusion has potential applications in various fields, including:
 - **Assistive AI:** Creating AI assistants that can understand and respond to both visual and textual information, which can improve accessibility and user experience.
 - **Creative Content Generation:** Developing AI tools that can generate images and captions based on user input, which may have applications in art, design, and marketing.
 - **Vision-Language Understanding:** Enabling AI systems to better understand and reason about the world by combining visual and textual information, which may have applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and medical imaging.

In conclusion, X-Fusion presents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal learning by offering a practical, efficient, and scalable approach for integrating visual capabilities into LLMs while preserving their existing knowledge and skills. The findings of this research have the potential to contribute to the development of more powerful and versatile AI systems that can seamlessly handle multiple modalities.
