# GPT4Motion: Scripting Physical Motions in Text-to-Video Generation via Blender-Oriented GPT Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> GPT4Motion, a training-free framework for text-to-video generation, effectively produces videos with physically accurate motions. The approach leverages GPT-4's planning capabilities to script Blender physics simulations, which then guide a Stable Diffusion XL model for video synthesis, resulting in superior physical realism and motion consistency compared to baseline models.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-video (T2V) generation methods often struggle to produce physically accurate and temporally coherent motions.
- Training-based T2V models incur high computational costs and require extensive datasets, while training-free methods suffer from motion incoherence and entity inconsistency.
- Creating physics-based 3D animations traditionally demands significant professional expertise and manual effort, limiting accessibility.

## Method

- GPT-4 translates natural language prompts into ==Blender== Python scripts, utilizing pre-collected 3D models and encapsulated Blender functions to simulate physical motions.
- Blender generates sequences of edge and depth maps from the simulations, which serve as physical conditions for subsequent video synthesis.
- A dual **ControlNet** approach guides Stable Diffusion XL (**SDXL**) with these maps, enhanced by a modified cross-frame ==attention mechanism== and consistent initial noise to ensure both physical accuracy and temporal consistency across frames.

## Results

- The framework successfully generated videos demonstrating physically accurate motions for rigid objects, cloth, and liquids, with precise control over physical properties like viscosity or wind strength.
- GPT4Motion consistently outperformed four baseline models (AnimateDiff, ModelScope, Text2Video-Zero, DirecT2V) in terms of physical realism, motion consistency, and alignment with user prompts.
- Quantitative evaluations showed superior performance, achieving highest scores in Motion Smoothness (0.993), CLIP score (0.260), and Temporal Flickering (0.990), supported by a user study where it received 100% preference for physical accuracy and minimal flickering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively act as planners to generate executable scripts for complex 3D physics simulations, simplifying the creation of realistic motion content.
> - The integration of a physics engine (Blender) provides robust, physically accurate motion constraints (via edge and depth maps) that significantly improve the realism of generated videos.
> - Combining dual ControlNet guidance with cross-frame attention in a diffusion model is crucial for maintaining both physical fidelity and temporal coherence, reducing flickering in synthesized video sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) has seen significant advancements, particularly in text-to-image (T2I) synthesis using diffusion models. Building on this success, researchers have extended these techniques to text-to-video (T2V) generation.

Early T2V approaches primarily involved training diffusion models directly in pixel or latent spaces, often requiring extensive video datasets and incurring substantial computational costs. To address this, some methods have focused on adapting existing T2I models for video, such as fine-tuning a pretrained T2I model for video editing, though this still typically necessitates an optimization process for each video generation.

More recently, training-free T2V approaches have emerged to mitigate computational burdens. While these methods reduce resource requirements, they frequently struggle with motion incoherence and entity inconsistency, especially when a single user prompt guides the generation of all frames. This limitation stems from the models' constrained understanding of temporal dynamics from abstract descriptions.

To enhance temporal coherence, some studies have incorporated large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 to generate detailed frame-by-frame descriptions or explicit spatiotemporal layouts from a single user prompt. While these LLM-assisted methods improve video quality over single-prompt approaches, maintaining precise motion coherence, particularly during significant motion shifts, remains challenging.

This paper introduces GPT4Motion, which positions itself within the landscape of training-free, LLM-assisted T2V generation. It differentiates itself by integrating the planning capabilities of LLMs with the physics simulation power of a 3D creation suite (Blender) and the image generation strength of T2I diffusion models. Previous uses of Blender in deep learning have primarily focused on synthetic data generation for model training or procedural 3D modeling driven by LLMs. However, its application to T2V generation for physically accurate motion has not been extensively explored. GPT4Motion leverages this novel combination to address the persistent issues of motion incoherence and physical inaccuracy in T2V synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for generating physically accurate videos from user textual prompts, particularly for scenarios involving basic physical motions.

The motivation behind GPT4Motion stems from several limitations observed in existing text-to-video (T2V) generation techniques:

1. **Computational Cost:** Many training-based T2V diffusion models, while producing promising results, require extensive datasets for training, leading to high computational expenses. Even methods that adapt existing text-to-image (T2I) models for video often involve per-video optimization, which can be resource-intensive. The authors are motivated to explore more cost-effective, training-free solutions.
2. **Motion Incoherence and Entity Inconsistency:** Training-free T2V methods, while reducing resource requirements, frequently encounter difficulties in achieving coherent motions and maintaining consistent entities across video frames. This challenge is amplified when models rely solely on a single user prompt, which may not adequately convey the complex temporal dynamics or physical interactions necessary for realistic video sequences.
3. **Lack of Physical Understanding:** Current T2V diffusion models often struggle to intrinsically capture complex physical knowledge, such as principles from Classical Mechanics or Fluid Mechanics. Consequently, they often fail to produce videos that adhere accurately to physical laws, resulting in unrealistic motion behaviors for objects like rigid bodies, cloth, or liquids.
4. **Complexity of 3D Content Creation:** Traditional video creation involving 3D software like Blender demands significant professional technical knowledge and intricate manual procedures (e.g., modeling, texturing, rigging, animation, lighting). The authors are motivated to simplify this process, making physics-based video generation more accessible.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes leveraging the distinct strengths of Large Language Models (LLMs), 3D physics engines, and T2I diffusion models. Specifically, GPT-4's semantic understanding and code generation capabilities are utilized for strategic planning; Blender's robust built-in physics engine is employed for accurate simulation of physical phenomena; and pretrained T2I diffusion models (like Stable Diffusion) are adapted for high-quality visual synthesis, constrained by the physical simulations. This integration aims to produce T2V content that is not only visually compelling and aligned with textual prompts but also physically coherent and temporally consistent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GPT4Motion proposes a training-free framework that integrates the planning capabilities of GPT-4, the physical simulation strengths of Blender, and the image generation abilities of Stable Diffusion. The methodology involves two main stages: Blender simulations driven by GPT-4 and subsequent video synthesis using these physical conditions.

#### 3.1. Blender Simulations via GPT-4

The initial step involves translating a user's textual prompt into a Blender Python script capable of simulating the described physical motion.

- **GPT-4's Role:** GPT-4 is employed to generate the Python script for Blender. While GPT-4 possesses knowledge of the Blender Python API, the authors identified challenges in direct script generation for complex 3D modeling tasks or due to rapid API updates and limited resources.
- **Addressing GPT-4 Limitations:**
 - **Leveraging External 3D Models:** Instead of having GPT-4 create complex 3D models from scratch, common 3D objects (e.g., a basketball) are pre-collected and automatically loaded into Blender via scripts. This bypasses the difficulty of GPT-4 generating intricate model geometry.
 - **Encapsulating Blender Functions:** To simplify script generation, the authors guide GPT-4 to generate code that calls pre-defined, reusable Python functions. These encapsulated functions cover common Blender operations for the target scenarios, including:
 - *Scene Initialization and Rendering:* Functions to clear the default scene, set up rendering parameters, and output image sequences (specifically edge maps and depth maps). Blender's Freestyle feature is used for edge maps, and the Z-channel is normalized for depth maps. The efficient "Workbench" rendering mode is selected, as edge and depth map rendering does not require ray tracing, reducing computational time.
 - *Object Creation and Import:* Functions to create basic geometric primitives (viewpoints, floors, cubes, spheres) and to import external 3D models.
 - *Physics Effect Functions:* Functions to assign physical properties to objects (e.g., rigid body, cloth, liquid) and configure physical effects (e.g., wind forces).
 - **Prompt Template Design:** A structured prompt template is designed for GPT-4. This template includes instructions, details about the encapsulated Blender functions, information about external assets, virtual world dimensions, and camera parameters. The user's textual prompt is integrated into this template, directing GPT-4 to generate the appropriate Blender Python script.
- **Output of Blender Simulation:** The generated Blender script drives Blender's physics engine to simulate the physical motion over time. This simulation produces sequences of edge maps and depth maps for each frame, serving as visual conditions for the subsequent video generation.

#### 3.2. Video Synthesis with Physical Conditions

The sequences of edge and depth maps generated by Blender are then used to guide a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model, Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL), to synthesize the final video frames.

- **Physics Motion Constraints:** To ensure the generated video frames accurately reflect the simulated physical motions and scene layouts, a dual ControlNet approach is employed.
 - A Canny-edge-based ControlNet is used to maintain accurate object edges and texture changes.
 - A depth-based ControlNet provides control over the 3D layout and depth information of the scene.
 - The intermediate results from both ControlNets are combined (added together) to provide comprehensive conditions to SDXL, ensuring both textural detail and 3D spatial consistency.
- **Temporal Consistency Constraint:** To address the challenge of temporal flickering and ensure consistency across frames, the standard self-attention (SA) mechanism within the U-Net of SDXL is modified into cross-frame attention (CFA).
 - For the *i*-th frame (F*i*), the query (Q*i*) is derived from F*i*.
 - However, the key (K*i,1*) and value (V*i,1*) are generated by concatenating the features of the *first frame* (F*1*) with the features of the *current frame* (F*i*), with a hyperparameter α controlling the influence of the current frame on the key representation.
 - This CFA mechanism enables each frame to attend not only to its own features but also to the features of the first frame, promoting content consistency throughout the video sequence.
 - Additionally, the same initial noise is used for SDXL to generate all frames, further enhancing temporal consistency.
- **Hyperparameter α:** The hyperparameter α is adjusted based on the physical scenario (e.g., 0.9 for rigid objects, 0.75 for cloth, 0.4 for liquid) to balance fidelity of moving objects and reduction of flickering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GPT4Motion framework was evaluated through experiments on various physical motion scenarios, quantitative comparisons with baselines, a user study, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Controlling Physical Properties

The method demonstrated its capability to generate videos that reflect controlled physical properties across three categories:

- **Rigid Objects (Basketball Drop and Collision):** Videos of basketballs free-falling and colliding with a floor showed realistic texture, spinning motion, and accurate bouncing behavior. The method also enabled precise control over the number of objects. For a basketball "thrown towards the camera," GPT-4 effectively calculated the necessary initial velocity based on physical principles (height difference, fall time, distance to camera), resulting in a visually convincing trajectory.
- **Cloth (Fluttering Flag and T-shirt):** The framework successfully simulated cloth motion under varying wind strengths. A flag exhibited complex ripple and wave patterns, while a T-shirt showed flapping and twisting motions with changes in creases and wrinkles, consistent with fabric physics.
- **Liquid (Water Pouring into a Mug):** Videos depicted water pouring into a mug, accurately illustrating different behaviors based on viscosity. Low viscosity water showed complex turbulence and merging, while higher viscosity water exhibited slower flow and increased adherence.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with Baselines

GPT4Motion was compared against four baselines: AnimateDiff, ModelScope, Text2Video-Zero, and DirecT2V, on scenarios involving free-falling basketballs, fluttering flags, and water pouring.

- **Overall Performance:** GPT4Motion consistently generated videos that aligned more accurately with the user prompts and exhibited superior physical realism and motion consistency compared to the baselines.
- **Specific Deficiencies in Baselines:**
 - Baselines often struggled with texture realism and motion consistency (e.g., basketball free fall).
 - Some baselines produced artifacts or distortions (e.g., fluttering flags), or failed to generate smooth, gradual transitions of physical phenomena.
 - For complex physical interactions like water pouring, baselines frequently failed to capture accurate physical effects or showed inconsistencies (e.g., a jittering mug).

#### 4.3. Quantitative Evaluation and User Study

- **Quantitative Metrics:** The method was quantitatively evaluated using Motion Smoothness (fluidity and physical accuracy), CLIP scores (text-video alignment), and Temporal Flickering. GPT4Motion achieved the highest scores across all three metrics (Motion: 0.993 ± 0.003, CLIP: 0.260 ± 0.022, Flickering: 0.990 ± 0.006), indicating superior performance in motion fluidity, prompt alignment, and reduced flickering compared to other models.
- **User Study:** A user study involving 30 participants confirmed these findings. GPT4Motion's results received 100% of the votes for physical accuracy, text-video alignment, and minimal video flickering, suggesting a strong human preference for the generated output.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation experiments were conducted to assess the contribution of key components:

- **Control Conditions:** Removing either the edge or depth condition individually led to degraded results.
 - Without the edge condition ("w/o edge"), the model failed to generate correct object edges.
 - Without the depth condition ("w/o depth"), additional artifacts appeared (e.g., extra cloth), and objects (flag) mixed with the background (cloud) due to a lack of depth-of-field information.
 - The joint use of both Canny-edge and depth ControlNets was essential for preserving object integrity and accurately handling scene layout.
- **First-Frame Attention (FFA):** Replacing cross-frame attention (CFA) with a setting where only the first frame (without attention to the current frame itself) contributed to keys and values ("FFA") resulted in incomplete object generation, where parts of the object merged with the background. This highlighted the importance of the current frame's features in maintaining object completeness.
- **Alpha (α) Values:** The hyperparameter α, which balances the influence of the first frame and the current frame in CFA, was found to be critical. Small α values led to distortions, while large α values caused flickering. An optimized α value (e.g., 0.75 for cloth) effectively maintained object fidelity while minimizing flickering.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GPT4Motion framework represents a notable advancement in the field of text-to-video (T2V) generation, demonstrating several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Physical Coherence and Realism:** The core contribution of GPT4Motion is its ability to generate videos with significantly improved physical accuracy, motion coherence, and entity consistency. By leveraging Blender's physics engine, it directly addresses a major limitation of existing T2V models, which often struggle to accurately represent complex physical interactions. This leads to more believable and realistic video content.
- **Novel Framework Integration:** The paper introduces a novel training-free framework that effectively combines three distinct technologies: the strategic planning and code generation capabilities of large language models (LLMs like GPT-4), the robust physics simulation power of a 3D creation suite (Blender), and the high-quality image generation of text-to-image diffusion models. This integration provides a new paradigm for T2V synthesis.
- **Increased Efficiency and Accessibility:** As a training-free framework, GPT4Motion avoids the high computational costs associated with training or extensive fine-tuning of large T2V models on vast video datasets. The use of Blender's efficient "Workbench" rendering mode for intermediate conditions (edge and depth maps) further contributes to overall efficiency. Furthermore, by translating natural language prompts into executable Blender scripts, the framework significantly simplifies the complex process of 3D animation and physics simulation, making high-quality, physics-driven video creation more accessible to users without specialized technical knowledge.
- **Demonstration of LLM Capabilities:** The work highlights the powerful planning and code generation capabilities of GPT-4 in a practical, physics-based application. It demonstrates that LLMs can effectively drive complex 3D software to create sophisticated visual content, extending their utility beyond text-based tasks into more intricate multimodal generation workflows. This suggests a broader potential for LLMs to act as intelligent "directors" for various computational design and simulation tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** GPT4Motion opens new avenues for research in T2V generation. Its success in handling basic physical motions provides a strong foundation for exploring more complex scenarios, potentially through decomposition of complex motions into a series of basic ones or by refining LLM instructions. It also inspires further investigation into mitigating residual flickering and expanding the range of controllable physical properties and materials. The integration of symbolic knowledge (physics) with generative models could lead to more robust and controllable AI systems for content creation.
