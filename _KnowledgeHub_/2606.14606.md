# Impedance MPC with Disturbance Estimation for Dexterous Hand Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A computationally efficient Impedance Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework with disturbance estimation was developed for dexterous robotic hands, enabling precise trajectory tracking while maintaining compliant contact. The system achieved a 0.1 mrad steady-state error and 6.6 mrad peak deflection under contact at a 500 Hz update rate, demonstrating sub-15 mrad precision and adaptability across diverse tendon-driven actuation types.

## Problem

- Achieving both high-precision trajectory tracking and safe, compliant contact forces in dexterous robotic hands due to a fundamental control trade-off.
- Existing impedance control methods suffer from steady-state errors or integral windup, while prior Impedance MPC formulations are too slow (10-30 Hz) or reactive for the precise, high-bandwidth control required for dexterous manipulation.
- Complexities arising from diverse tendon transmission types in dexterous hands, such as reflected inertia and nonlinearities, challenge the development of actuator-agnostic, high-performance control solutions.

## Method

- A two-layer, actuator-agnostic control architecture algebraically cancels tendon transmission dynamics, reducing the specific plant to a scalar double integrator, enabling high-frequency **==MPC==** operation.
- An integrating disturbance state, estimated via a steady-state Kalman filter, is appended to the error state within the MPC to achieve offset-free disturbance rejection.
- A receding-horizon Quadratic Program (QP) embeds physical and safety constraints (e.g., ISO/TS 15066 limits), utilizing precomputed components for computational efficiency and soft-constraint relaxation for recursive feasibility.

## Results

- The 500 Hz Impedance MPC with Kalman achieved a **0.1** mrad steady-state error and **6.6** mrad contact-onset peak deflection, representing a **32x** reduction in steady-state error and **23x** reduction in peak deflection compared to classical impedance.
- Successfully enabled a single finger to reach and hold sequential waypoints under contact within a 15 mrad precision window, a task where 100 Hz control variants failed to manage contact transients.
- Scaled to a 16-DOF LEAP Hand in simulation, the framework demonstrated stable grasping and disturbance rejection, incorporating robust solutions for inertia mismatch (gain scheduling), off-diagonal coupling (block estimation), and self-contact (contact-aware integration).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A high update rate (500 Hz) is critical for achieving sub-15 mrad precision, effectively managing both contact-onset and release transients in low-inertia, high-stiffness dexterous robot systems.
> - The combination of predictive MPC with an integrating Kalman disturbance estimator provides truly offset-free performance under contact, proactively canceling disturbances across the prediction horizon.
> - Actuator-agnostic feedforward cancellation of diverse tendon transmission dynamics, reducing the system to a constant-Ad double integrator, allows for a generalized and computationally efficient control core for various robot hand designs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of dexterous robotic manipulation requires control systems capable of simultaneously achieving precise trajectory tracking and maintaining safe, compliant contact with objects or human co-workers. This presents a tension for many control methodologies, as optimizing for one objective often compromises the other.

Classical impedance control, as proposed by Hogan, aims to shape the mechanical behavior at a joint to balance accuracy and safety. However, it often faces limitations in simultaneously optimizing stiffness and disturbance rejection. For instance, a fixed-gain classical impedance controller exhibits a steady-state error proportional to external contact force and inverse to stiffness. Integral action (PI impedance) can address steady-state error but may suffer from windup during sequential contact events, compromising predictability. Admittance control, conversely, prioritizes yielding to contact, which is suitable for physical human–robot interaction (pHRI) or rehabilitation but less so for autonomous grasping where contact is often considered a disturbance to be rejected.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers a framework to address both predictive constraint enforcement and offset-free disturbance rejection through an augmented estimator state. However, existing impedance MPC formulations often face computational bottlenecks. Some approaches optimize impedance parameters as decision variables, leading to update rates limited to 10–30 Hz, which is insufficient for the high-bandwidth control required for dexterous hands. Other formulations compensate estimated disturbances reactively only at the current control step, failing to suppress contact-release transients effectively or achieve the sub-15 mrad precision required for dexterous finger positioning under contact loads.

This work builds upon an established constant-A$_{d}$ offset-free Impedance MPC framework previously developed for redundant-manipulator pHRI, which provides theoretical guarantees for stability, recursive feasibility, and input-to-state stability (ISS). The current paper adapts and extends this architecture for the specific challenges of dexterous robotic hands, addressing the need for actuator-agnostic control, higher update rates, and robust disturbance rejection in the presence of various tendon transmission types and physical constraints. It positions itself as a solution that can achieve both high precision and safe, predictable contact forces, overcoming the limitations of prior impedance control and MPC approaches in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an actuator-agnostic Impedance Model Predictive Control (Impedance MPC) framework that enables dexterous robotic fingers to achieve precise trajectory tracking while maintaining safe and compliant contact, even under significant external disturbances. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges inherent in dexterous hand control:

1. **Resolving the Accuracy-Safety Trade-off:** Dexterous hands must accurately track desired trajectories for manipulation tasks, but also operate safely with grasped objects and human co-workers, which requires predictable and bounded contact forces (e.g., adhering to ISO/TS 15066 limits). These two requirements are in tension for fixed-gain controllers, where increased stiffness for accuracy can lead to unsafe forces, and compliance for safety can compromise precision. The motivation is to resolve this trade-off by dynamically managing contact forces as hard constraints while maintaining tracking performance.
2. **Addressing Tendon Transmission Complexities:** Dexterous hands frequently use tendon transmissions (e.g., hydraulic, cable, pneumatic, twisted-string, series-elastic) that introduce reflected inertia, damping, and platform-specific nonlinearities. These complexities make high-bandwidth, precise finger control challenging. The framework aims to be actuator-agnostic, providing a general solution adaptable to diverse transmission types without requiring a complete re-derivation of the control system.
3. **Achieving Offset-Free Disturbance Rejection at High Rates:** Classical impedance control exhibits steady-state errors under constant contact loads, while PI impedance can suffer from integral windup. Existing MPC formulations either operate at low update rates (10-30 Hz) due to complex optimization problems or compensate disturbances only reactively. The motivation is to develop an MPC that achieves offset-free disturbance rejection by integrating a predictive estimator state, capable of operating at high frequencies (e.g., 500 Hz) to effectively suppress both contact-onset and release transients below tight precision windows (e.g., 15 mrad).
4. **Embedding Physical Constraints:** Real-world robotic systems are subject to physical limits, such as motor actuation bounds, cavitation and seal pressure limits in hydraulics, and safety standards like ISO/TS 15066. These limits must be enforced robustly and predictably. The motivation is to embed these physical limits directly into the MPC optimization as hard constraints, rather than relying on post-hoc saturation, while ensuring recursive feasibility even under rigid impacts.
5. **Scalability to Multi-DOF Hands:** A practical control framework for dexterous hands must be scalable to multi-degree-of-freedom (DOF) systems. The motivation includes demonstrating the architecture's ability to control a complex 16-DOF hand, while also identifying and addressing specific challenges that arise in multi-finger, coupled, and self-contacting scenarios for robust performance.

The paper identifies specific contributions to achieve these objectives:
- A platform reduction and substitution map that algebraically cancels transmission dynamics, reducing diverse tendon architectures to a constant-A$_d$ double integrator, enabling high-frequency (500 Hz) operation with precomputed QP cost inverse.
- An optional contact channel that, if instrumented (e.g., slave piston pressure for hydraulics), can shorten contact-onset transients, though it is not essential for the offset-free guarantee.
- The embedding of platform-specific physical limits (e.g., hydraulic cavitation, seal pressure, ISO/TS 15066 contact force) as soft-relaxed constraints within the QP, ensuring recursive feasibility.
- Benchmarking and independent verification of realized impedance, demonstrating the performance gains over classical impedance and identifying the minimum update rate for meeting precision criteria.
- An extension to a 16-DOF LEAP Hand simulation, including diagnosis and solutions for issues arising in full-hand Kalman estimation (inertia mismatch, off-diagonal coupling, self-contact).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed control framework, Impedance MPC with Disturbance Estimation, adopts a two-layer architecture built upon the constant-A$_{d}$ offset-free Impedance MPC framework established in prior work. This architecture is designed to be actuator-agnostic, with a hydraulically actuated finger serving as the primary example platform.

**A. Two-Layer Architecture**

1. **Layer 1 – Feedforward Reduction:**
 The first layer employs an algebraic feedforward term to cancel the known dynamics of the specific tendon transmission. This process reduces any tendon-driven platform to a scalar double integrator as the residual plant. For the hydraulic example, the motor–piston–fluid–finger transmission is modeled as a lumped second-order plant. By inverting the effective joint inertia ($I_{\text{eff_f}}$) and damping ($b_{\text{eff_f}}$), which are derived from the system's physical parameters (e.g., piston areas, masses, damping coefficients, and finger Jacobian), a feedforward force ($F_{\text{ff}}$) is computed. This feedforward term uses the reference acceleration ($\ddot{\theta}_d$) for the inertia part and the actual velocity ($\dot{\theta}_f$) for the damping part to achieve exact cancellation of physical damping. The parameters ($I_{\text{eff_f}}$, $b_{\text{eff_f}}$) are identified from a step response. The paper provides a table summarizing substitutions for other tendon architectures (cable, pneumatic, twisted-string, series-elastic), indicating how their specific dynamics are similarly reduced.
 After feedforward, the residual error dynamics become a double integrator: $\ddot{e} = -\Gamma_e F_{\text{mpc}} + d(t)$, where $e = \theta_d - \theta_f$ is the error, $F_{\text{mpc}}$ is the corrective MPC force, $\Gamma_e = 1/I_{\text{eff_f}}$ is the effective compliance, and $d(t)$ lumps contact torque and unmodeled terms.

2. **Layer 2 – Impedance MPC with Disturbance Estimation:**
 The second layer consists of an Impedance MPC that operates on the residual error dynamics. The state $x_e = [e, \dot{e}]^\top$ is discretized into $x_e(k+1) = A_d x_e(k) + B_d F_{\text{mpc}}(k) + E_c d(k)$. A key feature is the constancy of the discrete-time state matrix $A_d$, which is a structural property inherited from the base framework due to the double integrator form. This allows prediction matrices and the Quadratic Program (QP) cost inverse $H^{-1}$ to be precomputed offline. For configuration-varying mechanisms, only $B_d(\theta_f)$ (and thus $\Gamma_e$, $H^{-1}$, and Kalman input blocks) is rescheduled online, maintaining computational efficiency.

**B. Offset-Free Disturbance Estimation**

An integrating disturbance state $\hat{d}$ is appended to the error state. This augmented state $[x_e, \hat{d}]^\top$ is estimated using a steady-state Kalman filter, primarily driven by joint encoder measurements. The disturbance state follows a random-walk model, $\hat{d}(k+1) = \hat{d}(k) + w_k$, where $w_k$ is zero-mean white process noise, allowing $\hat{d}$ to track constant and slowly varying contact loads. The Kalman filter's detectability condition for the augmented pair ensures zero steady-state error under constant disturbances. The convergence rate of the estimator, which influences the contact-onset peak, is tuned by the ratio of process noise variance to measurement noise covariance ($Q_{\text{proc,d}}/R_{\text{obs}}$).
An optional third measurement from instrumented slave pressure ($P_2$) can provide a direct, low-latency estimate of the contact-form disturbance, potentially shortening estimator lag, but is not required for the offset-free guarantee and was not used in the reported simulation results.

**C. Receding-Horizon Quadratic Program (QP)**

The MPC computes a sequence of optimal control inputs $U$ by solving a receding-horizon QP. The cost function minimizes a quadratic form of predicted errors and control inputs, with stage costs and a terminal weight.
The QP minimizes: $\frac{1}{2} U^\top H U + f^\top U$.
Constraints (12) are enforced on the control input:
- **Motor bounds:** Actuation limits ($F_{\text{min}}$, $F_{\text{max}}$) for the combined feedforward and MPC forces.
- **ISO/TS 15066 contact force limit:** A hard constraint (140 N) on the total contact force.
- **Jerk limit:** A bound on the change in MPC force ($|\Delta F_{\text{mpc}}(k)| \le \Delta F_{\text{max}}$).
- **Hydraulic platform constraints (example):** Cavitation ($P_{\text{vap}}$) and seal pressure ($P_{\text{max}}$) limits for the slave piston. These pressure limits are mapped to linear inequalities on the command force, assuming quasi-static conditions (fluid resonance settling within the control interval).
Only the first control input $F_{\text{mpc}}(0)$ from the optimal sequence is applied. The precomputed $H^{-1}$ allows for a fast, direct solution when no constraints are active ($U^* = -H^{-1}f$). For constrained scenarios, solvers like OSQP or qpOASES are used, warm-started, given the small horizon (N=10).

**D. Platform Constraint Embedding with Recursive Feasibility**

Physical limits like pressure, motor force, and ISO/TS 15066 are embedded as linear (or linearized quasi-static) inequalities within the QP. To guarantee recursive feasibility, especially under rigid impacts where hard physical limits might render the QP infeasible, these constraints are relaxed to penalized soft constraints. This means a slack variable $s_k \ge 0$ is introduced, which is penalized in the cost function, allowing the QP to return a minimum-violation command rather than faulting. Torque and jerk limits, however, are kept as hard box constraints.

**E. Inheritance of Guarantees**

The paper explicitly states that it does not re-derive closed-loop guarantees but inherits them from the base framework. This inheritance is valid provided four architectural assumptions are preserved: bounded-error cancellation by the feedforward, stabilizable residual dynamics (the double integrator), positive-definite cost functions, and convex constraints (maintained by linear inequalities and soft-constraint relaxation). The paper argues that the hand embodiments satisfy these assumptions, making the guarantees (nominal asymptotic stability, recursive feasibility, ISS) directly transferable. The offset-free property is also inherited under any detectable, asymptotically unbiased estimator satisfying the specified rank condition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed simulation studies across single-finger and multi-finger (16-DOF LEAP Hand) scenarios to evaluate the performance of the proposed Impedance MPC with Disturbance Estimation.

**A. Disturbance Rejection Under Sinusoidal Trajectory (Single Finger)**

This study compared seven controllers (Table II) tracking a sinusoidal trajectory under cyclic 1.5 Nm step contact torque.
- **Baselines:** Classical impedance (D1) showed a steady-state (SS) error of 150.1 mrad, matching theoretical predictions ($\tau_{\text{ext}}/K_d$). Admittance control (D2) yielded 494.9 mrad SS error. PI impedance (D3) achieved 74.4 mrad SS error but converged slowly and suffered from windup effects.
- **No-Kalman Impedance MPC:** At 100 Hz (D4), it halved the SS error to 83.3 mrad compared to D1, demonstrating the benefit of predictive control even without a disturbance estimator. At 500 Hz (D6), the SS error significantly reduced to 4.7 mrad, an approximately 32x improvement over classical impedance. The realized first-move stiffness increased from approximately 18 Nm/rad at 100 Hz to 323 Nm/rad at 500 Hz, a finding independently verified through gain extraction, closed-loop pole analysis, and steady-state prediction. This indicated that for the given tuning, the update rate, not merely the cost weight, was the dominant factor in achievable stiffness.
- **Impedance MPC + Kalman:** Adding the Kalman estimator (D5 at 100 Hz, D7 at 500 Hz) drove the steady-state error to 0.1 mrad at both rates. This demonstrated the estimator's effectiveness in achieving offset-free performance by pre-canceling the disturbance across the prediction horizon.
- **Transient Response:** At 100 Hz, the contact-onset peak remained high (D5: 150.6 mrad) due to the estimator's convergence time. However, at 500 Hz (D7), the Kalman MPC contained the onset peak to 6.6 mrad, an approximately 23x improvement over classical impedance, and also suppressed the contact-release transient.
- **Overall Best Performance:** D7 (Impedance MPC + Kalman at 500 Hz) achieved the best performance across all metrics: 0.5 mrad RMS total error, 0.6 mrad RMS contact error, 6.6 mrad peak deflection, and 0.1 mrad steady-state error.

**B. Precision Reach-and-Hold Under Object Contact (Single Finger)**

This study evaluated controller performance in reaching and holding three sequential waypoints under varying contact loads, with a 15 mrad precision advance criterion.
- **100 Hz Limitations:** G1 (stiff impedance) failed all waypoints due to large SS errors (100-200 mrad). G4 (MPC 100 Hz no Kalman) also failed due to high onset peaks (295 mrad), indicating the 10 ms ZOH window was too long for rapid contact events on this low-inertia plant. G5 (MPC + Kalman 100 Hz) achieved 0 mrad SS error but failed on release transients (300 mrad overshoot) because the estimator's stored disturbance took 50-100 ms to decay, leading to overcompensation. PI impedance (G3) showed approach errors from integral wind-up.
- **500 Hz Success:** G6 (MPC 500 Hz no Kalman) passed all three waypoints, keeping deflections within the 15 mrad criterion (peak 12 mrad, SS 3-6 mrad). G7 (MPC + Kalman 500 Hz) also passed all waypoints, achieving the same 12 mrad peak deflection with exactly zero steady-state error.
- **Conclusion:** The 500 Hz update rate was found to be necessary and sufficient for this plant and tuning to manage both contact-onset and release transients within the desired precision window, where the 100 Hz variants failed.

**C. 16-DOF Extension: LEAP Hand MuJoCo Simulation**

The single-finger controller was scaled to a 16-DOF LEAP Hand in a MuJoCo simulation, running independent per-joint scalar QPs at 500 Hz with gravity compensation.
- **Naive Kalman Failure on Thumb:** The naive per-joint Kalman failed on the thumb, which has complex dynamics. Three reasons were identified and addressed:
 - **Inertia Mismatch:** The thumb-axle inertia varies 26x across its workspace. A fixed-inertia Kalman treated this mismatch as a persistent disturbance. The solution was **gain scheduling** of the inertia online, reducing RMS jitter by approximately 18x (from 57 mrad/s to 3 mrad/s).
 - **Off-Diagonal Coupling:** Per-joint estimators cannot model off-diagonal mass coupling. Under a 2.5 N fingertip push, the per-joint Kalman led to 2329 mrad thumb deviation. The fix was a **coupled block estimator** for the 4x4 thumb mass block, which reduced deviation to 236 mrad, eliminating divergence.
 - **Self-Contact:** During curled grasp poses, thumb self-contact forces are not in the dynamic model, causing the integrating $\hat{d}$ to wind up. **Contact-aware conditional integration** was implemented to suspend (leak) disturbance integration on contacting joints, reducing thumb deviation to 133 mrad, below the no-Kalman baseline (203 mrad).
- **Overall 16-DOF Performance:** The hand demonstrated stable grasping and disturbance rejection across four grasp types (Open/Close, Precision Pinch, Power, Hook). Under a 2.5 N downward force (slipping-load surrogate), the hand deflected by 205 mrad (Power) and 506 mrad (Hook) (16-joint deviation norm). Measured against the post-release equilibrium, the transient decayed below 15 mrad within 0.49 s (Power) and 0.71 s (Hook). Contacting joints settled to shifted contact equilibria, with bounded, non-divergent hold jitter that the contact-aware estimator suppressed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an Impedance MPC framework that makes significant contributions to the control of dexterous robotic hands, with implications for manipulation in unstructured environments and human-robot collaboration.

The primary significance lies in the **simultaneous achievement of high-precision trajectory tracking and robust, safety-compliant contact handling** at high update rates. By demonstrating 0.1 mrad steady-state error under significant contact loads while enforcing ISO/TS 15066 safety limits, the controller addresses a long-standing tension in robotic control. The ability to manage both contact-onset and release transients within a 15 mrad precision window at 500 Hz represents a notable advance over classical impedance control and prior MPC formulations.

The **actuator-agnostic nature** of the framework, achieved through a two-parameter feedforward reduction to a constant-A$_{d}$ double integrator, is impactful. This design allows the core MPC and Kalman filter structure to be applied across diverse tendon transmission technologies (hydraulic, cable, pneumatic, twisted-string, series-elastic) with minimal adaptation, thereby broadening its applicability to various hand designs without requiring extensive re-engineering of the control core. The hydraulic example, known for its control challenges due to high force density and complex dynamics, serves as a rigorous validation.

The **computational efficiency** gained from the constant-A$_{d}$ architecture, enabling precomputation of the QP cost inverse, allows the system to run at 500 Hz. This high update rate is critical for reacting quickly to dynamic contact events and achieving precise control in dexterous manipulation tasks, pushing the boundaries of real-time MPC implementation on embedded hardware.

The robust **offset-free disturbance rejection** provided by the encoder-only Kalman estimator, coupled with the predictive capabilities of MPC, ensures that the system maintains desired positions even under persistent external forces. The intelligent embedding of **platform-specific physical constraints** (e.g., cavitation, seal pressure, jerk limits) directly into the QP, combined with soft-constraint relaxation for recursive feasibility, enhances the safety and reliability of the controlled system, preventing unintended operation outside of physical bounds or system faults during impacts.

Finally, the **scalability and nuanced solutions for multi-DOF hands** demonstrated with the 16-DOF LEAP Hand simulation are crucial for practical deployment. The identification and resolution of specific challenges related to inertia mismatch (gain scheduling), off-diagonal coupling (block estimator), and self-contact (contact-aware integration) in a complex multi-finger system provide valuable insights and methods for developing robust controllers for highly articulated robotic hands. This suggests potential for improving the reliability and performance of robotic hands in complex grasping and manipulation tasks, potentially reducing the need for extensive task-specific tuning.

In sum, this work contributes a computationally efficient, robust, and versatile control framework that could enhance the capabilities of dexterous robotic hands, enabling them to perform complex manipulation tasks with greater precision, safety, and adaptability in challenging real-world scenarios.
