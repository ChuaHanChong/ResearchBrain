# CoIRL-AD: Collaborative-Competitive Imitation-Reinforcement Learning in Latent World Models for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called CoIRL-AD integrates imitation learning and reinforcement learning using a competitive dual-policy approach within a latent world model for offline end-to-end autonomous driving, enhancing generalization in challenging scenarios and reducing L2 error and collision rates on the nuScenes benchmark without an external simulator.

## Problem

- End-to-end autonomous driving models, primarily based on imitation learning (IL), often generalize poorly to out-of-distribution or "long-tail" scenarios due to fixed data distributions.
- Integrating reinforcement learning (RL) into autonomous driving is challenging, especially in offline settings with expert-dominated real-world datasets lacking diverse behavioral exploration.
- Existing IL-RL integration methods suffer from training instability, objective conflicts, and biased world model predictions when policies deviate from expert data.

## Method

- **CoIRL-AD** proposes a competitive dual-policy framework with distinct IL and RL actors, both utilizing a shared latent ==world model== to predict future states for offline training.
- The RL actor employs a step-aware group sampling mechanism for efficient exploration and a rule-based reward function (imitation and collision avoidance) for long-horizon advantage estimation.
- A competitive mechanism adaptively transfers knowledge between the IL and RL actors based on their reward scores, mitigating objective conflicts and stabilizing training by preventing policy divergence and value overestimation.

## Results

- CoIRL-AD achieved lower L2 errors and collision rates on the nuScenes benchmark across 1s, 2s, and 3s horizons, resulting in the lowest combined L2 7 Col metric compared to the LAW baseline.
- The framework demonstrated substantially improved cross-city generalization and enhanced performance in challenging long-tail scenarios, which are typically difficult for IL-only models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the competitive dual-policy learning and the latent world model were crucial for stabilizing RL training, suppressing value overestimation, and preventing policy divergence, leading to better overall robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned latent world model can effectively enable model-based reinforcement learning in an offline setting, providing imagined future rollouts for long-horizon reward estimation without an interactive simulator.
> - Decoupling IL and RL policies and introducing a competitive interaction mechanism significantly mitigates gradient conflicts and stabilizes RL training on expert-dominated datasets.
> - Backward planning (inverse causal mask) in the planning head, combined with competitive learning, enhances the RL actor's exploration capabilities and improves overall performance in autonomous driving.
