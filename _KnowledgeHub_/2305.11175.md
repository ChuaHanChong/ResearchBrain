# VisionLLM: Large Language Model is also an Open-Ended Decoder for Vision-Centric Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> VisionLLM introduces a unified framework that leverages Large Language Models as open-ended decoders for diverse vision-centric tasks, including both image-to-text generation and structured visual perception like object detection and instance segmentation. The approach treats images as a foreign language, aligning all vision tasks with flexible language instructions and achieving competitive performance on standard benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional computer vision models are limited by pre-defined task formats, lacking the open-ended instruction-following capabilities seen in Large Language Models (LLMs) for NLP.
- Existing vision-language models (VLMs) primarily focus on image-to-text generation (e.g., captioning, VQA) and often neglect visual perception tasks requiring structured spatial outputs (e.g., object detection, instance segmentation).
- Current API-based VLM applications lack true end-to-end integration, relying on external, often rigid, vision models that hinder fine-grained understanding and synergistic multimodal reasoning.

## Method

- **VisionLLM**, a unified generalist framework, is proposed that treats images as a ==foreign language== and aligns all vision-centric tasks with language tasks defined by flexible natural language instructions.
- A ==Language-Guided Image Tokenizer== converts images into high-level image tokens, injecting language features from prompts via ==cross-attention== to create ==language-aware visual representations==.
- An ==LLM-based Open-Ended Task Decoder==, built on ==Alpaca== and expanded with ==location== and ==semantics-agnostic classification tokens==, uses an ==output-format-as-query== mechanism (with ==LoRA== fine-tuning) to generate structured visual outputs efficiently.

## Results

- VisionLLM with InternImage-H achieved **60.2% mAP** on COCO object detection, **86.7% P@0.5** on RefCOCO visual grounding, and **114.2 CIDEr** on COCO image captioning, demonstrating competitive performance across diverse tasks within a single model.
- The model successfully adapts to fine-grained language instructions, allowing for object-level customization (e.g., detecting specific classes or open-vocabulary descriptions) and output format customization (e.g., varying the number of points for mask representation).
- Ablation studies confirmed that language-guided image tokenization, a sufficient number of discrete localization tokens (**+9.9 AP** for **1025 bins**), and LoRA for training convergence are vital for the model's performance and generalizability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can function as open-ended decoders for complex vision-centric tasks, extending beyond image-to-text generation to include structured visual perception.
> - Training with varied task descriptions, object categories, and output formats, combined with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), is crucial for enabling the LLM's open-ended and generalized capabilities.
> - A novel 'output-format-as-query' decoding mechanism efficiently enables LLMs to generate structured visual outputs (like bounding boxes and masks) by performing parallel decoding, improving upon standard causal decoding for such tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in VisionLLM is situated at the crucial intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and computer vision, aiming to bridge the gap between their respective paradigms.

**Evolution of AI Generalism:**
The field of AI is rapidly moving towards generalist models capable of handling diverse tasks. In Natural Language Processing (NLP), LLMs like GPT-3, ChatGPT, and LLaMA have revolutionized the landscape by demonstrating impressive zero-shot and few-shot capabilities, understanding complex instructions, and exhibiting reasoning abilities. Their success has largely been attributed to their ability to process tasks defined through flexible language instructions.

**Challenges in Computer Vision:**
In contrast, computer vision (CV) has traditionally operated under a different paradigm. Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), while powerful (e.g., ResNet, ViT, Mask R-CNN), are typically pre-trained and then fine-tuned for specific, *pre-defined* tasks (e.g., object detection, image captioning, VQA). Attempts to create "vision generalist models" (e.g., OFA, Flamingo, Uni-Perceiver) have made progress by unifying various tasks into a sequence-to-sequence format, but they still struggle with the *open-ended task customization* that characterizes LLMs. Their capabilities are often restricted by the fixed format of their pre-defined tasks.

**Limitations of Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs):**
Recent advancements have seen the integration of vision and language.
- **Visual Prompt Tuning** (e.g., Painter, SegGPT) offers some flexibility for pure vision tasks using visual masks, but their input format deviates significantly from language instructions, making it difficult to leverage the reasoning and world knowledge of LLMs directly.
- **Instruction-tuned VLMs** (e.g., Flamingo, BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA) connect visual encoders with LLMs and are trained to follow natural language instructions. However, these models have primarily focused on *image-to-text generation tasks* (like image captioning and Visual Question Answering, VQA), largely neglecting *visual perception tasks* such as object detection, instance segmentation, and pose estimation. They often treat visual inputs as contextual information for language generation rather than as direct input for generating structured visual outputs.
- **API-based VLM applications** (e.g., Visual ChatGPT, MM-REACT, HuggingGPT, InternGPT) integrate existing vision APIs with LLMs. While convenient for high-level decision-making and planning through language prompts, they are not truly end-to-end. They rely on external, often rigid, vision models and struggle to capture fine-grained visual details or perform complex visual reasoning within a single, cohesive system.

**VisionLLM's Contribution to the Landscape:**
VisionLLM steps into this landscape by directly addressing these limitations. It proposes a novel, *unified generalist framework* that aims to bring the *open-ended, instruction-following capabilities of LLMs* to a *broader spectrum of vision-centric tasks*, including both image-to-text (captioning, VQA) and, crucially, *visual perception tasks* (object detection, instance segmentation, visual grounding). By treating images as a "foreign language" and aligning all vision-centric tasks with language tasks defined by flexible instructions, VisionLLM seeks to create a truly end-to-end system. This positions VisionLLM as a pioneering effort to make LLMs not just language decoders, but also open-ended decoders for complex visual tasks, setting a new baseline for generalist vision and language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VisionLLM stems from the observed disparity between the revolutionary open-ended capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and the more rigid, pre-defined task formats prevalent in computer vision.

**Motivation:**
1. **Bridging the Modality Gap:** While LLMs excel at understanding and executing user-tailored tasks through natural language instructions, this impressive flexibility has not seamlessly extended to pure vision or vision-language tasks due to inherent differences in data modalities and task output formats.
2. **Overcoming Pre-defined Task Limitations in Vision:** Traditional Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) and even recent "vision generalist models" are typically constrained to a pre-defined set of tasks. Adapting them to diverse, novel scenarios often incurs significant marginal costs in fine-tuning or requires specific task formulations, preventing them from matching the open-ended adaptability of LLMs.
3. **Enhancing End-to-End Integration for VLMs:** Existing approaches that combine vision and language often rely on API-based systems, where LLMs orchestrate calls to separate, specialized vision models. This leads to a fragmented pipeline that lacks true end-to-end integration, potentially hindering efficiency, fine-grained understanding, and the synergistic leverage of both modalities' strengths within a single system.
4. **Extending Instruction-Tuning to Visual Perception:** While instruction-tuning has proven effective for multimodal models in image-to-text tasks (e.g., captioning, VQA), these models typically do not address complex visual perception tasks like object detection or instance segmentation, which require structured spatial outputs (e.g., bounding boxes, masks).

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Develop an LLM-based Framework for Open-Ended Vision Tasks:** The core objective is to design VisionLLM, a framework that leverages the reasoning and parsing capabilities of LLMs to enable open-ended task execution for a wide range of vision-centric tasks.
2. **Achieve Unified Vision-Language Modeling:** To treat images as a "foreign language" and align the definition and management of vision-centric tasks with the methodologies of language models, enabling a unified perspective and processing pipeline.
3. **Enable Flexible Task Customization:** To allow for various levels of task customization using natural language instructions, ranging from coarse-grained task-level changes (e.g., switching between detection and captioning) to fine-grained object-level (e.g., specifying target categories) and output format customization (e.g., defining bounding box vs. polygon output).
4. **Demonstrate Strong Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** To show that this generalist LLM-based framework can achieve competitive performance on standard benchmarks for both traditional vision-language tasks and, significantly, visual perception tasks, matching or approaching the performance of specialist models.

In essence, VisionLLM aims to bring the "ChatGPT moment" of flexible, instruction-following AI to the domain of computer vision, allowing users to define and interact with visual tasks in an intuitive, open-ended linguistic manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VisionLLM's methodology revolves around a unified framework that treats images as a "foreign language" and aligns vision-centric tasks with language tasks, all manageable through flexible language instructions. It comprises three interconnected core components: Unified Language Instruction, Language-Guided Image Tokenizer, and an LLM-based Open-Ended Task Decoder.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The system operates as an end-to-end pipeline. Given an input image and language instructions, the model first processes the image through a language-guided tokenizer to generate image tokens that are semantically aligned with the prompt. These image tokens, combined with the language instructions, are then fed into an LLM-based decoder, which generates the task-specific output.

#### 3.2. Unified Language Instruction

This component is central to enabling flexible task definition and customization by translating diverse vision tasks into a language-model-compatible format.
- **Vision-Language Tasks (e.g., Image Captioning, VQA):** For these tasks, instructions are straightforward, similar to NLP prompts (e.g., "The image is `<image>`. Please generate a caption for the image:"). Placeholders like `<image>` and `<question>` are used.
- **Vision-Only Tasks (e.g., Object Detection, Instance Segmentation):** This is where VisionLLM introduces novel instruction design:
 - **Task Description:** A descriptive natural language sentence conveys the task. To enhance generality, a "self-instruct" like approach is used where an LLM generates a large number of varied task descriptions from a set of seed instructions.
 - **Unified Output Format:** For perception tasks requiring structured outputs, a unified tuple format `(C, P)` is proposed. `C` is a discretized class index (integer from 0), and `P = {xi, yi}` represents N points. Both `C` and `(x,y)` coordinates are transformed into discrete tokens.
 - For object detection and visual grounding, N=2 (top-left and bottom-right bounding box corners).
 - For instance segmentation, N > 8 points along the object boundary are used.
 - Coordinates are uniformly discretized into integers within a specified range (e.g., `[-range, range]`).
 - **Example:** For instance segmentation, an instruction might be: "Segment all the objects of category set `<class>` within the `<range>` of the image and generate a list of the format (c, x1, y1..., x8, y8). Here, c represents the index of the class label starting from 0, and (x1, y1...) correspond to the offsets of boundary points... The image is: `<image>`." This detailed specification allows the LLM to understand and produce structured visual outputs.

#### 3.3. Language-Guided Image Tokenizer

This component converts images into token representations, similar to how text is tokenized, but guided by language prompts.
- **Multi-scale Visual Features:** An image backbone (e.g., ResNet, InternImage) extracts multi-scale visual features from the input image.
- **Language Feature Injection:** A text encoder (e.g., BERT-Base) extracts language features from the given prompt. These language features are then injected into each scale of the visual features using cross-attention mechanisms. This process creates "language-aware visual features," aligning the modalities and making visual representations informative with respect to the language prompt.
- **High-Level Image Tokens:** A transformer-based network (e.g., Deformable DETR) with `M` randomly initialized queries is applied to these language-aware features. This network extracts `M` image tokens, each comprising an embedding (semantic information) and a location (positional information). This design allows for resolution-independent image representation and prompt-informed visual feature extraction.

#### 3.4. LLM-based Open-Ended Task Decoder

The decoder is built upon Alpaca (a LLaMA variant) and adapted for vision-centric tasks.
- **Vocabulary Expansion:** To handle numerical and categorical outputs efficiently, the LLM's vocabulary is expanded:
 - **Location Tokens:** A set of tokens like `(<p-512>, ..., <p512>)` represent discretized coordinate offsets relative to an image token's location. This converts continuous coordinate prediction into a discrete bin classification problem.
 - **Semantics-Agnostic Classification Tokens:** Tokens like `(<c0>, ..., <c511>)` replace verbose category names. The mapping between these tokens and actual category names (e.g., "person": `<c0>`) is dynamically provided within the language instruction's `<class>` set, enabling efficient and flexible open-vocabulary classification.
- **Output-Format-As-Query Decoding:** To address the inefficiency of causal (token-by-token) decoding for structured visual perception tasks, VisionLLM introduces this novel decoding method. The LLM first parses the desired *structural output format* (e.g., "(c, x1, y1, x2, y2)" for detection) from the instruction. These structural output tokens are then used as queries to the decoder, which generates the desired output *in parallel*. This unifies vision and language outputs as "token classification" and improves efficiency for perception tasks, while still supporting standard causal decoding for language generation.
- **Training Strategy:**
 - **Loss Function:** Both vision-language and vision-only tasks are supervised with cross-entropy loss, treating all outputs as token classification.
 - **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA):** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used to fine-tune the LLM, reducing computational costs and acting as a crucial bridge for aligning language and visual tokens.
 - **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1:** Initializes the language-guided image tokenizer (from pre-trained D-DETR and BERT) and LLM (Alpaca). Focuses on object detection with random task descriptions and categories, with most LLM parameters frozen except LoRA. This stage primarily aligns visual tokens.
 - **Stage 2:** Freezes the visual backbone and introduces unified supervision across multiple tasks (detection, segmentation, grounding, captioning, VQA). This stage leverages the LLM's full potential for multimodal understanding and task execution.

This comprehensive methodology allows VisionLLM to interpret complex language instructions, process visual information in a context-aware manner, and generate diverse, structured outputs, embodying its role as an open-ended decoder for vision-centric tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VisionLLM demonstrates compelling results across a wide array of vision-centric tasks, showcasing its capacity for task-level, object-level, and output format customization, as well as its robustness and generalizability.

#### 4.1. Task-Level Customization (Table 1)

VisionLLM achieved strong performance on four standard vision-centric tasks using a single, shared-parameter generalist model, simply by changing language instructions.
- **Object Detection (COCO):**
 - With ResNet-50, VisionLLM achieved 44.6 mAP, outperforming prior generalist methods like Pix2Seq (43.2 mAP) using the same backbone.
 - Notably, with InternImage-H, VisionLLM reached 60.2% mAP, which is highly competitive and "on par with detection-specific models," demonstrating the scalability of the generalist approach to high performance.
- **Visual Grounding (RefCOCO):**
 - Achieved 80.6 P@0.5 with ResNet-50 and 86.7 P@0.5 with InternImage-H, indicating its ability to effectively localize referred objects from textual descriptions, even when jointly trained with object detection (which can be a conflicting task).
- **Instance Segmentation (COCO):**
 - While showing a competitive mask AP50 (61.2% with InternImage-H), the mask AP75 was relatively lower. The authors attribute this to the information loss from coordinate discretization, the limited number of points used for mask representation, and the inherent differences in performance between point-based and direct mask prediction methods.
- **Image Captioning (COCO Karpathy Test Split):**
 - Achieved competitive BLEU-4 (31.0 with R50, 32.1 with InternImage-H) and CIDEr scores (112.5 with R50, 114.2 with InternImage-H) without using beam search or CIDEr optimization. This demonstrates its effectiveness in generating descriptive and contextually relevant captions.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):**
 - Qualitative results (Figure 2d, 10b) illustrate VisionLLM's capability in complex reasoning for VQA tasks, despite quantitative metrics not being provided due to dataset compatibility.
- **Comparison to Separate Training:** A model separately trained for each task (VisionLLM-R50_sep) performed slightly better in some tasks than the jointly trained model, acknowledging the challenge of multitasking conflicts, but highlighting the valuable trade-off for overall generalization.

#### 4.2. Object-Level & Output Format Customization (Table 2, Figures 8, 9)

VisionLLM effectively handles fine-grained customizations via language instructions.
- **Object-level Customization:** The model successfully adapts its detection targets based on the specified class set in the instruction (e.g., detecting only 10 classes vs. 80 classes, or specific open-vocabulary descriptions like "red gamepad"). Figure 9 clearly demonstrates this dynamic class selection capability.
- **Output Format Customization:** The model accurately adjusts the output format, such as the number of points used to represent an instance mask (e.g., 8, 14, 16, or 24 points). Figure 8 visually confirms that increasing the number of points leads to more refined object masks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3, 4, Figures 6, 7)

Ablation studies provided insights into key architectural and training choices.
- **Text Encoder in Language-Guided Image Tokenizer:** Crucial for visual grounding (cross-modal alignment), but less so for pure object detection. Freezing the text encoder significantly degrades performance for both tasks.
- **Image Tokenization Method:** The proposed query-based image tokenization method significantly outperformed a simpler average pooling method, highlighting its superior ability to capture varied object information.
- **Number of Localization Tokens:** Increasing the number of discrete localization tokens (bins) from 257 to 1025 substantially improved localization accuracy (+9.9 AP), indicating the importance of fine-grained spatial discretization.
- **Randomness during Training:** While introducing randomness (in task descriptions, object categories, and output formats) caused a slight drop in standard detection AP, it was essential for enabling the enhanced task customization and open-ended capabilities of the model.
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** LoRA was found to be critical for model convergence when training with randomness, acting as an effective bridge for aligning visual and language modalities. Without LoRA, the model failed to converge in diverse training scenarios.
- **Output-Format-As-Query Decoding:** This approach was more effective and converged better than a standard sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) decoding for open-ended visual tasks, addressing the causal framework's inefficiency.
- **Robustness to Prompt Changes:** VisionLLM showed remarkable robustness, with minimal performance variance (only 0.1 AP gap) across different task descriptions and random category orders in prompts, thanks to its training with diverse prompts (Figure 6).
- **Instruction Following Capability:** The model's ability to dynamically detect only the specified classes in the instruction and ignore others was clearly demonstrated (Figure 7), highlighting its strong adherence to given instructions.
- **Large-Vocabulary Object Recognition (LVIS):** Initial experiments on LVIS (1203 categories) showed promising generalist capabilities (18.9 mAP) by randomly sampling 80 classes during training, hinting at its potential for real-world large-vocabulary scenarios.

Overall, the results validate VisionLLM's core hypothesis: that LLMs can indeed serve as powerful, open-ended decoders for a wide array of vision-centric tasks, capable of fine-grained customization based on language instructions, while maintaining competitive performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VisionLLM represents a significant stride towards unifying vision and language models, with profound implications for the future of artificial general intelligence (AGI) and practical AI applications.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Breakthrough

The most significant contribution of VisionLLM is its pioneering approach to leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) as open-ended decoders for a *comprehensive range of vision-centric tasks*, including traditionally rigid visual perception tasks like object detection and instance segmentation. Unlike previous works that focused mainly on image-to-text generation or relied on fragmented API-based systems, VisionLLM offers a truly unified, end-to-end framework. It aligns vision tasks with LLM methodologies by treating images as a "foreign language" and defining tasks through flexible language instructions, a paradigm shift from conventional pre-defined task formats.

#### 5.2. Towards Unified Generalist AI

VisionLLM's ability to achieve strong performance across diverse vision and vision-language tasks (detection, segmentation, grounding, captioning, VQA) within a single, shared-parameter model marks a critical step towards realizing generalist AI. It demonstrates that a single model can adapt its behavior and output format based on nuanced language instructions, moving beyond the limitations of task-specific or generalist models constrained by rigid output schemas. This flexibility is key to building more adaptable and human-like AI systems.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Customization and User Interaction

The framework’s capacity for fine-grained task customization – from specifying target object categories and output formats to generating diverse task descriptions – dramatically enhances user interaction. Users can articulate complex visual queries in natural language, enabling rapid prototyping and deployment of vision models for novel or niche applications without extensive re-training or complex engineering. This drastically lowers the barrier to entry for customizing AI models for specific needs.

#### 5.4. Efficiency and Scalability

The introduction of vocabulary expansion (location and classification tokens) and the "output-format-as-query" decoding mechanism significantly improves the efficiency of LLMs for visual perception tasks. By enabling parallel decoding for structured outputs, VisionLLM overcomes the inherent inefficiency of causal LLMs for tasks like object localization. Furthermore, the use of LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, coupled with the reliance on open-source pre-trained models (Alpaca, D-DETR, BERT), makes the training process more accessible and resource-friendly.

#### 5.5. New Research Baseline

VisionLLM sets a new baseline for generalist vision and language models, demonstrating that a unified LLM-centric approach can achieve performance comparable to, and in some cases surpass, task-specific models on benchmarks like COCO object detection. This success encourages future research into deeper integration of modalities, more sophisticated instruction-following capabilities, and generalizable world knowledge for visual tasks.

#### 5.6. Broader Impact

The work promotes a deeper fusion of visual and language tasks, pushing the boundaries of multimodal AI. Its reliance on open-source components and resource-efficient training methods aligns with principles of responsible AI development, reducing the carbon footprint associated with large model training. The authors do not foresee immediate undesirable ethical or social impacts, suggesting that the primary impact will be in advancing AI capabilities for beneficial applications. As the model can precisely follow instructions for visual perception, its potential applications span from advanced robotics and autonomous systems to intuitive image editing and content generation tools, all controlled via natural language.
