# GPU-Parallel Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning with Demonstration Guided Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NVIDIA introduced MT-Libero, a GPU-parallel benchmark designed for heterogeneous multi-task robot manipulation, and proposed Demonstration Guided Policy Optimization (DGPO), an algorithm that adaptively leverages expert demonstrations to enhance multi-task learning efficiency. DGPO achieved a mean success rate of 85.2% on state-input tasks and 69.8% on visual-input tasks within MT-Libero, which itself scaled to 26,800 Samples Per Second (SPS) across 8 GPUs.

## Problem

- Existing GPU-parallel RL systems predominantly support training specialist policies, lacking a scalable infrastructure for generalist policies that learn multiple structured manipulation skills simultaneously.
- Multi-task reinforcement learning (MTRL) for complex robot manipulation faces challenges with sparse exploration signals and efficient optimization across diverse tasks, even with high-throughput simulators.
- Prior methods of integrating demonstrations into RL often treat them suboptimally, either diluting their impact in value learning or limiting policy improvement beyond observed behaviors, without adaptive task-aware guidance.

## Method

- MT-Libero, a GPU-parallel, heterogeneous multi-task RL benchmark, was constructed by compiling structured manipulation task families into a single vectorized training loop, separating task semantics from simulator execution.
- Demonstration Guided Policy Optimization (**DGPO**) extends PPO by introducing Importance-Weighted PPO (IW-PPO) for dynamic per-task gradient budget reallocation and Adaptive Behavior Cloning (Adaptive BC) for task-aware demonstration pressure.
- **DGPO**'s Adaptive BC applies an MSE regularizer to the actor's loss, pushing actions towards demonstrations with a strength that diminishes as per-task success rate increases, while IW-PPO reallocates gradient weights based on task success rates (EMA) rather than loss-based statistics.

## Results

- MT-Libero demonstrated high throughput (3,825 SPS on a single GPU, scaling to 26,800 SPS with 25,600 environments on 8 GPUs) and memory efficiency (8.2 GB VRAM for 1,600 environments vs. 2.3 TB RAM for MuJoCo baseline).
- DGPO achieved a mean success rate of **85.2%** for state-input policies and **69.8%** for visual-input policies across MT-Libero tasks, significantly outperforming baselines like MT-PPO (**42.5%** state-input) and MT-DAPG (**15.3%** state-input).
- Ablation studies confirmed Adaptive BC is fundamental for DGPO's learning capabilities (near **0%** success without it), and IW-PPO significantly improves success on harder tasks by redistributing gradient budget effectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating task semantics from simulator execution enables the efficient GPU-parallel execution of heterogeneous multi-task robot manipulation benchmarks within a unified training loop.
> - Dynamically reallocating PPO gradient budgets based on task success rates (IW-PPO) and adaptively applying behavior cloning (Adaptive BC) are crucial for efficiently learning multi-task policies from sparse rewards and demonstrations.
> - Demonstrations can effectively guide online reinforcement learning by providing tunable pressure on the actor, enabling robust learning for complex manipulation tasks without collapsing exploration or merely memorizing trajectories.
