# EvoLMM: Self-Evolving Large Multimodal Models with Continuous Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mohamed bin Zayed University of AI present EvoLMM, a framework enabling Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to autonomously improve their reasoning capabilities without human supervision. It utilizes a continuous self-consistency reward and an entropy-guided question generation mechanism, achieving consistent absolute accuracy gains of +2-3% across various multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) heavily depend on human-annotated data, metadata, or external reward models for training and fine-tuning.
- This dependency creates scalability bottlenecks, restricts generalization to novel domains, and increases the cost and effort of model development.
- Prior self-improvement techniques for LMMs often rely on discrete rewards, which are unstable and sub-optimal in multimodal settings, especially during early training.

## Method

- EvoLMM instantiates two cooperative roles, a ==Proposer== and a ==Solver==, from a single LMM backbone, operating in a closed-loop fashion.
- The Solver is trained using a novel ==continuous self-consistency reward==, calculated from the agreement among multiple answers generated by the model itself, providing stable gradients.
- The Proposer's reward is guided by the Solver's ==consensus entropy==, encouraging it to generate questions of moderate difficulty, thereby establishing an automatic curriculum for the Solver.

## Results

- EvoLMM achieved consistent gains of approximately **+2-3%** absolute accuracy across a broad suite of multimodal mathematical and visual reasoning benchmarks (e.g., ChartQA from **84.00%** to **86.70%**).
- The continuous self-consistency reward approach proved superior to discrete majority-vote rewards, which offered only marginal gains (**+0.3-0.6%**) and sometimes degraded performance.
- The framework demonstrated robust performance and architecture-agnostic improvements (**+1-3%** accuracy) across different LMM backbones (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, InternVL3-8B) and positive scaling with larger model sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A continuous self-consistency reward is more stable and effective for LMM self-improvement than discrete rewards, leading to consistent learning progression.
> - An entropy-guided Proposer reward mechanism can dynamically adjust question difficulty, fostering an emergent curriculum that drives the Solver to develop more structured reasoning.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies like LoRA are crucial for stabilizing the self-evolution process and preserving the LMM's general multimodal capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large multimodal models (LMMs) have led to improved capabilities in tasks requiring the integration of vision and language, such as image captioning, visual question answering, and multimodal reasoning. These models typically rely on large-scale pre-training and fine-tuning using extensively annotated datasets. However, the existing training paradigms for LMMs frequently depend on two main factors: (i) human-curated annotations or metadata, and (ii) externally defined reward models or evaluators, such as human-labeled correctness or knowledge-distilled reward networks.

These dependencies pose several challenges, including scalability limitations, difficulties in generalizing to new domains where annotated data is scarce, and restrictions on deployment in environments lacking access to annotations or external reward models.

In the realm of large language models (LLMs), research has explored self-evolving models that generate their own tasks and learn from them. Approaches like self-questioning and answering, often utilizing agreement among multiple sampled answers as a self-reward signal, have shown promise. Some frameworks further delineate roles such as Proposer, Solver, and Judge to facilitate co-evolution through reinforcement learning. However, these efforts are largely confined to language-only domains, where reasoning is explicit and correctness can be inferred via textual consistency or symbolic execution. Furthermore, these LLM-centric methods often still rely on implicit or explicit external evaluators, meaning the learning signal is not entirely independent of external priors.

For LMMs, several recent works have aimed to enhance models using self-rewarding training signals to reduce human supervision. One approach proposes a self-rewarding reasoning strategy that decomposes tasks into perception and reasoning steps, evaluating with internal bootstrapping. Another introduces a closed-loop framework, ViPER, that strengthens visual perception through image-level and instance-level reconstruction combined with reinforcement-style updates from internally synthesized data. While these represent progress in self-improvement, they frequently rely on structured intermediate signals such as reconstruction objectives, heuristic quality filtering, or semantic similarity-based scoring. This implies that their self-evolution remains partially guided by external priors or necessitates external models (e.g., for metadata generation), rather than emerging purely from the model’s internal reasoning dynamics. The Vision-Zero framework, for instance, uses a multi-agent social deduction game on images but requires synthetic image pairs generated by external generative models, introducing an element of implicit supervision.

The work presented in this paper aims to address these limitations by investigating a more fundamental form of LMM autonomy. It seeks to enable an LMM to enhance its reasoning capability without relying on human-annotated supervision, metadata, or any external reward signals, thereby differentiating itself from prior self-improving LMM approaches that maintain some dependency on external information or models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the reasoning capabilities of large multimodal models (LMMs) in a purely unsupervised manner. This involves eliminating the reliance on human-curated annotated data, associated metadata, or externally verified reward models during the training process.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations inherent in current LMM training paradigms:
- **Scalability Challenges**: The generation of human-curated annotations and the verification of reward models are resource-intensive and do not scale efficiently with the increasing size and diversity of multimodal datasets. This bottleneck hinders the continuous growth and deployment of LMMs.
- **Lack of Autonomy**: Current LMMs exhibit a significant dependency on external supervision, which limits their ability to independently learn and adapt. The research aims to foster greater autonomy in LMMs, allowing them to self-improve through intrinsic mechanisms.
- **Domain Generalization Limitations**: The need for task-specific annotations or reward models restricts the application of LMMs to novel domains where such resources are unavailable or prohibitively expensive. Developing an unsupervised framework could facilitate broader domain generalization.
- **Instability of Discrete Rewards**: Prior self-questioning approaches in LLMs that use discrete majority-vote rewards have shown instability, particularly in early training stages where model outputs are highly variable. This often leads to sparse, zero-reward updates that hinder gradual learning. The motivation is to overcome this by designing a more robust and continuous learning signal.
- **Emergence of a Learning Curriculum**: The research is motivated by the potential for an LMM to autonomously develop an implicit curriculum of learning. This means the model would gradually generate questions of increasing complexity and refine its reasoning over time without explicit human-designed stages.

To achieve these goals, the paper proposes a self-evolving framework, EvoLMM, with specific objectives:
- To instantiate two cooperative agents, a Proposer and a Solver, from a single LMM backbone, enabling them to interact in a closed-loop system.
- To develop a novel continuous self-rewarding mechanism based on multi-sample answer consistency, which provides dense and stable feedback for learning.
- To empirically demonstrate that this framework can yield consistent performance gains on multimodal math-reasoning benchmarks using only raw training images, without any ground-truth labels or external reward models.
- To establish a foundational baseline for future research into fully unsupervised and self-improving LMMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces EvoLMM, a self-evolving framework designed to enhance LMM reasoning without relying on human annotations, metadata, or external reward models. The core of EvoLMM involves the instantiation of two cooperative agents, a Proposer and a Solver, from a single Large Multimodal Model backbone, engaging in a continuous self-rewarding process.

**Problem Formulation:** The framework operates in an unsupervised self-evolving vision–language reasoning setting. Given an unlabeled collection of images $X = \{x\}$, the goal is for a pre-trained base LMM to improve itself by generating its own training signal directly from these images. This is achieved through two learned policies:
- **Proposer ($\pi_\phi(q | x)$):** This policy is responsible for generating a visually grounded question $q$ given an input image $x$.
- **Solver ($\pi_\theta(y | x, q)$):** This policy attempts to answer the question $q$ in the context of the image $x$.

**Continuous Self-Consistency Reward for Solver:**
For each question generated by the Proposer, the Solver generates $N$ independent answer samples $y_{1:N} \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot | x, q)$. An empirical answer distribution $p(a | x, q)$ is then computed. Unlike discrete majority-vote rewards, which can be sparse and unstable, EvoLMM employs a continuous self-consistency reward for the Solver. The reward $r_{sol_i}$ for each sample $y_i$ is defined as:
$r_{sol_i} = [p(y_i | x, q)]^\gamma \cdot (1 - \lambda_{len} \cdot \max\{0, (w_i - \tau)/\tau\})$
Here, $p(y_i | x, q)$ represents the Solver’s own agreement score for the sample $y_i$, reflecting its confidence in that answer based on the distribution of its $N$ samples. The parameter $\gamma \in (0, 1]$ controls the reward softness, with lower values accentuating mid-confidence differences. The term involving $\lambda_{len}$ applies a length penalty, where $w_i$ is the number of words before the `<answer>` tag and $\tau$ is a target brevity threshold. This continuous reward provides dense feedback, promoting both stable reasoning (high agreement among outputs) and concise answer formatting, even when reasoning is partially correct.

**Entropy-Guided Continuous Proposer Reward:**
To guide the Proposer away from generating trivial or unsolvable questions, it receives a smooth, band-pass reward based on the Solver’s answer entropy. The Solver-consensus entropy $H(x, q) = -\sum_a p(a | x, q) \log p(a | x, q)$ serves as a proxy for question difficulty. The Proposer reward $r_{prop}$ is defined as:
$r_{prop} = \exp\left(-\frac{(H(x, q) - \mu_H)^2}{2\sigma_H^2}\right)$
This Gaussian function peaks when the Solver's uncertainty (entropy $H$) is within a moderate range, centered at $\mu_H$ with spread $\sigma_H$. This encourages the Proposer to generate questions that are neither too easy (low entropy, near zero) nor too ambiguous/difficult (high entropy). This mechanism implicitly establishes an adaptive curriculum: as the Solver improves and becomes more consistent, its entropy for a given question decreases, prompting the Proposer to generate slightly more challenging, yet still solvable, questions to maintain a moderate entropy level and continue receiving optimal reward.

**Training Optimization:**
Both the Proposer and Solver are trained jointly in an online reinforcement learning loop using the REINFORCE policy gradient algorithm. To ensure stable learning without ground-truth labels, two key components are incorporated:
1. **Moving-average baselines ($b_A$):** These are used to reduce variance in the reward signals for both agents ($A \in \{\text{Solver, Proposer}\}$).
2. **Token-level KL regularization ($KL_A$):** This term monitors and constrains the divergence of the learned policies from the pre-trained base LMM ($\pi_{ref_A}$). The overall loss function for an agent A is:
 $L_A = - E_t[(r_A - b_A) \cdot \frac{1}{T_A} \sum_{t=1}^{T_A} \log \pi_A(y_t | h_t)] + \beta_A KL_A$
 where $h_t$ is the text-image context up to token $t$. A dynamic KL controller adjusts the coefficient $\beta_A$ to maintain a target divergence budget, which stabilizes learning. For the Proposer, the KL term is less tightly constrained, allowing it to explore new question spaces as it evolves.

**Training Details:** The base model used is the pre-trained Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Two lightweight LoRA adapters are employed for the Proposer and Solver roles, keeping the backbone frozen to preserve its general multimodal capabilities. Training is conducted on a single node with 8x AMD MI250X GPUs, utilizing bfloat16 precision. The Solver's reward is computed from $N=5$ sampled answers. The Proposer is updated every 5 iterations, while the Solver receives gradients at each step. The AdamW optimizer is used with a learning rate of 1e-6, weight decay of 0.01, and gradient clipping at 1.0. Specific reward parameters include a length penalty of 0.10 towards a 6-word target for the Solver, a softness exponent $\gamma = 0.7$, and a Gaussian entropy reward for the Proposer centered at $\mu = 0.90$ with $\sigma = 0.35$. Training proceeds for 6000 steps with a batch size of 1. Critically, no QA annotations, metadata, or external reward models are used at any stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EvoLMM involved comprehensive experiments across various multimodal mathematical and visual reasoning benchmarks, architectural configurations, and model sizes.

**Performance Against Baselines (Table 1):**
- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Baseline):** The base model exhibited proficiency in tasks requiring direct visual interpretation but demonstrated reduced performance on multi-step inference or symbolic reasoning tasks (e.g., MathVista, MathVerse), indicating limitations of pre-trained alignment alone for complex reasoning.
- **Discrete-Reward Adaptation:** A direct adaptation using a discrete majority-vote reward yielded only marginal gains (0.3% to 0.6%) and occasionally led to performance degradation. This outcome is attributed to the sparse nature of discrete rewards in early training stages when Solver outputs are highly variable, resulting in unstable optimization.
- **Continuous-Reward (EvoLMM – Ours):** EvoLMM, utilizing the continuous self-consistency reward, consistently achieved improvements across all evaluated benchmarks. Absolute gains ranged from +0.62% to +2.7%. For instance, accuracy on ChartQA increased from 84.00% to 86.70%, and on MathVista, it rose from 68.46% to 70.52%. Consistent improvements were also observed on ScienceQA (+1.2%) and other reasoning tasks. These results indicate that the continuous reward provides a more stable and informative gradient, enabling robust and annotation-free self-evolution.

**Comparison Across Training Settings (Table 2):**
- **LoRA (default):** The LoRA fine-tuning strategy demonstrated the most effective balance between stability and adaptability. It preserved the model’s pre-trained multimodal alignment while facilitating stable co-evolution between the Proposer and Solver, leading to consistent performance improvements.
- **QLoRA:** This quantized variant, while offering memory efficiency, exhibited slightly reduced performance gains compared to LoRA (e.g., 68.46% to 68.92% on MathVista). This suggests that quantization noise might impact the sensitivity of learning from fine-grained continuous rewards.
- **Full Fine-Tuning:** Full-parameter updates resulted in instability. Without explicit supervision, the Solver tended to overfit early biases, and the Proposer occasionally collapsed into repetitive or low-diversity question modes. Furthermore, the interaction with KL regularization, which constrains divergence from the base model, led to unfavorable drift and, in some cases, degradation of baseline performance. This highlights the importance of parameter-efficient strategies for unsupervised self-evolution.

**Self-Evolution Across Different Backbones (Table 3):**
The EvoLMM framework was applied to various LMM backbones, including Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, InternVL3-8B, Gemma-3-12B-it, and Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct. Consistent improvements of approximately +2% to +3% were observed across all tested models. This indicates that the proposed continuous self-consistency reward mechanism is architecture-agnostic and transferable, enhancing structured visual reasoning rather than relying on specific model alignments or handcrafted supervision.

**Scaling Behavior Across Model Sizes (Table 4):**
Evaluation across different model sizes within the Qwen2.5-VL family (7B and 72B) demonstrated that EvoLMM scales reliably. Both the 7B and 72B models showed consistent performance gains. Notably, larger models exhibited stronger absolute improvements (e.g., 88.20% to 91.04% on ChartQA for the 72B model), suggesting that increased model capacity amplifies the benefits of refinement through continuous agreement feedback.

**Curriculum Emergence (Figure 6 & 5):**
Analysis indicated that the Proposer implicitly develops a curriculum, shifting from generating overly easy or difficult questions to a stable distribution of moderate-difficulty queries. This progression reflects the emergence of more structured and grounded reasoning behaviors over time, as depicted qualitatively by examples of increasingly complex visual problems generated by the Proposer during training.

**Generalization to Open-Ended Benchmarks (Supplementary Table 5):**
The self-evolved Qwen-2.5-VL-7B showed consistent improvements over the baseline on open-ended multimodal benchmarks (RefCOCOg, DocVQA, OCRBench, Pope, RealworldQA, IFEval, MME) in a zero-shot setting, despite not being trained on these specific datasets or their annotations. This suggests that the benefits of self-evolution extend beyond the structured mathematical reasoning tasks used during training.

**Small Model Behavior (Supplementary Table 6):**
EvoLMM was effective for compact multimodal backbones (InternVL3-1B and Qwen3-VL-2B), demonstrating performance gains ranging from +2.1% to +3.3%. This indicates the framework's scalability and robustness across various model sizes, not just large models.

**Fine-Grained Benchmark Analysis (Supplementary Table 7):**
Per-category scores on ChartQA, MathVista, and InfoGraphic-VQA revealed that EvoLMM improved both data-reading and symbolic reasoning accuracy, with the largest gains observed in visual numerical comparison tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces EvoLMM, a framework that signifies a step toward more autonomous and scalable large multimodal models. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that LMMs can significantly enhance their visual reasoning abilities without relying on human-annotated data, explicit metadata, or external reward models. This addresses fundamental limitations in current LMM development, which often face scalability bottlenecks and domain generalization challenges due to extensive supervision requirements.

The development of a **continuous self-consistency reward mechanism** is a notable contribution. Unlike discrete, majority-vote rewards that can lead to sparse gradients and unstable learning, particularly in early training stages with variable multimodal outputs, the continuous reward provides a dense and stable learning signal. This enables robust optimization and consistent performance improvements, even in the presence of early-stage uncertainty or partial correctness in reasoning. This mechanism is crucial for fostering a stable self-evolutionary loop.

The **entropy-guided Proposer reward** is another significant aspect, as it facilitates the **emergent curriculum** within the self-play framework. By encouraging the Proposer to generate questions of moderate difficulty, the system implicitly progresses from simpler to more complex reasoning tasks without any handcrafted curriculum design. This adaptive learning environment allows both the Proposer and Solver to co-evolve, refining question generation and reasoning capabilities over time.

The demonstrated **architecture-agnostic nature and scalability** of EvoLMM across diverse LMM backbones (e.g., Qwen, InternVL, Gemma, Llama families) and model sizes (7B to 72B) underscores its broad applicability. This suggests that the proposed self-evolution strategy is not confined to specific model architectures or pre-training alignments but rather offers a generalized approach to improving multimodal reasoning. Furthermore, its effectiveness for smaller models highlights its potential utility across different computational resource constraints.

The ability of EvoLMM to generalize improvements to open-ended multimodal benchmarks, even without direct training on their specific data, suggests that the learned structured reasoning skills are transferable. This has implications for developing more versatile LMMs capable of handling a wider array of real-world scenarios.

In summary, this work provides a robust baseline for future research in fully unsupervised and self-improving LMMs. It lays groundwork for developing truly autonomous AI systems that can continuously learn and adapt in dynamic environments. The potential impact extends to reducing the significant costs associated with data annotation and expert supervision, thereby accelerating the deployment of advanced multimodal intelligence in domains where labeled data is scarce or expensive. Future research directions suggested by this work include further exploration of curriculum emergence, methods for self-generated data scaling, and achieving long-horizon reasoning capabilities without external supervision.
