# DiffWind: Physics-Informed Differentiable Modeling of Wind-Driven Object Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffWind is a physics-informed differentiable framework that jointly recovers invisible wind fields and visible object motion from sparse-view video. Wind is a grid-based Eulerian field, objects are 3D-Gaussian particle systems, coupled by a differentiable Material Point Method; a Lattice Boltzmann directional loss enforces fluid-dynamics validity. It reaches 47.15 dB average PSNR on synthetic scenes versus 36.68 for the best prior baseline, and supports forward simulation and wind retargeting.

## Problem

- Dynamic neural representations such as deformable NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting fit only visible motion over time, ignoring invisible physical causes like the wind field driving it.
- Differentiable physics simulators recover material or initial-velocity parameters but assume simple predefined motion such as gravity or dragging, and cannot handle complex fluid-object interaction.
- Video-based wind inference estimates only coarse wind speed or targets a single scenario like cloth, lacking generality, physical consistency, and full 4D dynamics recovery.

## Method

- ==Particle-grid coupling==: objects become deformable ==3D-Gaussian particle systems==, wind a ==Eulerian grid force field==, and a differentiable ==Material Point Method== transfers wind force to particles, updating each Gaussian's covariance via its deformation gradient.
- ==Sequential inverse reconstruction== optimizes only the per-timestep wind force field through differentiable simulation and rendering under an ==L1 plus D-SSIM photometric loss==, holding MLLM-inferred material priors fixed to avoid force-stiffness ambiguity.
- ==Physics-informed LBM loss==: ==HOME-LBM== evolves the wind field using object voxels as boundaries and an MLLM-inferred inlet direction, penalizing directional deviation of the reconstructed force field.

## Results

- On the seven-scene synthetic WD-Objects benchmark, novel-view PSNR averages **47.15 dB** versus **36.68** for Deformable-GS, **35.79** for GaussianPrediction, and **32.39** for 4D-GS, with LPIPS **0.0008**.
- Ablations: removing the physics-informed loss drops PSNR from **53.24** to **51.31**; dropping internal-point densification drops real-world PSNR from **27.90** to **27.31**; the 128-cubed grid costs **1.17** seconds per iteration.
- A **32**-participant user study on forward simulation rates DiffWind **4.34** of **5** for motion and physical realism, against **2.84** for DynamiCrafter, **2.49** for SVD, **2.34** for CogVideoX.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Match representation to physics: Eulerian grids for fluids, Lagrangian particles for deformable solids; the coupling interface, not one unified field, buys physical plausibility.
> - Photometric loss alone leaves external force and material stiffness entangled; fixing material priors plus a directional physics prior is what makes a recovered field transferable.
> - Optimizing sequentially per timestep, each starting from the previously solved state, avoids the gradient instability of backpropagating through long multi-step simulation rollouts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The modeling of wind-driven object dynamics from video observations is recognized as a complex challenge within computer graphics and computer vision. This complexity stems from several factors: the invisibility and spatio-temporal variability of wind, the intricate deformations of objects, and the unknown physical parameters governing object behavior. The ability to computationally recover both the underlying invisible wind field and the resulting visible object dynamics from visual input holds implications for applications such as augmented reality, virtual reality, visual effects, scientific analysis, and simulation-based editing.

Existing approaches in related fields exhibit specific limitations that DiffWind aims to address. Dynamic neural representations, including methods based on Deformable NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting, primarily focus on modeling object appearance and visible motion over time. These methods generally capture the "what" of dynamic scenes but do not inherently infer the underlying physical causes, such as external forces like wind fields. Consequently, they lack the ability to explain or predict object behavior under novel physical conditions.

Differentiable physics simulators, another area of related research, enable the optimization of motion parameters. However, these simulators are often constrained to simpler, predefined motion patterns (e.g., constant-force projectile motion) and typically do not accommodate complex fluid-object interactions. Furthermore, many current differentiable simulation approaches for system identification optimize initial velocities or material properties for a single starting state, which is insufficient for reconstructing continuously varying environmental forces like wind.

Video-based wind inference methods constitute a third category of related work. These methods aim to estimate wind characteristics, but they often infer only coarse wind speeds or are tailored to specific scenarios, such as cloth deformation. This specificity limits their generality and physical consistency across a broader range of objects and interaction types.

DiffWind positions itself in this landscape by proposing a unified framework that attempts to overcome these limitations. It seeks to jointly recover both visible object dynamics and invisible wind fields from video input, while also enforcing physical consistency through physics-informed constraints. This integrated approach aims to enable generalization to arbitrary wind conditions and facilitate applications that require a deeper understanding of the physical processes at play, such as forward simulation and wind retargeting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for DiffWind stems from the inherent difficulties in computationally understanding wind-driven object dynamics from video. The challenge is multi-faceted:
- **Invisibility and Variability of Wind:** Wind, being invisible, cannot be directly observed from video. Its spatio-temporal characteristics, including direction and magnitude, are highly dynamic and non-uniform, making accurate inference from indirect visual cues difficult.
- **Complex Object Deformations:** Objects under wind influence often undergo complex, non-rigid deformations (e.g., fluttering flags, swaying leaves, billowing fabrics). Modeling these deformations accurately requires capturing unknown physical parameters such as mass, elasticity, and geometry.
- **Limitations of Current Methods:** As discussed in the previous section, existing dynamic neural representations primarily capture visible motion without inferring underlying physical causes. Differentiable physics simulators, while capable of optimization, are often restricted to simpler predefined motions and struggle with complex fluid-object interactions. Video-based wind inference methods lack generality and physical consistency.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the DiffWind research are detailed as follows:

1. **Develop a Physics-Informed Differentiable Framework:** The core objective is to create a novel framework, named DiffWind, that integrates physical principles into a differentiable computational pipeline. This framework aims to unify the modeling of wind-object interactions with video-based reconstruction and forward simulation capabilities.
2. **Joint Reconstruction of Wind and Object Dynamics:** A central goal is to simultaneously recover both the visible dynamic motion of objects and the invisible spatio-temporal wind force field from sparse-view RGB videos. This is achieved through differentiable rendering and simulation.
3. **Ensure Physical Validity:** To address the limitation that purely photometric optimization may not yield physically plausible results, the framework aims to incorporate physics-informed constraints. Specifically, the Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM) is used to guide the reconstruction of the wind force field, enforcing compliance with fundamental fluid dynamics laws.
4. **Enable Forward Simulation:** Beyond reconstruction, an objective is for the method to naturally support forward simulation, allowing for the prediction of object behavior under novel or user-specified wind conditions.
5. **Facilitate Novel Applications:** The research aims to enable new applications arising from the accurate reconstruction and simulation capabilities, such as wind retargeting, where a reconstructed wind field can be applied to different objects.
6. **Construct a Comprehensive Dataset:** To support the development and evaluation of methods for wind-driven object dynamics, a new dataset, WD-Objects, comprising both synthetic and real-world wind-driven scenes, is introduced.

These objectives collectively aim to advance the computational understanding and manipulation of complex wind-object interactions by integrating visual data with robust physical modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffWind is presented as a physics-informed differentiable framework designed to model, reconstruct, and simulate wind-driven object dynamics. The approach is characterized by a distinct representation for wind and objects, coupled through a physics simulation method, and optimized via a differentiable pipeline.

**A. Physical Models Preliminaries:**
The framework utilizes two primary physical models:
- **Fluid Mechanics (Wind):** Wind field dynamics are governed by incompressible Navier-Stokes Equations, solved using the Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM). Specifically, the HOME-LBM variant is employed for its computational efficiency and accuracy, solving a discretized Boltzmann equation. The macroscopic quantities (density, momentum, stress tensor) of the wind field are derived from distribution functions.
- **Continuum Mechanics (Object):** Object deformations are described by continuum mechanics, specifically using a time-dependent deformation map. The evolution is governed by conservation of mass and momentum equations, with the Material Point Method (MPM) used as the differentiable solver. The Neo-Hookean constitutive model captures the non-linear stress-strain response of objects, relating Lame parameters to Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio.

**B. Wind-Object Interaction Modeling:**
- **Object Modeling:** Objects are represented using 3D Gaussians, a particle-based approach for high-quality reconstruction and rendering. To enhance physical simulation, several steps are taken to address inherent limitations of vanilla 3DGS:
 - **Regularization:** During optimization, `L_aniso` is applied to constrain Gaussian kernel anisotropy, and `L_o` encourages Gaussian opacities to be either near zero or one, aiming to keep Gaussians close to the object surface.
 - **Internal Point Densification:** An octree-based voxel filling algorithm from Kaolin is used to densify additional invisible internal points, providing improved geometric support and volume integrity, which is critical for robust physical simulation.
 - **Physical Property Inference:** To obtain physical parameters, a 3D-consistent feature field is trained via contrastive learning using 2D segmentation priors (from EntitySeg and Grounded-SAM). A multimodal large language model (MLLM) then infers physical properties (material name, density, Poisson’s ratio, Young’s modulus) for segmented 3D regions.
- **Interaction Modeling:** The Material Point Method (MPM) models the interaction. Each 3D Gaussian kernel and densified internal point is bound to an MPM particle. Wind is applied as a force field on the Eulerian grid of MPM, driving particle motion and object deformation. The covariance matrix of 3D Gaussians is updated based on the deformation gradient from MPM.

**C. Reconstruction Framework for Wind-Object Interaction:**
- **Differentiable Pipeline:** The simulation process (MPM) and rendering process (3D Gaussian Splatting) are formulated differentiably. This allows for gradient-based optimization of the invisible wind force field from observable object motion.
- **Optimization Target:** The primary optimization target is the spatio-temporal wind force field (`F_w`). Material parameters are initialized using MLLM-based reasoning and generally kept fixed to mitigate the ill-posedness of jointly optimizing both forces and material properties.
- **Loss Function:** An image-space photometric loss (`L_render`), combining L1 and D-SSIM losses, is minimized between rendered images and input video frames.
- **Sequential Optimization:** Optimization proceeds sequentially through time steps. The optimized object state from a previous step serves as the initial condition for the subsequent step, which is stated to improve gradient stability.

**D. Physics-Informed Optimization of Wind Force Field:**
- **Motivation:** To ensure physical validity beyond visual matching, a physics-informed loss is introduced.
- **Wind Source Inference:** Monocular depth maps are estimated from RGB videos (Video Depth Anything) and fed into an MLLM to infer the wind source direction. This inferred direction provides an inlet velocity boundary condition for the LBM simulator.
- **Wind Field Evolution:** HOME-LBM simulates the wind field evolution. Object particles define solid nodes on the LBM lattice, allowing the object's instantaneous position to influence the wind field dynamics. Macroscopic wind field quantities are computed from LBM distribution functions.
- **Physics-Informed Loss (`L_phys`):** This loss measures the directional difference between the reconstructed wind force field (`D_recon`) and the LBM-guided wind velocity field (`D_guide`). Given that aerodynamic drag force direction is aligned with wind velocity direction, this constraint guides the reconstruction of `F_w` towards physical plausibility. The total optimization loss includes both photometric and physics-informed terms.

**E. Implementation Details:**
The system is implemented using the Taichi GPU framework for physical simulation. Real-world data processing involves Detector-Free SFM for camera poses and EntitySeg/Grounded-SAM for 2D segmentation, with GPT-5 used for physical property reasoning. The simulation space is typically a 128^3 grid.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate DiffWind spanned reconstruction accuracy, simulation fidelity, generalization ability, and ablation studies.

**A. Datasets:**
The authors introduced WD-Objects, a novel dataset for wind-driven object dynamics. This dataset comprises:
- **Synthetic Data:** Seven object-level 3D Gaussians reconstructed from various sources. Dynamic scenes are simulated, and reference videos are rendered from eight orthogonal views (four for training, four for novel view synthesis evaluation) over 50 simulation time steps.
- **Real-World Data:** 360-degree surround videos of static scenes featuring objects like a pothos plant, a beanie hat, a pompon flower, and a tulip. Synchronized videos of these plants affected by a hairdryer provide ground truth for dynamic behavior.

**B. Evaluation on Reconstruction:**
- **Novel View Synthesis:** DiffWind was quantitatively compared against state-of-the-art dynamic scene reconstruction methods, including Deformable-GS, Efficient-GS, 4D-GS, and GaussianPrediction.
 - **Quantitative Results (Tables 1 & 2):** DiffWind consistently and significantly outperformed all baselines across synthetic and real-world datasets in terms of PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics, indicating higher accuracy in novel view rendering. For instance, on the synthetic dataset, DiffWind's average PSNR was 47.15, compared to the next best, Deformable-GS, at 36.68. Similarly, on the real-world dataset, DiffWind's average PSNR was 28.27, while the best baseline, 4D-GS, achieved 27.87.
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 4, Figures 10 & 11 in Appendix):** DiffWind generated more realistic novel views, attributable to its ability to model wind-driven object dynamics accurately. Baselines often exhibited artifacts or less plausible motion.
- **Reconstructed Wind Force Fields (Table 9 in Appendix):** For synthetic data, the reconstructed wind force fields were evaluated against ground truth. The average cosine similarity (CosSim) was 0.9249, and the normalized mean squared error (NMSE) was 0.0500. These metrics suggest that the recovered wind force fields maintained strong directional coherence and consistent relative magnitudes with the ground truth.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
- **Effectiveness of Physics-Informed Loss (Table 3):** Incorporating the physics-informed loss (`L_phys`) resulted in improved rendering quality during dynamic reconstruction (e.g., PSNR increased from 51.31 to 53.24), indicating that the physical constraints lead to more precise and physically plausible reconstructions.
- **Robustness to Physical Property (Table 4):** The method demonstrated robustness to variations in material parameters (Young’s modulus). While comparable performance was achieved with predefined "soft," "medium," and "hard" settings, values inferred by the MLLM yielded slightly better reconstruction results, supporting the MLLM-based estimation strategy.
- **Necessity of Invisible Internal Points Densification (Table 5):** Densiifying invisible internal points improved reconstruction results (e.g., PSNR increased from 27.31 to 27.90), validating its contribution to accurate geometric modeling and simulation support.
- **Influence of Grid Resolution (Table 6):** Reconstruction quality remained stable across grid resolutions (64^3, 128^3, 256^3). A 128^3 grid provided a favorable trade-off between fidelity and computational runtime (approximately 1.17 seconds per optimization iteration).

**D. Forward Simulation of Wind-Driven Object Dynamics:**
- **Simulation under Specified Wind Conditions:** DiffWind's forward simulation capabilities were evaluated and compared against publicly available video generation models (SVD, CogVideoX, VideoCrafter, DynamiCrafter).
 - **Human Evaluation (Table 7):** A user study with 32 participants showed that DiffWind's simulations achieved a significantly higher average visual quality rating (4.34 out of 5) compared to the video generation baselines (average ratings ranging from 2.33 to 2.84). This indicates superior perceived motion realism and physical plausibility.
 - **Qualitative Results (Figures 5, 7, 8, 9):** DiffWind generated realistic, 3D-consistent, and time-evolving wind-object interactions. In contrast, video generation models often suffered from issues like temporal incoherence, camera shake, unrealistic jittering, or lack of 3D consistency in object geometry.
- **Wind Retargeting:** The reconstructed wind force fields could be effectively applied to different, previously unseen objects in forward simulation, demonstrating the capability for wind retargeting (Figures 1, 2, 6). This illustrates that the framework successfully decouples the invisible wind force field from specific object dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiffWind represents a notable advancement in the computational modeling of complex environmental interactions from visual data. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Unified and Physics-Informed Approach:** The framework unifies wind-object interaction modeling, video-based reconstruction, and forward simulation within a differentiable pipeline. This integration allows for a more comprehensive understanding of dynamic scenes compared to methods that solely focus on appearance or kinematics. By incorporating physics-informed constraints via the Lattice Boltzmann Method, the system ensures that reconstructed wind fields are not only visually consistent but also physically plausible, addressing a key limitation of prior visually-driven methods.
- **Enhanced Reconstruction Accuracy and Fidelity:** The experimental results demonstrate that DiffWind significantly outperforms existing dynamic scene modeling approaches in terms of reconstruction accuracy for novel view synthesis. This superior fidelity is attributed to its capability to accurately model the underlying physical causes of object motion, rather than merely approximating visible dynamics.
- **Generalization and Predictability:** The ability to reconstruct invisible wind force fields and decouple them from specific objects enables generalization. This is evidenced by the successful application of forward simulation under novel wind conditions and, importantly, the new application of wind retargeting, where a learned wind field can drive the motion of different objects. This predictive capability has broad implications for interactive content creation and scientific analysis.
- **Advancement in Video-Based Wind-Object Interaction Modeling:** The research contributes to the frontier of video-based wind-object interaction modeling by tackling a challenging problem that involves invisible phenomena and complex deformable objects. The introduction of the WD-Objects dataset also provides a valuable resource for future research and benchmarking in this area.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The capabilities offered by DiffWind have potential impact across several domains:
 - **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Enabling realistic and interactive virtual environments where digital objects respond plausibly to simulated wind conditions.
 - **Visual Effects (VFX):** Streamlining the creation of complex wind-driven phenomena (e.g., cloth simulation, foliage movement) in film and gaming by inferring real-world wind patterns.
 - **Scientific Analysis:** Providing tools for analyzing fluid-structure interactions from observational data, potentially aiding in fields like aerodynamics, meteorology, or biomechanics.
 - **Simulation-Based Editing:** Offering intuitive controls for editing and manipulating dynamic scenes based on underlying physical parameters, leading to more creative and physically consistent content generation.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations and areas for future research. Currently, the framework focuses on object-level dynamics and does not account for interactions between multiple objects (e.g., collisions). Robustly handling multi-object collisions presents challenges due to discontinuous contact dynamics and complex force interactions, which complicate gradient-based optimization. Additionally, while the Material Point Method (MPM) is suitable for continuum objects, the framework is not simulator-agnostic. The authors suggest that replacing MPM with other differentiable simulators, such as the spring-mass method or the Finite Element Method (FEM), could extend its applicability to a wider range of non-continuum or different types of object motion behaviors. Addressing these limitations could further expand the scope and robustness of physics-informed differentiable modeling of dynamic scenes.
