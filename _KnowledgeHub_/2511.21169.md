# Kinematics-Aware Multi-Policy Reinforcement Learning for Force-Capable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tongji University developed a multi-policy reinforcement learning framework enabling humanoid robots to perform high-load loco-manipulation tasks by integrating proactive force interaction with robust whole-body coordination. The Unitree G1 robot, controlled by this framework, successfully pushed and pulled a 112.8 kg cart and maintained end-effector stability while carrying a 4 kg payload.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack robust capabilities for high-load industrial loco-manipulation tasks requiring proactive force interaction and stable whole-body coordination.
- Current monolithic reinforcement learning approaches for whole-body control face high dimensionality issues and cannot learn active force exertion due to data limitations.
- Decoupled control methods primarily focus on passive adaptation to disturbances, struggle with real-time requirements, and have difficulties coordinating upper and lower body movements effectively.

## Method

- A decoupled three-stage RL framework comprises distinct policies for upper-body manipulation, force-capable lower-body locomotion, and whole-body coordination; the upper-body policy (\pi_u) uses a heuristic reward function implicitly embedding forward kinematics priors, accelerating convergence and improving end-effector pose tracking.
- A lower-body policy (\pi_l) utilized a force-based curriculum learning strategy to progressively acquire robust locomotion and proactive force exertion capabilities.
- A high-level delta-command policy (\pi_h) was introduced to generate compensatory offsets, stabilizing the end-effector's height and orientation in the world frame against lower-body movements.

## Results

- In simulation, the kinematics-aware heuristic reward achieved superior upper-body pose tracking (0.03 m position error, 0.11 rad orientation error), while curriculum learning enabled the lower-body policy to converge efficiently by iteration 8000, acquiring proactive force exertion and robust locomotion.
- The delta-command policy reduced end-effector height oscillation by 57% during locomotion in simulation and effectively maintained end-effector stability with a 4 kg payload and during robot squats in real-world tests.
- The Unitree G1 humanoid robot successfully executed real-world high-payload loco-manipulation, including pushing and pulling a 112.8 kg cart with active force application.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A heuristic reward function incorporating both joint-space and task-space terms with dynamic weighting and scaling significantly accelerates training and improves accuracy for high-degree-of-freedom upper-body manipulation.
> - A force-based curriculum learning strategy is crucial for efficiently training the lower body to proactively exert and regulate forces, ensuring robust skill acquisition and generalization without forgetting basic locomotion.
> - A high-level delta-command policy can effectively provide world-frame stabilization for the end-effector by generating compensatory offsets, overcoming the challenges of decoupled control in maintaining manipulation task integrity during locomotion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots, with their anthropomorphic form, are increasingly seen as ideal candidates for deployment in industrial settings where existing tools and workflows are designed for human operators. However, the effective deployment of humanoids in these high-load industrial environments remains a significant challenge. This paper addresses a critical gap in the current research landscape of humanoid loco-manipulation, particularly concerning the integration of dexterity with proactive force interaction in scenarios demanding substantial physical engagement.

Historically, loco-manipulation research has branched into several key areas:

- **Model-based Approaches:** These methods traditionally frame loco-manipulation as an optimal control problem-. While capable of generating stable gaits and precise motions, they heavily rely on accurate system dynamics and environmental models. This dependence makes them highly susceptible to modeling errors, unpredictable variations in contact forces, payloads, friction, and sensor noise in real-world, uncontrolled industrial settings, thereby limiting their practical robustness and deployability-.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Locomotion:** RL has emerged as a powerful paradigm, overcoming many limitations of model-based approaches by learning robust gaits and behaviors directly from interaction. Significant progress has been made in enabling stable locomotion over diverse terrains, handling external disturbances, and performing complex skills like jumping or parkour-. This area is considered mature and provides a solid foundation.
- **RL-based Whole-Body Control for Loco-manipulation:**
 - **Monolithic Control:** These approaches attempt to control the entire robot as a single entity, often leveraging motion capture (MoCap) data for reference trajectories. While theoretically capable of natural, coordinated motions, they face challenges with high degrees of freedom (DoF), leading to low training efficiency and insufficient exploration. Crucially, MoCap data inherently lack information about force interactions, limiting the policy's ability to learn active force-exertion behaviors crucial for industrial tasks.
 - **Decoupled Modular Control:** To mitigate the high-dimensional optimization challenges of monolithic control, these frameworks separate the robot into upper-body (manipulation) and lower-body (locomotion) modules. While offering advantages in training efficiency, they typically rely on inverse kinematics (IK) for upper-body control, which is computationally intensive for high-DoF manipulators and struggles with real-time requirements and generalization in RL contexts. Furthermore, existing lower-body methods primarily focus on *passive adaptation* to disturbances rather than *active, proactive force exertion* on the environment, a fundamental requirement for heavy-load tasks. The coordination between these decoupled modules also remains a significant challenge.

This paper directly addresses the critical limitations of existing decoupled RL approaches. It aims to develop a framework that not only provides robust locomotion and dexterous manipulation but, crucially, incorporates *proactive force interaction capabilities* for high-load tasks and ensures *robust whole-body coordination* to maintain end-effector stability during locomotion. By integrating these elements, the authors seek to bridge the gap between current research capabilities and the demands of real-world industrial applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to enable humanoid robots to effectively perform labor-intensive industrial applications that require both robust locomotion and proactive force interaction with the environment, capabilities that existing loco-manipulation methods largely lack. Current humanoid capabilities, while advanced in dexterous, low-load manipulation, fall short in scenarios involving high payloads, bimanual handling of heavy components, operating large-scale tools, or transporting materials with carts. These tasks introduce significant dynamic coupling between upper-body force exertion and lower-body stability, making whole-body coordination exceptionally challenging.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Enhance Upper-body Training Efficiency and Performance:** To overcome the limitations of direct RL in high-dimensional continuous action spaces and the computational intensity of inverse kinematics, the authors aim to design a method that accelerates policy convergence and improves end-effector tracking accuracy for the upper body.
2. **Endow Lower Body with Proactive Force-Capable Skills:** To move beyond passive adaptation to external disturbances, a core objective is to enable the robot's lower body to actively exert and regulate interaction forces with the environment, a crucial skill for high-load industrial tasks like pushing or pulling heavy objects.
3. **Achieve Robust Whole-Body Coordination:** Within a decoupled control framework, ensuring that lower-body movements do not destabilize upper-body tasks (e.g., maintaining a stable end-effector pose during locomotion) is vital. The goal is to develop a mechanism that counteracts vertical end-effector displacements in the world frame caused by lower-body motion.
4. **Validate a Comprehensive Loco-Manipulation Framework:** The overarching objective is to integrate these specialized capabilities into a cohesive RL-based framework that can demonstrate robust performance in demanding high-payload loco-manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel reinforcement learning-based framework with a **decoupled three-stage training pipeline** for force-capable humanoid loco-manipulation, implemented on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot (29 actuated joints: 15 lower-body, 14 upper-body). The problem is formulated as a goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP).

The core of the methodology lies in the design and coordination of three distinct policies:

1. **Heuristic Upper-body Policy Optimization (π_u):**
 - **Goal:** Achieve precise end-effector pose tracking in the torso frame, crucial for manipulation tasks.
 - **Challenge:** Direct RL over high-dimensional continuous joint spaces is inefficient. Inverse kinematics (IK) is computationally expensive and less compatible with RL generalization.
 - **Approach:** A **heuristic reward function (R_u)** is designed, implicitly embedding forward kinematics (FK) priors.
 - `R_u` combines a **joint-space term (r_joint)** and a **task-space term (r_pose)**.
 - `r_joint` measures the discrepancy between current and target joint positions, guided by target joint angles sampled to ensure feasible poses.
 - `r_pose` directly captures the difference between current and target end-effector poses (Cartesian position and orientation).
 - **Dynamic Weighting and Scaling:** Weighting factors (α_joint, α_pose) and a scaling parameter (σ) are dynamically adjusted. Initially, `r_joint` is weighted higher, and σ is gradually reduced to focus on joint tracking and reduce exploration in sparse task-space rewards. As the policy converges, `r_pose` weight is increased for precise task-space tracking.
 - **Data Quality:** End-effector poses are sampled using Algorithm 1, which filters for physically feasible positions and orientations, and a mirror trick is applied for left-right symmetry.

2. **Force-Capable Lower-body Curriculum Learning (π_l):**
 - **Goal:** Endow the robot with robust locomotion capabilities and the ability to *proactively exert and regulate interaction forces* with the environment.
 - **Challenge:** Integrating force-capable training directly can hinder basic locomotion learning if target forces are too high initially.
 - **Approach:** A **force-based curriculum learning strategy** is developed.
 - An additional **force target (f*)** is introduced in the lower-body goal space, with its direction opposite to the robot's linear velocity.
 - The magnitude of `f*` is sampled from a dynamic range using a specific strategy (Equation 10), which includes periods of zero force, uniformly sampled forces, and maximum force. This prevents the agent from forgetting low-force capabilities after training on high forces.
 - During training, an equal and opposite force is applied to the robot's torso in simulation to make the training effective, adhering to Newton's third law.
 - **Curriculum Progression:** The upper bound of the force (`f_max_t`) is gradually increased only when the agent satisfies multiple evaluation metrics (`C`) related to linear velocity tracking (`C_lin`), angular velocity tracking (`C_ang`), distance traveled (`C_dis`), and successful attainment of the maximum sampled force (`C_force`). This ensures that the agent masters basic locomotion before progressing to higher force exertion.

3. **Whole Body Control via Delta-command Policy (π_h):**
 - **Goal:** Achieve robust whole-body coordination, specifically maintaining the end-effector's height and roll-pitch orientation constant in the *world frame*, thereby mitigating disturbances caused by lower-body motions.
 - **Challenge:** Decoupled upper-body control, primarily in the torso frame, is susceptible to lower-body movements, making world-frame end-effector stability difficult.
 - **Approach:** A **high-level delta-command policy** is introduced.
 - Unlike `π_u` and `π_l`, `π_h` does not directly produce motor actions. Instead, it generates a *compensatory offset* (`a_h_t`) for the upper-body policy's tracking objectives.
 - `a_h_t` represents the desired corrections for the end-effectors' z-axis height and roll-pitch orientation in the world frame.
 - This offset is added to the baseline upper-body goal (`G_u_ref`), effectively modifying the upper-body's target pose (`G_u_t`) to include world-frame stabilization. The xy-plane displacement and yaw angle are still controlled relative to the torso frame by `G_u_ref`.

**Overall Training and Execution:**
- **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used.
- **Simulation:** IsaacSim is employed for training and evaluation.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Domain randomization (Table III) is incorporated during training to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap, including variations in friction, mass, joint positions, and external pushes/forces.
- **Reward Functions:** All three agents are trained with regularization-based reward functions (Table II), promoting smooth, stable, and collision-free behaviors.
- **Hardware Deployment:** The learned policies are deployed in Just-In-Time (JIT) mode on the robot's onboard Jetson Orin NX, with inference implemented in C++ via ONNX Runtime for real-time performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluations, including ablation studies in simulation and real-world experiments on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of the proposed framework.

**A. Ablation Studies:**

- **Upper-body Training Pipeline:**
 - Comparison of four groups (optimal σ, poor σ, w/o pose, w/o joint) revealed that the **heuristic reward function, combining both joint-space and task-space terms with dynamically adjusted `σ` and weighting factors, achieved the best performance.**
 - The "optimal σ" group, which finely tunes the scaling parameter `σ`, showed the lowest position and orientation errors (0.03 m and 0.11 rad respectively), indicating rapid convergence and superior tracking.
 - "Poor σ" (with undesirable `σ` update steps) resulted in significantly higher errors (0.17 m, 1.32 rad), highlighting the importance of proper `σ` modulation.
 - "W/o pose" (only joint-space term) showed reasonable position accuracy but degraded orientation tracking (0.04 m, 0.21 rad), as small joint errors propagate.
 - "W/o joint" (only task-space term) reduced orientation error quickly but slowly converged on position error (0.05 m, 0.11 rad), lacking kinematic guidance.
 - **Conclusion:** The heuristic reward effectively embeds kinematic information, accelerating policy convergence and improving end-effector performance.

- **Lower-body Training Pipeline:**
 - Comparison between agents trained "with curriculum" and "w/o curriculum" learning.
 - **With curriculum learning, the policy converged as early as iteration 8000**, demonstrating efficient skill acquisition.
 - Without curriculum learning, no convergence was observed within the entire training horizon.
 - **Conclusion:** Curriculum learning, by progressively introducing force targets after basic locomotion skills are acquired, substantially accelerates and stabilizes the training process. The specific sampling strategy (Equation 10) also proved critical in maintaining generalization across varying force levels, preventing the agent from specializing only in high-force scenarios (as illustrated in Figure 3).

**B. Delta-command Policy Effectiveness:**

- In simulation, the delta-command policy **reduced the oscillation amplitude of the end-effector's height by 57%** (from 3.5 cm to 1.5 cm) during locomotion, effectively mitigating vertical disturbances caused by torso motion. This demonstrates its crucial role in maintaining world-frame end-effector stability.

**C. Real-world Experiments (Unitree G1):**

- **Upper-body Pose Tracking:** Qualitative results (Figure 7a) using long-exposure photography of LED-equipped end-effectors showed clear and accurate tracking of "T" and "J" shaped trajectories, indicating robust upper-body manipulation capabilities.
- **Payload Accommodation and End-effector Stabilization:**
 - The robot successfully maintained a stable standing posture while holding a 3.9 kg camera gimbal (Figure 7c), demonstrating the lower-body's ability to generalize to additional upper-body loads and the upper-body's capacity to accommodate payloads after domain randomization.
 - When a 4 kg payload was attached to the end-effector, the delta-command policy significantly reduced the downward displacement compared to without the policy (Figure 7b), proving its efficacy in counteracting payload-induced deviations.
 - The delta-command policy effectively maintained the end-effector's desired height (0.9 m) and orientation in the world frame even when the robot performed a 10 cm squat (Figure 7d), demonstrating its ability to compensate for lower-body induced movements.
- **High-payload Cart-Manipulation Tasks:**
 - **Cart-Pushing:** The robot successfully pushed a cart loaded with 112.8 kg (six 18.8 kg water buckets). It demonstrated the proactive force-capable skill by actively applying force (up to 40 N) to initiate movement and then transitioning to commanded velocity tracking (up to 0.8 m/s). It also applied a reverse counterforce to stop the cart (Figures 8a, 9a).
 - **Cart-Pulling:** Similarly, the robot pulled the 112.8 kg cart, applying a pulling force of 35 N, followed by velocity tracking (up to -0.4 m/s). A brief counterforce was also applied for stopping (Figures 8b, 9b).
 - **Observation of Posture Adaptation:** An interesting finding was the difference in leaning postures: a forward lean during pushing was not maintained during subsequent movement, while a backward lean during pulling was maintained, suggesting an efficient adaptation strategy for pulling with smaller active forces.
 - **Conclusion:** The coordinated integration of all three policies enabled the Unitree G1 to perform complex, high-payload loco-manipulation tasks in a real-world setting, demonstrating strong sim-to-real transfer and robust performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in its potential to bridge the gap between academic research and practical industrial applications. The proposed kinematics-aware multi-policy reinforcement learning framework offers several key contributions and implications:

1. **Enabling High-Load Industrial Applications:** The most impactful contribution is demonstrating a humanoid robot's capability to perform high-payload tasks such as pushing and pulling a 112.8 kg cart and carrying a 4 kg object. This directly addresses a critical limitation of previous methods, which primarily focused on low-load, dexterous manipulation. This breakthrough opens doors for humanoid deployment in labor-intensive industrial settings, including material handling, assistance in large-scale tool operation, and logistical support in automated production environments where human-like morphology is advantageous.
2. **Proactive Force Interaction:** The development of a force-based curriculum learning strategy is pivotal. By enabling the lower body to *actively exert and regulate forces* rather than merely passively adapting to disturbances, the robot gains a fundamental skill required for physical interaction with heavy objects and dynamic environments. This proactive capability expands the robot's operational envelope significantly beyond simple locomotion or impedance control.
3. **Enhanced Upper-body Control and Efficiency:** The heuristic reward function, which implicitly embeds forward kinematics priors, represents a novel and effective way to accelerate RL training for high-DoF manipulators. This approach leads to faster convergence and superior end-effector tracking performance, mitigating the challenges associated with high-dimensional action spaces and the computational burden of traditional inverse kinematics.
4. **Robust Whole-Body Coordination:** The introduction of the delta-command policy is an ingenious solution to a common problem in decoupled loco-manipulation frameworks: maintaining end-effector stability in the world frame despite lower-body movements. By generating compensatory offsets for upper-body targets, the policy ensures precise control of manipulation tasks even during dynamic locomotion, which is crucial for safety and reliability in industrial settings.
5. **Strong Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The successful deployment of the trained policies on the physical Unitree G1 robot without additional fine-tuning, across diverse and challenging tasks, highlights the robustness of the training methodology, especially the comprehensive domain randomization techniques employed. This validates the practical applicability of the framework.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust, efficient, and highly practical framework for humanoid loco-manipulation that significantly advances the state-of-the-art. By addressing critical limitations in force interaction, training efficiency, and whole-body coordination, it brings humanoid robots closer to becoming versatile and indispensable tools in demanding industrial environments. Future work focusing on expanding the robot's force control capabilities beyond simple forward/backward directions will further enhance its utility and versatility.
