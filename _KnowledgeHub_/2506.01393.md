# Improved Regret Bounds for Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound in Bayesian Optimization

> [!summary] Summary

## Problem

- Existing theoretical regret bounds for GP-UCB in the Bayesian setting were looser than those of other state-of-the-art Bayesian optimization algorithms.
- The standard analysis of GP-UCB relied on worst-case maximum information gain, which did not fully capture the algorithm's specific behavior.
- A recognized gap existed in the theoretical understanding of GP-UCB's efficiency for popular kernel functions like Matérn and Squared Exponential.

## Method

- A novel analytical framework was introduced to refine ==information gain bounds== by considering GP-UCB's specific query point distribution.
- Cumulative regret was decomposed into two terms: lenient regret for highly suboptimal points and regret for points close to the optimum.
- The maximum information gain was bounded within a shrinking, localized region around the objective function's maximizer, reflecting GP-UCB's search strategy.

- GP-UCB achieved an improved high-probability cumulative regret of "O(sqrt(T))" for Matérn kernels, under the condition "2

- For Squared Exponential kernels, the algorithm demonstrated an improved regret bound of "O(sqrt(T ln^2 T))", reducing the logarithmic dependency on input dimension d.
- The refined analysis positions GP-UCB's theoretical performance closer to the best-known bounds for other Bayesian optimization algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GP-UCB's exploration strategy naturally leads to query points concentrating around the function's maximizer, impacting information gain differently than worst-case scenarios.
> - A component of the regret, termed "lenient regret" (from significantly suboptimal queries), can be bounded by a constant factor for GP bandits.
> - Analyzing information gain within a dynamically shrinking domain around the optimum enables tighter regret bounds that reflect the algorithm's local search behavior.
