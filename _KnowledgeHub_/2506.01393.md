# Improved Regret Bounds for Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound in Bayesian Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers improved the theoretical regret bounds for the Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound (GP-UCB) algorithm in Bayesian optimization by developing a refined analysis that accounts for the algorithm's query point concentration. This approach yields an "O(sqrt(T))" cumulative regret for Matérn kernels (under 2ν+d ≤ ν²) and "O(sqrt(T ln^2 T))" for Squared Exponential kernels.

## Problem

- Existing theoretical regret bounds for GP-UCB in the Bayesian setting were looser than those of other state-of-the-art Bayesian optimization algorithms.
- The standard analysis of GP-UCB relied on worst-case maximum information gain, which did not fully capture the algorithm's specific behavior.
- A recognized gap existed in the theoretical understanding of GP-UCB's efficiency for popular kernel functions like Matérn and Squared Exponential.

## Method

- A novel analytical framework was introduced to refine ==information gain bounds== by considering GP-UCB's specific query point distribution.
- Cumulative regret was decomposed into two terms: lenient regret for highly suboptimal points and regret for points close to the optimum.
- The maximum information gain was bounded within a shrinking, localized region around the objective function's maximizer, reflecting GP-UCB's search strategy.

## Results

- GP-UCB achieved an improved high-probability cumulative regret of "O(sqrt(T))" for Matérn kernels, under the condition "2ν+d ≤ ν²".
- For Squared Exponential kernels, the algorithm demonstrated an improved regret bound of "O(sqrt(T ln^2 T))", reducing the logarithmic dependency on input dimension d.
- The refined analysis positions GP-UCB's theoretical performance closer to the best-known bounds for other Bayesian optimization algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GP-UCB's exploration strategy naturally leads to query points concentrating around the function's maximizer, impacting information gain differently than worst-case scenarios.
> - A component of the regret, termed "lenient regret" (from significantly suboptimal queries), can be bounded by a constant factor for GP bandits.
> - Analyzing information gain within a dynamically shrinking domain around the optimum enables tighter regret bounds that reflect the algorithm's local search behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Bayesian optimization (BO), particularly focusing on the theoretical performance guarantees of algorithms in the Gaussian process (GP) bandit setting. Bayesian optimization is a technique for optimizing black-box functions that are expensive to evaluate, often by constructing a probabilistic model of the function (typically a Gaussian Process) and using it to guide the search for the optimum. A core aspect of theoretical analysis in this field involves quantifying the cumulative regret, which measures the sum of differences between the optimal function value and the values observed at queried points over a series of steps.

The Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound (GP-UCB) algorithm, introduced by Srinivas et al., is a prominent and widely used algorithm in Bayesian optimization due to its conceptual simplicity and general theoretical framework. It operates on the principle of optimism in the face of uncertainty, selecting points that maximize an upper confidence bound on the function value. This algorithm has been a foundational element in the advancement of BO.

Historically, the theoretical understanding of GP-UCB's performance in the Bayesian setting (where the function is drawn from a known GP) has not significantly advanced beyond the initial bounds provided by Srinivas et al.. These existing bounds were known to be looser compared to those achieved by other state-of-the-art algorithms, such as the successive elimination-based algorithm proposed by Scarlett, which achieved a cumulative regret of $O(\sqrt{T} \ln T)$. This discrepancy highlighted a gap in the theoretical understanding of GP-UCB's true performance capabilities in the Bayesian setting. While frequentist analyses of GP bandits have seen improvements, the Bayesian counterpart for GP-UCB remained tethered to its initial bounds.

This work specifically addresses this theoretical gap by providing a refined analysis of the GP-UCB algorithm in the Bayesian setting. It aims to demonstrate that GP-UCB can achieve regret bounds comparable to or better than the previous state-of-the-art for certain kernel functions, thereby enhancing the theoretical standing of this widely used algorithm. The research builds upon foundational elements of GP bandit theory, including information gain, and integrates insights from related works, such as the analysis of lenient regret in GP bandits and input partitioning techniques, while establishing a distinct approach tailored to GP-UCB's specific behavior.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the existing regret upper bounds for the Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound (GP-UCB) algorithm in the Bayesian optimization setting. This setting assumes that the objective function is drawn from a known Gaussian process (GP) and observations may be subject to Gaussian noise.

The motivation for this work stems from a recognized disparity in the theoretical performance guarantees of GP-UCB. The initial regret upper bound for GP-UCB, established by Srinivas et al., was expressed as $O(\sqrt{\beta_T T \gamma_T(X)})$. For commonly used kernel functions, this translated to $O(\sqrt{T \ln^{d+2} T})$ for the Squared Exponential (SE) kernel and $O(T^{\frac{\nu+d}{2\nu+d}})$ (hiding polylogarithmic factors) for the Matérn kernel. These bounds were observed to be suboptimal when compared to other algorithms, notably the $O(\sqrt{T} \ln T)$ cumulative regret achieved by Scarlett's successive elimination algorithm. This gap suggested that the existing analysis might not fully capture the intrinsic efficiency of GP-UCB.

The key objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Improve Matérn Kernel Regret Bound:** To demonstrate that GP-UCB, under a Matérn kernel with a specific degree of smoothness (precise condition $2\nu+d \le \nu^2$), can achieve a cumulative regret of $O(\sqrt{T})$ (hiding polylogarithmic dependence) with high probability. This aims to strictly improve upon the existing $O(T^{\frac{\nu+d}{2\nu+d}})$ bound for GP-UCB.
- **Improve Squared Exponential Kernel Regret Bound:** To establish an improved cumulative regret bound of $O(\sqrt{T \ln^2 T})$ for GP-UCB when utilizing a Squared Exponential kernel. This objective seeks to improve the $O(\sqrt{T \ln^{d+2} T})$ upper bound provided by previous analyses, particularly by removing the dimension ($d$) dependence from the logarithmic factor.
- **Develop Refined Analytical Techniques:** To introduce a novel analytical approach that allows for a more refined analysis of the GP's information gain. The motivation here is to move beyond the worst-case information gain bounds by leveraging the specific concentration behavior of the input sequence generated by the GP-UCB algorithm. This involves capturing how GP-UCB's query points tend to cluster around the maximizer of the objective function, which can lead to a slower growth of information gain compared to algorithms designed to maximize information spread.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to ascertain whether GP-UCB's theoretical performance could be competitive with, or superior to, alternative algorithms by refining its regret analysis, thereby providing a more accurate understanding of its efficiency in Bayesian optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a rigorous theoretical analysis, building upon the established framework of GP-UCB while introducing novel techniques to refine the regret bounds.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Existing Framework

The work operates within the standard Bayesian optimization setting. The objective function $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ (where $\mathcal{X} = [0, r]^d$) is assumed to be drawn from a known zero-mean Gaussian Process $GP(0, k)$, where $k$ is either a Squared Exponential (SE) or Matérn kernel. Observations $y_t = f(x_t) + \epsilon_t$ are noisy, with $\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. The learner's goal is to minimize cumulative regret $R_T = \sum_{t=1}^T (f(x^*) - f(x_t))$, where $x^*$ is the global maximizer of $f$.

The GP-UCB algorithm (Algorithm 1) selects query points $x_t$ at each step by maximizing the acquisition function $\mu(x; X_{t-1}, y_{t-1}) + \beta_t^{1/2} \sigma(x; X_{t-1})$, where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the GP posterior mean and standard deviation, respectively, and $\beta_t$ is a confidence width parameter.

The existing analysis of GP-UCB by Srinivas et al. decomposes the regret. It leverages two high-probability events: a confidence bound ensuring the true function lies within the GP-UCB confidence interval, and a control over discretization error for continuous input domains (via a Lipschitz condition on the GP sample path, Lemma 1). This leads to a regret bound primarily dependent on the sum of posterior standard deviations: $\sum_{t=1}^T \sigma(x_t; X_{t-1})$. This sum is then upper-bounded using the maximum information gain (MIG) $\gamma_T(X)$, defined as $\sup_{x_1..., x_T \in \mathcal{X}} \frac{1}{2} \ln \det(I_T + \sigma^{-2} K(X_T, X_T))$. The standard analysis results in $R_T = O(\sqrt{\beta_T T \gamma_T(X)})$.

Crucially, the standard analysis uses the worst-case MIG, $\gamma_T(X)$, which assumes an optimal selection of query points to maximize information gain (e.g., as in the Maximum Variance Reduction (MVR) algorithm).

#### 3.2. Novel Approach: Refining Information Gain Bounds

The core of the improved methodology lies in observing that GP-UCB's input sequence $X_T$ does not necessarily maximize information gain across the entire domain. Instead, GP-UCB tends to concentrate its queries around the objective function's maximizer $x^*$. This concentration leads to a lower *realized* information gain $I(X_T)$ compared to the worst-case $\gamma_T(X)$. The new analysis aims to capture this algorithm-dependent behavior.

The proof strategy involves:

1. **Regret Decomposition:** The total cumulative regret $R_T$ is decomposed into two terms based on a sub-optimality threshold $\epsilon$:
 - $R_T^{(1)}(\epsilon) = \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}(\epsilon)} (f(x^*) - f(x_t))$, where $\mathcal{T}(\epsilon) = \{t \in [T] \mid f(x^*) - f(x_t) > \epsilon\}$. This represents "lenient regret" where the queried points are significantly suboptimal.
 - $R_T^{(2)}(\epsilon) = \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}^c(\epsilon)} (f(x^*) - f(x_t))$, where $\mathcal{T}^c(\epsilon) = [T] \setminus \mathcal{T}(\epsilon)$. This represents regret from points close to the optimum.

2. **Analysis of $R_T^{(1)}(\epsilon)$ (Lenient Regret):** This term is shown to be $O(1)$ (hiding polylogarithmic factors) for both kernels (Lemma 6). This part adapts techniques from Cai et al., which demonstrated that lenient regret in GP bandits can be bounded by a constant regardless of $T$. The intuition is that if a point is significantly suboptimal ($f(x^*) - f(x_t) > \epsilon$), the algorithm should explore points that reduce this uncertainty quickly, limiting the number of such "bad" queries.

3. **Analysis of $R_T^{(2)}(\epsilon)$ (Close-to-Optimum Regret):** This is the main contribution. For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, points $x_t$ such that $f(x^*) - f(x_t) \le \epsilon$ are guaranteed to lie within a small L2-ball around the unique maximizer $x^*$ (Lemma 20). This crucial property relies on regularity conditions of the GP sample path (Lemma 2), which states that the GP has a unique maximizer and exhibits local quadratic behavior around it.
 - To bound $R_T^{(2)}(\epsilon)$, the summation is further analyzed using a dyadic decomposition of time steps. This involves iteratively bounding the number of "bad" queries in shrinking time intervals.
 - For each interval $T_i = T/2^{i-1}$, the regret is bounded by $O(\sqrt{T_i \cdot \gamma_{T_i}(\mathcal{B}_2(r_i; x^*))})$, where $\gamma_{T_i}(\mathcal{B}_2(r_i; x^*))$ is the maximum information gain *within a shrinking ball* $\mathcal{B}_2(r_i; x^*)$ of radius $r_i$ around $x^*$. The radius $r_i$ itself depends on the previous regret bounds, making it shrink as time progresses.
 - This approach refines the information gain bound by considering it within a restricted, shrinking domain where the queried points are known to concentrate, rather than the entire input space $\mathcal{X}$.

4. **Information Gain on Restricted Domains (Appendix B):** A significant technical contribution is the derivation of new upper bounds for maximum information gain $\gamma_T(\mathcal{X})$ when $\mathcal{X}$ is a ball of radius $\eta$.
 - The authors show how to reduce the problem of bounding MIG on a $d$-dimensional ball to bounding MIG on a $(d+1)$-dimensional hypersphere (Lemma 9). This transformation is key because the eigensystem of dot-product kernels on hyperspheres (involving spherical harmonics) is analytically tractable, avoiding the need for assumptions like uniform boundedness of eigenfunctions that previous works sometimes relied upon.
 - By leveraging Mercer decomposition and the properties of spherical harmonics (Lemma 14, Addition Theorem), explicit bounds for eigenvalues and their decay rates are derived for SE (Lemma 17) and Matérn (Lemma 19) kernels.
 - These refined MIG bounds explicitly depend on the radius of the input domain $\eta$, which is crucial for the analysis of $R_T^{(2)}(\epsilon)$ where the effective input domain shrinks.

By combining the specific $O(1)$ bound for $R_T^{(1)}(\epsilon)$ and the refined, shrinking-ball-dependent bounds for $R_T^{(2)}(\epsilon)$, the paper establishes the improved overall regret bounds for GP-UCB.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The central contribution of this research is the establishment of improved high-probability cumulative regret bounds for the GP-UCB algorithm in the Bayesian optimization setting, under specific kernel functions. These results significantly narrow the theoretical gap between GP-UCB and other advanced BO algorithms.

The main findings are formally presented in **Theorem 3**:

- **For Matérn Kernel:** When using a Matérn kernel $k_{\text{Matérn}}$ with a smoothness parameter $\nu$ such that the condition $2\nu + d \le \nu^2$ holds (where $d$ is the input dimension), the GP-UCB algorithm achieves a cumulative regret of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ with high probability.
 - The notation $\tilde{O}(\cdot)$ indicates that polylogarithmic factors (in $T$) are hidden.
 - This result strictly improves upon the previous $O(T^{\frac{\nu+d}{2\nu+d}})$ upper bound for GP-UCB. For example, if $\nu \to \infty$, the original bound approaches $O(\sqrt{T})$, but for finite $\nu$ and $d$, the exponent $\frac{\nu+d}{2\nu+d}$ is greater than $1/2$. The new bound demonstrates that GP-UCB can achieve a $T^{1/2}$ dependence, matching the best-known dependence for other algorithms like Scarlett's, up to polylogarithmic factors.

- **For Squared Exponential (SE) Kernel:** When utilizing a Squared Exponential kernel $k_{\text{SE}}$, GP-UCB achieves a cumulative regret of $O(\sqrt{T \ln^2 T})$ with high probability.
 - This bound improves the existing $O(\sqrt{T \ln^{d+2} T})$ upper bound provided by Srinivas et al. for any $d \ge 1$. The key improvement here is the removal of the dimension $d$ from the exponent of the logarithmic factor, reducing it to $\ln^2 T$.

The improved bounds are achieved by a refined analysis that captures the concentration behavior of the input sequence generated by GP-UCB around the objective function's maximizer. This allows for the use of tighter, algorithm-dependent information gain bounds, rather than the more pessimistic worst-case information gain over the entire input domain. The proof relies on decomposing the regret into two parts and bounding the maximum information gain within a shrinking local region around the optimum, leveraging specific regularity conditions of GP sample paths.

The hidden constants in these bounds may depend on various parameters, including $\ln(1/\delta)$ (for the high-probability guarantee), the input dimension $d$, smoothness parameter $\nu$, lengthscale $\ell$, domain radius $r$, noise variance $\sigma^2$, and sample path-dependent constants (e.g., $c_{\text{sup}}, c_{\text{gap}}, \rho_{\text{quad}}, c_{\text{quad}}$) from Lemma 2.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the theoretical understanding of Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound (GP-UCB) in Bayesian optimization, with several implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Theoretical Advancement for GP-UCB

The primary significance is the substantial improvement of the regret upper bounds for GP-UCB. By demonstrating that GP-UCB can achieve $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ and $O(\sqrt{T \ln^2 T})$ cumulative regret for Matérn and Squared Exponential kernels, respectively, this work shows that GP-UCB's theoretical performance is much closer to, and in some aspects matches, the state-of-the-art regret bounds established by other more complex algorithms (e.g., Scarlett's $O(\sqrt{T} \ln T)$ bound). This fills a recognized gap in the theoretical literature and enhances GP-UCB's standing as a theoretically efficient algorithm, beyond its practical popularity.

A notable aspect of this improvement is that it is achieved *without* requiring the GP-UCB algorithm itself to be modified or to incorporate prior information about the sample path constants (e.g., $c_{\text{gap}}, c_{\text{quad}}$) into its execution. This contrasts with some other algorithms that achieve similar bounds, which might require such prior knowledge, making the improved analysis of the standard GP-UCB algorithm particularly impactful for practitioners.

#### 5.2. Insights into Algorithm Behavior

The core idea behind the improved bounds—leveraging the algorithm-dependent concentration of query points around the maximizer to refine information gain bounds—provides a deeper insight into why GP-UCB performs effectively in practice. This mechanism differentiates GP-UCB's actual information acquisition process from the worst-case maximum information gain often assumed in prior analyses, suggesting a pathway for more precise analyses of other bandit algorithms.

#### 5.3. Potential for Near-Optimality

Based on the existing $\Omega(\sqrt{T})$ lower bound for expected regret in Bayesian optimization, the $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ high-probability regret bound for the Matérn kernel suggests that GP-UCB might be near-optimal in the Bayesian setting, up to polylogarithmic factors. This raises the prospect for future research to rigorously establish this optimality, potentially by extending lower bounds to high-probability settings.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Research Directions

The paper also transparently discusses several limitations, which point towards important avenues for future research:

- **Smoothness Condition for Matérn Kernel:** The $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ result for the Matérn kernel requires an additional smoothness condition ($2\nu+d \le \nu^2$). Relaxing this condition while maintaining the improved bound is a challenge for future work, possibly requiring stronger regularity conditions on the GP sample path.
- **Extension to Expected Regret:** The derived high-probability bounds depend on sample path regularity constants whose dependence on the confidence level ($\delta_{GP}$) is not explicitly known. This prevents a direct extension to expected regret guarantees, which are typically achieved by dynamically decreasing $\delta_{GP}$ with $T$. Addressing this limitation would require further analysis of GP sample path properties or alternative quantification methods for regularity.
- **Applicability to Other Algorithms:** The analytical techniques rely on specific properties of GP-UCB, such as its sub-linear cumulative regret on any index subset and a high-probability lenient regret bound. Applying these methods to other major Bayesian bandit algorithms (e.g., Thompson Sampling, Information Directed Sampling) would require verifying or establishing similar properties for those algorithms.
- **Instance-Dependent Analysis in Frequentist Settings:** While the current work focuses on the Bayesian setting, the authors suggest that the proposed techniques could be beneficial for instance-dependent analysis in the frequentist setting, potentially yielding $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ instance-dependent regret for GP-UCB by replacing sample path conditions with specific growth conditions of the function.

In summary, this research provides a significant theoretical advancement for GP-UCB, solidifying its position as a highly efficient algorithm in Bayesian optimization. It also lays a robust foundation and outlines clear directions for further theoretical exploration in the field.
