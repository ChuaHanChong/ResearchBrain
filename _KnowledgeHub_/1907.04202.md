# Variational Inference MPC for Bayesian Model-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Panasonic Corp. and Ritsumeikan University developed Variational Inference MPC (VI-MPC), a unified Bayesian framework for stochastic Model Predictive Control, and an instance called Probabilistic Action Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PaETS). This method explicitly models multimodal uncertainty in optimal action trajectories, achieving competitive asymptotic performance against state-of-the-art model-free methods like SAC while retaining high sample efficiency (requiring 10x fewer interaction steps).

## Problem

- Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) methods, despite high sample efficiency, often suffer from model-bias and exhibit suboptimal asymptotic performance compared to model-free approaches.
- Existing uncertainty-aware MBRL primarily focuses on dynamics uncertainty, neglecting the explicit modeling of multimodal uncertainties inherent in optimal action trajectories.
- Stochastic Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, such as CEM and MPPI, have been derived independently, lacking a unified theoretical framework and often underestimating the diversity of optimal actions.

## Method

- Introduced Variational Inference **==MPC==** (VI-MPC), a unified Bayesian framework that reformulates various stochastic MPC methods as a moment matching problem for the optimal trajectory posterior.
- Proposed Probabilistic Action Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PaETS), a specific VI-MPC instantiation that models the optimal action distribution using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to capture multimodal optimal trajectories.
- Incorporated an entropy regularization term into the VI-MPC objective function to encourage broader action exploration and prevent the variational distribution from collapsing to a single mode.

## Results

- PaETS demonstrated superior asymptotic performance over existing MBRL baselines (PETS and MPPI) across all evaluated MuJoCo locomotion tasks (HalfCheetah, Ant, Hopper, Walker2d).
- The method achieved performance comparable to or surpassing the state-of-the-art model-free method, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), while maintaining about **10x** higher sample efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM with M>1) for action distribution was crucial for significant performance improvements, with entropy regularization providing additional, task-dependent benefits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified Bayesian framework, like VI-MPC, can generalize existing stochastic MPC methods, providing a clearer theoretical understanding and enabling systematic algorithm development.
> - Explicitly modeling multimodal uncertainty in optimal action trajectories (e.g., using GMMs) is critical for enhancing the asymptotic performance of MBRL to match or exceed model-free counterparts.
> - Entropy regularization, when combined with a multimodal action distribution, promotes diverse exploration of the state-action space, leading to more robust dynamics models and improved overall control performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC), addressing critical challenges in making intelligent systems more efficient and robust.

**MBRL and its Challenges:** MBRL methods aim to learn a model of the environment's dynamics, which is then used for planning and decision-making. A significant advantage of MBRL over model-free reinforcement learning (MFRL) is its superior *sample efficiency*, meaning it requires considerably less interaction with the real environment to learn. This is particularly valuable in robotics and real-world applications where data collection can be expensive, time-consuming, or even dangerous. However, MBRL traditionally suffers from the "model-bias problem," where the learned dynamics model might overfit to limited data, leading to suboptimal asymptotic performance compared to state-of-the-art MFRL methods.

**Uncertainty-Aware MBRL:** Recent advancements have focused on incorporating uncertainty into dynamics models to alleviate the model-bias problem. Techniques like Bayesian inference with Gaussian Processes, dropout as variational inference, or neural network ensembles have shown promise. Probabilistic Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PETS) stands out as a leading example, employing an ensemble of neural networks to model probabilistic dynamics and using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) for trajectory optimization within an MPC framework. PETS demonstrated that uncertainty-aware dynamics modeling can make MBRL competitive with top-tier MFRL methods like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) in simulated robotics tasks, particularly in terms of performance and sample efficiency.

**The Identified Gap:** Despite the success in modeling dynamics uncertainty, the authors highlight a crucial unaddressed area: the lack of explicit uncertainty awareness in the *trajectory optimization* process itself. Existing stochastic optimization methods used in MPC (e.g., CEM, MPPI, CMA-ES) primarily focus on finding a single optimal trajectory or policy parameters. They tend to underestimate uncertainty in optimal actions and trajectories, especially in environments with multimodal optimal solutions. Furthermore, these methods have often been derived independently, lacking a unified theoretical framework.

**Contribution to the Landscape:** This work directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel framework that not only incorporates uncertainty into the dynamics model (following the success of ensemble methods like PETS) but also explicitly accounts for multimodal uncertainties in the *optimal trajectories*. By reformulating various stochastic MPC methods within a unified Bayesian framework and introducing a variational inference approach, the paper aims to enhance the asymptotic performance of MBRL further, thereby pushing its competitiveness against MFRL methods while maintaining its inherent sample efficiency. It extends the "control as inference" paradigm by applying variational inference to the action space itself, rather than just using it to define optimality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:
1. **To introduce a novel Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, called Variational Inference MPC (VI-MPC), which generalizes and reformulates existing stochastic MPC methods in a unified Bayesian fashion.** This aims to provide theoretical clarity and a systematic approach to understanding and developing advanced MPC strategies.
2. **To propose a specific instance of the VI-MPC framework, named Probabilistic Action Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PaETS), capable of effectively involving multimodal uncertainties in optimal trajectories.** This instance directly targets the limitation of current uncertainty-aware MBRLs that primarily focus on dynamics uncertainty, neglecting the uncertainty inherent in optimal action sequences, particularly when multiple (sub-)optimal paths exist.

These objectives are driven by several motivations:
- **Enhancing MBRL Performance:** While MBRL offers significant sample efficiency, its asymptotic performance often lags behind model-free methods due to model inaccuracies. Incorporating uncertainty has been shown to mitigate this, but further improvements are needed. The authors believe that extending uncertainty awareness to optimal trajectories can bridge this performance gap.
- **Addressing Overlooked Uncertainty:** Existing stochastic trajectory optimization methods tend to find a single optimal solution and often underestimate the diversity and uncertainty present in optimal action sequences, especially in complex, non-linear environments where multiple plausible paths might exist.
- **Unifying Stochastic MPC Methods:** The independent derivation of methods like CEM, MPPI, and CMA-ES has obscured their theoretical relationships. A unified Bayesian framework, as proposed through VI-MPC, can provide a clearer understanding and facilitate systematic extensions.
- **Exploration and Sample Diversity:** Explicitly modeling multimodal optimal trajectories encourages active exploration of the state-action space, leading to more diverse training datasets. This, in turn, can further reduce model-bias and improve the robustness and generalization capabilities of the learned dynamics model.
- **Practical Applicability:** MPC is highly valued in industrial applications (e.g., manufacturing, autonomous vehicles, robotics) due to its explainability and ability to handle constraints. Improving its robustness and performance through Bayesian MBRL makes it more suitable for real-world deployments by companies like Panasonic.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology builds upon the "control as inference" framework, viewing Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) as a Bayesian inference problem. The approach involves a novel two-stage inference process: first for the forward dynamics model, and then for the optimal action trajectories.

#### 3.1. Dynamics Posterior Inference (p_D(θ))

The paper adopts the successful strategy from previous works, particularly PETS, for inferring the dynamics posterior `p_D(θ)`. This involves using an *ensemble of deep neural networks*. Each network in the ensemble `θ_i` is trained independently using stochastic gradient descent. This ensemble approach is chosen because it effectively captures multimodal uncertainty in the exact posterior, unlike methods such as dropout as variational inference, which tend to approximate the posterior with a single Gaussian distribution and can underestimate multimodality, as supported by the authors' preliminary experiments (Appendix A).

#### 3.2. Variational Inference MPC (VI-MPC) Framework

The core contribution is the introduction of VI-MPC, a general framework that reformulates various stochastic MPC methods (like CEM and MPPI) in a unified Bayesian fashion.
- **Reformulating Trajectory Optimization:** The authors observe that general stochastic optimization methods can be interpreted as *moment matching* of the optimal trajectory posterior `p(τ|O)`.
- **Variational Inference Objective:** VI-MPC aims to find a variational distribution `q_θ(τ)` that minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to the true posterior `p(τ, θ|O)`. By assuming `q_θ(τ) = q(a)p(s|a, θ)p_D(θ)`, this minimization problem is transformed into maximizing an objective function `E[log p(O|τ) - log q(a)]`.
- **Entropy Regularization:** A crucial aspect is the introduction of an entropy regularization term (`-log q(a)`), which encourages the variational action distribution `q(a)` to be broader and more diverse, thus capturing uncertainty more effectively. A tunable hyperparameter `α` (later absorbed into `λ` and `κ`) is introduced to control the weight of this regularization, transforming the objective to `E[log p(O|τ) - α log q(a)]`.
- **Update Law:** Using mirror descent, the authors derive an iterative update law for `q(a)`, allowing for systematic generalization of existing stochastic MPC methods based on their choice of `f(·)` (optimality definition), `q` (variational distribution model), and `κ` (entropy regularization weight). The framework is notationally represented as `VIMPC(<optimality def>, <variational dist>, <max ent>)`.
- **Optimality Likelihood Heuristic:** Based on empirical observations (Appendix B), the authors choose to use `W'(a) = f(E[r(τ)])` for the optimality likelihood instead of `W(a) = E[f(r(τ))]`. This heuristic, which "underestimates the optimality likelihood if an action generates uncertain trajectories," paradoxically led to better optimization performance in their preliminary tests, suggesting an "uncertainty avoidance" behavior.

#### 3.3. Probabilistic Action Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PaETS)

PaETS is a concrete instantiation of the VI-MPC framework designed to explicitly handle multimodal uncertainties in optimal trajectories.
- **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for q(a):** Instead of a single Gaussian, PaETS models the variational action distribution `q(a)` as a GMM with `M` components: `q(a) = Σ π_m N(a; μ_m, Σ_m)`. This allows `q(a)` to capture multiple modes of optimal actions.
- **Iterative GMM Parameter Update:** The paper derives an iterative scheme (Algorithm 1) to update the GMM parameters `φ = {(π_m, μ_m, Σ_m)}` based on weighted samples.
 1. **Action Sampling:** `K` actions are sampled from the current GMM `q(a; φ)`.
 2. **Trajectory Sampling and Reward Evaluation:** For each sampled action `a_k`, `P` trajectories are sampled from the learned dynamics ensemble `p_D(θ)`, and their rewards are evaluated to compute `W'(a_k)`.
 3. **Weighted Particle Update:** The weights of the sampled actions `w_k` are updated using the derived VI-MPC update law (Equation 8), incorporating `W'(a_k)` and the entropy regularization term based on `q(a_k)`.
 4. **GMM Parameter Estimation:** The GMM parameters `φ` are then updated (Equation 10), effectively fitting the GMM to these weighted particles using an approach akin to the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm.
- **MBRL Integration (Algorithm 2):** PaETS is integrated into the iterative MBRL loop, where `p_D(θ)` is periodically inferred, and the GMM for actions is optimized at each time step. The GMM's covariance matrices and mixture coefficients are reset at each new time step, promoting exploration (`warm-start`).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The authors claim that using GMM (M > 1) does not significantly increase computational complexity compared to PETS (M=1), as the additional operations are parallelizable and generally faster than the trajectory sampling bottleneck.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PaETS focuses on comparing its performance against state-of-the-art MBRL baselines and a leading MFRL method across several challenging MuJoCo locomotion tasks.

#### 4.1. Comparison to State-of-the-art Methods

- **Superior Asymptotic Performance:** PaETS consistently demonstrates better asymptotic performance than existing MBRL baselines, specifically PETS (`VIMPC('CEM', 'GMM(M=1)', False)`) and MPPI (`VIMPC('MPPI', 'GMM(M=1)', False)`), across all evaluated tasks (HalfCheetah, Ant, Hopper, Walker2d).
- **Competitive with MFRL:** PaETS achieves performance that is either comparable to or outperforms the state-of-the-art model-free method, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC). This is a significant finding, as bridging the asymptotic performance gap between MBRL and MFRL has been a long-standing challenge.
- **High Sample Efficiency:** Critically, PaETS maintains the inherent sample efficiency of MBRL, requiring significantly fewer interaction steps (about 10x less) with the environment compared to SAC to reach its performance levels.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to isolate the contributions of PaETS's two main components: the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) and entropy regularization (`κ`).
- **GMM (M > 1) is Crucial:** The results clearly indicate that using a GMM (M=5) for action distribution (`VIMPC(*, 'GMM(M=5)', *)`) leads to a *significant improvement* in episode reward compared to using a single Gaussian (M=1), which is essentially the PETS baseline. This strongly validates the hypothesis that capturing multimodality in optimal trajectories is vital for enhancing MBRL performance.
- **Entropy Regularization (κ > 0) is Beneficial, but Task-Dependent:** The effect of entropy regularization is positive but comparatively smaller than that of the GMM. It helps improve performance in certain tasks by encouraging `q(a)` to maintain multimodality and explore actions with lower probabilities, thereby preventing degeneration to a single mode. However, its optimal value `κ` shows task dependency.
- **Sensitivity to M:** Further analysis on the HalfCheetah task revealed that setting `M=5` for the GMM achieved the highest performance. While a larger `M` theoretically allows capturing more multimodality, practical constraints like a finite number of sampled actions `K` can lead to degradation if `M` is too large, as the GMM components may not be sufficiently approximated by the available samples.

#### 4.3. Diversity Analysis of Training Data (Appendix I)

- A t-SNE visualization of collected state-action pairs demonstrated that PaETS gathers a more diverse training dataset (indicated by a higher "cover ratio") compared to PETS and dropout-based methods. This supports the idea that modeling multimodal optimal trajectories encourages broader exploration of the state-action space, which is beneficial for reducing model-bias and improving model generalization.

#### 4.4. Preliminary Experiments (Appendices A and B)

- The preliminary experiment (Appendix A) confirmed that neural network ensembles are significantly more effective for dynamics uncertainty modeling than (α-)dropout methods in MBRL, justifying the authors' choice to use ensembles for `p_D(θ)`.
- The comparison between `W` and `W'` (Appendix B) showed that the heuristic `W'(a) = f(E[r(τ)])` (which avoids uncertainty) yielded higher rewards than `W(a) = E[f(r(τ))]` for both CEM and MPPI, validating its inclusion in the PaETS framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper makes significant contributions to the fields of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning and Model Predictive Control, with both theoretical and practical implications.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Advancements

- **Unified Bayesian Framework for MPC:** The introduction of the Variational Inference MPC (VI-MPC) framework is a major theoretical contribution. By systematically generalizing and reformulating various stochastic MPC methods (CEM, MPPI, etc.) within a unified Bayesian framework, it provides a clearer theoretical understanding of their underlying principles and relationships. This unification can streamline the development of new, more sophisticated MPC algorithms.
- **Multimodal Uncertainty in Optimal Trajectories:** The work extends the concept of uncertainty awareness beyond dynamics modeling to explicitly include multimodal uncertainty in *optimal action trajectories*. This addresses a critical, previously underexplored aspect in stochastic MPC, recognizing that optimal control policies can be inherently diverse in complex environments.
- **Entropy Regularization in MPC:** The explicit incorporation of entropy regularization into the MPC objective function, coupled with a multimodal action distribution, is a novel approach. This encourages exploration and prevents the optimization from prematurely converging to a single, potentially suboptimal, mode.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact and Enhanced MBRL Capabilities

- **State-of-the-Art MBRL Performance:** PaETS pushes the boundaries of MBRL, demonstrating competitive (or superior) asymptotic performance compared to leading model-free methods like SAC, while retaining its hallmark of significantly higher sample efficiency. This is crucial for making MBRL a more viable and attractive option for real-world robotics and control problems where data collection is expensive or impractical.
- **Robustness and Exploration:** By capturing multimodal optimal trajectories and using entropy regularization, PaETS naturally encourages broader exploration of the state-action space. This leads to more diverse training data (`D`), which can result in more robust and generalizable dynamics models and policies, ultimately improving system reliability.
- **Simplicity and Computational Efficiency:** The authors highlight that PaETS is a "simple extension" of general stochastic methods, incurring "no significant additional computational complexity." This ease of implementation and efficient operation makes it appealing for practical deployment in real-time control systems. The ability to parallelize GMM updates on GPUs further supports its practical feasibility.

#### 5.3. Broad Applicability and Future Directions

- **Generalizability:** The VI-MPC framework and PaETS are designed with generality in mind. The concept can be applied beyond the specific context of deep reinforcement learning to traditional MPC with deterministic dynamics, or advanced MPC with latent dynamics (e.g., from pixels using Deep Planning Networks). This broad applicability expands its potential impact across various control domains.
- **Combinatorial Optimization:** The paper suggests extending the framework to combinatorial optimizations by employing a categorical mixture model as the variational distribution, opening avenues for discrete MPC applications.
- **Future Research:** The work identifies several exciting future research directions:
 - **Meta-Learning Specifications:** Inferring optimal VI-MPC specifications (like optimality definition and regularization weight `κ`) as latent variables within the Bayesian framework, adapting them dynamically to specific tasks.
 - **Parallel Tempering for Diversity:** Integrating concepts like parallel tempering from Markov Chain Monte Carlo to adaptively vary "temperatures" (or `λ` in their context) of ensemble actions, further enhancing ensemble diversity.
 - **Real-time Implementations:** Further optimization for real-time applications in robotics, building on the success of prior MPPI implementations.

The collaboration with Panasonic underscores the practical drive behind this research, suggesting that these advancements are geared towards real-world impact in advanced control systems and robotics, where explainability, robustness, and sample efficiency are paramount.
