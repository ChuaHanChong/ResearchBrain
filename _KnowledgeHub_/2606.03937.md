# Entropy Is Not Enough: Unlocking Effective Reinforcement Learning for Visual Reasoning via Vision-Anchored Token Selection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University developed VEPO, a framework that integrates vision-sensitivity with token entropy to address the failure of traditional entropy-based token selection in reinforcement learning for visual reasoning. This method, using Jensen–Shannon Divergence and absolute entropy gap, achieved an average accuracy of 58.82% at 7B-scale, outperforming full-token baselines by +1.71 points and showing competitive performance against significantly larger models.

## Problem

- Traditional entropy-based token selection, effective in text-only RL tasks, collapses when applied to visual reasoning, often underperforming full-token baselines.
- This failure stems from the omission of critical, vision-sensitive tokens which frequently exhibit low entropy as visual evidence can resolve ambiguities.
- Existing multimodal RL approaches struggle to systematically measure and integrate visual signals with token entropy for effective credit assignment in complex visual reasoning.

## Method

- **VEPO** employs a dual pass: a standard rollout with the original image and a counterfactual pass with a noise-perturbed image to obtain distinct next-token distributions.
- Token-level visual dependency is quantified using ==Jensen–Shannon Divergence== (JSD) to measure distributional shifts and Absolute Entropy Gap (|∆H_t|) to capture changes in predictive uncertainty due to visual perturbation.
- A joint ==vision-entropy score== is computed by multiplicatively combining these visual signals with normalized token entropy, enabling the selection of tokens that are both visually grounded and semantically informative for policy gradient updates.

## Results

- VEPO-7B achieved an average accuracy of **58.82%** on visual reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the strongest entropy-only baseline by +2.28 points and the full-token GRPO baseline by +1.71 points.
- The method demonstrated consistent improvements on a 3B-scale model and showed competitiveness with several open-source multimodal models that are 4 to 10 times larger.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three components (JSD, absolute entropy gap, and policy entropy) are complementary and crucial for VEPO's performance, with visual signals contributing most significantly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The entropy-only token selection mechanism, while effective in text-only contexts, fails in visual reasoning because visually-grounded tokens often inherently exhibit low entropy due to visual disambiguation.
> - Visual dependency can be systematically captured at the token level by measuring distributional shifts (JSD) and changes in predictive uncertainty (|∆H_t|) when visual input is perturbed.
> - Effective credit assignment in multimodal reinforcement learning requires explicitly coupling visual sensitivity with token entropy, rather than solely relying on semantic uncertainty for identifying critical reasoning steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs): effective credit assignment across individual tokens in long reasoning trajectories. In text-only RL, a common empirical observation is that a small fraction of high-entropy tokens, often termed "forking tokens," are crucial for exploration and provide the majority of positive gradient signals for policy improvement. This entropy-driven mechanism has proven effective in tasks like mathematical reasoning.

However, the generalization of this mechanism to multimodal contexts, specifically visual reasoning, has remained an open question. Visual reasoning inherently requires intertwining precise perceptual grounding with semantic reasoning, which introduces complexities not present in text-only tasks. Existing multimodal RL methods have acknowledged the importance of visual perception but often either lack systematic ways to measure visual signals (e.g., similarity, KL Divergence) or fail to recognize that token entropy primarily reflects semantic exploration, which visual information may not effectively resolve. Prior work has shown that in visual contexts, visually-grounded tokens might exhibit low entropy because visual evidence can disambiguate predictions, making them more confident. This creates a potential misalignment where critical visual tokens are overlooked by an entropy-only selection mechanism.

This paper positions itself by diagnosing this specific failure mode of entropy-based token selection in visual reasoning. It then proposes a novel framework that systematically integrates visual sensitivity with token entropy, aiming to bridge the gap between semantic exploration and perceptual grounding in multimodal reinforcement learning. By doing so, it attempts to develop a more effective credit assignment strategy tailored for complex visual reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective reinforcement learning framework for visual reasoning that can accurately identify and assign credit to critical tokens. This framework aims to overcome the limitations of traditional entropy-based token selection mechanisms when applied to multimodal reasoning tasks involving visual input.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and identified deficiencies:

1. **Collapse of Entropy-Only Mechanism in Visual Reasoning**: Preliminary studies conducted by the authors demonstrate that the widely recognized entropy-based token selection mechanism, while successful in text-only tasks (such as mathematical reasoning), collapses when applied to visual reasoning. Specifically, selecting high-entropy tokens in visual reasoning consistently underperforms a full-token baseline and offers only marginal improvements over random selection. This indicates that token entropy alone is insufficient for identifying critical reasoning decision points when visual evidence is present.

2. **Omission of Vision-Sensitive Tokens with Low Entropy**: The underlying cause of this collapse is identified as the inherent omission of genuinely vision-sensitive tokens that naturally exhibit low entropy. Visual evidence can often reduce ambiguity and sharpen predictive distributions, leading to lower entropy for tokens that are critical for grounding textual reasoning in visual input. A purely entropy-based selection mechanism, by prioritizing high-entropy tokens, consequently overlooks these crucial, but low-entropy, visually grounded tokens. Quantitative analysis showed that top-entropy selection misses a substantial proportion (e.g., 40% at a 20% retention ratio) of tokens flagged by visual dependency metrics like Jensen–Shannon Divergence (JSD) and absolute entropy gap.

3. **Inadequacy of Existing Multimodal RL Approaches**: Current multimodal RL methods, despite acknowledging the importance of visual perception, are perceived as struggling to satisfy the demand for interleaving precise perceptual grounding with semantic reasoning. They either lack systematic measurements for visual signals or fail to appropriately integrate visual information with token entropy, which remains a key driver for semantic exploration in reasoning chains.

In response to these motivations, the research proposes VEPO, a framework designed to explicitly couple vision-sensitivity with token entropy during credit assignment. The goal is to ensure that policy updates are directed toward tokens that are simultaneously well-grounded in the visual input and semantically informative for driving exploration and accurate reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, VEPO (Vision-Entropy token-selection for Policy Optimization), is an effective reinforcement learning framework designed to integrate visual sensitivity with token entropy for policy optimization in visual reasoning tasks. The methodology can be broken down into several stages:

#### 3.1. Share Generation (Rollout and Counterfactual Pass)

For each training iteration, the policy model (`π_θ`) first generates responses (`o = (o_1..., o_T)`) given a query (`q`) and the original image (`I`). This is the standard rollout phase. Concurrently, a "counterfactual forward pass" is performed. A noise-perturbed image (`I'`) is created by applying Gaussian noise to the original image. For each token position `t` in the generated response, two full-vocabulary next-token distributions are obtained:
- `P_t(·)`: Conditioned on the original image `I`.
- `Q_t(·)`: Conditioned on the noise-perturbed image `I'`.
The discrepancy between `P_t` and `Q_t` is used to isolate the effect of visual corruption.

#### 3.2. Token-Level Visual Sensitivity Measurement

To quantify the visual dependency of each token, two complementary signals are computed:
- **Jensen–Shannon Divergence (JSD_t)**: This measures the distributional shift between `P_t` and `Q_t`. `JSD_t` is defined as `(1/2) * D_KL(P_t || M_t) + (1/2) * D_KL(Q_t || M_t)`, where `M_t = (1/2) * (P_t + Q_t)`. The JSD is a bounded, symmetric measure that quantifies how much the next-token prediction depends on the visual input, serving as an "epistemic component" capturing reducible disagreement across visual conditions.
- **Absolute Entropy Gap (|∆H_t|)**: This characterizes the change in the model's token predictive uncertainty due to visual perturbation. `H_t` is the Shannon entropy of `P_t`, and `H_mask_t` is the entropy of `Q_t`. The entropy gap `∆H_t = H_mask_t - H_t`. The absolute value `|∆H_t|` is used as a direction-agnostic signal, indicating how strongly visual corruption changes predictive uncertainty at position `t`. This measures the magnitude of "condition-wise aleatoric change."

These two visual signals are theoretically interpreted as complementary, capturing different aspects of predictive change under visual perturbation, neither determining the other alone.

#### 3.3. Joint Vision-Entropy for Token Selection

A token-level vision-entropy score (`c_t`) is defined to select tokens that are both visually dependent and highly informative.
- **Normalization**: `JSD_t`, `|∆H_t|`, and the original token entropy `H_t` are min-max normalized per response to `ˆj_t`, `|d∆H_t|`, and `ˆh_t`, respectively, scaling them to ``.
- **Joint Visual-Dependency Score (g_t)**: The normalized visual signals are combined using a multiplicative coupling: `g_t = 1 - (1 - ˆj_t)^α * (1 - |d∆H_t|)^(1-α)`. Here, `α ∈` balances the contributions of `ˆj_t` and `|d∆H_t|`. This multiplicative form acts as a soft probabilistic OR, ensuring that tokens with strong signals along either visual-sensitivity axis are captured, and saturating when either cue is strong.
- **Vision-Entropy Token Selection Score (c_t)**: This `g_t` score is then modulated by the normalized entropy `ˆh_t`: `c_t = g_t * ˆh_t`. This multiplicative interaction ensures that credit is directed toward tokens that are simultaneously visually grounded (high `g_t`) and informative (high `ˆh_t`).
- **Token Selection Mask (C_t)**: From the calculated `c_t` scores for all tokens in a response, a binary mask `C_t` is generated by selecting the top-`k` fraction of tokens. Only tokens where `C_t = 1` are retained for the policy gradient update.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Enhanced Visual Reasoning (Policy Optimization)

The standard GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization) loss is adapted to incorporate the token selection mask. The `L_VEPO` loss is computed as:
`L_VEPO = (Σ_t=1^T ℓ_t * m_t * C_t) / (Σ_t=1^T m_t * C_t)`
where `ℓ_t` is the per-token clipped policy gradient loss, and `m_t` is the standard response mask. By multiplying with `C_t`, VEPO concentrates gradient updates exclusively on the selected "visual forking tokens," aiming to improve both perceptual grounding and semantic exploration.

The policy model used for experiments is Qwen2.5-VL-7B/3B-Instruct, fine-tuned on a custom dataset derived from Geometry3K and MMK12, and evaluated on both in-domain and out-of-domain visual reasoning benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key findings through preliminary experiments, main results, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Experiments

- **Entropy Mechanism Collapse**: A controlled study showed that the entropy-based token selection mechanism, effective in text-only math reasoning (e.g., AIME’24), collapses in visual reasoning. On math reasoning, selecting a small fraction of high-entropy tokens (e.g., 10%-20%) outperformed both random selection and full-token training. However, on visual reasoning tasks, this mechanism underperformed the full-token baseline and showed only marginal gains over random selection, suggesting its inability to identify critical reasoning decision points when visual evidence is present.
- **Deficiency of Visual Signals**: Analysis revealed that many tokens highly dependent on visual input (indicated by high JSD or high absolute entropy gap `|∆H_t|`) tend to concentrate in the low-entropy region. This is because visual evidence can resolve ambiguities, leading to more confident (lower entropy) predictions. At a 20% token retention ratio, a purely entropy-based selection recovered only approximately 60% of these visually-sensitive tokens, indicating a significant representational gap.

#### 4.2. Main Results

- **Superior Performance of VEPO**: VEPO demonstrated leading performance on visual reasoning tasks. At the 7B-scale, VEPO achieved an average accuracy of 58.82%, significantly outperforming the strongest entropy-only baseline (Top 20% high entropy GRPO) by +2.28 points and the full-token GRPO baseline by +1.71 points. Consistent gains were observed across various benchmarks, especially in-domain tasks like Geo3K (+3.99) and MMK12 (+4.71).
- **Comparison with Visual-focused RL Methods**: VEPO consistently surpassed other representative visual-focused RL fine-tuning methods, including VPPO, PAPO-DAPO, NoisyRollout, and R1-ShareVL. This indicates that the explicit coupling of visual sensitivity with token entropy is more effective than approaches that treat visual signals in isolation or lack systematic visual measurements.
- **Scalability**: Evaluation on the 3B-scale Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct model yielded consistent improvements, with VEPO outperforming the Top 20% high entropy GRPO baseline by +3.15 points, confirming its scalability.
- **Competitiveness with Larger Models**: VEPO-7B matched or exceeded the performance of several open-source multimodal models that are 4 to 10 times larger, showcasing its efficiency and effectiveness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Contribution of Signals**: Removing any of the three signals (JSD, absolute entropy gap `|∆H_t|`, or policy entropy `ˆh_t`) from VEPO's token selection mechanism resulted in degraded average performance, confirming their complementary roles. The visual signals (JSD and `|∆H_t|`) contributed more significantly to performance, while entropy `ˆh_t` was particularly important for benchmarks dominated by long textual reasoning chains.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: The optimal configuration for VEPO was found to be a token retention ratio `k=0.2` and a visual sensitivity balancing coefficient `α=0.7`. This suggests that a sparser token selection is beneficial, and JSD plays a slightly more prominent role than `|∆H_t|`, but both are crucial.
- **Visual Signal Variants**: Replacing JSD with KL divergence (used in some prior works) resulted in a 0.76-point drop in average performance, suggesting JSD's bounded and symmetric nature is advantageous for token-level selection. Using `max(∆H_t, 0)` instead of `|∆H_t|` caused an 0.80-point drop, indicating that tokens whose entropy decreases upon visual disambiguation are also informative. Replacing multiplicative aggregation with additive fusion of visual signals led to a 0.69-point drop, validating the noisy-OR style multiplicative coupling.
- **Training Dynamics and Efficiency**: VEPO exhibited stable training dynamics and achieved higher rewards compared to baselines. It improved the coverage of vision-sensitive tokens (reducing the miss rate to 20% at k=0.2, compared to ~40% for top-entropy). While VEPO incurs an overhead due to the auxiliary forward pass, it was approximately 10% faster than full-token GRPO and only about 16% slower than top-entropy selection, representing a modest trade-off for the performance gains.
- **Qualitative Analysis**: Qualitative examples demonstrated that VEPO effectively identified visually-grounded content tokens (e.g., variables, operators, condition keywords linked to flowcharts) that are critical for reasoning. In contrast, entropy-only selections often focused on linguistic scaffolding (e.g., LaTeX formatting, discourse fillers) where uncertainty stemmed from linguistic variability rather than visual dependency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a significant contribution to the field of multimodal reinforcement learning, particularly for visual reasoning tasks.

**1. Acknowledging and Addressing a Critical Gap:** The paper rigorously diagnoses a previously underexplored failure mode: the collapse of the widely accepted entropy-driven credit assignment mechanism in visual reasoning. By demonstrating that vision-sensitive tokens often exhibit low entropy, the work highlights a fundamental mismatch between traditional RL principles (developed for text-only scenarios) and the unique demands of multimodal tasks. This diagnosis provides valuable insight into the complexities of credit assignment when visual information is integrated.

**2. Principled Method for Modality-Aware Credit Assignment:** The introduction of VEPO provides a principled and empirically effective solution to this problem. By explicitly integrating two complementary visual sensitivity signals (Jensen–Shannon Divergence and absolute entropy gap) with token entropy through a multiplicative coupling, VEPO allows the model to prioritize tokens that are simultaneously visually grounded and semantically informative. This approach moves beyond simply identifying uncertain tokens to identifying *meaningfully* uncertain or critical tokens in a multimodal context.

**3. Enhanced Performance and Scalability:** The experimental results demonstrate VEPO's leading performance compared to both entropy-only baselines and other visual-focused RL fine-tuning methods at comparable scales (7B and 3B models). Its ability to surpass larger open-source multimodal models underscores its efficiency and effectiveness, suggesting that a refined credit assignment mechanism can yield substantial performance gains without requiring prohibitively larger model capacities.

**4. Deeper Understanding of Multimodal Reasoning:** The work contributes to a deeper understanding of how visual and linguistic modalities interact during reasoning. The analysis of JSD and `|∆H_t|` as capturing "epistemic components" and "aleatoric changes," respectively, provides an information-theoretic interpretation of visual dependency. This theoretical underpinning, combined with empirical validation, sheds light on why different types of uncertainty (distributional shift vs. entropy change) are crucial for robust multimodal learning.

**5. Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines limitations, such as the computational overhead of auxiliary forward passes and the restricted exploration of noise variants, which could motivate future work in optimizing efficiency and robustness. It also calls for a more rigorous theoretical foundation for why visual-sensitive token selection improves multimodal RL optimization, opening avenues for theoretical advancements.

In summary, this research advances multimodal RL by providing a crucial diagnosis and a practical, theoretically-informed solution to the challenge of token-level credit assignment in visual reasoning. Its impact is likely to be seen in the development of more effective and robust multimodal models capable of sophisticated visual-linguistic understanding and generation.
