# RAGEN-2: Reasoning Collapse in Agentic RL

> [!summary] Summary
> RAGEN-2 identifies "template collapse" in multi-turn LLM agent reinforcement learning, a phenomenon where reasoning appears diverse but becomes input-agnostic. Researchers introduce mutual information (MI) as a diagnostic and propose SNR-Aware Filtering, which leverages reward variance, to mitigate this issue, leading to improved task success rates and reduced training time.

## Problem

- Training multi-turn Large Language Model (LLM) agents with reinforcement learning (RL) is challenging due to inherent instability.
- Existing metrics like entropy, while measuring within-input diversity, fail to detect if an agent's reasoning truly adapts to different inputs, leading to 'template collapse'.
- Models can rely on fixed, input-agnostic templates, causing reasoning quality degradation that current diagnostics cannot effectively capture.

## Method

- **RAGEN-2** introduces an ==information-theoretic framework== to diagnose ==template collapse==, decomposing reasoning diversity into ==input dependence (mutual information, MI)== and ==within-input diversity (conditional entropy)==.
- Template collapse is explained through a ==Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)== mechanism, where low reward variance in prompts weakens task-specific gradients, allowing input-agnostic regularization terms to dominate learning.
- ==SNR-Aware Filtering== is proposed as a mitigation strategy, which prioritizes training on prompts with higher within-input reward variance using a ==Top-p adaptive filtering== method.

## Results

- MI proxies consistently showed a significant decline early in training, confirming template collapse, whereas conditional entropy remained high or even spiked.
- **SNR-Aware Filtering** consistently improved peak task success rates across **seven** diverse environments, various RL optimizers, model scales (**0.5B** to **7B**), and input modalities.
- The filtering method added less than **0.1%** overhead for variance estimation and reduced per-step wall-clock time by **26–41%** by focusing gradient computation on high-signal prompts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Template collapse is a consistent failure mode in agentic RL, characterized by a decline in input dependence (MI) while within-input diversity (entropy) remains elevated, confirming that entropy is an insufficient diagnostic.
> - The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) of policy gradient updates is critical; low reward variance leads to weak task gradients that are overwhelmed by input-agnostic regularization, causing reasoning to become templated.
> - Mutual information-based metrics are superior to entropy for monitoring reasoning quality during training, showing a strong positive correlation with task performance and effectively predicting template collapse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical challenge in the development and training of multi-turn Large Language Model (LLM) agents using reinforcement learning (RL). Prior work has highlighted the inherent instability and difficulties associated with training these agents, often relying on metrics like reward for outcome stability and entropy for reasoning process stability. However, the authors identify a gap in existing diagnostic tools: entropy-based metrics, while measuring within-input diversity, do not effectively capture whether the agent's reasoning is actually responsive to different inputs.

The paper builds upon the understanding that LLM-agent RL can exhibit various forms of "collapse," including reasoning collapse (rationales becoming templated and less input-correspondent) and policy-level degeneracy (behavior concentrating on narrow patterns). This phenomenon echoes observations in self-training scenarios where models repeatedly trained on their own generated data can narrow their effective data distribution, amplifying certain modes and suppressing diversity.

Existing methods to stabilize RL training for LLMs, such as KL divergence constraints, entropy regularization, and various forms of reward shaping or curriculum learning, primarily focus on preventing optimization collapse or improving overall reward. However, they do not explicitly target the specific failure mode identified in this paper, where reasoning appears superficially diverse but loses its input-specific relevance. This work contributes to the landscape by introducing a novel diagnostic metric and a complementary intervention strategy that specifically addresses this overlooked aspect of reasoning quality, thereby aiming to improve the robustness and reliability of multi-turn LLM agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while reinforcement learning (RL) training of multi-turn LLM agents is known to be challenging, existing stability metrics are insufficient to diagnose a specific, detrimental failure mode. Reasoning quality is a direct determinant of task performance for these agents. Researchers commonly monitor reward for outcome stability and entropy for reasoning process stability, assuming they indicate healthy training.

However, the authors identify a significant limitation: entropy, which measures diversity within a single input, cannot detect whether the reasoning *actually adapts* to different inputs. The paper posits that models can rely on fixed templates that appear diverse on the surface for any given input but are fundamentally input-agnostic across different inputs. This phenomenon is termed **template collapse**.

The key objectives are structured around two central questions:

1. **How to diagnose template collapse?** (Q1) Existing entropy-based metrics track within-input variability but fail to capture input dependence across inputs. The objective is to develop a robust diagnostic tool that can detect this failure mode. This involves decomposing reasoning quality into two components: within-input diversity (quantified by entropy) and cross-input distinguishability (quantified by mutual information). The paper aims to introduce a family of mutual information (MI) proxies that can be used for online diagnosis without requiring external models.
2. **Why does template collapse happen?** (Q2) Understanding the underlying mechanism is crucial for developing effective mitigation strategies. The motivation here is to explain the occurrence of template collapse from a fundamental perspective. The authors hypothesize a **signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) mechanism**, where low reward variance within a prompt weakens task-specific gradients, allowing input-agnostic regularization terms (like KL divergence and entropy regularization) to dominate the learning process. This dominance erases cross-input reasoning differences, leading to templated responses.

Beyond diagnosis and explanation, a core objective is to **address and mitigate template collapse**. Based on the hypothesized SNR mechanism, the authors aim to propose a practical intervention strategy that improves both input dependence and overall task performance. This strategy, termed **SNR-Aware Filtering**, would select high-signal prompts for training based on reward variance, aiming to concentrate gradient budget on more informative updates.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more accurate diagnostics and mechanistic understanding of reasoning degradation in LLM agents, leading to the development of a framework for detecting and addressing template collapse to ensure more reliable and robust agent performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology in RAGEN-2 is structured around identifying, explaining, and mitigating "template collapse" in multi-turn LLM agent reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Diagnosing Template Collapse: Information-Theoretic Lens and Mutual Information Proxies

The paper introduces an information-theoretic framework to differentiate reasoning quality.
- **Decomposition of Reasoning Diversity:** The core idea is to decompose the marginal entropy of reasoning, $H(Z)$, into two components using Shannon's identity:
 $H(Z) = I(X; Z) + H(Z | X)$
 where $I(X; Z)$ represents **input dependence** (mutual information between input $X$ and reasoning $Z$), and $H(Z | X)$ represents **within-input diversity** (conditional entropy of reasoning given input). Existing entropy metrics primarily proxy $H(Z | X)$, thus failing to capture a decline in $I(X; Z)$. Template collapse is defined as a state where $H(Z | X)$ remains high (superficially diverse) but $I(X; Z)$ drops to zero (input-agnostic).
- **Mutual Information (MI) Proxy Family:** Since true mutual information is hard to compute for high-dimensional token sequences, an empirical proxy, $\hat{I}(X; Z)$, is proposed based on a retrieval intuition: if reasoning is input-dependent, a reasoning trace $Z$ should be more compatible with its true input $X_i$ than with other inputs $X_j$.
 - **In-Batch Cross-Scoring:** For a batch of $P$ prompts and $G$ reasoning samples per prompt, the method computes teacher-forced log-likelihoods, $\log p_\theta(Z_{i,k} | X_j)$, for every pair of a reasoning trace $Z_{i,k}$ (from prompt $X_i$) and a prompt $X_j$.
 - From this, two length-normalized quantities are derived:
 - `matched_i,k`: $\frac{\log p_\theta(Z_{i,k} | X_i)}{|Z_{i,k}|}$ (log-likelihood under the true source input).
 - `marginal_i,k`: $\frac{1}{|Z_{i,k}|} \log (\frac{1}{P} \sum_j \exp(\log p_\theta(Z_{i,k} | X_j)))$ (approximation of marginal log-likelihood via uniform mixture over batch prompts).
 - **Primary Proxies:**
 - **Retrieval-Acc (discrete):** Calculates the accuracy of correctly identifying the source prompt $X_i$ for a given reasoning trace $Z_{i,k}$ based on which $X_j$ yields the highest log-likelihood. Under collapse, this metric approaches chance level (e.g., $1/P$).
 - **MI-ZScore-EMA (continuous):** Estimated as $\frac{1}{PG} \sum_{i,k} (\text{matched}_{i,k} - \text{marginal}_{i,k})$. This approaches 0 under collapse. Z-score normalization and an exponential moving average (EMA) are applied for stability.
 - A family of other variants is explored, varying in turn scope, aggregation, and length normalization. These proxies are computed using existing training rollouts, incurring no extra model or inference cost.

#### 3.2. Explaining Template Collapse: The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) View

The paper attributes template collapse to an imbalance in the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of policy gradient updates.
- **Empirical Observation:** Analysis of gradient norms across prompts sorted by their within-input reward variance ($Var(R | X)$) revealed:
 1. Task gradient norm ($||g_{task}||$) increases monotonically with reward variance (RV). Low-RV prompts yield weak task gradients.
 2. Regularization gradient norm ($||g_{reg}||$, from KL and entropy terms) remains flat across all RV buckets.
 3. For low-RV prompts, updates are dominated by input-agnostic regularization.
- **Formalization via Gradient Decomposition:** Policy gradient updates are decomposed into a task-discriminative signal ($g_{signal}$), task-specific noise ($g_{task-noise}$), and regularization gradients ($g_{reg}$).
 - The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows that $||g_{task}(x)|| \leq \sqrt{Var(R | X=x) \cdot C}$, directly linking low reward variance to weak task gradients.
 - When $Var(R | X) \approx 0$, advantage estimates collapse, making $g_{task} \approx 0$. However, $g_{reg}$ persists, leading to updates driven primarily by input-agnostic regularization, which drives $I(X;Z) \rightarrow 0$ while $H(Z|X)$ can remain high due to entropy regularization.
- **SNR Quantification:** $SNR(x) = \frac{||g_{signal}(x)||}{||g_{task-noise}(x)|| + ||g_{reg}||}$. Low SNR pushes updates towards input-agnostic directions.

#### 3.3. Mitigating Template Collapse: SNR-Aware Filtering

Based on the SNR view, a direct mitigation strategy is proposed.
- **Principle:** Prioritize prompts with higher within-input reward variance, as these provide stronger task-discriminative information, making regularization less likely to dominate.
- **Method:** **SNR-Aware Filtering**
 - At each training iteration, estimate $Var(R | X)$ for each prompt by sampling $G$ trajectories and computing the sample variance of episode returns.
 - Retain only the top fraction of prompts by variance score, using a **Top-p filtering** strategy. This method, analogous to nucleus sampling, accumulates variance mass from the top-ranked prompts until a specified keep rate $\rho$ is reached. This adaptively selects high-signal prompts while allowing the number of kept prompts to vary based on the variance distribution.
 - The parameter updates are then computed only on this selected subset of high-signal prompts.
- **Experimental Testbed:** The method is evaluated across seven diverse environments (Sokoban, FrozenLake, MetaMathQA, Countdown, SearchQA, WebShop, DeepCoder) with varying characteristics (stochasticity, multi-turn, state type, reward density). Experiments use Qwen2.5-3B and compare with PPO, DAPO, GRPO, and Dr. GRPO algorithms, as well as different model scales and modalities (text and vision-language models). The total rollout budget is fixed, and the computational overhead of filtering is analyzed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results supporting the existence of template collapse, the efficacy of mutual information as a diagnostic, and the benefits of SNR-Aware Filtering.

#### 4.1. Template Collapse as a Consistent Failure Mode

- **MI Dynamics vs. Entropy:** Across all training configurations, RL-trained agents consistently exhibited template collapse. The mutual information (MI) proxy, $\hat{I}(X; Z)$ (measured as Retrieval-Acc), showed a significant decline early in training, often before task performance noticeably degraded. In contrast, conditional entropy, $H(Z | X)$, remained elevated or even spiked, indicating that reasoning appeared diverse within inputs but became increasingly input-agnostic across inputs. This divergence between MI and entropy confirms that entropy alone is an insufficient and potentially misleading diagnostic for reasoning quality.
- **Behavioral Manifestation:** Beyond metrics, template collapse manifested behaviorally as a systematic compression of reasoning. Across eight environments, the length of reasoning chains declined monotonically, indicating that agents converged towards shorter, more formulaic, and reusable templates.

#### 4.2. SNR-Aware Filtering Consistently Improves Performance

- **Comparison of Filtering Strategies:** When comparing different filtering strategies (Top-p, Top-k, no filtering), Top-p SNR-Aware Filtering consistently achieved higher task success rates throughout training. Top-p's adaptive selection mechanism, which adjusts the number of kept prompts based on the reward variance distribution, proved more effective than Top-k's fixed-count approach. The no-filter baseline consistently showed the weakest performance, confirming that processing low-signal prompts degrades learning.
- **Broad Generalizability:** SNR-Aware Filtering improved peak task success rates in most experimental settings. These gains were observed across:
 - **RL Optimizers:** PPO, DAPO, GRPO, and Dr. GRPO.
 - **Model Scales and Families:** Qwen2.5 (0.5B to 7B) and Llama3.2-3B.
 - **Input Modalities:** Both text-conditioned and image-conditioned Qwen2.5-VL models.
 This breadth suggests that SNR-Aware Filtering acts as a general-purpose control mechanism for SNR in agent RL.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Estimating reward variance added less than 0.1% to iteration time. Furthermore, with filtering (e.g., $\rho=0.9$), fewer groups entered gradient computation, leading to a 26–41% reduction in per-step wall-clock time. Configurations with group sizes $G \geq 4$ and SNR-Aware Filtering matched or outperformed baselines without additional compute cost.

#### 4.3. MI Diagnoses Collapse Better Than Entropy

- **Correlation with Performance:** A Spearman correlation analysis showed that MI-family metrics positively predicted task performance (e.g., Trajectory MI-ZScore reached +0.39). In contrast, Reasoning Entropy and Conditional Entropy metrics exhibited near-zero or negative correlations (between -0.11 and -0.14), validating MI as a superior training monitor.
- **Intervention Dynamics:** Sweeps across entropy regularization strength, KL constraint strength, and SNR-Aware Filtering keep rate demonstrated distinct effects. Entropy- and KL-based stabilizers primarily affected $H(Z | X)$ but rarely moved the model into a high-$\hat{I}(X; Z)$ regime with improved performance. SNR-Aware Filtering, however, showed a monotone improvement in both $\hat{I}(X; Z)$ and task success.

#### 4.4. Validation of the SNR Mechanism

- **Causal Evidence (Quartile Ablation):** Training separate runs on prompts grouped into reward variance quartiles (Q1 highest, Q4 lowest) showed that task performance and MI monotonically degraded from Q1 to Q4. This provides direct causal evidence that reward variance influences gradient quality and, consequently, input-dependent reasoning.
- **Controlled Noise Injection:** Increasing environmental stochasticity in FrozenLake predictably decreased MI, confirming that additional noise dilutes advantage estimates. The filtering advantage also attenuated at very high noise levels (80-100%), as the reward variance signal itself became less discriminative under extreme stochasticity.
- **Prompt-level vs. Trajectory-level Filtering:** Prompt-level SNR-Aware Filtering outperformed trajectory-level filtering, which only selected high-reward trajectories within prompts. This indicated that selecting prompts that *naturally* produce discriminative signals is more effective than attempting to amplify signal within inherently low-RV prompts.
- **Predicting Filtering Effectiveness:** The ratio $Std(RV)/Mean(RV)$ (computable from a single rollout batch) effectively predicted when SNR-Aware Filtering would be most beneficial. High ratios indicated bimodal RV distributions, allowing effective separation of signal-rich from noise-only prompts.
- **Adaptive Behavior:** Over training, the effective kept ratio in SNR-Aware Filtering automatically decreased, and the count of zero-variance prompts increased. This adaptive tightening of the filter concentrated updates on the shrinking pool of informative prompts.
- **Orthogonality to Entropy and Length:** Reward variance correlated weakly with conditional entropy (-0.14) and response length (0.12) but strongly with task reward (0.63). This suggests RV targets a distinct aspect of update quality, making it a complementary control knob.
- **Format Validity is Insufficient:** Format validity was largely decoupled from template collapse; runs could maintain near-perfect format validity while exhibiting low MI, reinforcing the need for content-sensitive diagnostics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research significantly impacts the understanding and development of robust LLM agents, particularly in multi-turn reinforcement learning settings.

**Identification of a Critical Failure Mode:** The explicit identification and characterization of "template collapse" as a silent yet pervasive failure mode is a key contribution. This phenomenon, where reasoning becomes input-agnostic despite appearing diverse by traditional entropy metrics, highlights a critical blind spot in existing agent training and monitoring practices.

**Novel and Effective Diagnostic Tool:** The introduction of mutual information (MI) proxies provides a much-needed, content-sensitive diagnostic for reasoning quality. By empirically demonstrating that MI correlates with task performance more reliably than entropy, this work offers a superior method for tracking the health of an agent's reasoning process during training. This enables earlier detection of degradation, allowing for timely intervention before task performance suffers significantly.

**Mechanistic Explanation:** The proposed Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) mechanism provides a clear, gradient-level explanation for why template collapse occurs. By showing that low within-input reward variance weakens task gradients, allowing input-agnostic regularization to dominate, the research offers a fundamental insight into the dynamics of agent learning. This mechanistic understanding is crucial for designing targeted interventions rather than relying on heuristic adjustments.

**Practical Mitigation Strategy:** SNR-Aware Filtering, leveraging reward variance as a lightweight SNR proxy, offers a practical and effective solution to mitigate template collapse. Its consistent performance improvements across diverse tasks, RL algorithms, model scales, and input modalities (text and vision-language) demonstrate its generalizability. The method can be integrated easily with existing training pipelines and even offers computational benefits by focusing gradient updates on more informative samples.

**Broader Implications for Agent Reliability:** By fostering more input-dependent and less templated reasoning, this research contributes to building more reliable and trustworthy LLM agents. Agents that genuinely adapt their reasoning to specific inputs are less prone to superficial responses and more capable of complex, context-aware decision-making. This is particularly important for applications requiring high levels of interpretability, robustness, and true problem-solving capabilities.

**Complementary Control Knob:** The finding that SNR-Aware Filtering is complementary to existing stabilization techniques like KL and entropy regularization (which primarily control within-input diversity) suggests a more holistic approach to agent training. It provides an additional "control knob" for practitioners to manage the quality of learning signals, leading to more stable and performant agents.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge several limitations. The SNR decomposition assumes a clean separation between task signal and regularization noise, which might not always hold perfectly. The study is confined to single-agent settings, leaving the propagation of template collapse in multi-agent RL as an open question. A potential risk is that a sufficiently capable model might learn to artificially inflate reward variance to game the filtering criterion. Additionally, the method's effectiveness relies on reward variance being a reliable signal, which may degrade in environments with sparse or excessively noisy rewards. Finally, aggressive filtering could narrow exploration coverage, necessitating per-task tuning of the keep rate.

Despite these limitations, RAGEN-2 offers valuable advancements in understanding and improving the training of LLM agents, with significant potential to enhance their reasoning abilities and overall robustness.
