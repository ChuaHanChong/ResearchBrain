# From Condensation to Rank Collapse: A Two-Stage Analysis of Transformer Training Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> This study by Chen and Luo proposes a two-stage theoretical framework detailing Transformer training dynamics under small initialization, explaining how outer parameters undergo an initial "condensation" before key-query matrices experience "rank collapse." The framework, supported by theoretical proofs and experimental validation on synthetic and real datasets, provides insights into how these models organize their parameters for effective learning.

## Problem

- A lack of comprehensive theoretical understanding regarding Transformer training dynamics beyond specific tasks or configurations, despite their widespread empirical success.
- The empirical observation that small initialization scales improve reasoning capabilities in language models, suggesting a powerful, but not fully understood, implicit regularization effect.
- Existing theoretical analyses of Transformers are often configuration-specific or task-dependent, lacking a general, architecture-centric framework.

## Method

- Developed a two-stage theoretical framework leveraging the ==gradient flow== analysis to systematically investigate linearized Transformer training dynamics under small initialization conditions.
- Proposed Stage 1, "Condensation," explaining how outer parameters (value projection, FFN weights) rapidly escape small initialization and align towards task-relevant directions.
- Introduced Stage 2, "Key-Query Rank Collapse," detailing how attention parameters (WQ, WK) become dynamically active only after outer parameters stabilize, leading to their asymptotic rank-1 structure.

## Results

- Theoretically proved that the "energy" functional related to outer parameters "blows up in finite time" from small initialization, and that the value projection matrix WV undergoes "condensation" (Theorems 1 and 2).
- Demonstrated that after the outer parameters stabilize in a quasi-stationary state, the key-query matrices (WQ, WK) become dynamically active and exhibit "asymptotic rank collapse" to rank-1 (Theorem 3).
- Experimental validation on synthetic datasets and the WikiText language modeling benchmark empirically confirmed these two-stage dynamics, showing consistent patterns of condensation and rank collapse in parameter matrices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Small initialization acts as a powerful implicit regularization, guiding the training of overparameterized Transformers towards structured, low-rank solutions through distinct phases.
> - Transformer training is characterized by a two-stage dynamic: an initial phase of "condensation" for outer parameters (WV, W[1], W[2]) followed by a delayed activation and "rank collapse" of key-query matrices (WQ, WK).
> - Condensation facilitates the alignment of outer parameter matrices towards task-relevant directions, effectively reducing their intrinsic rank and forming structured representations early in training, while rank collapse structures the core attention mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical gap in the theoretical understanding of Transformer models, which, despite their "exceptional empirical performance" across various fields including large language models, still lack a comprehensive characterization of their fundamental training dynamics. Most existing theoretical analyses of Transformers tend to be "configuration-specific" or focused "in specific tasks," such as in-context learning (ICL) settings, single attention block reparameterization, linear regression tasks, or Markov chain tasks. The authors explicitly pose a crucial question: "Can we analyze the characteristics of the training dynamics of transformers independently of specific tasks?" This positions their work as a pursuit of a more general and fundamental theory.

A significant motivation for this general analysis stems from empirical observations that "small initialization scales" improve "reasoning capabilities" in language models. This phenomenon points to the powerful role of "implicit regularization" in modern, "extremely overparameterized" models. In regimes where explicit regularization methods might be insufficient, implicit regularization provides intrinsic constraints that guide models towards generalizable solutions. The paper recognizes the "pivotal" role of this implicit bias in preventing severe overfitting and achieving remarkable performance.

In the broader landscape of related works, the paper situates itself by differentiating from prior studies on Transformer training dynamics, which largely investigated single attention layers or specific tasks like topic prediction and translation. While the emergence of ICL dynamics as a prominent area has led to analyses of Transformers in linear regression setups, and other research has explored the emergence of specific structures like induction heads, these are often task-specific. This paper aims for a more architecture-centric, task-independent analysis. It builds upon a line of research that applies the gradient flow framework, particularly in the context of "small initialization," to explain phenomena like parameter condensation and alignment in deep linear and nonlinear networks. By extending this framework, originally applied to simpler neural networks, to the more complex Transformer architecture, Chen and Luo aim to generalize existing "classical directional convergence results" and provide a unified theoretical foundation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research arises from the disconnect between the widespread empirical success of Transformer models and the limited theoretical understanding of their training dynamics. While Transformers have revolutionized various AI applications, a systematic characterization of how they learn, especially beyond specific task contexts, remains elusive.

A key empirical observation driving this study is the finding that initializing large language models with small scales can significantly enhance their reasoning capabilities. This suggests that "small initialization" acts as a powerful form of "implicit regularization." In the context of modern, extremely overparameterized models, where explicit regularization might fall short, implicit biases become crucial for guiding the model towards solutions that generalize well. The authors are motivated to explore this implicit regularization effect within the Transformer architecture.

Against this backdrop, the central objective of this work is to "systematically investigate linearized Transformer training dynamics under a small initialization setting." Specifically, the authors aim to "delineate different training dynamics for outer parameters versus attention parameters WQ and WK in Transformers." This leads them to propose and theoretically analyze a "two-stage framework" for the attention modules:

1. **Stage 1: Condensation:** To understand how models escape the initial small parameter regime and how parameters begin to organize themselves. The hypothesis is that asymmetric weight perturbations from random initialization drive non-degenerate gradient dynamics, causing "outer parameters" (like the value projection matrix, WV) to undergo "condensation," aligning towards task-relevant directions. During this stage, the core attention mechanism (softmax(QK^T)) is hypothesized to remain largely static.
2. **Stage 2: Key-Query Rank Collapse:** To explain how the attention mechanism itself becomes structured. The hypothesis is that once the outer parameters reach a quasi-steady state, the previously static key-query matrices (WQ and WK) become dynamically active. This activation is predicted to drive "asymptotic rank collapse" in their normalized forms, further refining the learned representations.

By dissecting the training process into these distinct phases, the authors seek to offer a robust theoretical foundation for Transformer optimization, independent of specific tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a rigorous theoretical approach, primarily leveraging the "gradient flow analytical framework" previously established in works like Zhou et al.. This framework analyzes the continuous-time limit of gradient descent, providing a powerful lens for studying the long-term behavior of neural network parameters.

To make the complex Transformer architecture amenable to theoretical analysis, the authors simplify the model:
- They consider a **one-layer Transformer model** for **binary classification tasks**.
- The model output is taken from the last dimension of the sequence.
- The **exponential loss function** (ℓ(q) = e−q) is used for its analytical tractability, with a note that the analysis can be extended to other loss functions like logistic loss.
- **Small Initialization:** All model parameters are initialized from Gaussian distributions scaled by a small perturbation parameter ε (ε ≪ 1). This is fundamental to their analysis, as it allows for a "perturbative expansion" of the empirical risk.

The core of the methodology involves a multi-stage theoretical analysis:

1. **Effective Dynamics Derivation:** A Taylor expansion of the empirical risk L(θ) around ε = 0 is performed to derive the "leading-order asymptotic form" of the loss. This simplified form then dictates "effective training dynamics" (Proposition 1), revealing that initial training primarily updates the fully-connected layers (W, W) and the value projection matrix (WV), while query/key matrices (WQ, WK) remain quasi-static.

2. **Blow-up Dynamics (Stage 1 Initiation):**
 - The authors first establish "conservation laws" (Proposition 2) within these effective dynamics, showing that certain quantities are preserved during training.
 - They define "non-degenerate initialization" (Definition 4), which almost surely holds for Gaussian initializations.
 - **Theorem 1 (Blow-up in finite time):** This theorem rigorously proves that, almost surely, the effective training dynamics lead to the "energy" functional (E = Wv W W) blowing up in finite time. This signifies a rapid escape from the small initialization regime, driven by non-degenerate gradient dynamics. The proof sketch involves using a Riccati-type differential inequality for E(t).

3. **Condensation Dynamics (Stage 1 Mechanism):**
 - The paper introduces a "Condensation condition" (Assumption 1) specifying positivity requirements for certain inner products and products of parameter matrices.
 - **Theorem 2 (Condensation):** Building on Assumption 1, this theorem proves that the effective dynamics drive the parameter matrix WV to undergo "condensation" (Definition 1). This means the rows (or columns) of WV align towards a specific direction, effectively reducing its rank. The proof involves analyzing geometric consistency, alignment dynamics between parameters and their derivatives, and deriving an energy upper bound to control the growth rate.

4. **Key-Query Dynamics (Stage 2 Activation):**
 - To analyze the second stage, an "Assumption 2 (Dynamics separation stage)" or its empirical variant (Assumption 2*) is introduced. This posits that after the initial blow-up, outer parameters stabilize in a quasi-stationary state, while attention parameters (WQ, WK) remain small.
 - **Proposition 3 (Effective dynamics during dynamics separation stage):** Under Assumption 2, it is shown that gradients for outer parameters become O(δ^2) while gradients for WQ and WK become O(δ), indicating their activation. The leading-order dynamics for WQ and WK are derived, forming a linear ordinary differential equation system.
 - **Theorem 3 (Asymptotic rank collapse):** This theorem proves that, based on the derived linear dynamics, the normalized key and query matrices exhibit "rank collapse" (Definition 2). Specifically, if a matrix F (derived from the data and stabilized outer parameters) possesses a unique largest singular value, both normalized WQ and WK asymptotically become rank-1.

The theoretical predictions are then validated through **experimental results**:
- **Synthetic Dataset:** A controlled "anchor function" based dataset is used with a one-layer Transformer to visually demonstrate condensation and rank collapse. Key assumptions (Assumption 1 and 2*) are empirically verified by tracking cosine similarity, relative norm changes, and effective rank of parameter matrices.
- **Real Dataset:** Experiments on the WikiText language modeling benchmark with a two-layer Transformer (incorporating GeLU and residual connections) are conducted to demonstrate the generality of the observed two-stage dynamics in a more realistic setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The central finding of this research is the elucidation of a **two-stage training dynamic** for Transformer models under small initialization, which governs the structural organization of their parameter matrices:

1. **Stage 1: Condensation of Outer Parameters (Value and FFN Weights)**
 - **Blow-up Dynamics (Theorem 1):** The initial training phase is characterized by a rapid escape from the small initialization regime. The theoretical analysis proves that, for almost all Gaussian initializations, the "energy" functional (E), which depends on the value projection matrix (WV) and the feed-forward network (FFN) weights (W, W), "blows up in finite time." This signifies a powerful, non-degenerate gradient dynamic that drives substantial parameter growth.
 - **Condensation Mechanism (Theorem 2):** Following this rapid growth, the outer parameter matrices, particularly WV, undergo "condensation." This is a process where the rows (or columns) of the matrix progressively align towards a specific, task-relevant direction, effectively reducing its intrinsic rank. This condensation is guaranteed under a proposed "condensation condition" (Assumption 1) that specifies initial alignment properties. Experimentally, this stage is confirmed by a fast decrease in training loss, outer parameters exhibiting significantly larger norm changes compared to attention parameters, the emergence of clear block structures in their cosine similarity matrices, and a steady decrease in their effective rank. Throughout this stage, the query (WQ) and key (WK) matrices of the attention mechanism remain largely static and unstructured, indicating their dormancy.

2. **Stage 2: Rank Collapse of Key-Query Matrices (Attention Activation)**
 - **Dynamics Separation (Proposition 3):** After the initial condensation, the system enters a distinct phase where the outer parameters (WV, W, W) reach a "quasi-stationary configuration." This is formalized by "Assumption 2," indicating that their gradients become significantly smaller (O(δ^2)), and their directional structure stabilizes. At this point, the previously static key-query matrices (WQ, WK) become dynamically active, with their gradients becoming dominant (O(δ)).
 - **Asymptotic Rank Collapse (Theorem 3):** This activation of WQ and WK drives their "asymptotic rank collapse." Specifically, the normalized WQ and WK matrices are shown to become rank-1 in the limit, provided a matrix F (which encapsulates dependencies on input data and the now-stabilized outer parameters) possesses a unique largest singular value. Experimentally, this stage is marked by a "prolonged plateau" in the training loss, where updates to the attention parameters begin to dominate. The plots show a sudden formation of structure in their similarity matrices and a "precipitous drop" in their effective rank, confirming the theoretical prediction of rank collapse.

**Experimental Validation:**
- **Synthetic Data:** Experiments on a synthetic dataset (using "anchor functions") visually demonstrated these two stages, along with a "further training" stage. The critical "Condensation condition" (Assumption 1) was empirically verified, showing its satisfaction rate rapidly approaching 1. The "Dynamics separation stage" (Assumption 2*) was also strongly supported, with outer parameter directions remaining extremely stable and their Frobenius norms growing substantially while attention parameters' norms stayed small.
- **Real Data:** Crucially, the same stage-wise dynamics were observed in a two-layer Transformer trained on the WikiText language modeling benchmark. This consistency across both simplified synthetic and complex real-world settings underscores the generality and robustness of the proposed two-stage framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant theoretical contribution to the field of deep learning by providing a detailed and generalized analysis of Transformer training dynamics, moving beyond task-specific or architectural-component-specific studies.

The **theoretical advancement** is multifold:
- It systematically explains how small initialization scales enable Transformers to escape degenerate initial regimes and how their parameters subsequently organize.
- The introduction and rigorous proof of "condensation" for outer parameters and "asymptotic rank collapse" for key-query matrices offer novel mechanistic insights into how Transformers develop structured representations during training.
- By integrating these phenomena within a two-stage gradient flow framework, the research generalizes and extends classical directional convergence results to the complex Transformer architecture, providing a more robust theoretical foundation.

The findings offer crucial **insights into implicit regularization**. They demonstrate how the intrinsic biases induced by small initialization guide the training process of highly overparameterized Transformer models towards low-rank, interpretable solutions. This helps to demystify why these models, despite their immense capacity, can generalize effectively and avoid severe overfitting.

The potential **impact on optimization and model design** is considerable. A deeper understanding of these two distinct training stages could inform:
- **Improved Initialization Strategies:** Tailoring initialization scales or types for different parameter groups based on their role in each stage.
- **Adaptive Optimization Algorithms:** Designing optimizers with stage-dependent learning rates or regularization techniques, for example, allowing rapid growth in Stage 1 and fine-tuning attention in Stage 2.
- **Architectural Insights:** Potentially guiding the design of more efficient or specialized Transformer variants by optimizing for the emergent low-rank structures.

Furthermore, by linking parameter organization (condensation and rank collapse) to effective training, the work provides a mechanistic bridge to understanding empirical observations, such as the **improved reasoning capabilities** of language models under small initialization. This suggests that these structural dynamics are fundamental to the acquisition of meaningful and generalizable knowledge.

While the current analysis is explicitly limited to **binary classification scenarios** due to technical complexity, as acknowledged by the authors, it provides a "principled foundation for future studies." Overcoming these limitations to extend the framework to multi-class, sequence-to-sequence, or deeper Transformer architectures with components like layer normalization would be a critical next step, bringing the theory closer to real-world LLM training practices. This work thus represents a significant step towards unifying theoretical understanding with the empirical success of Transformers, paving the way for more interpretable and robust AI systems.
