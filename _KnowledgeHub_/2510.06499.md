# Webscale-RL: Automated Data Pipeline for Scaling RL Data to Pretraining Levels

> [!summary] Summary
> Salesforce AI Research developed an automated Webscale-RL data pipeline to generate 1.2 million verifiable QA pairs from web-scale pretraining data, enabling efficient reinforcement learning for large language models. This approach enhanced general reasoning capabilities and improved data efficiency by over 100x compared to continual pretraining.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) trained via imitation learning often exhibit a 'training-generation gap' and limited robust reasoning abilities, especially when faced with distribution shifts.
- The application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to LLMs at web-scale is severely constrained by a critical data bottleneck, as existing high-quality RL datasets are vastly smaller and less diverse than pretraining corpora.
- Generating high-quality, verifiable question-answering (QA) pairs for RL training is exceptionally costly and time-consuming through traditional human annotation methods.

## Method

- An automated, multi-stage ==Webscale-RL data pipeline== was developed to systematically convert vast, unstructured web-scale pretraining documents into millions of diverse, verifiable question-answer pairs.
- The pipeline employs ==LLM-based filtering== to select high-quality documents, followed by ==domain classification== and persona assignment to enrich question diversity.
- A powerful LLM generates questions and short, verifiable answers entirely grounded in the source documents, with a subsequent LLM-based quality check for correctness and leakage prevention.

## Results

- The Webscale-RL model consistently outperformed all baselines, achieving an average improvement of **3.4 points** over the strongest baseline (GDR) across a suite of diverse benchmarks, with most pronounced gains in general knowledge and reasoning tasks.
- RL training demonstrated over a **100x** improvement in data efficiency, with approximately **10 million tokens** achieving MMLU-pro performance similar to continual pretraining with **1 billion tokens**.
- The 3B model fine-tuned with Webscale-RL narrowed the average performance gap to a larger 7B model from **10.6 points** to **6.1 points**, indicating enhanced capabilities for smaller models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automating RL data generation from existing web-scale pretraining corpora is a viable and highly effective strategy for overcoming data scarcity and improving LLM capabilities.
> - Reinforcement Learning, when supplied with web-scale, grounded data, dramatically boosts general reasoning and knowledge application across diverse domains in LLMs.
> - The approach achieves exceptional data efficiency, allowing RL training to reach comparable performance with orders of magnitude fewer tokens than traditional continual pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has largely been driven by imitation learning paradigms, such as pretraining with next-token prediction and supervised fine-tuning (SFT), on vast text corpora. While effective in many applications, this approach introduces a "teacher-forcing" dependency, which can make models susceptible to distribution shifts and create a discrepancy between training and generation dynamics. Consequently, models trained primarily through imitation learning may exhibit limitations in robust reasoning required for complex problem-solving.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an alternative capable of mitigating these issues. By training models to learn from online reward feedback on their own generations, RL can enable exploration of a wider solution space and reduce the training-inference gap. However, the application of RL at the scale of modern LLMs has faced a significant data bottleneck. Existing RL datasets are considerably smaller and less diverse than the web-scale corpora used for pretraining, typically comprising less than 10 billion tokens compared to trillions of tokens in pretraining datasets. This discrepancy is largely due to the high costs associated with generating high-quality, verifiable question-answering (QA) pairs, which are essential for effective RL-based reasoning tasks. Current RL applications in LLMs are often limited to specific reasoning tasks (e.g., math, coding) and typically occur in the post-training stage.

This research aims to address this data scarcity and diversity challenge for RL by proposing a scalable data pipeline that converts large-scale pretraining documents into an RL-ready format. This positions the work within the growing area of synthetic data generation for LLMs, but specifically focuses on preparing data for reinforcement learning at a web-scale, unlike many existing efforts that focus on smaller, specialized datasets or rely heavily on distillation from stronger teacher models for problem-solving. By bridging the data gap between pretraining and RL, the work seeks to unlock the potential for RL to enhance general reasoning capabilities and overall efficiency of LLMs at a foundational level.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to overcome the data bottleneck that constrains the scaling and effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Large Language Models (LLMs). Current LLM training, predominantly based on imitation learning (next-token prediction and supervised fine-tuning), faces inherent limitations such as vulnerability to distribution shifts and a gap between training and generation dynamics. RL presents an alternative by enabling models to learn from online reward feedback, thereby fostering exploration and reducing the training-inference gap, often with improved data efficiency.

Despite the demonstrated advantages of RL, its widespread adoption at scale is hindered by the scarcity and limited diversity of available RL datasets. While pretraining corpora contain trillions of tokens from diverse web sources, existing RL datasets are orders of magnitude smaller, typically in the range of billions of tokens, and often concentrated in narrow domains like mathematics or coding. This disparity arises from the substantial costs and complexities associated with generating high-quality, verifiable question-answering (QA) pairs, which are crucial for effective RL training, particularly for reasoning tasks. This limitation prevents RL from fully contributing to the general reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Scalable Data Pipeline**: To introduce "Webscale-RL," an automated and scalable data engine designed to systematically convert large-scale pretraining documents into millions of diverse and verifiable question-answer pairs suitable for RL training. This pipeline is intended to maintain the scale and diversity inherent in web-scale data.
2. **Construct a Large-Scale RL Dataset**: To build and present the "Webscale-RL dataset," utilizing the developed pipeline. This dataset aims to contain a significantly larger volume and broader diversity of RL-ready examples than currently available datasets, spanning multiple domains.
3. **Empirically Evaluate Performance Enhancement**: To conduct experiments demonstrating that models trained with RL on the Webscale-RL dataset achieve performance improvements over baseline methods, including continual pretraining on source data and advanced data refinement techniques, across a range of benchmarks.
4. **Assess Training Efficiency**: To provide empirical evidence that RL training with the Webscale-RL dataset is substantially more data-efficient than continual pretraining, requiring fewer tokens to achieve comparable performance gains.

Through these objectives, the research aims to establish a viable methodology for scaling RL data to pretraining levels, thereby enabling the development of more capable, robust, and efficient language models that can leverage the benefits of online learning on a broad array of knowledge and reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Webscale-RL methodology addresses the RL data bottleneck by introducing an automated pipeline that systematically converts narrative pretraining documents into verifiable question-answer (QA) pairs suitable for RL training. This approach is designed to preserve the scale and diversity of web-scale data.

The paper first outlines a fundamental distinction between pretraining and RL paradigms. Pretraining involves minimizing negative log-likelihood on a static, large-scale corpus, which can lead to distribution shift and a training-inference gap due to its teacher-forcing nature. In contrast, RL optimizes a model as a policy that generates answers online to maximize an expected reward, typically a binary reward (1 for exact match, 0 otherwise) for verifiable QA pairs, thereby narrowing the training-inference gap.

The Webscale-RL data pipeline comprises four main stages, leveraging a generative LLM (GPT-4.1) for most generative tasks and a more cost-effective LLM (GPT-4.1-mini) for classification and quality checks:

1. **Data Filtering**: This initial stage aims to remove low-quality input documents that are unlikely to yield verifiable QA pairs. It employs a two-step process:
 - **Heuristic Filtering**: Documents smaller than 50 tokens are removed.
 - **LLM-based Filtering**: An LLM identifies and removes non-informative pages (e.g., boilerplate, navigation elements) and non-self-contained fragments that lack sufficient context for answer verification. This filtering differs from previous approaches by prioritizing maximal preservation of original data diversity rather than strict multi-dimensional filtering (e.g., by difficulty or reasoning traces).

2. **Domain Classification and Persona Assignment**: After filtering, each document is classified into one of multiple predefined domains (e.g., commerce, healthcare) using an LLM-based classifier. This domain tag is crucial for selecting appropriate few-shot examples in the subsequent QA generation stage, ensuring contextual relevance. To further enhance the diversity of generated QA pairs, multiple personas (e.g., "medical expert," "patient") are assigned to each document. These personas dictate the style and perspective from which questions will be generated, aiming to capture different viewpoints and information needs from the same source material.

3. **Verifiable QA Generation**: In this stage, an LLM-based QA generator produces verifiable question-answer pairs. The generation process is conditioned on the source document, its classified domain, and the assigned persona. Few-shot examples from a domain-specific demonstration library guide the LLM. The generator is instructed to:
 - Generate questions from the perspective of the assigned persona.
 - Ensure both questions and answers are strictly grounded within the source material, avoiding external information.
 - Create questions that are self-contained, providing necessary context.
 - Require relatively short and verifiable ground-truth answers (e.g., a number, name, or phrase) directly extracted from the document, rather than elaborate explanations or distilled reasoning steps from a powerful LLM. This design choice aims to reduce generation complexity and reliance on advanced backend LLMs, enabling the use of more cost-effective models.

4. **Quality Check and Leakage Control**: To ensure the reliability of the generated RL dataset, an LLM-based verifier performs a multi-stage checking process:
 - **Correctness Verification**: Assesses whether the extracted answers are accurately grounded by the source document. This grounding-based verification is intended to be less biased by backend LLMs compared to accuracy-based post-processing.
 - **Leakage Prevention**: Checks that the question itself does not explicitly reveal the answer, ensuring the model's knowledge or reasoning capabilities are genuinely tested.
 QA pairs that fail these criteria are filtered out.

**Dataset Construction and Training Details**:
The Webscale-RL dataset was constructed by applying this pipeline to a subset of pretraining corpora (approximately 1 million documents total) including DCLM, Wikipedia, MegaMath, Stack-v2, OpenMathReasoning, and OpenCodeReasoning. The resulting dataset contains approximately 1.2 million verifiable QA pairs across more than nine domains. Data decontamination using `lm-eval-harness` was performed to remove overlaps with evaluation benchmarks. For experiments, a Qwen2.5-3B model was fine-tuned using GRPO on 150K data points from the Webscale-RL dataset, following an SFT warm-up on 10K high-quality examples. Baselines included continual pretraining on a 1M corpus and variants using data refinement techniques (QuRating, ProX, GDR), all followed by SFT. The study also compared performance against a Qwen2.5-7B base model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings regarding the characteristics of the Webscale-RL dataset and the empirical performance of models trained using the proposed pipeline.

**Webscale-RL Dataset Analysis:**
The Webscale-RL dataset, constructed through the automated pipeline, contains approximately 1.2 million verifiable QA pairs, spanning over nine distinct domains. This scale contrasts with existing RL datasets, which typically range from tens of thousands to a few million examples, often concentrated in specific areas like math or coding. The dataset's construction method, converting directly from diverse pretraining documents, grants it a high degree of scalability, indicating its potential for expansion to pretraining levels without being bottlenecked by human annotation or limited query sources characteristic of distillation-based datasets.

A comparative analysis of dataset diversity, using UMAP visualization of question embeddings (Qwen3-Embedding), shows that Webscale-RL data points are more uniformly distributed and scattered than those from datasets like Nemotron. This suggests a broader coverage of topics and knowledge areas, attributed to the varied pretraining sources and persona-driven question generation. While STEM-related domains (Math, Science, Code) are well-represented, Webscale-RL also includes substantial coverage in domains often underrepresented in existing RL datasets, such as Lifestyle (>8.6%) and Commerce (>3.3%).

**Experimental Results on Model Performance:**
Experiments were conducted by fine-tuning a Qwen2.5-3B model using GRPO on the Webscale-RL dataset, comparing its performance against several baselines: continual pretraining on the original source data, and three advanced data refinement techniques (QuRating, ProX, GDR) followed by continual pretraining. All baselines and the Webscale-RL model underwent an initial SFT phase with a 10K high-quality dataset to mitigate instruction-following biases in evaluation. Performance was assessed across a diverse set of benchmarks covering general knowledge, reasoning, math, STEM, and coding tasks.

The model trained with RL on the Webscale-RL dataset consistently outperformed all baselines across most benchmarks, demonstrating an average improvement of 3.4 points over the strongest baseline (GDR). Notably, this training allowed the 3B model to narrow the average performance gap to the Qwen2.5-7B model from 10.6 points to 6.1 points.

Specific performance gains were observed:
- **General Knowledge and Reasoning**: Significant improvements were seen on benchmarks such as MMLU-pro, Big-Bench, and GPQA-diamond, attributed to the dataset's diversity and breadth.
- **Mathematics**: A substantial increase in performance on MATH500 (from 47.6 to 58.0) brought the 3B model closer to the 7B model's score, supporting the notion that RL can effectively incentivize mathematical reasoning. Gains on GSM8K were comparatively smaller, potentially due to the base model's already strong performance.
- **Coding Tasks**: Improvements on MBPP and EvalPlus were relatively modest, which the authors suggest may reflect a lower proportion of coding-related data in the underlying pretraining corpus.

The consistent outperformance of RL over SFT-augmented continual pretraining baselines indicates that the gains are not solely from improved instruction adherence but stem from the reward-driven online learning signal inherent in RL.

**Performance Comparison of Scaling Training (Data Efficiency):**
The study also investigated the scaling efficiency of RL training compared to continual pretraining. To ensure a fair comparison on token efficiency, the number of tokens for RL training was calculated based on the size of the *original pretraining corpus* used to generate the Webscale-RL dataset, rather than the generated QA pairs themselves.

Results indicate that RL training consistently outperformed continual pretraining across MMLU-pro, Big-Bench, and average performance metrics at various training scales. Significantly, RL training demonstrated a substantial improvement in data efficiency. For example, on MMLU-pro, RL training utilizing approximately 10 million tokens (from the original pretraining corpus) achieved performance comparable to continual pretraining using 1 billion tokens, representing over a 100-fold improvement in data efficiency. This suggests that RL training not only leads to higher final performance but also scales more effectively and efficiently than standard teacher-forcing approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a systematic approach to overcome a critical limitation in the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to Large Language Models (LLMs): the scarcity and limited diversity of RL-ready data. By proposing the Webscale-RL pipeline and constructing the Webscale-RL dataset, the work establishes a viable methodology for scaling RL data to the magnitudes typically associated with pretraining corpora.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that an automated, scalable data engine can convert vast pretraining documents into millions of verifiable question-answer pairs for RL. This capability directly addresses the bottleneck that has previously confined RL applications to smaller scales or specialized domains. The Webscale-RL dataset's diversity, inherited from broad pretraining sources and enhanced by persona-driven generation, is shown to be superior to existing RL datasets, particularly in covering domains beyond typical STEM areas.

The empirical results indicate that training LLMs with RL on this web-scale, diverse dataset leads to performance improvements across a wide range of general knowledge, reasoning, and STEM benchmarks. Notably, a 3B-parameter model fine-tuned with Webscale-RL significantly narrowed the performance gap to a 7B-parameter base model, suggesting a practical pathway towards developing more capable and efficient smaller LLMs. This has implications for reducing computational resource demands and facilitating broader deployment of advanced language models.

Furthermore, the research highlights the substantial data efficiency of RL training with Webscale-RL, achieving performance comparable to continual pretraining with significantly fewer tokens—up to 100 times less in some instances. This efficiency implies reduced training costs and accelerated development cycles for future LLMs, making advanced capabilities more accessible.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Enhanced LLM Capabilities**: By enabling RL at web-scale, the work provides a mechanism for LLMs to develop more robust reasoning abilities, generalize across diverse real-world scenarios, and overcome limitations associated with pure imitation learning.
- **Cost-Effective Model Development**: The improved data efficiency of RL training can lead to more economical development and deployment of LLMs, potentially democratizing access to powerful AI models.
- **Scalability of RL**: The Webscale-RL pipeline offers a template for future research and development, allowing RL to be integrated earlier and more broadly into the LLM training lifecycle, moving beyond post-training fine-tuning.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations and outlines future research directions. The current Webscale-RL dataset exhibits smaller gains on coding benchmarks, suggesting a need for rebalancing the domain distribution of pretraining sources to enhance performance in specific areas (e.g., integrating repository-scale code data). Additionally, the reliance on a generative reward model for binary feedback, while effective, introduces substantial inference costs. Future work could explore more efficient reward models to facilitate scaling RL training to even larger models and datasets, further optimizing the overall process.
