# SANA-WM: Efficient Minute-Scale World Modeling with Hybrid Linear Diffusion Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed SANA-WM, a 2.6B-parameter open-source world model capable of generating high-fidelity, one-minute 720p videos with precise 6-DoF camera control. The system achieves visual quality comparable to larger industrial baselines while significantly reducing data, training, and inference costs, demonstrating strong performance on a new 60-second world-model benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing minute-scale, action-conditioned video generation models are computationally expensive, requiring extensive resources and multi-GPU inference, thus limiting accessibility for many researchers.
- Common distillation approaches from short-video generators are suboptimal, as teachers often lack sufficient supervision for minute-scale scene persistence and accurate trajectory following.
- Generating one-minute, 720p videos presents technical challenges in managing token counts and maintaining long-context coherence within model architectures.

## Method

- SANA-WM employs a 2.6B-parameter Hybrid Linear Diffusion Transformer, using a high-compression LTX2 tokenizer and a two-stage generation pipeline for 60-second, 720p video outputs.
- It integrates a dual-branch camera control mechanism, featuring a coarse branch (Ray-Local UCPE) for global 6-DoF pose and a fine branch (Raw-Frame Plücker Mixing) for precise intra-stride motion.
- A robust data annotation pipeline produces a 213K-clip corpus with enhanced 6-DoF pose estimation and 3D Gaussian Splatting augmentation, alongside a progressive four-stage training strategy.

## Results

- SANA-WM (with refinement) achieved RotErr of 4.50 degrees and TransErr of 1.39 on simple trajectories, and 8.34 degrees and 1.39 on hard trajectories, demonstrating superior camera control compared to baselines.
- It produced VBench Overall scores of 80.62 (simple) and 81.89 (hard) at 720p with single-GPU inference, matching visual quality of 480p models requiring eight GPUs.
- The full SANA-WM pipeline generates 60-second 720p clips at 22.0 videos per hour on a single H100 GPU, with a distilled variant achieving a 39x speedup on a single RTX 5090.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid architecture combining frame-wise Gated DeltaNet (GDN) for efficient recurrent context aggregation with standard softmax attention for exact long-range recall balances memory efficiency and scene persistence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on SANA-WM is situated within the expanding field of generative AI, specifically focusing on video generation and world modeling. World models are computational systems designed to learn and simulate environments, often used for embodied simulation and interactive applications. Recent advancements in this area have produced systems capable of generating long-duration, action-conditioned video rollouts, but these often necessitate extensive computational resources, large datasets, and multi-GPU inference.

Prior work in long-video generation has explored diffusion or flow-transformer backbones operating on compressed spatiotemporal latents, with strategies like autoregressive or block-wise rollout, diffusion forcing, and memory-aware inference to handle extended contexts. Similarly, interactive world models have diversified in their control interfaces, ranging from robot actions to keyboard controls, language commands, and camera-based movements. Camera-controlled generation is closely related to novel-view synthesis and geometric video generation, utilizing various camera representations such as extrinsics, intrinsics, epipolar constraints, Plücker raymaps, and unified positional encodings (UCPE). Addressing long-horizon consistency often involves explicit memory, spatial warping, or persistent 3D/4D scene representations.

Efficient sequence modeling for long visual horizons has seen a shift from standard softmax attention, which scales quadratically with context length, towards more efficient alternatives such as linear attention, state-space models, convolutional mixers, and delta-rule recurrences like Gated DeltaNet (GDN). Some architectures combine these efficient layers with occasional exact attention to balance recall and efficiency. High-compression tokenizers also contribute by reducing the number of visual tokens processed.

SANA-WM positions itself by addressing the challenge of natively training a high-fidelity, camera-controllable, one-minute world model while minimizing data, training, and inference costs. Instead of distilling long-rollout models from short-video generators, which may lack sufficient long-horizon supervision, SANA-WM proposes a native training approach optimized for efficiency. It integrates advancements in efficient attention mechanisms, precise camera control, and multi-stage generation, alongside a robust data annotation pipeline, to achieve minute-scale video generation with a reduced computational footprint compared to existing large-scale industrial baselines and open-source systems. This work aims to make minute-scale world modeling more accessible for academic and research purposes by reducing hardware and training requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, open-source world model capable of generating high-fidelity, one-minute (60-second) videos at 720p resolution with precise 6-DoF (degrees of freedom) camera control. This model is designed to synthesize content based on an initial frame, a text prompt, and a specified camera trajectory, while maintaining scene identity and adhering to the input motion.

The motivation behind SANA-WM stems from several identified challenges in the current landscape of long-horizon world modeling:
- **High Computational Costs:** Existing minute-scale, action-conditioned video generation systems frequently require large models, extensive datasets, prolonged training schedules, and multi-GPU inference, making them inaccessible for many researchers or smaller institutions.
- **Limitations of Distillation:** A common approach to reduce costs is to distill long-rollout models from short-video generators. However, this method is considered suboptimal because short-horizon "teachers" often provide insufficient supervision for critical aspects of minute-scale generation, such as maintaining scene persistence and accurate trajectory following over extended durations.
- **Technical Challenges of Minute-Scale Generation:** Generating one-minute, 720p videos presents significant technical hurdles related to token count management and maintaining long-context coherence within the model architecture.
- **Precise Action Conditioning:** Accurately controlling the generated video to faithfully follow continuous 6-DoF camera trajectories, rather than merely aligning with text prompts, is a complex requirement that existing methods may struggle with at scale.
- **Visual Quality Degradation:** When training a base generator under limited data and compute resources, there is a risk of degraded visual quality in the generated outputs.

To address these motivations, the SANA-WM project aimed to develop a system that achieves visual quality comparable to large-scale industrial baselines while substantially improving efficiency across data usage, training compute, and inference hardware. The overarching goal is to enable the native training of a high-fidelity, camera-controllable, one-minute world model under accessible cost constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SANA-WM is a 2.6B-parameter open-source world model developed to generate minute-long, 720p videos with precise camera control. The methodology centers on four core architectural and data pipeline designs, coupled with a progressive training strategy.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture and Efficiency Measures

The core of SANA-WM is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, which utilizes a high-compression LTX2 tokenizer to reduce the number of visual tokens. The model employs 20 transformer blocks, with a model dimension of 2240 and 20 attention heads. To manage memory and computation for minute-scale sequences, it integrates several efficiency-focused techniques:
- **LTX2-VAE:** Replaces a baseline VAE for superior spatiotemporal compression, resulting in representations 2.0x smaller than ST-DC-AE and 8.0x smaller than Wan2.1-VAE.
- **Context-Parallel (CP) Training:** Shards the latent sequence along the temporal dimension across multiple GPUs, allowing for exact GDN state recovery with minimal communication.
- **Fused Triton Kernels:** Custom high-performance kernels for operations like RMSNorm, ReLU, key scaling, UCPE/RoPE preparation, and GDN recurrent scans, reducing memory bandwidth bottlenecks and improving efficiency.
- **Zero-Initialized Projections:** For camera conditioning, zero-initialized projections are used to preserve the pretrained model at initialization, facilitating stable adaptation.
- **Distillation and Quantization:** A few-step distilled variant is developed for faster deployment, and NVFP4 quantization is applied to further enhance inference efficiency on consumer-grade hardware.
- **Attention-Sink Tokens:** For the distilled variant, attention-sink tokens and local temporal windows are used in softmax attention layers to maintain constant memory and per-chunk latency regardless of rollout length.

#### 3.2. Four Core Designs

1. **Hybrid Linear Diffusion Transformer Backbone:**
 - **Frame-wise Gated DeltaNet (GDN):** Adapts the Gated DeltaNet architecture for efficient recurrent context aggregation. Unlike token-wise GDN, SANA-WM processes one latent frame per recurrent step, maintaining a compact DxD recurrent state.
 - **Algebraic Stabilization:** Introduces a key-scaling factor (1/√(D·S)) for GDN to prevent state explosion and ensure stable convergence, crucial for minute-scale sequences where unbounded state growth can lead to drift.
 - **Combination with Softmax Attention:** The backbone interleaves 15 frame-wise GDN blocks with 5 standard softmax attention blocks (at layers 3, 7, 11, 15, 19). This hybrid design aims to balance the memory efficiency of GDN for recurrent context with the exact long-range recall capabilities of softmax attention, important for scene persistence and camera-conditioned motion.
 - **Bidirectional and Chunk-Causal Variants:** Supports both bidirectional generation (summing forward and reversed-time scans) and a chunk-causal variant for autoregressive rollout, which resets the reversed scan at chunk boundaries for local future context.

2. **Dual-Branch Camera Control:**
 - **Coarse Branch (Ray-Local UCPE):** Operates at the latent-frame rate, capturing global 6-DoF camera pose. For each latent token, a ray-local basis is constructed, and camera-branch attention-head vectors are split into geometric and standard RoPE channels, with a pose-transformed representation. This branch uses its own QKV projections but shares GDN gates with the main branch.
 - **Fine Branch (Raw-Frame Plücker Mixing):** Compensates for fine camera motion within each VAE temporal stride, where a latent token summarizes eight raw frames. Pixel-wise Plücker raymaps from raw-frame camera poses are packed into a 48-channel tensor, passed through a zero-initialized 3D patch embedder, and added to the self-attention output of each block via a zero-initialized projection. This allows for precise control despite aggressive video compression.

3. **Two-Stage Generation Pipeline:**
 - **Long-Video Refiner:** An independent refiner is applied to stage-1 SANA-WM outputs. This refiner is trained on paired latents (degraded vs. high-fidelity) using a truncated-sigma flow matching formulation, encouraging refinement over full reconstruction.
 - **Reference Conditioning:** To enhance identity and appearance consistency, a clean slice of the target latent is prepended as reference tokens, acting as fixed key-value anchors and excluded from the loss. The refiner is initialized from a 17B LTX-2 model and fine-tuned with LoRA adapters on synthetic and real videos.

4. **Robust Annotation Pipeline:**
 - **Data Sources:** Re-annotates seven open-source video sources (SpatialVID-HQ, DL3DV, OmniWorld, Sekai, MiraData) to create a 213K-clip corpus.
 - **Modified VIPE Pose Engine:** Enhances VIPE's depth estimation by replacing Metric3D-Small with Pi3X (for multi-frame consistent 3D structure) and MoGe-2 (for metric-scale anchoring). It also includes per-frame intrinsic optimization for robust internet video annotation. Dataset-specific modes are used for sources with ground-truth depth or poses.
 - **3DGS Augmentation:** For static-scene datasets like DL3DV, FCGS 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions are used to render novel one-minute videos with designed camera paths. These rendered videos are then refined with DiFix3D to reduce splatting artifacts.
 - **Filtering and Captioning:** Applies visual and motion quality filters (e.g., saturation, VMAF, optical-flow consistency, scene-cut removal, camera-specific filters on FoV, focal consistency, pose smoothness, and scale variation). For captioning, scene-static prompts describe objects and layout, avoiding camera action language to enforce motion control through the pose branch.

#### 3.3. Progressive Training Strategy

Training proceeds in four stages:
1. **Efficient VAE Adaptation:** LTX2-VAE is adapted to the new latent distribution, re-initializing patchify and output projection layers.
2. **Hybrid Architecture Adaptation:** The pre-trained SANA-Video model is adapted to the Hybrid GDN-Softmax architecture on short video clips.
3. **Minute-Scale Extension and Action Conditioning:** Sequence length is extended to one minute, and Dual-Branch Camera Control is integrated.
4. **Chunk-Causal Fine-Tuning and Few-Step Distillation:** A chunk-causal variant is fine-tuned for autoregressive rollout, followed by self-forcing distillation to reduce sampling steps. Attention-sink tokens and local temporal windows are added for deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SANA-WM was evaluated on a custom 60-second world-model benchmark comprising 80 initial scenes and two trajectory splits (Simple and Hard). The evaluation involved metrics for camera control accuracy (RotErr, TransErr, CamMC), visual quality (VBench scores), inference efficiency (Memory, Throughput), revisit memory (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), and temporal degradation (ΔIQ).

#### 4.1. Camera Control Accuracy

SANA-WM demonstrated stronger action following capabilities compared to prior open-source baselines such as Infinite-World, LingBot-World, HY-WorldPlay, and Matrix-Game 3.0. On the Simple-Trajectory split, SANA-WM achieved a RotErr of 7.59 degrees, a TransErr of 1.59, and a CamMC of 1.63. With the addition of the second-stage refiner, these improved to 4.50 degrees, 1.39, and 1.41 respectively. On the Hard-Trajectory split, SANA-WM without refinement recorded a RotErr of 10.02 degrees, TransErr of 1.66, and CamMC of 1.72, which were further reduced to 8.34 degrees, 1.39, and 1.44 with the refiner. These results indicate that the refined SANA-WM outputs achieved the lowest errors across these metrics, surpassing both 480p and 720p baselines.

#### 4.2. Visual Quality

The visual quality of SANA-WM was assessed using VBench. With the second-stage refiner, SANA-WM attained VBench Overall scores of 80.62 on the Simple-Trajectory split and 81.89 on the Hard-Trajectory split. These scores are comparable to LingBot-World (81.82/81.89), while SANA-WM operates at 720p resolution with single-GPU inference per clip, contrasting with LingBot-World's 480p generation on eight GPUs.

#### 4.3. Inference Efficiency

SANA-WM exhibited high inference efficiency. The model requires 51.1 GB of memory and achieves a throughput of 24.1 videos per hour for 60-second, 720p clip generation on a single H100 GPU. When combined with the refiner, the full pipeline utilizes 74.7 GB of memory, remaining within the 80 GB H100 budget, and maintains a throughput of 22.0 videos per hour. This throughput is noted as 3.7 times faster than the fastest 480p baseline evaluated. A distilled variant of SANA-WM can generate a 60-second 720p clip in 34 seconds on a single RTX 5090 GPU with NVFP4 quantization, demonstrating a 39x overall speedup compared to the H100, 60-step baseline.

#### 4.4. Revisit Memory and Temporal Stability

SANA-WM demonstrated competitive performance in maintaining scene identity under revisit conditions. After refinement, it achieved 14.46 dB PSNR on Simple trajectories and 14.80 dB on Hard trajectories. The refiner also contributed to reducing long-horizon visual drift, with ΔIQ (the difference in imaging quality between the first and last 10-second windows) dropping from 3.79/3.09 for stage-1 AR outputs to 1.17/0.31 for refined outputs on Simple/Hard splits, indicating greater temporal stability.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Progressive Training:** Ablations demonstrated that integrating the LTX2 VAE maintained quality while significantly reducing peak memory and improving latency. The hybrid GDN/softmax backbone further enhanced overall quality while maintaining efficient memory usage.
- **GDN Key Scaling:** The proposed 1/√(D·S) key scaling factor was shown to be critical for training stability, with other scaling variants leading to immediate gradient instability and training failures.
- **Camera Conditioning:** Studies on camera conditioning revealed that input-level Plücker conditioning provided minor improvements, while attention-level PRoPE and UCPE significantly improved control accuracy. The dual UCPE + Plücker mixing branch achieved the lowest Pi3X errors.
- **Efficiency Scaling:** Recurrent/linear variants of the model exhibited compact clean-context states, leading to efficient memory scaling, whereas an all-softmax attention model consumed excessive memory and failed at 60-second sequence lengths.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (Figures 5, 8, 11) indicated that SANA-WM preserved scene identity and viewpoint-consistent structure under complex camera actions more effectively than baselines, which often exhibited blurring, layout changes, or collapse. 3D reconstructions from generated videos (Figure 12) also suggest coherent 3D cues over minute-long, camera-controlled rollouts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SANA-WM represents a contribution to the field of long-horizon, action-conditioned video generation and world modeling. The system's design addresses the challenge of balancing high visual fidelity and precise 6-DoF camera control with computational efficiency, making minute-scale 720p generation more accessible.

**Key Contributions and Implications:**
- **Accessible World Modeling:** By significantly reducing the required data, training compute, and inference hardware, SANA-WM lowers the barrier for academic researchers and smaller laboratories to engage in studies of camera-controlled video generation, long-horizon scene consistency, and action-conditioned evaluation. This increased accessibility can broaden participation in embodied AI, robotics research, and interactive content prototyping.
- **Efficiency-Oriented Architecture:** The introduction of a hybrid GDN-softmax attention backbone, coupled with the LTX2 high-compression video latent tokenizer, offers a method for efficient long-context modeling that maintains performance. The dual-branch camera control mechanism allows for precise 6-DoF trajectory adherence at both coarse and fine granularities.
- **Enhanced Visual Fidelity and Consistency:** The two-stage generation pipeline, which includes a dedicated long-video refiner, contributes to improved visual quality and temporal consistency across minute-long sequences. This addresses a common challenge of visual degradation over extended durations in generative models.
- **Robust Data Annotation and Evaluation:** The development of a robust data annotation pipeline for recovering accurate metric-scale 6-DoF camera poses from public videos, along with a new one-minute world-model benchmark, provides valuable resources for training and evaluating long-horizon world models.

**Potential Impact:**
The enhanced efficiency of SANA-WM could reduce the marginal computational cost associated with generating large volumes of evaluation data or synthetic rollouts, which is relevant for various simulation-based research endeavors. It supports a practical workflow where initial trajectories can be explored efficiently with the stage-1 model, with promising rollouts then refined for higher fidelity.

**Limitations and Broader Considerations:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, including the model's current scale limits, the absence of explicit 3D scene memory, and potential for visual drift in dynamic scenes, rare viewpoints, or significantly longer rollouts. From a broader impact perspective, issues common to high-quality video generation systems are pertinent:
- **Misinformation Risk:** Generated videos might be mistaken for real observations if their provenance is not clearly documented.
- **Safety-Critical Misinterpretation:** Simulated rollouts could be over-interpreted as faithful predictions in safety-critical domains like robotics or autonomous driving, requiring verification with real-world data.
- **Bias Inheritance:** The model inherits biases and coverage limitations from its public video sources, generated benchmark images, and filtering/captioning tools, potentially leading to non-uniform performance across diverse cultures, environments, or sensitive human-centered scenarios.

Future work outlined includes scaling models and data further, exploring alternative control interfaces (e.g., robot actions, point tracking), strengthening persistent scene memory, and developing robust real-time or streaming refiners. Practical deployment necessitates clear documentation of data provenance, generation settings, intended use, known failure modes, and potentially the retention of watermarks or other provenance signals.
