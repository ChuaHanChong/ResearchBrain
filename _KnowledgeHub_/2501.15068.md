# An Atomic Skill Library Construction Method for Data-Efficient Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from JD Explore Academy and academic partners developed a "Three-Wheeled" framework for embodied manipulation, enabling robots to dynamically build and update an atomic skill library. This method allows for comparable or improved performance with significantly less data, showing enhanced generalization to novel tasks with success rates up to 80-100% on reordered block tasks, across different VLA models.

## Problem

- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demand massive task-specific data, leading to a "data explosion" problem that hinders real-world scalability.
- Existing atomic skill decomposition methods rely on manually predefined, static skill sets, which lack adaptability and cannot expand to unforeseen tasks or dynamic environments.
- Current embodied manipulation systems struggle to efficiently generalize to new tasks and varying environmental configurations without extensive, costly retraining.

## Method

- Introduces a "==Three-Wheeled Self-Driven== **Atomic Skill Library Construction** Method" comprising interconnected VLP (Vision-Language-Planning), VLA (Vision-Language-Action), and Atomic Skill Wheels.
- Utilizes a VLP Agent Wheel, integrating models like Prismatic, Dino-X, SAM-2, and GPT-4, for automatic, intelligent task decomposition and planning, incorporating scene descriptions and spatial intelligence.
- Employs an Atomic Skill Wheel for dynamic skill definition, library expansion through few-shot fine-tuning of VLA models (e.g., Octo, RDT-1B), and self-driven adaptation based on VLA performance characteristics.

## Results

- Achieved comparable or superior manipulation performance with significantly less data than end-to-end baselines, for example, improving the 'Pour Water' success rate by **20%** (**80%** vs **60%**) for out-of-distribution objects.
- Demonstrated enhanced performance with the same total data volume by strategically distributing it for skill acquisition, leading to a **40%** increase in success rate for 'Pick & Place Banana' (**70%** vs **30%**) with out-of-distribution objects.
- Enabled robust adaptability to entirely new tasks, with the system successfully performing novel sequences (e.g., reordered block tasks) with 80-**100%** success rates, whereas end-to-end methods consistently failed (**0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically constructing and updating an atomic skill library in a data-driven manner effectively overcomes the limitations of both end-to-end data explosion and static skill sets, enabling continuous learning and adaptability.
> - Integrating powerful VLP models like GPT-4 with robust visual perception (object detection, segmentation, spatial relations) provides a strong foundation for intelligent task decomposition and effective management of atomic skills.
> - Structuring data collection around specific atomic skills, rather than entire end-to-end tasks, significantly enhances data efficiency and promotes generalization capabilities across varying object positions and novel task sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in embodied artificial intelligence: enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks effectively and efficiently in diverse, unstructured real-world environments. The broader research landscape for embodied manipulation has witnessed significant advancements, particularly with the advent of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and the availability of vast embodied datasets. These end-to-end approaches, often leveraging generative AI and multimodal data integration, have shown impressive progress in demonstrating generalization within specific settings.

However, the authors correctly identify a critical limitation: the "data explosion" problem. Traditional end-to-end models require massive amounts of task-specific data for training, making it infeasible to scale to the innumerable and complex scenarios encountered in the real world. Every new task or significant environmental change often necessitates extensive data collection and retraining, hindering practical application and generalizability.

Previous attempts to overcome this by decomposing complex tasks into "atomic skills" (e.g., SkillMaN, hierarchical decomposition methods, RoboMatrix) have shown promise. These methods aim to create fine-grained, reusable skills that can be combined to perform various tasks, thereby reducing the overall data requirement. However, a significant drawback of these prior works is their reliance on *manually predefined skill sets*. Such static skill libraries are inherently limited; they cannot dynamically adapt or expand to new tasks or unforeseen situations, thus failing to fully address the versatility required for general embodied manipulation.

This paper positions itself as a significant step forward by introducing a *data-driven method for dynamically constructing and updating an atomic skill library*. It seeks to overcome the limitations of both end-to-end data explosion and static predefined skill sets. By proposing a "three-wheeled" framework that integrates Vision-Language-Planning (VLP) for automatic task decomposition, VLA models for skill execution, and a self-driven mechanism for skill definition and library expansion, the work directly contributes to the pursuit of more adaptive, data-efficient, and generalizable robot intelligence. It aims to bridge the gap between impressive research demonstrations and practical, scalable deployment in dynamic real-world environments. The paper builds upon existing powerful unimodal and multimodal understanding technologies (like large language models, object detectors, and segmentation models) and state-of-the-art VLA models, enhancing their utility through a novel architectural and operational framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems directly from the critical limitations of current embodied manipulation systems, particularly the "data explosion" problem. While end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated certain generalization capabilities, their performance severely degrades when confronted with new environments and tasks that deviate from their training distribution. The sheer complexity and diversity of real-world scenarios make it impossible to enumerate and collect data for every conceivable end-to-end task, leading to impractical data demands and hindering widespread practical application.

Existing approaches that attempt to decompose tasks into atomic skills offer a potential solution by promoting skill reuse and reducing data requirements. However, these methods are largely constrained by predefined, static skill sets, which lack the flexibility to adapt to dynamically evolving task requirements or expand to cover new, unforeseen skills. This inflexibility renders them inefficient for general embodied manipulation in dynamic real-world settings.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this research are clearly defined:

1. **Develop a Practical, Data-Driven Atomic Skill Library Construction Method:** The core objective is to move beyond manually predefined skill sets and create a system that can automatically define, acquire, and manage a library of atomic skills in a data-driven manner. This method should be capable of continuous self-updating and expansion.
2. **Achieve Data-Efficient Embodied Manipulation:** The overarching goal is to significantly reduce the data costs associated with training embodied manipulation models. By focusing on learning and reusing atomic skills rather than entire end-to-end tasks, the method aims for exponential improvement in data collection efficiency.
3. **Enhance Generalization and Adaptability to New Tasks:** The proposed framework seeks to enable robots to efficiently adapt to new tasks and scenarios with minimal or no additional data collection. This involves allowing the learned atomic skills to be reused across different contexts and facilitating the learning of missing skills with few-shot data.
4. **Integrate Vision-Language-Planning (VLP) with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) for Skill Management:** A specific objective is to build a robust framework that utilizes VLP for intelligent task decomposition and planning, and leverages state-of-the-art VLA models for skill execution, ensuring that these modules can dynamically interact and update.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to lay the groundwork for more scalable, flexible, and truly intelligent embodied manipulation systems that can operate effectively in the messy, unpredictable real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel **"Three-Wheeled Self-Driven Atomic Skill Library Construction Method"** designed to dynamically build and update an atomic skill library, thereby enabling data-efficient embodied manipulation. The framework comprises three interconnected modules, each capable of updating as relevant technology evolves: the VLP Agent Wheel, the VLA Wheel, and the Atomic Skill Wheel.

#### 3.1. VLP Agent Wheel (Vision-Language-Planning)

This module is responsible for task decomposition during the training phase and task planning during inference. It integrates visual perception, language understanding, and spatial intelligence to generate subtasks from high-level instructions.
- **Scene Description:** An off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM), Prismatic, is used to generate a detailed scene description from an observation image.
- **Spatial Intelligence:** To overcome limitations of basic visual information, the VLP agent incorporates spatial awareness. Dino-X, an object detection model, identifies task-related objects and their bounding box positions. SAM-2, a meticulous segmentation model, refines these into precise object masks. A rule-based algorithm then deduces spatial relationships between detected objects (e.g., "cup on table," "bottle near cup").
- **Task Decomposition and Planning:** The collected visual perception (scene description, object positions, spatial relations) and the user's text-based task instruction are fed into GPT-4 (a large language model). GPT-4 is prompted to generate a complete execution plan by decomposing the task into sequential subtasks and specifying the next subtask to be executed. This intelligent reasoning enables effective task breakdown during training and dynamic planning during inference.

#### 3.2. VLA Wheel (Vision-Language-Action)

This module is responsible for the execution of individual atomic skills. The framework is designed to be agnostic to the specific VLA model used, allowing for integration with any state-of-the-art VLA method (e.g., RDT-1B, Octo, used in experiments).
- **Skill Granularity Determination:** The performance characteristics of the chosen VLA model, specifically its *plasticity* (how well it adapts from pre-trained to fine-tuned for new robotic platforms) and *adaptability* (its capacity to generalize to diverse objects, scenes, and spatial configurations), dictate the granularity of atomic skill definitions. Higher plasticity and adaptability allow for coarser, more general skill definitions.
- **Skill Acquisition via Few-Shot Fine-tuning:** Once atomic skill definitions are formed, the VLA model is fine-tuned using a small number of collected trajectory data samples for each specific atomic skill. This "few-shot learning" approach significantly accelerates skill acquisition compared to traditional end-to-end training.
- **Empirical Validation of VLA Performance:** The authors conducted experiments with RDT-1B, fine-tuning it with a blend of open-source and proprietary trajectories. They found that collecting a small dataset with varying object positions for a skill, followed by few-shot training, significantly improved generalization for object positions. They also optimized training steps (1,000 steps provided the best trade-off). These insights inform the data collection and training strategy for atomic skills.

#### 3.3. Atomic Skill Wheel (Library Construction and Dynamic Update)

This is the core "self-driven" component that manages the atomic skill library.
- **Initial Construction:** For a set of initial tasks, the VLP module decomposes them into subtasks. A high-level semantic abstraction module (leveraging LLMs like GPT-4, informed by VLA performance) abstracts these subtasks into a set of general atomic skill definitions. Trajectory data is then collected for these definitions, and the VLA model is fine-tuned to realize these skills, forming the initial atomic skill library.
- **Dynamic Expansion and Adaptation:**
 - **Known Skills:** When a new task is presented, the VLP module plans its execution. If all required atomic skills are already present in the library, the task is executed directly without any further data collection or fine-tuning.
 - **Missing Skills:** If the VLP identifies a subtask requiring a skill not yet in the library, the high-level semantic abstraction module is reactivated. It takes the new subtask, the required skill granularity (determined by VLA performance), and the existing skill definitions as input to generate a *new* atomic skill definition. Only the trajectory data specific to this missing skill is collected, and the VLA is few-shot fine-tuned for it, dynamically expanding the library.

This "three-wheeled" design ensures a closed-loop system where VLP enables intelligent task breakdown, VLA provides the execution capability, and the Atomic Skill module continuously learns and expands the library in a data-driven, efficient, and adaptable manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to demonstrate the data efficiency, performance, and adaptability of their "Three-Wheeled" method against traditional end-to-end approaches, using state-of-the-art VLA models (Octo and RDT-1B) on an ALOHA dual-arm robot in real-world settings. Four challenging tasks were selected: "Pour Water," "Pick & Place Banana," "Pick & Place Pen," and "Move blocks in order." The tasks involved complexities like varying object positions (In-Distribution (ID) vs. Out-of-Distribution (OOD)) and sophisticated operations. Performance was measured by task success rates over 10 trials.

The results are presented under three main comparisons: "End-to-end" (traditional method), "Ours" (skill-based with less data but same distribution as end-to-end), and "Ours-plus" (skill-based with the same data volume as end-to-end, but distributed across more diverse locations).

**Q1: Data Efficiency (Comparable Performance with Less Data)**
- The "Ours" method consistently demonstrated performance comparable to, or even better than, the end-to-end baseline, despite utilizing significantly less data.
- **Example:** In the "Pour Water" task, when both the bottle and cup were OOD, "Ours" (with RDT-1B) achieved an 80% success rate, a 20% improvement over the end-to-end RDT-1B (60%) with less data. Similar trends were observed across other tasks and VLA backbones.
- **Conclusion:** This finding directly supports the claim that the proposed atomic skill library approach effectively mitigates the "data explosion" problem, allowing for high performance with reduced data requirements.

**Q2: Enhanced Performance (Better Performance with Same Data Volume)**
- The "Ours-plus" method, which used the same total data volume as end-to-end but leveraged the skill-based approach to collect data from more diverse locations, showed significant improvements in success rates, particularly for OOD scenarios.
- **Example:** For "Pick & Place Banana" with RDT-1B, when both the banana and plate were OOD, "Ours-plus" achieved a 70% success rate, a substantial 40% increase compared to the end-to-end RDT-1B (30%).
- **Conclusion:** By structuring data collection around atomic skills, the method enables better generalization across varying object positions and environmental configurations, leading to superior performance for the same overall data investment.

**Q3: Adaptability to New Tasks**
- This was evaluated using the "Move blocks in order" task, presenting novel sequences of block manipulation (e.g., Red-Blue-Green, Blue-Green-Red).
- **Results:** End-to-end methods completely failed on these new task orders (0% success rate), demonstrating their inherent limitation to known task sequences. In stark contrast, the "Ours" method successfully performed the required atomic skills across these different new tasks, achieving high success rates (e.g., RDT-1B "Ours" achieved 80-100% success rates for reordered block tasks).
- **Conclusion:** The atomic skill library significantly enhances the system's ability to adapt to and execute novel tasks by recombining learned skills, addressing a critical drawback of end-to-end learning.

**Q4: Applicability to Different VLA Backbone Models**
- The experiments were conducted using two distinct, high-performing VLA models: Octo and RDT-1B.
- **Results:** The improvements in data efficiency, manipulation performance, and adaptability to new tasks were consistently observed across both Octo and RDT-1B, relative to their respective end-to-end baselines.
- **Conclusion:** This demonstrates the robustness and generalizability of the proposed "Three-Wheeled" framework, indicating that it can be effectively integrated with and enhance various state-of-the-art VLA architectures.

In summary, the experimental findings robustly validate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed method. It successfully addresses the "data explosion" problem, significantly improves generalization to novel scenarios and tasks, and is applicable across different VLA backbones, paving the way for more practical and adaptable embodied manipulation systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, with the potential for substantial impact on both academic research and practical applications.

**Addressing a Core Challenge:** The most immediate and profound impact lies in directly confronting and providing a viable solution to the "data explosion" problem. This issue has long been a critical bottleneck, preventing embodied manipulation systems from scaling beyond limited, controlled environments to the vast complexity of the real world. By demonstrating how to achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly less data, and how to generalize to new tasks with minimal additional learning, the paper opens new avenues for data-efficient robot learning.

**Enabling Real-World Deployment:** The ability to construct a dynamic, self-updating atomic skill library that can adapt to new tasks and environments with few-shot learning is a game-changer for practical robotics. This approach drastically reduces the cost and effort associated with deploying and maintaining robots in diverse settings, such as logistics (JD.com's core business), manufacturing, healthcare, or domestic service. Service robots, currently limited by their inability to handle novel situations, could become far more versatile and useful.

**Shifting the Paradigm of Robot Learning:** The "Three-Wheeled" framework proposes a paradigm shift from monolithic, task-specific end-to-end learning to a more modular, compositional, and continuously evolving skill-based intelligence. This move allows for greater interpretability, reusability of learned components, and a more robust learning process that can accumulate knowledge over time. This approach could inspire similar modular architectures in other complex AI domains.

**Advancing Embodied AI Research:** For the research community, this work provides a strong foundation for future exploration into automatic skill discovery, hierarchical reinforcement learning, and lifelong learning for embodied agents. It highlights the power of combining modern large language models (for planning and abstraction) with robust visual-language-action models (for execution) and clever data management strategies. The emphasis on spatial intelligence within the VLP module is also a notable advancement, addressing a key limitation in previous planning systems.

**Broader Economic and Societal Impact:** The industrial backing from JD.com, Haier, and other robotics companies underscores the commercial relevance of this research. More capable and adaptable robots could revolutionize industries, improve efficiency in supply chains, automate hazardous tasks, and enhance the quality of life through advanced service robotics. The national funding also points to the strategic importance of this technology for China's advancements in AI and robotics.

In conclusion, this paper does not merely present an incremental improvement; it offers a fundamentally different and more scalable approach to embodied manipulation. By making robots more data-efficient, adaptable, and capable of dynamic self-improvement, it brings the vision of truly general-purpose intelligent robots significantly closer to reality.
