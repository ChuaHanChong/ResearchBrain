# Cog3DMap: Multi-View Vision-Language Reasoning with 3D Cognitive Maps

> [!summary] Summary
> Cog3DMap builds an explicit 3D memory for MLLMs: a recurrent pipeline fuses pointmap prediction, semantic ViT features, and geometric features into non-redundant, spatially-unique 3D tokens fed to the LLM decoder, beating prior implicit/redundant token approaches on spatial reasoning benchmarks while cutting visual tokens up to 90.2%.

## Problem

- MLLM visual representations are predominantly semantic and lack explicit geometric grounding, forcing implicit inference of 3D scene structure from raw pixels.
- Prior geometry-augmented methods still require the MLLM to implicitly infer 3D structure from augmented tokens rather than reasoning over explicit geometry.
- Existing visual-geometry-model augmentation assigns identical 3D coordinates to multiple overlapping-view tokens, giving a redundant, non-unique spatial structure the MLLM must disentangle.

## Method

- **Recurrent 3D Cognitive Map** construction: a ==Mem-Module== iteratively updates a memory state of (position, semantic feature, geometric feature) tokens across multi-view images, mirroring incremental cognitive-map formation.
- ==Distance-thresholded memory update rule== that partitions prior tokens into retained/updated sets and merges new observations, producing one unique token per 3D coordinate instead of duplicated overlapping tokens.
- ==Semantic-geometric fusion== via reused MLLM ViT encoder features plus a learnable projector on Point3R-derived geometric features, feeding compact fused tokens directly into the MLLM decoder.

## Results

- Achieves **8.7** percentage-point average gain over VLM-3R-7B on VSTI-Bench, with a **27.5** percentage-point jump on camera movement prediction.
- Sets new state-of-the-art on VSI-Bench with **65.1** average versus VST-7B's **61.2**, a **3.9** percentage-point improvement.
- On RoboFAC, matches or beats RoboFAC-4B while using **26.8%** to **90.2%** fewer visual tokens depending on horizon length, and ablations show fusing geometric features via a projector raises CIDEr from **65.2** to **79.4** on Scan2Cap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit, spatially-unique memory tokens outperform implicit or redundant geometry-augmented tokens because they remove the burden of disentangling overlapping observations from the language decoder.
> - Stacking extra 3D positional embeddings on top of already-geometric features can hurt performance, since geometric features already encode sufficient spatial information redundantly.
> - Recurrent neighborhood-averaging memory updates are a compactness-accuracy sweet spot but degrade under highly dynamic scenes where overwritten coordinates erase distinct object motion histories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in general visual understanding by integrating vision with the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). However, a persistent challenge for these models is achieving precise spatial understanding, particularly from multi-view images. Current MLLMs primarily operate with visual representations that are semantic in nature, lacking explicit geometric grounding. This limitation hinders their ability to perform fine-grained spatial reasoning, such as localizing objects, understanding relative arrangements, or predicting spatial relationships in a 3D environment.

Existing approaches to address this issue typically fall into two categories. One line of research augments visual tokens with geometric cues derived from external visual geometry models, such as pointmap estimators. While this introduces some spatial information, MLLMs are still required to implicitly infer the underlying 3D structure from these augmented tokens. Another approach involves training MLLMs on spatially-annotated datasets constructed from ground-truth 3D annotations. However, the acquisition of 3D data is resource-intensive, which limits the scale and diversity of such training datasets compared to 2D image datasets.

A further limitation identified in these existing geometry-augmented MLLMs is the redundancy present in their visual token representations. When multiple visual tokens originate from overlapping viewpoints but represent the same spatial area, they often carry identical 3D coordinates. This lack of a non-redundant spatial structure forces the MLLM to disentangle and process overlapping information before it can effectively perform spatial reasoning, adding computational burden and complexity.

This work draws inspiration from cognitive science, specifically the concept of "cognitive maps." In human cognition, a cognitive map is an internal representation that encodes spatial relationships among locations in an environment, acquired incrementally from sequential observations. Cog3DMap proposes to bridge the gap in current MLLM capabilities by developing a computational counterpart to this human cognitive process. It aims to provide MLLMs with an explicit, compact, and non-redundant 3D representation of a scene, thereby enabling more direct and interpretable spatial reasoning, unlike previous methods that rely on implicit inference or suffer from token redundancy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the precise spatial understanding and reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) when presented with multi-view images. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in current MLLM architectures and their approaches to spatial cognition:

1. **Lack of Explicit Geometric Grounding:** Current MLLMs largely process visual information as semantic representations. While effective for general visual tasks, this approach inherently lacks explicit geometric grounding, making it difficult for models to understand precise 3D spatial relationships, object locations, and environmental layouts from multi-view inputs. The paper highlights that models are often forced to implicitly infer 3D structure, which limits their spatial reasoning accuracy and interpretability.

2. **Inefficiency and Redundancy in Existing Spatial Augmentations:** Previous methods that inject spatial information often augment visual tokens with geometric features. However, these augmented tokens can be redundant, particularly when multiple tokens from overlapping viewpoints represent the same 3D spatial location. This redundancy necessitates that the MLLM perform an additional processing step to disentangle and aggregate this overlapping information, thereby increasing computational load and complexity during reasoning.

3. **Cost and Scalability of 3D Data Acquisition:** Approaches that rely on explicit 3D inputs, such as processed point clouds, are constrained by the high cost and complexity of acquiring and annotating 3D data. This limits the scale of training datasets and the practicality of deploying such models in real-world scenarios where raw multi-view images are more readily available.

To address these motivations, the key objectives for Cog3DMap are:

- **Construct an Explicit 3D Memory (Cognitive Map):** Develop a recurrent framework that progressively integrates multi-view visual observations into a unified 3D memory map. This map is designed to explicitly store spatial coordinates, associating each location with a token that contains both semantic and geometric information.
- **Enable Direct Spatial Reasoning:** By providing the MLLM decoder with this explicit, compact, and non-redundant 3D map, the framework aims to facilitate more direct and interpretable spatial reasoning, moving beyond implicit inference from auxiliary features.
- **Integrate Semantic and Geometric Features Effectively:** Propose a strategy to combine semantic features, derived from the MLLM's own vision encoder, with geometric features, obtained from an external 3D reconstruction model, into a single enriched visual token. This integration is intended to provide comprehensive information for spatially grounded reasoning.
- **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Performance:** Evaluate the proposed framework on various spatial reasoning benchmarks to establish competitive or superior performance compared to existing methods, thereby validating the efficacy of the explicit 3D cognitive map representation.
- **Enhance Token Efficiency:** Reduce the number of visual tokens required for spatial understanding by eliminating redundancy in the 3D memory, particularly for processing longer sequences of images or videos, to improve computational efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Cog3DMap is structured as a framework that recurrently constructs an explicit 3D memory, termed a "3D Cognitive Map," from multi-view images, and subsequently uses this map to enable spatial reasoning in an MLLM. The overall pipeline involves two main phases: recurrent construction of the 3D cognitive map and MLLM inference using this map.

#### 3.1. Recurrent Construction of 3D Cognitive Map

The process begins with an empty memory state, M_0 = ∅. For each incoming image I_n in a sequence, the memory state is updated from M_{n-1} to M_n, where M_n is a set of tokens {(p_k, f_k, g_k)}, each consisting of a 3D position p_k, a semantic feature f_k, and a geometric feature g_k. This update procedure involves three stages:

1. **Pointmap Prediction:** A pre-trained recurrent transformer, referred to as the `Mem-Module` (specifically, Point3R), processes the current image I_n. This module predicts a pointmap P_n (a 2D map of 3D points) and extracts an intermediate geometric feature map G_n. Critically, this prediction is conditioned on the geometric features {g_k} from the preceding memory state M_{n-1}, allowing for incremental scene understanding.

2. **Semantic Feature Extraction:** To acquire rich semantic information aligned with the MLLM's language capabilities, the pre-trained vision encoder of the MLLM (ViT-Encoder) is used to extract a semantic feature map F_n directly from I_n. This step ensures that the semantic features are naturally compatible with the language decoder without requiring extensive additional alignment training.

3. **Memory Update:** This stage aggregates information from the current image I_n with the existing memory state M_{n-1} to form the updated state M_n, focusing on compactness and non-redundancy.
 - **Patch-wise Feature Computation:** For each patch (u, v) in the current image I_n, 3D coordinates p_{u,v} and semantic features f_{u,v} are calculated by averaging across pixels within the patch from P_n and F_n, respectively. Patch-wise geometric features g_{u,v} are extracted from P_n and G_n using an additional encoder to preserve fine-grained structural information that simple averaging might discard. These newly computed patch-wise tokens form M_new_n.
 - **Memory Partitioning:** The preceding memory state M_{n-1} is partitioned into two sets: M_upd_{n-1} (tokens whose 3D positions overlap with the current observation) and M_ret_{n-1} (tokens far from the current view). A token (p_k, f_k, g_k) from M_{n-1} is assigned to M_upd_{n-1} if its minimum 3D Euclidean distance to any token in M_new_n is less than a pre-defined threshold δ; otherwise, it is assigned to M_ret_{n-1}. This δ is dynamically allocated based on scene scale.
 - **Updating and Adding Tokens:** Tokens in M_upd_{n-1} are updated by averaging their features with those of neighboring new tokens in M_new_n (those within distance δ), forming ˆM_upd_{n-1}. Additionally, tokens in M_new_n that do not overlap with any existing memory token (i.e., minimum distance ≥ δ from any token in M_{n-1}) are identified as M_add_n and directly added to the memory.
 - **Final Memory State:** The updated memory state M_n is then constructed by combining the retained, updated, and newly added tokens: M_n = M_ret_{n-1} ∪ ˆM_upd_{n-1} ∪ M_add_n. This mechanism maintains a compact representation, updating existing information and expanding to newly observed areas.

#### 3.2. MLLM Inference

After processing all N multi-view images, the final memory state M = M_N is obtained. To integrate this 3D cognitive map into the MLLM:

- **Feature Fusion:** For each token in M, its semantic feature f_k and geometric feature g_k are fused into a single visual token v_k = f_k + Prj(g_k), where Prj(·) represents learnable projector layers. The control experiments indicated that geometric features already encode sufficient spatial information, making additional explicit 3D positional embeddings redundant.
- **Input to MLLM Decoder:** These fused visual tokens {v_k} are then provided to the MLLM decoder, along with the text query x, to generate the natural language response y.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **MLLM Backbone:** The Qwen3-VL-8B model is adopted for its balance of performance and efficiency.
- **Geometric Model:** A pre-trained Point3R is used for pointmap prediction and geometric feature extraction, and its parameters are frozen during the MLLM training.
- **Vision Encoder:** The MLLM's vision encoder for semantic feature extraction is also frozen; only the MLLM decoder and the projector Prj(·) are fine-tuned.
- **Temporal Reasoning:** For tasks requiring temporal understanding, Cog3DMap uses Qwen3-VL's video input format, incorporating temporal separators and 1D-RoPE for special pointer tokens to associate visual tokens with specific timestep information.
- **Training Stability:** During training, memory tokens are randomly subsampled to a maximum of 8,000 to ensure stability, while the full set of tokens is utilized during evaluation.
- **Resources:** Training was conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Cog3DMap was conducted across various multi-view spatial understanding benchmarks and control experiments, demonstrating its performance, efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks

- **VSTI-Bench (Joint Spatial and Temporal Understanding):** Cog3DMap demonstrated an 8.7 percentage point (p) improvement in average score over the previous state-of-the-art model, VLM-3R-7B. Notably, it achieved a 27.5%p improvement in "camera movement prediction," which is attributed to its design of encoding geometric, semantic, and temporal cues within a unified 3D representation.
- **VSI-Bench (Global Spatial Scene Understanding):** Cog3DMap established a new state-of-the-art, outperforming the previous leading model, VST-7B, by 3.9%p on average. It exhibited strong performance in tasks such as absolute distance estimation, relative direction, and appearance order. The authors attribute this to the compact, non-redundant 3D map, where each spatial location is represented by a unique token, which simplifies reasoning for the MLLM compared to methods with redundant tokens.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on the RoboFAC Benchmark (Efficiency and Compactness)

On the RoboFAC dataset, which involves dynamic interactions in robotic environments, Cog3DMap was evaluated for its token efficiency compared to a baseline Qwen3-VL model trained on the same dataset (RoboFAC-4B).
- **Short-horizon tasks:** Cog3DMap achieved 2.7%p higher accuracy with 26.8% fewer visual tokens.
- **Medium-horizon tasks:** Cog3DMap surpassed RoboFAC-4B by 2.1%p while requiring 68.9% fewer tokens.
- **Long-horizon tasks:** Cog3DMap nearly matched RoboFAC-4B's accuracy with a 90.2% reduction in token count.
These results demonstrate that Cog3DMap effectively compresses visual information, leading to substantial reductions in visual tokens, especially for longer video sequences, without compromising task performance.

#### 4.3. Control Experiments (Scan2Cap)

- **Positional Embedding Strategy:**
 - Incorporating geometric features (g_k) via a projector provided the most significant improvement, boosting C@0.5 from 65.2 to 79.4 (+14.2) over using semantic features (f_k) alone.
 - Among explicit positional embeddings for p_k, HRoPE achieved the highest gain (71.7 C@0.5) when used only with f_k.
 - However, appending explicit 3D positional embeddings (Learnable-PE, 4D-RoPE, HRoPE) on top of the already included geometric features (f_k + Prj(g_k)) consistently led to a slight performance decrease (e.g., 79.4 to 78.0, 77.9, 76.6 C@0.5). This suggests that geometric features from Point3R already encode sufficient spatial information, rendering additional 3D coordinate embeddings redundant. The default configuration adopted is `f_k + Prj(g_k)`.
- **3D Feature Integration Strategy:**
 - Cog3DMap (75.9 CIDEr on Qwen3-VL-4B) significantly outperformed `3D-REPA` (representation alignment, 31.2 CIDEr) and `VGM-Aug` (direct geometric feature augmentation without compression, 58.3 CIDEr). This performance gain is attributed to Cog3DMap's compact 3D memory map, which eliminates redundant information through spatial aggregation, thereby simplifying the spatial reasoning task for the MLLM.
- **Effect of Changing the Number of Frames:** The model trained with 32 frames exhibited robust and consistent performance when evaluated with varying numbers of input frames (16, 32, 64, 128, 256, and 512 frames) on Scan2Cap. While optimal performance was observed at 32 frames (training configuration), the maintained consistency across different frame counts supports the effectiveness of the 3D map in providing a stable representation regardless of the number of views.
- **Static vs. Dynamic Threshold δ:** Using a dynamic threshold δ for the memory update mechanism yielded a slight improvement in performance compared to a static δ, and, more significantly, contributed to more stable training, especially across scenes with varying scales.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Visualizations of attention maps on Scan2Cap and RoboFAC demonstrate that Cog3DMap assigns high attention weights to visual tokens relevant to the text query, forming spatially coherent clusters in 3D space. This indicates that the model acquires robust 3D spatial understanding and grounding, even without explicit supervision for spatial alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Cog3DMap presents advancements in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and their capacity for spatial understanding, with several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning for MLLMs:** Cog3DMap directly addresses a recognized limitation of MLLMs by providing explicit geometric grounding. This enables MLLMs to perform more precise, accurate, and interpretable spatial reasoning from multi-view images, moving beyond primarily semantic interpretations. This is crucial for applications requiring detailed understanding of object locations, relationships, and scene layouts.
- **Improved Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** By constructing a compact and non-redundant 3D memory map, Cog3DMap significantly reduces the number of visual tokens that the MLLM needs to process. The demonstrated reduction of up to 90.2% in visual tokens on long-horizon tasks, while maintaining or improving performance, suggests that this approach can make spatial reasoning more computationally efficient and scalable for large-scale environments or extended video sequences. This could mitigate some of the computational bottlenecks associated with large MLLMs.
- **Cognitive Inspiration in AI:** The framework's design, inspired by the concept of "cognitive maps" from human cognitive science, represents a step towards developing AI systems that process and represent spatial information in a manner analogous to human cognition. This biomimetic approach could lead to more robust and generalized spatial intelligence in AI.
- **Broader Applicability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across diverse benchmarks, including static multi-view scenarios (VSTI-Bench, VSI-Bench, Scan2Cap) and dynamic robotic videos (RoboFAC), indicates that Cog3DMap has broad applicability for spatial understanding tasks in various contexts. It offers a promising direction for scene-level spatial understanding without relying on dense, pixel-level computations, which are often resource-intensive.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The paper also identifies several limitations and areas for future research:

- **Highly Dynamic Scenes:** The current recurrent memory update mechanism, which aggregates features based on spatial neighborhood, is suboptimal for highly dynamic scenes. If multiple objects pass through the same spatial region, features at shared coordinates might be overwritten, making it challenging to maintain distinct motion histories for individual objects. Future work is needed to develop more robust update strategies for such scenarios and corresponding training/evaluation datasets.
- **Two-Stage Training:** Cog3DMap currently relies on a pre-trained geometric feature extractor (Point3R), leading to a two-stage training pipeline. Developing a fully end-to-end training pipeline would be a valuable direction for achieving more robust spatial understanding from multi-view images, potentially simplifying deployment and improving joint optimization.
