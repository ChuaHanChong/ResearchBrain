# Hidden in plain sight: VLMs overlook their visual representations

> [!summary] Summary
> UC Berkeley researchers found that Vision-Language Models frequently underperform on vision-centric tasks because their language model components fail to fully leverage the high-quality visual representations available from their vision encoders, instead often defaulting to linguistic priors. The study demonstrated that fine-tuning the LLM significantly improves visual utilization and reduces reliance on these priors.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit significantly worse performance on vision-centric tasks compared to their standalone vision encoders, often performing near random chance.
- There is an unreliable rank-ordering of vision encoders when integrated into VLMs; top-performing standalone encoders do not consistently yield the best VLMs.
- The underlying reason for VLMs failing to effectively use demonstrably rich visual information from their encoders remained unclear.

## Method

- Developed a ==dual evaluation strategy== comparing direct ==visual encoder readouts== with full VLM performance on a suite of "vision-centric" tasks.
- Conducted ==diagnostic analyses== to probe potential bottlenecks: ==degradation of visual representations, brittleness to linguistic prompts==, and the LLM's ability to utilize visual information.
- Systematically ==fine-tuned== individual ==VLM components (vision encoder, projector, LLM)== on vision-centric tasks to isolate the source of performance limitations.

## Results

- A universal and substantial performance drop was observed from direct vision encoder evaluation to full VLM evaluation across all vision-centric tasks, often to near-chance levels.
- VLMs' answer distributions closely matched those generated when given blank visual inputs, confirming a reliance on linguistic priors over visual evidence.
- Fine-tuning the LLM component on vision-centric tasks yielded the largest performance improvements, alongside improved visual attention and mitigation of language biases, compared to fine-tuning other VLM components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task-relevant visual information is largely preserved within the VLM's internal representations, indicating that the visual input itself is not degraded by the projector or LLM layers.
> - VLMs often default to "blind" language priors or biases when answering, ignoring available visual evidence, which significantly contributes to their underperformance.
> - The language model (LLM) component is the primary bottleneck, as it fails to effectively attend to, integrate, and reason over the rich visual features it receives.
