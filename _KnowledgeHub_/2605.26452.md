# Robust Koopman Control Barrier Filters for Safe Actor-Critic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Robust Koopman-CBF SAC, a data-driven framework integrating Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) with a Koopman Control Barrier Function (CBF) safety filter, to provide pointwise safety guarantees for nonlinear robotic systems. This approach explicitly accounts for Koopman model approximation error, achieving zero constraint violations on CartPole tasks and reducing violations by 96.8% on Quadrotor 2D tracking.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) often leads to unsafe behaviors in physical systems due to unconstrained exploration, lacking step-wise safety guarantees.
- Classical Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) typically require precise knowledge of system dynamics and hand-designed barrier functions, limiting their application to complex, unknown nonlinear systems.
- Integrating safety filters with off-policy deep RL algorithms creates inconsistencies between the nominal actions proposed by the policy and the filtered actions executed in the environment.

## Method

- The Robust **Koopman-CBF SAC** framework combines Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) with a data-driven ==Koopman== ==Control Barrier Function== (CBF) safety filter.
- It learns approximate linear Koopman models from state-action-next state data using Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) in a lifted feature space, allowing for affine barrier functions.
- A "projected residual margin" is estimated from data to quantify Koopman model approximation error, which is then used to tighten the CBF condition within a robust Quadratic Program (QP) to guarantee safety.
- The framework integrates the filter into the SAC learning loop by training critics on the executed safe actions and regularizing the actor towards the CBF-feasible action set.

## Results

- The method achieved zero constraint violations for CartPole stabilization and tracking tasks while matching or exceeding the episode returns of unconstrained SAC.
- It reduced constraint violations by **96.8%** (from **12.55%** to **0.40%**) on Quadrotor 2D tracking tasks with comparable performance to unconstrained SAC.
- In complex, contact-rich locomotion tasks (Safety Gymnasium), large Koopman model approximation errors led to high slack rates, deep violations, and reduced filter effectiveness, highlighting limitations in specific environments.
- A strong correlation was found between a small projected residual margin (≈10−3≈ 10^{-3}≈10−3) and effective zero-violation safety, contrasting with large margins which indicated filter ineffectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating a projected residual margin for Koopman model error into the CBF condition is critical for achieving robust pointwise safety guarantees in data-driven settings.
> - Training the critic on actually executed (filtered) actions and regularizing the actor towards CBF-feasible actions effectively addresses policy-filter mismatch, promoting stable learning and safety internalization.
> - The projected residual margin acts as a reliable pre-training diagnostic, indicating the likely effectiveness of the Koopman-CBF filter in a given environment and for specific barrier functions.
> - The effectiveness of Koopman-CBF filters is highly dependent on the manageability of model error in the direction of the barrier function and the relative degree of the constraint.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned at the intersection of safe reinforcement learning (RL), control barrier functions (CBFs), and Koopman operator theory. The broader field of robotics and autonomous systems increasingly demands that learning-based control policies not only optimize performance but also ensure safety, particularly in continuous-control scenarios where constraint violations can lead to physical damage or instability.

Current approaches to safe RL often rely on reward penalties or constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs). Reward penalties can discourage unsafe behavior but typically do not offer step-wise safety guarantees, are sensitive to weighting, and can lead to unpredictable trade-offs between safety and performance. CMDP methods, while providing guarantees for expected cumulative costs, usually do not enforce pointwise forward invariance of specific safe sets. This work aims to address these limitations by focusing on pointwise safety guarantees.

Control barrier functions (CBFs) offer a control-theoretic framework for enforcing forward invariance of safe sets by minimally modifying a nominal control input through a quadratic program (QP). However, classical CBF synthesis traditionally assumes known system dynamics and requires hand-designed barrier functions, which can be restrictive for unknown, nonlinear robotic systems often encountered in data-driven RL.

Koopman operator theory provides a means to lift nonlinear dynamics into a higher-dimensional space of observables where the dynamics are approximately linear. Data-driven methods like Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) can learn these lifted linear models from data. This linearity in the lifted space can simplify control design, enabling convex control formulations like linear Model Predictive Control (MPC) for nonlinear systems. Recent research has begun to connect Koopman representations with CBF synthesis and neural Koopman-CBF learning for unknown nonlinear systems.

This paper distinguishes itself from existing work by explicitly integrating a data-driven, robust Koopman-CBF safety filter into an off-policy actor-critic RL framework, specifically Soft Actor-Critic (SAC). Key distinctions include:
- Using a projected residual margin to account for finite-dimensional Koopman approximation error, which directly links model uncertainty to the CBF constraint.
- Training critics on actions *actually executed* by the environment after filtering, addressing a common mismatch in safety-filtered RL where policies might optimize for unfiltered actions while the environment reacts to filtered ones.
- Regularizing the actor towards the Koopman-CBF feasible set to reduce dependence on the filter over training.
- Providing empirical diagnostics (slack rate, intervention rate, residual margin) to assess filter effectiveness and identify structural limitations.

In essence, this work seeks to combine the sample efficiency of off-policy deep RL with the strong, pointwise safety guarantees of CBFs, leveraging data-driven Koopman models to bridge the gap for unknown nonlinear systems, while explicitly managing the challenges introduced by model approximation errors and policy-filter interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, data-driven safety filtering mechanism for model-free deep reinforcement learning that can provide step-wise safety guarantees for nonlinear robotic systems. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

- **Ensuring Pointwise Safety in Robotics:** Robotic systems operating in physical environments face critical constraints such as avoiding collisions, respecting actuator limits, maintaining balance, and adhering to workspace boundaries. Violating these constraints can lead to system damage or unsafe operation. Existing RL methods, including reward penalties and Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO), typically provide expected or cumulative cost guarantees over trajectories, rather than strict pointwise safety guarantees at every time step, which is often insufficient for safety-critical applications.

- **Overcoming Limitations of Traditional CBFs:** While Control Barrier Functions offer principled mechanisms for enforcing forward invariance through minimally invasive safety filters, their practical application in RL is hindered by two main requirements: the need for an accurate analytical model of system dynamics and the necessity of hand-designing appropriate barrier certificates. These assumptions are often violated in complex, partially unknown, and highly nonlinear robotic systems.

- **Leveraging Data-Driven Koopman Models:** The paper seeks to overcome the reliance on known dynamics by using Koopman operator theory. Koopman methods lift nonlinear dynamics into a space of observables where the system's evolution is approximately linear. This linearity can transform a complex nonlinear CBF condition into a tractable affine constraint on the control input, which can be enforced using a lightweight Quadratic Program (QP) safety layer. This approach moves the complexity of nonlinear system identification offline to the Koopman model learning phase, while maintaining computational efficiency online.

- **Addressing Koopman Approximation Error for Safety:** A central challenge with finite-dimensional Koopman models is that they are only approximations of the true infinite-dimensional Koopman operator. If the safety filter ignores this prediction error, constraints satisfied in the approximate lifted model might be violated by the true system. Therefore, a key motivation is to robustify the CBF condition against this model uncertainty, ensuring that safety guarantees are maintained even with approximate dynamics.

- **Maintaining Consistency in Safety-Filtered RL:** In RL frameworks where a safety filter modifies the actor's proposed action before execution, a mismatch can arise. The environment experiences the *filtered* action, but the actor's policy and the critic's value estimates might be implicitly or explicitly updated based on the *nominal, unfiltered* action. This inconsistency can lead to suboptimal learning or hinder the actor's ability to learn truly safe policies. A specific objective is to design the RL updates to align with the physically executed safe actions and to encourage the actor to generate naturally safe actions.

In summary, the motivation is to create a robust, data-driven, and theoretically grounded framework for safe reinforcement learning that can deliver strong pointwise safety guarantees for complex nonlinear robots, explicitly accounting for model uncertainties and policy-filter interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Robust Koopman-CBF SAC, integrates a data-driven Koopman model and a robust CBF safety filter into the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) reinforcement learning framework. The approach unfolds in four main stages: Koopman model learning, lifted barrier construction with residual margin estimation, robust Koopman-CBF safety filtering, and consistent integration with SAC.

#### 3.1. Koopman Model Learning

The process begins with collecting a dataset of transitions, $D_K = \{(y_i, u_i, y_i^+)\}$, from interactions with the unknown nonlinear robotic system. Here, $y_t$ represents an augmented modeling state (e.g., physical state $x_t$ combined with tracking error $e_t$).
1. **Lifting Map ($\psi$):** A dictionary of nonlinear observables, $\psi$, is used to map the modeling state $y_t$ into a higher-dimensional lifted state $z_t = \psi(y_t)$. The dictionary includes the original state coordinates along with radial basis functions (RBFs), whose centers are determined by k-means clustering on the collected data. This ensures that physical constraints, linear in the original state, remain affine in the lifted space.
2. **EDMD Model Fitting:** An Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) approach is used to fit a finite-dimensional, controlled Koopman predictor of the form $z_{t+1} \approx Az_t + Bu_t$. The matrices $A$ and $B$ are learned using ridge regression on the lifted dataset $Z = [\psi(y_1), \dots, \psi(y_N)]$ and $U = [u_1, \dots, u_N]$, where $Z^+$ contains the successor lifted states. This procedure approximates the infinite-dimensional Koopman operator in the chosen observable space. The model's accuracy is evaluated using one-step and multi-step Mean Squared Error (MSE).

#### 3.2. Lifted Barrier Construction and Projected Residual Margin

1. **Affine Lifted Barriers:** For each safety constraint, a lifted affine barrier function is defined as $h_{K,j}(z) = c_j^\top z + d_j$. If the original state coordinates are part of $z$, physical constraints (e.g., position limits) that are linear in the original state can be directly translated into affine functions in the lifted space. For constraints requiring higher relative degrees (e.g., altitude constraint in Quadrotor), composite barriers are constructed (e.g., $h(z) = \alpha(y - y_{min}) + \beta \dot{y}$) to ensure the control input appears in the one-step barrier variation.
2. **Projected Residual Margin ($\rho_j$):** To account for the approximation error of the finite-dimensional Koopman model ($r_t = z_{t+1} - (Az_t + Bu_t)$), a robust margin $\rho_j$ is introduced for each barrier. This margin is calculated from a held-out calibration dataset, $D_{cal}$. Instead of bounding the full residual norm, the method focuses on the *projected residual* relevant to each barrier: $\delta_{i,j} = |c_j^\top r_i|$. The robust margin $\rho_j$ is then set as a high empirical quantile (e.g., 95th percentile) of these projected residuals. This approach is less conservative than using a full-norm residual bound and directly quantifies the impact of model error on the specific barrier condition. The theoretical framework also discusses using split conformal quantiles for high-probability residual coverage guarantees.

#### 3.3. Robust Koopman-CBF Safety Filter (QP)

At each time step, the actor proposes a nominal action $u_{nom,t}$. A Quadratic Program (QP) safety filter then computes a minimally modified safe action $u_{safe,t}$.
1. **Objective Function:** The QP minimizes the deviation from the nominal action, augmented with a penalty for slack variables: $\min_{u,\xi} \frac{1}{2} \|u - u_{nom,t}\|^2_2 + \lambda_\xi \sum_{j=1}^J \xi_j^2$.
2. **Constraints:**
 - **Robust Lifted CBF Condition:** For each barrier $j$, the condition $h_{K,j}(Az_t + Bu) \geq (1-\eta_j)h_{K,j}(z_t) + \rho_j$ is enforced. By substituting the affine form of $h_{K,j}(z)$, this transforms into an affine inequality in $u$: $a_j^\top u + \xi_j \geq b_j(z_t)$, where $a_j = B^\top c_j$ and $b_j(z_t)$ captures the rest of the terms.
 - **Input Bounds:** Actuator limits $u_{min} \leq u \leq u_{max}$ are enforced.
 - **Slack Variables:** Non-negative slack variables $\xi_j \geq 0$ are included for each constraint. These variables diagnose infeasibility (e.g., under strict actuator limits or large model error) but weaken the formal safety certificate if positive.
The QP is convex, ensuring a unique solution if the feasible set is non-empty.

#### 3.4. Integration with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)

The integration with SAC is designed to ensure consistency between the learning algorithm and the environment's actual dynamics.
1. **Replay Buffer:** The replay buffer stores tuples that include both the nominal action $u_{nom,t}$ and the *executed safe action* $u_{safe,t}$, along with other standard SAC elements.
2. **Critic Update:** Critically, the critics ($Q_\phi$) are trained using the *executed safe action* ($u_{safe,t}$) from the replay buffer. Similarly, for computing the target value, the next nominal action $u_{nom,t+1}$ is sampled from the actor's current policy and then passed through the same Koopman-CBF safety filter to obtain $u_{safe,t+1}$. This ensures that the critic estimates the action-value function for the *filtered closed-loop policy*, preventing a mismatch between what the critic learns and what the environment experiences.
3. **Actor Update:** The actor's loss function includes the standard SAC maximum-entropy objective term and a **CBF proposal penalty** term $\lambda_h \ell_{cbf}(z_t, u_{nom,t})$. This penalty is a quadratic term that penalizes the actor for proposing nominal actions that would violate the CBF constraints *before* filtering. This encourages the actor to learn policies that inherently produce safe actions, thereby reducing the filter's intervention rate over time.

**Theoretical Analysis:** The paper provides theoretical results on the convexity of the KCBF-QP, discrete-time CBF forward invariance (assuming no slack and sufficient residual coverage), and high-probability residual coverage under conformal prediction (with a discussion of limitations under policy-induced distribution shift).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Robust Koopman-CBF SAC was conducted on two benchmark families: Safe-Control-Gym (CartPole and Quadrotor 2D) and Safety Gymnasium (SafetyWalker2d and SafetyHalfCheetah-v1). Key findings include:

#### 4.1. CartPole (Stabilization and Tracking)

- **Zero Constraint Violations:** KCBF-SAC achieved a 0.000 violation rate across all seeds for both CartPole stabilization and tracking tasks. This demonstrates highly reliable pointwise safety enforcement.
- **Performance Matching:** The method matched or slightly exceeded the episode returns of unconstrained SAC (e.g., 88.6 vs. 88.5 for stabilization, 97.0 vs. 95.6 for tracking). This indicates that safety was achieved without a performance penalty.
- **Low Intervention Rate:** On tracking, the final intervention rate was approximately 0.02%, suggesting the actor successfully internalized the constraints and rarely required filter corrections. The minimum barrier value ($h_{min}$) remained positive, confirming the safe set was maintained.
- **Explanation:** The success on CartPole is attributed to a very small projected residual margin ($\rho = 9 \times 10^{-4}$). The position constraints are linear in the original state and directly included in the lifted space, making the lifted barrier exact. The EDMD model's prediction error in the barrier direction was negligible, allowing the robust CBF condition to maintain a significant safety margin without slack activation.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** Lagrangian and penalty-based baselines failed to reliably satisfy the position constraints, exhibiting higher violation rates and, in some cases, high return variance.

#### 4.2. Quadrotor 2D (Stabilization and Tracking)

- **Significant Violation Reduction:** On Quadrotor 2D tracking, KCBF-SAC reduced the violation rate from 12.55% (unconstrained SAC) to 0.40%, representing a 96.8% reduction.
- **Return Performance:** Returns were comparable to unconstrained SAC on stabilization. On tracking, KCBF-SAC achieved 170.6 $\pm$ 32.1 compared to SAC's 195.8 $\pm$ 0.2, with higher variance due to episodic slack activations during aggressive maneuvers.
- **Importance of Composite Barrier:** This success was critically dependent on using a composite barrier for the altitude constraint, which has a relative degree of 2. The composite barrier (combining altitude and vertical velocity) effectively reduced the relative degree to 1, enabling the CBF-QP to produce meaningful control corrections. Without it, the QP became degenerate.
- **Low Residual Margin:** The projected residual margin $\rho = 1.3 \times 10^{-3}$ was comparable to CartPole, indicating the Quadrotor dynamics were well-captured by EDMD once the barrier's relative degree was appropriately addressed.

#### 4.3. Safety Gymnasium (Walker2d and HalfCheetah)

- **Exposing Structural Limitations:** Results on these high-dimensional, contact-rich locomotion tasks were mixed and revealed significant limitations.
- **Walker2d:** KCBF-SAC reduced violations from 77.8% (SAC) to 37.0%. However, SAC+Lagrangian outperformed KCBF-SAC on safety (3.34% violation) at comparable returns, demonstrating that a distributional constraint method can be more effective than a step-wise CBF filter when its structural assumptions are not met.
 - **Failure Modes:** High projected residual $\rho = 0.698$ (due to complex contact dynamics) led to the robust CBF constraint being either trivially satisfied (when already unsafe and $|(1-\eta)h_K(z_t)| > \rho$) or infeasible under actuator limits, causing frequent slack activation. The minimum barrier value ($h_{min}$) remained deeply negative throughout training, indicating persistent constraint violations.
- **HalfCheetah-v1:** KCBF-SAC reduced violations from 97.9% (SAC) to 82.8%, but at a severe performance cost (70% return reduction).
 - **Dominant Model Error:** The projected residual $\rho = 1.782$ was exceptionally large, often exceeding typical barrier values. This rendered the CBF constraint trivially satisfied in most states, making the filter largely inactive. The deeply negative $h_{min}$ confirmed continuous and deep constraint violations.
 - **Policy Collapse:** The actor-side CBF penalty, despite minimal QP corrections, still discouraged high-velocity actions, leading to a significant return penalty without achieving effective safety.
- **Ablation on Walker2d:** Experimentation with the CBF discount parameter ($\eta$) showed sensitivity. Tighter constraints (lower $\eta$) led to either policy collapse (actor failing to learn locomotion effectively due to pervasive filter intervention) or pervasive constraint infeasibility (high slack activation) if the residual margin was large.

#### 4.4. Koopman Residual as a Predictor of Filter Effectiveness

A crucial finding is the strong correlation between the projected residual margin $\rho$ (computable offline) and KCBF-SAC's effectiveness. Environments with low $\rho$ (CartPole $\approx 10^{-4}$, Quadrotor $\approx 10^{-3}$) achieved near-perfect safety, while those with high $\rho$ (Walker $\approx 0.7$, HalfCheetah $\approx 1.8$) struggled significantly. This suggests $\rho$ acts as a reliable pre-training diagnostic to predict when the Koopman-CBF filter will be effective.

In summary, the method provides robust, zero-violation safety in systems where Koopman models are highly accurate and barrier relative degrees are manageable. However, its effectiveness significantly degrades in high-dimensional, contact-rich systems with large model approximation errors, where the robustness margin $\rho$ becomes dominant or the QP becomes frequently infeasible.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions and has important potential impacts on the field of safe reinforcement learning and robotic control:

- **Bridging Model-Free RL and Certifiable Safety:** The primary significance of this work lies in its attempt to bridge the gap between model-free deep reinforcement learning, which excels in learning complex policies from data, and control-theoretic safety guarantees provided by Control Barrier Functions. By leveraging data-driven Koopman models, the method offers a way to incorporate strong, pointwise safety conditions into RL frameworks without requiring explicit analytical models of nonlinear dynamics.

- **Robustness to Model Approximation Error:** The introduction of a projected residual margin ($\rho$) is a crucial advancement. It directly addresses a known limitation of finite-dimensional Koopman approximations by explicitly quantifying and compensating for their error in the CBF condition. This makes the safety guarantees more robust and transparent by linking model uncertainty to controller performance.

- **Consistent RL Training with Safety Filters:** The proposed integration with SAC, particularly training critics on executed safe actions and penalizing the actor for unsafe proposals, resolves a common mismatch in safety-filtered RL. This promotes faster and more consistent learning of genuinely safe policies, rather than policies that rely solely on post-hoc filtering.

- **Actionable Diagnostics for Filter Effectiveness:** The work provides clear diagnostics—projected residual margin ($\rho$), intervention rate, slack rate, and minimum barrier value ($h_{min}$)—that can be measured and interpreted to assess the filter's performance and inherent limitations. The strong empirical correlation between $\rho$ and filter effectiveness establishes $\rho$ as a valuable pre-training diagnostic, allowing practitioners to predict where the method will succeed or struggle.

- **Clarifying Structural Conditions for Effectiveness:** The experimental results clearly delineate the conditions under which Koopman-CBF filters are effective. They perform well when Koopman models are accurate ($\rho$ is small) and when barriers can be designed with appropriate relative degrees (e.g., through composite barriers). Conversely, they expose the limitations in systems with high irreducible model error (e.g., contact-rich dynamics) or when basic structural assumptions are violated (e.g., high-relative-degree constraints leading to QP degeneracy or infeasibility). This clarifies the scope of applicability for current Koopman-CBF approaches.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enabling Safer Robot Learning:** For low-to-medium-dimensional robotic tasks with relatively smooth dynamics, this method offers a path to deploying RL agents with high confidence in their safety. This could accelerate the use of RL in real-world applications such as industrial automation, logistics, and assistive robotics, where safety is paramount.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The identified limitations (relative degree issues, irreducible prediction error, policy collapse, one-step certificate, static barrier design, slack degradation) directly motivate several critical future research directions. These include developing discrete-time higher-order CBFs, multi-step Koopman prediction horizons, online Koopman model adaptation, joint learning of lifting maps and barrier certificates, and advanced conformal prediction techniques robust to distribution shift.
- **Benchmarking and Evaluation:** The use of standardized benchmarks and detailed performance metrics, including safety diagnostics, sets a precedent for rigorous evaluation of safe RL methods, fostering more transparent comparisons and faster progress in the field.

In conclusion, this research presents a principled, data-driven approach to safe RL, demonstrating its efficacy while also providing critical insights into its inherent structural limitations. The findings are significant for advancing the practical deployment of RL in safety-critical robotic systems and for guiding the development of more robust and generalizable safe learning algorithms.
