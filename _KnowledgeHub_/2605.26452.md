# Robust Koopman Control Barrier Filters for Safe Actor-Critic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Robust Koopman-CBF SAC, a data-driven framework integrating Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) with a Koopman Control Barrier Function (CBF) safety filter, to provide pointwise safety guarantees for nonlinear robotic systems. This approach explicitly accounts for Koopman model approximation error, achieving zero constraint violations on CartPole tasks and reducing violations by 96.8% on Quadrotor 2D tracking.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) often leads to unsafe behaviors in physical systems due to unconstrained exploration, lacking step-wise safety guarantees.
- Classical Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) typically require precise knowledge of system dynamics and hand-designed barrier functions, limiting their application to complex, unknown nonlinear systems.
- Integrating safety filters with off-policy deep RL algorithms creates inconsistencies between the nominal actions proposed by the policy and the filtered actions executed in the environment.

## Method

- The Robust **Koopman-CBF SAC** framework combines Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) with a data-driven ==Koopman== ==Control Barrier Function== (CBF) safety filter.
- It learns approximate linear Koopman models from state-action-next state data using Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) in a lifted feature space, allowing for affine barrier functions.
- A "projected residual margin" is estimated from data to quantify Koopman model approximation error, which is then used to tighten the CBF condition within a robust Quadratic Program (QP) to guarantee safety.
- The framework integrates the filter into the SAC learning loop by training critics on the executed safe actions and regularizing the actor towards the CBF-feasible action set.

## Results

- The method achieved zero constraint violations for CartPole stabilization and tracking tasks while matching or exceeding the episode returns of unconstrained SAC.
- It reduced constraint violations by **96.8%** (from **12.55%** to **0.40%**) on Quadrotor 2D tracking tasks with comparable performance to unconstrained SAC.
- In complex, contact-rich locomotion tasks (Safety Gymnasium), large Koopman model approximation errors led to high slack rates, deep violations, and reduced filter effectiveness, highlighting limitations in specific environments.
- A strong correlation was found between a small projected residual margin (≈10−3\approx 10^{-3}≈10−3) and effective zero-violation safety, contrasting with large margins which indicated filter ineffectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating a projected residual margin for Koopman model error into the CBF condition is critical for achieving robust pointwise safety guarantees in data-driven settings.
> - Training the critic on actually executed (filtered) actions and regularizing the actor towards CBF-feasible actions effectively addresses policy-filter mismatch, promoting stable learning and safety internalization.
> - The projected residual margin acts as a reliable pre-training diagnostic, indicating the likely effectiveness of the Koopman-CBF filter in a given environment and for specific barrier functions.
> - The effectiveness of Koopman-CBF filters is highly dependent on the manageability of model error in the direction of the barrier function and the relative degree of the constraint.
