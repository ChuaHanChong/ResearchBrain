# MiniHack the Planet: A Sandbox for Open-Ended Reinforcement Learning Research

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Facebook AI Research and collaborators introduced MiniHack, an open-source sandbox that utilizes NetHack's game engine and description language to provide a highly customizable and scalable platform for reinforcement learning research. This framework enables the creation of diverse environments to target specific RL challenges, demonstrating that current baseline algorithms struggle significantly as task complexity increases.

## Problem

- Existing complex RL benchmarks (e.g., full games) make it difficult to isolate and evaluate specific agent capabilities, often leading to overfitting to fixed levels.
- Simplified RL environments are effective for prototyping but demand significant engineering effort to scale and incorporate richer dynamics.
- Researchers lacked a customizable, performant, and richly dynamic 2D grid-world environment design tool that bridges the gap between these extremes.

## Method

- Develops **MiniHack**, a sandbox framework built upon ==NetHack's game engine== and its native ==description file (des-file) DSL==, for defining custom RL environments.
- Provides a user-friendly Python ==`LevelGenerator`== class and ==Gym interface== to programmatically construct environments, define flexible ==reward functions==, and manage observation/action spaces.
- Leverages NetHack's pre-implemented entities (580+ monster types, 450+ item types) and their complex interaction rules to easily create rich and diverse tasks.

## Results

- Baseline RL algorithms successfully solve simpler MiniHack tasks but exhibit significant performance degradation or outright failure when faced with incrementally increased complexity in navigation and skill acquisition challenges.
- MiniHack effectively ports and enriches existing benchmarks like MiniGrid, demonstrating that even minor additions of NetHack's complexities push current RL baselines to their limits.
- The framework supports Unsupervised Environment Design (UED), with PAIRED-trained agents achieving improved zero-shot transfer to complex, unseen environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MiniHack provides a unique bridge between highly complex, fixed game environments and overly simplified testbeds, offering both control and inherent richness.
> - The modular design facilitates isolating specific RL challenges and enables systematic, incremental scaling of environmental complexity.
> - By utilizing NetHack's pre-existing game logic and assets, MiniHack allows for the rapid creation of complex, dynamic environments with minimal engineering effort.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of deep reinforcement learning (RL) heavily relies on the availability of challenging and diverse benchmarks. Historically, environments like the Arcade Learning Environment (ALE) and MuJoCo have driven significant progress in model-free RL and continuous control. However, the paper identifies a critical gap in the current landscape of RL benchmarks, which MiniHack aims to address.

**Current Landscape Challenges:**
- **Complex Full Games:** Popular benchmarks such as Atari, StarCraft II, Dota 2, Procgen, Obstacle Tower, and the full NetHack game offer rich, complex environments. However, their "game-as-benchmark" nature means they are not designed for isolating and testing specific capabilities of RL agents (e.g., exploration, memory, planning). They often present an "all-or-nothing" challenge that can mask the limitations of methods in specific areas, and their fixed levels can lead to overfitting.
- **Simplified Small-Scale Environments:** Conversely, environments like MiniGrid, DeepMind Lab, Alchemy, MetaWorld, and bsuite are designed for prototyping and testing specific open problems in a controlled, simplified manner. While excellent for proof-of-concept research, systematically scaling these environments to incorporate richer dynamics or greater complexity typically demands significant, arduous engineering effort. This creates a "rock and a hard place" situation for researchers moving beyond initial hypotheses.
- **Existing Environment Design Frameworks:** Frameworks like PyVGDL, GVGAI, and Griddly allow for custom testbed design. However, they often require users to build complex entities and interaction dynamics from scratch. While Griddly, for example, is lauded for its speed, its ability to support the *richness* of interaction seen in a game like NetHack without extensive custom coding remains an open question. Malmo Platform and MineRL, built on Minecraft, offer rich dynamics but are computationally intensive.

**MiniHack's Contribution to the Landscape:**
MiniHack positions itself as a crucial bridge between these two extremes. It is a "sandbox framework" that offers the ease of designing novel RL environments, similar to small-scale environments, but with the rich entities and complex interaction mechanics inherited from the classic roguelike game NetHack. This allows researchers to:
1. **Isolate Specific RL Problems:** Easily create controlled, proof-of-concept tasks for exploration, transfer learning, unsupervised environment design (UED), or language-assisted RL.
2. **Incrementally Scale Difficulty:** Once a hypothesis is validated in a simple MiniHack environment, researchers can gradually increase complexity by adding NetHack's monsters, objects, dungeon features, or stochastic dynamics with minimal engineering. This offers a "controllable path" for assessing subsequent hypotheses, a feature lacking in full-game benchmarks.
3. **Leverage Existing Richness:** Instead of building complex game logic from scratch, MiniHack taps into NetHack's hundreds of pre-implemented entities (monsters, items) and their intricate interaction rules via the game's Domain Specific Language (DSL). This allows for the creation of rich and diverse custom environments with just a few lines of code.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** Built on the C/C++ engine of NetHack (and NLE), MiniHack boasts high simulation speeds, making large-scale experimentation feasible, distinguishing it from more computationally demanding 3D environments like Minecraft.
5. **Support for Open-Ended Research:** By providing a flexible platform for procedural generation and environment customization, MiniHack directly supports research into open-ended learning, curriculum learning, and generalization, areas where current RL methods often struggle.

In essence, MiniHack carves out a unique niche by offering a customizable, performant, and richly dynamic 2D grid-world environment design tool, filling the critical need for scalable, targeted RL research testbeds that can systematically probe and push the boundaries of current algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the MiniHack project is to introduce **MiniHack, a powerful sandbox framework designed for easily creating novel and enriching existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) environments.** This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Gap in RL Benchmarking:** The primary motivation stems from the limitations of existing RL benchmarks. On one hand, widely adopted benchmarks (e.g., Atari, StarCraft II) are entire games that are not explicitly designed for evaluating specific capabilities of RL methods in isolation. On the other hand, small-scale, tightly controlled environments (e.g., MiniGrid, bsuite) are useful for prototyping but become excessively difficult and time-consuming to extend to richer, more complex scenarios beyond simple proof-of-concept results. MiniHack aims to provide a "one-stop shop" that offers both control and complexity.

2. **Enabling Targeted Research on Open RL Problems:** Many fundamental challenges in RL remain unsolved (e.g., exploration, transfer learning, unsupervised environment design, language-assisted RL, credit assignment, memory). Researchers need environments that can be precisely tailored to test specific hypotheses related to these problems without being overwhelmed by unrelated game complexities or lacking sufficient richness. MiniHack's design allows for the creation of environments that specifically target these capabilities.

3. **Leveraging NetHack's Richness and Complexity:** The developers recognized the immense, pre-implemented complexity and interaction dynamics within NetHack – one of the richest grid-based video games. Instead of building such systems from scratch, MiniHack's motivation is to "tap into the richness of the game with its hundreds of pre-implemented entities and the complex interaction mechanics between them," but in a controlled, modular fashion, unlike the full NetHack Learning Environment (NLE).

4. **Facilitating Incremental Difficulty Scaling:** Researchers often need to assess how RL methods perform as environmental complexity increases. MiniHack is explicitly motivated by the need to "systematically extending such environments and gradually dropping simplifying assumptions." It allows for the easy addition of layers of complexity (e.g., monsters, objects, stochastic elements) to existing tasks, providing a clear curriculum for evaluating agent robustness and generalisation.

5. **Ease of Use and Accessibility:** A significant motivation is to make environment design "fast and convenient to use." By offering a human-readable description language (des-file format) and a simple Python interface, MiniHack seeks to lower the barrier for researchers to create custom testbeds without requiring deep engineering knowledge of game development.

6. **Promoting Generalization and Robustness:** The default procedural generation capabilities of MiniHack environments are motivated by the need to evaluate systematic generalization of RL agents, moving beyond solutions that simply overfit to a fixed set of levels.

In summary, MiniHack's objectives are to empower RL researchers with a highly flexible, rich, and performant toolkit for creating custom, scalable benchmarks that precisely address the current limitations of RL agents in complex and open-ended learning scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MiniHack's methodology revolves around leveraging the intricate systems of the classic roguelike game NetHack to create a highly customizable and scalable reinforcement learning sandbox. The core approach combines a domain-specific language for environment definition with Pythonic wrappers and a modular design.

#### 3.1. Core Leveraging of NetHack

The fundamental approach of MiniHack is to build upon the NetHack game engine, specifically utilizing its:
- **Description File (des-file) Format:** This is NetHack's native Domain Specific Language (DSL) for describing game levels. MiniHack adopts this human-readable format, allowing users to define intricate environments with variables, loops, conditional statements, probability distributions, and commands for procedural generation (e.g., `MAZEWALK`, `RANDOM_CORRIDORS`). This DSL is compiled into binary by NetHack's level compiler, which MiniHack then wraps as Gym environments.
- **Rich Game Assets and Dynamics:** MiniHack directly inherits NetHack's vast array of pre-implemented entities, including over 580 monster types (each with unique characteristics, attacks, and abilities like shape-changing or teleportation) and 450 item types (weapons, armor, tools, potions, spells). Crucially, it also leverages NetHack's complex interaction mechanics between these entities, which would be arduous to design from scratch in other frameworks.

#### 3.2. Environment Customization and Interface

- **Gym Interface:** All MiniHack environments are exposed through the popular OpenAI Gym interface, ensuring compatibility with a wide range of existing RL algorithms and tools.
- **`LevelGenerator` (Python Interface):** For ease of use, MiniHack provides a Python `LevelGenerator` class. This allows researchers to construct environments programmatically, defining map layouts (either from scratch or using pre-drawn ASCII strings), adding objects, monsters, traps, and terrain features via simple Python calls, without needing to learn the des-file DSL directly.
- **`RewardManager`:** This component offers flexible control over reward functions. Users can specify events that trigger positive or negative rewards, control if events are repeatable, and define complex termination conditions based on single events, sequences of events, or logical combinations of event groups. This allows for highly tailored sparse or dense reward signals.
- **Observation Space:** MiniHack supports diverse observation modalities, including:
 - **Symbolic:** Agent-centered or global views of grid cells (glyphs, characters, colors, specials).
 - **Textual:** On-screen messages, screen descriptions (textual descriptions for each map cell), and inventory information.
 - **Pixel-based:** High-fidelity pixel observations using NetHack's official tileset.
 - **Cropped Observations:** Facilitates egocentric learning by providing configurable cropped views around the agent.
- **Action Space Management:** NetHack has a large, structured, and context-sensitive action space. MiniHack allows practitioners to easily restrict this space for targeted skill discovery (e.g., movement-only for navigation tasks, or a larger set including inventory management for skill acquisition). It also handles "factorized autoregressive actions," where an initial action requires a sequence of follow-up choices (e.g., "put on ring" -> "choose ring" -> "choose hand").

#### 3.3. Task Suite and Experimentation

MiniHack provides a diverse suite of benchmark tasks categorized into:
- **Navigation Tasks:** Focus on goal-reaching, pathfinding, and obstacle interaction (e.g., `Room`, `Corridor`, `KeyRoom`, `MazeWalk`, `River`, `HideNSeek`, `CorridorBattle`, `Memento`, `MazeExplore`). These test exploration, planning, and memory, often with smaller action spaces. Tasks are designed with incremental difficulty (e.g., fixed vs. random start/goal, dark rooms, monsters, traps, lava).
- **Skill Acquisition Tasks:** Require agents to master interactions with NetHack's rich objects and actions (e.g., `Eat`, `Pray`, `Wear`, `LockedDoor`, `LavaCross`, `Wand of Death (WoD)`, `Quest`). These often involve large, factorized action spaces and sparse rewards, demanding complex sequences of interactions.
- **Ported Environments:** Demonstrates MiniHack's flexibility by porting existing benchmarks like MiniGrid (`MultiRoom`) and Boxoban. Crucially, these ported environments can then be easily "enriched" with NetHack complexities (e.g., adding monsters, locked doors, lava to a MiniGrid map) to test the limits of current methods.

#### 3.4. Evaluation and Baselines

The paper outlines best practices for evaluating RL methods on MiniHack, emphasizing consistency in environment parameters (action space, observations, reward, character), reporting median performance over multiple runs, and providing full learning curves and wall-clock times.
Baselines are provided using:
- **TorchBeast:** IMPALA, Random Network Distillation (RND), and Rewarding Impact-Driven Exploration (RIDE) are implemented.
- **RLlib:** DQN, A2C, and PPO algorithms are provided for broader algorithm compatibility.
- **Unsupervised Environment Design (UED):** MiniHack's customizability allows integration with UED algorithms like PAIRED, where an adversary dynamically adjusts environment parameters during training to generate a challenging curriculum.

This comprehensive methodology allows MiniHack to serve as a versatile, performant, and extensible platform for cutting-edge RL research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with MiniHack serve to validate its utility as a challenging and extensible RL benchmark platform, revealing the current limitations of baseline RL algorithms when faced with increasing complexity and novel interaction dynamics.

#### 4.1. Challenging Navigation Tasks

- **Incremental Difficulty:** MiniHack's navigation tasks, starting from simple room traversal, demonstrate a clear progression in difficulty. Baselines (IMPALA, RND, RIDE) often solve simpler versions (e.g., `Room-5x5`, `KeyRoom-S5`).
- **Limitations with Complexity:** However, as layers of complexity are added—such as larger procedurally generated mazes (`MazeWalk-45x19`), partial observability (`Room-Dark`), the introduction of monsters (`Room-Monster`) or deadly traps/lava (`River-MonsterLava`, `Room-Ultimate`)—baseline agents struggle significantly or fail to make any progress. For instance, `River-MonsterLava` and larger `KeyRoom-Random-S15` variants are beyond current baseline capabilities, highlighting hard exploration, planning, and generalization challenges.
- **Memory and Planning:** Tasks like `Memento` (requiring memory of an initial cue) and `CorridorBattle` (requiring strategic monster luring) further expose weaknesses in long-term planning and effective use of environment features.

#### 4.2. Demanding Skill Acquisition Tasks

- **Single Skill Acquisition:** Simple skill acquisition tasks involving a single object-action interaction (e.g., `Eat`, `Pray`, `WoD-Easy`) can be solved by baselines. These require the agent to learn context-sensitive follow-up actions (Confirmation, InventorySelect, Direction).
- **Sequential and Distracting Challenges:** Tasks requiring sequences of entity-action relations, especially in the presence of environmental randomization and distracting elements, prove highly challenging. For example, `WoD-Medium` (requiring pickup, approach, zap) and `LavaCross` (requiring finding and using one of several levitation/freezing objects) see limited to no progress by baselines. This suggests a significant difficulty in efficient state-action space exploration for complex, factorized action spaces and sparse rewards.

#### 4.3. Power of Porting and Enrichment

- **Incremental Hardening:** MiniHack successfully ports environments from other benchmarks like MiniGrid (`MultiRoom`) and Boxoban. The key finding here is MiniHack's ability to easily *enrich* these existing tasks with NetHack's complexities.
- **Exposing Limits:** Even minor additions (e.g., locked doors, monsters, lava tiles instead of walls) to `MultiRoom-N2` and `N4` environments lead to substantial drops in baseline performance, with the "Extreme" versions resulting in no learning progress. This powerfully demonstrates MiniHack's utility in systematically pushing RL algorithms to their limits within a well-controlled experimental setting.

#### 4.4. Support for Unsupervised Environment Design (UED)

- **PAIRED Demonstration:** The paper successfully demonstrates the integration of MiniHack with the PAIRED algorithm for UED. An adversarial environment generator, tasked with placing objects in a small 5x5 room, creates a curriculum.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** The protagonist agent trained with PAIRED, despite only seeing the smaller adversarial environments, shows improved zero-shot performance on challenging, out-of-distribution environments like `MultiRoom-N2-Extreme`, a task where conventional training completely fails. This confirms MiniHack's suitability for UED research.

#### 4.5. Agent Architecture Comparison

- **Architectural Sensitivity:** Experiments comparing small, medium, and large IMPALA architectures on selected tasks reveal that performance is sensitive to architecture. While medium and large models are generally on par, a small model performs poorly on some complex tasks (`Room-Ultimate-15`, `CorridorBattle`) but surprisingly outperforms larger models on another (`Corridor-R3`). This highlights MiniHack's potential as a testbed for investigating the impact of model architecture on RL agent training.

#### 4.6. RLlib Integration

- **Broad Algorithm Support:** The successful integration of MiniHack with RLlib is demonstrated, providing baselines for DQN, A2C, and PPO on a simple task (`Room-15x15`). This showcases MiniHack's compatibility with a wide array of RL algorithms and frameworks, enhancing its accessibility for researchers.

Overall, the findings consistently show that MiniHack effectively creates challenging RL environments that quickly push baseline methods to their limits, particularly in areas requiring advanced exploration, planning, skill acquisition, and generalization. The ability to incrementally increase complexity and support advanced research paradigms like UED is robustly demonstrated.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MiniHack represents a highly significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning, with substantial potential impact across several research fronts. It effectively addresses a critical methodological gap in RL research by offering a unique blend of customizability, richness, and computational efficiency.

#### 5.1. Enabling Targeted and Granular Research

The most profound impact of MiniHack lies in its ability to facilitate targeted research. Researchers can now design environments that precisely isolate specific challenges in RL—such as deep exploration, long-term planning, multi-step skill acquisition, or memory retention—without the confounding complexities of full games or the oversimplification of toy environments. This granularity is crucial for understanding the mechanisms underlying successful RL agents and for diagnosing failures in current methods. It allows for a systematic, scientific approach to developing and evaluating new algorithms.

#### 5.2. Driving Progress in Hard RL Problems

By providing a platform where current RL baselines quickly fail with increased complexity, MiniHack offers a fertile ground for developing next-generation RL algorithms. Problems like systematic generalization, effective state-action space exploration, and learning complex, factorized action sequences are notoriously difficult. MiniHack's task suite, especially its `Skill Acquisition` and `Extreme` versions of `Ported Environments`, directly challenges these areas, pushing the community to innovate.

#### 5.3. Advancing Open-Ended Learning and Curriculum Generation

The framework's robust support for procedural generation and the demonstration of its use with Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) algorithms like PAIRED are highly impactful. This positions MiniHack as a key testbed for open-ended learning, curriculum learning, and meta-reinforcement learning. The ability to dynamically adjust environment parameters and generate diverse, never-before-seen levels fosters research into agents that can learn continuously and generalize broadly, moving beyond memorization.

#### 5.4. Fostering Reproducibility and Standardization

By offering a common Gym-compatible interface, providing a suite of tasks, releasing baselines, and outlining best practices for evaluation, MiniHack significantly contributes to standardizing RL research. This promotes reproducibility, allows for fair comparisons between different algorithms, and reduces the engineering overhead for researchers to get started with challenging benchmarks. The open-source nature of the framework further encourages community contributions and collaboration.

#### 5.5. Practicality and Efficiency

Leveraging the highly optimized C/C++ engine of NetHack ensures that MiniHack environments are computationally efficient, often outperforming other popular benchmarks (e.g., significantly faster than ALE, ProcGen, and MineRL). This efficiency is critical for large-scale experimentation, hyperparameter tuning, and training complex deep RL models, making advanced research more accessible to a broader range of institutions.

#### 5.6. Future Directions and Broader Applications

MiniHack's versatility extends to various emerging RL research areas:
- **Language-Assisted RL:** The rich textual observations and command-line nature of NetHack can naturally support research into agents that learn from or use natural language instructions.
- **Transfer Learning:** The ability to create increasingly complex tasks within the same environment family makes it an ideal platform for studying transfer learning, from simple skills to more complex game situations.
- **Human-in-the-Loop RL:** Its human-readable des-file format could potentially be used in human-in-the-loop environment design or for integrating human knowledge into curriculum generation.

In conclusion, MiniHack is not just another benchmark; it is a powerful research tool that strategically fills a critical void in the RL landscape. By combining the ease of custom environment design with the deep, pre-existing complexity of NetHack, it provides a flexible, performant, and challenging platform that is poised to accelerate progress on some of the most fundamental and pressing problems in reinforcement learning.
