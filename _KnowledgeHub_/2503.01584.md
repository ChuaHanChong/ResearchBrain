# SENSEI: Semantic Exploration Guided by Foundation Models to Learn Versatile World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SENSEI introduces a framework for model-based Reinforcement Learning agents that guides intrinsic exploration using semantic understanding distilled from Vision Language Models (VLMs). It enables agents to learn versatile world models that facilitate significantly faster acquisition of downstream tasks across various environments, including robotic simulations and complex video games.

## Problem

- Traditional intrinsic motivation methods in RL often lead to discovering low-level, semantically uninteresting interactions, hindering efficient learning in complex environments.
- Existing approaches that leverage foundation models for exploration frequently rely on unrealistic assumptions like language-grounded environments or continuous external querying, limiting real-world applicability.
- Agents typically lack an internal, predictive understanding of 'interestingness' within their world model, making planning for semantically valuable experiences inefficient.

## Method

- **SENSEI** distills a semantically grounded intrinsic reward function from VLM annotations by comparing pairs of observations for 'interestingness' in an offline phase.
- It integrates this learned semantic reward into a ==DreamerV3-based recurrent world model==, allowing the agent to predict semantic value internally and combine it with ==epistemic uncertainty== for balanced exploration.
- An adaptive exploration policy, inspired by ==Go-Explore==, dynamically weights semantic reward and model uncertainty to prioritize either seeking interesting states or exploring novel interactions within them.

## Results

- SENSEI significantly outperforms baselines like Plan2Explore in task-free exploration by discovering semantically relevant interactions (e.g., picking up keys, opening drawers) in MiniHack and Robodesk.
- World models pre-trained with SENSEI's semantically guided exploration enable downstream tasks to be learned **orders of magnitude faster** than with baselines (e.g., in MiniHack and Robodesk).
- In the complex Pokémon Red environment, SENSEI GENERAL (which auto-generates environment descriptions) drives superior map progress and battle performance, ultimately allowing the agent to defeat the first Gym Leader through iterative VLM-based reward refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs can effectively distill human notions of 'interestingness' into a trainable reward function, providing high-level semantic guidance for RL agents from raw pixel observations.
> - Learning an *internal* predictive model of semantic interestingness within the agent's world model is crucial for efficient planning and leveraging VLM knowledge.
> - Combining semantic guidance with uncertainty-driven exploration is essential to prevent agents from getting stuck in local optima, ensuring continuous and diverse discovery of meaningful behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a fundamental challenge in Reinforcement Learning (RL): **efficient and meaningful exploration in complex, sparse-reward environments**. This area has been a long-standing "dream" in RL, aiming to enable agents to learn autonomously without manually crafted task rewards, similar to how children learn through curious play.

Historically, intrinsic motivation in RL has relied on general principles such as state-space coverage (Bellemare et al., 2016), novelty or surprise (Schmidhuber, 1991; Pathak et al., 2017), and information gain for world models (Pathak et al., 2019; Sekar et al., 2020). While these methods have shown success, they often lead to the discovery of low-level interactions that may not be semantically meaningful or useful for eventual task completion. The paper highlights a "chicken-or-egg" problem: how can an agent know something is interesting before it has experienced its consequences?

In parallel, the rise of **Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Language Models (VLMs)** has opened new avenues for injecting human-like priors and "notions of interestingness" into AI systems. Several recent works (Klissarov et al., 2023; Du et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023a) have explored leveraging these foundation models to guide exploration. However, SENSEI critically points out significant limitations in these approaches:
1. **Unrealistic Assumptions:** Many methods assume language-grounded environments, access to high-level actions, or exhaustive offline datasets, which are often not available for embodied agents (e.g., in robotics).
2. **Lack of Internal Model:** Most existing methods rely on continuous querying of the foundation model or a simple distilled module for guidance, without learning an *internal model of interestingness* within the agent's own world model. This limits their efficiency and generalizability.

SENSEI positions itself at the intersection of these two trends, offering a novel solution that combines the strengths of model-based RL with the semantic understanding of VLMs, while addressing the aforementioned limitations. It builds on state-of-the-art model-based RL approaches like DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023) and Plan2Explore (Sekar et al., 2020), which excel at learning world models from pixels and using ensemble disagreement for uncertainty-based exploration. By integrating VLM feedback into this powerful framework, SENSEI aims to bridge the gap between general novelty-seeking and semantically informed exploration, leading to more versatile and human-aligned learning. This represents a crucial step towards robust, autonomous agents capable of learning complex behaviors in open-ended environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SENSEI is to develop a framework that enables model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents to perform **semantically meaningful exploration** through intrinsic motivation, guided by the rich understanding embedded in Vision Language Models (VLMs). This aims to move beyond generic curiosity mechanisms and inject human-like notions of "interestingness" into an agent's exploration strategy.

The specific motivations driving the development of SENSEI can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Traditional Intrinsic Motivation:** While methods based on information gain or novelty are effective, they often lead agents to focus on low-level, uninteresting interactions (e.g., a robot endlessly hitting a desk). SENSEI seeks to guide exploration towards more purposeful, high-level behaviors relevant to potential future tasks, akin to how children engage in meaningful play.
- **Leveraging Human Priors through Foundation Models:** The vast knowledge encoded in LLMs and VLMs, primarily derived from human-generated data, presents a unique opportunity to imbue agents with human notions of what constitutes "interesting" or "sensible" behavior. SENSEI aims to effectively distill and utilize this semantic bias.
- **Addressing Unrealistic Assumptions in Existing FM-Guided Exploration:** Current approaches often assume ideal conditions, such as environments with inherent language descriptions, access to exhaustive offline datasets, or the ability to execute high-level actions. SENSEI is motivated to provide a more realistic solution for embodied agents that operate on raw image observations and low-level actions.
- **Developing an Internal Model of "Interestingness":** A critical motivation is for the agent to learn an *internal, predictive model* of semantic interestingness within its world model. This contrasts with methods that continuously query an external foundation model, which can be computationally expensive and limit the agent's ability to plan for interesting futures in imagination. This internal model allows for more efficient and robust guidance.
- **Enabling Versatile World Model Learning:** The goal is not just to improve exploration but, as a consequence, to learn more versatile and useful world models. By guiding agents to explore semantically relevant state-action spaces, SENSEI aims to equip the world model with knowledge crucial for rapidly solving a variety of downstream tasks.
- **Providing a General and Scalable Tool:** The authors aim for SENSEI to be a general framework that can leverage increasingly powerful VLMs. This positions SENSEI as a future-proof solution for autonomous learning across diverse environments, from robotic simulations to complex video games.

In essence, SENSEI strives to enable RL agents to "play" intelligently, guiding their free exploration towards the discovery of rich, meaningful behaviors that are both novel and useful, thereby laying a strong foundation for rapid skill acquisition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SENSEI (SEmaNtically Sensible ExploratIon) is a framework designed to equip model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents with an intrinsic motivation for semantically meaningful behavior. It integrates Vision Language Models (VLMs) and an adaptive exploration strategy into a DreamerV3-based world model architecture. The methodology unfolds in three distinct but interconnected phases: Reward Function Distillation, World Model Learning, and Exploration Policy Optimization.

#### 3.1. Reward Function Distillation: MOTIFate SENSEI

The process begins by distilling a semantically grounded intrinsic reward function, $R_ψ$, from VLM annotations. This phase is conducted offline, prior to active task-free exploration.
1. **Initial Data Collection ($D_{init}$):** SENSEI starts with a dataset of observations (e.g., images) collected from basic self-supervised exploration, typically using an information-gain-based method like Plan2Explore, ensuring a diverse initial sampling of the environment. No reliance on pre-existing expert datasets is assumed.
2. **Environment Description:** A brief textual description of the environment is provided to the VLM. This can be human-generated or, as shown in the "SENSEI GENERAL" variant, automatically generated by the VLM itself from an initial image.
3. **VLM-Based Pairwise Comparison:** A pre-trained VLM (e.g., GPT-4) is prompted to compare pairs of observations from $D_{init}$ with respect to their "interestingness." The VLM provides a preference (Image 1 is more interesting, Image 2 is more interesting, both, or neither). This process generates a dataset of VLM preferences.
4. **Reward Function Training:** Using standard preference-based RL techniques (similar to MOTIF, Klissarov et al., 2023, but vision-based), a neural network reward function $R_ψ: O \rightarrow R$ is trained to minimize a cross-entropy loss over the VLM-annotated preference pairs. This results in a learnable, semantically grounded reward model that can assign an "interestingness" score $r^{sem}_t = R_ψ(o_t)$ to any observation $o_t$.

#### 3.2. World Model: Let Your SENSEI Dream

During exploration, the agent learns a world model that not only predicts environmental dynamics but also incorporates this learned notion of semantic interestingness.
1. **Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM):** Following DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023), SENSEI employs an RSSM as its world model. The RSSM processes observations $o_t$ and actions $a_t$ to learn a compact latent state representation, consisting of a stochastic state $z_t$ and a deterministic memory $h_t$. It predicts future latent states, deterministic memories, and reconstructs observations.
2. **Semantic Reward Prediction Head:** A key extension in SENSEI is that the RSSM is additionally trained with an output head to predict the semantic exploration reward $\hat{r}^{sem}_t$ directly from its latent state $(h_t, z_t)$. This means the agent learns an *internal model of interestingness*, allowing it to predict semantic value without constantly querying the external $R_ψ$ or decoding to images.
3. **Epistemic Uncertainty:** To ensure balanced exploration and avoid getting stuck in local optima (where semantic reward might be high but novelty is exhausted), SENSEI integrates epistemic uncertainty. Following Plan2Explore (Sekar et al., 2020), an ensemble of N models is trained to predict the next stochastic latent states. The disagreement among these ensemble predictions (quantified as variance, $r^{dis}_t$) serves as an intrinsic reward for novelty or model uncertainty.

#### 3.3. Exploration Policy: Go and Explore with SENSEI

The agent's exploration policy is optimized to leverage both semantic interestingness and epistemic uncertainty.
1. **Adaptive Reward Weighting (Go-Explore Inspired):** Instead of a fixed weighted sum, SENSEI employs a dynamic weighting strategy, inspired by Go-Explore (Ecoffet et al., 2021):
 - If the predicted semantic reward $\hat{r}^{sem}_t$ is below a certain quantile threshold $Q_k$ (indicating an "uninteresting" state), the agent's exploration reward $r^{expl}_t$ heavily prioritizes maximizing $\hat{r}^{sem}_t$ (i.e., a low weight $\beta_{go}$ for $r^{dis}_t$). This encourages the agent to "go" to semantically interesting regions.
 - If $\hat{r}^{sem}_t$ is high ($\geq Q_k$, an "interesting" state), the agent's exploration reward shifts to more strongly favor maximizing $r^{dis}_t$ (i.e., a higher weight $\beta_{explore}$ for $r^{dis}_t$). This encourages the agent to "explore" new behaviors and discover novel interactions *within* or *around* interesting states.
 - The quantile $Q_k$ is estimated via a moving average, making the threshold adaptive.
2. **Policy Optimization:** An exploration policy is learned using model-based RL (DreamerV3) by maximizing this combined, adaptively weighted exploration reward $r^{expl}_t$. This policy is then used to generate actions in the environment, gathering new experience to further refine the world model and the policy itself.

By combining VLM-derived semantic guidance with uncertainty-driven exploration within a powerful world model framework, SENSEI aims to achieve efficient, meaningful, and versatile autonomous learning from raw pixel observations and low-level actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate SENSEI's effectiveness across diverse environments, including MiniHack (grid-world game), Robodesk (robotic simulation), and Pokémon Red (complex open-ended video game).

#### 4.1. Reward Function of SENSEI

The paper first validates that the distilled VLM-MOTIF reward function ($R_ψ$) effectively assigns high semantic rewards to genuinely "interesting" and task-relevant events. For instance, in MiniHack, $R_ψ$ shows clear spikes when an agent picks up a key or reaches a door/chest with the key. In Robodesk, rewards increase during object interactions (opening a drawer, pushing blocks). In Pokémon Red, high rewards correspond to winning battles or inflicting damage. This confirms that VLMs can indeed distill meaningful human priors into a trainable reward function.

#### 4.2. Task-Free Exploration

- **MiniHack (KeyRoom-S15, KeyChest):** SENSEI significantly outperforms Plan2Explore (P2X) in discovering semantically interesting interactions, such as picking up keys, opening locked doors, or finding chests with keys. This leads to more frequent completion of the underlying tasks during task-free exploration. A crucial ablation demonstrates that purely optimizing for semantic rewards (VLM-MOTIF without uncertainty) often gets stuck in local optima, highlighting the necessity of combining semantic guidance with information gain to continually push the frontier of experience.
- **Robodesk:** SENSEI leads to more object interactions compared to both Plan2Explore and Random Network Distillation (RND), a strong model-free baseline. While P2X often performs arm stretches and RND moves the arm in the center, SENSEI's semantic guidance encourages diverse interactions with buttons, blocks, drawers, and the sliding cabinet.
- **Generality (SENSEI GENERAL):** The study shows that SENSEI does not require a human-provided environment description. A variant (SENSEI GENERAL) where the VLM first generates an environment description and then uses it for annotations performs comparably to the human-prompted version, demonstrating the approach's robustness and potential for full automation.

#### 4.3. Fast Downstream Task Learning

A core hypothesis is that world models learned through semantically rich exploration facilitate faster learning of downstream tasks.
- **MiniHack:** World models pre-trained with SENSEI exploration data enable DreamerV3 agents to learn to solve new tasks (KeyRoom-S15, KeyChest) significantly faster than agents trained with Plan2Explore world models or learning from scratch (DreamerV3/PPO). In KeyRoom-S15, SENSEI showed an improvement of roughly two orders of magnitude in sample efficiency over PPO.
- **Robodesk:** Similar results are observed, with SENSEI-pre-trained world models leading to more sample-efficient policy learning for tasks like "Drawer-Medium" and "Upright-Block-Off-Table."

#### 4.4. Task-Based Exploration in Complex Environments (Pokémon Red)

In the highly complex, open-ended world of Pokémon Red, SENSEI GENERAL (combined with extrinsic task rewards) demonstrates superior goal-directed exploration:
- **Map Progress:** SENSEI consistently discovers more new map segments and reaches critical game locations, including the first Gym, outperforming Plan2Explore and DreamerV3 (which struggles significantly).
- **Battle Progress:** SENSEI agents achieve higher Pokémon levels, indicating more successful engagement with the game's battle system and training of their team.
- **Iterative Refinement:** A pivotal finding is the success of a "second generation" of VLM annotations. By incorporating data from SENSEI's own exploration into the VLM-MOTIF training, the reward function is refined to correctly value previously out-of-distribution states (e.g., inside the Pewter City Gym). This iterative refinement allows the agent to defeat the first Gym Leader (Brock) and obtain a badge, a significant milestone unachieved by baselines. This highlights a powerful mechanism for continuous learning in vast environments.

In summary, SENSEI successfully leverages VLMs to guide intrinsic exploration towards semantically meaningful behaviors, leading to more versatile world models, faster downstream task learning, and goal-directed progress in complex, open-ended domains, all while working from raw pixel observations and low-level actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SENSEI represents a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning, particularly in the domain of intrinsically motivated exploration, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Semantic Gap in Exploration:** SENSEI effectively addresses a long-standing challenge in intrinsic motivation: guiding agents towards behaviors that are not just novel or uncertain, but also *semantically meaningful* and potentially useful for future tasks. By distilling human notions of interestingness from VLMs, it imbues agents with a higher-level understanding of their environment, moving beyond low-level, aimless interactions.
2. **Robustness and Real-World Applicability:** Unlike prior work that often relies on unrealistic assumptions (e.g., language-grounded environments, high-level actions, exhaustive offline datasets), SENSEI operates directly on raw image observations and low-level actions. This makes the framework far more applicable to real-world embodied agents, such as robots, where such ideal conditions are rarely met. The successful demonstration in a robotic simulation (Robodesk) and complex video games (MiniHack, Pokémon Red) underscores its versatility.
3. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency through Internal World Models:** A key innovation is SENSEI's ability to learn an *internal, predictive model of interestingness* within its world model. This allows the agent to "dream" and plan for semantically interesting futures in imagination, significantly boosting sample efficiency. The results show SENSEI-learned world models enable dramatically faster downstream task learning, outperforming state-of-the-art model-free and model-based baselines by orders of magnitude in some cases. This efficiency is critical for complex tasks where real-world data collection is expensive or time-consuming.
4. **Leveraging the Power of Foundation Models:** SENSEI provides a practical blueprint for how to harness the rapidly evolving capabilities of VLMs for autonomous agent learning. As VLMs become more powerful and general, SENSEI's ability to distill their semantic understanding into actionable intrinsic rewards will only grow, positioning it as a crucial tool for future AI development. The "SENSEI GENERAL" variant further highlights the potential for automating the entire process, including environment description generation.
5. **Iterative Learning and Adaptation in Open-Ended Environments:** The demonstration of "second-generation" VLM annotations in Pokémon Red is particularly impactful. It shows that SENSEI can iteratively refine its understanding of "interestingness" by incorporating data from newly explored, out-of-distribution regions. This adaptive capacity is vital for open-ended learning in vast, complex environments, suggesting a path towards agents that continuously improve their internal models and expand their behavioral repertoire over extended periods.
6. **Potential for Safer and More Ethical AI:** The authors explicitly mention the ethical implications, noting that by guiding agents towards semantically meaningful exploration, SENSEI offers a step towards more energy-efficient and potentially safer autonomous learning. Aimless exploration in real-world scenarios can lead to damage or unsafe situations. By focusing on purposeful interactions, SENSEI reduces these risks, contributing to the responsible deployment of autonomous learning agents.

In essence, SENSEI represents a significant stride towards the long-standing goal of truly autonomous and intrinsically motivated agents. By intelligently combining model-based RL with the semantic prowess of foundation models, it lays a robust foundation for agents capable of learning complex, human-aligned behaviors in an efficient and scalable manner across diverse, challenging environments.
