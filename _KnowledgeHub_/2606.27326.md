# Hallucination in World Models is Predictable and Preventable

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego reframe hallucination in visual world models as a data coverage issue, developing three model-internal signals that predict hallucination with a robust negative Spearman correlation of approximately -0.80. Their work introduces the MMBench2 dataset and demonstrates that coverage-aware training and targeted data collection can prevent hallucination, improving downstream Model Predictive Control performance on unseen tasks by adapting models with as few as 50 trajectories.

## Problem

- Generative world models produce 'hallucinated' trajectories that appear visually fluent but diverge from ground-truth dynamics, leading to silently incorrect decisions in model-based reinforcement learning.
- A lack of suitable datasets hinders systematic study of world model hallucination across diverse tasks and controlled data environments.
- Prior work often focuses on architectural scaling to address hallucination, overlooking data coverage as a primary cause.

## Method

- Developed **MMBench2**, a novel dataset with 65,600 trajectories across 210 continuous control tasks in 10 diverse domains, providing ground-truth actions, rewards, and live simulators.
- Introduced three model-internal, label-free ==hallucination predictors== (Tokenizer Round-Trip Residual, Flow Instability, Inter-Seed Variance) that quantify model uncertainty and instability.
- Proposed data-centric mitigation strategies: coverage-aware training (uniform sampling across tasks) during pretraining and targeted online data collection guided by hallucination predictors as curiosity rewards.

## Results

- Dynamism-normalized hallucination predictors demonstrated a robust negative Spearman correlation of approximately -**0.80** with open-loop rollout ΔPSNRΔ PSNRΔPSNR, indicating strong predictive power for rollout error.
- Coverage-aware training improved Rollout ΔPSNRΔ PSNRΔPSNR by +**0.88** dB and reduced all three hallucination predictors, broadly mitigating different hallucination types during pretraining.
- Targeted data collection using curiosity policies (e.g., based on unorm,ru_{norm,r}unorm,r) significantly improved Model Predictive Control performance on unseen tasks from **0.276** (zero-shot) to **0.325** with just 50 trajectories per task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucination in world models is fundamentally a data coverage problem, occurring primarily in under-represented regions of the state-action space, making it predictable and preventable.
> - Distinct modes of hallucination (perceptual, action-marginalized, scene-diverging) can be systematically characterized and are reliably tracked by lightweight, model-internal signals.
> - Adjusting data sampling during pretraining and leveraging hallucination predictors for targeted data collection are effective, data-efficient strategies for reducing hallucination and improving generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern generative world models are designed to simulate future states of an environment, providing action-controllable trajectories that are increasingly realistic. These models are foundational components in model-based reinforcement learning, where they inform planning and policy decisions. However, a significant challenge is the phenomenon of hallucination, where model rollouts appear visually fluent and superficially plausible but diverge from the ground-truth dynamics of the environment.

The concept of "hallucination" in generative models is borrowed from natural language processing (NLP), where it refers to the generation of factually incorrect text, and has also been observed in image and video generation. In the context of world models, hallucination is considered particularly consequential because these corrupted trajectories are directly fed into downstream planners and policies. This can lead to silently incorrect decisions during control, compromising the reliability and performance of reinforcement learning agents.

Prior research on world models has often focused on architectural advancements, suggesting that larger backbones and increased computational resources might address these issues. This work posits an alternative perspective, arguing that hallucination in world models is primarily a data coverage problem. This perspective aligns with existing literature in offline reinforcement learning, which highlights the importance of data coverage in bounding policy improvement, and uncertainty estimation in deep learning, which seeks to quantify model reliability. While previous work has used ensemble disagreement or prediction error as exploration signals in single-task reinforcement learning, this paper extends these ideas to the broader problem of generative world model fidelity across diverse tasks and proposes novel, model-internal signals for detection.

A key challenge in studying hallucination in generative world models has been the lack of suitable datasets that offer comprehensive control over the training corpus, include behaviorally diverse data spanning multiple tasks and domains, and provide live environments for interactive probing of coverage gaps. This research addresses this gap by introducing MMBench2, a new dataset designed specifically to facilitate in-depth analysis and mitigation of world model hallucination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central hypothesis motivating this research is that hallucination in world models is predominantly a data coverage issue, occurring primarily in regions of the state-action space where the model has limited training experience. Based on this hypothesis, the authors propose that hallucination should be both predictable using lightweight data-centric signals available at runtime and preventable by adjusting the data the model is trained on, rather than relying solely on architectural scaling.

The work pursues several key objectives:

1. **Characterize Hallucination Modes:** To systematically identify and categorize distinct types of hallucination that manifest within the generative world model pipeline. The goal is to understand how failures at different stages—from observation encoding to multi-step dynamics prediction—contribute to the overall hallucination phenomenon.
2. **Develop Hallucination Predictors:** To create a set of model-internal signals that can accurately predict when and where a world model is likely to hallucinate, without requiring external labels or additional training. These predictors are intended to serve as diagnostic tools for runtime detection.
3. **Mitigate Hallucination through Coverage-Aware Training:** To investigate whether adjusting the data sampling strategy during large-scale pretraining, specifically by upweighting under-represented regions of the state-action space, can reduce hallucination across various modes simultaneously. This objective tests the core hypothesis that coverage gaps are a unified cause of different hallucination types.
4. **Mitigate Hallucination through Targeted Online Data Collection:** To explore the use of the developed hallucination predictors as "curiosity rewards" to guide targeted online data collection. The aim is to demonstrate that by actively seeking out and collecting data from regions where the model exhibits high uncertainty or hallucination, a pretrained world model can be efficiently adapted to entirely new or unseen environments.
5. **Establish a Comprehensive Benchmark:** To develop and release MMBench2, a massively multitask dataset with ground-truth actions, rewards, and live simulators across a broad range of tasks and domains. This resource is intended to enable comprehensive study of visual world modeling, hallucination, and generalization, addressing limitations of existing benchmarks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish that data coverage is a fundamental lever for controlling hallucination in world models and to provide practical methods for its detection and mitigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research methodology involved several integrated components: the development of a new dataset, training a large visual world model, categorizing hallucination types, designing predictive signals, and implementing mitigation strategies through both training adjustments and targeted data collection.

**A. MMBench2 Dataset:**
MMBench2 is a novel dataset comprising 65,600 trajectories, equivalent to 427 hours of 224x224 video at 15 fps (23 million frames). It spans 210 continuous control tasks across 10 diverse domains (e.g., DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill3, Atari). Crucially, it provides ground-truth action and reward labels for every trajectory, along with access to live simulators for all tasks. The dataset incorporates diverse behaviors, including human play data, and partitions tasks into a 200-task pretraining corpus and 10 held-out unseen transfer tasks to evaluate generalization.

**B. World Model Architecture and Training:**
A 350-million-parameter visual world model, largely following the Dreamer 4 architecture, was trained. Its training proceeds in two stages:
1. **Video Tokenizer:** A symmetric encoder-decoder Transformer. The encoder processes 224x224 RGB frames, "patchified" into 256 tokens, and projects them into a 64x64 latent code `z`. The decoder reconstructs images from these codes. It is trained with a masked auto-encoding objective, where a fraction of input patches are masked and the model reconstructs them using pixel MSE and LPIPS loss. This component comprises 100 million parameters.
2. **Dynamics Model:** A 250-million-parameter block-causal Transformer operates on packed latent tokens produced by the frozen tokenizer. It is conditioned on action tokens (derived from a 2-layer MLP on 16-dimensional padded actions), shortcut-conditioning tokens (encoding noise level and step size), 32 packed spatial latent tokens, and 4 register tokens. The model is trained using a shortcut flow-matching objective, which combines an empirical one-step regression term with a self-consistency bootstrap.
After pretraining the tokenizer and dynamics model on the MMBench2 training corpus, reward prediction and behavior cloning (BC) policy heads are initialized. The reward predictor is trained via multi-step discrete regression, and the BC policy is a deterministic Gaussian policy trained to predict ground-truth actions via MSE loss.

**C. Characterizing Hallucination:**
The authors identify three distinct hallucination modes, each linked to a different stage of the world model pipeline:
1. **Perceptual Hallucination:** Occurs at the encoder-decoder stage (H=0). The tokenizer reconstructs an observation differently from the ground truth, mapping out-of-distribution scene structures to known in-distribution exemplars.
2. **Action-Marginalized (Ignored) Hallucination:** Occurs within the dynamics model. The predicted next latent is largely insensitive to the input action, causing the rollout to ignore the agent's actions and instead generate an action-marginalized future. This is operationally exposed by shuffling actions and measuring the change in flow MSE.
3. **Scene-Diverging Hallucination:** Occurs during multi-step autoregressive rollouts, where physically implausible events are predicted. This mode is attributed to poor data coverage in specific state regions.

**D. Detecting Hallucination:**
Three model-internal predictors are developed, requiring no labels or additional training:
1. **Tokenizer Round-Trip Residual ($u_r$):** Measures the latent-space residual of a decode-encode round-trip of the dynamics-predicted next latent ($||\hat{z} - \text{Encode}(\text{Decode}(\hat{z}))||$). A large residual indicates the predicted latent falls off the tokenizer's manifold, signaling perceptual hallucination.
2. **Flow Instability ($u_f$):** Quantifies how much the denoiser's clean-target prediction changes between successive Euler integration substeps. High instability indicates a poorly conditioned dynamics head, suggestive of an unstable prediction.
3. **Inter-Seed Variance ($u_s$):** Measures the variance of next-latent predictions across multiple independent denoising trajectories with fixed past and action. High variance points to epistemic uncertainty, indicating regions where multi-step rollouts will diverge.
All three predictors are normalized by scene dynamism ($u_{norm} = u/m$, where $m$ is the per-step RMS change of the latent representation) to prevent confounding with scene activity.

**E. Mitigating Hallucination:**
Two data-centric intervention strategies are proposed:
1. **Coverage-Aware Training:** The existing MMBench2 dataset is resampled to upweight under-represented regions of the state-action space. Specifically, uniform sampling across tasks is used instead of uniform sampling across frames.
2. **Targeted Data Collection:** The hallucination predictors are used as curiosity rewards for online data collection in live environments. Candidate trajectories are rolled out in the world model, scored by predicted hallucination, and the highest-ranked trajectory is executed in the real environment. Data is collected in a closed-loop manner (H=32, replanning every K=16 steps). Various behavior policies are explored, including random, no-op, expert, transformed expert, structured noise, $u_{norm,r}$-based curiosity, and human play.

**F. Evaluation Metrics:**
The performance of the world model and mitigation strategies is assessed using:
- **Reconstruction PSNR:** For encoder/decoder quality.
- **Rollout PSNR gain (dB):** For generated rollout quality relative to a frame-repeating baseline ($\Delta PSNR \leq 0$ indicates scene divergence).
- **Action Shuffle Ratio:** For action sensitivity in the dynamics model (ratio $\leq 1.1$ indicates action marginalization).
- **Hallucination Predictors:** The dynamism-normalized $u_{norm,r}$, $u_{norm,f}$, $u_{norm,s}$.
- **Downstream Task Performance:** Normalized score (0-1) via closed-loop Model Predictive Control (MPC) using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted using the 350M-parameter world model on MMBench2, yielded several key findings:

**A. Hallucination Detection:**
The three proposed dynamism-normalized hallucination predictors ($u_{norm,r}$, $u_{norm,f}$, $u_{norm,s}$) demonstrate strong predictive capabilities for actual hallucination events. A robust negative Spearman correlation of approximately -0.80 was observed between each predictor and open-loop rollout $\Delta PSNR$. This indicates that all three mechanistically distinct predictors reliably track the same underlying rollout error. Furthermore, these predictors consistently outperformed their unnormalized counterparts and other baselines (e.g., latent scene motion) in terms of AUROC for detecting "action ignored" and "scene divergent" events (Table 7, Appendix E).

**B. Coverage-Aware Training:**
The implementation of coverage-aware training, which adjusts sampling to be uniform across tasks rather than frames, demonstrably reduced hallucination. Specifically, applying this strategy to both the tokenizer and the dynamics model during pretraining resulted in the best overall improvements. This included increases in Reconstruction PSNR (+0.44 dB), Action-shuffle ratio (+0.29), and Rollout $\Delta PSNR$ (+0.88 dB), alongside reductions in all three hallucination predictors ($u_{norm,r}$ by -0.20, $u_{norm,f}$ by -0.07, and $u_{norm,s}$ by -0.14) (Table 1). This supports the hypothesis that addressing data coverage gaps at training time can broadly mitigate different types of hallucination.

**C. Targeted Data Collection for Mitigation:**
Targeted data collection, guided by the hallucination predictors, proved effective in mitigating hallucination and improving model performance on unseen tasks.
- A pretrained world model showed some zero-shot transfer ability to unseen tasks (normalized MPC score of 0.276).
- Collecting just 50 trajectories per unseen task using the $u_{norm,r}$-based curiosity policy for finetuning significantly improved performance to a normalized MPC score of 0.325. This result was comparable to using expert policies or human play data (0.362), despite the curiosity policy not relying on privileged behavior. In contrast, finetuning with random actions (0.228) or no-op actions (0.163) yielded substantially lower performance (Table 2).
- Combining all data collection methods (including curiosity-driven data) achieved the highest overall performance (0.390 normalized score), indicating synergistic benefits.

**D. Generalization to Unseen Tasks and Tokenizer Comparison:**
While the base pretrained model exhibited some generalization, finetuning was critical for robust performance on entirely new environments. A comparison with off-the-shelf tokenizers (Table 3) revealed that while external tokenizers like Wan 2.1 VAE generalized better zero-shot to unseen tasks than the base in-domain tokenizer, they underperformed on tasks seen during pretraining. Crucially, after finetuning on unseen tasks, the in-domain tokenizer achieved significantly superior performance on these novel tasks (38.04 PSNR vs. 36.62 for Wan 2.1 VAE), with a greatly reduced performance gap between seen and unseen tasks ($\Delta_{S-U}$ of +1.62 dB vs. -0.17 dB for Wan 2.1 VAE). This indicates the continued value of in-domain finetuning, even when powerful off-the-shelf components are available.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of generative world modeling by re-characterizing hallucination as a problem rooted in data coverage rather than solely in architectural limitations. This reframing offers a different perspective for understanding and addressing a critical failure mode in advanced AI systems.

The practical impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Diagnostic Tools:** The development of three distinct, label-free, and training-free hallucination predictors provides immediate utility for researchers and practitioners. These tools enable real-time detection and diagnosis of hallucination events within world models, offering insights into model reliability during operation.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** The introduction of coverage-aware training demonstrates that significant reductions in hallucination can be achieved simply by adjusting data sampling during large-scale pretraining, incurring no additional data collection cost. This has implications for making large world models more robust and data-efficient.
- **Data-Efficient Adaptation:** The framework for targeted data collection, leveraging hallucination predictors as curiosity rewards, offers a method for efficiently adapting pretrained world models to new, unseen environments. The ability to achieve performance comparable to expert or human data collection with as few as 50 trajectories per task represents a notable advance in reducing the data requirements for deploying AI agents in novel settings.
- **New Research Infrastructure:** The release of MMBench2, along with code, model checkpoints, and a browser interface, establishes a valuable open-source resource for the research community. This dataset enables further investigation into visual world modeling, data efficiency, generalization, and the complexities of hallucination across a diverse range of tasks and environments.
- **Towards Reliable AI:** By providing concrete methods to predict and prevent hallucination, this research contributes to the development of more reliable and trustworthy model-based reinforcement learning agents. Reducing silent failures in world models is essential for their successful application in complex, real-world control tasks.

While the study operates at the 350 million-parameter scale across simulated control tasks, the insights regarding data coverage and the proposed methodologies for detection and mitigation could generalize to larger, billion-parameter models and real-robot applications, though this remains an area for future empirical investigation.
