# Towards Deploying VLA without Fine-Tuning: Plug-and-Play Inference-Time VLA Policy Steering via Embodied Evolutionary Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong introduced VLA-Pilot, a training-free, plug-and-play method to steer pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies at inference time. This approach significantly enhances robotic manipulation success rates, boosting average MSR by up to +0.31 in real-world tasks and achieving performance comparable to fine-tuning with 50 demonstrations, without requiring any new training data.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often experience performance degradation when deployed on new or out-of-distribution (OOD) robotic tasks.
- Fine-tuning VLA policies for each new task is resource-intensive, costly, requires extensive data collection, and risks losing the generalist capabilities of the pre-trained model.
- Existing inference-time steering methods rely on narrowly trained verifiers with limited generalization and are restricted to selecting from a fixed set of actions, which fails if no suitable candidate is initially generated.

## Method

- **VLA-Pilot** introduces a ==training-free, plug-and-play inference-time steering== method that leverages an ==Embodied Policy Steering Chain-of-Thought (EPS-CoT)== module, using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and spatial keypoints for open-world, generalized steering objective reasoning.
- An ==Evolutionary Diffusion== algorithm iteratively optimizes action proposals by applying truncated forward and reverse diffusion processes to selected "elite" actions, refining them for better task alignment beyond simple selection.
- ==Iterative Steering Refinement== provides a closed-loop correction mechanism, allowing the MLLM to act as a ==self-critic==, refine the steering reward, and re-initiate the process for enhanced robustness.

## Results

- VLA-Pilot consistently improved VLA baselines, boosting average Manipulation Success Rate (MSR) by **+0.15** in simulation and by up to **+0.31** for DiVLA and **+0.30** for RDT-1B in real-world robotic tasks.
- It outperformed existing inference-time steering methods like V-GPS and FOREWARN, particularly on complex and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, achieving an average MSR of **0.50** in OOD settings compared to **0.12** and **0.19** for baselines.
- The method achieved performance comparable to supervised fine-tuning (requiring **50 demonstrations**) across real-world tasks and demonstrated strong cross-embodiment generalization on an unseen Franka Panda robot, with MSR improvements ranging from **+0.21 to +0.31**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained VLA models possess latent knowledge sufficient for diverse tasks, which can be effectively extracted and aligned at inference time through intelligent steering, potentially rendering fine-tuning unnecessary for many deployment scenarios.
> - Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), when grounded with embodied information, can serve as generalized, open-world verifiers to infer robust steering objectives, addressing the generalization limitations of previous task-specific verifiers.
> - Combining generative diffusion processes with black-box evolutionary optimization allows for dynamic, adaptive refinement of action proposals, enabling the system to evolve suboptimal actions towards task alignment rather than just selecting from a static set.
