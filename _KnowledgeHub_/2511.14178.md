# Towards Deploying VLA without Fine-Tuning: Plug-and-Play Inference-Time VLA Policy Steering via Embodied Evolutionary Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong introduced VLA-Pilot, a training-free, plug-and-play method to steer pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies at inference time. This approach significantly enhances robotic manipulation success rates, boosting average MSR by up to +0.31 in real-world tasks and achieving performance comparable to fine-tuning with 50 demonstrations, without requiring any new training data.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often experience performance degradation when deployed on new or out-of-distribution (OOD) robotic tasks.
- Fine-tuning VLA policies for each new task is resource-intensive, costly, requires extensive data collection, and risks losing the generalist capabilities of the pre-trained model.
- Existing inference-time steering methods rely on narrowly trained verifiers with limited generalization and are restricted to selecting from a fixed set of actions, which fails if no suitable candidate is initially generated.

## Method

- **VLA-Pilot** introduces a ==training-free, plug-and-play inference-time steering== method that leverages an ==Embodied Policy Steering Chain-of-Thought (EPS-CoT)== module, using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and spatial keypoints for open-world, generalized steering objective reasoning.
- An ==Evolutionary Diffusion== algorithm iteratively optimizes action proposals by applying truncated forward and reverse diffusion processes to selected "elite" actions, refining them for better task alignment beyond simple selection.
- ==Iterative Steering Refinement== provides a closed-loop correction mechanism, allowing the MLLM to act as a ==self-critic==, refine the steering reward, and re-initiate the process for enhanced robustness.

## Results

- VLA-Pilot consistently improved VLA baselines, boosting average Manipulation Success Rate (MSR) by **+0.15** in simulation and by up to **+0.31** for DiVLA and **+0.30** for RDT-1B in real-world robotic tasks.
- It outperformed existing inference-time steering methods like V-GPS and FOREWARN, particularly on complex and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, achieving an average MSR of **0.50** in OOD settings compared to **0.12** and **0.19** for baselines.
- The method achieved performance comparable to supervised fine-tuning (requiring **50 demonstrations**) across real-world tasks and demonstrated strong cross-embodiment generalization on an unseen Franka Panda robot, with MSR improvements ranging from **+0.21 to +0.31**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained VLA models possess latent knowledge sufficient for diverse tasks, which can be effectively extracted and aligned at inference time through intelligent steering, potentially rendering fine-tuning unnecessary for many deployment scenarios.
> - Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), when grounded with embodied information, can serve as generalized, open-world verifiers to infer robust steering objectives, addressing the generalization limitations of previous task-specific verifiers.
> - Combining generative diffusion processes with black-box evolutionary optimization allows for dynamic, adaptive refinement of action proposals, enabling the system to evolve suboptimal actions towards task alignment rather than just selecting from a static set.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the field of robotic manipulation, specifically concerning the deployment of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Recent advancements in VLA models, often trained on large-scale datasets, have demonstrated capabilities in generalizing across various robotic tasks and acquiring a wide range of skills. These models enable robots to perform diverse actions by sampling from learned skill distributions based on context.

However, a recurring issue is the performance degradation observed when these pre-trained VLA policies are deployed on downstream tasks, especially those that differ from the training distribution. The conventional approach to mitigate this degradation is fine-tuning the VLA policy with task-specific data. This strategy, while effective, is often impractical in real-world scenarios due to the substantial costs associated with collecting high-quality demonstration data and the intensive computational resources required for retraining. Furthermore, fine-tuning risks compromising the generalist capabilities acquired by the pre-trained policies.

An alternative approach that has emerged is inference-time policy steering. This paradigm aims to enhance robot behavior at runtime without modifying the underlying pre-trained policy. Existing inference-time steering methods typically involve using an external verifier to evaluate and select the most task-aligned actions from a fixed set of proposals generated by the pre-trained policy. These verifiers often require additional training, leading to limitations in generalization, particularly when faced with out-of-distribution tasks. Moreover, if the pre-trained VLA policy fails to generate any suitable candidate actions, selection-based steering methods cannot recover successful behavior, leading to deployment failures.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by introducing a method, VLA-Pilot, that aims to overcome the limitations of both fine-tuning and existing inference-time steering approaches. It seeks to enable zero-shot deployment of VLA policies without fine-tuning or additional data collection, while improving generalization and task alignment through a novel combination of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) as open-world verifiers and an evolutionary diffusion process for action optimization. This approach shifts the focus from modifying the underlying policy to effectively leveraging its latent capabilities at inference time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the robust zero-shot deployment of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies on diverse downstream robotic manipulation tasks without requiring any additional fine-tuning or data collection. The authors aim to develop a plug-and-play inference-time policy steering method that can significantly improve the success rates and generalization capabilities of off-the-shelf VLA models across different robotic embodiments and task scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges in the current state of VLA models and their real-world application:

- **Performance Degradation in Downstream Deployment:** Despite their generalization potential, pre-trained VLA policies frequently experience a drop in performance when applied to new or slightly varied downstream tasks. This gap between training and deployment hinders their practical utility.
- **Impracticality of Fine-Tuning:** The conventional solution of fine-tuning requires extensive, costly, and time-consuming collection of task-specific demonstration data. It also demands significant computational resources for retraining. These requirements make fine-tuning an impractical solution for rapid deployment in dynamic real-world environments. Additionally, fine-tuning can lead to "catastrophic forgetting," where the policy loses its broad generalist skills in favor of task-specific optimization.
- **Suboptimal Mode Selection:** The authors hypothesize that deployment failures are not always due to the pre-trained VLA policy's inability to generate correct behavior, but rather its struggle with consistently selecting the optimal action mode from its generative distribution at runtime. The desired behavior might exist within the policy's repertoire but is not reliably executed.
- **Limitations of Existing Inference-Time Steering:** Current inference-time steering methods, while promising, face two main issues:
 - **Limited Generalization of Verifiers:** Verifiers in existing methods typically require explicit training and often exhibit constrained generalization because their training data distribution is narrow. This makes them less effective for out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.
 - **Static Action Selection:** These methods are generally limited to selecting actions from a fixed set of proposals. If the pre-trained VLA policy fails to generate any sufficiently task-aligned candidate action, the verifier cannot create a new, feasible action, leading to steering failures. This implies a need for an approach that can *optimize* or *evolve* actions rather than just select them.

To address these limitations, the authors introduce VLA-Pilot, which is motivated by the idea of maximizing the utility of existing VLA models at inference time, rather than solely pursuing larger datasets or architectures. The underlying belief is that pre-trained VLA models already encapsulate sufficient latent knowledge to address new tasks, and this knowledge can be extracted and aligned with task objectives through an effective steering mechanism. The core idea is to leverage Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for their open-world reasoning capabilities to infer generalized steering objectives, and to employ an Evolutionary Diffusion process to actively optimize action proposals beyond mere selection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-Pilot is designed as a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time policy steering method for pre-trained diffusion-based VLA policies. It operates in three main steps: Steering Objective Reasoning, Action Proposal Optimization, and Iterative Steering Refinement.

**A. Steering Objective Reasoning**
The first step involves inferring a task-aligned steering objective reward, $R(a_t; c_t)$, from the given task context $c_t$, which includes a visual observation $o_t$ and a language instruction $l$. VLA-Pilot addresses this using an **Embodied Policy Steering Chain-of-Thought (EPS-CoT)** module, which leverages the open-world reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically GPT-4o in the implementation. This replaces the need for training task-specific verifiers and aims to improve generalization.

The EPS-CoT module decomposes the reasoning process into four interleaved stages:
1. **Steering Goal Confirmation:** The MLLM rephrases and verifies the language instruction to ensure alignment between the user's task demand and the system's steering goal.
2. **Scenario Understanding:** The MLLM interprets the task context, identifying potential action modes based on visual observations. This involves understanding environmental affordances, spatial relationships, and task-relevant entities.
3. **Embodied Augmentation:** To ground the reasoning with concrete robot-environment interactions, EPS-CoT integrates spatial keypoints. These keypoints, representing robot end-effector positions and object locations, are extracted using vision foundation models such as DINO for feature extraction and SAM for masking and keypoint clustering.
4. **Steering Reward Reasoning:** Based on the scenario understanding and embodied information, the MLLM infers the task-aligned steering objective and generates corresponding scoring reward code. This reward is implemented as a non-differentiable black-box scoring function to capture the potentially vague nature of natural language instructions and simplify MLLM reasoning.

**B. Action Proposal Optimization**
To enhance the task alignment of initial action proposals, VLA-Pilot introduces an **Evolutionary Diffusion** algorithm. This algorithm synergistically combines the multimodal expressiveness of the diffusion process with the black-box optimization principles of evolutionary search.

The process is as follows:
1. **Initial Action Proposal Sampling:** An initial set $A_0$ of $M$ actions is sampled independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) from the pre-trained VLA policy, $\pi_{vla}(a_t | c_t)$.
2. **Evolutionary Search Loop:** This loop iteratively evaluates and mutates the action proposals using the steering objective reward $R(a_t; c_t)$. For each evolutionary iteration $k$:
 - **Proposal Scoring:** Each action proposal in the current set $A_{k-1}$ is scored using $R(a_t; c_t)$.
 - **Elite Selection:** High-scoring "elite" proposals $E_k$ are selected from $A_{k-1}$ based on a softmax distribution derived from their scores. A temperature parameter $\tau$ controls the sharpness of this selection.
 - **Elite Diffusion:** To introduce diversity and explore task-aligned actions, a truncated forward diffusion process is applied to the elite proposals. This adds a controlled amount of noise, moving the proposals off the original VLA data manifold.
 - **Elite Denoising:** Subsequently, a truncated reverse diffusion process is applied, utilizing the noise predictor from the pre-trained VLA policy. This step denoises the perturbed elite proposals, bringing them back onto the original VLA data manifold and ensuring their feasibility within the policy's generative distribution. The resulting refined proposals form $A_k$ for the next iteration.
3. **Final Action Selection:** After $K$ evolutionary iterations, the highest-scoring action from the final set $A_K$ that best aligns with the steering objective is selected for execution.

**C. Iterative Steering Refinement**
VLA-Pilot incorporates an **Iterative Steering Refinement** mechanism to enable closed-loop correction of both the inferred steering objective and the executed actions. This module augments the EPS-CoT reasoning with a "reflection" step.

After an action $a_t^\star$ is executed, the MLLM (via EPS-CoT) is provided with:
- The robot's initial action $a_0$ before steering.
- The selected execution proposal $a_t^\star$.
- The updated post-execution task context $\bar{c}_t$.
- The reasoning history $H_t$ from the preceding EPS-CoT step.

The MLLM acts as a self-critic, using this reflection-augmented input to refine the steering reward $R(a_t; c_t)$ and produce a steering success indicator $s$. If the MLLM detects inconsistencies or if the executed action is misaligned (i.e., $s = False$), a new reward is regenerated, and the refinement process continues. This iterative process repeats until the MLLM confirms success or a maximum number of retries is reached, aiming to enhance the accuracy and contextual relevance of the steering.

The entire workflow begins with initial objective reasoning, followed by action optimization. Post-execution, the refinement step provides feedback to potentially re-initiate the process, forming a closed-loop system for continuous improvement within a single task execution attempt.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulation (RoboTwin and ManiSkill3 benchmarks) and real-world settings using a dual-arm DOBOT X-Trainer system, and cross-embodiment generalization was tested on a Franka Panda robot. Two quantitative metrics were used: Manipulation Success Rate (MSR) and Steering Objective Alignment (SOA).

**A. VLA-Pilot Improves Pre-trained VLA Policies:**
- **Simulation:** VLA-Pilot consistently improved the performance of VLA baselines. When integrated with RDT-1B, it achieved an average MSR of 0.65 across six simulation tasks, representing a 0.15 improvement over the base RDT-1B policy (0.50 MSR).
- **Real-World:** In real-world experiments, VLA-Pilot significantly boosted the average MSR of both DiVLA and RDT-1B across six downstream tasks by +0.31 and +0.30, respectively. For instance, in "Mug Handling," DiVLA's MSR was 0.54, while DiVLA+VLA-Pilot achieved 0.75 MSR, indicating improved selection of task-relevant actions. Failures in baseline VLA policies were often attributed to inconsistent selection rather than action infeasibility.

**B. VLA-Pilot Outperforms Steering Baselines:**
- Compared to V-GPS and FOREWARN (other inference-time steering methods) integrated with DiVLA, VLA-Pilot demonstrated higher average MSR and SOA.
- While all methods performed similarly on simpler tasks (e.g., Mug Handling, Bag Handling), VLA-Pilot showed a clear advantage in complex tasks (e.g., Bimanual Bussing, Zippering). This was attributed to the Evolutionary Diffusion strategy, which can evolve action candidates towards feasible and task-aligned distributions, unlike selection-only baselines that may fail if initial proposals are suboptimal.
- Under Out-of-Distribution (OOD) settings, VLA-Pilot maintained a strong average MSR of 0.50, significantly outperforming V-GPS (0.12) and FOREWARN (0.19). This generalization capability was linked to the use of MLLMs for open-world objective reasoning, which are not constrained by dataset-specific verifiers.

**C. VLA-Pilot Matches Fine-tuning Performance:**
- VLA-Pilot achieved performance comparable to supervised fine-tuning methods (DiVLA-finetune and RDT-1B-finetune, both trained on 50 demonstrations) across all six real-world tasks. This finding suggests that for many deployment scenarios, policy fine-tuning may be unnecessary if latent capabilities of pre-trained VLA models can be effectively leveraged through inference-time steering.

**D. VLA-Pilot Achieves Cross-Embodiment Generalization:**
- When deployed on a Franka Panda robot in a zero-shot setting, VLA-Pilot showed notable performance gains over the pre-trained DiVLA baseline. Improvements ranged from +0.21 (Bag Handling) to +0.31 (Table Bussing) in MSR, demonstrating consistent and robust behavior across different robotic embodiments.

**E. Ablation Study:**
- Removing the EPS-CoT module (replacing it with heuristic prompting) resulted in an 8% drop in MSR in simulation, emphasizing the importance of multimodal reasoning and embodied grounding for objective inference.
- Replacing Evolutionary Diffusion with a selection-only mechanism reduced MSR by 11%, confirming the role of adaptive action optimization beyond static ranking.
- Removing Iterative Steering Refinement led to a 5% MSR drop, indicating the importance of closed-loop correction for robustness.

**F. Inference-time Latency:**
- The average per-step inference latency for VLA-Pilot was reported as 2.41 seconds. The primary bottleneck was identified as the MLLM-based reward reasoning module (0.72s for EPS-CoT and 0.76s for Iterative Refinement). This latency represents a trade-off between generalization and efficiency, comparable to other FM-based steering pipelines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA-Pilot method presents several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Enabling Zero-Shot Deployment without Fine-Tuning:** The most direct significance is VLA-Pilot's ability to enable the zero-shot deployment of pre-trained VLA models across diverse downstream tasks and robotic embodiments without requiring any additional policy fine-tuning or data collection. This bypasses the substantial costs and computational demands typically associated with adapting VLA policies, making real-world robotic deployment more practical and accessible.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as open-world verifiers, VLA-Pilot demonstrates superior generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks compared to prior methods that rely on narrowly trained verifiers. The integration of an Evolutionary Diffusion strategy allows for dynamic optimization of action proposals, extending beyond static selection and enabling the system to refine suboptimal or infeasible initial actions towards task alignment. This addresses a key limitation of previous inference-time steering approaches, enhancing the robustness of VLA policies in varied and complex scenarios.
- **Optimizing Existing VLA Models' Utility:** The research highlights a paradigm shift, focusing on maximizing the utility of *existing* pre-trained VLA models during inference rather than solely pursuing larger datasets or architectures. The findings suggest that pre-trained VLA models already encapsulate sufficient latent knowledge to solve new tasks, and this knowledge can be effectively extracted and aligned with task objectives through the proposed steering mechanism. This implies that significant performance gains can be achieved through intelligent inference-time strategies.
- **A Modular and Universal Plug-in:** VLA-Pilot is designed as a "plug-and-play" method, implying it can be readily integrated with various diffusion-based VLA architectures. This modularity makes it a versatile tool for researchers and practitioners looking to enhance the performance of their existing VLA models without modifying their core training pipeline.
- **Performance Comparable to Fine-Tuning:** The demonstration that VLA-Pilot can achieve performance levels comparable to supervised fine-tuning methods (requiring 50 expert demonstrations) is a notable practical impact. This positions VLA-Pilot as a lightweight, generalizable, and cost-effective alternative for deploying VLA policies, especially in scenarios where data collection for fine-tuning is prohibitive.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The method's ability to ground MLLM reasoning with embodied information (spatial keypoints from DINO and SAM) represents an advancement in integrating high-level language understanding with low-level robotic control. The iterative steering refinement mechanism further contributes to closed-loop intelligence, allowing for reactive correction and improved task alignment under dynamic conditions.

Despite its effectiveness, the paper acknowledges limitations, including its reliance on diffusion-based VLA architectures, the non-trivial inference-time latency introduced by MLLMs, and the current dependence on keypoint-based vision grounding. Future work aims to extend the paradigm to broader VLA architectures, optimize MLLM integration for reduced latency, and incorporate richer embodied feedback. Nevertheless, VLA-Pilot represents a substantial step towards more adaptable, generalizable, and deployable robotic manipulation systems in real-world environments.
