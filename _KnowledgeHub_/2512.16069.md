# A Task-Driven, Planner-in-the-Loop Computational Design Framework for Modular Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Italian Institute of Technology and the University of Trento developed a unified computational framework for the task-driven design and motion planning of modular manipulators. The system optimizes manipulator morphology and mounted pose, producing physically feasible designs that meet kinematic, dynamic, and collision constraints for single- and bi-branch robots, demonstrating improved efficiency and robustness in task execution.

## Problem

- Traditional manipulators, with their fixed morphologies, struggle to adapt to new or evolving task requirements.
- Existing computational design frameworks for modular manipulators are often limited to redundant, single-branch structures, hindering their application to non-redundant systems or tasks requiring larger workspaces where high proximal joint torques become problematic.
- The simultaneous optimization of a manipulator's discrete morphology, its continuous mounted pose, and the generation of feasible motions under real-world constraints presents a significant hybrid optimization challenge.

## Method

- A task-driven computational framework was developed, integrating a task planner, a motion planner (utilizing Hierarchical ==Model Predictive Control== for single-branch and Assist Branch Trajectory Optimization for bi-branch systems), and a design optimization module.
- The hybrid discrete-continuous design optimization problem was reformulated into a fully continuous problem by mapping discrete module selections and arrangements to continuous variables using a novel sorting-based function.
- The continuous optimization problem is solved using the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES), which concurrently optimizes continuous representations of morphology, mounting hole configurations, and the manipulator's mounted pose.

## Results

- The framework consistently generated physically feasible manipulator designs and motion plans for pick-and-place, polishing, and drilling tasks, satisfying all specified kinematic, dynamic (joint torque limits), and collision avoidance constraints.
- CMA-ES demonstrated faster convergence and achieved lower final cost values compared to a Genetic Algorithm baseline, particularly highlighted in the polishing task.
- For tasks requiring manipulation in larger workspaces, the optimization frequently converged to bi-branch morphologies, which effectively reduced proximal joint torques and enabled task execution that single-branch designs could not physically achieve without exceeding torque limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Converting the mixed discrete-continuous co-design problem into a fully continuous one via a sorting-based mapping function allows for the efficient application of gradient-free optimizers like CMA-ES, leading to faster convergence and higher quality solutions.
> - Bi-branch manipulator designs, where an assistive branch offloads torque from the primary branch, provide a critical solution for extending operational capabilities in large workspaces without requiring more powerful (and expensive) base modules.
> - A planner-in-the-loop framework ensures that designs are not only functionally optimal but also physically realizable, by integrating strict kinematic, dynamic, and collision avoidance constraints directly into the optimization process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulators have been extensively developed for various industrial applications, including pick-and-place, welding, and polishing. Traditional manipulators are designed with fixed morphologies, limiting their adaptability to new or changing task requirements. Modular manipulators, composed of pre-manufactured and interchangeable modules, offer enhanced adaptability by allowing rapid assembly into diverse configurations. However, the deployment of such systems presents challenges in concurrently optimizing manipulator morphology and its mounted pose while generating feasible motions under kinematic, dynamic, and environmental constraints for specific tasks.

Existing computational design frameworks for modular manipulators have integrated general-purpose planners with morphological design, enabling task-driven co-adaptation of motion and design. However, these planners often support only redundant manipulators or are primarily designed for single-branch robotic structures. Single-branch designs, when extended for larger workspaces, can encounter high proximal joint torques, potentially exceeding the limits of basic joint modules. While multi-branch manipulators offer advantages in load redistribution and handling complex tasks, current design frameworks lack the generality and efficiency to handle both single- and multi-branch cases, particularly concerning the optimization of their mounted pose within a task context. This research aims to address these identified gaps by developing a unified framework capable of handling both redundant and non-redundant manipulators, as well as single- and multi-branch morphologies, through integrated motion planning and design optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified, task-driven computational framework for the design and motion planning of modular manipulators. This framework is intended to overcome several limitations of existing approaches, specifically:

- **Unified Motion Planning:** To develop a motion planner capable of generating feasible trajectories for both redundant and non-redundant manipulator morphologies under multi-subtask scenarios. This addresses the challenge of traditional planners that are often limited to redundant systems or struggle with conflicts in non-redundant cases when attempting to execute tasks demanding full control of both position and orientation.
- **Co-optimization of Morphology and Mounted Pose:** To enable the simultaneous optimization of a manipulator’s discrete morphology configuration and its continuous mounted pose. This is motivated by the understanding that both aspects collectively determine performance, and independent optimization can lead to suboptimal or impractical designs. The challenge lies in the hybrid nature of the search space (discrete morphology, continuous pose).
- **Support for Bi-branch Morphologies:** To introduce and integrate bi-branch morphologies into the design optimization process. This is motivated by the observation that single-branch manipulators can suffer from excessive proximal joint torques in large workspaces, which limits their application. Bi-branch structures, with an assistive branch, can offload torque from the primary branch, extending operational capabilities without requiring more powerful base modules. The framework aims to automatically select between single-branch and bi-branch designs based on task requirements.
- **Constraint Satisfaction:** To incorporate kinematic, dynamic (e.g., joint torque limits), and physical constraints (e.g., collision avoidance) directly into the optimization process, ensuring that the generated designs and motions are physically feasible and safe.
- **Flexible Design Objectives:** To allow for customization of design objectives, such as maximizing manipulability, minimizing joint effort, and reducing the number of modules, thereby enabling flexible design targeting various performance goals.

The overarching motivation is to enhance the adaptability and deployment efficiency of modular manipulators for complex and diverse tasks by providing a comprehensive computational tool that integrates planning and design optimization in a unified, task-driven manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed computational design framework integrates three main components: a task planner, a motion planner, and a design optimization module.

#### 3.1. Task Planning

The desired task is specified by defining an end-effector reference trajectory in Cartesian space, represented as a sequence of desired poses (position and orientation) over time.
- **Position Trajectory Generation:** Position trajectories are generated using n-th order polynomial interpolation, minimizing acceleration while satisfying waypoints, velocity, acceleration limits, and collision avoidance constraints.
- **Orientation Trajectory Generation:** Orientation trajectories are generated by smoothly interpolating between predefined waypoints using an exponential map formulation, ensuring differentiable continuity.

#### 3.2. Task-Driven Motion Planning

This component generates feasible joint-space trajectories for both single-branch and bi-branch manipulators.

- **Hierarchical Model Predictive Control (HMPC) for Task Execution (Single-branch):**
 - **Kinematics Model:** The end-effector state (position, orientation, linear velocity, angular velocity) is defined, and its relationship to joint velocities and accelerations is characterized by the Jacobian matrix.
 - **State-space Formulation:** A predictive model for the end-effector state is formulated using forward Euler integration.
 - **Two-level MPC:**
 - **High-level MPC:** Solves a quadratic programming (QP) problem to compute optimal joint velocities and accelerations for high-priority sub-tasks, minimizing tracking error and regularization terms, subject to joint position, velocity, and acceleration limits.
 - **Low-level MPC:** Refines the joint-space trajectory for lower-priority sub-tasks, treating the high-level MPC outputs as hard constraints to ensure that high-priority tasks are not compromised. Kinematic constraints are enforced.
 - The HMPC formulation requires the number of Degrees of Freedom (DoFs) to exceed the task dimensionality at each priority level to maintain redundancy-based planning.

- **Assist Branch Trajectory Optimization (Bi-branch):** For bi-branch morphologies, an additional optimization is performed for the assist branch.
 - The objective is to minimize the overall joint effort of the entire manipulator and keep the assist branch close to its initial joint state, while satisfying full-body dynamics and maintaining the main-branch's HMPC-determined states.
 - This is formulated as a non-linear programming problem (NLP) and solved using Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP).
 - To ensure motion smoothness, the optimized joint movements of the assist branch are refined using B-spline interpolation.

#### 3.3. Design Optimization: Morphology and Mounted Pose Determination

This module concurrently optimizes the manipulator's morphology (module selection and arrangement) and its mounted pose (position and orientation of the base).

- **Morphology Representation:** A state vector `Cv` encodes module selection and assembly sequence. Unique identifiers are assigned to basic modules, end-effectors, and two "Semicolon of Manipulator" (SoM) virtual modules. SoMs partition `Cv` into segments, with the first segment attached to the base, and subsequent segments representing operational branches (main and assist) mounted on a "Y" module. An auxiliary vector `S` specifies mounting positions for secondary branches on the Y-module. This encoding allows for seamless switching between single- and bi-branch configurations.
- **Sorting-based Mapping Function:** To enable continuous optimization, discrete module selection and arrangement, along with mounting hole order, are mapped to continuous variables. Each module is assigned a continuous score `M` ∈. A sorting map `g(M)` ranks these scores in descending order to generate the discrete state vector `Cv`. Similarly, mounting hole selections are encoded using a continuous vector `h` and mapped via `S = g(h)`.
- **Design Optimization Formulation:** The objective is to minimize a cost function `E` that combines several criteria:
 - **Task Execution Requirement and Objective:** End-effector tracking performance is quantified by the average squared error between the actual and desired poses. Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) is used to generate smooth, time-varying tracking error bounds, which are incorporated as constraints.
 - **Dynamic Constraints:** Joint torques are calculated from planned joint states and end-effector forces using the manipulator's inverse dynamics. These calculated torques must satisfy hardware-defined maximum actuation limits.
 - **Collision Avoidance:** Modules are approximated as capsules for efficient collision detection. Constraints are imposed to ensure minimum signed distance from environmental obstacles (using a Signed Distance Field, SDF) and to prevent self-collision between robot capsules.
 - **Functional Metrics:** The cost function includes terms to minimize overall joint effort (`F_eff`) and maximize manipulability (`M_man`), with a task-specific sub-Jacobian used for manipulability calculation in non-redundant cases. The number of modules (`len(Cv)`) is also penalized.
 - **Redundancy Constraints:** Ensures that the number of DoFs exceeds the task dimensionality for both high- and low-priority sub-tasks, enabling the HMPC to function effectively.
- **CMA-ES-based Optimization:** The entire optimization problem, which is now formulated as a fully continuous problem, is solved using the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) algorithm. This gradient-free optimization algorithm iteratively updates the continuous morphology scores (`M`), mounting hole scores (`h`), and the continuous mounted pose (`Pm`) towards optimal solutions. A critical factor `δ` in the objective function ensures that designs violating hard constraints (tracking accuracy, collision, dynamics, redundancy) receive a high penalty, effectively filtering infeasible solutions before optimizing for functional metrics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework's effectiveness was validated through extensive simulations and physical experiments across three representative tasks: pick-and-place, polishing, and drilling.

#### 4.1. General Findings

1. **Feasible and Constrained Designs:** The framework consistently generated various designs (morphologies and mounted poses) that satisfied both kinematic and dynamic constraints, while also avoiding environmental collisions, for pick-and-place, polishing, and drilling tasks.
2. **Flexible Design Objectives:** Customizing objective functions (e.g., maximizing manipulability, minimizing joint effort, reducing module count) allowed for flexible design targeting specific performance goals.
3. **Bi-branch Morphology Generation:** The framework successfully designed bi-branch morphologies capable of operating in large workspaces, effectively offloading torque from the primary branch without requiring the manufacture of more powerful basic modules. This represents an automatic selection capability between single-branch and bi-branch morphologies.

#### 4.2. Task-Specific Results

- **Pick-and-Place with Obstacle Avoidance:**
 - **Robustness:** The optimization converged to optimal solutions within 100 iterations across four distinct initial guesses (two single-branch, two bi-branch), consistently resulting in single-branch morphologies.
 - **Performance:** All optimized designs met tracking accuracy requirements and satisfied dynamic and collision constraints. The final designs ranged from 4 to 5 DoFs, meeting the HMPC redundancy requirement while penalizing excessive module usage. Tracking errors remained within GPR-derived tolerance bounds throughout the planned motion.

- **Polishing Task:**
 - **Objective Preference Impact:** Different objective preferences yielded distinct optimal morphologies.
 - *Scenario 1 (Combined Manipulability/Effort):* Morphology A (9 modules, 6 DoFs) achieved a balance.
 - *Scenario 2 (Max Manipulability):* Morphology B (9 modules, 7 DoFs) showed a 38.39% to 115.00% increase in manipulability compared to other scenarios, benefiting from increased redundancy.
 - *Scenario 3 (Min Joint Effort):* Morphology C (8 modules, 6 DoFs) reduced total joint effort by using fewer modules and a lighter structure.
 - **Comparison with Baseline (GA):** CMA-ES demonstrated faster convergence and achieved lower final cost values compared to a Genetic Algorithm (GA) baseline that used discretized pose space. This highlights the advantage of CMA-ES operating in a continuous domain over discretization-based methods.
 - **Hardware Validation:** Hardware experiments with the three optimized morphologies (A, B, C) successfully executed the polishing task. Tracking errors remained consistently below specified thresholds, demonstrating real-world applicability.

- **Drilling Task in Larger Workspace:**
 - **Bi-branch Convergence:** For this expanded task requiring manipulation in a larger workspace (doubled from previous work), the CMA-ES algorithm often converged to bi-branch solutions, even when initialized with single-branch morphologies. This was driven by the strict enforcement of torque limits.
 - **Torque Reduction:** A key finding was the effectiveness of the assist branch in reducing proximal joint torques. For example, Morphology A had a passive assist branch (0 DoF) but still effectively reduced torque. Morphology D, with a 2-DoF assist branch, performed best with the lowest combined cost.
 - **Hardware Validation:** Hardware tests with the optimized bi-branch Morphology D showed that joint torques remained below limits, unlike the single-branch version (where the "common elbow joint" torque exceeded limits). This demonstrated that bi-branch designs are functionally advantageous for physically satisfying requirements in large-scale workspaces. The assist branch actively adjusted its posture in real time to redistribute loads and reduce torque.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advance in the computational design and deployment of modular robotic manipulators. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Enhanced Adaptability and Versatility:** The framework's ability to automatically design and plan motions for both redundant and non-redundant manipulators, along with single- and bi-branch morphologies, significantly expands the adaptability of modular robots. This allows for tailored designs that can effectively handle a wider array of complex industrial and service tasks with varying kinematic and dynamic demands.
- **Improved Design Efficiency and Quality:** By reformulating the hybrid discrete-continuous design optimization problem into a fully continuous one via a sorting-based mapping function, the framework enables the use of efficient gradient-free optimizers like CMA-ES. This improves convergence speed and the likelihood of identifying high-quality, globally optimal solutions compared to traditional combinatorial or discretized methods, reducing the time and cost associated with manual robot design.
- **Practical Feasibility and Robustness:** The rigorous integration of real-world physical constraints—including kinematic limits, dynamic torque limits, and comprehensive collision avoidance (self-collision and environment)—ensures that the generated designs are not merely theoretical but physically feasible and safe for deployment. The demonstrated robustness across different initial guesses and task scenarios further supports its practical applicability.
- **Novel Bi-branch Paradigm:** The introduction and systematic optimization of bi-branch manipulators, particularly their ability to automatically emerge from the optimization process, addresses a critical limitation of single-branch designs in large workspaces: excessive proximal joint torques. This innovation allows for extended reach and higher payload capacity without necessitating the manufacture of more powerful, and thus more expensive, base modules, leading to more cost-effective and capable modular systems.
- **Foundation for Future Co-Design:** The planner-in-the-loop framework lays groundwork for a more integrated co-design paradigm where control and morphology are deeply intertwined. Future work aiming for a unified MPC-based controller for real-time control of both single- and bi-branch systems could further enhance the adaptability and robustness of modular manipulators in dynamic and uncertain environments.

Overall, this research contributes to making modular manipulators more accessible, adaptable, and efficient for a broad spectrum of real-world applications, ranging from manufacturing to complex human-robot collaboration scenarios.
