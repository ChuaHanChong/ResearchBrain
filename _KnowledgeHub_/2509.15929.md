# Improving Monte Carlo Tree Search for Symbolic Regression

> [!summary] Summary
> A refined Monte Carlo Tree Search framework integrates an extreme bandit allocation strategy and evolution-inspired state-jumping actions to enhance symbolic regression. This approach significantly improves sample efficiency and robustness, achieving competitive performance on benchmarks while discovering accurate and parsimonious mathematical expressions with lower computational cost.

## Problem

- Symbolic Regression (SR) is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem that requires the simultaneous search for both functional form and parameters, making it computationally intensive.
- Traditional MCTS bandit strategies are designed to maximize expected cumulative rewards, which is misaligned with SR's objective of finding a single, globally optimal expression.
- Standard MCTS's sequential, token-by-token construction limits exploration efficiency within the vast and complex expression space, potentially trapping the search in local optima.

## Method

- Developed an "==extreme bandit==" allocation strategy for MCTS, specifically tailored for identifying the globally optimal expression, with theoretical optimality guarantees for certain reward distributions.
- Integrated evolution-inspired state-jumping actions (mutation and crossover from genetic programming) directly into the MCTS framework to enable non-local transitions and more efficient exploration.
- Implemented a bidirectional propagation mechanism and priority queues within MCTS nodes to efficiently share high-reward expressions and substructures across the search tree.

## Results

- Achieved competitive average recovery rates on ground-truth benchmarks (e.g., **93.25%** on Nguyen, **100%** on Nguyen_C and Jin), matching or surpassing state-of-the-art methods like NGGP and DSR.
- Ranked second in median test R^2 on SRBench Black-box benchmarks while producing substantially simpler models, demonstrating a favorable balance between accuracy and parsimony.
- Empirical analysis confirmed that state-jumping actions reduce the estimated tail decay rate of reward distributions (e.g., from `a "∈" [4, 6]` to `[2, 4]` on Nguyen-4), correlating with improved computational efficiency and faster convergence compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symbolic regression's objective requires an "extreme bandit" strategy, focused on identifying the best possible outcome, rather than traditional MCTS approaches that maximize cumulative rewards.
> - Incorporating non-local, state-jumping actions effectively reshapes the reward landscape, reducing the tail decay rate of reward distributions and leading to faster, more stable convergence.
> - The hybrid MCTS framework effectively synergizes guided search with global search capabilities, demonstrating a robust strategy for complex combinatorial optimization problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Symbolic Regression (SR) is a methodology aimed at discovering interpretable and concise analytical expressions to represent data-driven relationships. Unlike traditional regression, which optimizes parameters within fixed model structures, SR simultaneously searches for both the functional form and its parameters within a combinatorial space. This characteristic renders SR a computationally intensive, NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem. The resulting expressions have applications across various scientific and engineering domains, including finance, materials science, climatology, and real-time control systems.

Historically, Genetic Programming (GP) has been the predominant heuristic for SR, evolving populations of candidate expressions through biologically inspired operations like mutation and crossover. However, GP often generates overly complex formulas and exhibits sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning. More recently, learning-based methods have emerged to leverage structural regularities in symbolic expressions. Deep neural networks have been employed to directly generate formulas, though their generalization capabilities beyond training distributions can be limited.

An alternative approach involves framing SR as a sequential decision-making task, amenable to Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques. Inspired by successes in domains like game playing, RL-based SR, including policy-gradient methods and recurrent neural networks, constructs expressions token by token. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), known for its guided search that balances exploration and exploitation, has also been adapted to navigate the vast space of symbolic programs.

This work contributes to the MCTS for SR landscape by addressing specific limitations of existing MCTS applications. Traditional MCTS bandit strategies are typically designed to maximize expected cumulative rewards, which may not align with SR's objective of finding a single, globally optimal expression. Furthermore, the sequential, token-by-token construction inherent in standard MCTS can limit the efficiency of exploring distant regions of the expression space. The authors propose an improved MCTS framework that integrates a specialized "extreme bandit" allocation strategy and incorporates "state-jumping" actions inspired by GP, positioning this work as a hybrid approach that enhances MCTS's capabilities for combinatorial optimization in SR.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges of symbolic regression and the specific limitations observed in existing MCTS-based approaches. Symbolic regression aims to identify the single best mathematical expression, which is fundamentally a global optimization problem. While MCTS has shown promise in SR, its traditional formulation—focused on maximizing expected cumulative rewards—is not optimally aligned with this "best-arm identification" objective. Additionally, MCTS typically constructs expressions sequentially, token by token, which can lead to inefficient exploration of a vast and complex combinatorial search space, potentially trapping the search in local optima or requiring extensive computational resources to discover novel functional forms.

The paper identifies two critical distinctions between SR, when formulated as an RL problem, and conventional RL:
1. **Global Optimality vs. Cumulative Reward**: The primary objective in SR is to find the *globally optimal state* (i.e., the highest reward, corresponding to the best-fitting and simplest expression), rather than to maximize the expected cumulative rewards over a trajectory.
2. **Flexible Action Space**: The action space in SR is not restricted to fixed, stepwise transitions. It can be designed to include more flexible "state-jumping" mechanisms, allowing for non-local transitions that bypass intermediate steps.

Addressing these motivations, the key objectives and contributions of this work are:

- **Formulating an Extreme-Bandit Allocation Strategy**: The first objective is to develop a bandit allocation strategy specifically tailored for identifying the globally optimal expression. This strategy, analogous to the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithm but adapted for extreme values, is designed to enhance the identification of high-reward states. The authors aim to derive optimality guarantees for this strategy under specific reward distribution assumptions, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for its use in SR.
- **Introducing Evolution-Inspired State-Jumping Actions**: The second objective is to integrate non-local, evolution-inspired actions (mutation and crossover, borrowed from genetic programming) directly into the MCTS framework. These "state-jumping" actions are intended to enable more efficient exploration by facilitating direct transitions to promising regions of the search space, rather than relying solely on sequential symbol construction. A further motivation is that these actions are expected to reshape the reward landscape, potentially making the search process more robust and efficient.
- **Developing a Refined MCTS Framework**: The overarching goal is to combine these innovations into a refined MCTS framework for symbolic regression. This framework aims to achieve competitive performance against state-of-the-art libraries on various benchmarks while also yielding new insights into the application of RL methods for combinatorial optimization problems characterized by the need for global optimality and flexible action spaces.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose an improved MCTS framework for symbolic regression, built upon two core innovations: an extreme bandit allocation strategy and evolution-inspired state-jumping actions.

#### 3.1. Symbolic Regression as a Markov Decision Process (MDP)

The problem of symbolic regression is first formulated as an MDP. Expressions are represented as binary trees and converted into symbol sequences via pre-order traversal. The MDP components are defined as follows:
- **State Space (S)**: Consists of all partial symbol sequences or incomplete binary expression trees, constrained by a maximum depth (H).
- **Action Space (A)**: Is state-dependent, comprising arithmetic operators, elementary functions, constants, and variables. At the maximum tree depth, actions are restricted to terminal symbols (constants or variables).
- **Transition Function (P)**: Is deterministic. An action appends a symbol to the sequence, expanding the expression tree by adding a child symbol to the deepest non-full operator node.
- **Reward Function (R)**: Yields zero for non-terminal states. For a complete and valid expression (terminal state), the reward is given by `1 / (1 + NRMSE)`, where NRMSE is the normalized root mean square error against the data. This formulation seeks to maximize the terminal reward, aligning with the objective of finding the best expression.
- **Discount Factor (β)**: Set to 1, as all reward is concentrated at the terminal state.

#### 3.2. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) Enhancements

The standard MCTS algorithm involves four phases: Selection (using a criterion to traverse the tree), Expansion (adding a new node for an unvisited action), Simulation (rollout from the new node to a terminal state), and Backpropagation (updating statistics along the visited path). The proposed method introduces modifications to the selection and exploration phases.

##### 3.2.1. Extreme Bandit Allocation Strategy

Recognizing that symbolic regression seeks the single best outcome rather than maximizing expected cumulative rewards, the authors adapt the multi-armed bandit problem to an "extreme bandit" setting. The objective is to discover the arm (action) that yields the maximum possible reward.
- **Proposed Strategy**: The selection step at state `s` for the (T+1)-th round is `I_{T+1}:= arg max_k { Q_hat_k,T + 2c * (ln T / T_k,T)^gamma }`, where `Q_hat_k,T` is the *highest observed reward* from arm `k` so far (not the average), `T_k,T` is the number of times arm `k` has been selected, and `c > 0, gamma > 0` are hyperparameters.
- **Theoretical Guarantees**: The paper establishes upper bounds on the "performance gap" (difference between maximum possible reward and expected highest reward) and "regret" (difference between expected highest reward from optimal arm and expected highest reward from chosen strategy) under the assumption that reward distributions exhibit polynomial decay near their maximum (modeled by a Beta distribution `P(x; a, b) = 1 - (1 - x/b)^a`).
 - Theorem 1 demonstrates that for such "polynomial-like arms," the performance gap `G(T)` is `O(1/T^(1/a1))` and the regret `R(T)` is `O(ln T / T^(1+1/a1))`, where `a1` is the tail decay rate of the optimal arm. This result suggests the UCB-extreme strategy is optimal for this class of problems, provided hyperparameters `c` and `gamma` satisfy specific conditions (`1/gamma >= a1` and `2*a1*c^(1/gamma) >= 2 + 1/a1`).
 - Theorem 2 provides lower bounds, reinforcing the optimality claims.
 - Theorem 3 presents a negative result for "exponential-like arms," indicating that distributions with faster decay near their maximum make identifying the best expression significantly harder, leading to a performance gap of `O(1/ln T)`.

##### 3.2.2. Evolution-Inspired State-Jumping Actions

To improve exploration and overcome the limitations of sequential symbol construction, the framework incorporates mutation and crossover operations from genetic programming as "state-jumping" actions.
- **Priority Queue**: Each MCTS node maintains a priority queue (`Q(v)`) storing the top-N highest-reward expressions (trajectories) that pass through it.
- **Probabilistic State-Jumping**: During the selection phase, before standard UCB-extreme application, a state-jumping action (mutation or crossover) is probabilistically triggered at a node `v`. The probability `p_s` is depth-dependent, decreasing exponentially with depth to favor exploration nearer the root.
- **Action Application**: If triggered, expressions are drawn from `Q(v)` and subjected to mutation (e.g., uniform, node replacement, subtree insertion/shrinkage) or crossover (single-point crossover with another expression from `Q(v)`). These operations generate new, potentially distant, states (expressions) directly at the current node, bypassing sequential symbol choices.
- **Bidirectional Propagation**:
 - **Backward Propagation**: Standard MCTS backpropagation updates visit counts and Q-values, and also updates the `Q(v)` of ancestral nodes with any new high-reward trajectory discovered.
 - **Forward Propagation**: After a new MCTS node is expanded, its parent node propagates information (including partial trajectories and associated rewards) downwards to its child nodes. Similarly, after a state-jumping action, information is propagated both upward and downward. This mechanism ensures that high-reward substructures and complete expressions are efficiently shared and retained across the MCTS tree.

#### 3.3. Algorithm Integration

Algorithm 1 outlines the integrated process: MCTS proceeds as usual, but before selecting a child node, a probabilistic check `ξ1 < p_s` decides whether to attempt a state-jumping action (mutation or crossover). Random exploration `ξ3 < ϵ` is also incorporated, defaulting to UCB-extreme exploitation. Newly generated states from state-jumping (or standard rollouts) update node statistics and priority queues via bidirectional propagation. The aim is for this hybrid strategy to improve exploration efficiency and enhance interpretability by leveraging successful substructures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed MCTS framework on various symbolic regression benchmarks, including ground-truth and black-box datasets, and analyzes the impact of its key components.

#### 4.1. Algorithm Analysis (Nguyen Benchmarks)

- **Reward Distribution Characterization**: Empirical analysis on Nguyen benchmarks (ground-truth datasets) revealed that the reward distributions for most test cases exhibit polynomial tail decay rates, approximately in the range `a ∈`. This observation is relevant for the theoretical guarantees of the extreme bandit strategy.
- **Impact of State-Jumping Actions**: The integration of state-jumping actions significantly reshapes the reward landscape. For instance, in Nguyen-4, the estimated `a` value for the reward distribution drops below 2 after introducing state-jumping, compared to `a ∈` with standard MCTS. Across other Nguyen test cases, `a` values shifted to ``. This reduction in `a` indicates that state-jumping leads to a higher probability of finding high-reward expressions, correlating with faster and more stable convergence.
- **UCB-Extreme Strategy Performance (Alone)**: MCTS equipped solely with the UCB-extreme strategy (without state-jumping) could achieve optimal recovery performance for estimated tail decay rates `a1 >= 6`. However, its performance was highly sensitive to the exploration constant `c`, requiring careful tuning, and it struggled with problems involving more complex target expressions like Nguyen-4. This suggests that while theoretically sound, practical application requires additional mechanisms.
- **Ablation Study (Table 2)**: An ablation study on the Nguyen benchmark quantified the contributions of the two main innovations:
 - **Model A (random selection with state-jumping)**: Showed degraded performance compared to the full algorithm, indicating the positive contribution of the UCB-extreme strategy.
 - **Model B (UCB-extreme without state-jumping)**: Performed significantly worse than the full algorithm, underscoring the substantial contribution of the evolution-inspired state-jumping actions.
 - **Model C (standard UCB1 without state-jumping)**: Demonstrated very poor performance, highlighting the effectiveness of the UCB-extreme strategy over traditional UCB1 for SR.
 These results confirm that state-jumping actions are a primary driver of performance, effectively reshaping the reward landscape and facilitating the generation of complex, high-quality expressions. The UCB-extreme strategy also provides a measurable benefit.

#### 4.2. Comparative Study

The proposed algorithm (Algorithm 1) was benchmarked against several state-of-the-art symbolic regression methods.

- **Basic Benchmarks (Ground-Truth Datasets, Table 1)**:
 - The algorithm achieved competitive average recovery rates against DSR, GEGL, NGGP, and PySR across Nguyen, Nguyen_C, Jin, and Livermore benchmarks.
 - For example, on the Nguyen benchmark, it achieved an average recovery rate of 93.25%, comparable to NGGP (92.33%) and surpassing DSR (83.58%) and PySR (74.41%). It achieved 100% recovery on Nguyen_C and Jin benchmarks. On the more challenging Livermore benchmark, it achieved 71.41%, matching NGGP (71.09%) and outperforming others.
- **SRBench Black-Box Benchmarks (Figure 3)**:
 - Evaluation on the SRBench Black-box benchmark (122 problems) showed that the method strikes a favorable balance between accuracy and model complexity.
 - It ranked second in terms of median test R^2, just behind Operon, while simultaneously producing substantially simpler models.
 - On the Pareto frontier of accuracy versus model size, the method positioned itself among the top-performing approaches, alongside Operon, GP-GOMEA, and DSR, indicating its ability to find accurate and concise expressions.
- **Runtime Performance (Table 6)**:
 - On the Nguyen benchmark, the algorithm demonstrated substantially faster single-core runtimes and required fewer expression evaluations compared to NGGP, despite achieving comparable recovery rates. This indicates improved computational efficiency.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the proposed extreme bandit allocation strategy, coupled with evolution-inspired state-jumping actions and bidirectional propagation, provides a robust and efficient MCTS framework for symbolic regression. The algorithmic innovations collectively contribute to reshaping the search landscape, enabling the discovery of accurate and parsimonious mathematical expressions with competitive computational cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an advancement in the application of Monte Carlo Tree Search to the domain of symbolic regression, offering both theoretical and practical contributions.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Tailored MCTS for Global Optimization**: The work highlights a crucial distinction between traditional MCTS (focused on maximizing cumulative rewards) and the specific requirements of symbolic regression (identifying a single globally optimal expression). The proposed extreme bandit allocation strategy directly addresses this, offering a theoretically grounded approach for "best-arm identification" in MCTS, which has broader implications for other global combinatorial optimization problems. The derived optimality guarantees under polynomial reward decay assumptions provide a formal basis for its effectiveness.
- **Enhanced Exploration and Robustness**: The integration of evolution-inspired state-jumping actions (mutation and crossover) into the MCTS framework is a significant algorithmic innovation. This mechanism allows for non-local transitions in the search space, overcoming the limitations of purely sequential symbol construction. Empirically, these actions reshape the reward landscape, making it more amenable to discovery (as indicated by the reduced tail decay rate of the reward distribution). This not only improves exploration efficiency but also enhances the robustness of the search process, enabling the algorithm to escape local optima and discover more complex expressions.
- **Bridging GP and RL**: By tightly embedding genetic programming operations within MCTS via bidirectional propagation and priority queues, the work demonstrates an effective hybrid strategy. Unlike prior modular approaches that alternate between GP and RL, this integrated design allows for a more synergistic use of information and exploration strategies, combining the guided search of MCTS with the global search capabilities of evolutionary algorithms.
- **Competitive Performance and Efficiency**: The proposed framework achieves competitive performance against leading state-of-the-art symbolic regression libraries across a diverse range of benchmarks, including challenging black-box datasets. Its ability to find expressions that strike a favorable balance between accuracy and model complexity, coupled with improved runtime efficiency, underscores its practical utility.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Symbolic Regression Tools**: The improved MCTS framework could become a valuable tool in the symbolic regression practitioner's toolkit, particularly for problems where interpretable and concise models are critical. Its competitive performance and efficiency may encourage broader adoption in scientific discovery and engineering applications.
- **Inspiration for Combinatorial Optimization**: The insights gained from adapting MCTS for global optimality and incorporating flexible, non-sequential actions have the potential to influence the design of RL algorithms for other complex combinatorial optimization problems. Any domain requiring the identification of a single optimal solution within a vast search space could benefit from similar algorithmic considerations.
- **Theoretical Development in Bandit Problems**: The theoretical analysis of the extreme bandit problem, particularly the derivation of regret bounds under specific reward distribution assumptions, contributes to the ongoing research in multi-armed bandit theory. This work provides a concrete application and validation for such specialized bandit strategies.
- **Foundation for Future Hybrid RL**: The successful integration of GP-inspired operations into MCTS could pave the way for more sophisticated hybrid RL algorithms. Future research might explore dynamic adaptation of state-jumping probabilities, learning optimal mutation/crossover strategies, or applying similar hybridizations to other search paradigms.

#### 5.3. Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Reward Distribution Assumptions**: The theoretical guarantees of the extreme bandit strategy rely on specific assumptions about the reward distribution (polynomial decay). Future work could investigate relaxing these assumptions to extend the strategy's applicability.
2. **Unsolved Challenging Problems**: Certain highly challenging problems, such as Nguyen-12, remain difficult to solve completely, indicating room for further improvement in the framework's capabilities.
3. **Generalization to Broader Domains**: While promising, extending these specific innovations to a wider array of reinforcement learning and combinatorial optimization domains requires further investigation and adaptation.

Overall, this research provides a significant step forward in making MCTS a more effective and efficient algorithm for symbolic regression, with implications for both practical applications and theoretical developments in artificial intelligence.
