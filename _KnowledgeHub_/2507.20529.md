# Enhancing Spatial Reasoning through Visual and Textual Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> The SpatialVTS method enhances Vision Language Models (VLMs) for spatial reasoning by integrating visual and textual thinking without requiring explicit depth or mask information. It achieved an 82.03% average accuracy on qualitative spatial benchmarks and demonstrated quantitative performance comparable to models that utilize additional depth cues.

## Problem

- Vision Language Models (VLMs) struggle with complex spatial reasoning, particularly interpreting implicit spatial cues and performing multi-step inference.
- Existing spatial reasoning datasets often suffer from incorrect labels, templated questions, and a lack of explicit reasoning processes (Chain-of-Thought), hindering effective VLM training.
- Many approaches for enhanced spatial reasoning rely on additional, often costly to acquire, visual information like depth maps or segmentation masks, limiting their generality.

## Method

- Introduces ==SpatialVTS== (Spatial reasoning through Visual and Textual thinking Simultaneously), a method that integrates ==Spatial Visual Thinking== for identifying relevant explicit and implicit visual cues and ==Spatial Textual Thinking== for logical, step-by-step inference.
- Employs a "seeking the cause by grasping the result" strategy during dataset reconstruction to generate high-quality, process-oriented ==Chain-of-Thought rationales== for training.
- Reconstructs and manually corrects existing spatial reasoning datasets (SpatialRGPT, VPT) to improve data quality, add annotations for "potential targets," and convert them to a purely image-text format suitable for generalized VLMs.

## Results

- SpatialVTS achieved **82.03%** average accuracy on qualitative spatial reasoning benchmarks (WhatsUP, VSR, BLINK-Spatial, SRGPT-QUAL), outperforming all other baselines.
- The model demonstrated quantitative spatial perception (Q-Spatial++, SRGPT-QUAN) almost on par with SpatialRGPT, a baseline that specifically utilizes depth information and segmentation masks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the textual thinking (CoT) phase is critical, with its removal leading to a **3-7%** performance drop, and that the reconstructed dataset effectively contributes to improved performance with larger data sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial reasoning in VLMs can be significantly enhanced by explicitly integrating both visual cue identification (including implicit cues) and multi-step textual reasoning.
> - High-quality, process-oriented training data, particularly with Chain-of-Thought rationales, is crucial for developing robust and generalized spatial reasoning capabilities in VLMs.
> - Achieving advanced spatial perception in VLMs is possible using only standard RGB images and text, without the need for additional modalities like depth maps or segmentation masks, making the approach more versatile.
