# Enhancing Spatial Reasoning through Visual and Textual Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> The SpatialVTS method enhances Vision Language Models (VLMs) for spatial reasoning by integrating visual and textual thinking without requiring explicit depth or mask information. It achieved an 82.03% average accuracy on qualitative spatial benchmarks and demonstrated quantitative performance comparable to models that utilize additional depth cues.

## Problem

- Vision Language Models (VLMs) struggle with complex spatial reasoning, particularly interpreting implicit spatial cues and performing multi-step inference.
- Existing spatial reasoning datasets often suffer from incorrect labels, templated questions, and a lack of explicit reasoning processes (Chain-of-Thought), hindering effective VLM training.
- Many approaches for enhanced spatial reasoning rely on additional, often costly to acquire, visual information like depth maps or segmentation masks, limiting their generality.

## Method

- Introduces ==SpatialVTS== (Spatial reasoning through Visual and Textual thinking Simultaneously), a method that integrates ==Spatial Visual Thinking== for identifying relevant explicit and implicit visual cues and ==Spatial Textual Thinking== for logical, step-by-step inference.
- Employs a "seeking the cause by grasping the result" strategy during dataset reconstruction to generate high-quality, process-oriented ==Chain-of-Thought rationales== for training.
- Reconstructs and manually corrects existing spatial reasoning datasets (SpatialRGPT, VPT) to improve data quality, add annotations for "potential targets," and convert them to a purely image-text format suitable for generalized VLMs.

## Results

- SpatialVTS achieved **82.03%** average accuracy on qualitative spatial reasoning benchmarks (WhatsUP, VSR, BLINK-Spatial, SRGPT-QUAL), outperforming all other baselines.
- The model demonstrated quantitative spatial perception (Q-Spatial++, SRGPT-QUAN) almost on par with SpatialRGPT, a baseline that specifically utilizes depth information and segmentation masks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the textual thinking (CoT) phase is critical, with its removal leading to a **3-7%** performance drop, and that the reconstructed dataset effectively contributes to improved performance with larger data sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial reasoning in VLMs can be significantly enhanced by explicitly integrating both visual cue identification (including implicit cues) and multi-step textual reasoning.
> - High-quality, process-oriented training data, particularly with Chain-of-Thought rationales, is crucial for developing robust and generalized spatial reasoning capabilities in VLMs.
> - Achieving advanced spatial perception in VLMs is possible using only standard RGB images and text, without the need for additional modalities like depth maps or segmentation masks, making the approach more versatile.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial reasoning, the ability to understand spatial arrangements and relationships in 2D and 3D space, is a fundamental component for advanced AI systems, particularly in Visual Question Answering (VQA) and robotics. Despite the rapid advancements in Vision Language Models (VLMs), tasks requiring nuanced spatial understanding—such as determining 3D object positions or complex spatial relationships—remain challenging.

Historically, research in spatial reasoning often relied on explicit scene representations like spatial scene memories or scene graphs. These approaches frequently framed spatial problems as path-finding tasks within these structured representations. More recently, with the advent of powerful VLMs, the focus has shifted towards enhancing their inherent spatial understanding capabilities through data-driven methods and by integrating richer visual elements such as depth maps or region masks.

This paper positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing the inherent limitations of current VLMs in spatial reasoning, particularly their struggle with implicit spatial cues and complex, multi-step inference. While other works like SpatialRGPT have sought to integrate additional visual elements (e.g., depth, mask information) to improve spatial understanding, this paper takes a different, more generalized approach. Liang et al. argue that many spatial reasoning tasks require going beyond explicitly mentioned objects and leveraging *potential* visual cues, combined with a robust, multi-step textual reasoning process.

The work builds upon the success of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) and its adaptation to VLMs. Unlike previous CoT-based VLM works that primarily focus on interpretability or general reasoning, SpatialVTS specifically tailors CoT for spatial challenges. Crucially, the authors aim to achieve enhanced spatial reasoning *without introducing additional explicit information like masks or depth data*. This differentiates it from methods like SpatialRGPT, which heavily rely on such external data, making SpatialVTS potentially more applicable to general-purpose VLMs that operate primarily on RGB images and text.

Furthermore, the paper tackles a critical often-overlooked aspect: the quality and structure of existing spatial reasoning datasets. Many benchmarks, while extensive, suffer from issues like incorrect labels or lack of process-oriented thinking, hindering effective VLM training. By undertaking significant dataset reconstruction and manual correction, Liang et al. address a fundamental data bottleneck in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision Language Models (VLMs) by simultaneously integrating visual and textual thinking processes.** The authors propose a novel method called SpatialVTS (Spatial reasoning through Visual and Textual thinking Simultaneously) to achieve this goal.

The key motivations driving this research are:

- **Addressing VLM Deficiencies in Spatial Reasoning:** Despite rapid progress, VLMs still struggle with complex spatial reasoning tasks, especially those requiring inference beyond direct object identification. The authors highlight that accurately interpreting spatial relationships is crucial for advanced applications like embodied intelligent decision-making and human-computer interaction.
- **Leveraging Implicit Visual Cues:** The authors observe that many spatial problems require analyzing not just the objects explicitly mentioned in a question, but also *potential* related visual cues in the image that can act as references (e.g., using a building's stories to estimate vertical distance between two objects). Existing VLMs often fail to identify and utilize these implicit cues.
- **Stimulating Long-Term, Logical Textual Reasoning:** Simply identifying objects is insufficient; robust spatial reasoning demands logical, step-by-step inference. The paper argues that enabling VLMs to conduct "long-term thinking" based on visual cues and dialogue, similar to human reasoning, is essential for tackling complex spatial questions and avoiding simplistic, potentially incorrect answers.
- **Improving Data Quality and Structure:** The authors identify significant shortcomings in existing spatial reasoning datasets, such as incorrect labels, templated questions leading to uncorrelated visual content, and a lack of explicit reasoning processes (Chain-of-Thought). These data issues undermine effective model training and generalization.
- **Achieving Generalization without Additional Modalities:** A significant motivation is to improve spatial reasoning without relying on additional, often costly to acquire, visual information like masks or depth data. This allows for a more generalized and efficient approach applicable to standard RGB images, making the model more versatile.

In essence, the paper aims to imbue VLMs with a more human-like reasoning process that combines smart visual perception (identifying relevant explicit and implicit cues) with deliberate, logical textual inference to solve challenging spatial problems effectively and generalize across diverse scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed SpatialVTS method comprises two main phases: **Spatial Visual Thinking** and **Spatial Textual Thinking**, complemented by a crucial **Dataset Reconstruction** strategy.

#### 3.1. Spatial Visual Thinking

This phase focuses on identifying and extracting all potential visual cues relevant to a spatial reasoning problem, going beyond just the explicitly mentioned objects.

- **Target Identification:** The VLM is trained to automatically generate location-related specific tokens for not only the objects referenced in the question but also other *potential* objects in the image that could aid in spatial reasoning (e.g., a building's height for estimating vertical distance, as shown in Figure 1).
- **Bounding Box Representation:** To simplify VLM interpretation and generation of bounding boxes, the method follows a discrete token-based representation inspired by VPT. An image is divided into a `k x k` grid (e.g., 8x8 cells). A region's location is represented by the indices of the grid cells containing its top-left and bottom-right pixels. This avoids the ambiguity of continuous coordinates and makes the bounding box information digestible as tokens within the VLM's language context (e.g., `<x_0><y_3><x_3><y_6>`).
- **Visual Cue Encoding:** Unlike some methods that use separate feature extractors (e.g., DINOv2 in VPT) for enhanced fine-grained understanding of candidate regions, SpatialVTS encodes these candidate regions using the *same* visual encoder as the main image. This maintains consistency and aims for a more unified visual understanding.

#### 3.2. Spatial Textual Thinking

This phase leverages the identified visual cues and explicitly stimulates the model's logical reasoning ability to derive the final answer.

- **Multi-Modal Input:** The VLM takes the original question, the initial image, and the visual cues (cropped images of the relevant regions obtained from Spatial Visual Thinking, re-inputted into the vision encoder) as input.
- **Prompt-Driven Reasoning:** An elaborately designed prompt guides the VLM to perform long-term reasoning. This is crucial because many spatial questions require multi-step inference, not just direct answers. This process encourages the model to provide rationales and intermediate steps before concluding.
- **"Seeking the Cause by Grasping the Result" Strategy:** To effectively train the model for reasoning, a novel strategy is employed for generating the reasoning process during dataset creation (Figure 4a). Instead of asking the model to reason *to* an answer, the question, visual cues, and the *correct answer* are simultaneously fed into a public visual language model. This "backwards" approach helps in finding a rational and correct reasoning path that leads to the given answer, ensuring the quality of the generated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales. During training and inference, the model then uses these generated rationales to learn to provide its own step-by-step reasoning based solely on the problem and visual cues (Figure 4b).

#### 3.3. Dataset Reconstruction

Recognizing the limitations of existing spatial reasoning datasets, the authors undertake a significant effort to clean, correct, and restructure training data.

- **Sources:** The dataset is built upon SpatialRGPT and VPT datasets.
- **Addressing Input Format Limitations:** Original SpatialRGPT required explicit `<mask>` or `<depth>` placeholders. The reconstruction replaces these with generic region labels (e.g., `Region`, `Region`) and marks corresponding bounding boxes on the image, making the input purely image and plain text, suitable for generalized VLMs.
- **Improving Data Quality:**
 - **Manual Correction:** A substantial manual annotation effort is performed to filter out unreasonable questions and correct numerous incorrect answers present in the automatically generated SpatialRGPT dataset.
 - **Uncorrelated Questions:** Questions randomly templated without proper image context are identified and removed or corrected.
 - **Adding Potential Targets:** Bounding box annotations for *potential* helpful targets (beyond those explicitly mentioned) are added, along with rationales explaining their relevance.
- **Introducing Process-Oriented Thinking:** For both SpatialRGPT and VPT datasets, the "seeking the cause by grasping the result" strategy (described above) is used with public VLMs to automatically generate detailed, long-term reasoning processes (rationales) for each question, linking visual cues to the final answer. This transforms the datasets from simple QA pairs into rich, reasoning-infused examples.

By implementing these sophisticated visual and textual thinking phases, coupled with a meticulously reconstructed dataset, SpatialVTS aims to teach VLMs a more profound and robust form of spatial reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate SpatialVTS demonstrate its effectiveness across various spatial reasoning tasks, both qualitative and quantitative.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Training Details:** The model is trained on a merged dataset of 100k samples from the manually corrected SpatialRGPT dataset and 340k samples from the reconstructed VPT dataset (140k spatial relation and 200k general questions). Qwen2-VL serves as the base model, trained on 8 NVIDIA H800 GPUs.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **Qualitative Spatial Reasoning:** WhatsUP, VSR, BLINK-Spatial, and the qualitative split of SRGPT-Bench (SRGPT-QUAL). These cover hundreds of spatial relation concepts (e.g., "up/down," "front/behind").
 - **Quantitative Spatial Reasoning:** Q-Spatial++ and the quantitative split of SRGPT-Bench (SRGPT-QUAN). These assess the model's perception of absolute scales and distances.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Qualitative:** Accuracy is determined by scoring answers in using DeepSeek-V3-0324, accepting scores > 0.5.
 - **Quantitative:** Accuracy is based on the maximum ratio between ground truth ($d^*$) and estimated distance ($\hat{d}$), $\delta = \max(\frac{\hat{d}}{d^*}, \frac{d^*}{\hat{d}})$. Answers satisfying $\delta \le 2$ are accepted (denoted as $\delta_{\le2}$).
- **Baselines:** Generalized VLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and spatially-focused VLMs (VPT, SpaceThinker, SpatialRGPT). Notably, SpatialRGPT uses additional depth and mask information, posing a challenging baseline for SpatialVTS, which explicitly avoids such extra inputs.

#### 4.2. Main Results

- **Qualitative Spatial Reasoning (Table 1):**
 - SpatialVTS (vision/text) achieves the **best overall average accuracy of 82.03%**, significantly outperforming all other baselines.
 - For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B scores 67.26%, VPT scores 74.47%, and SpatialRGPT scores 59.00% on average.
 - SpatialVTS demonstrates strong performance across individual benchmarks, indicating a robust and generalized understanding of spatial concepts, which is crucial given the vagueness and biases in single benchmarks.

- **Quantitative Spatial Reasoning (Figure 6):**
 - SpatialVTS (vision/text) **outperforms all VLMs trained solely with text and images** on both Q-Spatial++ ($\delta_{\le2}$) and SRGPT-QUAN ($\delta_{\le1.25}$).
 - Remarkably, its performance is **almost on par with SpatialRGPT**, a model that specifically utilizes depth information and segmentation masks during training. This is a significant finding, as SpatialVTS achieves comparable quantitative spatial perception *without* these explicit additional modalities.
 - The results highlight that spatial visual and textual thinking can effectively compensate for the lack of explicit depth cues.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Textual Thinking (Table 2):**
 - Removing the textual thinking (CoT) phase from training (i.e., "without text") leads to a performance drop of 3% on SpatialRGPT-QUAN and 7% on Q-Spatial++.
 - This confirms that the explicit textual reasoning process is a critical component for enhancing both quantitative and qualitative spatial understanding.

- **Impact of Data Amount (Table 3):**
 - Training with a smaller dataset (250k samples, including 120k CoT samples) still yields strong performance, comparable to VPT, despite VPT being trained on 800k data points and using an additional visual encoder.
 - Increasing the dataset size to the full 440k samples further improves performance (e.g., from 72.89% to 78.21% on VSR), validating the quality and effectiveness of the reconstructed dataset and the CoT-based approach.

#### 4.4. Case Studies (Figure 7)

- The case studies visually illustrate SpatialVTS's superior reasoning:
 - In a quantitative distance estimation task, SpatialVTS accurately uses a TV's known width as a reference scale, providing a reasonable estimate, unlike SpatialRGPT which gives a significantly deviated answer without analysis.
 - For qualitative tasks, SpatialVTS provides concise and critical reasoning (e.g., "left is relative to the camera's perspective"), contrasting with SpaceThinker's trivial descriptions.
 - Compared to VPT, which also identifies key target boxes, SpatialVTS demonstrates more in-depth visual cue analysis and more accurate answers, focusing on subtle relationships like the gap between a mat and a wall to determine fit.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that SpatialVTS, through its integrated visual and textual thinking and high-quality, reasoning-infused training data, significantly advances VLM spatial reasoning capabilities. It achieves state-of-the-art or near state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks, critically, without requiring additional explicit depth or mask information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Enhancing Spatial Reasoning through Visual and Textual Thinking" carries significant implications for the development of more capable and intelligent Vision Language Models, with potential impacts across several domains.

### 6. Advancement in General-Purpose VLM Capabilities

The most significant contribution of this work is demonstrating that complex spatial reasoning can be substantially enhanced in VLMs *without* relying on explicit additional information like depth maps or segmentation masks. Many prior state-of-the-art models for spatial tasks integrate these additional modalities, which can be computationally expensive and difficult to acquire at scale for diverse scenarios. SpatialVTS's ability to achieve comparable or superior performance using only RGB images and text suggests a more generalizable and practical approach for enhancing foundational VLMs. This paves the way for models that can understand spatial relationships in a wide array of visual data without needing specialized input formats.

### 7. Emphasis on Implicit Visual Cues and Contextual Reasoning

The paper's insight that spatial reasoning often requires considering "potential objects" or implicit visual cues (like a building's stories or a TV's size for reference) is crucial. By explicitly training models to identify and utilize these subtle contextual elements through "Spatial Visual Thinking," SpatialVTS moves beyond simple object detection to a more nuanced understanding of visual scenes. This paradigm shift could lead to VLMs that are better at interpreting real-world situations, where context and implicit relationships are paramount.

### 8. Validation of Chain-of-Thought for Spatial Reasoning

The success of the "Spatial Textual Thinking" phase, particularly the ablation study demonstrating its importance, strongly validates the application of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for spatial tasks. By encouraging the model to generate step-by-step rationales, SpatialVTS makes the reasoning process more transparent and robust. This approach can lead to less "pattern collapse" and fewer hallucinations, which are common issues when models provide simple, unreasoned answers. The "seeking the cause by grasping the result" strategy for CoT generation also offers a valuable technique for constructing high-quality reasoning data.

### 9. Importance of High-Quality, Process-Oriented Data

The extensive dataset reconstruction and manual correction efforts undertaken by the authors highlight a critical bottleneck in VLM development: the quality and structure of training data. By cleaning existing datasets, correcting errors, and adding detailed reasoning processes, the authors demonstrate that meticulously curated data, especially with explicit reasoning traces, can significantly boost model performance and generalization. This underscores the need for continued investment in high-quality, human-curated datasets for advanced reasoning tasks, rather than solely relying on large-scale, automatically generated, but often flawed, data.

### 10. Potential Impact on Downstream Applications

Enhanced spatial reasoning has broad implications for various downstream AI applications:
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Robots and embodied agents need precise spatial understanding to navigate, manipulate objects, and interact with their environment effectively. SpatialVTS could enable more robust decision-making in complex 3D spaces.
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** Improved spatial understanding allows for more natural and intuitive human-AI interactions, such as interpreting spatial commands ("move this object to the left of that one") or answering questions about object locations.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** For VQA systems, the ability to reason about complex spatial relationships will unlock answers to more sophisticated questions that go beyond simple object recognition.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** Better spatial reasoning can contribute to more immersive and interactive AR/VR experiences by allowing systems to accurately place and manipulate virtual objects in real-world or virtual environments.

In conclusion, Liang et al.'s work represents a significant step forward in making VLMs truly understand the spatial world. By demonstrating an effective and generalized approach to spatial reasoning through integrated visual and textual thinking, and by highlighting the critical role of data quality, this research provides a strong foundation for future advancements in AI's perception and reasoning capabilities. The declared intention to publish the model and datasets will further accelerate research in this crucial area.
