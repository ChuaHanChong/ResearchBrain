# RetouchIQ: MLLM Agents for Instruction-Based Image Retouching with Generalist Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> RETOUCHIQ presents an MLLM agent designed for instruction-based image retouching, translating natural language into precise, executable operations within professional software. The system addresses the inherent subjectivity of creative editing tasks through a Generalist Reward Model and Policy-Guided Reward Training, producing professional-grade, instruction-aligned image edits.

## Problem

- Existing AI methods for image editing (traditional and diffusion-based) either lack flexibility for natural language instructions or alter original image content, hindering precise, controlled retouching.
- Applying reinforcement learning to subjective creative tasks like image retouching is challenging due to the difficulty in defining reliable, objective reward signals for varied aesthetic goals.
- Reward models trained on synthetically perturbed edits face a distribution shift when evaluating complex edits generated by a policy model, leading to suboptimal guidance during RL.

## Method

- An MLLM agent (==policy model==) is developed to interpret natural language instructions and translate them into a reasoning trace and a sequence of executable, parameterized editing operations for professional software.
- A Generalist Reward Model (GRM) is introduced, which is an MLLM designed to dynamically generate context-aware evaluation metrics and scalar rewards, overcoming the challenge of subjective feedback for creative tasks.
- Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT) is implemented to align the reward model's training distribution with the policy's actual outputs, mitigating distribution shift by using policy-generated "weak" edits for GRM refinement.

## Results

- RETOUCHIQ consistently produces qualitative results that are more semantically aligned with user instructions and professionally styled compared to existing general-purpose MLLMs and diffusion-based editing methods.
- On the RETOUCHEVAL benchmark, RETOUCHIQ-GRM achieved state-of-the-art performance across all evaluation metrics (L1, L2, Semantic Consistency, Perceptual Quality, Overall Score) and categories.
- The Generalist Reward Model (GRM) trained with Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT) achieved the highest accuracy in distinguishing strong from weak edits on policy-generated data, directly correlating with the best performing policy model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can be effectively leveraged as executable agents that translate high-level natural language instructions into precise, controllable parameter adjustments for professional-grade creative software.
> - Subjective creative tasks benefit significantly from a dynamically generated, context-aware reward model (GRM) that evaluates edits based on self-generated metrics, providing reliable feedback for reinforcement learning.
> - Aligning the reward model's training data distribution with the policy's evolving outputs through Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT) is critical for stable and effective RL, resolving issues of distribution shift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "RETOUCHIQ" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), AI agents, reinforcement learning (RL), and computer vision for image editing.

**MLLMs as General-Purpose Agents:** Recent advancements in MLLMs have expanded their capabilities beyond basic vision-language understanding (like perception and captioning) to acting as general-purpose agents. These agents can invoke external tools, leveraging the broad reasoning abilities of language models with the specialized expertise of domain-specific systems. This paradigm, where LLMs plan and execute actions using external tools, is gaining significant traction across various domains, including retrieval, image generation, and autonomous workflows. RETOUCHIQ contributes to this trend by demonstrating MLLMs' potential in the highly specialized and creative domain of professional image retouching.

**Challenges in Image Editing with AI:**
- **Traditional Methods:** Early works in image retouching focused on learning to predict editing operations for aesthetically pleasing results. While providing explainable procedures, these models often lacked the flexibility to interpret nuanced, user-specific textual instructions.
- **Diffusion-Based Models:** More recently, diffusion models have shown impressive capabilities in generating and editing images based on prompts. However, a significant limitation is their stochastic nature, which often leads to unintended alterations of original image content and structure, making them less suitable for precise, controlled retouching where content preservation is crucial. RETOUCHIQ explicitly addresses this by using executable editing tools rather than generative processes.
- **MLLM Agents for Image Editing:** Current research has begun to explore MLLM agents that interact with external editing tools. These models demonstrate potential in understanding user intentions and reasoning about editing steps. However, a critical hurdle remains: the inherently subjective nature of creative photo retouching. Unlike objective tasks (e.g., math problems) where verifiable, rule-based rewards suffice, creative editing can have multiple equally valid outcomes for a single request. Existing pixel-level or reference-based rewards prove unreliable in this subjective landscape, leading to suboptimal training for RL-based MLLM agents.

**Novelty of RETOUCHIQ:** This work directly tackles the "subjectivity challenge" in image retouching. It moves beyond conventional rule-based or reference-dependent reward mechanisms by introducing a **generalist reward model (GRM)**. This GRM is an RL fine-tuned MLLM designed to dynamically evaluate edited results by generating a set of context-aware metrics on a case-by-case basis. This approach offers a significant leap in providing reliable, high-quality, instruction-consistent gradients for reinforcement learning in creative domains. Furthermore, the introduction of **Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT)** addresses the critical issue of distribution shift between synthetic weak edits and real policy-generated edits, leading to more robust and stable performance.

By bridging high-level aesthetic goals with precise, executable parameter control in professional software, and by innovating on the reward mechanism for subjective tasks, RETOUCHIQ positions itself as a leading advancement in developing intelligent, explainable, and controllable AI assistants for creative professionals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the RETOUCHIQ project stem from the limitations of current AI-driven image editing solutions, particularly in addressing the subjective nature of creative tasks:

1. **Enable Instruction-Based Executable Image Retouching with MLLM Agents:** The core objective is to allow users to provide natural language instructions for image editing, which an MLLM agent can then interpret and translate into precise, executable operations using professional-grade editing tools. This aims to bridge the gap between high-level artistic intent and low-level parameter adjustments, making sophisticated editing accessible to a wider audience, including those without specialized technical or creative expertise in software like Adobe Lightroom.

2. **Overcome the Challenge of Subjective Rewards in Reinforcement Learning:** A central motivation is to address the inherent difficulty of applying reinforcement learning (RL) to creative tasks like image retouching. In such subjective domains, multiple valid edits can exist for a single instruction, making conventional rule-based or reference-image-dependent rewards unreliable. The paper aims to develop a robust mechanism for providing high-quality, context-aware reward signals that can effectively guide RL training.

3. **Develop a "Generalist Reward Model" (GRM):** To achieve the above, a key objective is to design and implement an MLLM-based GRM that can dynamically generate relevant evaluation metrics for a given editing instruction and then provide scalar feedback based on these metrics. This allows the reward system to be flexible and adapt to diverse aesthetic goals, moving beyond fixed, handcrafted evaluation criteria.

4. **Mitigate Distribution Shift in Reward Model Training:** The researchers identify that training a reward model on synthetically perturbed weak edits can lead to a distribution shift when evaluating real outputs from a policy model that produces complex, combined edits. Therefore, an objective is to propose a novel training scheme (Policy-Guided Reward Training, PGRT) to align the data distributions of the policy editing model and the reward critic model, thereby ensuring stronger and more stable reward estimation.

5. **Achieve High-Quality, Semantically Consistent, and Explainable Edits:** Ultimately, the project aims for RETOUCHIQ to produce aesthetically consistent, instruction-aligned, and publication-quality image edits that surpass existing MLLM-based and diffusion-based systems. A secondary objective is to provide explainable reasoning throughout the editing process, offering transparency and control to the user.

In essence, RETOUCHIQ seeks to empower MLLM agents to act as intelligent, flexible, and interpretable assistants for creative image editing by developing a sophisticated, context-aware reward mechanism for reinforcement learning, thereby addressing a fundamental challenge in applying AI to subjective artistic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RETOUCHIQ proposes a novel MLLM agent framework for instruction-based executable image retouching, built upon a two-stage training strategy involving supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), significantly enhanced by a custom-designed Generalist Reward Model (GRM) and Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT).

#### 3.1. Overall RETOUCHIQ Pipeline

The system comprises a **policy model** that takes a user's natural language instruction and an input image. It generates two outputs:
1. A **reasoning trace**: A textual description of the semantic interpretation and aesthetic intent.
2. A **sequence of parameterized editing tool-use operations**: Executable commands (e.g., `exposure=+0.9; contrast=-30`).
These operations are designed to be applied via professional image processing software (specifically Adobe Lightroom).

#### 3.2. Data Preparation

A high-quality dataset is crucial for training. The ideal datum includes:
- A before-and-after image pair (I₀, I)
- A user editing goal (g) in natural language
- A reasoning process (q) for optimal strategy
- An editing step sequence (e)
The data collection pipeline starts with real user editing trajectories, providing (I₀, I, e) pairs. Since `g` and `q` are missing, an **MLLM-based annotator** is used. This annotator infers `g` from (I₀, I, e) and then generates `q` to simulate the reasoning an MLLM agent would follow to produce `e`. A systematic filtering procedure ensures the quality and consistency of these generated instructions and reasoning processes. The final dataset consists of 190K image–instruction pairs.

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Stage for Policy Model

The policy model, initialized from a pretrained MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), is fine-tuned using the curated instruction–reasoning–edit corpus. Given `I₀` and `g`, the model is trained to autoregressively generate `q` and `e`. The objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the target token sequence. This stage provides a strong initial mapping between linguistic aesthetic intent and executable editing parameters.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Stage for Policy Model

SFT alone cannot guarantee optimal aesthetic alignment across diverse inputs due to the subjective nature of image editing. RL refines the policy by allowing it to explore various reasoning paths and editing strategies.
- **The Problem with Conventional Rewards:** Traditional verifiable rewards (e.g., pixel-wise similarity to a single ground truth) are inadequate for subjective tasks where multiple valid edits can exist.
- **RETOUCHIQ's Solution: Generalist Reward Model (GRM):** The RL stage utilizes a reward model `r_ϕ` to evaluate how well an edited result satisfies the instruction. The policy aims to maximize the expected reward, which includes both the GRM's feedback and a format reward `r_format`. The policy samples candidate edits, receives feedback, and updates parameters via gradient ascent.

#### 3.5. Generalist Reward Model (GRM) Design and Training

The GRM is an MLLM-based model designed to provide flexible, context-aware reward signals.
- **GRM Output:** Given (I₀, Edited\_I) and instruction `g`, the GRM produces two sequential outputs:
 1. A self-generated set of **metrics** describing successful edit characteristics.
 2. A **scalar reward value** reflecting overall quality based on these metrics.
- **GRM Data Preparation:**
 - A paired comparison dataset is constructed with strong and weak edits.
 - For each (I₀, I) pair, `I` is treated as a "strong" edit. A "weak" edit (I_w) is created by intentionally perturbing the editing configuration `e` using an MLLM-based perturber. These perturbations are designed to be plausible but perceptually inferior.
 - Filtering ensures `I_w` is indeed weaker than `I`.
 - Another MLLM annotator then annotates each (I, I_w) pair, generating metrics `m` and scores (`s`, `s_w`). Only annotations where `s > s_w` are kept.
 - The GRM training set comprises 10K perturbed samples.
- **GRM Training Stages:**
 1. **GRM SFT:** The GRM is fine-tuned to produce metrics and scores consistent with these annotations, using an autoregressive loss.
 2. **Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT) in RL:** This is a critical innovation to address the distribution shift between synthetically perturbed weak edits and the more complex edits generated by the policy model. In this stage, the "weak" edits (`I_w`) are generated by the policy model itself, rather than through perturbation. The reward model is trained via RL to assign a higher scalar reward to the user-edited (strong) image (`I_u`) compared to the policy-generated (potentially weak) image (`I_w`). The objective `J(ϕ)` penalizes the model when it incorrectly assigns a higher score to a weaker edit.
- **Alternating Training:** When training RETOUCHIQ, the policy model and the reward model are optimized in an alternating strategy to progressively enhance each other's performance. Initially, the GRM's RL stage uses only perturbed edits, as the policy model is not yet trained.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

- Both policy and reward models are based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- GLM-4.5V is used for the MLLM annotator and perturber.
- Adobe Lightroom is leveraged for its professional-grade editing tools.

This comprehensive approach allows RETOUCHIQ to learn from high-quality data, adapt to subjective aesthetic preferences through a flexible reward mechanism, and continuously improve by aligning the reward model with the policy's evolving output distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate RETOUCHIQ's superior performance in instruction-based image retouching, particularly highlighting the effectiveness of its generalist reward model and policy-guided reward training.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Comparisons (Figure 6)

- **General-purpose MLLMs (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini-2.5):** Tend to over-edit images and struggle to infer coherent, natural parameter configurations in a zero-shot manner, leading to less appealing or unnatural results (e.g., over-processed bride, incorrect temperature).
- **Diffusion-based methods (e.g., Flux Pro):** Fail to preserve original image structure, introducing identity and environmental distortions, which is problematic for precise retouching where content integrity is crucial (e.g., altered background elements, changed facial features).
- **MLLM Agents (e.g., MonetGPT, JarvisArt):** While better than general-purpose MLLMs, they still exhibit deficiencies. MonetGPT struggles with style changing tasks (e.g., not achieving a "dreamy vintage" look). JarvisArt makes incorrect adjustments for customized requests, such as wrong color temperature or failure to perform black-and-white conversions.
- **RETOUCHIQ:** Consistently produces results that are more semantically aligned with user instructions and professionally styled. It demonstrates strong capabilities in challenging scenarios like nighttime balancing, tone harmonization, quality enhancement, style transformation, and local retouching, closely approaching professional-level retouching.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation on RETOUCHEVAL and MIT-Adobe5K (Tables 1 & 2)

- **RETOUCHEVAL Benchmark:** RETOUCHIQ-GRM (the full model with generalist reward and RL) achieves the best performance across all categories (Quality Enhancement, Style Transformation, Local Retouching) and metrics (L1, L2, Semantic Consistency (SC), Perceptual Quality (PQ), Overall Score (O)).
 - It significantly outperforms diffusion-based models (Flux-Pro) and general-purpose MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5) by large margins in both objective (L1, L2) and subjective (SC, PQ, O) metrics.
 - It also consistently surpasses other MLLM agents (MonetGPT, JarvisArt), demonstrating its superior ability to interpret and execute complex instructions while maintaining aesthetic quality.
 - The inclusion of RL fine-tuning with the generalist reward (`RETOUCHIQ-GRM`) consistently improves performance over the SFT-only version (`RETOUCHIQ-SFT`), validating the value of multimodal reward alignment. Even a variant trained with a rule-based reward (`RETOUCHIQ-Rule`) performs worse than `RETOUCHIQ-GRM`, underscoring the limitations of conventional rewards.
- **MIT-Adobe5K Benchmark:** RETOUCHIQ-GRM also shows superior results on this public benchmark for general image enhancement (higher SSIM, lower LPIPS, higher PSNR), indicating that the model generalizes well beyond its curated instruction dataset.

#### 4.3. The Role of Reward Model and PGRT (Figure 5)

This section provides crucial insights into the effectiveness of the proposed reward mechanisms:
- **Reward Model Accuracy:** The accuracy of the reward model (its ability to correctly distinguish between strong and weak edits) is measured under different configurations:
 - **Off-the-shelf MLLM:** Very low accuracy, indicating it cannot reliably act as a reward model.
 - **Trained on Perturbed Data (SFT only):** Shows improved accuracy on perturbed data but struggles with actual policy-generated data due to distribution shift.
 - **Trained with PGRT (Combined Data):** Achieves the highest accuracy on *actual policy-generated data*, confirming that PGRT successfully aligns the reward model's distribution with the policy's output.
- **Policy Model Performance:** The performance of the policy model (on the RETOUCHEVAL benchmark) is directly correlated with the quality of the reward model used for its RL training:
 - Policy trained with an off-the-shelf reward model performs poorly.
 - Policy trained with a reward model that only saw perturbed data performs better but is still limited by the distribution shift.
 - Policy trained with the GRM refined by **PGRT yields the best overall performance**, demonstrating that PGRT effectively produces a reward model that provides superior guidance for the policy model's learning process.

In summary, RETOUCHIQ sets a new state-of-the-art for instruction-based image retouching. Its key strength lies in its ability to handle the subjective nature of creative editing through a dynamically generated, context-aware reward system, further optimized by a novel training strategy that resolves distribution mismatch issues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RETOUCHIQ represents a significant advancement in the field of AI-driven creative applications, with profound implications for both research and industry.

#### 5.1. Addressing Fundamental Challenges in AI for Creative Tasks

The most significant contribution of RETOUCHIQ is its innovative approach to overcoming the inherent subjectivity of creative tasks in the context of reinforcement learning. By proposing a **Generalist Reward Model (GRM)** that dynamically generates evaluation metrics and provides scalar feedback, the paper offers a robust solution for training MLLM agents in domains where traditional, rule-based rewards are inadequate. This "flexible, context-aware supervision" is a paradigm shift, enabling MLLMs to learn and adapt to diverse and often ambiguous aesthetic goals, which is crucial for real-world creative work.

#### 5.2. Advancing MLLMs as Executable Agents

RETOUCHIQ pushes the boundaries of MLLM agents by demonstrating their capability to translate high-level natural language instructions into precise, executable operations within professional software interfaces (like Adobe Lightroom). This signifies a move beyond mere image understanding or generation towards **controllable and transparent manipulation**. Unlike black-box diffusion models, RETOUCHIQ's output is an interpretable reasoning trace and a set of explicit parameter adjustments, which is invaluable for users who need to understand, learn from, or fine-tune the AI's actions.

#### 5.3. Novel Training Paradigm for Reward Models

The introduction of **Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT)** is a critical methodological innovation. By aligning the reward model's training data distribution with the actual outputs of the policy model, PGRT effectively mitigates the common problem of distribution shift. This leads to a more accurate and stable reward signal, which, in turn, significantly enhances the performance and robustness of the policy model. This alternating training strategy between the policy and reward models provides a blueprint for future research in training AI agents for complex, open-ended tasks.

#### 5.4. High-Quality and Explainable Professional Image Editing

The quantitative and qualitative results unequivocally demonstrate RETOUCHIQ's ability to produce aesthetically consistent, instruction-aligned, and professional-grade image edits. Its superior performance over state-of-the-art diffusion-based models and other MLLM agents highlights its practical utility. The explainable reasoning trace provides users with insights into *why* certain edits were made, fostering trust and enabling learning—a crucial aspect for adoption in professional workflows.

#### 5.5. Potential Impact on Industry and Creative Professionals

- **Enhanced Productivity:** Creative professionals, from photographers to graphic designers, can use RETOUCHIQ to automate repetitive tasks or explore diverse aesthetic styles with simple text prompts, freeing up time for more conceptual and creative work.
- **Accessibility:** It lowers the barrier to entry for complex image editing software, allowing casual users or those without extensive technical expertise to achieve high-quality results.
- **New Creative Workflows:** The ability to precisely control edits via natural language could unlock entirely new ways of interacting with creative tools, potentially leading to novel artistic expressions and workflows.
- **Adobe's Ecosystem:** Given its origin at Adobe Research and use of Adobe Lightroom, RETOUCHIQ has a clear path toward integration into Adobe's suite of creative tools, further solidifying the company's leadership in AI-powered creative technology.

In conclusion, RETOUCHIQ is a landmark contribution that moves beyond static image manipulation to dynamic, intelligent, and user-intent-driven editing. By tackling the core challenges of subjectivity and reward alignment, it paves the way for a new generation of flexible, explainable, and executable AI assistants that empower human creativity rather than merely replacing it.
