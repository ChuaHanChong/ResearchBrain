# WM-DAgger: Enabling Efficient Data Aggregation for Imitation Learning with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WM-DAgger introduces a framework that automates data aggregation for imitation learning by leveraging a world model to synthesize physically consistent out-of-distribution recovery trajectories. This approach significantly enhances robotic policy robustness and generalization, achieving up to 96.7% success in soft bag pushing and improving pick-and-place success on unseen objects by over 50 percentage points compared to standard behavioral cloning.

## Problem

- Imitation learning suffers from compounding errors, where small policy inaccuracies lead to out-of-distribution (OOD) states and task failure, limiting reliability.
- The DAgger framework addresses OOD states but requires continuous, resource-intensive human intervention for corrective actions, making it unscalable for complex applications.
- Existing generative methods for OOD data synthesis often produce single-frame or physically inconsistent data, which is inadequate for capturing continuous, dynamic recovery trajectories.

## Method

- Developed an ==Eye-in-Hand Action-Conditioned World Model (EAC-WM)== (EAC-WM) using an Action2Image conditioning module and a specialized training strategy to predict high-fidelity, action-conditioned visual dynamics.
- Implemented a ==Corrective Action Synthesis== Module to generate task-oriented recovery actions by intentionally deviating from expert paths and then synthesizing trajectories back to the expert manifold.
- Integrated a ==Consistency-Guided Filtering== Module that uses DINOv2 embeddings and an adaptive threshold to discard physically implausible or hallucinated synthesized trajectories, ensuring data fidelity.

## Results

- WM-DAgger achieved a **96.7**% success rate in soft bag pushing with **20** demonstrations, outperforming DMD (**56.7**%) and standard BC (**30.0**%), and attained **73.3**% with only 1-shot demonstration.
- On pick-and-place with unseen objects, WM-DAgger maintained **63.3****-76.7**% success rates, demonstrating superior generalization compared to standard BC (**0.0****-10.0**%).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the corrective action synthesis module (**0.0**% success without directional constraint) and the consistency-guided filtering module (degraded to **46.7**% without filtering) for effective policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World models can effectively synthesize physically consistent OOD recovery data, thereby automating the DAgger process and overcoming its human-in-the-loop scalability bottleneck.
> - Generating task-oriented corrective actions with directional constraints and filtering out world model hallucinations are crucial for creating high-quality synthetic supervision that prevents policy divergence.
> - Including goal-agnostic "play data" during world model training significantly improves the model's ability to internalize diverse environmental dynamics, leading to higher quality synthesized recovery data and more robust policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Imitation learning (IL) is a paradigm for training robotic policies by mimicking expert demonstrations. A fundamental challenge in IL is the problem of compounding errors. Even minor inaccuracies in a learned policy can cause a robot to deviate into states not covered by the original expert demonstrations, known as out-of-distribution (OOD) states. In these OOD states, the policy's performance degrades further, leading to task failure.

The Data Aggregation (DAgger) framework was developed to address compounding errors by iteratively collecting additional expert data from OOD states encountered during policy execution. While DAgger can improve policy robustness, its practical scalability is limited by its reliance on continuous human intervention to provide corrective actions and demonstrations in OOD scenarios. This human-in-the-loop requirement becomes a bottleneck for deploying IL in complex or large-scale applications.

Recent advancements have explored generative models, such as diffusion-based approaches, to synthesize OOD recovery data autonomously. However, these methods often focus on single-frame generation, which restricts their ability to model continuous dynamics and complex physical interactions inherent in recovery processes. Without an explicit understanding of environmental dynamics, these models may produce visually compelling but physically inconsistent or implausible samples.

World Models (WMs) represent a class of models capable of learning and simulating environmental dynamics, predicting future states given historical observations and actions. Recent developments in powerful generative WMs, like Cosmos-Predict, trained on large-scale video data, offer potential for synthesizing continuous and physically consistent trajectories. This capability makes WMs a candidate for generating OOD recovery data, thereby reducing the need for human involvement in DAgger-like frameworks.

This work, WM-DAgger, positions itself at the intersection of imitation learning, data aggregation, and world models. It aims to leverage the dynamic simulation capabilities of WMs to address the scalability limitations of DAgger by autonomously generating OOD recovery data. WM-DAgger specifically tackles the challenges associated with using WMs for this purpose, including the absence of expert-provided recovery actions and the inherent hallucination issues of generative models, thus offering a more efficient and scalable approach to robust imitation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient data aggregation framework for imitation learning, termed WM-DAgger, that mitigates the problem of compounding errors without requiring continuous human involvement. The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in current imitation learning paradigms:

1. **Compounding Errors in Imitation Learning:** Standard behavioral cloning (BC) methods, which directly map observations to actions from expert demonstrations, are susceptible to compounding errors. Small deviations from the expert trajectory during execution lead the robot into OOD states. Since the policy has not been trained on these states, it tends to generate larger errors, culminating in task failure. This limits the reliability and real-world applicability of policies learned through imitation.

2. **Scalability Limitations of DAgger:** The DAgger framework addresses compounding errors by iteratively querying an expert for corrective actions in OOD states and adding this data to the training set. While effective, this continuous human supervision is resource-intensive and impractical for widespread adoption, particularly in scenarios requiring vast amounts of OOD recovery data or numerous tasks. The goal is to automate this data aggregation process.

3. **Deficiencies of Existing Generative Data Synthesis:** Prior attempts to synthesize OOD data using generative models, such as diffusion models, typically focus on single-frame generation. This approach is insufficient for capturing the continuous, dynamic nature of recovery trajectories, especially in tasks involving complex physical interactions or deformable objects. A more robust mechanism for generating physically consistent sequences of states and actions is required.

4. **Challenges in Leveraging World Models for Data Aggregation:** World Models offer a promising avenue due to their ability to learn and predict environmental dynamics. However, directly applying WMs to synthesize OOD recovery data presents two significant challenges:
 - **Absence of Expert Supervision for Recovery Actions:** In a human-in-the-loop DAgger, experts provide optimal recovery actions. When synthesizing trajectories autonomously within a WM, this explicit expert supervision is absent. Generating arbitrary recovery actions could lead to suboptimal or even misleading supervision, hindering policy training.
 - **World Model Hallucinations:** Generative WMs, while powerful, are prone to producing hallucinations, such as object morphing or physically implausible state transitions. Training a policy on such erroneous data would lead to learning incorrect physical dynamics, yielding marginal performance improvements or even degrading the policy's effectiveness in the real world.

To address these challenges, WM-DAgger sets the following specific objectives:

- To develop an **Eye-in-Hand Action-Conditioned World Model (EAC-WM)** capable of synthesizing high-fidelity, action-conditioned visual dynamics relevant to robotic manipulation tasks.
- To design a **Corrective Action Synthesis Module** that generates task-oriented recovery actions to guide the robot back to the expert manifold from OOD states, ensuring that synthesized supervision is meaningful and prevents policy divergence.
- To implement a **Consistency-Guided Filtering Module** that identifies and discards physically implausible or hallucinated synthesized trajectories, thereby ensuring the fidelity and utility of the generated recovery data for policy training.
- To integrate these components into a unified framework that can augment few-shot expert demonstrations with large-scale, synthetically generated OOD recovery data to train robust robotic policies.
- To validate the efficacy and generalizability of WM-DAgger across a diverse set of real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in success rates with minimal expert demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WM-DAgger is a world-model-driven framework designed to synthesize OOD states and corresponding recovery trajectories for training robust imitation learning policies. The methodology consists of three main phases: World Model Training, World Model for Data Aggregation, and Policy Training.

#### 3.1. World Model Training

The first phase involves training an Eye-in-Hand Action-Conditioned World Model (EAC-WM) to learn physically consistent environmental dynamics.

- **Overall Architecture:** EAC-WM is built upon the GE-Sim framework, utilizing a Cosmos-Predict2.5 (2B) foundation model as its backbone. It is designed to capture and synthesize eye-in-hand visual dynamics. A key addition is the **Action2Image conditioning module**. Given a context window of `p` historical observations and `q` future actions, EAC-WM predicts `q` subsequent visual states. Robotic control input includes Cartesian translation, quaternion orientation, and gripper state.
- **Action2Image Conditioning:** This module projects sparse action vectors into dense, pixel-aligned geometric conditions. It derives camera rotation and translation from the action vector using a fixed hand-eye calibration matrix. For each pixel, it computes a 3D ray's unit viewing direction. Relative action (translation and directional shift) between frames is transformed into high-dimensional relative ray transformations, forming a dense motion condition (`∆O` and `∆D` tensors). The scalar gripper state is broadcasted to create a gripper condition (`C_grip`). These are concatenated to form `C_geo`, a dense geometric condition. This bridges the dimensional gap between low-dimensional actions and high-dimensional visual conditions.
- **Video-Action Tokenization:** Inputs (historical and target future frames, and `C_geo`) are projected into a compact latent space using a pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to generate visual image tokens and action tokens, respectively. This shared latent space facilitates interaction within the world foundation model.
- **World Model Training and Data Strategy:** EAC-WM is initialized with the Cosmos-Predict2.5 foundation model, which provides general physical priors from internet-scale video-only data (`D_I`). It is then adapted to specific manipulation dynamics through post-training on synchronized visual-action sequences from **Play Data** (`D_P`) and **Task Data** (`D`). Play data involves unscripted human exploratory movements to internalize scene-specific geometry, while Task data comprises expert demonstrations for specific manipulation tasks. The model is trained using the Rectified Flow framework, which learns to map noise to data deterministically. A Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) is trained to predict the velocity field, minimizing the Mean Squared Error of the predicted flow, conditioned on historical context tokens and dense geometric action conditions.

#### 3.2. World Model for Data Aggregation

With the trained EAC-WM, this phase generates OOD recovery data.

- **Corrective Action Synthesis Module:** This module synthesizes recovery data for OOD states by intentionally deviating from expert demonstrations. For an expert trajectory, a pivot timestep `m` is randomly selected. A random unit vector `v_d` is sampled for deviation, ensuring its angle with the subsequent expert action `a_{m+1}` is greater than 120 degrees. This constraint prevents synthesizing recovery actions that contradict the task objective. The deviation displacement for each action in the synthesized trajectory is set to the average action displacement from the Task Data to maintain realistic speed. The synthesis involves two symmetric phases over a horizon `k`:
 1. **Deviation Phase:** The robot moves from the expert pose at `m` to a perturbed OOD state along `v_d`.
 2. **Recovery Phase:** The robot returns from the perturbed state back to the original expert manifold at `m` along `-v_d`.
 The world model predicts visual states for this synthesized action sequence. Only the **Recovery Phase** is retained for policy training, explicitly teaching the policy to recover from OOD states.
- **Consistency-Guided Filtering Module:** To address WM hallucinations, this module filters out physically implausible or inconsistent synthesized trajectories. It leverages the principle of temporal error accumulation, where the terminal frame of a synthesized trajectory (`I_{2k}`) is expected to exhibit the highest level of potential hallucination. If `I_{2k}` remains visually and physically consistent with the corresponding real expert observation (`I_m`) at the same viewing location, the entire synthesized trajectory is considered valid. Visual consistency is quantified by extracting embedding features from both frames using a pretrained DINOv2 encoder and calculating their cosine similarity. An adaptive thresholding strategy is employed, discarding trajectories with below-average similarity scores.

#### 3.3. Policy Training

The final phase involves training the robotic policy.

- The training set (`D_aug`) is constructed by aggregating the original expert demonstrations (`D`) with the validated synthetic recovery trajectories (`D_virtual`).
- The action chunking paradigm is adopted, where the policy predicts a sequence of future actions over a horizon `H` given an observation.
- The policy (Gr00t N1.5 is used as the backbone) is trained to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted action chunk and the ground-truth actions in `D_aug`. This process allows the policy to internalize both expert behaviors and the ability to recover from OOD states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of WM-DAgger was evaluated across four real-world robotic manipulation tasks: soft bag pushing, pick-and-place, ballot insertion, and towel folding.

#### 4.1. Visualization of Generated Data

- **EAC-WM Fidelity:** Visualizations demonstrate that EAC-WM generates observations with high visual fidelity and physical consistency across various tasks (soft bag pushing, pick-and-place, towel folding, ballot insertion). It captures complex physical dynamics such as the deformation of a soft bag and the wrinkling of a towel under different 6-DoF action sequences.
- **Comparison with DMD:** A direct visual comparison with Diffusion Meets DAgger (DMD) for soft bag pushing showed that EAC-WM maintains superior structural and physical integrity over time. DMD exhibited severe visual morphing, whereas EAC-WM realistically simulated dynamics, such as a bag dropping, over multiple timesteps.

#### 4.2. Task 1: Soft Bag Pushing

This task involved pushing a deformable soft bag into a target basket, with randomized initial bag poses. Evaluations were conducted with 30 runs across 10 initial bag locations.

- **Overall Results (Table I):**
 - **Standard Behavioral Cloning (BC)** performed poorly, achieving only a 30.0% success rate even with 20 expert demonstrations, primarily due to compounding errors leading to unrecoverable OOD states.
 - **Diffusion Meets DAgger (DMD)** showed some improvement, reaching 56.7% with 20 demonstrations, but was constrained by its limited physical consistency in synthesized frames.
 - **WM-DAgger** demonstrated significantly higher success rates: 73.3% with only 1-shot demonstration, and reaching 96.7% with 20 demonstrations. This indicates that synthesizing physically consistent, goal-directed recovery trajectories effectively enables the policy to recover from OOD states with minimal human input.
- **Impact of Scaling Generated Data (Table II):** Policy success rate correlated positively with the amount of WM-DAgger generated data. Performance significantly improved from 300 to 1500 synthesized samples (46.7% to 96.7%), with diminishing returns observed beyond 1500 samples, suggesting an optimal quantity for effective OOD-recovery learning.
- **Impact of Play Data (Table III):** Excluding play data during EAC-WM training reduced the success rate from 96.7% to 83.3%. This suggests that goal-agnostic exploratory play data is beneficial for the world model to internalize diverse environmental physical dynamics, leading to higher quality synthesized data.
- **Module Ablation Studies (Table III):**
 - **Without Directional Constraint (Corrective Action Synthesis Module):** Removing the directional constraint resulted in complete task failure (0.0% success rate). The robot exhibited erratic movements, failing to orient towards the soft bag, demonstrating that unconstrained recovery actions provide misleading supervision.
 - **Without Filtering (Consistency-Guided Filtering Module):** Bypassing the filtering mechanism caused the success rate to degrade to 46.7%. This confirms that unfiltered hallucinations introduce biases and limit the effectiveness of data aggregation.

#### 4.3. Task 2: Pick-and-Place

This task required grasping a rigid object and placing it into a target box, evaluated on three seen and two unseen objects (30 trials per object).

- **Online Validation Results (Table IV):**
 - Standard BC frequently failed on seen objects (13.3-6.7%), often due to gripper drift and failure to recover.
 - WM-DAgger achieved substantially higher success rates on seen objects (83.3-90.0%), demonstrating robust recovery capabilities.
- **Generalizability across Unseen Objects (Table IV):**
 - Standard BC showed low success rates on unseen objects (0.0-10.0%).
 - WM-DAgger maintained moderate success rates (63.3-76.7%) on unseen objects, suggesting that synthesizing diverse visual and physical states enhances generalization by preventing the policy from memorizing specific visual textures.

#### 4.4. Task 3: Ballot Insertion

This task involved grasping and inserting a deformable ballot paper into a narrow slot, demanding high precision and contact-rich interaction.

- **Online Validation Results (Table V):**
 - Standard BC struggled (13.3% success) due to the narrow slot and sensitivity to minor execution drifts.
 - WM-DAgger achieved a 73.3% success rate, indicating its ability to equip the policy with closed-loop adjustment capabilities for high-precision tasks through massive recovery action synthesis.

#### 4.5. Task 4: Towel Folding

This task involved grasping and folding a deformable towel, requiring complex 6-DoF spatial trajectories and dealing with severe morphological variations.

- **Online Validation Results (Table VI):**
 - Standard BC failed completely (0.0% success rate) due to the exacerbated compounding errors in a 6-DoF action space.
 - DMD also struggled (10.0%) as generating physically consistent towel dynamics is challenging for non-WM generative models.
 - WM-DAgger achieved a 46.7% success rate. The EAC-WM's ability to simulate deformable objects under 6-DoF manipulation effectively generated physically grounded recovery data, leading to a more robust policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WM-DAgger introduces a significant contribution to the field of robotic imitation learning by addressing the long-standing challenge of compounding errors and the scalability limitations of traditional data aggregation frameworks.

The key significance of this work lies in:

- **Enabling Efficient Data Aggregation without Human Involvement:** By leveraging World Models, WM-DAgger automates the generation of OOD recovery data, circumventing the need for continuous, expensive human expert intervention. This enhancement makes the DAgger paradigm scalable and practical for real-world robotic deployments across a wider range of tasks and environments.
- **Enhancing Policy Robustness and Generalization:** The framework explicitly trains policies to recover from OOD states by providing large-scale, physically consistent recovery trajectories. This leads to policies that are more robust to execution errors and can generalize better to variations in object properties (e.g., unseen objects in pick-and-place) and environmental conditions.
- **Advancing World Model Applications in Robotics:** This research demonstrates a novel and effective application of World Models beyond prediction and planning. By using WMs as high-fidelity supervisors for data synthesis, it highlights their potential for actively improving robot learning pipelines. The introduction of Action2Image conditioning and the specialized training strategy further refines WM capabilities for eye-in-hand robotic contexts.
- **Addressing World Model Limitations:** The proposed Corrective Action Synthesis Module and Consistency-Guided Filtering Module are critical for practical deployment. They systematically address the inherent challenges of WM-generated data, specifically the lack of expert supervision for recovery actions and the prevalence of hallucinations. This makes the synthesized data reliable and prevents the policy from learning from misleading or physically implausible information.
- **Demonstrating Performance with Few-Shot Demonstrations:** The ability of WM-DAgger to achieve high success rates with as few as one or five expert demonstrations is impactful. This significantly reduces the initial data collection burden, making it easier to bootstrap new robotic tasks.
- **Broad Applicability across Manipulation Tasks:** The validation across diverse tasks—including rigid and deformable object manipulation, 6-DoF action spaces, and high-precision contact tasks—suggests the framework's versatility and potential for broad application in areas such as logistics, manufacturing, and service robotics.

The potential impact of WM-DAgger extends to accelerating the development and deployment of reliable embodied intelligent systems. By providing a scalable and robust method for imitation learning, it could facilitate the creation of autonomous robots capable of performing complex tasks in unstructured environments, reducing development costs and increasing system reliability. Future work focusing on extending the framework to dexterous multi-finger hands, by integrating morphological priors and kinematic topologies, indicates a pathway towards even more advanced manipulation capabilities.
