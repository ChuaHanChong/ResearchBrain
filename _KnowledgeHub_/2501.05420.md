# RoboPanoptes: The All-seeing Robot with Whole-body Dexterity

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford and Columbia Universities developed RoboPanoptes, a modular robotic system that integrates 21 distributed cameras with a high-degree-of-freedom body to enable whole-body dexterity. Using a visuomotor diffusion policy trained on human demonstrations, the robot successfully performs complex manipulation tasks like unboxing, sweeping, and stowing by leveraging its entire body for interaction and comprehensive visual feedback.

## Problem

- Conventional robots are limited by single end-effectors and centralized cameras, restricting dexterity and causing perception occlusions in complex environments.
- High-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robots are difficult to control for complex manipulation, often relying on rigid, non-transferable methods rather than adaptable learning.
- Integrating comprehensive, robust whole-body sensing (especially vision) with whole-body manipulation has been challenging due to implementation complexity and vulnerability to sensor failures.

## Method

- Development of **RoboPanoptes**, a modular robot with 9 degrees of freedom and 21 distributed cameras for comprehensive whole-body vision.
- A leader-follower teleoperation system for intuitive data collection, capturing human demonstrations of whole-body actions and multi-camera observations.
- A ==visuomotor diffusion policy== incorporating ==view-dependent positional encoding== and a ==multi-view cross-attention transformer==, trained with =="blink training"== for robustness to sensor failures.

## Results

- RoboPanoptes achieved high success rates (**94.4%** Unboxing, **96.6%** Sweeping, **83.3%** Stowing) on three real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating novel whole-body capabilities.
- The whole-body vision and dexterity approach outperformed single-camera baselines, addressing occlusions and improving efficiency, such as sweeping objects **2.9x** faster than end-effector-only teleoperation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that view-dependent positional encoding enhances spatial awareness and precision, while "blink training" provides robustness against camera failures and latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining high-degree-of-freedom physical dexterity with comprehensive, distributed whole-body vision allows robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that leverage the entire body.
> - Encoding dynamic camera poses within a visuomotor policy (view-dependent positional encoding) is crucial for accurate spatial awareness and precise manipulation in multi-camera systems.
> - Training with simulated sensor failures ("blink training") significantly enhances the robustness of policies to real-world camera dropouts and latency in multi-sensor setups.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Traditional robotic manipulation systems frequently rely on controlling a single end-effector (e.g., a gripper or hand) and employ a centralized camera (e.g., environment- or wrist-mounted) for perception. This approach, while common, inherently limits a robot's interaction capabilities and its perceptual understanding of complex environments, particularly in scenarios involving occlusion or broad spatial awareness.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a system that integrates "whole-body dexterity" with "whole-body vision."
**High Degree-of-Freedom (DoF) Robot Systems:** Prior research has explored high-DoF robotic systems, such as snake robots, for complex configurations and locomotion in confined environments. While these systems demonstrate advanced movement, their application in manipulation has often been limited to simple grasping, hard-coded control strategies, or optimization-based path planning with pre-defined primitives. RoboPanoptes extends the utility of high-DoF systems by combining them with advanced vision and learning.
**Whole-body Manipulation:** Existing definitions of whole-body manipulation emphasize redundancy, a floating base, and multiple contact points, often manifested in systems like quadrupeds with arms or bimanual robots with mobile bases. However, these systems typically optimize body configurations to achieve end-effector trajectories, primarily restricting environmental contact to the end-effector. Furthermore, their sensing capabilities are often limited by single-camera setups, leading to occlusions in cluttered environments. RoboPanoptes aims to actively leverage the entire robot body surface for contact and manipulation, while simultaneously addressing the sensing limitations.
**Whole-body Sensing:** Previous efforts in whole-body sensing have primarily focused on range, tactile, and force sensing to improve collision avoidance, contact detection, and compliant motion. While effective for proximity and contact, these methods provide limited semantic understanding of the environment. Vision-based whole-body sensing, such as optical skin systems, has been proposed but faced implementation challenges related to data transmission and integration complexity. RoboPanoptes advances vision-based whole-body sensing by implementing a functional and robust distributed camera system for dexterous manipulation, demonstrating its sufficiency for imitation learning of complex tasks.

RoboPanoptes distinguishes itself by integrating a high-DoF, modular robot body with a comprehensive, distributed camera system and a learning-based control policy. This combination aims to enable new manipulation capabilities that leverage the entire robot body for both sensing and interaction, addressing the limitations of existing approaches in dexterity, perception, and control complexity.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop RoboPanoptes, a robotic system that achieves whole-body dexterity through whole-body vision, while maintaining practicality in terms of build, extension, and deployment.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in conventional robot manipulation systems:
- **Limited Dexterity:** Most robots rely solely on end-effectors, restricting their ability to handle multiple objects simultaneously, manipulate objects larger than themselves, or navigate constrained spaces using broader body contacts. There is a need for robots that can utilize their entire body surface for manipulation.
- **Limited Perception:** Centralized cameras often suffer from occlusions, making it challenging for robots to fully perceive their own state, the environment, and critical interaction points, especially in dynamic or cluttered scenarios. Comprehensive, multi-perspective visual feedback is necessary for complex whole-body coordination.
- **Control Complexity for High-DoF Systems:** Programming complex behaviors for high-DoF robots is demanding, often requiring manual state representations and motion patterns that are not transferable. There is a need for more intuitive and adaptable control methods, such as learning from human demonstrations.
- **Learning Efficiency with High-Dimensional Sensory Input:** A system with numerous distributed cameras generates a complex, high-dimensional observation space. Policies need to process this information efficiently, understand dynamic spatial relationships between cameras and the environment, and be robust to sensor failures.
- **System Robustness:** Increasing the number of sensors can increase vulnerability to individual sensor failures, leading to system performance degradation due to out-of-distribution observations. A robust policy needs to maintain reliability under partial sensor failure or latency.

Specifically, the design objectives for RoboPanoptes' capabilities include:
- **Motion flexibility:** The robot should be able to leverage all its body parts for object manipulation and collision avoidance.
- **Visual coverage:** The system aims to minimize occlusions from the environment or the robot's own body.
- **Rapid adaptability:** The robot should be able to learn new tasks from human demonstration without extensive reprogramming.

The design objectives for RoboPanoptes' practicality include:
- **Robustness:** The system should be resilient to sporadic sensor delays and failures, a common issue with multi-camera setups.
- **Modular design:** The hardware should allow for easy customization of degrees of freedom and camera configurations.
- **Reproducibility:** The system should be low-cost and constructed from off-the-shelf and 3D-printed components to facilitate replication by other researchers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboPanoptes system integrates modular hardware, an intuitive data collection interface, and a whole-body visuomotor policy designed to address the aforementioned challenges.

#### 3.1. Modular Hardware Design

RoboPanoptes consists of nine modular body units and one head unit, designed for extendability, visual coverage, and reproducibility.
- **Vision-Actuation Body Module:** Each body module contains a DYNAMIXEL XC330-M288-T servo motor (1 DoF, ±90° rotation) and two Adafruit Ultra Tiny USB Cameras (GC0307 image sensor, 640x480 resolution, 50° FOV). The cameras are mounted on the front and back of each link, pointing outwards and perpendicular to the link's axis of rotation, providing omnidirectional vision. Wire fixtures are integrated into the links to manage the numerous USB and power cables, preventing occlusion and maintaining motion flexibility. Eight body modules result in 16 cameras.
- **Head Module:** The head module, acting as an end-effector, is designed as a parallel hook for picking and pulling tasks. It features four downwards-facing cameras and one upwards-facing camera, bringing the total camera count to 21. An LED light is also included to illuminate dark spaces. This modular design allows for flexible end-effector swaps.

#### 3.2. Data Collection Interface

To facilitate intuitive control and rapid skill acquisition for the high-DoF robot, a leader-follower teleoperation system is employed.
- **Leader-Follower System:** The leader and follower robots have identical structures. A human operator directly manipulates the leader robot, whose joint positions are mirrored in real-time by the follower robot using PID position control at 30 Hz.
- **Data Recording:** During demonstrations, images from all 21 cameras and the follower robot's joint angles are recorded at 10 Hz. The leader robot's joint angles are recorded as target actions for policy learning. This method bypasses the challenges of inverse kinematics or manual pattern programming for complex behaviors.

#### 3.3. Whole-body Visuomotor Policy

The policy is trained on collected demonstrations to infer whole-body actions (9 joint angle sequences) from whole-body vision (21 camera images). It is based on Diffusion Policy, incorporating specific designs to handle the complexity of whole-body vision.
- **Observation and Action Space:** At each time step `t`, the policy takes `T_o=2` previous observations (21 RGB images `I_t`, 21 camera poses `P_t`, and 9 joint angles `J_t`) as conditional input. It predicts `T_p=16` actions, executing `T_a=8` of them. Actions are target joint angles.
- **Coordinating Moving Cameras with View-Dependent Positional Encoding:**
 - Camera poses (3D position, 6D orientation via first two columns of rotation matrix) are computed from forward kinematics based on current joint angles.
 - Each position and orientation vector is projected into a 192-dimensional vector using fully-connected linear layers. This positional encoding provides the policy with spatial awareness of the dynamic camera arrangement.
- **Learning Semantic Correspondences using a Multi-view Cross-Attention Transformer:**
 - Images (224x224) are processed by a frozen pre-trained vision encoder (CLIP ViT-B/16) to extract a 768-dimensional class token feature for each image.
 - These features are projected to 384 dimensions and concatenated with the corresponding 384-dimensional camera pose embeddings, forming 768-dimensional "whole-body vision tokens" (21 in total).
 - These vision tokens, concatenated with projected joint angles, form a (21+9)x768-dimensional representation of the robot and environment state.
 - A time-series diffusion transformer architecture is used. Noisy actions are input as tokens to the transformer decoder, while the whole-body observation tokens are passed as conditional input to multi-head cross-attention layers. This mechanism helps learn semantic correspondences between multi-view images, proprioception, and robot actions.
- **Robustness to Unreliable Cameras with Blink Training:**
 - To counter camera dropouts and variable latencies inherent in low-cost cameras, a "blink training" strategy is implemented.
 - During training, camera inputs are randomly masked out (simulating a 5% failure rate per camera at each time step). This strategy aims to enhance the policy's robustness to partial sensor failures during deployment.

#### 3.4. Practical Considerations

Several engineering aspects were critical for the system's robust performance:
- **Camera Selection:** Adafruit Ultra Tiny USB Cameras were chosen for their compact size, sufficient FOV, and UVC standard compatibility, avoiding latency issues of wireless cameras or instability of Raspberry Pi camera ribbon cables.
- **Wire Management:** Custom fixtures on the robot links secure wires, while removing bulky rubber jackets from USB cables increased flexibility without compromising data transmission.
- **Data Communication Bandwidth:** PCIe USB extension boards (20 Gbps each) were used to overcome desktop PC bandwidth limitations, allowing simultaneous streaming from 23 cameras.
- **Multiprocessing:** Each camera's data acquisition runs in its own process at 30 FPS, storing the latest frames in a shared memory space to minimize processing delays and manage large visual data streams concurrently.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates RoboPanoptes' capabilities across three real-world manipulation tasks: Unboxing, Sweeping, and Stowing. Comparisons are made against various baselines, including single-camera setups and ablations of the proposed policy components.

#### 4.1. Unboxing Task

- **Task Description:** The robot must locate a small hole on a box, enter the box, drag it closer if needed, lift the lid, and slide it open.
- **Challenges:** Constrained space, visual occlusions of the hole, dexterity for fine-grained lid manipulation, and multiple body contacts for dragging the box.
- **Test Scenarios:** 18 rollouts covering initial object pose variation (T1), random camera dropout (T2), generalization to different box appearances (T3), random camera latency (T4), and robot appearance changes (T5).
- **Baselines:** Top-down Camera, Head Camera, w/o Camera Pose (no view-dependent positional encoding), w/o Blink Training.
- **Performance:** RoboPanoptes achieved a 94.4% success rate.
 - **Top-down Camera:** Failed due to occlusion of the hole and ambiguity of required reaching height.
 - **Head Camera:** Consistently failed to move towards the box, executing a fixed forward trajectory, suggesting overfitting to proprioception due to box being out of view.
 - **w/o Camera Pose:** Less accurate in locating and entering the hole, and failed to open the lid, indicating that view-dependent positional encoding enhances spatial awareness.
 - **w/o Blink Training:** Performed worse in T2 (camera dropout) and showed slightly reduced robustness in other scenarios, confirming the importance of blink training.

#### 4.2. Sweeping Task

- **Task Description:** The robot sweeps objects (small or large, on table or under shelf) into a target zone around its base.
- **Challenges:** Multiple body contacts for efficient sweeping, conforming to objects, sweeping under narrow spaces, lifting for tall objects, and handling visual occlusions of objects under shelves or scattered around.
- **Test Scenarios:** 18 rollouts including 24 small blocks (T1), unseen small objects (T2), large training object (T3), unseen large object (T4), and mixed objects (T5).
- **Baselines:** Top-down Camera, Neck Cameras (four cameras along the robot's neck), ResNet Encoder (vision encoder trained from scratch), w/o Camera Pose, w/o Blink Training, and Teleop EE (teleoperating only the head link for comparison of efficiency).
- **Performance:** RoboPanoptes achieved a 96.6% success rate.
 - **Top-down Camera:** Missed objects under the shelf due to occlusion and toppled tall objects by sweeping at an incorrect height.
 - **Neck Cameras:** Missed out-of-view objects (e.g., behind the robot) and struggled to recover, possibly due to proprioception overfitting.
 - **ResNet Encoder:** Often swept towards incorrect or empty regions, suggesting issues with semantic understanding without a pre-trained vision encoder.
 - **w/o Camera Pose:** Missed blocks and knocked down tall objects, supporting the benefit of view-dependent positional encoding for spatial reasoning.
 - **w/o Blink Training:** Less robust, failing to retrieve blocks pushed further away.
 - **Efficiency:** RoboPanoptes achieved 3.2 s/block (with whole-body sweeping), compared to 9.3 s/block for end-effector-only teleoperation, demonstrating efficiency gains from whole-body manipulation.

#### 4.3. Stowing Task

- **Task Description:** A multi-step task involving opening a drawer, picking up two pairs of shoes (one by one), placing them inside, and closing the drawer.
- **Challenges:** Long-horizon sequential actions, precision for hooking and placing, navigating cluttered drawer space, and collision avoidance.
- **Test Scenarios:** 18 rollouts including known shoes (T1), unseen shoes (T2), and unseen objects (cups) (T3).
- **Baselines:** Top-down Camera, w/o Camera Pose.
- **Performance:** RoboPanoptes achieved an 83.3% success rate.
 - **Top-down Camera:** Failed at the initial step of opening the drawer, missing the handle due to occlusion and height ambiguity.
 - **w/o Camera Pose:** Actions were less precise, leading to failures like missing shoes or misaligning the drawer, particularly during large whole-body motions. This suggests the importance of view-dependent positional encoding for precise manipulation in cluttered environments.
 - The learned policy demonstrated robust retry-and-recovery behaviors for sub-goal failures, indicating its ability to capture complex sequences from demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboPanoptes system demonstrates a tangible advance in robotic manipulation by integrating whole-body vision and whole-body dexterity into a functional and practical platform. Its modular design, intuitive teleoperation interface, and robust visuomotor policy collectively enable capabilities that extend beyond traditional end-effector-centric manipulation systems.

The key significance of this work lies in:
- **Demonstrating Novel Capabilities:** RoboPanoptes successfully executes complex tasks like unboxing in narrow spaces, sweeping multiple or oversized objects, and multi-step stowing in cluttered environments. These tasks explicitly leverage multiple contact points, extensive body usage, and comprehensive visual feedback, which are challenging for conventional robots.
- **Addressing Perception and Control Challenges:** The whole-body vision system effectively handles occlusions and provides comprehensive environmental understanding. The visuomotor policy, based on diffusion transformers with view-dependent positional encoding and cross-attention, efficiently processes high-dimensional, dynamic visual input.
- **Enhancing Robustness:** The "blink training" strategy demonstrates a practical approach to making multi-sensor systems resilient to individual camera failures and latencies, a crucial aspect for real-world deployment.
- **Improving Efficiency:** For tasks like sweeping, whole-body manipulation showed higher efficiency compared to end-effector-only approaches, indicating the potential for faster task completion.
- **Practicality and Reproducibility:** The system's reliance on low-cost, off-the-shelf, and 3D-printed components, along with the public availability of hardware designs and code, lowers the barrier for other researchers to replicate and build upon this work.

Potential impacts of RoboPanoptes include:
- **Expanding Robot Application Domains:** The capabilities demonstrated by RoboPanoptes could enable robots to perform more complex tasks in unstructured and constrained environments, such as logistics, household chores, or disaster response, where traditional robots struggle due to limited reach, perception, or manipulation options.
- **Driving Future Research in Whole-body Robotics:** The system serves as a proof-of-concept and a benchmark for future research in whole-body dexterity and vision. It highlights the importance of integrated hardware and learning approaches.
- **Advancing Learning-based Control:** The success of the visuomotor policy, particularly with high-DoF and high-dimensional sensory inputs, contributes to the field of imitation learning and diffusion models for robotics.

However, the system also presents limitations. These include motor overheating issues during rapid movements, limitations in reach, and precision challenges with diverse object shapes. Future work could involve utilizing stronger and more precise motors, extending to mobile manipulation for increased operational range, and exploring wireless on-board computation to remove physical tethers. Further research could also investigate automatic optimization of morphology and sensor placement using generative models, building on the foundations established by RoboPanoptes.
