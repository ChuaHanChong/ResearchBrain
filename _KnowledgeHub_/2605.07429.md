# Towards Photorealistic and Efficient Bokeh Rendering via Diffusion Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified diffusion-based framework, MagicBokeh, efficiently renders photorealistic bokeh effects from low-quality, high-zoom mobile images by integrating real-world image super-resolution and bokeh generation. This single-step approach achieves superior visual quality and computational efficiency compared to two-stage methods, with a 71.7% user preference on real-world mobile photos.

## Problem

- Mobile devices struggle to produce natural, optically realistic bokeh due to compact optical designs and small apertures.
- High digital zoom on mobile cameras degrades image quality, which existing bokeh rendering methods designed for high-quality inputs cannot effectively handle.
- Two-stage pipelines, first performing image super-resolution and then bokeh rendering, suffer from error accumulation and reduced computational efficiency.

## Method

- A unified diffusion-based framework, **MagicBokeh**, integrates real-world image ==super-resolution== (Real-ISR) and controllable bokeh rendering in a single step.
- An alternative training strategy cyclically optimizes the model for super-resolution and bokeh generation to mitigate conflicting objectives.
- Focus-aware Mask Attention (FAMA) explicitly decouples focused and defocused regions, guiding attention for accurate reconstruction and blur, and a degradation-aware depth module enhances depth estimation accuracy for low-quality inputs.

## Results

- MagicBokeh achieved superior quantitative performance on a synthetic dataset (e.g., PSNR 24.23, SSIM **0.8623**) and faster inference (**0.1062s** for 512x512) compared to state-of-the-art two-stage methods.
- A user study on real-world high-zoom mobile images indicated a strong preference for MagicBokeh, with **71.7%** of participants selecting its outputs as the best for aesthetic and realistic bokeh.
- Ablation studies validated that components like Focus-aware Mask Attention and the alternative training strategy significantly improved perceptual quality and mitigated inter-task interference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models possess an inherent "bokeh prior" and can effectively learn to generate fine-grained details for both image restoration and artistic effects like bokeh.
> - Jointly optimizing real-world image super-resolution and bokeh rendering in a single-stage diffusion framework can overcome the inefficiencies and error accumulation of sequential pipelines.
> - Careful architectural modifications, training strategies, and attention mechanisms are crucial for managing conflicting objectives and ensuring photorealistic results in multi-task diffusion models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computational photography continually seeks to enhance image quality and artistic effects, particularly on mobile devices with inherent hardware limitations. Bokeh rendering, the simulation of shallow depth-of-field effects, is a prominent area within this field. Mobile devices, constrained by compact optical designs and small apertures, face challenges in producing natural, optically realistic bokeh.

Existing bokeh rendering methods generally fall into two categories: classical rendering methods based on physical optics models (e.g., ray tracing, light scattering simulation) and learning-based methods that leverage large-scale datasets. While these methods have demonstrated the ability to generate visually pleasing bokeh, they primarily assume high-quality (HQ), all-in-focus input images. This assumption creates a significant limitation when applied to images captured under less ideal conditions, such as those obtained through high digital zoom on mobile cameras. High digital zoom often introduces quality degradation, including amplified noise, blurred subject boundaries, and loss of fine details, which can lead to unrealistic texture synthesis and aesthetic issues when processed by conventional bokeh rendering techniques. Furthermore, inaccuracies in disparity maps often result in unnatural partial occlusion artifacts or color bleeding in classical methods, while learning-based methods still encounter difficulties with low-quality (LQ) inputs.

A straightforward, but often inefficient and error-prone, approach to address degraded inputs is a two-stage pipeline: first performing real-world image super-resolution (Real-ISR) to enhance image quality, and then applying a bokeh rendering method. However, this sequential pipeline suffers from error accumulation, where imperfections from the ISR stage are amplified during bokeh rendering, and reduced computational efficiency due to two separate model inferences.

This research integrates into the landscape by proposing a unified, single-stage framework that simultaneously addresses the challenges of image quality degradation (specifically Real-ISR) and photorealistic bokeh rendering for high-zoom mobile photography. The work leverages recent advancements in diffusion models, which have demonstrated robust capabilities in generating fine-grained details and strong generalization performance in tasks like Real-ISR. The observation that generative models often contain inherent bokeh information further motivates the exploration of diffusion-based approaches for this combined task, aiming to overcome the limitations of both single-purpose bokeh renderers and multi-stage pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified and efficient framework capable of generating photorealistic bokeh effects from low-quality (LQ) images, specifically addressing those captured under high digital zoom conditions on mobile devices.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are outlined as follows:

1. **Addressing Hardware Limitations and Digital Zoom Degradation:** Mobile devices are inherently limited by compact optical designs, such as small apertures, which impede the creation of natural, optically realistic bokeh. Furthermore, the use of high digital zoom levels in mobile photography exacerbates image quality issues, leading to reduced resolution, amplified noise, blurred subject boundaries, and loss of fine details. Existing bokeh rendering methods are largely designed for high-quality, all-in-focus inputs and struggle significantly when confronted with these degraded images. The research aims to overcome these inherent limitations.

2. **Overcoming Inefficiencies and Error Accumulation of Two-Stage Pipelines:** A naive solution involves a two-stage process: first enhancing image quality through Real-world Image Super-Resolution (Real-ISR), and then applying bokeh rendering. This approach, however, introduces two main problems:
 - **Error Accumulation:** The output of Real-ISR networks may not be perfect, leading to errors that can be amplified during the subsequent bokeh rendering process, ultimately degrading the overall image quality.
 - **Reduced Efficiency:** Requiring two separate model inferences significantly impacts computational efficiency, making it less suitable for real-time or mobile device applications.
 The work is motivated by the need for a unified approach that avoids these drawbacks.

3. **Leveraging Diffusion Models for Joint Optimization:** The recent success of diffusion models, particularly in generating fine-grained image details and their effectiveness in Real-ISR tasks, provides a basis for this research. The authors observe that images produced by generative models often contain inherent bokeh information, suggesting that these models possess a "bokeh prior." This motivates the investigation into whether a diffusion-based framework can jointly optimize both image quality enhancement (Real-ISR) and bokeh rendering within a single process.

4. **Improving Controllability and Visual Fidelity:** Integrating Real-ISR and bokeh rendering into a single model can introduce conflicting optimization objectives, potentially leading to performance degradation. The research aims to develop specific mechanisms, such as an alternative training strategy and focus-aware masked attention, to manage these conflicts, thereby improving both the controllability of the bokeh effect and the visual fidelity of the generated images, especially in complex natural scenes and around subject edges.

5. **Enhancing Depth Estimation for Low-Quality Inputs:** Accurate depth estimation is crucial for realistic bokeh rendering. However, the performance of standard depth estimation models (e.g., Depth Anything v2) can deteriorate rapidly when applied to low-quality images. A specific objective is to develop a degradation-aware depth module that can provide more robust and accurate depth estimates from degraded inputs, which is essential for guiding the bokeh rendering process effectively.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond the limitations of current hardware and two-stage computational photography pipelines to enable high-quality, efficient, and controllable bokeh rendering on mobile devices, particularly for high-zoom photographs, by integrating advanced generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, MagicBokeh, is a unified diffusion-based framework designed for single-step, photorealistic, and efficient bokeh rendering from low-quality (LQ) high-zoom images. The methodology integrates Real-ISR and bokeh rendering within a single architecture, employing several novel components to address the challenges of conflicting objectives and degraded inputs.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

MagicBokeh comprises two primary components:
1. **HQ Feature Extraction:** Responsible for extracting high-quality features from the input LQ images.
2. **Controllable Bokeh Rendering:** Manages the bokeh effect generation using a dedicated module and focus-aware attention mechanism.

#### 3.2. Single-Step HQ Feature Extraction

The framework directly processes LQ images without explicit noise injection, aiming to maximize semantic content retention while minimizing uncertainty from random noise sampling. Key aspects include:
- **LoRA Integration:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is injected into both the VAE encoder (trained only in the initial ISR training stage) and a modified lightweight U-Net. This allows fine-tuning for HQ feature recovery.
- **Loss Functions:** Supervision is achieved using L2 loss and LPIPS loss.
- **U-Net Architecture Modification:** Based on a Stable Diffusion 2.1 (SD2.1) model, the U-Net is compressed by removing all cross-attention layers and the mid-stage module through block pruning. The text encoder is also removed, eliminating prompt dependency and reducing computational overhead, based on observations that text prompts offered limited practical benefits for HQ feature extraction while incurring significant costs.

#### 3.3. Controllable Bokeh Rendering Module

To enable precise and controllable bokeh effects, ControlNet is incorporated as a conditional control module:
- **Defocus Map Condition:** ControlNet receives a defocus map as the structural condition. This map is calculated using the formula: `r = K |d - df|`, where `d` is the pixel's disparity, `df` is the user-specified focal disparity, and `K` is the blur intensity. `r` represents the blur radius.
- **Effect:** This module allows the model to generate visually plausible bokeh with controllable depth-of-field (DoF) while preserving semantic consistency in the in-focus regions.

#### 3.4. Alternative Training Strategy

To mitigate the conflicting optimization objectives between Real-ISR and bokeh rendering, and to address training sample imbalance, an alternative training strategy is proposed:
- **Pre-training:** The model is initially pre-trained for super-resolution to establish strong Real-ISR capabilities.
- **Cyclical Training:** Training alternates between two phases:
 - **Bokeh Rendering Phase:** Focuses on HQ bokeh rendering. LQ all-in-focus images are used as inputs, conditioned by defocus maps, to generate HQ bokeh outputs. During this phase, the original diffusion model and the pre-trained HQ feature extraction model are frozen, and only ControlNet and the bokeh LoRA layers within the focus-aware mask attention modules are trained.
 - **Real-ISR Phase:** Focuses on Real-ISR. Pairs of LQ and HQ images are used, with an all-zero defocus map representing an all-in-focus condition. Optimization is restricted solely to the SR LoRA layers within the U-Net of the diffusion network.
- **Benefit:** This strategy is designed to reduce inter-task interference and improve overall quality.

#### 3.5. Focus-aware Mask Attention (FAMA)

To explicitly decouple Real-ISR from bokeh rendering and ensure accurate reconstruction of focused regions without being affected by defocused areas, a focus-aware mask attention mechanism is introduced:
- **Mechanism:** FAMA modulates the self-attention layers within the U-Net.
- **Attention Mask:** A binary focus attention mask `M` is derived from the defocus map, partitioning the image into foreground (focused) and background (defocused) regions. The mask is designed such that `M(x,y) = 0` for focused regions (where the binary mask value is 1) and `M(x,y) = -∞` for defocused regions. This mask is resized to match the attention layer's resolution.
- **Attention Calculation:** The attention mechanism is modified as: `Attention = softmax((QK^T + M) / sqrt(d)) V`.
- **ISR Phase:** During the Real-ISR phase, the attention mask `M` is set to 0, ensuring the entire image is considered for restoration.
- **Benefit:** This mechanism guides the ISR component to prioritize the focused subject area and the bokeh rendering component to enhance background blur, improving controllability and image quality.

#### 3.6. Degradation-aware Depth Estimation

To address the degradation of depth estimation accuracy on LQ images, a self-feature distillation framework is proposed:
- **Teacher-Student Model:** A pre-trained Depth Anything v2 model is used as both the teacher and student baseline networks.
- **Training:** HQ images are fed into the teacher network, and simulated degraded images are fed into the student network. Feature distillation ensures consistency between the features extracted by both encoders, while output supervision guides the student model to produce more accurate depth maps.
- **Benefit:** Enhances the robustness and accuracy of depth estimation specifically for LQ inputs.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

- **Training Data:** HQ feature extraction trained on LSDIR and a subset of FFHQ. SR bokeh dataset generated using a custom ray-tracing-based renderer (similar to MPIB and Dr.Bokeh), synthesizing LQ-HQ pairs via Real-ESRGAN degradation pipeline. Images are upscaled to 512x512.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** A combination of full-reference (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS, FID) and no-reference (NIQE, MANIQA, MUSIQ, CLIPIQA) metrics are used.
- **Hardware & Training:** Training was conducted on 4 NVIDIA L40 GPUs for approximately 20 hours. AdamW optimizer with specified learning rates was used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MagicBokeh involved both quantitative analysis on synthetic datasets and qualitative assessment through a user study on real-world images, along with ablation studies to validate individual components.

#### 4.1. Results on Synthetic Degradation Dataset (EBB400-LQ)

- **Quantitative Performance:** MagicBokeh demonstrated superior quantitative performance across most metrics compared to two-stage pipelines combining state-of-the-art diffusion-based Real-ISR methods (OSEDiff, S3Diff) with established bokeh rendering methods (BokehMe, Dr.Bokeh, BokehDiff).
 - It achieved the highest PSNR (24.23), SSIM (0.8623), and the lowest LPIPS (0.2786) and DISTS (0.1600).
 - MagicBokeh also exhibited competitive FID (72.43) and strong perceptual metrics (MUSIQ 58.83, MANIQA 0.4138).
 - The paper noted that while BokehDiff might occasionally yield higher non-parameterized metrics, this was attributed to inaccurate focus distributions, which do not reflect realistic bokeh effects.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A significant finding was the computational efficiency of MagicBokeh. It achieved an inference time of 0.1062 seconds for a 512x512 input image on an L40s GPU, which is notably faster than all compared two-stage methods (ranging from 0.1648s to 2.9883s).
- **Qualitative Performance:** Qualitative comparisons indicated that MagicBokeh produced more visually plausible bokeh effects. It addressed issues prevalent in two-stage methods, such as inefficiency, failure to generate realistic bokeh in complex natural scenes, and unnatural edge transitions caused by Real-ISR artifacts. The unified approach, leveraging diffusion prior and proposed training strategies, delivered superior bokeh quality and image restoration.

#### 4.2. User Study on Real-world Dataset

- **Dataset:** A custom dataset of 50 real-world LQ images captured with high digital zoom (5x–15x) using an iPhone 13 Pro was used.
- **Human Preference:** A user study involving 50 participants showed a strong preference for MagicBokeh. The method was chosen as the best by 71.7% of participants, significantly outperforming all other two-stage approaches, which received preferences ranging from 0.8% to 3.1%. This indicated its effectiveness in producing aesthetically pleasing and realistic bokeh effects on authentic mobile photography.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

A comprehensive ablation study on the EBB400-LQ dataset validated the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Effect of Focus-aware Mask Attention (FAMA):** Removing FAMA (w/o FAMA variant) resulted in minor differences in PSNR but a noticeable degradation in no-reference metrics (e.g., lower CLIP-IQA, higher NIQE, lower MUSIQ, lower MANIQA). This suggested that FAMA successfully decoupled focused subjects from out-of-focus areas, leading to improved perceptual quality without significant changes in fidelity metrics.
- **Effect of Alternative Training Strategy:** The variant trained without the alternative training strategy (w/o Strategy) showed poorer performance across several perceptual metrics (higher LPIPS, lower CLIP-IQA, higher NIQE, lower MUSIQ, lower MANIQA, higher FID) compared to the full model. This confirmed that the alternative training strategy effectively reduced inter-task interference, enhancing both subject Real-ISR quality and background bokeh blur.
- **Effect of Degradation-Aware Depth Module (DA Depth):** Comparing the model with and without the DA depth module showed that while quantitative metrics exhibited minor differences, qualitative comparisons revealed that the DA depth module provided better depth estimation results for LQ images, contributing to overall improved bokeh quality. This module also demonstrated superior depth estimation on degraded NYUv2 and KITTI datasets compared to Depth Anything v2.

#### 4.4. Further Application: Refocusing

MagicBokeh demonstrated generalization to the task of refocusing. When given an LQ input image with a specific focus and a new focal disparity, the method could reconstruct sharp image areas previously blurred by bokeh and produce smooth blur transitions to refocus on new regions of interest (e.g., shifting focus from a coffee cup to background chairs). This showcased its versatility beyond initial bokeh rendering.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Towards Photorealistic and Efficient Bokeh Rendering via Diffusion Framework" holds several significant implications and potential impacts on computational photography, particularly for mobile devices.

1. **Advancement in Mobile Computational Photography:** The most immediate impact is the significant advancement in enabling high-quality bokeh rendering on mobile devices. By addressing the fundamental limitations of small apertures and the quality degradation associated with high digital zoom, MagicBokeh expands the creative possibilities for smartphone photographers. This could lead to more sophisticated and user-friendly built-in bokeh features in future mobile camera systems.

2. **Unified and Efficient Framework:** The development of a single-step, unified diffusion-based framework that integrates Real-world Image Super-Resolution (Real-ISR) with bokeh rendering is a key contribution. This approach overcomes the inefficiencies and error accumulation inherent in traditional two-stage pipelines. The demonstrated computational efficiency (lower inference time) is crucial for real-time applications and integration into resource-constrained mobile hardware.

3. **Enhanced Realism and Fidelity:** MagicBokeh's ability to produce photorealistic bokeh effects, even from degraded inputs, represents a step forward in visual fidelity. The method addresses complex challenges such as maintaining sharp subject boundaries, synthesizing realistic textures, and handling partial occlusions, which have been persistent issues in prior work. This directly contributes to a more natural and aesthetically pleasing user experience.

4. **Improved Controllability:** The introduction of focus-aware mask attention and a controllable bokeh rendering module provides users with greater control over the depth-of-field effect and subject-background separation. This level of granular control can enhance artistic expression and make the bokeh rendering process more adaptable to diverse photographic scenarios and user preferences.

5. **Robustness to Real-world Degradation:** The proposed degradation-aware depth module significantly improves the accuracy of depth estimation from low-quality inputs. This enhances the overall robustness of the bokeh rendering system, making it more reliable and consistent across various real-world shooting conditions where image degradation is common.

6. **Broader Applicability:** The demonstrated capacity for applications beyond simple bokeh rendering, such as dynamic refocusing, suggests a broader utility for the underlying framework. This flexibility could inspire the development of other advanced image manipulation and enhancement tools that leverage similar unified, diffusion-based architectures.

7. **Contribution to Diffusion Model Research:** The specific strategies—alternative training, focus-aware attention, and degradation-aware depth distillation—contribute to the body of knowledge on how to effectively adapt and fine-tune large diffusion models for complex, multi-objective image processing tasks. This research demonstrates a practical application of diffusion models for tasks requiring both image generation and restoration capabilities.

In summary, MagicBokeh offers a robust, efficient, and high-quality solution for an important computational photography challenge, with the potential to significantly enhance mobile photography capabilities and influence future research in unified generative models for image processing.
