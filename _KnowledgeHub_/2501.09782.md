# SMPLest-X: Ultimate Scaling for Expressive Human Pose and Shape Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> SMPLest-X scales expressive human pose and shape estimation instead of adding architecture. It benchmarks 40 datasets under a new Mean Primary Error metric, adds the synthetic SynHand hand benchmark, and trains minimalist ViT models whose task tokens and transformer decoder replace explicit hand and face cropping. Scaling data and model size drops whole-body mean primary error from over 110 mm to 58.9 mm and hand error to 31.6 mm.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art EHPS methods chase architectural novelty while training on a handful of confined datasets, bottlenecking performance across scenarios and blocking generalization to unseen environments.
- Mainstream multi-stage pipelines crop hands and face into separate sub-networks, yielding inconsistent mesh articulation and implausible wrist rotations under occlusion, truncation, and blur.
- Prior large-scale human-centric pretraining covers only 2D tasks, skeletons, or body-only SMPL, and no whole-body benchmark isolates complex, non-relaxed, high-resolution hand poses.

## Method

- A systematic benchmark trains one model per dataset across 40 EHPS datasets and ranks them by the proposed ==Mean Primary Error==, guiding which data to scale with.
- **SynHand**, a synthetic close-up dataset built with SMPL-XL and XRFeitoria, pairs AMASS bodies with InterHand, AGORA, and GRAB hand poses for accurate, far-from-relaxed hand labels.
- SMPLer-X keeps a ==component guiding module== cropping hand and face regions; **SMPLest-X** removes it for a ==six-layer decoder== with ==80 task tokens==, a ==hand-orientation head==, and ==wrist consistency loss==.

## Results

- Scaling from 5 to 40 datasets and ViT-Small to ViT-Huge cuts whole-body mean primary error from over **110 mm** to **58.9 mm** and hand error from **62** to **31.6 mm**.
- SMPLest-X-H40 sets AGORA test records at **96.2 mm** NMVE and **30.8/31.5 mm** hand MVE, and transfers to unseen ARCTIC at **40.3 mm** PVE.
- Ablations: swapping component guiding for the decoder cuts hand-PA-MPE by **15%**; top-10 ranked datasets give **89.2 mm** MPE versus **115.1 mm** for bottom-10.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diversity of data domains, not raw instance count, drives gains: 7.5M to 10M instances inside fixed domains costs 43% more compute for nothing.
> - At a fixed budget, diverse out-of-domain data beats extra in-domain data, and generalist pretraining beats single-dataset training even on that dataset's own test split.
> - Explicit part-cropping modules are dispensable: learnable task tokens attend to hands and face unaided, so simpler one-stage designs improve articulated hand accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Expressive Human Pose and Shape Estimation (EHPS) from monocular images or videos is a critical task in computer vision, unifying body, hand, and face motion capture. It has transformative applications across various industries, including animation, gaming, and fashion. The de facto standard representation for EHPS is the parametric human model, such as SMPL-X, which can represent the human body, face, and hands.

Historically, state-of-the-art methods in EHPS have primarily focused on developing innovative architectural designs and training them on a limited selection of available datasets. While these architectural advancements have shown encouraging progress, they often lead to models that perform well on their specific training distributions but struggle to generalize across the diverse scenarios encountered in real-world "in-the-wild" images. This lack of generalization capability, often attributed to domain gaps between datasets, has become a significant bottleneck for deploying robust EHPS systems.

This paper represents a paradigm shift from purely architectural innovation to an exploration of **data scaling** and **model scaling**—a trend observed in other domains like Natural Language Processing and general computer vision with the rise of foundation models. The authors argue that while architectural designs are important, the potential of scaling up training data and model sizes for EHPS has been underexplored. Previous works in large-scale training for human-centric vision (e.g., HumanBench, UniHCP) have focused on 2D tasks or body-only 3D pose estimation, leaving comprehensive EHPS (body, hands, *and* face) largely untouched by a systematic scaling investigation. HaMeR and NLF touched upon aspects of hand/body reconstruction with mixed data, but not the holistic EHPS scaling.

The authors explicitly address this gap by:
- **Systematically benchmarking existing datasets**: They acknowledge the proliferation of diverse datasets but highlight their underutilization, emphasizing the need for a systematic approach to leverage these resources for universal models.
- **Investigating the impact of data and model scale**: They aim to determine if "scaling up" leads to generalist models with superior performance and transferability, similar to successes seen in other AI fields.
- **Introducing a new dataset and metric**: Recognizing limitations in existing hand pose evaluation, they contribute SynHand and the Mean Primary Error (MPE) metric to provide a more holistic and challenging benchmark for EHPS.

By focusing on scaling, SMPLest-X positions itself at the forefront of foundation model development for human-centric AI, aiming to create highly robust and generalizable models that can serve as strong baselines or pre-trained components for a wide array of downstream EHPS tasks and specialized applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations driving this research are multi-fold:

1. **Investigate the Impact of Data Scaling on EHPS**: The authors critically assess the current practice of training innovative architectural designs on confined datasets. Their first motivation is to systematically explore how scaling up the training data, encompassing a wide range of scenarios, impacts the generalization capabilities and overall performance of EHPS models. They posit that current methods suffer from performance bottlenecks and generalization issues due to limited data exposure.
2. **Establish a Systematic Benchmark for EHPS Datasets**: Recognizing the growing number of diverse EHPS datasets, a key objective is to perform a systematic investigation across 40 datasets. This involves benchmarking their individual efficacy across five major evaluation benchmarks (AGORA, UBody, EgoBody, 3DPW, EHF) to gain insights into data utility and domain gaps, thereby guiding optimal data selection for robust models.
3. **Propose a Holistic Evaluation Metric (MPE)**: To address the inconsistencies and complexity of evaluating EHPS models across varied benchmarks, the authors propose the Mean Primary Error (MPE) for whole-body and hand pose. This new metric aims to provide a unified, holistic measurement of generalization capabilities, simplifying performance comparison.
4. **Introduce a New Dataset for Comprehensive Hand Evaluation (SynHand)**: Motivated by the observation that many whole-body datasets lack diverse and high-resolution hand poses, often featuring relaxed hands or low-resolution details, the authors aim to contribute SynHand. This synthetic dataset is specifically designed to overcome these challenges, featuring diverse hand poses in close-up human shots with accurate SMPL-X annotations, serving as a critical benchmark for hand pose estimation.
5. **Develop Minimalistic, Scalable Foundation Models (SMPLer-X, SMPLest-X)**: To decouple their investigation from algorithmic design complexities and focus purely on scaling, the authors aim to develop simple, efficient, and scalable architectural frameworks. These "bare essentials" designs are intended to facilitate massive data and parameter scaling without the influence of intricate algorithmic components, providing a clean foundation for studying scaling laws.
6. **Explore the Scaling Laws of Model Sizes in EHPS**: Leveraging Vision Transformers (ViT) as backbones (up to ViT-Huge), another objective is to systematically study how increasing model size impacts EHPS performance and generalization, identifying potential diminishing returns.
7. **Demonstrate the Efficacy of Generalist and Specialist Models**: Ultimately, the goal is to build generalist foundation models that exhibit strong and balanced performance across diverse test benchmarks and excellent transferability to unseen environments. Furthermore, they aim to show that these generalist models can be effectively finetuned into domain-specific specialists to achieve further performance boosts, setting new state-of-the-art records.

In essence, the core motivation is to understand and harness the power of scale (both data and model size) to overcome the generalization limitations of current EHPS methods, leading to more robust, transferable, and ultimately more impactful human pose and shape estimation technologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic and comprehensive approach centered on data and model scaling, supported by rigorous benchmarking and the introduction of new evaluation tools.

#### 3.1. Data Collection and Benchmarking

The authors gathered **40 EHPS datasets**, categorized into three types:
1. **Motion Capture Datasets**: Studio-based with optical or vision-based multi-view systems (e.g., ARCTIC, Human3.6M), offering high-quality 3D annotations but often physical constraints.
2. **Pseudo-Annotated Datasets**: Re-annotated existing 2D image datasets with parametric human annotations (e.g., MSCOCO, MPII), leveraging 2D diversity but with potentially lower 3D annotation quality.
3. **Synthetic Datasets**: Produced with rendering engines (e.g., AGORA, BEDLAM), providing accurate 3D annotations and scalability, but with synthetic-real domain gaps.

To guide data selection for scaling, they performed a **systematic benchmark** where a basic model (SMPLer-X with ViT-S) was trained on individual datasets and evaluated on the training sets of five major benchmarks (AGORA, UBody, EgoBody, 3DPW), ranked by MPE. This informed the selection of higher-ranked datasets for subsequent scaling experiments.

#### 3.2. New Evaluation Metrics and Dataset

- **Mean Primary Error (MPE)**: Introduced to provide a holistic measure of generalizability, MPE averages primary metrics (PVE for AGORA, UBody, EgoBody, EHF; MPJPE for 3DPW) across whole-body evaluations. A similar **hand-MPE** is defined for wrist-aligned hand pose.
- **SynHand Dataset**: A novel synthetic dataset specifically designed for comprehensive hand pose evaluation in a whole-body setting. It addresses the scarcity of diverse and high-resolution hand poses in existing datasets by leveraging SMPL-XL and XR-Feitoria, with camera placements optimized for close-up shots and hand poses sampled from InterHand, AGORA, and GRAB.

#### 3.3. Model Architectures: SMPLer-X and SMPLest-X

The authors intentionally designed **minimalistic foundation models** to isolate the impact of scaling from complex algorithmic designs. Both frameworks consist of an encoding stage (Vision Transformer backbone), a decoding stage, and a regression stage.

- **SMPLer-X**: A relatively conventional architecture that includes an intermediate step for component (hands and face) guiding in the decoding stage. It predicts bounding boxes for hands and faces from feature maps, crops regions of interest, and feeds them into respective regression heads along with task-specific tokens.
- **SMPLest-X**: An even simpler version that eliminates the component guiding module entirely. It replaces this module with a standard transformer decoder, effectively integrating localization and parameter estimation into a single, end-to-end process. This design aims to reduce information loss and error accumulation, particularly enhancing hand estimation in a one-stage manner.

Both models utilize **Vision Transformers (ViT)** (Small, Base, Large, Huge variants) as backbones for their scalability. Task tokens (T\_task) are learned representations concatenated with image tokens (T\_img) to guide the decoder towards specific human parameters (body pose, hand pose, facial expressions, orientations, and translations). SMPLest-X extends the task tokens significantly (from 25 to 80, including specific hand pose and orientation tokens).

#### 3.4. Scaling Strategies and Training Protocol

- **Data Scaling**: Experiments were conducted with increasing numbers of datasets and total training instances: 5 datasets (0.75M instances), 10 (1.5M), 20 (3.0M), 32 (4.5M), and 40 (7.5M and 10M instances). A **balanced sampling strategy** was adopted, where all selected datasets were uniformly upsampled or downsampled to the same length to prevent larger datasets from dominating.
- **Model Scaling**: Four ViT backbone sizes were used: ViT-Small (32M parameters), ViT-Base (103M), ViT-Large (327M), and ViT-Huge (662M for SMPLer-X, 687M for SMPLest-X).
- **Training Scheme**: Models were trained for 10 epochs (5 for ViT-Huge with largest datasets) on 16 V100 GPUs with Adam optimizer and cosine annealing.
- **Generalist vs. Specialist Models**: The scaling experiments produced "generalist" foundation models. To create "specialist" models, these generalists were then finetuned on specific target datasets (e.g., AGORA training set) plus a few high-ranking datasets selected from their internal benchmark, using the same balanced sampling strategy.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

The authors conducted several ablation studies to validate their choices:
- **Data Sampling Strategies**: Compared balanced, weighted, and concatenated sampling, finding balanced to be effective and intuitive.
- **Training Schemes**: Compared training with top-ranked vs. bottom-ranked datasets to confirm the efficacy of their dataset selection strategy.
- **Effectiveness of Tokens**: Visualized attention maps of specific task tokens in SMPLest-X to understand their role in localizing relevant image features.
- **CNN Backbone Scaling**: Extended data scaling experiments to a CNN-based architecture (Hand4Whole) to verify that scaling benefits are not exclusive to transformers.

Overall, the methodology is robust, systematically exploring the scaling landscape in EHPS through carefully designed experiments and architectural choices, alongside the development of new evaluation tools.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the profound impact of data and model scaling on EHPS performance and generalization.

#### 4.1. Impact of Data Scaling

- **Consistent Performance Improvement**: As the number of training datasets and instances increased, models consistently showed improved performance in terms of MPE (Mean Primary Error), hand-MPE, and hand-PA-MPE. For example, the whole-body MPE for SMPLer-X-S reduced from 110.8 mm (5 datasets, 0.75M instances) to 77.3 mm (40 datasets, 10M instances). Hand-MPE for SMPLer-X-S decreased from 60.3 mm to 46.0 mm under similar scaling.
- **Diminishing Returns**: While performance generally improved with more data, the study observed diminishing returns at around 10M training instances from 40 diverse data sources. Increasing instances from 7.5M to 10M within the same 40-dataset domain often resulted in only marginal performance gains despite significant computational cost increases. This suggests a saturation point where sheer data volume alone offers less benefit.
- **Importance of Data Diversity**: Training with a diverse set of datasets was crucial for generalizability. Models trained on single datasets performed well in-domain but poorly cross-domain (Table 1), highlighting the need for multi-dataset training to bridge domain gaps.

#### 4.2. Impact of Model Scaling

- **Larger Models Perform Better**: Across all data scales, larger ViT backbones consistently led to better performance. For instance, comparing SMPLer-X with 40+ datasets and 10M instances, the MPE improved from 77.3 mm (ViT-S) to 59.9 mm (ViT-H), and hand-MPE improved from 46.0 mm to 35.6 mm.
- **Marginal Benefits Decrease**: Similar to data scaling, the marginal benefits of increasing model size also decreased beyond a sufficiently large model. While ViT-H significantly outperformed ViT-L, the performance gain was not always proportional to the parameter increase (ViT-H has more than twice the parameters of ViT-L).

#### 4.3. Performance of Generalist Foundation Models

- **Superior Generalization**: The generalist foundation models, particularly the largest ones like SMPLest-X-H40, demonstrated exceptionally strong and balanced performance across all five major whole-body benchmarks (AGORA, UBody, EgoBody, 3DPW, EHF) and six hand benchmarks (SynHand, AGORA, EgoBody, UBody, ARCTIC, EHF).
- **Record-breaking Performance**: The foundation models significantly reduced the whole-body mean primary errors from over 110 mm to below 60 mm, and hand primary errors from over 62 mm to 31 mm (Figure 1). SMPLest-X-H40 achieved the lowest MPE of 58.9 mm among all generalist models.
- **Excellent Transferability**: These generalist models exhibited impressive transferability to *unseen* environments and datasets, such as DNA-Rendering and ARCTIC, outperforming state-of-the-art models trained with conventional, limited data sampling strategies (Tables 8, 11, 12).

#### 4.4. Architectural Efficacy: SMPLest-X vs. SMPLer-X

- **SMPLest-X Excels in Hand Estimation**: By removing the "component guiding module" and replacing it with a simpler transformer decoder (as in SMPLest-X), a significant performance boost in hand pose estimation was observed. SMPLest-X-H40 achieved a hand-PA-MPE of 9.3 mm and hand-MPE of 31.6 mm, which were 15% and 13% lower, respectively, than SMPLer-X-H40+ (Table 5). This highlights the advantages of a pure one-stage approach for articulated hand capture.
- **Effective Task Tokens**: Visualization of attention maps in SMPLest-X (Figure 8) confirmed that the extended task tokens effectively attend to respective information in the image feature space (e.g., body pose token on torso, hand pose tokens on fingers, hand orientation tokens on wrists and upper body) without explicit component guidance.

#### 4.5. Specialist Models

- **Further Performance Boosts with Finetuning**: Finetuning the generalist foundation models on specific target datasets allowed them to evolve into domain-specific specialists, delivering outstanding performance. Notably, SMPLest-X-H40 finetuned on AGORA set a new record on the AGORA leaderboard, being the first model to hit 96.2 mm in NMVE and 31.1 mm in hand MVE, and achieving SOTA performance on EgoBody and UBody benchmarks (Table 6, 7, 9, 10).

#### 4.6. Ablation Study Insights

- **Sampling Strategy Robustness**: The foundation model's performance was not highly sensitive to the data sampling strategy (balanced, weighted, concatenated), with balanced sampling being intuitive and efficient (Table 14).
- **Effective Training Scheme**: Selecting higher-ranked datasets (based on their performance on training sets of key benchmarks) for multi-dataset training led to significantly better performance compared to selecting lower-ranked datasets (Table 15).
- **CNN Backbones also Benefit from Scaling**: Experiments with a CNN-based architecture (Hand4Whole) also showed substantial performance boosts through data scaling, confirming that the benefits of scaling are not exclusive to transformer-based models (Table 16).

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that both data and model scaling are highly effective in reducing errors and enhancing the generalization capabilities of EHPS models. The proposed minimalistic architectures, particularly SMPLest-X, when combined with massive and diverse training data, establish new state-of-the-art results across a multitude of benchmarks and exhibit strong transferability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "SMPLest-X" paper makes profound contributions to the field of expressive human pose and shape estimation, with significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Redefining EHPS Research Paradigm**: This work fundamentally shifts the focus of EHPS research from purely architectural innovation to a systematic exploration of data and model scaling. By demonstrating that scaling alone can lead to state-of-the-art performance and robust generalization, it suggests that prior algorithmic studies might have been premature without fully exploring the impact of massive data. This re-prioritization can guide future research towards more impactful algorithmic advancements built upon strong, scaled foundations.

2. **Creation of Robust Generalist Foundation Models**: The development of SMPLer-X and SMPLest-X as minimalistic, scalable foundation models is a major achievement. These models are trained on an unprecedented scale (up to 40 diverse datasets and 10 million instances) and achieve balanced, strong results across a wide array of benchmarks and even unseen scenarios. This establishes a new class of generalist models for EHPS, analogous to large language models or general vision models in other domains.

3. **Systematic Benchmarking and Data Insights**: The paper's comprehensive study of 40 EHPS datasets and the establishment of a systematic benchmark provide invaluable insights into the landscape of available data. This guide helps researchers understand data utility, identify domain gaps, and make informed choices for building robust and transferable EHPS models. The proposed Mean Primary Error (MPE) metric simplifies performance evaluation, offering a more holistic measure of generalization.

4. **Advancement in Hand Pose Estimation**: The introduction of the SynHand dataset specifically addresses a critical gap in detailed and diverse hand pose data within a whole-body context. Coupled with the superior hand estimation capabilities of SMPLest-X (achieving significant improvements in hand-PA-MPE and hand-MPE), this work significantly pushes the boundaries of accurate and expressive hand motion capture, crucial for applications like VR/AR, human-computer interaction, and robotics.

5. **Practical Utility through Fine-tuning Paradigm**: The demonstration that generalist foundation models can be effectively fine-tuned into domain-specific specialists with minimal cost offers immense practical utility. This "benchmark-finetuning paradigm" empowers users to rapidly adapt a powerful pre-trained model to their specific application or dataset, democratizing access to high-performance EHPS solutions and reducing the immense computational burden of training from scratch. This makes the foundation models a "plug-and-play" component for various systems and downstream tasks.

6. **Insights into Scaling Laws and Limitations**: The discovery of diminishing returns in data scaling (at around 10M instances within the current data domain) and model scaling provides crucial guidance for future resource allocation. It suggests that while scaling is powerful, there are practical limits, implying that the next breakthroughs in EHPS will likely come from genuine algorithmic innovations *after* sufficient pretraining, rather than solely from further increasing data or model size.

In essence, "SMPLest-X" provides not just new state-of-the-art models but a new roadmap for EHPS research, emphasizing the foundational role of scale while pointing towards the continued necessity of algorithmic ingenuity. Its impact will likely be felt across academic research, industrial applications, and the development of next-generation human-centric AI systems.
