# Controllable Logical Hypothesis Generation for Abductive Reasoning in Knowledge Graphs

> [!summary] Summary
> CtrlHGen introduces a framework for controllable abductive reasoning in knowledge graphs, enabling the generation of logical hypotheses that adhere to user-specified semantic content and structural complexity constraints. It achieves high condition adherence and improved semantic similarity compared to prior methods, while also demonstrating the limitations of large language models for precise structured reasoning.

## Problem

- Prior abductive reasoning systems in knowledge graphs lacked controllability, producing an overwhelming number of redundant or irrelevant logical hypotheses.
- Generating long and complex logical hypotheses faced 'hypothesis space collapse,' making it difficult for models to learn correct structures.
- Reinforcement learning approaches suffered from 'hypothesis reward oversensitivity,' where minor errors in generated hypotheses severely destabilized training.

## Method

- **CtrlHGen** employs a two-stage training paradigm, starting with supervised learning and followed by reinforcement learning.
- A sub-logical decomposition strategy augments the dataset, enabling the model to learn complex logical structures from simpler components and address hypothesis space collapse.
- A refined reinforcement learning reward function incorporates multiple similarity metrics (Jaccard, Dice, Overlap) and an explicit condition adherence reward to stabilize training and ensure control compliance.

## Results

- CtrlHGen achieved condition-adherence accuracies exceeding **80%** and improved semantic similarity across various control conditions and benchmark datasets.
- State-of-the-art Large Language Models demonstrated consistently poor performance, achieving single-digit Jaccard scores, for generating structured logical hypotheses.
- The sub-logical decomposition strategy was shown to significantly improve the model's ability to generate complex logical patterns, while the refined reward function stabilized reinforcement learning and enhanced generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Controllability significantly enhances the practical utility of abductive reasoning by allowing users to guide hypothesis generation for specific semantic content or structural complexity.
> - Large Language Models consistently exhibit poor performance in structured abductive reasoning over knowledge graphs, struggling with precise logical inference and structured data comprehension.
> - Dataset augmentation through sub-logical decomposition is effective in mitigating hypothesis space collapse, enabling models to learn and compose complex logical structures.
> - A refined, multi-component reward function is vital for stable reinforcement learning in structured generation tasks, overcoming the oversensitivity of single metrics like Jaccard similarity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Abductive reasoning, identified as a fundamental form of logical inference, involves generating the most plausible explanation for an observed phenomenon. Its applications span various domains, including clinical diagnosis, anomaly detection, and scientific discovery, where it helps in hypothesizing causal links, localizing faults, and deducing unknown entities.

Traditional approaches to abductive reasoning, particularly in the context of large language models (LLMs), often perform well in common-sense settings but encounter difficulties in specialized domains such as healthcare or business due to privacy constraints and the need for high-quality, structured information. Knowledge graphs (KGs) offer a structured foundation that can support more reliable abductive reasoning by representing interconnected facts and domain knowledge.

Prior work, specifically AbductiveKGR (Bai et al., 2024b), introduced the task of abductive reasoning in knowledge graphs by formulating it as the generation of complex logical queries over structural knowledge. This approach typically employed a supervised–reinforcement learning framework. However, a significant limitation identified in existing abductive reasoning methods over KGs is the lack of controllability. In large-scale knowledge graphs containing millions of facts, a single observation can lead to the generation of numerous plausible but often redundant or irrelevant hypotheses. This hinders the practical utility of abductive reasoning, as users require hypotheses tailored to specific interests or contexts.

This paper addresses this gap by introducing the task of *controllable* logical hypothesis generation for abductive reasoning in knowledge graphs. It aims to enhance the practical value of abductive reasoning by enabling users to guide the hypothesis generation process through specified semantic content or structural complexity. The work positions itself as an advancement that refines KG-based abductive reasoning by incorporating user-driven control mechanisms, thereby moving beyond the generation of an undifferentiated set of explanations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and develop a method for *controllable abductive reasoning* in knowledge graphs. This aims to improve the practical utility of abductive reasoning by addressing the limitations of existing methods that generate numerous plausible but potentially redundant or irrelevant hypotheses from a single observation. The motivation is rooted in the need for hypotheses that are precisely aligned with user intent or specific aspects of an inquiry, particularly in large and complex knowledge graphs.

The authors identify two critical aspects of control:
1. **Controlling Semantic Content:** This involves guiding the hypothesis generation to focus on specific semantic domains or aspects. For instance, in clinical diagnosis, a user might require explanations related to pathology, treatment, or susceptible populations, even when multiple plausible explanations exist. This control mechanism is crucial for narrowing down vast hypothesis spaces to relevant insights in specialized fields (e.g., directing attention to "pathology" for a disease observation would yield hypotheses about underlying mechanisms, as illustrated in Fig. 1a).
2. **Controlling Structural Complexity:** This enables users to adjust the level of detail or granularity in the generated hypotheses. Depending on the reasoning scenario, users might prefer simpler, concise explanations or more complex, richer logical structures. This control aligns with varying information needs and cognitive preferences for adjustable information density (e.g., explaining an observation of NBA players with a simple shared attribute versus a more complex combination of attributes including negations, as shown in Fig. 1b).

Implementing these control mechanisms, especially for long and complex logical hypotheses, presents two key challenges that directly motivate the proposed solutions:
1. **Hypothesis Space Collapse:** As the length or structural complexity of logical hypotheses increases, the number of valid or plausible hypotheses decreases significantly. This "collapse" makes it difficult for models to learn and generate diverse, yet correct, complex logical structures under structural complexity control. The model struggles to understand the intricate logic required to produce accurate, lengthy hypotheses (as depicted in Fig. 2a). This motivates the development of a strategy to enable the model to better grasp complex logical structures.
2. **Hypothesis Reward Oversensitivity:** Previous abductive reasoning methods used metrics like the Jaccard score as a reward mechanism in reinforcement learning to evaluate semantic alignment. However, the Jaccard score is highly sensitive; even minor deviations or errors in a generated hypothesis can lead to a drastic drop in its score. This oversensitivity can destabilize the reinforcement learning process, making it difficult for the model to learn effectively and guide it towards incorrect directions during exploration (as illustrated in Fig. 2b). This motivates the refinement of the reward function to provide smoother gradients and better guide the learning process.

To address these challenges, the research aims to propose a framework, CtrlHGen, that integrates novel techniques for dataset augmentation and a refined reward mechanism within a supervised-reinforcement learning paradigm, thereby achieving robust and controllable logical hypothesis generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, CtrlHGen (Controllable Logical Hypothesis Generation), is designed for abductive reasoning in knowledge graphs and operates within a two-stage training paradigm: supervised learning followed by reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

A knowledge graph is defined as $G = (V, R)$, where $V$ are entities and $R$ are binary relations. An observation $O$ is a set of entities. A logical hypothesis $H$ is a first-order logic query (existential quantifiers, AND, OR, NOT) in disjunctive normal form: $H(V_?) = \exists V_1, \ldots, V_k : e_1 \lor \cdots \lor e_n$, where each $e_i$ is a conjunction of relational atoms. The conclusion $[H]_G$ is the set of entities $V_?$ for which $H$ is true on $G$.
Controllable abductive reasoning aims to find a hypothesis $H$ that (1) is the most plausible explanation for observation $O$ (i.e., $[H]_G$ closely matches $O$) and (2) satisfies a given control condition $C$.

#### 3.2. Observation-Hypothesis Pairs Construction

To address the "hypothesis space collapse" challenge, a dataset augmentation strategy based on sub-logical decomposition is introduced:
- **Sampling:** Initial observation-hypothesis pairs are created by sampling logical hypotheses based on 13 predefined patterns from the knowledge graph. The conclusions of these hypotheses on the graph serve as observations.
- **Augmentation by Sub-logic Decomposition:** For a complex hypothesis–observation pair $(H, O)$ derived from a complex logical pattern $P$, the hypothesis $H$ is recursively decomposed into simpler sub-hypotheses $H_{sub}$ according to identifiable sub-logical patterns $P_{sub}$. Corresponding sub-observations $O_{sub}$ are generated by executing these sub-hypotheses on the knowledge graph. This process generates additional training pairs $\{(H_{sub}, O_{sub})\}$ where sub-hypotheses are subsets of the original, maintaining structural and semantic alignment. This strategy enables the model to progressively learn complex logical patterns by assembling simpler components.

#### 3.3. Supervised Training of Controllable Hypothesis Generation

A Transformer-based decoder-only architecture is employed as the conditional generative model.
- **Objective:** The model is optimized to generate target hypothesis sequences $H$ given an observation sequence $O$ and a control condition $C$, using an autoregressive loss $L_{AR} = -\sum \log p_\theta(h_i | h_1, \ldots, h_{i-1}, O, C)$.
- **Two-Stage Training:**
 1. **Unconditional Training:** The model is initially trained to generate hypotheses from observations without control conditions, acquiring a general hypothesis generation capability.
 2. **Conditional Fine-tuning:** The model is then fine-tuned with control conditions $C$ concatenated with observation tokens as input.
- **Control Conditions (C):** Control conditions are designed to guide hypothesis generation from two perspectives:
 - **Semantic Focus:** A specific entity or relation token from the target hypothesis (e.g., $C \in \{T_e\}$ or $C \in \{T_r\}$).
 - **Structural Constraint:**
 - A predefined logical pattern (represented in a Lisp-like language).
 - The exact number of entities in the hypothesis (e.g., $C \in \{[\text{ne}]\}$).
 - The exact number of relations in the hypothesis (e.g., $C \in \{[\text{nr}]\}$).

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning

To enhance generalization and improve adherence to control conditions, the generative model is further fine-tuned using reinforcement learning. The reward function integrates two components:
- **Semantic Alignment Reward ($R_{sem}$):** This reward measures the semantic consistency between the generated hypothesis's conclusion $[H]_G$ and the observation $O$. To mitigate "hypothesis reward oversensitivity" (where Jaccard alone can be too strict), $R_{sem}$ is calculated as a weighted combination of three similarity coefficients:
 $R_{sem}([H]_G, O) = \lambda_1 \cdot \text{Jaccard}([H]_G, O) + \lambda_2 \cdot \text{Dice}([H]_G, O) + \lambda_3 \cdot \text{Overlap}([H]_G, O)$.
 Jaccard provides strict set-level agreement, while Dice and Overlap coefficients offer smoother gradients and greater tolerance to minor discrepancies, stabilizing training.
- **Condition Adherence Reward ($R_{cond}$):** This is a binary reward (1 if hypothesis $H$ satisfies condition $C$, 0 otherwise) designed to explicitly encourage compliance with the user-specified constraints.
- **Overall Reward ($\hat{R}$):** The two rewards are combined using a hyperparameter $\alpha$: $\hat{R}(H, O, C, G) = \alpha \cdot R_{sem}([H]_G, O) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot R_{cond}(H, C)$.
- **Optimization:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used to update the model. GRPO maximizes the expected reward over a *group* of $k$ hypotheses sampled for a given observation and condition, rather than optimizing individual outputs. A KL divergence term is included to prevent the policy from deviating too far from the reference model, further stabilizing training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Experiments were conducted on three knowledge graph datasets: DBpedia50, WN18RR, and FB15k-237. Evaluation metrics included Jaccard, Dice, and Overlap scores for semantic similarity, Accuracy for condition adherence, and Smatch score for structural similarity.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Controllability (Table 1)

- **Improved Performance with Control:** CtrlHGen, under various conditional constraints, demonstrated notable improvements in semantic similarity compared to the unconditional baseline (AbductiveKGR). Most condition-adherence accuracies exceeded 80%, indicating successful integration of control.
- **Structural vs. Semantic Conditions:** Structural conditions (e.g., `pattern`, `relation-number`) generally yielded better semantic similarity scores than semantic-focused ones (e.g., `specific-entity`, `specific-relation`). The fixed-format `pattern` condition achieved the best results across semantic metrics.
- **Adherence Preferences:** The model showed a stronger adherence preference for `specific-relation` conditions compared to `specific-entity`, while both similarly enhanced semantic similarity.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Large Language Models (LLMs) (Table 2)

- **LLMs' Limitations:** Several advanced LLMs (GPT-4o, Kimi K2, Grok-3, DeepSeek-V3, GPT5, DeepSeek-V3 + RAG) performed significantly poorer than CtrlHGen, even when provided with 2-hop subgraphs to compensate for their lack of KG structural knowledge.
- **Reasons for LLM Performance:**
 - **Lack of Structured Data Comprehension:** LLMs struggled to generate correct structured query graphs, focusing more on semantic meaning than precise structural requirements.
 - **Scalability Issues:** Large numbers of observed entities led to lengthy textual representations from 2-hop subgraphs, further challenging LLMs.
 - **Knowledge Conflicts:** Embedded knowledge in LLMs sometimes contradicted the knowledge graph, leading to incorrect inferences for domain-specific data.
- **GPT5 (Thinking mode) and RAG:** While GPT5 (Thinking mode) achieved improved performance over other LLMs, especially in semantic control, it still maintained a considerable gap compared to CtrlHGen. Deepseek-V3 with RAG showed improved semantic control but minimal change under structural control, suggesting RAG's primary benefit lies in semantic retrieval rather than strict structural constraint adherence.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study for Sub-logical Decomposition (Fig. 5)

- **Effectiveness of Augmentation:** The sub-logical decomposition strategy significantly improved the Jaccard Index across logical patterns, particularly for complex structures involving disjunctions and negations. This improvement was attributed to an enhanced understanding of internal logical structure rather than reliance on external prompts, as condition adherence accuracy remained similar.
- **Broad Benefits:** Improvements were also observed for simpler patterns, indicating a general benefit from decomposing logic into sub-components.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study for Reward Function (Table 3)

- **RL Benefits:** Reinforcement learning notably improved generalization and reduced accuracy variance compared to supervised-only training.
- **Smoothed Semantic Rewards:** Removing Dice and Overlap rewards weakened performance, suggesting that the Jaccard score alone was too strict and could hinder convergence. The combined semantic reward provided a smoother learning signal.
- **Condition-Adherence Reward:** Excluding the condition-adherence reward slightly improved semantic similarity but substantially degraded condition adherence. This confirmed that the dual reward mechanism effectively balanced both objectives.

#### 4.5. Visualization of Controllability (Fig. 6)

- A visualization of the distribution of predicate relations in generated hypotheses demonstrated the model's controllability. Without specific constraints, the model tended to generate more complex hypotheses. However, when relation-number constraints were applied, the generated hypotheses consistently aligned with the desired number of predicates, illustrating the model's ability to adhere to structural controls.

#### 4.6. Case Studies (Fig. 8, Fig. 9, Fig. 11 in Appendix)

- **Logic Control:** Case studies confirmed that increasing the complexity of logical pattern conditions led to the generation of more fine-grained and detailed hypotheses for a given observation, while maintaining high semantic quality.
- **Entity Semantic Control:** The model successfully identified connections for strongly related entities and captured hidden associations for seemingly unrelated ones, utilizing logical operators (e.g., OR) to ensure adherence to constraints and high semantic quality.
- **Multi-round Dialogue (Appendix):** A multi-round interaction demonstrated the system's ability to dynamically adjust control conditions based on user intent, gradually refining hypotheses from broad to specific and simple to complex, showcasing its potential in interactive scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel and significant advancement in abductive reasoning within knowledge graphs by addressing the critical limitation of uncontrollability. The proposed CtrlHGen framework offers several contributions with potential impact:

- **Enabling Controllable Abductive Reasoning:** By formally defining and implementing the task of controllable abductive reasoning, the paper moves beyond generating a generic set of explanations to producing hypotheses tailored to specific user needs or analytical contexts. This directly addresses the problem of redundant or irrelevant hypotheses prevalent in large knowledge graphs.
- **Enhanced Practical Utility:** The ability to control both the semantic content and structural complexity of generated hypotheses has substantial practical implications. In domains such as clinical diagnosis, scientific discovery, and anomaly detection, targeted explanations are paramount. For instance, a clinician might request hypotheses focusing solely on treatment options or disease pathologies, while a scientist might seek simpler or more intricate causal chains. This capability can streamline decision-making and accelerate discovery processes.
- **Methodological Innovations for Complex Logical Generation:**
 - The **sub-logical decomposition dataset augmentation strategy** is a significant contribution to mitigating the "hypothesis space collapse" challenge. By enabling models to learn complex logical structures from simpler, semantically aligned components, it facilitates the generation of longer and more intricate hypotheses, which is crucial for comprehensive reasoning.
 - The design of a **smoothed semantic reward function** (incorporating Dice and Overlap coefficients alongside Jaccard) and a dedicated **condition-adherence reward** effectively tackles the "hypothesis reward oversensitivity" problem. This dual reward system ensures more stable and accurate reinforcement learning, balancing semantic fidelity with adherence to user-specified controls.
- **Advancing Neuro-Symbolic AI:** This work contributes to the integration of machine learning and logical reasoning. By training a generative model to produce structured logical queries while adhering to symbolic constraints, CtrlHGen offers a step towards more interpretable and controllable AI systems, especially in scenarios where logical transparency is required.
- **Superior Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated improvements in both semantic similarity and control adherence over existing baselines and large language models indicate the robustness and effectiveness of the CtrlHGen framework. The ability to outperform LLMs in structured logical reasoning tasks highlights the continued importance of specialized, neuro-symbolic approaches for knowledge graph-centric applications.

In conclusion, this paper's introduction of controllable abductive reasoning and its robust methodological solutions position it as a foundational work for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and user-centric reasoning systems in complex knowledge environments.
