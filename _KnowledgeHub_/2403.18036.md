# Move as You Say, Interact as You Can: Language-guided Human Motion Generation with Scene Affordance

> [!summary] Summary
> Afford-Motion generates language-guided human motion inside 3D scenes by inserting a scene affordance map, a joint-to-surface distance field, as an intermediate representation. An Affordance Diffusion Model built on a Perceiver predicts that map from point cloud plus text, then an Affordance-to-Motion Diffusion Model synthesizes motion from it, beating cVAE and one-stage diffusion baselines on HumanML3D and HUMANISE while generalizing to unseen scenes.

## Problem

- No generative model jointly handles language, 3D scenes, and motion; cVAE and diffusion approaches entangle the three modalities and generalize poorly to new scenes.
- Paired language-scene-motion data is scarce; existing human-scene-interaction datasets lack motion quality, scene-layout diversity, and interaction descriptions entirely, starving data-hungry generative models.
- HUMANISE supplies descriptions but covers few action types with fixed-form templated utterances, so models cannot produce diverse interactions from free-form natural language.

## Method

- ==Affordance map==: per-frame Gaussian-normalized L2 distance field between scene points and SMPL-X skeleton joints, temporally max-pooled into one map encoding both grounding region and scene geometry.
- **Affordance Diffusion Model (ADM)**: a ==Perceiver== encode-process-decode backbone denoising the affordance map conditioned on the RGB point cloud and frozen CLIP text features, predicting the clean signal rather than noise.
- **Affordance-to-Motion Diffusion Model (AMDM)**: ==Point Transformer== affordance encoder with U-net decoder feeding a Transformer whose ==cross-attention== layers fuse noisy motion, language, and affordance features under an MSE objective.

## Results

- On HumanML3D, FID improves to **0.352** versus MDM's **0.489**, with Top-3 R-Precision **0.733** against **0.707** and comparable diversity, without auxiliary geometric losses.
- On HUMANISE, goal distance drops to **0.156** from cVAE's **0.422** and one-stage diffusion's **0.185**, contact rises to **96.04** from **84.06**, perceptual quality **3.55** versus **2.25**.
- Perceiver ADM beats MLP on grounding (min distance **0.756** versus **0.904**, goal distance **0.156** versus **0.394**); on unseen scenes contact reaches **88.63** versus **46.64** for one-stage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing a hard three-modality joint distribution through an explicit intermediate representation buys data efficiency: each stage learns an easier conditional from scarce paired data.
> - Distance-field affordances carry geometry, not just target location, so grounding accuracy and physical plausibility improve together and transfer to unseen scene layouts.
> - Falling diversity scores can signal sharper grounding rather than mode collapse; read diversity alongside grounding and contact before judging a generative model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within Artificial Intelligence and Computer Vision, specifically focusing on **language-guided human motion generation within 3D environments**. It builds upon existing successes while aiming to address critical limitations in the current state of the art.

**Background:**
Traditionally, human motion generation has been conditioned on various single modalities:
- **Text-to-motion synthesis:** Generating motion from textual descriptions (e.g., HumanML3D, MDM).
- **Audio-to-motion:** Creating motions from audio signals.
- **Scene-conditioned motion:** Generating motions that are physically plausible within a 3D scene.

More recently, the research landscape has shifted towards **Human-Scene Interaction (HSI)**, particularly when guided by natural language. This complex task requires simultaneously considering:
1. **Semantic consistency:** Motion must align with the language description (e.g., "sit on the chair").
2. **Physical plausibility:** Motion must obey physical laws and avoid collisions with the scene.
3. **Scene grounding:** Motion must accurately interact with specified objects or locations within the 3D environment.

**Challenges in the Existing Landscape:**
The authors identify two primary challenges that hinder significant progress in language-guided HSI:
1. **Lack of Powerful Generative Models for Joint Modeling:** Existing models struggle to jointly and effectively integrate natural language, complex 3D scene geometry, and dynamic human motion. Directly applying conditional generative models (like cVAEs or diffusion models) often leads to suboptimal outcomes due to the intricate entanglement of these distinct modalities. Current direct alignment approaches struggle because 3D scenes provide spatial constraints, while language offers semantic direction – their roles are complementary, not easily merged directly in a single latent space.
2. **Data Scarcity:** Generating high-quality, diverse, and semantically rich HSI requires extensive paired data (language-scene-motion). Existing HSI datasets are either limited in quality/diversity, lack crucial HSI descriptions, or are constrained by fixed-form utterances (e.g., HUMANISE). This data scarcity severely impacts the generalization capabilities of data-hungry generative models.

**This Work's Unique Contribution to the Landscape:**
The paper proposes a **novel two-stage framework that utilizes scene affordance as an intermediate representation**. This approach strategically positions itself to bridge the gap between 3D scene grounding and conditional motion generation, thereby addressing the identified challenges.

- **Decoupling Complexity:** Instead of directly modeling the joint distribution of three complex modalities, the framework decouples the problem: first, grounding the language and scene into an "affordance map," and then, generating motion conditioned on this simplified, geometrically rich affordance map alongside the language. This simplifies the learning problem for each stage.
- **Leveraging Affordance:** The concept of "affordance" (what the environment *offers* for interaction) is traditionally used for scene understanding. This paper redefines it as a *generalized distance field* between human joints and scene surfaces and applies it as a *generative intermediate representation*. This is a significant shift, transforming affordance from an analytical tool to a synthetic one, directly facilitating motion generation and improving grounding.
- **Diffusion Models:** The work leverages the recent success of diffusion models in high-fidelity data generation, applying them to both the affordance map generation (ADM) and the motion generation (AMDM) stages.

By focusing on an intermediate, scene-grounded representation, this research aims to improve semantic faithfulness, physical plausibility, and most importantly, **generalization to unseen scenarios and descriptions**, a persistent bottleneck in multimodal generative AI. It distinguishes itself by providing a robust, data-efficient pathway to complex HSI synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **generate plausible, language-guided human motions that are accurately grounded within 3D environments.** This overarching goal is driven by a clear set of motivations stemming from the limitations of existing methods and the growing demand for more sophisticated interactive virtual agents.

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Joint Multimodal Modeling:** To develop a generative model capable of effectively and jointly modeling natural language descriptions, 3D scene geometry, and dynamic human motion. This aims to overcome the difficulty previous models faced in integrating these diverse and complex modalities.
2. **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** To mitigate the intensive data requirements of powerful generative models, especially diffusion models, in the context of limited comprehensive, high-quality, language-scene-motion datasets. The goal is to enable effective training and robust performance even with relatively scarce data.
3. **Enhanced Scene Grounding and Physical Plausibility:** To ensure that the generated human motions are not only semantically consistent with the input language but also physically plausible within the given 3D scene, accurately interacting with specific objects or regions. This includes avoiding collisions and maintaining realistic contact.
4. **Improved Generalization:** To significantly boost the model's ability to generalize to previously unseen language descriptions and 3D scenes, allowing for versatile and adaptable motion synthesis in novel contexts. This is crucial for real-world applications where training data cannot cover all possible scenarios.

**Motivation:**

The motivations behind these objectives are multifaceted, driven by both fundamental scientific inquiry and practical application demands:

- **Bridging Modality Gaps:** The inherent complexity of combining language, 3D scenes, and human motion has proven a daunting obstacle. Researchers are motivated to find effective intermediate representations that can simplify this intricate multimodal learning problem, enhancing alignment and interpretability.
- **Addressing Real-World Needs:** The ability to generate controllable and scene-aware human motions has immense practical value in various domains:
 - **Animation and Film Production:** Automating the creation of realistic character movements that interact naturally with virtual environments, reducing manual labor and enhancing creative possibilities.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Populating virtual worlds with intelligent, interactive avatars that respond to linguistic commands and environmental cues.
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Creating high-quality, diverse synthetic datasets of human-scene interactions to train other AI models (e.g., for perception, robotics) where real-world data collection is expensive or difficult.
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Informing the development of more intelligent agents that can understand and execute complex instructions within 3D environments, enabling more natural human-robot collaboration.
- **Advancing Generative AI:** Pushing the boundaries of generative models, particularly diffusion models, into more complex, multimodal, and controllable synthesis tasks. The success in this domain demonstrates the versatility and power of these models.
- **Understanding Human-Environment Interaction:** From a cognitive science perspective, modeling human-scene interaction necessitates an understanding of "affordances" – how the environment invites certain actions. This research explores a computational realization of this concept to facilitate human-like behavior generation.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond basic text-to-motion or scene-aware motion generation towards a truly intelligent system that can understand and enact human behavior in rich, interactive 3D contexts based on natural language commands, paving the way for more immersive and adaptive virtual and robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed solution is a **novel two-stage framework** that strategically employs **scene affordance as an intermediate representation**. This design decouples the complex multimodal generation task into more manageable sub-problems, enhancing both grounding and generalization.

**Overall Framework (Illustrated in Fig. 2):**
The framework processes a 3D scene point cloud and a language description to generate a human motion sequence. It comprises two distinct diffusion models:
1. **Affordance Diffusion Model (ADM):** Predicts an explicit scene affordance map conditioned on the 3D scene and language.
2. **Affordance-to-Motion Diffusion Model (AMDM):** Generates plausible human motions conditioned on the predicted affordance map and the language description.

**Detailed Breakdown of Components:**

#### 3.1. Scene Affordance Map (Intermediate Representation)

- **Definition:** The affordance map (C) is designed to abstract essential details of a 3D indoor scene, support generalization, accurately ground interaction regions, and preserve vital geometric information.
- **Calculation:** It's derived from the **distance field** between points in the 3D scene (S) and human skeleton joints (x_i) across a motion sequence.
 - For each scene point and each human skeleton joint (22 joints from SMPL-X model, excluding hands/jaw/eyes, specifically pelvis, hands, feet, neck for calculation), the $L_2$ distance is computed.
 - This distance field is transformed into a **normalized distance map** using an exponential function: $c(n,j) = \exp(-0.5 * d(n,j)^2 / \sigma^2)$, where $\sigma=0.8$ is a normalizing factor. This assigns higher weights to points closer to joints.
 - Finally, a **max-pooling operation over the temporal dimension** across all frames of the motion sequence produces the static affordance map C, capturing potential interaction regions over time.
- **Benefits:**
 - **Precise Grounding:** Delineates interaction regions, enhancing 3D scene grounding.
 - **Geometric Understanding:** Provides a sophisticated understanding of scene-human geometric interplay.
 - **Data Efficiency:** Simplifies the input for the motion generation stage, mitigating data scarcity issues.
 - **Generalization:** Facilitates adaptation to novel scene geometries and scenarios.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Affordance Diffusion Model (ADM)

- **Objective:** To learn the distribution of language-grounded affordance maps ($p_\theta(C_0 | S, L)$).
- **Input:** Noisy affordance map ($C_t$), 3D scene point cloud (S), and language description (L).
- **Output:** Denoised affordance map ($C_0$).
- **Architecture:** Based on the **Perceiver architecture** (Jaegle et al., 2021, 2022).
 - **Encode Block:** Encodes point features (from pre-trained Point Transformer) and the noisy affordance map as "input features." These act as key and value for an attention module. Language features (from frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32) and diffusion step embeddings form "latent features" that act as the query.
 - **Process Block:** Refines the latent features through multiple self-attention layers.
 - **Decode Block:** Uses another attention module, where input features attend to the refined latent features, for per-point feature refinement.
- **Training:** Directly estimates the input signal ($C_0$) using a simple Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss: $L_{MSE} = E[||C_0 - G_\theta(C_t, t, S, L)||^2_2]$.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Affordance-to-Motion Diffusion Model (AMDM)

- **Objective:** To generate plausible human motions conditioned on the predicted affordance map and language descriptions ($p_\phi(X_0 | C, S, L)$).
- **Input:** Noisy motion sequence ($X_t$), predicted affordance map (C), 3D scene (S), and language description (L). (Note: S is implicitly handled via C).
- **Output:** Denoised human motion sequence ($X_0$).
- **Architecture:** Comprises an affordance map encoder and a Transformer backbone.
 - **Affordance Encoder:** Utilizes a **Point Transformer architecture** (Zhao et al., 2021) to extract multi-resolution feature maps from the affordance map. These features are then processed by U-Net decoder layers.
 - **Transformer Backbone:** Stacks self-attention and cross-attention layers. The noisy motion sequence, language features (from frozen CLIP), and diffusion timestep embeddings are concatenated. In each cross-attention layer, this concatenation attends to the affordance features to fuse multimodal information.
- **Training:** Similar to ADM, it directly estimates the input signal ($X_0$) using an MSE loss: $L_{MSE} = E[||X_0 - G_\phi(X_t, t, C, S, L)||^2_2]$.
- **Training Strategy for Two Stages:** During AMDM training, the authors found that randomly replacing a proportion (50% for HumanML3D, 0-50% for novel set) of the ground truth affordances with predicted ones helps mitigate overfitting and improve generalization.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Text Features:** Frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32 is used for extracting text features in both stages.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with a fixed learning rate of $10^{-4}$.
- **GPUs:** ADM trained on 2 NVIDIA A100 GPUs (batch size 64/GPU), AMDM on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs (batch size 32/GPU).
- **Motion Representation:** Per-frame human pose uses SMPL-X body joint positions ($J=22$ joints). For visualization and physical metrics, these are converted to body meshes via a two-stage optimization (neural network prediction + MSE optimization).
- **Data Augmentation:** HumanML3D augmented with a floor and randomly positioned furniture; HUMANISE and PROX scenes cropped into 4x4m chunks. Scene point clouds downsampled to 8192 points.

This two-stage, affordance-centric diffusion framework represents a robust and innovative approach to tackle the inherent complexities of multimodal human-scene interaction generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments on established benchmarks and a specially curated evaluation set to demonstrate the efficacy and generalization capabilities of their proposed two-stage framework.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on HumanML3D

- **Context:** HumanML3D is a text-to-motion dataset augmented by the authors with a floor to introduce a basic 3D scene context.
- **Metrics:** FID (Fréchet Inception Distance), R-Precision, MultiModal Dist., Diversity, MultiModality. Lower FID and higher R-Precision are generally better, while others are compared to "Real" distribution.
- **Results (Table 1):**
 - **FID:** Our model significantly outperforms all baselines (Language2Pose, T2M, MDM), achieving an FID of 0.352 compared to MDM's 0.489 (after bug fixes). This indicates that the generated motions closely match the distribution of real motions.
 - **R-Precision & MultiModal Dist.:** Our model shows enhanced performance compared to MDM, suggesting better relevance and variation relative to text descriptions.
 - **Diversity & MultiModality:** Our model maintains comparable diversity levels to MDM, demonstrating that improved performance doesn't come at the cost of reduced motion variety.
- **Conclusion:** Even with a simple scene augmentation (a floor), integrating scene affordance enriches motion details and leads to superior text-to-motion generation performance, particularly in terms of FID.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on HUMANISE

- **Context:** HUMANISE is a human-scene interaction dataset, focusing on aligning motions with 3D scenes and semantic descriptions.
- **Metrics:** Goal distance (grounding accuracy), Average Pairwise Distance (APD, for diversity), contact and non-collision scores (physics-based), and human perceptual quality/action scores.
- **Results (Table 2):**
 - **Grounding & Physics:** Our model (both encoder and decoder variants) significantly outperforms cVAE and one-stage diffusion baselines across `goal dist.`, `contact`, and `non-collision` scores. This demonstrates superior accuracy in grounding motions to specific scene objects and higher physical plausibility (e.g., more contacts, fewer collisions).
 - **Perceptual Quality:** Our model achieves higher `quality` and `action` scores from human evaluators, indicating that generated motions are perceived as more realistic and semantically consistent with instructions.
 - **Diversity (APD):** The `APD` metric is lower for our model compared to baselines. The authors explain this as a consequence of the model's enhanced precision in motion generation and grounding, leading to less "random" diversity and more focused, plausible interactions.
- **Qualitative Observations (Fig. 3):** Baselines (cVAE, one-stage) frequently fail to ground target objects accurately or suffer from human-scene collisions/non-contacts. Our method generates motions that are well-grounded and physically realistic.
- **Affordance Generation Evaluation (Table 3):** When evaluating the first stage (ADM) using grounding metrics (`min dist.`, `pelvis dist.`, `all dist.`), the Perceiver architecture for ADM consistently yields the most accurate affordance maps, outperforming MLP and Point Transformer variants. This validates the design choice for ADM.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Novel Evaluation Set (Generalization)

- **Context:** A specially curated dataset with unseen scenes (from ScanNet, PROX, Replica, Matterport3D) and novel language descriptions (crafted by Turkers) to rigorously test generalization.
- **Metrics:** Similar to HumanML3D for motion quality (R-Precision, FID, MultiModal Dist., Diversity, MultiModality) and HUMANISE for interaction quality (contact, non-collision, quality/action scores). Ground truth motions are not available for this set, so HumanML3D test set is used as reference for some metrics.
- **Results (Table 4):**
 - **Overall Performance:** Our approach shows considerable enhancements in `FID` (7.887 vs. 11.848 for one-stage @ Enc), while maintaining comparable `R-Precision` and `MultiModal Dist.`. This indicates robust generation of plausible motions aligned with instructions.
 - **Diversity & Physicality:** Our model generates a wider variety of human motions (`MultiModality` is higher), and demonstrates superior `contact` scores (71.98 vs. 46.64 for one-stage @ Enc), confirming improved physical realism and interaction.
 - **Perceptual Quality:** Improved `quality` and `action` scores further affirm the naturalness and semantic accuracy of the generated motions in unseen scenarios.
- **Qualitative Observations (Fig. 4):** Our method is capable of generating natural and accurately grounded human motions for novel language-scene pairs (e.g., "A man dances on the bed happily" in an unseen bedroom). Baselines struggle significantly in such generalization scenarios.
- **Failure Cases (Fig. 5):** The model still struggles with entirely unfamiliar human-scene interactions (e.g., hand washing without facing the tap correctly) or overly complex language descriptions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 5)

- **Purpose:** Examined the impact of different ADM architectures (MLP, Point Transformer, Perceiver) on the subsequent motion generation in AMDM on the HUMANISE dataset.
- **Results:** Perceiver-based ADM (which produces the best affordance maps) also leads to the best motion generation results in terms of `goal dist.` and `contact` scores, confirming the importance of accurate affordance map generation for downstream motion synthesis.

In summary, the experimental results unequivocally demonstrate the superiority of the proposed two-stage affordance-based framework over existing baselines across multiple benchmarks. The most significant finding is its remarkable generalization capability, which is crucial for real-world applicability and sets it apart from prior work.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Move as You Say, Interact as You Can" holds significant implications for the fields of computer vision, generative AI, and embodied intelligence. Its core innovation and demonstrated performance contribute substantially to overcoming long-standing challenges in synthesizing realistic and interactive human motions.

**Key Significance of the Work:**

1. **Novel Framework for Multimodal Integration:** The introduction of a two-stage diffusion model framework that effectively leverages **scene affordance as an intermediate representation** is a significant methodological advancement. This elegantly decouples the complex task of jointly modeling language, 3D scenes, and human motion, making each sub-problem more tractable. It offers a generalizable blueprint for handling intricate multimodal generation tasks where direct end-to-end learning is challenged by data scarcity and representational complexity.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Generalization:** One of the most critical challenges in generative AI, especially for multimodal tasks, is the immense data requirement. By distilling complex scene and language information into a concise affordance map, the method substantially **mitigates the impact of limited paired language-scene-motion datasets**. The strong performance on the novel evaluation set, featuring unseen scenes and language prompts, unequivocally demonstrates its **remarkable generalization capabilities**, a crucial leap towards real-world applicability.
3. **Enhanced Scene Grounding and Physical Plausibility:** The explicit use of affordance maps drastically improves the model's ability to **accurately ground human motions within 3D scenes** and ensures **physical plausibility** (e.g., correct contacts, minimal collisions). This moves beyond merely generating "realistic-looking" motions to generating motions that correctly *interact* with the environment, which is vital for believable simulations and intelligent agents.
4. **Advancement of Diffusion Models:** The work showcases the versatility and power of diffusion models in a complex, multi-modal, and conditional generation setting. By applying them effectively in both stages (for affordance map generation and subsequent motion generation), it pushes the boundaries of what these generative models can achieve.

**Potential Impact:**

The implications of this research are broad and far-reaching, affecting several domains:

- **Animation and Game Development:** The ability to generate complex, scene-aware character animations from natural language prompts will revolutionize content creation workflows. Animators could describe desired actions, and the system would automatically generate physically realistic and contextually appropriate movements, significantly reducing manual effort and accelerating production.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** This technology can populate virtual environments with highly realistic and interactive virtual agents. Users could interact with AI characters that respond intelligently to spoken commands and environment cues, creating more immersive and believable experiences.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** The work provides a computational model for understanding and generating human-environment interaction. This can inform the design of more intelligent robots that can interpret human instructions, perceive affordances in their environment, and execute complex manipulation or navigation tasks more naturally and safely alongside humans. It could also facilitate synthetic data generation for training robotic policies.
- **Synthetic Data Generation for Machine Learning:** Creating diverse and high-fidelity datasets of human-scene interactions is crucial for training other perception, planning, and control systems. This framework offers a powerful tool for generating such synthetic data at scale, overcoming limitations of real-world data collection.
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** Enables more intuitive interfaces where users can guide virtual avatars or robotic systems through natural language, leading to more accessible and effective human-AI collaboration.
- **Digital Twins and Simulation:** The technology could be used to simulate human behavior in various built environments, aiding in architectural design, urban planning, or safety assessments by visualizing human interactions within proposed spaces.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the **slow inference times inherent to diffusion models** and the **persistent challenge of data scarcity**. Future work will likely focus on faster sampling methods for diffusion models (e.g., distillation, consistency models) and developing more data-efficient learning paradigms or leveraging foundation models for better zero-shot generalization. Addressing these will further solidify the practical utility of this promising framework.

In conclusion, this research marks a significant step forward in generating intelligent and interactive human behaviors in 3D digital environments. By innovatively leveraging scene affordance, it offers a robust solution to core challenges in multimodal AI, promising substantial impact across various scientific and industrial applications.
