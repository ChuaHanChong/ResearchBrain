# Reasoning Palette: Modulating Reasoning via Latent Contextualization for Controllable Exploration for (V)LMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba Group, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Zhejiang University introduce Reasoning Palette, a framework that uses latent contextualization to strategically modulate reasoning in large language and vision-language models. This approach consistently improves reinforcement learning performance, achieving an average gain of +3.09 points on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, and boosts multimodal inference by enhancing grounding capabilities to 87.53% Pass@32 on RefCOCO.

## Problem

- Large language and vision-language models (LLMs/VLMs) often exhibit limited diversity in their reasoning paths during inference and reinforcement learning (RL) training, hindering effective exploration.
- Existing stochastic sampling methods primarily provide low-level token variations but fail to modulate the model's high-level internal planning or strategic approach.
- This lack of strategic control impedes the discovery of robust reasoning strategies and limits the efficiency of RL training for complex problem-solving.

## Method

- A ==Variational Autoencoder== (VAE) is trained on diverse question-answer pairs to learn a compact, continuous latent space representing distinct reasoning strategies.
- Latent vectors sampled from this space are decoded into continuous prefix embeddings, which are then prepended to the input prompt of a (V)LM, guiding its reasoning trajectory.
- The framework is integrated into reinforcement learning (RL) training, using scheduled latent-conditioned prefixes to enable multi-granular, strategic exploration of diverse reasoning paths.

## Results

- Latent-guided inference, particularly with domain-specific latents, improved LLM performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with math-biased latents achieving 72.4 Pass@8 on MATH500.
- For vision-language models, the method boosted referring expression comprehension, reaching **87.53%** Pass@32 on RefCOCO when combining latent guidance with stochastic sampling.
- Integrating Reasoning Palette into reinforcement learning consistently enhanced performance, yielding an average gain of +3.09 points over strong baselines across multiple mathematical reasoning tasks for Qwen3-**8B**-Base models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modulating an LLM's internal state with latent contextualization prior to generation can unlock significant performance gains by encouraging diverse "internal planning."
> - A VAE can learn an interpretable and disentangled latent space that effectively encodes high-level reasoning strategies like mathematical, code-based, or multi-hop QA patterns.
> - Strategic exploration through high-level latent contextualization in RL training leads to "late-stage overtaking" and superior asymptotic performance by facilitating the discovery of better reasoning policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"Reasoning Palette" addresses several critical and evolving areas within the broader research landscape of large language and vision-language models (LLMs/VLMs).

Firstly, it squarely tackles the **challenge of effective exploration in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs**. RL, particularly with verifiable rewards (RLVR), has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing LLM reasoning by eliciting step-by-step thought processes. However, a well-recognized bottleneck in RL training for LLMs is the limited diversity of reasoning paths explored through standard stochastic sampling techniques (e.g., temperature sampling). These methods often generate "closely related trajectories" rather than fundamentally different strategies. Reasoning Palette directly confronts this by introducing a mechanism for **structured, strategic exploration** at a higher level than token-by-token randomness. This aligns with a growing body of work that seeks to improve RL efficiency for LLMs by designing more sophisticated exploration strategies, moving beyond local diversity to encourage global, strategic variation.

Secondly, the paper contributes to the field of **controllable text and multimodal generation**. The ability to steer LLMs towards specific styles, tones, or reasoning patterns is a major goal. Traditional approaches include discrete prompting (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, few-shot prompting) and continuous "soft" prompting methods like soft prompt tuning and prefix-tuning. While these methods learn continuous representations to guide model behavior, "Reasoning Palette" introduces a novel **stochastic latent variable** for this control. This allows for *sampling* over different reasoning strategies and offers an "on-demand injection" of diverse reasoning modes, making the control explicit, interpretable, and variable. The use of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to learn this latent space also connects to the long-standing theme of **latent representation learning** in generative modeling, particularly VAEs for learning continuous, sampleable, and disentangled representations. This work extends such latent variable models to modulate the *internal planning* and *reasoning trajectory* of LLMs/VLMs, rather than just generating diverse outputs.

Thirdly, the work extends to **multimodal reasoning with Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**. As VLMs become increasingly capable, enabling them to ground language in visual contexts and perform complex tasks like referring expression comprehension, the need for controllable and robust reasoning pathways becomes even more critical. "Reasoning Palette" demonstrates its applicability to VLMs, enhancing their grounding capabilities by injecting domain-agnostic yet semantically rich latent contexts. This showcases its generalizability beyond pure language tasks, positioning it as a tool for improving diverse forms of AI reasoning.

Finally, the paper builds upon and integrates with existing advanced RL algorithms for LLMs, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and Reward-Label Optimized Off-policy (RLOO). By showing consistent performance gains when integrated with these state-of-the-art methods, "Reasoning Palette" demonstrates its compatibility and additive value within the current ecosystem of LLM post-training paradigms. The "exploration-exploitation" trade-off, a fundamental concept in RL, is also explicitly discussed and managed through the proposed scheduling strategies, further cementing its relevance in the RL community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "Reasoning Palette" is to **enhance the exploration capacity and sustained learning capability of large (vision-) language models during both inference and reinforcement learning (RL) training by endowing them with a mechanism for strategic, high-level reasoning modulation.**

The authors identify a critical motivation and problem statement:
- **The Problem of Limited Strategic Diversity:** Standard stochastic sampling schemes used in LLM/VLM inference and RL training often lead to "redundant reasoning paths with little high-level diversity." While token-level stochasticity encourages local variations, it rarely provides mechanisms to shape the model's *internal planning* or *overall strategic approach*. This mismatch between low-level token variability and the high-level variability needed to discover effective reasoning strategies limits the efficiency and robustness of RL training. Models tend to explore similar paths, rather than probing distinct strategic modes.

- **Empirical Motivation from Noise Injection (Figure 1):** A key insight that motivated this research was the observation that simply prepending a single randomly sampled Gaussian noise embedding to the input of a base LLM (Qwen-4B-Base) could dramatically boost its Pass@k performance, even under greedy decoding. This striking result suggested that modulating the model's internal state *prior* to generation, even with simple noise, could unlock substantial performance gains by implicitly encouraging diverse "internal planning" or "reasoning contexts." However, naive noise injection often leads to misalignment.

Against this backdrop, the authors aim to:
1. **Introduce a structured latent space:** Create a compact, continuous latent space that explicitly encodes diverse high-level reasoning strategies (e.g., mathematical, code-based, QA logic) rather than relying on unstructured noise.
2. **Enable controllable pre-generative modulation:** Develop a framework where a sampled latent variable acts as a "latent context" that can be decoded into learnable token prefixes. These prefixes are prepended to the input prompt, thereby guiding the model's internal planning and steering its entire reasoning trajectory *before* any output token is generated. This allows sampling over distinct reasoning strategies.
3. **Facilitate efficient and sustained RL exploration:** Integrate this latent modulation into RL optimization. By sampling different latent contexts per episode, the policy can explore diverse "strategic families" rather than revisiting near-duplicate chains of thought. This is hypothesized to improve the chance of discovering high-reward behaviors and accelerate learning.
4. **Provide interpretability and targeted control:** Design the system such that the learned latent space is interpretable, allowing for post-hoc analysis of reasoning patterns and enabling deliberate, targeted intervention to modulate model behavior by sampling from specific regions of the latent space.
5. **Ensure practical adaptability:** Develop a lightweight framework that can be easily absorbed by pre-trained models with minimal retraining (e.g., via a brief supervised fine-tuning warm-up), without sacrificing general capabilities.
6. **Generalize to multimodal settings:** Demonstrate that this approach is effective for both text-only LLMs and multimodal VLMs.

In essence, "Reasoning Palette" seeks to provide a "palette" of reasoning modes that models can strategically explore, moving beyond superficial token-level diversity to profound, high-level strategic variability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"Reasoning Palette" proposes a novel latent-modulation framework built upon a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) that allows for strategic contextualization of large (vision-)language models (V)LMs. The methodology consists of three main stages: latent space learning, SFT adaptation for latent-guided inference, and controllable exploration in RL.

#### 3.1. Latent Space Learning for Reasoning Patterns

The core of the framework is a **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)** designed to learn a compact, continuous latent space `Z` encoding diverse reasoning strategies.
- **Input Representation:** Instead of raw text, the VAE operates on a contextual summary of question-answer pairs `(q, o)`. The sequence `[q; o]` is first passed through the model's frozen token embedding layer `E(·)`, and the resulting embeddings are then **mean-pooled** to produce a fixed-dimensional aggregated embedding `h ∈ R^d`. This mean-pooling provides an order-insensitive summary, suitable for capturing high-level reasoning style rather than surface-level details. `h` resides in the same space as individual token embeddings, simplifying subsequent integration.
- **VAE Architecture:**
 - **Encoder (E_ϕ):** An MLP maps the aggregated embedding `h` to the parameters (`μ`, `σ`) of a diagonal Gaussian distribution, `z ∼ N(μ, diag(σ^2))`. This defines the posterior distribution `q_ϕ(z | h)`.
 - **Decoder (D_ψ):** Another MLP reconstructs the original embedding `h` from a sampled latent code `z`, producing `ˆh = D_ψ(z)`.
- **Training Objective:** The VAE is trained to minimize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), balancing reconstruction accuracy (`||h - ˆh||^2`) with a KL divergence term that regularizes the latent space (`KL(q_ϕ(z | h) || p(z))`, where `p(z)` is a standard isotropic Gaussian prior `N(0, I)`). A hyperparameter `β` controls this trade-off, aiming for a smooth, disentangled, and semantically meaningful latent manifold.
- **Data Curation:** The VAE is trained on a curated dataset of high-quality `(q, o)` pairs spanning diverse reasoning domains like mathematics, code generation, and multi-hop question answering (e.g., MetaMathQA, CodeParrot, ShareGPT Vicuna). This diverse training data ensures that the learned latent space captures a rich repertoire of distinct reasoning patterns.

#### 3.2. Latent-Guided Inference and SFT Adaptation

Once the VAE is trained, its latent space is used to modulate the base (V)LM's reasoning behavior.
- **Latent-Guided Inference:** During inference, a latent vector `z` is sampled from the standard Gaussian prior `N(0, I)`. This `z` is then decoded by `D_ψ` into a sequence of `L` prefix embeddings, `p_z = (D_ψ(z^(1))..., D_ψ(z^(L)))`. Each `z^(ℓ)` can be an independent sample or the same `z` tiled. These continuous prefix embeddings `p_z` are then prepended to the input token embeddings of the prompt `q`, forming `[p_z; E(q)]`, which is fed into the (V)LM for autoregressive generation. The prefix length `L` is a tunable hyperparameter, allowing control over the strength of guidance.
- **SFT Adaptation:** To ensure the base (V)LM can effectively respond to these novel, latent-conditioned prefixes without losing its general instruction-following abilities, a **lightweight supervised fine-tuning (SFT) warm-up phase** is performed.
 - **SFT Dataset:** Crucially, the SFT dataset `D_SFT` is constructed by sampling latents directly from the *prior* `z ∼ N(0, I)`, decoding them into prefixes `p = D_ψ(z)`, and pairing these synthetic prefixes with original prompt-response pairs `(q, o)` from the VAE training corpus. This helps the model generalize to the isotropic Gaussian prior used during inference and RL.
 - **Minimal SFT:** The SFT duration is strictly limited (typically 10 iterations) to prevent the model from overfitting to specific response patterns or downweighting the prefix's impact. This balance ensures the model learns to condition on arbitrary prefix embeddings while retaining sufficient stochasticity.
 - **Prefix Length for SFT:** During SFT, the prefix length `L` is fixed to 1, providing minimal intervention during adaptation. This allows for subsequent increases in `L` (e.g., `L=4` or `8`) during inference and RL for richer guidance.

#### 3.3. Controllable Exploration in RL

The latent variable `z` is leveraged as an auxiliary control signal to induce structured exploration during reinforcement learning.
- **Latent-Conditioned Policy:** For each training episode, a latent code `z ∼ N(0, I)` is sampled, decoded into `L` prefix embeddings `p`, and the policy `π_θ` is conditioned on this prefix: `π_θ(o | q, z)`. The RL objective is augmented to maximize the expected reward over `z` sampled from its prior.
- **Multi-Granular Exploration Control:** The framework enables control along two dimensions:
 - **Temporal Scheduling:** The strength of latent guidance is modulated over training. Two strategies are proposed:
 - **Two-Phase:** For the first 50% of training, all rollouts use latent prefixes (`L=8`) for maximum diversity; for the remaining 50%, latent guidance is completely disabled (`L=0`), allowing exploitation.
 - **Linear Decay:** Over the full training duration, the *fraction* of latent-guided rollouts per group linearly decreases from 100% to 0%, while `L=8` is maintained for those guided rollouts. This provides a smoother transition.
 - **Intra-Group Diversity Control:** Within each training batch (or GRPO group), the proportion of rollouts receiving latent-conditioned prefixes can be regulated, balancing exploration with stable policy updates.
- **Integration with RL Algorithms:** Reasoning Palette is integrated with state-of-the-art RL algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and Reward-Label Optimized Off-policy (RLOO). The objective function is modified to include the scheduled latent-guided and unguided rollouts.

#### 3.4. Extension to Vision-Language Models (VLMs)

For VLMs, the approach is similar. Visual inputs are encoded into image tokens and integrated into the text prompt. The sampled noise vector `z` is reshaped and passed through a lightweight GPT-style decoder to produce prefix embeddings, which are then prepended to the combined (image + text) prompt embeddings. This demonstrates the modularity and generalizability of the latent modulation concept to multimodal inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of Reasoning Palette across various reasoning benchmarks for both inference and reinforcement learning settings.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Latent-Guided Inference

- **Motivation Confirmation (Fig. 1):** The initial observation that injecting a single Gaussian noise token embedding into Qwen-4B-Base significantly boosts Pass@k accuracy (e.g., from 52.9% to 85.3% on GSM8K for Pass@32) under greedy decoding strongly motivates the approach. This highlights that strategic variation, not just token-level stochasticity, drives performance gains.
- **LLMs: Targeted Intervention (Table 1):** On a supervised fine-tuned Qwen3-4B-Base model, using latents specifically biased towards the "math" domain (by encoding math problem-solution pairs) yielded the best Pass@8 performance across mathematical benchmarks (MATH500, Olympic, GSM8K). For instance, math-domain latents achieved 72.4 on MATH500, outperforming code- or QA-biased latents, demonstrating interpretable and controllable reasoning.
- **VLMs: Enhanced Grounding (Table 2, Fig. 4 & 5):** When applied to a Qwen2.5VL-3B backbone for referring expression comprehension, latent-guided inference significantly improved Pass@32 accuracy on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg datasets.
 - "Latent-guided (greedy)" decoding outperformed "Baseline (greedy)" by substantial margins (e.g., 72.07% vs. 2.0% on RefCOCO), indicating the power of structured pre-generative exploration.
 - Combining latent guidance with stochastic decoding ("Latent-guided + sampling") achieved the best results across all datasets (e.g., 87.53% on RefCOCO), suggesting complementarity between strategic diversity and token-level stochasticity.
 - Crucially, the improvement from latent guidance alone surpassed that from sampling alone, validating the unique value of the proposed method for multimodal reasoning. Qualitative results (Fig. 5) visually confirmed improved grounding for challenging cases.
- **Latent Space Analysis (Fig. 3):** Dimensionality reduction (PCA and t-SNE) of the learned latents `z` and their decoded prefixes `p = D_ψ(z)` revealed clear clustering aligned with reasoning domains (math, code, QA).
 - Advanced math datasets (competition_math, PRM800K) showed overlapping distributions, reflecting shared reasoning styles.
 - MetaMathQA, while still math-focused, formed a distinct sub-region, suggesting sensitivity to fine-grained reasoning styles (e.g., pedagogical vs. competitive).
 - Code generation and general QA formed separate clusters. The QA cluster displayed the largest spread, indicating its broad spectrum of reasoning strategies.
 - This structural coherence in both latent vectors and decoded prefixes confirmed that the VAE successfully learned a disentangled and functional mapping, translating high-level semantics into effective behavioral guidance.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning with Latent Guidance

- **Consistent Performance Gains (Table 3):** Reasoning Palette consistently outperformed state-of-the-art RL baselines (GRPO and RLOO) across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AMC23, GSM8K, MinervaMath, MATH500, OlympiadBench) and across different Qwen3 model sizes (1.7B, 4B, 8B).
 - For Qwen3-8B-Base + RLOO, Reasoning Palette with Linear Decay achieved an average performance gain of +3.09 points, with notable increases on complex domains like AMC23 (+4.38) and MinervaMath (+4.29).
 - The "Linear Decay" schedule generally yielded slightly better final performance than "Two-Phase," suggesting that a smoother transition from exploration to exploitation is more effective.
- **Improved Learning Dynamics (Fig. 6):** Analysis of training curves showed that latent-augmented variants exhibited slower initial accuracy gains but ultimately surpassed the GRPO baseline in the latter half of training. This "late-stage overtaking" pattern indicates that:
 - Early exploration with diverse reasoning paths (some suboptimal) initially slows convergence.
 - However, this thorough exploration exposes the policy to higher-quality regions of the behavior space.
 - During the subsequent exploitation phase (when latent diversity is reduced), the model consolidates these better behaviors, leading to higher asymptotic performance and a smoother transition from exploration to exploitation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Reasoning Palette" framework represents a significant advancement in the development and training of large (vision-)language models, with several key significances and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Exploration Efficiency in RL:** This is arguably the most critical impact. By shifting RL exploration from low-level, token-centric stochasticity to high-level, strategic diversity, Reasoning Palette addresses a long-standing bottleneck in training complex reasoning agents. This structured exploration can lead to faster discovery of effective reasoning strategies, more robust policies, and ultimately, higher performance ceilings for LLMs/VLMs in challenging problem-solving domains. This could accelerate the development of more capable AI assistants and problem-solvers.

2. **Controllability and Interpretability of Reasoning:** The framework provides an explicit and interpretable mechanism for controlling the reasoning style of (V)LMs. Users can sample from a latent space that disentangles different reasoning patterns (e.g., mathematical deduction, code generation logic, multi-hop QA), allowing for targeted intervention. This move beyond "black-box" model behavior towards transparent, steerable reasoning is invaluable for debugging, understanding model strengths/weaknesses, and tailoring AI behavior to specific task requirements. It opens doors for "reasoning-aware" prompting and analysis.

3. **Generalizability Across Modalities:** Demonstrating effectiveness for both text-only LLMs and multimodal VLMs (e.g., for referring expression comprehension) highlights the broad applicability of the latent contextualization idea. This suggests that the principle of modulating internal planning via a learned latent space is a fundamental technique for improving diverse forms of AI reasoning, regardless of input modality.

4. **Practical and Lightweight Integration:** The method is designed to be lightweight, utilizing a brief SFT warm-up to align base models to the latent conditioning without compromising their general capabilities. This ease of integration makes "Reasoning Palette" a practical tool for researchers and developers working with pre-trained large models, offering a performance boost without requiring extensive architectural changes or prohibitively expensive retraining.

5. **New Research Directions:** The work opens up several avenues for future research. One could explore more sophisticated VAE architectures for disentangling an even finer-grained spectrum of reasoning styles, investigate adaptive prefix lengths during inference, or develop methods for dynamically learning the optimal exploration schedule in RL. The interpretable latent space could also be used to synthesize new reasoning patterns or transfer styles between tasks.

In conclusion, "Reasoning Palette" offers a powerful and practical solution for imbuing LLMs and VLMs with a greater capacity for diverse and controllable reasoning. By enabling strategic exploration at a high level, it promises to yield more efficient RL training, more robust model performance, and a deeper understanding of how these advanced AI systems arrive at their solutions.
