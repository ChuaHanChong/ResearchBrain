# DFM-VLA: Iterative Action Refinement for Robot Manipulation via Discrete Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> DFM-VLA is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that employs discrete flow matching for iterative, full-sequence action refinement in robot manipulation. This approach yielded an average success length of 4.44 on CALVIN, a 95.7% average success rate on LIBERO, and a 70.8% average success rate in real-world bimanual tasks, along with a 2.4x inference speedup when using adaptive KV caching.

## Problem

- Existing autoregressive (AR) and discrete diffusion (DD) Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from an "irreversible commitment" problem, where early decoding errors in action sequences cannot be corrected.
- This limitation leads to error propagation and degraded performance in long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks that demand precise and consistent actions.
- Current discrete VLA systems lack robust mechanisms for iterative, full-sequence refinement of generated action tokens.

## Method

- **DFM-VLA** integrates ==Discrete Flow Matching== (DFM) to enable iterative, full-sequence ==action refinement== by modeling ==probability paths== between a noisy state and a target action sequence.
- The framework explores two ==velocity-field constructions==, with an ==action-embedding-guided== approach providing more effective signals for refining action tokens.
- A ==two-stage decoding strategy== is implemented, combining an iterative ==stochastic refinement== stage with a deterministic ==greedy validation== stage, further enhanced by ==adaptive KV caching== for improved efficiency.

## Results

- DFM-VLA+Embed achieved an average success length of **4.44** on the CALVIN benchmark and the highest overall average success rate of **95.7%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming strong AR and continuous diffusion baselines.
- In real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, DFM-VLA+Embed demonstrated a **70.8%** average success rate, surpassing π0-FAST (AR, **42.5%**) and other diffusion baselines.
- With adaptive KV caching, DFM-VLA achieved a **2.4x** inference speedup over autoregressive decoding while maintaining superior action quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative refinement through discrete flow matching effectively addresses the "irreversible commitment" problem, allowing correction of any token across the action sequence in VLA models.
> - Parameterizing probability paths using action-embedding guidance offers superior optimization signals and faster convergence compared to an auxiliary velocity head for discrete action generation.
> - A balanced two-stage decoding process, which allocates steps between stochastic exploration and deterministic convergence, optimizes action quality and stability in robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation relies on Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models that map language instructions and visual observations to robot actions. A common approach in VLA systems is to discretize actions into tokens, enabling scalable training by leveraging the capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

Existing discrete VLA systems primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Autoregressive (AR) methods**: These models decode action tokens sequentially. While effective, their inherently left-to-right decoding order means that once an erroneous token is generated, it cannot be revised in subsequent iterations.
2. **Discrete Diffusion (DD) methods**: These approaches offer improved parallelism and often reduced latency compared to AR methods. However, similar to AR methods, they typically produce tokens in early iterations that, once generated, are fixed and cannot be corrected. Updates are often restricted to masked positions rather than allowing full-sequence refinement.

Both AR and DD paradigms share a fundamental limitation, termed the "irreversible commitment problem" by the authors: early decoding errors in the action sequence cannot be effectively corrected, leading to error propagation and degradation in downstream robotic task performance. This issue is particularly critical in robotic control where even minor errors can lead to task failure.

The paper posits that recent advancements in Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) for Large Language Models (LLMs) and VLMs offer a potential solution. DFM methods have demonstrated competitive performance in text reasoning, editing, and image/video generation by enabling iterative token refinement and flexible generation trajectories guided by a velocity field. This work introduces DFM principles to the action modality, aiming to overcome the limitations of existing AR and DD VLA paradigms by allowing for continuous refinement of the entire action sequence. By enabling tokens to be revisited and corrected, DFM-VLA seeks to improve the robustness and accuracy of robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a VLA framework that can perform iterative, full-sequence action refinement, addressing the "irreversible commitment problem" prevalent in existing autoregressive and discrete diffusion VLA decoding paradigms. The authors aim to enhance the quality and consistency of robot manipulation by enabling the model to dynamically correct previously generated action tokens.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges in current VLA models:
- **Irreversible Token Errors:** Both autoregressive and discrete diffusion VLAs, once an action token is generated, typically fix it. This means that if an error occurs early in the action sequence, it cannot be rectified in later decoding steps, leading to error accumulation and potentially task failure in complex, long-horizon robotic tasks. The lack of a robust correction mechanism impedes the reliability of these systems in real-world applications.
- **Propagation of Errors:** In robotic manipulation, the impact of an early error can be significant, propagating through the entire action chunk and leading to incorrect subsequent actions or even catastrophic failures. An effective VLA model needs a mechanism to mitigate such error propagation.
- **Need for Holistic Refinement:** Current methods often update actions either sequentially (AR) or by focusing only on masked tokens (DD). The authors motivate the need for a mechanism that allows for holistic refinement, where the entire action sequence can be revisited and updated based on improved context and confidence, leading to more accurate and coherent action plans.
- **Leveraging Discrete Flow Matching Advances:** The success of Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) in other modalities (e.g., text, image, video generation and editing) suggests its potential applicability to discrete action generation. DFM provides a principled framework for modeling probability paths and deriving velocity fields that guide iterative refinement. The authors are motivated to transfer these principles to the domain of robotic action sequences.
- **Improving Performance and Efficiency:** Beyond just correctness, the authors aim to achieve these improvements while maintaining or enhancing inference efficiency, a critical factor for practical robotic deployment.

By addressing these motivations, the DFM-VLA framework aims to produce more robust, accurate, and consistent robot behaviors, particularly in tasks requiring long-horizon planning and precise execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DFM-VLA is a Vision–Language–Action (VLA) framework that integrates language, vision, and action within a discrete formulation, leveraging a discrete action velocity field for iterative full-sequence refinement of action tokens.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Discrete Flow Matching (DFM)

The work builds on discrete flow matching, a framework for transforming a source distribution into a target data distribution over a discrete space using time-indexed probability paths.
- **Probability Paths:** DFM defines a family of time-indexed distributions, $p_t(x)$, that smoothly interpolate between a source $p(x)$ and a target $q(x)$ distribution. For action tokens, a mixture path is used, where $p_t(x_i | x_{i1})$ interpolates between a base distribution and a point mass at the target token, guided by a time-dependent scheduler $\kappa_t(x_{i1})$.
- **Probability Velocities:** To realize these probability paths, a Continuous-Time Markov Chain (CTMC) governs the dynamics. The core is a probability velocity $u_t$, or transition rate, which describes how the current state $x_t$ moves towards the target $x_1$. This velocity field is the main quantity learned during training, modeling discrete state transitions.

#### 3.2. Architecture

DFM-VLA adopts an architecture similar to UniVLA, using a unified discrete token formulation across modalities.
- **Tokenization:** Text is tokenized using the Emu3 tokenizer. Visual observations (both third-person and wrist-view images) are discretized via a VQ tokenizer with a compression ratio of 4, resulting in 25x25 (625) tokens. Continuous actions are discretized using FAST, followed by Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) to compress action tokens into a vocabulary of 1024.
- **Modality Distinctions:** Boundary markers (e.g., `boi/eoi` for image, `boa/eoa` for action) are used to distinguish modalities. Noising and prediction are applied exclusively to the action modality.

#### 3.3. Modeling Velocities

The paper explores two methods for constructing the token-level probability velocity field:

- **Auxiliary Velocity Head Formulation (L_head):**
 - Inspired by EditFlow, this approach models velocities using an additional head. Given noisy action tokens $x_t$ and context $l$, a backbone network $f_{\theta}$ produces hidden states $h_t$. An auxiliary velocity prediction head, $u_{head_t}$, then maps these hidden states to replacement velocities $u_{\theta t}(\cdot | x_t) = u_{head_t}(h_t)$.
 - The training objective, $L_{head}$, focuses on positions where the current token $x_i_t$ differs from the target token $x_{i1}$, optimizing to predict the correct transition rates.

- **Action-Embedding-Guided Velocity Modeling (L_ce):**
 - This approach parameterizes the probability path in a metric-induced form. A distance function $d(x_i, x_{i1})$ is defined in the action token embedding space, where distance is zero if and only if the tokens are identical.
 - The conditional path $p_t(x_i | x_{i1})$ is defined using a softmax over $-\beta_t \cdot d(x_i, x_{i1})$, where $\beta_t$ is a monotonic schedule (instantiated as $c(t/(1-t))^{\alpha}$). This formulation ensures that tokens semantically closer to the target $x_{i1}$ receive higher probability as time progresses.
 - The kinetic-optimal velocity is derived by minimizing transport energy under flow constraints. This velocity moves probability mass only from tokens that are further from $x_{i1}$ to those that are closer, ensuring a monotonic refinement process.
 - **Training:** The model predicts the target action sequence $x_1$ by outputting per-position categorical logits. The training objective is the expected cross-entropy loss, $L_{ce}$. The authors note that the embedding-guided objective converges faster and achieves better performance.

#### 3.4. Inference

Inference is conducted in a two-stage decoding strategy: an iterative refinement stage followed by a deterministic validation stage. The total number of decoding steps is $T_{fine} + T_{val}$.

- **Iterative Refinement Stage (T_fine steps):**
 - This stage employs an Euler discretization of the Continuous-Time Markov Chain (CTMC).
 - At each step $k$, the model samples a predicted clean action $x_{pred_1}$ from $p_{\theta}(\cdot | x_t, l)$.
 - It then computes the velocity field $u_t$ using either Eq. 7 (embedding-guided) or Eq. 3 (velocity head).
 - A total outgoing rate $\lambda_i$ for each token is calculated. Based on a jump probability $(1 - e^{-h\lambda_i})$, tokens are either kept or updated. If a jump occurs, the new token is sampled proportionally to the normalized rates, favoring states closer to $x_{pred_1}$.
 - This process allows previously updated tokens to be revised again, facilitating iterative correction across the full sequence.

- **Deterministic Validation Stage (T_val steps):**
 - In the final $T_{val}$ steps, stochastic jumps are disabled, and the model switches to a greedy decoding strategy.
 - The next state $x_{t+h}$ is determined by taking the `argmax` of the predicted categorical distribution $softmax(p_{1|t}(\cdot|x_t))$.
 - This hybrid design balances exploratory refinement in early iterations with deterministic convergence towards the end, aiming for more stable and reproducible trajectories.

- **Adaptive KV Caching:** To improve inference efficiency, DFM-VLA adopts an adaptive KV caching strategy similar to FAST-dLLM. Instruction and observation KV caches are mostly fixed, while the action-side cache is dynamically updated based on cosine similarity of features. This reuse contributes to latency reduction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated DFM-VLA against state-of-the-art VLA methods across simulated benchmarks (CALVIN, LIBERO) and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, along with ablation studies on design choices and an analysis of efficiency.

#### 4.1. Benchmarks Performance

- **CALVIN Benchmark (Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation):**
 - DFM-VLA consistently outperformed most strong VLA baselines in long-horizon tasks.
 - DFM-VLA+Embed achieved the highest average success length of 4.44 out of a maximum of 5.
 - It showed improved 3-step and 5-step completion rates (0.892 vs. 0.862 and 0.780 vs. 0.690) compared to unified AR VLAs (e.g., UniVLA*), indicating stronger long-horizon consistency.
 - The method also surpassed continuous diffusion VLA models across all reported metrics, demonstrating the competitiveness of discrete action refinement.
 - DFM-VLA+Head, while slightly weaker than DFM-VLA+Embed, still outperformed other baselines, suggesting the general efficacy of velocity-guided action regeneration.

- **LIBERO Benchmark (Cross-Task Generalization):**
 - DFM-VLA demonstrated clear performance gains across all task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).
 - DFM-VLA+Embed achieved an overall average success rate of 95.7%, which was the highest among compared methods.
 - It outperformed reasoning-guided discrete VLAs (e.g., ThinkAct, MolmoAct, CoT-VLA) and improved upon continuous diffusion models like DreamVLA (+3.1 points) and FlowVLA (+7.6 points).
 - It achieved the highest scores on the Object (98.8%) and Long (92.6%) suites, indicating strong performance in object-level generalization and long-horizon compositional reasoning. These results suggest that direct action refinement with coarse action signals is more effective than relying on auxiliary modality chain-of-thought guidance.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Schedule Hyperparameters (c and α):** DFM-VLA exhibited robustness to moderate changes in the schedule parameters `c` (overall scale of $\beta_t$) and `α` (temporal curvature). The default setting of `c=3` and `α=1` provided the best trade-off, achieving an average length of 4.44 on CALVIN and 95.7% on LIBERO. Minor fluctuations were observed with `c=5` or `α=0.5`, indicating stable refinement dynamics within a reasonable hyperparameter range.
- **Effect of Two-Stage Decoding Allocation:** With a fixed total of 16 decoding steps, allocating 14 steps to iterative refinement ($T_{fine}$) and 2 steps to deterministic validation ($T_{val}$) yielded the best performance (4.44 on CALVIN, 95.7% on LIBERO). Pure iterative refinement ($T_{val}=0$) and excessive validation steps negatively impacted performance, suggesting that a balance between exploration and deterministic convergence is beneficial.
- **Comparison of Velocity Field Constructions:** The embedding-guided formulation (L_ce) consistently outperformed the auxiliary velocity head formulation (L_head) in both convergence speed and final task performance on LIBERO, reaching 95.7% success rate with 20k training steps, compared to 93.5% for the velocity head formulation. This indicates that embedding guidance provides a more informative and smoother optimization signal.

#### 4.3. In-Depth Analysis

- **Effectiveness of Decoding Method:** DFM-VLA achieved the best action quality compared to AR and DD decoding strategies, given the same architecture and number of decoding steps.
- **Inference Efficiency:** With Adaptive KV Caching enabled, DFM-VLA maintained strong performance while delivering the fastest decoding speed, achieving a 2.4x latency speedup over autoregressive decoding and outperforming DD with adaptive caching. This indicates a favorable quality-efficiency trade-off.
- **Effect of Training Data Size:** DFM-VLA consistently outperformed AR and DD baselines across all tested data scales (10%, 50%, 100%) on CALVIN. The benefits were particularly pronounced in low-data regimes (e.g., +1.50 Avg. Len. over AR and +0.37 over DD at 10% data), while maintaining consistent advantages as data scaled up.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

- DFM-VLA was evaluated on a bimanual AgileX platform across three real-world manipulation tasks: bimanual collaborative lifting (Pot Lift), grasping and placing elongated vegetables (Place Veg. to Pot), and grasping and placing blocks onto a plate with varying height (Place Block to Plate).
- DFM-VLA+Embed achieved the highest success rates on all tasks, with an average success rate of 70.8%. This surpassed the continuous diffusion baseline RDT (60.0%) by 10.8 percentage points and the discrete diffusion baseline Dream-VLA (54.2%) by 16.6 percentage points.
- The results indicate that DFM-VLA's ability to refine action tokens corrects errors in real time, outperforming methods where wrongly decoded tokens cannot be refined. Its unified discrete input-output space also appears to better preserve semantic understanding from VLM backbones, contributing to its robust performance in real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DFM-VLA framework introduces a novel decoding paradigm for discrete Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models, directly addressing a fundamental limitation present in conventional autoregressive (AR) and discrete diffusion (DD) approaches: the "irreversible commitment problem." By enabling iterative, full-sequence action refinement through token-level velocity modeling, DFM-VLA allows for the dynamic correction of previously generated action tokens. This capability represents a significant advancement in enhancing the robustness and consistency of robotic manipulation policies.

The demonstrated consistent performance gains across diverse benchmarks—including long-horizon tasks on CALVIN, cross-task generalization on LIBERO, and various real-world manipulation scenarios—highlight the practical value of this approach. The improvements in average success length and success rates indicate that DFM-VLA can generate more reliable and precise action sequences, leading to fewer task failures and more consistent robot behavior. This is particularly impactful for complex, multi-step tasks where early errors can propagate and derail an entire operation.

Furthermore, the research reveals that embedding-guided velocity modeling is a more effective strategy than an auxiliary velocity head, offering faster convergence and superior final performance. The two-stage decoding strategy, combining exploratory refinement with deterministic validation, provides a balanced approach to action generation. The integration of adaptive KV caching also contributes to a favorable quality-efficiency trade-off, which is crucial for the deployment of VLA models on physical robotic platforms where both performance and inference speed are critical.

The finding that DFM-VLA provides substantial benefits even in low-data regimes suggests its potential to mitigate data scarcity challenges often encountered in robotics, making it a more accessible and powerful tool for a broader range of applications.

This work contributes to the field of robot learning by providing a robust mechanism for discrete action token correction, drawing inspiration from advances in discrete flow matching in other generative modeling domains. The DFM-VLA framework may inspire future research into more sophisticated iterative refinement strategies, dynamic error correction mechanisms, and novel ways to leverage flow-matching principles for embodied AI, ultimately paving the way for more capable and reliable general-purpose robot manipulation systems.
