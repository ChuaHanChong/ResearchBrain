# BIEVR-LIO: Robust LiDAR-Inertial Odometry through Bump-Image-Enhanced Voxel Maps

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zürich developed BIEVR-LIO, a LiDAR-Inertial Odometry system that enhances robustness and accuracy in geometrically challenging environments by leveraging subtle surface details. The system consistently achieved lower error metrics on most datasets with weak geometric structure and maintained stable odometry where baseline methods diverged, demonstrating its effectiveness across diverse sensors and platforms.

## Problem

- LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) systems significantly degrade or diverge in environments with limited geometric information, such as long corridors, tunnels, or vast flat areas.
- Existing map representations in LIO often lack the resolution to capture subtle but informative geometric variations, smoothing out crucial details.
- Current point sampling strategies for real-time operation can inadvertently discard fine-grained geometric cues, especially in challenging scenes.

## Method

- A novel **Bump-Image-Enhanced Voxel Representation (BIEVR)** is introduced, encoding high-resolution local surface deviations from a dominant plane as a 2D "bump-image" within each voxel.
- A map-informed, ==two-stage point sampling strategy== prioritizes points from voxels with high ==Mean Image Distance (MID)== scores, indicating richer geometric detail, for high-resolution registration.
- A loosely-coupled state estimation incorporates a specialized ==Jacobian component== derived from the bump image gradient, enabling the exploitation of subtle in-plane geometric variations for robust registration.

## Results

- BIEVR-LIO achieved the lowest Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Error (RE) on most sequences of the ENWIDE dataset (e.g., IntersectionS: **0.231 m** / **0.7%**) and competitive results on the Newer College dataset, outperforming intensity-augmented methods without using intensity.
- Demonstrated substantial improvements in robustness in highly uninformative GEODE datasets, such as metro tunnels (e.g., Shield1: **0.256 m** over **700m**) and flat terrain (MARS-LVIG FeaturelessGNSS02: **4.93 m** / **12.9%**), where other state-of-the-art methods diverged.
- The BIEVR map's fine-grained geometry was successfully used for downstream elevation mapping, enabling a quadruped robot to robustly navigate obstacles and climb stairs with minimal additional computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Environments typically considered "uninformative" for LIO often contain subtle geometric variations that, if properly captured and utilized, can provide sufficient constraints for robust pose estimation.
> - Encoding local surface deviations in a high-resolution "bump-image" within voxels allows for efficient storage and retrieval of fine-grained geometric details that are critical for registration.
> - Prioritizing sampling in areas of the map identified as geometrically richer (via MID score) significantly improves both the robustness and computational efficiency of LIO in challenging scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Ego-motion estimation is a foundational component for numerous mobile robotics applications. LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) has gained wide adoption due to its accuracy and robustness in various environments. However, the performance of LIO systems degrades significantly in environments that offer limited geometric information, such as long, straight corridors, tunnels, or vast, flat outdoor areas. In such scenarios, the observed geometry provides insufficient constraints for point cloud registration, which can lead to reduced accuracy or even divergence of the LIO system.

The existing research landscape has addressed this challenge through several avenues. Some approaches focus on detecting degeneracy, but these often rely on auxiliary odometry sources, thereby increasing system complexity, or are only effective for short degenerate segments. Another line of research integrates LiDAR intensity information as an additional cue for registration. While intensity-augmented methods can achieve performance improvements, their applicability is limited to LiDAR sensors that provide dense intensity data, excluding those with sparse or irregular scan patterns.

In terms of map representations, traditional LIO systems frequently use point cloud maps, often indexed by kd-trees, which necessitates aggressive downsampling to achieve real-time performance, potentially leading to a loss of fine geometric detail. More advanced voxel-based methods improve lookup efficiency but often approximate local geometry as planes or Gaussians, limiting the level of detail that can be captured. Distance fields and spherical harmonics have also been explored, but these can be computationally expensive to update or inherently smooth subtle features crucial for robust registration.

Point sampling strategies also represent a key area of development. Modern 3D LiDARs generate large quantities of points, requiring downsampling for real-time operation. Common methods include fixed-size voxel grid filtering or sampling specific features (edges, planes). Some advanced techniques sample points based on the Hessian of the optimization problem to focus on informative regions, but these can be computationally intensive and might inadvertently discard crucial details due to coarse initial downsampling.

BIEVR-LIO positions itself within this context by proposing a novel approach to enhance LIO robustness in geometrically sparse environments. It addresses the limitation of existing methods that often fail to exploit subtle geometric variations present even in "uninformative" real-world scenes. The paper argues that these subtle details, if captured and utilized appropriately, can render the odometry problem well-constrained. By introducing a high-resolution map representation and a map-informed sampling strategy, BIEVR-LIO aims to extract and leverage this fine-grained geometric information directly, without relying on additional sensors, LiDAR intensity, or overly simplistic geometric approximations. This places it as a contribution to improving the fundamental robustness of LIO systems, particularly in challenging environments that typically cause failure or significant degradation in performance for many current state-of-the-art methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to enhance the robustness and accuracy of LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) systems, particularly in geometrically challenging environments where current state-of-the-art methods often exhibit degraded performance or diverge. These challenging environments include scenarios with limited structural information, such as long straight tunnels, large flat terrains, or open fields.

The motivation for this research stems from a key observation: environments typically considered "uninformative" for LIO are rarely entirely degenerate in a strict mathematical sense. Instead, real-world scenes almost always contain subtle geometric variations – minor irregularities in walls, textured ground, or small structural elements – which, in principle, provide sufficient information to constrain point cloud registration. However, existing LIO systems commonly fail to exploit these fine-grained details because their map representations lack the necessary resolution to capture such subtle structures effectively under real-time operational constraints. Furthermore, current point sampling strategies may inadvertently discard these crucial details during downsampling, or focus computation on less informative areas.

Based on this overarching motivation, the specific objectives of BIEVR-LIO are:

1. **Develop a High-Resolution Map Representation:** Introduce a novel "Bump-Image-Enhanced Voxel Representation (BIEVR)" that can explicitly encode and preserve high-resolution geometric information. This representation should capture local surface deviations from a dominant plane within each voxel, thereby maintaining fine-grained details that are often smoothed out or ignored by other map structures.
2. **Enable Efficient Scan-to-Map Registration:** Design the BIEVR map to facilitate efficient and direct scan-to-map registration. The goal is to perform registration without requiring the computation of intermediate geometric primitives, while still allowing for constant-time map lookups and efficient updates, even when integrating full undistorted LiDAR scans.
3. **Propose a Map-Informed Point Sampling Strategy:** Address the challenge of utilizing the high-resolution map effectively. Develop a sampling strategy that leverages the information within the BIEVR map to prioritize points in geometrically informative regions. This strategy aims to improve robustness in uninformative environments by focusing computational resources on areas that provide stronger registration constraints, while simultaneously reducing overall computational cost compared to uniform high-resolution sampling.
4. **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Performance:** Evaluate the proposed system across a diverse range of sensors, robotic platforms, and environments, including both geometrically rich and challenging scenes. The objective is to demonstrate performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods in well-constrained scenarios and to achieve substantial improvements in robustness and accuracy in environments where baseline methods typically fail or diverge.
5. **Show Applicability for Downstream Tasks:** Illustrate that the fine-grained geometric details captured by BIEVR-LIO can be beneficial for other robotics applications, such as elevation mapping for robot locomotion, thus extending its utility beyond ego-motion estimation.

By achieving these objectives, BIEVR-LIO aims to provide a more robust and widely applicable LIO solution for mobile robots operating in increasingly complex and varied real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BIEVR-LIO operates as a loosely-coupled LiDAR-Inertial Odometry system designed to exploit subtle geometric details in LiDAR data while maintaining computational efficiency. The system integrates IMU measurements for pointwise scan undistortion and to provide an initial pose estimate for registration. The core design principles involve a novel high-resolution map representation, a map-aware point sampling strategy, and a two-stage state estimation process.

**A. Bump-Image-Enhanced Voxel Representation (BIEVR)**

The BIEVR map addresses the limitation of traditional map representations that struggle to capture fine geometric details. Instead of approximating surfaces as simple planes, BIEVR explicitly captures local deviations from a dominant plane within each voxel. This dominant plane serves as an image plane, and per-pixel deviations (heights) from this plane are stored in a 2D "bump-image."

1. **Iterative Plane Estimation:** Inspired by iG-LIO, a voxel-wise update strategy is employed. Undistorted LiDAR points, transformed to the world frame using the current pose estimate, are grouped into voxels (0.5 m side length) via a spatial hash function. For each voxel, the sum of points (`s`), sum of outer products (`C`), and point count (`n`) are incrementally updated. From these, the voxel centroid (`μ`) and covariance matrix (`Σ`) are derived. The voxel normal is determined as the eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of `Σ`. A local coordinate system is then established with its origin at `μ` and its z-axis aligned with the normal, defining a transform `T_VG` from global to voxel frame.
2. **Bump Image Update:** Each voxel stores a height image (`I`) representing fine-grained surface deviations. A voxel is initialized when it accumulates at least four points. The image size (width `W` and height `H`) is determined by projecting the voxel's eight corners onto the x-y plane of the voxel coordinate system and calculating the extrema. The pixel resolution `r` is set to 0.05 m. For each input point falling into a voxel, its coordinates are projected into the image frame to determine pixel coordinates (`u`, `v`) and its height (`z`) above the plane. Pixel values in the bump image are updated using a weighted average, where points closer to the LiDAR receive higher weights (`w_i = min(0.5, ||Lp_i||^-1)`). After updates, a Gaussian filter with a 1-pixel radius is applied to mitigate noise. If the voxel normal changes by more than 3 degrees, the image coordinate system is updated, and the existing bump image is reprojected and resampled onto the new plane. The system integrates the full undistorted input scan into the map.
3. **Implementation:** BIEVR-LIO uses a hashmap with Morton code for O(1) average-case voxel lookup. Each voxel stores `T_CG`, the height image `I`, pixel weights `W`, `s`, `C`, `n`, and its Mean Image Distance (MID) score. Map updates are parallelized. To manage memory, a Least Recently Used (LRU) cache evicts older voxels when capacity is exceeded.

**B. Map-Informed Point Sampling**

To balance high-resolution map utilization with computational efficiency, a map-aware, two-stage sampling strategy is introduced.

1. **Mean Image Distance (MID):** A metric called Mean Image Distance (MID) is computed for each voxel. This is the average of the absolute pixel values `||I(u, v)||` over observed pixels in the bump image. A higher MID indicates greater non-planarity and richer geometric detail, suggesting a higher potential for providing informative registration Jacobians. This metric is computationally efficient as it is updated during the mapping process.
2. **Two-Stage Sampling:**
 - The undistorted point cloud is initially downsampled at a high resolution (0.1 m) and transformed into the world frame using the IMU-based pose estimate.
 - The system queries the corresponding map voxel for each downsampled point. The 300 voxels with the highest MID scores are identified, and *all* points within these selected voxels are retained.
 - Points in the remaining voxels (those not among the top 300 MID scores) are further downsampled at a coarser resolution (0.5 m).
 - The combined set of high-resolution points from informative voxels and low-resolution points from less informative regions is then used for the registration process.

**C. Loosely-Coupled State Estimation**

BIEVR-LIO estimates the robot state, including rotation, translation, velocity, accelerometer/gyroscope biases, and gravity direction. It adopts a loosely-coupled design to prevent inertial residuals from overwhelming subtle geometric cues during pose estimation.

1. **IMU-Based State Prediction & Motion Compensation:** A continuous-time model is used to compute preintegrated rotation and translation increments between IMU timestamps corresponding to consecutive LiDAR scans. These increments are used to undistort LiDAR points to the most recent timestamp and to provide an initial pose estimate (registration prior `T_GI_k`).
2. **Registration:** The sampled point cloud is aligned directly to the BIEVR map. This is achieved by minimizing the height error between projected input points and the interpolated pixel values in their corresponding voxel height-maps.
 - **Residual Formulation:** For each input point `I_p_i`, its projection into the voxel's image frame (`C_p_i`) yields its height `[C_p_i]_z` and pixel coordinates (`u_i`, `v_i`). The residual `r_i(ξ)` is defined as the difference between `[C_p_i]_z` and the interpolated image value `I(u_i, v_i)`. Points projecting to unobserved pixel regions are omitted.
 - **Jacobian Components:** The Jacobian of the residual with respect to the pose perturbation `ξ` is decomposed into two parts. The first part is analogous to a classical point-to-plane error, describing how the residual changes as the point moves towards the image plane. The second part, derived from the height image gradient, describes how the residual changes if the point moves parallel to the plane, i.e., how the image value changes as the point projects to neighboring pixels. These two additional Jacobian directions are critical for augmenting the optimization with information from subtle in-plane variations, thereby enhancing robustness in geometrically uninformative regions.
 - **Minimization:** The non-linear least-squares problem is solved using Levenberg-Marquardt minimization with a Huber loss function.
3. **Sliding-Window Inertial Optimization:** Separately from the pose estimation, IMU measurements are incorporated via a 10-second sliding-window optimization to enforce temporal inertial consistency. Gravity direction and IMU biases are estimated as window-constant variables. Poses determined by LiDAR registration are fixed as anchors, and the optimization targets velocity, gravity, and biases using IMU residuals. This approach avoids covariance tuning and preserves sensitivity to geometric details.

**D. Parameters**
A single, unified set of parameters (voxel side length 0.5 m, bump-image resolution 0.05 m, coarse/fine downsample resolutions 0.5 m / 0.1 m, 300 fine-resolution voxels, Huber Loss Delta 0.1 m, IMU optimization window 10 s) is used across all experiments to demonstrate the method's generalizability and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BIEVR-LIO encompassed a wide range of experiments across multiple LiDAR sensors, robotic platforms, and environmental conditions, using a single, unified set of parameters.

**A. Odometry Results**

1. **Newer College Dataset (Geometrically Informative):** In this dataset, captured with an Ouster OS0-128 LiDAR, BIEVR-LIO demonstrated competitive performance. It achieved the lowest Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Error (RE) on the Cloister (0.053 m / 0.2%) and Stairs (0.056 m / 0.6%) sequences, outperforming several baselines including COIN-LIO on these specific sequences. The system handled abrupt motions robustly, indicating effective use of IMU undistortion and registration priors despite its loosely-coupled design.
2. **ENWIDE Dataset (Weak Geometric Structure):** This dataset features handheld outdoor sequences with an Ouster OS0-128 in geometrically challenging environments. BIEVR-LIO was the only geometry-only method that remained stable across all sequences, achieving the lowest error metrics on most. For example, on IntersectionS (0.231 m / 0.7%), IntersectionD (0.404 m / 0.8%), FieldS (0.159 m / 0.3%), KatzenseeS (0.194 m / 0.3%), and KatzenseeD (0.243 m / 0.6%), BIEVR-LIO reported the lowest errors. Notably, it generally outperformed COIN-LIO, an intensity-augmented method, despite not utilizing intensity information, which underscores the effectiveness of exploiting subtle geometric cues.
3. **GEODE Dataset (Uninformative Geometry):** This dataset, featuring Livox Avia LiDAR data from tunnels and open fields, presented highly uninformative geometry. BIEVR-LIO demonstrated substantial improvements in robustness where many baseline methods diverged. In the challenging Shield metro tunnel sequences (Shield1, Shield4, Shield5), where longitudinal constraints arise from subtle structures like train beds and wall cutouts, BIEVR-LIO maintained robust odometry (e.g., Shield1: 0.256 m / 2.1% over 700m), while most other methods failed. It also achieved the lowest ATE on Tunneling2 (0.096 m / 1.2%), a sequence with limited views of informative structure.
4. **MARS-LVIG Dataset (High-Altitude UAV):** For high-altitude UAV flights with sparse Livox Avia point clouds, BIEVR-LIO robustly handled the sequences. On the FeaturelessGNSS02 sequence, where the UAV flew at low altitude over flat terrain, BIEVR-LIO maintained stable odometry (4.93 m / 12.9%), whereas both FAST-LIO2 and iG-LIO diverged, highlighting its ability to extract constraints from subtle ground irregularities.
5. **GrandTour Dataset (Legged Platform, Lower Resolution LiDAR):** In experiments with a quadruped robot equipped with a Hesai XT32 LiDAR, BIEVR-LIO demonstrated consistent and accurate performance across sequences, including confined indoor sections (ARC-4: 0.051 m / 0.6%, LEE-1: 0.024 m / 0.1%) where other LIOs experienced drift or divergence.

**B. Ablation Study**

An ablation study was conducted to analyze the impact of the BIEVR map representation and the map-informed point sampling strategy.

- **BIEVR Map and Jacobian Components:**
 - Comparing point-to-plane alignment using principal voxel planes (Plane|×|HR) versus using each BIEVR pixel as a high-resolution plane without the additional Jacobian direction (Bump|×|HR) showed that while both performed similarly in well-constrained environments (Cloister), the BIEVR representation offered improved accuracy in sparser scenes (FieldS).
 - Crucially, enabling the additional Jacobian direction, which exploits the height image gradient (BIEVR|✓|HR), was essential for robustness in the most challenging Shield1 sequence. Without this, the system struggled or diverged, confirming the importance of this specific Jacobian component for leveraging subtle surface variations.
- **Map-Informed Sampling:**
 - The proposed map-informed dual-resolution sampling (ID) consistently outperformed both fixed high-resolution sampling (HR) and random dual-resolution sampling (RD). RD performed worst, as high-resolution samples were often allocated to uninformative regions.
 - ID improved the signal-to-noise ratio by focusing computation on informative areas. It also significantly reduced the number of points used for registration (approximately a factor of four compared to HR sampling), leading to lower runtime.

**C. Runtime Analysis**

BIEVR-LIO achieved competitive runtime performance. The map-informed sampling strategy played a role in reducing the number of points processed during registration, thereby contributing to lower overall runtime compared to global high-resolution sampling. The majority of computation time was spent in the combined state update, encompassing the inertial update and the registration step. Map updates and sampling times scaled with the number of observed voxels and input points.

**D. Elevation Mapping (Downstream Task)**

The research demonstrated the practical utility of the BIEVR map for downstream applications. In real-world experiments, the fine-grained geometry captured by BIEVR-LIO was used to generate elevation maps for a quadruped robot's locomotion policy. The robot successfully traversed obstacles (stacked pallets) and climbed a staircase, accurately placing its feet based on the BIEVR-derived elevation map. This indicates that the map provides sufficient detail for locomotion planning with minimal additional computational overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The BIEVR-LIO research presents several significant contributions to the field of mobile robotics and LiDAR-Inertial Odometry, with notable potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Challenging Environments:** A primary impact is the significant improvement in LIO robustness and accuracy, particularly in environments historically problematic for LiDAR-based systems. By effectively exploiting subtle geometric variations that traditional methods miss, BIEVR-LIO enables reliable ego-motion estimation in scenarios like long tunnels, flat outdoor areas, and sparsely structured scenes where other approaches often fail or diverge. This expands the operational domain of mobile robots into more complex and less structured environments.
2. **Novel and Efficient Map Representation:** The Bump-Image-Enhanced Voxel Representation (BIEVR) is a key innovation. It offers a method for storing high-resolution geometric details, specifically local surface deviations, in an efficient voxel-based structure. This representation facilitates constant-time lookups and efficient updates, including the integration of full undistorted LiDAR scans, without the computational burden or detail loss associated with approximating surfaces with simpler primitives. This design advances map representation techniques for real-time robotic applications.
3. **Intelligent Resource Allocation through Map-Informed Sampling:** The proposed map-informed point sampling strategy contributes to both robustness and computational efficiency. By quantifying the "informativeness" of map regions using the Mean Image Distance and selectively increasing point density in these areas, the system focuses computational resources where they are most beneficial. This approach enhances the signal-to-noise ratio in registration, improves performance in challenging scenes, and reduces overall processing time, making high-resolution mapping feasible for real-time systems.
4. **Generalizability and Broad Applicability:** The demonstration of consistent performance across multiple LiDAR sensors (Ouster, Livox, Hesai), diverse platforms (handheld, UGV, UAV, quadruped), and varied environments using a single, unified set of parameters highlights the generalizability of BIEVR-LIO. This indicates a robust and adaptable solution that can be readily deployed in a wider range of real-world robotic systems without extensive per-configuration tuning.
5. **Facilitation of Downstream Robotics Tasks:** The ability of BIEVR-LIO to capture fine-grained geometric details also benefits other robotic tasks. The successful application of the BIEVR map for elevation mapping and subsequent locomotion planning on a quadruped robot demonstrates its utility beyond mere odometry. This capability can streamline robotic system architectures by providing a rich, unified geometric representation that serves both localization and planning needs with minimal additional computation.
6. **Foundation for Future LIO Advancements:** The work also lays a foundation for future research. The identified limitations, such as potential performance reduction for very low-resolution LiDARs or in strictly degenerate environments lacking any geometric variation, suggest clear avenues for improvement, including incorporating multi-scale image pyramids or LiDAR intensity information. This contributes to the ongoing development of more robust and comprehensive LIO solutions.

In summary, BIEVR-LIO's significance lies in its capacity to push the boundaries of LIO performance in complex environments, offering a robust, efficient, and generalizable framework that leverages subtle geometric cues, thereby advancing the capabilities of autonomous mobile robots.
