# 3D-Anchored Lookahead Planning for Persistent Robotic Scene Memory via World-Model-Based MCTS

> [!summary] Summary
> Huawei Technologies' 3D-Anchored Lookahead Planning (3D-ALP) enables robots to maintain persistent 3D spatial memory, facilitating robust multi-step manipulation even when objects are occluded. It demonstrated a success rate of 0.650 ± 0.109 on memory-required tasks in simulation, significantly outperforming reactive baselines.

## Problem

- Reactive robotic policies (e.g., VLA models) inherently lack object permanence and persistent scene memory, causing task failures when target objects become occluded or leave the camera's field of view.
- Current robot architectures process only the immediate camera frame, preventing the retention of spatial information about unseen objects, which is crucial for multi-step tasks requiring recall.
- Directly applying traditional UCT-MCTS to continuous robotic manipulation introduces specific structural failure modes that hinder its effectiveness and reliability in 3D environments.

## Method

- The **3D-ALP** system implements a persistent 3D camera-to-world (c2w) anchor, continuously updated via robot forward kinematics, allowing the robot to retain spatial coordinates of known positions within the ==MCTS== tree.
- It utilizes a 3D-consistent generative world model (InSpatio-WorldFM) as an oracle, enabling the ==MCTS== engine to render predicted future camera frames from arbitrary c2w queries for simulated rollouts.
- A hybrid geometric-semantic scorer combines Vision-Language Model (VLM) semantic evaluations with a kinematic depth penalty, addressing VLM 'depth blindness' and guiding the planner toward kinematically feasible and precise interactions.
- The ==MCTS== engine integrates four structural fixes, such as Max-Q value action selection and recursive depth resetting, specifically designed to overcome inherent challenges in adapting UCT for continuous robotic control.

## Results

- In a 5-step sequential reach task, 3D-ALP achieved a success rate (SR) of 0.650 ± 0.109 on memory-required steps (4-5), representing an absolute gain of +0.645 over a greedy reactive baseline (0.006 ± 0.008 SR).
- On the most challenging step 5, involving chained memory of two prior positions, 3D-ALP achieved a 0.822 SR, while the greedy baseline achieved 0.000 SR.
- An ablation study demonstrated that persistent tree search memory (MCTS D=1) alone accounted for **82%** of the performance gain (+0.533 SR) on memory-required steps, indicating its critical role independent of deeper lookahead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persistent 3D tree search memory alone is the primary contributor to performance gains in memory-dependent robotic tasks involving occlusion, accounting for 82% of the observed success rate improvement.
> - Combining semantic scoring from VLMs with geometric distance penalties is critical for grounding visual predictions in 3D reality, effectively mitigating VLM limitations in depth perception during planning.
> - Specific algorithmic adaptations to UCT-MCTS, such as Max-Q selection and proper depth resetting, are essential for its successful and robust application in continuous action spaces of robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern robotic manipulation systems increasingly rely on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which are often characterized as "System 1" reactive policies. These policies map the current camera frame directly to a control action, demonstrating effectiveness on single-step tasks. However, a fundamental limitation of these reactive approaches is their lack of object permanence and persistent scene memory. When objects become occluded or leave the camera's field of view, reactive agents cannot recall their previous positions, leading to failure in multi-step tasks that require spatial memory, such as returning to a previously visited but now unseen location. This limitation is architectural, as these systems lack a mechanism for storing persistent scene state.

The work presented in this paper addresses this gap by introducing a planning architecture designed to provide robots with persistent 3D spatial memory, enabling robust multi-step manipulation even when target objects are not directly observable. It positions itself as orthogonal to existing world model-based approaches (e.g., DreamerV3, TD-MPC2, GigaWorld ecosystem) which primarily use world models for synthetic data generation, value estimation, or training-time supervision. Unlike these systems, 3D-ALP leverages a world model at test time purely as a planning oracle, without requiring task-specific training, and explicitly targets the problem of spatial memory loss under occlusion.

In the context of Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for robot planning, while MCTS has been successful in discrete games (e.g., AlphaGo, MuZero), its application to continuous robotic manipulation introduces specific challenges. The paper identifies and resolves several structural failure modes in adapting UCT-MCTS for this continuous domain. It distinguishes itself from similar MCTS-based VLA augmentation (e.g., VLA-Reasoner) by focusing on maintaining a persistent SE(3) spatial anchor that survives occlusion, a capability not addressed by value-guided search operating solely on visible states. The research also empirically quantifies the performance collapse of reactive policies on memory-dependent tasks, providing evidence for the limitations identified.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed failure of contemporary reactive robotic manipulation policies, particularly those based on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, when confronted with tasks requiring spatial memory of occluded objects. While these "System 1" policies are efficient and capable for immediate, single-step interactions, their architectural design typically processes only the current camera frame. This inherent limitation prevents them from retaining information about object positions once those objects are no longer directly visible. For a robot tasked with a sequence of actions, such as visiting multiple locations and then returning to an initial, now-occluded, position, a reactive agent is fundamentally unable to perform the recall necessary for successful task completion. The authors describe this as a limitation of the architecture itself, rather than merely a capacity issue of the underlying models.

Against this background, the key objective of this work is to develop a planning architecture that confers persistent scene memory upon a robot, thereby enabling it to perform multi-step manipulation tasks robustly, even when targets are spatially occluded. This overarching objective is broken down into three specific aims:

1. **Maintenance of a Persistent 3D Camera-to-World (c2w) Anchor:** The system aims to continuously update and store the robot's camera pose (position and orientation) in a global 3D world frame after each physical action. This c2w anchor is designed to persist across manipulation steps, fundamentally not resetting when objects leave the camera's view.
2. **Integration of a 3D-Consistent World Model:** The system proposes to utilize a 3D-consistent generative world model (specifically InSpatio-WorldFM) as an oracle within a planning framework. This world model would enable the generation of predicted camera frames from any queried c2w pose, allowing a planning algorithm to simulate future states in an imagined 3D space.
3. **Development of a Hybrid Geometric-Semantic Scorer:** To ensure that visual predictions are grounded in the robot's kinematic reality and to mitigate known depth perception shortcomings of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), a hybrid scoring mechanism is intended. This scorer combines semantic evaluations from VLMs with a geometric penalty based on 3D distance, guiding the planner toward kinematically feasible and precise interactions.

The core insight motivating these objectives is that by preserving previously computed c2w values within a planning tree, the robot can effectively "remember" the spatial coordinates of visited or targeted positions. This persistent 3D anchor, combined with a world model that can render hypothetical future states, is intended to allow the planner to navigate back to earlier configurations without requiring real-time visual evidence of the target, thereby overcoming the critical limitation of reactive policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, 3D-Anchored Lookahead Planning (3D-ALP), integrates System 2 reasoning (MCTS) with a 3D-consistent world model to achieve persistent robotic scene memory. It comprises four main components: a Kinematic Bridge (FK), a World Model oracle (InSpatio-WorldFM), a Hybrid Scorer, and an MCTS Engine. These components operate in a dual-timescale manner, differentiating between planning and execution phases.

**System Overview:**
During the **planning time**, the MCTS engine initiates candidate joint actions by querying the Kinematic Bridge. For each potential action, the resulting camera-to-world (c2w) pose is passed to the World Model, which generates a predicted future camera frame. These predicted frames are then evaluated by the Hybrid Scorer, and the scores are backpropagated through the MCTS tree to select the most optimal action.
During the **execution time**, the selected action is physically executed by the robot. The real camera frame is observed, and the 3D anchor (`c2w_t+1`) is updated via the robot's forward kinematics (FK). Crucially, the MCTS tree is re-rooted at the child node corresponding to the executed action, which preserves all previously computed subtree values, thereby maintaining spatial memory across steps.

**Key Components in Detail:**

1. **Persistent 3D Anchor:** The camera-to-world pose (`c2w_t ∈ SE(3)`) represents the camera's position and orientation in the global world frame. This anchor is updated using the robot's forward kinematics (FK) after each physical movement, rather than being reset. This design ensures that even if an object becomes occluded, its last known `c2w` position and associated value estimate remain stored within the MCTS tree, enabling the robot to recall and target it. To counteract positional error accumulation, the world model's reference latent (`z_ref`) is also updated by blending the real camera frame (`z_ref ← 0.7 ⋅ Enc(I_real) + 0.3 ⋅ z_ref`). The blending ratio of 0.7 was empirically determined to minimize anchor drift.

2. **World Model Oracle:** InSpatio-WorldFM is employed as the 3D-consistent generative world model. Its function is to render predicted camera frames from arbitrary `c2w` queries posed by the MCTS engine. This capability allows the MCTS to perform rollouts in an imagined 3D environment, simulating the visual outcome of hypothetical actions.

3. **Hybrid Geometric-Semantic Scorer:** Off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as Florence-2, can assign high semantic scores for 2D pixel overlap, irrespective of the actual 3D depth, potentially leading to incorrect evaluations (e.g., a gripper hovering above a target might score similarly to one in contact). To address this "depth blindness," 3D-ALP introduces a hybrid scorer. This scorer multiplies the VLM's semantic score by a kinematic depth penalty:
 `S_total = S_semantic ⋅ max(0, 1 − ||c2w[:3,3] − c2w_goal[:3,3]||^2)`.
 This mechanism ensures that branches where the predicted end-effector position is geometrically distant from the target are heavily discounted, regardless of their visual plausibility in a 2D projection. For specific ablation studies, a pure geometric oracle (`S_exact = max(0, 1 - d_3D)`) is used, replacing the semantic term with a constant value of 1, to isolate the contribution of tree search memory.

4. **MCTS Engine with Four Structural Fixes:** Adapting UCT-MCTS from discrete board games to the continuous domain of robotic manipulation required addressing four specific failure modes:
 - **(F1) Zero-action exploitation trap:** Standard UCB1 favors the most visited child. In early planning stages, the "stay still" action can accumulate visits, causing the robot to stutter. The fix implemented is to select actions based on Max-Q value, explicitly filtering out zero-magnitude actions.
 - **(F2) Tree depth decay:** After rerooting the MCTS tree (via `advance_root`), reused child nodes would retain their original depth values, effectively shrinking the lookahead horizon. This is resolved by recursively resetting depth values (`reset_depths`) after every rerooting operation.
 - **(F3) Standard averaging penalty:** UCT typically backpropagates the mean score. This can dilute the value of a high-scoring path if it is surrounded by many poor sibling branches. The solution adopted is Max-MCTS, which backpropagates the maximum value found in a subtree rather than the mean.
 - **(F4) UCB1 constant mismatch:** The standard UCB1 exploration constant `c = √2 ≈ 1.414` is calibrated for binary score domains (e.g., win/loss). For continuous kinematic distance scores (typically small values), this constant causes the exploration term to overwhelm exploitation. The fix involves empirically calibrating `c` to `0.02`, ensuring that the exploitation signal dominates while still allowing for meaningful exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of 3D-ALP primarily focused on its ability to maintain persistent spatial memory in multi-step manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring recall of occluded objects.

**Experiment E3: Multi-Step Coherence**
- **Setup:** A Franka Panda arm in a MuJoCo simulation was tasked with a 5-step sequential reach task. The first three steps involved reaching objects visible from the current camera frame, while steps 4 and 5 required returning to previously visited positions that were no longer directly observable (i.e., memory-required steps). To isolate the architectural contribution of 3D-ALP, all agents were provided with identical geometric scoring (Eq. 4), bypassing visual rendering time. 3D-ALP was configured with a lookahead depth (D) of 2, a branching factor (B) of 4, a 2-second planning budget, and 10 physical actions per step. A greedy reactive baseline sampled the same number of actions per step but executed the one minimizing immediate distance without memory.
- **Results:** The study involved 30 episodes across 3 random seeds. The findings demonstrated a substantial performance disparity between 3D-ALP and the greedy baseline on memory-required steps.
 - On memory-required steps (4–5), 3D-ALP achieved a success rate (SR) of 0.650 ± 0.109. In contrast, the greedy reactive baseline achieved a SR of 0.006 ± 0.008, which is statistically indistinguishable from random chance. This represented an absolute gain (∆) of +0.645 for 3D-ALP.
 - Specifically, on step 5, which involved chained memory of two prior positions, 3D-ALP achieved a SR of 0.822, whereas the greedy baseline achieved 0.000 SR.
 - An ablation study with MCTS D=1 (1-step lookahead) yielded an SR of 0.539 ± 0.064 on memory steps, indicating that the majority of the performance gain (82%, or +0.533 SR) was attributable to the persistent tree search memory alone, even with minimal lookahead.
 - Deeper lookahead (D=2) further improved performance, particularly on the most challenging step 5, increasing its SR from 0.622 (D=1) to 0.822 (+0.200). This suggests that the second depth layer enabled the planner to simulate and chain multiple recall steps.
 - On non-memory required steps (1-3), the greedy baseline achieved a higher SR (0.748 ± 0.029) compared to 3D-ALP D=2 (0.463 ± 0.064). This indicates that while 3D-ALP excels at memory tasks, the greedy policy can be more effective for immediate, visible targets when no memory is required.

**Phase 0–1 Verification**
Prior to the main E3 experiment, foundational components were verified:
- **Phase 0 (Geometric Consistency):** Demonstrated perfect SSIM (1.000) and ORB keypoint matching (1.000, 391/391 keypoints matched) across 5 trajectories, confirming the 3D anchor’s deterministic and path-independent behavior.
- **Phase 1 (Kinematic Bridge):** Confirmed a 0.00° angular error, 0.5 ms latency, and a scoring monotonicity of 1.00 for the kinematic bridge. A critical implementation detail involved correcting a freezing issue with MuJoCo’s `cam_xpos` by using the hand body xpos/xmat with a fixed camera-to-wrist offset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The presented work, 3D-Anchored Lookahead Planning (3D-ALP), addresses a core limitation in current robotic manipulation systems: the absence of persistent spatial memory for multi-step tasks involving object occlusion. By demonstrating that a reactive baseline collapses to near-zero success rates on memory-required steps while 3D-ALP maintains high success, the research quantifies the gap in capabilities and highlights the necessity of architectural changes beyond merely increasing model capacity.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Overcoming Object Permanence Limitation:** The primary significance lies in providing a robust mechanism for robotic agents to recall the 3D spatial locations of objects even when they are no longer visually present. This capability is fundamental for complex, multi-step manipulation sequences in unstructured or partially observed environments.
- **Architectural Innovation:** 3D-ALP's modular design, incorporating a persistent 3D anchor, a world model for imagined rollouts, a hybrid scorer, and a tailored MCTS engine, offers a principled approach to integrating System 2 reasoning into robotic control.
- **Empirical Validation of Memory Importance:** The ablation study clearly decomposes the performance gains, attributing the majority (82%) to persistent tree search memory. This provides empirical evidence that spatial memory is a critical driver for success in tasks involving occlusion, distinguishing its contribution from deeper lookahead planning.
- **Addressing MCTS Challenges in Robotics:** The identification and resolution of four structural failure modes in applying UCT-MCTS to continuous robotic manipulation contribute to the practical deployment of tree search methods in robotics.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Robots equipped with persistent spatial memory could operate more effectively in complex, dynamic environments, completing longer-horizon tasks that require memory and planning beyond immediate visual cues. This could extend to applications in manufacturing, logistics, exploration, and service robotics.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The composable nature of 3D-ALP, with clearly separable interfaces for the MCTS engine, world model oracle, and scorer, provides a flexible framework for future advancements. Researchers can substitute components (e.g., faster world models, more sophisticated scorers) without redesigning the entire planning engine.
- **Multi-robot Coordination:** The concept of shared `c2w` anchors could be extended to multi-robot systems, allowing robots to propagate spatial memory across a fleet. This could enable collaborative spatial memory, where one robot's observation of an occluded object could update another's spatial understanding, potentially via frameworks like Dec-MCTS.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations and outlines a "Phase 2" roadmap for addressing them:
- **VLM Scoring Bottleneck:** A key limitation identified is the inability of current off-the-shelf VLMs (e.g., Florence-2) to provide spatially precise, dense rewards from generative frames, leading to flat reward landscapes for MCTS. The hybrid scorer partially mitigates this by enforcing kinematic proximity. Future work proposes latent-space scoring using Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) for dense gradients and depth-augmented scoring (e.g., with Depth Anything V2) to integrate depth estimation more naturally.
- **Render Bottleneck:** The current world model (InSpatio-WorldFM) exhibits a high render latency (≈2400 ms/frame), which restricts MCTS throughput within typical planning budgets. Future efforts will explore faster world models like LeWorldModel to enable sub-millisecond latent rollouts and improve planning efficiency.
- **Simulation Gap:** All experiments were conducted in MuJoCo simulation. Real-robot validation on physical platforms like the Franka Panda or SO-100 arm is identified as future work to assess the system's performance in real-world scenarios.
