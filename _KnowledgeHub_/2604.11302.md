# 3D-Anchored Lookahead Planning for Persistent Robotic Scene Memory via World-Model-Based MCTS

> [!summary] Summary
> Huawei Technologies' 3D-Anchored Lookahead Planning (3D-ALP) enables robots to maintain persistent 3D spatial memory, facilitating robust multi-step manipulation even when objects are occluded. It demonstrated a success rate of 0.650 ± 0.109 on memory-required tasks in simulation, significantly outperforming reactive baselines.

## Problem

- Reactive robotic policies (e.g., VLA models) inherently lack object permanence and persistent scene memory, causing task failures when target objects become occluded or leave the camera's field of view.
- Current robot architectures process only the immediate camera frame, preventing the retention of spatial information about unseen objects, which is crucial for multi-step tasks requiring recall.
- Directly applying traditional UCT-MCTS to continuous robotic manipulation introduces specific structural failure modes that hinder its effectiveness and reliability in 3D environments.

## Method

- The **3D-ALP** system implements a persistent 3D camera-to-world (c2w) anchor, continuously updated via robot forward kinematics, allowing the robot to retain spatial coordinates of known positions within the ==MCTS== tree.
- It utilizes a 3D-consistent generative world model (InSpatio-WorldFM) as an oracle, enabling the ==MCTS== engine to render predicted future camera frames from arbitrary c2w queries for simulated rollouts.
- A hybrid geometric-semantic scorer combines Vision-Language Model (VLM) semantic evaluations with a kinematic depth penalty, addressing VLM 'depth blindness' and guiding the planner toward kinematically feasible and precise interactions.
- The ==MCTS== engine integrates four structural fixes, such as Max-Q value action selection and recursive depth resetting, specifically designed to overcome inherent challenges in adapting UCT for continuous robotic control.

## Results

- In a 5-step sequential reach task, 3D-ALP achieved a success rate (SR) of 0.650 ± 0.109 on memory-required steps (4-5), representing an absolute gain of +0.645 over a greedy reactive baseline (0.006 ± 0.008 SR).
- On the most challenging step 5, involving chained memory of two prior positions, 3D-ALP achieved a 0.822 SR, while the greedy baseline achieved 0.000 SR.
- An ablation study demonstrated that persistent tree search memory (MCTS D=1) alone accounted for **82%** of the performance gain (+0.533 SR) on memory-required steps, indicating its critical role independent of deeper lookahead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persistent 3D tree search memory alone is the primary contributor to performance gains in memory-dependent robotic tasks involving occlusion, accounting for 82% of the observed success rate improvement.
> - Combining semantic scoring from VLMs with geometric distance penalties is critical for grounding visual predictions in 3D reality, effectively mitigating VLM limitations in depth perception during planning.
> - Specific algorithmic adaptations to UCT-MCTS, such as Max-Q selection and proper depth resetting, are essential for its successful and robust application in continuous action spaces of robotic manipulation.
