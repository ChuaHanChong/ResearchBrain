# Towards Reasoning Era: A Survey of Long Chain-of-Thought for Reasoning Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology and collaborating institutions provide a systematic survey of Long Chain-of-Thought (Long CoT) in Large Language Models, establishing a formal distinction from Short CoT. The survey proposes a novel taxonomy based on deep reasoning, extensive exploration, and feasible reflection, and analyzes key phenomena observed in advanced reasoning models.

## Problem

- A lack of a comprehensive and systematic survey specifically dedicated to Long Chain-of-Thought (Long CoT) in Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Difficulty in unifying understanding of Long CoT's distinctions from Short CoT and addressing fragmented research efforts.
- Unresolved debates regarding phenomena such as "overthinking" (where longer reasoning chains can degrade performance) and "inference-time scaling."

## Method

- The authors formally define ==Long CoT== by relaxing constraints on ==reasoning depth==, ==extensive exploration==, and ==feasible reflection== compared to Short CoT.
- A novel taxonomy is developed to categorize existing Long CoT methods based on their enabling characteristics: deep reasoning, extensive exploration, and feasible reflection.
- Key phenomena associated with Long CoT are systematically analyzed to provide insights into its behavior and limitations.

## Results

- A formal distinction and unified framework for Long CoT, defining it through its core capabilities: deep reasoning, extensive exploration, and feasible reflection.
- Presentation of a comprehensive taxonomy classifying Long CoT methodologies across various aspects, including reasoning formats, learning approaches, reflection mechanisms (feedback and refinement), and exploration strategies.
- Detailed analysis and explanations of key phenomena observed in Long CoT, such as its emergence, reasoning boundaries, "overthinking," and the "Aha Moment."

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Long CoT is defined by three essential and integrated capabilities: deep reasoning, extensive exploration, and feasible reflection.
> - A novel, multi-faceted taxonomy categorizes Long CoT methods, detailing formats, learning, feedback types, refinement techniques, and exploration strategies.
> - Phenomena like "overthinking" (performance decline from excessive reasoning) and the "Aha Moment" (sudden reasoning breakthroughs via RL) are inherent to Long CoT.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed unprecedented growth, leading to the development of highly capable models that can perform a wide array of complex tasks. A significant breakthrough in this domain has been the advent of "Reasoning Large Language Models (RLLMs)," exemplified by models like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1. These models demonstrate impressive abilities in complex areas such as mathematics, coding, and multidisciplinary knowledge reasoning. A central factor underlying their success is the implementation of "Chain-of-Thought (CoT)" reasoning, which allows models to break down complex problems into intermediate, logical steps.

Initially, much research focused on "Short CoT," which typically involves a linear, shallow reasoning process. However, the emergence of more intricate problems has pushed the boundaries, leading to the development of "Long Chain-of-Thought (Long CoT)." This paradigm involves deeper, more extensive, and iterative reasoning processes. Despite the growing adoption and success of Long CoT characteristics in advanced RLLMs, the authors identify a critical gap: a comprehensive and systematic survey specifically dedicated to Long CoT was lacking. This deficiency has limited a unified understanding of Long CoT's distinctions from Short CoT and has complicated ongoing academic and industry debates on phenomena like "overthinking" (where excessive reasoning length can sometimes degrade performance) and "inference-time scaling" (how computational resources during inference impact reasoning depth).

This survey directly addresses this void by providing a foundational framework for understanding Long CoT. It systematically categorizes existing reasoning paradigms, analyzes their core characteristics, and elucidates key observed phenomena. By doing so, the paper consolidates disparate research efforts, offers a structured perspective on the evolution and capabilities of RLLMs, and positions itself as a crucial reference point for future advancements. It moves beyond merely reporting on individual CoT techniques to providing a holistic view of how Long CoT functions, how it is trained, and how it can be evaluated, thereby shaping the research agenda for the next generation of reasoning-capable AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the recognized importance of Long Chain-of-Thought (Long CoT) in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly Reasoning Large Language Models (RLLMs) such as OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1. Despite Long CoT's demonstrated success in tackling complex tasks like mathematical problem-solving and coding, a comprehensive and systematic understanding of its underlying mechanisms, distinctions, and associated phenomena has been notably absent from the literature. This lack of a unified perspective has led to fragmentation in research, complicating debates on critical issues like "overthinking" (where longer reasoning chains can sometimes be detrimental) and the optimal strategies for "inference-time scaling" (how computational resources influence reasoning depth).

To address these challenges and provide a cohesive framework for future research, the authors set forth the following key objectives:

1. **Systematic Distinction and Novel Taxonomy:** To clearly differentiate Long CoT from traditional Short CoT. This involves formalizing their differences and introducing a new taxonomy to categorize existing and emerging reasoning paradigms. The goal is to provide a clear conceptual framework for understanding the evolution and characteristics of these reasoning approaches.
2. **Exploration of Key Characteristics:** To delve into the defining characteristics that enable Long CoT to handle more complex tasks and produce more efficient, coherent outcomes compared to shallower Short CoT. These characteristics are identified as:
- **Deep Reasoning:** The capacity for extensive logical processing across numerous interconnected nodes.
- **Extensive Exploration:** The ability to generate and explore multiple parallel or uncertain logical paths.
- **Feasible Reflection:** The mechanism for revisiting, verifying, and refining previous logical steps based on feedback.
3. **Investigation of Key Phenomena:** To systematically explore and provide insights into notable phenomena observed with Long CoT in practice. This includes understanding the "emergence" of Long CoT capabilities, the manifestation of "overthinking," the principles behind "inference-time scaling" during testing, and the "Aha Moment" (a sudden breakthrough in reasoning).
4. **Identification of Research Gaps and Future Directions:** To pinpoint significant unaddressed challenges and promising avenues for future research. This includes exploring areas such as the integration of multi-modal reasoning, strategies for improving efficiency, and the development of enhanced knowledge frameworks.

By fulfilling these objectives, the survey aims to offer a structured overview of Long CoT, inspire new research directions, and contribute significantly to the continued development and refinement of reasoning large language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology primarily involves a systematic and analytical review of the existing literature on Long Chain-of-Thought (Long CoT) reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors adopt a structured approach to synthesize a vast and rapidly growing body of research, organizing it around a novel conceptual framework.

The core of their methodology revolves around:

1. **Defining and Distinguishing Long CoT:** The authors formalize the concept of Long CoT by drawing clear distinctions from Short CoT. This is achieved by relaxing specific constraints typically associated with Short CoT:
- **Reasoning Depth (Deep Reasoning):** While Short CoT is limited by a small upper boundary (`B_s`) on logical nodes, Long CoT relaxes this constraint to a much larger boundary (`B_l`), allowing for deeper and more intricate logical deductions (`k <= B_l` and `B_s << B_l`).
- **Exploration of Logical Nodes (Extensive Exploration):** Short CoT follows a sequential, linear path (`j=1`). Long CoT, conversely, permits the exploration of multiple parallel and uncertain logical nodes (`exists m, forall i, forall j <= m, n_i -> n_{i+j}`), enabling a broader search for solutions.
- **Revisiting Connections (Feasible Reflection):** Unlike Short CoT's acyclic reasoning (`n_i != n_j` for `i != j`), Long CoT incorporates reflection by allowing the reasoning path to return to a previously visited node (`exists i < j <= k, n_i = n_j`). This involves two sub-components:
 - **Feedback:** Evaluating the correctness and quality of overall or intermediate outputs (`F_i, n_j <- Feedback(CoT_i_L)`).
 - **Refinement:** Adjusting steps or modifying the logical flow based on feedback (`en_{i+1} <- Refine(n_{i+1} | CoT_i_L, F_i, n_j)`).

2. **Developing a Novel Taxonomy:** Based on these three core characteristics (deep reasoning, extensive exploration, and feasible reflection), the authors construct a detailed taxonomy. This taxonomy systematically categorizes existing Long CoT methods.
- **Deep Reasoning** is further broken down by *format* (natural language, structured language, latent space) and *learning* (imitation, self-learning).
- **Feasible Reflection** is categorized by *feedback type* (overall, process, hybrid) and *refinement technique* (prompt-based, SFT-based, RL-based).
- **Extensive Exploration** is analyzed through *scaling strategies* (sequential, parallel) and *exploration mechanisms* (internal via RL/rewards, external via human/model-driven search).

3. **Analyzing Key Phenomena:** The paper delves into observed behaviors associated with Long CoT, such as its emergence, reasoning boundaries, the "overthinking" phenomenon, inference-time scaling, the selection between Process Reward Models (PRM) and Outcome Reward Models (ORM), the "Aha Moment," and Reinforcement Learning Entropy. For each phenomenon, the authors synthesize research explanations and underlying principles.

4. **Surveying Evaluation Methods and Resources:** The survey provides an exhaustive overview of the metrics (e.g., Accuracy, Pass@k), decoding strategies (e.g., Greedy, Beam Search, Major@k), and benchmarks (categorized into Outcome and Process Benchmarks, with advanced evaluations for Agentic, Embodied, and Multimodal Reasoning) used to assess Long CoT capabilities. It also lists open-sourced training frameworks and datasets, highlighting community contributions.

By systematically defining, categorizing, analyzing, and evaluating Long CoT, the authors provide a structured and comprehensive understanding of this complex area, aiming to serve as a foundational guide for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

This survey meticulously synthesizes a vast body of literature on Long Chain-of-Thought (Long CoT), yielding several key findings and presenting a comprehensive overview of the field:

1. **Formal Distinction and Unified Framework for Long CoT:** The paper's primary contribution is formally distinguishing Long CoT from Short CoT. It defines Long CoT through three essential and integrated capabilities: **deep reasoning**, **extensive exploration**, and **feasible reflection**. This unification of capabilities contrasts with the independent development observed in earlier Short CoT approaches. This conceptual clarity provides a crucial framework for understanding and categorizing subsequent research.

2. **Comprehensive Taxonomy of Long CoT Methodologies:** The survey introduces a novel, multi-faceted taxonomy that organizes existing Long CoT methods based on these three core characteristics:
- **Deep Reasoning for Long CoT:**
 - **Formation:** Methods employ diverse formats for reasoning, including natural language (e.g., CoT, MathPrompter), structured language (e.g., Program-of-Thought, Chain-of-Code), and latent space reasoning (e.g., planning tokens, thought vectors, recurrent blocks).
 - **Learning:** Deep reasoning capabilities are enhanced through imitation (learning from human-annotated data, advanced RLLMs, or scaling-augmented RLLMs like MCTS) and self-learning (using direct sampling with implicit rewards or tree search-guided preference learning). Performance tables demonstrate that models leveraging these techniques achieve high scores across benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH.
- **Feasible Reflection for Long CoT:**
 - **Feedback:** Feedback mechanisms are crucial for error detection and quality assessment. This includes overall feedback (Outcome Reward Models, rule extraction, or RLLMs acting as judges), process-level feedback (Process Reward Models trained with human or outcome annotations, or RLLMs generating step-by-step critiques), and hybrid approaches combining both.
 - **Refinement:** Techniques for correcting errors based on feedback are categorized into prompt-based generation (iterative self-correction via prompting), SFT-based imitation (learning error correction from advanced models), and RL-based learning (training models to self-refine using reinforcement signals, often triggering "Aha moments").
- **Extensive Exploration for Long CoT:**
 - **Exploration Scaling:** This involves extending reasoning paths, categorized into sequential scaling (increasing length within a single generation) and parallel scaling (generating multiple paths and verifying results). The paper notes that while scaling improves performance, there are limitations, such as "overthinking" from excessive sequential scaling and an upper bound on performance for parallel scaling (e.g., not surpassing Best-of-N).
 - **Internal Exploration:** This focuses on training models to intrinsically generate diverse exploration paths, primarily through advanced Reinforcement Learning (RL) strategies (e.g., reward-free RL like OREO, or reward-based RL like PPO, GRPO) and tailored reward strategies (rule-rewarded based on correctness, format, or length, and model-rewarded using ORMs or PRMs).
 - **External Exploration:** This involves leveraging external systems or structured search algorithms, categorized into human-driven exploration (fixed pipelines, tree/graph-structured search prompted by humans) and model-driven exploration (dynamic search structures like Beam Search, A* Search, or Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) guided by model feedback).

3. **Analysis of Key Phenomena:** The survey provides detailed explanations for phenomena observed in Long CoT:
- **Emergence:** Long CoT capabilities emerge through contextual examples and can be activated in pre-trained models via decoding modifications or specific prompts.
- **Reasoning Boundary:** RLLMs have inherent limits to their reasoning capacity, and exceeding this boundary leads to performance decline ("overthinking").
- **Inference-Time Scaling:** While direct scaling improves performance, there are diminishing returns and limitations related to error accumulation and inherent model constraints.
- **PRM & ORM Selection:** The debate between process (step-level) and outcome (final answer) supervision in RL is analyzed, with findings suggesting that outcome-level rewards can be sufficient.
- **"Aha Moment":** This phenomenon, often triggered by RL with rule-based rewards, represents a sudden self-reflection and breakthrough in reasoning.
- **Reinforcement Learning Entropy:** Policy entropy influences exploration, and its collapse during early training can limit reasoning improvement, necessitating control mechanisms.

4. **Comprehensive Evaluation Landscape:** The paper provides a thorough review of evaluation metrics (Accuracy, Pass@k, Cons@k, EM, Rank, Best-of-N), decoding strategies, and a wide array of benchmarks across various domains (mathematics, coding, commonsense, science, medicine, agentic/embodied, multimodal), highlighting the diverse challenges and assessment methods in the field.

5. **Identification of Future Frontiers:** The survey concludes by outlining six critical future directions: Multimodal, Multilingual, Agentic & Embodied, Efficient, Knowledge-Augmented, and Safety-focused Long CoT, each with its own challenges and promising research avenues.

In essence, the paper's main findings underscore that Long CoT is a sophisticated, integrated reasoning system built upon iterative cycles of deep processing, diverse exploration, and adaptive self-correction. Its effectiveness is tied to managing these components efficiently and robustly, with ongoing challenges in extending these capabilities to new modalities, languages, and real-world scenarios while ensuring safety and efficiency.
