# LLM-as-a-Verifier: A General-Purpose Verification Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> A general-purpose verification framework leverages large language models to provide fine-grained, continuous feedback for evaluating agentic solutions, leading to performance gains across coding, robotics, and medical benchmarks. The approach also enhances reinforcement learning efficiency by providing dense reward signals.

## Problem

- Existing LLM judges often provide coarse discrete scores, resulting in high tie rates and poor discrimination between complex solutions.
- Learned reward models have limited generalizability to new tasks or domains and require extensive training data.
- Verification of LLM-generated solutions has been an underexplored scaling axis compared to generation capabilities, lacking a systematic approach for quality improvement.

## Method

- A probabilistic **LLM-as-a-Verifier** framework is introduced, extracting ==continuous reward signals== by leveraging the full distribution of scoring token logits, rather than discrete scores.
- Verification quality is scaled along three dimensions: increased ==score granularity==, ==repeated evaluations== to reduce variance, and ==criteria decomposition== for complex tasks.
- A **Probabilistic Pivot Tournament (PPT)** is developed as a cost-efficient O(Nk)O(Nk)O(Nk) algorithm for ranking candidate solutions from a pool, avoiding O(N2)O(N^2)O(N2) comparisons.

## Results

- Achieved improved accuracy on Terminal-Bench V2 (**86.5%**), SWE-Bench Verified (**78.2%**), and MedAgentBench (**73.3%**), surpassing existing baselines and large foundation models.
- Demonstrated **87.4%** trajectory preference accuracy on RoboRewardBench, outperforming trained robotics reward models and reducing Mean Absolute Error with human annotations from **1.11** to **0.72**.
- Improved sample efficiency in RL by approximately **1.8x** for off-policy tasks and **1.1x** for on-policy tasks by incorporating dense verifier rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verification quality can be systematically improved by scaling score granularity, repeated evaluation, and criteria decomposition, leading to more calibrated and discriminative assessments.
> - Fine-grained verifier scores serve as an effective proxy for task progress in agentic systems, correlating strongly with chronological advancement in both coding and robotics tasks.
> - Continuous feedback from the verifier can be directly applied as dense reward signals in reinforcement learning, significantly improving sample efficiency for both off-policy and on-policy algorithms without requiring environment-specific reward shaping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has been largely driven by scaling paradigms in pre-training data and compute, post-training optimization, and test-time inference. While these approaches have significantly advanced LLM generation capabilities, the paper identifies "verification"—the process of determining the correctness or quality of a solution—as a relatively underexplored scaling axis.

Existing approaches to verification typically involve two main categories:
1. **Standard LLM Judges:** These models are prompted to assign discrete scores (e.g., on a 1-5 scale) to candidate solutions. A limitation of this approach is that it often collapses complex scoring distributions into coarse discrete values, leading to high tie rates and reduced discriminative power, especially when comparing nuanced solutions.
2. **Learned Reward Models:** These models are explicitly trained on datasets to provide reward signals. While effective in specific domains, their performance is constrained by the availability and quality of training data, often limiting their generalization to new tasks or domains without further fine-tuning.

This work positions itself to address the limitations of both categories by proposing "verification scaling" as a distinct method for improving LLM performance. It introduces a general-purpose framework that aims to provide fine-grained feedback without requiring additional training, thereby offering a more versatile and scalable solution than learned reward models and a more discriminative one than discrete LLM judges. The framework is designed to be applicable across multiple modalities (text, image, video) and various agentic tasks, including coding, robotics, and medical applications.

In the context of related work, the paper notes that its contribution enhances existing test-time scaling methods (like best-of-N selection) by providing a more reliable selector. It also differentiates itself from other LLM-as-a-judge methodologies by focusing on long-horizon agent trajectories and systematically investigating how verification quality scales with specific parameters, rather than solely evaluating isolated natural language responses. Furthermore, it contributes to the field of verifiable reward models by offering a training-free framework that leverages intrinsic model capabilities for dense feedback, a contrast to many methods that train separate reward models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish verification as a new, scalable dimension for enhancing the capabilities of large language models (LLMs). The authors motivate this by observing a disparity: while LLM generation has benefited significantly from various scaling paradigms, the verification process—evaluating the correctness or quality of generated solutions—has not experienced comparable advancements.

Specifically, the core motivations and objectives are:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Verification Methods:** The paper highlights two main shortcomings in existing verification practices:
 - **Coarse Scoring of LLM Judges:** Standard LLM judges typically output discrete scores (e.g., 1-5), which can lead to frequent ties among complex solutions and inadequate discrimination between correct and incorrect outputs. This is exemplified by the observation that discrete judges yielded a 27% tie rate on Terminal-Bench, hindering the ability to reliably identify optimal trajectories even when one exists.
 - **Limited Generalizability of Learned Reward Models:** Reward models trained on specific datasets are often unable to generalize effectively to new domains or tasks, necessitating continuous data collection and retraining. The goal is to provide a general-purpose, training-free alternative.
- **Unlocking Verification Scaling:** The central hypothesis is that verification quality can be systematically improved by scaling along several dimensions. The research aims to demonstrate the effectiveness of three specific scaling axes:
 - **Score Granularity:** To move beyond discrete scores and enable finer distinctions between solutions, which is hypothesized to reduce tie rates and improve the signal-to-noise ratio in evaluations.
 - **Repeated Evaluation:** To reduce variance in individual evaluations and average out potential biases or noise, thereby increasing the reliability of verification.
 - **Criteria Decomposition:** To address the complexity of monolithic evaluation criteria in long-horizon tasks by breaking them into simpler, more manageable sub-criteria, thus reducing prompt bias and capturing a more comprehensive view of solution quality.
- **Developing a Cost-Efficient Ranking Mechanism:** Recognizing that scaling verification can increase computational cost, a key objective is to introduce a practical, budget-efficient algorithm for selecting the best solution from a pool of candidates. This is crucial for real-world application, where evaluating all possible pairwise comparisons (an $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ problem) might be prohibitive.
- **Extending Applications of Fine-Grained Feedback:** Beyond merely selecting the best solution, the research aims to demonstrate that the fine-grained signals produced by the verifier can serve additional valuable purposes:
 - **Estimating Task Progress:** To provide a continuous scalar proxy for how far an agent has progressed through a task, enabling monitoring and potentially early intervention in agentic systems.
 - **Dense Reward for Reinforcement Learning (RL):** To address the credit assignment problem in RL by offering a rich, continuous reward signal that can improve the sample efficiency of both off-policy and on-policy algorithms, without requiring environment-specific reward shaping or reward model training.

The overarching motivation is to transform verification from a limiting factor into a scalable asset, thereby enabling LLMs to more effectively tackle complex, long-horizon agentic tasks across diverse domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, "LLM-as-a-Verifier," is a probabilistic verification framework designed to provide fine-grained feedback without requiring additional training.

**Fine-Grained Reward Estimation:**
Unlike traditional LLM judges that output a single discrete score, LLM-as-a-Verifier estimates the quality of candidate solutions by leveraging the full distribution of scoring token logits.
1. **Scoring Prompts:** Given a task prompt ($x$), a language model ($p_\theta$), a criterion ($c$), and two candidate trajectories ($\tau_i$, $\tau_j$), prompts are constructed to elicit conditional distributions $p_\theta(v | x, c, \tau)$ for an ordered set of score tokens $V_{score} = \{v_1, \ldots, v_G\}$. The prompt structure includes explicit tags like `<score_A>` and `<score_B>` to guide the LLM to output a score within a specified range (e.g., 1-20). Log-probabilities from these scoring tokens are extracted.
2. **Continuous Reward Calculation (Eq. 3.1):** The reward for a trajectory is computed as the expectation over the distribution of scoring token logits, averaged across multiple evaluation criteria ($C$) and repeated verifications ($K$):
 $R(x, \tau) = \frac{1}{CK} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{g=1}^{G} p_\theta(v_g | x, c, \tau) \phi(v_g)$, where $\phi(v_g)$ maps each score token to a scalar value.
3. **Pairwise Preference:** The continuous rewards $R(x, \tau)$ are normalized to a range and converted into pairwise preference probabilities using the Bradley-Terry model (Eq. 3.2):
 $P(\tau_i \succ \tau_j | x) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-(R(x, \tau_i) - R(x, \tau_j)))} $.

**Verification Scaling Axes:** The framework systematically explores three dimensions for scaling verification:
1. **Score Granularity ($G$):** Instead of a coarse discrete scale (e.g., 1-5), the method uses a broader range of score tokens (e.g., 1-20) and computes a continuous score by taking the expectation over the logit distribution of these tokens. This is posited to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and better differentiate between similar-quality solutions.
2. **Repeated Evaluation ($K$):** Multiple independent evaluations are performed for each trajectory, and their scores are averaged. This Monte Carlo estimation aims to reduce the variance of the reward estimate, mitigating noise or spurious features from individual verification passes.
3. **Criteria Decomposition ($C$):** For complex, long-horizon tasks, a single monolithic evaluation criterion is replaced by an ensemble of simpler sub-criteria. For code agents, this includes "Specification," "Output," and "Errors." Averaging scores across these criteria is intended to reduce prompt bias and provide a more comprehensive assessment.

**Cost-Efficient Ranking: Probabilistic Pivot Tournament (PPT):**
To address the $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ cost of a full round-robin tournament for $N$ candidate trajectories, the paper introduces PPT, a five-stage pipeline:
1. **Candidates:** A pool of $N$ trajectories is generated.
2. **Ring Pass:** A random Hamiltonian cycle compares $N$ adjacent pairs. Each candidate appears once in the 'A' slot and once in the 'B' slot, effectively canceling potential positional biases of the LLM verifier. Initial preferences ($w_i/c_i$) are aggregated.
3. **Pivot Selection:** The top-$k$ candidates from the ring pass (based on their mean preference scores) are selected as a set of "pivots" ($\mathcal{P}$). This concentrates the remaining verification budget on the most promising candidates.
4. **Pivot Tournament:** All non-pivot candidates are compared against every pivot, and all pivot-vs-pivot pairs are also compared. This reduces the total number of comparisons to $\mathcal{O}(Nk)$ for $k \ll N$.
5. **Selection:** All comparisons are aggregated into win masses ($w_i$) and counts ($c_i$), and the candidate with the highest normalized score ($w_i/c_i$) is chosen.

**Applications:**
The framework is applied in several contexts:
- **Test-Time Scaling:** LLM-as-a-Verifier, in conjunction with PPT, is used to select the optimal trajectory from a generated pool, improving the performance of agents on benchmarks.
- **Progress Tracking:** The fine-grained verifier scores are used as a proxy for task progress. The Value-Order Correlation (VOC) metric (Spearman rank correlation between chronological step and verifier score) quantifies this. Extensions for tools like Claude Code and Codex are developed to visualize this progress.
- **Dense Reward for Reinforcement Learning:** The continuous verifier scores are utilized as dense reward signals to improve RL sample efficiency.
 - For **off-policy RL (DSRL-SAC)**, a shaped reward $r_t = r_{env,t} + \lambda \rho_t$ is used, where $\rho_t$ is the verifier's progress curve for trajectory prefixes.
 - For **on-policy RL (GRPO)**, a reasoning reward $r_{reasoning,i}$ derived from the probabilistic pivot tournament is added to the standard correctness and format rewards ($r_i = r_{correct,i} + r_{format,i} + \beta r_{reasoning,i}$).

The research primarily uses Gemini 2.5 Flash as the verifier model due to its log-probability access, with Qwen 3.6 35B for vision-language tasks. A two-stage workaround is also described for models that do not expose token-level log-probabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several findings demonstrating the effectiveness and broad applicability of the LLM-as-a-Verifier framework across various dimensions and benchmarks.

**Verification Scaling:**
- **Score Granularity ($G$):** Increasing granularity from $G=1$ to $G=20$ consistently improved verification accuracy on Terminal-Bench V2 from 73.1% to 77.5% (Figure 4). This improvement is attributed to an increased Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR), which rose from 0.775 to 0.799, indicating better calibration and separation between correct and incorrect solutions (Table 1). A case study on "query-optimize" showed that a continuous verifier with $G=5$ eliminated ties (0/100) that a discrete judge produced (88/100), and further scaling to $G=20$ increased correct rankings from 69/100 to 77/100 (Table 2).
- **Repeated Evaluation ($K$):** Verification accuracy on Terminal-Bench V2 improved from 74.7% at $K=1$ to 77.5% at $K=16$ (Figure 4). The continuous verifier at $K=1$ already matched a discrete judge at $K=16$ in accuracy while yielding zero ties, highlighting the strength of fine-grained scoring (Figure 7).
- **Criteria Decomposition ($C$):** Ensembling three sub-criteria (Specification, Output, Errors) resulted in 78.3% accuracy, surpassing the 75.2%-76.4% achieved by any single criterion alone (Figure 4).

**Cost-Efficient Ranking (Probabilistic Pivot Tournament - PPT):**
- PPT effectively reduced the computational budget for ranking candidates from $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(Nk)$ (Table 9). With $k=3$ pivots, PPT achieved 66.17% accuracy using 4,723 queried pairs, outperforming the V1 baseline, which achieved 65.85% with 7,000 pairs. Accuracy consistently increased with more pivots, reaching 67.13% with $k=9$ (9,630 pairs).

**State-of-the-Art Performance in Test-Time Scaling:**
LLM-as-a-Verifier demonstrated improved performance across diverse agentic benchmarks:
- **Terminal-Bench V2:** Achieved 86.5% accuracy, surpassing the Pass@1 of 83.1% and the leading baseline GPT-5.5 (84.7%) (Table 3, Figure 1). The gains generalized across different agent harnesses (Table 8).
- **SWE-Bench Verified:** Attained 78.2% accuracy, outperforming the mean Pass@1 of 76.1% and individual models like Claude Opus 4.5 (76.8%) (Table 3, Figure 1).
- **RoboRewardBench:** Achieved 87.4% trajectory preference accuracy (Table 4), exceeding a discrete LLM-as-a-Judge baseline (70.8%) and trained robotics reward models such as RoboReward-8B (81.4%), Robometer-4B (78.8%), and TOPReward (74.7%). It also reduced the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) with human annotations from 1.11 to 0.72 (Table 5).
- **MedAgentBench:** Secured 73.3% accuracy, surpassing the Pass@1 of 70.2% and leading baselines (Table 3, Figure 1).

**Fine-Grained Verifier Signals as a Proxy for Task Progress:**
- The verifier scores correlated with chronological task progression, quantified by Value-Order Correlation (VOC). On Terminal-Bench V2, successful trajectories exhibited a Spearman VOC of 0.848, significantly higher than failed trajectories at 0.769 (Table 6), indicating its ability to track progress and identify stalls.
- In robotics, LLM-as-a-Verifier achieved a VOC of 0.966 on RoboRewardBench, substantially outperforming existing robotics reward models (e.g., RoboReward-8B at 0.877, TOPReward at 0.565) (Table 7).

**Dense Reward for Reinforcement Learning:**
- **Off-policy RL (LIBERO with DSRL-SAC):** Utilizing dense progress rewards from LLM-as-a-Verifier improved sample efficiency by approximately $1.8 \times$ and achieved a higher final success rate (0.76 vs. 0.69) compared to sparse reward baselines (Figure 9, left).
- **On-policy RL (MATH with GRPO):** Incorporating reasoning rewards from the verifier led to an approximate $1.1 \times$ improvement in sample efficiency for fine-tuning Qwen3-8B (Figure 9, right).

**Logit-Restricted Frontier Models:**
- A two-stage workaround for models without logit access (e.g., GPT-5.5) successfully recovered calibrated reward signals. This approach (GPT-5.5 reasoning forwarded to Gemini 2.5 Flash for continuous scoring) outperformed direct use of GPT-5.5's discrete scores, achieving 80.1% accuracy at $K=1$ and eliminating ties, compared to GPT-5.5's 74.9% accuracy and 10.9% tie rate (Table 12).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work establishes verification as a distinct and scalable axis for improving the capabilities of large language models, analogous to established paradigms like pre-training, post-training, and test-time compute. This conceptual framing introduces a new avenue for enhancing LLM performance.

The proposed LLM-as-a-Verifier framework offers a general-purpose, training-free approach to obtaining fine-grained feedback, which has several potential impacts:

- **Improved Evaluation Reliability and Granularity:** By moving from discrete to continuous scoring based on token logits and scaling across granularity, repeated evaluations, and criteria decomposition, the framework provides a more calibrated and discriminative assessment of solutions. This directly addresses the limitations of conventional LLM judges, which often suffer from high tie rates and coarse evaluations, leading to more trustworthy and precise performance metrics for agentic systems.
- **Enhanced Agent Performance in Diverse Domains:** The framework's ability to achieve state-of-the-art results across challenging benchmarks in coding, robotics, and medical fields demonstrates its practical utility. This suggests that even without fundamental improvements to an agent's generation capabilities, better verification can effectively select superior solutions from a candidate pool, leading to overall performance gains. This generalizability, without requiring domain-specific fine-tuning, indicates broad applicability.
- **Facilitating Real-time Agent Monitoring and Safety:** The finding that fine-grained verifier signals correlate strongly with task progress provides a mechanism for real-time monitoring of autonomous agents. This capability can enable developers to observe an agent's progress, detect stagnation or failure modes early, and potentially intervene. Such monitoring is particularly critical for high-stakes applications like medical diagnostics or complex system operations, where safety and reliability are paramount.
- **Boosting Reinforcement Learning Efficiency:** The integration of LLM-as-a-Verifier as a dense reward signal for reinforcement learning addresses a long-standing challenge of sparse rewards and credit assignment. By providing continuous, informative feedback, the framework significantly improves the sample efficiency of both off-policy and on-policy RL algorithms. This could accelerate the development and training of agents for complex tasks in robotics and reasoning, potentially reducing the extensive computational resources traditionally required.
- **Broadening Accessibility and Practicality:** The "training-free and plug-and-play" nature of LLM-as-a-Verifier, along with extensions for popular coding platforms like Claude Code and Codex, enhances its accessibility for developers and researchers. This allows existing agentic systems to immediately benefit from improved verification without complex integration efforts or large-scale data collection for reward model training.
- **Opening New Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the need for logit access in frontier models (partially addressed by the two-stage workaround), and opportunities for adaptive compute allocation or learned criteria decomposition, suggest fertile ground for future research in verification and agent intelligence. The potential for extending dense rewards to multi-turn RL further highlights its foundational impact.
