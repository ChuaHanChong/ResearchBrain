# Traversing Narrow Paths: A Two-Stage Reinforcement Learning Framework for Robust and Safe Humanoid Walking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Science and Technology of China developed a two-stage reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to robustly and safely traverse narrow paths by combining a physics-based foothold planner with learned low-level tracking and high-level modification policies. The framework achieved a 100% success rate on 0.2m wide beams on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, outperforming a state-of-the-art baseline.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust and safe locomotion on narrow paths due to limited footholds and high sensitivity to perception inaccuracies or control delays.
- Traditional model-based control methods are susceptible to modeling discrepancies and contact uncertainties, leading to imprecise foot placement on constrained terrains.
- Purely end-to-end reinforcement learning methods for locomotion can overfit simulator assumptions, engage in unsafe exploration, and often lack interpretability in safety-critical applications.

## Method

- A two-stage reinforcement learning framework is proposed, integrating a 3D-LIPM foothold planner, a high-level foothold modifier, and a low-level foothold tracker, all utilizing minimal sensing (proprioception and a compact anterior terrain height map).
- Stage-I trains a robust low-level foothold tracker on flat ground, intentionally perturbing target footholds to enhance its ability to adapt to small variations.
- ==Stage-II== trains a high-level foothold modifier to refine the physics-based foothold targets on narrow paths, prioritizing safety and precision using terrain perception and specific reward terms for 'foothold safety' and 'beam balance'.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **100%** success rate in simulation across varying narrow beam widths and lengths, significantly outperforming a 'No-Modifier' baseline (**15%** success) and an 'RL-Only' baseline (**0%** success).
- On a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, the framework achieved a **100%** success rate and **100%** traversal rate on a 0.2m wide, 3m long beam over 20 trials, surpassing the BeamDojo baseline (**80%** success, **88.16%** traversal rate).
- An ablation study demonstrated that removing intentional disturbances during Stage-I training reduced success to **50%** and increased centerline deviation, confirming the importance of this robustness-building step.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining an interpretable physics-based planner with learned residual adjustments (a hybrid approach) is more effective and safer for narrow path traversal than purely model-based or end-to-end RL methods.
> - Introducing intentional target perturbations during low-level training (Stage-I) is crucial for building a robust foothold tracker capable of handling the dynamic, modified targets generated by the high-level policy on challenging terrains.
> - Minimal exteroceptive sensing (a compact anterior terrain height map) can be effectively integrated into a high-level modifier to provide sufficient information for precise and safe foot placement without overly complex vision pipelines, aiding sim-to-real transfer.
