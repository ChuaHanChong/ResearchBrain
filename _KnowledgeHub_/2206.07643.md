# Coarse-to-Fine Vision-Language Pre-training with Fusion in the Backbone

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research, UCLA, and NYU collaborated on FIBER, a vision-language model that unifies high-level image understanding and fine-grained region-level tasks. It leverages a fusion-in-the-backbone architecture and a two-stage coarse-to-fine pre-training strategy, achieving state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks with improved efficiency and reduced reliance on costly annotations.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language models specialize in either high-level image understanding or fine-grained region-level tasks, lacking a unified framework capable of handling both seamlessly.
- Traditional end-to-end vision-language pre-training models struggle to extend effectively to bounding box prediction and other region-level tasks.
- High computational and data annotation costs are associated with fine-grained vision-language pre-training, particularly for tasks requiring high-resolution inputs and expensive bounding box annotations.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Fusion-In-the-Backbone (FIBER)== architecture, which integrates ==cross-attention modules deeply into vision and language backbones== with a ==learnable gating mechanism== for efficient multimodal fusion.
- Employs a ==two-stage coarse-to-fine pre-training strategy==, starting with large-scale image-text paired data for general understanding and then specializing with a smaller amount of image-text-box data for region-level tasks.
- Integrates an object detection head directly on top of the language-aware image features produced by the fused vision backbone, ensuring deep interaction for precise localization tasks.

## Results

- Achieves new state-of-the-art on VQAv2 (**78.0%**), outperforming models like BLIP (**77.8%**) and SimVLM (**77.6%**) that were trained on significantly larger datasets.
- Surpasses larger models such as GLIP-L on phrase grounding (**8**7.4%** R@1** on Flickr30k Entities) and LVIS rare object detection, utilizing **25x** less fine-grained pre-training data.
- Attains state-of-the-art CIDEr scores for base-size models on COCO image captioning and demonstrates **1.5x** faster training for object detection compared to GLIP while maintaining comparable performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep multimodal fusion directly within vision and language backbones, rather than in separate layers, enhances interaction efficiency and overall model performance across diverse tasks.
> - A phased coarse-to-fine pre-training strategy can significantly reduce reliance on expensive, fine-grained annotations while still achieving superior results on region-level tasks.
> - A single, unified architecture is capable of effectively bridging the gap between high-level image understanding (e.g., VQA, captioning) and fine-grained object localization tasks (e.g., object detection, phrase grounding).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP), which has seen tremendous growth, drawing inspiration from the successes of large-scale language model pre-training. Historically, VLP models aimed to connect visual and textual information to address tasks like visual question answering (VQA), image captioning, and image-text retrieval. These tasks primarily test "high-level understanding" of images, often relying on global image features and lower-resolution inputs. Early VLP models like ViLBERT, LXMERT, and UNITER often relied on pre-trained object detectors to extract region features offline, thereby limiting their end-to-end nature. More recently, end-to-end VLP models such as ALBEF, METER, SimVLM, and BLIP emerged, using vision transformers or convolutional networks as image backbones to directly process raw image pixels, achieving state-of-the-art performance on image-level understanding tasks.

Concurrently, a separate but related line of research focused on "region-level understanding" tasks, such as phrase grounding, referring expression comprehension, and object detection (OD). Models like MDETR and GLIP reformulated traditional vision-only tasks like OD as vision-language problems, enabling the leverage of textual descriptions for fine-grained object localization. These tasks typically require high-resolution image inputs and precise bounding box predictions.

The critical challenge in the VLP landscape, which FIBER aims to address, is the **lack of a unified framework capable of seamlessly handling both high-level image understanding and fine-grained region-level understanding tasks**. Existing end-to-end VLP models excel at image-level tasks but struggle to extend effectively to bounding box prediction. Conversely, region-focused models like MDETR and GLIP, while proficient at bounding box tasks, have not demonstrated strong support for traditional VLP tasks like captioning or retrieval. This specialization leads to a fragmented ecosystem where different models are required for different types of multimodal problems. FIBER introduces a unified model and a two-stage pre-training strategy to bridge this gap, aiming to provide a single architecture that can achieve state-of-the-art performance across the entire spectrum of vision-language tasks, from VQA to object detection, with improved efficiency and reduced data requirements. This is clearly highlighted in Table 1 of the paper, which positions FIBER as the only model capable of supporting all considered VL tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of FIBER are articulated clearly in the paper's abstract and introduction:

1. **Develop a Unified Vision-Language Model Architecture:** The core objective is to create a single, end-to-end VLP model architecture that can effectively tackle the full spectrum of vision-language tasks. This includes tasks requiring high-level, image-wide understanding (e.g., VQA, image captioning, image-text retrieval) and those demanding fine-grained, region-level comprehension and localization (e.g., phrase grounding, referring expression comprehension, object detection). The motivation stems from the observation that most existing models specialize in one type of task, leading to a fragmented and less efficient research and application landscape.

2. **Enhance Multimodal Fusion Efficiency and Performance:** A key architectural objective is to improve how vision and language modalities interact. Unlike previous approaches that typically append dedicated fusion layers on top of separate uni-modal backbones, FIBER aims to integrate multimodal fusion "deep into the model." The motivation here is to achieve more profound and earlier interaction between modalities, which the authors hypothesize will lead to better performance and more memory-efficient models. This deep fusion, through cross-attention inserted directly into the backbones, is designed to make the visual features "language-aware" and vice-versa throughout the encoding process.

3. **Propose an Efficient Two-Stage Pre-training Strategy:** The authors are motivated to address the high computational and data annotation costs associated with fine-grained VLP tasks, particularly those requiring high-resolution inputs and expensive bounding box annotations. Their objective is to introduce a coarse-to-fine pre-training pipeline that leverages readily available, large-scale image-text data (coarse-grained stage) to provide a strong foundation, which then efficiently initializes the model for fine-grained tasks using a comparatively smaller amount of image-text-box data (fine-grained stage). This strategy aims to reduce the reliance on vast amounts of costly box-level annotations, making pre-training more scalable and economical.

In essence, FIBER seeks to answer the challenging question: "Can we have a unified framework for efficient VL pre-training that benefits both image-level and region-level VL tasks?" The motivation is to overcome the limitations of specialized models, reduce training costs, and achieve superior, consistent performance across a diverse range of multimodal benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FIBER's methodology is characterized by two core innovations: a novel model architecture for deep multimodal fusion and a two-stage coarse-to-fine pre-training strategy.

#### 3.1. Fusion-In-the-Backbone (FIBER) Architecture

The FIBER architecture (Figure 2 in the paper) is built upon standard vision and language backbones but radically alters the multimodal fusion mechanism.
- **Deep Multimodal Fusion:** Instead of placing a few dedicated transformer layers for fusion *after* the uni-modal image and text encoders (as is common in many previous VLP models like METER and GLIP), FIBER inserts cross-attention modules *directly into* the image and text backbones. This allows for earlier and more profound interaction between the visual and linguistic representations.
- **Gating Mechanism:** Each inserted cross-attention layer is equipped with a learnable gating parameter, $\alpha$. This parameter is initialized to 0, allowing the model to start training with minimal cross-modal interference and gradually learn the optimal level of interaction. This mechanism also enables the model to easily switch between a dual-encoder setting (cross-attention off, $\alpha=0$) for fast tasks like retrieval and a fusion-encoder setting (cross-attention on, $\alpha \neq 0$) for tasks requiring deep interaction.
- **Backbones:** FIBER adopts a **Swin Transformer** (Swin-Base) as its vision backbone and **RoBERTa** (RoBERTa-Base) as its language backbone. The Swin Transformer is crucial for handling high-resolution image inputs efficiently due to its hierarchical design and linear computational complexity with respect to image size, addressing a limitation of vanilla transformers in vision tasks. Cross-attention blocks are specifically inserted into the top 6 layers of both the vision and text encoders.
- **Object Detection Head:** For region-level tasks, an object detection head (e.g., Dynamic Head combined with an FPN) is added on top of the text-aware image features extracted by the Swin backbone. This integration means that the image features fed to the FPN are *already language-aware* due to the deep fusion in the backbone, a key distinction from models like GLIP where fusion occurs within the OD head itself. This design choice contributes to FIBER's efficiency and performance on detection tasks.

#### 3.2. Coarse-to-Fine Two-Stage Pre-training

FIBER employs a two-stage pre-training paradigm designed to efficiently leverage different types of data and address diverse task characteristics.

- **Stage 1: Coarse-grained Pre-training:**
 - **Data:** Utilizes large-scale, readily available image-text paired data, including COCO, Conceptual Captions, SBU Captions, and Visual Genome (approximately 4 million images in total). These datasets do not require bounding box annotations.
 - **Input Resolution:** Low (384x384 pixels) to enable larger batch sizes and faster training.
 - **Objectives (Fusion Encoder & Dual Encoder):**
 - **Image-Text Contrastive (ITC) Loss:** The cross-attention modules are switched off, and FIBER functions as a dual encoder. It maximizes similarity for positive image-text pairs and minimizes for negative pairs, learning initial unimodal representations and basic cross-modal alignment.
 - **Masked Language Modeling (MLM) Loss:** The cross-attention modules are switched on, and FIBER acts as a fusion encoder. Randomly masked text tokens are predicted based on the image and unmasked text, fostering multimodal contextual understanding.
 - **Image-Text Matching (ITM) Loss:** Also using the fusion encoder, the model predicts whether an image-text pair is matched. Crucially, it samples global hard negatives based on similarities computed during ITC, enhancing the model's ability to distinguish relevant from irrelevant pairs.
 - **Purpose:** This stage provides a robust, general-purpose vision-language understanding foundation, initializing all shared parameters effectively.

- **Stage 2: Fine-grained Pre-training:**
 - **Initialization:** The model from the coarse-grained stage is used for initialization, with only the object detection head parameters randomly initialized.
 - **Data:** Employs image-text-box data, curated from sources like MDETR and Objects365 (approx. 0.8 million images). This data includes explicit bounding box annotations, which are more expensive to acquire.
 - **Input Resolution:** High (800x1333 pixels) to support precise region-level localization.
 - **Objectives (Fusion Encoder + OD Head):**
 - **Bounding Box Localization Loss:** Supervises the prediction of precise bounding boxes by the OD head.
 - **Word-Region Alignment Loss:** The classification head of the object detector is replaced by a grounding score, computed as the dot product between text-aware region features (from the OD head) and image-aware contextualized text features (from the RoBERTa backbone). This loss teaches the model to align specific phrases with image regions.
 - **Purpose:** This stage specializes the model for region-level tasks, leveraging the strong general understanding learned in Stage 1 to reduce the dependency on vast amounts of fine-grained data.

#### 3.3. Adaptation to Downstream Tasks

FIBER's flexible architecture allows it to be adapted to various tasks:
- **VQA/VL Classification:** Uses the fusion encoder, concatenating final multimodal representations for classification.
- **Image-Text Retrieval:** Utilizes the dual encoder for efficient retrieval or the fusion encoder for re-ranking.
- **Image Captioning:** Adapted as a sequence-to-sequence model, with image-to-text cross-attentions and causal masks for autoregressive generation.
- **Phrase Grounding, Object Detection, Referring Expression Comprehension:** Directly uses the fine-grained pre-trained fusion encoder with the OD head to predict bounding boxes corresponding to text queries.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FIBER demonstrates consistent and substantial performance improvements across a wide array of vision-language tasks, validating its architectural and pre-training innovations.

- **Efficiency Gains:** FIBER's "fusion in the backbone" architecture proves to be more parameter-efficient than comparative models. It adds only about 26 million fusion parameters, significantly less than GLIP or METER (which add ~110 million). This architectural advantage translates to practical benefits, with FIBER achieving 1.5x faster training times on object detection tasks compared to GLIP while maintaining comparable performance (Table 2).

- **Vision-Language Classification (VQAv2, NLVR2):** FIBER-B (base-size model) establishes new state-of-the-art results for models pre-trained on similar data (4 million images). Remarkably, it surpasses models like BLIP (trained on 129 million images) and SimVLM (trained on 1.8 billion images) on VQAv2, highlighting the effectiveness of its fusion mechanism and pre-training objectives (Table 3).

- **Image-Text Retrieval:** In the dual-encoder setting, FIBER-B delivers competitive performance on Flickr30k and COCO retrieval (Table 3). When leveraging its fusion encoder for re-ranking or ensembling with the dual encoder, FIBER significantly improves retrieval performance, achieving strong results (e.g., 90.96 R@1 on Flickr30k Image Retrieval) while balancing efficiency and accuracy (Table 4).

- **Image Captioning (COCO, NoCaps):** FIBER-B shows strong generalization to generative tasks. Without CIDEr optimization, it outperforms other base-size models. With GOLD integration and CIDEr optimization, FIBER-B achieves absolute state-of-the-art CIDEr scores on COCO for base-size models, even outperforming models trained on hundreds of millions of images (Table 5).

- **Phrase Grounding (Flickr30k Entities):** This is a particularly strong result. FIBER-B achieves 87.4 Recall@1, surpassing GLIP-L (a larger model trained with 25x more fine-grained data) while using significantly less fine-grained data during pre-training. This demonstrates the critical advantage of FIBER's coarse-to-fine pre-training and deep fusion in enabling efficient learning from limited fine-grained annotations. Even without coarse-grained VLP, FIBER-B outperforms GLIP-B on this task (Table 6).

- **Referring Expression Comprehension (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg):** FIBER-B outperforms larger state-of-the-art models like OFA-L, particularly on challenging splits like RefCOCO+ testB and RefCOCOg, which involve longer and more complex referring expressions (Table 7). This highlights FIBER's superior ability to ground intricate linguistic descriptions to specific image regions.

- **Object Detection (COCO, LVIS, ODinW):** FIBER-B achieves better Average Precision (AP) across COCO, LVIS, and the aggregated ODinW benchmarks compared to similarly sized GLIP-B (Table 8). Crucially, on the LVIS dataset, which features a long tail of rare objects, FIBER-B significantly outperforms GLIP-L (a larger model trained on 25x more fine-grained data) in terms of APr (Average Precision on rare objects), indicating exceptional generalization to novel and infrequent object classes. Furthermore, FIBER shows better few-shot data efficiency on the ODinW datasets (Figure 5).

- **Ablation Studies (Appendix):**
 - The learnable gating parameter $\alpha$ in the cross-attention modules is crucial for robust performance, especially when increasing the number of fusion layers (Table 9).
 - All coarse-grained pre-training objectives (MLM, ITM with hard negative mining, ITC) are necessary for optimal performance across VQA and retrieval tasks (Table 10).
 - The two-stage coarse-to-fine pre-training strategy consistently provides gains across fine-grained tasks (OD, REC) compared to training only with fine-grained data (Table 11).
 - The choice of Swin Transformer and RoBERTa as backbones proves to be optimal for FIBER's unified approach (Table 12).
 - Post-fine-grained pre-training, direct use of the model for VQA showed a performance drop, reinforcing the necessity of the two-stage approach for optimal performance across tasks of different characteristics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FIBER represents a significant step forward in the field of vision-language pre-training, with several key areas of impact:

1. **Unification of Vision-Language Tasks:** FIBER is the first end-to-end VLP model that convincingly demonstrates strong, consistent performance across both high-level image understanding tasks (VQA, captioning, retrieval) and fine-grained region-level tasks (phrase grounding, object detection, referring expression comprehension). This unification simplifies the model landscape, potentially leading to a single foundation model for a broad range of multimodal applications, rather than relying on specialized architectures. This addresses a major limitation of prior work and sets a new benchmark for comprehensive multimodal understanding.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Reduced Data Dependency:** The "fusion in the backbone" architectural design significantly improves computational efficiency (fewer fusion parameters, faster training) compared to previous methods. More importantly, the coarse-to-fine pre-training strategy dramatically reduces the reliance on expensive and cumbersome box-level annotated data. By leveraging readily available image-text pairs for initial coarse-grained learning, FIBER achieves superior performance on fine-grained tasks with substantially less specialized data than its predecessors. This has a profound impact on the scalability and accessibility of VLP research and development, particularly for resource-constrained environments or for languages/domains with limited fine-grained annotations.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** FIBER establishes new state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across a diverse suite of benchmarks. Its ability to outperform larger models or those trained with magnitudes more data (e.g., outperforming GLIP-L on phrase grounding and LVIS rare objects, and BLIP/SimVLM on VQA) highlights the potency of its architectural innovations and pre-training strategy. This strong performance across the board validates the efficacy of deep fusion and staged learning.

4. **Improved Generalization and Data Efficiency:** The impressive results on tasks like LVIS (rare objects) and ODinW (few-shot learning) underscore FIBER's robust generalization capabilities. The model effectively transfers knowledge learned from broad image-text data to tackle novel objects and scenarios with limited examples, which is crucial for real-world deployment where exhaustive annotations are impractical.

5. **Future Directions for Multimodal AI:** FIBER's success paves the way for further research into deeply integrated multimodal architectures and more sophisticated pre-training paradigms. It inspires explorations into scaling these unified models, extending them to other modalities (e.g., video, audio), and developing even more data-efficient learning strategies.

**Potential Negative Societal Impact:** The authors responsibly acknowledge potential negative societal impacts. Like all large-scale models trained on vast datasets, FIBER can inherit and propagate undesirable societal biases present in the pre-training data. Addressing these biases through careful data debiasing, filtering, and ethical evaluation before deployment is crucial. Additionally, the computational resources required for large-scale pre-training incur environmental costs, motivating future work towards minimizing energy consumption and developing more sustainable AI practices.

In conclusion, FIBER represents a significant advancement by providing a unified, efficient, and high-performing framework for vision-language understanding. Its innovations have the potential to democratize access to advanced multimodal AI capabilities and influence the direction of future research in this dynamic field.
