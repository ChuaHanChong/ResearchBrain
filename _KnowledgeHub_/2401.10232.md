# ParaHome: Parameterizing Everyday Home Activities Towards 3D Generative Modeling of Human-Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> ParaHome is a home-scale capture system combining 70 synchronized RGB cameras with an IMU body suit and mocap gloves, plus 3D ArUco cube markers, to record body, dexterous hand, and articulated-object motion in one parametric 3D space. It yields 486 minutes across 207 captures, 38 participants, 22 objects, with text labels, SMPL-X fits, and generative-modeling demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing motion datasets capture body without objects, or hand-object grasps in static poses, missing joint body plus dexterous finger plus object dynamics in one space.
- Prior human-object captures use unnatural table or single-object setups with limited action diversity, so sequential and concurrent multi-object manipulation in a home context is absent.
- Surface-attached markers on objects are occluded whenever hands grasp them, causing tracking failure and laborious manual post-processing; articulated multi-part objects are rarely parameterized at all.

## Method

- ==3D ArUco cube markers== (6cm, all faces) attached to each object part, tracked across 70 cameras; per-part revolute or sliding joints give parameterized articulation states for multi-part objects.
- Cross-system spatial alignment: body markers on 11 near-rigid parts optimize true skeleton offsets with foot constraints, and a fingertip touch protocol on a calibration cube calibrates hand skeleton scale.
- Post-processing fuses the two modalities: occlusion-free mocap wrists interpolate jittery hand markers, and near-rigid body-joint transforms fill dropped object trajectories; ==SMPL-X== shape plus autoencoder motion-manifold pose fitting.

## Results

- Cube-marker tracking succeeds on **100%** of a 4-minute sequence versus **0.76** to **0.93** for simulated surface markers (4 to 40 markers); triangulation reprojection error is **1.021** pixels.
- Hand alignment reaches **11 mm** average fingertip position error over **86** touches, below finger width; post-processing recovers dropped hand markers to within **9 mm** joint error.
- Diffusion motion synthesis trained on ParaHome mixed evenly with HumanML3D reaches FID **0.85** and R-Precision **0.73**, far better than either alone (**148.31** and **74.66**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Volumetric marker cubes beat surface markers under grasping occlusion: giving a tracked target multiple non-coplanar faces buys robustness that simply adding more flat markers does not.
> - Heterogeneous sensing pays off when modalities fail differently; occlusion-free wearable mocap can repair vision dropouts, and vision can anchor drifting inertial global position.
> - Narrow manipulation-rich data blends productively with broad locomotion data: balanced mixing preserved both distributions and enabled interpolated hybrid actions neither dataset contained.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The understanding and generation of human-object interactions (HOIs) are fundamental challenges in artificial intelligence, robotics, computer graphics, and virtual reality. For machines to truly comprehend and interact with the physical world in a human-like manner, they must be able to perceive, predict, and synthesize complex, dexterous, and sequential manipulations of objects. This paper directly addresses a critical bottleneck in this research area: the severe lack of high-quality, large-scale 3D datasets capturing natural and casual HOIs.

Prior research and existing datasets, as highlighted in the paper's introduction and related work, suffer from several limitations:
- **Limited Scope:** Many datasets focus solely on human motion without objects, human body motion without dexterous hand manipulation, or static hand-object interactions rather than dynamic ones.
- **Simplicity:** Interactions often involve simple, atomic actions like grasping a single object, lacking the complexity of sequential or concurrent manipulations.
- **Unnatural Settings:** Data is frequently collected in constrained environments, such as simple table setups or specialized labs, which do not reflect real-world, everyday activities in natural home environments.
- **Technical Challenges:** Capturing detailed 3D movements of both the full human body, dexterous hands, and multiple objects simultaneously, while dealing with severe occlusions and multi-scale motions, is inherently difficult. Articulated objects, with their multiple movable parts, add another layer of complexity.

ParaHome directly confronts these limitations by introducing a system and dataset specifically designed to capture the dynamic 3D movements of humans and diverse objects within a common home environment. It bridges the gap between general human motion capture and specialized hand-object interaction datasets by integrating full-body motion with dexterous hand movements and accurately tracking multiple rigid and articulated objects. The inclusion of sequential and concurrent manipulations, paired with text descriptions, further enhances its utility for advancing generative modeling of HOIs. This work represents a significant step towards creating the foundational data necessary for developing intelligent systems that can truly understand and mimic human behavior in complex, interactive scenarios, pushing the boundaries of realism and functional understanding in HOI research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is two-fold: first, to develop a robust and comprehensive 3D capture system named ParaHome, and second, to leverage this system to collect a novel, large-scale, high-quality human-object interaction (HOI) dataset. The overarching motivation stems from the current inability of machines to adequately understand and mimic the intricate ways humans interact with the physical world in daily life. This deficit is largely attributed to the scarcity of granular 3D interaction data captured in natural, dynamic settings.

More specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:
1. **Overcome Data Limitations:** To address the fundamental challenge of lacking large-scale 3D HOI datasets that represent natural and casual interactions. Existing datasets are insufficient due to limited scope (e.g., body only, static interactions, single objects), unnatural capture environments, or restricted action diversity.
2. **Capture Comprehensive 3D Signals:** To capture the dynamic 3D movements of the entire human body, including dexterous hand manipulations, alongside the precise 3D movements of multiple, diverse objects within a common spatio-temporal space.
3. **Ensure Naturalness and Context:** To collect data in a realistic home environment, allowing participants to perform everyday activities involving sequential and concurrent interactions with multiple objects, reflecting the complexity and fluidity of real-life scenarios.
4. **Incorporate Articulated Objects:** To include articulated objects with multiple movable parts, represented by 3D parameterized models, which is crucial for modeling realistic manipulation that goes beyond simple rigid body interactions.
5. **Provide Rich Annotations:** To pair the captured 3D motion data with descriptive text annotations, facilitating the development of language-conditioned generative models.
6. **Enable Generative Modeling:** To provide a rich dataset that supports the development of advanced generative models capable of synthesizing plausible human-object interactions, thereby contributing to fields like robotics, virtual character animation, and human-computer interaction. The paper aims to demonstrate this potential through initial generative modeling experiments.

In essence, the researchers are motivated by the need for a foundational dataset that can enable machines to learn the nuanced "connection between human action and environmental responses" – a capability vital for true intelligence in interactive physical environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ParaHome system is designed to robustly capture intricate 3D human-object interactions (HOIs) in a natural home environment measuring 12.4m². The methodology integrates heterogeneous capture technologies and employs sophisticated data processing and alignment techniques to achieve high fidelity.

#### 3.1. Hardware System and Architecture

The core of ParaHome consists of:
- **Multi-view Camera System:** 70 synchronized RGB industrial cameras (BFLY-31S4C-C) operating at 30Hz with 2048x1536 resolution. Three types of lenses (3mm, 5mm, 6mm) are strategically deployed to cover the entire room and capture fine details. The cameras are calibrated using Structure-from-Motion via COLMAP.
- **Wearable Motion Capture:** An Xsens IMU-based motion suit for full-body tracking and Manus hand gloves for dexterous hand motion capture. These systems provide occlusion-robust human motion data.
- **Synchronization:** All cameras and wearable devices are gen-locked using a common square wave signal to ensure precise temporal alignment.
- **Computational Infrastructure:** 1 master and 18 slave desktop machines manage camera control and data processing, equipped with 1G ethernet boards and SSDs. 15 LED lights provide consistent illumination.

#### 3.2. Modeling and Tracking 3D Objects

- **3D ArUco Marker Design:** To overcome occlusion issues prevalent during human-object interactions, custom 3D ArUco markers (cube-shaped, 6cm spanning) are attached to objects. This design ensures that at least some markers remain visible even when objects are heavily occluded by hands.
- **Object Modeling:** High-quality 3D meshes of all objects are obtained via an Einstar3D scanner and manual alignment. Articulated objects (e.g., laptop, drawer, refrigerator) are manually segmented into rigidly moving parts, and parameterized structures (revolute or sliding joints with articulation axis and pivot points) are built.
- **Tracking:** Object 6DoF rigid transformations (translation and orientation) are computed by detecting ArUco markers in multi-view images, triangulating 2D marker points into 3D, and using the Kabsch algorithm. Object-specific dynamic states (articulation parameters) are derived from the relative transformations of markers on movable parts.

#### 3.3. Capturing 3D Human Motions

A major challenge is spatially aligning the heterogeneous wearable mocap data (which suffers from global drift and imperfect skeleton scales) with the multi-camera system's world coordinate space.
- **Body Alignment:** 3-4 ArUco markers are attached to 11 near-rigid body parts (torso, hands, arms, legs) for a dedicated alignment capture. Participants perform a range-of-motion sequence. An optimization process determines the authentic body skeleton configuration (joint offsets) and body marker locations by minimizing the mean-squared error between forward kinematics output (from mocap data) and multi-view triangulated marker positions, with foot contact constraints.
- **Hand Calibration:** A novel hand alignment protocol uses a calibration structure (three cubes with known corner vertices). Participants touch specified corners with their fingertips. An optimization scheme determines authentic hand skeleton configuration (scale and per-joint offsets) and the positions of wrist-attached 3D markers in the hand-centric coordinate system. Losses include fingertip-to-corner distance, wrist position consistency with body mocap, and penetration avoidance.

#### 3.4. Post-Processing to Enhance Qualities

- **Enhancing Hand Tracking:** A weighted interpolation combines global hand locations from hand-attached markers (camera system) and wrist positions from body mocap. In cases of tracking failure, the system falls back to occlusion-free body mocap data for robustness.
- **Enhancing Object Tracking:** If object tracking fails due to severe occlusion, the system infers if the object is close to the human. If so, it leverages the human body motion (assuming a near-rigid connection) to interpolate missing object trajectories.
- **Fitting Human Body Model (SMPL-X):** SMPL-X shape parameters are fitted to each participant using HQ-SAM for body segmentation masks and optimizing rendered SMPL-X to match masks and projected joints. For pose parameters, an autoencoder is trained on AMASS and Xsens data to learn a natural body motion manifold. A latent vector derived from Xsens input is optimized to fit SMPL-X body pose to Xsens wrist, ankle, feet, and fingertip positions, improving fitting quality.
- **Synthesizing Realistic RGB:** To broaden research opportunities, the 3D ParaHome data is used to generate synthetic RGB images. ControlNet, a diffusion-based image synthesis model, is conditioned on rendered depth maps, 2D body joint cues, and text annotations to produce realistic RGB images from various viewpoints (egocentric, high-angle, front-facing).

#### 3.5. Dataset Collection

The ParaHome system was used to collect data from 38 participants, interacting with 22 diverse objects (including articulated ones) in a home environment. The dataset comprises 208 captures and 486 minutes of sequences, covering scenarios designed to elicit natural, sequential, and concurrent manipulations under verbal guidance, capturing unique individual styles.

#### 3.6. Generative Modeling Experiments

To demonstrate the dataset's utility, the authors performed two initial generative modeling tasks:
- **Text-conditioned motion synthesis:** Using a diffusion model (MDM framework), trained on a mix of ParaHome and HumanML3D datasets, to generate full-body and hand motions from text descriptions.
- **Object-guided body motion synthesis:** Extending a transformer-based model, an Object State Encoder maps object state sequences (rigid motion + articulation parameters) to generate corresponding full-body human motions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings regarding both the robustness of the ParaHome capture system and the utility of the collected dataset for generative modeling of human-object interactions.

#### 4.1. System and Capture Evaluation

- **Camera System Efficacy:** The use of 70 synchronized cameras proves highly effective, showing a near-linear increase in marker detection ratio without saturation as more cameras are added (Fig. 7a), indicating optimal coverage and reduced redundancy.
- **3D Marker Cube Superiority:** Quantitative evaluation demonstrates the significant advantage of the custom 3D ArUco cube markers over traditional surface-attached markers. While simulated surface markers suffered from tracking failures (as low as 0.76 success ratio for 7 markers), the 3D cube markers maintained a perfect tracking success ratio during a 4-minute complex manipulation sequence (Table 1). This confirms their robustness against severe occlusions caused by hand grasping and multiple objects.
- **High Tracking Quality:** The system achieves a low average reprojection error of approximately 1.02 pixels for triangulated ArUco corners, both across the entire scene and specifically during object manipulation (Table 1), indicating precise 3D object tracking.
- **Comparable Jitter Levels:** Temporal jitter analysis (derivative of acceleration) shows that the object tracking quality from cameras is comparable to that of high-quality IMU-based wearable motion capture systems (Fig. 7b), crucial for capturing subtle interactions.
- **Precise Hand Alignment:** The novel hand alignment procedure achieves an impressive Average Position Error (APE) of 11 mm between fingertips and target marker corners during calibration (across 86 touches). Considering typical finger width, this demonstrates high precision in spatial alignment of the hands within the camera system.

#### 4.2. Post-Processing Validation

- **Enhanced Object Tracking:** The proposed post-processing method, which leverages human body motion cues to interpolate missing object trajectories, significantly outperforms naive linear interpolation. It demonstrates much better recovery of tracking failures and reduction of jitters (Fig. 7c, 7d), crucial for maintaining data quality during occlusions.
- **Enhanced Hand Tracking:** The hand tracking enhancement algorithm effectively recovers undetected markers, resulting in a low error of 9 mm between recovered and original hand joint positions, proving the robustness of the system to intermittent hand marker occlusions.

#### 4.3. Generative Modeling Demonstrations

- **Text-Conditioned Motion Synthesis:**
 - Training a diffusion model (MDM) with an evenly mixed dataset (ParaHome + HumanML3D) yields the best performance across various metrics (R-Precision, Multimodal Distance, FID, Diversity, Multimodality) compared to training on either dataset alone (Table 2).
 - The mixed model achieves an R-Precision of 0.73 and a low FID of 0.85, closely approximating the distribution of the combined dataset.
 - Qualitative results demonstrate the model's ability to synthesize diverse and natural human motions, including blending distinct styles from both datasets (e.g., walking and drinking simultaneously, Fig. 9 left), showcasing ParaHome's contribution to complex, manipulation-focused motion generation.
- **Object-Guided Body Motion Synthesis:**
 - The transformer-based model successfully synthesizes plausible full-body human motions conditioned on sequences of object states (Table 3, Fig. 9 right).
 - Using relative spatial cues (person's root location relative to the object) as an input condition (denoted `w/ Sp→tp`) consistently improves the accuracy of motion synthesis across various objects (refrigerator, drawer, sink, washing machine) and metrics (Mean Positional Errors for joints and root). For instance, for refrigerator interaction, global joint error improved from 9.26 cm to 6.57 cm.
 - Root-centered body skeleton representation generally yields better accuracy for pose-dependent attributes (e.g., rc-joints, rc-wrists), while SMPL-X notation can be better for global orientation and root position in some cases (Table 9 in supplementary). Smaller prediction window sizes generally improve accuracy.
 - Qualitative results (Fig. 16 in supplementary) show convincing performance in generating human movements that appropriately interact with the target objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ParaHome research makes several significant contributions that promise to have a substantial impact across multiple scientific and engineering disciplines.

#### 5.1. Pioneering Dataset for Complex HOIs

ParaHome introduces a truly novel dataset that fills critical gaps in the existing landscape of 3D human-object interaction data. Its unique combination of features – full-body and dexterous hand motions, multiple rigid and articulated objects, sequential and concurrent manipulations, natural home environment, and corresponding text descriptions – sets a new standard for realism and complexity. This dataset will serve as an invaluable resource for researchers, enabling the development and rigorous evaluation of models that can understand, predict, and generate human-like interactions with unprecedented detail and contextual richness.

#### 5.2. Advanced Capture System Design

The ParaHome capture system itself represents a significant methodological advancement. Its robust integration of multi-view RGB cameras with IMU-based wearable motion capture, coupled with innovative solutions like the 3D ArUco cube markers and detailed calibration protocols for body and hands, provides a blueprint for high-fidelity data acquisition in challenging, occlusion-prone environments. The validation of its components, demonstrating low reprojection errors, comparable jitter levels to high-end mocap, and superior occlusion handling, testifies to its technical excellence and reliability.

#### 5.3. Enabling Generative AI for Human-Object Interactions

The most immediate and impactful contribution is the enablement of next-generation generative AI for HOIs. The paper demonstrates this potential through initial experiments in text-conditioned motion synthesis and object-guided body motion synthesis. This capability has profound implications for:
- **Robotics:** Empowering domestic robots to learn and execute complex manipulation tasks by observing human demonstrations, leading to more adaptable and intelligent robotic assistants.
- **Virtual Characters and Animation:** Revolutionizing the realism of virtual agents in video games, virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and film production by synthesizing highly believable and context-aware interactions.
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** Developing more intuitive and natural interfaces where AI systems can anticipate and respond to human intentions based on observed interactions.
- **Understanding Human Behavior:** Providing a rich quantitative resource for cognitive scientists and biomechanists to study the intricate motor control, planning, and dynamics involved in everyday human activities.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions and Benchmarking

The research also lays out clear avenues for future work, including moving towards markerless capture, replicating the system in diverse environmental settings, and exploring more sophisticated generative models for HOI prediction and synthesis. By making the dataset publicly available, ParaHome establishes a new benchmark that will push the state-of-the-art, encouraging the research community to tackle more challenging, realistic, and functionally grounded problems in 3D understanding and generation. The synthetic RGB data further expands its utility, allowing for data augmentation and testing of 2D/2.5D vision models in scenarios previously only accessible through real-world capture.

In summary, ParaHome addresses a critical data gap with an innovative capture system and a rich dataset, poised to accelerate advancements in AI systems capable of truly understanding and interacting with the physical world through natural human-object interactions.
