# Synthetic Data Generation and Vision-based Wrinkle and Keypoint Detection for Bimanual Cloth Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A perception framework for bimanual cloth unfolding that pairs a permutation-invariant heatmap CNN for corner keypoints with a YOLOv8-plus-OpenCV pipeline that isolates the longest structural wrinkle and extracts grasping points from its convex hull. Trained on a domain-randomized Blender dataset with auto-annotated labels, it reaches 1.7615 pixel mean position error and transfers to real fabrics without fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Annotated real-world cloth data is scarce because manual labeling is labor-intensive, and continuous deformation plus self-occlusion defeats standard pose estimation for textiles.
- Keypoint-only frameworks break on completely folded garments: every external corner is occluded, so the robot has no valid grasping point to start manipulation.
- Prior detectors are unstable or produce false positives on severe folds, and contour-curvature grasp reasoning misses grasp regions hidden inside internal folds.

## Method

- Blender ==domain-randomized== generation pipeline: mass-spring cloth dropped from random heights, randomized textures, lighting and camera, 500 episodes yielding 5000 auto-annotated images.
- ==Permutation-invariant CNN== emitting a single-channel Gaussian heatmap, with ==local soft-argmax== sub-pixel refinement and ==greedy distance-based non-maximum suppression== capping output at four corners.
- ==YOLOv8== wrinkle boxes post-processed by Canny edges, morphological closing, ==ellipse fitting== to pick the longest major axis, then ==convex-hull== maximum-distance endpoints as bimanual grasp targets.

## Results

- Keypoint CNN reaches a mean position error of **1.7615 ± 0.7461** pixels on the synthetic test set, with training and validation loss converging and no overfitting.
- Against baselines on folded cloth, error drops from **26.0830 ± 27.1104** pixels for the keypoint-pose baseline and **10.4520 ± 4.1205** for the YOLOv5 detector.
- Both models transfer to physical fabrics with **zero** fine-tuning, detecting keypoints and wrinkles and generating grasp lines where baselines fail under full occlusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When a primary feature can vanish entirely under occlusion, pair it with a fallback feature that survives that state rather than hardening the primary detector.
> - Heatmap peaks plus suppression give permutation invariance for free, avoiding the brittle fixed ordering that regression-based corner detectors impose.
> - Physics-driven domain randomization can carry cloth perception to real fabrics without fine-tuning, but reported errors here are synthetic-set only, not real-world measured.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The robotic manipulation of deformable objects, particularly textiles, represents a significant challenge in robotics. This field is characterized by the high degrees of freedom inherent in deformable materials, continuous deformation dynamics, and frequent self-occlusions, which collectively complicate robust visual perception and state estimation. Accurate perception of features such as corners (keypoints) and internal folds (wrinkles) is crucial for tasks like bimanual unfolding and ironing.

Existing research has explored various approaches to address these challenges. One prominent area involves the use of learning-based methods that rely on large datasets for training visual perception models. To circumvent the labor-intensive process of manual annotation for real-world data, many studies leverage domain-randomized physics simulations to generate synthetic datasets, facilitating Sim-to-Real transfer. Examples include Blender-based frameworks for deformable object pose estimation using keypoints. Geometric approaches have also been investigated, focusing on extracting garment contours and employing polygonal approximation with curvature analysis to identify graspable regions.

Despite these advancements, a gap persists in the robust handling of completely folded garments. Current methods that rely primarily on keypoint detection often fail when external corners are entirely occluded, leaving robots unable to initiate manipulation. Geometric methods similarly struggle when key grasping regions are hidden within complex internal folds, as they primarily depend on visible boundary structures. The challenge intensifies in scenarios requiring continuous manipulation and adaptation to the evolving state of the cloth.

This paper positions itself to address this specific limitation by integrating keypoint reasoning with an internal wrinkle-aware perception system. It aims to enable robust bimanual garment unfolding even when corners are severely occluded or concealed within complex folded configurations, thereby extending the capabilities of robotic cloth manipulation beyond partially unfolded or flat configurations. The work draws insights from broader advancements in semantic scene understanding, 3D perception, and generative models, which have demonstrated the importance of stable feature association under partial occlusions and the effectiveness of semantic-aware feature representations for sparse and ambiguous visual structures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties robots face when manipulating completely folded garments. In such states, traditional perception methods that rely on identifying garment corners (keypoints) are often rendered ineffective due to severe self-occlusions. This lack of visible, graspable features prevents robots from initiating unfolding processes. Training robust perception models for deformable object manipulation necessitates extensive datasets, and manual annotation for real-world data is time-consuming and labor-intensive. While synthetic data generation offers a viable alternative, existing frameworks have not fully addressed the initial state of a completely folded garment where keypoints are obscured.

The key objectives of this work are articulated through its primary contributions:

1. **Develop a Robust Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline:** To overcome the scarcity of annotated real-world data, a key objective was to create an automated, domain-randomized synthetic data generation pipeline using Blender. This pipeline was designed to simulate cloth physics and automatically export annotated keypoints, providing a scalable solution for dataset creation. Additionally, it aimed to supplement this synthetic data with manually labeled renders and real-world data specifically for wrinkle detection, enhancing the diversity and realism of the training dataset.
2. **Establish a Real-time Hybrid Perception Framework:** A central objective was to design a perception framework capable of identifying both garment keypoints and structural wrinkles in real-time. This framework aimed to integrate a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for permutation-invariant keypoint detection and a YOLOv8-OpenCV pipeline for extracting grasping points from structural wrinkles. The goal was to ensure the system could reliably identify graspable features under varying occlusion levels, particularly when keypoints are not visible.
3. **Propose a Fused Bimanual Manipulation Algorithm:** The research aimed to develop a bimanual manipulation algorithm that leverages the outputs of the hybrid perception system. This algorithm was intended to implement a state-machine approach, transitioning from wrinkle-based stretching for fully folded garments to keypoint-based ironing once garment corners become visible. This objective sought to provide a structured and adaptive strategy for unfolding textiles, addressing the entire manipulation process from initial, highly occluded states to a fully unfolded configuration.

By addressing these objectives, the researchers aimed to bridge the gap in robotic cloth manipulation, enabling robots to handle previously challenging scenarios involving completely folded textiles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach encompassing synthetic data generation, two distinct visual perception models (for keypoints and wrinkles), and a fused bimanual manipulation algorithm.

#### 3.1. A. Synthetic Data Generation

To address the data scarcity for training perception models, an automated pipeline was developed using Blender. This pipeline generated a large-scale synthetic dataset designed for domain randomization.

- **Physics Simulation:** The cloth simulation within Blender utilized a mass-spring physics model based on Newton’s second law. Garments were dropped from random heights, and upon impact with a table collision plane, the solver computed reaction forces, leading to realistic deformations and folds.
- **Dataset Size and Randomization:** The script executed 500 episodes, producing 5000 labeled images. Each episode randomized several parameters:
 - Cloth textures and optional color tints (20 distinct textures used).
 - Table textures (8 high-resolution textures used).
 - Initial drop positions and orientations for the garment.
 - Lighting and camera poses.
- **Annotation Output:** The pipeline automatically extracted ground-truth annotations, including garment corner keypoints and segmentation masks, alongside RGB renders.

#### 3.2. B. Keypoints Detection

The keypoint detection component utilized a custom Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture.

- **Network Architecture:** The CNN was designed to output a single-channel spatial heatmap. This approach enabled a permutation-invariant representation, where each visible garment corner was represented as a distinct Gaussian peak within the heatmap, without imposing a predefined order.
- **Inference Process:**
 - **Peak Extraction:** During inference, the network extracted multiple peak candidates from the generated heatmap.
 - **Sub-pixel Refinement:** To achieve enhanced accuracy for 2D coordinates, a local soft-argmax (centroid) refinement was applied within a predefined window around each detected peak. These refined coordinates were then mapped back to the original image space.
 - **Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS):** A greedy distance-based NMS filter was implemented in the image space to address potential noise or overlapping heatmaps. If two predicted keypoints were closer than a specified pixel radius, the one with the lower confidence score was suppressed. This process ensured that only distinct, highly confident keypoints were retained, with the output capped to a maximum of four corners.

#### 3.3. C. Wrinkle Detection

When keypoints were occluded in highly folded cloth states, a wrinkle detection module was employed.

- **Dataset:** The wrinkle detection model was trained using a combined dataset. This included a subset of 500 images from the custom synthetic Blender data, which were manually annotated with bounding boxes around folds using the AnyLabeling tool. This was merged with the Wrinkle Detector 2.0 Dataset from Roboflow.
- **Detection Model:** A YOLOv8 architecture was utilized to predict bounding boxes around potential folds in the fabric.
- **Grasping Point Extraction (OpenCV Pipeline):** To derive precise grasping points from the detected bounding boxes, an OpenCV-based post-processing pipeline was implemented:
 - **Grayscale Conversion and Edge Detection:** Each Region of Interest (ROI) corresponding to a wrinkle bounding box was converted to grayscale, and edges were extracted using the Canny detector.
 - **Morphological Operations and Contour Extraction:** Morphological closing and dilation operations were applied to connect fragmented edges, followed by contour extraction.
 - **Primary Wrinkle Identification:** To isolate the most prominent fold, an ellipse was fitted to each extracted contour, and its major axis length was measured. The contour with the longest major axis was identified as the primary structural wrinkle, and a binary mask was generated for it.
 - **Grasping Coordinate Calculation:** The convex hull of the primary wrinkle mask was computed. The Euclidean distances between all points on the hull were then calculated to identify the two points separated by the maximum distance. These two endpoints were designated as the target 2D grasping coordinates for the robotic arms, defining the longest line across the wrinkle.

#### 3.4. D. Fused Bimanual Manipulation Algorithm

A state-machine algorithm was proposed to integrate the outputs from both the keypoint and wrinkle detection modalities for bimanual cloth manipulation.

- **Input and Goal:** The algorithm took a continuous camera feed of a completely folded cloth as input, with the goal of achieving a fully unfolded garment (indicated by the detection of four keypoints).
- **Wrinkle Stretching Action (Initial State):**
 - If fewer than two keypoints were detected, or if the detected keypoints were not collinear, the system entered the wrinkle stretching phase.
 - The longest wrinkle line (Lw) was extracted from the wrinkle mask (Mw).
 - The midpoint (mw) of Lw was computed, and a perpendicular bisector (Bw) was determined at mw.
 - Two points (P1, P2) were found where Bw intersected the cloth edges.
 - Robot 1 grasped P1, and Robot 2 grasped P2. The arms then moved outwards to stretch the cloth. This action was repeated until at least two collinear keypoints emerged.
- **Keypoint-based Ironing Action (Transition State):**
 - Once at least two collinear keypoints were detected, the system transitioned to the keypoint-guided ironing phase.
 - Robot 1 and Robot 2 grasped the detected keypoints.
 - The end-effectors moved forward/backward to smooth the cloth.
- **Termination:** The loop continued until all four keypoints of the garment were localized, indicating a fully unfolded state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance of the proposed perception framework and its application in a bimanual manipulation algorithm.

**Keypoint Detection Performance:**
The keypoint detection model was evaluated on a synthetic test set, measuring the Mean Position Error (MPE) between predicted 2D pixel coordinates and ground truth.

- **Training Convergence:** Both training and validation losses for the keypoint detection CNN converged steadily, indicating effective learning without signs of overfitting.
- **Performance Metrics:** The validation MPE and PCK@8 (Percentage of Correct Keypoints) metrics stabilized rapidly during training.
- **Final MPE:** The model achieved a final MPE of 1.7615 ± 0.7461 pixels. This low error and variance suggest that the network accurately localized features despite complex cloth deformations present in the synthetic data.
- **Qualitative Visualization:** Visualizations on synthetic data demonstrated the accurate extraction of keypoints and the calculation of wrinkle-based grasping points and directional vectors for unfolding.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
A significant finding was the ability of the trained models to transfer to physical fabrics without any fine-tuning.

- **Robustness:** The system successfully detected keypoints and wrinkles on real-world textiles, validating the efficacy of the Sim-to-Real pipeline.
- **Real-world Application:** The framework demonstrated the capability to predict accurate unfolding trajectories on physical fabrics, showcasing its practical applicability. Images displayed accurate keypoint detection on real cloth in both unfolded and folded states, along with raw wrinkle detection bounding boxes and the post-processed extraction of the longest structural wrinkle, maximal fold line, and generated grasping points with unfolding directions.

**Baseline Comparisons:**
The proposed approach was compared against existing state-of-the-art baselines, specifically focusing on performance in completely folded scenarios where keypoints are occluded.

- **Lips et al.:** This baseline exhibited an MPE of 26.0830 ± 27.1104 pixels. Its primary limitation was unstable behavior with severe folds and a lack of wrinkle detection capabilities, leading to high keypoint prediction errors when keypoints were obscured.
- **Cheng Li:** This YOLOv5-based approach yielded an MPE of 10.4520 ± 4.1205 pixels. It demonstrated a limitation by generating false positives, misclassifying complex folds as keypoints in highly occluded states.
- **Proposed Method:** The current work achieved an MPE of 1.7615 ± 0.7461 pixels. Its performance was attributed to robust grasping point extraction via longest wrinkle isolation, maintaining accuracy even when keypoints were fully occluded.
- **Qualitative Comparison:** Qualitative analysis illustrated that while baselines could succeed on partially unfolded fabrics, they frequently failed in completely folded states. The proposed method, in contrast, consistently identified structural wrinkles to extract reliable grasping points, thereby addressing scenarios where baselines either produced unstable predictions or false positives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that contributes to the field of robotic manipulation of deformable objects, particularly textiles. Its significance and potential impact can be considered across several dimensions:

1. **Addressing the "Fully Folded Garment" Problem:** The primary contribution is providing a robust solution for manipulating completely folded garments, a scenario where previous keypoint-centric approaches often fail due to severe self-occlusions. By integrating wrinkle detection, the system can initiate manipulation even when traditional features are hidden. This capability expands the operational envelope for robotic textile handling.
2. **Enhanced Visual Perception in Challenging Conditions:** The developed perception framework demonstrates reliable feature extraction (keypoints and wrinkles) under complex deformations and occlusions. The low Mean Position Error (MPE) for keypoint detection and the effective wrinkle-based grasping point extraction indicate a robust visual understanding of garment states, which is a fundamental requirement for autonomous manipulation.
3. **Demonstrated Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The ability of the models to transfer directly to real-world physical fabrics without fine-tuning highlights the effectiveness of the domain-randomized synthetic data generation pipeline. This reduces the dependency on labor-intensive real-world data collection and annotation, potentially accelerating research and development in deformable object manipulation.
4. **Foundation for Advanced Manipulation Algorithms:** The proposed bimanual manipulation algorithm, which transitions between wrinkle-based stretching and keypoint-based ironing, establishes a structured baseline strategy. This algorithmic approach can serve as a foundational policy for future Reinforcement Learning (RL) endeavors in simulating and transferring complex manipulation tasks to physical robots.
5. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The advancements in robust cloth perception and manipulation have implications for various robotic applications. These include:
 - **Laundry and Domestic Robotics:** Automating tasks like folding and unfolding clothes in household settings.
 - **Industrial Textile Handling:** Improving efficiency in manufacturing processes involving fabrics, such as garment production or textile inspection.
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Developing robots that can assist individuals with daily living activities, including dressing or handling garments.
6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The availability of code and datasets can facilitate further research and development within the community. This openness may encourage replication, extension, and comparative studies, fostering collaborative progress in deformable object manipulation.

In summary, this work advances the state-of-the-art in robotic cloth manipulation by providing a perception and manipulation framework capable of handling highly challenging initial states of garments. Its demonstrated robustness and practical transferability lay a foundation for more autonomous and versatile robotic systems interacting with deformable materials.
