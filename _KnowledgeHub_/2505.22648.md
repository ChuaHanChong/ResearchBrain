# WebDancer: Towards Autonomous Information Seeking Agency

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for autonomous information seeking, WebDancer, developed by Alibaba Group's Tongyi Lab, introduces a systematic end-to-end pipeline for large language models to perform multi-turn web research. It utilizes advanced data synthesis methods and a two-stage supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning strategy, achieving 51.5% average on GAIA with QwQ-32B, substantially outperforming vanilla ReAct baselines.

## Problem

- Existing information-seeking agents often struggle with the complexity of real-world multi-step reasoning and deep information retrieval beyond simple QA.
- Current datasets are typically shallow, solvable with few search turns, and lack the depth and diversity needed to train robust agents for long-horizon web exploration.
- Training paradigms like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) face issues with generalization and efficient exploitation of information-seeking behavior in complex, multi-tool environments.

## Method

- **WebDancer** synthesizes challenging, diverse, and deep information-seeking QA pairs using ==CRAWLQA== (mimicking human browsing) and E2HQA (iteratively evolving simple questions into complex ones).
- It employs a two-stage training approach: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) provides a 'cold start' for multi-step reasoning and action coupling, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) using the DAPO algorithm for enhanced generalization.
- High-quality 'Thought-Action-Observation' trajectories are generated from the synthesized QA pairs using both short and long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) constructions and filtered through a three-stage validity, correctness, and quality assessment process.

## Results

- WebDancer with QwQ-**32B** achieved **51.5%** average on GAIA and **47.9%** on WebWalkerQA, marking substantial improvements over vanilla ReAct baselines (**37.8%** on GAIA, **24.1%** on WebWalkerQA) and surpassing GPT-4o's vanilla ReAct implementation.
- Reinforcement learning consistently enhanced generalization and consistency for non-reasoning models (Qwen-**7B**, Qwen-**32B**), leading to improved Pass@1, Pass@3, and Cons@3 metrics.
- The framework demonstrated robustness on challenging BrowseComp benchmarks, including multilingual tasks, indicating its efficacy in complex reasoning and diverse information-seeking scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic capabilities are critical for complex information-seeking tasks, as non-agentic approaches performed poorly on challenging benchmarks.
> - High-quality synthetic data, especially when combined and robustly filtered, significantly improves agent performance and is crucial for developing robust web agents.
> - A two-stage training pipeline (SFT for initial behavioral grounding, then RL for generalization) is efficient; SFT is essential for establishing fundamental multi-step, multi-tool instruction-following capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the forefront of research into **autonomous AI agents**, specifically focusing on **information seeking within dynamic web environments**. The broader landscape is currently dominated by the rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), which have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding and generating human-like text. However, a significant challenge remains: equipping these models with the ability to autonomously interact with real-world environments, perform multi-step reasoning, and actively seek information beyond their pre-training knowledge.

Existing approaches to information-seeking agents generally fall into two categories:
1. **Prompt Engineering:** Directly guiding LLMs/LRMs using sophisticated prompts (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) to execute complex tasks. While "training-free," these methods often struggle to effectively leverage the full reasoning potential for truly complex, interactive tasks.
2. **Fine-tuning or Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Incorporating search or browser capabilities into agents through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or RL. While these methods internalize capabilities, prior work often utilized relatively simple datasets (e.g., 2Wiki dataset achieving over 80% performance), which do not fully capture the complexity and challenges of real-world, multi-turn information seeking. Current SFT/RL paradigms also face issues with generalization and efficient exploitation of information-seeking behavior.

The paper identifies key challenges in building autonomous information-seeking agents:
- Acquiring high-quality, fine-grained browsing data.
- Constructing reliable, long-horizon reasoning trajectories.
- Designing scalable and generalizable training strategies for out-of-distribution web environments and complex interaction patterns.

WebDancer aims to address these gaps by proposing a systematic, end-to-end pipeline for building robust, long-term information-seeking web agents from scratch, akin to advanced systems like OpenAI's Deep Research and x.ai's Grok DeepSearch. It leverages the ReAct framework (Reasoning and Acting) as a suitable paradigm for coupling reasoning with action in interactive settings. The work contributes to the growing body of research on **agentic AI**, **tool-augmented LLMs**, and the application of **reinforcement learning for complex decision-making in open-ended environments**. It distinguishes itself by focusing on a data-centric and training-stage perspective to improve data quality and training efficiency, which are crucial for developing more capable and generalizable web agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to **unlock autonomous multi-turn information-seeking agency** in large language models, specifically aiming to build a web agent capable of "deep research" from scratch, similar to advanced systems like OpenAI's Deep Research.

The key motivations driving this work stem from several identified limitations in the current state of information-seeking agents:

1. **Complexity of Real-World Problems:** Addressing intricate real-world problems requires not just single-step information retrieval but in-depth information seeking coupled with multi-step reasoning, which current methods often fall short of.
2. **Limitations of Existing Datasets:** Widely used QA datasets are often shallow, solvable with a few search turns, and lack the depth and diversity needed to train robust agents for long-horizon web exploration. Existing challenging datasets like GAIA and WebWalkerQA are primarily for evaluation and are too small for effective training.
3. **Inefficiency of Current Training Paradigms:** While SFT and RL have shown promise, they often face generalization issues in complex environments. Direct on-policy RL can be inefficient in multi-tool settings, as early training steps might prioritize basic tool usage over advanced reasoning.
4. **Absence of a Systematic Guideline:** The research community lacks a cohesive, end-to-end paradigm for building such agents, encompassing data construction, trajectory sampling, and multi-stage training.

To counter these motivations, the authors explicitly state their aim to provide the research community with a **systematic guideline** for building autonomous information-seeking agents from both a data-centric and training-stage perspective. This involves developing scalable methods for generating high-quality, complex QA data and proposing an efficient, two-stage training strategy that combines the benefits of supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. The ultimate goal is to enable LLMs to perceive, decide, and act autonomously in dynamic web environments to accomplish human-like tasks effectively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WebDancer framework proposes a cohesive, end-to-end paradigm for building agentic information-seeking agents, emphasizing a **data-centric and training-stage perspective**. This approach is structured into four key stages:

**Stage I: Browsing Data Construction (Deep Information Seeking Dataset Synthesis)**
To overcome the limitations of shallow and small existing datasets, WebDancer synthesizes challenging, diverse, and deep information-seeking QA pairs using two primary methods:
- **CRAWLQA:** This method mimics human browsing by crawling root URLs of authoritative websites (e.g., arXiv, GitHub, Wiki) and recursively navigating subpages via hyperlinks. GPT-4o is then prompted to generate synthetic QA pairs from the collected content, focusing on diverse question types (COUNT, MULTI-HOP, INTERSECTION) to ensure specificity and relevance.
- **E2HQA (Easy-to-Hard QA):** This strategy involves iteratively evolving simple, fact-seeking "SimpleQA" style questions into complex multi-step problems. An initial simple question ($Q_n$) targets an entity ($E_n$). LLMs construct a query based on $E_n$ to search for related information ($C_n$). This content is then used to restructure a new query ($R_n$), replacing the original entity, thereby progressively increasing the question's complexity while preserving the answer's validity. This allows for controlled scaling of the number of steps required to solve the problem.

**Stage II: Agent Trajectories Rejection Sampling**
After generating QA pairs, high-quality "Thought-Action-Observation" trajectories, based on the ReAct framework, are sampled. The agent's action space is restricted to `search`, `visit`, and `answer`.
- **Short and Long CoT Construction:** Two methods are used to generate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories:
 - **Short CoT:** Directly uses a powerful model, GPT-4o, within the ReAct framework to collect trajectories.
 - **Long CoT:** Sequentially provides a Large Reasoning Model (LRM), QwQ-Plus, with historical actions and observations to autonomously decide the next action. The LRM's intermediate reasoning is recorded as "thought" even if not explicitly provided as input to subsequent steps.
- **Trajectories Filtering:** A three-stage funnel-based filtering framework ensures quality:
 - **Validity Control:** Discards non-compliant (non-ReAct format) data, especially under long-content conditions.
 - **Correctness Verification:** Retains only trajectories leading to correct answers, verified by GPT-4o.
 - **Quality Assessment:** Applies rules to filter out trajectories with excessive actions, hallucinations, or severe repetitions, and further filters based on criteria like Information Non-redundancy, Goal Alignment, and Logical Reasoning and Accuracy. This ensures that only high-quality, coherent trajectories are used for training, with unutilized QA pairs reserved for the RL stage.

**Stage III: Agent Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Cold Start**
The filtered ReAct-formatted trajectories are used to fine-tune a policy model ($\pi_\theta$).
- **Purpose:** SFT provides a "cold start," enhancing the model's capability to couple multiple reasoning and action steps, teaching it the behavioral paradigm of alternating reasoning with action, while preserving its original reasoning capabilities.
- **Loss Function:** The loss function is designed to mask out contributions from "Observation" tokens, focusing on optimizing the agent's autonomous decision steps (Thought and Action) to avoid interference from external feedback.

**Stage IV: Agent Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Enhanced Generalization**
Building upon the SFT-initialized model, RL is employed to internalize agency capabilities and enhance multi-turn, multi-tool usage with outcome-based rewards.
- **DAPO Algorithm:** The Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO) algorithm is used. DAPO optimizes the policy ($\pi_\theta$) to produce higher-reward outputs. It samples candidate agentic executions and updates the policy to maximize a specific objective function that includes an advantage estimator and importance sampling ratio clipping.
- **Dynamic Sampling:** A crucial mechanism in DAPO that over-samples and filters out prompts with 100% or 0% accuracy. This ensures the agent focuses on learning from high-quality signals and ignores invalid or noisy synthetic QA instances that were not used in SFT.
- **Agentic Action Rollout:** During RL, the agent performs iterative Thought-Action-Observation sequences within a real-world search environment. Each round begins with thought generation, followed by a tool call (`search` or `visit`), and concludes with an observation. The rollout ends with an `answer` action.
- **Reward Design:** A composite reward function is used: $R(\hat{y}_i, y) = 0.1 \times \text{score}_{\text{format}} + 0.9 \times \text{score}_{\text{answer}}$. `score_format` is binary (1 for strict adherence to ReAct format), and `score_answer` is also binary (1 if the response is judged correct by an LLM-as-Judge, Qwen-72B-Instruct, as F1/EM metrics are unreliable for non-verifiable answers). The higher weight on `score_answer` reflects the primary goal.

This systematic pipeline, from data generation to a two-stage training approach (SFT + RL), is designed to efficiently develop robust and generalizable information-seeking web agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The WebDancer framework was empirically evaluated on challenging deep information-seeking benchmarks: GAIA and WebWalkerQA, with additional tests on BrowseComp (English) and BrowseComp-zh (Chinese). The primary evaluation metric was Pass@1, using an LLM-as-Judge paradigm.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Superiority of Agentic Approaches:** Frameworks without agentic capabilities (e.g., Qwen-2.5-7B Base, GPT-4o Base, RAG) performed poorly, underscoring the critical need for active information-seeking and agentic decision-making for these complex tasks.
2. **WebDancer's Strong Performance:**
 - **Significant Gains over Baselines:** WebDancer consistently showed substantial improvements over vanilla ReAct baselines across different model scales (Qwen-2.5-7B, Qwen-2.5-32B, QwQ-32B). For instance, with QwQ-32B, WebDancer achieved 51.5% average on GAIA compared to Vanilla ReAct's 37.8%, and 47.9% on WebWalkerQA compared to 24.1%.
 - **Competitiveness with Closed-Source Models:** In its best configuration (QwQ-32B), WebDancer even surpassed the performance of GPT-4o's vanilla ReAct implementation, demonstrating the efficacy of its training paradigm in enhancing underlying base models. While OpenAI Deep Research achieved higher scores on GAIA (67.4%), WebDancer's performance (51.5% with QwQ-32B) is highly competitive among open-source frameworks.
 - **Robustness on Challenging Benchmarks:** WebDancer maintained strong performance on the more challenging BrowseComp (En.) and BrowseComp-zh (Zh.) datasets, indicating its robustness in complex reasoning and multilingual information-seeking scenarios.
3. **Impact of Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Generalization and Consistency:** For non-reasoning models (Qwen-7B, Qwen-32B), RL led to substantial improvements in Pass@1, Pass@3, and Cons@3 (consistency over three attempts). This suggests RL effectively enhances generalization and enables more efficient sampling of correct responses.
 - **Emergent Sophisticated Reasoning:** RL frameworks facilitated the emergence of more sophisticated reasoning strategies and longer, more coherent reasoning trajectories, allowing models to optimize over sequences of decisions and learn from delayed rewards.
 - **Limitations for LRMs:** For LRMs like QwQ-32B, while consistency slightly improved, the gains in Pass@1 and Pass@3 after RL were marginal, possibly due to sparse reward signals from excessively long trajectories. This suggests that continuous on-policy optimization might offer limited benefits for LRMs in some agentic tasks.
4. **Crucial Role of High-Quality Data:**
 - **Effectiveness of Synthetic Datasets:** The constructed CRAWLQA and E2HQA datasets proved effective. Ablation studies (Figure 3) showed that a combination of these datasets, especially after robust filtering, significantly improved performance. "Final" filtered data performed better than "All" unfiltered data under low-data regimes.
 - **SFT for Cold Start is Essential:** SFT for cold start was identified as crucial. A single RL setting without SFT yielded significantly limited performance (only 5% Pass@3 on GAIA with QwQ), highlighting SFT's role in establishing fundamental multi-step, multi-tool instruction-following capabilities.
5. **Challenges in CoT Knowledge Transfer:** Transferring "thinking pattern knowledge" from strong reasoner models (Long-CoT) to smaller instruction models (Short-CoT) proved difficult. While Long-CoT showed good performance for reasoning models, it led to higher invalid rates (e.g., repetition, exceeding context length) in non-reasoning models, indicating that these patterns don't easily transfer to models optimized primarily for task-following.
6. **Dynamic Web Environment Instability:** Agent performance in web environments exhibited fluctuations largely independent of decoding temperature, suggesting that the non-stationary, evolving nature of the real-world web environment itself is a significant factor in agent instability and generalization challenges.

The case study (Figure 8) further illustrated WebDancer's emergent sophisticated thinking patterns, including step-by-step decomposition, hypothesis testing, handling information gaps, and iterative reflection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WebDancer research makes several significant contributions to the field of autonomous AI agents and information retrieval, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Systematic Framework for Agent Development:** The paper provides a crucial end-to-end systematic framework for building deep information-seeking web agents from scratch. This structured approach, encompassing data synthesis, trajectory sampling, SFT, and RL, offers a clear roadmap for researchers and developers, streamlining the process of creating capable agentic models.
2. **Advancements in Data Generation:** The introduction of scalable QA data synthesis methods (CRAWLQA and E2HQA) is highly significant. These methods address the critical bottleneck of insufficient and shallow training data, enabling the creation of diverse and challenging datasets essential for training agents for long-horizon, multi-step reasoning in real-world web environments. This reduces reliance on manually curated datasets, which are expensive and limited.
3. **Effective Two-Stage Training Paradigm:** The proposed two-stage training pipeline, combining SFT for an effective "cold start" with subsequent on-policy RL (using DAPO) for enhanced generalization, is a key innovation. This strategy efficiently leverages both human-demonstrated behaviors and outcome-based rewards, addressing issues of training stability and sample efficiency that plague many RL-only approaches in complex agentic tasks.
4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** WebDancer's strong empirical performance on challenging benchmarks like GAIA, WebWalkerQA, and BrowseComp demonstrates its enhanced ability to generalize and operate robustly in dynamic web environments. This brings autonomous agents closer to tackling real-world, open-ended information-seeking tasks effectively.
5. **Insights into Agent Learning Dynamics:** The detailed analysis provides valuable insights into critical aspects of agent training, such as the differential impact of RL on reasoning vs. non-reasoning models, the importance of SFT for foundational capabilities, the challenges of CoT knowledge transfer, and the inherent instability introduced by dynamic web environments. These findings inform future research directions and help optimize training strategies.
6. **Democratization of Agentic AI:** By focusing on open-source models (Qwen series, QwQ-32B) and providing an end-to-end pipeline, this work contributes to democratizing access to powerful agentic AI capabilities. This can foster broader innovation and application development within the AI community.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Productivity:** Autonomous web agents like WebDancer could significantly boost productivity in various domains by automating complex research tasks, data collection, and information synthesis for scientists, analysts, and general users.
- **Next-Generation Search and Assistants:** These agents lay the groundwork for more intelligent and proactive search engines and virtual assistants capable of deep information retrieval and multi-step problem-solving rather than just keyword-based results.
- **Scientific Discovery and Education:** Facilitating autonomous information seeking could accelerate scientific discovery by rapidly processing and synthesizing vast amounts of online information, and enhance educational experiences by providing personalized and in-depth research support.
- **Foundation for Advanced AI Systems:** The systematic approach and insights gained from WebDancer contribute foundational knowledge for building even more sophisticated AI systems capable of truly general-purpose intelligence and autonomous operation in diverse digital environments.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by authors):**
The authors acknowledge limitations such as the current integration of only two basic information-seeking tools, the need to expand to document-level research and generation tasks, challenges in efficient data utilization (especially in RL), high rollout costs in RL, and the need for hybrid thinking models that dynamically control reasoning length. Addressing these will be crucial for the continued advancement of autonomous information-seeking agents. The broader impact section also highlights concerns about misinformation propagation and misuse, emphasizing the need for responsible deployment.
