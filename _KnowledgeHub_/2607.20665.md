# Safe and Scalable Multi-Drone Payload Transport via CBF-based Reinforcement Learning with Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> A learning-based framework enables safe and scalable cooperative multi-drone payload transport, achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer from a 2D simulation to real 3D systems. The approach allows fully distributed operation in dynamic environments, with teams of up to six drones successfully avoiding moving obstacles.

## Problem

- Existing multi-drone payload transport systems lack scalability due to reliance on centralized control frameworks and challenges posed by complex coupled dynamics.
- Ensuring safety, particularly reactive collision avoidance in dynamic and unstructured environments, remains challenging for distributed multi-drone systems.
- Learning-based control methods for multi-drone transport often struggle with robust sim-to-real transfer and the integration of explicit safety guarantees.

## Method

- A learning-based framework utilizing a ==minimal 2D abstraction== for efficient simulation and ==domain randomization== to enhance policy robustness and generalization.
- **Discrete Graph Control Barrier Function Proximal Policy Optimization (DGPPO)** for learning shared-parameter distributed policies that ensure safety through ==DGCBFs== in the observation space.
- A formal analysis bridging discrete-time safety guarantees from DGCBFs to continuous-time execution on physical robots via ==tracking-error–tightened constraints==.

## Results

- DGPPO achieved superior safety rates in simulation compared to other distributed policy optimization methods while maintaining competitive task performance.
- A single learned policy demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, successfully controlling up to **six** physical drones for payload transport in **10** diverse scenarios, even for team sizes beyond its training range (3-5 drones).
- Multiple independent drone teams successfully transported payloads, perceiving each other as dynamic obstacles via LiDAR and executing collision-free trajectories without additional policy fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A minimal 2D abstraction can effectively capture essential multi-drone payload dynamics, enabling efficient policy learning and successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to 3D hardware.
> - The integration of Discrete Graph Control Barrier Functions (DGCBFs) within a reinforcement learning framework allows for learning robust, fully distributed, and reactively safe control policies in dynamic multi-agent settings.
> - Domain randomization of team size and physical parameters during training enables a single learned policy to generalize effectively to unseen team sizes and diverse real-world conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Cooperative payload transportation by multi-robot systems is a fundamental problem in robotics, with cable-suspended multi-drone systems representing a promising instantiation due to their structural simplicity, modularity, and adaptability. These systems hold potential for various practical applications such as last-mile delivery, disaster relief, automated construction, and logistics.

However, existing approaches face limitations in scalability and safety, particularly when deployed in dynamic and unstructured real-world environments. The complex coupled dynamics among drones, cables, and payloads present significant challenges. Prior research efforts in multi-drone payload transport have largely focused on centralized control frameworks, which, while capable of precise object transport for small teams, often require global state information and extensive computation, thereby limiting their scalability in hardware demonstrations. Decentralized and distributed control strategies have been explored, leveraging local sensing and limited communication, with some demonstrating feasibility for two or three drones. However, many rely on leader-based architectures or are primarily validated in simulation.

Learning-based control approaches are recognized for their potential to handle nonlinear dynamics and partial observability. Nevertheless, successful sim-to-real transfer in this domain remains limited, and explicit safety guarantees are frequently not addressed. In terms of safety, current safety-aware methods for multi-drone transport often depend on centralized, model-based optimization or internal system redundancy, which can hinder scalability and distributed deployment.

This work addresses the gap in achieving scalable and safe multi-drone payload transport with reactive environment-level safety under fully distributed execution. Specifically, the authors highlight that, to their knowledge, no prior work has demonstrated real-world cable-suspended multi-drone transportation with a safe, fully distributed policy in dynamic environments involving moving obstacles. The research aims to advance the practical feasibility of learning-based, distributed aerial collaboration in complex physical environments, moving beyond the limitations of small team sizes, structured environments, and centralized safety mechanisms prevalent in existing literature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a learning-based framework that enables safe and scalable cooperative multi-drone payload transportation. The motivation stems from several key challenges inherent in current multi-drone systems:

- **Complexity of Coupled Dynamics:** The intricate interactions between drones, cables, and payloads make modeling and control challenging, particularly in real-world scenarios.
- **Scalability Limitations:** Most real-world demonstrations of multi-drone payload transport are limited to a few drones (typically two or three) due to the challenges of managing coupled interactions, inter-agent dynamics, sensing noise, and hardware heterogeneity as team size increases. Larger teams are desirable for increased payload capacity, better load distribution, and deployment flexibility.
- **Safety Requirements in Dynamic Environments:** Ensuring safety, such as collision avoidance and workspace boundary adherence, is critical for real-world deployment, especially in cluttered or dynamic environments with moving obstacles. Existing safety mechanisms often rely on centralized control and explicit environment representations, which are not conducive to scalable, distributed systems.
- **Gap in Distributed Safety and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** There is a need for fully distributed policies that can reactively ensure safety in complex environments, combined with robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities for learning-based approaches, which have predominantly remained in simulation.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this work are articulated through its main contributions:

- **Develop an efficient simulation and domain randomization strategy for scalable and safe policy learning:** This involves creating a computationally efficient simulation environment based on a minimal 2D abstraction that captures essential drone-payload coupling, coupled with domain randomization techniques to ensure the learned policy generalizes across varying team sizes and physical parameters while enforcing safety constraints.
- **Bridge learned discrete-time safety to continuous-time robotic execution:** This objective aims to formalize the conditions under which safety guarantees obtained from discrete-time learned control barrier functions can be extended to continuous-time execution on physical robotic systems, accounting for the realities of hierarchical tracking control and bounded tracking errors.
- **Demonstrate scalable and safe real-world multi-drone transportation:** The ultimate goal is to validate the proposed framework through extensive hardware experiments, showcasing reliable payload transport with teams of varying sizes (including those exceeding training ranges) and in dynamic environments where other drone teams act as moving obstacles, all executed by a single, learned, fully distributed policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a learning-based framework for multi-drone cooperative payload transport, structured around a 2D abstraction, a distributed reinforcement learning algorithm with integrated safety, domain randomization, and a formal analysis for continuous-time safety.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is formulated as a multi-agent constrained optimal control problem under partial observability. Each of `N` drones collaboratively transports a suspended payload to a target pose (`p_g`, `θ_g`) while avoiding unsafe configurations (collisions, boundary violations). The state of drone `i` is `x_k_i = (p_k_i, v_k_i)`, and the control input `u_k_i` is commanded horizontal acceleration. The full system state includes all drone states, payload position, orientation, and velocities. Drones operate with partial observations (`o_k_i`), comprising inter-agent features, payload features, and obstacle features (LiDAR-based), along with the goal pose. Safety is defined by `M` local constraint functions (`h^(m)_i`) for each agent. The objective is to learn a distributed policy `π` that minimizes a cumulative task cost while satisfying these safety constraints.

#### 3.2. 2D Abstraction for Multi-Drone Transport

To manage the complexity of full 3D dynamics and facilitate efficient learning and sim-to-real transfer, a minimal 2D abstraction is adopted. This abstraction models drones as point masses connected to a planar rigid body payload via virtual springs. Key assumptions include regulated altitude, cable tension proportional to 2D projected length, negligible payload roll/pitch, and fixed, evenly distributed cable attachment points. The payload dynamics are simplified to planar translation and yaw, driven by the sum of virtual spring forces from the drones. These dynamics are discretized for policy learning and safety certification.

#### 3.3. Learning Safe Distributed Policies with DGPPO

- **Discrete Graph Control Barrier Function Proximal Policy Optimization (DGPPO):** The framework employs DGPPO, a sim-to-real transfer approach where the policy is trained in simulation and then deployed on hardware without fine-tuning. DGPPO enables learning a shared-parameter distributed policy for multi-drone systems under partial observability. Each agent's policy utilizes local graph-based observations and a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) for internal memory. DGPPO jointly learns a control policy and a constraint value function, which serves as a Discrete Graph Control Barrier Function (DGCBF). DGCBFs ensure safety by certifying it directly in the observation space using local information, allowing for distributed safety assessment and filtering of unsafe policy updates.
- **Domain Randomization:** To enhance policy robustness and generalization, domain randomization is applied during training. The number of agents is randomly selected from `{3, 4, 5}` for each episode. The virtual spring stiffness `K`, which models the cable tension, is also randomized within a range of `[0.05, 0.30] N m^-1`. This randomization aims to make the policy effective across varied tether dynamics and improve sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.4. Continuous-Time Safety under Hierarchical Control

A formal safety analysis is provided to bridge the gap between discrete-time safety guarantees from DGCBFs and continuous-time execution on physical systems. It assumes a hierarchical control architecture where discrete-time decisions from the policy are tracked by a high-bandwidth low-level controller. Under assumptions of bounded tracking error (`ε_trk`), Lipschitz safety constraints, and observation sensitivity to tracking error, the framework introduces "tracking-error–tightened constraints." Theorem 1 demonstrates that if the distributed policy ensures these tightened constraints are forward invariant at discrete decision steps on the reference observations, then the original continuous-time constraints hold for all time on the actual physical system.

#### 3.5. Simulation Experiments

The approach is evaluated in a JAX-based simulation environment using the 2D abstraction. Scenarios involve `N ∈ {3, 4, 5}` agents transporting a rigid `N`-gon payload amidst circular obstacles. Agents are equipped with planar LiDAR sensors and communicate with neighbors within a specified radius. Observations include kinematic states, payload pose/velocity, goal, and LiDAR returns, with injected uniform noise to simulate sensing uncertainty. Policies are trained for `10^5` steps across 128 parallel environments.
- **Reward Function:** Designed to encourage payload navigation to the target pose, penalizing position and orientation errors, high thrust when far from the goal, control effort, and jerkiness.
- **Safety Constraints:** Per-agent constraints quantify inter-agent collisions, agent-obstacle collisions, and deviation from assigned payload attachment vertices. These are margin-shifted and clipped.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Average reward and safety rate (fraction of agents satisfying all safety constraints).
- **Backbone Comparison:** DGPPO is compared against other policy optimization backbones such as InforMARL (with penalty variants) and MAPPO-Lagrangian variants.

#### 3.6. Hardware Experiments

The learned policies are deployed zero-shot on Crazyflie 2.1 quadrotors. Drones are uniformly distributed around 3D-printed or laser-cut rigid payloads connected by 0.50m cables. A centralized computer handles state estimation, but execution is decentralized, with local observations provided to each agent's policy. The policy outputs planar accelerations (10 Hz), which are integrated into desired waypoints tracked by an onboard PID controller. Mean position tracking error is typically between 3-5 cm. Experiments include 10 test scenarios (5 "Easy," 5 "Hard") with varying obstacle configurations, as well as generalization tests with teams of 3 to 6 drones.

#### 3.7. Multi-Group Multi-Drone Transportation

The framework's ability to handle dynamic obstacles is tested with multi-group hardware experiments. Two independent teams (e.g., a 5-agent group and a 3-agent group) transport separate payloads while perceiving the other team's drones as dynamic obstacles via LiDAR, without additional fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations, both in simulation and on hardware, indicate the efficacy and generalization capabilities of the proposed framework.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Performance Comparison:** DGPPO demonstrated a favorable reward-safety trade-off compared to other distributed policy optimization backbones, including Lagrangian and penalty-based variants. It consistently maintained high safety rates while achieving competitive task rewards. For instance, under a stricter goal threshold, DGPPO achieved an average hit rate of 84% compared to 59% for the best penalty variant.
- **Scalability:** The learned policy exhibited robust generalization to team sizes outside the training range, effectively controlling 6 and 8 drones despite being trained on teams of 3-5 drones. Safety rates remained high across these larger team configurations.
- **Robustness to Uncertainty:** The trained DGPPO policy showed robustness to sensing and communication uncertainties. Under 2% message dropout, the safety rate decreased from a nominal 76.2% to 72.4%. With 5% dropout and significant LiDAR noise (σ = 0.045m), the safety rate still remained above 50%, demonstrating a graceful degradation of performance.

#### 4.2. Hardware Experiments (Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer)

- **Successful Task Completion:** Teams of three drones successfully transported payloads to desired goal positions and orientations in all 10 designed hardware test scenarios (5 easy, 5 hard). This involved both straightforward trajectories and more complex maneuvers requiring active obstacle avoidance.
- **Scalability and Generalization:** A single learned policy, trained with domain randomization for team sizes 3-5, successfully generalized to unseen configurations, including stable payload transport with 4, 5, and 6 drones. Although one 6-drone "Hard" scenario saw the payload reach the target region but not perfectly settle into the final pose (0.5m position error), this was attributed to reward shaping limitations for terminal pose refinement rather than a safety failure.
- **Robustness to Dynamic Environments:** In multi-group hardware experiments, two independent teams (a 5-agent and a 3-agent group) successfully transported their respective payloads. Agents perceived drones from the other group as dynamic obstacles via LiDAR sensing and executed collision-free trajectories without any additional fine-tuning of the policy. This demonstrated the framework's capability to operate safely in dynamic and interactive environments.
- **Empirical Validation of Abstraction:** The successful zero-shot transfer from a minimal 2D simulation abstraction to real-world 3D Crazyflie systems provides empirical support for the effectiveness of modeling cable-suspended transport with a simplified decision abstraction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant advancement in the field of multi-robot systems, particularly for cooperative aerial payload transportation. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a framework for **safe, scalable, and fully distributed multi-drone operations in complex real-world dynamic environments**, which has been a persistent challenge.

Key impacts include:

- **Enabling Scalable Multi-Drone Systems:** By successfully deploying a single learned policy on teams of up to six drones, exceeding the training range, the research addresses a critical limitation of existing methods that struggle to scale beyond a few agents. This opens avenues for larger aerial teams to undertake tasks requiring higher payload capacities or distributed forces, such as in automated construction or heavy-lift logistics.
- **Enhanced Safety in Dynamic Environments:** The integration of Discrete Graph Control Barrier Functions (DGCBFs) within a reinforcement learning framework, coupled with a formal bridge to continuous-time safety, provides a robust mechanism for reactive collision avoidance and constraint satisfaction. The multi-group experiments, where other drone teams act as dynamic obstacles, highlight the policy's ability to ensure safety in inherently unpredictable and interactive settings without prior explicit modeling of dynamic obstacles.
- **Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot transfer of policies learned in a minimal 2D abstraction to physical 3D quadrotors without fine-tuning is a notable achievement. This suggests that high-fidelity 3D modeling may not always be necessary for initial policy learning in complex physical systems, potentially reducing development time and computational resources. The empirical validation of the 2D abstraction and the preservation of learned safety certificates in the real world are valuable contributions to sim-to-real methodology.
- **Practical Feasibility of Distributed Learning-Based Control:** The work establishes the practical feasibility of fully distributed, learning-based aerial collaboration, moving beyond theoretical or simulation-only results. This is crucial for real-world applications where centralized control may be impractical due to communication limitations or single points of failure.
- **Broadening Application Scope:** The capabilities demonstrated in this paper could enable the use of multi-drone systems in more challenging and unstructured applications, including disaster response (e.g., transporting aid through debris), complex industrial logistics (e.g., moving large components in factories), and adaptable infrastructure construction.

While currently limited to planar payload transport with evenly distributed cable attachment points, the framework provides a foundation for future extensions. Future work to investigate broader attachment configurations, varied cable properties, diverse payload variations, and full three-dimensional transportation holds the potential to further expand the impact and applicability of this research.
