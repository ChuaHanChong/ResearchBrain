# Thinking with Images for Multimodal Reasoning: Foundations, Methods, and Future Frontiers

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey introduces "Thinking with Images," a new paradigm where AI models use visual representations as dynamic, manipulable intermediate steps in reasoning, moving beyond static visual inputs. It proposes a three-stage evolutionary framework—Tool-Driven Visual Exploration, Programmatic Visual Manipulation, and Intrinsic Visual Imagination—to categorize and guide the development of AI systems capable of active visual cognition.

## Problem

- Current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) primarily "Think about Images," encoding visual inputs into static features, with subsequent reasoning conducted almost exclusively within the textual domain.
- This creates a "semantic gap" where the richness of continuous visual data is compressed and becomes a passive, fixed context for discrete symbolic thought.
- This limitation leads to brittleness in tasks requiring iterative visual engagement, fine-grained detail analysis, or complex physical reasoning.

## Method

- The paper defines the =="Thinking with Images" paradigm==, where vision transitions from a passive input to a dynamic, manipulable cognitive workspace, akin to a human's mental sketchpad.
- It proposes a ==three-stage evolutionary framework== -- ==Tool-Driven Visual Exploration==, ==Programmatic Visual Manipulation==, and ==Intrinsic Visual Imagination== -- to categorize and guide the development of AI systems capable of active visual cognition.
- The survey systematically reviews core methods (==prompt-based==, ==SFT-based==, ==RL-based==) for implementing these stages and analyzes relevant evaluation benchmarks and applications.

## Results

- The survey formally establishes and distinguishes the "Thinking with Images" paradigm, defining it by its core principle: utilizing visual representations as manipulable and verifiable intermediate steps in a cognitive process.
- It proposes a three-stage evolutionary framework for increasing cognitive autonomy: Tool-Driven Visual Exploration, Programmatic Visual Manipulation, and Intrinsic Visual Imagination.
- The paper identifies unique challenges to the "Thinking with Images" paradigm, including high computational cost, error propagation due to visual information density, architectural divides between modalities, and challenges in cross-task generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The transition from "Thinking about Images" to "Thinking with Images" is crucial for overcoming the "semantic gap" in multimodal reasoning and achieving more robust, human-aligned AI.
> - The proposed three-stage evolutionary framework (Tool-Driven Visual Exploration, Programmatic Visual Manipulation, Intrinsic Visual Imagination) provides a novel taxonomy for understanding how Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) can develop active visual reasoning capabilities.
> - "Thinking with Images" enables dynamic perceptual exploration, structured visual reasoning (using images as cognitive scratchpads), and goal-oriented generative planning, which are key for advanced multimodal intelligence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper positions itself at the forefront of multimodal AI research by explicitly identifying and structuring a fundamental paradigm shift: the evolution from models that "Think *about* Images" to those that can truly "Think *with* Images."

Historically, the rapid progress in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has largely been driven by adapting textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to multimodal contexts. In this established "Thinking about Images" paradigm, visual inputs are processed once and encoded into static features, with subsequent reasoning conducted almost exclusively within the textual domain. While powerful for many tasks, this approach suffers from a "semantic gap," where the richness of continuous visual data is compressed and becomes a passive, fixed context for discrete symbolic thought. This limitation leads to brittleness in tasks requiring iterative visual engagement, fine-grained detail, or complex physical reasoning.

This survey argues that a "new revolution is quietly unfolding," where vision transitions from a passive input to a dynamic, manipulable cognitive workspace. This is akin to human cognition, which often uses vision as a "mental sketchpad." The paper distinguishes itself from prior surveys by focusing specifically on the *mechanisms* that enable this active visual thinking. Earlier reviews, such as those by Yin et al., Zhang et al. [2024c], and Wu et al. [2023a], primarily catalogue architectures, pre-training corpora, and evaluation protocols, often treating visual information as a context rather than an active medium of thought. Even more recent surveys on multimodal CoT [Wang et al., 2025g] or perception-reason-plan pipelines [Li et al., 2025e] largely conceptualize the visual modality as passive, where models describe or annotate an image once before proceeding with textual deliberation or tool calling.

In contrast, "Thinking with Images" fundamentally redefines what constitutes a reasoning step. The paper systematically categorizes this evolution across three stages of increasing cognitive autonomy: from external tool exploration, through programmatic manipulation, to intrinsic imagination. This structured overview provides a novel taxonomy for understanding how LMMs are developing internal visual reasoning capabilities, making the image an active participant in the thought process rather than merely a static source of information. By charting this progression and outlining foundational methods, evaluations, applications, and challenges, the survey provides a critical roadmap for future research toward more powerful and human-aligned multimodal AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this survey stems from the identified fundamental limitation of current multimodal reasoning: the reliance on text-centric reasoning over a static visual context. While textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) has been transformative, it creates a "semantic gap" between rich perceptual data and discrete symbolic thought. Humans, however, often "think with images," using vision as a dynamic mental sketchpad to explore, manipulate, and generate visual information during reasoning. This survey argues that AI needs to evolve similarly.

The key objectives of this survey are four-fold:
1. **Establish Foundational Principles:** To formally define the "Thinking with Images" paradigm, articulate its cognitive imperative (why it's needed), and propose a three-stage evolutionary framework that charts its progression in increasing cognitive autonomy.
2. **Comprehensive Methodological Review:** To provide a systematic review of the core methods and implementation approaches (prompt-based, SFT-based, RL-based) characterizing each of the three stages: Tool-Driven Visual Exploration, Programmatic Visual Manipulation, and Intrinsic Visual Imagination.
3. **Analyze Evaluation Benchmarks and Applications:** To examine the critical landscape of evaluation benchmarks designed to assess these advanced visual reasoning capabilities and explore the transformative applications where this paradigm shows significant promise.
4. **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** To delineate the unique challenges inherent to "Thinking with Images" (e.g., computational cost, information density, architectural divides, generalization) and outline promising future research frontiers to address these issues and unlock the paradigm's full potential.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to offer a clear roadmap for future research, inspiring the development of more robust, intuitive, and human-aligned multimodal AI systems that can genuinely "think with images" rather than merely "think about images."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey, the methodology of this paper is to systematically review, categorize, and synthesize existing research in the emerging field of "Thinking with Images." The core approach is built upon a novel **three-stage conceptual framework** that organizes the evolution of multimodal reasoning based on increasing cognitive autonomy, specifically defining how models utilize visual information as intermediate steps.

The three stages are:
1. **Stage 1: Tool-Driven Visual Exploration:** In this initial stage, the model acts as a planner or orchestrator. It learns to select and invoke a predefined suite of external visual tools (e.g., object detectors, OCR, zoom functions) to query and analyze visual content. The intelligence lies in sequencing these fixed capabilities for targeted data gathering.
2. **Stage 2: Programmatic Visual Manipulation:** This stage represents a leap in autonomy where the model becomes a "visual programmer." Instead of merely selecting tools, it generates executable code (e.g., Python scripts using computer vision libraries) to perform custom, tailored visual operations. This enables compositional flexibility, dynamic control flow, and enhanced interpretability.
3. **Stage 3: Intrinsic Visual Imagination:** At the most advanced stage, the model achieves full cognitive autonomy by internally generating new visual states as part of its reasoning process, without relying on external execution environments. This involves unifying generative and reasoning capabilities to perform implicit latent reasoning, explicit visual reasoning (via "visual scratchpads"), or interleaved multimodal reasoning.

For each of these three stages, the survey further dissects the **implementation approaches**:
- **Prompt-Based Approaches:** These methods leverage in-context learning, where carefully designed prompts guide large language models (LLMs) or LMMs to orchestrate tools or generate code. This approach enables visual reasoning without parameter updates.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-Based Approaches:** SFT is used to teach models procedural competence by fine-tuning them on datasets that demonstrate multi-step visual reasoning, tool invocation, code generation, or the creation of intermediate visual representations.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-Based Approaches:** RL enables models to discover and optimize policies for effective tool use, programmatic code generation, or intrinsic visual simulation through environmental interaction and reward feedback, moving beyond imitation learning.

Beyond this core taxonomy, the survey also includes dedicated sections on:
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Reviewing existing and identifying needs for new benchmarks that can adequately assess multi-step visual reasoning, particularly those requiring visual manipulation or generation (e.g., mathematical reasoning, STEM, puzzles, code generation, chart/table reasoning, real-world applications).
- **Implementation Frameworks:** Discussing the software infrastructure (e.g., LangChain, LLaMAFactory, VeRL) that supports the development and training of these "Thinking with Images" models across different implementation approaches.
- **Applications:** Exploring various domains (Interactive Systems/UIs, Embodied AI/Robotics, AI for Science, Healthcare/Medicine, Education/Training) where the paradigm offers significant transformative potential.
- **Future Directions:** Identifying open challenges and outlining research avenues, such as computational/cognitive efficiency, safety/trustworthiness, novel benchmarks, and expansion to other dynamic modalities (audio, video) and embodied agency.

This structured, systematic, and forward-looking approach allows the survey to provide a comprehensive and insightful overview of a rapidly evolving and highly impactful field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core "findings" of this survey are encapsulated in its comprehensive taxonomy and the articulation of the "Thinking with Images" paradigm's necessity and inherent challenges. The paper systematically synthesizes a fragmented research landscape into a coherent framework, offering a structured understanding of how AI is moving towards more human-like visual cognition.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Paradigm Shift Defined:** The survey formally establishes and distinguishes "Thinking with Images" from the prior "Thinking about Images" paradigm. It defines the former by its core principle: utilizing visual representations as manipulable and verifiable intermediate steps in a cognitive process. This shift transforms vision from a passive input to a dynamic cognitive workspace, crucial for overcoming the "semantic gap" and brittleness of text-centric reasoning.
2. **Three-Stage Evolutionary Framework:** The most significant contribution is the proposed three-stage evolution of cognitive autonomy:
 - **Stage 1 (Tool-Driven Visual Exploration):** Models orchestrate a fixed set of external visual tools (e.g., OCR, object detectors, zoom/crop functions) to perform active perceptual inquiry. This allows for targeted data gathering and semantic grounding but is limited by the predefined toolset.
 - **Stage 2 (Programmatic Visual Manipulation):** Models generate executable code to perform custom visual analyses, offering compositional flexibility, dynamic control flow, and enhanced interpretability. This allows for tailored problem-solving but still relies on an external execution environment.
 - **Stage 3 (Intrinsic Visual Imagination):** Models achieve full cognitive autonomy by internally generating new visual states (e.g., imagined future frames, diagrams with auxiliary lines) as a native part of their thought process. This unifies generation and reasoning, enabling implicit simulation and true visual chains of thought within a closed cognitive loop.
3. **Implementation Methodologies:** For each stage, the survey details how prompt-based, SFT-based, and RL-based approaches are employed to enable these capabilities, illustrating how different training paradigms contribute to the development of visual thinking skills.
4. **Cognitive Imperative and Capabilities:** The paper identifies three key capabilities enabled by "Thinking with Images":
 - **Dynamic Perceptual Exploration:** Active, iterative inquiry to transcend single holistic interpretations.
 - **Structured Visual Reasoning:** Using the image as a "cognitive scratchpad" to externalize and ground abstract logical steps.
 - **Goal-Oriented Generative Planning:** Leveraging generative capacity for simulation and "perceptual self-critique" (e.g., predicting consequences of actions).
5. **Unique Challenges Identified:** The paradigm, while powerful, introduces significant obstacles:
 - **Computational Cost:** The "token explosion" of visual information makes long, multi-step visual reasoning computationally prohibitive.
 - **Information Density:** Errors in visual steps propagate in uniquely damaging ways, establishing false ground truths.
 - **Architectural Divide:** Most models use modular designs, creating bottlenecks between language and pixel domains, hindering fluid iterative visual thinking.
 - **Cross-Task Generalization:** Different visual tasks require distinct reasoning strategies, posing a challenge for a single, generalizable visual thinker.

In essence, the survey's "results" are not experimental outcomes but a highly structured, forward-looking analysis that maps the current state, defines the necessary evolution, and outlines the critical path for multimodal AI to truly reason with images.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this survey lies in its timely and comprehensive articulation of a nascent yet profoundly impactful paradigm shift in artificial intelligence. By systematically defining "Thinking with Images," the paper provides a crucial conceptual framework and roadmap for the future of multimodal AI.

**Significance:**

- **Definitive Conceptual Framework:** The survey is the first to systematically categorize the mechanisms enabling the transition from passive visual processing to dynamic visual cognition. The three-stage taxonomy (Tool-Driven Exploration, Programmatic Manipulation, Intrinsic Imagination) provides researchers with a shared vocabulary and structured understanding of this complex field, fostering more organized and targeted research efforts.
- **Highlighting a Cognitive Imperative:** The paper compellingly argues that for AI to achieve more robust, human-aligned intelligence, it must overcome the limitations of text-centric reasoning and genuinely "think with images." This imperative is grounded in how human cognition leverages visual modalities for dynamic perception, structured reasoning, and generative planning.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By meticulously reviewing existing methods (prompting, SFT, RL), benchmarks, and implementation frameworks, the survey not only synthesizes current knowledge but also explicitly identifies critical gaps and promising future directions. This includes calls for more efficient architectures, robust self-correction mechanisms, safety-aware development, and novel evaluation methodologies that assess the *process* of visual thought, not just the final outcome.
- **Bridging Academia and Industry:** The multi-institutional authorship, including researchers from both leading academic institutions and major industry players like Microsoft, lends significant authority to the survey. This collaboration bridges theoretical advancements with practical considerations, influencing both fundamental research and the development of real-world AI applications.

**Potential Impact:**

The "Thinking with Images" paradigm has the potential to revolutionize a wide array of domains:

- **Interactive Systems and User Interfaces:** Enabling AI agents to intuitively understand and interact with digital interfaces (web, desktop, mobile) purely from pixel inputs, leading to more versatile and generalizable UI automation.
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Empowering robots with advanced planning capabilities through internal visual simulation of actions and their consequences, fostering more robust and intelligent autonomous agents in complex physical environments.
- **AI for Science:** Transforming scientific discovery by allowing models to actively analyze complex visual data (e.g., microscopy, remote sensing), simulate physical phenomena, and generate visual hypotheses, moving beyond data parsing to active scientific reasoning.
- **Healthcare and Medicine:** Enhancing clinical decision-making by enabling AI to perform explainable, multi-step visual diagnoses from medical images, guided by anatomical knowledge, and even simulate clinical consultations, improving reliability and trustworthiness.
- **Education and Training:** Creating dynamic and personalized AI tutors that can visually demonstrate concepts, sketch solutions in real-time, and provide contextual feedback based on visual observations, making abstract subjects more accessible and engaging.

Ultimately, this survey lays the groundwork for AI systems that possess a deeper, more integrated form of multimodal intelligence. The proposed "blueprint for a Unified Visual Thinker" (featuring a Metacognitive Controller and a multi-stage Visual Cognitive Workspace) envisions an AI that can intelligently choose the most effective cognitive strategy—from simple inference to complex visual imagination—for any given task. This capability is fundamental to building more general, robust, and truly intelligent AI agents that can not only comprehend the visual world but also actively reason within it, blurring the lines between perception, thought, and action.
