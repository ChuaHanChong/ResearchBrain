# Scaffolding Dexterous Manipulation with Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University and Karlsruhe Institute of Technology developed a hierarchical framework for dexterous robotic manipulation where Vision-Language Models generate coarse motion plans, which a low-level reinforcement learning policy then precisely tracks. This approach eliminates the need for human demonstrations and complex reward engineering, achieving up to 81% success in simulation and successful zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Collecting high-quality and accurate human demonstrations for training complex dexterous robotic hands is difficult and expensive.
- Designing intricate, task-specific reward functions for reinforcement learning in high-dimensional dexterous manipulation environments is time-consuming and error-prone.
- Existing trajectory-tracking reinforcement learning methods for dexterous manipulation still rely on explicit human demonstrations, limiting their generalizability and scalability.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical== framework combines an off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM) for high-level, coarse 3D keypoint-based motion planning with a low-level residual Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy for precise execution.
- The VLM translates natural language instructions and initial images into sequences of 3D keypoints and wrist poses (scaffolds) for objects and the robot hand.
- A low-level RL policy is trained in simulation using dense, task-agnostic rewards to track these VLM-generated trajectories, promoting stable grasping and fine-tuned control, with sim-to-real transfer enabled by domain randomization.

## Results

- The method achieved an average **81%** success rate across eight simulated dexterous tasks with few-shot VLM plans, outperforming baselines like pre-recorded trajectories (**19%**) and IKER (**32%**).
- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical Allegro hand on a KUKA LBR iiwa arm, achieving **90%** success for "Place Bottle onto Plate" and **85%** for "Slide Box to Bottle."
- Ablation studies showed that VLM-generated trajectory guidance provided a more effective optimization signal than complex, hand-crafted rewards for reinforcement learning, leading to higher success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively provide coarse, semantically-aware motion plans that serve as sufficient "scaffolds" for dexterous manipulation, replacing the need for explicit human demonstrations.
> - Utilizing VLM-generated trajectories to define simple tracking rewards significantly reduces the complexity of reward engineering for reinforcement learning in dexterous tasks.
> - A residual policy structure allows the RL agent to refine motions and adapt to environments while staying anchored to VLM-generated plans, enabling robust sim-to-real transfer without further human demonstrations.
