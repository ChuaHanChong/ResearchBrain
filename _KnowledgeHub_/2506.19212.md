# Scaffolding Dexterous Manipulation with Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University and Karlsruhe Institute of Technology developed a hierarchical framework for dexterous robotic manipulation where Vision-Language Models generate coarse motion plans, which a low-level reinforcement learning policy then precisely tracks. This approach eliminates the need for human demonstrations and complex reward engineering, achieving up to 81% success in simulation and successful zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Collecting high-quality and accurate human demonstrations for training complex dexterous robotic hands is difficult and expensive.
- Designing intricate, task-specific reward functions for reinforcement learning in high-dimensional dexterous manipulation environments is time-consuming and error-prone.
- Existing trajectory-tracking reinforcement learning methods for dexterous manipulation still rely on explicit human demonstrations, limiting their generalizability and scalability.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical== framework combines an off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM) for high-level, coarse 3D keypoint-based motion planning with a low-level residual Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy for precise execution.
- The VLM translates natural language instructions and initial images into sequences of 3D keypoints and wrist poses (scaffolds) for objects and the robot hand.
- A low-level RL policy is trained in simulation using dense, task-agnostic rewards to track these VLM-generated trajectories, promoting stable grasping and fine-tuned control, with sim-to-real transfer enabled by domain randomization.

## Results

- The method achieved an average **81%** success rate across eight simulated dexterous tasks with few-shot VLM plans, outperforming baselines like pre-recorded trajectories (**19%**) and IKER (**32%**).
- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical Allegro hand on a KUKA LBR iiwa arm, achieving **90%** success for "Place Bottle onto Plate" and **85%** for "Slide Box to Bottle."
- Ablation studies showed that VLM-generated trajectory guidance provided a more effective optimization signal than complex, hand-crafted rewards for reinforcement learning, leading to higher success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively provide coarse, semantically-aware motion plans that serve as sufficient "scaffolds" for dexterous manipulation, replacing the need for explicit human demonstrations.
> - Utilizing VLM-generated trajectories to define simple tracking rewards significantly reduces the complexity of reward engineering for reinforcement learning in dexterous tasks.
> - A residual policy structure allows the RL agent to refine motions and adapt to environments while staying anchored to VLM-generated plans, enabling robust sim-to-real transfer without further human demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation in robotics, which involves fine-grained control with human-like hands, has been a persistent challenge. Traditional approaches, such as imitation learning from human demonstrations, have faced limitations due to the difficulty in collecting high-quality, accurate data for complex dexterous hardware. Efforts to re-target demonstrations from simpler interfaces, like human hands, often introduce errors that are problematic for precise tasks.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) combined with sim-to-real transfer has emerged as an alternative, offering large-scale experience generation in simulation to alleviate data bottlenecks. However, this approach shifts the complexity to reward function design. For dexterous manipulation, hand-crafting complex, task-specific reward functions is required, especially for guiding exploration in high-dimensional action spaces.

To mitigate complex reward design, some RL methods have incorporated bootstrapping from demonstrations, often through trajectory tracking. In this paradigm, a policy is rewarded for tracking reference wrist and object positions from demonstrations, allowing RL to refine motions. While this provides dense, task-agnostic rewards and guides exploration, it reintroduces the reliance on demonstrations, which are expensive to collect and may not generalize to novel scenarios.

This work addresses the dependency on explicit human demonstrations in trajectory-tracking RL by proposing the use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate coarse motion plans, referred to as "scaffolds." This approach leverages the semantic and spatial reasoning capabilities of VLMs, which have been trained on extensive and diverse real-world datasets. The paper positions itself by stating that precise details from explicit reference trajectories are often unnecessary, as RL ultimately refines the motion. Instead, coarse VLM-generated plans can provide sufficient guidance and reward signals, aiming to overcome the data scarcity and reward design challenges inherent in previous dexterous manipulation methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalizable framework for training dexterous robotic manipulation policies without relying on human demonstrations or manually engineered reward functions. This overarching goal is pursued through several specific objectives and motivations:

- **Eliminate Demonstration Dependency:** Prior work in trajectory-tracking RL for dexterous manipulation, while effective, paradoxically reintroduced the reliance on large datasets of human demonstrations. These demonstrations are expensive, difficult to collect for high-dimensional dexterous hands, and often do not generalize well to new environments or task variations. The research aims to remove this dependency.
- **Simplify Reward Design:** Standard RL for dexterous manipulation necessitates the creation of intricate, task-specific reward functions to guide exploration and achieve desired behaviors. This process is time-consuming, prone to errors, and hinders the scalability of RL to a broader range of tasks. The objective is to reduce this burden by using VLM-generated trajectories to define a simpler, task-agnostic tracking reward.
- **Leverage Vision-Language Models for High-Level Planning:** The central hypothesis is that modern VLMs, due to their training on vast datasets, possess the commonsense spatial and semantic knowledge required to interpret natural language instructions and synthesize high-level, coarse motion plans. The work aims to demonstrate that these models can effectively identify task-relevant elements (keypoints) and generate plausible trajectories for both the robot hand and manipulated objects.
- **Develop a Hierarchical Control Framework:** The research seeks to establish a modular, hierarchical approach where a VLM handles high-level strategic planning (generating coarse trajectories), and a low-level reinforcement learning policy is responsible for precise execution and adaptation by tracking these trajectories. This separation is intended to delineate planning and control responsibilities effectively.
- **Achieve Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A critical motivation is to ensure that policies learned in simulation can transfer effectively to real-world robotic hands without requiring further human demonstrations or extensive fine-tuning. This involves designing the low-level policy to be robust to visual perturbations and discrepancies in physical parameters, often through techniques like domain randomization.
- **Enable Generalization:** The approach aims to allow generalization to new unseen initial conditions, object configurations, and novel trajectories by randomizing VLM-generated plans during training and leveraging the VLM's ability to generate new plans at test time.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to create more scalable, adaptable, and less data-intensive methods for training dexterous robots by strategically integrating the high-level reasoning capabilities of VLMs with the adaptive control of RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a hierarchical framework that combines a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for high-level trajectory generation with a low-level residual Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy for precise tracking and control.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The goal is to learn a hierarchical policy for dexterous manipulation with visual observations and natural language instructions. The low-level policy's input comprises the robot's proprioceptive state (wrist pose `w`, finger joint positions `q`, and velocities `q̇`) and estimated 3D keypoints from RGB-D images (`I`, `D`). The action space consists of desired wrist and finger joint positions (`w_targ`, `q_targ`).

#### 3.2. High-Level Policy (πh) - VLM-based Trajectory Generation

The high-level policy is implemented using an off-the-shelf VLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash Thinking) to translate a natural language instruction (`L`) and an initial image (`I1`) into a coarse 3D keypoint-based motion plan (`τ`). This plan includes sequences of `k` 3D keypoints (`x(i)1:T`) for objects and a sequence of predicted wrist poses (`˜w1:T`). This process involves three phases:

1. **Keypoint Detection:** The VLM identifies task-relevant 2D keypoints (`u(i)`) in the initial image based on a language prompt. These 2D keypoints are then projected to 3D world coordinates (`x(i)`) using depth information (`D`) and camera parameters.
2. **Trajectory Generation:** Given the language description (`L`), the VLM generates waypoint sequences of a shorter length (`n < T`) for each initial 3D keypoint and the wrist position (`w1`). This phase relies on the VLM's spatial reasoning to translate semantic descriptions (e.g., "hammering") into physically plausible motions while respecting object relationships and physical constraints.
3. **Interpolation:** The `n` waypoints are linearly interpolated to match the full trajectory horizon `T`, forming the final plan `τ` (`x(i)1:T`, `˜w1:T`). This smooths the motion.

**Few-Shot Improvement (Optional):** To enhance VLM plan accuracy, successful plans from past executions can be used as in-context examples in subsequent VLM queries. This iterative refinement process aims to correct common VLM planning errors.

#### 3.3. Low-Level Control with Reinforcement Learning (πl)

The low-level policy `πl` is trained to execute and precisely track the VLM-generated plan `τ`. This is achieved using residual reinforcement learning within a plan-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP).

1. **Dense Keypoint Rewards:** The reward function `rτ(ot)` encourages consistent tracking between estimated 3D keypoints (`ˆx(i)t`) and planned keypoints (`˜x(i)t`). It consists of two terms:
 - **Keypoint Tracking:** An exponential function of the negative mean Euclidean distance between `ˆx(i)t` and `˜x(i)t`.
 - **Maintaining Contact:** An exponential function of the negative inverse of the number of finger tips in contact with the environment (`Ncontact(ot)`), promoting stable grasping.
 This reward structure is simpler than complex, hand-crafted rewards. The wrist trajectory (`˜w1:T`) is not directly rewarded for tracking, allowing the policy flexibility in wrist adjustments.
2. **Residual Policy Parameterization:** To guide the agent's exploration, `πl` is implemented as a residual policy. Instead of directly predicting absolute wrist actions, it predicts offsets (`∆w`) to the VLM-generated wrist plan (`˜wt`). The final action is `at = (˜wt + ∆w, qtargt)`. This ensures that the policy defaults to following the VLM's wrist trajectory while allowing RL to optimize small deviations and finger motions. `πl` is parameterized as a multi-layer perceptron.
3. **Termination Conditions:** To improve training efficiency, episodes are terminated early if the mean keypoint tracking error exceeds a threshold `δ`. A curriculum is applied, annealing `δ` linearly from an initial value (`δinit`) to half of that value over training, initially allowing broader exploration and later promoting precise tracking.

#### 3.4. Full Pipeline (Training and Evaluation)

- **Training:** The low-level policy `πl` is trained using PPO on a diverse set of `N` initial conditions and corresponding VLM-generated plans sampled from `πh`. Training is performed in a massively parallelized simulation environment (ManiSkill3). In simulation, ground-truth object information is used for keypoint tracking in `o_l`.
- **Evaluation/Inference:** At test time, a new plan is generated by the VLM `πh` from real-world RGB-D images and a natural language command. This plan is then supplied to the frozen, learned policy `πl` for closed-loop control. Keypoint positions for `o_l` in the real world are estimated using a pose estimator (FoundationPose). Sim-to-real transfer is enabled through domain randomization during simulation training and mapping wrist actions to arm joints using DLS-IK for the physical robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted a comprehensive evaluation across eight simulated dexterous manipulation tasks and three real-world tasks, categorized by abilities such as semantic understanding, unstructured motion, articulated object manipulation, and precise manipulation.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Effectiveness of VLM Scaffolds (Figure 4):** The method demonstrated high success rates in simulation. The few-shot variant achieved an average success rate of 81%, closely approaching the Oracle (manually defined keypoints and hard-coded trajectories) which had an average success rate of 98%. The zero-shot variant achieved 72% success. In comparison, a pre-recorded trajectory baseline averaged 19% success, and an Iterative Keypoint Rewards (IKER) baseline averaged 32% success, indicating the superiority of VLM-generated plans over VLM-generated reward functions or fixed trajectories.
- **Iterative Refinement (Figure 5):** Providing successful plans as in-context examples to the VLM (iterative refinement) consistently improved performance. All tasks showed an increase in success rate after the first iteration, with diminishing returns after the second. The average success rate improved from 72% (zero-shot) to 81% after refinement. The "Fridge" task showed the most significant improvement, attributed to correcting VLM errors like world axis flips.
- **VLM Component Ablation (Figure 7, Right):**
 - Replacing VLM keypoint detection with an oracle improved performance most for the "Sponge" task, suggesting VLM keypoint specification was a primary bottleneck there.
 - Replacing VLM trajectory generation with an oracle improved performance most for the "Drawer" task, indicating that VLM planning was the main bottleneck for that task.
 - These ablations highlight that the criticality of VLM keypoint detection versus trajectory generation can vary by task.
- **Impact of Number of Waypoints (Figure 7, Left):** For most tasks (Apple, Sponge, Pliers), performance saturated with approximately 10 waypoints. The "Hammer" task, which involves repetitive motion, benefited from 20 waypoints, suggesting that tasks requiring complex or continuous motions may benefit from denser trajectories. Very sparse plans (3 or 5 waypoints) generally degraded performance.
- **Comparison to RL and IL Baselines (Figures 9 & 10):**
 - RL with simple rewards (using oracle keypoints but no trajectory guidance) achieved a mean success rate of 33%, which was significantly lower than the proposed method with oracle keypoints (80%).
 - RL with complex, hand-crafted rewards (using oracle keypoints but no trajectory guidance) achieved a mean success rate of 62%, still below the proposed method with oracle keypoints. This indicates that VLM-generated trajectory guidance provides a more effective optimization signal than complex reward engineering alone.
 - Imitation Learning (Diffusion Policy) required 50 perfect demonstrations to achieve a mean success rate of 85%, which is comparable to the proposed method's few-shot performance (81%) without needing any human demonstrations.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Failure Modes (Figure 6)

An analysis of failure cases revealed that the most frequent failure mode was "incomplete trajectory tracking" by the low-level policy, accounting for 26% of rollouts. This indicates a bottleneck either in the low-level policy's ability to follow the generated path or in the realism/feasibility of the VLM-proposed trajectories. Keypoint detection errors contributed to 5% of failures, while trajectories that were fully tracked but did not achieve task success accounted for 3% of failures.

#### 4.3. Real-World Results (Figure 8)

The system demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical Allegro hand mounted on a KUKA LBR iiwa arm.
- **Place Bottle onto Plate:** Achieved 90% success rate.
- **Slide Box to Bottle:** Achieved 85% success rate.
- **Hammer Three Times:** Achieved 65% success rate.
These results indicate that the modular, trajectory-based framework, supported by domain randomization in simulation and state-based observations, effectively generalizes to physical hardware without human demonstrations or manual reward design. Qualitative observations suggest VLM keypoint detection worked better on real-world images than on simulation images. The primary real-world failure mode was keypoint tracking errors by the pose estimator, especially under occlusion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for dexterous robotic manipulation that integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with reinforcement learning, addressing several long-standing challenges in the field.

**Significance:**

- **Elimination of Human Demonstrations:** The work significantly advances dexterous manipulation by removing the requirement for expensive and difficult-to-collect human demonstrations. This lowers the entry barrier for developing new manipulation skills and expands the range of tasks that can be tackled.
- **Reduced Reward Engineering Complexity:** By leveraging VLMs to generate coarse motion plans, the method simplifies the reward function design in reinforcement learning. The use of a dense, trajectory-tracking reward is less complex and more scalable than intricate, task-specific reward functions traditionally required for dexterous tasks.
- **Leveraging VLM Semantic and Spatial Reasoning:** The research demonstrates a practical and effective application of VLMs' inherent commonsense knowledge, semantic understanding, and spatial reasoning for robotic control. This goes beyond using VLMs for direct policy generation or opaque reward function coding, instead using them as high-level planners to "scaffold" more precise low-level control.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The hierarchical approach, where VLMs generate diverse plans and RL policies are trained to track these plans under randomized conditions, promotes generalization to novel initial states and configurations. The few-shot iterative refinement further enhances the robustness of VLM planning.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on complex dexterous tasks underscores the practicality of the approach. This is achieved through a combination of modular design, state-based observations, and domain randomization, reducing the need for real-world data collection and fine-tuning.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** By automating the generation of high-level plans and simplifying reward design, the method could significantly accelerate the development and deployment of new dexterous manipulation skills in various applications, from industrial automation to service robotics.
- **Broader Task Applicability:** The ability to handle tasks requiring semantic understanding, articulated object manipulation, and unstructured motion without task-specific engineering could enable robots to perform a wider array of real-world tasks that were previously too complex or data-intensive.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This framework provides a new direction for integrating large-scale foundation models (VLMs) with adaptive control systems. Future work could explore richer VLM outputs (e.g., beyond keypoints to include orientation or force cues), dynamic feedback loops between high-level planning and low-level control, and applications to deformable object manipulation.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The work contributes to the broader field of embodied artificial intelligence by demonstrating a pathway for intelligent agents to learn complex physical interactions in the real world based on high-level linguistic instructions and visual observations.

While limitations such as VLM reasoning time, reliance on rigid-body interactions, and the sim-to-real gap for highly precise tasks remain, this work establishes a significant step towards more autonomous and versatile dexterous manipulation capabilities.
