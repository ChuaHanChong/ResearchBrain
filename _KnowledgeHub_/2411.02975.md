# Transformer-Based Fault-Tolerant Control for Fixed-Wing UAVs Using Knowledge Distillation and In-Context Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> A transformer-based fault-tolerant control method is introduced for fixed-wing UAVs, utilizing knowledge distillation and in-context adaptation to enable trajectory tracking even after structural damage or actuator failures. This approach achieved significantly lower crash rates and superior tracking accuracy compared to baseline controllers, demonstrating a 4% crash rate in a challenging "Damaged Wing" scenario where other methods failed between 9% and 100%.

## Problem

- Traditional fixed-wing UAV flight control systems struggle to maintain performance and stability when dynamics are severely altered by structural damage or actuator failures.
- Existing fault-tolerant control (FTC) methods often involve complex fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) systems, requiring precise models and significant computational resources.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for FTC face challenges with partial observability (unobserved dynamic changes) and typically lack inherent stability guarantees for safety-critical applications.

## Method

- A teacher-student knowledge distillation framework is employed, where a privileged online RL agent (DreamerV3) serves as the teacher, generating expert trajectories under extensive domain randomization for various fault conditions.
- A lightweight Decision Transformer (DT) acts as the student, trained offline on these expert trajectories using only partial observations, leveraging its attention mechanism for in-context adaptation to dynamic changes.
- A Neural Lyapunov function is integrated into the Decision Transformer's training loss to promote stable behavior and ensure the controller drives the system towards equilibrium, even in the presence of faults.

## Results

- The Decision Transformer policy consistently outperformed traditional FCS and other RL-based baselines across all nominal and fault scenarios, demonstrating superior tracking accuracy and significantly lower crash rates.
- In the highly challenging "Damaged Wing" scenario, the DT achieved a **4%** crash rate, substantially outperforming other methods that had crash rates ranging from **9%** (RL+DR) to **100%** (base RL agent).
- The DT model, with approximately **0.8** million parameters, proved computationally efficient and demonstrated lower tracking errors than LSTM and TCN architectures, highlighting the efficacy of transformer-based control for both accuracy and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decision Transformers can effectively learn robust fault-tolerant control policies from expert demonstrations under partial observability, matching or even outperforming a privileged teacher with full state information.
> - Transformer attention mechanisms enable implicit fault detection and adaptive control by dynamically weighting relevant historical information to adjust control strategies in response to sudden dynamic changes.
> - The incorporation of Neural Lyapunov functions into offline RL training enhances the stability of learning-based controllers, providing a measure of stability during inference, which is crucial for safety-critical systems.
