# Transformer-Based Fault-Tolerant Control for Fixed-Wing UAVs Using Knowledge Distillation and In-Context Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> A transformer-based fault-tolerant control method is introduced for fixed-wing UAVs, utilizing knowledge distillation and in-context adaptation to enable trajectory tracking even after structural damage or actuator failures. This approach achieved significantly lower crash rates and superior tracking accuracy compared to baseline controllers, demonstrating a 4% crash rate in a challenging "Damaged Wing" scenario where other methods failed between 9% and 100%.

## Problem

- Traditional fixed-wing UAV flight control systems struggle to maintain performance and stability when dynamics are severely altered by structural damage or actuator failures.
- Existing fault-tolerant control (FTC) methods often involve complex fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) systems, requiring precise models and significant computational resources.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for FTC face challenges with partial observability (unobserved dynamic changes) and typically lack inherent stability guarantees for safety-critical applications.

## Method

- A teacher-student knowledge distillation framework is employed, where a privileged online RL agent (DreamerV3) serves as the teacher, generating expert trajectories under extensive domain randomization for various fault conditions.
- A lightweight Decision Transformer (DT) acts as the student, trained offline on these expert trajectories using only partial observations, leveraging its attention mechanism for in-context adaptation to dynamic changes.
- A Neural Lyapunov function is integrated into the Decision Transformer's training loss to promote stable behavior and ensure the controller drives the system towards equilibrium, even in the presence of faults.

## Results

- The Decision Transformer policy consistently outperformed traditional FCS and other RL-based baselines across all nominal and fault scenarios, demonstrating superior tracking accuracy and significantly lower crash rates.
- In the highly challenging "Damaged Wing" scenario, the DT achieved a **4%** crash rate, substantially outperforming other methods that had crash rates ranging from **9%** (RL+DR) to **100%** (base RL agent).
- The DT model, with approximately **0.8** million parameters, proved computationally efficient and demonstrated lower tracking errors than LSTM and TCN architectures, highlighting the efficacy of transformer-based control for both accuracy and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decision Transformers can effectively learn robust fault-tolerant control policies from expert demonstrations under partial observability, matching or even outperforming a privileged teacher with full state information.
> - Transformer attention mechanisms enable implicit fault detection and adaptive control by dynamically weighting relevant historical information to adjust control strategies in response to sudden dynamic changes.
> - The incorporation of Neural Lyapunov functions into offline RL training enhances the stability of learning-based controllers, providing a measure of stability during inference, which is crucial for safety-critical systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) has expanded significantly into complex and critical applications, necessitating high operational safety and reliability. Traditional Flight Control Systems (FCSs) in industrial UAVs typically rely on classical control techniques, such as cascaded Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers. While these methods offer simplicity, reliability, and computational efficiency, they exhibit limitations when UAV dynamics are severely altered by structural damage or actuator failures. This is particularly challenging for fixed-wing UAVs, which possess complex, nonlinear dynamics that can be significantly disrupted by such compromises.

Current fault-tolerant control (FTC) approaches often involve complex fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) systems, which identify faults in real-time and then adapt controller parameters. These methods require precise system models and significant computational resources, often making them impractical for low-level UAV control. Other nonlinear FTC techniques, such as fuzzy logic systems, sliding mode control, and robust control, have been explored but often entail their own complexities in implementation and online adaptation.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising alternative for managing complex, high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics, including in fault management. However, applying RL to FTC, especially when dealing with sudden and unobserved dynamic changes, often requires framing the problem as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). This can increase the complexity of RL algorithms and lead to suboptimal performance. Much of the existing RL research in FTC for aerial vehicles has focused on multicopters or on inner-loop attitude control for fixed-wing UAVs, leaving a gap for comprehensive outer-loop fault-tolerant control in fixed-wing platforms. Furthermore, a key challenge in learning-based control methods like RL and Imitation Learning (IL) is ensuring stability, an aspect that traditional control theory intrinsically provides. Some recent works have attempted to integrate stability guarantees, for example, through Neural Lyapunov functions.

Transformers, initially developed for Natural Language Processing (NLP), have demonstrated capabilities in sequence modeling, capturing long-range dependencies, and rapid adaptation to dynamic system variations. Their application has extended to RL, with models like the Decision Transformer (DT) framing RL as a sequence modeling problem. Knowledge Distillation (KD) is another relevant technique, used to transfer knowledge from a complex "teacher" model to a simpler "student" model, often to address partial observability by distilling privileged expert knowledge.

This research paper positions itself by combining these advanced techniques to address the identified gaps. It proposes a novel transformer-based approach for fault-tolerant control in fixed-wing UAVs. Unlike traditional methods, it directly maps outer-loop reference values (altitude, heading, and airspeed) to control commands, thereby bypassing inner-loop controllers and explicit fault-detection layers. It leverages a teacher-student knowledge distillation framework, where a privileged expert RL agent (teacher) informs a Decision Transformer (student) that operates with partial observations. This framework specifically aims to handle POMDP challenges and incorporates Lyapunov-based concepts during training to promote stable behavior, moving beyond typical RL approaches that may lack such guarantees.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a fault-tolerant control method for fixed-wing Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) that can adapt in real-time to dynamic changes resulting from structural damage or actuator failures. The goal is not merely to prevent a crash, but to enable the UAV to continue tracking specified outer-loop reference values for altitude, heading, and airspeed, even after significant alterations to its aerodynamic characteristics.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations and challenges in existing UAV control systems:

- **Inadequacy of Traditional Control Systems in Failure Scenarios:** Industry-standard Flight Control Systems (FCSs), predominantly using classical control techniques (e.g., cascade PID), are robust under nominal conditions but struggle when UAV dynamics deviate substantially from their design specifications. Such deviations, caused by structural damage or actuator failures, can lead to control divergence and catastrophic failure. The requirement for modern UAVs to perform complex and critical missions necessitates a more resilient control system.
- **Complexity of Existing Fault-Tolerant Methods:** Traditional fault-tolerant control (FTC) methods typically rely on real-time fault detection, identification, and subsequent adjustment of controller parameters. This process is complex, computationally intensive, and often requires precise system models, making it challenging to implement efficiently in the highly nonlinear and dynamic environment of UAV flight.
- **Limitations of Reinforcement Learning for Partial Observability:** While Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers potential for handling high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics, its application to fault-tolerant control is complicated by partial observability. Faults often manifest as sudden, unobserved changes in dynamics, framing the problem as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). Many RL algorithms are designed for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), and adapting them to POMDPs adds considerable complexity. Furthermore, much RL research in this area has focused on multicopters or only the inner attitude control loop for fixed-wing UAVs, without addressing full outer-loop control.
- **Desire for Direct Mapping and Simplified Control Architecture:** The research aims to simplify the control architecture by directly mapping outer-loop reference values (altitude, heading, airspeed) to control surface and throttle commands. This approach seeks to bypass the need for complex inner-loop controllers and explicit fault-detection layers, which typically complicate system design and introduce latency.
- **Leveraging Advanced Machine Learning for Adaptability:** The motivation includes exploring the attention mechanisms and in-context learning capabilities of transformer models. These properties are hypothesized to enable controllers to autonomously detect and adapt to dynamic changes during inference without requiring explicit weight updates, thereby offering a more flexible and adaptive solution than traditional gain-scheduled or model-adaptive controllers.
- **Addressing Stability Concerns in Learning-Based Control:** A recognized challenge with learning-based control methods is the lack of inherent stability guarantees, unlike classical control theory. The work is motivated to integrate Lyapunov-based concepts into the offline RL training process to encourage stable model behavior and provide a measure of stability analysis for the learned policies.
- **Computational Efficiency for Deployment:** While advanced, the method aims for a lightweight model that can be efficiently deployed on embedded platforms, addressing a practical constraint for real-world UAV applications.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for a highly adaptive, robust, and computationally feasible control system for fixed-wing UAVs that can maintain performance and stability under unforeseen and significant damage conditions, leveraging recent advancements in machine learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research frames the fault-tolerant control problem for fixed-wing UAVs as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The objective is for the UAV to track set-points for altitude (h), heading (Ψ), and airspeed (V_T) using control surfaces (aileron, elevator, rudder) and throttle, even when aerodynamic characteristics are significantly altered by faults.

The approach involves a multi-stage training and deployment strategy:

**A. Privileged Agent Training through Online Reinforcement Learning (Teacher Policy)**

1. **Environment Setup:** The flight control task is formulated as a POMDP, where the RL agent receives partial observations (o_t) but faces sudden, unobserved changes in UAV dynamics due to faults.
2. **State and Action Space:**
 - **Observations (o):** Include tracking errors (ε_h, ε_Ψ, ε_V_T), altitude (h), aerodynamic angles (α, β), attitude angles (φ, θ), angular velocity (p, q, r), linear velocity (u, v, w), and linear accelerations (n_x, n_y, n_z).
 - **Actions (a):** Deflections of control surfaces (δ_a, δ_e, δ_r) and throttle (δ_T).
3. **Reward Function Design:** A weighted sum of components is used to guide the agent:
 - `r_h`, `r_Ψ`, `r_V_T`: Tracking rewards, designed to penalize deviations from reference values.
 - `r_att`: Encourages smooth attitude adjustments using angular rates and roll angle.
 - `r_δ`: Penalizes large control inputs to minimize effort.
4. **Addressing Partial Observability with Privileged Information and Domain Randomization (DR):**
 - To overcome the POMDP challenge during teacher training, a "privileged" agent is used. This agent receives not only the partial observations (o_t) but also the true physical parameters characterizing the environment (e.g., aerodynamic coefficients, center of gravity, weight). This effectively transforms the POMDP into an MDP for the teacher.
 - **Domain Randomization (DR)** is applied to these physical parameters, randomly altering the UAV's dynamics at the start and during each episode. This exposes the agent to a wide range of damaged and fault conditions during training. Parameters like C_Lα, C_D0, C_Yβ, C_mα, and control surface effectiveness (C_Lδe, C_lδa, etc.) are scaled within defined ranges, along with center of gravity and weight. Initial and reference flight conditions are also randomized.
5. **RL Algorithm:** The DreamerV3 algorithm is chosen for its efficiency, robustness, and generalization capabilities. DreamerV3 is a model-based RL algorithm that learns a world model from past trajectories and then refines a policy in a learned latent space.

**B. Knowledge Distillation via Decision Transformer (Student Policy)**

1. **Dataset Collection:** The trained privileged agent (teacher) is used to generate a large dataset of expert trajectories under various randomized fault and flight conditions.
2. **Offline Reinforcement Learning:** From these expert trajectories, only the partial observations (o_t), actions (a_t), and rewards (r_t) are retained. This dataset, which represents a POMDP, is then used to train a Decision Transformer (DT) as the student agent in an offline RL setting.
3. **Decision Transformer Architecture:** The DT is a causal decoder-only transformer model. It leverages its attention mechanisms and in-context learning to adapt its control actions based on a history (context window) of past observations, actions, and rewards, without explicit weight updates during inference. This allows it to infer dynamic changes and adapt its behavior to unseen fault scenarios.
 - Model size: Approximately 0.8 million parameters.
 - Configuration: 4 heads, 3 layers, 128 embedding dimension, maximum sequence length of 60 steps (6 seconds).
 - Training parameters: Batch size of 256, learning rate of 1e-04, weight decay of 1e-04.
 - Dataset size: 2 million timesteps, encompassing 200,000 trajectories.

**C. Neural Lyapunov Function Learning**

1. **Stability Integration:** To enhance the stability of the control policy, a Neural Lyapunov function V_θ(s) is incorporated into the DT's training process.
2. **Lyapunov State:** The Lyapunov state `s` is defined as `[ε_h, ε_Ψ, ε_V_T, φ, p, q, r]`, representing key errors and angular rates.
3. **Koopman Eigenfunction Approximation:** A Koopman eigenfunction `φ(s_0)` associated with the initial state `s_0` is approximated using a Laplace average of the L2 norm of the state `f(s) = ||s||^2` along trajectories (`φ(s_0) ≈ Σ e^(-λt) f(s_t) / Σ e^(-λt)`). This average provides a decaying measure capturing contraction properties.
4. **Neural Network for Lyapunov Function:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with two hidden layers (256 neurons each, ReLU activations) and a squared activation output layer is trained to approximate these Koopman eigenfunction values.
5. **Lyapunov Loss Function:**
 - **MSE Loss (L_MSE):** Minimizes the squared error between the MLP's output `V_θ(s)` and the approximated `φ(s_0)`.
 - **Auxiliary Loss Terms:** Enforce Lyapunov properties:
 - Positive Definiteness: `V(s) > 0` for `s ≠ 0` and `V(0) = 0`.
 - Decrease Condition: A hinge loss `L_dec = max{0, V_θ(s_t+1) - V_θ(s_t) + ε}` penalizes increases in the Lyapunov function along a trajectory.
 - **Overall Lyapunov Loss (L_Lyapunov):** A weighted combination of `L_MSE` and `L_dec`.
6. **Combined Loss:** The learned `L_Lyapunov` is integrated into the Decision Transformer's total loss (`L_total = L_DT + γ L_Lyapunov`), guiding the DT to select actions that not only maximize reward but also lead towards more stable states.

**D. Simulation and Testing Environment**

- **Flight Dynamics Model:** JSBSim, a high-fidelity 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) flight dynamics model, is used for all experiments and training.
- **Operating Conditions:** UAV physics model operates within altitude ranges of ft and airspeed of ft/s.
- **Real-world Uncertainty Modeling:** External perturbations (Gaussian wind gusts N(0, 2 ft/s)) and sensor noise (Gaussian N(0, 0.02x)) are incorporated during both training and testing to enhance robustness and sim-to-real transferability.
- **Testing Scenarios:** Seven specific, pre-defined failure and damage scenarios (e.g., Jammed Rudder, Broken Aileron, Damaged Semi-Wing) are used for validation, distinct from the random variations used in training. Failures and set-point changes occur at specified timesteps.

This comprehensive methodology aims to create a robust, adaptive, and stable fault-tolerant control system for fixed-wing UAVs using a combination of advanced RL, transformer architectures, and stability-promoting techniques.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed transformer-based fault-tolerant control method for fixed-wing UAVs:

**A. Performance of Decision Transformer (DT) vs. Privileged Teacher**

- A comparison between the DT student agent (trained with partial observations) and the privileged teacher agent (trained with full environmental information) showed similar performance across all failure scenarios, as measured by mean episode return.
- This indicates the DT's capability to learn robust behaviors from expert trajectories in a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) setting and adapt to dynamic variations solely from context. The observation that the student can match or even slightly outperform the teacher, despite limited observations, is attributed to the nature of offline RL, where the student extracts essential decision patterns and generalizes effectively through return-conditioning, potentially overcoming instabilities in the teacher's online training.

**B. Comparative Study Against State-of-the-Art Methods**

- The DT policy was evaluated against several baseline control systems, including:
 - RL Policy (DreamerV3 without Domain Randomization, assuming MDP)
 - RL Policy + DR (DreamerV3 with Domain Randomization, addressing POMDP)
 - Industry FCS (baseline PID controllers)
 - Industry FCS + RL + DR (FCS with an RL-trained Gain-Scheduling algorithm)
- **Superior Performance:** The DT policy consistently exhibited superior performance across all nominal and failure scenarios, achieving higher tracking accuracy and significantly lower crash rates (Table II).
 - In nominal conditions, all controllers showed high performance.
 - In various fault scenarios (e.g., Jammed Rudder, Broken Aileron, Shifted CG, Damaged Tail), the DT achieved 0% crash rates and maintained high tracking accuracy, outperforming all other methods which showed varying degrees of crashes and higher return penalties.
 - **Challenging "Damaged Wing" Scenario:** This scenario, involving significant dynamic changes, proved to be the most challenging. The baseline FCS, base RL agent, and FCS+RL exhibited high crash rates (92%, 100%, and 60% respectively) and substantial performance degradation. The RL+DR agent also had a 9% crash rate. In contrast, the DT method achieved the lowest crash rate (4%) and superior tracking accuracy for altitude, heading, and airspeed, successfully regaining control and tracking setpoints despite severe damage (Figure 4b, Figure 7).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The DT model, with approximately 0.8 million parameters, is significantly more lightweight than the DreamerV3 agent (~26 million parameters). This suggests the DT's potential for efficient real-time deployment despite its superior performance.
- **Comparison with Other Sequence Models:** The DT also demonstrated significantly lower tracking errors compared to LSTM and Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) architectures, particularly in failure scenarios (Table III), validating the superiority of transformer-based control for both accuracy and robustness.

**C. Interpretability through Attention Mechanisms**

- Analysis of the Decision Transformer's attention mechanism provided insights into its adaptive behavior.
- In nominal conditions, attention was distributed evenly across past states within the context window.
- After a failure occurred, the DT adapted its focus, concentrating attention more heavily on time steps immediately following the failure event (Figure 6). This demonstrates that the DT dynamically weights relevant recent information to adjust its control strategy in response to sudden changes in UAV dynamics, without explicit fault detection.

**D. Stability Analysis with Neural Lyapunov Function**

- The incorporation of a Neural Lyapunov function (V_θ(s)) into the DT training process was evaluated for its impact on stability.
- **Nominal Conditions:** Both a baseline DT and the Lyapunov-enhanced DT showed asymptotic stability, with the Lyapunov function values converging to near zero, indicating a stable equilibrium (Figure 8a).
- **Failure Scenarios:** In failure conditions, both models initially showed a surge in "energy" (V_θ(s)) after a fault occurred. However, when new reference values were introduced later in the episode, the Lyapunov-enhanced DT maintained significantly lower V_θ(s) values compared to the baseline DT, and its energy converged more effectively to near zero (Figure 8b). This finding suggests that incorporating Lyapunov-based loss terms during training actively promotes greater stability in the controller's behavior during inference, especially under challenging and dynamic conditions.

In summary, the main findings highlight that the proposed transformer-based controller, leveraging knowledge distillation and a Lyapunov-enhanced Decision Transformer, provides a robust, adaptive, and stable solution for fault-tolerant control in fixed-wing UAVs, significantly outperforming conventional and other learning-based methods in complex failure scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) control, particularly for safety-critical operations. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

- **Enhanced Operational Safety and Reliability:** The primary significance lies in its direct contribution to improving the safety and reliability of fixed-wing UAVs. By enabling UAVs to maintain control and track mission-critical reference values (altitude, heading, airspeed) even after severe structural damage or actuator failures, the method reduces the risk of catastrophic incidents. This is crucial for applications such as search and rescue, autonomous medical transportation, or critical infrastructure inspection, where system failures can have severe consequences.
- **Simplified Fault-Tolerant Control (FTC) Architecture:** The proposed approach bypasses the need for complex inner-loop controllers and explicit fault detection/identification layers. Traditional FTC systems often require computationally intensive routines for online system identification and parameter re-tuning. By shifting this complexity to an offline training phase and relying on the in-context learning of transformers, the method offers a streamlined and more efficient architecture, potentially reducing development and implementation overhead.
- **Advancement in Learning-Based Control for Safety-Critical Systems:** This work demonstrates the practical applicability and advantages of advanced machine learning techniques, specifically transformer architectures and knowledge distillation, in a safety-critical control domain. It bridges the gap between sophisticated deep learning methods and the stringent requirements of real-world control systems, which traditionally favor classical, analytically provable approaches.
- **Robustness to Partial Observability:** The teacher-student knowledge distillation framework effectively addresses the challenge of partial observability inherent in fault-tolerant control problems. The student Decision Transformer learns robust policies from expert trajectories without direct knowledge of the underlying fault state, enabling adaptation to a wide range of unforeseen failure scenarios. This capability is vital for real-world deployment where full information about a fault may not be available.
- **Integration of Stability Guarantees:** By incorporating Neural Lyapunov functions into the offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) training, the research tackles a long-standing challenge in learning-based control: ensuring stability. The Lyapunov-enhanced Decision Transformer demonstrates improved stability characteristics under failure conditions, offering a pathway toward more theoretically grounded and trustworthy autonomous systems. This integration helps mitigate concerns about the "black-box" nature of neural network controllers.
- **Feasibility for Embedded Systems:** Despite leveraging advanced deep learning, the resulting Decision Transformer model is relatively lightweight (under one million parameters). This characteristic, coupled with the potential for aggressive quantization techniques, suggests its feasibility for deployment on resource-constrained embedded processors commonly found in UAV flight controllers. This addresses a critical practical constraint for real-world integration, making the technology accessible for current hardware.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Development:** The success of this simulation-based study lays a strong foundation for future work, including hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulations and real-flight experiments. Validation under real-world constraints such as limited onboard computation, sensor noise, and challenging flight envelopes will be the next crucial step, paving the way for the adoption of such adaptive, reliable, and intelligent control systems in future aerial robotic platforms.

In summary, this research contributes a novel, robust, and computationally viable approach to fault-tolerant control for fixed-wing UAVs, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with learning-based methods in a safety-critical domain.
