# SAPG: Split and Aggregate Policy Gradients

> [!summary] Summary
> Developed by researchers at Carnegie Mellon University, SAPG is an on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm designed to efficiently utilize massively parallel simulation environments by training multiple diverse policies and aggregating their experiences via off-policy updates. This method addresses performance plateaus in standard on-policy algorithms, yielding up to 66% higher success rates on complex AllegroKuka manipulation tasks and better asymptotic performance compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional on-policy RL algorithms like PPO experience performance saturation when naively scaled to ultra-large batch sizes in massively parallel simulation environments, failing to fully leverage the abundant data.
- Using a single policy to sample actions across numerous parallel environments leads to redundant data and limited exploration, as most sampled actions concentrate around the policy's mean.
- Existing distributed RL approaches, such as population-based training (PBT), often discard data from suboptimal policies, resulting in incomplete utilization of collected experiences.

## Method

- The Split and Aggregate Policy Gradients (**SAPG**) algorithm divides N parallel environments into M blocks, assigning a distinct policy to each block, and then aggregates data from these M policies through off-policy updates.
- A Leader-Follower aggregation scheme is employed, where a designated 'leader' policy aggregates importance-sampled off-policy data from 'follower' policies, while followers maintain diversity through unique latent conditioning and entropy regularization.
- The standard PPO clipped surrogate objective is augmented with an off-policy term, allowing the incorporation of diverse experiences from all policies into the updates for a more robust learning signal.

## Results

- SAPG consistently achieved **12%** to **66%** higher success rates on hard AllegroKuka manipulation tasks compared to DexPBT, including a **66%** improvement on the Reorientation task and over double the successes on Two Arms Reorientation.
- On in-hand reorientation tasks (AllegroHand, ShadowHand), SAPG exhibited higher asymptotic performance than off-policy PQL and significantly surpassed PPO and PBT, with a **21%** margin in episode rewards on AllegroHand.
- Quantitative analysis using PCA and MLP reconstruction error metrics confirmed that SAPG fostered greater diversity in the visited state space compared to PPO, indicating more effective exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling single-policy on-policy RL with larger batch sizes in massively parallel environments leads to performance plateaus due to data redundancy, necessitating strategies to enhance data diversity.
> - A 'split' approach (training multiple diverse policies) combined with an 'aggregate' approach (off-policy updates with importance sampling) effectively utilizes large-scale simulation data by fostering and leveraging varied exploration strategies.
> - Explicitly encouraging policy diversity, for instance through latent conditioning and differentiated entropy regularization on follower policies, is crucial; symmetric aggregation of data from converging policies can negate the benefits of diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) broadly categorizes into off-policy and on-policy methods. While off-policy methods generally exhibit higher sample efficiency by reusing past data, on-policy methods, particularly Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), often achieve superior asymptotic performance. This characteristic makes on-policy RL a preferred choice for complex applications such as sim2real robotics and large-scale games, where extensive simulation data can be generated.

Recent advancements in GPU-driven simulation environments, like IsaacGym, have drastically increased the capacity for collecting large volumes of data, enabling the parallel simulation of tens of thousands of environments. This technological shift allows for batch sizes in RL training that are orders of magnitude larger than traditionally used benchmarks. However, existing on-policy RL algorithms, including PPO, exhibit limitations in effectively leveraging this increased data capacity. Studies indicate that their performance saturates beyond a certain batch size, suggesting an inefficient utilization of massively parallelized environments. This saturation is attributed to the data sampling mechanism, where a single policy, typically a Gaussian, leads to redundant action sampling across many environments, thus reducing data diversity.

Previous efforts in distributed reinforcement learning have focused on parallelizing experience collection or training components. More recent works leveraging GPU-based simulators often augment training with techniques like teacher-student training or curriculum learning, but largely continue to train a single policy. Population-based training (PBT) frameworks distribute environments among multiple policies and use hyperparameter mutation; however, they may still discard data from "worse" performing policies, leading to incomplete data utilization. Off-policy policy gradient methods can reuse data from any policy but often yield lower asymptotic performance than on-policy counterparts, inspiring techniques to incorporate off-policy data into on-policy frameworks using importance sampling.

This work addresses the challenge of scaling on-policy RL to efficiently exploit ultra-large-scale parallel environments. It aims to overcome the performance saturation observed in conventional on-policy methods by introducing a mechanism to foster data diversity and aggregate experiences from multiple policies. By doing so, it seeks to improve upon existing distributed and population-based training approaches, ensuring that a larger proportion of collected data contributes meaningfully to learning, ultimately leading to higher asymptotic performance in complex tasks.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm capable of effectively leveraging the unprecedented data generation capacity offered by massively parallel simulation environments. The goal is to achieve significantly higher asymptotic performance in challenging tasks where traditional on-policy methods, even with large batch sizes, fail to progress or exhibit performance saturation.

This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **On-policy RL's Performance Potential:** On-policy methods, despite their sample inefficiency, are known to converge to higher asymptotic performance compared to off-policy methods. This makes them desirable for applications like sim2real robotics and complex games where the final performance of the learned policy is paramount, and extensive simulation is feasible.
2. **Unutilized Simulation Capacity:** Recent advancements in GPU-accelerated simulators, such as IsaacGym, allow for the parallel execution of tens of thousands of environments. This enables the collection of hundreds of thousands to millions of data points per policy update. However, the authors observe that current on-policy algorithms, specifically PPO, do not proportionally benefit from this increased data volume. Their performance saturates beyond a certain batch size, indicating an inefficiency in how this abundant data is used.
3. **Lack of Data Diversity in Large Batches:** The core issue identified is that when a single policy (e.g., a Gaussian policy) is used to sample actions across an extremely large number of parallel environments, most sampled actions tend to concentrate near the mean. This results in many environments executing highly similar actions, leading to redundant or duplicated data. Such data redundancy limits the effective exploration of the state-action space, hindering further policy improvement despite the sheer volume of collected experience.
4. **Desire for Optimal Data Utilization:** The authors aim to design a method that ensures the vast amount of data collected from massively parallel environments is efficiently utilized. This involves introducing mechanisms to diversify the collected experience and aggregate it intelligently, preventing the waste of computational resources and simulation time.
5. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Approaches:** While population-based training (PBT) attempts to utilize multiple policies, it often relies on hyperparameter mutation and discards data from suboptimal policies. This work seeks to aggregate data from all policies, including those that might be "worse" overall but still discover valuable high-reward trajectories, thereby making a more comprehensive use of the generated experiences.

By addressing these points, the research aims to unlock the full potential of on-policy RL in a large-scale simulation setting, leading to more robust and capable agents for complex manipulation and control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed algorithm, Split and Aggregate Policy Gradients (SAPG), introduces a modification to standard on-policy RL to enhance performance in environments offering large batch sizes. While built upon Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), its core concepts are presented as generally applicable to other on-policy methods.

**Core Principle:**
SAPG's fundamental approach is a divide-and-conquer strategy. Instead of training a single policy across all `N` parallel environments, it divides the environments into `M` blocks, each assigned to a separate policy. This setup encourages data diversity. The key innovation lies in then fusing the collected data from these `M` policies back together via off-policy updates, ensuring that information from all experiences contributes to a more optimal policy.

**Detailed Methodology:**

1. **Splitting Environments and Policies:**
 - The total `N` environments are divided into `M` blocks, with each block containing `N/M` environments.
 - `M` distinct policies, denoted π1..., πM, are trained, each operating within its assigned block of environments.

2. **Aggregating Data via Off-Policy Updates:**
 - A primary challenge in on-policy RL is its inability to reuse data from past or different policies. SAPG overcomes this by employing importance sampling to incorporate off-policy data into the policy updates.
 - **Off-Policy Loss Formulation:** The standard PPO clipped surrogate objective (L_on, Equation 2) is augmented with an off-policy term (L_off, Equation 3). For updating policy πi using data from policy πj, the off-policy ratio `r_πi(s, a) = πi(s,a) / πj(s,a)` and an off-policy correction term `μ = πi,old(s,a) / πj(s,a)` are used. The `clip` function ensures stability, similar to PPO.
 - **Combined Policy Loss:** The total policy loss for πi is a weighted sum of its on-policy loss and the aggregated off-policy loss: `L(πi) = L_on(πi) + λ · L_off(πi; X)`, where `X` is the set of policies from which off-policy data is drawn, and `λ` is a scaling factor.
 - **Critic Updates:** The critic uses `n`-step returns for on-policy data and `1`-step returns for off-policy data to calculate target values. These are combined into a total critic loss `L_critic(πi) = L_critic_on(πi) + λ · L_critic_off(πi)`.

3. **Aggregation Schemes:**
 The paper explores different strategies for selecting which policies contribute off-policy data:
 - **Symmetric Aggregation:** Every policy `i` receives off-policy data from all other `M-1` policies (`X = {1..., M} \ {i}`). To manage noise from off-policy data, `λ=1`, and the off-policy data is subsampled to match the size of the on-policy data.
 - **Leader-Follower Aggregation (Default in SAPG):** This scheme breaks symmetry to maintain diversity. One policy is designated as the "leader" (e.g., π1), and it aggregates off-policy data from all other `M-1` "follower" policies (`X = {2..., M}`). The follower policies, in contrast, only use their own on-policy data for updates (`X = ϕ`). Similar to symmetric aggregation, off-policy data for the leader is subsampled.

4. **Encouraging Policy Diversity:**
 To ensure the `M` policies explore different strategies rather than converging:
 - **Latent Conditioning:** All policies share a common neural network backbone (Bθ for actor, Cψ for critic). However, each policy is also conditioned on a unique, local set of learned parameters (ϕj). The shared parameters (θ, ψ) are updated by gradients from all policies, while the local parameters (ϕj) are updated only by their respective policy's objective. This allows for shared learning while maintaining individual policy characteristics. The dimension of ϕj varies with task complexity.
 - **Entropy Regularization:** An additional entropy loss `H(π(a | s))` is added to the follower policies' objectives, each with a different scaling coefficient `λ_ent(i-1)`. This encourages followers to explore more broadly, with different exploration-exploitation trade-offs, thereby generating diverse trajectories. The leader policy does not receive this entropy regularization.

5. **Algorithm (SAPG) Workflow (Algorithm 1):**
 - Initialize shared parameters (θ, ψ) and local parameters (ϕj) for each policy.
 - In an iterative loop:
 - Each of the `M` policies collects experience from its assigned `N/M` environments, storing it in separate data buffers (D1..., DM).
 - The leader policy's dataset (D1) is augmented with importance-sampled transitions (`D'1`) from the followers' datasets (D2..., DM).
 - A combined loss `L` is calculated: `L_off` from `D'1`, `L_on` from `D1`, and `L_on` from `D2` to `DM`.
 - Shared parameters (θ, ψ) are updated using gradients from `L`.
 - Local parameters (ϕj) are updated based on their individual policy objectives.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted using IsaacGym, a GPU-accelerated simulator, allowing for 24,576 parallel environments. Six challenging manipulation tasks are used: four hard Allegro-Kuka tasks (Regrasping, Throw, Reorientation, Two Arms Reorientation) and two easier in-hand reorientation tasks (Shadow Hand, Allegro Hand). Baselines include vanilla PPO (with increased batch size), Parallel Q-Learning (PQL, an off-policy method), and DexPBT (population-based training). Performance metrics are episode successes for Allegro-Kuka tasks and episode rewards for in-hand reorientation tasks. Ablation studies examine the impact of different aggregation schemes, off-policy data ratios, and entropy regularization. Diversity of exploration is quantitatively measured using PCA and MLP reconstruction errors on visited states.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted demonstrate SAPG's capacity to leverage large-scale parallel environments, yielding performance improvements over existing methods.

**1. PPO Performance Saturation:**
The paper corroborates that conventional PPO, when naively scaled to massively parallel environments by increasing batch size, experiences performance saturation. Figure 2 illustrates that PPO's asymptotic performance plateaus after a certain batch size across different tasks (Shadow Hand, Allegro Kuka Throw), indicating its inability to effectively utilize additional data beyond this point. This provides empirical evidence for the core motivation of SAPG.

**2. Superior Performance on Hard AllegroKuka Tasks:**
For the complex AllegroKuka tasks (Regrasping, Throw, Reorientation, Two Arms Reorientation), which involve high degrees of freedom and complex object interactions, SAPG consistently achieved higher success rates compared to baselines.
- **Regrasping, Throw, and Reorientation:** SAPG demonstrated 12% to 66% higher success rates than DexPBT, a population-based training method designed for similar settings. For instance, on the Reorientation task, SAPG exhibited a 66% improvement over PBT.
- **Two Arms Reorientation:** This particularly challenging task saw SAPG achieve over twice the number of successes on average compared to DexPBT.
- Notably, vanilla PPO and PQL (an off-policy method) were largely unable to learn effective behaviors on these hard tasks, often failing to achieve any positive success.

**3. High Asymptotic Performance on In-Hand Reorientation Tasks:**
On the relatively easier AllegroHand and ShadowHand reorientation tasks, SAPG also exhibited strong performance characteristics:
- While PQL (off-policy) was initially more sample-efficient, SAPG consistently achieved higher asymptotic performance in the long run. On the AllegroHand task, SAPG surpassed PQL by a 21% margin in episode rewards. On the ShadowHand task, SAPG achieved comparable peak performance to PQL, but outperforming PPO and PBT.
- PPO and PBT generally performed worse on these tasks. PPO struggled with efficiently leveraging the large batch size, and PBT's hyperparameter mutation benefits diminished in simpler environments where default hyperparameters were often sufficient. The higher asymptotic performance of SAPG aligns with the practical importance of maximizing agent capability in large-scale simulation settings.

**4. Ablation Study Insights:**
Ablation studies provided insights into SAPG's design choices:
- **Symmetric Aggregation:** The variant where all policies symmetrically aggregated data from each other performed significantly worse across all tasks. This suggests that allowing policies to converge in behavior, which symmetric aggregation may induce, negates the benefits of data diversity.
- **Absence of Off-Policy Aggregation:** Removing the off-policy combination mechanism, effectively reducing it to `M` independent PPO policies, also resulted in substantially lower performance. This underscores the importance of data aggregation for leveraging the collective experience.
- **High Off-Policy Ratio (without subsampling):** Using all available off-policy data without subsampling led to worse performance on AllegroHand and ShadowHand, and lower asymptotic performance on Reorientation, despite being initially more sample efficient. This indicates that while more data can be beneficial early on, the increased noise from off-policy data can hinder convergence to optimal policies later in training.
- **Entropy Regularization:** The use of entropy regularization on follower policies, particularly with a coefficient of 0.005, improved performance by up to 16.5% on the Reorientation task. This suggests that explicit exploration encouraged by entropy regularization can be beneficial for discovering emergent strategies in complex environments.

**5. Evidence for Increased Exploration Diversity:**
Quantitative analysis using PCA and MLP reconstruction error metrics confirmed that SAPG fostered greater diversity in the visited state space compared to PPO:
- **PCA Reconstruction Error:** SAPG showed a slower rate of decrease in reconstruction error with increasing PCA components (Figure 7). This implies that SAPG's policies explored a wider range of state dimensions, resulting in a more diverse set of visited states that are less compressible.
- **MLP Reconstruction Error:** Training feedforward networks to reconstruct states visited by SAPG's policies consistently yielded higher training errors across various hidden layer sizes (Figure 8). This indicates that the state distributions generated by SAPG were more diverse and less compressible, making them harder for small MLPs to reconstruct effectively. These findings support the hypothesis that SAPG's design promotes broader exploration.

In summary, SAPG demonstrated a robust ability to scale on-policy RL to massively parallel environments, overcoming the limitations of single-policy training by fostering and leveraging data diversity through a split-and-aggregate approach with off-policy updates. This resulted in notable performance gains on complex manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The SAPG algorithm addresses a critical limitation in the current landscape of reinforcement learning, specifically the inefficient utilization of massively parallel simulation environments by conventional on-policy methods. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Scalable On-Policy RL:** SAPG offers a method for on-policy RL algorithms to effectively leverage the immense data generation capacity of modern GPU-accelerated simulators (e.g., IsaacGym), which can simulate tens of thousands of environments simultaneously. This is a crucial step towards fully realizing the potential of large-scale simulation for RL training.
2. **Achieving Higher Asymptotic Performance:** By overcoming the performance saturation observed in algorithms like PPO when scaled naively, SAPG consistently achieves superior or state-of-the-art asymptotic performance on challenging dexterous manipulation tasks. This is particularly impactful for applications where peak performance and policy robustness are paramount, such as advanced robotics and sim2real transfer.
3. **Enhanced Data Utilization and Exploration Diversity:** The core mechanism of splitting environments, training diverse policies, and aggregating data via importance sampling directly addresses the problem of redundant data sampling and limited exploration. By encouraging individual policies to explore varied strategies (e.g., through latent conditioning and entropy regularization) and then fusing their experiences, SAPG ensures that a greater diversity of high-reward trajectories is discovered and utilized for learning. This marks a departure from methods that discard data from "suboptimal" policies.
4. **Practical Implications for Robotics and Industrial Applications:** The ability to train highly performant agents for complex manipulation tasks in simulation has direct benefits for real-world robotic deployments. By enabling more effective learning in simulation, SAPG contributes to the development of more capable and robust robot policies, potentially reducing the need for extensive real-world experimentation. The emphasis on asymptotic performance aligns with the requirements of practitioners who prioritize the final capability of an agent within a reasonable training budget.
5. **Generalizability of the Approach:** While developed on PPO, the underlying principles of SAPG—decentralized policy exploration combined with centralized data aggregation—are presented as generally applicable to other on-policy RL algorithms. This suggests its potential to enhance a broader range of on-policy methods.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the trade-offs involved in data aggregation and diversity encouragement. The findings on symmetric vs. leader-follower aggregation, the impact of off-policy data ratios, and the role of entropy regularization can guide future research in designing more sophisticated and efficient distributed RL architectures. The quantitative metrics for diversity of exploration (PCA and MLP reconstruction error) also provide a novel way to analyze and compare exploration strategies in complex, high-dimensional state spaces.

In conclusion, SAPG represents a methodological advancement that makes on-policy reinforcement learning more robust and scalable in the era of massively parallel simulation, with significant implications for the development of advanced intelligent agents capable of solving complex real-world control problems.
