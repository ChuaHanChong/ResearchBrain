# MVFST-RL: An Asynchronous RL Framework for Congestion Control with Delayed Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> MVFST-RL is a framework for RL-based network congestion control that never blocks the sender. It couples MVFST (QUIC in C++), TorchBeast/IMPALA with V-trace off-policy correction, and Pantheon emulators, and augments state with an action history to handle delayed actions. On six calibrated Pantheon networks it reaches a throughput-delay trade-off competitive with classic algorithms, but fails to generalize to an unseen scenario.

## Problem

- Prior RL congestion control adopts Gym-style synchronous interfaces where the sender blocks while the policy computes, wasting bandwidth infeasible in real deployments.
- Blocking agents cost real throughput: 25ms and 50ms policy stalls transmit 1.1% and 11.4% fewer bytes over a 60-second emulated AWS California to Mexico link.
- Existing systems either centralize control over a whole topology, require hand-written traffic patterns and topologies, or force tiny models via 10ms step budgets.

## Method

- Formulates control as an ==MDP with delayed actions==, augmenting state with a length-h history of one-hot past actions plus resulting cwnd, giving a 100 + h(|A|+1) vector.
- Builds an asynchronous stack: QUIC sender in **MVFST** sends 100ms statistic windows by RPC to ==TorchBeast== actors running ==IMPALA== with ==V-trace== off-policy correction, modified to bootstrap value at episode termination.
- Uses a log throughput minus beta log delay reward with per-scenario online mean and standard deviation normalization, and a 2x1024 MLP trunk feeding a single-layer ==LSTM== with policy and baseline heads.

## Results

- On the Nepal to AWS India emulator, jointly trained over **six** scenarios, the agent beats a random-action baseline with slightly higher throughput and much lower delay, competitive with Cubic, BBR, Copa-class schemes.
- On a held-out token-bucket policer network (**20ms** RTT versus **54ms** minimum in training, one-packet buffer versus fourteen), the model clusters in the low-throughput high-delay corner, showing overfitting.
- Ablations over five seeds: entropy coefficient **0.01** was optimal, lower values collapsed to premature determinism, higher values hurt reward, and removing the LSTM significantly degraded episode reward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When inference latency perturbs the environment, treat it as a delayed-action MDP: conditioning on recent action history restores approximate Markov structure without changing the learner.
> - Off-policy-corrected asynchronous actor-learner training is useful beyond throughput, it is the enabling requirement whenever the environment physically cannot pause for a gradient or forward pass.
> - Per-scenario reward normalization fixes scale mismatch across environments but does not scale to randomized conditions, and narrow training scenarios still yield brittle out-of-distribution policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Network congestion control is a fundamental component for maintaining the operability and efficiency of large computer networks, including the internet. Historically, congestion control algorithms have relied on hand-crafted heuristics that operate reactively to network conditions. While these methods have been foundational, they often lack predictive capabilities and optimal performance across diverse and dynamic network environments.

In recent years, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an alternative approach to automatically optimize control strategies in various domains. Early work demonstrated the potential of offline-learned congestion control, with more recent RL-based approaches showing promise in simulated environments. These RL methods aim to learn policies that dynamically adjust data transmission rates to maximize throughput, minimize queuing delay, and reduce packet loss.

Despite the theoretical promise and successes in simulations, the transfer of RL-based congestion control (RLCC) methods to real-world production systems has been limited. A primary obstacle is the architectural paradigm often adopted by existing RLCC research. Many current RL environments for congestion control are built upon frameworks designed for synchronous interactions, such as OpenAI Gym, where the network sender must pause and wait for the RL agent to compute and deliver the next action. This blocking behavior introduces significant delays, leading to under-utilization of bandwidth and a substantial negative impact on overall network throughput in real-world scenarios where policy lookup times can be orders of magnitude longer than conventional methods. Furthermore, deploying complex machine learning models on network servers with limited resources often necessitates offloading inference, exacerbating these delays.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the practical deployment challenges of RLCC. It moves away from the synchronous blocking paradigm to propose and evaluate an asynchronous RL framework, aiming to bridge the gap between simulation-based RLCC successes and their practical application in production network environments. It contrasts with approaches like Iroko, which uses a centralized policy suitable for smaller networks, and PCC-RL, which implements a blocking RL agent, or Park, which uses RPC but remains synchronous with very short step-times.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MVFST-RL is to overcome the practical barriers preventing the deployment of Reinforcement Learning-based congestion control (RLCC) in real-world production networks. The authors identify several key problems and set specific objectives to address them:

1. **Addressing the Performance Impact of Synchronous RL Agents:** Existing RLCC research typically uses a synchronous interaction model where the network sender blocks while waiting for the RL agent to compute and provide the next action. This blocking leads to significant under-utilization of bandwidth and reduced throughput, as network data transmission is stalled. The paper explicitly demonstrates this problem, showing that delays of merely 25ms or 50ms for policy lookup can result in substantial reductions (1.1% and 11.4% respectively) in cumulative bytes sent over a 60-second period.
 - **Objective:** To formulate congestion control with an asynchronous RL agent capable of handling delayed actions, thereby eliminating the need for the sender to block and ensuring continuous data flow.

2. **Mitigating Latency and Resource Constraints of RL Inference:** Policy lookups for machine learning models are generally much slower than the execution of hand-crafted heuristics. Furthermore, network servers often have constrained computational resources, necessitating the offloading of RL inference to dedicated servers, which introduces additional communication latency. These factors collectively contribute to the delay in action updates, making synchronous RL approaches impractical for high-performance networking.
 - **Objective:** To develop a scalable framework that leverages state-of-the-art asynchronous RL training techniques with off-policy correction, specifically IMPALA with V-trace, to enable efficient and non-blocking operation in a decoupled actor-learner architecture.

3. **Facilitating Seamless Deployment in Production Systems:** For RLCC to be viable in real-world networks, it must integrate with existing production-grade transport protocols.
 - **Objective:** To build the framework, named MVFST-RL, on MVFST, which is Facebook's C++ implementation of the QUIC transport protocol used in its production networks. This direct integration is intended to allow for a streamlined path to deployment.

4. **Ensuring Realistic Evaluation of RLCC Performance:** Evaluating RLCC algorithms requires realistic network environments that accurately mimic real-world conditions, rather than relying on simplified or hand-specified topologies and traffic patterns.
 - **Objective:** To utilize the Pantheon benchmark platform for network emulation. Pantheon provides emulated networks calibrated with Bayesian Optimization to match diverse real-world network conditions, obviating the need for manual scenario configuration.

5. **Improving Modeling for Delayed Actions:** Introducing asynchronous interaction with delayed actions can lead to deviations from the standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) assumption, where the next state depends only on the current state and action.
 - **Objective:** To investigate and implement modeling improvements, specifically by augmenting the state space with a history of past actions, to mitigate the non-Markovian dynamics introduced by delayed actions and improve the learning stability and performance of the RL agent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MVFST-RL framework is designed around an environment-driven, asynchronous Reinforcement Learning agent interaction model. The methodology addresses the challenges of delayed actions and real-world deployment through a combination of MDP reformulation, a custom reward function, a specific RL architecture, and a detailed model design.

#### 3.1. Congestion Control as an MDP with Delayed Actions

The problem of congestion control is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). However, given the asynchronous nature and delayed application of actions, a modification to the standard MDP formulation is introduced.
- **Delayed Action Impact:** When an action $a_t$ is computed by the agent at time $t$ but applied after a delay $\delta$, the environment continues to transmit data based on the previous action $a_{t-1}$ during the interval $[t, t + \delta]$. Consequently, the next observed state $s_{t+1}$ depends not only on $s_t$ and $a_t$ but also on $a_{t-1}$.
- **State Space Augmentation:** To restore Markovian dynamics, the state space $\mathcal{S}$ is augmented to $\hat{\mathcal{S}} = \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}^h$, where $h$ is the length of the action history. The augmented state $\hat{s}_t$ includes the current network statistics $s_t$ and a history of the $h$ most recent actions $(a_{t-1}..., a_{t-h})$.

#### 3.2. Environment State and Reward Function

- **Environment State (Appendix A.1):** The state vector aggregates network statistics over a 100ms window, along with the action history.
 - **Features:** From each Acknowledgement (ACK) packet within the window, 20 features are gathered, including latest RTT, minimum RTT, smoothed RTT, queuing delay, congestion window size in bytes, bytes in flight, bytes sent/received/re-transmitted/acknowledged/lost, instantaneous throughput, re-transmission counts, and a flag for persistent congestion.
 - **Normalization and Aggregation:** Time-based features (e.g., RTT) are scaled by $10^{-3}$, and byte-based features by $10^{-4}$. For each of the 20 features, sum, mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum values are computed across the 100ms window, resulting in 100 features.
 - **Action History:** The history of $h$ past actions is encoded as one-hot vectors, concatenated with the congestion window value (`cwnd`) resulting from each of those actions. For the experiments, $h=16$ and the action space size is $|A|=5$, leading to a total state vector length of $100 + 16 \times (5 + 1) = 196$.
 - **Throughput Calculation:** Based on a rolling window of the last 10 ACKs, with a minimum window duration of 100ms. Throughput is linearly decreased if no ACKs are received for an extended period.
- **Reward Function:** The reward is formulated as $\log(t + \epsilon) - \beta \log(d + \epsilon)$, where $t$ is the average throughput (MB/sec) and $d$ is the maximum delay (ms) during the 100ms window. $\beta$ is a trade-off parameter (set to 0.75 in experiments), and $\epsilon$ is a small value for numerical stability. This log-based reward is chosen to handle widely varying throughput and delay scales across different network scenarios, an issue encountered with linear reward functions.
- **Reward Normalization:** To prevent the model from favoring scenarios with higher reward magnitudes, an online estimate of the mean and standard deviation of the reward is maintained for each training scenario, used to normalize the reward during training.

#### 3.3. MVFST-RL Framework Architecture

The framework integrates three core components:
1. **MVFST:** Facebook's C++ implementation of the QUIC transport protocol, forming the network environment.
2. **TorchBeast:** A PyTorch implementation of IMPALA, enabling fast, asynchronous, and parallel RL training.
3. **Pantheon:** A network emulation platform calibrated with Bayesian Optimization to simulate real-world network conditions.

- **Asynchronous Interaction:** The RL congestion controller, integrated into MVFST, asynchronously sends aggregated network state updates to an RL agent running in a separate thread, typically every 100ms. The agent then executes its policy, and the resulting action (an update to `cwnd`) is asynchronously applied back to the transport layer (Figure 2a). Crucially, the network sender does not block during this process.
- **Training Architecture (Figure 2b):** The training system employs a decoupled actor-learner architecture.
 - **Actors:** 40 parallel actors are used, each corresponding to a sender-receiver setup within a randomly chosen Pantheon emulator instance. These actors perform policy rollouts by interacting with their respective network environments.
 - **Learner:** The learner asynchronously receives trajectories (sequences of state, action, reward, next state) from the actors. It then performs gradient updates on the policy using the V-trace off-policy correction algorithm from IMPALA. This asynchronous interaction allows actors to continue collecting data without waiting for the learner's updates.
 - **Bootstrap on Episode Termination:** To ensure consistent learning and prevent the LSTM from anticipating episode ends at deployment, state values are bootstrapped even for terminal states, i.e., $V(s_t) = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1})$.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture

The RL agent's policy and value function are parameterized by a neural network.
- **Trunk:** A two-layer fully-connected neural network, each with 1024 units and ReLU activation.
- **Recurrent Layer:** A single-layer Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network processes the extracted features and the un-normalized reward, allowing the model to capture temporal dependencies.
- **Output Heads:** The LSTM output is fed into two separate heads: one for the policy (outputting probabilities for each of the $|A|$ actions) and another for the policy gradient baseline (value function).

#### 3.5. Action Space (Appendix A.2)

The action space consists of 5 discrete relative updates to the current congestion window (`cwnd`):
$\mathcal{A} = \{\text{cwnd, cwnd/2, cwnd - 10, cwnd + 10, cwnd} \times 2\}$
These updates are bounded between 2 and 2000 MSS (Maximum Segment Size). The initial `cwnd` is 10 MSS.

#### 3.6. Training Procedure

- **Episodic Training:** Each Pantheon sender-receiver instance runs for 30 seconds per episode, and the generated trajectory is used for training. This design choice aims to expose the agent to varied network dynamics, including initial slow-start phases and steady-state behavior.
- **Hyperparameters:** An initial learning rate of $10^{-4}$ is used with RMSProp optimizer. The trade-off parameter $\beta$ in the reward function is set to 0.75, and $\epsilon$ to $10^{-5}$.
- **Duration:** Training is conducted for a total of 5 million steps.
- **Deployment:** After training, the model can be exported using TorchScript and executed directly in C++ on the network host, eliminating the need for RPC during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MVFST-RL involved testing its performance on both seen and unseen network scenarios, as well as conducting ablation studies to understand the contribution of specific architectural choices.

#### 4.1. Performance on Seen Network Scenarios

- **Competitive Trade-off:** When tested on a Pantheon emulator calibrated against the "Nepal to AWS India" network (one of the six scenarios used during training), MVFST-RL demonstrated a meaningful trade-off between throughput and delay (Figure 3a). The performance of MVFST-RL was competitive with various conventional and learning-based congestion control algorithms such as FillP, Verus, TCP Cubic, Sprout, PCC-Vivace, TCP BBR, TCP Vegas, LEDBAT, PCC-Allegro, and SCReAM.
- **Superior to Baseline:** Compared to `rl-random`, a baseline agent that takes random actions from the same action space, MVFST-RL achieved slightly higher throughput while significantly reducing delay.
- **Throughput-Delay Profile:** Across the six training scenarios, the trained model consistently tended to achieve lower throughput compared to algorithms optimized purely for maximum throughput, but concurrently exhibited lower delay. The authors suggest that adjusting the reward function's trade-off parameter ($\beta$) could potentially shift this balance towards higher throughput if desired.

#### 4.2. Performance on Unseen Network Scenarios

- **Limited Generalization:** MVFST-RL was evaluated on a held-out network scenario that was not part of the training set. This scenario involved a token-bucket policer, resulting in qualitatively different network conditions, specifically a lower RTT (20ms vs. a minimum of 54ms in training) and a significantly smaller buffer queue capacity (one packet vs. a minimum of fourteen packets in training). In this unseen environment, the model's performance was notably degraded compared to other congestion control algorithms (Figure 3b), clustering among solutions with lower throughput and higher delay.
- **Overfitting to Training Conditions:** The authors attribute this lack of generalization to the limited variability in the training conditions, suggesting that the model may have overfit to the specific characteristics of the training scenarios. They propose domain randomization during training as a potential future direction to improve robustness and generalization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Entropy Regularization:** The impact of IMPALA's entropy regularization coefficient on the total reward per episode was investigated (Figure 3c). The study found that:
 - Low entropy regularization could lead to unstable training and premature convergence to deterministic, potentially sub-optimal, policies.
 - High entropy regularization negatively affected performance by promoting excessively random agent behavior.
 - An optimal value of 0.01 was determined for this hyper-parameter, balancing exploration and stability.
- **Role of LSTM:** Experiments without the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) component in the model architecture revealed significantly degraded performance (Figure 3d vs. 3e). This result highlights the crucial role of recurrent connections within the model, which are posited to be particularly beneficial for capturing temporal dependencies and handling the partial observability inherent in network environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MVFST-RL framework presents a significant step towards the practical deployment of Reinforcement Learning for network congestion control by addressing a critical operational limitation: the blocking behavior of synchronous RL agents.

- **Enabling Real-World RL Deployment:** The primary contribution is demonstrating a viable method for integrating RL into high-performance network systems without incurring performance penalties from policy computation delays. By formulating congestion control with an asynchronous RL agent that handles delayed actions, MVFST-RL mitigates the under-utilization and throughput degradation associated with synchronous approaches. This makes RLCC more amenable to real-world operational environments, such as data centers or the Internet.

- **Practical Framework and Integration:** The framework's implementation on MVFST, Facebook's production-grade QUIC protocol, along with the use of TorchBeast for scalable asynchronous training and Pantheon for realistic network emulation, provides a robust and deployable solution. The ability to export the trained model via TorchScript for direct C++ execution on the network host further streamlines the deployment process by eliminating RPC overhead during inference. This tight integration with a production transport protocol suggests a clearer path to real-world evaluation and potential adoption.

- **Improved MDP Modeling:** The augmentation of the MDP state space with a history of past actions is a practical modeling improvement that effectively mitigates the non-Markovian dynamics introduced by delayed actions. This methodological refinement contributes to the stability and learning capability of the RL agent in such challenging environments.

- **Open-Source Contribution:** The public release of MVFST-RL's source code (on GitHub) can foster further research and development in the field of RL for network control. It provides a foundation for other researchers and practitioners to build upon, test new algorithms, and explore different network control problems.

- **Identification of Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies important avenues for future work, notably the challenge of learning robust policies that generalize well to unseen and qualitatively different network scenarios. The suggestion of employing domain randomization during training to enhance generalization and developing more scalable reward normalization strategies across highly diverse environments are crucial for advancing the practical utility of RLCC. Further investigation into the trade-off coefficient ($\beta$) in the reward function is also noted as a means to optimize for specific throughput-delay profiles. These insights are valuable for guiding subsequent research efforts in the domain.
