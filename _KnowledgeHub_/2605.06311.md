# Toward Visually Realistic Simulation: A Benchmark for Evaluating Robot Manipulation in Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Nanjing University researchers and collaborators introduced VISER, a benchmark for robot manipulation that achieves high visual realism through physically-based rendering and an MLLM-driven asset generation pipeline. It demonstrates an average 0.92 Pearson correlation with real-world robot performance, providing a more reliable platform for evaluating Vision-Language-Action models by explicitly addressing the sim-to-real visual gap.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation simulations suffer from a "sim-to-real visual gap," where unrealistic lighting and material properties undermine policy transfer to the real world.
- Current simulation benchmarks often prioritize physical realism and task diversity but neglect visual fidelity, impairing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' geometric reasoning and spatial grounding.
- Common issues like simplified materials lacking specular highlights or inaccurate shadows lead to discrepancies, causing VLA policies that succeed in simulation to fail in real environments.

## Method

- Developed **VISER**, a benchmark designed for high visual realism in robot manipulation, utilizing ==ray tracing== for plausible lighting and ==physically-based rendering (PBR)== materials for assets.
- Created an ==MLLM-driven asset generation pipeline== producing over 1,000 3D assets with high-fidelity PBR textures without baked-lighting artifacts.
- Established a comprehensive suite of evaluation tasks, including basic skills, reconstructed real-world scenarios, and ==automatically generated long-horizon tasks== to assess VLA models.

## Results

- VISER achieved an average **Pearson r = 0.92** between simulation and real-world performance for evaluated policies, significantly outperforming existing benchmarks.
- The 3D asset dataset features **over 1,000 objects** with high-fidelity PBR materials, demonstrating superior visual quality (**VLM-S: 55.35**) compared to previous datasets like RoboTwin and ManiTwin.
- Evaluations on VISER revealed that current mainstream VLA models exhibit suboptimal performance on out-of-distribution tasks and struggle with increasing scene complexity or **multi-step long-horizon instructions**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specular highlights are critical for VLA models to accurately perceive object geometry, with their absence causing significant performance drops in tasks requiring cavity localization.
> - Accurate contact shadows are essential for precise spatial grounding and depth perception, as their absence or unrealistic presence severely degrades VLA performance.
> - Enhancing visual realism in simulation, specifically through PBR materials and realistic lighting, directly leads to a strong and reliable sim-to-real correlation for robot manipulation policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is experiencing rapid advancements, particularly with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models capable of achieving generalization across various tasks and environments. A critical component for advancing these policies is the ability to reliably evaluate their performance, robustness, and generalization capabilities. While real-world evaluation offers the highest fidelity, it is inherently costly, time-consuming, and presents scalability limitations. Consequently, simulation-based evaluation has become a crucial alternative, offering efficiency and safety for assessing policies in novel or complex scenarios.

However, a significant challenge in simulation-based evaluation is the "sim-to-real gap," which refers to the discrepancy between simulated and real-world environments. This gap can be categorized into physical and visual discrepancies. While physical discrepancies have seen progress through methods like system identification, the visual gap, particularly concerning visual realism, has remained largely underexplored in the context of robot manipulation policies.

Existing simulation benchmarks and datasets for robot manipulation, such as LIBERO, CALVIN, ManiSkill, Behavior-1K, Habitat 2.0, SimplerEnv, RoboCASA, VLABench, RoboTwin, and ManiTwin, primarily focus on task diversity or employing domain randomization techniques to mitigate the visual gap. Many of these benchmarks are built on physics engines like MuJoCo, which prioritize physical realism and fast rasterization but often compromise on visual fidelity. Datasets leveraging 3D generation models, while scalable, frequently suffer from "baked-lighting artifacts" or lack the high-fidelity textures necessary for accurate real-world representation. These limitations often result in a mismatch between simulated and real-world evaluation, where a policy performing well in simulation might fail in real environments with complex materials and illumination.

This paper addresses this specific lacuna by focusing on enhancing visual realism in simulation environments. It positions itself as a systematic effort to identify the critical visual factors (lighting and material properties) that contribute to the sim-to-real gap and to develop a benchmark, VISER, that directly addresses these deficiencies. By providing a photorealistic simulation environment with high-fidelity assets and a validated sim-to-real correlation, this work aims to improve the reliability of simulation as a proxy for real-world robotic performance, thereby enabling more effective training and evaluation of advanced VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized unreliability of existing simulation-based evaluations for robot manipulation policies, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While simulation offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative to real-world testing, a significant "sim-to-real visual gap" undermines its predictive power. Current benchmarks, despite covering a range of tasks and sometimes employing domain randomization, have largely overlooked the importance of visual realism, specifically regarding lighting and material properties. This oversight leads to scenarios where policies that succeed in simulation may fail in the real world due to misinterpretations of object geometry, spatial relationships, or surface characteristics.

The paper identifies that factors such as the absence of realistic specular highlights in materials and accurate shadows from lighting significantly impair VLA models' geometric reasoning and spatial grounding capabilities. For instance, simplified materials can prevent models from accurately perceiving object shapes, and the lack of proper shadows can cause objects to appear "floating," hindering precise contact point determination. The reliance on techniques like "green-screening" in some benchmarks, while attempting to bridge the visual gap, introduces physically implausible lighting artifacts (e.g., missing shadows and incorrect reflections), further exacerbating the problem. The authors' systematic analysis (detailed in Section 3) confirmed that these visual factors are indeed critical but neglected, thereby motivating the development of a new, more visually realistic benchmark.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Systematic Analysis of the Sim-to-Real Visual Gap:** To conduct an isolated and systematic study of the effects of lighting and material properties on VLA performance. This involves identifying which specific visual factors (e.g., specular highlights, types of shadows) critically influence geometric reasoning and spatial grounding in robot manipulation tasks.
2. **Development of a Visually Realistic Benchmark (VISER):** To introduce VISER as a novel benchmark specifically designed to provide high visual realism for evaluating robot manipulation in simulation.
3. **Construction of a High-Fidelity 3D Asset Dataset:** To create a large-scale dataset of over 1,000 3D assets featuring physically-based rendering (PBR) materials, ensuring clean, high-fidelity textures without baked-lighting artifacts that commonly plague assets generated by existing 3D generation models.
4. **Proposal of an Automated Asset Generation Pipeline:** To develop an efficient and scalable pipeline for generating physically plausible assets. This pipeline leverages Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for material-aware part segmentation and material retrieval, facilitating the creation of high-quality assets.
5. **Establishment of Diverse and Standardized Evaluation Tasks:** To build a comprehensive suite of evaluation tasks, including basic manipulation skills (grasping, placing, pushing), reconstructed tasks from real-world scenarios, and generated long-horizon tasks, all designed to enable scalable and reproducible assessment of VLA models.
6. **Demonstration of Strong Sim-to-Real Correlation:** To validate that the enhanced visual realism in VISER significantly boosts the consistency between simulation and real-world performance, thereby improving the reliability of simulation-based evaluations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on systematically analyzing the visual sim-to-real gap and subsequently constructing a visually realistic benchmark, VISER, to address identified shortcomings.

#### 3.1. Sim-to-Real Visual Gap Analysis (Section 3)

The authors began by conducting a systematic study to isolate and quantify the impact of specific visual factors on VLA performance.
- **Experimental Setup:** Experiments were carried out within the SimplerEnv framework, built upon the SAPIEN simulator. The key modification involved replacing SimplerEnv's "green-screening" approach with real assets and employing ray tracing for photorealistic rendering. A WidowX robot was used, and various policies from the BridgeData-v2 dataset were evaluated. Simulation reliability was measured by aligning success rates between simulated and real-world environments.
- **Factors Analyzed:**
 - **Specular Highlights:** To study their effect, materials were rendered with roughness set to 1.0 and metallic to 0.0, simulating a purely diffuse material without specular reflections. This was compared against rendering with distinct specular highlights. A "put eggplant in pot" task was used, where accurate geometry understanding of the pot's cavity was crucial.
 - **Shadows:** The impact of contact shadows was investigated by comparing scenarios: 1) without shadows (simulating "green-screening"), 2) with hard shadows (from rasterization or strong directional light), and 3) with soft shadows (from ray tracing). A "put spoon on towel" task was utilized, requiring precise spatial grounding and distance perception.

#### 3.2. VISER Construction Pipeline (Section 4)

Based on the insights from the visual gap analysis, the VISER benchmark was constructed through a multi-stage pipeline designed for visual realism.
- **MLLM-driven Material Retrieval and Refinement (Section 4.1):** This pipeline aims to generate high-fidelity PBR materials for 3D assets, addressing issues like "baked-lighting artifacts" prevalent in generated assets.
 - **Preliminary Asset Generation:** Initial textured meshes were sourced from public datasets or generated using models like Tencent Hunyuan3D-v3.0. GPT-4o was used to generate object lists and prompts, while GPT-4o-mini estimated object scales and densities. CoACD was used for collision geometry generation.
 - **Material-Aware Part Segmentation:** Objects were rendered from 32 perspectives to address viewpoint occlusions. A Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) performed material-aware segmentation. To ensure consistency across views, the MLLM first established a unified global part list based on material classes.
 - **Material Retrieval and Refinement:** Each segmented part was captioned by the MLLM to retrieve semantically matching PBR materials from a material library (e.g., Matsynth). Detailed descriptions for library materials were synthesized using MLLM-rendered reference material balls to enhance retrieval accuracy.
 - **Precise Segmentation and Inspection:** For fine-grained segmentation, textual descriptions and MLLM-generated bounding boxes were provided as prompts to SAM3. A secondary MLLM-based evaluator iteratively refined prompts by providing additional positive point prompts to SAM if a mask was incomplete.
 - **PBR Texture Generation:** High-quality multi-view masks were projected into UV space, where they were integrated with the retrieved PBR materials to generate final textures for individual components.
- **Layout Generation (Section 4.2):** To overcome the time-consuming and unscalable nature of manual scene design in previous benchmarks, an automated approach for generating plausible layouts was developed.
 - **Scene Graph Construction:** Given an input description or image, an LLM extracted objects and constructed a scene graph representing desired relative spatial relations.
 - **Spatial Coordinate Estimation:** The LLM was provided with object bounding boxes and table dimensions to estimate precise 2D spatial coordinates, simplifying the problem to a 2D plane projection.
 - **Object Instantiation:** After validating the coordinates, objects were instantiated within the simulation engine.

#### 3.3. VISER Benchmark Construction (Section 5.2)

The benchmark incorporates diverse tasks to provide comprehensive evaluation.
- **Task Categories:** Tasks are divided into basic skills (pick up, put in, push near, pick from, open) and long-horizon tasks with abstract goals (e.g., "prepare the ingredients for an apple pie and clear the table").
- **Task Types:**
 - **Curated Tasks:** 14 standardized tasks covering various manipulation categories.
 - **Reconstructed Tasks:** 8 tasks faithfully reconstructed from real-world benchmarks (e.g., BridgeDataV2 scenes) to enable sim-to-real correlated evaluation.
 - **Generated Tasks:** Automatically created by recombining assets and layouts based on descriptions via the layout generation pipeline.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics (Section 5.3)

- **Basic Tasks:** Success was determined by monitoring the spatial trajectories and physical states of target objects.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** For these complex tasks, the Qwen-3-VL model was employed to analyze execution videos, assessing both functional success (terminal state) and procedural correctness (logical sequence of actions). This automated measure is denoted as Agent Score (AS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the importance of visual realism in simulation and the performance of the proposed VISER benchmark.

#### 4.1. Sim-to-Real Visual Gap Analysis (Section 3.2)

The systematic analysis demonstrated the critical role of specific visual factors in VLA performance:
- **Specular Highlights:** The absence of specular highlights significantly impaired geometric understanding. In the "put eggplant in pot" task (Figure 2a), the VLA model failed to localize the pot's cavity without specular highlights, leading to a success rate drop from 90% to 10% (Table 2), contrasting with a 100% success rate in the real world for grasping the eggplant.
- **Shadows:** Contact shadows were found to be essential for accurate spatial grounding and depth perception. Without shadows (akin to "green-screening"), the "put spoon on towel" task (Figure 2b) showed a success rate of only 12%, compared to 42% in the real world. The presence of hard shadows further degraded performance to nearly 0% (Table 3), indicating they act as noise in synthetic environments. This underscores the necessity of PBR materials and plausible soft shadows.

#### 4.2. 3D Asset Dataset Quality (Section 5.1 & 6.1)

The VISER asset dataset consists of 12 super categories, 319 categories, and 1049 objects, covering common tabletop merchandise.
- **Material Fidelity:** The assets feature high-fidelity PBR materials with rich surface details (e.g., metallic scratches, ceramic-like textures), critically avoiding baked-lighting artifacts. This was a direct result of the MLLM-driven material retrieval and refinement pipeline.
- **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 7, Figure 8):** Compared to existing datasets like RoboTwin and ManiTwin, VISER assets exhibited superior material fidelity. Furthermore, in comparison to state-of-the-art material generation/retrieval methods (MaterialMVP, Hunyuan3D-v3.1, Make It Real), the proposed approach captured intricate details more effectively and successfully decoupled lighting from materials.
- **Quantitative Comparison (Table 4):** A qualitative assessment using VLM-S (Gemini-3.1 score for visual quality) and CLIP-S (CLIP image-to-text consistency score) showed VISER's dataset achieving superior performance (VLM-S: 55.35; CLIP-S: 25.20) compared to RoboTwin (VLM-S: 45.66; CLIP-S: 21.35) and ManiTwin (VLM-S: 38.27; CLIP-S: 20.75).

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Correlation (Section 6.2)

The benchmark demonstrated a strong correlation between policy performance in simulation and the real world:
- **High Pearson Correlation Coefficient:** Across different policies and reconstructed BridgeDataV2 tasks, VISER achieved an average Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.92 with real-world performance (Table 5). For specific policies, VISER showed a correlation of 0.9988 for Octo and 0.8496 for OpenVLA, significantly outperforming SimplerEnv which exhibited a correlation of 0.8860 for Octo and a negative correlation of -0.2712 for OpenVLA. This indicates a high alignment between VISER's simulation-based evaluations and actual robotic outcomes.

#### 4.4. Evaluation of VLAs (Section 6.3)

Mainstream VLA models (Octo, OpenVLA, X-VLA) were evaluated on VISER's diverse tasks, including Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios with varying difficulty levels (Table 6):
- **Suboptimal OOD Performance:** While VLAs showed basic ability to process unseen scenes and instructions, their performance remained suboptimal on most OOD tasks.
- **Impact of Distractors and Complexity:** Success rates significantly dropped when background distractors were introduced or when instructions became increasingly complex (multi-step tasks). For instance, in "Pick up paper cup," the success rate for OpenVLA dropped from 0.4 (lv.1) to 0.0 (lv.3) when the cup was placed behind a kettle. Long-horizon tasks also showed low Agent Scores, indicating challenges in abstract goal understanding and multi-step reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VISER represents a significant contribution to the field of robot manipulation and the evaluation of VLA models, with several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Simulation Reliability for Robot Manipulation:** By systematically identifying and addressing the "visual realism gap," VISER provides a more reliable and high-fidelity simulation environment. The demonstrated strong sim-to-real correlation (average Pearson coefficient of 0.92) means that evaluation results obtained in VISER are more predictive of real-world robot performance than those from previous benchmarks. This directly improves the trustworthiness of simulation as a proxy for real-world testing.
2. **Advancement of VLA Model Development:** VISER enables a more accurate and nuanced assessment of VLA models' capabilities, particularly concerning their geometric reasoning and spatial grounding abilities, which were shown to be critically dependent on visual realism. By revealing the limitations of current VLA models on out-of-distribution tasks and complex scenarios (e.g., with distractors or multi-step instructions), the benchmark highlights key bottlenecks for future research and development in generalized robot intelligence.
3. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Asset and Task Generation:** The proposed MLLM-driven pipeline for material retrieval and refinement offers a scalable solution for generating high-fidelity 3D assets with physically-based rendering properties, free from common artifacts like baked lighting. Coupled with the automated layout generation process, this methodology reduces the laborious and time-consuming manual effort typically associated with creating diverse and realistic simulation environments and tasks. This accelerates the creation of large-scale datasets necessary for training and evaluating next-generation robotic policies.
4. **Foundation for Future Research:** VISER provides a robust foundation for various research directions. It can facilitate the development of more robust VLA models, explore advanced rendering techniques for even greater realism, and enable the investigation of how different visual cues impact robot learning and decision-making. The open-source nature of the benchmark and dataset encourages community engagement and collaborative research.
5. **Reduced Reliance on Real-World Experimentation:** By offering a more reliable simulation environment, VISER can help reduce the significant costs, safety concerns, and time commitment associated with extensive real-world robot experiments. This allows researchers to iterate more rapidly on policy design, conduct more exhaustive testing, and explore a wider range of scenarios virtually before deploying to physical robots.

In summary, VISER addresses a fundamental limitation in robot manipulation research by bridging the visual sim-to-real gap. Its visually realistic assets, diverse tasks, and validated correlation with real-world performance promise to accelerate progress in developing more capable, robust, and generalizable robotic systems.
