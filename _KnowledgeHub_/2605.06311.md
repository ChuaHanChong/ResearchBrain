# Toward Visually Realistic Simulation: A Benchmark for Evaluating Robot Manipulation in Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Nanjing University researchers and collaborators introduced VISER, a benchmark for robot manipulation that achieves high visual realism through physically-based rendering and an MLLM-driven asset generation pipeline. It demonstrates an average 0.92 Pearson correlation with real-world robot performance, providing a more reliable platform for evaluating Vision-Language-Action models by explicitly addressing the sim-to-real visual gap.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation simulations suffer from a "sim-to-real visual gap," where unrealistic lighting and material properties undermine policy transfer to the real world.
- Current simulation benchmarks often prioritize physical realism and task diversity but neglect visual fidelity, impairing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' geometric reasoning and spatial grounding.
- Common issues like simplified materials lacking specular highlights or inaccurate shadows lead to discrepancies, causing VLA policies that succeed in simulation to fail in real environments.

## Method

- Developed **VISER**, a benchmark designed for high visual realism in robot manipulation, utilizing ==ray tracing== for plausible lighting and ==physically-based rendering (PBR)== materials for assets.
- Created an ==MLLM-driven asset generation pipeline== producing over 1,000 3D assets with high-fidelity PBR textures without baked-lighting artifacts.
- Established a comprehensive suite of evaluation tasks, including basic skills, reconstructed real-world scenarios, and ==automatically generated long-horizon tasks== to assess VLA models.

## Results

- VISER achieved an average **Pearson r = 0.92** between simulation and real-world performance for evaluated policies, significantly outperforming existing benchmarks.
- The 3D asset dataset features **over 1,000 objects** with high-fidelity PBR materials, demonstrating superior visual quality (**VLM-S: 55.35**) compared to previous datasets like RoboTwin and ManiTwin.
- Evaluations on VISER revealed that current mainstream VLA models exhibit suboptimal performance on out-of-distribution tasks and struggle with increasing scene complexity or **multi-step long-horizon instructions**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specular highlights are critical for VLA models to accurately perceive object geometry, with their absence causing significant performance drops in tasks requiring cavity localization.
> - Accurate contact shadows are essential for precise spatial grounding and depth perception, as their absence or unrealistic presence severely degrades VLA performance.
> - Enhancing visual realism in simulation, specifically through PBR materials and realistic lighting, directly leads to a strong and reliable sim-to-real correlation for robot manipulation policies.
