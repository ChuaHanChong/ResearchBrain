# Eliminating Inductive Bias in Reward Models with Information-Theoretic Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, developed Debiasing via Information optimization for RM (DIR), an information-theoretic method to eliminate inductive biases in reward models used for LLM alignment. This approach significantly improved reward model robustness and led to LLMs with enhanced performance across various benchmarks, exhibiting reduced length, sycophancy, and format biases.

## Problem

- Reward models (RMs) in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) are susceptible to "reward hacking," where they exploit spurious correlations or inductive biases (e.g., length, sycophancy, format) from low-quality human preference data.
- Existing debiasing methods are often limited to specific bias types, capture only simple linear correlations, or lack robust theoretical justification, failing to address diverse and complex non-linear dependencies.
- Inductive biases compromise the stability, reliability, and generalization of RLHF, causing LLMs to generate undesirable behaviors instead of truly helpful and harmless ones.

## Method

- DIR introduces an information-theoretic framework inspired by the ==Information Bottleneck== principle, optimizing a mutual information objective that maximizes relevant preference information while minimizing information linked to irrelevant bias attributes.
- The method utilizes variational bounds (Barber-Agakov and CLUB) to estimate and optimize the mutual information terms, translating the objective into a tractable loss function that minimizes the Bradley-Terry ranking loss and reduces correlation between RM hidden states and bias attributes.
- It employs a lightweight variational network to estimate bias-related mutual information and handles complex biases by converting them into relative categorical attributes, allowing for concurrent debiasing of multiple bias types.

## Results

- DIR achieved the lowest Pearson correlation coefficient (r = **0.468**) for length bias on RM-Bench, significantly mitigating the "longer is better" bias, and attained the highest overall RM-Bench score of 70.18, with over 16 points gain on "Hard" subsets.
- LLMs fine-tuned with DIR-debiased RMs consistently showed higher average accuracy (e.g., 66.20 for Llama3.1-**8B**-Instruct) across diverse benchmarks and achieved superior win rates on ArenaHard (**54.3%** vs. baseline) while producing more concise responses.
- The method successfully mitigated sycophancy bias (highest preference accuracy on a semi-sycophantic dataset) and format bias (win-rates close to **50%** for bold/list formats) and effectively handled concurrent multi-biases, improving robustness against both length and sycophancy simultaneously.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating the RM debiasing problem within an information-theoretic framework provides a unified and theoretically grounded approach to handle diverse and complex non-linear inductive biases.
> - Explicitly minimizing mutual information between reward model representations and specific bias attributes effectively disentangles genuine human preferences from spurious correlations present in training data.
> - The use of relative bias attributes and difference-based hidden state representations enhances the efficacy of debiasing, making the method robust and compatible with other alignment techniques like DPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly aligned with human values and preferences through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). A core component of RLHF is the Reward Model (RM), which is trained on human preference data to score LLM responses. However, a recognized challenge in RLHF stability and effectiveness is the presence of "reward hacking," where RMs exploit spurious correlations or inductive biases in low-quality human preference data rather than discerning true content quality. Such biases include response length (favoring longer responses), stylistic or format patterns, and sycophantic phrasing.

Existing approaches to mitigate these inductive biases in RMs have demonstrated limitations. Some methods target specific types of bias (e.g., length) or model correlations using only simple linear measures, such as Pearson coefficients, which may not capture complex, non-linear dependencies. Other techniques involve adding auxiliary model heads (e.g., for length prediction) without robust theoretical justification or imposing overly restrictive constraints that risk distorting the reward landscape (e.g., enforcing distributional invariance using Maximum Mean Discrepancy). General compression strategies like the Information Bottleneck have been applied but without explicit constraints on biased attributes, thus not guaranteeing bias mitigation.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel, information-theoretic framework designed to uniformly handle diverse and complex inductive biases with theoretical grounding. It aims to develop a debiasing method that can capture arbitrary non-linear correlations, is applicable to various bias types, and does not compromise the underlying reward landscape, thus extending the applicability and robustness of RM debiasing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and theoretically justified method to eliminate inductive biases in reward models (RMs) used in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Reward Model Hacking**: Reward models are susceptible to "reward hacking," where they learn to prioritize irrelevant attributes correlated with human preference annotations instead of genuine response quality. This leads to RMs that misguide LLMs, causing them to generate undesirable behaviors (e.g., overly verbose or sycophantic responses) rather than truly helpful and harmless ones. The paper explicitly mentions that "reward model hacking is non-negligible" due to the low quality and inherent biases in human preference data.

2. **Mitigating Diverse Inductive Biases**: Common inductive biases include response length (humans often prefer more detailed, thus longer, responses), sycophancy (favoring responses that flatter the user), and stylistic/format patterns (e.g., use of lists or bold text). Current debiasing methods often target only a single type of bias or model only simple linear correlations. The authors highlight the need for a unified framework capable of handling "more complex and diverse inductive biases."

3. **Enhancing Generalization and Reliability of RLHF**: Inductive biases critically hinder the reliability and generalization ability of RLHF systems. When an RM is biased, the LLM optimized with it learns to exploit these flaws, leading to a degradation in true performance and an unstable training process. The goal is to produce RMs that are "more robust and reliable" and lead to "better generalization abilities" for downstream LLMs.

4. **Providing Theoretical Justification**: Many existing debiasing methods lack comprehensive theoretical guarantees, often relying on heuristic disentanglement or simple correlation penalties. The authors are motivated to provide a "uniform[ly] eliminate inductive biases... with theoretical guarantees," drawing inspiration from information theory, specifically the information bottleneck principle. This aims to establish a more rigorous foundation for debiasing.

5. **Overcoming Limitations of Prior Work**:
 - Methods relying on Pearson Coefficient are limited to capturing only linear correlations.
 - Approaches using separate RM heads for bias prediction are often heuristic and lack theoretical backing.
 - Methods enforcing restrictive external constraints (e.g., distributional invariance via MMD) risk distorting the genuine reward landscape.
 - General information compression strategies (e.g., InfoRM) do not explicitly constrain biased attributes, thus not guaranteeing bias mitigation.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond piecemeal and limited solutions by developing a theoretically robust, broadly applicable, and effective information-theoretic framework for debiasing RMs, thereby improving the alignment, stability, and generalization of LLMs in real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel information-theoretic debiasing method called Debiasing via Information optimization for RM (DIR). This method is inspired by the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle and aims to optimize reward models by simultaneously maximizing relevant information and minimizing irrelevant, biased information.

#### 3.1. Information-Theoretic Objective Formulation

The core of DIR is an objective function that models the debiasing problem using mutual information (MI). Given an input query `x`, a pair of responses `y` and `y_bar`, and a concerned bias attribute `b` associated with `(x, y, y_bar)`, the objective is formulated as:
`max_phi I(1_{y > y_bar}; x, y, y_bar) - lambda * I(1_{y > y_bar}; b)`
Here, `phi` represents the parameters of the reward model.
- The first term, `I(1_{y > y_bar}; x, y, y_bar)`, is the "Preference Term." It aims to maximize the mutual information between the RM's preference prediction (`1_{y > y_bar}`) and the actual content quality of the response pair (`x, y, y_bar`).
- The second term, `I(1_{y > y_bar}; b)`, is the "Debiasing Term." It aims to minimize the mutual information between the RM's preference prediction and the irrelevant bias attribute `b`.
- `lambda > 0` is a hyper-parameter that balances the trade-off between learning preferences and debiasing.

#### 3.2. Variational Mutual Information Estimation

Directly optimizing MI is computationally intractable. Therefore, the authors utilize variational bounds to estimate the MI terms:

- **Preference Term Estimation**:
 - The Barber-Agakov (BA) lower bound is used to approximate `I(1_{y > y_bar}; x, y, y_bar)`.
 - The BA bound implies that maximizing this MI term is equivalent to maximizing the log-likelihood of the RM's prediction with respect to the ground-truth human preference data.
 - This translates to minimizing the standard Bradley-Terry ranking loss:
 `L_Preference(phi) = - (1/B) * Sum_{i=1 to B} [log sigma(r_phi(x_i, y_w,i) - r_phi(x_i, y_l,i))]`
 where `B` is the batch size, `r_phi` is the RM score, `y_w` is the preferred response, and `y_l` is the rejected response. Minimizing this loss encourages the RM to assign higher scores to preferred responses.

- **Debiasing Term Estimation**:
 - The Data Processing Inequality and the Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound (CLUB) are applied to minimize `I(1_{y > y_bar}; b)`.
 - The authors consider the RM forward process as a Markov Chain `(b -> (x, y, y_bar) -> H -> 1_{y > y_bar})`, where `H` represents the last hidden states of the RM's transformer backbone.
 - The data processing inequality states that `I(1_{y > y_bar}; b) <= I(H; b)`. Therefore, minimizing `I(H; b)` serves as a proxy for minimizing the debiasing term.
 - The CLUB upper bound for `I(H; b)` is estimated using a variational approximation network `q_psi(b|H)`:
 `L_Debiasing(phi, psi) = - (1/B) * Sum_{i=1 to B} [log q_psi(b_i | H_i) - (1/B) * Sum_{j=1 to B} log q_psi(b_j | H_i)]`
 Minimizing `L_Debiasing` reduces the correlation between the biased attribute `b` and the RM's hidden representation `H`.

#### 3.3. Overall Objective and Training Procedure

The final tractable learning objective for DIR is:
`min_phi L_Preference(phi) + lambda * L_Debiasing(phi, psi)`

To ensure the accuracy of the CLUB estimator `q_psi(b|H)`, it is simultaneously trained by maximizing its log-likelihood on the batch samples. This is represented by `L_Estimator(psi) = (1/B) * Sum_{i=1 to B} log q_psi(b_i | H_i)`.

The training proceeds iteratively within each batch (Algorithm 1):
1. Sample a batch of preference triplets `(x, y_w, y_l, b)`.
2. Encode responses into embeddings `H`.
3. **Bias Estimator Update**: Update `q_psi(b|H)` by minimizing `L_Estimator(psi)` for a few steps (maximizes likelihood to accurately approximate `p*(b|H)`).
4. **Reward Model Update**:
 - Calculate `L_Preference(phi)`.
 - Calculate `L_Debiasing(phi, psi)`.
 - Compute the total loss `L_Total(phi, psi) = L_Preference(phi) + lambda * L_Debiasing(phi, psi)`.
 - Update the reward model `r_phi` parameters by minimizing `L_Total(phi, psi)`.

#### 3.4. Handling Relative Bias Attributes

For certain biases like response length, directly predicting absolute values from compressed representations can be challenging. DIR introduces the concept of "relative bias attributes." For instance, length bias is converted into a binary indicator: `b = 1{length(y) > length(y_bar)}`, indicating whether the chosen response is longer than the rejected one. This transforms continuous or complex biases into categorical labels, suitable for the variational network `q_psi`.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- The variational network `q_psi` is implemented as a lightweight two-layer categorical classifier (Softmax(MLP(H))) for categorical bias labels (e.g., "longer/shorter" for length, "sycophantic/in-sycophantic" for sycophancy).
- The representation `H` for `q_psi` is derived from the difference between the hidden states of the preferred and rejected responses: `Delta_h = h_phi(x, y_w) - h_phi(x, y_l)`, to emphasize the distinct features.
- Reward models are fine-tuned from Llama3.1-8B-Instruct. Downstream policy models are fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with PPO or DPO.

#### 3.6. Extension to Concurrent Multi-Biases

DIR can be extended to handle multiple concurrent biases by introducing independent mutual information terms, each with its own debiasing head:
`L_Total(phi) = L_Preference(phi) + lambda_length * L_Debiasing(phi, psi_length) + lambda_syco * L_Debiasing(phi, psi_syco)`

This methodology provides a principled, theoretically grounded, and flexible approach to tackle various inductive biases in reward modeling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate DIR covered three types of inductive biases: response length, sycophancy, and format, along with scenarios involving concurrent multi-biases.

#### 4.1. Length Debiasing

- **Reward Model Evaluation (RM-Bench)**:
 - Standard BT (Bradley-Terry) RMs showed a strong positive correlation between response length and reward scores (Pearson r = 0.533), indicating a "longer is better" bias.
 - DIR significantly mitigated this bias, achieving the lowest Pearson correlation coefficient (r = 0.468) among all evaluated methods, with a flatter reward-score curve across different response lengths (Figure 2).
 - On RM-Bench, DIR (Ours-10.0) achieved the highest overall score of 70.18, with particularly strong improvements on "Hard" subsets (64.41, >16 points over next best), suggesting better attention to content quality (Table 8).

- **PPO Evaluation (Downstream LLM Performance)**:
 - Policies fine-tuned with DIR-debiased RMs consistently achieved higher average performance across diverse benchmarks (GSM8K, MMLU, Hellaswag, etc.). For Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, DIR yielded an average accuracy of 66.20, outperforming baselines (Table 1).
 - On ArenaHard-v0.1, DIR-guided policies demonstrated the highest win rates against strong opponents (e.g., 54.3% vs. baseline, 41.9% vs. GPT-4o) while producing shorter responses (679 tokens vs. 754 baseline for Llama3.1 base), indicating a better trade-off between preference and conciseness (Figure 3).
 - DIR also achieved the highest win rates on MT-Bench and superior length-controlled win rates on AlpacaEval (Table 9, Table 10), further confirming improved open-ended dialogue quality and preference alignment without verbosity.

- **PPO Training Stability**: DIR resulted in more stable PPO training, evidenced by higher RLHF rewards and the lowest, most stable Approximate KL divergence, indicating controlled deviation from the reference model (Figure 4).

- **Training Cost and Ablations**:
 - DIR's computational cost was comparable to existing methods in terms of GPU memory and training time (Table 11).
 - Ablation studies confirmed that using a difference-based representation (`Delta_h`) for the debiasing module outperformed concatenation (Table 12).
 - The debiasing coefficient `lambda=1` was found to strike an optimal balance between bias reduction and preference learning performance (Figure 5).

- **Combination with DPO**: DIR effectively enhanced Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). When integrated, DIR-boosted DPO yielded higher win rates and better length control on ArenaHard (Table 2) and improved performance on various benchmarks (Table 3), demonstrating compatibility with other alignment methods.

#### 4.2. Sycophancy Debiasing

- On a semi-sycophantic dataset, DIR consistently achieved the highest preference accuracy across natural, adversarial, and overall settings, even under high contamination ratios. This indicated its ability to mitigate the influence of sycophantic signals and focus on intrinsic response quality, outperforming both vanilla BT and InfoRM (Table 4).

#### 4.3. Format Debiasing

- Vanilla BT models exhibited strong format bias (e.g., 89.0% win-rate for "Bold" format).
- DIR successfully neutralized format bias, achieving win-rates close to the ideal 50% threshold for bold and list formats.
- Crucially, DIR outperformed the specialized Format Decoupling (FD) method on challenging subsets of RewardBench (e.g., "Chat Hard," "Safety," "Reasoning"), demonstrating superior generalization while effectively debiasing (Table 5).

#### 4.4. Concurrent Multi-Biases

- When simultaneously debiasing length and sycophancy, the joint DIR model (Ours-Len-Syco) achieved the best overall RM-Bench performance and significantly improved scores on the "Hard" subset. It also reduced the Pearson correlation for length bias relative to BT (Table 6).
- On sycophancy stress tests, the joint model substantially outperformed BT on all metrics (All/Nat/Adv), confirming that debiasing multiple biases concurrently leads to improved robustness against both, suggesting a balanced trade-off rather than conflicting gradients (Table 7).

#### 4.5. Case Study

- A case study on ArenaHard-v0.1 showed that a DIR-trained model provided a "more professionally helpful" and concise response for setting up passwordless SSH, recommending modern algorithms (ed25519) and critical security practices (disabling password authentication) that even GPT-4o omitted, according to an LLM-based judge (Figure 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) by addressing the pervasive issue of inductive biases in reward models (RMs).

1. **Unified and Theoretically Sound Debiasing**: The introduction of Debiasing via Information optimization for RMs (DIR) offers a unified, information-theoretic framework to tackle diverse and complex inductive biases. Unlike prior methods often limited to specific bias types or linear correlations, DIR, inspired by the information bottleneck and utilizing variational bounds for mutual information, provides theoretical justification for handling non-linear relationships between RM outputs and biased attributes. This approach allows for a more comprehensive and principled solution to bias mitigation.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization of RMs**: By explicitly disentangling genuine human preference signals from spurious correlations (such as response length, sycophancy, and formatting style), DIR improves the intrinsic quality of the reward models. The experimental results demonstrate that DIR-debiased RMs exhibit lower susceptibility to these biases, leading to more reliable reward signals that guide LLM training more effectively.

3. **Improved Downstream LLM Performance and Alignment**: The consistent performance gains observed across various LLM capability benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MMLU, ArenaHard, MT-Bench) indicate that debiased RMs translate into better-aligned and more robust LLMs. The ability of DIR-guided policies to achieve higher win rates with more concise responses, as shown in ArenaHard, suggests that the method not only mitigates bias but also fosters more efficient and human-preferred behaviors. This directly impacts the helpfulness and harmlessness of LLM outputs.

4. **Increased Stability of RLHF Training**: The monitoring of PPO training dynamics revealed that DIR contributes to greater training stability, characterized by higher RLHF rewards and lower, more stable Approximate KL divergences. This addresses a critical challenge of training instability in RLHF, making the alignment process more predictable and less prone to collapse or overfitting.

5. **Practical Applicability and Scalability**: The method's ability to be combined with existing alignment techniques like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its competitive computational cost underscore its practical applicability. The flexibility to handle concurrent multi-biases with a simple extension further highlights its scalability to real-world datasets that often contain multiple co-occurring biases.

In conclusion, DIR offers a principled, scalable, and broadly applicable solution for building more reliable and balanced alignment systems for LLMs. By providing RMs that accurately reflect human values without succumbing to data artifacts, this work contributes to the development of more trustworthy and human-consistent artificial intelligence.
