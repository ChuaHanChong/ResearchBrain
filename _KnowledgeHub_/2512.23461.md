# Eliminating Inductive Bias in Reward Models with Information-Theoretic Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, developed Debiasing via Information optimization for RM (DIR), an information-theoretic method to eliminate inductive biases in reward models used for LLM alignment. This approach significantly improved reward model robustness and led to LLMs with enhanced performance across various benchmarks, exhibiting reduced length, sycophancy, and format biases.

## Problem

- Reward models (RMs) in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) are susceptible to "reward hacking," where they exploit spurious correlations or inductive biases (e.g., length, sycophancy, format) from low-quality human preference data.
- Existing debiasing methods are often limited to specific bias types, capture only simple linear correlations, or lack robust theoretical justification, failing to address diverse and complex non-linear dependencies.
- Inductive biases compromise the stability, reliability, and generalization of RLHF, causing LLMs to generate undesirable behaviors instead of truly helpful and harmless ones.

## Method

- DIR introduces an information-theoretic framework inspired by the ==Information Bottleneck== principle, optimizing a mutual information objective that maximizes relevant preference information while minimizing information linked to irrelevant bias attributes.
- The method utilizes variational bounds (Barber-Agakov and CLUB) to estimate and optimize the mutual information terms, translating the objective into a tractable loss function that minimizes the Bradley-Terry ranking loss and reduces correlation between RM hidden states and bias attributes.
- It employs a lightweight variational network to estimate bias-related mutual information and handles complex biases by converting them into relative categorical attributes, allowing for concurrent debiasing of multiple bias types.

## Results

- DIR achieved the lowest Pearson correlation coefficient (r = **0.468**) for length bias on RM-Bench, significantly mitigating the "longer is better" bias, and attained the highest overall RM-Bench score of 70.18, with over 16 points gain on "Hard" subsets.
- LLMs fine-tuned with DIR-debiased RMs consistently showed higher average accuracy (e.g., 66.20 for Llama3.1-**8B**-Instruct) across diverse benchmarks and achieved superior win rates on ArenaHard (**54.3%** vs. baseline) while producing more concise responses.
- The method successfully mitigated sycophancy bias (highest preference accuracy on a semi-sycophantic dataset) and format bias (win-rates close to **50%** for bold/list formats) and effectively handled concurrent multi-biases, improving robustness against both length and sycophancy simultaneously.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating the RM debiasing problem within an information-theoretic framework provides a unified and theoretically grounded approach to handle diverse and complex non-linear inductive biases.
> - Explicitly minimizing mutual information between reward model representations and specific bias attributes effectively disentangles genuine human preferences from spurious correlations present in training data.
> - The use of relative bias attributes and difference-based hidden state representations enhances the efficacy of debiasing, making the method robust and compatible with other alignment techniques like DPO.
