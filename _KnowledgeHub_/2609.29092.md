# DAWN: Noise-Robust Quadruped Parkour via Depth-Denoising World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DAWN builds depth-noise robustness into a world model for quadruped parkour through two training-only changes to WMP's RSSM: the encoder receives noisy depth while the decoder is supervised on clean depth, and a SimCLR-style contrastive loss aligns noisy and clean latent states. No inference overhead, no deployment filter tuning. It reaches 96.9% simulated success and zero-shot Unitree Go1 parkour over 18 cm stairs, 70 cm gaps, 45 cm steps.

## Problem

- Vision-based locomotion trains on clean depth and defers noise to hand-tuned post-processing filters, whose 12-24 interdependent, scene-dependent parameters are rarely reported, blocking reproduction.
- Existing remedies stay input-level, domain randomization or hand-crafted augmentation with limited representational capacity, or separate reconstruction modules needing extra cameras and LiDAR trained apart from the policy.
- World-model denoising has only been applied to proprioception, whose noise is low-dimensional and near i.i.d., unlike spatially structured depth noise concentrated at discontinuities.

## Method

- ==Denoising reconstruction objective== feeds noisy observations to the ==RSSM== encoder while the decoder targets clean depth, so the KL bottleneck suppresses noise features that never reduce reconstruction loss.
- ==Contrastive latent alignment== applies an ==NT-Xent loss== over projection-head embeddings, pairing clean and noisy encodings of the same scene as positives; the head is discarded at deployment.
- Total objective sums denoising and weighted contrastive terms over the unchanged RSSM with ==PPO== on the deterministic state, using a D435i noise model: Gaussian sensor noise, gradient-based edge dropout, far-particle artifacts.

## Results

- At nominal noise DAWN averages **96.9%** success over stairs, gaps and steps, closing **77%** of the WMP-to-oracle gap and cutting WMP's stair tracking error by **42%**.
- At hardest difficulty DAWN reaches **88.2%**; over noise-trained WMP, denoising alone adds **5.7** points, contrastive alone **4.6**, both together **11.5**, exceeding their sum.
- Under out-of-distribution noise doubling, DAWN loses **6.5** points versus **17.5** for clean-trained WMP. Outdoors on hardware: **80%** on 18 cm stairs, **60%** on 70 cm gaps, **70%** on 45 cm steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exposing a policy to noise during training is not the same as structuring its representation against noise; randomization fits observed noise levels and extrapolates poorly beyond them.
> - An input-target mismatch turns any reconstruction-based world model into a denoiser for free, and reconstruction-level plus representation-level regularizers can compose superadditively.
> - Gains only materialize once noise exceeds the benign indoor regime; indoors every method saturates, and the synthesized noise model remains specific to one sensor.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

DAWN tackles a practical problem in legged locomotion. A quadruped needs depth images to anticipate gaps and steps, but the depth sensor can lose pixels at the very edges that reveal those obstacles. Existing vision-based methods often handle this with deployment-time filters whose settings depend on lighting, materials, and distance. Those settings are often not reported. DAWN instead trains the world model itself to make sense of corrupted depth. This replaces a separately calibrated filter with learned processing inside the existing model, rather than adding an inference-time module. In real-world tests, the policy was deployed zero-shot on a Unitree Go1 using raw RealSense D435i depth, with the camera's built-in filters disabled.

Project-page preview, which the authors introduce with: “From direct sunlight to pitch darkness—same model, zero recalibration.”

### 2. Teach the model to clean up

The baseline is World Model-based Perception (WMP) by Lai et al. ([[2409.16784|paper]]). Its world model is a recurrent state-space model (RSSM), which compresses recent observations and actions into a state used by the locomotion policy. WMP trains its encoder and its reconstruction target on the same clean observation.

DAWN leaves that architecture and the policy unchanged and changes only the training signals. During training, the encoder receives noisy depth plus the same proprioception, while the decoder must reconstruct the corresponding clean observation. According to the authors, the clean target rewards retaining terrain geometry rather than corruption.

A second signal works on the learned representation. A projection head maps clean and noisy latent states from the same scene and timestep into a contrastive space. There, the two states form a positive pair, and states from different scenes act as negatives. The projection head is removed at deployment, so it regularizes learning without adding an inference module.

Training still relies on a noise model. The simulated D435i-characteristic noise combines Gaussian sensor noise, edge dropout, and far-particle noise.

### 3. Separate the two contributions

A skeptic might suggest simply training WMP on noisy depth. The paper tests this baseline as WMP w/ N. The ablation compares DAWN with WMP w/ N, clean-trained WMP, and two variants that each keep only one of the proposed losses. All runs use noise ×1.0 and three seeds, and they are scored by success rate (SR), the percentage of episodes that traverse the terrain without falling.

All methods are near ceiling at low difficulty and separate from level 4 onward. At the hardest level, DAWN reaches 88.2% SR. Relative to noise-only training, denoising alone adds 5.7 percentage points, contrastive alignment alone adds 4.6, and the combination adds 11.5. The authors read this as evidence that the two signals are complementary. That reading is an interpretation of the ablation, not a separately proven account of how the latent representation changes.

Left: average SR across difficulty levels 1–5 for DAWN, DAWN w/o C, DAWN w/o D, WMP w/ N, and WMP. Right: t-SNE of latent states, colored by terrain, with filled and open points for clean and noisy input. The paper reports clean/noisy overlap within terrain groups; the plot is qualitative.

### 4. Does it transfer to the robot?

On the Go1, each difficulty level received 10 trials. Indoors, the methods performed comparably, near 100%. The performance gap emerged outdoors, where sunlight-related IR interference and material variation worsen depth corruption. At a 70 cm outdoor gap, DAWN succeeded in 60% of trials, compared with 40% for WMP w/ N and 30% for clean-trained WMP. The robot traversed the tested maxima of 18 cm stairs, 70 cm gaps, and 45 cm steps.

Real-robot SR for DAWN, WMP w/ N, and WMP on Stairs, Gap, and Step, indoors and outdoors, with 10 trials per difficulty.

### 5. What changed at deployment

DAWN moves robustness to depth noise from a hand-tuned deployment filter into the world model's training. The evidence covers this robot, sensor, and tested terrain. The training noise model targets the D435i, and the authors name other depth sensors as future work.
