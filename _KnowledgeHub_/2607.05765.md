# Image2Sim: Scaling Embodied Navigation via Generative Neural Simulator

> [!summary] Summary
> Image2Sim presents a real-time neural simulation framework that converts passive RGB-D image sequences into interactive 3D environments for embodied navigation, supporting scalable generation of vision-language-action data. The system renders panoramic RGB-D at 45.6 FPS while maintaining high fidelity, and when scaled to 10 million training samples, it improves R2R-CE Success Rate from 46.1% to 66.3%, demonstrating effective zero-shot transfer to real-world robots.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation lacks scalable, high-fidelity, physically grounded, and interactive 3D environments, hindering robust real-world generalization.
- Existing data sources (real-world scans vs. synthetic) present a fundamental trade-off between visual fidelity and scalability, or suffer from a 'sim-to-real' gap.
- Generative video models often lack the rigid-body consistency and explicit collision structures necessary for reliable closed-loop agent interaction in 3D environments.

## Method

- **Image2Sim** is a real-time neural simulation framework that constructs interactive environments from posed RGB-D image sequences by ==decoupling 3D spatial anchoring from photorealistic observation synthesis==.
- A ==Geometry-Aware Neural Simulator== separates ==feed-forward 3D scene construction== using ==dense 3D feature Gaussians== from one-step panoramic RGB-D rendering via a ==geometry-aware pixel-flow model==.
- An automated ==embodied data engine== generates comprehensive vision-language-action annotations, including ==object-centric target mining==, ==physics-aware trajectory generation==, and ==VLM-based natural language instruction synthesis==.

## Results

- Achieved real-time panoramic RGB-D rendering at **45.6 FPS** with high fidelity (e.g., PSNR **17.43**, SSIM **0.470**), demonstrating robustness to noise and various input formats.
- Navigation policies trained exclusively with Image2Sim data showed strong zero-shot generalization, improving R2R-CE Success Rate from **46.1%** to **66.3%** and SPL from **41.3%** to **61.5%** when scaled to **10 million** samples.
- Demonstrated effective zero-shot transfer to a real-world robot (Hello Robot Stretch 3), improving path-following Success Rate from **8/20** to **11/20** and goal-oriented Success Rate from **5/20** to **9/20**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling 3D spatial anchoring from photorealistic observation synthesis allows for combining real-world visual fidelity with scalable, geometrically consistent, and real-time interactive neural simulation.
> - A geometry-aware pixel-flow model, guided by alpha-gated source states and momentum-based self-distillation, enables robust one-step generative completion that overcomes sparsity and noise in feed-forward 3D reconstructions.
> - Scalable neural simulation with automated generation of diverse scenes, physically valid trajectories, and VLM-annotated instructions yields unsaturated, log-linear gains in embodied navigation, effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation aims to develop intelligent agents capable of interpreting multimodal goals, reasoning within 3D environments, and reliably reaching designated destinations in the real world. Progress in this field has been influenced by advancements in large language models, vision foundation models, and visual generative models, which have demonstrated significant capability gains through data scaling. However, embodied navigation has not achieved comparable progress in robust real-world generalization, primarily due to limitations in the availability of scalable, high-fidelity, physically grounded, and interactive 3D environments.

Current approaches to generating training data for embodied navigation typically involve two paradigms:
1. **Real-world 3D scans:** Datasets such as Matterport3D, HM3D, Gibson, and Replica offer high visual fidelity, realistic layouts, and strong geometric and physical grounding. However, they are expensive, labor-intensive to acquire and process, which limits their scalability and diversity.
2. **Synthetic procedural environments:** Platforms like ProcTHOR, AI2-THOR, and Habitat's synthetic scenes provide better scalability, controllability, and task diversity. Nevertheless, they often exhibit a "sim-to-real gap" due to unrealistic assets, simplified layouts, and differences in rendering statistics compared to the real world.

Recent advancements in generative video models offer potential for realistic visual data synthesis, but they often lack the rigid-body consistency and explicit collision structures essential for reliable closed-loop interaction required in embodied AI.

Neural scene representations, exemplified by NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), can achieve high-fidelity rendering. However, these methods typically require costly per-scene optimization, which limits their scalability for generating large numbers of interactive environments. Feed-forward reconstruction methods offer efficiency but can degrade under noisy or sparse inputs, leading to incomplete geometry. Generative world models can synthesize realistic observations but frequently lack persistent 3D structure and explicit navigability.

This work, Image2Sim, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the fundamental tradeoff between visual fidelity and scalability. It proposes a neural simulation framework that constructs interactive environments directly from large-scale posed RGB-D image sequences. By decoupling 3D spatial anchoring from photorealistic observation synthesis, Image2Sim aims to combine the visual realism of real-world captures with the scalability of synthetic generation, while ensuring explicit 3D physical grounding and real-time interaction capabilities. It also extends beyond rendering to encompass automated generation of vision, action, and language data, tackling the bottleneck of both diverse environments and high-quality instruction annotations, which is an underexplored dimension in scaling Vision-Language Navigation (VLN).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for Image2Sim stems from the observation that progress in embodied navigation has been hampered by a lack of suitable data sources. Existing data pipelines present a fundamental tradeoff: real-world 3D scans offer high visual fidelity but are limited in scale and costly to acquire, while synthetic environments are scalable but often suffer from a significant sim-to-real gap. Generative video models, while producing realistic visuals, lack the consistent 3D structure and collision awareness needed for reliable agent interaction. This situation suggests that scalable embodied data requires both real-world visual fidelity and explicit 3D physical grounding, a combination that existing methods struggle to provide simultaneously.

The key objectives of the Image2Sim project are detailed as follows:

1. **To develop a real-time neural simulation framework that constructs high-quality interactive environments from posed RGB-D image sequences at scale.** This addresses the need for abundant and diverse training environments that are representative of the real world. The central idea is to decouple 3D spatial anchoring, which ensures physical grounding, from photorealistic observation synthesis, which guarantees visual fidelity.
2. **To propose a Geometry-Aware Neural Simulator that explicitly separates feed-forward 3D scene construction from photorealistic panoramic RGB-D rendering.** This architectural design aims to create geometrically grounded, high-fidelity, and computationally efficient environments. The goal is to support real-time panoramic RGB-D synthesis (e.g., approximately 40 FPS on a single RTX 4090 GPU), thereby enabling efficient closed-loop interaction for agent training.
3. **To integrate a fully automated embodied data engine within the simulation framework.** Beyond just rendering, the system aims to generate complete vision-language-action triplets. This includes object-centric target mining, physics-aware trajectory generation that respects collision constraints and explores diverse goals, and the use of large vision-language models for natural language instruction annotation aligned with these trajectories. The objective is to produce large-scale datasets, such as over 10 million navigation training samples across approximately 20,000 interactive scenes, without manual annotation.
4. **To provide empirical evidence validating scalable neural simulation as a practical path for improving embodied navigation.** The research seeks to demonstrate that navigation models trained entirely within these neural environments can achieve strong improvements on major embodied navigation benchmarks. Furthermore, it aims to show that these models can transfer effectively to real-world zero-shot settings, thereby validating the utility of Image2Sim-generated data in bridging the sim-to-real gap and facilitating robust generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Image2Sim is a real-time neural simulation framework designed to convert passive scene captures into executable neural simulators for scalable embodied navigation data synthesis. The system is built upon the principle of decoupling 3D spatial anchoring from photorealistic observation synthesis. It comprises three core components: feed-forward construction of a persistent 3D feature-Gaussian scene, geometry-aware one-step panoramic RGB-D rendering, and executable motion simulation for trajectory and instruction generation.

#### 3.1. Feed-Forward 3D Scene Construction

The framework begins by lifting posed RGB-D frames into a persistent 3D representation without requiring per-scene optimization.

- **Semantic-Geometric Pixel Lifting:** For each input RGB-D frame, a dual-stream encoder extracts dense visual features. A frozen DINOv3 backbone provides high-level semantic features, while a lightweight geometric detail stream preserves local details from RGB, metric depth, surface normals, and ray directions. The ray-direction encoding ensures compatibility with both pinhole and panoramic cameras. These semantic and geometric features are fused by a FeatureUpsampler and then decoded by a Gaussian prediction head into dense 3D feature Gaussians. Each Gaussian `g_j` is defined by its center (`μ_j`), anisotropic scale (`s_j`), rotation (`q_j`), opacity (`α_j`), RGB values (`c_j`), and compact semantic features (`f_j`). The Gaussian centers are deterministically unprojected from depth and voxel-downsampled, explicitly anchoring the representation to the metric scene geometry. This feed-forward process amortizes scene construction costs across environments, enabling real-time conversion.
- **Projection and Semantic Grounding:** For a given target panoramic pose, the 3D Gaussian scene is splatted into the panorama coordinate system to generate projected RGB (`~I_p`), depth (`~D_p`), semantic feature (`~F_p`), and opacity (`~A_p`) maps, along with the panoramic ray-direction map (`R_p`). Observed regions are supervised by an L1 RGB-D reconstruction loss. The projected feature maps are tokenized by a semantic aligner and aligned with DINOv3 features extracted from the ground-truth target RGB panorama using a cosine similarity loss. This supervision ensures the 3D Gaussian representation encodes local appearance and semantic layout cues necessary for novel-view completion.

#### 3.2. Geometry-Aware One-Step Pixel Flow Rendering

Image2Sim addresses the challenge of sparse and noisy Gaussian projections by combining explicit 3D grounding with generative completion through a geometry-aware pixel-flow renderer.

- **Alpha-Gated Source State:** To guide the generative process, an alpha-gated source state (`z_src`) is constructed. This state combines the projected RGB-D prior (`~X_p` = [`~I_p`, `~D_p`]) with the accumulated opacity map (`~A_p`), which indicates the reliability of the 3D projection. High-opacity regions retain close proximity to the projected 3D evidence, while low-opacity regions receive larger perturbations and are subject to generative completion. A spatially varying noise scale `Σ(~A_p)` is defined, where `σ_small` is used for high opacity and `σ_large` for low opacity.
- **Single-Step Pixel-Flow Renderer Architecture:** The denoised prediction (`x_θ`) is parameterized by a UNet-style architecture. A convolutional encoder processes the alpha-gated source state `z_src` and the geometric condition `C_p`. A deep transformer bottleneck then captures global layout dependencies. Within this bottleneck, time step information is injected via AdaLN, and semantic tokens from the semantic aligner (`Φ(C_p)`) are injected via SPADE-like spatially-adaptive normalization to provide high-level layout guidance. During convolutional upsampling, multi-scale skip connections are modulated by an alpha pyramid derived from `~A_p`. These alpha-gated skips preserve reliable projected evidence in high-opacity regions and suppress noisy features in missing regions.
- **Training the Pixel-Flow Renderer:** The renderer is trained using a continuous-time MeanFlow formulation to enable one-step generation. The network learns to predict the average velocity for one-step transport from a noisy source state (`z_t`) to the target observation (`X_p`). An opacity-adaptive weighting map `W(~A_p)` is applied to the flow loss, concentrating learning signals on unobserved regions (`~A_p ≈ 0`) that require generative completion, while preserving reliable 3D projections.
- **Momentum-Based Self-Distillation:** To enhance stability and suppress artifacts in occluded regions, a momentum-based self-distillation loss is employed. A teacher network, updated by an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student parameters, is conditioned on a privileged source state constructed from the ground-truth RGB-D panorama. The student network is then encouraged to match the multi-scale decoder features of the teacher, transferring clean latent structures. The complete training objective combines reconstruction, semantic alignment, flow, distillation, and a perceptual LPIPS loss.

#### 3.3. Motion Simulation and Trajectory Generation

Image2Sim extends beyond visual rendering to support physically valid agent motion.

- **Navigable Area Extraction and Graph Construction:** The Gaussian scene is voxelized into a dense 3D grid, and traversability is determined based on semantic labels (e.g., floors) and geometric obstacle constraints (e.g., floor height, free-space connectivity, clearance from occupied regions). GPU-parallel ray marching queries occupancy within the agent's footprint to enforce collision constraints and support plausible sliding. This process forms a traversable voxel connectivity graph `M=(V,E)`.
- **Path Planning:** Paths are planned over `M` using a path-length and collision-aware edge cost function, similar to the NavFn planner. This encourages the planner to favor safer corridors over merely shortest paths.
- **Kinematic Simulation:** A lookahead pure-pursuit controller converts discrete graph paths into smooth, executable motion. The simulator then replays these trajectories to render temporally consistent panoramic RGB-D observations along the precise kinematic path, ensuring physical validity (e.g., rejecting invalid motions, detecting geometric penetration).

#### 3.4. Automated Instruction Generation

To generate comprehensive vision-language-action annotations, Image2Sim implements an automated pipeline.

- **Trajectory Segmentation:** Each sampled feasible trajectory is segmented into semantic macro-steps based on accumulated translation, yaw rotation, and terminal STOP events, bridging the granularity gap between low-level actions and human language.
- **Multi-Modal Annotation:** Each macro-step is mapped to a motion primitive and aligned with the panorama rendered at its starting pose. This interleaved observation-motion sequence is then fed to a large vision-language model (Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) to synthesize natural language navigation instructions. A style-conditioned annotation router diversifies the instructions across fine-grained path-following, high-level goal-oriented descriptions, and natural human demands. This fully automated process enables the scalable generation of embodied navigation data without manual trajectory or instruction annotation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for Image2Sim evaluated its performance across novel-view rendering, cross-simulator zero-shot navigation generalization, data scaling laws, architectural ablations, and real-world navigation.

#### 4.1. Novel-View Rendering Evaluation

- **Quality and Speed:** Image2Sim achieves a balanced tradeoff between visual fidelity, robustness to noise, and computational efficiency. On the RealSee3D-Real dataset, which features sparse and noisy LiDAR depth, the panoramic variant of Image2Sim maintains a PSNR of 17.43 and SSIM of 0.470 while operating at 45.6 FPS. This performance significantly surpasses Gaussian-only feed-forward methods like AnySplat in rendering quality, which suffer from structural degradation under unobserved regions.
- **Robustness to Noise and Input Formats:** The geometry-aware pixel-flow model effectively completes structural blind spots and suppresses noise, while the Gaussian projection and alpha guidance prevent unconstrained hallucination. Image2Sim consistently performs well with both pinhole and panoramic inputs, demonstrating its adaptability to heterogeneous capture formats, which is valuable for scaling neural simulation from diverse real-world images and videos. Pure generative baselines like DiT360 and SE3DS, while producing plausible pixels, do not meet real-time requirements (e.g., 0.3-3.4 FPS).

#### 4.2. Cross-Simulator Zero-Shot Navigation Generalization

- **Benchmark Performance:** Image2Nav, the baseline navigation model trained exclusively within the Image2Sim framework, was evaluated zero-shot on established benchmarks such as R2R-CE, RxR-CE, and REVERIE-CE, which are typically used for models trained and evaluated within the Habitat simulator.
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** Despite a substantial domain shift between Image2Sim and Habitat, Image2Nav achieved strong results, surpassing various in-domain methods including EfficientVLN and DualVLN. For instance, with a 90° FOV pinhole camera, Image2Nav achieved an SR of 66.3% and SPL of 61.5% on R2R-CE Val Unseen, and a nDTW of 70.5% on RxR-CE Val Unseen. With a 180° FOV equirectangular image, these metrics improved further (SR 70.3%, SPL 65.6% on R2R-CE Val Unseen; nDTW 71.8% on RxR-CE Val Unseen).
- **Generalizable Behavior:** These consistent improvements across path-following and object-centric tasks suggest that the data generated by Image2Sim provides effective supervision, enabling policies to learn generalizable navigation behaviors rather than overfitting to simulator-specific biases.

#### 4.3. Scaling Law for Navigation Training Data

- **Consistent Gains with Data Scale:** Scaling the Image2Sim-generated data from a base of 35K human-annotated R2R/RxR samples to 10 million samples led to consistent and unsaturated gains across all navigation metrics. For example, on R2R-CE, the Success Rate (SR) improved from 46.1% to 66.3%, and the Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) increased from 41.3% to 61.5%.
- **Log-Linear Trend:** The observed improvements revealed a clear log-linear trend, indicating that current navigation generalization capabilities are still strongly data-limited.
- **Coupled Scaling:** The gains were attributed not only to instruction augmentation but also to Image2Sim's ability to jointly expand the number of interactive scenes, feasible trajectories, rendered observations, and generated instructions. This exposure to broader distributions of layouts, visuals, actions, and language supports Image2Sim's role as a scalable embodied data engine.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the pixel-flow model components using Matterport3D-360 demonstrated the importance of each design choice:

- **Pixel Flow Model:** Removing the pixel flow model and relying solely on 3DGS projection severely degraded rendering quality (e.g., LPIPS 0.518, PSNR 17.75), confirming that deterministic splatting alone cannot overcome inherent sparsity and holes in feed-forward reconstructions.
- **Semantic Features:** Removal of the projected semantic feature map led to clear performance drops, indicating the necessity of high-level semantics for constraining generative completion in unobserved regions.
- **Alpha-Gated Fusion:** Disabling alpha-gated fusion, which explicitly separates reliable projected evidence from uncertain missing regions, reduced reconstruction fidelity, suggesting that the model might overwrite valid 3D geometry.
- **Self-Distillation:** Removing momentum-based self-distillation also degraded performance, underscoring the EMA teacher's role in stabilizing one-step generation and suppressing artifacts in weakly observed regions.

#### 4.5. Real-World Navigation Evaluation

- **Real-World Transfer:** Experiments conducted on a Hello Robot Stretch 3 in a home-like environment, where no scene objects or layouts were part of the training data, demonstrated Image2Nav's zero-shot generalization to the real world.
- **Improved Performance:** Image2Nav achieved superior performance in both path-following and goal-oriented navigation scenarios compared to strong baselines. It improved path-following SR from 8/20 to 11/20 and goal-oriented SR from 5/20 to 9/20.
- **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap:** These results indicate that Image2Sim-generated data improves transfer beyond simulator benchmarks, particularly for goal-oriented navigation, supporting the claim that scalable, physically executable, and visually diverse neural simulation can help reduce the sim-to-real gap for embodied navigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Image2Sim introduces a novel approach to addressing a critical bottleneck in embodied AI research: the scarcity of scalable, high-fidelity, and physically grounded interactive environments. By providing a framework that converts passive visual captures into executable neural simulators, this work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Data Scaling for Embodied AI:** The core contribution is an automated and scalable pipeline for generating large volumes of high-quality vision-language-action data. This directly tackles the data limitation that has hindered robust real-world generalization in embodied navigation, analogous to how large datasets have propelled advancements in other AI domains like large language models and vision foundation models. The demonstrated scaling laws suggest a clear path for future progress through continued data scaling.
2. **Mitigating the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Image2Sim's capacity to generate physically executable trajectories and diverse language-action supervision, combined with its ability to synthesize visually realistic and geometrically consistent environments, enables the training of navigation policies that exhibit strong cross-simulator generalization and effective zero-shot transfer to real-world robots. This is a crucial step towards making embodied agents deployed in real environments more reliable and robust.
3. **Novel Neural Simulation Architecture:** The decoupling of 3D spatial anchoring via a feed-forward feature Gaussian model from photorealistic observation synthesis via a geometry-aware one-step pixel flow model represents a significant architectural innovation. This design allows for real-time interaction (e.g., 40 FPS panoramic RGB-D rendering) while maintaining high visual fidelity and geometric consistency, striking a balance that traditional simulators or prior neural rendering methods struggled to achieve simultaneously.
4. **Reduced Manual Labor and Cost:** The fully automated nature of the data engine, from object mining and physics-aware trajectory generation to VLM-based instruction annotation, significantly reduces the manual effort and cost traditionally associated with creating complex 3D environments and corresponding task annotations. This democratization of high-quality data generation can accelerate research cycles and make advanced embodied AI development more accessible.
5. **Broadened Research Scope:** By providing a scalable source of interactive and realistic training data, Image2Sim could accelerate research not only in navigation but also in other embodied AI tasks that require agents to perceive, reason, and act in complex 3D environments, such as manipulation, object interaction, and human-robot collaboration.
6. **Foundation for Future Embodied Learning Paradigms:** The framework's support for real-time interaction and physically valid motion lays the groundwork for advanced learning paradigms, including online DAgger and reinforcement learning, which require continuous, interactive feedback in diverse environments.

In summary, Image2Sim represents a practical and scalable solution for generating high-fidelity, interactive, and physically grounded environments. Its impact is likely to be substantial in pushing the boundaries of embodied navigation and, by extension, other areas of embodied AI, by providing the crucial ingredient of data scale and quality necessary for robust real-world performance.
