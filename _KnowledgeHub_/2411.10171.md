# Imagine-2-Drive: Leveraging High-Fidelity World Models via Multi-Modal Diffusion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Imagine-2-Drive is a world-model RL framework for autonomous driving combining DiffDreamer, a stable-video-diffusion world model predicting future observations and rewards simultaneously, with DPA, a diffusion-based multi-modal policy actor trained via PPO inside the imagined rollouts, improving Route Completion by 15% and Success Rate by 20% over prior world models in CARLA.

## Problem

- RSSM-based world models rely on single-step transition prediction, letting errors compound over multi-step planning and causing planned states to drift from the on-policy distribution.
- Traditional RL policies for driving are deterministic or single-Gaussian, so they cannot capture the multi-modal nature of decision-making in complex driving scenarios.
- Prior video-diffusion world models improve prediction fidelity but are paired with these limited policy classes, so their versatility is not fully exploited for planning.

## Method

- **DiffDreamer**: a ==Stable Video Diffusion==-based world model that replaces the first frames with real past observations and predicts H future observations and rewards jointly, reducing compounding drift.
- **DPA (Diffusion Policy Actor)**: a ==U-Net diffusion policy== that denoises trajectories over K steps, formulated as a ==denoising MDP== nested inside the environment MDP and optimized with ==PPO==.
- ==Fourier trajectory embedding== and an added ==reward-prediction MLP head== condition DiffDreamer on DPA's waypoint trajectories, plus ==dynamics and structure preservation losses== for coherent future frame generation.

## Results

- Imagine-2-Drive achieves **83.33%** Success Rate and **82.13%** Route Completion in CARLA, beating Dreamer-V3 (**63.33%**/**67.53%**) and Iso-Dreamer (**56.66%**/**60.33%**) baselines.
- DiffDreamer improves prediction fidelity over Dreamer-V3 and Iso-Dreamer by **72** FID points and **290** FVD points, confirming higher-fidelity future state prediction.
- Swapping DPA into Iso-Dreamer and Dreamer-V3 baselines raises SR by at least **10%** and RC by at least **8%**, isolating DPA's contribution independent of the world model used.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting future frames simultaneously via video diffusion, rather than autoregressive one-step rollout, mitigates compounding error accumulation in long-horizon world-model planning.
> - Nesting a diffusion policy's denoising process as an MDP inside the environment MDP lets multi-modal trajectory distributions be optimized directly with standard RL objectives like PPO.
> - Grounding diffusion-based future prediction on real past frames rather than fully noised inputs reduces drift and better preserves dynamic consistency with initial conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The "Imagine-2-Drive" paper addresses critical challenges at the intersection of **Reinforcement Learning (RL), World Models (WMs), Diffusion Models, and Autonomous Driving**. Its contribution is significant because it tackles known limitations of existing approaches by integrating novel techniques.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Autonomous Driving:** RL offers a powerful paradigm for autonomous driving, enabling agents to learn complex control policies directly from experience. However, traditional online RL methods are notoriously **sample-inefficient** and **unsafe** for real-world autonomous vehicles, requiring vast amounts of real-world interaction, which is both costly and dangerous.

**The Rise of World Models (WMs) in RL:** To mitigate these issues, World Model-based Reinforcement Learning (WMRL) has gained traction. WMRL agents learn an internal model of the environment's dynamics, allowing them to "imagine" future states and rewards without constant real-world interaction. This significantly improves sample efficiency and enables foresight. Early WMRLs, like the Dreamer series, often rely on **Recurrent State Space Models (RSSMs)** that predict future states one step at a time in a latent space. A fundamental limitation of these **single-step transition models** is the **accumulation of errors** over long prediction horizons, leading to state drift and degraded policy performance. Additionally, the fidelity of predictions in latent space can be lower, impacting planning accuracy.

**Generative Models, particularly Diffusion Models, for World Modeling:** More recent research has explored using generative models, especially **video diffusion models**, for future state prediction. Models like Vista, DriveDreamer, and MagicDrive leverage diffusion's ability to generate high-fidelity, diverse future sequences. Unlike RSSM-based models, these can often generate multiple future observations **simultaneously**, which intrinsically helps in mitigating compounding errors. Imagine-2-Drive builds upon this by employing a Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) architecture as its core world model, aiming for superior prediction fidelity.

**Diffusion Models for Policy Learning:** Simultaneously, diffusion models have emerged as a robust approach for **policy learning and trajectory generation** in robotics. Unlike traditional RL policies (often deterministic or single Gaussian-based) that struggle to capture the inherent **multi-modal nature** of decision-making (e.g., different ways to navigate around an obstacle), diffusion policies can model diverse behavioral distributions. Works like Diffuser and Diffusion Policy have demonstrated their effectiveness in generating complex, multi-modal trajectories. This is particularly crucial in autonomous driving, where a vehicle might have several safe and optimal paths to choose from depending on context. The challenge is typically training these diffusion policies within an RL framework, as many existing works rely on supervised learning from expert demonstrations. Recent works like DDPO and DPPO have started to bridge this gap by formulating the denoising process as an MDP and optimizing diffusion models with RL objectives.

**Imagine-2-Drive's Novel Integration:** This paper strategically unifies these two powerful trends. It directly addresses the shortcomings of previous WMRL approaches by:
1. **Enhancing World Model Fidelity:** Integrating a high-fidelity, video diffusion-based world model (DiffDreamer) that predicts future observations simultaneously, thereby overcoming error accumulation inherent in auto-regressive, single-step models.
2. **Enabling Multi-Modal Policy Learning:** Introducing a diffusion-based policy actor (DPA) that explicitly models diverse and multi-modal trajectory distributions, which is essential for complex driving scenarios.
3. **Synergistic Training:** Training this multi-modal policy directly within the high-fidelity imagination space of the world model, allowing for sample-efficient policy optimization without extensive online interactions.

By combining these elements, "Imagine-2-Drive" pushes the boundaries of WMRL, making it more applicable and robust for safety-critical domains like autonomous driving, where both accurate future prediction and diverse, adaptive decision-making are paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the "Imagine-2-Drive" framework stem from critical limitations observed in existing World Model-based Reinforcement Learning (WMRL) and traditional policy learning approaches, especially in the context of autonomous driving:

1. **Address Low Prediction Fidelity and Compounding Errors in World Models:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing WMRLs, particularly those based on Recurrent State Space Models (RSSMs) like the Dreamer series, model environment dynamics in a latent space and predict future states one step at a time. This auto-regressive nature leads to **compounding errors** over longer planning horizons, causing imagined states to drift significantly from reality. This degradation in prediction fidelity limits the usefulness of the world model for robust long-term planning, which is crucial for autonomous driving.
 - **Objective:** To develop a **high-fidelity world model (DiffDreamer)** capable of generating accurate future observations over an extended horizon, ideally by predicting future states simultaneously rather than sequentially, thereby mitigating error accumulation.

2. **Enable Multi-Modal Decision-Making in Complex Driving Scenarios:**
 - **Motivation:** Autonomous driving scenarios are inherently complex and often present multiple plausible and safe actions (e.g., swerving left or right to avoid an obstacle). Traditional RL policies, which are typically deterministic or model decisions as a single Gaussian distribution, struggle to capture this **multi-modal nature of decision-making**. Such policies can be brittle and lack the adaptability required for diverse and dynamic driving environments.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a **multi-modal diffusion-based policy actor (DPA)** that can effectively model and generate diverse behavioral patterns and trajectories, allowing the autonomous agent to explore a broader range of safe and optimal responses.

3. **Enhance Sample Efficiency and Reduce Online Interaction Costs and Risks:**
 - **Motivation:** Training autonomous driving agents in the real world is incredibly expensive, time-consuming, and risky. Even in high-fidelity simulators like CARLA, extensive online interactions can be resource-intensive. WMRL aims to alleviate this by allowing agents to learn in an "imagination" space.
 - **Objective:** To create a WMRL framework that **maximizes sample efficiency** by extensively leveraging the learned world model for policy training, significantly reducing the need for costly and unsafe online interactions with the physical or simulated environment.

4. **Improve Overall Policy Robustness and Performance in Autonomous Driving:**
 - **Motivation:** The combined issues of inaccurate future predictions and restrictive policy representations lead to sub-optimal and less robust driving behaviors.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate that by integrating a high-fidelity world model with a multi-modal policy, the resulting framework can achieve **superior performance** in standard autonomous driving benchmarks, leading to higher success rates, route completion, and fewer infractions compared to existing model-based and model-free baselines.

In essence, Imagine-2-Drive is motivated by the desire to build more capable and reliable autonomous driving agents by fundamentally improving the underlying learning mechanisms – specifically, by enhancing the realism and predictive power of the internal world model and by broadening the expressive capacity of the agent's decision-making policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Imagine-2-Drive is a novel World Model-based Reinforcement Learning (WMRL) framework designed for long-horizon trajectory generation in autonomous driving, utilizing only front-facing camera inputs. Its methodology revolves around an iterative training loop between a high-fidelity world model and a multi-modal diffusion policy actor.

The framework comprises three key components:

1. **State Encoder:**
 - **Purpose:** To address the partial observability inherent in using a single-frame camera observation, which prevents a pure Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation. The State Encoder approximates an MDP by providing temporal context.
 - **Approach:** It processes a fixed-length sequence of `P` past front-view RGB camera observations along with the current observation `o_t`. Each observation `o_i` is tokenized into an embedding using a pre-trained EfficientNet-B0 model. These individual tokens are then combined with positional encodings and fed into a decoder-only transformer backbone. The transformer's output tokens are concatenated, flattened, and passed through MLP layers to yield a final compact state embedding `s_t`. This `s_t` encapsulates the temporal information necessary for decision-making.

2. **DiffDreamer (High-Fidelity World Model):**
 - **Purpose:** To overcome the limitations of compounding errors and low prediction fidelity found in traditional one-step, RSSM-based world models. DiffDreamer simulates future states and rewards, enhancing sample efficiency by enabling policy learning in an "imagination" space.
 - **Approach:**
 - **Foundation:** Leverages a **Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)** architecture as its core for predicting multiple future observations simultaneously.
 - **Inputs:** Given `P` past and current observations `{o_i}_{t-P:t}` and a sequence of actions (waypoint trajectory `τ`) predicted by DPA, DiffDreamer predicts `H` future observations `{ô_i}_{t+1:t+H}`.
 - **Dynamic Consistency:** To ensure predictions are grounded in initial conditions and reduce drift, the first `P` noisy frames in the SVD model are replaced with the actual `P` past and current observations. The current observation is also repeated and concatenated with past and noisy frames for better conditioning.
 - **Fourier Trajectory Encoding:** The input trajectory `τ` from DPA is transformed using a Fourier embedding (FFT). This allows the model to capture both low and high-frequency components of motion patterns, effectively representing smooth and periodic movements.
 - **Reward Prediction:** An additional MLP module is integrated into DiffDreamer to predict future rewards `r_t` based on the predicted state `s_t` and action `a_t`. This completes the MDP formulation within the world model, allowing for policy optimization based on imagined rewards.
 - **Loss Function:** The training loss `L_M` for DiffDreamer combines:
 - `L_diffusion`: Standard noise reconstruction loss for the SVD model.
 - `L_dynamics`: Encourages accurate capturing of motion dynamics.
 - `L_structure`: Reinforces structural integrity and spatial relationships in generated scenes (both `L_dynamics` and `L_structure` are adopted from Vista).
 - `L_r`: A mean squared error loss for the reward prediction head.

3. **Diffusion Policy Actor (DPA):**
 - **Purpose:** To generate diverse and multi-modal waypoint trajectories (`τ`) conditioned on the current state `s_t`, overcoming the limitations of deterministic or single-Gaussian policies.
 - **Approach:**
 - **Architecture:** Utilizes a U-Net architecture, similar to Diffuser, to predict a sequence of actions (trajectory). It performs `K=50` denoising steps for sampling.
 - **Policy Optimization with RL:** Unlike behavioral cloning, DPA is trained using Reinforcement Learning to maximize the cumulative sum of rewards `J_RL(π_θ)`.
 - **Denoising as an MDP:** The core innovation is formulating the denoising process of the diffusion model as a K-step Markov Decision Process (MDP), which is then augmented within the environment's MDP. Rewards are assigned only to the final denoising step, corresponding to the executed action sequence.
 - **PPO Optimization:** Policy parameters `θ` are optimized using **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**. PPO provides a stable and variance-reduced gradient estimator, applying a clipping ratio `ε` to prevent drastic policy updates. An advantage estimator `A_π_old` is used for further variance reduction. A state-value estimator `V_θ` is trained via TD(0) to support the advantage calculation.
 - **Action Space:** The action space is simplified to predict incremental `∆X` and `∆Y` in the ego-agent's local frame. `∆X` is fixed to 1 (ensuring forward progress), and `∆Y` (lateral movement) is discretized into 11 bins within `(-0.5, 0.5)` meters, encoded as one-hot embeddings.

**Iterative Training Procedure (Algorithm 1, Fig. 3):**
The two main modules, DiffDreamer (`M_ϕ`) and DPA (`π_θ`), are trained alternately in a synchronized manner:
1. **Collect Experience:** `π_θ` interacts with the CARLA simulator, collecting real-world transitions (`s_t`, `a_t`, `r_t`) and storing them in an experience buffer.
2. **Train World Model:** `M_ϕ` is trained in a supervised manner using batches of data tuples (`observations`, `actions`, `rewards`) sampled from the experience buffer. During this phase, `π_θ` is kept fixed. A warm-start pretraining for `M_ϕ` is performed initially.
3. **Train Policy Actor:** `π_θ` is optimized within the "imagination" space of the *frozen* world model `M_ϕ`. Given an initial state, `π_θ` generates a trajectory `τ`, which `M_ϕ` then uses to predict future observations and rewards. The predicted rewards `r_t` contribute to the cumulative return for policy optimization using PPO. This process iterates for a fixed episode length within the imagined environment, significantly reducing reliance on online simulator interactions.

This iterative, closed-loop training ensures that the world model provides an increasingly accurate environment for the policy, and the policy generates diverse and effective behaviors for the world model to learn from.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in the CARLA simulator demonstrate the superior performance of Imagine-2-Drive against both world model-based and model-free baselines using single RGB images as input.

**1. Driving Performance and Episodic Returns (Table I, Fig. 6):**
- **Overall Superiority:** Imagine-2-Drive significantly outperforms all baselines across standard autonomous driving metrics. It achieves an **83.33% Success Rate (SR)**, a 20% improvement over the best baseline (Dreamer-V3 at 63.33%).
- **Route Completion and Safety:** It shows an **82.13% Route Completion (RC)**, a 14.6% improvement over Dreamer-V3 (67.53%), and drastically reduces **Infractions/km to 0.70**, cutting the best baseline's infractions by more than half (Dreamer-V3 at 1.52).
- **Model-Based vs. Model-Free:** The results confirm that world model-based methods (Iso-Dreamer, Dreamer-V3, Imagine-2-Drive) consistently surpass model-free approaches (DQN, PPO) due to their explicit environment modeling and efficient planning capabilities.
- **Episodic Returns:** Figure 6 illustrates that Imagine-2-Drive consistently achieves higher episodic returns over 1 million simulation steps, indicating more effective learning and better long-term reward accumulation.

**2. Prediction Fidelity of the World Model (Table III, Fig. 5):**
- **DiffDreamer's High Fidelity:** DiffDreamer, the world model component of Imagine-2-Drive, demonstrates superior prediction fidelity compared to other world models like DriveGAN, Iso-Dreamer, and Dreamer-V3.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** It achieves significantly lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 17.09 and Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 130.39. These scores are substantially better (e.g., 72 lower FID and 290 lower FVD than the next best) indicating higher quality, more realistic, and temporally consistent future video predictions.
- **Mitigating Error Accumulation:** This superior fidelity is attributed to the SVD architecture's ability to incorporate temporal attention and generate future observations simultaneously, preventing the error accumulation typical of auto-regressive models.
- **Qualitative Visualization:** Figure 5 visually confirms DiffDreamer's robust ability to accurately predict future observations conditioned on an input trajectory, showcasing its reliable trajectory prediction capabilities.

**3. Ablation Studies:**
- **DPA Analysis (Table II, Fig. 4):**
 - **Impact on Policy Performance:** When the traditional SAC actor in Iso-Dreamer and Dreamer-V3 is replaced with DPA, the performance metrics (SR, RC, Infraction/km) improve significantly. For instance, "DPA + Dreamer-V3" achieves an 83.33% SR and 75.09% RC, outperforming vanilla Dreamer-V3 (63.33% SR, 67.53% RC). This highlights the crucial impact of DPA in modeling diverse behaviors and generating effective trajectories.
 - **Multi-Modal Nature:** Figure 4 qualitatively demonstrates DPA's core strength – its multi-modal nature. Under identical initial conditions, but with different seeds, DPA generates distinct yet plausible trajectories (e.g., navigating around a green car by swerving left or right). This proves its ability to capture a rich distribution of behaviors, a capability lacking in traditional deterministic or single-Gaussian policies.

- **DiffDreamer Analysis (Table II):**
 - **World Model Contribution:** By comparing "DPA + Dreamer-V3" with "Imagine-2-Drive (ours)" (which is DPA + DiffDreamer), the results show Imagine-2-Drive's superior performance (83.33% SR, 82.13% RC vs. 83.33% SR, 75.09% RC). This isolates and validates DiffDreamer's contribution, emphasizing that its higher prediction fidelity (as shown in Table III) directly translates to better policy learning and overall driving performance. Accurate future state predictions are critical for robust decision-making.

- **Context Length Analysis (Fig. 7):**
 - **Importance of Temporal Context:** The performance of Imagine-2-Drive is shown to degrade significantly when the context length (CL) for the State Encoder is reduced to CL=1. This is because a single frame provides insufficient historical information for robust state inference in partially observable environments.
 - **Optimal Context:** Longer context lengths (CL=3, CL=5) capture richer temporal dependencies, leading to improved state estimation, more informed decision-making, and enhanced episodic returns, validating the POMDP approximation strategy.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that the synergistic combination of a high-fidelity video diffusion-based world model (DiffDreamer) and a multi-modal diffusion policy actor (DPA) significantly advances the state-of-the-art in WMRL for autonomous driving, leading to safer, more efficient, and more adaptable agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Imagine-2-Drive" presents a significant advancement in the field of World Model-based Reinforcement Learning (WMRL) and its application to autonomous driving, carrying substantial potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **Advancement in WMRL Fidelity and Robustness:** The introduction of DiffDreamer, a high-fidelity video-diffusion based world model, directly addresses the long-standing problem of compounding errors and low prediction fidelity in traditional WMRL architectures. By generating future observations simultaneously rather than auto-regressively, Imagine-2-Drive enables more accurate and temporally consistent long-horizon planning. This breakthrough makes WMRL a much more viable and trustworthy paradigm for complex, safety-critical applications.

2. **Enabling Multi-Modal and Adaptive Autonomous Driving:** The Diffusion Policy Actor (DPA) is a crucial innovation. Traditional RL policies often struggle with the multi-modal nature of real-world decision-making. DPA’s ability to model and generate diverse, plausible trajectories under the same initial conditions (as shown in Figure 4) is a game-changer for autonomous driving. This allows vehicles to exhibit more human-like, adaptive, and flexible behaviors, such as choosing multiple safe paths around an obstacle, rather than being confined to a single, potentially brittle, deterministic action. This leads to more robust and safer autonomous systems.

3. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Reduced Risk:** By leveraging a high-fidelity world model for policy training, Imagine-2-Drive drastically reduces the need for extensive and costly online interactions in real-world or high-fidelity simulated environments. This improved sample efficiency is vital for practical deployment of autonomous vehicles, where real-world data collection and training are inherently risky, expensive, and time-consuming. The ability to learn primarily in an "imagination" space accelerates development cycles and lowers barriers to entry.

4. **Broader Applicability to Robotics and Other Domains:** While specifically demonstrated in autonomous driving, the core principles of Imagine-2-Drive – combining high-fidelity, simultaneous future prediction with multi-modal diffusion policies trained via RL – are highly generalizable. The authors themselves mention extending this work to "robotic manipulation, where accurate long-horizon planning and multimodal prediction are crucial." This framework could be transformative for other complex robotics tasks requiring fine motor control, diverse interaction strategies, and predictive capabilities, such as dexterous manipulation, human-robot collaboration, and complex navigation in unstructured environments.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research. It provides a strong baseline for developing even more sophisticated world models that incorporate diverse modalities (e.g., LiDAR, radar) or richer semantic understanding. Further research could explore more advanced diffusion policy architectures, novel RL optimization techniques for diffusion models, or methods to incorporate safety constraints more explicitly within the multi-modal trajectory generation process. The iterative training scheme also provides a robust foundation for investigating more dynamic co-evolution strategies between world models and policies.

In conclusion, "Imagine-2-Drive" represents a significant step forward by synergistically integrating cutting-edge generative models into the reinforcement learning pipeline. It not only addresses critical limitations of existing WMRL but also introduces a powerful paradigm for learning adaptable, multi-modal behaviors in complex, safety-critical domains, with a profound potential to impact the future of autonomous systems.
