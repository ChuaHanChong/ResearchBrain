# RewardAnything: Generalizable Principle-Following Reward Models

> [!summary] Summary
> "RewardAnything" introduces a new paradigm for reward models that can interpret and follow natural language principles, allowing them to generalize to diverse and unseen evaluation criteria without retraining. This generative model, trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization, achieves state-of-the-art performance on the challenging RM-Bench (86.4% accuracy) and strong generalization on the new RABENCH (81.9% accuracy, Kendall's "u03c4" 65.27).

## Problem

- Traditional Reward Models (RMs) are rigid, struggling to generalize to diverse human preferences and requiring costly retraining for new scenarios.
- Existing RMs often learn implicit biases from static preference datasets, leading to issues like favoring response length over factual accuracy, and lack interpretability.
- The need for continuous data collection and retraining for each new preference limits the scalability and efficiency of LLM alignment.

## Method

- Proposed a =="principle-following" reward model paradigm==, where RMs interpret natural language principles to guide evaluation.
- Introduced ==REWARDANYTHING==, a generative reward model that outputs structured evaluations (reasoning, scores, rankings) in a single pass.
- Developed ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a comprehensive reward function (format and accuracy components) for training, fostering better generalization.
- Created ==RABENCH==, a new benchmark with a robust LLM-consensus judgment pipeline, to evaluate principle-following capabilities on unseen principles.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the challenging RM-Bench (**86.4%** overall accuracy, **84.4%** on "hard" setting), demonstrating effective bias mitigation through principles.
- Demonstrated strong generalization on the novel RABENCH benchmark with **81.9%** overall accuracy (Kendall's τ **65.27**), performing comparably to powerful general-purpose LLMs like GPT-4.1.
- Successfully aligned a Qwen3-8B LLM using only a natural language safety principle and GRPO, resulting in improved refusal behavior and maintaining general response quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language principles are crucial for guiding reward models, enabling strong generalization to unseen evaluation criteria and mitigating biases inherent in traditional preference datasets.
> - Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) significantly outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for training principle-following RMs, facilitating better generalization.
> - Generating explicit reasoning alongside evaluations is integral to the model's accuracy and interpretability in applying principles.
