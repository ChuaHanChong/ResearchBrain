# RewardAnything: Generalizable Principle-Following Reward Models

> [!summary] Summary
> "RewardAnything" introduces a new paradigm for reward models that can interpret and follow natural language principles, allowing them to generalize to diverse and unseen evaluation criteria without retraining. This generative model, trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization, achieves state-of-the-art performance on the challenging RM-Bench (86.4% accuracy) and strong generalization on the new RABENCH (81.9% accuracy, Kendall's "u03c4" 65.27).

## Problem

- Traditional Reward Models (RMs) are rigid, struggling to generalize to diverse human preferences and requiring costly retraining for new scenarios.
- Existing RMs often learn implicit biases from static preference datasets, leading to issues like favoring response length over factual accuracy, and lack interpretability.
- The need for continuous data collection and retraining for each new preference limits the scalability and efficiency of LLM alignment.

## Method

- Proposed a =="principle-following" reward model paradigm==, where RMs interpret natural language principles to guide evaluation.
- Introduced ==REWARDANYTHING==, a generative reward model that outputs structured evaluations (reasoning, scores, rankings) in a single pass.
- Developed ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a comprehensive reward function (format and accuracy components) for training, fostering better generalization.
- Created ==RABENCH==, a new benchmark with a robust LLM-consensus judgment pipeline, to evaluate principle-following capabilities on unseen principles.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the challenging RM-Bench (**86.4%** overall accuracy, **84.4%** on "hard" setting), demonstrating effective bias mitigation through principles.
- Demonstrated strong generalization on the novel RABENCH benchmark with **81.9%** overall accuracy (Kendall's τ **65.27**), performing comparably to powerful general-purpose LLMs like GPT-4.1.
- Successfully aligned a Qwen3-8B LLM using only a natural language safety principle and GRPO, resulting in improved refusal behavior and maintaining general response quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language principles are crucial for guiding reward models, enabling strong generalization to unseen evaluation criteria and mitigating biases inherent in traditional preference datasets.
> - Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) significantly outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for training principle-following RMs, facilitating better generalization.
> - Generating explicit reasoning alongside evaluations is integral to the model's accuracy and interpretability in applying principles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen tremendous advancements, but a fundamental challenge remains: aligning their behavior with diverse and often nuanced human preferences. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as the dominant paradigm for this alignment, with Reward Models (RMs) serving as crucial proxies for human judgment. RMs learn to predict human preferences, thereby providing a scalar reward signal to guide LLM policy optimization.

However, the authors identify significant limitations in the current RM landscape:
1. **Rigidity and Limited Generalization:** Traditional RMs are typically trained on fixed preference datasets, which capture a single, often implicit, preference distribution. This makes them rigid and unable to adapt to varying real-world needs, such as prioritizing conciseness in one scenario and detailed explanations in another. Adapting to new requirements usually demands costly and time-consuming collection of new preference data and retraining of the RM.
2. **Bias and Interpretability Challenges:** Existing RMs often learn from outcome-level supervision (chosen vs. rejected responses) without explicit rationales or underlying principles. This can lead to RMs inferring implicit values through spurious correlations (e.g., favoring longer responses over accurate ones) and exhibiting biases. The lack of transparent reasoning also hinders interpretability, making it difficult to understand or predict model behavior.

This paper addresses these critical bottlenecks by proposing a fundamental paradigm shift: **principle-following reward models**. This concept is analogous to the "instruction-following" revolution in LLMs, where models learned to generalize across diverse tasks by understanding natural language instructions, obviating the need for task-specific retraining. By enabling RMs to interpret and adhere to dynamically provided natural language specifications of reward principles, the work aims to make RMs flexible, generalizable tools that eliminate the need for retraining for each new preference scenario.

This work directly builds upon and extends recent efforts to improve RM quality, such as incorporating reasoning steps (e.g., RM-R1) or using explicit instructions (e.g., SALMON). While SALMON introduced "instructable reward models," the authors note a lack of systematic study or benchmarking of their adaptability to diverse, novel principles, leaving the generalization potential unexplored. "RewardAnything" fills this gap by formalizing the concept, introducing a comprehensive benchmark (RABENCH), and presenting a concrete solution, thus pushing the boundaries toward more adaptive, transparent, and efficient LLM alignment. It fundamentally rethinks how RMs acquire and apply evaluative criteria, moving from implicit pattern matching to explicit, principle-grounded reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the "RewardAnything" research are:

1. **Overcome RM Rigidity and Improve Generalization:** The primary motivation is to address the inability of current reward models to adapt to diverse and dynamic human preferences without requiring extensive, costly retraining. The authors seek to develop RMs that can generalize to novel preference criteria simply by being provided with natural language principles, making them far more adaptable for real-world applications.
2. **Mitigate Bias and Enhance Interpretability:** Current RMs often infer preferences implicitly from static datasets, leading to biases (e.g., favoring length over factual accuracy) and a lack of transparency. A key objective is to enable RMs to learn and apply *explicit* natural language principles, thereby promoting more transparent, controllable, and less biased AI alignment. This allows users to directly specify desired evaluation logic, rather than relying on the model to infer it.
3. **Reduce Resource Intensity and Scalability Issues:** The traditional cycle of collecting task-specific preference data and retraining RMs for every new scenario is resource-intensive and limits practical application at scale. The authors aim to eliminate this bottleneck by proposing a principle-following paradigm that allows a single RM to serve diverse needs without continuous data collection and retraining.
4. **Establish a Robust Evaluation Framework:** To properly assess the proposed principle-following capability, the authors recognize the need for a dedicated benchmark. A key objective is to develop RABENCH, a comprehensive evaluation suite that specifically measures how well RMs generalize to novel natural language principles across different domains and varying levels of specificity.
5. **Demonstrate Practical Utility for LLM Alignment:** Beyond theoretical advancements and benchmark performance, a crucial objective is to show how a principle-following RM like RewardAnything can seamlessly integrate with existing RLHF methods and effectively align LLMs to complex, nuanced behaviors using only natural language principles as guidance.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to make reward modeling more flexible, efficient, transparent, and directly steerable by human intent, mirroring the advancements seen in instruction-following for LLMs themselves.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "RewardAnything" project introduces a novel approach to reward modeling, comprising a conceptual shift, a new benchmark, and a new model architecture and training strategy.

#### 3.1. Conceptualization of Principle-Following Reward Models

The authors formally define principle-following for RMs: given a natural language principle (P), a prompt (Q), and candidate responses (X_i), the RM must produce an evaluation (score S and ranking Π) that reflects adherence to P. This capability should generalize to unseen principles. To systematically explore this, they curated 200 distinct natural language principles, categorized into five fundamental aspects of text quality:
- **Logic:** Pertaining to reasoning and strategic flow.
- **Content:** Specifying information to be presented (e.g., including examples).
- **Structure:** Defining organization and layout.
- **Style:** Specifying preferences for linguistic choices.
- **Tone:** Capturing emotional nuances (e.g., encouraging, humorous).
Each principle was designed to be clear, specific, and actionable.

#### 3.2. RABENCH: A Benchmark for Evaluating Principle-Following

To rigorously evaluate principle-following, the authors developed RABENCH.
- **Evaluation Set Design:**
 - **Principles:** 50 distinct principles were sampled (non-overlapping with training principles).
 - **Prompts:** Sourced from the diverse RewardBench dataset (general chat, math, coding, safety). Original responses/labels were discarded to focus on principle-following.
 - **Responses:** 10 different LLMs (GPT, Claude, Qwen, LLaMA, DeepSeek) generated responses, each instructed to follow a given principle, yielding varied adherence levels.
- **Ground Truth Judgment:**
 - Four state-of-the-art LLMs (Claude-3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4.1, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini 2.5 Pro) acted as independent judges, assigning scores (1-5) and rankings.
 - A **consensus algorithm with Dynamic Programming** (Algorithm 1 in Appendix F) was applied to synthesize these LLM judgments. This algorithm finds the longest subsequence of responses where every pairwise preference is supported by a minimum number (e.g., K=3) of judges. This provides a robust, agreed-upon partial order for the ground truth.
- **Quality Assurance:** A rigorous human verification process was conducted where two annotators independently validated each principle-prompt-responses-consensus judgment set. Only elements validated by both annotators were kept, yielding 1002 preference rankings (equivalent to 31806 traditional preference pairs) with an 89% agreement rate (Cohen’s κ of 0.57).

#### 3.3. REWARDANYTHING: Our Approach to Principle-Following RMs

REWARDANYTHING is designed as a **generative RM** that takes a principle, prompt, and a set of candidate responses as input, and outputs a structured evaluation (reasoning, scores, and a best-to-worst ranking) in a single LLM call. This contrasts with traditional RMs which are typically discriminative or require multiple pairwise comparisons.

- **Group Relative Preference Learning (GRPL):** REWARDANYTHING is trained using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, a reinforcement learning algorithm. Unlike standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT), GRPO refines the model's policy (its ability to generate evaluations) by focusing on reinforcing outputs that accurately reflect the relative quality distinctions among a group of responses based on the principle, rather than just predicting absolute scores. This RL approach is believed to foster better generalization.
- **Reward Design:** The efficacy of GRPO relies on a custom reward function `r(O_model, O_gt)` which compares the model's generated evaluation (`O_model`) against the ground truth (`O_gt`). This reward is a weighted sum of two main components:
 1. **Format Reward (`r_f`):** Encourages comprehensive, well-structured, and consistent evaluation outputs. It penalizes issues like invalid JSON, missing keys, inconsistent scores/rankings, and rewards explicit reasoning within designated tags. This ensures parsable and thoughtfully constructed outputs.
 2. **Accuracy Reward (`r_a`):** Measures alignment with ground truth beyond simple rank correlation. It's a weighted sum of four sub-metrics:
 - **Weighted Reversed-Pair Penalty:** Emphasizes misrankings where there's a large true quality difference.
 - **Score Distribution Matching:** Encourages the RM to use the scoring scale consistently with the ground truth.
 - **Partial Credit for Close Scores:** Provides a denser learning signal by rewarding predictions close to the true score.
 - **Ranking Agreement:** Uses Kendall’s τ and top-k consensus to capture both global and critical top-item ordinal similarity.
This comprehensive reward structure guides GRPO to learn fine-grained relative performance and promote interpretable evaluations.

- **Training Data Generation:** Training data for REWARDANYTHING was synthetically generated using a distinct set of 150 principles (non-overlapping with RABENCH) and prompts from the decontaminated Skywork-Reward trainset. The process mirrored RABENCH's ground truth generation, but without human verification, resulting in ~4,000 training examples (173K preference pairs).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the superior performance and versatility of REWARDANYTHING across traditional benchmarks and its novel principle-following capabilities.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking Reward Models and Mitigating Bias

- **RM-Bench Performance:** REWARDANYTHING was evaluated on RM-Bench, a challenging benchmark known for its "hard" setting that tests an RM's ability to prioritize factual correctness over superficial cues like length or formatting. By providing REWARDANYTHING with a clear principle emphasizing content accuracy, the model achieved **State-of-the-Art (SotA) overall performance** (86.4% accuracy), particularly excelling on the "hard" setting (84.4%). This surpasses general-purpose LLMs and other discriminative/generative RMs, demonstrating its efficacy not just as a general reward model, but also its unique ability to mitigate biases inherent in preference datasets by explicitly following desired evaluation logic through principles.
- **What Makes a Good Principle?** An ablation study on RM-Bench's "hard" split showed that principles with clearly defined objective priorities (e.g., "Accuracy Only" or "Length < Accuracy") lead to significantly better performance, enabling REWARDANYTHING to override length bias. Similarly, principles that provide "Objectives + Structured Rules" (e.g., "Correctness is paramount... Among correct responses, the more detailed one is better.") yielded the best results, outperforming vague principles or those that merely list objectives without guidance on their interplay. This highlights the crucial role of clear, well-structured principles in guiding RM behavior.
- **RABENCH Performance:** On the newly introduced RABENCH, designed specifically for principle-following, REWARDANYTHING demonstrated strong generalization capabilities. It achieved an **overall accuracy of 81.9%** (Kendall's τ 65.27, NDCG 97.84), performing comparably to very powerful general-purpose LLMs like GPT-4.1. This confirms that while traditional RMs struggle to adapt to explicit, diverse principles, REWARDANYTHING effectively addresses this gap, showcasing its ability to follow unseen principles.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on REWARDANYTHING

Ablation studies provided key insights into the contribution of different components to REWARDANYTHING's performance on RABENCH:
- **Training Ablations:**
 - **-Principles:** Removing explicit principles during training and inference (mimicking traditional RMs) caused a significant performance degradation (from 81.9% to 67.4%), underscoring the critical role of principle guidance.
 - **Listwise → Pairwise:** Converting listwise training data to pairwise comparisons reduced accuracy to 73.2%, highlighting the efficiency and finer-grained distinction capabilities of listwise training.
 - **GRPO → SFT:** Replacing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) led to the largest degradation (to 62.3%). This suggests GRPO's reinforcement learning approach fosters better generalization compared to SFT, which may lead to overfitting.
- **GRPO Reward Ablations:**
 - **-Relative Preference:** Using only exact matching in the accuracy reward (instead of relative preference signals) resulted in a slight drop (to 78.2%), indicating the importance of dense, relative signals for efficient learning.
 - **-Format:** Removing the format reward had a smaller impact (to 78.5%), suggesting the accuracy reward implicitly requires correct formatting, though explicit format reward likely aids training stability.
- **Inference Ablation:**
 - **-Reasoning:** Disabling the generation of explicit reasoning during inference significantly degraded performance (to 73.9%). This confirms that the explicit reasoning process is integral to REWARDANYTHING’s ability to accurately apply principles, providing an interpretable evaluation process.

#### 4.3. Case Study: Aligning LLM with Natural Language Principles

To demonstrate practical utility, REWARDANYTHING was used to align Qwen3-8B for nuanced safety behaviors.
- **Setup:** The alignment used only a natural language principle (detailed in Figure 4, focusing on responsible refusals, minimizing over-rejection, and providing constructive alternatives for unsafe queries) and 2000 prompts from PKU-SafeRLHF. No preference data or custom RM training was needed. GRPO was used with REWARDANYTHING as the *sole* reward source. A baseline was also aligned using Skywork RM.
- **Results:** The REWARDANYTHING-aligned model ("Ours-Aligned") showed significant improvements:
 - Slightly reduced incorrect rejections for safe prompts.
 - Maintained a high refusal rate for unsafe content.
 - Transformed more necessary refusals into constructive, empathetic, and alternative-providing responses, showcasing a more responsible, human-like approach.
 - Outperformed both the original Qwen3-8B and the Skywork-Aligned model on MT-Bench for overall text quality, indicating that nuanced safety alignment did not compromise general response quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "RewardAnything" research represents a significant step forward in the field of Large Language Model alignment, with profound implications for both research and practical application:

1. **Paradigm Shift in LLM Alignment:** By enabling Reward Models to interpret and follow natural language principles, this work proposes a fundamental shift from rigid, data-driven implicit alignment to flexible, user-controllable explicit alignment. This is analogous to the instruction-following revolution in LLMs, promising to unlock new levels of adaptability.
2. **Enhanced Flexibility and Generalization:** The ability of REWARDANYTHING to adapt to novel principles without retraining eliminates the need for costly and time-consuming data collection and model re-alignment for every new preference or task. This makes LLM alignment far more agile and scalable, allowing a single RM to serve diverse needs (e.g., conciseness for chatbots, detail for research assistants).
3. **Bias Mitigation and Improved Interpretability:** By explicitly guiding RMs with natural language principles, REWARDANYTHING can directly mitigate biases (like length preference) that often arise from implicit learning on fixed datasets. The generative nature of the model, producing explicit reasoning alongside scores and rankings, also significantly enhances interpretability, making it clearer why a particular judgment was made.
4. **Lowering the Barrier to Customized LLM Alignment:** The case study vividly demonstrates that complex, nuanced LLM behaviors (like balanced safety and helpfulness) can be achieved by simply specifying a natural language principle and using a relatively small set of prompts, without the need for bespoke preference datasets or extensive RM training. This significantly lowers the technical and resource barrier for developers and users to customize LLMs to their specific values and requirements.
5. **New Research Directions:** The introduction of RABENCH provides a crucial benchmark for future research on principle-following RMs, enabling systematic evaluation and progress tracking. Furthermore, the work opens up new avenues for exploring the optimal design of natural language principles, their robustness, and their potential for adversarial manipulation, akin to the field of prompt engineering.

In conclusion, "RewardAnything" is not merely an incremental improvement; it lays the groundwork for a new generation of LLM alignment techniques that are more efficient, transparent, and directly responsive to human intent, paving the way for truly adaptable and ethically steerable AI systems.
