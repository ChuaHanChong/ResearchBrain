# Layer by Layer: Uncovering Hidden Representations in Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Intermediate layers in large language models often provide superior representations for downstream tasks compared to final layers, yielding 2-16% performance improvements on average across 32 text embedding tasks. This work introduces a unified framework based on matrix-based entropy to explain this phenomenon and enable unsupervised identification of optimal layers.

## Problem

- The common assumption that the final layer of large language models provides the most effective representations for downstream tasks may be suboptimal.
- There is a lack of comprehensive understanding regarding how information is processed and transformed within the intermediate layers of LLMs.
- Existing methods for analyzing neural network representation quality are diverse and lack a unified theoretical framework.

## Method

- Proposed a unified theoretical framework based on ==matrix-based entropy== to quantify and compare representation quality across neural network layers.
- Utilized seven specific metrics, derived from the framework, covering ==information-theoretic, geometric, and augmentation-invariance properties== of representations.
- Conducted extensive empirical validation across a wide range of language models (transformers, state-space models) and vision models, on diverse downstream tasks.

## Results

- Intermediate layers achieved **2%** to **16%** average performance improvements over final layers across 32 MTEB text embedding tasks.
- Unsupervised layer selection using the proposed metrics (e.g., DiME) led to a **3%** average MTEB score boost for Pythia-410M models.
- Autoregressive models (e.g., Pythia, Llama3, AIM) exhibit a pronounced mid-layer 'compression valley' in prompt entropy, while masked/bidirectional models (e.g., BERT) show more uniform entropy across layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intermediate layers in autoregressive models consistently yield superior representations for downstream tasks, challenging the reliance on final layers.
> - The proposed matrix-based entropy metrics effectively correlate with downstream performance, enabling unsupervised selection of optimal layers.
> - The training objective, particularly autoregression, significantly influences the layer-wise information compression and representation dynamics, observed across both language and vision models.
