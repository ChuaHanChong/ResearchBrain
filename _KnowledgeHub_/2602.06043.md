# Shared LoRA Subspaces for almost Strict Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Johns Hopkins University developed "Share," a Parameter-Efficient Continual Finetuning (PaCT) framework that dynamically updates a single, shared low-rank subspace for large pre-trained models. Operating without data replay or increased model size, Share achieves up to 100x parameter reduction and 281x memory savings while maintaining performance comparable to non-continual LoRA and demonstrating backward knowledge transfer in various tasks.

## Problem

- Large pre-trained models require computationally expensive and memory-intensive fine-tuning for new tasks, often leading to catastrophic forgetting of previous knowledge.
- Existing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA often necessitate storing separate adapters for each task, resulting in a growing memory footprint.
- Traditional continual learning approaches typically rely on data replay, model expansion, or increasing model size, which violate strict continual learning constraints.

## Method

- The "Share" framework proposes a Parameter-Efficient Continual Finetuning (PaCT) approach that learns and dynamically refines a single, shared low-rank subspace across tasks.
- It replaces numerous task-specific LoRA adapters with a continually evolving representation, using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to identify and update principal basis vectors.
- Knowledge is integrated by analytically recalculating task coefficients for all tasks onto the newly updated subspace, operating without data replay or an increasing model size.

## Results

- Achieved up to 100x parameter reduction and 281x memory savings compared to standard LoRA methods for multi-task adaptation.
- Demonstrated performance comparable to or marginally exceeding non-continually trained LoRA and upper-bound baselines, under strict replay-free conditions, including evidence of backward knowledge transfer (e.g., CoLA score improving from 56.00 to 59.81).
- Maintained robust performance across diverse modalities, including natural language understanding (GLUE), image classification, 3D pose estimation, and text-to-image generation, and effectively handled asynchronous learning of multiple adapters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LoRA adapters for diverse tasks and modalities can be effectively represented and continually learned within a common, shared low-rank subspace.
> - Dynamically refining this shared subspace and analytically recalculating task coefficients enables backward knowledge transfer, where performance on older tasks can improve after learning new ones.
> - Strict continual learning, characterized by replay-free operation and minimal parameter growth, is achievable with high efficiency and performance for large pre-trained models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical and rapidly growing challenge in modern artificial intelligence: the efficient and continual adaptation of large pre-trained models (such as Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Diffusion models) to new, diverse tasks without incurring prohibitive computational costs or suffering from "catastrophic forgetting."

The broader landscape is characterized by:
- **The rise of large foundation models:** These models demonstrate impressive general capabilities but require fine-tuning for specific applications.
- **Inefficiencies of traditional fine-tuning:** Full model fine-tuning is computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and prone to catastrophic forgetting (where learning new tasks degrades performance on previously learned ones).
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods:** Techniques like Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) have emerged to reduce the number of trainable parameters during adaptation, making fine-tuning more accessible. However, standard LoRA often requires training and storing separate adapters for each task, leading to an increasing memory footprint and hindering cross-task knowledge transfer.
- **Continual Learning (CL):** This field aims to enable models to learn a sequence of tasks incrementally without forgetting past knowledge. Traditional CL methods often rely on data replay (storing samples from past tasks), regularization, or expanding model architectures (e.g., mixture-of-experts), which can violate strict CL conditions (no data replay, no additional models, no increase in model size).
- **The "Universal Weight Subspace Hypothesis":** The authors' previous work posited that neural network weights often converge to a shared, layer-wise subspace across tasks, which can be leveraged for efficient training and model merging. This paper directly operationalizes and extends this hypothesis to a continual learning setting.

"Share" positions itself at the intersection of PEFT and CL, specifically aiming to bridge the gap where existing PEFT methods lack mechanisms for *strict* continual learning and integrated knowledge transfer, and current CL methods often require compromises in terms of memory, replay data, or model size. The paper explicitly contrasts with methods like O-LoRA that learn orthogonal subspaces (limiting knowledge sharing) and mixture-of-experts approaches that accumulate separate models. By proposing a method that learns and dynamically updates a *single, shared low-rank subspace*, Share seeks to fulfill the demanding criteria of "almost strict continual learning" – minimal parameter growth, no data replay, and knowledge integration across tasks – while maintaining efficiency. It tackles the practical problem of managing hundreds or thousands of LoRA adapters by compressing them into a more manageable, continually evolving representation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to develop a highly efficient and effective framework for continual fine-tuning of large pre-trained models that can adapt to new tasks seamlessly and accumulate knowledge over time without suffering from catastrophic forgetting or excessive resource demands.

The key objectives and motivations underpinning the "Share" approach are:

- **Overcome Catastrophic Forgetting:** Address the fundamental challenge in continual learning where models tend to forget previously learned knowledge when trained on new tasks.
- **Enhance Parameter and Memory Efficiency:** Significantly reduce the number of trainable parameters and memory footprint compared to traditional fine-tuning and standard LoRA implementations, especially as the number of tasks grows.
- **Enable Strict Continual Learning:** Develop a replay-free method that does not require access to previous task data, nor does it necessitate an ever-growing model size or multiple task-specific adapters, thus adhering closely to the "strict continual learning" paradigm.
- **Facilitate Knowledge Integration and Transfer:** Design a mechanism for continuous knowledge accumulation, allowing models to leverage insights from past tasks (forward transfer) and even improve performance on older tasks as new knowledge is acquired (backward transfer), unlike methods that train isolated task experts.
- **Operationalize the Universal Weight Subspace Hypothesis:** Empirically validate and build upon the group's prior theoretical work, demonstrating that LoRA adapters for similar tasks and modalities indeed share a common low-rank subspace, and this subspace can be continually learned and refined.
- **Support Scalable Deployment:** Create a solution that can efficiently replace numerous task-specific LoRA adapters with a single, evolving model, making it practical for large-scale, asynchronous model serving and personalization scenarios.
- **Broad Applicability:** Demonstrate the method's effectiveness across diverse tasks and modalities, including natural language understanding, image classification, 3D pose estimation, and text-to-image generation.

Ultimately, the motivation is to make lifelong learning with large AI models more practical, resource-efficient, and accessible to a wider range of researchers and users, mitigating environmental impact and democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"Share" proposes a novel Parameter-Efficient Continual Finetuning (PaCT) approach that revolves around learning and dynamically updating a single, shared low-rank subspace. The method operates under a strict problem setting: a pre-trained model adapts to a sequence of tasks, receiving either task-specific data or a pre-trained LoRA adapter, *without access to past task data*.

The core idea is that LoRA adapters share a common low-rank subspace. By identifying the principal basis vectors of this subspace, any new adapter can be approximated as a linear combination of these vectors, reducing the need for individual storage. Share maintains two sets of parameters: frozen principal basis vectors and trainable task-specific coefficients. The learning process unfolds in three phases:

1. **Step 1: Initialization**
 - **Goal:** Form an initial foundational subspace (principal basis vectors).
 - **Process:** If `N ≥ 1` existing LoRA adapters are available, their `B` and `A` matrices are reshaped into stacked rank-`r` vectors (`D_A`, `D_B`). Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is performed on these mean-centered stacked matrices to extract the top `k` principal basis vectors (`α₀`, `β₀`). If no adapters are available, one is trained on initial data.
 - **Parameterization:** These `k` principal basis vectors (`α`, `β`) are kept frozen. Only randomly initialized task-specific coefficients (`ϵ_α`, `ϵ_β`) of a very small pseudo-rank `p` are learned, leading to significant parameter reduction compared to LoRA.
 - **Key Aspect:** This initialization is entirely data- and gradient-free (if adapters are provided), making it efficient.

2. **Step 2: Continual Adaptation (Data-Driven)**
 - **Goal:** Efficiently adapt to new task data (`Sₜ₊₁`) while preserving the foundational subspace.
 - **Process:** When new data arrives, a small number (`φ < k`) of *temporary* basis vectors (selected from the top existing ones) along with their coefficients are initialized and fine-tuned on the new task data.
 - **Parameterization:** This temporary expansion requires a significantly smaller number of parameters (`φ(n + d + 2p)`) compared to a full LoRA adapter (`r(n + d)`). Both temporary basis vectors and coefficients are optimized.

3. **Step 3: Merging and Finetuning**
 - **Goal:** Integrate new knowledge into the foundational subspace and update task coefficients.
 - **Knowledge Integration:**
 - First, all *previous* task-specific adapters (`ˆAᵢ`, `ˆBᵢ`) are reconstructed using the *current* shared basis vectors (`αₜ₋₁`, `βₜ₋₁`) and their respective coefficients (`ϵᵢ_α`, `ϵᵢ_β`).
 - Then, these reconstructed adapters (for `i=1` to `t-1`) are stacked together with the newly learned (from Step 2) or directly provided (`∆Wₜ`) adapter (`ˆAₜ`, `ˆBₜ`) to form new factor data matrices (`Dₜ_A`, `Dₜ_B`).
 - **Factor Update:** SVD is performed on these new, combined factor data matrices to obtain updated shared principal basis vectors (`αₜ`, `βₜ`). This dynamically refines the foundational subspace.
 - **Coefficient Recalculation:** For *all* tasks (from `1` to `t`), new coefficients (`ϵᵢ_α`, `ϵᵢ_β`) are *analytically calculated* by projecting each (reconstructed or new) task adapter onto the *newly updated* principal subspace (`αₜ`, `βₜ`). This step is crucial for preserving knowledge and is gradient-free.
 - **Optional Finetuning (Share-full):** Optionally, the newly calculated task-specific coefficients (`ϵₜ`) can be further fine-tuned using the new task data (or some limited replay for previous tasks, if strict CL constraints are relaxed) to boost performance.

**Theoretical Analysis:** The paper provides theoretical guarantees through Proposition 1 (Incremental Subspace Error Bound), based on the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem, demonstrating that the reconstruction error of the continually approximated subspace is bounded by the sum of squared singular values beyond `k`. Theorem 2 provides generalization error bounds for Share, showing that its performance can be tighter than full parameter training when a new task largely lies within the shared principal subspace.

**Hyperparameter Ablation:** Ablation studies guide the selection of key hyperparameters: `k` (number of principal factors) is chosen based on explained variance (e.g., 60%), `p` (pseudo-rank for coefficients) is often effective even at `p=1`, and `φ` (temporary factors for adaptation) is typically a small fraction of `k` (e.g., `[1, k/4]`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Share is comprehensive, spanning diverse modalities and model architectures, and is conducted under rigorous, replay-free continual learning settings. The key findings are:

- **Exceptional Parameter and Memory Efficiency:**
 - Achieves up to **100× parameter reduction** and **281× memory savings** compared to traditional LoRA methods.
 - A single Share model can effectively replace hundreds of task-specific LoRA adapters, drastically reducing the memory footprint for large-scale deployments.
 - Storage complexity scales favorably: `O(k * (n + d) + T * k * p)` for Share versus `O(T * r * (n + d))` for LoRA.

- **Comparable to Superior Performance:**
 - Despite the significant efficiency gains and operating under strict continual learning constraints (replay-free, no parameter growth), Share maintains performance **comparable to, and in some cases marginally surpassing, non-continually trained LoRA** and even upper-bound baselines (jointly trained models).

- **Evidence of Backward Knowledge Transfer:**
 - In the Continual GLUE benchmark (Natural Language Understanding), Share demonstrates instances of **backward knowledge transfer**, where performance on earlier tasks *improves* after learning subsequent tasks (e.g., CoLA score improving from 56.00 to 59.81). This is a rare and highly desirable outcome in continual learning, attributed to Share’s dynamic subspace refinement and analytical coefficient recalculation.

- **Strong Performance Across Diverse Domains:**
 - **Natural Language Understanding (GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa):** Share-full (with optional coefficient finetuning) achieved an average performance of 83.44% with only 0.012M parameters (0.29MB), marginally outperforming non-continual LoRA's 83.43% which required 7.2M parameters (81.6MB).
 - **Image Classification (ViT-B/16 on CIFAR-100, Food-101, Caltech-101, Flowers-102):** Share matched the upper bound on CIFAR-100 (94.20% accuracy) using only 0.10M parameters and consistently showed the lowest forgetting rates among all competing replay-free methods, outperforming prompt-based and other adapter-based approaches.
 - **3D Object Pose Estimation (ResNet50 on Pascal3D+):** Share demonstrated superior performance across all occlusion levels (L0-L3), outperforming replay-based methods like iNeMO with a 96% parameter reduction (1M parameters vs. 25M for baselines).
 - **Text-to-Image Generation (Flux model):** Qualitative results showed Share maintaining performance comparable to individual LoRAs, achieving up to a 20× reduction in model size. This is highlighted as one of the earliest works in PEFT CL for such multimodal models without replay or mixture-of-experts setups.

- **Effective for Asynchronous Learning and Serving at Scale:**
 - In a novel setup involving 50 LoRAs arriving incrementally, Share demonstrated robust knowledge retention (90-99% of original performance) for in-distribution tasks.
 - For out-of-distribution tasks, Share significantly outperformed non-continual model merging methods like TIES (avg. Rouge-L of 55.89 vs. 21.12 for TIES) while approaching the performance of individual LoRAs, showcasing strong generalization and memory savings (96×).
 - The factor subspace quality was observed to improve incrementally with each adapter integration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Share" framework presents a significant advancement in the field of continual learning and parameter-efficient tuning, with far-reaching implications for the development and deployment of large-scale AI systems.

**1. Enabling Practical Lifelong Learning:**
Share moves closer to realizing truly "lifelong learning" systems by providing a mechanism for large pre-trained models to continually acquire new knowledge without catastrophic forgetting or the need for constant, costly retraining. Its replay-free nature removes a major practical hurdle associated with data privacy and storage.

**2. Democratizing Access to Large AI Models:**
By achieving massive parameter and memory reductions (up to 100x parameters, 281x memory), Share significantly lowers the computational barrier to fine-tuning and continually adapting state-of-the-art large models. This has a direct positive impact on academic researchers, small teams, and individual developers with limited computational resources, fostering broader participation in advanced AI research and application.

**3. Environmental Sustainability:**
The reduction in computational resources directly translates to lower energy consumption and a smaller carbon footprint associated with training and deploying large AI models. This contributes to the broader goal of making AI development more environmentally sustainable, aligning with responsible AI practices.

**4. Scalable and Asynchronous Model Serving:**
The ability of a single Share model to compress and dynamically manage hundreds of LoRA adapters opens new possibilities for scalable AI services. It facilitates efficient model serving where a base model can be personalized for numerous users by switching lightweight coefficients, or continually updated with new knowledge streams asynchronously. This is crucial for dynamic, real-world AI applications.

**5. Theoretical and Algorithmic Advancement:**
Share's methodology, particularly the dynamic learning and refinement of a shared low-rank subspace via SVD and analytical coefficient recalculation, represents a novel algorithmic contribution. The empirical demonstration of backward knowledge transfer, a highly desirable but often elusive phenomenon in CL, underscores the effectiveness of its knowledge integration strategy. This work also provides a strong empirical validation of the "Universal Weight Subspace Hypothesis" in a continual learning context.

**6. Broad Applicability and Versatility:**
The extensive experimental validation across diverse modalities (NLU, image classification, 3D pose estimation, text-to-image generation) and different backbone architectures demonstrates Share's general applicability and robustness, positioning it as a versatile solution for a wide range of AI tasks.

**Future Directions:** The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current reliance on a single type of backbone model and the need for high-quality adapters in purely adapter-based scenarios. Future work aims to extend Share to integrate knowledge from diverse, fully trained models and enhance cross-domain/cross-task knowledge transfer for multi-modal models. These extensions would further amplify Share's potential impact.

In conclusion, "Shared LoRA Subspaces for almost Strict Continual Learning" offers a highly significant and practical framework that pushes the boundaries of parameter-efficient and continual learning. Its innovations promise to make advanced AI more accessible, sustainable, and capable of truly lifelong adaptation.
