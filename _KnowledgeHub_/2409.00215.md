# Constraint-Aware Intent Estimation for Dynamic Human-Robot Object Co-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the GRASP Laboratory, University of Pennsylvania, developed a framework for dynamic human-robot object co-manipulation that estimates 6 Degrees of Freedom human intent and adapts robot assistance without relying on force-torque sensors. The approach achieved task completion times 83% of standard admittance control and reduced human linear impulse by 24% in experiments involving co-carrying a 4.5kg load.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to intuitively interpret full 6 Degrees of Freedom (position and orientation) human intent in co-manipulation tasks, often relying on predefined goals or translational movements.
- Many existing physical human-robot collaboration methods require force-torque (F/T) sensors, which can provide ambiguous measurements with extended objects and increase hardware complexity.
- Ensuring safe and fluent collaboration, especially with powerful robots, requires dynamic adaptation of robot assistance and consideration of both human and robot physical constraints (e.g., reachability, manipulability).

## Method

- Human intent (goal and motion for 6DoF) is modeled using first-order Dynamical Systems (DS) for both Cartesian position and rotational orientation.
- Online, real-time intent estimation is performed by two independent particle filters (for position and orientation) that use only motion tracking data, not force-torque sensor input.
- A confidence-based variable impedance control scheme adaptively adjusts robot assistance, providing active help when intent is clear and becoming passive otherwise, with particle trimming to ensure human manipulability and robot kinematic constraints are met.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved task completion times **83%** faster than standard admittance control and **97%** faster than a task adaptation baseline (using robot-side F/T sensors) in a 4.5kg object co-manipulation task.
- Human effort was reduced, with the method requiring **24%** less linear impulse and **23%** less angular impulse compared to admittance control.
- The framework demonstrated online adaptation to individual human preferences and successfully operated without force-torque sensors, mitigating issues of ambiguous force measurements experienced by F/T-dependent baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive 6DoF human intent, encompassing both goal and desired motion, can be robustly estimated from past motion data alone, effectively bypassing the limitations of force-torque sensors in dynamic co-manipulation scenarios.
> - Dynamical Systems (DS) provide a suitable model for human intent, allowing for combined goal and motion estimation while offering theoretical guarantees for global asymptotic stability.
> - Directly incorporating human manipulability and robot kinematic constraints (reachability, joint limits) into the intent estimation and control process is crucial for generating feasible, comfortable, and safe co-manipulation motions for both agents.
