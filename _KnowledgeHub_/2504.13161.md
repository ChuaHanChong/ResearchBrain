# Nemotron-CLIMB: CLustering-based Iterative Data Mixture Bootstrapping for Language Model Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Nemotron-CLIMB, developed by NVIDIA and Georgia Institute of Technology, introduces an automated framework for discovering semantic domains in unstructured web data and iteratively optimizing data mixtures for large language model pre-training. This approach achieved a 2.0% average performance improvement over Llama-3.2 1.2B on general reasoning benchmarks with a 950M parameter model and released two new datasets, NEMOTRON-CLIMBLAB and NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX.

## Problem

- Vast, heterogeneous web datasets for LLM pre-training lack explicit domain labels, making optimal data mixture selection challenging.
- Existing data mixing methods often rely on predefined domains or labor-intensive manual curation, limiting scalability and adaptability.
- The complex, non-linear relationship between data composition and LLM performance makes brute-force optimal mixture search computationally prohibitive.

## Method

- An ==unsupervised clustering method==, utilizing text embeddings and pruning/merging, automatically identifies semantically coherent domains within large-scale web data.
- An ==iterative bootstrapping framework==, employing smaller 'proxy models' and a regression 'predictor', efficiently searches for optimal data mixture weights.
- A ==coordinate descent approach== refines the sampling strategy and predictor simultaneously, focusing the search on promising configurations to reduce computational cost.

## Results

- CLIMB's optimal data mixture consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines (Random, DoReMi, RegMix) by achieving up to **60.41%** average accuracy for 1B models on 40B tokens.
- A **950M** CLIMB model, trained on **400B** tokens with the optimized mixture, surpassed Llama-3.2 1.2B by **2.0%** on average across general reasoning benchmarks.
- The research contributed two new high-quality datasets: NEMOTRON-CLIMBLAB (a **1.2-trillion-token** clustered corpus) and NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX (a **400-billion-token** optimized dataset for efficient pre-training).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automated domain discovery from unstructured data, rather than reliance on predefined labels, enables more effective data mixture optimization for LLM pre-training.
> - An iterative search strategy, leveraging proxy models and a predictor, significantly reduces the computational overhead of identifying high-performing data compositions.
> - Optimal data mixtures not only contain content relevant to downstream tasks but also exhibit crucial diversity among selected clusters to enhance model generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language model (LLM) pre-training has rapidly scaled in recent years, with models now consuming trillions of tokens. A central challenge in this scaling is the effective utilization of vast, heterogeneous datasets, primarily sourced from web content. These datasets, like Common Crawl, often lack explicit domain labels, making it difficult to ascertain optimal data compositions for specific tasks or even general capabilities. Manually curating high-quality, labeled datasets, such as The Pile, is labor-intensive and expensive, hindering scalability and adaptability.

Recent research has increasingly emphasized the critical role of data quality and mixture in LLM performance, moving beyond simply increasing data volume. Studies have shown that refining models on targeted, high-quality, and domain-specific data during later pre-training phases (often termed "mid-training") can significantly boost performance on benchmarks related to math, reasoning, or coding. For instance, OLMo 2 and other works have demonstrated gains from strategically mixing high-quality web data with curated STEM or encyclopedic content.

However, existing approaches to data mixture optimization face several limitations:
1. **Lack of explicit domain labels:** General-purpose heuristics (e.g., perplexity, educational value) are often used to filter web data, but these may not capture the most informative content for specific domains or complex inter-domain relationships.
2. **Complexity of optimal mixing:** Even with datasets possessing domain annotations, the non-linear relationship between data composition and model performance makes optimal mixture selection non-trivial. For example, enhancing coding capabilities might require not just programming content but also complementary knowledge from mathematics or reasoning.
3. **Reliance on predefined domains:** Current learning-based data mixing methods, such as DoReMi and RegMix, often assume the existence of clear, natural domain distinctions within the dataset, which is not always the case for raw web-scale corpora.

Nemotron-CLIMB addresses these gaps by proposing an automated framework that **discovers domains from unstructured data** and iteratively optimizes their mixture. Unlike methods that rely on pre-existing labels or domain definitions, CLIMB uses embedding-based clustering to automatically identify semantic domains. It then employs an iterative bootstrapping strategy, leveraging proxy models and a predictor, to efficiently navigate the vast search space of data compositions. This positions CLIMB as a significant advancement in data-centric AI, offering a scalable, automated solution to a fundamental problem in LLM pre-training, thereby enabling more efficient development of both generalist and domain-specialist models. Its contribution of new, high-quality datasets further pushes the envelope for reproducible research in data mixture optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind Nemotron-CLIMB stems from the persistent challenge of optimally composing pre-training datasets for large language models. Despite the recognized benefits of an ideal data mixture for enhancing LLM performance and generalization, its identification remains a demanding problem.

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **Automated Domain Discovery:** To develop an unsupervised method for automatically identifying and grouping semantically coherent "domains" or clusters within large-scale, unstructured web data, thereby removing the reliance on manually curated labels.
2. **Efficient Data Mixture Optimization:** To design an iterative framework that can efficiently search for and refine optimal data mixtures. This involves approximating the complex relationship between data composition and model performance using smaller "proxy models" and a "predictor," significantly reducing the computational cost compared to brute-force methods.
3. **Enhanced LLM Performance:** To demonstrate that the data mixtures discovered by this automated process lead to superior pre-training performance for LLMs, both in general reasoning tasks and in specialized domain adaptation, under equivalent computational budgets.
4. **Contribution of High-Quality Datasets:** To provide new, publicly available, high-quality pre-training datasets (NEMOTRON-CLIMBLAB and NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX) derived from the CLIMB framework, facilitating further research in data-centric LLM development.
5. **Insights into Optimal Mixtures:** To analyze the characteristics of the identified optimal data mixtures, thereby offering deeper understanding into what constitutes effective pre-training data.

Ultimately, Nemotron-CLIMB aims to streamline and optimize the data curation process for LLM pre-training, making it more flexible, adaptive, and computationally efficient, without sacrificing performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Nemotron-CLIMB (CLIMB) is a novel, automated framework designed to discover, evaluate, and refine data mixtures for language model pre-training. It operates in two main phases: Data Preprocessing and Iterative Bootstrapping for Mixture Weight Search.

#### 3.1. Data Preprocessing

This phase focuses on transforming raw, large-scale textual data into semantically meaningful clusters.
1. **Text Embedding:** Raw documents from source datasets (e.g., Nemotron-CC, smollm-corpus) are mapped into a high-dimensional embedding space using a powerful embedding model (specifically, `stella_en_400M_v5`). This step is crucial for capturing semantic relationships between documents, as opposed to mere lexical similarities.
2. **Embedding Clustering:** The generated embedding vectors are then grouped into a large initial number of clusters (`K_init` set to 1000) using a clustering algorithm like K-means (implemented via the FAISS library). The high `K_init` ensures fine-grained initial clusters.
3. **Cluster Pruning and Merging:** To refine the clusters, a two-step process is applied:
 - **Pruning:** Low-quality clusters are identified and removed based on scores from FastText models trained to evaluate overall quality, educational value, informational value, and advertisement score. These FastText models were themselves trained on 1 million texts annotated by Nemotron-340B using a carefully designed prompt. A loose threshold of 3.0 results in `K_pruned` (e.g., 240) high-quality clusters.
 - **Merging:** The remaining `K_pruned` clusters are then merged into a smaller, more manageable number of `K_enhanced` (e.g., 20 or 21) clusters based on the Euclidean distance between their centroids. This reduces the number of "domains" to facilitate subsequent mixture optimization. The entire filtered dataset, now organized into `K_enhanced` clusters, forms the basis for the mixture search.

#### 3.2. Iterative Bootstrapping: Mixture Weight Search

This phase formulates the optimal data mixture selection as a bi-level optimization problem, solved through an iterative bootstrapping process.
1. **Problem Formulation:** The goal is to find optimal mixture weights `α` for the `K_enhanced` data clusters that maximize downstream task performance `ℓ(α, ω)`, where `ω` are the model weights trained with `α`. Training a full LLM for every possible `α` is computationally prohibitive.
2. **Approximation with Predictor:** To overcome computational costs, a regression model `f_θ(α)` (the "predictor") is trained to approximate `ℓ(α, ω)`. This predictor learns the relationship between mixture weights `α` and observed performance `P` from a subset of (mixture weights, performance) pairs.
3. **Iterative Coordinate Descent:** Instead of a single-shot sampling and prediction, CLIMB uses an iterative coordinate descent method to simultaneously refine the sampling strategy `S` and the predictor `f_θ`. This focuses the search on more promising subspaces.
 - **Initialization:** In the first iteration, a small set of mixture configurations `S_1` is randomly sampled (with a Dirichlet distribution bias) and proxy models are trained on these mixtures to obtain initial performance scores.
 - **Iterative Refinement (k = 2 to K):** For subsequent iterations:
 - **Subroutine 1: Configuration Sampling:** The current predictor `f_k(s)` is used to evaluate all unsampled configurations in the weight space. A new set of `M` configurations is randomly sampled from the top `N` ranked configurations (based on predicted performance) to balance exploitation and exploration. These new samples are added to the existing set `S_k` to form `S_{k+1}`.
 - **Subroutine 2: Predictor Fitting:** A new predictor `f_{k+1,θ}` is trained by minimizing the loss `L` over all sampled configurations in `S_{k+1}`. This updated predictor is then used for the next sampling step.
4. **Proxy Models for Efficiency:** To reduce computational overhead, smaller "proxy models" (e.g., 62M or 350M parameters) are used for training during the iterative search process, rather than the full target LLM (e.g., 1B parameters). The predictor learns from the proxy model's performance.
5. **Predictor Implementation:** LightGBM, an efficient gradient boosting decision tree algorithm, is used as the regression model for the predictor, known for fitting mixture-performance pairs well with limited data. Regularization and early stopping are employed to prevent overfitting.

The iterative process progressively refines the search space, focusing computational resources on data mixtures likely to yield high performance. After a predefined number of iterations (e.g., three), the best configuration predicted by the final predictor is selected as the optimal data mixture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Nemotron-CLIMB demonstrates significant advancements in data mixture optimization for LLM pre-training, substantiated by a series of rigorous experiments.

#### 4.1. Superiority over Data Mixture Baselines

- **Performance on 40B Tokens:** When continuously training 350M and 1B models on 40B tokens using CLIMB's optimal mixture, it consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including Random selection, DoReMi, and RegMix.
 - For the 350M target model, CLIMB achieves an average accuracy of 54.83%, surpassing Random (52.17%) and the previous best, RegMix (53.78%).
 - For the 1B target model, CLIMB achieves an average accuracy of 60.41%, outperforming all baselines.
- **Generalization:** The performance gains, despite optimizing only on validation sets of PIQA, ARC_E, and HellaSwag, generalize robustly across a wider range of 12 reasoning benchmarks, indicating that CLIMB effectively captures essential reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.2. State-of-the-Art Performance for Scaled Models

- **400B Token Pre-training:** When a 1B model is continuously trained on 400B tokens using the optimal data mixture discovered by CLIMB, it achieves state-of-the-art performance among models of similar scales (sub-500M and sub-1.2B).
- **Llama-3.2-1B Exceeded:** The CLIMB 950M model outperforms Llama-3.2 1.2B by a noticeable margin of 2.0% on average across general reasoning benchmarks (e.g., PIQA, ARC_C, ARC_E, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, SIQA, MMLU, OBQA, BoolQ, RACE, Lambda, TruthfulQA). This demonstrates the effectiveness of CLIMB's data mixture in enhancing model capabilities at larger scales.

#### 4.3. Optimization for Specific Domains

- **MMLU Domain Adaptation:** CLIMB effectively optimizes data mixtures for specific domains, as shown by experiments on MMLU benchmarks (STEM, Humanities, Social Sciences).
- **Significant Improvements:** CLIMB consistently improves performance across iterations, outperforming both Random sampling and CLIMB-Best@N (a baseline that directly searches for best parameters on randomly sampled configurations using the target model). For example, in the 1B model, CLIMB-iter3 achieves a Social Sciences accuracy of 41.79%, surpassing CLIMB-Best@N by 1.13% and showing a significant improvement from CLIMB-iter1 (40.18%). This highlights the potential for developing highly specialized models without manual curation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Search Compute Budget:** Increasing the total search compute budget (e.g., 150% or 200% more searches) leads to further performance gains, indicating that more exhaustive data mixture optimization is beneficial when resources are available.
- **Compute Allocation:** An allocation ratio of 4:2:1 across three iterations (e.g., 64, 32, 16 searches) yields the best performance, striking a balance between depth (iterations) and breadth (searches per iteration).
- **Proxy Model Effects:** Using larger proxy models (e.g., 350M vs. 62M) for the iterative search consistently, though marginally, improves the final target model's performance, suggesting stronger proxies provide more accurate guidance.
- **Cluster Analysis:** Analysis of final weights shows that a few specific clusters (e.g., C8, C9, C18, C19 for general reasoning) account for the majority of the weight in optimal mixtures. Both the relevance of cluster content to downstream tasks and the diversity among selected clusters are crucial.
- **Robustness:** CLIMB is robust to the initial number of clusters (`K_init`), initial mixture weight schemes (Random vs. Dirichlet), and the choice of proxy model (62M proxy model still performs strongly). The predictor achieves a high Spearman rank correlation (94%) with ground truth performance, demonstrating its accuracy.

#### 4.5. New High-Quality Datasets

- **NEMOTRON-CLIMBLAB:** A filtered 1.2-trillion-token corpus, semantically reorganized into 20 distinct clusters, is introduced as a research playground for data mixing studies.
- **NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX:** A compact, high-quality 400-billion-token dataset, extracted using CLIMB's optimal mixture, is designed for efficient pre-training. A 1B model trained from scratch on NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX significantly outperforms models trained on other datasets (Nemotron-CC, SmolLM, DCLM-baseline, FineWeb-Edu) under the same token budget. This dataset exhibits a more balanced cluster distribution compared to continuous pre-training mixtures, which require a broader foundation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Nemotron-CLIMB framework represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model pre-training and data curation, offering several layers of significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Automated and Efficient Data Optimization

CLIMB's primary significance lies in its ability to **automate the discovery, evaluation, and refinement of data mixtures** for LLM pre-training. This automation critically reduces the reliance on labor-intensive, manual curation and predefined domain labels, which have been major bottlenecks in scaling and adapting LLMs. By leveraging unsupervised clustering and an iterative bootstrapping process with lightweight proxy models, CLIMB makes the optimization process much more computationally efficient and scalable, a crucial factor given the astronomical costs of LLM training.

#### 5.2. Enhanced LLM Performance and Generalization

The empirical results demonstrate that data mixtures optimized by CLIMB lead to **superior performance** for LLMs, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines and achieving better scaling trends. This indicates that intelligently balanced and domain-aware data compositions are key to unlocking higher model capabilities. The significant gains across various reasoning tasks and the ability to optimize for specific domains mean that CLIMB can contribute to the development of more robust generalist LLMs and highly specialized domain-expert models.

#### 5.3. Facilitating Domain Adaptation and Specialization

CLIMB's capacity to identify optimal mixtures for specific domains (e.g., STEM, Social Sciences) is particularly impactful. This enables researchers and developers to efficiently adapt LLMs for niche applications without extensive manual data engineering. Such domain adaptation is crucial for deploying LLMs in high-stakes fields like finance, healthcare, or scientific research, where accuracy and specialized knowledge are paramount.

#### 5.4. Contribution to Open Science and Reproducibility

The release of **NEMOTRON-CLIMBLAB** (a 1.2-trillion-token clustered corpus) and **NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX** (a 400-billion-token optimized dataset) is a substantial contribution to the research community. These datasets provide valuable resources for further studies in data mixture, quality, and curriculum learning, fostering reproducibility and accelerating progress in data-centric AI. The availability of structured, high-quality data derived from automated methods sets a new standard for dataset generation.

#### 5.5. Deeper Insights into Data Dynamics

The analysis of cluster weights and their evolution across iterations offers valuable **insights into what constitutes an optimal data mixture**. The findings suggest that both the relevance of content to downstream tasks and the diversity among different clusters are critical. This kind of data-driven understanding can guide future research into more principled data selection and mixing strategies, moving beyond heuristic-based approaches.

#### 5.6. Potential Societal Impacts

- **Positive Impacts:** The framework contributes to more efficient and scalable LLM pre-training, potentially lowering the computational barrier for developing advanced AI models. This could democratize access to powerful LLM technologies and accelerate research in various domains.
- **Negative Impacts and Mitigations:** The paper acknowledges that optimizing data mixtures might lead to an overrepresentation of certain domains and underrepresentation of others, potentially exacerbating existing biases or creating new knowledge disparities. While CLIMB allows for more controlled data composition, safeguards are crucial to ensure fairness and mitigate unintended biases in domain representation. The automation of domain-aware data selection without explicit human annotation also raises concerns about unintended biases embedded in automatically clustered data. Future work must focus on ensuring transparency and interpretability in data selection processes and developing mechanisms to audit and rectify potential biases in the automatically generated datasets.

In conclusion, Nemotron-CLIMB pushes the boundaries of efficient and effective LLM pre-training by automating a critical and previously laborious step. Its methodological innovations and concrete contributions of high-quality datasets are poised to have a lasting impact on how large language models are built and refined for diverse applications.
