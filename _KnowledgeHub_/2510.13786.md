# The Art of Scaling Reinforcement Learning Compute for LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced a predictive framework for Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Large Language Models (LLMs) using a sigmoidal compute-performance curve, enabling performance extrapolation from smaller runs. Their ScaleRL recipe, demonstrated over 100,000 GPU-hours, achieves an asymptotic reward of 0.61 on verifiable math problems, outperforming established methods while exhibiting predictable scaling across model size, generation length, and multi-task settings.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) training for Large Language Models (LLMs) lacked a principled, generalizable framework to understand and predict performance at scale, unlike pre-training.
- The current approach to RL scaling relied on "ad-hoc solutions" from isolated studies, hindering the identification of truly scalable methods.
- Prohibitive compute costs made extensive large-scale experimentation impractical for most researchers to test new RL algorithms efficiently.

## Method

- A novel predictive scaling framework was introduced, employing a ==sigmoidal compute-performance== curve to model and extrapolate RL performance with increasing computational resources.
- A comprehensive, multi-stage empirical study was conducted, utilizing over 400,000 GPU-hours, to systematically evaluate the impact of various RL design choices on asymptotic performance and compute efficiency.
- The most effective design choices were integrated into a unified, predictable, and scalable RL recipe named ScaleRL.

## Results

- The proposed sigmoidal compute-performance curves effectively predicted RL performance, validated by ScaleRL's 100,000 GPU-hour run where extrapolations from early training accurately matched final outcomes.
- ScaleRL achieved a higher asymptotic reward (A=**0.61**) and improved compute efficiency on verifiable math problems, outperforming established methods like DeepSeek (GRPO) and Qwen-2.5 (DAPO).
- The ScaleRL recipe demonstrated stable and predictable scaling behavior across diverse compute dimensions, including larger model sizes (**17B**×16 MoE), extended generation lengths (32k tokens), and multi-task settings (math + code).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymptotic performance (A) is not universal for RL in LLMs, but is strongly influenced by loss function choice (CISPO/GSPO achieving higher asymptotes) and numerical precision (FP32 for LM logits being critical).
> - Many RL design choices primarily enhance compute efficiency (B) or shift the midpoint (C_mid) of the scaling curve, rather than increasing the absolute asymptotic performance (A).
> - The ability to extrapolate performance using the sigmoidal curve from lower-compute runs is essential for distinguishing genuinely scalable methods from those that only appear effective at smaller scales.
