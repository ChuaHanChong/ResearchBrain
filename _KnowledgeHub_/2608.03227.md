# PFM-HR: Pose Flow Matching for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> PFM-HR introduces a novel, reusable motion prior for physics-based humanoid control that leverages Flow Matching to learn local pose geometry from large-scale, unordered pose data. By integrating a 'Pose Geometry Score' (PGS) into the reinforcement learning reward, the system guides policy optimization toward plausible joint transitions, improving sample efficiency and tracking accuracy, particularly for dynamic humanoid motions.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning policies often struggle to discover complex joint dependencies for humanlike humanoid motion without explicit guidance.
- Existing data-driven motion priors are frequently coupled with policy training, limiting their modularity, reusability, and scaling to diverse tasks.
- Efficient pose-wise priors lack explicit evaluation of joint co-variation during transitions, while temporal priors require ordered motion data and are computationally intensive during online inference.

## Method

- A **PFM-HR** ==Flow Matching== model is pretrained on large, unordered humanoid pose datasets to learn the marginal pose distribution, capturing local pose geometry.
- A ==Pose Geometry Score (PGS)== is derived from the Jacobian of the pretrained Flow Matching denoiser, which measures the alignment of policy-induced joint changes with the prior's learned co-variation patterns.
- The PGS is calibrated against reference motion percentiles and used to modulate the ==reinforcement learning== tracking reward, attenuating it for implausible pose transitions to guide policy exploration.

## Results

- PFM-HR improved sample efficiency on **six** dynamic MimicKit tasks, enabling convergence for Backflip and Double Kong where vanilla ADD failed and converging faster than PDF-HR on these challenging motions.
- In real-world deployment on a physical humanoid robot, the method reduced simulation samples needed to achieve an **80%** success rate by **15.1%** to **30.8%** for dynamic skills compared to a baseline approach.
- For general motion tracking, PFM-HR reduced average position error by **7.6%** and rotational error by **3.6%** compared to vanilla ADD across various episode lengths, demonstrating superior accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An unconditional Flow Matching denoiser, trained on marginal pose samples, implicitly encodes the local geometry of pose variation, with its Jacobian proportional to the conditional covariance of clean poses.
> - Leveraging the denoiser's Jacobian allows for an efficient, geometry-aware score (PGS) to quantify the plausibility of joint-coordinate changes without requiring explicit temporal supervision or pose-distance fields.
> - Modulating the tracking reward based on the percentile of the online PGS relative to reference motion provides an effective, data-driven mechanism to bias RL policies towards structured and coordinated humanoid movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are increasingly being developed for operation in human-centric environments, requiring capabilities to navigate and interact in spaces designed for humans. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for enabling advanced humanoid locomotion and control. However, achieving coordinated, humanlike motion often goes beyond dynamic feasibility, necessitating guidance on complex joint dependencies. Policies trained with standard task and tracking rewards often struggle to discover these dependencies through exploration alone, especially for highly dynamic movements.

Data-driven motion priors offer a solution by guiding policy optimization towards behaviors observed in demonstrations. Early approaches, such as DeepMimic, track reference trajectories to reproduce complex skills. Adversarial methods, like Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) and Adversarial Differential Discriminators (ADD), learn discriminator-based rewards to promote realistic motion and reduce manual reward engineering. While effective, these discriminator-based priors are typically trained concurrently with the control policy, which limits their modularity and reuse across different tasks. Scaling these methods necessitates extensive policy training and large motion datasets.

More recent research has explored frozen, reusable priors to enhance modularity. Pose Distance Fields for Humanoid Robots (PDF-HR) learns a pose-distance field from retargeted humanoid data to improve downstream tracking. However, PDF-HR requires precomputed pose-distance supervision and evaluates each pose independently, without explicitly considering the relative joint changes that produced it. Score-matching Motion Priors (SMP) repurposes a pretrained motion diffusion model as an RL reward through score distillation, thereby capturing temporal motion structure. This approach, however, requires ordered motion clips for training and incurs repeated, potentially computationally intensive, score-model evaluations during online RL.

This background highlights a gap: current efficient pose-wise priors do not adequately evaluate how joints co-vary during transitions, while temporal priors demand ordered motion data and heavier online inference. The PFM-HR research addresses this by developing a reusable prior that can be trained on large-scale unordered pose data, offering guidance on joint co-variation without the need for temporal segmentation or explicit distance supervision, and remaining computationally efficient during policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance physics-based humanoid motion tracking by introducing a novel, reusable motion prior that overcomes limitations of existing data-driven approaches. The authors aim to improve both the sample efficiency and tracking accuracy of RL policies, particularly for dynamic and complex humanoid movements.

The motivation for PFM-HR stems from several challenges in the current landscape of data-driven motion priors for humanoid control:
1. **Guidance on Joint Dependencies:** RL policies, even with task and tracking rewards, often receive limited explicit guidance on the coordinated variation of joints required for humanlike motion. This deficiency makes learning dynamic motions particularly difficult in high-dimensional control spaces.
2. **Modularity and Reusability of Priors:** Many effective priors, especially adversarial ones, are trained jointly with the control policy. This tight coupling limits their reuse across different tracking tasks or robot morphologies, requiring retraining for each new application.
3. **Data Requirements and Efficiency Trade-offs:**
 - Pose-wise priors (e.g., PDF-HR) offer reusability but often necessitate labor-intensive precomputation of distance supervision and score individual poses without considering the continuity or geometric plausibility of pose transitions. They lack an explicit mechanism to evaluate how joint coordinates change together.
 - Temporal priors (e.g., SMP) can capture motion dynamics but require ordered motion clips for training, which can be restrictive for large, diverse datasets where temporal labels might not always be available or desired. Furthermore, their online evaluation can be computationally expensive due to repeated score model queries during RL.

To address these motivations, the authors propose PFM-HR, a Flow Matching prior. The core motivation behind using Flow Matching is its ability to learn from large-scale, unordered collections of individual poses, avoiding the need for temporally segmented data or explicit pose-distance supervision. The key insight underpinning PFM-HR is that a denoiser model, even when trained only on marginal pose samples (i.e., individual, unordered poses), implicitly captures the local geometry of pose variation. Specifically, under the Gaussian Flow Matching path, the Jacobian of the population-optimal denoising map is proportional to the conditional covariance of clean poses. This mathematical property implies that the denoiser's directional response reflects conditional modes of joint co-variation.

This insight motivated the development of the Pose Geometry Score (PGS). The PGS is designed to quantify how the policy-induced joint coordinate changes during rollouts align with the local pose geometry encoded by the Flow Matching prior. By using PGS to modulate the tracking reward, the system can guide policy exploration towards structured and plausible pose transitions, effectively providing geometry-aware guidance. The prior is trained independently and remains frozen during downstream RL, promoting modularity and reusability across diverse tracking tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PFM-HR methodology integrates a pretrained Flow Matching pose prior into a reinforcement learning framework for humanoid motion tracking. The approach consists of a pose representation, a Flow Matching prior learning phase, the formulation of a Pose Geometry Score (PGS), and a score calibration mechanism for reward modulation.

**A. Robot Pose Representation:**
A humanoid pose at control step *k* is represented by its *N_J* actuated joint coordinates, *q_k* ∈ *Q* ⊂ **R***N_J*. These joint coordinates are normalized for input to the pose prior. The change in pose during a rollout is characterized by the finite-difference joint-coordinate velocity, *ξ_env,k* = (*q_k* - *q_k-1*) / *Δt*, where *Δt* is the control interval. This velocity vector captures the pattern of joint-coordinate changes induced by the policy. A normalized version, *d_k* = *ξ_env,k* / (||*ξ_env,k*||₂² + *ε_num*), is used to retain relative changes while reducing sensitivity to overall magnitude.

**B. RL-based Motion Tracking:**
The humanoid motion tracking is formulated as a finite-horizon discrete-time Markov Decision Process (MDP). An RL policy *π_θ* is trained to maximize a cumulative reward *J(π_θ)*. The reward *r_k* typically combines a task progress term (*r_G,k*) and a reference motion agreement term (*r_T,k*), weighted by scalars *w_G* and *w_T*. PFM-HR augments this reward with the guidance from the frozen Flow Matching pose prior.

**C. Pose Prior Learning with Flow Matching:**
An unconditional Flow Matching model is pretrained on a large-scale corpus of individual, unordered humanoid poses, learning the marginal pose distribution *p_data*. The training process utilizes a linear Flow Matching path: *z_t* = *t*x + (1-*t*)ϵ, where *x* is a clean pose from *p_data*, *ϵ* is an independent Gaussian sample, and *t* is sampled from a logit-normal distribution. This path interpolates between a Gaussian source at *t*=0 and the pose distribution at *t*=1. The conditional velocity is *v* = x - ϵ.
The velocity field is parameterized by a clean-pose predictor, *ˆx_ϕ(z_t, t)*. The predicted velocity is *v_ϕ(z_t, t)* = (*ˆx_ϕ(z_t, t)* - *z_t*) / max(1-*t*, *δ*). The model is optimized using the Flow Matching objective: *L_FM(ϕ)* = E*t,x,ϵ*[||*v* - *v_ϕ(z_t, t)*||₂²]. For *t* ≤ 1-*δ*, minimizing this objective is equivalent to clean pose regression, where the population optimal predictor is the conditional mean *F*_t*(z)* = E[x | *z_t* = *z*]. The learned approximation *F_ϕ(z, t)* is frozen during subsequent policy learning.

**D. Pose Geometry Score (PGS):**
The PGS is designed to measure the alignment between policy-induced joint changes and the conditional co-variation structure encoded by the prior.
The key property utilized is that the Jacobian of the population-optimal denoiser, *J*_t*(z)* = ∇*z* *F*_t*(z)*, is proportional to the conditional covariance *C_t(z)* = Cov[x | *z_t* = *z*]. This means the off-diagonal entries of the Jacobian describe conditional co-variation between joint coordinates.
During policy rollout, at step *k*, a query *e_q_k-1* = *t_eval* *q_k-1* + (1-*t_eval*)ϵ_k is constructed, where *t_eval* ≤ 1-*δ* is a fixed evaluation timestep and *ϵ_k* is a Gaussian sample. The learned Jacobian *J_ϕ,k* = *J_ϕ(e_q_k-1, t_eval)* is then applied to the normalized joint-coordinate change pattern *d_k*.
The Pose Geometry Score (PGS) is defined as *s_PGS,k* = ||*J_ϕ,k* *d_k*||₂². A high PGS indicates that the relative joint changes align with co-variation modes strongly represented in the pose prior. This score is computed efficiently via a Jacobian-vector product, avoiding explicit construction of the full Jacobian matrix.

**E. Score Calibration and Reward Modulation:**
1. **Evaluation-Timestep Selection:** The choice of *t_eval* is critical. Experiments showed that intermediate timesteps (e.g., *t_eval*=0.75) provided the best balance of fast convergence and low position errors in general motion tracking. Strong corruption (small *t*) reduces pose specificity, while near *t*=1, the denoiser loses discriminative power.
2. **Reference-Calibrated Reward Modulation:** To incorporate PGS into the reward, the score is calibrated against the reference motion. Before policy training, PGS values are computed for all transitions in the reference motion *R_τ*, forming an empirical Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) *bF_τ*. During policy rollout, the online *s_PGS,k* is mapped to its reference percentile *u_τ,k* = *bF_τ(s_PGS,k)*. This percentile is then converted into a reward multiplier *ρ_τ,k* using a clipped exponential function: *ρ_τ,k* = clip(((p_good - *u_τ,k*) / (p_good - p_bad)), 0, 1), and *r_P,k* = exp(-*α* *ρ_τ,k*), where *p_good*=0.05, *p_bad*=0.01, and *α*=0.5.
The final reward becomes *r_k* = *w_G* *r_G,k* + *w_T* *r_T,k* *r_P,k*. This mechanism ensures that the tracking reward is unchanged for highly plausible transitions (above *p_good*) and maximally attenuated for weakly supported patterns (below *p_bad*), biasing policy optimization towards coordinated humanoid poses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments compared PFM-HR against vanilla ADD and ADD augmented with PDF-HR, using an ADD-based motion tracking backbone. The priors were pretrained on the BONES-SEED pose corpus.

**A. Single-trajectory Motion Tracking:**
On nine MimicKit tasks, PFM-HR demonstrated improved performance, particularly on dynamic motions.
- **Sample Efficiency:** PFM-HR achieved the best sample efficiency (fewer samples to reach ≥80% success rate) on six tasks, including Jump, Cartwheel, Backflip, Sideflip, Speed Vault, and Double Kong. Notably, PFM-HR enabled convergence for Backflip and Double Kong, where vanilla ADD failed, and converged faster than PDF-HR on these challenging tasks. PDF-HR showed better efficiency on Walk, Run, and Spinkick.
- **Tracking Accuracy:** PFM-HR attained the lowest or tied-lowest positional error on eight out of nine tasks, with margins being generally small. It also achieved the lowest rotational error on six tasks (Jump, Spinkick, Cartwheel, Backflip, Sideflip, Double Kong) and tied on one (SpeedVault).
- **Overall:** PFM-HR provided a balance between convergence speed and tracking accuracy, especially for highly dynamic motions, by guiding learning towards plausible joint-coordinate transitions.

**B. Real-world Deployment:**
PFM-HR was integrated into the BeyondMimic training and deployment pipeline, serving solely as a frozen prior during simulation training.
- For four dynamic skills (spinkick, kick combo, cartwheel, backflip), PFM-HR consistently reduced the number of simulation samples required to reach a success rate of 80% (SR ≥ 80%). Reductions ranged from 15.1% to 30.8% compared to the original BeyondMimic method.
- Successful executions on a physical humanoid robot demonstrated PFM-HR's compatibility with real-world deployment without requiring additional computation during deployment.

**C. General Motion Tracking:**
For general motion tracking, a single policy was trained on a subset of LaFAN1 and evaluated with 10s, 20s, and 30s tracking horizons.
- PFM-HR achieved the lowest position and rotation errors across all three episode lengths.
- Averaged across horizons, it reduced position error by 7.6% over vanilla ADD and 10.3% over PDF-HR. Rotational error was reduced by 3.6% over ADD and 7.3% over PDF-HR.
- PDF-HR's performance was comparable to or, in some cases, worse than vanilla ADD in this setting.

**D. Ablation Study:**
- **Impact of Prior-Data Scale and Coverage:** Training PFM-HR on larger pose corpora (e.g., 60 million poses from BONES-SEED) generally improved tracking performance across locomotion and acrobatic motions. Efficient expansion from a 30M to 60M prior required 65 GPU-hours, significantly less than training from scratch (100 GPU-hours) or generating pose-distance supervision for PDF-HR (>500 GPU-hours).
- **Reward Formulation Comparison:** PFM-HR was compared to FM-Recon (a variant using the same denoiser for pose reconstruction-based scoring). PFM-HR showed larger improvements on dynamic motions. Computationally, one Jacobian-vector product for PFM-HR (0.75 ms) was more efficient than three denoiser evaluations for FM-Recon (1.8 ms).
- **Prediction Parameterization Comparison:** Comparing v-prediction and x-prediction for the Flow Matching model revealed similar performance on basic locomotion. However, x-prediction, used in PFM-HR, performed better on dynamic motions (e.g., Backflip), suggesting it provides a more effective Jacobian response for finite-capacity models.

**E. Robustness of Online PGS Evaluation (Appendix B):**
- **Evaluation Timestep:** An evaluation timestep of *t_eval* = 0.75 was found to provide the strongest structural discriminability for PGS, consistently distinguishing coordinated joint changes from perturbed ones.
- **Stability under Stochastic Corruption:** The single-sample online PGS estimate demonstrated high consistency. It achieved a Spearman rank correlation of 0.976 and a calibration-region agreement of 97.9% with a high-accuracy 128-sample reference, while being significantly more efficient (0.75 ms per evaluation step).
- **Robustness to Pose-Change Perturbations:** PGS was found to be largely invariant to the overall magnitude of a joint-coordinate change, but highly sensitive to disruptions in the inter-joint coordination pattern (e.g., angular perturbations, joint permutations, independent sign flips).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PFM-HR research presents a significant advancement in data-driven motion priors for humanoid robot control, addressing several key challenges in the field.

**Significance:**
- **Novelty in Prior Learning:** PFM-HR introduces a scalable and reusable Flow Matching pose prior trained directly on large-scale, unordered pose data. This circumvents the need for explicit pose-distance supervision or labor-intensive temporal segmentation of motion clips, which are limitations of previous methods.
- **Geometry-Aware Guidance:** The development of the Pose Geometry Score (PGS) is a central contribution. By leveraging the conditional covariance interpretation of the denoiser's Jacobian, PFM-HR can assess the alignment of policy-induced pose changes with the local joint co-variation patterns learned by the prior. This provides richer, more nuanced guidance than simple pose plausibility scores.
- **Efficiency and Reusability:** The prior is pretrained independently and remains frozen during downstream RL, fostering modularity. The PGS is computed efficiently via a Jacobian-vector product, requiring minimal online computational overhead during policy learning and real-world deployment. This efficiency and reusability across diverse tracking tasks are critical for practical applications.
- **Improved Performance for Dynamic Motions:** PFM-HR demonstrably improves sample efficiency and tracking accuracy, particularly for complex, highly dynamic, and acrobatic motions where existing methods either struggle or fail to converge. This suggests the geometry-aware guidance is especially beneficial in high-dimensional and challenging control scenarios.
- **Compatibility with Real-World Systems:** The successful integration with the BeyondMimic pipeline and deployment on a physical humanoid robot validates its practical applicability without requiring modifications to the deployment strategy.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated RL for Humanoids:** By providing a strong, geometry-aware learning signal, PFM-HR can significantly reduce the amount of simulation data and training time required to teach humanoid robots complex and humanlike skills. This can lead to faster development cycles for humanoid capabilities.
- **Broader Adoption of Humanoid Robots:** More robust and versatile humanoid control, particularly for dynamic interactions, could expand the domains where these robots can operate, from logistics and assistance to entertainment and dangerous environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes that local geometry learned from unordered poses can offer effective control guidance, even without explicit temporal supervision. This opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated sign-sensitive scores, temporally conditioned Flow Matching priors, or combining this approach with other temporal modeling techniques, while retaining the benefits of efficiency and reusability.
- **Reduced Engineering Effort:** By providing an automated, data-driven reward modulation mechanism, PFM-HR can potentially reduce the extensive manual reward engineering often required to achieve high-quality humanoid motion, democratizing access to advanced control techniques.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as acknowledged by the authors):**
- PFM-HR, by design, captures local co-variation in the marginal pose distribution rather than explicit temporal dynamics. Therefore, it cannot assess the likelihood, direction, or ordering of temporal transitions. For instance, opposite transitions may receive similar geometric evaluations.
- The reliability of PGS is dependent on the coverage of the training pose dataset; guidance may be less informative for poses far from the training distribution.
Future work is planned to explore sign-sensitive scores and temporally conditioned priors to address these limitations while aiming to preserve the efficiency and reusability demonstrated by PFM-HR.
