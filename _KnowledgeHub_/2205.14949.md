# HiViT: Hierarchical Vision Transformer Meets Masked Image Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> HiViT is a hierarchical Vision Transformer architecture designed for efficient Masked Image Modeling pre-training. It removes computationally intensive local operations in early stages, enabling a 1.9x speed-up over Swin Transformer during MIM while achieving 84.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K.

## Problem

- Existing hierarchical Vision Transformers (ViTs) like Swin Transformer face computational inefficiencies when applied to Masked Image Modeling (MIM) due to complex structural operations.
- Their "local inter-unit operations" (e.g., shifting window attention, patch merging) prevent the efficient discarding of masked tokens, leading to higher computational costs during MIM pre-training.
- The challenge is to design a hierarchical ViT that retains the benefits of multi-resolution feature maps while enabling efficient MIM pre-training by addressing these computational bottlenecks.

## Method

- HiViT introduces a modified ==hierarchical Vision Transformer== architecture that minimizes or removes "local inter-unit operations," which are inefficient for ==Masked Image Modeling (MIM)== pre-training.
- Early stages of the network replace ==window attention== with simple MLP blocks and use linear merging operations for downsampling.
- The architecture consolidates stages, removing the fourth stage found in typical hierarchical ViTs and extending the third (main) stage to apply ==global attention== across tokens.

## Results

- HiViT-B achieves **83.8%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K in supervised learning and **84.2%** when pre-trained with MIM, outperforming ViT-B by **2.0%** and Swin-B by **0.3%**.
- It demonstrates a **1.**9x** speed-up** over Swin Transformer during Masked Image Modeling pre-training, indicating substantial computational savings without performance loss.
- HiViT shows strong transfer learning capabilities, achieving **5**1.2%** APbox** and **4**4.2%** APmask** on COCO for object detection and **5**1.2%** mIoU** on ADE20K for semantic segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Local inter-unit operations like window attention in hierarchical Vision Transformers create a computational bottleneck for efficient Masked Image Modeling (MIM).
> - These computationally intensive local operations can be replaced with simpler MLP blocks and linear merging operations without sacrificing performance, while significantly improving MIM pre-training efficiency.
> - The hierarchical structure itself is more important for performance than the specific local inter-unit operations, and consolidating network stages can improve both efficiency and accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as powerful alternatives to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for computer vision tasks. Among these, two distinct design paradigms have evolved: plain ViTs that process image patches at a uniform resolution, and hierarchical ViTs that use multi-resolution feature maps for improved representation learning. Simultaneously, self-supervised learning through Masked Image Modeling (MIM) has become a dominant pre-training approach for vision models.

However, existing hierarchical ViTs face computational challenges when applied to MIM due to their complex structural operations. HiViT (Hierarchical Vision Transformer) addresses this challenge by presenting a novel architecture that combines the benefits of hierarchical design with the computational efficiency required for effective MIM pre-training.

### 2. Background and Motivation

Vision Transformers process images by splitting them into patches and applying self-attention mechanisms. While plain ViTs like the original ViT model use a uniform resolution throughout the network, hierarchical ViTs (such as Swin Transformer) progressively reduce spatial dimensions while increasing feature dimensions, similar to CNNs. This hierarchical approach has shown advantages in capturing multi-scale visual information.

Masked Image Modeling has emerged as a powerful self-supervised pre-training technique, where portions of an image are masked, and the model is trained to reconstruct the missing content. For plain ViTs, MIM is computationally efficient because masked tokens can be discarded early in the process. However, hierarchical ViTs cannot easily discard masked tokens due to their "local inter-unit operations" (like shifting window attention and patch merging), resulting in computational inefficiency.

The key motivation for HiViT is to design a hierarchical architecture that maintains the representational advantages of multi-resolution feature maps while enabling efficient MIM pre-training by addressing the computational bottleneck of local inter-unit operations.

### 3. HiViT Architecture

HiViT introduces a strategically modified hierarchical architecture based on three key observations about operations in vision transformers:

1. **Intra-unit operations**: Functions like self-attention and MLPs that process each token independently.
2. **Global inter-unit operations**: Functions that connect all tokens but can be computed on visible tokens only during MIM.
3. **Local inter-unit operations**: Functions like window attention and patch merging that create dependencies between neighboring tokens, preventing efficient masking.

The core insight of HiViT is that removing or minimizing local inter-unit operations can maintain performance while enabling efficient MIM. The architecture consists of three stages:

1. **Early stages (1-2)**: Replace window attention with simple MLP blocks, retaining the downsampling functionality through linear merging operations.
2. **Main stage (3)**: Apply global attention across all tokens using vanilla transformer blocks.
3. **Stage consolidation**: Remove the fourth stage typical in Swin Transformer and extend the third stage, creating a more balanced computational distribution.

This design allows HiViT to:
- Maintain a hierarchical structure with multi-resolution features
- Enable efficient MIM by eliminating the operations that prevent token dropping
- Preserve performance in both supervised and self-supervised learning settings

### 4. Masked Image Modeling with HiViT

For MIM pre-training, HiViT follows a similar approach to SimMIM:

1. Randomly mask a portion (typically 60%) of input image patches.
2. Pass only the visible patches through the encoder (HiViT).
3. Predict pixel values for the masked regions using a lightweight decoder.
4. Optimize using a reconstruction loss (mean squared error) between the predicted and original pixel values.

The key advantage of HiViT over other hierarchical models is computational efficiency. By removing local inter-unit operations, HiViT can discard masked tokens early in the network, similar to plain ViTs. This results in substantial computational savings—achieving a 1.9x speed-up compared to Swin Transformer during MIM pre-training.

The mathematical formulation for the MIM objective can be expressed as:

$$L_{MIM} = \frac{1}{|M|} \sum_{i \in M} \|D(E(x_{\bar{M}}))_i - x_i\|^2$$

Where $M$ represents the set of masked patches, $x_{\bar{M}}$ denotes the visible patches, $E$ is the HiViT encoder, and $D$ is the lightweight decoder.

### 5. Performance Analysis

HiViT demonstrates impressive performance across multiple benchmarks and tasks:

**Image Classification on ImageNet-1K:**
- HiViT-B achieves 83.8% top-1 accuracy in supervised learning, outperforming ViT-B by 2.0% and Swin-B by 0.3%.
- When pre-trained with MIM for 800 epochs and fine-tuned for 100 epochs, HiViT-B reaches 84.2% accuracy, showing the effectiveness of the self-supervised approach.

**Computational Efficiency:**
- HiViT achieves a 1.9x speed-up over Swin Transformer during MIM pre-training.
- The computational savings come without sacrificing model performance, demonstrating that local inter-unit operations can be removed without harming effectiveness.

**Scaling Properties:**
- HiViT scales well to larger models, with HiViT-L achieving 84.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K.
- The performance gap between HiViT and other models tends to increase with model size, suggesting better scaling properties.

### 6. Ablation Studies

The researchers conducted extensive ablation studies to validate their design choices:

**Effect of Removing Local Inter-unit Operations:**
- Replacing window attention with MLPs in early stages had a minimal impact on accuracy while significantly improving computational efficiency.
- The hierarchical structure itself was found to be more important for performance than the specific local inter-unit operations.

**Stage Configuration:**
- Merging the fourth stage into the third stage improved both efficiency and accuracy.
- Extending the main stage to 20 transformer blocks provided a good balance between early feature processing and later semantic understanding.

**Relative Positional Encoding:**
- Adding relative positional encoding to the main stage improved performance, suggesting the importance of spatial information for hierarchical models.

These findings validate the core hypothesis that local inter-unit operations can be replaced with simpler alternatives while maintaining the advantages of hierarchical structures.

### 7. Applications and Transfer Learning

Beyond image classification, HiViT demonstrates strong transfer learning capabilities:

**Object Detection on COCO:**
- HiViT-B achieves 51.2% APbox and 44.2% APmask, outperforming comparable ViT and Swin models.
- When pre-trained with MIM, HiViT shows further improvements, highlighting the value of self-supervised pre-training for downstream tasks.

**Semantic Segmentation on ADE20K:**
- HiViT achieves 51.2% mIoU, demonstrating effective transfer to dense prediction tasks.
- The multi-resolution features of the hierarchical architecture prove beneficial for segmentation tasks that require fine-grained spatial information.

These results indicate that HiViT serves as an effective backbone for various computer vision tasks, balancing efficiency and performance.

### 8. Conclusion

HiViT represents a significant advance in vision transformer design by addressing the computational inefficiency of hierarchical ViTs in masked image modeling. By identifying and removing the local inter-unit operations that prevent efficient masking, HiViT maintains the benefits of hierarchical structure while enabling efficient self-supervised learning.

The architecture achieves competitive or superior performance compared to both plain and hierarchical ViTs across a range of tasks, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. Importantly, it does so with improved computational efficiency during MIM pre-training, making it a practical option for large-scale self-supervised learning.

The insights from HiViT may influence future vision transformer designs, suggesting that careful architectural choices can maintain performance while improving efficiency. As self-supervised learning continues to grow in importance, models like HiViT that are specifically designed for efficient MIM will likely play an increasingly important role in computer vision research and applications.
