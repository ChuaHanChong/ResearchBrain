# Demonstration-Efficient Guided Policy Search via Imitation of Robust Tube MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a method for training demonstration-efficient and robust deep neural network policies by imitating a Robust Tube Model Predictive Control (RTMPC) expert. It introduces a Sampling Augmentation strategy that leverages the RTMPC's invariant tube to generate robust training data, enabling zero-shot transfer of policies from a nominal simulation environment to a real-world quadrotor subjected to unseen wind disturbances with a single demonstration.

## Problem

- Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers high performance but is computationally too expensive for real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- Traditional Imitation Learning (IL) methods are often demonstration-inefficient, requiring large datasets for robust policy learning, which is impractical for physical robots.
- Learned policies lack robustness to unmodeled disturbances and model mismatches (sim2real/lab2real gaps), failing to perform reliably in real-world, perturbed environments.

## Method

- A Robust Tube ==Model Predictive Control== (RT**MPC**) serves as the expert controller, designed to explicitly account for bounded additive uncertainties by keeping the system state within a predefined invariant 'tube'.
- A novel Sampling Augmentation (SA) strategy is introduced, which samples additional state-action pairs from within the RTMPC's invariant tube, centered around nominal trajectories.
- A compact Deep Neural Network (DNN) policy is trained via Behavior Cloning or DAgger on these augmented datasets, compressing the robust expert behavior into an efficient, deployable form.

## Results

- The Sampling Augmentation (SA) strategy achieved **100%** success rate in disturbed environments using only a single demonstration, representing up to a **6x** improvement in demonstration efficiency over baselines.
- Policies trained with SA demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to a physical quadrotor, successfully tracking trajectories and maintaining hover under unseen wind disturbances.
- The learned DNN policy provided a two-orders-of-magnitude reduction in latency (0.25ms on GPU, 1.66ms on TX2) compared to the expert RTMPC (55.3ms), enabling real-time onboard execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The robust control invariant set (tube) computed by Robust Tube Model Predictive Control (RTMPC) provides an effective model for the states a system might visit under bounded disturbances.
> - Leveraging this tube allows for a novel Sampling Augmentation (SA) strategy that generates robust training data without requiring physical application of disturbances during demonstration collection.
> - This principled data augmentation addresses covariate shift, enabling policies to generalize from nominal training environments to disturbed real-world conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of advanced control theory and machine learning for robotics: enabling computationally expensive, high-performance controllers to operate efficiently and robustly on real-world systems.

**Model Predictive Control (MPC)** is widely recognized for its ability to handle complex system dynamics, constraints, and optimization objectives, leading to impressive performance in agile robotic applications. However, its significant computational demands often hinder real-time deployment on resource-constrained onboard systems.

To overcome MPC's computational bottleneck, a common approach has emerged: **policy compression** using machine learning, particularly **Imitation Learning (IL)** or **Guided Policy Search (GPS)**. These methods involve training a computationally efficient representation, typically a Deep Neural Network (DNN), to mimic the behavior of a high-fidelity expert controller (like MPC) by observing its demonstrations. This allows the learned DNN policy to execute complex behaviors much faster than the original MPC.

However, existing IL methods face several significant limitations, which this work directly aims to mitigate:
- **Demonstration Inefficiency:** Traditional IL methods, such as Behavior Cloning (BC) or even Dataset Aggregation (DAgger), typically require a large number of demonstrations to achieve satisfactory performance. This makes training on real robots impractical due to the time, cost, and potential safety risks involved in collecting extensive real-world data. It often necessitates reliance on high-fidelity simulations, which themselves pose challenges.
- **Covariate Shift and Robustness:** A major hurdle in transferring policies from a training environment (e.g., simulation or a controlled lab setting) to a real-world deployment environment is the **sim2real gap** or **lab2real gap**. This gap arises from unmodeled disturbances, sensor noise, model mismatches, and dynamic changes in the real world that were not present or fully accounted for during training. When the learned policy encounters states outside its training distribution, performance degrades, often catastrophically.
 - **Domain Randomization (DR)** is a popular technique to address the sim2real gap by introducing variability (randomizing parameters, applying disturbances) during training to make the policy more robust to variations in the deployment environment. However, DR requires the ability to apply these disturbances during training, which can be unpractical or unsafe in physical lab environments. It also typically demands even more demonstrations to cover the expanded state space.
 - **DAgger** helps mitigate compounding errors and covariate shifts by iteratively retraining the policy on states visited by the *learned* policy, but it still requires multiple expert interventions and demonstrations.

This paper's innovative contribution lies in its synthesis of **Robust Tube Model Predictive Control (RTMPC)** with IL. RTMPC is a powerful variant of MPC that explicitly accounts for bounded disturbances by ensuring the system's state remains within a "tube" around a nominal trajectory. The key insight is to leverage the robust control invariant set (the "tube") computed by the RTMPC. This tube inherently provides information about the states the system might visit when subjected to disturbances.

By using this information to inform a novel **data augmentation strategy (Sampling Augmentation - SA)**, the authors propose a method that achieves:
- **High demonstration efficiency:** Significantly reducing the number of required demonstrations, potentially to just one.
- **Enhanced robustness:** Enabling "zero-shot transfer" from a nominal training environment (e.g., simulation without disturbances) directly to a real-world environment with previously unseen disturbances or model errors.
- **Practicality:** Unlike DR, the SA approach does not require physically applying disturbances during training, making it more amenable to lab-to-real-world transfers.

The work differentiates itself from related efforts, such as the LAG-ROS framework, by emphasizing minimal requirements – namely, the tube and a data augmentation strategy – to achieve demonstration efficiency and robustness. This decoupling from complex nonlinear models simplifies controller design and reduces computational complexity. In essence, this research pushes the boundaries of deployable autonomy by making advanced control policies simultaneously efficient, robust, and easy to train.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a **demonstration-efficient strategy for compressing computationally expensive Model Predictive Controllers (MPC) into computationally efficient deep neural network (DNN) policies via Imitation Learning (IL).**

The primary motivations driving this work stem from critical limitations of existing MPC and IL approaches for real-world robotic deployment:

- **Computational Burden of MPC:** While MPC offers superior performance and constraint satisfaction, its high computational cost often precludes its direct, real-time execution on resource-constrained robotic platforms. This necessitates a more efficient representation, hence the drive for policy compression.

- **Demonstration Inefficiency in Imitation Learning:** Standard IL techniques (like Behavior Cloning or DAgger) typically demand a large volume of expert demonstrations to train a robust policy. This is highly problematic for physical robots, where collecting extensive real-world data is time-consuming, expensive, and potentially unsafe (e.g., if the expert policy makes errors or the robot crashes). The ideal scenario is to learn from as few demonstrations as possible.

- **Lack of Robustness to Distribution Shifts (Sim2Real/Lab2Real Gaps):** A learned policy trained in a simulated or controlled laboratory environment often performs poorly when deployed in the real world. This **covariate shift** arises from differences in dynamics, unmodeled disturbances (e.g., wind, unperceived obstacles), or sensor noise not present during training. Existing methods to address this, such as Domain Randomization (DR), require applying these disturbances during training, which might be impractical, difficult to implement accurately, or even unsafe on a real robot. The motivation is to achieve robustness without this requirement.

- **Enabling Zero-Shot Transfer:** A specific, ambitious goal is to enable **zero-shot transfer**. This means training a policy in a nominal, undisturbed environment (like a simple simulation or a lab without external perturbations) using minimal data, and then deploying it directly and robustly in a real-world environment characterized by bounded, previously unseen disturbances. This capability would drastically accelerate the development and deployment cycle for autonomous systems.

In summary, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between powerful but slow controllers and efficient but fragile learned policies. Their objective is to create a methodology that allows for the rapid, safe, and robust deployment of complex robotic behaviors by leveraging the inherent robustness properties of an advanced control technique (RTMPC) to significantly improve the data efficiency and generalization capabilities of imitation learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology revolves around **Robust Tube Model Predictive Control (RTMPC)** and a novel **Sampling Augmentation (SA)** strategy for data generation, which is then used to train a deep neural network (DNN) policy via Imitation Learning (IL).

#### 3.1. Expert Demonstrator: Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Robust Tube MPC (RTMPC)

The foundation of the expert behavior is a linear MPC designed for trajectory tracking of a system subject to bounded additive uncertainties.
- **Nominal MPC:** The MPC minimizes a quadratic cost function over a finite prediction horizon ($N$), subject to state and input constraints. It assumes nominal system dynamics (without disturbances) for its predictions.
- **Robust Tube MPC (RTMPC):** To introduce robustness, the expert MPC is extended to an RTMPC based on. The key concept of RTMPC is to ensure that the actual system state, even under bounded disturbances, remains within a predefined "tube" centered around a nominal, disturbance-free trajectory. This is achieved by:
 - **Ancillary Controller:** An additional feedback policy, $u_t = \check{u}^*_0(x_t) + K(x_t - \check{x}^*_0(x_t))$, where $\check{u}^*_0(x_t)$ and $\check{x}^*_0(x_t)$ are the optimal feedforward action and reference computed by the RTMPC, and $K$ is a stabilizing feedback gain (e.g., from an LQR problem). This controller actively counteracts perturbations to keep the system within the tube.
 - **Invariant Tube Set (Z):** A pre-computed, robust control invariant set $Z$ defines the shape and width of the tube. This set ensures that if the state is within the tube, it will remain within the tube for all possible bounded disturbances. Importantly, $Z$ represents the model of states the system *may visit* when subject to disturbances.
 - **Tightened Constraints:** The original MPC constraints are tightened by an amount that accounts for the maximum deviation within the tube, ensuring feasibility even under disturbances.

#### 3.2. Addressing Covariate Shift via Sampling Augmentation (SA)

The paper frames the demonstration-efficiency issue in IL as the need to compensate for covariate shifts between the training domain (S, e.g., simulation without disturbances) and the deployment domain (T, e.g., real world with disturbances).
- **Problem:** Standard IL (like Behavior Cloning) optimizes performance in the training domain (S), offering no guarantees in the target domain (T) due to distribution shifts. Domain Randomization (DR) attempts to bridge this by sampling disturbances during training, but it requires applying these disturbances, which is not always practical or safe.
- **SA Intuition:** The crucial insight is that the RTMPC's invariant tube $Z$ provides an approximation of the *support* of the state distribution that the system might encounter in the *perturbed target domain*.
- **SA Mechanism:** Instead of *applying* disturbances during training, the proposed SA strategy *generates additional state-action pairs* from within the robust tube during demonstration collection. For every state $x_t$ observed in a nominal demonstration collected in the source domain (S):
 1. The RTMPC computes its nominal optimal reference $\check{x}^*_0(x_t)$ and optimal feedforward action $\check{u}^*_0(x_t)$.
 2. Additional "augmented" states $x^+$ are sampled from within the tube, centered around $\check{x}^*_0(x_t)$ (i.e., $x^+ \in \check{x}^*_0(x_t) + Z$).
 3. For each $x^+$, the corresponding robust action $u^+$ is easily computed using the ancillary controller: $u^+ = \check{u}^*_0(x_t) + K(x^+ - \check{x}^*_0(x_t))$.
 4. These $(x^+, u^+)$ pairs are added to the training dataset, effectively enriching the training data with states and actions relevant to disturbed conditions, without needing to experience actual disturbances during demonstration collection.

#### 3.3. Tube Approximation and Sampling Strategies

Since the set $Z$ can be complex, it is approximated as a hyper-rectangle $\hat{Z}$. Two sampling strategies are investigated:
- **Dense Sampling:** Samples extra states from all $2^{n_x}$ vertices of the hyper-rectangle $\hat{Z}$ (where $n_x$ is the state dimension). This is more conservative but computationally expensive.
- **Sparse Sampling:** Samples one extra state from the center of each of the $2n_x$ facets of $\hat{Z}$. This is less conservative but significantly more computationally efficient, scaling linearly with state size.

#### 3.4. Learning Algorithm and Policy Architecture

- The augmented datasets are used to train a DNN policy via standard IL algorithms, specifically **Behavior Cloning (BC)** and **DAgger**.
- The DNN architecture chosen is a 2-hidden layer, fully connected network with 32 neurons per layer and ReLU activation. Its input combines the current state and the reference trajectory, and its output is the commanded thrust and tilt.

This methodology provides a principled way to generate robust training data, implicitly addressing the covariate shift by providing expert actions for states likely to be encountered under real-world perturbations, thus greatly enhancing demonstration efficiency and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive numerical and hardware evaluations to validate their proposed Sampling Augmentation (SA) strategy, comparing it against baseline Imitation Learning (IL) methods (Behavior Cloning (BC) and DAgger), both with and without Domain Randomization (DR).

#### 4.1. Evaluation Setup

- **System:** Trajectory tracking control for a multirotor (quadrotor).
- **Expert:** A Robust Tube MPC (RTMPC) for the quadrotor, where disturbances were modeled as force-like perturbations up to 30% of the robot's weight.
- **Learned Policy:** A 2-hidden layer DNN (188 input dimensions: state + reference; 3 output dimensions: thrust, roll, pitch).
- **Training Environment:** Custom-built nonlinear quadrotor simulation without disturbances.
- **Deployment (Target) Environments:**
 - **T1:** Simulation with wind-like disturbances (adversarially sampled from the bounded set $W$).
 - **T2:** Simulation with model errors (mismatches in drag coefficients).
 - **Hardware:** Real quadrotor in the presence of leaf blower generated wind disturbances.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Robustness:** Success Rate (percentage of episodes without state constraint violation/crashes).
 - **Performance:** MPC Stage Cost (closeness of the learned policy's trajectory to the expert's).
 - **Demonstration Efficiency:** Number of demonstrations required to achieve full success rate.

#### 4.2. Numerical Results (Single Trajectory Tracking - Fig. 4, Table I)

- **Demonstration Efficiency:** The most striking finding is the superior demonstration efficiency of SA. Both **BC+SA** and **DAgger+SA (Sparse)** achieved 100% success rate (full robustness) in the *target domain* (with disturbances) after **only a single demonstration**. In contrast, baseline BC required 6 demonstrations, DAgger needed 6, BC+DR required 9, and DAgger+DR needed 6 to achieve similar robustness *at convergence*, but often didn't reach 100% success rate in the target domain. This demonstrates an **up to 6x improvement in sample efficiency**.
- **Robustness:** SA methods consistently achieved 100% success rate in the target domain (T1 with wind, T2 with model errors), indicating high robustness to unseen perturbations. Baseline methods, even with DR, often struggled to reach full robustness, highlighting that SA more effectively compensates for covariate shifts.
- **Performance:** Sparse SA combined with DAgger achieved the closest performance to the expert RTMPC (lowest expert gap). Dense SA showed slightly worse performance, potentially due to the increased data complexity for the small DNN. Sparse SA was confirmed as the preferred approach due to its balance of effectiveness and computational efficiency.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The compressed DNN policy exhibited a **two-orders-of-magnitude reduction in latency** (0.25ms on laptop GPU, 1.66ms on onboard TX2 CPU) compared to the expert RTMPC (55.3ms), making it suitable for real-time onboard deployment.

#### 4.3. Hardware Evaluation (Single Trajectory Tracking - Fig. 5, Fig. 6)

- **Zero-Shot Transfer Validation:** Policies trained with SA (sparse) from a single simulation demonstration were directly deployed on a physical quadrotor.
- **Hovering under Wind:** The quadrotor successfully maintained hover despite significant wind disturbances from a leaf blower (Fig. 5), demonstrating the learned policy's robustness and zero-shot transfer capability from simulation to hardware.
- **Trajectory Tracking under Wind:** The robot successfully tracked an eight-shaped trajectory while enduring strong wind from an array of leaf blowers (Fig. 6). The observed altitude errors and commanded thrust changes confirmed the presence of disturbances and the policy's ability to robustly counteract them, even though these specific disturbances were unseen during training.

#### 4.4. Numerical and Hardware Evaluation (Generalization to Multiple Trajectories - Fig. 7, Fig. 8)

- **Generalization:** The SA strategy also showed superior performance in learning to track *multiple, unseen* trajectories sampled from a distribution (e.g., circles, position steps, eight-shapes). Numerically (Fig. 7), SA again achieved high robustness and lower expert gap with significantly fewer demonstrations (less than half compared to baselines) for generalizing to new trajectories.
- **Hardware Generalization:** A policy trained with only 10 demonstrations using DAgger+SA (sparse) successfully tracked various arbitrarily chosen trajectories in hardware, even under strong wind disturbances (Fig. 8). This further validates the ability of SA to learn generalized robust policies from limited data.

In essence, the findings consistently demonstrate that the proposed Sampling Augmentation strategy, guided by RTMPC, provides an unprecedented level of **demonstration efficiency and robustness**, enabling practical zero-shot transfer of complex control policies to real-world, disturbed environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of learning-based control for autonomous systems, with profound implications and potential impact:

1. **Enabling Practical Real-World Deployment of Advanced Controllers:** The primary significance lies in addressing the long-standing challenge of deploying computationally intensive, high-performance controllers like MPC on resource-constrained robotic platforms. By compressing RTMPC into a highly efficient DNN policy, the authors effectively bridge the gap between theoretical optimality and practical real-time execution.

2. **Breakthrough in Demonstration Efficiency:** The ability to achieve robust performance from a *single demonstration* (or very few demonstrations) is a groundbreaking result. This drastically reduces the time, cost, and complexity associated with data collection, especially on physical robots where extensive demonstrations are often impractical, expensive, or even dangerous. This makes the development cycle for new robotic behaviors much faster and safer.

3. **True Zero-Shot Transfer from Nominal Environments:** The demonstrated zero-shot transfer from a disturbance-free simulation (or lab) to a real-world environment with unseen perturbations is a major achievement. This capability means that developers do not need to meticulously model or physically apply disturbances during the training phase. Instead, they can train policies in ideal conditions and expect robust performance in challenging real-world scenarios, accelerating the transition of robotic technologies from labs to diverse applications.

4. **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Uncertainties:** The core innovation of leveraging the RTMPC's invariant tube for data augmentation provides a principled way to infuse robustness into the learned policy. Unlike Domain Randomization, which requires physically simulating or applying disturbances, the Sampling Augmentation strategy derives the knowledge of potential state deviations from the RTMPC's theoretical guarantees. This means the learned policy is inherently aware of how to respond to bounded model errors and external perturbations it has never explicitly experienced during training.

5. **Broader Applicability and Impact across Robotics:** While demonstrated on a quadrotor for trajectory tracking, the underlying principle of leveraging robust control theory (tube MPC) to inform data augmentation for imitation learning is generalizable. This methodology could be extended to other complex robotic systems (e.g., legged robots, manipulators, autonomous vehicles) and a wider range of tasks, enabling safer and more reliable autonomous operation in uncertain environments.

6. **Paving the Way for Adaptive and Generalizable Systems:** The work lays a strong foundation for future research in adaptive control and learning. As mentioned in future work, designing an adaptation strategy would further enhance the learned policy's ability to handle even larger or time-varying uncertainties, pushing towards truly autonomous and resilient robotic systems capable of operating in highly dynamic and unpredictable real-world settings.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a highly practical and theoretically grounded solution to a significant bottleneck in robotics. By making advanced control policies simultaneously efficient, demonstration-sparse, and robust to real-world uncertainties, it significantly lowers the barrier to deploying complex autonomous behaviors, thereby accelerating innovation in fields ranging from aerial delivery and inspection to exploration and human-robot interaction.
