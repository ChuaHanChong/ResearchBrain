# Unleashing the Power of Pre-trained Language Models for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> LaMo, a framework developed by researchers including those from Tsinghua University and Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, effectively transfers knowledge from pre-trained language models to offline reinforcement learning for motion control tasks. It shows enhanced performance, particularly in sparse-reward and data-limited environments, improving over Decision Transformer by up to 50% in certain sparse-reward tasks and 99% in low-data Atari regimes.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning (RL) faces challenges in real-world applications due to costly or risky online data collection, often relying on limited pre-collected datasets.
- Transformer-based offline RL methods, such as Decision Transformer (DT), are data-intensive, requiring large amounts of in-domain data for robust performance.
- Previous attempts to integrate pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) into decision-making tasks have not consistently surpassed existing DT or value-based offline RL algorithms.

## Method

- **LaMo** initializes the ==Decision Transformer== with pre-trained Language Model (GPT-2) weights and applies ==Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT) via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== to adapt the largely frozen layers, preserving pre-trained knowledge.
- ==Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs)== replace standard linear projections for input and output embeddings, providing non-linear transformations to bridge the domain gap between language and motion control data.
- An ==auxiliary language prediction loss== is introduced during fine-tuning to stabilize training, prevent overfitting to limited RL datasets, and help retain original language understanding abilities.

## Results

- LaMo achieved superior performance in sparse-reward tasks (Kitchen, Reacher2d), outperforming DT by up to **50%** and securing the highest average scores across most variants compared to value-based methods.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced learning capability in low-data regimes, notably improving over DT by **99%** in **0.1%** data Atari tasks and maintaining performance advantages as data scaled.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of MLPs for embeddings, LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation, and the auxiliary language loss for training stability and preventing overfitting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained language models possess valuable sequential modeling and generalized knowledge that can be effectively transferred to offline reinforcement learning for motion control, offering robustness in low-data and sparse-reward scenarios.
> - Custom non-linear embeddings, specifically MLPs, are essential for bridging the inherent domain gap between abstract language representations and concrete RL states/actions.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) combined with an auxiliary language prediction loss enables robust adaptation to new tasks without catastrophic forgetting, preserving generalizable knowledge while mitigating overfitting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of offline reinforcement learning (RL), which focuses on deriving optimal policies from pre-collected datasets without further interaction with the environment. This paradigm is particularly relevant for real-world applications where online data collection can be costly, time-consuming, or risky.

A significant development in offline RL has been the adoption of Transformer architectures, notably with the introduction of Decision Transformer (DT). DT reframes RL as a conditional sequence modeling problem, allowing the Transformer to learn policies by predicting future actions based on sequences of desired returns, states, and previous actions. However, Transformers are known for being data-intensive, often requiring extensive pre-training on vast datasets to achieve robust performance.

Concurrently, Large Language Models (LLMs), also based on Transformer architectures, have demonstrated significant advancements in language understanding and generation. These models, pre-trained on diverse and massive linguistic corpora, exhibit capabilities such as few-shot and zero-shot learning, allowing them to generalize to new tasks with limited specific training data.

The broader research landscape has seen increasing efforts to integrate the capabilities of pre-trained LMs into decision-making tasks. Initial approaches have included using LMs for high-level task decomposition or converting environment states into language descriptions. More recent work, such as Wiki-RL, directly uses pre-trained LMs as an initialization for DT to process low-level agent states and actions in motion control tasks. However, these earlier attempts have not consistently achieved performance levels superior to pure DT methods or value-based offline RL algorithms. This suggests that the full potential of LMs in this domain had not been effectively leveraged.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a framework designed to more effectively transfer the knowledge and sequential modeling capabilities of pre-trained LMs to offline RL, particularly focusing on motion control, with the aim of improving performance, especially in data-limited and sparse-reward scenarios. It builds upon the DT framework and parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques to bridge the domain gap between language and sequential decision-making.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general framework that effectively utilizes pre-trained Language Models (LMs) for offline Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically for motion control tasks. The authors aim to enhance the performance of Transformer-based sequential decision-making methods, particularly in challenging scenarios characterized by limited in-domain data and sparse rewards.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several factors:

1. **Challenges in Offline RL:** Offline RL is crucial for real-world applications where interacting with the environment for data collection is expensive, risky, or impractical. However, learning from pre-collected, potentially suboptimal datasets presents significant challenges, especially when the available in-domain data is limited.
2. **Data Efficiency of Transformers:** While Decision Transformers (DT) have shown promise in reframing RL as a sequence modeling problem, they inherit the data-hungry nature of general Transformer architectures. This means that a large volume of specific RL trajectory data is often required to achieve satisfactory performance, which contradicts the data-limited scenarios prevalent in real-world offline RL.
3. **Leveraging Pre-trained LM Capabilities:** Large Language Models (LLMs), also based on Transformers, have demonstrated strong few-shot and zero-shot learning abilities, alongside robust generalization, due to their extensive pre-training on vast and diverse linguistic datasets. The authors hypothesize that this rich, pre-trained knowledge, particularly the sequential reasoning capabilities embedded within LMs, could be beneficial for sequential decision-making problems in RL.
4. **Addressing Limitations of Prior Work:** Previous attempts to integrate LMs into decision-making, such as Wiki-RL, have utilized pre-trained LMs as initializations for DT. However, these methods have not consistently surpassed the performance of pure DT or established value-based offline RL algorithms. This indicates that a more refined approach is needed to fully "unleash the power" of LMs for effective knowledge transfer to the RL domain.
5. **Improving Performance in Difficult RL Settings:** The paper specifically aims to improve agent performance in sparse-reward tasks, which are inherently more challenging for RL algorithms due to infrequent positive feedback. Additionally, a motivation is to close the performance gap observed between DT-based methods and more traditional value-based offline RL algorithms in dense-reward tasks.
6. **Developing a Robust Framework:** The research seeks to identify and integrate specific architectural and training components that facilitate the preservation and effective adaptation of pre-trained linguistic knowledge to motion control, thereby creating a robust and generalizable offline RL framework.

In essence, the core motivation is to bridge the gap between the powerful sequential modeling and generalization abilities of LMs and the data-efficiency and performance requirements of offline RL, especially under constrained data availability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Language Models for Motion Control (LaMo), is designed to integrate pre-trained Language Models (LMs) into an offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) setting, specifically building upon the Decision Transformer (DT) architecture. The methodology involves a two-stage process: pre-training on language tasks and subsequent fine-tuning for offline RL, incorporating four key design components.

#### 3.1. Pre-training on Language Tasks

The initial stage involves acquiring or pre-training language models. The authors primarily utilize the publicly available pre-trained weights of the GPT-2 architecture from Hugging Face. For ablation studies, they also pre-train GPT-2 models themselves using the WikiText corpus with a standard next-token prediction objective (Equation 4). This allows for investigating the impact of pre-training quality (e.g., fewer steps, randomly shuffled corpus, or randomly initialized weights) on downstream RL performance.

#### 3.2. Fine-tuning for Offline Reinforcement Learning

Following the LM pre-training, the model is adapted for offline RL tasks through a fine-tuning process that integrates four critical designs:

1. **LM Initialization of Decision Transformer:** A pre-trained LM (typically GPT-2) is used to initialize the weights of the Decision Transformer. This transfers the sequential processing capabilities and generalized knowledge acquired during language pre-training to the RL domain.
2. **Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) for Embeddings:** The standard linear projections typically used for input embeddings (return-to-go, states, actions) and output projections in LMs are replaced with Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). This non-linear transformation is posited to augment the model's representation learning capabilities, enabling it to better bridge the domain gap between abstract language tokens and concrete motion control data. For environments like Kitchen and MuJoCo, 3 MLP layers are used; for Atari, which uses image inputs, 3 CNN layers are used to process the visual observations before feeding them into the MLPs.
3. **Frozen Weights and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** During fine-tuning, the majority of the pre-trained Transformer layers are frozen. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) is applied using the LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) technique. LoRA injects low-rank matrices into the attention weights (Q, K, V) of the Transformer, where only these low-rank matrices are trainable. This approach significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters (to approximately 0.7% of the total model parameters), aiming to preserve the generalized knowledge from pre-training while allowing for efficient adaptation to the specific offline RL domain.
4. **Auxiliary Language Prediction Loss:** An auxiliary language prediction loss ($L_{language}$) is introduced during the fine-tuning stage. The model is simultaneously trained on language prediction tasks using the WikiText corpus, temporarily switching its input and output projections back to the LM's original ones. This component is intended to stabilize the training process, prevent overfitting to the potentially limited RL datasets, and help the model retain its original language understanding abilities. The overall objective function becomes a combination of the standard DT decision-making loss and this auxiliary language loss (Equation 5).

#### 3.3. Experiment Setup and Evaluation

- **Environments:** Experiments are conducted across 8 tasks from three distinct domains: Kitchen (sparse reward, state input), MuJoCo (dense reward, state input), and Atari (dense reward, image input). A custom Reacher2d task is also used.
- **Datasets:** D4RL datasets (Fu et al., 2020) and d4rl-atari are used, including `Medium`, `Complete`, and `Partial` variants. To evaluate robustness, performance is assessed under varying data sample ratios (e.g., 0.005, 0.01, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1).
- **Baselines:** LaMo is compared against prominent offline RL algorithms including Decision Transformer (DT), Wiki-RL (a related LM-initialized DT method), Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), TD3+BC, Behavior Cloning (BC), BCQ, and NFQ.
- **Metrics:** Performance is reported using normalized scores (0 for random policy, 100 for expert policy) following D4RL conventions. Scores are averaged over the last 20K (or 10K/12.5K for specific tasks) training steps over 3 random seeds. Ablation studies further examine the contribution of each proposed component.

The methodology integrates advanced language model pre-training with parameter-efficient fine-tuning and domain-bridging non-linear projections, alongside a regularization term, to transfer and adapt cross-domain knowledge for robust offline reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of LaMo across various tasks, data regimes, and reward densities yielded several findings:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

LaMo demonstrated strong performance across the majority of evaluated tasks. The method generally outperformed existing Transformer-based offline RL approaches, such as Decision Transformer (DT) and Wiki-RL, and exhibited competitive or superior performance compared to value-based methods like Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) and Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) in many scenarios.

#### 4.2. Sparse-Reward Tasks

In sparse-reward tasks (Kitchen and Reacher2d), LaMo showed a notable advantage.
- It consistently surpassed DT and Wiki-RL, with improvements over DT reaching up to 50% in certain tasks.
- Compared to value-based methods, LaMo achieved the highest average scores across these tasks, securing the best performance in 7 out of 9 sparse-reward task variants and second-best in the remaining 2.
- LaMo also appeared to mitigate overfitting issues observed in CQL in Kitchen tasks, where CQL's performance could drop significantly over training.

#### 4.3. Dense-Reward Tasks

For dense-reward tasks (Atari and MuJoCo), LaMo also exhibited improvements.
- In Atari tasks, LaMo achieved the highest average scores, particularly under low-data regimes. It outperformed DT significantly, with average improvements over DT of 99% in the 0.1% data regime and 24% in the 100% data regime.
- In MuJoCo tasks, LaMo achieved the highest average scores in low-data regimes. When data scale was larger (e.g., 10% or 100%), LaMo remained competitive with DT and other strong value-based baselines, occasionally trailing CQL in specific tasks like Halfcheetah and Walker2d. The authors attributed this to the inherent suitability of dense-reward tasks for value-based methods, while LaMo improved DT's competitiveness in these settings.

#### 4.4. Ability in Low-Data Regime

A consistent finding was LaMo's enhanced learning capability in scenarios with limited data samples (e.g., 0.5%, 1% of the full dataset). As demonstrated in Figure 3, LaMo maintained a performance advantage over both CQL and DT as the data sample ratio increased, suggesting that the inductive bias from pre-trained LMs contributes to more effective learning from scarce data.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **MLPs for Embeddings:** Replacing linear projections with MLPs for input and output embeddings proved crucial. "LaMo w/o MLP" consistently underperformed the full LaMo, indicating that non-linear transformations are essential for bridging the domain gap and preventing overfitting.
- **LoRA vs. Full Fine-tuning and Frozen Parameters:** LoRA, with its minimal trainable parameters (0.7%), demonstrated superiority over both full-weight fine-tuning (which suffered from overfitting) and completely freezing Transformer layers. This suggested that parameter-efficient adaptation is key to leveraging the generalizable knowledge from LMs without overwriting it. The paper also noted that even with randomly initialized frozen weights, LoRA could outperform DT, implying that the architectural depth combined with efficient adaptation offers benefits.
- **Language Pre-training vs. Visual Pre-training:** For Atari tasks, comparing language pre-training (LaMo) with visual pre-training (ImageGPT) showed that language pre-training provided a significant advantage. While visual pre-training did offer some improvement over a standard DT, the results indicated that proficiency in sequential modeling, rather than just raw feature extraction, was more beneficial for motion control tasks.
- **Auxiliary Language Prediction Loss:** The inclusion of the auxiliary language loss was found to stabilize the training process and prevent overfitting, particularly in challenging sparse-reward tasks like Kitchen. This allowed LaMo to maintain robust performance and retain language understanding abilities during RL fine-tuning. Its impact on performance varied across tasks, showing a critical enhancement in Kitchen tasks but primarily serving as a regularizer elsewhere.
- **Quality of Pre-trained Models:** Higher quality language pre-training (e.g., a fully trained GPT-2) generally led to better downstream RL performance compared to early-stopped or randomly pre-trained models.
- **Model Size:** GPT2-small provided satisfactory performance, with GPT2-medium offering incremental improvements in some Kitchen tasks, indicating a positive but not dramatically scaling effect of larger model sizes within the tested range.
- **Comparison with Diffusion-QL:** LaMo outperformed Diffusion Q-learning (Diffusion-QL) across three MuJoCo tasks, particularly in low-data regimes.

In summary, the results indicated that LaMo effectively leverages pre-trained LMs for offline RL through its specific architectural and training modifications, demonstrating improved performance, particularly in data-limited and sparse-reward environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, with the introduction of the LaMo framework, carries several points of significance and potential impact within the field of reinforcement learning and artificial intelligence more broadly.

#### 5.1. Novel Integration of Pre-trained LMs for Motion Control

LaMo represents a significant step in effectively transferring the rich knowledge and sequential modeling capabilities of pre-trained Large Language Models (LMs) to low-level motion control tasks within the offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigm. While prior works have explored similar integrations, LaMo's systematic combination of LM initialization, non-linear MLP embeddings, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), and an auxiliary language loss demonstrates a more robust and effective method for cross-domain knowledge transfer.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Performance in Challenging RL Scenarios

The framework demonstrates superior empirical performance, particularly in sparse-reward tasks and scenarios with limited offline data. This is a crucial advancement, as sparse rewards and data scarcity are common and challenging characteristics of real-world RL problems. By outperforming strong baselines, including other Transformer-based methods and value-based algorithms, LaMo offers a more reliable approach for learning effective policies under these constraints.

#### 5.3. Addressing Data Efficiency Challenges of Transformers

Transformers, while powerful, are known for their data-hungry nature. LaMo offers a method to mitigate this by initializing with a pre-trained LM, which brings a vast amount of generalized knowledge, thereby reducing the dependency on large volumes of in-domain RL data. This makes Transformer-based RL more practical for applications where data collection is prohibitive.

#### 5.4. Validation of Key Architectural and Training Components

The comprehensive ablation studies within the paper highlight the individual contributions of each proposed component. The findings underscore the importance of:
- **Non-linear Embeddings:** Replacing linear projections with MLPs is shown to be critical for effectively bridging the domain gap between abstract language representations and concrete physical states/actions.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning (LoRA):** This technique proves effective in adapting the pre-trained model to a new domain without catastrophic forgetting or overfitting, demonstrating a balance between leveraging prior knowledge and learning new task-specific information.
- **Auxiliary Language Loss:** The auxiliary objective for language prediction serves as a regularizer, stabilizing training and notably preventing overfitting in certain sparse-reward tasks, while also preserving the model's original language capabilities.

#### 5.5. Broader Implications for Generalist AI Agents

The success of LaMo in simultaneously retaining language understanding abilities while excelling in motion control tasks suggests a potential path towards more generalist AI agents capable of operating across diverse modalities and tasks. This approach could contribute to developing models that leverage knowledge from various domains, such as text, vision, and action, in a more integrated manner.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The work also opens several avenues for future research, including:
- Exploring ways to more deeply leverage the "reasoning" abilities of LMs to enhance offline RL performance beyond just sequential modeling.
- Investigating the scalability of LaMo with even larger language models (e.g., LLaMA, GPT-4) as computational resources become available.
- Further studying the phenomenon of "intrinsic dimensionality" and its role in transfer learning, especially the observation that even randomly initialized, efficiently adapted Transformers can outperform standard DTs.
- Developing methods for more explicit feature alignment between language and motion control domains to potentially maximize transfer benefits.

In conclusion, LaMo provides a framework that not only pushes the state-of-the-art in offline RL for challenging settings but also deepens the understanding of how pre-trained knowledge from language models can be effectively harnessed for sequential decision-making, with significant implications for the development of more versatile and data-efficient autonomous agents.
