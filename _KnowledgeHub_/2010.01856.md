# My Body is a Cage: the Role of Morphology in Graph-Based Incompatible Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The study investigates the utility of explicit morphological graph structures in graph-based incompatible control, finding that a transformer-based method called AMORPHEUS, which foregoes such structures, achieves superior performance and sample efficiency compared to prior GNN-based approaches across various continuous control tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Multitask Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) methods struggle with incompatible environments, which feature varying state and action space dimensions.
- Traditional neural networks cannot adapt to these variable input/output sizes, necessitating specialized architectures like Graph Neural Networks (GNNs).
- GNN-based continuous control models commonly rely on an agent's physical morphology to define graph structures, an assumption not thoroughly validated.

## Method

- Conducted ablation studies on leading ==GNN==-based MTRL methods (SMP, NERVENET) by altering their input graph topologies to assess the impact of morphological inductive bias.
- Introduced **AMORPHEUS**, a novel ==transformer==-based architecture designed for incompatible continuous control, which processes agents as sets of limbs without explicit graph connections.
- Evaluated AMORPHEUS on established MTRL benchmarks against GNN competitors using the ==TD3== algorithm, focusing on learning efficiency and final performance.

## Results

- Ablation experiments on SMP and NERVENET showed that simplified "star" or "fully connected" graph topologies performed comparably to or even slightly better than morphology-derived graphs.
- AMORPHEUS demonstrated consistently higher sample efficiency and significantly better average returns than SMP and NERVENET across all evaluated MTRL benchmarks, including Walker++ and the Walkers dataset.
- Analysis of AMORPHEUS's attention masks revealed dynamic, state-dependent communication patterns that adapted to the agent's actions, exhibiting periodic changes coordinated with gait cycles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit morphological graph structures do not reliably enhance performance for graph-based continuous control agents and can complicate efficient message passing within GNNs.
> - A flexible, fully-connected attention mechanism, as seen in transformers, facilitates more effective communication between state components than fixed sparse graph structures in incompatible MTRL.
> - Comprehensive node features, without predefined structural connections, are sufficient for learning complex control policies in environments with varying agent morphologies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated within the field of Multitask Reinforcement Learning (MTRL), an area focused on training agents to perform multiple tasks simultaneously, aiming for benefits such as improved performance, generalization, data efficiency, and robustness. A significant challenge in MTRL is addressing "incompatible environments," where the dimensionality of state or action spaces varies across tasks. Conventional neural network architectures, such as Multi-layer Perceptrons (MLPs), are ill-suited for such environments due to their requirement for fixed-size inputs and outputs.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a potential solution for MTRL in incompatible environments, as they can inherently process graphs of arbitrary sizes and topologies. A key aspect of GNN application in domains like continuous control (e.g., robotics) has been the integration of structural inductive biases derived from the agent's physical morphology. In this paradigm, an agent's body parts (limbs, torso) are represented as nodes, their features as node labels, and physical connections between parts as graph edges. Existing methods, such as NERVENET and Shared Modular Policies (SMP), have adopted this approach, operating under the assumption that encoding explicit morphological information within the graph structure is beneficial for learning.

This paper challenges this assumption. It investigates whether the morphological structure, commonly used to define GNN message-passing schemes, actually contributes to performance or if its benefits are outweighed by the computational difficulties it introduces for message propagation. By questioning this foundational assumption, the work seeks to refine the understanding of effective architectures for incompatible MTRL and proposes an alternative approach based on transformers that does not rely on explicit morphological graph structure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to critically evaluate the role of explicit morphological information in graph-based continuous control methods designed for incompatible Multitask Reinforcement Learning. The authors identify two main motivations and hypotheses guiding their investigation:

1. **Revisiting the Efficacy of Morphological Inductive Bias**: Prior work in graph-based continuous control (e.g., NERVENET and SMP) postulates that the use of an agent's physical morphology to structure the input graph is beneficial for model performance. This paper aims to validate or refute this assumption. The implicit hypothesis is that the explicit morphological information, encoded as fixed graph edges, might not provide the anticipated performance gains, and its actual impact has not been thoroughly examined.
2. **Addressing Message Passing Challenges in Sparse GNNs**: The authors hypothesize that any potential benefits derived from encoding morphological domain knowledge in a sparse graph structure might be counteracted by the inherent difficulties such sparse graphs impose on message passing within GNNs. In sparsely connected graphs, crucial state information may need to traverse multiple "hops" to reach distant nodes. The paper posits that learning this multi-hop communication effectively in practice can be challenging for GNNs, potentially leading to information loss or propagation bottlenecks. This difficulty could outweigh the advantages of a structural inductive bias.
3. **Proposing an Alternative Architecture**: Motivated by the second hypothesis, the paper aims to develop a new method, AMORPHEUS, that eschews explicit morphological graph structures in favor of an architecture that facilitates unrestricted communication between all state components. The goal is to determine if a model that operates on a fully connected conceptual graph (like transformers, which use attention as a form of global aggregation) can overcome the hypothesized message-passing limitations of morphology-constrained GNNs, thereby achieving superior performance in incompatible MTRL environments.

In essence, the research seeks to determine if the "cage" of a fixed, morphology-derived graph structure is an enabling factor or a constraint on learning effective policies in complex, variable-morphology environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach involving ablation studies on existing GNN-based methods and the development and evaluation of a novel transformer-based architecture.

#### 3.1. Ablation Studies on Existing GNN-based Methods

To investigate the impact of morphological information, the authors conducted ablation experiments on two prominent GNN-based incompatible MTRL methods:
- **Shared Modular Policies (SMP)** (Huang et al., 2020)
- **NERVENET** (Wang et al., 2018)

The core idea was to alter the graph topology provided to these models while keeping other aspects consistent, and observe the resulting performance changes. Three different graph topologies were tested:
- **Morphology**: The original, physically connected graph based on the agent's limb structure. This represents the full morphological information.
- **Star**: A simplified graph where all nodes are connected only to a central "torso" node, effectively removing complex relational information but maintaining arbitrary connectivity.
- **Line**: A graph with a sequential connection of nodes, introducing a structural bias unrelated to the agent's actual morphology.
- **Fully Connected**: For NERVENET, which can process non-tree graphs, a fully connected topology was also tested, allowing direct communication between all nodes. SMP is limited to tree structures.

These ablations were performed on standard continuous control MTRL benchmarks: Walker++ and Humanoid++ for SMP, and the Walkers benchmark (comprising Hopper, HalfCheetah, FullCheetah, Walker, and Ostrich agents) for NERVENET. Performance was measured by the average undiscounted MTRL return over three random seeds, along with standard error.

#### 3.2. Proposed Method: AMORPHEUS

Motivated by the potential limitations of morphology-constrained GNNs, the authors propose **AMORPHEUS**, a transformer-based method for incompatible MTRL in continuous control.
- **Architecture**: AMORPHEUS adopts an "encode-process-decode" structure:
 - **Encoder**: A linear layer processes each node's observation independently. Node observations include limb type, relative position, velocities, rotations, angles, and angle ranges.
 - **Processor (Transformer)**: The core of AMORPHEUS. It consists of multiple transformer blocks, where self-attention enables direct communication between all input nodes (effectively a fully connected graph). This design eliminates the need for multi-hop message propagation inherent in sparse GNNs. Each transformer block includes multi-head attention (two heads per layer) and feedforward layers, with residual connections and Layer Normalization.
 - **Decoder**: An MLP processes each node's output independently from the transformer, generating actions (for the actor policy) or value estimates (for the critic). A residual connection from the initial input features to the decoder output is included.
- **Modularity**: Like SMP and NERVENET, AMORPHEUS is modular and can handle environments with varying numbers of limbs and connectivities, including those not encountered during training.
- **Training Algorithm**: The method is built upon the TD3 (Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient) algorithm, similar to the SMP codebase. Separate networks are trained for the actor and critic, both sharing the AMORPHEUS architecture but without weight sharing.

#### 3.3. Experimental Evaluation of AMORPHEUS

AMORPHEUS was evaluated against SMP and NERVENET on the same MTRL benchmarks from Huang et al. (2020) (Walker++, Cheetah++, Humanoid++, Hopper++, and combinations thereof) and Wang et al. (2018) (Walkers benchmark). The evaluation focused on sample efficiency and final average returns. Hyperparameters were tuned, including a reduced learning rate for both AMORPHEUS and SMP for better performance.

#### 3.4. Attention Mask Analysis

To provide insights into the learned behavior of AMORPHEUS, the authors analyzed its attention masks:
- **State-Dependent Masks**: Investigated how attention patterns change dynamically based on the agent's state within a rollout.
- **Temporal Patterns**: Examined the emergence of periodic attention patterns coordinated with the agent's gait cycles.
- **Evolution During Training**: Tracked how the distribution of attention mask weights evolves from early training stages to convergence.
- **Layer-Specific Dynamics**: Analyzed differences in the rate of change of attention masks across different transformer layers.

#### 3.5. Ablation of Residual Connection

A further ablation was performed on AMORPHEUS by removing the residual connection from the input features directly to the decoder output. This aimed to verify that the performance gains of AMORPHEUS were not solely attributable to this specific architectural detail.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. On the Role of Morphology in Existing GNN-based Methods

- **SMP Ablations**: For SMP on Walker++ and Humanoid++ environments, the "star" graph topology, which provides no explicit morphological information beyond node features, performed comparably to or marginally better than the "morphology" graph. The "line" graph generally performed worse, suggesting that some structural biases can be detrimental if they complicate message propagation.
- **NERVENET Ablations**: On the Walkers benchmark, NERVENET variants with "star," "line," and "fully connected" topologies performed similarly to the "morphology" graph, with the "star" variant showing a slight advantage. The "fully connected" variant learned slower initially but reached comparable final performance.
- **Conclusion from Ablations**: These results indicate that explicit morphological information encoded in the graph structure does not consistently enhance the performance of existing GNN-based continuous control methods. In some cases, fixed graph structures might even hinder learning by impeding efficient message passing.

#### 4.2. Performance of AMORPHEUS

- **Superior Performance**: AMORPHEUS consistently and substantially outperformed SMP (the previous state-of-the-art) across all tested incompatible MTRL benchmarks from Huang et al. (2020), including Walker++, Cheetah++, Humanoid++, Hopper++, and their various combinations. This superiority was observed in both sample efficiency (faster learning) and final average returns.
- **Enhanced Stability**: AMORPHEUS demonstrated greater stability in learning, successfully training in regimes (e.g., Cheetah++ combined with other environments) where SMP had previously been reported to be unstable.
- **Generalization to Different Benchmarks**: AMORPHEUS also outperformed both SMP and NERVENET on the Walkers benchmark from Wang etal. (2018), further validating its effectiveness across different sets of agents and tasks.
- **Sufficiency of Node Features**: The performance of AMORPHEUS suggests that the rich information contained within the individual node features (e.g., relative limb positions, types, velocities) is sufficient for learning complex control policies, even without an explicitly provided graph topology.

#### 4.3. Attention Mask Analysis in AMORPHEUS

- **State-Dependent Communication**: AMORPHEUS learned to generate dynamic, state-dependent attention masks. This adaptability allows the model to selectively focus on different parts of the agent's body based on the current observation and task requirements.
- **Periodic Patterns**: The attention masks exhibited non-trivial behavior, including periodic cycles coordinated with the agent's gait. For instance, in a Walker-7 agent, nodes showed increased attention to an upper leg when it was closer to the ground, reflecting a learned understanding of movement mechanics.
- **Layer-Specific Dynamics**: Attention masks in earlier transformer layers tended to change more slowly within a rollout compared to those in downstream layers. This indicates a potential hierarchy in how information is processed, with earlier layers possibly forming more stable, general representations, and later layers adapting more rapidly to specific situational demands.
- **Evolution During Training**: Attention masks evolved from more uniform distributions early in training to less uniform, more focused distributions as training progressed, suggesting that the model learns to prioritize information flow.

#### 4.4. Zero-shot Generalization and Residual Connection Ablation

- **Zero-shot Potential (Preliminary)**: Initial experiments on zero-shot generalization showed AMORPHEUS having higher average returns on 5 out of 7 tested tasks compared to SMP, though with high variance, suggesting potential for generalization despite differences between training and testing tasks.
- **Residual Connection**: Removing the residual connection from input features to the decoder output in AMORPHEUS did not significantly degrade its performance, indicating that this architectural detail was not the primary driver of AMORPHEUS's improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Reinforcement Learning, particularly Multitask RL and learning in incompatible environments, with several significant implications:

1. **Re-evaluation of Morphological Inductive Biases**: The most direct impact is the challenge to the conventional wisdom that explicit morphological information, encoded as graph edges, is a necessary or even beneficial inductive bias for GNNs in continuous control. The ablation studies demonstrate that simpler or fully connected graph structures can perform equally well or better for existing GNN-based methods. This suggests that the "cage" of morphology, while intuitive, might actually hinder learning by imposing rigid communication constraints.
2. **Advancement in Incompatible MTRL Architectures**: The introduction of AMORPHEUS, a transformer-based method, provides a robust and high-performing alternative to traditional GNNs for incompatible continuous control. By demonstrating superior sample efficiency and final performance across various benchmarks, AMORPHEUS establishes transformers as a viable and potentially more effective architectural choice for handling variable state and action spaces.
3. **Emphasis on Node Features**: The success of AMORPHEUS, which relies primarily on rich node features and flexible attention mechanisms rather than explicit graph topology, underscores the importance of comprehensive individual entity descriptions. It suggests that sufficient information for relational reasoning can often be implicitly derived from node features alone, without needing predefined structural connections.
4. **Implications for GNN Design**: The findings prompt a re-evaluation of GNN design principles, particularly concerning message passing in sparse graphs. The paper implies that if multi-hop communication is difficult to learn, or if a global understanding of the state is crucial, a fully connected approach with attention might be preferable. This could lead to a broader adoption of attention-based architectures or hybrid models that dynamically infer or augment graph structures.
5. **Interpretability of Policies**: The analysis of state-dependent and gait-coordinated attention masks offers a degree of interpretability into the learned policies. Understanding which parts of the agent's body are being attended to and how these patterns change can provide insights into the internal decision-making process of the agent, which is valuable for debugging and trust in autonomous systems.
6. **Future Research Directions**: The work opens several avenues for future research. This includes exploring more computationally efficient transformer variants (addressing the quadratic complexity in the number of nodes), investigating alternative MTRL learning algorithms (e.g., gradient averaging, task balancing), and further probing the types of structural biases that transformers learn implicitly from data. The preliminary zero-shot generalization results also warrant more detailed investigation into AMORPHEUS's transfer capabilities.
