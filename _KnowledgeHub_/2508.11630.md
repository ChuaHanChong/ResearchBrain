# Thyme: Think Beyond Images

> [!summary] Summary
> Thyme introduces a paradigm for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to enhance reasoning and perception by autonomously generating and executing code for image manipulation and computation. This approach achieves substantial performance improvements across nearly 20 benchmarks, frequently outperforming larger models in high-resolution perception tasks.

## Problem

- Existing open-source MLLMs offer limited functionality for visual information processing, typically restricted to simple cropping or generating auxiliary images with quality issues and high computational cost.
- Proprietary MLLMs demonstrate rich image manipulation and computational capabilities through code generation, a feature largely absent in open-source alternatives.
- MLLMs often struggle with precise high-resolution perception and complex multi-step reasoning tasks, due to static input interpretation and lack of dynamic interaction with visual data.

## Method

- Thyme enables MLLMs to autonomously generate and execute Python code for diverse image operations (e.g., cropping, rotation, contrast enhancement) and mathematical computations.
- A robust, secure ==external sandbox== is employed to execute the generated code, handle errors, and feed results back to the model for iterative reasoning and problem-solving.
- A ==two-stage training strategy== (Supervised Fine-Tuning followed by Reinforcement Learning) is used, featuring specialized data construction and ==GRPO-ATS== for efficient acquisition of tool-use capabilities.

## Results

- Thyme-7B achieves substantial performance improvements across nearly **20** benchmarks, notably outperforming larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-32B and InternVL3-8B in high-resolution perception tasks.
- Significant gains are observed in challenging reasoning domains, including MathVision Mini and LogicVista, by externalizing complex calculations into executable Python code.
- Ablation studies confirm the efficacy of masking sandbox content and the 'only last round' training strategy in SFT, while case studies visually demonstrate successful autonomous cropping, rotation, contrast enhancement, and complex calculations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Masking sandbox execution results during Supervised Fine-Tuning is crucial; it prevents the model from predicting outputs directly and forces it to learn underlying reasoning for code generation.
> - The GRPO-ATS (Adaptive Temperature Sampling) in RL, using `τ=0.0` for code generation and `τ=1.0` for text, effectively balances precision for executable code with exploration for natural language responses.
> - A composite reward function, which includes a consistency reward applied only for correct answers, is more effective for guiding complex reasoning than direct rewards for process quality or successful code execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "Thyme: Think Beyond Images" is situated at the forefront of multimodal large language model (MLLM) development, specifically addressing the evolving concept of how these models interact with visual information. It directly builds upon and seeks to transcend current paradigms of "thinking with images."

**Current Landscape:**
The paper identifies two main categories of existing approaches:
1. **Thinking with Generated Images:** Models create auxiliary images (ee.g., diagrams, sketches) to aid their reasoning. While beneficial for tasks requiring imagination or abstract visual cues (like solving geometric problems), this approach often suffers from limitations in image quality, which can compromise fine-grained details crucial for core visual perception tasks. Additionally, the computational cost and inference latency associated with image generation hinder real-time application.
2. **Thinking with Cropping:** Models are trained to identify and select relevant image regions (e.g., via bounding box outputs), which are then externally cropped and processed. This method improves perception accuracy and reduces hallucination. However, its functionality is typically limited to simple cropping, lacking the diverse capabilities of more advanced, proprietary models.

**The Gap Thyme Addresses:**
The core motivation for Thyme stems from the observation that no open-source MLLM currently offers the rich feature set and high autonomy demonstrated by proprietary models like OpenAI's O3 (OpenAI, 2025). These advanced proprietary systems can perform a wide array of image manipulations (cropping, rotation, contrast enhancement) and simultaneously enhance logical reasoning through code generation. Thyme aims to bridge this gap by introducing an open-source paradigm that allows MLLMs to *actively* manipulate visual information and perform computations through autonomously generated and executed code. This shifts the interaction from merely "thinking *with* images" to "thinking *beyond* images" by making the visual input a dynamic, manipulable entity within the reasoning loop.

By integrating diverse image operations and mathematical computations with an autonomous code execution framework, Thyme pushes MLLMs toward more sophisticated, human-like iterative problem-solving. It moves beyond static visual inputs or limited visual processing by enabling the model to dynamically interrogate, refine, and transform visual information as an integral part of its analytical process. This aligns with the broader trend in AI research to equip LLMs with tool-use capabilities, effectively transforming them into more versatile and powerful agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of "Thyme: Think Beyond Images" is to significantly advance the capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by enabling them to perform highly autonomous and diverse visual information processing and computational tasks through self-generated executable code. The authors explicitly outline four core principles guiding their vision:

1. **Rich Functionality:** To equip MLLMs with a wide array of common image operations—such as cropping, scaling, rotating, and contrast enhancement—alongside the capacity for complex mathematical computations. The motivation here is to overcome the functional limitations of existing open-source MLLMs that are often restricted to simple visual operations like bounding box-based cropping.
2. **High Autonomy:** To allow Thyme to independently assess problem complexity, decide whether image operations or computations are necessary, determine which specific operations to apply, and execute these functions by dynamically generating code. This principle is crucial for moving beyond human-guided tool use towards a more intelligent, self-directed AI.
3. **Efficient End-to-End Training:** To develop a training methodology that minimizes computational costs while rapidly unlocking these diverse model capabilities. The goal is to achieve broad functionality without exorbitant resource demands, making advanced multimodal tool use more accessible. The stated SFT stage requiring only 200 GPU hours highlights this efficiency objective.
4. **Significant and Stable Performance Gains:** To demonstrate that the Thyme paradigm leads to consistent and substantial improvements across a broad spectrum of tasks. The motivation is to prove the practical utility of their approach, particularly in challenging high-resolution perception and complex reasoning scenarios, which are known weaknesses in many MLLMs.

In essence, the motivation for Thyme stems from the desire to create an open-source MLLM that can rival or exceed the multimodal reasoning and interactive capabilities of proprietary state-of-the-art models (like OpenAI O3), by actively manipulating and computing with visual data rather than merely interpreting static images. This aims to unlock a new level of intelligent interaction where the model can proactively transform its visual input to facilitate deeper understanding and problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Thyme's methodology is built upon a novel paradigm where multimodal large language models (MLLMs) autonomously generate and execute code to perform image processing and computational operations. This is achieved through a robust pipeline and a two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline of Thyme

As depicted in Figure 2, the pipeline involves an iterative interaction between the Thyme model and an external sandbox:
- **User Input:** The process begins with a user providing an image and a question.
- **Model Reasoning & Decision:** The MLLM first analyzes the problem and decides whether code generation is necessary.
 - If the problem is simple or already resolved by previous operations, the model directly provides an answer.
 - If code is required, the model autonomously generates Python code. This code can perform single or combined operations such as cropping, zooming, rotation, contrast enhancement, or mathematical computations. The input parameters for these operations (e.g., coordinates, factors, angles) are also determined by the model.
- **Sandbox Execution:** The generated code is sent to an external, secure sandbox environment.
- **Result Feedback:** The sandbox executes the code and returns the execution results (e.g., a processed image, a numerical computation result) to the model.
- **Iterative Interaction:** The model then uses these results for further reasoning, potentially generating more code or providing a final answer, continuing until the question is resolved.

#### 3.2. Sandbox Building

A critical component of Thyme is its specialized sandbox, designed for security, robustness, and to reduce the model's coding burden:
- **Security and Robustness:**
 - **Dangerous Operation Filtering:** Code is scanned for potentially harmful operations (e.g., `remove`, `unlink`) and execution is skipped if detected.
 - **Time Limits:** A maximum execution time (empirically 10 seconds) is enforced to prevent infinite loops or excessive computation.
- **Reducing Model's Coding Burden:**
 - **Automatic Formatting:** Python's `autopep8` is used for code formatting and indentation.
 - **Parameter Correction:** The `ast` module inspects code for variables like `box` or `coord` and adjusts them to fit image boundaries, preventing runtime errors.
 - **Preset Variables:** Common local variables (`image_path`) and packages (`cv2`) are pre-imported.
 - **Output Handling:** The sandbox checks for new variables indicating processed outputs (`processed_path`) to ensure results are correctly captured even if not explicitly defined in the model's output.
 - **Historical Context:** Variables from previous code executions are recorded and provided in multi-round invocations to maintain context.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Strategy

Thyme employs a comprehensive two-stage training strategy: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Stage 1: Thyme-SFT Cold Start**
This stage focuses on teaching the model foundational code generation and execution abilities.
- **SFT Data Construction:** A high-quality dataset of approximately 500K samples is curated from over 4 million raw sources, categorized by increasing complexity:
 - **No Code Generation (100k samples):** Trains the model to answer simple questions directly.
 - **Real-World Thyme Data (50k QA pairs):** Requires code-based operations, verified by sandbox execution and an auxiliary MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), then manually screened.
 - **Manually Constructed Thyme Data:**
 - **Cropping (28k samples from V\*):** Uses ground truth bounding boxes to teach rationales and code for region-of-interest extraction.
 - **Rotation (14k samples):** Images randomly rotated (30° to 335°) with ground truth angles to teach rotation code.
 - **Low-Contrast Enhancement (10k samples from LLaVA-OV-Image OCR):** Teaches contrast enhancement for improved text recognition.
 - **Computational Code (15k samples from MM-Eureka, Retool):** Generated from Gemini 2.5 Pro solutions and transformed by GPT-4o into lean, executable math code.
 - **Multi-Round Conversation:** Manually constructed data teaches error correction (e.g., re-cropping based on feedback) and further refinement of operations.
- **SFT Training Strategy:**
 - **System and User Prompts:** Strict templates (Tables 1, 2) define task format and expected output.
 - **Train on the Last Round Only:** For multi-round dialogues, only the final round's logic and results are used for loss calculation, preventing the model from learning suboptimal intermediate steps.
 - **Sandbox Content Exclusion:** Sandbox execution results (`<sandbox_output>`) are masked during loss computation to avoid direct prediction by the model.
 - **Math Data Annealing:** Math data, being smaller, is fine-tuned separately with a lower learning rate after initial training on image-related data, and all rounds are learned (not just the last) due to weaker inter-round correlations.

**Stage 2: Thyme-RL**
This stage refines the model's decision-making and execution precision using reinforcement learning.
- **RL Data Construction and Annotation:**
 - **Public Data Selection:** Filtered from existing datasets (V\*, arXivQA, ThinkLite-VL) for perception and reasoning.
 - **Complex Image Data & Question Construction:** Manually collected 30,000 high-resolution images (>2048 pixels) with questions targeting small, difficult-to-recognize objects (<5% image resolution), requiring zoom for identification. These questions cover OCR, attribute, positional, quantity, object recognition, and chart understanding. Data undergoes rigorous cross-checking and expert review.
- **RL Algorithm Design:**
 - **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):** An on-policy variant of GRPO (Shao et al., 2024b) is used. It samples groups of interaction trajectories, calculates an advantage function based on final rewards, and updates the policy, with a KL divergence penalty to maintain stability.
 - **GRPO-ATS (Adaptive Temperature Sampling):** A key innovation. It dynamically adjusts the sampling temperature:
 - `τ = 0.0` for **code generation** to ensure precision and validity, minimizing errors that could lead to execution failure. This also improves sample efficiency and prevents the model from avoiding code generation due to high error rates.
 - `τ = 1.0` for **text reasoning** to encourage exploration and diverse linguistic expression.
 - **Sampling Optimization:** An early termination mechanism uses the Rabin-Karp rolling hash to detect repetitive outputs (substrings repeating and exceeding 50% of output length), immediately halting sampling to save computational resources. Dialogue turns are also capped.
- **Reward Function Design:** The final reward `r` is a composite, emphasizing correct answers:
 - `Final Reward r = Result Reward × (1 + 0.5 × Consistency Reward) + 0.5 × Formatting Reward.`
 - **Formatting Reward:** Encourages adherence to `<think></think>` and `<answer></answer>` structure.
 - **Result Reward:** Uses a hybrid approach: rule-based comparison for strict answers, and an auxiliary MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B) for semantic equivalence in open-ended cases.
 - **Consistency Reward:** An auxiliary MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) evaluates if the final answer logically follows from the reasoning steps. Crucially, the consistency bonus is applied *only when the model's answer is correct*, preventing models from gaming the reward by being consistently wrong.

The base model used is Qwen 2.5 VL 7B. Training is performed on 32 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, requiring ~224 GPU hours for SFT (including 8 for annealing) and over 1200 GPU hours for RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental analysis and ablation studies demonstrate that Thyme yields significant and consistent performance gains across a wide array of benchmarks, validating its novel approach to multimodal reasoning.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Table 3)

- **Significant and Consistent Gains:** Thyme-7B consistently achieves substantial performance improvements over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline across nearly 20 benchmarks, spanning perception, reasoning, and general tasks. The gold-colored font in Table 3 highlights these improvements.
- **Outperforming Larger Models in Perception:** Notably, Thyme-7B frequently outperforms even much larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-32B and InternVL3-8B in perception tasks (e.g., HRbench-4K/8K FSP/FCP, MME-Real Perception). This suggests that Thyme's dynamic image manipulation capabilities, akin to "test-time scaling," are more effective for enhancing perception than simply increasing model size.
- **Enhanced Reasoning:** By converting complex computations into executable code, Thyme achieves significant improvements in various reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVision Mini, MathVista Mini, LogicVista, WeMath). While larger models still show advantages in reasoning (suggesting inherent knowledge scaling), Thyme enhances visual recognition quality and helps externalize complex computation.
- **General Task Improvements:** Thyme demonstrates robust gains in general benchmarks, including a substantial reduction in hallucination (7.3% overall improvement in Hallucination benchmark) and improvements in MMStar, MMVet Hard, and Chart QA. OCR Bench and BLINK show minor or slightly negative changes, indicating areas where the base model is already highly proficient or where code interaction offers less benefit.

#### 4.2. Deep Dive into Perception Tasks (Table 4, MME-Realworld example)

- Thyme-7B shows remarkable improvements in challenging perception and reasoning domains where the baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) performs poorly.
- For "Monitoring" tasks, Thyme improves perception by 27.14% and reasoning by 81.57%.
- For "Autonomous Driving," perception improves by 64.99% and reasoning by 33.16%.
- In contrast, for tasks where the baseline already has high accuracy (e.g., OCR, Diagram and Table), Thyme's improvements are more modest (1.56% to 11.04%). This indicates that Thyme's capabilities are most impactful in areas of higher difficulty and complexity.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Training Strategies (Table 5)

- **Importance of Masking Sandbox Content:** Masking sandbox labels and outputs significantly enhances performance, preventing the model from learning to directly predict sandbox results.
- **"Only Last Round" Strategy:** For multi-round interactions, training only on the final round prevents the model from learning inefficient patterns (e.g., making an initial suboptimal attempt and then correcting it).
- **Code Comments:** Removing comments from SFT data negatively impacts performance, suggesting that the process of generating comments aids the model in developing a more logical understanding of code.
- **Math Data Annealing:** While not showing dramatic overall metric gains compared to "Only Last Round," this specialized annealing effectively teaches the model to generate computation-related code, which is crucial for its functionality.

#### 4.4. Influence of RL Reward Design (Table 6)

- **Consistency Reward's Value:** The consistency reward consistently leads to performance improvements. This ensures that the model's final answer is logically derived from its preceding reasoning steps, especially when combined with the rule that consistency is only rewarded for correct answers.
- **Ineffectiveness of Process and Code Rewards:** Directly rewarding the "quality of the thinking process" (Process Reward) or the "proportion of successfully executed code" (Code Reward) did not yield positive gains and sometimes had a negative impact. The process reward was found to be subjective and easily "hacked," while the code reward could incentivize unnecessary or trivial code generation, adding overhead without real benefit. This highlights the complexity of designing effective reward functions in RL.

#### 4.5. Analysis of RL Learning Process (Figure 7)

- **Response Length Optimization:** The average response length rapidly decreases early in RL training, indicating that the model quickly learns to avoid generating unnecessary code for simple tasks that can be answered directly.
- **Improved Accuracy and Consistency:** The result reward shows a steady upward trend, confirming effective learning. Concurrently, the consistency reward significantly increases, moving from an initial average of 0.15 to nearly 0.35 while the result reward converges to ~0.7. This demonstrates that RL, particularly with the consistency reward, effectively mitigates contradictions between the model's reasoning and its answers.

#### 4.6. Case Studies (Figures 8-13)

The provided case studies visually illustrate Thyme's capabilities:
- **Cropping & Zooming:** Successfully identifies small, distant text/numbers on signs, crops/zooms relevant areas, and extracts correct information. Also demonstrates reasoning to conclude an object is *not* present after zooming.
- **Rotation & Contrast Enhancement:** Correctly reorients a tilted image to improve OCR and enhances low-contrast text for better readability and extraction.
- **Complex Calculations:** Successfully generates and executes Python code to solve multi-step mathematical problems derived from image-based data.

#### 4.7. Bad Cases (Figures 14-16)

The authors also present failure cases, acknowledging limitations:
- **Complex Problems Without Coding:** Thyme occasionally fails to generate necessary code for high-resolution images, answering directly despite the need for further visual processing.
- **Unuseful Coding:** For trivially simple calculations, Thyme sometimes opts to write code, introducing unnecessary overhead, and can even make errors in variable assignment within the code.
- **Inaccurate Cropping:** While sometimes reaching the correct final answer, the model may crop an irrelevant region during its thinking process, indicating imperfect tool use.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Thyme: Think Beyond Images" represents a significant leap forward in the capabilities of multimodal large language models, moving beyond passive visual understanding to active, autonomous visual information manipulation and computation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Redefining Multimodal Interaction:** Thyme's core paradigm, "Think Beyond Images," fundamentally redefines how MLLMs interact with visual data. Instead of merely consuming static images, models can now actively interrogate, transform, and refine visual information through self-generated code. This elevates MLLMs closer to human-like problem-solving, where iterative analysis and dynamic information processing are key.
2. **Bridging the Gap with Proprietary Models:** By enabling a rich set of image manipulations (cropping, rotation, contrast enhancement) and mathematical computations via executable code within an open-source framework, Thyme directly challenges and aims to match or exceed the functionalities previously exclusive to proprietary models like OpenAI O3. This democratizes access to advanced multimodal AI capabilities, fostering broader research and application in the scientific community.
3. **Enhanced Performance in Challenging Scenarios:** The demonstrated significant and consistent performance gains across nearly 20 benchmarks, especially in high-resolution perception and complex reasoning tasks, highlights Thyme's practical utility. Its ability to drastically improve performance in areas like "Monitoring" and "Autonomous Driving" (over 25% improvement) indicates its potential to address critical weaknesses in MLLMs dealing with real-world, high-fidelity visual inputs.
4. **Robust and Efficient Training Methodology:** The two-stage SFT+RL strategy, coupled with novel components like GRPO-ATS (Adaptive Temperature Sampling) and a carefully designed reward function, provides a robust recipe for teaching MLLMs complex tool use. The efficiency of the SFT stage (200 GPU hours for foundational capabilities) is particularly noteworthy, suggesting a pathway for more scalable development of advanced MLLMs. GRPO-ATS, by balancing exploration in text generation with precision in code generation, offers a generalizable technique for training models that require both creative reasoning and strict adherence to syntax/logic.
5. **Catalyst for Future Research and Development:** The commitment to open-sourcing the datasets, sandbox, and code is a major contribution. This allows researchers worldwide to build upon Thyme, experiment with its components, and develop new applications or refine its methodologies. It can accelerate progress in areas such as advanced visual analytics, scientific data interpretation, medical imaging, and robot perception.
6. **Insights into Tool Use and Reasoning:** The ablation studies on SFT strategies and RL reward design offer valuable insights into the intricacies of training MLLMs for tool use. The findings regarding the limited utility of subjective "process rewards" and simple "code execution rewards" highlight the need for nuanced, task-aligned reward functions (like the consistency reward) to guide complex reasoning effectively. This informs future research into human preference alignment and multimodal reinforcement learning.
7. **Addressing Current Limitations:** By acknowledging its limitations—such as dependence on the base model's localization and code generation precision, and the lack of specific benchmarks for certain image manipulations—Thyme also clearly outlines critical avenues for future research. This includes integrating stronger foundation models, developing specialized evaluation metrics, and refining tool-use mechanisms to be more robust and adaptable.

In conclusion, Thyme marks a pivotal moment in multimodal AI, shifting the paradigm from passive observation to active, intelligent interaction with visual information through code. Its open-source nature, coupled with demonstrated performance and robust methodology, positions it as a foundational work with the potential to significantly influence the trajectory of MLLM research and application in the years to come.
