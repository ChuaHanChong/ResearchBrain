# AnyRecon: Arbitrary-View 3D Reconstruction with Video Diffusion Model

> [!summary] Summary
> AnyRecon, a framework developed by Shanghai AI Lab and collaborators, enables high-quality, large-scale 3D reconstruction from sparse, arbitrary input views by iteratively generating novel views using a video diffusion model and refining geometry. It consistently outperforms baselines on metrics like PSNR and SSIM, while achieving a 20x speedup in inference for novel view generation.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D reconstruction methods require dense multi-view images, limiting their application to sparse, real-world data such as handheld videos or internet media.
- Sparse-view 3D reconstruction is an ill-posed problem, leading to extensive unobserved regions, geometric ambiguities, and challenges in synthesizing plausible details in disoccluded areas.
- Existing diffusion-based novel view synthesis approaches often condition on only one or two captured frames, restricting global scene context and struggling to maintain precise geometric consistency or scalability for long trajectories and unordered inputs.

## Method

- **AnyRecon** is an iterative framework that alternates between generating segments of novel view trajectories using a ==video diffusion model== and refining a global 3D geometry memory with the newly synthesized views.
- Introduces an ==unordered contextual video diffusion== module with ==non-compressive latent encoding== and a ==global scene memory== to flexibly condition on an arbitrary number of captured views while preserving fine-grained spatial alignment.
- Incorporates ==geometry-aware conditioning== (3D geometry memory update, geometry-driven view selection) and efficiency optimizations (==context-window sparse attention==, ==4-step diffusion distillation==) to ensure scene-level consistency and scalability.

## Results

- AnyRecon consistently achieved superior quantitative performance compared to baselines (Difix3D+, ViewCrafter, Uni3C) on the DL3DV and Tanks and Temples datasets, showing higher PSNR (e.g., **20.95 vs 17.88** on DL3DV interpolation) and SSIM, along with lower LPIPS.
- The framework demonstrated a **20x reduction in inference latency**, generating a 40-frame sequence in **90 seconds** (compared to **1820 seconds** for the dense attention baseline), primarily due to 4-step distillation and context-window sparse attention.
- Qualitatively, AnyRecon generated plausible new content, completed missing regions, and maintained both structural integrity and appearance consistency for seamless view interpolation and long-range extrapolation across diverse scenes, surpassing baselines that often exhibited artifacts or inconsistencies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving a non-compressive latent representation in video diffusion models is crucial for maintaining high-frequency spatial and geometric details, particularly when handling sparse views with large viewpoint gaps.
> - Explicitly grounding the generative process in an incrementally updated 3D geometry memory, guided by geometry-aware view selection, effectively mitigates geometric drift and ensures scene-level consistency across extended and arbitrary trajectories.
> - Significant computational speedups (e.g., 20x faster inference) can be achieved in high-fidelity diffusion-based generation through a combination of efficient sparse attention mechanisms and robust diffusion distillation without substantial quality degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses fundamental challenges in novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction, problems central to computer vision and graphics with applications in virtual environments, augmented reality, and visual effects. Traditional neural reconstruction methods, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), typically require dense multi-view images captured under controlled conditions. This requirement limits their applicability to real-world data, which often consists of sparse, irregular captures like handheld videos or internet media.

Sparse-view 3D reconstruction is an ill-posed problem due to extensive unobserved regions and geometric ambiguities stemming from limited input data. Prior efforts to mitigate this involved incorporating geometric priors, regularization techniques, or auxiliary supervision from pre-trained models (e.g., depth maps, normal maps). While these methods improve reconstruction to some extent, they often struggle with synthesizing plausible details in disoccluded areas, motivating the shift towards generative models.

Recent advancements in diffusion models have been leveraged to synthesize novel views and infer 3D structure. Initial approaches used image generative models, while subsequent work adopted video generation models to enhance cross-view coherence through temporal modeling. Within diffusion-based methods, some condition models on projected point cloud renderings, offering coarse geometric guidance but limiting real-image conditioning to one or two frames. This constraint can weaken appearance fidelity and global context. Other approaches rely solely on RGB images and camera poses, implicitly learning geometric consistency, but may struggle with precise pose alignment and spatial consistency, particularly with irregular observations.

AnyRecon positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the limitations of existing diffusion-based sparse-view reconstruction methods. It aims to achieve high-quality and large-scale 3D reconstruction from arbitrary and unordered sparse inputs, addressing the constraint of limited reference views and the challenges of maintaining geometric consistency across wide viewpoint changes and long trajectories. The work integrates explicit geometric control with a video diffusion model designed for flexible conditioning cardinality and scalability, distinguishing it from methods that either rely on minimal reference views or implicitly learn geometry without explicit guidance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable high-quality and large-scale 3D reconstruction from sparse, arbitrary, and unordered input views. The work is motivated by several challenges inherent in current 3D reconstruction techniques, particularly when applied to real-world, casually captured data:

1. **Addressing Sparse-View Limitations:** Traditional neural reconstruction methods require dense input views, which are not typical of real-world captures. Existing sparse-view methods often struggle to synthesize plausible details in unobserved or disoccluded regions. Diffusion models offer a potential solution by leveraging generative priors to hallucinate consistent geometry and appearance, but their application to sparse inputs presents challenges.

2. **Enhancing Generative Model Conditioning:** Current diffusion-based novel view synthesis approaches typically condition on only one or two capture frames. This limited conditioning restricts the model's ability to capture global scene context and maintain fine-grained appearance fidelity. The motivation is to allow the diffusion model to flexibly condition on an arbitrary number of captured RGB images alongside geometric priors.

3. **Handling Arbitrary and Unordered Inputs:** Existing video diffusion frameworks are often optimized for sequential inputs, relying on temporal continuity assumptions. Real-world sparse captures, however, can be unordered and feature large viewpoint gaps, making these frameworks suboptimal. A key objective is to develop a diffusion model that can handle non-sequential, arbitrary inputs without compromising geometric consistency.

4. **Ensuring Geometric Consistency and Control:** While some diffusion models implicitly learn geometric consistency, this often results in challenges with precise pose alignment and spatial coherence, limiting their use in robust 3D reconstruction. The work aims to preserve explicit geometric control throughout the generation process, ensuring that synthesized views adhere to a consistent 3D structure.

5. **Achieving Scalability for Large Scenes and Trajectories:** Reconstructing large, complex scenes from sparse inputs requires processing long trajectories that cannot fit into a diffusion model's memory at once. This necessitates a robust iterative reconstruction strategy capable of segment-by-segment processing while maintaining global consistency. The objective includes developing a framework that is computationally efficient and can scale to extended sequences and diverse scene layouts.

6. **Optimizing Computational Efficiency:** Supporting flexible input cardinality and high-fidelity generation can lead to significant computational demands, particularly with the expanded sequence length in diffusion transformers. The motivation is to introduce architectural and sampling optimizations to reduce complexity and accelerate inference, making the framework practical for real-world applications.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between advanced generative models and the practical demands of 3D reconstruction from sparse, uncontrolled real-world captures, offering a scalable, robust, and geometrically consistent solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnyRecon is a scalable framework for sparse-view 3D reconstruction that supports arbitrary and unordered inputs while maintaining geometric consistency across wide viewpoint changes. The method operates through an iterative loop that alternates between diffusion-based trajectory generation and geometry refinement using the newly generated views.

The framework comprises three main stages.

#### 3.1. Initial Geometry Construction

- All available input views are organized into a `captured view bank` (I_cap).
- An initial 3D geometry memory (M_geo) is established from I_cap using a feed-forward point map estimation method, such as VGGT or $\pi_3$.

#### 3.2. Novel View Generation

- The target novel view trajectory (V_novel) is divided into smaller segments for efficiency.
- For each segment, `geometry-aware retrieval` is performed to select spatially informative views (I_sel) from I_cap that are relevant to the current segment.
- These selected views (I_sel), along with point-cloud renderings (I_render) and visibility masks (M_t) derived from the current M_geo, are fed into the `unordered contextual video diffusion` module.
- The diffusion module synthesizes novel views ($\hat{I}_{novel}$) for the target trajectory segment.
- To manage computational complexity, `context-window sparse attention` and a `4-step diffusion distillation strategy` are integrated.

#### 3.3. Geometry Updating

- 3D geometry from the newly synthesized views ($\hat{I}_{novel}$) is extracted using a point map estimation model ($\pi_3$) in conjunction with the original captured views.
- This newly reconstructed geometry replaces and updates the global M_geo.
- This updated M_geo then guides the retrieval and generation steps for subsequent trajectory segments, closing the iterative loop.

The core technical components are detailed as follows:

#### 3.4. Unordered Contextual Video Diffusion

This module is designed to handle diverse, non-sequential inputs and maintain precise spatial alignment.
- **Global Scene Memory:** To support an arbitrary number of conditioning views, retrieved reference views (I_sel) are prepended to the input sequence, serving as a persistent global key-value (KV) memory cache within the video diffusion transformer. This allows the model to treat capture views as a flexible 3D memory, enabling generation along arbitrary spatial trajectories, decoupled from the capture sequence.
- **Non-Compressive Latent Encoding:** Unlike traditional video diffusion models that use temporal compression (e.g., 3D-VAEs) relying on temporal smoothness, AnyRecon employs a frame-wise 2D VAE. This approach bypasses temporal pooling, preserving a one-to-one mapping between latent tokens and pixel coordinates. This design aims to prevent feature entanglement between disparate views in sparse-view scenarios with large viewpoint gaps, thereby maintaining precise spatial-temporal alignment.

#### 3.5. Efficient Sparse Attention and 4-Step Diffusion Sampling

These optimizations address computational efficiency for high-fidelity synthesis across extended sequences.
- **Context-Window Sparse Attention:** To mitigate the O(L^2) computational complexity arising from expanded sequence length (L), this mechanism restricts the receptive field of each frame in the target trajectory to a local temporal window (e.g., 8 preceding and 8 succeeding adjacent views) and the selectively retrieved, geometry-aligned reference views (I_sel). This focuses attention on visually relevant regions, enhancing scalability.
- **4-Step Diffusion Sampling:** The pre-trained Wan video diffusion model is distilled into a student network using Distribution Matching Distillation. This process compresses the denoising process into just 4 sampling steps (from 50 steps), using a discretized noise schedule. The optimization minimizes the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the student's generated distribution and the real distribution, approximated via score difference between a frozen teacher and a trainable critic. This achieves a reported 20x speedup in generation.

#### 3.6. Geometry-Aware Conditioning Strategy

This strategy couples the diffusion process with an explicit 3D representation to ensure scene-level consistency over long trajectories.
- **3D Geometry Memory Update:** The M_geo is maintained as an incrementally updated point cloud. After synthesizing a segment of the novel trajectory, 3D geometry from these newly generated views, combined with original views, is extracted using $\pi_3$. This updated geometry replaces the existing M_geo, integrating new scene details and anchoring subsequent segments to a spatially consistent global structure. This mechanism is designed to prevent geometric drift across extended trajectories.
- **Geometry-Driven View Selection:** For large-scale environments where the capture view bank is extensive, an informative subset of reference views must be selected. Instead of relying on Field-of-View (FOV) or image-level similarity heuristics, AnyRecon performs retrieval based on the current 3D geometry memory (M_geo). M_geo is rendered from the target novel viewpoint to generate a visibility index map, which identifies the source-view attribution for each visible 3D point. A score is computed for each candidate view based on the number of visible points originating from it. The top-k views by score are selected, ensuring that conditioning views provide direct geometric support for the current generation and improving robustness under occlusion.

#### 3.7. Implementation Details

- **Base Model:** Fine-tuning of the Wan2.1-I2V-14B model using LoRA with a rank of 32.
- **Training Stages:**
 1. Full self-attention fine-tuning for 100k iterations to adapt priors to the geometry-controlled input space.
 2. Sparse attention mechanism warm-up for 10k iterations (block size 2x8x8, attention to I_sel and 8 adjacent views).
 3. DMD2 distillation for 30k iterations to compress denoising to 4 steps.
- **Hardware and Optimizer:** 64 NVIDIA A800 GPUs, AdamW optimizer. Learning rate: 1e-4 (initial stages), 1e-5 (distillation).
- **Training Data:** DL3DV-10K dataset. Randomized conditioning sampling: first frame fixed, N $\in$ additional views sampled (50% from first 20 frames, 50% from entire 40-frame window).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates AnyRecon through extensive experiments, focusing on quantitative metrics and qualitative visualizations across various reconstruction settings.

#### 4.1. Comparison Results

- **Metrics:** Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS).
- **Baselines:** Difix3D+, ViewCrafter, and Uni3C.
- **Datasets:** DL3DV-Evaluation set (10 scenes) and Tanks and Temples Dataset (5 scenes).
- **Configurations:**
 - **Interpolation:** 1st, 21st, and 40th frames as captured views.
 - **Extrapolation:** 1st, 11th, 21st, and 31st frames as conditioning inputs.
- **Quantitative Performance (Table 1):**
 - AnyRecon consistently achieved higher PSNR and SSIM, and lower LPIPS values across both DL3DV and Tanks and Temples datasets, for both interpolation and extrapolation settings.
 - For DL3DV (Interpolation): AnyRecon recorded PSNR of 20.95, SSIM of 0.656, and LPIPS of 0.151, outperforming Difix3D+ (PSNR 17.88, SSIM 0.551, LPIPS 0.290), ViewCrafter (PSNR 15.86, SSIM 0.463, LPIPS 0.394), and Uni3C (PSNR 16.33, SSIM 0.471, LPIPS 0.319). Similar performance differentials were observed for extrapolation and on the Tanks and Temples dataset.
 - **Efficiency:** AnyRecon demonstrated significantly lower inference latency, requiring 105 seconds per 40-frame sequence (512x896 resolution) compared to Difix3D+ (1200s), ViewCrafter (170s), and Uni3C (340s).
- **Qualitative Observations (Figs. 6 & 7):**
 - Difix3D+ exhibited artifacts and struggled with large viewpoint gaps.
 - ViewCrafter and Uni3C, while leveraging video diffusion, showed limitations in aligning with captured observations, leading to geometric inconsistencies, loss of fine details, and color shifts.
 - AnyRecon was observed to complete missing regions, hallucinate plausible new content, and maintain both structural integrity and appearance consistency across diverse scenes, including seamless view interpolation and long-range extrapolation.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Temporal Compression (Table 2, Fig. 3c-e):**
 - Both full and partial temporal compression led to visual fidelity degradation, particularly in preserving fine-grained structural details (e.g., intricate metal grids appeared fractured or blurred). This was attributed to temporal down-sampling discarding high-frequency spatial information.
 - The non-compressive latent encoding used in AnyRecon maintained complete geometric details and sharp textures by operating at the original resolution of both rendered and captured views.
- **Distillation and Sparse Attention (Table 2, Fig. 3e,i,j):**
 - The dense attention baseline (50 diffusion steps) achieved the highest reconstruction quality but was computationally expensive (1820s inference time for 40 frames).
 - Applying 4-step distillation reduced latency to 140s with a marginal drop in PSNR (0.24 dB).
 - Integrating sparse attention further reduced inference time to 90s (a 20x speedup compared to the original dense baseline). While this introduced a slight decrease in metrics (e.g., PSNR 20.95), the visual quality remained competitive, and the computational efficiency significantly improved.
- **Global Scene Memory (Table 3, Fig. 8):**
 - A baseline without global scene memory (conditioning only on a single initial frame, but with geometry from all views) showed a noticeable drop in performance (PSNR 20.18 vs 20.95, SSIM 0.634 vs 0.656, LPIPS 0.205 vs 0.151).
 - Qualitatively, this baseline struggled to recover high-fidelity textures, resulting in missing details and color shifts (e.g., on tableware and background walls).
 - The global scene memory, by maintaining raw captured views, allowed the diffusion network to query uncorrupted, high-frequency textural details, effectively suppressing geometric artifacts and restoring complex structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnyRecon presents a framework that addresses several limitations in sparse-view 3D reconstruction using diffusion models, offering advancements in flexibility, geometric consistency, and efficiency.

The key contributions and their potential impact include:

1. **Flexible Reconstruction from Arbitrary Inputs:** By enabling robust 3D reconstruction from arbitrary and unordered conditioning views, AnyRecon expands the applicability of neural reconstruction methods. This allows for processing real-world, casually captured data such as handheld videos or internet media, which typically do not conform to the dense, controlled acquisition setups required by traditional methods. This flexibility can democratize access to 3D content creation, making it easier to convert everyday captures into explorable 3D scenes.

2. **Enhanced Geometric Consistency through Explicit Control:** The introduction of a geometry-aware conditioning strategy, including a 3D Geometry Memory and geometry-driven capture-view retrieval, establishes a closed loop between generation and reconstruction. This design ensures that the diffusion process is continuously guided by spatially informative observations, mitigating issues like geometric drift, inconsistent rendering, and error accumulation across extended trajectories. This explicit geometric grounding can lead to more accurate and visually coherent 3D models, particularly for complex and large-scale environments.

3. **Improved Scalability and Efficiency:** The combination of non-compressive latent encoding, context-window sparse attention, and 4-step diffusion distillation significantly reduces computational overhead and accelerates inference. Achieving a 20x speedup compared to a dense baseline, while maintaining competitive visual quality, makes AnyRecon more practical for real-world applications that require faster processing or operation on more constrained hardware. This efficiency is crucial for processing long-trajectory video sequences and large-scale scenes segment-by-segment.

4. **Broader Applications:** The ability to generate high-fidelity, geometrically consistent novel views from sparse inputs has implications for various fields. These include creating immersive virtual environments, enhancing augmented reality experiences, generating visual effects, and facilitating 3D content creation from diverse sources. The framework's robustness across irregular inputs, large viewpoint gaps, and long trajectories suggests its utility in applications where complete and consistent 3D models are needed from incomplete visual data.

The primary limitation noted, dependence on the quality of initial 3D geometric memory and potential failure with minimal view overlap, indicates areas for future research into robustness under extreme sparsity. However, the demonstrated advancements in handling flexible inputs, maintaining geometric coherence, and improving efficiency position AnyRecon as a notable contribution to the field of 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis.
