# PhysGen: Rigid-Body Physics-Grounded Image-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysGen, developed by researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, integrates rigid-body physics simulation into image-to-video generation to produce visually and physically realistic videos from a single input image. It enables controlled generation of object motions and interactions that adhere to physical laws, outperforming existing data-driven methods in physical plausibility and photorealism.

## Problem

- Current image-to-video (I2V) generation methods often produce visually appealing results but frequently fail to capture physically plausible object motions and interactions.
- Data-driven I2V approaches lack explicit physical understanding, leading to animations that exhibit physically inconsistent dynamics.
- Traditional graphics-based physics simulation struggles with visual realism and cannot easily operate on real-world images, limiting its applicability for I2V generation.

## Method

- **PhysGen** employs a ==perception-simulation-rendering== pipeline that integrates vision foundation models, a physics engine, and generative diffusion models.
- A perception module, leveraging models like ==GPT-4V== and ==Grounded-SAM==, analyzes the input image to infer object properties, geometries, and physical parameters (e.g., mass, friction).
- A rigid-body physics engine simulates object motion based on inferred properties and user-specified forces, followed by a generative refinement step using a pretrained ==video diffusion model== to transform simulation results into photorealistic video frames.

## Results

- PhysGen successfully simulates diverse physical phenomena, including rolling, falling, collisions, and complex multi-object dynamics, from various input images.
- The system enables fine-grained control over object motions by allowing users to specify different initial forces, resulting in varied but physically plausible outcomes.
- Human evaluation studies demonstrate that PhysGen significantly outperforms existing I2V methods in both physical realism and photorealism, generating consistent and realistic physical motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining large vision models for scene understanding with explicit physics simulation bridges the gap between data-driven video generation and traditional graphics-based simulation.
> - Inferring physical properties and object geometries from a single image, combined with a training-free physics simulation, enables the creation of physics-grounded videos.
> - Guiding generative diffusion models with physics simulation ensures both visual realism and adherence to physical laws, producing more consistent and plausible object motions than purely data-driven methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Creating realistic videos from still images that obey the laws of physics has long been a challenge in computer vision. Current image-to-video (I2V) generation methods often produce visually appealing results but frequently fail to capture physically plausible object motions and interactions. PhysGen addresses this limitation by integrating rigid-body physics simulation into the video generation process.

PhysGen, developed by researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, takes a novel approach to I2V generation by combining the reasoning capabilities of large vision models with explicit physics simulation and high-quality video rendering. The system enables users to generate physically plausible videos from a single input image by specifying forces or other control parameters, producing results that are both visually and physically realistic.

### 2. Research Context

Image-to-video generation has seen significant advances with the rise of diffusion models and other deep learning techniques. However, these methods typically lack explicit physical understanding, which often leads to animations that look visually pleasing but exhibit physically implausible motions.

The field has broadly developed along two separate paths:

1. **Data-driven approaches**: Methods like DynamiCrafter, I2VGen-XL, and SEINE utilize large-scale training on video datasets to learn temporal dynamics implicitly. While producing visually impressive results, these approaches often fail to capture consistent object physics.

2. **Graphics-based simulation**: Traditional approaches that use physics engines provide precise control and realistic dynamics but struggle with visual realism and cannot easily operate on real-world images.

PhysGen bridges this gap by leveraging both approaches—using large vision models to understand image content and physical properties, simulating physics explicitly, and then generating realistic video frames through diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology

PhysGen's architecture consists of three core components that work together to create physically grounded videos from still images:

The pipeline follows a "perception-simulation-rendering" workflow that integrates vision foundation models, physics simulation, and generative diffusion models:

1. **Perception Module**: Analyzes the input image to infer object properties, geometries, and physical parameters
2. **Dynamics Simulation**: Simulates physics-based motion of objects under user-specified forces and interactions
3. **Rendering and Refinement**: Transforms the simulation results into realistic-looking video frames

### 4. Perception Module

The perception module is responsible for understanding the content of the input image and extracting the physical properties needed for simulation. This module employs several state-of-the-art vision models:

1. **Segmentation**: GPT-4V is prompted to identify object categories present in the image. Grounded-SAM then performs instance-level segmentation to extract precise object masks.

2. **Physical Properties Reasoning**: GPT-4V infers physical parameters such as mass, elasticity, and friction coefficients based on the object's visual characteristics. For example:

```
Object: Glass Ball
Mass: 0.5 kg (Medium-light for a small glass ball)
Elasticity: 0.7 (Glass is rigid but will bounce)
Friction Coefficient: 0.2 (Glass is smooth, low friction against surfaces)
```

3. **Geometry Primitives**: Objects are approximated as either circular or polygonal shapes suitable for physics simulation.

4. **Intrinsic Decomposition**: Surface normals, albedo, and scene lighting are estimated to enable realistic relighting when objects move.

5. **Background Inpainting**: The system recovers the complete background by inpainting areas occluded by foreground objects.

### 5. Dynamics Simulation

The dynamics simulation stage uses the physical parameters extracted during perception to create a physics-based animation of how objects would move under specified forces. The simulation:

1. Represents each object as a rigid body with the inferred mass, friction, and elasticity
2. Integrates Newton's equations of motion using the following formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x}_{t+1} &= \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{v}_t \Delta t + \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{a}_t \Delta t^2 \\
\mathbf{v}_{t+1} &= \mathbf{v}_t + \mathbf{a}_t \Delta t
\end{aligned}
$$

3. Models collisions between objects using impulse-based collision resolution
4. Simulates friction using Coulomb's model of friction
5. Incorporates gravity and user-specified forces

The simulation takes place in 2D image space, which allows PhysGen to operate efficiently while capturing the most important aspects of object dynamics for the purpose of video generation.

### 6. Rendering and Refinement

The final stage transforms the physics simulation into a photorealistic video through a three-step process:

1. **Composition**: Foreground objects are transformed according to the simulation results and alpha-blended with the inpainted background.

2. **Relighting**: A Lambertian shading model is used to adjust the appearance of objects based on their new orientations:

$$I = k_d (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})$$

Where $I$ is the pixel intensity, $k_d$ is the albedo, $\mathbf{n}$ is the surface normal, and $\mathbf{l}$ is the light direction.

3. **Generative Refinement**: A pretrained video diffusion model enhances the visual quality by adding realistic details such as shadows, reflections, and subtle motion effects.

The refinement step is guided by the physics simulation to ensure that the generated video remains faithful to the simulated dynamics while enhancing visual realism. This is achieved through a fusion weight that balances physics constraints with the generative model's output.

### 7. Results and Evaluation

PhysGen demonstrates impressive capabilities in generating physics-based videos across a variety of scenarios:

1. **Diverse Physics Phenomena**: The system successfully simulates various physical interactions including rolling, falling, collisions, and complex multi-object dynamics.

2. **Control Flexibility**: The approach enables fine-grained control over object motions by allowing users to specify different initial forces, resulting in different but physically plausible outcomes.

3. **Realistic Motion and Appearance**: The generated videos exhibit both physically plausible motion and high visual quality, with realistic lighting effects, shadows, and temporal consistency.

### 8. Comparisons with Existing Approaches

PhysGen outperforms existing state-of-the-art I2V methods in both physical realism and photorealism. Human evaluation studies clearly demonstrate the superiority of PhysGen's approach:

The comparison with current methods reveals:

1. **SEINE, DynamiCrafter, I2VGen-XL**: While these data-driven approaches can generate visually appealing videos, they frequently violate physics constraints and fail to produce consistent object motions.

2. **PhysGen (Ours)**: By integrating explicit physics simulation, PhysGen generates videos that are both visually realistic and physically plausible, with objects exhibiting correct motion trajectories, appropriate bouncing behavior, and realistic interactions.

Quantitative metrics further support PhysGen's advantages, with lower FID scores indicating better overall quality and higher human preference ratings for physical plausibility.

### 9. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive results, PhysGen has several limitations that suggest directions for future research:

1. **Rigid-Body Assumption**: The current implementation only handles rigid objects and cannot simulate deformable materials like fabric or fluid.

2. **2D Physics Simulation**: PhysGen operates in 2D image space, which limits its ability to capture complex 3D interactions and perspective effects.

3. **Generation Artifacts**: The system sometimes produces artifacts at the boundaries of moving objects or fails to maintain consistent object appearance throughout the video.

Future work could extend PhysGen to:
- Support deformable object simulation
- Incorporate full 3D scene understanding and physics simulation
- Improve the accuracy of physical parameter estimation
- Handle more complex scene understanding and multi-object interactions

### 10. Conclusion

PhysGen represents a significant advancement in image-to-video generation by bridging the gap between data-driven generation and physics-based simulation. The integration of large vision foundation models for perception, physics engines for simulation, and generative diffusion models for rendering enables the creation of videos that are both visually appealing and physically plausible.

The system's ability to generate controllable, physics-grounded videos from single images opens up new possibilities for applications in content creation, gaming, education, and simulation. By explicitly modeling physical properties and dynamics, PhysGen takes an important step toward more realistic and controllable video generation, demonstrating that combining model-based approaches with modern generative AI can achieve results that neither could accomplish alone.

PhysGen's training-free approach also provides a promising direction for future research, suggesting that leveraging the reasoning capabilities of large pretrained models and integrating them with physical priors can help overcome data limitations and produce more principled generative systems.
