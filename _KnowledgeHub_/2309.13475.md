# Detecting and Mitigating System-Level Anomalies of Vision-Based Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework integrates offline Hamilton-Jacobi reachability analysis to identify system-level failures in vision-based controllers with an online deep learning anomaly detector and a safety-preserving fallback mechanism. This system reduced the failure space, represented by the Backward Reachable Tube volume, and maintained high detection performance in an autonomous aircraft taxiing application.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems with vision-based controllers are susceptible to failures when encountering novel or out-of-distribution inputs, posing safety risks.
- Existing anomaly detection methods primarily focus on component-level errors, which are insufficient to prevent cascading system-level failures.
- Automatically acquiring diverse datasets of system-level anomalous inputs for high-dimensional visual data and varied environmental conditions is challenging.

## Method

- An offline phase uses ==Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) Reachability analysis== in a simulator to automatically discover and label system-level failure states (==Backward Reachable Tube - BRT==) and their corresponding visual inputs under diverse environmental conditions.
- A ==deep neural network (DNN) classifier== is trained on this automatically generated dataset of labeled safe and anomalous images to serve as an ==online anomaly detector (AD)==.
- During online operation, the trained AD continuously monitors input images; upon detection of an anomaly, the system switches from its primary vision-based controller to a ==safety-preserving fallback controller== to mitigate potential failures.

## Results

- The learned anomaly detector achieved high recall (**90.34%-97.44%**) and accuracy (**89.72%-98.19%**) when tested on unseen environmental conditions and new airports, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities.
- The system-level anomaly detector outperformed component-level baselines (prediction error and ensemble uncertainty-based methods) in accurately identifying true system-level failures and understanding their context.
- The integrated anomaly detection and fallback mechanism demonstrably prevented system failures, reducing the Backward Reachable Tube volume (e.g., from **25.75%** to **18.24%** for one runway in an aircraft taxiing scenario), while introducing acceptable computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability analysis can effectively and automatically identify system-level failure states and their corresponding visual inputs within a simulator, providing crucial training data for anomaly detection.
> - A deep learning-based anomaly detector, trained on reachability-derived system-level failure labels, can generalize effectively to unseen environmental conditions and implicitly learn semantic failure modes without explicit programming.
> - Integrating a system-level anomaly detector with a simple fallback controller creates a robust safety pipeline that significantly reduces the system's failure space and prevents catastrophic outcomes with minimal latency.
