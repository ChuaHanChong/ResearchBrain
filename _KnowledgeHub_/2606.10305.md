# SARM2: Multi-Task Stage Aware Reward Modeling for Self Improving Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A reward modeling approach, SARM2, combines action-primitive stage estimation with a multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts to generate dense, accurate per-step rewards across diverse long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. This model underpins SPIRAL, a self-improvement framework that iteratively refines manipulation policies using autonomous robot rollouts, reducing reliance on human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Developing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for long-horizon robotic manipulation requires expensive human demonstrations and supervised fine-tuning, limiting generalization and scalability.
- Current task-specific reward models are not generalizable across tasks, necessitating extensive re-annotation and retraining for each new skill.
- General Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based reward models provide coarse and noisy signals, insufficient for the fine-grained, per-step supervision needed for complex multi-stage tasks.

## Method

- A multi-task ==stage-aware reward== model, **SARM2**, is introduced, integrating an action-primitive based stage estimator with a multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) value decoder to generate dense and generalizable progress estimates.
- SARM2's stage estimator classifies action primitives from visual and proprioceptive input, while the MMoE decoder specializes value predictions based on these primitives, overcoming interference from diverse dynamics.
- The SPIRAL framework utilizes SARM2's dense rewards within an on-policy residual reinforcement learning loop, enabling iterative policy refinement and self-improvement through autonomous robot interactions.

## Results

- SARM2 demonstrated superior reward modeling accuracy, achieving lower demonstration MSE (**0.006** on S1) and higher rollout classification (ρ=**0.667**) compared to VLM-based baselines that showed over-optimistic estimation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the action-primitive stage estimator and the multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts design significantly contribute to SARM2's ability to capture task structure and improve accuracy.
- The SPIRAL framework, leveraging SARM2's dense rewards, consistently improved policy success rates across tasks (e.g., 12/12 successful trials on Folding Shorts Flat) over multiple rounds of autonomous self-improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing long-horizon tasks into a small, recurring vocabulary of action primitives provides a robust, task-agnostic intermediate representation for stage estimation.
> - A multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) architecture, conditioned on predicted action primitives, effectively specializes value prediction for diverse primitive dynamics, overcoming limitations of monolithic value heads.
> - Dense and accurate per-step reward signals are critical for enabling stable and effective policy self-improvement through on-policy reinforcement learning, significantly surpassing the capabilities of sparse rewards or coarse VLM-based signals.
