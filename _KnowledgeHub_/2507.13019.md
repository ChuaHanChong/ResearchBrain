# Rethinking the Embodied Gap in Vision-and-Language Navigation: A Holistic Study of Physical and Visual Disparities

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and Tongji University introduce VLN-PE, a physically realistic platform for Vision-and-Language Navigation that supports diverse robot embodiments and challenging environmental conditions. The work systematically evaluates existing VLN models on this platform, revealing substantial performance degradation due to physical constraints and visual disparities, and demonstrates the effectiveness of co-training across embodiments for more robust agents.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) models and benchmarks rely on idealized simulation assumptions, overlooking crucial physical complexities like robot locomotion dynamics, viewpoint shifts, and motion errors inherent in diverse robot types.
- The lack of an integrated platform to support cross-embodiment data collection, training, and systematic evaluation hinders the development of VLN models robust enough for real-world robotic deployment.
- A significant 'embodied gap' persists between the performance of VLN algorithms in idealized simulations and their practical transferability to physical robots.

## Method

- The team developed **VLN-PE**, a novel physically realistic VLN platform built on ==GRUTopia (NVIDIA Isaac Sim)== that offers robust ==physics simulation== and supports ==humanoid== (Unitree H1, G1), ==quadruped== (Unitree Aliengo), and ==wheeled== (Jetbot) robots with advanced RL-based controllers.
- VLN-PE incorporates a diverse range of environments, including physically corrected ==Matterport3D== scenes, high-fidelity GRUScenes, and realistic ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== rendered environments, along with new datasets (GRU-VLN10, 3DGS-Lab-VLN).
- The platform includes ==physical realism metrics== like ==Fall Rate (FR)== and ==Stuck Rate (StR)== alongside standard VLN metrics, facilitating comprehensive evaluation of ego-centric VLN methods, including a new ==Recurrent Diffusion Policy (RDP)== baseline and an enhanced ==VLMaps== approach.

## Results

- Zero-shot transfer of VLN-CE models to VLN-PE resulted in a significant **34%** relative drop in Success Rate, highlighting the limitations of current methods in physical settings.
- In-domain training or fine-tuning on VLN-PE data, particularly with 'One-for-All' co-training across embodiments, yielded superior performance compared to direct transfer, confirming the need for physically-aware data.
- Real-world experiments with a Unitree Go2 robot validated that models fine-tuned with VLN-PE data demonstrated improved adaptation and generalization compared to those trained solely on idealized VLN-CE data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLN models, including 7B-parameter MLLMs, exhibit a substantial 34% relative drop in Success Rate (SR) when transferred from idealized simulations to the physically realistic VLN-PE environment.
> - Co-training VLN models with data from different robot embodiments (humanoid, quadruped, wheeled) significantly boosts overall performance and adaptability, enabling the development of 'One-for-All' unified models.
> - Multi-modal input (RGB+Depth) is crucial for robust navigation, as RGB-only models show significant performance degradation (11-12% SR drop) under varying lighting conditions and challenging visual environments like 3DGS scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a critical task in embodied AI, requiring an agent to interpret natural language instructions and navigate through environments. The paper frames its contribution within the historical evolution of VLN, which began with idealized "oracle-based" navigation on predefined topologies (e.g., MP3D simulator) and progressed to "continuous navigation" with low-level oracle actions using pseudo-robots (e.g., VLN-CE with Habitat). This work introduces **VLN-PE**, marking the next logical step: **"physically realistic navigation with robot-controlled locomotion."** This directly addresses a significant and long-standing gap in embodied AI: the transferability and robustness of algorithms from highly idealized simulations to real-world physical deployment.

The broader research landscape of VLN has seen advancements in cross-modal alignment, semantic understanding, and multimodal large language models (MLLMs), as well as diffusion policies for trajectory generation and map-based navigation methods. However, the authors argue that these advancements often rely on **overly idealized assumptions** about robot movement and control, neglecting critical physical challenges such as viewpoint shifts, collisions, falling, deadlocks, and motion errors. Most existing benchmarks are designed for simplified agents (wheeled or point-based) and do not account for the unique locomotion characteristics of diverse robot types (humanoid, quadruped, wheeled).

VLN-PE directly bridges this "embodied gap" by:
- **Introducing physical realism:** Moving beyond pseudo-robots to actual robot dynamics, precise locomotion control, and sensory feedback from motion disturbances.
- **Enabling cross-embodiment evaluation:** Systematically evaluating VLN methods across different robot types, which is largely underexplored.
- **Diversifying environments:** Expanding beyond the limited visual diversity of MP3D to include high-quality synthetic scenes and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendered environments, which introduce more realistic visual challenges.
- **Revealing limitations:** For the first time, systematically analyzing the performance degradation of state-of-the-art VLN models when transferred from ideal simulated to physically realistic settings.

By providing a full-stack platform for data collection, training, and evaluation under these realistic conditions, VLN-PE directly pushes the VLN field towards developing more robust, generalizable, and practical models suitable for real-world robotic applications. It positions itself as a crucial tool for future research in sim-to-real transfer and unified embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: despite significant advancements in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) models within simulated environments, a substantial **"embodied gap"** persists between these models and their practical deployment on physical robots. Current simulation-based VLN benchmarks often rely on idealized assumptions that fail to reflect the complexities and challenges of real-world robotic interaction.

The authors identify several specific issues that highlight this gap:
- **Idealized Movement Assumptions:** Existing models often assume perfect robot movement and control, neglecting physical issues like viewpoint shifts due to locomotion, falling, deadlocks, and motion errors inherent in diverse robot types (wheeled, humanoid, quadruped).
- **Lack of Cross-Embodiment Support:** Most benchmarks are designed for simplified wheeled or point-based agents, overlooking the unique locomotion and sensory characteristics of different robot embodiments.
- **Demand for Integrated Platforms:** The rapid progress in locomotion algorithms (especially RL-based methods for legged robots) necessitates an integrated VLN platform that can support cross-embodiment data collection, training, and evaluation under realistic dynamics.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:
1. **Introduce VLN-PE:** Develop and present a novel, physically realistic VLN platform and benchmark capable of supporting cross-embodiment (humanoid, quadruped, wheeled) data collection and systematic evaluation.
2. **Systematically Evaluate Existing Methods:** For the first time, conduct a comprehensive analysis of several ego-centric VLN methods (including classification models, a diffusion model, and an LLM-based approach) within physical robotic settings on VLN-PE.
3. **Identify Key Disparities:** Quantify the performance degradation of current VLN models due to real-world factors, specifically investigating the impact of:
 - **Cross-Embodiment Perception:** How robots perceive and interpret environments through their unique sensory systems (e.g., camera height).
 - **Controller Engagement:** The influence of realistic physical controllers during data acquisition and validation.
 - **Environmental Conditions:** The effects of diverse environmental factors, such as light intensity variations.
4. **Promote Robust VLN Research:** Provide tools and insights to inspire the community to rethink current VLN limitations and advance more robust, practical, and generalizable VLN models for physical embodied AI development.

Essentially, the paper seeks to answer a crucial question: "To what extent do physical embodiment constraints and visual environmental variations impact the performance of existing VLN methods?"

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **VLN-PE**, a novel platform and benchmark designed for physically realistic Vision-and-Language Navigation.

#### 3.1. VLN-PE Platform Features

- **Physically Realistic Simulation:** Built on **GRUTopia** (which uses NVIDIA Isaac Sim), VLN-PE provides robust physics simulation, supporting various robot types with realistic dynamics.
- **Cross-Embodiment Support:** Explicitly supports humanoid robots (Unitree H1, G1), quadruped robots (Unitree Aliengo), and wheeled robots (Jetbot). It offers RL-based controllers as APIs for operating these diverse embodiments.
- **Extensible Scenes:**
 - **Matterport3D (MP3D):** 90 scenes converted to USD format, with manual fixes for reconstruction errors (floor gaps) and alignment to VLN-CE coordinates. Disk lighting is used to control light intensity.
 - **GRUScenes:** 10 high-quality synthetic indoor scenes with improved visual fidelity and physical realism.
 - **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** Re-built scenes from scanned environments (e.g., a laboratory), offering highly realistic perceptual experiences via online rendering.
- **Datasets:**
 - **R2R:** The standard VLN dataset, filtered to exclude stair-navigation episodes, for baseline evaluation.
 - **GRU-VLN10:** A new dataset (3 scenes for training, 7 for unseen tests) built from GRUScenes, with LLM-generated and manually validated instructions.
 - **3DGS-Lab-VLN:** A supplementary dataset for 3DGS-rendered environments with training and evaluation episodes.
- **Metrics:** Beyond standard VLN metrics (Trajectory Length (TL), Navigation Error (NE), Success Rate (SR), Oracle Success Rate (OS), Success Rate weighted by Inverse Path Length (SPL)), VLN-PE introduces two crucial physical realism metrics: **Fall Rate (FR)** and **Stuck Rate (StR)**.
- **Locomotion Policy:** Employs state-of-the-art RL-based locomotion policies (e.g., for humanoid and quadruped robots) that are consistent between simulation and real-robot deployments, facilitating sim-to-real transfer. Supports flash control, move-by-speed control, and move-along-path control.

#### 3.2. Baseline VLN Methods Evaluated

The study focuses on ego-centric VLN methods, aligning with typical robot perception setups (single RGB-D pinhole cameras).
- **End-to-end Train-based Methods:**
 - **Single-step Discrete Action Classification:**
 - **Seq2Seq:** A foundational model with an LSTM instruction encoder, ResNet50 vision/depth encoders, and a GRU for action prediction.
 - **Cross-modal Attention (CMA):** Builds on Seq2Seq, adding cross-modal attention mechanisms between instruction and visual/depth features.
 - **NaVid:** A video-based MLLM (7B parameters) built on LLaMa-VID, using a vision encoder, query generator, LLM, and cross-modality projectors for RGB-only navigation. Outputs discrete action types and quantitative arguments (distance, rotation degrees).
 - **Multi-step Continuous Prediction:**
 - **Recurrent Diffusion Policy for VLN (RDP):** A novel baseline introduced in this paper. It uses LongCLIP for RGB and instruction encoding, ResNet50 for depth. Information is aligned via multi-head cross-modal attention. A recurrent GRU maintains historical observations. A Transformer acts as the diffusion decoder, predicting continuous relative displacement and yaw angle for 8 future waypoints. It also includes a dedicated MLP stop prediction head to address diffusion models' difficulty in precise stopping.
- **Modular Map-based Train-free Method:**
 - **Improved VLMaps:** A zero-shot approach leveraging LLMs to parse natural language instructions into subgoals localized on a semantic map. The authors enhance it with:
 - An **exploration policy** (inspired by VLFM) using LSeg for frontier detection and evaluating viewpoint likelihood for landmarks.
 - Support for **room-level descriptions** using CLIP-based classification for room context alignment.
 - An **A\* algorithm** for local planning with obstacle avoidance (assigning higher costs to dilated/unexplored areas) and reorientation before moving.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted on NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs. The study systematically investigates:
- Zero-shot transfer performance from VLN-CE to VLN-PE.
- In-domain training and fine-tuning on VLN-PE data.
- Impact of physical controllers on performance.
- Impact of cross-embodiment data for "One-for-All" models.
- Impact of varying lighting conditions (day, night, camera light).
- Generalization to new scenes (GRU-VLN10, 3DGS-Lab-VLN).
- Real-world validation using a Unitree Go2 robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on VLN-PE reveal critical insights into the limitations of current VLN methods when exposed to physical and visual realities:

1. **Significant Performance Degradation in Physical Environments:**
 - Existing VLN-CE models (Seq2Seq-Full, CMA-Full, NaVid) exhibit a substantial **34% relative drop in Success Rate (SR)** when transferred directly from idealized VLN-CE simulations to the physically realistic VLN-PE environment. This highlights a clear gap between pseudo-motion training and actual physical deployment.
 - NaVid, a 7B-parameter MLLM, shows better zero-shot generalization than smaller models but still experiences notable performance drops (18% SR decline).
 - Map-based LLM solution (VLMaps) demonstrates reasonable zero-shot performance, suggesting its potential as an alternative approach.

2. **Importance of In-domain Training and Fine-tuning:**
 - Models trained from scratch on VLN-PE's in-domain data (without augmentation) outperform those transferred from Habitat even with extensive augmentation.
 - Fine-tuning existing SoTA weights (e.g., CMA+) on VLN-PE data significantly improves performance, surpassing NaVid's zero-shot results. This underscores the need for diverse, physically-aware training data.
 - Adjusting camera height to match the robot's (e.g., Humanoid H1) improves transferability, indicating sensitivity to viewpoint.

3. **Controller Engagement is Crucial for Legged Robots:**
 - Model performance is maximized when the physical locomotion controller used for data collection is consistent with that used for evaluation. Discrepancies lead to significant performance drops.
 - Training with controller-generated data helps mitigate robot falls and stuck instances, improving overall stability for legged robots.

4. **Cross-embodiment Sensitivity and the "One-for-All" Solution:**
 - VLN models are highly sensitive to motion dynamics and camera height. Zero-shot performance varies greatly across different robot types (e.g., quadruped's low camera height severely degrades performance).
 - Robot-specific viewpoint data enhances model adaptation.
 - **Co-training across different robot embodiments (humanoid, quadruped, wheeled) significantly enhances overall performance and enables a "One-for-All" model.** This multi-view learning approach leads to the best results, suggesting a path towards unified, versatile VLN models.

5. **Multi-Modal Fusion Improves Robustness to Environmental Conditions:**
 - RGB-only models (NaVid) suffer significant performance degradation (e.g., 12-11% SR drop) in low-light conditions or with varying light sources.
 - Models that utilize both RGB and depth inputs (CMA, RDP) are more robust and less affected by lighting variations, emphasizing the value of multi-modal environmental information for generalization.

6. **Limited Generalization of Standard Datasets:**
 - MP3D-style datasets are insufficient to capture environmental shifts. Small models (CMA-CLIP, RDP), when fine-tuned on the small-scale GRU-VLN10 dataset (new scenes), outperform NaVid's zero-shot performance.
 - NaVid completely fails on the 3DGS-Lab-VLN dataset (only 5.8 SR), likely due to rendering noise introduced by 3DGS, which disrupts RGB perception for large models. This highlights a crucial challenge for MLLMs and the importance of diverse, realistic evaluation environments.

7. **New Baselines (RDP and Improved VLMaps):**
 - **RDP**, the proposed diffusion-based method for continuous dense waypoint prediction, outperforms Seq2Seq and CMA when trained from scratch, demonstrating the promise of diffusion policies in VLN.
 - NaVid, while showing lower fall and stuck rates (attributed to world knowledge of MLLMs for obstacle avoidance), struggles with precise goal recognition, often rotating continuously near the goal. This indicates room for improvement in MLLM-based stopping mechanisms.

8. **Real-world Impact:**
 - Real-world experiments using a Unitree Go2 robot confirm that models fine-tuned with VLN-PE data exhibit improved adaptation and generalization compared to those trained solely on VLN-CE, validating the practical effectiveness of the platform.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VLN-PE represents a significant step forward for the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and embodied AI, offering several profound implications:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** VLN-PE directly confronts and begins to bridge the longstanding "embodied gap" between idealized simulation environments and the complexities of real-world robotic deployment. By incorporating realistic robot dynamics, diverse embodiments, and environmental challenges, it provides a much-needed platform to develop and evaluate VLN models that are truly robust and deployable in physical settings. The real-world validation with the Unitree Go2 robot underscores this practical utility.

2. **Inspiring More Robust VLN Models:** The systematic evaluation on VLN-PE exposes critical limitations of current state-of-the-art VLN methods, even large MLLMs, revealing their sensitivity to physical factors (camera height, locomotion dynamics) and visual conditions (lighting, rendering artifacts). These findings serve as a crucial call to action for the research community to rethink current VLN limitations and prioritize the development of more resilient and generalizable models.

3. **Foundation for Unified Embodied AI:** The demonstration that co-training across different robot embodiments leads to "One-for-All" models is a groundbreaking result. This suggests a viable pathway toward creating truly general-purpose embodied agents capable of operating across various hardware platforms without needing robot-specific retraining, significantly reducing development costs and increasing versatility.

4. **Promoting Multi-Modal and Physically-Aware Perception:** The stark contrast in performance between RGB-only and RGB+Depth models under varying lighting conditions highlights the critical importance of multi-modal sensing for robust navigation. This encourages future research to integrate diverse environmental information (depth, radar, etc.) to enhance model generalization and reliability.

5. **Expanding Research Horizons with Diverse Environments:** By introducing high-quality synthetic scenes and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) environments, VLN-PE moves beyond the visual limitations of traditional MP3D datasets. This allows for more comprehensive evaluation of model generalization to novel visual distributions and challenges, fostering research into models that are less prone to overfitting to specific visual styles or artifacts.

6. **Facilitating Advanced Robotic Control Integration:** The platform's support for RL-based locomotion controllers and continuous action prediction methods (like RDP) opens avenues for integrating VLN with advanced control-theoretic approaches (e.g., MPC). This can lead to smoother, more stable, and safer navigation behaviors, particularly for complex legged robots.

7. **Open-Source and Extensible Framework:** The commitment to making the code available and the platform's extensibility for integrating new scenes and robot types ensures that VLN-PE will serve as a foundational tool. It empowers researchers to explore novel VLN solutions, gather diverse data, and benchmark their methods under increasingly realistic conditions, thereby accelerating progress in the field.

In essence, VLN-PE is not just another benchmark; it's a paradigm shift in how VLN research is conducted, pushing the community towards tackling real-world complexities and ultimately accelerating the development of truly practical and intelligent embodied agents.
