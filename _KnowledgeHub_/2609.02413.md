# WildFab: Multi-Axis 3D Printing from Models in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> WildFab is a computational framework that generates support-free spatial toolpaths and collision-free multi-axis 3D printing motions directly from unrepaired real-world geometry. It couples a neural unsigned distance field with a regularized generalized winding number field, localizing surfaces at winding-gradient ridges and checking collisions coarse-to-fine. It handles non-manifold B-reps, voxels, scans and open meshes, reaching near-100% support-free ratios.

## Problem

- Multi-axis slicing pipelines assume watertight solid inputs and tetrahedral meshes, so design, scan and topology-optimization geometry must first be repaired, which is slow and alters intended shape.
- Signed distance fields require a well-defined volumetric domain, while unsigned distance fields lack orientation and solid-void semantics and become numerically unreliable near the surface.
- Winding-number slicers give inside-outside classification but no metric distance or closest-point projection, so they cannot support spatial toolpath offsetting or multi-axis collision checking.

## Method

- ==Hybrid query field== pairs a ==SIREN== neural UDF for differentiable distance and direction with a ==regularized generalized winding number field== whose smoothed Poisson kernel gives solid-void indication and stable near-surface gradients.
- ==Iterative two-step waypoint tracing== alternately projects samples onto optimized guidance-field level sets and onto reg-GWN gradient-magnitude ridges, using bidirectional line search along the UDF gradient direction.
- ==Coarse-to-fine collision checking==: fast UDF gradient-consistency rejection filters candidates, then a time-varying reg-GWN field verifies survivors, feeding differentiable collision response into a ==quaternion motion-frame field==.

## Results

- Ridge-based localization cuts surface-localization error from **7.3e-4** for UDF level sets to **2.4e-5**, about two orders of magnitude, and reduces near-surface distance error fiftyfold.
- Support-free ratio rises from **92.1%** to **99.2%** on the ArtShell model, and from a **25-95%** planar baseline to nearly **100%** across 20 non-manifold Thingi10K models.
- UDF filtering rejects **99.5%** of non-colliding cases; over **100k** waypoints trace in under **10 seconds**, and CT scans show roughly **0.7 mm** error, about **0.5%** of model size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pair complementary fields according to their strengths rather than forcing one representation to serve distance, orientation and containment queries at once.
> - Gradient-magnitude ridges of a regularized field localize boundaries far more reliably than level sets, and stay robust to inconsistent point-cloud normal orientations.
> - Cheap approximate filtering plus expensive exact verification makes memory-heavy geometric queries tractable; extremely sharp or small-scale features are still smoothed by the neural UDF.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-axis 3D printing represents an advanced manufacturing approach that utilizes spatial motion with high-degrees-of-freedom machines to deposit material along non-planar trajectories. This technique offers potential advantages over conventional planar-layer-based solutions, including a reduction in support structures, improved surface quality by mitigating the staircase effect, and enhanced structural strength through toolpath alignment with stress directions. Realizing these benefits, however, requires robust computational pipelines capable of decomposing complex geometries into curved layers and spatial toolpaths.

A significant challenge in the current landscape of multi-axis 3D printing is the typical assumption of watertight solid inputs. Geometric models derived from various sources, such as design workflows, engineering simulations (e.g., topology optimization), or direct data acquisition (e.g., 3D scanning), frequently deviate from this ideal. Such "models in the wild" may exhibit features like mixed open-surface and closed-solid components, non-manifold structures, self-intersections, nested surfaces, or raw incomplete point clouds. Current pipelines often address these imperfections by necessitating extensive and time-consuming geometry repair processes, which can involve operations such as offsetting, Boolean processing, or manual rebuilding into watertight solids. This repair can inadvertently alter the original intended geometry, potentially leading to undesired fabrication outcomes, such as the thickening of thin shells or incorrect classification of nested regions.

Existing geometric representations and computational methods each possess limitations when confronting these "wild" geometries:
- **Signed Distance Fields (SDFs)** provide distance and topological information but require a well-defined volumetric domain, making them unsuitable for open or non-manifold surfaces.
- **Unsigned Distance Fields (UDFs)** can represent open and non-manifold surfaces but lack inherent orientation and solid-void semantics. They also tend to exhibit instability and inaccuracy near the surface boundary.
- **Generalized Winding Numbers (GWNs)** can infer inside-outside information from incomplete data, as used in some planar slicing applications. However, they do not intrinsically provide metric distance information required for collision detection or accurate surface boundary definition for mixed solid-shell models, nor do they directly support smooth optimization.

This research addresses the gap by proposing a computational framework that can directly process these complex, imperfect geometries without requiring prior repair. By introducing a hybrid geometric representation and associated computational methods, WildFab aims to expand the applicability of multi-axis 3D printing to a broader range of real-world design inputs, thereby streamlining the end-to-end design-to-fabrication workflow.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop WildFab, an end-to-end computational framework for multi-axis 3D printing capable of directly generating support-free spatial toolpaths and collision-free robot motions from "models in the wild." This objective is motivated by several challenges inherent in conventional multi-axis 3D printing pipelines when confronted with real-world geometric data.

The core motivations stem from the limitations of existing methods in handling imperfect geometric inputs:
- **Prevalence of Complex Geometries:** Models originating from design, engineering, or reverse-engineering workflows frequently contain complex and non-ideal characteristics. These include mixed open-surface and closed-solid structures, nearly coincident surfaces, non-manifold regions, self-intersections, and non-watertight meshes. Examples cited include artistic models, topology-optimization results, and raw scanned point clouds.
- **Inefficiency and Alteration by Geometry Repair:** Traditional multi-axis 3D printing pipelines typically mandate a watertight solid input. This necessitates extensive geometry repair for "wild" models, often involving manual intervention, boolean operations, or offsetting. Such processes are time-consuming, model-dependent, and, critically, can alter the original intended geometry. For instance, open surfaces might be inappropriately converted to volumetric solids, thin shells might be thickened and intersect adjacent features, or nested surfaces could be incorrectly filled. The authors aim to circumvent this repair stage entirely to preserve geometric fidelity and reduce preprocessing time.
- **Deficiencies of Existing Geometric Representations:** A robust computational framework for multi-axis 3D printing requires a geometric representation that can reliably support several key queries:
 1. **Solid-Surface-Void Identification:** The ability to distinguish between material, surface, and empty space accurately for defining fabrication domains.
 2. **Accurate Surface Localization:** Precise identification of surface boundaries for accurate toolpath placement.
 3. **Differentiable Distance and Direction Queries:** Continuous and differentiable queries for surface distance and direction, essential for efficient toolpath offsetting, motion planning, and collision avoidance algorithms.

The research is specifically motivated by the observation that no single existing representation (SDF, UDF, or GWN) fully satisfies these three requirements simultaneously for "models in the wild." SDFs require watertightness, UDFs lack orientation and surface stability, and GWNs do not provide distance information or accurate boundaries for mixed models. This gap led the authors to propose a hybrid query field that integrates the complementary strengths of neural UDF and regularized GWN to overcome these limitations and provide a foundation for robust multi-axis 3D printing from diverse, real-world inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WildFab is a computational framework designed for multi-axis 3D printing, operating directly on "models in the wild." The pipeline converts diverse input formats into oriented point clouds, then constructs a hybrid query field, which serves as the foundation for spatial toolpath generation and collision-free motion planning.

### 4. Input Processing and Hybrid Query Field Construction

The framework accommodates various input geometries, including non-manifold B-rep models, voxelized topology-optimization results, implicit neural fields, raw scanned point clouds, and non-watertight meshes. These inputs are initially converted into oriented point clouds, from which the hybrid query field `Q(x) = (f_udf(x), w_delta(x))` is constructed within a normalized computational domain `Ω`.
- **Neural Unsigned Distance Field (UDF):** The `f_udf(x)` component is a neural network, specifically a SIREN-based architecture, trained to represent the non-negative distance from any query point `x` to the input surface. It provides continuous, differentiable distance and gradient queries. The training loss incorporates terms for Dirichlet boundary conditions, boundary loop constraints, an eikonal term for unit-speed distance propagation, and a heat-kernel-inspired diffusion regularizer to suppress high-frequency artifacts and ghost geometries.
- **Regularized Generalized Winding Number (Reg-GWN):** The `w_delta(x)` component is a scalar field that provides an inside/outside measure relative to the surface. It is computed using a double-layer potential formulation, where the Poisson kernel is regularized by a function `s(t)` to remove singularities that typically occur when query points coincide with surface points. This regularization transforms the discontinuous jump of a standard GWN into a differentiable transition, creating a distinct gradient-magnitude ridge `||∇w_delta||` near the surface. The evaluation of Reg-GWN is accelerated using an octree structure.

### 5. Boundary Detection and Surface Localization

The hybrid query field enables robust surface localization, addressing the individual limitations of UDF (near-surface ambiguity) and GWN (inability to define open boundaries). The surface `S` is defined as the ridge set of the Reg-GWN gradient magnitude `||∇w_delta(x)||` within a narrow band `Ω_delta` (defined by `f_udf(x) <= 2*delta`). The search for this ridge is guided by the negative UDF gradient `-∇f_udf(x)`, which points towards and is perpendicular to the surface. This approach provides a high-precision boundary definition, improving localization accuracy by orders of magnitude compared to UDF-only methods.

### 6. Spatial Toolpath Generation

An implicit neural guidance field `g(x)` is optimized across the computational domain. Its gradient `∇g(x)` defines the local printing direction and is optimized to satisfy several fabrication objectives:
- **Support-Free Constraint (L_SF):** Ensures deposition angles are within a printable range, typically 45 degrees, to eliminate the need for support structures.
- **Surface Smoothness Regularization (L_smooth):** Promotes continuous spatial layers and uniform layer height distribution by regularizing the guidance field's gradient projected onto the local tangent plane.
- **Critical Boundary Protection (L_BP):** Preserves slicing topology near open boundaries and prevents discontinuities in generated toolpaths, particularly important for mixed thin-shell and solid models.
The multi-axis printing toolpath `P_k` for a given slicing value `u_k` is defined by the intersection of the guidance field's level set `g(x) = u_k` and the Reg-GWN ridge-based surface `S`. Waypoints are generated using an iterative two-step projection scheme:
1. A point is projected onto the target guidance level set using a damped Newton iteration.
2. The point is then refined towards the Reg-GWN gradient-magnitude ridge surface by performing a bidirectional line search along the UDF gradient direction, seeking the maximum `||∇w_delta||` response.
All computations for waypoint generation are parallelized on the GPU, utilizing the differentiable nature of both UDF and Reg-GWN.

### 7. Collision-Free Motion Planning

After generating an initial, ordered toolpath, a motion frame field, represented as an implicit quaternion field `q: Ω → H`, is optimized to determine collision-free tool orientations at each waypoint. The optimization minimizes `L_motion`, which includes:
- **Collision Loss (L_coll):** Quantifies nozzle penetration into the printed object.
- **Support-Free Constraint (L_SF):** Re-evaluated based on the actual tool orientation `R(q(p))z`.
- **Motion Smoothness (L_SM):** Encourages smooth changes in tool orientation along the path.
Collision checking employs a coarse-to-fine strategy for efficiency and robustness:
1. **Coarse Filtering (UDF-based Rejection):** For each transformed nozzle edge, a rapid check is performed. Potential collision candidates are identified if the edge intersects the already-printed region (based on the guidance field `g(x)`) and exhibits inconsistent UDF gradients at its endpoints. This step efficiently rejects a large majority of non-colliding cases (e.g., 99.5%).
2. **Fine Verification (Time-Varying Reg-GWN):** For the remaining ambiguous cases, a more precise verification is conducted using a *time-varying Reg-GWN field* (`w_delta-TV(x)`). This field is constructed dynamically from the set of waypoints already designated as "printed" before the current waypoint `p`. A collision is confirmed if the sampled points along the nozzle edge show a high coefficient of variation in `||∇w_delta-TV(x)||`, indicating a clear crossing of the dynamically growing object's boundary. This step corrects false positives from UDF-only checking, which might occur near UDF singularities or due to the global nature of the UDF not accounting for the printed volume's temporal evolution.
This hybrid collision-checking approach is parallelizable on the GPU and provides differentiable feedback for optimizing the quaternion field. The final output is an optimized oriented waypoint sequence for robotic multi-axis 3D printing.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The WildFab framework was validated through extensive computational experiments on a diverse set of challenging geometric inputs and physical fabrication using a robotic multi-axis 3D printing system.

### 9. Computational Results

- **Diverse Input Handling:** The system successfully processed a range of models termed "in the wild," including non-manifold parametric surfaces (e.g., ArtShell, Klein Bottle), voxelized topology optimization results (e.g., TO-Kitten), implicit neural models (e.g., garment decoder outputs), raw scanned point clouds (e.g., Goddess sculpture), and non-watertight meshes from the Thingi10K dataset. This demonstrated the framework's capability to operate directly on complex geometries without requiring conventional repair processes that can alter the original design.
- **Support-Free Toolpaths:** The generated spatial toolpaths consistently achieved high support-free ratios. For example, the support-free ratio for the ArtShell model improved from 92.1% (planar 3DP baseline) to 99.2% using WildFab. Similarly, for a range of Thingi10K models, support-free ratios increased from a baseline of 25%-95% to nearly 100%. The system preserved critical geometric features, such as thin shells and non-manifold junctions, effectively aligning toolpaths with these regions.
- **Collision-Free Motion Planning:** The coarse-to-fine collision checking strategy demonstrated efficiency and accuracy. For the ArtShell model, an initial 17% collision region (when using the guidance field gradient directly as printing direction) was successfully resolved. The motion optimization process typically converged within 5 iterations, with each iteration requiring approximately 4 seconds. The UDF-based coarse filter rejected over 99.5% of non-colliding cases, substantially reducing the computational load for the more intensive time-varying Reg-GWN verification step.
- **Efficiency:** While neural UDF construction consumed the most significant portion of preprocessing time (around 70%), waypoint generation for over 100,000 points was completed within 10 seconds for most models. Reg-GWN queries, accelerated by a GPU CUDA implementation and octree structure, were efficient, generally taking less than 20 ms for a 128 resolution. The coarse-to-fine collision checking provided a 73x speedup compared to an exclusive time-varying Reg-GWN approach.

### 10. Physical Fabrication

- **Robotic System Validation:** Models were physically fabricated using an 8-DOF robotic printing platform (a 6-DOF industrial robot arm combined with a 2-DOF rotary table). The fabricated parts exhibited preservation of global shapes and challenging geometric features, such as high-curvature ribbons and self-intersection structures, without requiring external support structures.
- **Internal Structure Quality:** Micro-CT scans of printed models (e.g., TO-Kitten, Klein Bottle) confirmed the accurate reproduction of both sparse infill patterns within solid regions and delicate thin-shell structures. The Klein Bottle demonstrated single-path printing results in non-manifold regions.
- **Dimensional Accuracy:** Quantitative analysis of fabrication error from CT scans indicated an average distance error of approximately 0.7 mm, or about 0.5% of the model size, suggesting a high degree of fidelity between the digital design and the physical print.

### 11. Extended Applications

- **Planar 3D Printing:** The framework extended naturally to planar-based 3D printing, enabling direct slicing and printing of complex models such as nested onion structures and mixed surface-volume models (e.g., human body with clothing). This demonstrated superior performance over conventional slicers, which often fail to preserve intended nested structures or mixed geometries.
- **Robotic Manufacturing:** The hybrid representation proved beneficial for other robot-assisted manufacturing tasks, exemplified by a robot-assisted welding scenario for a rocket Grid-Fin model. It enabled fast collision checking and safe motion planning on geometries with non-manifold cross-shaped junctions and thin lattice structures.

### 12. Discussion and Limitations

- **Robustness to Normal Orientation:** The Reg-GWN gradient-magnitude ridge, used for surface localization, was demonstrated to be robust to variations in normal orientation and nearby surface interference, as long as the regularization width was appropriately chosen.
- **Parameter Sensitivity:** The regularization width `δ` for Reg-GWN was identified as critical, with `δ = h/2.5` (where `h` is sample spacing) providing a balance between smoothness and accuracy.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** WildFab consistently outperformed existing multi-axis slicing pipelines (e.g., S3-Slicer, INF-3DP) by directly processing "wild" models without geometry repair. These conventional methods often introduced unwanted volumes, self-intersections, or lost original shell/solid information due to their reliance on watertight volumetric inputs.
- **Neural UDF Smoothing:** While effective, the neural UDF component might introduce local smoothing on extremely sharp or small-scale features, leading to some geometric loss relative to the original input. This suggests an area for future improvement in fidelity and feature preservation.

### 13. Significance and Potential Impact

WildFab represents a significant advancement in the field of multi-axis 3D printing and broader computational fabrication, with several key areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Expanded Applicability of Multi-Axis 3D Printing:** By enabling direct processing of "models in the wild" – geometries often characterized by non-manifold structures, mixed solid-shell components, self-intersections, or raw point clouds – WildFab greatly broadens the range of designs and data sources compatible with multi-axis 3D printing. This moves beyond the restrictive requirement for watertight solid inputs, which has historically limited the practical adoption of advanced 3D printing techniques.
- **Streamlined Design-to-Fabrication Workflow:** The elimination of the time-consuming and potentially geometry-altering repair stage for imperfect models simplifies and accelerates the entire design-to-fabrication pipeline. Designers and engineers can transition from conceptual models, simulation outputs, or scanned data directly to printable toolpaths without extensive manual intervention or pre-processing, fostering more agile and efficient development cycles.
- **Enhanced Fabrication Quality and Capabilities:** The framework facilitates support-free fabrication, which reduces material waste, post-processing effort, and enables the creation of more complex geometries. The precise surface localization and collision-free motion planning contribute to improved surface quality, consistent layer thickness, and the ability to fabricate delicate features or non-manifold regions accurately. For applications like topology optimization, it enables printing of complex internal structures without inaccessible supports.
- **Novel Geometric Representation Paradigm:** The proposed hybrid query field, combining neural UDF and regularized GWN, offers a robust and versatile geometric representation. This representation addresses the complementary limitations of its constituent fields, providing reliable solid-surface-void identification, accurate surface localization, and differentiable queries crucial for complex geometric computations. This approach could inspire new representations in other areas of computational geometry.
- **Foundation for Broader Robotic Manufacturing:** Beyond 3D printing, the principles of the hybrid query field and the coarse-to-fine collision-checking strategy demonstrate applicability to other robot-assisted manufacturing tasks. The ability to handle complex, imperfect geometries for robust collision detection and motion planning is a fundamental requirement in various robotic fabrication, assembly, and inspection scenarios, potentially generalizing WildFab's impact.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The framework's availability as open-source code fosters further research and development in computational fabrication, allowing other researchers and practitioners to build upon and extend its capabilities.

In conclusion, WildFab contributes a robust and practical solution to a long-standing challenge in advanced manufacturing, bridging the gap between real-world geometric data and the requirements of sophisticated robotic fabrication processes.
