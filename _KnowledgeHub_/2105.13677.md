# ResT: An Efficient Transformer for Visual Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanjing University introduced ResT, an efficient multi-scale Vision Transformer that integrates CNN-like hierarchical feature extraction with novel efficient self-attention and flexible positional encoding. This architecture achieves state-of-the-art performance in image classification, object detection, and instance segmentation, outperforming previous Transformer models and optimized CNNs while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Vision Transformers often incur high computational and memory costs due to the quadratic complexity of standard Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA), especially for high-resolution images.
- Many early Vision Transformers struggle with capturing fine-grained, low-level features and are limited to fixed input resolutions, hindering their application in dense prediction tasks requiring multi-scale analysis.
- The expressive power and diversity of individual attention heads can be limited when their embedding dimensions are short, potentially impacting overall network performance.

## Method

- ResT proposes ==Efficient Multi-Head Self-Attention (EMSA)== which utilizes depth-wise convolutions to reduce spatial dimensions of keys and values, significantly cutting computational costs, and incorporates a 1x1 convolution with Instance Normalization for improved cross-head interaction and diversity.
- A ==hierarchical architecture==, inspired by ResNet, is implemented with a stacked convolutional stem module and overlapping patch embeddings to effectively extract low-level features and generate multi-scale representations.
- Fixed positional encodings are replaced by a flexible ==Pixel Attention (PA) module==, which uses a depth-wise convolution and sigmoid activation to provide adaptive positional information, allowing ResT to handle arbitrary input sizes.

## Results

- ResT-Small achieved **79.6%** Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, outperforming PVT-Tiny by **+4.5%** with similar FLOPs, and ResT-Lite surpassed ResNet-18 by **+7.5%** Top-1 accuracy.
- On COCO 2017, ResT-Small achieved **40.3** box AP in object detection and **39.6** box AP / **37.2** mask AP in instance segmentation, demonstrating gains of **+3.6** box AP and **+2.9** box AP / **+2.1** mask AP over PVT-Tiny.
- Ablation studies confirmed that EMSA achieved **+0.2%** Top-1 accuracy and **2.4x** higher throughput than standard MSA, and the Pixel Attention module provided **+0.84%** Top-1 accuracy improvement over learnable embeddings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatially reducing keys and values with depth-wise convolutions within self-attention provides a highly effective way to improve efficiency without sacrificing performance, enabling better scaling for high-resolution inputs.
> - Combining cross-head interaction via a 1x1 convolution with diversity restoration through Instance Normalization significantly enhances the expressiveness and robustness of multi-head attention.
> - Integrating CNN-style hierarchical feature extraction, including an effective stem and multi-scale patch embeddings, allows Transformers to efficiently capture both local and global features, making them highly suitable for a broad range of computer vision tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of computer vision, specifically in the architectural design of deep learning backbones. Historically, **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**, such as ResNet and RegNet, have dominated computer vision tasks due to their efficiency, parameter sharing, and local feature aggregation capabilities. However, CNNs inherently suffer from limited and fixed receptive fields, making them less effective at modeling long-range dependencies, which are crucial for understanding global scene context in tasks like instance segmentation.

The advent of **Transformer networks**, originally designed for natural language processing, marked a significant paradigm shift. The **Vision Transformer (ViT)** pioneered the application of Transformers to image recognition by treating image patches as sequences of tokens. Subsequent works like DeiT, PVT, and Swin Transformer refined this approach, demonstrating Transformers' remarkable ability to capture global relationships and achieve state-of-the-art performance. These models address CNNs' limitations regarding long-range dependencies, dynamically adapting their receptive fields.

Despite their success, Vision Transformers introduced new challenges:
1. **Computational Cost:** The Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) mechanism in standard Transformers scales quadratically with spatial dimensions, leading to immense memory and computational overheads, especially for high-resolution images.
2. **Fixed Input Resolution:** Many early Vision Transformers operated on fixed-size input tokens and positional encodings, making them less suitable for dense prediction tasks (e.g., object detection, segmentation) that require multi-scale feature maps and adaptability to arbitrary input sizes.
3. **Low-Level Feature Extraction:** Direct tokenization of raw image patches in models like ViT struggles to capture fine-grained, low-level features (e.g., edges, corners) that are fundamental to image understanding and effectively handled by CNNs.
4. **Attention Head Diversity:** Short embedding dimensions per head in MSA can limit their expressive power and ability to capture diverse representations.

ResT directly tackles these four major shortcomings. It aims to develop a "general-purpose backbone" inspired by the success and versatility of ResNet, but built upon an efficient Transformer architecture. By integrating CNN-like hierarchical feature extraction, novel efficient self-attention, and flexible positional encoding, ResT seeks to merge the strengths of both architectural families while mitigating their respective weaknesses. This work contributes to the growing trend of **hybrid architectures** that combine convolutional and attention mechanisms, and to the continuous effort to make Transformers more efficient and adaptable for real-world computer vision applications, particularly those requiring dense predictions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **efficient multi-scale vision Transformer, named ResT**, that can serve as a robust and general-purpose backbone for various image recognition tasks. The authors explicitly state that ResT aims to remedy several critical issues present in existing Transformer architectures for vision.

The core motivations behind ResT's design stem from the identified limitations of current vision Transformers:

1. **To enhance low-level feature extraction:** Existing Transformer backbones, like ViT, perform straightforward tokenization directly on raw input images, which often fails to capture fundamental low-level features such as edges and corners. ResT is motivated to integrate mechanisms that effectively extract this crucial local information from the early stages of processing.
2. **To improve memory and computational efficiency of self-attention:** The standard Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) in Transformers has a quadratic computational complexity with respect to spatial or embedding dimensions. This leads to vast overheads, particularly for high-resolution images, making training and inference inefficient. A key motivation for ResT is to significantly reduce these costs without compromising performance.
3. **To optimize the performance and diversity of multi-head attention:** When the embedding dimension for each attention head is short, the dot product between query and key might not be sufficiently informative, potentially impairing network performance. ResT aims to enable richer interactions across attention heads while maintaining their inherent diversity, ensuring each head contributes meaningfully.
4. **To achieve flexibility for arbitrary input sizes and multi-scale feature representation:** Traditional Vision Transformers and their positional encodings often operate at a fixed scale, making them less suitable for vision tasks that require dense predictions, such as object detection and instance segmentation, which inherently benefit from hierarchical, multi-scale feature maps. ResT is designed to process input images of arbitrary sizes without requiring interpolation or fine-tuning, thus making it more versatile for downstream tasks.

In essence, ResT's motivation is to build a Transformer-based backbone that marries the global reasoning capabilities of Transformers with the local feature extraction and hierarchical processing benefits traditionally associated with CNNs, all while being computationally efficient and adaptable to diverse visual recognition challenges. The naming "ResT" after ResNet further underscores the ambition to create a highly effective and widely applicable backbone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ResT's methodology is characterized by a hybrid architectural design, drawing inspiration from ResNet's hierarchical structure while introducing several novel components to optimize the Transformer block for computer vision. The overall pipeline of ResT mirrors that of ResNet, comprising a stem module for initial low-level feature extraction, followed by four stages to construct hierarchical feature maps, and finally a classification head. Each stage consists of a patch embedding module, a positional encoding module, and a stack of efficient Transformer blocks.

The key methodological innovations are:

#### 3.1. Efficient Multi-Head Self-Attention (EMSA)

To address the quadratic computational cost and limited interaction across heads in standard MSA, ResT proposes EMSA:
- **Memory-Efficient Reduction:** Instead of processing query (Q), key (K), and value (V) at full spatial resolution, EMSA first reshapes the 2D input token to a 3D feature map ($\hat{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_m \times h \times w}$). A depth-wise convolution operation is then applied to $\hat{x}$ (for K and V generation) to reduce its spatial dimensions (height and width) by a factor `s`. This significantly reduces the length of the token sequence for K and V, leading to a substantial reduction in computation (from $O(n^2)$ to $O(n^2/s^2)$ where $n$ is spatial dimension). The kernel size, stride, and padding for this depth-wise convolution are carefully chosen (s+1, s, s/2 respectively) to ensure effective downsampling.
- **Cross-Head Interaction:** A standard $1 \times 1$ convolutional operation (`Conv(·)`) is introduced after the `QK^T / sqrt(d_k)` dot product matrix but before the `Softmax`. This $1 \times 1$ convolution effectively models interactions among different attention heads, allowing each head's attention function to depend on information from all other keys and queries. This aims to compensate for the "short-length limitation" of individual attention heads.
- **Restoring Diversity with Instance Normalization (IN):** While the $1 \times 1$ convolution enhances interaction, it risks diminishing the diversity that multiple attention heads are designed to provide. To counteract this, Instance Normalization (`IN(·)`) is applied after the `Softmax` on the dot product matrix. This reintroduces diversity among the attention heads by normalizing independently for each instance, preventing them from becoming too similar due to the global interaction.
- The overall EMSA formulation is: `EMSA(Q, K, V) = IN(Softmax(Conv(QK^T / sqrt(d_k))))V`.

#### 3.2. Patch Embedding with Overlapping Convolutions

To better capture low-level features and create hierarchical representations, ResT deviates from the simple, non-overlapping patch tokenization seen in ViT:
- **Stem Module:** The initial patch embedding module (stem) is crucial for extracting low-level information. Instead of a single large convolutional layer or simple patch splitting, ResT uses a stack of three $3 \times 3$ standard convolution layers (with padding 1 and strides 2, 1, and 2 respectively). This sequential convolution allows for more nuanced feature learning and a $4 \times$ spatial reduction, similar to early layers in CNNs. Batch Normalization and ReLU are applied to the first two layers.
- **Hierarchical Downsampling:** In subsequent stages (2, 3, 4), the patch embedding module performs further downsampling. It uses a standard $3 \times 3$ convolution with stride 2 and padding 1 to reduce the spatial dimension by $2 \times$ (overall $4 \times$ compared to the previous stage's input) and expands the channel dimension by $2 \times$. This progressive reduction in spatial resolution and increase in channel capacity creates a multi-scale feature pyramid, which is essential for dense prediction tasks.

#### 3.3. Positional Encoding as Spatial Attention (PA)

To enable ResT to handle input images of arbitrary sizes without interpolation or fine-tuning, the fixed-scale positional encodings of traditional Transformers are replaced with a flexible spatial attention mechanism:
- **Pixel Attention (PA) Module:** Positional encoding is re-conceptualized as a spatial attention module. Given an input token `x`, PA calculates pixel-wise weights by applying a $3 \times 3$ depth-wise convolution (with padding 1) to `x`, followed by a sigmoid activation function `σ(·)`. The encoded output is then `x * σ(DWConv(x))`. This allows the positional information to be conditioned on the local neighborhood of the input token, making it adaptive.
- **Integration:** The PA module is embedded directly into the patch embedding module at the beginning of each stage, allowing positional information to be fused naturally within the hierarchical feature extraction process.

#### 3.4. Classification Head and Model Variants

The classification head consists of a global average pooling layer applied to the output feature map of the last stage, followed by a linear classifier.
The authors provide four model variants: ResT-Lite, ResT-Small, ResT-Base, and ResT-Large, differing in channel dimensions, number of heads, reduction factors (s), and the number of Transformer blocks per stage. These variants allow for benchmarking across different computational budgets and comparison with diverse existing models.

In summary, ResT's methodology is a sophisticated integration of CNN-style hierarchical processing with a reimagined, efficient, and flexible Transformer block, addressing core limitations to achieve high performance and versatility in visual recognition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of ResT is comprehensive, covering image classification on ImageNet-1k and downstream tasks like object detection and instance segmentation on MS COCO2017. The results consistently demonstrate ResT's superior performance and efficiency compared to both state-of-the-art CNNs and other Vision Transformers.

#### 4.1. Image Classification on ImageNet-1k (224x224 input)

- **Superiority over Existing Transformers:** ResT significantly outperforms previous Transformer-based architectures with similar model complexities.
 - **ResT-Small** (13.66M params, 1.9 GFLOPs) achieves **79.6% Top-1 accuracy**, a notable **+4.5% improvement** over PVT-Tiny (13.2M params, 1.9 GFLOPs) at 75.1%.
 - **ResT-Lite** (10.49M params, 1.4 GFLOPs) achieves **77.2% Top-1 accuracy**, a significant **+7.5% improvement** over ResNet-18 (11.7M params, 1.8 GFLOPs) at 69.7%.
 - For larger models, **ResT-Base** (30.28M params, 4.3 GFLOPs) reaches **81.6% Top-1**, slightly surpassing Swin-Tiny (28.29M params, 4.5 GFLOPs) at 81.3% by +0.3%.
 - **ResT-Large** (51.63M params, 7.9 GFLOPs) achieves **83.6% Top-1**, again slightly better than Swin-Small (49.61M params, 8.7 GFLOPs) at 83.3% by +0.3%.
- **Superiority over State-of-the-Art ConvNets:** ResT also outperforms highly optimized CNNs like RegNet. With similar model complexity, ResT shows an average improvement of **1.7% in Top-1 Accuracy** over RegNet variants, despite RegNet being the product of extensive architecture search.

#### 4.2. Object Detection on COCO 2017 (RetinaNet framework)

- **Enhanced Performance for Dense Prediction:** ResT demonstrates strong performance on object detection, highlighting its effectiveness as a backbone for downstream tasks.
 - **ResT-Small** achieves **40.3 box AP**, outperforming PVT-T (36.7 box AP) by **+3.6 box AP** with similar computational cost.
 - **ResT-Base** achieves **42.0 box AP**, surpassing PVT-S (40.4 box AP) by **+1.6 box AP**.
 - **ResT-Large** (44.8 box AP) is competitive with Swin-S (44.5 box AP), showing a slight edge.

#### 4.3. Instance Segmentation on COCO 2017 (Mask RCNN framework)

- **Strong Generalization for Segmentation:** Similar to object detection, ResT excels in instance segmentation.
 - **ResT-Small** achieves **39.6 box AP and 37.2 mask AP**, exceeding PVT-T (36.7 box AP, 35.1 mask AP) by **+2.9 box AP and +2.1 mask AP**.
 - **ResT-Base** achieves **41.6 box AP and 38.7 mask AP**, bringing consistent **+1.2 box AP and +0.9 mask AP gains** over PVT-S (40.4 box AP, 37.8 mask AP), with a slightly larger model size.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (ImageNet-1k, simplified settings)

A detailed ablation study validates the effectiveness of individual design elements:
- **Stem Module:** The proposed stacked $3 \times 3$ convolution stem is more effective than PVT's $4 \times 4$ convolution and ResNet's $7 \times 7$ conv + max pooling, yielding **+0.92% and +0.64% Top-1 accuracy improvements**, respectively.
- **EMSA Reduction Strategy:** Depth-wise Conv2d for spatial reduction in EMSA performs best. Average Pooling is a close alternative (-0.24% Top-1) with no extra parameters, while Max Pooling performs significantly worse.
- **EMSA Components (1x1 Conv and IN):** Both the $1 \times 1$ convolution (for cross-head interaction) and Instance Normalization (for diversity restoration) are crucial. Removing IN degrades Top-1 accuracy by **0.9%**, and removing both leads to a **1.16% drop**, underscoring their combined importance.
- **Positional Encoding (PE):** Positional encoding is vital; removing it reduces Top-1 accuracy by **1.34%**. The proposed Pixel Attention (PA) module significantly outperforms learnable embeddings (LE) and group linear (GL) by **+0.84% Top-1 accuracy**, demonstrating its effectiveness and flexibility.
- **EMSA vs. Standard MSA (Efficiency):** A direct comparison reveals that EMSA achieves slightly better Top-1 accuracy (+0.2%) with fewer FLOPs (-0.2G) and **2.4x higher throughput** (1246 vs. 512 images/s) than standard MSA.
- **Layer Normalization (LN) for Downstream Tasks:** Adding Layer Normalization at the output of each stage (before FPN) improves object detection AP by an average of **+0.8 box AP**, confirming its importance for downstream applications.
- **Robustness to Training Tricks:** The ablation studies conducted with simpler data augmentation and hyperparameters (similar to ResNet's original training) demonstrated that ResT-Lite still outperformed ResNet-18 by +3.1% Top-1 accuracy, indicating that its improvements are due to architectural design rather than solely relying on aggressive training tricks.

In conclusion, the findings firmly establish ResT as a highly competitive and efficient backbone, achieving state-of-the-art results across a range of computer vision tasks while addressing critical limitations of prior Transformer architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ResT holds significant implications for the field of visual recognition and deep learning architecture design. Its potential impact spans both theoretical understanding and practical applications:

1. **Establishing a New State-of-the-Art Backbone:** ResT's consistent outperformance against leading CNNs (like ResNet, RegNet) and existing Vision Transformers (PVT, Swin) across image classification, object detection, and instance segmentation benchmarks positions it as a highly potent general-purpose backbone. This demonstrates that an efficient, multi-scale Transformer can indeed rival or surpass the best convolutional architectures, marking a key step in the evolution of vision models.

2. **Addressing Core Limitations of Vision Transformers:** This work effectively tackles several major impediments to the widespread adoption of Transformers in computer vision:
 - **Computational Efficiency:** The Efficient Multi-Head Self-Attention (EMSA) dramatically reduces the quadratic complexity of standard self-attention, making ResT significantly faster and less memory-intensive. This is crucial for deploying Transformer models on resource-constrained devices or in real-time applications.
 - **Multi-Scale Feature Learning:** By adopting a hierarchical architecture with overlapping patch embeddings, ResT successfully generates multi-scale feature representations, which are indispensable for dense prediction tasks that require rich contextual information at various spatial resolutions.
 - **Flexibility for Arbitrary Input Sizes:** The innovative design of positional encoding as spatial attention (PA) enables ResT to handle images of arbitrary sizes without re-interpolation or fine-tuning. This enhances its adaptability to diverse real-world scenarios, from high-resolution medical imaging to variable-resolution surveillance footage.
 - **Low-Level Feature Capture:** The CNN-like stem module and patch embedding strategy address the weakness of early Vision Transformers in extracting fundamental low-level features, integrating the benefits of local inductive biases.

3. **Advancing Hybrid Architecture Design:** ResT exemplifies the power of a successful hybrid approach, intelligently blending the strengths of CNNs (hierarchical feature extraction, local context) with Transformers (global attention, dynamic receptive fields). This success provides a strong impetus for future research into synergistic architectures that move beyond pure CNN or pure Transformer paradigms, exploring optimal combinations for various vision tasks.

4. **Practical Applicability and Deployment:** The improved efficiency and versatility of ResT make it a highly practical choice for real-world computer vision systems. Its ability to perform well across multiple tasks with competitive computational costs means it could replace existing backbones in various deployed applications, leading to better performance or more efficient resource utilization. The commitment to making code and models publicly available further accelerates its potential for adoption and further development by the research community.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** By demonstrating effective solutions to long-standing challenges, ResT opens new avenues for research. This includes exploring more advanced forms of efficient self-attention, novel ways to integrate positional information, and further optimizing hybrid architectures. The paper's explicit hope that "our approach will foster further research in visual recognition" underscores its foundational nature.

In conclusion, ResT represents a significant step forward in making Vision Transformers more practical, efficient, and versatile for a wide range of computer vision tasks. Its innovative design elements effectively bridge the gap between convolutional and attention-based architectures, setting a new standard for general-purpose backbones.
