# InterGen: Diffusion-based Multi-human Motion Generation under Complex Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> InterGen generates two-person interaction motions from text using a diffusion model with two weight-sharing transformer denoisers linked by mutual attention, a non-canonical world-frame motion representation, and distance-map plus relative-orientation regularizers applied only at low noise levels. Trained on the new InterHuman dataset (7,779 clips, 23,337 text descriptions), it reaches FID 5.918 versus 6.775 for the best prior method.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-motion diffusion models target a single person, modeling one body's kinematics while ignoring the spatial relations that define human-to-human interaction.
- Interaction datasets lack either natural language or accurate capture: action-label corpora use verb-object tags, and multi-person captures hold only dozens of annotated sequences.
- Canonical root-relative motion representations discard global spatial relations, and fine-tuning a single-person prior on scarce two-person data yields unnatural, poorly generalizing interactions.

## Method

- **InterHuman** dataset: 76-camera capture of 7,779 two-person clips (6.56 hours, about 107M video frames), ==SMPL== fitting, and three crowdsourced descriptions per clip totaling 23,337 texts.
- Cooperative denoisers: two ==transformer== denoisers with fully shared weights, self-attention followed by a ==mutual-attention== layer keyed on the partner's hidden states, encoding the commutative property of interaction data.
- Non-canonical representation keeping global joint positions and velocities in one world frame, plus masked joint-distance-map and relative-orientation losses damped to diffusion timesteps below 0.7T.

## Results

- On the InterHuman test set InterGen reaches FID **5.918** and Top-1 R-Precision **0.371**, beating RIG (**6.775** / **0.285**), ComMDM (**7.069** / **0.223**) and MDM (**9.167** / **0.153**).
- Ablations: canonical representation degrades FID to **11.322** and Top-1 to **0.134**; removing weight sharing gives FID **8.059**; dropping the distance-map loss gives **6.653**.
- Loss-schedule sweep peaks at t below 0.7T (FID **5.918**) versus **7.037** applying regularization at all timesteps and **7.862** with none; a LLaMA-7B text encoder lowers Top-1 to **0.345**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Symmetry in the data distribution can be baked into architecture: weight-shared denoisers with cross-attention stop one agent from dominating capacity, cheaper than adding parameters.
> - Auxiliary geometric losses should be gated by noise level; enforcing them on very noisy diffusion steps biases the estimator and degrades sample quality.
> - When relative geometry matters, avoid representations built on integrated local velocities; drift that is harmless for single-body synthesis destroys inter-body contact.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion generation has witnessed rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of deep generative models. Initial efforts leveraged Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to synthesize human movements conditioned on various inputs like action categories, music, or speech. More recently, the integration of large-scale language models (LLMs) and diffusion models has revolutionized this domain, enabling more intuitive and high-quality generation of human motions from natural language text prompts. Methods like MDM (Tevet et al., 2022b) and MLD (Chen et al., 2023) have democratized access to text-guided single-person motion generation.

However, a significant gap in this landscape has been the **multi-human interaction scenario**. Most prior research and available datasets have predominantly focused on single-person motions, largely overlooking the intricate challenges posed by interactions between multiple individuals. These challenges are multifaceted, including:
1. **Data Scarcity:** A severe lack of accurately captured multi-person interaction data explicitly paired with natural language descriptions.
2. **Modeling Complexity:** The need to effectively model spatial relationships, physical constraints, and the inherent symmetry when multiple humans interact. Simply extending single-person models to multiple individuals often leads to unnatural interactions, mode collapse (e.g., one person moving realistically while the other does not), or physical impossibilities like body penetration.

Concurrent works have attempted to address this, but with limitations. ComMDM (Shafir et al., 2023) fine-tunes single-person generators for two-person scenarios, but its performance is constrained by limited interaction patterns in its training data. RIG (Tanaka and Fujiwara, 2023) tries to recover 3D interaction motions from noisy depth data and translate labels, resulting in unrealistic motions and poor text-matching quality. These attempts highlight the dire need for both a dedicated, high-quality multimodal dataset and a specialized algorithmic framework.

InterGen positions itself as a pioneering solution by directly tackling these limitations. It makes a significant contribution to the research landscape by:
- **Introducing InterHuman:** The first large-scale, high-quality multimodal dataset specifically designed for two-person interactions, complete with accurate skeletal motions and diverse natural language descriptions. This dataset is crucial for advancing research in this under-explored area.
- **Proposing a Tailored Diffusion Model:** Developing a diffusion-based framework specifically designed to handle the complexities of two-person interactions, addressing issues of symmetry, global spatial relationships, and physical plausibility, which single-person models inherently cannot.

By providing both the necessary data infrastructure and a robust generative model, InterGen significantly pushes the boundaries of text-driven human motion generation, opening up new avenues for research in realistic multi-human behavior synthesis and understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind InterGen stems from the observation that while human motion generation has made impressive strides, it largely fails to capture the rich and complex dynamics of human-to-human interactions. The authors argue that reflecting how humans interact is essential for depicting diverse cultures and societies, and for creating truly immersive digital experiences.

The **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **To enable "layman users to customize high-quality two-person interaction motions, with only text guidance."** This emphasizes user-friendliness and accessibility, aiming to empower non-experts to generate complex interactive animations.
2. **To address the critical data gap in multi-human interaction research.** This involves creating a novel, large-scale multimodal dataset that provides accurate skeletal motions for two-person interactions along with rich natural language descriptions.
3. **To overcome the algorithmic challenges of modeling complex human-to-human interactions within a generative framework.** This includes developing a diffusion-based model that can effectively handle:
 - The **symmetry of human identities** during interactions.
 - The **precise spatial relations** between interacting performers in a global context.
 - The **physical plausibility** and **coherence** of interactive motions.
4. **To demonstrate the versatility of the proposed approach** through various downstream applications, showcasing its utility beyond basic motion generation.

In essence, InterGen aims to bridge the gap between sophisticated single-person motion generation and the largely unaddressed challenge of realistic, text-conditioned multi-person interaction synthesis, providing both the data and the methodology necessary for this advancement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InterGen's methodology is characterized by a dual approach: a significant **data contribution** and a meticulously tailored **diffusion-based generative model**.

#### 3.1. Data Contribution: The InterHuman Dataset

Recognizing the severe lack of suitable datasets, the authors first contribute **InterHuman**, a novel multimodal dataset.
- **Scale and Diversity:** It comprises approximately 107 million frames across 7,779 motion sequences, totaling 6.56 hours of diverse two-person interactions. These interactions range from everyday activities (e.g., hugging, greeting) to professional motions (e.g., Taekwondo, Latin dancing).
- **Capture System:** Motions were captured using a high-fidelity system of 76 calibrated RGB cameras. SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) parameters were extracted from these multi-view videos to obtain accurate 3D skeletal motions.
- **Multimodal Annotation:** For each motion clip, 23,337 unique natural language descriptions were gathered via Amazon Mechanical Turk, providing rich textual labels from multiple perspectives. This multi-modality is crucial for training text-conditioned generative models.
- **Augmentation:** The dataset was augmented by mirroring motions and swapping performer orders, along with corresponding text modifications, to enhance diversity and enforce the inherent symmetry of interactions.

#### 3.2. Algorithmic Approach: The InterGen Diffusion Model

The core of InterGen is a carefully designed diffusion model built upon three key technical innovations:

**a) Human Interaction Representation:**
- **Problem with Canonical Representation:** The authors argue that standard canonical motion representations (relative to the root joint) discard crucial global spatial information. For multi-person interactions, this leads to problematic motion drifting due to cumulative integration errors when deriving absolute trajectories.
- **Proposed Non-canonical Representation:** InterGen introduces a novel non-canonical representation that directly encodes global joint positions and velocities in the *world frame*, alongside local rotations and foot-ground contact features. This bypasses cumulative integration, explicitly preserves precise spatial relations between individuals, and avoids drift. Each motion state `x_i` includes global joint positions `j_p_g`, global velocities `j_v_g`, local rotations `j_r`, and contact features `c_f`.

**b) Human Interaction Diffusion Model with Cooperative Denoisers:**
- **Symmetry Assumption:** A fundamental insight is the commutative property of two-person interactions: `p(x_a, x_b) ≡ p(x_b, x_a)`. This implies that the underlying distribution and the denoising function should be symmetric with respect to the two performers.
- **Cooperative Transformer-style Denoisers:** To implement this symmetry, InterGen employs two transformer-style denoisers (`D_θ`), one for each performer. Critically, these denoisers **explicitly share weights**, ensuring they operate on the same motion manifold and possess equal capacity.
- **Mutual Attention Mechanism:** To foster coherent interaction, a novel mutual attention mechanism is introduced. During denoising, each performer's denoiser receives not only its own noisy motion and text condition but also queries the hidden states of its counterpart's denoiser. This explicit communication at different feature levels ensures that the generation of one person's motion is contextually aware of the other's, preventing disparate and unrealistic actions.

**c) Additional Regularization Losses and Damping Scheme:**
- **Geometric Losses:** Standard foot contact loss (`L_foot`) and joint velocity loss (`L_vel`) are used for single-person physical plausibility. A new **Bone Length Loss (`L_BL`)** is added for the non-canonical representation to maintain skeleton consistency.
- **Interactive Losses (Novel):**
 - **Masked Joint Distance Map (DM) Loss (`L_DM`):** Measures the spatial interference between the two people. It computes a joint distance map and is *masked* to apply only when the horizontal distance between the two individuals falls below a certain threshold. This enforces implicit physical constraints, preventing body penetration.
 - **Relative Orientation (RO) Loss (`L_RO`):** Encourages realistic relative facing directions. It estimates the 2D relative orientation between the two people around the Y-axis and aligns it with the ground truth, reflecting how humans naturally orient themselves during interaction.
- **Regularization Loss Schedule (Damping Scheme):** The authors observe that applying regularization losses when the diffusion noise level is very high (large `t`) can force the model to predict averaged, implausible poses. To counter this, a damping scheme is introduced where regularization losses are only applied when the sampled diffusion timestep `t` is below a specific threshold (`t <= t_bar`). This allows the model to focus on generating coherent motion at high noise levels and fine-tune physical and interaction plausibility as the noise decreases.

The model is trained using AdamW with classifier-free guidance, leveraging a frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 model for text encoding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InterGen demonstrates superior performance and versatility in generating two-person interaction motions from text prompts.

#### 4.1. InterHuman Dataset Validation

The InterHuman dataset proves to be a critical enabler, providing the necessary scale and diversity of multimodal data (accurate skeletal motions + natural language) for training advanced text-to-motion generative models specifically for interactions. Its comprehensive nature (daily to professional interactions) addressed a significant research gap.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance

InterGen quantitatively outperforms all tested baselines, including single-person methods adapted for two people (TEMOS, T2M, MDM) and dedicated two-person methods (ComMDM, RIG).
- **R-Precision (Top-1, Top-2, Top-3):** InterGen achieves significantly higher scores (e.g., Top-1 R-Precision of 0.371 compared to 0.285 for RIG and 0.223 for ComMDM), indicating superior text-motion consistency and retrieval accuracy.
- **Frechet Inception Distance (FID):** InterGen records the lowest FID (5.918), signifying that its generated motions are closer in distribution to real interaction motions.
- **Multimodal Distance (MM Dist):** A lower MM Dist (5.108) indicates better alignment between generated motion embeddings and their corresponding text embeddings.
- **Diversity and MModality:** InterGen maintains competitive diversity (5.108) and multimodality (2.141), showing its ability to generate varied and high-quality motions for a given prompt, avoiding mode collapse.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Performance

Qualitative results (Figures 7, 8) visibly showcase InterGen's ability to generate "more natural interaction states, more diverse motions, and more accurate alignment of the global relative orientations and translations between the two performers" compared to baselines. The generated interactions appear realistic, coherent, and precisely match the provided text descriptions, even for complex scenarios like Latin dance or boxing.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies robustly validate the necessity and effectiveness of InterGen's key technical designs:
- **Non-canonical Representation:** Replacing it with canonical representation leads to significant performance drops (e.g., R-Precision Top-1 drops from 0.371 to 0.134, FID increases from 5.918 to 11.322). Qualitatively, canonical representation results in severe motion drifting and a loss of realistic spatial interaction (Fig. 9), confirming the importance of direct world-frame encoding.
- **Cooperative Denoisers:**
 - Removing the mutual attention mechanism (concatenation-based conditioning) degrades performance across all metrics, leading to less plausible interactions.
 - Removing weight sharing between denoisers drastically worsens results (R-Precision Top-1 drops to 0.153, FID increases to 8.059), indicating a failure to balance motion capacity and leading to unrealistic, imbalanced interactions (e.g., one person moving expertly, the other not, as seen in Fig. 9). This confirms that weight sharing is crucial for enforcing the interaction symmetry and shared motion manifold.
- **Interactive Losses (`L_DM`, `L_RO`):**
 - Without `L_DM`, motions suffer from body penetration and unnatural spatial relations, with significant performance drops.
 - Without `L_RO`, the relative orientations between performers become inconsistent with the actions, leading to unrealistic interactions. Both losses are critical for enforcing geometric and social plausibility.
- **Regularization Loss Schedule:** The damping scheme (applying regularization only for `t <= 0.7T`) significantly outperforms applying losses at all timesteps or not applying them at all. This validates the strategy of adaptively applying physical constraints based on the noise level.
- **Text Encoders:** CLIP-ViT-L/14 consistently outperforms LLaMA-7B as a text encoder for this task, suggesting that pre-trained visual-language models provide better aligned semantic embeddings for motion generation than general-purpose LLMs in this context.

#### 4.5. Downstream Applications

InterGen demonstrates its practical utility through several applications:
- **Person-to-person generation:** Generating one person's motion conditioned on a frozen motion of another, showcasing controlled and diverse interactive responses.
- **Trajectory control:** Guiding two-person interactions along predefined trajectories while maintaining text-conditioned motion styles.
- **Interaction inbetweening:** Generating coherent and diverse intermediate motion sequences between specified start and end poses, combined with text prompts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InterGen represents a significant advancement in the field of human motion generation, particularly for multi-human interactions. Its potential impact is substantial across several domains:

1. **Enabling New Research Directions:** The contribution of the InterHuman dataset is arguably one of the most significant aspects. By providing a large-scale, high-quality, multimodal dataset for two-person interactions, InterGen effectively kickstarts and fuels future research into complex multi-human behavior analysis, understanding, and synthesis. It establishes a strong baseline for subsequent advancements.

2. **Bridging the Human-Computer Interaction Gap:** The ability to generate complex, realistic two-person interactions from simple text prompts significantly lowers the barrier for content creation. This empowers animators, game developers, and VR/AR content creators to prototype and generate intricate interactive scenes more efficiently and intuitively, without requiring extensive manual keyframing or motion capture sessions for every interaction variant.

3. **Advancing Digital Entertainment and Immersive Experiences:** In industries like gaming, film, and virtual/augmented reality, realistic multi-character interactions are crucial for immersion. InterGen's capability to generate diverse and compelling interactive motions on demand can lead to more dynamic game environments, lifelike cinematic sequences, and believable social simulations in virtual worlds.

4. **Novel Algorithmic Solutions:** The proposed architectural designs, including the non-canonical motion representation, cooperative denoisers with weight sharing and mutual attention, and specialized interaction losses with a damping schedule, offer robust solutions to fundamental challenges in multi-agent generative modeling. These innovations could inspire similar approaches in other multi-agent or multi-modal generative tasks beyond human motion.

5. **Foundation for Broader Applications:** While currently focused on two-person interactions, InterGen provides a strong foundation that could be extended to more complex scenarios involving larger groups or crowds (e.g., team sports, crowd dynamics simulations). It could also lay groundwork for more sophisticated human-robot interaction or assistive technology scenarios by generating contextually appropriate human responses.

Despite some acknowledged limitations (e.g., only two people, fixed length motions, minor jittering/penetration artifacts), InterGen offers a cornerstone for future work, serving as both a powerful baseline and a rich data resource. Its innovative approach to modeling symmetry and spatial relations in diffusion models sets a new standard for text-guided multi-human motion generation.
