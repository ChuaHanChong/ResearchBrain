# High-Dimensional Calibration from Swap Regret

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce TreeCal, a general algorithm for online multi-dimensional calibration applicable to arbitrary convex prediction spaces and norms, achieving improved upper bounds for various settings. The work also establishes strengthened lower bounds, demonstrating that high-dimensional calibration inherently requires a number of rounds exponentially dependent on the desired per-round calibration error.

## Problem

- Existing multi-dimensional online calibration methods often exhibited an exponential dependence on dimension, limiting their practical use in high-dimensional scenarios.
- A unified theoretical framework and algorithm for multi-dimensional calibration over arbitrary convex sets and arbitrary norms was lacking.
- Prior analyses were often tailored to specific prediction spaces or distance metrics, requiring a more general and simplified approach.

## Method

- The paper redefines multi-dimensional online calibration as an online full ==swap regret== minimization problem, leveraging Bregman divergences as proper scoring rules.
- It proposes TreeCal, a generic algorithm derived from the TreeSwap framework, which implicitly uses Follow-The-Leader (FTL) to play distributions over empirical averages of past outcomes.
- The analysis links TreeCal (TreeSwap.FTL) to a clairvoyant TreeSwap.BTL (Be-The-Leader) variant to derive upper bounds on calibration error and connects performance to optimal rates in online linear optimization (OLO).

## Results

- TreeCal achieves a ‖‖²-calibration error of ‖‖²_T ≤ ɛT in T ≥ (diam(P)/√ɛ)^O(Rate(P,‖‖)/ɛ) rounds for any bounded convex set P and norm ‖‖.
- The algorithm recovers the d^O(1/ɛ²) bound for ℓ1-calibration on the d-dimensional simplex and achieves a dimension-independent (1/ɛ)^O(1/ɛ²) bound for ℓ2-calibration on the ℓ2-ball.
- Strengthened lower bounds demonstrate that achieving ɛ-calibration in high dimensions requires T ≥ exp(c · min{d^(1/14), ɛ^(-1/6)}) rounds for ℓ1-calibration and T ≥ exp(Ω(min{d^(1/14), ɛ^(-1/7)})) for ℓ2-calibration, establishing an exponential dependence on 1/ɛ.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Calibration error can be precisely equated to full swap regret when utilizing Bregman divergence loss functions, offering a powerful theoretical reduction.
> - The 'rate ρ' of a strongly convex function, which defines the Bregman divergence, dictates calibration performance and is fundamentally linked to optimal regret rates in online linear optimization.
> - TreeCal's universality and reliance on simple empirical averages provide an efficient, generic solution that does not require problem-specific regularizers or OLO subroutines during execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The problem of online calibration, where a forecaster repeatedly makes predictions for a sequence of events, is a fundamental area in machine learning, statistics, and game theory. For binary outcomes, seminal work by Foster and Vohra [FV98] established that an online forecaster can achieve a sublinear O(T^(2/3)) calibration error, implying an average per-round error of ǫ after O(ǫ⁻³) rounds. This result highlighted the feasibility of efficient online calibrated predictions.

Many real-world applications, however, involve multi-dimensional outcomes, where predictions and outcomes belong to a d-dimensional convex set P. Extending classical calibration concepts to this multi-dimensional setting has been challenging. Prior methods, including extensions of Foster and Vohra's algorithm, often yielded bounds that exhibited an exponential dependence on the dimension `d` (e.g., (1/ǫ)^Ω(d) rounds for ǫ average per-round error). This exponential dependence made these algorithms impractical for high-dimensional scenarios.

Recently, [Pen25] introduced a new algorithm that significantly advanced the field by demonstrating polynomial dependence on `d` for ℓ1-calibration over the d-dimensional simplex, achieving ǫT error in d^O(1/ǫ²) rounds. This was the first known algorithm to achieve such a polynomial rate in `d` for fixed ǫ. [Pen25] also provided a lower bound of d^Ω(log 1/ǫ) rounds, indicating that a fully polynomial bound in both `d` and 1/ǫ might not be achievable.

This current paper builds upon and generalizes these recent advancements. It aims to provide a unified theoretical framework and an algorithm for multi-dimensional calibration that operates over an arbitrary convex set P and with respect to an arbitrary norm ‖·‖. It addresses the specificity of [Pen25]'s analysis by offering a more general approach rooted in swap regret minimization. Furthermore, it strengthens the existing lower bounds, indicating fundamental limitations on achievable calibration rates. The work connects online calibration to online linear optimization (OLO), leveraging insights from regret minimization literature to analyze calibration performance. This research contributes to the ongoing effort to understand the theoretical limits and practical algorithms for robust and reliable forecasting in complex, high-dimensional environments, with applications in areas like fairness in machine learning and game theory.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly efficient and universally applicable algorithm for multi-dimensional online calibration. The work aims to provide a unified analytical framework that demonstrates the performance of this algorithm across various settings, extending beyond specific choices of prediction spaces or distance metrics.

The key motivations driving this research are:

1. **Generalization of High-Dimensional Calibration**: Existing breakthroughs, such as that by [Pen25], are often tailored to specific scenarios (e.g., ℓ1-calibration over the simplex). A significant motivation is to generalize these results to arbitrary convex sets P in R^d and arbitrary norms ‖·‖. This broader applicability is crucial for practical settings where prediction spaces and desired evaluation metrics can vary widely.

2. **Unified and Simplified Analysis**: The analysis of prior high-dimensional calibration algorithms has been described as nuanced and specific to certain norms or problem structures. A core objective is to provide a simpler, more unified analytical framework. By connecting calibration to the well-studied problem of swap regret minimization, the authors aim to leverage established theoretical tools to simplify the derivation of calibration bounds across different settings. This approach makes the underlying mechanisms of calibration more transparent and potentially extends to new problem variants.

3. **Theoretical Connection to Online Linear Optimization (OLO)**: The paper explicitly seeks to establish a concrete link between online calibration and online linear optimization (OLO). It shows that the "rate" parameter (ρ) that dictates calibration performance is intimately related to the optimal regret rates achievable in OLO problems, specifically those where actions are drawn from P and losses from the dual norm unit ball. This connection is motivated by the desire to use well-understood concepts from OLO, such as strongly convex regularizers, to characterize and bound calibration error, thereby providing a deeper theoretical understanding of the problem's complexity.

4. **Development of an Efficient and Generic Algorithm**: The goal is to design an algorithm, TreeCal, that is not only theoretically sound but also practical. A key aspect of its design is its universality; the same algorithm should function effectively for any P and ‖·‖ without requiring explicit knowledge of problem-specific regularizers or OLO subroutines. This minimizes algorithmic complexity and makes it readily deployable. TreeCal is motivated by the TreeSwap algorithm for swap regret minimization, demonstrating that a specific instantiation (with Follow-The-Leader) can achieve strong calibration guarantees.

5. **Strengthening Lower Bounds**: Complementing the development of new algorithms, the research is motivated by a need to better understand the fundamental limits of online calibration. The paper aims to strengthen existing lower bounds, particularly by demonstrating that an exponential dependence on 1/ǫ (the desired per-round calibration error) is necessary for high-dimensional calibration. This strengthens previous bounds, such as d^Ω(log 1/ǫ), and resolves a theoretical question regarding the worst-case time complexity, indicating that sub-exponential dependence in 1/ǫ is impossible for a broad range of settings. This is achieved by leveraging recent strong lower bounds for swap regret.

In essence, the research is motivated by a dual pursuit: advancing the algorithmic frontier for high-dimensional online calibration through a unified, general, and efficient approach, while simultaneously deepening the theoretical understanding of its inherent computational limits through stronger lower bounds and connections to other online learning paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach, combining a novel algorithmic perspective with a rigorous theoretical analysis, drawing connections to established online learning frameworks.

#### 3.1. Problem Reduction: From Calibration to Swap Regret Minimization

The core of the methodology is to reframe the online calibration problem as an online full swap regret minimization problem. This is a generalization of ideas from recent works [LSS25, FKO+25].
1. **Bregman Divergences as Loss Functions**: The authors identify that for a chosen convex function R: P → R, the Bregman divergence D_R(y|p) = R(y) - R(p) - ⟨∇R(p), y - p⟩ can serve as a loss function for calibration.
2. **Proper Scoring Rules**: A key property of Bregman divergences is that they are "proper scoring rules." This means that if `y` is drawn from a distribution, the prediction `p` that minimizes the expected loss D_R(y|p) is precisely the expected value E[y]. This property ensures that minimizing the average Bregman divergence naturally encourages predictions to align with the conditional average of outcomes.
3. **Equivalence**: Lemma 2.2 formally establishes that for these Bregman divergence-based loss functions, the total D_R-calibration error is exactly equal to the full swap regret. This reduction transforms the calibration problem into one of minimizing swap regret.
4. **"Rate ρ" and Strong Convexity**: To bound the squared-norm calibration error (‖·‖²), the chosen function R must be α-strongly convex with respect to the norm ‖·‖ (meaning D_R(y|p) ≥ α‖y - p‖²). The maximum value of D_R(y|p) over P is defined as the "range ρ" of R. This ρ effectively quantifies the "difficulty" of the problem instance and is shown to be related to optimal regret rates in online linear optimization (Theorem 2.3, Lemma 2.4).

#### 3.2. Algorithm Design: TreeCal as an Instance of TreeSwap

The proposed algorithm, **TreeCal (Algorithm 1)**, leverages the structure of the TreeSwap algorithm [DDFG24, PR24] for full swap regret minimization.
1. **Tree Structure**: TreeCal operates over a hierarchical tree structure defined by base-H L-tuples for time steps T. Each node Γ(l)_k in the tree corresponds to an interval of time.
2. **Sub-forecasters and Averaging**: TreeCal assigns an action p(l)_kh to each node. At each time step `t`, TreeCal's prediction x_t is a uniform distribution over the actions assigned to the nodes on the root-to-leaf path corresponding to `t`. Crucially, these node-specific actions p(l)_kh are set to be the empirical average of all outcomes y_s observed *thus far* within the parent interval Γ(l-1)_k.
3. **Equivalence to TreeSwap.FTL**: Lemma 3.2 proves that TreeCal is equivalent to running the generic **TreeSwap (Algorithm 2)** with the **Follow-The-Leader (FTL)** algorithm as its subroutine. In this context, FTL's "losses" are the Bregman divergences D_R(y_t|p) derived from the observed outcomes y_t. The proper scoring rule property of Bregman divergences ensures that FTL, when faced with these losses, naturally computes empirical averages, making TreeCal's simple averaging strategy equivalent to TreeSwap.FTL.
4. **Universality**: It's emphasized that TreeCal itself does not require knowledge of the specific norm ‖·‖, the strongly convex function R, or the rate ρ. It's a single, generic algorithm. The choice of R and ρ only influences the *guaranteed calibration rate* in the analysis, not the algorithm's execution.

#### 3.3. Analysis Technique: Bridging with "Be-The-Leader" (BTL)

Directly bounding the swap regret of TreeSwap.FTL for arbitrary Bregman divergence losses is challenging. Therefore, the analysis takes an indirect route:
1. **Introducing TreeSwap.BTL**: The authors introduce a "clairvoyant" variant, **Be-The-Leader (BTL)**, where BTL_h(ℓ_1:h) minimizes the sum of losses up to the *current* time step `h` (including ℓ_h). BTL inherently has zero external regret.
2. **Bounding TreeSwap.BTL's Swap Regret**: Using Theorem C.1 (a result from [DDFG24] on TreeSwap), and the fact that BTL has zero external regret, the swap regret of TreeSwap.BTL for Bregman divergence losses (bounded by ρ) can be bounded as O(ρT/L), where L is the depth of the tree. This directly translates to a bound on the calibration error of TreeSwap.BTL.
3. **Comparing FTL and BTL**: The crucial step is to relate the implementable TreeSwap.FTL (TreeCal) to the un-implementable TreeSwap.BTL. This is done by showing that the predictions of FTL (empirical averages up to t-1) are "close" to those of BTL (empirical averages up to t). This closeness is quantified by ‖p_h - ˜p_h‖² ≤ 2 * diam(P)² (Lemma C.4).
4. **Labeled Calibration/Swap Regret**: A technical detail involves using "labeled" versions of calibration error and swap regret. This addresses scenarios where different nodes in the tree might produce identical actions. By labeling actions with their tree node identifier, the analysis ensures that the "worst-case" scenario (where each distinct prediction path is analyzed independently) is robustly bounded.
5. **Final Bound Derivation**: By combining the bound on TreeSwap.BTL's calibration error with the bound on the difference between FTL and BTL predictions (Claim C.3), the authors derive the final calibration error bound for TreeCal. Optimal parameters for H and L (tree arity and depth) are chosen to minimize the bound.

#### 3.4. Lower Bound Methodology

The lower bound proof leverages existing results:
1. **Swap Regret Lower Bound**: Theorem 4.1 from [DFG+24] provides a strong lower bound for swap regret in linear online optimization, stating that T must be exponential in parameters related to dimension and desired accuracy.
2. **Reduction from Swap Regret to Calibration**: A classic reduction from [FV98] (formalized in Lemma 4.2) shows that an algorithm guaranteeing ǫ-calibration can be used to construct an algorithm with bounded swap regret.
3. **Contradiction**: By assuming the existence of an algorithm that achieves calibration faster than the derived lower bound, this assumption leads to a contradiction with the known swap regret lower bound, thereby establishing the necessity of the exponential time complexity. This argument is applied to both ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms.

#### 3.5. Extension to Pure Calibration

The methodology also includes an extension to "pure calibration" (where the learner samples a single action from their distribution) using **SampleTreeCal (Algorithm 3)**. This involves repeating TreeCal's predictions over `S` rounds and using concentration inequalities (Lemma E.2) to relate the expected pure calibration error to the distributional calibration error.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings and results, advancing the understanding of high-dimensional online calibration.

#### 4.1. Unified Algorithm for Multi-Dimensional Calibration (TreeCal)

- The paper introduces **TreeCal (Algorithm 1)**, a generic algorithm for online calibration of multi-dimensional forecasts. A notable feature of TreeCal is its universality: it operates identically regardless of the specific convex set P (prediction space) or the norm ‖·‖ (distance metric) being considered. It does not require knowledge of an optimal regularizer or an online linear optimization (OLO) subroutine during execution. Instead, it plays a distribution over simple empirical averages of past observations.

#### 4.2. Upper Bounds on Calibration Error

The paper establishes concrete upper bounds on the calibration error of TreeCal:

- **Squared-Norm Calibration (Theorem 3.1)**: For any bounded convex set P and arbitrary norm ‖·‖, TreeCal guarantees a ‖·‖²-calibration error of Cal_‖·‖²_T ≤ ǫT, provided the number of rounds T is at least (diam(P)/√ǫ)^O(Rate(P,‖·‖)/ǫ). Here, Rate(P,‖·‖) quantifies the existence of a 1-strongly convex function R (with respect to ‖·‖) whose Bregman divergence has a range of at most ρ.
- **Norm Calibration (Corollary C.5)**: By applying Jensen's inequality, this result implies that TreeCal guarantees a ‖·‖-calibration error of Cal_‖·‖_T ≤ ǫT for T ≥ (diam(P)/ǫ)^O(Rate(P,‖·‖)/ǫ²).
- **Connection to Online Linear Optimization (Corollary C.6)**: When P is centrally symmetric, the Rate(P,‖·‖) term is shown to be equivalent (up to constant factors) to Rate_OLO(P,‖·‖), which represents the optimal regret rate for OLO problems with action set P and dual norm unit ball as the loss set. This formally links calibration performance to fundamental properties of OLO.
- **Recovery of Existing and Novel Bounds**:
 - For the d-dimensional simplex (P=∆^d) and ℓ1-norm, by using the negative entropy regularizer (which has Rate(P,‖·‖) = log d), TreeCal achieves ℓ1-calibration error in T = (1/ǫ)^O(log d/ǫ²) = d^O(1/ǫ²) rounds. This recovers the key result of [Pen25].
 - For the ℓ2-ball (P=B^d_2) and ℓ2-norm, using the Euclidean regularizer (R(x) = ‖x‖₂²), which has Rate(P,‖·‖) = 1, TreeCal achieves ℓ2-calibration error in T = (1/ǫ)^O(1/ǫ²) rounds. Notably, this bound is independent of the dimension `d`.

#### 4.3. Strengthened Lower Bounds on Calibration Error

The paper provides strengthened lower bounds, demonstrating the inherent complexity of high-dimensional online calibration:

- **ℓ1-Calibration Lower Bound (Theorem 1.4 / 4.3)**: For any ǫ > 0 and sufficiently large `d`, any algorithm guaranteeing an ℓ1-calibration error of ǫT over the d-dimensional simplex requires T ≥ exp(c · min{d^(1/14), ǫ^(-1/6)}) rounds for some constant c > 0. This significantly strengthens the d^Ω(log 1/ǫ) lower bound from [Pen25] by establishing an exponential dependence on 1/ǫ, implying that achieving arbitrary precision `ǫ` necessarily requires exponentially many rounds.
- **ℓ2-Calibration Lower Bound (Theorem D.2)**: A similar lower bound is proven for ℓ2-calibration over the unit ℓ2-ball, showing that T ≥ exp(Ω(min{d^(1/14), ǫ^(-1/7)})) time steps are needed. This bound is shown to be nearly tight (up to a polynomial in the exponent) for d ≥ ǫ⁻².

#### 4.4. Extension to Pure Calibration

- The work extends its analysis to the "pure calibration" setting, where the learner samples a single forecast p_t from their distribution x_t at each round. **SampleTreeCal (Algorithm 3)**, a variant of TreeCal, is shown to achieve an expected ‖·‖² pure calibration error of ǫT. The required number of rounds T depends on Rate(L,‖·‖⋆) (related to the dual norm) in addition to the parameters from the distributional calibration bound (Theorem E.1). This also recovers the pure calibration bound from [Pen25].

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of online learning and forecasting, with potential impacts across theoretical and applied domains.

#### 5.1. Generalization and Unification of High-Dimensional Calibration

- **Broader Applicability**: By providing a unified framework for multi-dimensional calibration over arbitrary convex sets P and arbitrary norms ‖·‖, the paper significantly broadens the scope of calibrated forecasting. This moves beyond specific cases (like the simplex with ℓ1-norm) to address a wider range of real-world scenarios in machine learning, economics, and game theory where diverse prediction spaces and evaluation metrics are common.
- **Simplified Analysis**: The observation that TreeCal is an instance of TreeSwap with FTL, and the subsequent analysis via the BTL algorithm, provides a parsimonious and unified analytical approach. This simplifies the understanding of calibration bounds, making the underlying mechanisms more accessible and potentially facilitating the derivation of bounds for new variants of calibration problems.

#### 5.2. Algorithmic Efficiency and Practicality

- **Generic Algorithm**: TreeCal is presented as a single, generic algorithm that does not require complex subroutines or knowledge of problem-specific parameters (like optimal regularizers) at runtime. Its simplicity, relying on empirical averages, contributes to its high efficiency and ease of implementation. This addresses a practical need for straightforward and robust calibration methods in high-dimensional settings.

#### 5.3. Fundamental Theoretical Contributions

- **Tightened Upper Bounds**: The derived upper bounds, particularly the d-independent bound for ℓ2-calibration over the ℓ2-ball, establish new benchmarks for online calibration performance in specific settings. The connection to online linear optimization provides a deeper theoretical understanding of the factors influencing calibration rates (e.g., the "rate ρ" of a strongly convex regularizer).
- **Stronger Lower Bounds**: The most impactful theoretical contribution is the strengthening of lower bounds, demonstrating that an exponential dependence on 1/ǫ is necessary for achieving ǫ-calibration in high-dimensional settings. This resolves a critical question regarding the fundamental limits of online calibration, showing that a truly "polynomial" solution (in both `d` and 1/ǫ) is impossible for certain problem instances. This result guides future research by setting a realistic expectation for what algorithms can achieve.

#### 5.4. Interdisciplinary Impact

- **Machine Learning and Fairness**: Calibrated predictions are crucial for reliable decision-making in machine learning, especially in sensitive applications like risk assessment and medical diagnostics, and are foundational for notions of fairness (e.g., multicalibration). The improved algorithms and understanding of limitations can lead to more robust and fair ML systems.
- **Game Theory and Economics**: Calibration and swap regret are central concepts in online learning in games and are related to notions of equilibrium. The work's unification of calibration and swap regret via a common algorithmic framework could foster new developments in understanding strategic interactions and learning in multi-agent systems.
- **Online Learning Theory**: The paper contributes to the broader theory of online learning by bridging seemingly distinct problem formulations (calibration, swap regret, online linear optimization) through a unified algorithmic and analytical lens. This cross-pollination of ideas can lead to new insights and techniques applicable across various online decision-making problems.

In summary, this research not only offers a powerful and general algorithm for high-dimensional online calibration but also significantly advances the theoretical understanding of its capabilities and inherent limitations. Its impact lies in its potential to enable more reliable and fair predictive systems while pushing the boundaries of online learning theory.
