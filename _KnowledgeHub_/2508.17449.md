# Robotic Manipulation via Imitation Learning: Taxonomy, Evolution, Benchmark, and Challenges

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic survey of imitation-learning-based robotic manipulation policies, analyzing 120 papers (82 core) via a structured taxonomy covering control strategy, input modality, pretraining, and purpose. It compiles cross-benchmark quantitative comparisons (CALVIN, RLBench, LIBERO, MetaWorld, SimplerEnv, COLOSSEUM) and traces the field's evolution from diffusion and flow-matching to autoregressive and affordance-driven policies, surfacing open challenges in generalization, embodiment diversity, and benchmark standardization.

## Problem

- Prior surveys on robotic manipulation and VLA models lack a structured, comparable summary format covering purpose, technical implementation, priors, strengths, and limitations per paper.
- No prior survey compiles a chronological, timeline-based view tracing how key manipulation techniques methodologically evolved across diffusion, flow matching, and autoregressive paradigms.
- Existing reviews rarely aggregate quantitative benchmark results across studies, leaving no integrated, comparative performance view across tasks and environments.

## Method

- A ==four-perspective hierarchical taxonomy== classifying 82 representative papers by purpose, pretraining strategy, input modality, and control strategy, spanning ==action-generation== and ==task-planner== policy families.
- A structured per-paper summary table (Tab. I/II) recording core priors, technical tools, strengths, limitations, and average monthly citation counts as an impact indicator, selected from an initial pool of about 150 papers.
- A ==cross-benchmark quantitative compilation== aggregating leaderboard and reported results from CALVIN, RLBench, LIBERO, MetaWorld, SimplerEnv, and COLOSSEUM into unified comparison tables.

## Results

- On CALVIN zero-shot long-horizon evaluation, SeeR-Large reaches average trajectory length **4.28** versus **0.31** for the earliest MCIL baseline across 34 tasks.
- On LIBERO's four task suites, UniVLA and OpenVLA-OFT achieve about **95%** average success rate with VLA pretraining, versus roughly **65-88%** for non-pretrained baselines like SmolVLA and DP.
- On COLOSSEUM's 12-axis perturbation benchmark, SAM2Act shows only a **4.3**-point average performance drop versus a **61.8**-point drop for ACT, indicating large generalization-robustness gaps across methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Field evolution shows a consistent trajectory from diffusion-based continuous control toward flow-matching efficiency and VLM-integrated autoregressive/affordance-driven generalist policies.
> - High task success on curated benchmarks does not imply robustness; generalization under systematic perturbation (COLOSSEUM) reveals order-of-magnitude performance gaps invisible in standard success-rate leaderboards.
> - The field lacks unified, standardized benchmarks and cross-embodiment input/output spaces, which structurally limits fair comparison and transfer of manipulation policies across robot platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself critically within the rapidly expanding field of robot learning, specifically focusing on **imitation learning (IL) for robotic manipulation (RM)**. The authors clearly state their motivation: to provide "the first systematic review dedicated to imitation learning for robotic manipulation," synthesizing methods, benchmarks, and challenges, particularly considering research conducted between 2021 and 2025 due to rapid advancements.

Previous surveys have touched upon broader aspects of robot learning, deep reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation, in-contact tasks, grasp synthesis, and embodied learning for object-centric manipulation. However, this work differentiates itself by offering a uniquely focused and granular examination of IL's role in RM. Key distinctions from related surveys, such as Zheng et al., include:
- **Scope and Depth:** Identifying and analyzing a larger, more representative set of 82 influential papers with structured summaries covering purpose, technical implementation, taxonomy, input formats, priors, strengths, limitations, and citation metrics.
- **Chronological Evolution:** Tracing the methodological shifts in IL techniques for Robotic Manipulation Policies (RMPs), from diffusion and flow matching to autoregressive and affordance-driven policies.
- **Quantitative Benchmarking:** Compiling and comparing benchmark results across various tasks and environments, offering an integrated view of performance.
- **Forward-Looking Analysis:** Outlining open challenges and promising future directions, aiming to mobilize community efforts.

This survey addresses a crucial need for a structured overview in a domain characterized by fast-paced development, the emergence of large-scale datasets (like Open-X Embodiment), and the integration of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). By acting as a comprehensive reference, it helps both newcomers grasp the current landscape and active researchers efficiently locate relevant works and identify gaps. It effectively synthesizes the transition from task-specific, data-intensive methods to more generalizable, data-efficient, and semantically aware policies, often driven by pre-trained foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this survey is to provide a systematic, comprehensive, and up-to-date review of imitation learning techniques applied to robotic manipulation. This core objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Centrality of Robotic Manipulation:** RM is identified as a fundamental capability for autonomous robots to interact with and transform their environments, crucial for automation across diverse domains (manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, household).
- **Emergence of Imitation Learning:** IL has proven to be a powerful paradigm for robots to quickly acquire complex manipulation skills from human demonstrations, circumventing the challenges of explicit rule-based programming or the trial-and-error nature of reinforcement learning.
- **Rapid Field Development:** The field of IL-based RMPs is evolving rapidly, particularly amplified by advances in computer vision and large language models (LLMs). A current, structured synthesis is needed to keep pace with these developments (focusing on 2021-2025).
- **Bridging Knowledge Gaps:** The survey aims to serve as a dual resource:
 - **For Newcomers:** To provide a rapid and comprehensive understanding of the current research landscape, aiding their entry into the field.
 - **For Active Researchers:** To help them efficiently locate related works, assess approaches, and understand the core information (purpose, input, pre-training, strengths, limitations, citation metrics).
- **Mobilizing Collective Efforts:** By presenting a hierarchical taxonomy, quantitative comparisons across benchmarks, and a summary of application areas and existing challenges, the authors intend to highlight critical research gaps and promising directions, thereby accelerating progress within the community towards scalable and general-purpose RMPs.
- **Highlighting the Role of Foundation Models:** The survey implicitly aims to showcase how the principles of large-scale pretraining, successful in NLP and CV, are increasingly being adapted and refined for embodied AI, particularly through VLAs and diffusion models.

In essence, the authors seek to bring order and clarity to a complex, dynamic field, providing both a historical context of evolution and a forward-looking perspective on future research trajectories and challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this survey is a systematic literature review and synthesis, structured to provide a comprehensive overview of imitation learning in robotic manipulation. The authors detail their approach in the "Paper Selection Criteria and Content Extraction" section:

- **Initial Collection:** An initial pool of approximately 150 papers was gathered through continuous monitoring of the literature and targeted keyword searches. This suggests a blend of proactive tracking and systematic querying.
- **Selection Criteria for Representative Studies:** From the initial pool, a subset of papers was selected based on two primary criteria:
 - **Scientific Quality:** Intrinsic merit and technical rigor of the work.
 - **Community Impact:** Quantified by average monthly citation counts and indicators of social media engagement. This ensures that the selected papers are not only scientifically sound but also influential and recognized by the broader research community.
- **Chronological and Categorical Organization:** Selected papers were organized chronologically within each methodological category. This allows for tracing the evolution of techniques over time. To manage scope, only one representative paper from each series (e.g., RT-1 from the RT family) was typically included.
- **Structured Content Extraction:** For each selected paper, the authors extracted and summarized specific information points:
 - **Purpose:** The motivation and desirable properties of the method.
 - **Technical Implementation and Hierarchical Classification:** Details about the underlying algorithms (e.g., diffusion models, flow matching, autoregressive models) and how they fit into the proposed taxonomy.
 - **Input Formats:** The types of sensory data used (e.g., RGB, depth, proprioception, object state, text).
 - **Key Priors:** Explicit or implicit knowledge embedded in the models (e.g., chain-of-thought, affordance, kinematic constraints).
 - **Strengths and Limitations:** Critical assessment of each method's advantages and disadvantages.
 - **Citation Metrics:** Quantitative measure of impact (average monthly citations).
- **Hierarchical Taxonomy Development:** The core of the survey's organization is a novel hierarchical taxonomy (detailed in Section II and Fig. 1, Tab. I). This classification system categorizes RMPs based on:
 - **Control Strategy:** Action generation (continuous/discrete) and Task planner (generative models, naive classification, affordance prediction).
 - **Action Generation Subtypes:** Diffusion model-based, flow matching-based, naive regression, autoregressive, and naive classification.
 - **Task Planner Subtypes:** Generative models, naive classification, and affordance prediction.
- **Classification by Purpose:** Further categorization based on the problem settings and challenges addressed: single-task, multi-task/single-scenario, multi-scenario, real-world scenarios, continual learning, generalization (intra-task, inter-task, embodiment), data efficiency, and sampling efficiency.
- **Pretraining Methods Analysis:** A dedicated section explores how models leverage action data (in-domain/out-of-domain) and action-free data for pretraining.
- **Quantitative Evaluation:** The authors compiled and presented benchmark results from various robotic manipulation environments (CALVIN, RLBench, LIBERO, Meta-World, SimplerEnv, COLOSSEUM) using relevant metrics (success rate, task completion time, Avg. Len, SPL, generalization metrics). This involved reviewing papers and leaderboards to provide objective comparisons.

This meticulous approach ensures a structured, data-driven, and critical examination of the literature, providing both a broad overview and specific insights into individual works.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey distills a wealth of information into several key findings, organizing the complex landscape of robotic manipulation via imitation learning:

- **Comprehensive Taxonomy of RMPs:** The authors successfully developed a hierarchical taxonomy (Fig. 1, Table I) classifying RMPs based on their **control strategy** (action generation vs. task planner) and further subdividing them by the **type of action produced** (continuous vs. discrete) or the **method for target prediction**. This includes a detailed analysis of diffusion model-based, flow matching-based, naive regression, autoregressive, naive classification, and affordance prediction approaches. This taxonomy provides a much-needed framework for understanding the diverse methodologies.
- **Chronological Evolution and Methodological Shifts:** The survey effectively traces the evolution of IL techniques from traditional methods to advanced generative models (diffusion models, flow matching) and large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) and transformers. It highlights key shifts such as the progression from Diffusion Policy to 3D Diffusion Policy and then to equivariant diffusion policies, as well as the rise of autoregressive models conditioned on multimodal inputs, epitomized by models like Gato, VIMA, RT-1, and PaLM-E.
- **Impact of Pretraining and Foundation Models:** A significant finding is the increasing reliance on large-scale pretraining, both with action data (in-domain and out-of-domain) and action-free data. The survey demonstrates how the success of LLMs and VLMs has spurred the development of "generalist agents" by leveraging vast datasets and transformer architectures, leading to policies with enhanced semantic reasoning and generalization capabilities (e.g., Open-X Embodiment dataset, Octo, π0).
- **Categorization by Purpose and Challenges Addressed:** The survey effectively categorizes research based on its purpose, including efforts towards single-task mastery, multi-task learning in single/multi-scenario settings, real-world deployment, continual learning, and various facets of generalization (intra-task, inter-task, embodiment). It also identifies critical research challenges such as data efficiency (reducing reliance on labeled data through 3D representations, actionless data, equivariance) and sampling efficiency (improving control frequency via flow matching, action chunking, token caching).
- **Quantitative Performance Synthesis:** The survey presents a valuable compilation of quantitative comparison results across major benchmarks like CALVIN, RLBench, LIBERO, MetaWorld, SimplerEnv-Google Robot, and COLOSSEUM (Tables III-VIII). These tables objectively illustrate the state-of-the-art in different task settings (e.g., zero-shot long-horizon, multi-task, generalization to perturbations) and across various metrics (success rate, Avg. Len, generalization decrease). For instance, it highlights models like SeeR-Large and RoboVLM as top performers on CALVIN long-horizon tasks, and EquAct on RLBench success rates.
- **Identification of Commonly Used Priors:** The authors summarize prevalent prior knowledge integrated into RMPs, including Chain-of-Thought (for task decomposition), affordance knowledge (for object interaction), waypoints (for intermediate goals), kinematic constraints (for physical realism), and equivariance constraints (for data efficiency).
- **Mapping to Real-world Applications:** The survey links the discussed methodologies to practical applications, categorizing them into Primitive Manipulation, Contact-Rich Assembly, Kitchen Assistant, Tool-Mediated Manipulation, and Garbage Cleaning, showcasing the breadth of robotic capabilities being developed.
- **Key Open Challenges:** The survey synthesizes a set of persistent and emerging open challenges:
 - **Insufficient Generalization:** Policies remain sensitive to scene/task variations and struggle with real-world complexities.
 - **Diverse Robot Configurations:** Difficulty in developing unified policies for heterogeneous robot designs and DOFs.
 - **Lack of Unified Benchmarks:** The absence of standardized, user-friendly, and comprehensive benchmarks hinders consistent evaluation and progress.
 - **Dependence on Expert Data:** High reliance on costly labeled data and limited adaptability during test time.
 - **Collaborative and Dexterous Manipulation:** These advanced forms of manipulation (dual-arm, high-DOF) are still in nascent stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a highly significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for researchers and practitioners working with imitation learning. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

- **Standardization and Clarification:** By presenting the "first systematic review dedicated to imitation learning for robotic manipulation" and offering a comprehensive taxonomy, the paper provides a much-needed framework for understanding, classifying, and comparing diverse research efforts. This standardization helps to clarify terminology and methodological distinctions, reducing ambiguity in a rapidly evolving domain.
- **Accelerating Research Progress:**
 - **For Newcomers:** It serves as an invaluable entry point, offering a distilled understanding of foundational concepts, state-of-the-art methods, and key challenges, thereby lowering the barrier to entry and enabling faster integration into the research community.
 - **For Active Researchers:** It acts as a comprehensive reference, allowing efficient identification of related work, assessment of technical implementations, and understanding of strengths/limitations. This can prevent redundant efforts, foster cross-pollination of ideas, and highlight promising avenues for future work.
- **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The detailed discussion of open challenges (generalization, embodiment diversity, data efficiency, benchmark standardization, collaborative manipulation) explicitly points to critical areas requiring further investigation. The proposed solutions, such as embedding bio-inspired inductive biases and developing foundation models, offer strategic guidance for future research, potentially leading to more robust, adaptable, and general-purpose RMPs. The authors’ reference to their position paper on bio-inspired foundation models further underscores their commitment to guiding the field.
- **Promoting Robust Evaluation:** By compiling and analyzing quantitative results across various benchmarks, the survey underscores the importance of rigorous evaluation. It highlights existing benchmarks and metrics, implicitly advocating for standardized testing protocols, which is crucial for objectively measuring progress and enabling fair comparisons between different approaches. The identification of "lack of unified benchmarks" as a major challenge explicitly calls for community action in this regard.
- **Inspiring Cross-Disciplinary Integration:** The emphasis on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, large-scale pretraining, and the integration of concepts from LLMs (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) highlights the increasing interdisciplinary nature of robotics research. This survey can inspire further integration of advancements from computer vision and natural language processing into embodied AI, fostering innovation.
- **Informing Application Development:** The mapping of methodologies to real-world application areas provides a clear view of where IL-based RMPs are currently making an impact and where further development is needed. This is valuable for both academic research and industrial application, guiding the development of robots for manufacturing, healthcare, and everyday assistance.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is a critical synthesis that structures the past, illuminates the present, and charts a course for the future of imitation learning in robotic manipulation, poised to significantly influence the direction and accelerate the pace of research in this vital field.
