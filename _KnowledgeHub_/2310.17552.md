# Model-Based Runtime Monitoring with Interactive Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Texas at Austin developed a model-based runtime monitoring algorithm for interactive imitation learning that leverages human interventions as implicit risk cues to anticipate robot failures. This approach achieved 32% higher task success in simulation and 40% higher on hardware compared to baselines, while also enabling 16% more efficient use of human supervision.

## Problem

- Learning-based robotic systems often lack generalization and robustness, hindering their deployment in real-world, high-stakes applications.
- Interactive imitation learning (IIL) demands continuous human supervision, leading to a prohibitive workload for human operators.
- Current runtime monitoring methods either fail to anticipate future errors (Out-of-Distribution detection) or require explicit, often unsafe, failure data for training (supervised methods).

## Method

- A **Sirius-Runtime** model-based ==runtime monitoring== algorithm is integrated into an ==interactive imitation learning== (IIL) framework to continuously learn and predict failures.
- A ==Conditional Variational Autoencoder== (cVAE) dynamics model simulates future robot trajectories in a latent space to proactively anticipate undesirable states.
- A ==failure classifier== is trained using ==human interventions as implicit labels== for risky or undesirable states, bypassing the need for explicit failure examples.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an average of **32%** higher task success rates in simulation and **40%** higher on physical hardware compared to existing baselines.
- It demonstrated **16%** more efficient utilization of human workload (Normalized Return On Human Effort) compared to state-of-the-art interactive imitation learning baselines.
- Unit tests showed the error predictor had higher **IOU** and lower **DCI** values, indicating its predictions aligned more closely with human-labeled failure regions and intervention timings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human interventions, even without explicit failure labels, provide sufficient implicit supervisory signals to train a failure predictor aligned with human risk assessment.
> - Utilizing a learned dynamics model for future latent state prediction allows for proactive detection of potential failures, enabling timely human intervention.
> - The combination of out-of-distribution (OOD) detection and an intervention-informed failure classifier enhances robustness by addressing both unfamiliar situations and subtle, fine-grained error patterns.
