# Matrix-Game 3.0: Real-Time and Streaming Interactive World Model with Long-Horizon Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Skywork AI developed Matrix-Game 3.0, an interactive world model that achieves real-time, 720p generation at up to 40 FPS with a 5B parameter model. This system integrates advancements in data generation, model architecture, and inference optimization to maintain memory-enabled long-term temporal consistency over minute-long sequences.

## Problem

- Existing interactive world models struggle to concurrently deliver high-resolution output, real-time performance, and long-horizon memory consistency.
- A significant data bottleneck exists in acquiring large-scale, high-quality, and precisely annotated video datasets required for training robust interactive models.
- Current approaches face trade-offs between maintaining long-horizon memory, coherence, and controllability, and managing error accumulation during streaming inference.

## Method

- Developed an industrial-scale data engine to generate high-quality Video–Pose–Action–Prompt quadruplet data from synthetic, AAA game, and real-world sources.
- Designed an error-aware interactive Diffusion Transformer (DiT) base model with a novel camera-aware memory retrieval and injection mechanism for long-horizon consistency.
- Implemented a training–inference aligned few-step distillation pipeline and a real-time acceleration module featuring DiT quantization, pruned VAE, and GPU-based memory retrieval.

## Results

- Achieved real-time generation speeds of up to 40 FPS at 720p resolution with a 5B parameter model in an asynchronous 8+1 GPU setup.
- The memory-augmented base model successfully recovered previously observed scene structures and fine-grained appearance details during scene revisitation, demonstrating effective long-term memory.
- Scaling the model to 28B parameters resulted in enhanced temporal consistency in scene layout, character identity, and vivid motion dynamics under continuous camera and action changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training an interactive base model with error injection and collection enhances its robustness to imperfect historical contexts, crucial for stable streaming inference.
> - A unified self-attention mechanism, processing memory, history, and current latents with camera-aware Plücker encoding, enables effective long-horizon memory integration.
> - Strategic inference accelerations, such as INT8 quantization, VAE pruning, and GPU-based memory retrieval, are essential for achieving real-time high-resolution generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Matrix-Game 3.0 is situated within the broader research landscape of generative AI, specifically focusing on the creation of "world models" capable of simulating complex environmental dynamics and predicting future observations based on user actions. This field has implications for robotics, entertainment, and extended reality (XR) applications.

Recent advancements in diffusion-based short video generation have demonstrated the potential of these models as world simulators, offering high-resolution, temporally coherent clip synthesis. However, the existing challenge for these models lies in their inability to simultaneously achieve memory-enabled long-term temporal consistency, high-resolution fidelity, and real-time generation speed. Current models, such as those designed for offline video generation (e.g., Sora, Kling), lack explicit action modeling and struggle with long-horizon consistency, often relying on implicit mechanisms that are insufficient for extended, interactive scenarios.

Prior interactive world models, like Genie, Genie-2, and Genie-3, have progressively demonstrated the feasibility of learning interaction-conditioned world dynamics and real-time simulation, but details regarding their training recipes and inference stacks are often proprietary or undisclosed. Open-source efforts, including OASIS, Matrix-Game 2.0, HY-Gamecraft-2, Lingbot-World, RELIC, and WorldPlay, have explored various aspects such as real-time streaming, causal autoregressive diffusion, and memory-augmented mechanisms. Matrix-Game 2.0 and HY-Gamecraft-2 achieved real-time streaming but lacked stable minute-long consistency. Lingbot-World improved long-horizon geometric consistency by scaling context length, but integrating this with memory and real-time deployment remained challenging. RELIC leveraged camera-aware KV caches for memory but introduced computational overhead that hindered high-resolution real-time application. These prior works generally exhibit trade-offs between long-horizon consistency, high-resolution output, and real-time performance.

Matrix-Game 3.0 aims to address these identified limitations by offering a unified framework that concurrently advances in data generation, model architecture, and inference optimization. It builds upon Matrix-Game 2.0 by introducing systematic improvements to facilitate the practical deployment of industrial-scale world models. The work attempts to bridge the gap between strong video generation capabilities and the stringent requirements of interactive, persistent, and real-time world simulation, particularly regarding long-term memory and consistency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Matrix-Game 3.0 is to develop a memory-augmented interactive world model capable of simultaneously achieving memory-enabled long-term temporal consistency and high-resolution real-time generation, thereby enhancing applicability in practical, real-world scenarios.

Specifically, the key objectives articulated by the authors include:
- Achieving real-time, long-form video generation at 720p resolution.
- Operating at a frame rate of up to 40 FPS with a 5B parameter model.
- Maintaining stable memory consistency over minute-long sequences.
- Improving generation quality, dynamics, and generalization through scaling to larger models (e.g., 2x14B or 28B).
- Providing a practical pathway toward industrial-scale deployable world models.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges observed in the current landscape of interactive video generation and world modeling:
1. **Lack of Simultaneous Capabilities:** Existing models generally struggle to concurrently deliver high-resolution output, real-time performance, and long-horizon memory consistency. When applied to interactive settings, they often produce incoherent short segments or suffer from high latency, limiting their utility as reliable world simulators.
2. **Data Bottleneck:** Learning robust interactive controllability and memory capabilities requires large-scale, precisely annotated video datasets, which web-scale scraping or single game simulators cannot adequately provide. There is a need for diverse, high-quality data spanning various viewpoints and scene dynamics.
3. **Modeling Trade-offs:** Bridging strong bidirectional video priors with streaming inference often involves compromises between long-horizon memory, coherence, controllability, and error accumulation. Current distillation methods might overlook base model robustness to error accumulation or the distribution gap between student and base models. Scaling context length for consistency often conflicts with real-time inference requirements.
4. **Deployment Challenges:** While industrial systems have reportedly achieved real-time high-resolution interaction, the underlying training recipes and full inference pipelines are frequently proprietary, hindering reproducibility and broader research progress. There is a need for open, detailed approaches to accelerate real-time performance.

To address these motivations, Matrix-Game 3.0 proposes a co-designed solution across three tightly coupled factors: data, modeling, and deployment, aiming to systematically overcome the identified limitations and advance the state-of-the-art in interactive world modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Matrix-Game 3.0 integrates advancements across data, model architecture, and inference acceleration. The system comprises four primary components: an error-aware interactive base model, a camera-aware long-horizon memory mechanism, a training–inference aligned few-step distillation pipeline, and a real-time inference acceleration module.

#### 3.1. Data System

An industrial-scale data engine was developed to produce high-quality Video–Pose–Action–Prompt quadruplet data. This engine integrates three complementary sources:
- **Unreal Engine–based Synthetic Pipeline (Unreal-Gen):** Utilizes Unreal Engine 5 to generate cinema-quality video from over 1,000 custom scenes. It ensures tick-level synchronization of rendered RGB frames, player world position/rotation, camera 6-DoF pose, and discrete action vectors. Features include a NavMesh–RL hybrid agent for autonomous exploration, stochastic camera control, and a combinatorial character assembly system yielding over 10^8 unique variants for visual diversity. The pipeline includes industrial-grade automation for batch rendering, cloud synchronization, and automated quality assurance.
- **Scalable AAA Game Data Recording System:** A four-layer decoupled architecture enables synchronized capture from multiple AAA titles (e.g., GTA V, Red Dead Redemption 2). It automates agent control, state extraction, OBS Studio segmented recording (60s segments), and physics-based WSAD inference to classify actions, exporting MP4 video with per-frame CSV containing action vectors, camera parameters, and full pose information. This system emphasizes scalability and reliability with automatic recovery mechanisms.
- **Real-World Data Augmentation:** Incorporates four existing real-world video datasets: DL3DV-10K, RealEstate10K, OmniWorld-CityWalk, and SpatialVid-HD. These datasets provide diverse photometric variations and natural camera trajectories, covering indoor, urban, aerial, and vehicular scenes. All real-world data are uniformly re-annotated using ViPE to ensure consistent coordinate conventions and pose representations.

**Data Annotation and Quality Filtering:** Fine-grained caption descriptions are generated for all datasets using InternVL3.5-8B, following a four-tier hierarchical schema (narrative, static scene, dense temporal captions, and perceptual quality scores). Trajectory and speed filtering are applied based on local geometric consistency, global motion anomaly, and camera speed to remove pose errors and abnormal motion, yielding a high-quality curated training set.

#### 3.2. Error-Aware Interactive Base Model

The base model is an action-guided self-correcting Diffusion Transformer (DiT). Key principles include:
- **Architectural Consistency:** A unified bidirectional DiT architecture is used for both the multi-step base model and the few-step distilled model, aiming to prevent instability from heterogeneous teacher–student architectures.
- **Robustness to Imperfect Contexts:** The model is trained to handle imperfect historical contexts (e.g., self-generated history latents during streaming inference) rather than only clean ground-truth data. This is achieved by introducing **error collection and error injection** via an error buffer (E). Residuals between model output and clean estimates are collected, and sampled residuals perturb history latents during training (˜x_i = x_i + γδ), fostering self-correction.
- **Action Control:** Discrete keyboard actions are incorporated via a dedicated Cross-Attention module, and continuous mouse-control signals are injected through Self-Attention, enabling precise and stable interactive responses.

The model partitions input sequences into past (k) and current (N-k) latent frames, with Gaussian noise added to current frames, and the flow-matching objective applied only to the current frames.

#### 3.3. Long-Horizon Memory

To enable streaming interactive generation with memory, Matrix-Game 3.0 employs a camera-aware memory retrieval and injection mechanism.
- **Joint Self-Attention Mechanism:** Instead of a separate memory branch, retrieved memory latents, temporally aligned recent latents (past frames), and current prediction noisy latents are processed together within the same DiT self-attention space. This allows for direct information exchange across spatial and temporal levels.
- **Camera-Aware Memory Selection and Plücker Encoding:** Memory frames are retrieved based on camera pose and field-of-view overlap to ensure relevance. An optional persistent "sink latent" derived from the first frame provides a global anchor for scene style. Relative camera geometry between the current target and selected memory is explicitly encoded via Plücker-style cues to maintain view-consistent scene alignment.
- **Error Correction for Memory Pathway:** To mitigate train-inference mismatch, the shared latent error buffer E is used for memory latents, recent past frame latents, and current prediction latents. Perturbations (˜x_1:k = x_1:k + γ_hδ, ˜m_1:r = m_1:r + γ_mδ) are applied to both retrieved memory and recent history latents during training, enabling the model to learn from imperfect conditioning contexts.
- **Temporal Awareness in Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE):** Original frame indices of memory, history, and prediction latents are injected into temporal RoPE. A head-wise perturbed RoPE base (ˆθ_h = θ_base(1 + σ_θϵ_h)) is introduced during training to break periodic synchronization across attention heads, reducing positional aliasing and discouraging overly literal copying from distant memory.

#### 3.4. Training-Inference Aligned Few-Step Distillation

A multi-segment autoregressive distillation strategy, inspired by Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) and Self-Forcing paradigms, is designed to reduce error accumulation and achieve streaming inference with a bidirectional model.
- **Multi-segment Self-Generated Inference:** The bidirectional student model is trained to mimic actual few-step inference by rolling out over multiple segments. Each segment starts from random noise, with past frames taken from the tail of the previous segment and memory retrieved from an online-updated pool. This scheme aims to align training with real-world streaming use cases.
- **DMD Objective:** The objective minimizes the reverse KL divergence between the targeted data distribution and the student distribution at sampled timesteps, approximated by the difference between two score functions. This ensures that the distilled model generates distributions similar to the teacher while operating in an autoregressive, few-step manner.

#### 3.5. Real-Time Inference Acceleration Module

Several strategies are deployed to achieve efficient real-time inference:
- **DiT Quantization:** INT8 quantization is applied to the attention projection layers in the DiT model, while other components maintain original precision, reducing computation and memory overhead.
- **VAE Pruning:** A lightweight VAE, MG-LightVAE, is trained with decreased hidden dimensions (e.g., 50% or 75% pruning ratios), leading to significant decoding speedups (e.g., ×2.6 to ×5.2). `torch.compile` is also used for VAE decoder acceleration.
- **GPU-based Memory Retrieval:** Camera-aware memory retrieval is implemented on GPUs using a sampling-based approximation (s_approx(i, j) = 1/N Σ 1_(j)^n) instead of exact frustum-overlap scores, significantly reducing retrieval time for long iterative generation.
- **Asynchronous Deployment:** The system utilizes an asynchronous setup (e.g., 8 GPUs for DiT inference and 1 GPU for VAE decoding) to achieve overall throughput.

#### 3.6. Large Model Scaling Up

Matrix-Game 3.0 scales up to a MoE-28B backbone to improve generalization, dynamic modeling, and long-horizon consistency.
- **Decoupled Training:** The high-noise model (responsible for action control) is trained with action-accurate data, while the low-noise model (for visual detail refinement) is trained independently with Internet video data to leverage unlabeled data and improve generalization.
- **Progressive Scaling:** Resolution and video clip length are progressively scaled during training to accelerate convergence and stabilize long-horizon behavior.
- **Viewpoint-Specialized Design:** Two separate high-noise models are trained for first-person and third-person views, respectively, while sharing a common low-noise model, allowing efficient resource allocation and supporting diverse interactive experiences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Matrix-Game 3.0 demonstrates advancements in real-time interactive world modeling, particularly in long-horizon consistency and generation quality.

#### 4.1. Base Model Performance

- **Interactive Control:** The interactive base model exhibits basic controllability, with characters responding to actions while maintaining a stable background without noticeable drift. Camera movements result in reasonable zoom-in and zoom-out relationships consistent with motion.
- **Memory-based Scene Revisitation:** In controlled scene-revisitation experiments, the memory-augmented base model successfully recovers previously observed scene structures and fine-grained appearance details (e.g., local geometry, object configurations, facade patterns, texture-level cues) when returning to earlier viewpoints. This suggests effective use of long-range memory facilitated by camera-aware retrieval and unified self-attention.
- **28B Model Scalability:** Scaling to a 28B model (2x14B) on diverse AAA-game and Unreal-engine synthetic scenes results in generated videos exhibiting strong temporal consistency in scene layout, character identity, and object relations. The model maintains vivid motion dynamics under continuous camera and action changes, indicating improved stability and expressiveness in long-video generation.

#### 4.2. Distillation Model Performance

- The distilled model effectively inherits the memory capabilities of the memory-augmented base model. It successfully reproduces previously occluded content when revisited.
- The model also demonstrates accurate generation of newly emerging scenes, maintaining consistent style and content throughout the sequence without noticeable drift in later stages.

#### 4.3. Real-Time Inference

- **Overall Throughput:** Matrix-Game 3.0 achieves up to 40 FPS real-time generation at 720p resolution with a 5B model in an asynchronous 8+1 GPU setup.
- **Ablation Study on Acceleration Components:**
 - Removing INT8 quantization for DiT reduces FPS from ~40 to 27.38 (a drop of 12.62 FPS).
 - Removing MG-LightVAE (pruned VAE) reduces FPS to 25.79 (a drop of 14.21 FPS).
 - Removing GPU-based memory retrieval causes the most significant drop, reducing FPS to 6.60 (a drop of 33.40 FPS). This indicates GPU retrieval is the most critical acceleration component.
- **VAE Pruning Efficacy:**
 - **MG-LightVAE (50% pruned):** Maintains strong reconstruction quality (PSNR 31.84, SSIM 0.99) compared to the original Wan2.2 VAE (PSNR 33.79, SSIM 0.99), while substantially reducing decoder-only inference time from 0.76s to 0.30s (approx. ×2.5 speedup).
 - **MG-LightVAE (75% pruned):** Provides further speedup, reducing decoder-only time to 0.13s (approx. ×5.8 speedup), with a larger but still acceptable degradation in reconstruction fidelity (PSNR 31.14, SSIM 0.99).
- Qualitative examples show that the 50% pruned MG-LightVAE preserves main scene structure and visual content adequately.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Matrix-Game 3.0 presents a significant step toward the realization of practical, industrial-scale deployable world models. By systematically addressing the challenges of data generation, model architecture design, and inference efficiency, the work demonstrates the concurrent achievement of capabilities that were previously seen as trade-offs in existing research.

The significance of this work lies in:
- **Bridging Key Gaps:** It effectively bridges the gap between high-resolution, long-horizon video generation and the stringent requirements of real-time interactive systems. This is a foundational capability for advancing immersive experiences.
- **Comprehensive Solution:** The co-design approach across data, modeling, and deployment provides a comprehensive framework that could serve as a blueprint for future world model development. The upgraded data engine, in particular, addresses a crucial bottleneck by producing large-scale, high-quality, and precisely annotated data, which is essential for training robust interactive models.
- **Improved Model Robustness and Consistency:** The error-aware training and camera-aware memory mechanisms enhance the model's ability to maintain spatiotemporal consistency over extended durations and under imperfect historical contexts. This robustness is critical for stable and coherent long-term interactive rollouts.
- **Practical Real-Time Performance:** The achieved real-time generation speed (up to 40 FPS at 720p) through a combination of distillation, quantization, VAE pruning, and GPU-accelerated retrieval makes interactive world models more viable for applications requiring immediate feedback.
- **Scalability and Generalization:** The demonstrated improvements with larger models (28B) and viewpoint-specialized designs indicate a pathway for scaling these systems further, potentially leading to higher fidelity, more dynamic behavior, and broader generalization across diverse environments and interaction paradigms.

The potential impact of Matrix-Game 3.0 extends to various domains:
- **Interactive Entertainment and Gaming:** It could enable more immersive and persistent virtual worlds that respond dynamically to user actions over long playtimes, enhancing realism and engagement.
- **Extended Reality (XR):** The ability to generate coherent, real-time interactive environments is fundamental for advanced VR, AR, and mixed reality applications, offering more fluid and believable virtual spaces.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** World models with long-horizon memory and real-time predictive capabilities can facilitate more sophisticated planning, control, and decision-making for autonomous agents operating in complex, dynamic environments.
- **Industrial Workflows:** Applications requiring interactive simulation or virtual prototyping could benefit from the stable, controllable, and real-time generation capabilities demonstrated.

Future directions suggested by the authors, such as scaling models and data further, developing more efficient architectures for higher resolutions and longer sequences, and exploring advanced memory mechanisms, indicate that this work lays a foundation for continued research and development in interactive world modeling.
