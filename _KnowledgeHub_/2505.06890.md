# Image Classification Using a Diffusion Model as a Pre-Training Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new self-supervised learning framework, the Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT), utilizes diffusion models with a joint representation-conditioning mechanism to achieve high image classification accuracy for hematoma detection in brain CT scans. The approach surpassed the DINOv2 baseline by 6.15% in accuracy, leveraging minimal labeled data.

## Problem

- Traditional self-supervised contrastive learning methods often struggle with fine-grained local details and are sensitive to viewpoint variations or occlusions.
- Existing diffusion model applications for self-supervised representation learning lack specific optimization for downstream recognition and integrated latent-level conditioning mechanisms.
- Medical imaging domains, such as hematoma detection, face significant challenges due to high data annotation costs and scarcity of labeled datasets.

## Method

- A ==Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT)== is proposed, extending ==DiT== by integrating high-level representations from a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder as conditioning signals.
- The ==ViT encoder== and the diffusion model's denoising network are ==jointly trained== to optimize the learned representations for the noise prediction task.
- A ==two-stage self-supervised learning framework== involves pre-training RCLDT on large unlabeled datasets, followed by fine-tuning on minimal labeled data for downstream classification.

## Results

- The Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT) achieved a classification accuracy of **0.9497** and an F1-score of **0.8235** for hematoma detection.
- RCLDT improved accuracy by **+6.15%** and F1-score by **+13.60%** over the DINOv2 baseline on the hematoma classification task.
- The model demonstrated superior image generation quality with an FID score of **10.00**, along with a pre-training loss two orders of magnitude lower than the unconditional baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative reconstruction-based self-supervised learning, when properly conditioned, can yield representations superior to contrastive learning for image classification, particularly in detailed tasks.
> - Jointly training a representation encoder with a diffusion model optimizes the representations specifically for the denoising process, enhancing their utility for feature extraction.
> - Integrating explicit latent-level representation conditioning enables the diffusion model to predict latent images with greater accuracy and at earlier denoising steps, improving fine-detail capture.
