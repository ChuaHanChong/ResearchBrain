# Image Classification Using a Diffusion Model as a Pre-Training Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new self-supervised learning framework, the Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT), utilizes diffusion models with a joint representation-conditioning mechanism to achieve high image classification accuracy for hematoma detection in brain CT scans. The approach surpassed the DINOv2 baseline by 6.15% in accuracy, leveraging minimal labeled data.

## Problem

- Traditional self-supervised contrastive learning methods often struggle with fine-grained local details and are sensitive to viewpoint variations or occlusions.
- Existing diffusion model applications for self-supervised representation learning lack specific optimization for downstream recognition and integrated latent-level conditioning mechanisms.
- Medical imaging domains, such as hematoma detection, face significant challenges due to high data annotation costs and scarcity of labeled datasets.

## Method

- A ==Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT)== is proposed, extending ==DiT== by integrating high-level representations from a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder as conditioning signals.
- The ==ViT encoder== and the diffusion model's denoising network are ==jointly trained== to optimize the learned representations for the noise prediction task.
- A ==two-stage self-supervised learning framework== involves pre-training RCLDT on large unlabeled datasets, followed by fine-tuning on minimal labeled data for downstream classification.

## Results

- The Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT) achieved a classification accuracy of **0.9497** and an F1-score of **0.8235** for hematoma detection.
- RCLDT improved accuracy by **+6.15%** and F1-score by **+13.60%** over the DINOv2 baseline on the hematoma classification task.
- The model demonstrated superior image generation quality with an FID score of **10.00**, along with a pre-training loss two orders of magnitude lower than the unconditional baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative reconstruction-based self-supervised learning, when properly conditioned, can yield representations superior to contrastive learning for image classification, particularly in detailed tasks.
> - Jointly training a representation encoder with a diffusion model optimizes the representations specifically for the denoising process, enhancing their utility for feature extraction.
> - Integrating explicit latent-level representation conditioning enables the diffusion model to predict latent images with greater accuracy and at earlier denoising steps, improving fine-detail capture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within deep learning for computer vision: **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, **generative models (specifically diffusion models)**, and **Transformer-based architectures**, with a particular application focus on **medical image analysis**.

Historically, deep learning models for image recognition have heavily relied on large-scale labeled datasets. However, the immense cost and effort associated with manual annotation, especially in specialized domains like medicine, have propelled the development of SSL. Traditional SSL approaches, predominantly **contrastive learning** (e.g., MoCo, SimCLR, DINO), learn robust visual representations by maximizing agreement between different augmented views of the same image. While highly successful, these methods have known limitations: they often prioritize global features over fine-grained local details, exhibit vulnerability to partial occlusions or viewpoint variations, and can be sensitive to the selection of negative samples. DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) is cited as a strong, contemporary baseline in this contrastive learning paradigm.

More recently, **generative models** have emerged as a promising alternative for representation learning. Among these, **diffusion models** have garnered significant attention, surpassing older models like GANs and VAEs in terms of training stability and image generation quality. The inherent mechanism of diffusion models—gradually adding noise and then learning to reverse this process to reconstruct the original image—allows them to capture both global and local semantic structures robustly. Prior work (Xiang et al., 2023) has also demonstrated their resilience to viewpoint changes and occlusions, suggesting their potential to overcome the key shortcomings of contrastive learning.

Despite this promise, the research landscape for explicitly optimizing diffusion models for **downstream recognition performance** (as opposed to pure generation) and for **self-supervised representation learning** remains relatively nascent. Many existing approaches adapt diffusion models originally designed for generative tasks without specific modifications for feature extraction. A crucial gap identified by the authors is the lack of frameworks that integrate **latent-level conditioning mechanisms** to effectively guide the denoising process in a representation-aware manner, thereby enhancing feature utility for classification.

This paper addresses these gaps by:
1. **Shifting the SSL paradigm:** Moving from instance-level discrimination (contrastive learning) to a generative reconstruction-based approach using diffusion models.
2. **Leveraging Transformer architectures:** Building upon the **Latent Diffusion Model (LDM)** and **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)** (Peebles and Xie, 2022), which offer faster processing and enable fair comparison with ViT-based contrastive models. DiT, in particular, is noted for its Transformer-based denoising network, offering scalability and efficiency.
3. **Introducing a novel conditioning mechanism:** Proposing a "Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer" that explicitly uses features derived from a Vision Transformer (ViT) to guide the diffusion process, with the ViT encoder jointly trained to optimize these representations. This aims to overcome the "lack of frameworks that integrate latent-level conditioning mechanisms" noted in the introduction.
4. **Tackling real-world challenges in medical imaging:** Applying the framework to hematoma detection in brain CT scans, a domain characterized by high annotation costs and data imbalance. The paper seeks to develop high-performance classification models with minimal labeled data, a crucial objective in healthcare and other specialized fields. It also directly references "Diffusion Classifier" (Li et al., 2023), noting its limitation of requiring pre-training on large *labeled* datasets, which the proposed SSL framework aims to circumvent.

In essence, this work contributes to advancing SSL by demonstrating the superior capabilities of tailored diffusion models over contrastive learning in specific, challenging downstream tasks, while pushing the methodological frontier of how representations can be effectively learned and utilized within generative frameworks for classification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to propose a novel self-supervised learning (SSL) framework that effectively leverages diffusion models for image representation learning, with the ultimate goal of achieving high image classification performance, especially in scenarios with limited labeled data.

The key motivations driving this research can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Contrastive Learning:** While successful, contrastive learning methods (like DINO, SimCLR, MoCo) are inherently biased towards learning global, discriminative features, often at the expense of local or fine-grained information. This makes them susceptible to variations like partial occlusions or changes in viewpoint. Their reliance on carefully chosen negative samples also introduces stability and performance challenges. The authors are motivated to explore a generative reconstruction-based paradigm using diffusion models, which are known to capture both global and local semantic structures robustly and consistently.
- **Addressing the Gaps in Diffusion Model-based SSL:** Current research on using diffusion models for SSL and downstream recognition tasks is relatively sparse and often involves adapting models originally designed for pure generation. There's a perceived lack of frameworks that explicitly optimize diffusion models for effective feature extraction, particularly those that incorporate latent-level conditioning mechanisms to guide the denoising process in a representation-aware manner.
- **Reducing High Annotation Costs, Especially in Medical Imaging:** A significant motivation stems from the high cost and specialized expertise required for annotating large datasets, particularly in critical domains like medical imaging. The paper explicitly targets hematoma detection in brain CT images, where expert radiologists are needed for labeling, and data imbalance is a common challenge. By developing an SSL framework, the authors aim to enable the creation of high-performance classification models with minimal reliance on costly labeled data.
- **Developing a Novel Architecture: The Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT):** To achieve the above goals, the authors are motivated to design and implement a new architecture. The core idea is to enhance self-supervised learning by integrating a representation-conditioning mechanism into a Transformer-based diffusion model (DiT). This mechanism uses latent representations from a Vision Transformer (ViT) to condition the diffusion process, allowing the model to more effectively extract and refine image features during pre-training. A key innovation here is the *simultaneous training* of both the diffusion model's denoising network and the ViT encoder that provides the conditioning representations, aiming to acquire representations optimized for the diffusion task.
- **Improving Feature Robustness and Versatility:** By shifting to a generative reconstruction-based paradigm, the research aims to learn richer and more robust visual representations from unlabeled data. Such representations are expected to be more versatile and beneficial for various downstream tasks beyond image classification, including detection, segmentation, and multimodal learning.

In essence, the paper seeks to demonstrate that diffusion models, when properly designed with representation-aware conditioning and optimized for feature extraction, can provide a more effective and robust self-supervised learning paradigm than current contrastive methods, particularly for data-scarce and annotation-intensive applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers around a two-stage self-supervised learning (SSL) framework that leverages a novel architecture called the **Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT)**.

#### 3.1. Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT)

The core innovation lies in this architecture, which extends the Latent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) by integrating a representation-conditioning mechanism.
- **Base Architecture:** The RCLDT is built upon the **Latent Diffusion Model (LDM)** paradigm, where the diffusion process occurs in a compressed latent space rather than pixel space, and the denoising network is a **Transformer-based architecture (DiT)**. This choice offers computational efficiency and enables fair comparison with ViT-based models.
- **Conditioning Mechanism:** Unlike traditional DiT models that might be conditioned on class labels, the RCLDT is conditioned on high-level *representations*. These representations `r` are obtained from a **Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder (f_ϕ)**, which takes the noise-free latent image `z_0` as input.
- **Integration of Representations:** The extracted representation `r` is strategically injected into the denoising network at two points:
 1. It is combined with the output of a "decode" layer before being fed into the denoising network.
 2. It is combined at the beginning of each of the `N` blocks within the Transformer-based denoising network.
- **Joint Training:** A critical distinction of this approach is that the ViT encoder (`f_ϕ`) and its parameters (`ϕ`) are trained *simultaneously* with the denoising network (`g_θ`) and its parameters (`θ`). This joint optimization ensures that the learned representations `r` are specifically tailored to facilitate the diffusion model's noise prediction task, leading to more effective feature extraction. The loss function minimized is the mean squared error between the actual noise `ϵ` and the predicted noise `g_θ(z_t, r, t)`.

#### 3.2. Self-Supervised Learning Framework

The overall SSL framework follows a standard two-stage paradigm:

1. **Pre-training Stage:**
 - The RCLDT is trained on a **large, unlabeled dataset**.
 - During this stage, the model learns to effectively predict noise by leveraging the representation `r` as a condition, thereby implicitly learning rich visual features from the unlabeled data.
 - For comparative analysis, an **Unconditional Latent Diffusion Transformer** (without the representation-conditioning mechanism) is also pre-trained on the same unlabeled data.

2. **Downstream Adaption Stage (Image Classification):**
 - This stage utilizes a **small, labeled dataset** for the specific classification task.
 - **Tuning:** The weights of the pre-trained denoising network are transferred. The representation-conditioning mechanism is *removed*, and a **class label-conditioning mechanism** is added. The model is then fine-tuned on the labeled training subset of the downstream task data.
 - **Testing (Diffusion Classifier Zero):** After tuning, the denoising network weights are frozen. For classification inference, the paper introduces **Diffusion Classifier Zero**, a modification of the Diffusion Classifier (Li et al., 2023). Instead of measuring the error between predicted and actual noise, Diffusion Classifier Zero measures the mean squared error `||z_0 - z'_0||^2` between the original latent image `z_0` and the *predicted latent image* `z'_0` (derived from the predicted noise) for each possible class label. The class label `y_pred` that minimizes this error is selected as the classification output. This method is proposed for its perceived ease of visually distinguishing differences in latent images compared to noise.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** Brain CT images for hematoma detection.
 - **Pre-training Data:** Collected from 11 facilities (10,313 images), treated as unlabeled data during pre-training.
 - **Downstream Task Data:** Collected from 1 facility (1,424 training, 178 validation, 179 test images), with labels indicating the presence or absence of hematoma. This highlights the "minimal labeled dataset" objective.
 - **Preprocessing:** Images resized to 256x256, with contrast adjustment and artifact removal as per prior medical imaging research.
- **Model Specifications:** DiT-B (Base size) model, characterized by specific embedding dimensions, number of blocks, and multi-head attention heads.
- **Hyperparameters:** Batch size 16, image size (3, 256, 256), latent image size (4, 32, 32), patch size 2, learning rate 0.0001, total timesteps 1,000.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Generation Task:** Frechet Inception Distance (FID), Spatial FID (sFID), Inception Score (IS), Precision, and Recall.
 - **Classification Task:** Accuracy, F1-Score, Recall, and Precision (to account for potential class imbalance).
- **Baselines:**
 - **Generation:** VQ-GAN, VQ-Diffusion, Unconditional Latent Diffusion Transformer, Class-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer.
 - **Classification:** ResNet, ViT (trained without pre-training); DINOv2 (strong contrastive SSL baseline), Unconditional Latent Diffusion Transformer (SSL pre-trained). The Class-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer was excluded from classification comparison due to its pre-training on class labels, which would be an unfair advantage in an SSL context.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (RCLDT) in both image generation quality and, more critically, in zero-shot image classification for hematoma detection.

#### 4.1. Generation Results

- **Superior Image Quality:** The RCLDT achieved significantly lower FID (10.00) and sFID (12.20) scores compared to all other generative models, including VQ-GAN (FID 27.69), VQ-Diffusion (FID 22.55), Class-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer (FID 25.69), and Unconditional Latent Diffusion Transformer (FID 24.42). It also showed high Precision (0.94) and Recall (0.99). These metrics indicate that images generated by the RCLDT are of higher quality and their distribution is closer to that of the real pre-training data.
- **High Reproducibility of Fine Details:** Qualitative evaluation (Figure 7) revealed that RCLDT-generated samples most accurately reproduced fine details of the original brain CT images, such as the shape of hematomas, lateral ventricles, and subtle intracranial wrinkles, compared to unconditionally and class-conditioned generated images. This highlights the ability of representation conditioning to capture intricate local features.
- **Absence of Mode Collapse:** All Latent Diffusion Transformer variants, including RCLDT, successfully generated diverse brain images without exhibiting mode collapse, a common issue in other generative models.

#### 4.2. Classification Results (Hematoma Detection)

- **State-of-the-Art Classification Performance:** The RCLDT achieved the highest accuracy of **0.9497** and F1-score of **0.8235** for hematoma classification, using only a minimal labeled dataset for fine-tuning.
- **Significant Outperformance of Baselines:**
 - **Compared to DINOv2:** The RCLDT demonstrated a notable improvement of **+6.15% in Accuracy** (0.9497 vs. 0.8882) and **+13.60% in F1-score** (0.8235 vs. 0.6875) over DINOv2, a powerful contrastive learning baseline. This is a crucial finding, indicating that the generative reconstruction-based SSL paradigm can surpass leading discriminative SSL methods in specific challenging scenarios.
 - **Compared to Unconditional LDT:** The Unconditional Latent Diffusion Transformer, while performing well (Accuracy 0.8882, F1-score 0.7058), was significantly outperformed by RCLDT, demonstrating the critical role of the representation-conditioning mechanism.
 - **Outperformance of Non-Pre-trained Models:** Both RCLDT and the Unconditional LDT (which were pre-trained) substantially outperformed models trained from scratch on the small labeled dataset (ResNet 50: Accuracy 0.7150, F1-score 0.4950; ViT B: Accuracy 0.8659, F1-score 0.5200). This confirms the effectiveness of self-supervised pre-training.

#### 4.3. Impact of Representation-Conditioning Mechanism

The study meticulously investigated *why* representation conditioning led to such significant improvements:

- **Lower Pre-training Loss:** The RCLDT successfully reduced the pre-training loss (mean squared error between true and predicted noise) to a significantly smaller value (3.39 × 10⁻⁴) compared to the Unconditional Latent Diffusion Transformer (3.13 × 10⁻²), a difference of approximately two orders of magnitude. This indicates a much higher accuracy in noise prediction due to the conditioning mechanism.
- **Earlier and Finer Latent Image Prediction:** Analysis of the predicted latent images `z'_0` across timesteps (Figure 9) revealed that the RCLDT could predict an image closely resembling the original `z_0` at much earlier (larger) timesteps (e.g., around t=800-900). As the timestep decreased, it performed finer and more accurate adjustments. In contrast, the unconditional model only achieved a reasonable prediction around t=400 and produced blurred, low-quality images at higher timesteps. This suggests that representation conditioning allows for a more stable and accurate denoising process over a wider range of timesteps.

These findings collectively underscore that the jointly trained representation-conditioning mechanism facilitates deeper and more accurate training of the diffusion model, enabling the acquisition of richer and more effective representations for downstream image classification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the fields of self-supervised learning, generative modeling, and medical image analysis, with broad potential impact:

- **Advancement of Self-Supervised Learning Paradigm:** The paper successfully demonstrates a novel, generative reconstruction-based SSL framework using diffusion models that can surpass traditional contrastive learning methods. This offers a compelling alternative paradigm for representation learning, especially for tasks requiring the capture of fine-grained local details, where contrastive methods may struggle. It opens new avenues for exploring the full potential of generative models beyond mere data synthesis.
- **High Performance in Data-Scarce Domains:** The outstanding performance in hematoma detection using a minimal labeled dataset is highly significant. In domains like medical imaging, where data annotation is exceptionally costly, time-consuming, and requires specialized expertise, methods that can learn effectively from unlabeled data are revolutionary. This framework offers a practical solution to mitigate the dependence on large, expertly labeled datasets, potentially accelerating research and development in clinical diagnostic tools.
- **Enhanced Diffusion Model Utility for Downstream Tasks:** By introducing and validating the Representation-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Transformer, the work extends the utility of diffusion models beyond pure image generation. It shows how explicit, jointly-trained representation conditioning can transform these generative models into powerful feature extractors suitable for various recognition tasks. This methodological innovation helps bridge the gap between generative modeling and discriminative task performance.
- **Specific Impact on Medical Image Analysis:** The focus on hematoma detection directly addresses a critical need in neuroradiology. Improved accuracy and reduced reliance on extensive labeled datasets could lead to more efficient and accessible diagnostic AI tools for conditions like intracranial hemorrhage. The methodology's principles could be broadly applicable to other medical imaging challenges, such as tumor detection, lesion segmentation, and disease classification in diverse anatomical regions (e.g., fundus images, skin lesions), where similar data scarcity and imbalance issues exist.
- **Broader Applicability to Other Industries:** Beyond medicine, the challenges of high annotation costs and data imbalance are prevalent in various industrial applications (e.g., semiconductor defect inspection, manufacturing quality control, anomaly detection) and cultural heritage (e.g., ancient document analysis, art forgery detection). The generalizable nature of this pre-training framework suggests its potential to improve vision tasks in these sectors, making AI solutions more feasible and cost-effective.
- **Foundational Insights into Diffusion Models:** The detailed analysis of how representation conditioning impacts pre-training loss and the quality/timing of latent image prediction provides valuable insights into the internal workings of conditioned diffusion models. This understanding can guide future research in designing more efficient and effective diffusion-based architectures for diverse machine learning tasks.
- **Introduction of "Diffusion Classifier Zero":** The proposed classification strategy, by measuring the error on predicted latent images rather than noise, offers a potentially more intuitive and robust approach to leveraging diffusion models for classification, which could be adopted by future research.

In summary, this research not only presents a cutting-edge deep learning model but also offers a pragmatic solution for real-world problems, particularly in fields struggling with data labeling constraints. Its potential to democratize the application of advanced AI in critical domains is substantial.
