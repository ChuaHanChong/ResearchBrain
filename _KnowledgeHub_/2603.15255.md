# SAGE: Multi-Agent Self-Evolution for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SAGE framework introduces a multi-agent self-evolution system enabling large language models to autonomously improve reasoning capabilities in mathematics and code generation. It achieves up to a 2.5% overall average accuracy improvement on various Qwen models and demonstrates strong out-of-distribution generalization, relying only on a small seed set and automatic verification signals.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning methods for LLM reasoning heavily depend on large, human-curated datasets for verifiable rewards, creating scalability and autonomy bottlenecks.
- Prior self-play and multi-agent systems often suffer from instability, curriculum drift, and a lack of explicit planning or robust quality control in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks.
- Current multi-agent frameworks struggle to integrate structured planning and consistent quality assurance for intricate problem decomposition and solution generation.

## Method

- SAGE implements a ==closed-loop, multi-agent system== where a shared LLM backbone instantiates four specialized roles: ==Challenger== (generates tasks), ==Planner== (creates solution plans), ==Solver== (executes plans), and ==Critic== (assesses task/plan quality).
- It utilizes a ==dual-role Critic== agent to filter self-generated tasks and validate solution plans, ensuring high-quality training signals and preventing curriculum degradation.
- The framework employs ==Task-Relative REINFORCE++== for synchronized parameter updates across all agents, coordinating heterogeneous objectives for stable, continuous co-evolution with minimal human supervision.

## Results

- SAGE consistently improved reasoning performance across Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen3-4B models, achieving up to **2.5%** higher overall average accuracy compared to base models.
- The framework demonstrated strong out-of-distribution generalization, achieving the best or near-best OOD Average performance across all tested backbones and notably improving LiveCodeBench scores.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three trainable agents (Challenger, Planner, Solver) are critical, with the Critic's quality filtering being particularly important for code generation and the Challenger crucial for OOD generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit planning (by the Planner) and rigorous quality control (by the Critic) are essential for stable and effective self-evolution in complex, multi-step reasoning domains.
> - A self-generated curriculum, continuously refined by a quality-aware Critic and an adversarial Challenger, can lead to substantial out-of-distribution generalization capabilities in LLMs.
> - Specialized multi-agent collaboration within a unified LLM backbone, coupled with coordinated reward mechanisms, fosters effective division of labor and synergistic improvement in reasoning tasks.
