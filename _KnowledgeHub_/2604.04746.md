# Think in Strokes, Not Pixels: Process-Driven Image Generation via Interleaved Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta Superintelligence Labs and UC San Diego developed a process-driven image generation method that integrates interleaved vision and text reasoning within a unified multimodal model. This approach achieved an overall compositional accuracy of 0.83 on GenEval and 0.76 on WISE, demonstrating improved handling of complex visual logic and world knowledge while significantly reducing training data and inference costs compared to previous process-based techniques.

## Problem

- Existing image generation models, often using single-pass generation, struggle to produce images with complex visual logic, precise spatial layouts, and accurate object relations.
- Prior multimodal reasoning methods are often "visually blind" to spatial misalignments or decouple reasoning from the image synthesis process, limiting self-correction during generation.
- Multi-step generation faces challenges in representing and evaluating ambiguous partially completed images, making error detection and dynamic refinement difficult.

## Method

- A ==process-driven image generation paradigm== is proposed, reformulating image synthesis as a co-evolving trajectory of textual plans and visual states.
- A unified ==multimodal model== ($P_\theta$) autoregressively generates an ==interleaved sequence== of textual reasoning and intermediate visual states through a recurring four-stage cycle: ==Plan, Sketch, Inspect, and Refine==.
- A multi-stage data construction pipeline was developed to generate process-oriented interleaved reasoning traces, training the model to reason over partially formed images and identify inconsistencies.

## Results

- The method achieved an overall compositional accuracy of **0.83** on the GenEval benchmark, outperforming the base **BAGEL-7B** (**0.77**) and competing with larger state-of-the-art models.
- It improved world knowledge reasoning, scoring **0.76** on the WISE benchmark, an increase of **6** percentage points over the base BAGEL-7B.
- The approach demonstrated higher training and inference efficiency, utilizing **11x** less training data and achieving an **8x** speedup in inference compared to prior process-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating visually-grounded reasoning with textual planning enables models to dynamically perceive and correct visual misalignments throughout the generation process.
> - Training the model with diverse editing instructions and self-sampled critiques significantly improves its ability to perform flexible and coherent visual edits and correct errors.
> - Supervising on concrete visual states (semantic partitioning) rather than blurry latent noise leads to more effective visual grounding and complexity-adaptive reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image generation has seen advancements, with models capable of producing images from textual prompts. Current models, such as SDXL, DALL-E 3, and FLUX, demonstrate the ability to generate images, and unified multimodal models (e.g., Chameleon, Emu3, Janus, BAGEL) integrate both visual understanding and generation capabilities within a single framework. These models often operate by generating an entire image in a single pass.

However, existing approaches exhibit limitations when handling complex visual logic, precise spatial layouts, object relations, and fine-grained attributes. Single-pass generation methods necessitate committing to an entire scene without intermediate visual feedback, which can lead to inaccuracies. While textual chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has been explored to enhance reasoning in text domains and applied to multimodal settings, it primarily remains language-driven. Such textual reasoning is described as "visually blind," unable to perceive spatial misalignments or dynamically evolving object states during the visual synthesis process.

Furthermore, other approaches like multimodal CoT, tool-augmented sketching, or post-hoc refinement strategies often decouple reasoning from generation. In these methods, reasoning typically occurs either before image sampling or after an image has been partially or fully generated, limiting the potential for continuous, interactive feedback. This means that while refinement might occur, it is often an outcome-based repair rather than an intrinsic part of the reasoning process during image construction.

This work addresses this gap by proposing a "process-driven image generation" paradigm. It integrates interleaved reasoning that is grounded in both vision and text, using a unified multimodal foundation model. This approach aims to reformulate image generation as a co-evolving trajectory of textual plans and visual states, thereby enabling a model to engage in self-correction during the image synthesis process, rather than relying on external tools or post-hoc adjustments. The proposed method seeks to achieve truly interleaved reasoning where textual generation and visual synthesis mutually inform each other throughout the multi-step process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

- **Address Brittleness in Visual Logic:** Current image generation models can produce plausible but visually incorrect images, particularly concerning elementary visual logic, such as precise spatial relationships (e.g., an object appearing "beside" another when the prompt specifies "above"). The researchers aim to improve the compositional accuracy of generated images.
- **Overcome Limitations of One-Shot Generation:** Existing models typically generate an entire image in a single forward pass. This "black-box" approach requires the model to resolve all complex elements—spatial layouts, object relationships, and fine-grained attributes—simultaneously without iterative feedback. The motivation is to move away from this single-pass commitment to a more incremental and verifiable generation process.
- **Integrate Visually-Grounded Reasoning:** While textual chain-of-thought (CoT) has been applied to image generation, it lacks genuine visual perception during reasoning. The work is motivated by the need for a paradigm shift from purely language-driven reasoning to a multimodal approach where visual intermediate states can directly inform and constrain subsequent textual reasoning, allowing for dynamic perception and correction of visual misalignments.
- **Enable Self-Correction during Generation:** Current refinement methods often occur post-generation. A core motivation is to enable models to detect and correct errors as they emerge throughout the synthesis process, transforming generation from a black-box operation into a controllable, self-correcting dialogue between reasoning and vision.
- **Manage Ambiguous Intermediate States:** A significant challenge in multi-step generation is how to represent and evaluate partially completed images. Incomplete visual states are inherently ambiguous (e.g., an object might be missing because it is "not yet drawn" or "incorrectly omitted"). The research aims to develop methods for constructing a generation path, designing intermediate states, and teaching models to identify and interpret their own mistakes based on these evolving states.
- **Achieve Joint Co-evolution of Text and Vision Reasoning:** The objective is to train a unified multimodal model that can autoregressively generate interleaved multi-modal tokens, allowing a single model to perform task decomposition, visual generation, self-validation, and refinement in a sequential, integrated manner without relying on external models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces "process-driven image generation," conceptualizing image synthesis as a trajectory of interleaved intermediate states. This approach alternates between textual and visual rationales across multiple steps, facilitating a progressive and iterative construction of the scene.

**Framework Overview:**
The core framework employs a unified multimodal model, Pθ, to autoregressively generate an interleaved sequence of textual reasoning steps (s^(i)) and intermediate visual states (v^(i)), ultimately converging to a final image (I). This process is structured around a recurring four-stage cycle:

- **Stage I (Plan):** The model interprets the initial prompt and accumulated context. It then generates an incremental textual instruction (`<ins>`) specifying an update (e.g., what to add or modify) and a global scene description (`<des>`) representing the intended state of the overall generation.
- **Stage II (Sketch):** Based on the textual plan from Stage I, the model synthesizes a partial visual draft. This draft reflects the intended modification, conditioned on the prior visual context.
- **Stage III (Inspect):** The model performs a dual inspection:
 - It checks the textual incremental instruction and global scene description against the raw complete prompt to identify potential inconsistencies.
 - It evaluates the produced visual draft against the planned textual instruction to detect mismatches or misalignments.
- **Stage IV (Refine):** If discrepancies are identified during inspection, the model generates a textual refinement plan (`<refine>`) and subsequently revises the visual state. This ensures the evolving scene remains consistent with the prompt and previous steps.

This tightly coupled loop allows for error detection and correction as they emerge. Textual intermediate states (`s^(i)`) include both step-specific painting instructions and global descriptions, along with refinement signals (`<refine>`) when issues are detected. Visual states (`v^(i)`) manifest as rough sketches during planning and refined representations during refinement, explicitly marked by `<|vision_start|>` and `<|vision_end|>` tokens.

**Intermediate Reasoning Collection (Data Pipeline):**
To train the model to reason over partially formed images, a multi-stage data construction pipeline was developed to generate process-oriented interleaved reasoning traces. This pipeline comprises three subsets:

1. **Multi-Turn Generation Subset:** This dataset provides the foundation for multi-stage generation.
 - **Scene Graph Subsampling:** Prompts are represented as scene graphs (object, attribute, and relation nodes/edges). Subgraphs are sampled to derive incremental, logically ordered step-level prompts, ensuring consistent expansion of the scene without contradictions.
 - **Ground Truth Synthesis:** Ground truth for each step is synthesized using Flux-Kontext and filtered with GPT.
 - **Instruction Augmentation:** To diversify the model’s action space beyond additive updates, a subset of step instructions is rewritten using GPT to include operations like attribute modification, swapping, and removal, fostering richer visual editing capabilities.

2. **Instruction-Intermediate Conflict Reasoning Subset:** This subset focuses on enhancing textual reasoning by teaching the model to identify conflicts between intermediate textual states and the original prompt.
 - **Self-Sampling Strategy:** Intermediate reasoning traces (textual descriptions of partially completed images) are sampled from a fine-tuned multi-turn generation model.
 - **GPT as Judge:** GPT is used to evaluate the consistency of these sampled traces with the original raw prompt. For detected conflicts, GPT generates a textual analysis and a corrective instruction.

3. **Image–Instruction Alignment Reasoning Subset:** This subset addresses visual misalignment by training the model to evaluate consistency between current image drafts and step-level instructions.
 - **Dataset Refinement:** The Gen-Ref dataset is extended and annotated into two categories:
 - **Positive Samples:** Images consistent with instructions, with GPT providing explanations for alignment.
 - **Negative Samples:** Images misaligned with instructions, with GPT providing error analysis and refinement instructions.

**Model and Training Objectives:**
- **Model Backbone:** BAGEL-7B (Deng et al., 2025), a unified multimodal model, serves as the base, fine-tuned end-to-end.
- **Training Objectives:**
 - **Textual Loss (Cross-Entropy):** Applied autoregressively to textual segments (`s^(i)`) and specific tokens (`<|vision_start|>`, `<|vision_end|>`) to enable seamless modality transitions.
 - **Visual Loss (MSE):** Utilizes the Rectified Flow paradigm (Liu et al., 2022) to generate images, optimizing the difference between predicted and target visual states.
 - **Overall Objective:** A weighted combination of textual CE loss and visual MSE loss.

**Inference:**
During inference, given a textual prompt, the model autoregressively generates an interleaved reasoning trajectory. Modality shifts are governed by special tokens. The process concludes when the final image (I) is emitted, marked by an end-of-sequence token.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations demonstrating the capabilities of the process-driven image generation paradigm.

**Quantitative Evaluation on Benchmarks:**

- **GenEval Benchmark (Table 2):** This benchmark assesses compositional text-to-image generation focusing on object-centric attributes.
 - The proposed method, built on BAGEL-7B, achieved an overall score of 0.83, surpassing the base BAGEL-7B score of 0.77 by 6 absolute percentage points.
 - The model exhibited notable gains in relational and attribute-sensitive tasks, specifically in the "Position" and "Color Attributes" categories. For example, in Position, the score increased from 0.51 (base BAGEL) to 0.72 (ours).
 - The performance of the 7B parameter model was competitive with, or exceeded, that of the 12B state-of-the-art generation-only model FLUX.1-dev (0.82) and other unified models like Janus-Pro-7B (0.80).

- **WISE Benchmark (Table 3):** This benchmark evaluates world knowledge reasoning in text-to-image generation.
 - The process-driven approach achieved an overall score of 0.76, an increase of 6 absolute percentage points over the base BAGEL-7B score of 0.70.
 - Significant improvements were observed across various domains, particularly in "Time," "Biology," and "Chemistry," indicating enhanced generalization to complex concepts requiring world knowledge. For instance, the "Time" category improved from 0.69 (base BAGEL) to 0.82 (ours).

**Analysis of Process-driven Reasoning (Table 4):**
Comparisons were made against process-based baselines to assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed paradigm.

- **Comparison with Training-Free Baselines:**
 - Using GPT-4o as an external planner for the base BAGEL model resulted in a performance score of 0.60, indicating that external guidance alone, without specialized fine-tuning, is insufficient for grounding multi-step instructions into stable visual intermediates.
 - Employing GPT-4o as an external inspector for single-round refinement yielded a score of 0.80, showing marginal gains and suggesting that verbal feedback does not translate effectively into precise visual updates unless the model's generative path is aligned through process-level training.
- **Comparison with Training-Based Baseline (PARM):**
 - The proposed method achieved a higher accuracy of 0.83 on GenEval compared to PARM's 0.77 (RL+TTS).
 - It demonstrated efficiency advantages with a significant reduction in training data (62K samples vs. PARM's 688K, an 11x reduction).
 - Inference cost was also lower, with an average of 131 sampling steps per image, an approximate 8x speedup compared to PARM's 1000 steps (which utilized a Best-of-20 search strategy).
 - The paper attributes these advantages to "semantic partitioning," where supervision is applied to concrete visual states, as opposed to PARM's "pixel-level supervision" on blurry latent noise, leading to superior grounding and complexity-adaptive reasoning.

**Ablation Study and Analysis:**
Ablation studies were conducted on the GenEval benchmark to examine the contribution of different components:

- **Diverse Step-Level Instructions (Table 5):** Augmenting the multi-turn generation dataset with diverse editing instructions (e.g., attribute modification, swapping, removal) beyond purely additive operations led to clear performance improvements, particularly in "Position" and "Color Attributes." This suggests that exposing the model to varied forms of visual reasoning enables more flexible and coherent edits.
- **Consistency of Critique Space (Table 6):** Supervising refinement using self-sampled critiques (i.e., identifying alignment conflicts within model-generated trajectories and proposing corrections with GPT) yielded substantially larger gains than symbolic corrections derived from scene graphs. This indicates that aligning supervision with the model’s internal reasoning dynamics and actual failure modes is more effective.
- **Complementary Intermediate State Constraints (Table 7):**
 - "Instruction-intermediate conflict" supervision (focused on textual consistency) improved semantic and spatial consistency, particularly in the "Position" category (+5% absolute gain).
 - "Image-instruction alignment" supervision (focused on visual grounding) showed significant gains in "Counting" (+12% absolute gain).
 - Combining both mechanisms achieved the highest performance across tasks, demonstrating that semantic-level and visual-level intermediate checks address distinct and complementary failure modes.

**Qualitative Evaluation (Figures 4, 5):**
Visualizations illustrate the iterative plan–sketch–inspect–refine cycle. The model demonstrated the ability to detect and correct two types of intermediate errors: conflicts between the step-level instruction and the overall prompt, and mismatches between the generated draft and the instruction. The model was observed to distinguish between incomplete but correct intermediate progress and true inconsistencies. Generated images exhibited visual fidelity, fine-grained details, and aesthetic appeal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a process-driven interleaved reasoning paradigm for image generation, which represents a shift from single-pass, black-box image synthesis to a multi-step, interpretable, and self-correcting process.

The reported improvements in compositional accuracy, particularly for complex spatial relations and object attributes (as demonstrated on GenEval), indicate a methodological advancement in handling intricate visual logic that challenges conventional generative models. The enhanced performance on the WISE benchmark suggests that the interleaved reasoning trajectory enables models to integrate world knowledge more effectively during generation, leading to a more robust understanding and representation of diverse concepts.

A significant aspect of this research is the demonstration of training and inference efficiency. By achieving higher performance with substantially less training data and reduced inference costs compared to prior process-based approaches, the method presents a more scalable solution for complex image generation tasks. The reliance on "semantic partitioning" as a supervisory mechanism, focusing on concrete visual states rather than abstract latent noise, contributes to better visual grounding and complexity-adaptive reasoning.

The systematic collection of intermediate reasoning data, including multi-turn generation, instruction-intermediate conflicts, and image–instruction alignment subsets, provides a framework for training models to interpret, critique, and refine their own evolving outputs. This approach may contribute to developing generative AI systems that are more transparent and controllable.

The findings related to the importance of diverse step-level instructions and self-sampled critiques suggest that enabling models to learn from their own generated error trajectories is more effective than relying solely on symbolic, externally derived corrections. This indicates a potential pathway toward more autonomous and adaptive generative models.

In terms of potential impact, this work could contribute to applications requiring high fidelity and precise control over generated content, such as graphic design, virtual environment creation, or specialized scientific visualization. The explicit, interpretable nature of the generation process could also facilitate human-in-the-loop systems, allowing for real-time interaction and refinement. The paradigm of co-evolving textual plans and visual states may be extensible to other generative tasks involving sequences of multimodal outputs, such as video generation or 3D scene construction, which the authors identify as future directions.
