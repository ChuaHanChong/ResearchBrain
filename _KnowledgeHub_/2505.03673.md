# RoboOS: A Hierarchical Embodied Framework for Cross-Embodiment and Multi-Agent Collaboration

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboOS is an open-source embodied system using a biologically inspired Brain-Cerebellum hierarchy: a cloud MLLM (RoboBrain) for global planning/perception, a modular Cerebellum Skill Library for low-latency execution, and Real-Time Shared Memory for multi-agent state sync, enabling cross-embodiment multi-robot collaboration validated in real-world restaurant, household, and supermarket tasks.

## Problem

- End-to-end VLA models like OpenVLA and pi0 show weak long-horizon planning and poor task generalization across diverse robotic scenarios.
- Hierarchical VLA frameworks such as Helix, Gemini-Robotics, and GR00T-N1 lack cross-embodiment compatibility and scalable multi-agent coordination.
- Existing multi-robot collaboration research struggles with cross-embodiment adaptation, inefficient task allocation, and inadequate dynamic error correction in real deployments.

## Method

- ==Embodied Brain Model== built on **RoboBrain** (Qwen2.5VL-7B) performs global task decomposition, agent-based tool invocation, spatiotemporal memory updates, and self-corrective replanning.
- ==Cerebellum Skill Library== is a modular plug-and-play toolkit standardizing manipulation, navigation, and specialized tools across heterogeneous robot embodiments via robot profiles.
- ==Real-Time Shared Memory== uses a ==Redis-optimized dynamic scene graph== plus temporal and robotic memory to synchronize spatial, task, and embodiment state across agents.

## Results

- RoboBrain-1.5-OS achieves **81.74%** average multi-robot planning accuracy, beating Qwen2.5-VL-7B by **28.14** points and DeepSeek-V3-685B by **5.53** points.
- It improves over RoboBrain-1.0 by **3.64%** on pointing, **16.96%** on affordance mAP, and **40.77%** on trajectory prediction error metrics.
- Edge-cloud deployment via FlagScale achieves average command response latency under **0.001 seconds**, validated across restaurant, household, and supermarket real-world demos with **2-3** heterogeneous robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level cognition (cloud brain) from low-level execution (edge cerebellum) lets a single MLLM coordinate arbitrary heterogeneous robot embodiments without per-robot retraining.
> - A shared spatiotemporal memory structure is the key enabler for multi-agent coordination, not just faster individual policies.
> - Scaling cross-embodiment collaboration to industrial settings like warehouses remains untested since real-world validation was limited to service-robotics scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Current Limitations in Embodied AI

The field of embodied intelligence is rapidly advancing, aiming to integrate AI with physical agents to perform complex tasks in real-world environments. However, current robotic systems face several critical limitations, especially when scaled to industrial ecosystems:
- **Limited Cross-Embodiment Adaptability**: Many robotic systems are designed for specific hardware, making it challenging to transfer learned skills or system architectures across different robot types (e.g., single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid, wheeled).
- **Inefficient Task Scheduling**: Orchestrating multiple robots, particularly in dynamic environments, requires sophisticated task scheduling mechanisms that can handle dependencies, resource allocation, and real-time adjustments. Current systems often struggle with this complexity.
- **Insufficient Dynamic Error Correction**: Real-world environments are inherently unpredictable. Robotic systems need robust mechanisms to detect, diagnose, and recover from execution failures or unexpected changes, a capability often lacking in existing models.

The paper categorizes existing Vision Language Action (VLA) models into two main types, each with its own shortcomings:
- **End-to-End VLA Models (e.g., OpenVLA, RDT-1B, π0)**: These models directly map visual and textual inputs to actions. While effective for short-horizon tasks, they exhibit "inadequate long-horizon planning and task generalization," making them unsuitable for complex, multi-step operations.
- **Hierarchical VLA Models (e.g., Helix, Gemini-Robotics, GR00T-N1, Hi-Robot, π0.5)**: These frameworks decompose long-horizon tasks into subtasks, addressing some planning limitations. However, they typically "suffer from a lack of cross-embodiment compatibility and multi-agent coordination capabilities," hindering their deployment in heterogeneous multi-robot scenarios.

#### 1.2. RoboOS's Contribution to the Field

RoboOS is positioned as a direct response to these pervasive challenges, aiming to bridge the gaps identified in current VLA models. By proposing the "first open-source embodied system built on a Brain-Cerebellum hierarchical architecture," RoboOS seeks to unify high-level cognitive planning with low-latency physical execution, while crucially enabling seamless collaboration among diverse robotic embodiments. This approach directly addresses the fragmented compatibility and coordination issues that plague existing hierarchical VLA frameworks, pushing the frontier towards more generalized, adaptable, and collaborative embodied intelligence. The "paradigm shift from single-agent to multi-agent intelligence" signifies its ambition to move beyond individual robot capabilities to truly coordinated, large-scale robotic operations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop and introduce **RoboOS**, a novel, open-source embodied system capable of resilient, cognition-enabled multi-agent collaboration across diverse robotic platforms and complex real-world scenarios. This system aims to revolutionize paradigms in autonomous manufacturing, adaptive service robotics, and cyber-physical production architectures.

The primary motivations driving the development of RoboOS stem directly from the identified limitations in current embodied intelligence systems:
1. **Addressing Cross-Embodiment Adaptability**: To overcome the constraint of hardware-specific designs, RoboOS aims to provide a unified framework that can seamlessly integrate and control heterogeneous robots (e.g., single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid, wheeled) within a single collaborative ecosystem.
2. **Enhancing Task Scheduling and Long-Horizon Planning**: To move beyond the limitations of both end-to-end and existing hierarchical VLA models, RoboOS seeks to implement robust mechanisms for decomposing complex, long-horizon tasks into manageable subtasks and efficiently scheduling them among multiple agents.
3. **Improving Dynamic Error Correction and Robustness**: Recognizing the unpredictability of real-world environments, a key motivation is to equip robotic systems with advanced capabilities for real-time error detection, diagnosis, and adaptive replanning, ensuring task completion even in the face of unexpected events.
4. **Enabling Scalable Multi-Agent Collaboration**: The ultimate goal is to facilitate efficient and coordinated operations among multiple robots, a critical requirement for next-generation industrial and service applications. This includes mechanisms for shared understanding, communication, and load balancing.
5. **Fostering Open-Source Development**: By releasing RoboOS as an open-source system, the authors aim to accelerate research and development in embodied AI, providing a common platform and benchmark for the community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboOS is architected as a biologically inspired Brain-Cerebellum hierarchical framework, designed to manage the complexities of multi-robot collaboration. Its methodology is built upon three core components and an integrated workflow pipeline, optimized for edge-cloud deployment.

#### 3.1. RoboOS Framework: Brain-Cerebellum Architecture

The architecture draws inspiration from the human brain, separating high-level cognitive functions (Brain) from low-level skill execution (Cerebellum). This hierarchical decoupling allows for robust decision-making and efficient, responsive physical actions.

#### 3.2. Core Components

##### 3.2.1. Embodied Brain Model (RoboBrain)

Deployed in the cloud, RoboBrain serves as the cognitive core, leveraging a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM). While any suitable MLLM can be used, RoboOS adopts an enhanced version of RoboBrain, specifically tuned for embodied scenarios. Its key functionalities include:
- **Global Perception**: Processes multimodal inputs for 3D scene reconstruction and historical state tracking, maintaining a comprehensive understanding of the environment.
- **High-Level Decision-Making**: Orchestrates multi-agent task decomposition, breaking down complex tasks into smaller, manageable subtasks, and generates affordance-aware trajectory plans.
- **Dynamic Error Correction**: Utilizes continuous three-level feedback loops to detect failures and facilitate real-time replanning, ensuring task robustness.
- **Multi-Stage Training**: RoboBrain (based on Qwen2.5VL-7B) undergoes multi-stage training to enhance capabilities like multi-robot task planning, agent-based tool invocation, and spatiotemporal memory updates. This includes specialized datasets like Multi-Robot-45k for collaborative task decomposition and Robotic-Agent-144k for error recovery.

##### 3.2.2. Cerebellum Skill Library

This modular, plug-and-play toolkit is distributed and deployed on individual robot terminals (edge devices). It is responsible for low-latency execution of robot-specific skills, supporting diverse embodiments:
- **Manipulation Skills**: Integrates both VLA-based tools (e.g., OpenVLA, RDT-1B) for generalized manipulation and expert-based tools for specialized grasping and interaction.
- **Navigation Skills**: Supports traditional mapping, localization, and navigation (SLAM) alongside Vision-Language-Navigation (VLN) tools (e.g., MapNav, NavID).
- **Specialized Skills**: Handles complex interactions like contact-rich tasks, deformable object manipulation, and dexterous hand control.
- **Standardized Profiles**: Uses standardized tool and robot profiles to ensure seamless integration and interoperability across heterogeneous platforms.

##### 3.2.3. Real-Time Shared Memory

Optimized with Redis, this component acts as a spatiotemporal synchronization hub, providing a dynamic, shared knowledge base crucial for multi-agent coordination:
- **Spatial Memory**: Maintained as a dynamic scene graph, hierarchically structuring environmental data (floors, rooms, objects) with attributes like position, affordances, and semantic labels, updated in real time from multi-view RGB-D inputs.
- **Temporal Memory**: Records task execution history, feedback, tool-calling logs, and other temporal data, supporting adaptive decision-making and learning.
- **Robotic Memory**: Stores real-time system attributes of each robot, including motion domain constraints, joint states, and battery levels, enabling optimal task allocation and fault prediction.

#### 3.3. Workflow Pipeline for Multi-Robot Collaboration

RoboOS implements a four-step pipeline for multi-robot tasks:
1. **Global Task Decomposition**: Upon receiving a global task, RoboBrain utilizes Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to query shared memory (spatial, temporal, robotic) and robot skill libraries. It then generates a structured reasoning trace and a subtask graph (G) defining task dependencies.
2. **Topological Subtask Allocation**: A "Monitor" dynamically schedules subtasks from graph G. It distinguishes between "Single-Robot Subtask" and "Collaboration Subtask," employing parallel allocation for independent tasks and sequential allocation for dependent ones, ensuring real-time adaptability to dynamic robot states.
3. **Distributed Subtask Agent**: A dedicated Robotic Agent is deployed for each subtask. It autonomously selects and invokes tools from the Cerebellum Skill Library, using feedback and partial spatial memory for dynamic error recovery and iterative tool refinement.
4. **Dynamic Memory Updating**: As robots perceive and act, scene graphs are incrementally updated. New observations, interaction effects, and robot states are logged in Temporal and Robotic Memory, maintaining a continuously accurate and current shared understanding.

#### 3.4. Edge-Cloud Deployment and Training Strategy

RoboOS optimizes scalability through an **edge-cloud communication** model, built on the **FlagScale framework**. This ensures high-frequency interactions and scalable deployment of cloud inference. Edge robots establish bidirectional communication with the cloud-based RoboBrain via an efficient publish-subscribe mechanism, achieving sub-millisecond command response latency. FlagScale also manages TB-level historical data for task replay and knowledge transfer, and supports parallel inference for large models across distributed devices.

The **RoboBrain-1.5-OS** model is trained in three stages on 20 servers with 8x A800 GPUs:
1. **STAGE-1 (3M samples)**: Full-parameter supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using large-scale VLM datasets (General, ScanView, VG, Spatial-R, Temporal-R) for foundational perception and reasoning.
2. **STAGE-2 (2.3M samples)**: SFT using curated robotic datasets (Planning, Pointing, Affordance, Trajectory) to improve core embodied capabilities, retaining 10% of STAGE-1 data to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
3. **STAGE-3 (249k samples)**: SFT with RoboOS-Enhanced datasets (Multi-Robot-45k, Robotic-Agent-144k) for adaptability and multi-agent planning, followed by Group Relative Preference Optimization (GRPO) for tool-calling samples, further enhancing robustness and error recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive empirical validation for RoboOS, particularly focusing on the performance of its core cognitive component, RoboBrain-1.5-OS, and demonstrating its capabilities in real-world multi-robot collaboration scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance on Embodied Capabilities

RoboBrain-1.5-OS, the core MLLM powering RoboOS, was rigorously evaluated against various baselines, including comparable Vision-Language Models (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, RoboBrain-1.0) and larger general-purpose LLMs (e.g., Qwen3-14B, DeepSeek-V3-685B). The evaluation focused on four key embodied capabilities:
- **Multi-Robot Planning**: RoboBrain-1.5-OS achieved outstanding performance, surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 28.14% and even outperforming the much larger DeepSeek-V3-685B by 5.53% in Accuracy-Rate (AR) across restaurant, household, and supermarket scenarios. This highlights its superior ability to decompose tasks and coordinate actions among multiple agents.
- **Pointing Prediction**: The model demonstrated superior performance in pointing accuracy, outperforming all baselines on the Where2Place benchmark. It improved over RoboBrain-1.0 by 3.64%.
- **Affordance Prediction**: RoboBrain-1.5-OS achieved higher mean Average Precision (mAP) for affordance prediction, surpassing RoboBrain-1.0 by 16.96%. This indicates a better understanding of actionable object properties.
- **Trajectory Prediction**: Utilizing Discrete Fréchet Distance (DFD), Hausdorff Distance (HD), and RMSE, RoboBrain-1.5-OS showed significant improvements in predicting manipulation trajectories, outperforming RoboBrain-1.0 by 40.77% in RMSE, demonstrating enhanced precision and naturalness of movements.

These results collectively affirm that RoboBrain-1.5-OS effectively integrates general visual-language understanding with robot-specific planning and execution capabilities, making it a robust foundation for the RoboOS framework.

#### 4.2. Real-World Collaboration Demonstrations

Beyond benchmark metrics, RoboOS was validated through comprehensive real-world demonstrations across diverse interactive scenarios, showcasing its versatility and efficacy in cross-embodiment collaboration:
- **Restaurant Scenario**: A Unitree G1 humanoid robot and an Agilex dual-arm robot collaborated to prepare and deliver a burger based on a customer order. RoboBrain-1.5-OS successfully managed scene-aware reasoning and task decomposition, dynamically allocating subtasks to different robot types.
- **Household Scenario**: A Realman single-arm robot and an Agilex dual-arm robot coordinated to fulfill tasks such as fetching specific items (e.g., "Give me an orange and a knife"). This demonstrated effective joint operation for object retrieval and placement.
- **Supermarket Scenario**: Robots assisted a customer with gift selection and packaging. The Agilex robot executed a VLA-cerebellum skill to "open the gift bag," while the Realman robot selected and placed gifts, illustrating nuanced collaboration and the integration of specialized skills.

These demonstrations underscore RoboOS's practical applicability in supporting heterogeneous embodiments and executing long-horizon, collaborative tasks, thereby validating its architectural design and integrated components in dynamic environments.

#### 4.3. Inference Efficiency with FlagScale

The paper also presents an evaluation of the FlagScale framework, which underpins RoboOS's edge-cloud deployment for inference efficiency.
- **FP16 Performance**: FlagScale demonstrated significant latency reductions for 85-token generation in FP16 precision. For a batch size of 1, FlagScale reduced End-to-Token Latency (E2TL) by 48.3% to 62.9% across different Tensor Parallelism (TP) configurations (TP1-TP4). Even at a larger batch size of 16, improvements ranged from 16.4% to 20.8%, indicating enhanced batch processing scalability compared to the baseline.
- **W8A16 Performance**: Quantization to W8A16 precision further improved latency. For single-query inference (batch size 1), FlagScale achieved E2TL reductions of 54.0% to 65.2%. Across all batch sizes and TP modes, W8A16 consistently showed 12%-17% lower absolute latency values than FP16, confirming the benefits of quantization while maintaining stable relative improvements from FlagScale's optimizations.

These results confirm that FlagScale provides a robust and efficient inference backbone for RoboOS, enabling the ultra-low-latency responsiveness essential for real-time embodied AI systems and supporting scalable deployment across distributed devices.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboOS represents a significant leap forward in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly for multi-agent robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Key Contributions

- **Pioneering Open-Source Framework**: RoboOS is highlighted as the "first open-source embodied system built on a Brain-Cerebellum Hierarchical Architecture." This open-source nature is crucial for fostering community collaboration, accelerating research, and promoting widespread adoption and further development in multi-agent intelligence.
- **Transformative Multi-Agent Paradigm**: By enabling a shift from single-agent to multi-agent intelligence, RoboOS addresses a critical bottleneck in the scalability and applicability of robotic systems in complex environments.
- **Robust Hierarchical Architecture**: The biologically inspired Brain-Cerebellum design, with its distinct Embodied Brain Model, Cerebellum Skill Library, and Real-Time Shared Memory, provides a robust and flexible framework for integrating high-level cognitive planning with low-latency physical execution.
- **Cross-Embodiment Versatility**: The system's demonstrated adaptability across heterogeneous robots (single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid, wheeled) is a major breakthrough, offering a unified control paradigm that transcends hardware-specific limitations.
- **Enhanced Cognitive Capabilities**: RoboBrain-1.5-OS significantly improves multi-robot planning, pointing, affordance, and trajectory prediction, showcasing superior reasoning and operational precision crucial for complex tasks.
- **Scalable and Efficient Deployment**: Optimizations via edge-cloud communication and the FlagScale distributed inference framework ensure that RoboOS can be deployed at scale, supporting high-frequency interactions and large-scale multi-robot operations with ultra-low latency.

#### 5.2. Future Directions and Limitations

The potential impact of RoboOS is far-reaching, promising to revolutionize several industrial and service sectors:
- **Autonomous Manufacturing**: Enables sophisticated multi-robot assembly lines and automated quality control, where heterogeneous robots can collaborate seamlessly.
- **Adaptive Service Robotics**: Facilitates the deployment of versatile robot teams in dynamic environments such as restaurants, hotels, and retail spaces, performing tasks like delivery, cleaning, and customer assistance.
- **Cyber-Physical Production Architectures**: Provides a foundational framework for intelligent, interconnected systems where physical robots and digital intelligence operate in concert.
- **Logistics and Warehousing**: Enhances efficiency in complex sorting, retrieval, and transport tasks by coordinating diverse robotic platforms.

While the current work successfully demonstrates RoboOS's capabilities in restaurant, household, and supermarket settings, the authors explicitly acknowledge a **limitation** regarding the scope of experimental environments. They state that due to current experimental environment constraints, they "were unable to explore additional contexts, such as factory settings and other industrial environments, including warehouses, assembly lines, and logistics hubs." These industrial scenarios often involve highly complex tasks and stringent real-time requirements, which are crucial for validating the system's full potential.

Future work should ideally focus on extending RoboOS's validation to these more demanding industrial environments. This would involve testing its resilience to higher operational stresses, its adaptability to specialized machinery, and its ability to manage even larger fleets of robots under diverse manufacturing and logistical workflows. Addressing these gaps will further solidify RoboOS's position as a truly general-purpose and scalable solution for advanced embodied intelligence. The open-source nature of RoboOS provides an excellent foundation for the broader research community to contribute to these future directions.
