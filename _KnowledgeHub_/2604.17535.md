# OPSDL: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Long-Context Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Baidu Inc. researchers developed OPSDL, an on-policy self-distillation method that leverages an LLM's own short-context capabilities to improve its long-context understanding. The approach achieved the highest performance among trainable methods on RULER and LongBench V2, demonstrating substantial gains at extended context lengths while preserving short-context abilities.

## Problem

- Large Language Models exhibit a persistent gap between their maximum architectural context window and their practical effective context capacity.
- Existing post-training methods for long-context LLMs often require extensive human-annotated data, suffer from unstable optimization due to sparse reward signals, or introduce complexity with auxiliary components.
- LLMs are prone to hallucinations and difficulties in faithfully utilizing relevant evidence when processing long contexts.

## Method

- **OPSDL** utilizes the LLM itself as a dynamic teacher, employing its robust short-context behavior to supervise its long-context generation.
- The method constructs triplets of (long context, relevant short context, model-generated query) without human annotation, ensuring the query is answerable from both contexts.
- It defines a token-level advantage as the log-probability ratio between short-context (teacher) and long-context (student) distributions, using a ==policy gradient== objective to align the long-context policy with the short-context anchor.

## Results

- OPSDL achieved the highest overall performance among all trainable methods on the RULER and LongBench V2 benchmarks across 7B, 14B, and 32B model scales.
- The method demonstrated substantial performance gains at extended context lengths, notably improving over base instruct models by up to +48.70 points on RULER at 128K tokens for the 7B model.
- It preserved short-context capabilities, showing only marginal degradation (approx. **1.3** percentage points on average) across general benchmarks like MMLU and Hellaswag, unlike other baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An LLM's inherent proficiency within shorter, well-aligned contexts can be effectively leveraged as a self-referential teacher for its performance in longer contexts.
> - Token-level supervision, derived from the discrepancy between short and long context probabilities, provides a dense and targeted learning signal that overcomes the limitations of sparse, sequence-level rewards.
> - The on-policy nature and self-contained training framework contribute to a scalable, stable, and sample-efficient approach for long-context learning without requiring external data or auxiliary models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) capable of effectively processing and reasoning over extensive contexts is identified as a significant challenge for real-world applications. Such applications include comprehending lengthy documents, understanding large code repositories, and performing multi-hop reasoning over dispersed information. While architectural innovations, such as advanced positional encoding and attention mechanisms, have expanded the maximum context window LLMs can technically accept, empirical evidence indicates a persistent discrepancy between this maximum capacity and a model's effective context capacity. This suggests that merely increasing the architectural context window does not automatically translate to robust long-context understanding.

Current approaches to optimize LLM performance for long contexts include several post-training paradigms. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on long-context training data is a direct method but is constrained by the availability and quality of such data and is susceptible to distribution shifts. More advanced methods involve preference optimization and reinforcement learning techniques. For instance, LongReward constructs reward signals from multidimensional LLM feedback and applies DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) to improve long-context performance. LongPO proposes a self-evolution framework that uses short-context generations as positive samples and long-context generations as negative ones for DPO-based iterative optimization. SoLoPO further refines this by decomposing alignment into short-context optimization and a short-to-long consistency constraint. QwenLong-L1 treats long-context reasoning as a long-horizon decision-making problem, employing warm-up SFT and progressive context scaling for stable RL training.

These existing post-training methods, while demonstrating effectiveness, commonly face certain limitations. They often necessitate high-quality, human-annotated training data or rely on sparse, sequence-level reward signals. Such sparse signals can lead to unstable and sample-inefficient optimization, particularly when dealing with long contexts. Furthermore, some methods introduce additional system complexity by relying on auxiliary components like frozen verifier models or learned reward models, which also makes the training pipeline dependent on the availability and quality of these external resources.

In parallel, On-Policy Distillation (OPD) and On-Policy Self-Distillation have gained attention. Generalized Knowledge Distillation (GKD) minimizes reverse KL divergence on student-generated trajectories with teacher supervision. Self-distillation variants like OPSD and SDPO enable the model to act as its own teacher by incorporating privileged information (e.g., verified reasoning traces or environmental feedback). OPCD internalizes contextual knowledge and prevents forgetting by distilling from teacher-enriched contexts. However, these methods typically construct a "stronger" teacher by enriching the input context or relying on externally provided correct trajectories, which might limit the model's intrinsic reasoning capabilities. The present work, OPSDL, positions itself by addressing the identified limitations of existing long-context training methods and by taking an alternative approach to self-distillation, specifically by *reducing* context for the teacher rather than enriching it.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that effectively extends the functional context length of Large Language Models, thereby bridging the gap between their maximum architectural context window and their practical effective context capacity. The motivation for developing OPSDL stems from a detailed analysis of the limitations inherent in current post-training methodologies for long-context LLMs.

The key motivations are as follows:

1. **Overcoming Data Dependency**: Many existing methods for improving long-context capabilities, such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or those relying on preference optimization, require large volumes of high-quality, human-annotated data. Acquiring such data, especially for diverse and complex long-context scenarios, is resource-intensive and often a bottleneck. OPSDL aims to eliminate this dependency by generating its own training signals.
2. **Addressing Optimization Instability and Inefficiency**: Methods that use sparse, sequence-level reward signals, like those in DPO-based approaches, can lead to unstable and sample-inefficient optimization. This issue is exacerbated in long-context scenarios where credit assignment across many tokens becomes challenging. The objective is to provide a more dense and stable training signal.
3. **Reducing System Complexity**: Current solutions often involve auxiliary components, such as external reward models or verifiers, which increase the overall system complexity and introduce external dependencies into the training pipeline. A motivation for OPSDL is to simplify this by making the training process self-contained.
4. **Leveraging Inherent Short-Context Capability**: A core observation is that LLMs inherently possess strong capabilities when operating within their well-aligned, shorter context windows, where noise from irrelevant information is minimized. The motivation is to leverage this "inherent short-context teacher" ability within the model itself to guide its performance in long-context settings.
5. **Mitigating Hallucinations and Encouraging Faithful Evidence Use**: In long contexts, LLMs can be prone to "hallucinations" or attending to irrelevant information. OPSDL is motivated to provide fine-grained supervision that encourages the model to faithfully utilize relevant evidence presented in the context and to disregard irrelevant content, thereby improving factual consistency and reasoning accuracy.
6. **Ensuring Generalizability and Preservation of Capabilities**: The method aims to achieve substantial improvements in long-context performance across various model sizes and benchmarks without degrading the model's general performance on standard short-context tasks.

In summary, OPSDL is motivated by the need for a scalable, stable, and sample-efficient approach to long-context learning that does not rely on external supervision or auxiliary components, by leveraging the model's own internal short-context expertise to supervise its long-context generation through dense, token-level signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OPSDL (On-Policy Self-Distillation for Long-Context Language Models) operates on the principle that an LLM's inherent short-context capabilities can serve as a self-teacher to improve its performance in long-context scenarios. The method involves a self-referential distillation process, where the same model acts as both student and teacher, guided by a token-level reverse KL divergence objective.

**Problem Formulation:**
The core idea is to optimize the model, parameterized by $\theta$ and denoted as $\pi_{\theta}$, such that its generation distribution when conditioned on a long context, $\pi_{\theta}(y | C_L, Q)$, closely approximates its own high-quality distribution when conditioned on a relevant short context, $\pi_{\theta}(y | C_S, Q)$. Here, $C_L$ is the long-context input, $C_S$ is a relevant short context extracted from $C_L$, and $Q$ is a query or instruction.

**Data Construction:**
OPSDL constructs $(C_L, C_S, Q)$ triplets from raw long documents without requiring human annotation or preference pairs.
1. **Long Document Sampling:** A long document $C_L$ is sampled from a training corpus.
2. **Short Context Extraction:** A contiguous segment $C_S$ is extracted from $C_L$. This $C_S$ is designed to contain the core evidence relevant to a potential query and to fit within the model's well-trained short-context window.
3. **Query Generation:** A question $Q$ is generated by the model itself, conditioned on $C_S$, using a Self-Instruct-like approach. This ensures that $Q$ targets information present in both $C_L$ and $C_S$, facilitating a meaningful comparison between the long-context student and the short-context teacher. This process yields triplets where the query is answerable from both contexts.

**Short-to-Long Self-Distillation via Policy Gradient:**
The method leverages the model itself as a dynamic teacher, where its predictions under the short context $C_S$ serve as a "robust anchor" for its long-context generation.

1. **Teacher and Student Policies:**
 - **Student Policy:** The model generates a response sequence $y = \{y_1..., y_T\}$ by sampling from its policy conditioned on the long context: $\pi_{\theta}(y_t | C_L, Q, y_{<t})$.
 - **Teacher Policy:** For each token $y_t$ in the generated sequence, the teacher probability $P(y_t)$ is computed using the *same model parameters $\theta$* but conditioned on the short context: $\pi_{Teacher}(y_t | C_S, Q, y_{<t})$. This forms a self-referential teacher-student structure where the teacher co-evolves with the student.

2. **Token-Level Advantage:**
 Instead of external rewards or annotations, a token-level advantage $A_t(y_t)$ is defined as the log-probability ratio between the short-context (teacher) and long-context (student) distributions:
 $A_t(y_t) = \log \left( \frac{\pi_{Teacher}(y_t | C_S, Q, y_{<t})}{\pi_{\theta}(y_t | C_L, Q, y_{<t})} \right)$

 This advantage function provides a fine-grained, token-level signal with specific interpretations:
 - **Positive $A_t(y_t) > 0$**: Indicates that the teacher (short-context behavior) assigns a higher probability to $y_t$ than the student (long-context behavior). This signals that the student may be under-weighting evidence clearly present in the short context, often due to context utilization degradation in long inputs. The objective aims to increase the student's probability for such tokens.
 - **Negative $A_t(y_t) < 0$**: Suggests that the student assigns a higher probability to $y_t$ than the teacher. This implies the model might be hallucinating or attending to irrelevant content introduced by the long context. The objective aims to suppress the student's probability for these tokens.
 - **Near-zero $A_t(y_t) \approx 0$**: Both distributions largely agree, indicating the token's generation is unaffected by context length, thus receiving a negligible gradient signal.

 This design ensures that only tokens where the long-context policy deviates from the short-context anchor receive a non-trivial gradient, thereby providing targeted and efficient learning. This advantage function serves as an unbiased estimator for the gradient of the point-wise reverse KL divergence.

3. **Policy Gradient Objective:**
 The model is optimized using a policy gradient objective over the long-context distribution $\pi_{\theta}( \cdot | C_L, Q)$:
 $L_{PG}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|C_L,Q)} \left[ \sum_{t=1}^{T} A_t(y_t) \cdot \log \pi_{\theta}(y_t | C_L, Q, y_{<t}) \right]$
 This objective guides the long-context policy to increase the probability of tokens that the short-context teacher deems likely, while simultaneously suppressing tokens that might arise from irrelevant long-context distractions. The on-policy nature ensures that the training signal remains aligned with the model's current behavior, contributing to sample efficiency.

**Overall Training Procedure:**
The OPSDL algorithm iteratively performs the data construction phase to build $(C_L, C_S, Q)$ triplets. In the training phase, for each iteration, it performs an on-policy rollout to generate a response $y$ under the long context. Subsequently, it computes token-level reverse-KL advantages $A_t$ for each token using the short-context teacher. Finally, the policy $\pi_{\theta}$ is updated based on these advantages.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of OPSDL was evaluated through comprehensive experiments across various model scales and long-context benchmarks.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Data Construction**: Training data was generated from raw long documents using a reverse construction strategy, similar to LongPO. For each long document ($C_L$), a shortened chunk ($C_S$) was extracted, and the model then generated an instruction ($Q$) conditioned on $C_S$ via Self-Instruct. This process yielded $(C_L, C_S, Q)$ triplets without requiring human annotations or preference responses, simplifying data preparation and mitigating distribution mismatch.
- **Baselines**: The method was compared against standard long-context post-training approaches:
 - **Long-SFT**: Supervised fine-tuning on long-context training data by minimizing negative log-likelihood.
 - **LongPO**: DPO-based method optimizing on self-generated preference pairs (short-context responses preferred over long-context ones).
- **Backbone Models**: Qwen2.5-Instruct series were used at three scales: 7B, 14B, and 32B parameters.
- **Comparison with Optimized Models**: OPSDL's performance was also assessed against Qwen2.5-Instruct-1M and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-1M, which are models specifically trained for million-token contexts through dedicated, extensive long-context training pipelines.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**:
 - **RULER**: A synthetic suite for quantifying long-context modeling capacity, evaluating tasks like retrieval and tracking.
 - **LongBench V2**: A multiple-choice benchmark designed to test deep understanding and reasoning over long documents across various domains and context lengths.

**Main Results:**

1. **Overall Performance Leadership**: OPSDL consistently achieved the highest overall performance among all trainable methods (excluding the pre-optimized -1M variants) across all evaluated model scales (7B, 14B, 32B) on both RULER and LongBench V2 benchmarks (Table 1).
 - For instance, at the 7B scale, OPSDL improved the total average performance from 51.68 (base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) to 56.61, surpassing Long-SFT (53.99) and LongPO (55.42).
 - At 14B and 32B scales, OPSDL also demonstrated superior performance compared to Long-SFT. LongPO notably failed to converge during reproduction at these larger scales.

2. **Substantial Improvements at Extended Context Lengths**: The most significant performance gains were observed at longer context lengths. Base instruct models showed considerable performance degradation beyond 64K tokens, a trend that OPSDL largely mitigated.
 - At 128K tokens on RULER, OPSDL improved over the base instruct models by +48.70 points for the 7B model, +34.25 points for the 14B model, and +30.29 points for the 32B model. These improvements were consistently larger than those achieved by Long-SFT and LongPO.

3. **Narrowing the Gap to Dedicated Long-Context Models**: OPSDL substantially closed the performance gap with the Qwen2.5-Instruct-1M variants, which underwent extensive, multi-stage, dedicated long-context training.
 - On the RULER average, OPSDL reduced the performance gap to the 1M variant from 13.10 to 3.94 for the 7B model and from 10.57 to 3.28 for the 14B model. This suggests OPSDL offers an effective alternative without such extensive pre-training.

4. **Generality Across Benchmarks**: OPSDL showed performance improvements on both synthetic benchmarks (RULER), which test fundamental long-context capabilities, and realistic benchmarks (LongBench V2), which assess complex long-document reasoning. This indicates that the method enhances genuine long-context reasoning rather than overfitting to specific patterns.

5. **Preservation of Short-Context Capabilities**: Crucially, OPSDL maintained performance on general short-context benchmarks with only marginal degradation.
 - On MMLU, ARC-C, Hellaswag, and Winogrande, the average performance drop was approximately 1.3 percentage points relative to the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model. Performance on MT-Bench remained virtually unchanged.
 - In contrast, Long-SFT exhibited a notably larger degradation of 3–4 percentage points on average across these benchmarks, highlighting OPSDL's ability to enhance long-context capabilities without significantly compromising general model performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed OPSDL method presents several significant contributions to the field of large language model development, particularly concerning their long-context capabilities.

1. **Enhanced Long-Context Reasoning**: The consistent and substantial performance improvements demonstrated across various model sizes and extended context lengths (up to 128K tokens) indicate that OPSDL effectively enhances LLMs' ability to process and reason over long documents. This directly addresses a critical limitation identified in the broader research landscape.
2. **Scalability and Stability**: By employing an on-policy self-distillation framework with token-level supervision, OPSDL offers a scalable and stable training approach. This is evident from its ability to train stably across 7B, 14B, and 32B parameter models, in contrast to some baseline methods like LongPO, which encountered convergence issues at larger scales.
3. **Reduced Resource Dependency and Increased Sample Efficiency**: A key impact of OPSDL is its independence from external resources. It eliminates the need for human-annotated supervision data, auxiliary reward models, or complex reward engineering. This significantly lowers the computational and human effort costs associated with training long-context LLMs, contributing to superior sample efficiency.
4. **Generalizability and Model Agnosticism**: The method's effectiveness across different models within the Qwen2.5-Instruct series (7B to 32B parameters) suggests its generalizability across various architectures. Its improvements on both synthetic (RULER) and realistic (LongBench V2) benchmarks further confirm that it fosters genuine long-context reasoning.
5. **Preservation of Short-Context Abilities**: The finding that OPSDL achieves substantial long-context gains with minimal degradation to short-context and general benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, MT-Bench) is notable. This ensures that the specialization for long contexts does not come at the cost of overall model utility.
6. **Simplicity and Effectiveness**: OPSDL offers a comparatively simple yet effective paradigm for long-context post-training. Its mechanism of leveraging the model's own short-context behavior as a dynamic self-teacher, through dense token-level reverse KL divergence, provides a targeted and efficient learning signal that allows for robust policy-level self-evolution. This can serve as a lightweight alternative to more complex, multi-stage training pipelines used for models specifically optimized for very long contexts (like the Qwen2.5-Instruct-1M variants).

In summary, OPSDL represents a robust and efficient strategy for improving the long-context capabilities of LLMs, with implications for a wide range of applications requiring extensive contextual understanding, while addressing key limitations of existing training paradigms.
