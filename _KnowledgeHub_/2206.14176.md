# DayDreamer: World Models for Physical Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The DayDreamer approach enables sample-efficient learning for physical robots by utilizing world models to plan and learn in a compact latent space, eliminating the need for simulators or demonstrations. This method allowed diverse robots, including a quadruped and robotic arms, to acquire complex skills such as walking and pick-and-place within hours of real-world interaction.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithms require millions of interactions, making direct learning on physical robots impractical due to time, hardware wear, and cost.
- Traditional DRL often relies on simulators for training, which introduces a "sim-to-real" gap that hinders real-world deployment.
- Learning complex robot behaviors in unstructured physical environments remains challenging without extensive pre-programming or demonstrations.

## Method

- Employ the **DayDreamer** ==Dreamer algorithm=='s ==world model== to enable planning and learning in a compact ==latent space==, rather than directly from high-dimensional sensory observations.
- Implement an ==online learning== framework where the robot continuously collects real-world experience, which is used to update the world model and ==actor-critic policy==.
- Utilize an ==asynchronous actor-learner architecture== to parallelize data collection and model/policy updates, improving efficiency.

## Results

- An A1 quadruped learned to walk in **1 hour** of real-world training, while a UR5 robotic arm achieved pick-and-place performance approaching human levels (**3 objects per minute**) after **8 hours**.
- The approach demonstrated significantly higher sample efficiency compared to state-of-the-art model-free baselines (e.g., SAC, Rainbow DQN, PPO, DrQv2) across all evaluated tasks.
- Robots successfully adapted to real-world changes, such as the A1 recovering from physical pushes within **10 minutes** and the XArm adjusting to varying lighting conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World models, specifically the Dreamer algorithm, allow robots to learn complex behaviors by "imagining" outcomes in a latent space, drastically reducing the need for real-world interactions.
> - The approach demonstrates high generalizability, using the same algorithm and hyperparameters across four diverse robot platforms and tasks.
> - Learning directly in the real world bypasses the sim-to-real gap common in robotics, facilitating practical deployment.
> - The online learning framework allows robots to continuously adapt to changing environmental conditions, crucial for real-world deployment.
