# Combining On-Policy Optimization and Distillation for Long-Context Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage post-training pipeline, featuring Distilled Group Relative Policy Optimization (dGRPO), enhances large language models' long-context reasoning capabilities up to 128K tokens while preserving short-context performance. This method improved average RULER performance from 64.5 to 74.8 and 128K token accuracy from 11.0 to 44.5.

## Problem

- Current LLM alignment strategies face challenges in long-context reasoning, including positional extrapolation, attention dilution, and exposure bias, leading to performance degradation.
- Off-policy methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning and Knowledge Distillation suffer from exposure bias and error accumulation in long sequences.
- On-policy reinforcement learning methods, such as GRPO, are often unstable and sample-inefficient due to sparse reward signals, potentially degrading reasoning structure over long horizons.

## Method

- A two-stage post-training pipeline is proposed, starting with an off-policy warm-up (Supervised Fine-Tuning) to bootstrap long-context behavior.
- The second stage utilizes Distilled Group Relative Policy Optimization (**dGRPO**), which integrates sparse outcome-level rewards from ==GRPO== with dense token-level guidance from On-Policy Distillation.
- A new synthetic, multilingual dataset called LONGBLOCKS is introduced, used in a mixed data strategy (10% short-context, 90% long-context) to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- The dGRPO recipe achieved substantial improvements in long-context task performance, with average RULER performance increasing from 64.5 to 74.8 and 128K token accuracy rising from 11.0 to 44.5.
- Short-context capabilities were preserved or marginally enhanced, demonstrating that the alignment process did not lead to catastrophic forgetting.
- The proposed dGRPO model outperformed the base Qwen3-1.7B model and several larger external long-context baselines (e.g., LLAMA-3.2-3B, GEMMA3-4B, QWEN3-4B + YaRN) on long-context benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of sparse outcome-level rewards with dense token-level teacher guidance, as in dGRPO, enables more stable and effective on-policy learning for long-context tasks.
> - An off-policy warm-up stage (e.g., SFT) is essential for cold-starting long-context behavior and stabilizing the subsequent on-policy optimization process.
> - Utilizing a stronger, external teacher model for distillation within dGRPO yields substantially greater improvements in long-context performance than self-distillation.
