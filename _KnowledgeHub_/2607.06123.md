# MP-MPPI: A Motion Primitive Guided Sampling-Based Optimizer for Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> MP-MPPI enhances Model Predictive Path Integral by integrating precomputed, dynamically feasible motion primitives as additional samples, leading to improved global optimality and exploration. The method enables a quadcopter to navigate dense obstacle fields more effectively, increasing mean traversal distance by 38.8% and eliminating collisions in simulated environments while maintaining real-time performance.

## Problem

- Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithms like MPPI often struggle with local optimality, hindering rapid adaptation in complex or dynamically changing environments.
- Balancing exploration for global optima and maintaining closed-loop system stability is challenging in MPPI; larger perturbations can lead to instability.
- Existing enhancements to MPPI for global optimality frequently introduce computational overhead, require specific system dynamics, or reduce parallelization efficiency.

## Method

- **MP-MPPI** augments the standard ==Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)== algorithm by incorporating a fixed number of precomputed, dynamically feasible ==motion primitives== into its sample set.
- These motion primitives, generated from ==state lattice== references, serve as structured, exploratory control sequences alongside the typical noisy perturbations.
- The combined set of samples (noisy and motion primitives) is then used in the same ==weighted sum update rule== as base MPPI, effectively leveraging GPU parallelization for real-time control.

## Results

- MP-MPPI improved mean traversal distance in a dense obstacle field by **38.8%** (from **48.0m** to **66.6m**) and eliminated collisions over **100** simulated runs, compared to one collision for base MPPI.
- In a reactive obstacle avoidance scenario, MP-MPPI successfully navigated around a sudden wall obstacle where the base MPPI algorithm failed and collided.
- The algorithm maintained high update frequencies (above **100 Hz**) on a laptop GPU, exhibiting linear computational scalability with prediction horizon length and minimal latency increase with more samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating precomputed, structured motion primitives can guide sampling-based optimizers like MPPI towards better global solutions and enhance exploration without compromising stability.
> - This 'low-cost, high-impact' modification maintains real-time computational efficiency, as demonstrated by its effective utilization of GPU parallelization.
> - The balance between the number of motion primitives and standard noisy samples is a critical design parameter that influences overall performance and convergence properties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and agile control systems for field robotic platforms, such as quadcopters, operating in unpredictable real-world environments, represents a significant area of current research. These systems require controllers that can navigate complex obstacle fields in real-time while maintaining stability and efficiency. Traditional path planning approaches often exhibit limitations in dynamic, fast-changing environments and can struggle with real-time performance.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) has emerged as a methodology that addresses the gap between high-level planning and low-level control by utilizing a system model to predict future states and optimize control inputs over a finite horizon. Conventionally, MPC problems are solved using gradient-based optimization. However, the increasing capabilities of parallel computing have facilitated the adoption of sampling-based MPC methods, which offer advantages such as not requiring differentiable system dynamics.

Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) is a prominent sampling-based MPC technique. MPPI operates by generating numerous perturbed control sequences, evaluating their associated costs, and then combining them into a new optimal control sequence, weighting lower-cost sequences more heavily. A key challenge with MPPI is the balance between exploration and exploitation. The magnitude of perturbations determines the search space: small perturbations lead to local optimality and slower adaptation to environmental changes, while large perturbations can induce instability in the closed-loop system. This limitation means MPPI can be slow to transform control inputs into globally optimal solutions, especially in rapidly changing or highly constrained environments.

The existing research landscape has explored several modifications to MPPI to enhance its global optimality and exploration capabilities:
- **Iterative Noise Scaling:** Methods like DIAL-MPC and MPOPI progressively adjust noise magnitude from high to low to achieve optimality. However, this often increases computation time and can reduce parallelization efficiency.
- **Bidirectional Optimization:** BiC-MPPI attempts to optimize control inputs bidirectionally, but it necessitates invertible system dynamics.
- **Output Sampling:** O-MPPI modifies the algorithm to sample outputs instead of inputs, also requiring invertible dynamics.
- **Higher-Level Planner Guidance:** RRT-guided MPPI integrates path planners, but such planners can struggle with real-time performance, hindering replanning in dynamic settings.
- **Diversified Parallelization:** AERO MPPI and U-MPPI run multiple parallel instances to optimize various objectives. This approach can reduce the number of samples available for each instance, potentially leading to suboptimal results.
- **Feature-Based Sampling:** Homburger et al. introduced feature-based MPPI, which samples around specific features like emergency braking. This method has limitations on the number of samples each feature can receive, potentially impacting performance during normal operation.
- **Modified Sampling Distributions:** Biased-MPPI and C-Uniformity MPPI modify the sampling distribution. While offering flexibility, extensively altering the distribution may introduce harmful biases and negatively affect convergence or performance.

The proposed MP-MPPI method seeks to address these limitations by introducing a "low-cost, high-impact" modification to MPPI. It aims to improve global optimality and exploration by integrating precomputed, dynamically feasible motion primitives as informative samples, alongside the standard MPPI perturbations. This approach is intended to enhance the controller's ability to navigate complex environments and avoid collisions without incurring the computational overhead or stability issues associated with other methods, while leveraging the parallel computing capabilities of GPUs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an enhanced Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) algorithm, termed MP-MPPI, that improves global optimality and exploration capabilities for robotic control in complex, real-world environments, particularly for agile quadcopter navigation.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in existing control strategies:
1. **Demand for Robust and Agile Field Robotics:** There is a growing need for robotic systems capable of operating outside controlled laboratory environments, in unpredictable and dynamic real-world settings. Agile quadcopters, due to their speed and maneuverability, are frequently used as benchmarks for evaluating new control algorithms in challenging tasks like obstacle field navigation.
2. **Limitations of Traditional Path Planning:** Classic path planning algorithms often struggle to meet the real-time performance requirements and adaptability needed for fast-moving and changing environments, leading to failures in dynamic scenarios.
3. **Bridging High-Level Planning and Low-Level Control:** Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers a framework to bridge this gap by predicting future states and optimizing control inputs based on a system model. However, the efficacy of MPC in highly dynamic settings is contingent on its ability to rapidly find optimal or near-optimal solutions.
4. **Challenges with Standard MPPI:**
 - **Local Optimality:** MPPI's reliance on small perturbations around a nominal control input can limit its search to locally optimal solutions. This can prevent the controller from discovering significantly better trajectories, especially when the environment changes abruptly or presents complex, non-convex obstacles.
 - **Slow Adaptation:** When perturbations are small, the control sequence adapts slowly, making the controller unresponsive to rapid environmental changes.
 - **Stability vs. Exploration Trade-off:** Increasing the magnitude of perturbations to enhance exploration and global optimality often compromises the stability of the closed-loop system, resulting in degraded control performance.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** While MPPI benefits from parallelization, existing enhancements often introduce computational overhead or reduce the number of samples per instance, potentially affecting real-time viability.

To address these motivations, the proposed MP-MPPI method aims to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Enhance Exploration and Global Optimality:** By integrating precomputed, dynamically feasible motion primitives, MP-MPPI seeks to provide the optimizer with "structured samples" that guide it towards a broader range of feasible trajectories, thereby improving the chances of finding globally optimal or near-optimal solutions beyond the immediate local minima.
- **Maintain Real-time Performance:** The method is designed as a low-cost modification to MPPI, suitable for effective parallel implementation on GPUs, ensuring that improved exploration does not compromise the fast, reactive behavior essential for real-time control.
- **Improve Robustness in Collision Avoidance:** A key objective is to demonstrate superior performance in challenging tasks such as obstacle field navigation and rapid collision avoidance compared to the base MPPI algorithm.
- **Achieve Robust Quadcopter Control:** Validate the proposed algorithm's ability to maintain robust control of a simulated quadcopter in demanding scenarios.

By fulfilling these objectives, the research aims to contribute to the development of more capable autonomous systems that can perform complex navigation tasks in unstructured environments, potentially matching or exceeding human performance in certain aspects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces MP-MPPI, an extension of the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) method, designed to enhance global optimality and exploration through the incorporation of motion primitives.

#### 3.1. Optimal Control Problem (OCP) Formulation

The foundation of the control strategy is the standard optimal control problem (OCP), where the objective is to minimize a cost function $\ell(x_t, u_t)$ over a prediction horizon $N$, with an additional final cost term $\ell_f(x_N)$. The system state $x_t$ and control inputs $u_t$ are subject to system dynamics $x_{t+1} = f(x_t, u_t)$, state constraints $x_t \in \mathcal{X}$, input constraints $u_t \in \mathcal{U}$, and an initial state $x_0 = x_{\text{init}}$.

#### 3.2. Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)

The base MPPI algorithm is a sampling-based method for solving the OCP:
- **Sample Generation:** It generates $M$ perturbed control sequences $u_t^{(k)} = u_t + \epsilon_t^{(k)}$, where $u_t$ is the nominal control and $\epsilon_t^{(k)}$ are perturbations drawn from a normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$.
- **Trajectory Rollout:** For each perturbed control sequence, a trajectory $x_t^{(k)}$ is simulated using a 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) integration method based on the system dynamics.
- **Cost Evaluation:** A cost $S^{(k)}$ is computed for each trajectory by summing the stage costs $\ell(x_t^{(k)}, u_t^{(k)})$ over the horizon and adding the final cost $\ell_f(x_N^{(k)})$.
- **Control Update:** The optimal control sequence $u_t$ is updated using a weighted sum of the perturbed control sequences:
 $w_k = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{\lambda}(S^{(k)} - \rho)\right)$, where $\rho$ is the minimum cost for numerical stability, and $\lambda$ is a temperature variable.
 The new control sequence is $u_t \leftarrow \frac{\sum_{k=1}^M w_k u_t^{(k)}}{\sum_{k=1}^M w_k}$.
- **Tuning Parameters:** The covariance matrix $\Sigma$ influences the rate of adaptation (lower $\Sigma$ leads to local optimality, higher $\Sigma$ to faster convergence but potentially instability), and $\lambda$ adjusts the weighting uniformity (larger $\lambda$ gives more uniform weights).

#### 3.3. MP-MPPI: Motion-Primitive-guided MPPI

The proposed MP-MPPI method extends the base MPPI by incorporating $N_p$ additional samples in the form of motion primitives.
- **Motion Primitives:** These are precomputed sequences of control inputs designed to achieve specific types of motion (e.g., turns, velocity changes) while ensuring dynamic feasibility.
- **Motion Primitive Generation:** State lattice motion primitives are utilized. These are generated by solving the OCP for a set of $N_p$ distinct point references. This process creates a library of dynamically feasible control sequences $u_t^{(p,j)}$.
- **Combined Sampling:** In each optimization loop, the algorithm generates $M$ standard noisy samples and draws $N_p$ samples from the precomputed motion primitive library. The total number of samples evaluated becomes $K = M + N_p$.
- **Weighted Sum Update:** The combined set of $K$ samples (noisy samples and motion primitives) is then used in the same weighted sum update law as the base MPPI algorithm to compute the new optimal control sequence. This approach allows the motion primitives to inform the controller with structured, exploratory trajectories.

The complete MP-MPPI algorithm is structured to be suitable for parallel implementation.

#### 3.4. Case Study: Quadcopter Navigation

The methodology was applied and evaluated using a custom quadcopter simulator:
- **Computational Environment:** The algorithm was implemented using the JAX Python library for GPU-vectorized computations, executed on an RTX 2000 Ada Laptop GPU. The target update frequency was above 100 Hz.
- **Quadcopter Dynamics:** A rigid body dynamics model for a quadcopter was employed, representing linear motion (position $p$, velocity $v$) and angular motion (quaternion $q$, angular velocity $\omega$) driven by body forces $f_B$ and torques $\tau_B$. RK4 integration was used, with quaternion normalization at each step.
- **Cost Function:** A quadratic cost function $\ell(x, v, \omega, q, u)$ was defined, penalizing deviations from reference position, velocity, angular velocity, and orientation (using a quaternion error metric). It also penalized control input magnitude and rapid changes in control inputs.
- **Obstacle Avoidance:** Obstacle avoidance was integrated by adding a large penalty $c_{\text{obs}}$ to the trajectory cost if any part of the predicted trajectory (including a safety distance $d_{\text{safe}}$ for the drone's physical dimensions) intersected with an obstacle.
- **Parameter Tuning:** Detailed parameters for the drone model, MPPI controller (e.g., prediction horizon $N=15$, samples $K=1024$, time step $\Delta t=100$ ms, cost coefficients, covariance matrix $\Sigma$, temperature $\lambda$), and motion primitive generation (e.g., reference lattice points for $x, y, z$ in `[-5, 0, 5]`) were provided to establish the experimental setup.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed MP-MPPI method was evaluated through a series of simulations, comparing its performance against the base MPPI algorithm.

#### 4.1. Obstacle Field Navigation

The first evaluation involved navigating a dense obstacle field. The task required a quadcopter to traverse an environment populated by 300 randomly placed pillars (1 m diameter) within a 20 m by 100 m bounding box. The drone was commanded to follow a moving point reference 10 m ahead of it along the centerline. Each simulation lasted 5 seconds, starting from a hovering state. The experiment was repeated 100 times for both algorithms.

- **MPPI Performance:** The base MPPI algorithm achieved a mean traversal distance of 48.0 m with a standard deviation of 18.0 m. During the 100 runs, the MPPI controller registered one collision with an obstacle.
- **MP-MPPI Performance:** The MP-MPPI controller, enhanced with 27 motion primitives, demonstrated an improved mean traversal distance of 66.6 m, with a reduced standard deviation of 12.6 m. Notably, the MP-MPPI controller avoided all collisions across the 100 runs.

These results indicate that the inclusion of motion primitives significantly enhanced the controller's ability to navigate complex obstacle fields, leading to greater distances traversed and improved collision avoidance performance. The improved mean traversal distance and reduced standard deviation suggest more consistent and effective navigation.

#### 4.2. Single Update Analysis (Reactivity)

To assess the reactive behavior of the algorithms, a scenario was simulated where the drone was moving towards a wall at 10 m/s from a distance of 5 m, and suddenly became aware of the obstacle.

- **MPPI Behavior:** The MPPI algorithm generated a trajectory that resulted in a collision with the wall. This behavior suggests that the magnitude of its local perturbations was insufficient to reorient the trajectory quickly enough to avoid the obstacle.
- **MP-MPPI Behavior:** In the same scenario, the MP-MPPI algorithm successfully generated a trajectory that avoided collision with the wall. This observation demonstrates that the motion primitive samples provided the controller with trajectories representing the necessary evasive maneuvers, even if these were not locally optimal within the range of standard MPPI perturbations.

This analysis highlights the ability of MP-MPPI to leverage motion primitives for larger, more exploratory control changes when required for urgent obstacle avoidance, overcoming the limitations of MPPI's local search.

#### 4.3. Controller Update Frequency

The real-time viability of the MP-MPPI algorithm was benchmarked by evaluating its update frequency across varying numbers of samples and prediction horizons.

- **Computational Scalability:** The results show that the computation times for the online optimization step exhibited a linear trend with respect to the prediction horizon length.
- **GPU Efficiency:** The algorithm effectively utilized the GPU, as evidenced by only marginal differences in latency when varying the number of samples from 128 to 1024.

These findings suggest that MP-MPPI can evaluate additional motion primitive samples with a limited impact on the overall update frequency, maintaining the rapid feedback necessary for stable control in agile robotic systems.

#### 4.4. Discussion and Limitations

The study acknowledges several aspects for further exploration:
- The full extent of the performance improvement introduced by motion primitives has not been comprehensively quantified.
- Further investigation is required to determine if kinematically feasible motion primitives offer superior convergence properties or introduce less bias compared to arbitrary ancillary controllers.
- The balance between the number of motion primitives and noisy samples is identified as a critical design parameter requiring careful tuning. An excessive number of motion primitives could potentially lead the controller to weight suboptimal trajectories, thereby reducing overall performance. Future work could focus on methods for optimally generating the motion primitive library for specific tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces MP-MPPI as an enhanced Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) algorithm, characterized by the authors as a "low-cost, high-impact" extension. This method integrates precomputed motion primitives into the sampling-based optimization loop to improve global optimality and exploration capabilities.

The significance of this work lies in its demonstrated ability to enhance the performance of sampling-based model predictive control in demanding robotic tasks. Specifically, the findings suggest:
- **Improved Navigation in Complex Environments:** MP-MPPI enables a quadcopter to navigate dense obstacle fields more effectively, traversing greater distances and avoiding collisions where the base MPPI algorithm may fail. This capability is critical for autonomous systems operating in unstructured or cluttered real-world settings.
- **Enhanced Reactive Behavior:** The algorithm demonstrates a superior capacity for rapid adaptation and collision avoidance in time-critical situations. By leveraging motion primitives, the controller can identify and execute evasive maneuvers that are outside the scope of local perturbations, contributing to robust and safer operation.
- **Computational Efficiency and Real-time Viability:** The implementation leverages parallel computing on GPUs, maintaining high update frequencies even with the addition of motion primitives. This characteristic supports the use of MP-MPPI in real-time control applications where rapid decision-making is essential. The "low-cost" aspect refers to its ability to achieve these improvements without requiring high-end computational resources or introducing significant latency.
- **Advancement in MPC Methodology:** MP-MPPI provides a concrete method for addressing the exploration-exploitation trade-off inherent in sampling-based MPC, offering a structured way to guide the optimizer towards better global solutions without relying on complex, non-real-time planners or methods that demand specific system properties (e.g., invertible dynamics).

The potential impact of MP-MPPI extends to various applications requiring agile and robust autonomous systems, including but not limited to:
- **Drone Racing and Inspection:** Enabling faster and safer navigation in complex aerial environments.
- **Search and Rescue Operations:** Improving the ability of drones to traverse hazardous terrains.
- **Logistics and Delivery:** Facilitating more efficient and collision-free movement of autonomous vehicles in dynamic environments.

By providing a practical and computationally efficient approach to integrate structured exploration into real-time model predictive control, this research contributes to bridging the gap between theoretical optimal control and the practical demands of autonomous systems in unpredictable real-world scenarios.
