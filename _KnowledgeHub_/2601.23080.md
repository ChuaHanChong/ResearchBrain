# Robust and Generalized Humanoid Motion Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Institute of Technology and Humanoid Robotics (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. developed a whole-body control framework for humanoid robots that achieves robust and generalized motion tracking using only 3.5 hours of curated data. The system demonstrates high-fidelity control for dynamic, contact-rich behaviors, integrates fall recovery, and shows resilience to imperfect reference motions in both simulation and on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- High-fidelity imitation in humanoid robotics typically relies on policies trained for specific motions, limiting generalization and unified modeling across diverse skills.
- Existing motion tracking controllers often exhibit suboptimal accuracy and stability, especially during dynamic maneuvers, and current state-of-the-art methods require exceptionally large datasets (e.g., over 700 hours of motion data).
- Most control policies lack integrated fall recovery mechanisms, making humanoid robots vulnerable to disturbances and necessitating separate modules for self-recovery, which complicates integration and limits real-world robustness.

## Method

- A dynamics-conditioned command aggregation framework is introduced, utilizing Multi-head Attention to selectively interpret and filter command information based on the robot's current physical state and dynamics, making it robust to noisy inputs.
- Fall recovery is intrinsically integrated into a single-stage, end-to-end training process by exposing the policy to randomized unstable poses and providing a recovery window during training.
- The policy is trained on a compact, quality-driven dataset of approximately 3.5 hours, leveraging an asymmetric actor-critic scheme with a residual joint position action space and a dense reward function for efficient learning.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines (Any2Track, GMT) in simulation, achieving higher success rates and lower Mean Per-Joint Position Error across MoCap, video-derived, and ground-interaction motions, with a notable **94.6%** success rate on noisy video-derived motions.
- The system demonstrated exceptional robustness to command noise, maintaining stable tracking even under **1500%** jitter in reference commands, while baseline methods failed beyond **200%** noise levels, attributed primarily to the dynamics-conditioned cross-attention mechanism.
- Successful real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot validated robust sim-to-real transfer, showcasing high-fidelity tracking of complex breakdance-style and video-derived motions, autonomous recovery from external pushes, and stable performance under real-time teleoperation inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamics-conditioned cross-attention is critical for robustly aggregating command information under noisy or distribution-shifted inputs, enabling the policy to intelligently filter unreliable segments of reference motions.
> - Integrating fall recovery directly into the main control policy not only allows for autonomous self-recovery but also enriches the policy's experience with diverse contact patterns, thereby improving overall robustness for complex, contact-rich behaviors.
> - A carefully curated, compact dataset of approximately 3.5 hours of motion data can be sufficient to train a highly generalized and robust whole-body controller for humanoids, reducing the resource demands compared to data-hungry approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots hold immense promise due to their morphological compatibility with human environments and their versatile high-dimensional actuation. However, realizing their full potential necessitates a general whole-body controller capable of coordinating complex movements, managing changing contacts, and maintaining stability over extended periods. This paper addresses critical limitations within the current landscape of humanoid control research.

Historically, high-fidelity imitation in humanoid robotics often relied on policies trained for specific motions or a limited set of behaviors. While effective for singular tasks, this approach limits unified modeling and generalization across diverse skills. Recent efforts have shifted towards unified whole-body control, employing large-scale motion tracking objectives and more practical data acquisition methods. Controllers like GMT and Any2Track have attempted to train universal trackers, showing progress but often exhibiting suboptimal accuracy and stability, especially during dynamic maneuvers or rapid contact transitions. Moreover, impressive systems like SONIC achieve high motion coverage and naturalness but demand exceptionally large datasets (over 700 hours of motion data) and substantial computational resources, thereby raising a significant barrier to iterative research and deployment for many institutions.

Another major challenge is the lack of integrated fall recovery mechanisms in most control policies. Real-world operation invariably exposes robots to disturbances, making autonomous recovery essential for continuous, safe, and long-horizon tasks. Existing solutions often treat fall recovery as a separate module, complicating policy integration and limiting overall robustness.

This work directly tackles these gaps by proposing a framework that aims to achieve effective learning with *limited data*, maintain *stable closed-loop tracking even with imperfect reference motions*, and *integrate fall recovery* seamlessly within a single training and policy pipeline. It seeks to develop a general whole-body controller that is data-efficient, robust to noisy inputs, and intrinsically capable of self-recovery, thereby advancing the field towards more practical, versatile, and safely deployable humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalized whole-body control framework for humanoid robots that can effectively track a diverse range of motions, including highly dynamic and contact-rich behaviors, with high fidelity and stability. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the practical challenges in deploying humanoid robots:

1. **Robustness to Imperfect Reference Motions:** Practical reference motions, whether derived from human motion capture, video estimations, or high-level planners, frequently contain noise, inconsistencies, and artifacts after being retargeted to a robot's morphology. Current control policies often struggle with such imperfections, as local defects can be amplified during closed-loop execution, leading to tracking drift or outright failure, particularly in dynamic scenarios. The authors aim to overcome this by enabling the policy to intelligently interpret and filter command inputs.
2. **Generalization Across Diverse Motions with Limited Data:** Learning a single policy capable of diverse humanoid motions traditionally requires extensive datasets, as exemplified by methods like SONIC that use hundreds of hours of data. This paper seeks to achieve broad motion coverage and generalization without such large-scale data requirements, making research and deployment more accessible and efficient. The motivation here is to find a compact yet diverse and high-quality dataset sufficient for training a capable policy.
3. **Enhanced Closed-Loop Stability and Tracking Accuracy:** Many existing motion tracking controllers exhibit suboptimal tracking accuracy and stability, especially during highly dynamic maneuvers and transitions involving complex ground contacts. The authors are motivated to improve these aspects, ensuring consistent performance even under challenging motion regimes.
4. **Integrated Fall Recovery for Real-World Reliability:** For safe and continuous operation in the real world, humanoid robots must be able to recover from falls or significant disturbances autonomously. Separating fall recovery from the main control policy limits overall robustness and necessitates frequent human intervention. A core motivation is to integrate a self-recovery mechanism directly into the primary control policy, broadening the robot's operational state space and significantly improving its real-world resilience.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the need for a more practical, robust, and data-efficient approach to humanoid whole-body control, capable of operating reliably in complex, real-world environments with less reliance on perfect input data or massive training resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a novel dynamics-conditioned command aggregation framework coupled with an integrated fall recovery curriculum, designed for single-stage, end-to-end training.

**A. Humanoid Motion Dataset:**
The foundation of the training is a curated motion corpus derived from LAFAN1 and AMASS. Critically, the authors emphasize a "quality-driven construction" process, involving rigorous quality control to remove infeasible, low-quality, or redundant motion segments. This results in a compact, yet diverse and high-quality dataset of approximately 3.5 hours, contrasting sharply with data-hungry approaches and highlighting the efficiency of their proposed policy architecture.

**B. Motion Tracking Formulation:**
The policy operates within an asymmetric actor-critic training scheme.
- **Observation Space:** The actor receives a proprioceptive observation (gravity direction, base angular velocity, joint positions/velocities, previous action) and a command observation (reference base linear/angular velocities, gravity direction, joint positions). The critic, for more accurate value estimation, additionally receives privileged information such as reference base height and body link poses.
- **Action Space and Low-level Control:** The policy outputs a residual joint position command, which is added to the reference joint configuration to form the PD setpoint. This residual formulation allows for efficient exploration around the reference pose and improves tracking accuracy. Joint torques are then computed via a standard joint-level PD controller.
- **Reward Function:** A dense reward function is employed, combining rewards for tracking accuracy (keypoint alignment, relative pose, and velocity consistency) with regularization terms for safety and smoothness (penalizing rapid action changes, joint limit violations, and non-target contacts).

**C. Policy Learning Framework – Dynamics-Conditioned Command Aggregation:**
This is the core architectural innovation, leveraging Multi-head Attention (MHA) to selectively aggregate command information.
- **History Encoder (Causal Temporal Encoder):** A causal Transformer processes the most recent 10-step proprioceptive observations. Each observation is embedded via an MLP, combined with sinusoidal positional encoding, and then passed through a causal self-attention block. The resulting features are aggregated via element-wise max pooling to generate a compact "dynamics embedding" (h_t). This embedding serves as the query signal, representing the robot's current physical state and dynamics.
- **Command Encoder (Multi-head Cross-Attention):** This encoder takes the dynamics embedding (projected into a query vector) and a contextual command window (2L+1 steps, with L=10, covering past and future reference commands). The command window tokens, also embedded and positionally encoded, serve as the keys and values. A single dynamics-conditioned cross-attention block then aggregates these command tokens. By conditioning the attention weights on the current dynamics (h_t), the encoder adaptively emphasizes command elements consistent with the robot's current physical feasibility and down-weights unreliable or noisy segments. This filtering mechanism is crucial for robustness against imperfect reference inputs.

**D. Fall Recovery Integration:**
To enhance robustness and safety, fall recovery is intrinsically integrated into the training process.
- **Randomized Recovery Initialization:** In 15% of parallel training environments, the robot is reset to randomized, unstable poses. To assist early exploration, an annealed upward pulling force (linearly reducing from [0, 200N] to negligible) is applied, guiding the policy towards recoverable states without relying on external assistance in the final policy. This broadens the policy's exposure to unstable configurations and diverse contact patterns.
- **Termination Conditions:** Standard episode termination occurs for time limits or instability (excessive base orientation, insufficient height). Crucially, in recovery environments, these instability criteria are suppressed for a 3-second "recovery window," allowing the policy to complete stand-up maneuvers without premature resets. If recovery fails within this window, the episode is terminated.

**E. Training Setup:**
The policy is trained efficiently in the Isaac Gym simulator on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, utilizing 5,680 parallel environments. The entire process is conducted end-to-end, without the need for distillation or multi-stage training, which simplifies the learning pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates significant advancements in humanoid motion tracking, validated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 robot.

**A. Performance Evaluation:**
1. **Comparison with Baselines:** The proposed method was quantitatively compared against two state-of-the-art trackers, Any2Track and GMT, across diverse motion sources: MoCap data, video-derived motions, and ground-interaction motions.
 - **Superior Performance:** The proposed method consistently achieved the highest success rates (Succ. ↑) and lowest Mean Per-Joint Position Error (E_MPJPE ↓) across all categories.
 - **Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** The improvement was most pronounced on video-derived motions (94.6% success rate, 46.56 E_MPJPE vs. 54.3% and 112.16 for Any2Track), indicating strong generalization and robustness to unseen motion styles and the inherent noise from video-based estimation.
2. **Ablations on Policy Architecture:**
 - **Causal History Encoder:** Replacing the causal history encoder with a CNN-based variant ("Ours CNN HistEnc") led to consistent performance degradation, highlighting the importance of the causal temporal encoding for stable and accurate tracking.
 - **Dynamics-Conditioned Cross-Attention:** Substituting the cross-attention command encoder with a self-attention variant ("Ours SelfAttn CmdEnc") resulted in a much larger performance drop, especially for video-derived and ground-interaction motions. This unequivocally demonstrates the critical role of dynamics-conditioned cross-attention in robustly aggregating commands under noisy or distribution-shifted inputs.
3. **Ablations on Fall Recovery:**
 - **Integrated Robustness:** While fall recovery training did not compromise tracking accuracy on MoCap or video-derived motions, its removal ("Ours w/o Fall Recovery") caused a substantial drop in performance on ground-interaction motions (70.5% success rate vs. 90.1%), increasing E_MPJPE significantly. This confirms that integrated fall recovery not only enables self-recovery but also enriches contact experience, improving robustness for complex contact-rich behaviors.

**B. Robustness Evaluation Under Command Noise:**
The method's robustness to command noise was rigorously tested by injecting varying levels of jitter (up to 1500%) into reference commands during a Charleston dance motion.
- **Exceptional Noise Tolerance:** Baseline methods (GMT, Any2Track) degraded rapidly with increasing noise, struggling beyond 200% noise levels. In stark contrast, the proposed method demonstrated significantly stronger robustness across all metrics (E_MPJPE, E_MPKPE, E_vel, E_acc), maintaining stable tracking even under 1500% noise.
- **Architectural Contribution:** Ablation studies confirmed that the cross-attention command encoder was the primary mechanism for filtering noisy commands, while the causal history encoder offered complementary benefits by stabilizing the dynamics representation.

**C. Real-World Experiments on Unitree G1:**
The learned policy was successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, showcasing robust sim-to-real transfer and generalization.
- **Complex Motion Tracking and Recovery:** The robot successfully tracked challenging breakdance-style motions with frequent ground contacts and autonomously recovered from external pushes, resuming tracking without manual intervention (Fig. 4).
- **Video-Derived Motion Generalization:** High-fidelity tracking of unseen, video-derived motion styles was achieved, demonstrating effective generalization despite noise from human motion estimation pipelines (Fig. 1b).
- **Real-Time Teleoperation:** The policy exhibited stable and precise tracking under noisy real-time inputs from consumer-grade VR and professional motion-capture suits, enabling complex whole-body behaviors like crawling and high kicks (Fig. 1c, d).
- **Joystick-Driven Locomotion:** The tracker seamlessly integrated into a joystick-driven locomotion system, serving as a robust low-level controller for a motion synthesis module. It tolerated abrupt transitions in synthesized reference trajectories while maintaining stylized, coherent locomotion on the physical robot (Fig. 5).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of humanoid robotics by addressing several critical limitations that hinder the practical deployment of these complex systems.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Pioneering Data Efficiency:** The work demonstrates that a robust and generalized whole-body controller can be trained using a remarkably compact dataset (approximately 3.5 hours of curated motion data). This stands in stark contrast to state-of-the-art methods requiring hundreds of hours of data, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for research and development in humanoid control.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Imperfect Data:** The dynamics-conditioned command aggregation framework is a novel and highly effective mechanism for interpreting and filtering noisy or inconsistent reference motions. This capability is crucial for real-world scenarios where perfectly clean reference data is rarely available, enhancing the robot's resilience against sensory drift, human operator inconsistencies, and estimation artifacts.
- **Integrated Autonomy and Safety:** By seamlessly integrating fall recovery into the single-stage training pipeline, the policy gains intrinsic self-recovery capabilities. This not only improves safety but also enables long-horizon, continuous operation without the need for frequent human intervention after disturbances, making humanoid robots more autonomous and reliable.
- **Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The robust deployment on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, combined with successful real-time teleoperation and diverse motion tracking, provides strong empirical validation of the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of the learned policy.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Research and Development:** The data-efficient and robust framework can accelerate research in humanoid control, allowing researchers to iterate faster and explore more complex behaviors without being bottlenecked by massive data collection or computational resources.
- **Broader Real-World Applications:** The enhanced robustness and integrated fall recovery make humanoid robots more viable for a wider range of real-world tasks, including logistics, assistance in human environments, and specialized industrial applications. The ability to track dynamic, contact-rich motions and recover from falls is foundational for reliable interaction with unstructured environments.
- **Foundation for High-Level Intelligence:** By providing a highly stable and adaptive low-level controller, this work paves the way for integrating more sophisticated high-level planning, perception, and human-robot interaction modules. The robust tracker can serve as a reliable "motor system" for complex cognitive functions.
- **Commercial Adoption:** The involvement of Humanoid Robotics (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. underscores the commercial relevance of this research. A robust, generalized, and data-efficient control solution has direct implications for reducing development costs and increasing the reliability of commercial humanoid robot products.
- **Democratization of Humanoid Robotics:** By making advanced control achievable with fewer resources, this research could help democratize access to humanoid robotics research and application, enabling a broader community to contribute to this exciting field. Future work incorporating global localization and enhanced integration with upstream modules will further expand the scope of possible applications.
