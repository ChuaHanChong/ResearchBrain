# MotionBooth: Motion-Aware Customized Text-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionBooth introduces a framework for generating customized text-to-video content, enabling animating specific subjects with precise, user-defined control over both subject and camera movements. The approach achieves superior subject fidelity, temporal consistency, and motion adherence, outperforming existing baselines in metrics like R-CLIP and FVD, through a novel training-free inference mechanism.

## Problem

- Existing customized text-to-video (T2V) methods struggle to simultaneously learn specific subject appearances and preserve diverse motion generation capabilities, often leading to static videos or degraded backgrounds.
- Universal and precise control over subject and camera motion is lacking, with current approaches either underutilizing base T2V model motion priors or requiring computationally expensive, motion-specific training.
- Integrating customized subjects with flexible motion control without sacrificing video quality or incurring high computational costs remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A training stage fine-tunes a pre-trained ==text-to-video diffusion model== on a few subject images, utilizing novel ==subject region loss==, ==video preservation loss==, and ==subject token cross-attention loss== to prevent overfitting and link subjects to motion signals.
- A ==training-free inference== mechanism controls subject motion by directly manipulating ==cross-attention maps== based on user-provided bounding boxes.
- Camera movement is controlled by a novel ==latent shift module== that manipulates the noised latent representation, enabling precise camera panning and zooming without additional training.

## Results

- MotionBooth consistently outperformed baselines (DreamBooth, CustomVideo, DreamVideo) on both Zeroscope and LaVie models, demonstrating higher subject fidelity (R-CLIP, R-DINO), text alignment (CLIP-T), temporal consistency (T-Cons), and lower flow error.
- For camera movement control, the training-free latent shift module in MotionBooth achieved superior FVD, CLIP-T, T-Cons, and significantly reduced flow error compared to training-based methods like AnimateDiff and CameraCtrl.
- A human preference study indicated MotionBooth as the most preferred method across all evaluated criteria, particularly for subject appearance alignment (**81.6%** preference), reinforcing quantitative findings on user satisfaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-tuning T2V models for customized subjects requires a combination of region-specific losses and general video preservation losses to prevent overfitting to backgrounds and maintain motion diversity.
> - Explicitly linking the customized subject token to its spatial location via a cross-attention loss during training is crucial for enabling effective, training-free subject motion control during inference.
> - Training-free manipulation of cross-attention maps and latent representations during inference can effectively control both subject and camera movements without incurring additional training costs for specific motion types.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI has seen rapid advancements, particularly in text-to-image (T2I) and text-to-video (T2V) synthesis. This paper specifically addresses the challenge of *customized* video generation, where a user aims to animate a particular subject (e.g., a specific pet or toy) within a video, with precise control over its movement and the camera's perspective.

Initially, customized generation emerged in the T2I domain with methods like Textual Inversion and DreamBooth, which learn a subject's appearance from a few reference images and then generate diverse images featuring that subject. Following these successes, customized T2V generation has gained increasing interest due to its potential applications in personal content creation and film production.

However, extending customized T2I principles to video presents several complex challenges:
1. **Subject Learning vs. Motion Preservation:** Fine-tuning a pre-trained T2V model on a limited set of subject images can lead to overfitting, causing the model to lose its general video generation capabilities. This often results in "moving images" with minimal or static motion, or degraded backgrounds.
2. **Lack of Universal Motion Control:** Existing customized T2V methods often either underutilize the motion diversity of base T2V models (e.g., VideoBooth, which generates short clips with limited motion) or require specific motion learning for each type of camera or subject motion (e.g., DreamVideo, which needs fine-tuning on video data for predefined motion prototypes). These approaches are computationally expensive and lack flexibility for user-defined, novel motions.
3. **Computational Cost:** Training-based methods for motion control, such as AnimateDiff or CameraCtrl, typically rely on large-scale datasets and can incur high computational costs, making them less accessible for personalized applications.

MotionBooth positions itself as a framework that seeks to resolve the conflict between subject learning and video motion preservation. It aims to achieve universal and precise motion control for customized subjects without requiring specific motion training or sacrificing the generative quality of the base T2V model. By proposing a training-free inference mechanism for motion control, it addresses the computational and flexibility limitations of prior art. This work directly builds upon diffusion-based T2V models and attention manipulation techniques seen in image generation, extending them to the more complex temporal domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of MotionBooth is to enable motion-aware customized video generation, allowing users to animate specific subjects with precise, user-defined control over both the subject's movement and the camera's perspective.

This overarching objective is driven by several detailed motivations and specific goals:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Customized T2V Methods:** Current methods frequently suffer from either generating static videos or requiring extensive, per-motion-type fine-tuning, which is resource-intensive and limits creative flexibility. MotionBooth aims to overcome these by preserving the diverse motion prior inherent in pre-trained T2V models.
- **Efficient Subject Learning:** To accurately capture a subject's appearance and attributes, the framework aims to efficiently fine-tune a text-to-video model using only a small number of images (typically 3-5). This minimizes data requirements and training time.
- **Preventing Overfitting and Degradation:** A crucial motivation is to prevent the fine-tuning process from causing the model to overfit to the limited training images' backgrounds or degrade the base T2V model's general video generation capabilities (e.g., leading to blurred backgrounds or lack of diverse scenes).
- **Integrating Subject and Motion Control:** The framework seeks to explicitly connect the learned customized subject with mechanisms for motion control. This involves ensuring that manipulation signals for motion effectively influence the customized subject.
- **Developing Training-Free Motion Control:** A significant goal is to introduce inference-time techniques for managing both subject and camera motions without requiring additional training for specific motion types. This enhances the universality and adaptability of the motion control, making it plug-and-play with various T2V models.
- **Maintaining Video Quality and Fidelity:** The ultimate motivation is to generate high-quality videos that maintain the appearance fidelity of the customized subject, exhibit realistic and diverse motion, and preserve temporal consistency, all while adhering to user-defined motion parameters.

In essence, MotionBooth is motivated by the desire to provide a flexible, efficient, and high-quality solution for personalized video creation that has been challenging for prior methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionBooth's methodology is structured into two main phases: a training phase for subject learning and an inference phase for motion control.

#### 3.1. Subject Learning (Training Stage)

The training stage involves fine-tuning a pre-trained Text-to-Video (T2V) diffusion model using a few images of a specific subject. This process integrates three novel loss functions designed to address the challenges of overfitting, video quality degradation, and explicit linkage for motion control.

- **Preliminary:** T2V diffusion models learn by reconstructing noise in a latent space. An input video is encoded into a latent `z_0`, noise `ϵ` is added to get `z_t`, and the model `ϵ_θ` is trained to predict `ϵ`. The standard reconstruction loss is `L = E[||ϵ - ϵ_θ(z_t, c, t)||²]`.
- **Subject Region Loss (L_sub):**
 - **Motivation:** To prevent the model from overfitting to the backgrounds of the limited training images.
 - **Approach:** Instead of computing the diffusion reconstruction loss over the entire image, `L_sub` is calculated exclusively within the subject's masked region. Subject masks are extracted (e.g., using SAM).
 - **Formulation:** `L_sub = E[||(ϵ - ϵ_θ(z_t, c_i, t)) ⋅ M||²]`, where `M` is the binary subject mask resized to the latent space, and `c_i` is a prompt like "a [V] [class name]".
- **Video Preservation Loss (L_vid):**
 - **Motivation:** Fine-tuning a T2V model solely on images can degrade its inherent video generation capabilities, leading to over-smoothed or unrealistic backgrounds in generated videos. This loss aims to preserve the model's original video knowledge.
 - **Approach:** Jointly train the model with common video data and their captions (`c_v`). Unlike prior works using class-specific preservation data, MotionBooth uses common, diverse videos, which are found to be more effective for subject learning and generalization.
 - **Formulation:** `L_vid = E[||ϵ - ϵ_θ(z_t, c_v, t)||²]`.
- **Subject Token Cross-Attention Loss (L_stca):**
 - **Motivation:** To enable precise subject motion control during inference, it is necessary to explicitly link the unique subject token (`[V]`) introduced during training to the subject's spatial position within the cross-attention maps. Standard fine-tuning does not inherently ensure this strong link.
 - **Approach:** A Binary Cross-Entropy Loss is applied to the cross-attention map (`A`) corresponding to the subject tokens (`[V] [class name]`). This loss encourages higher attention values within the subject's masked region and lower values outside it.
 - **Formulation:** `L_stca = - [M log(A) + (1 - M) log(1 - A)]`.
- **Overall Loss Function:** The total loss during training is a weighted sum of these components: `L = L_sub + λ_1 L_vid + λ_2 L_stca`, where `λ_1` and `λ_2` are hyperparameters (set to 1.0 and 0.01, respectively).

#### 3.2. Motion Control (Inference Stage)

During inference, MotionBooth employs training-free techniques to control both subject and camera motions.

- **Subject Motion Control:**
 - **Input:** A sequence of bounding boxes `[B_1, B_2, ..., B_L]` specifying the desired position and size of the subject in each frame.
 - **Approach:** Direct manipulation of cross-attention maps during the denoising process. This is a plug-and-play method that does not require additional training.
 - **Mechanism:** In the cross-attention layers, the attention scores (`QKᵀ/√d`) are modified by adding a term `αS`. The matrix `S` is defined such that it has positive values within the bounding box region for subject language tokens (`[V] [class name]`) and large negative values (negative infinity) outside these regions. This amplifies attention within the desired subject region and suppresses it elsewhere.
 - **Temporal Aspect:** Stronger attention amplification is applied in earlier denoising steps (before a threshold `τ`), while suppression outside the bounding box persists throughout generation. This aligns with the observation that object layout is formed early in diffusion models.
 - **Context Preservation:** Information from other text tokens (e.g., verbs, background descriptions) is hypothesized to be preserved through the `<start>` and `<end>` tokens, which typically carry high softmax values in Transformer-based encoders.
- **Camera Movement Control:**
 - **Input:** Horizontal and vertical camera movement ratios `c_cam = [c_x, c_y]`.
 - **Approach:** A novel latent shift module that directly manipulates the noised latent representation.
 - **Mechanism:** At each denoising step `t` within a specific range `[σ_1, σ_2]`, the noised latent `z_t` is shifted according to `c_x` and `c_y`. The challenge of filling the "missing" regions caused by the shift is addressed by sampling tokens from the *original* noised latent along the x and y axes. This "straight-sampling" is based on the assumption that new visible regions are semantically similar to adjacent areas. Subject tokens are filtered out during sampling to avoid their unwanted replication. The total camera shift is spread over multiple denoising steps.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Base Models:** Zeroscope and LaVie.
- **Training:** 300 steps, AdamW optimizer, learning rate 5e-2, weight decay 1e-2. 500 randomly chosen preservation videos from Panda-70M.
- **Inference:** 50-step DDIM scheduler, classifier-free guidance scale 7.5.
- **Video Resolution:** 576x320x24 (Zeroscope) and 512x320x16 (LaVie).
- **Training Time:** Approximately 10 minutes on a single NVIDIA A100 80G GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MotionBooth involved comprehensive quantitative and qualitative comparisons against established baselines, alongside ablation studies and a human preference study.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Metrics:**
 - **Subject Fidelity:** Region CLIP similarity (R-CLIP) and Region DINO similarity (R-DINO) between subject images and generated frame regions.
 - **Text Alignment:** CLIP image-text similarity (CLIP-T) between entire frames and text prompts.
 - **Temporal Consistency:** CLIP image features similarity between consecutive frames (T-Cons.).
 - **Motion Fidelity:** Flow error, comparing predicted optical flow with ground-truth camera motion.
 - **Video Quality (for Camera Control):** Fréchet Video Distance (FVD).

- **Motion-Aware Customized Video Generation (Table 1):**
 - MotionBooth consistently outperformed all baselines (DreamBooth, CustomVideo, DreamVideo) across both Zeroscope and LaVie base models.
 - It achieved higher R-CLIP, R-DINO, CLIP-T, and T-Cons scores, while demonstrating lower Flow error. This indicates superior subject preservation, text alignment, temporal consistency, and adherence to desired motion.
 - DreamVideo, which incorporates video data, showed competitive T-Cons and flow error, supporting MotionBooth's use of video preservation loss. CustomVideo exhibited lower R-DINO, suggesting less effective subject generation in specified positions.

- **Camera Movement Control (Table 2):**
 - MotionBooth, despite using a training-free latent shift module, achieved superior performance in FVD, CLIP-T, T-Cons, and Flow error compared to training-based methods like AnimateDiff and CameraCtrl.
 - For instance, with Zeroscope, MotionBooth significantly reduced flow error (0.190 vs. CameraCtrl's 0.807) and with LaVie (0.296 vs. CameraCtrl's 0.807), demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of the latent shift approach.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Customized Objects and Subject Motions (Fig. 6):**
 - MotionBooth generated videos with accurate subject motion alignment, strong text-prompt adherence, and high overall video quality.
 - Baselines like DreamBooth and CustomVideo often produced videos with vague or degraded backgrounds, suggesting that training without video preservation data impairs background generation.
 - CustomVideo and DreamVideo also struggled with subject appearance, potentially due to partial model fine-tuning.

- **Camera Motion Control (Fig. 7):**
 - MotionBooth produced videos that accurately followed user-defined camera movements (e.g., pan up-right, down-left) while maintaining temporal consistency and high aesthetic quality.
 - AnimateDiff was limited to basic movements and could not handle user-defined directions. CameraCtrl, while accepting user input, resulted in subpar aesthetics and "flash movements" of objects.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

- **Training Technologies:** The ablation studies confirmed the necessity of all proposed loss components:
 - `w/o mask` (without subject region loss) significantly decreased R-DINO by 0.256, underscoring its role in preventing background overfitting.
 - `w/o STCA` (without subject token cross-attention loss) showed a decrease in R-DINO and an increase in flow error, indicating its importance for connecting subjects with motion control.
 - `w/o video` (without video preservation loss) resulted in lower R-CLIP, R-DINO, and T-Cons scores, highlighting the critical role of video data in maintaining video generation capabilities.
 - Using `w/ class video` (class-specific preservation videos) instead of common videos yielded worse results, suggesting that diverse common videos are more beneficial for generalization.

#### 4.4. Human Preference Study (Fig. 8)

- A user study involving 7 colleagues evaluated 30 groups of videos from MotionBooth and baselines across four criteria: subject motion alignment, camera movement alignment, subject appearance alignment, and temporal consistency.
- MotionBooth was the most preferred method across all evaluation aspects, particularly in subject appearance alignment (receiving 81.6% preference). This reinforces the quantitative findings and indicates superior user satisfaction.

#### 4.5. Limitations

- **Multiple Object Generation:** MotionBooth struggles with generating videos involving multiple distinct objects, sometimes merging their appearances.
- **Challenging Motions:** The model may fail to accurately depict motions that are semantically unlikely for the customized subject (e.g., a plushie riding a bike).
- **High Camera Speed:** While capable of handling significant camera movements, performance degrades under extremely high camera movement speeds, leading to incomplete or flawed scene generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MotionBooth represents a significant advancement in the field of customized text-to-video generation by offering a unified and flexible framework for animates subjects with controllable motion.

**Technical Contributions:**
- **Resolution of Core Conflict:** The work successfully addresses the fundamental challenge of simultaneously learning specific subject appearances and preserving the diverse motion capabilities of pre-trained T2V models.
- **Novel Loss-Augmented Training:** The introduction of subject region loss, video preservation loss, and subject token cross-attention loss collectively enhances subject fidelity, video quality, and the integration of subjects with motion signals during training.
- **Innovative Training-Free Motion Control:** The development of direct cross-attention map manipulation for subject motion and the latent shift module for camera motion provides highly adaptable and efficient control mechanisms during inference, eliminating the need for expensive motion-specific training.

**Practical Implications:**
- **Enhanced Creative Control:** MotionBooth empowers artists, content creators, and individuals to generate personalized videos with unprecedented control over how customized subjects move and how the camera frames the action. This opens avenues for more dynamic and specific storytelling.
- **Reduced Computational Cost:** The training-free nature of its motion control techniques makes the generation process more accessible and less resource-intensive compared to methods requiring extensive fine-tuning for each motion type.
- **Broader Adaptability:** The plug-and-play design of the motion control methods suggests potential adaptability to future, more powerful T2V models (e.g., Sora), which could further expand its utility.

**Potential Societal Impact:**
- **Positive Impacts:** The framework can foster creativity in personalized content creation, animation, marketing, and educational materials by simplifying the production of complex video scenarios.
- **Potential Negative Impacts:** Like other advanced generative AI models, MotionBooth carries the risk of misuse in creating deepfakes or spreading disinformation due to its ability to generate realistic customized videos. Bias present in training datasets could also be amplified, potentially reinforcing stereotypes.
- **Mitigation:** The authors acknowledge these risks and implicitly suggest that ongoing research and ethical guidelines are crucial for responsible deployment. Making models and code publicly available could also foster transparency and collaborative efforts to address these issues.

**Future Directions:**
The identified limitations suggest future research avenues, including improving the framework's ability to handle multiple interacting objects, enhancing its understanding and generation of complex or semantically challenging motions, and potentially integrating with more advanced T2V backbones to mitigate existing drawbacks.
