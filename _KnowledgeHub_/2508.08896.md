# Towards Affordance-Aware Robotic Dexterous Grasping with Human-like Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba's DAMO Academy and collaborating universities developed AffordDex, a framework that generates dexterous robotic grasps integrating natural human-like motion and an understanding of object affordances. This system achieved higher success rates and significantly improved human-likeness (e.g., HLS of 8.6 for seen objects) while notably reducing contact with inappropriate object regions (e.g., AS of 4 for seen objects) compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous grasping methods often prioritize low-level stability metrics, neglecting functional correctness and safety for subsequent manipulation tasks.
- Learning-based policies for robotic grasping frequently produce kinematically awkward or unnatural hand postures that lack human-like fluidity.
- Prior affordance prediction models are typically task or category-specific, limiting generalization and failing to robustly identify universally inappropriate contact regions (negative affordances).

## Method

- AffordDex introduces a two-stage training framework that first instills human motion priors and then refines the policy with affordance awareness.
- The Human Hand Trajectory Imitating (HTI) stage pre-trains a base policy via imitation learning on human hand motion data to capture natural kinematics.
- A Negative Affordance-aware Segmentation (NAA) module, using procedural texturing, multi-view rendering, and VLM-guided classification (GPT-4V, SAM, CLIP), identifies and projects functionally inappropriate contact regions onto 3D objects, followed by teacher-student distillation to incorporate this knowledge into a vision-based policy.

## Results

- AffordDex demonstrated the highest success rates across all evaluation categories, while also significantly improving the Human-likeness Score (HLS) (e.g., **8.6** vs. **5.4** for UniDexGrasp++ on seen objects).
- The framework achieved a considerably lower Affordance Score (AS), indicating superior avoidance of negative affordance regions (e.g., 4 vs. 28 for UniDexGrasp++ on seen objects).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Human Hand Trajectory Imitating (HTI) and Negative Affordance-aware Segmentation (NAA) modules are crucial for achieving human-like and affordance-aware grasps, and their components are shown to enhance other RL-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning of human-like motion priors and functional affordance understanding, then synergizing them, yields more natural, safer, and task-appropriate robotic grasps.
> - Fine-grained open-vocabulary negative affordance segmentation can be effectively achieved by reformulating the task as a VLM-guided classification problem, enabling robots to avoid unsafe or functionally incorrect object regions.
> - Explicitly incorporating human hand trajectory imitation significantly enhances the anthropomorphic quality of robotic movements, promoting intuitive interaction and greater efficiency in human-centric tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous grasping is a foundational capability for general-purpose embodied AI, crucial for robots to interact robustly and intelligently with their environments. The field has seen rapid advancements, moving beyond simple end-effectors like parallel-jaw grippers towards multi-fingered dexterous hands that mimic human anatomy. These anthropomorphic hands offer enhanced flexibility, precision, and task adaptability, making them vital for complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments.

Historically, dexterous grasping has been tackled by traditional motion planning methods, which struggle with the high degrees of freedom (DOFs) of multi-fingered hands. This challenge has propelled the adoption of learning-based approaches, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL). Recent works, such as UniDexGrasp (Xu et al. 2023) and UniDexGrasp++ (Wan et al. 2023), have achieved high success rates in generalizing grasps to novel objects, often by decoupling static grasp pose generation from dynamic trajectory planning or by employing geometry-aware curriculum learning. Other methods, like DexGrasp Anything (Zhong et al. 2025), leverage diffusion models for generative grasp pose synthesis, incorporating physical constraints.

However, a critical limitation in much of the prior art is a narrow focus on low-level grasp stability metrics. While achieving a stable grasp is essential, it often overlooks two crucial aspects:
1. **Human-like kinematics and naturalness:** Many learning-based policies, especially pure RL agents, can produce physically realistic but kinematically awkward or unnatural joint configurations. This lack of "human-likeness" can make robots seem less intuitive to interact with and might hinder complex multi-step tasks designed for human-centric manipulation.
2. **Affordance-aware positioning:** Grasping is not merely about holding an object but about preparing it for subsequent manipulation. Existing methods frequently neglect the functional context of an object, leading to grasps that, while stable, are functionally incorrect or unsafe (e.g., grasping a knife by its blade rather than its handle). Learning affordances—the action possibilities an object offers—is a complex challenge, often resulting in task- or category-specific models that don't generalize well.

"Towards Affordance-Aware Robotic Dexterous Grasping with Human-like Priors" directly addresses these two gaps. It positions itself as a significant advancement by moving beyond mere stable grasping to integrate human motion priors and an understanding of "negative affordances." By doing so, AffordDex aims to generate grasps that are not only successful and generalizable but also natural in motion and functionally appropriate for downstream tasks, thereby advancing the development of truly versatile embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, **AffordDex**, capable of generating universal dexterous grasping policies that inherently understand both **human-like motion priors** and **object affordances**. The overarching goal is to enable general-purpose embodied AI systems to perform dexterous manipulation more effectively and safely in real-world, multi-step scenarios.

The motivations driving this work stem from critical limitations identified in previous research:
- **Beyond Low-Level Stability:** Existing methods primarily optimize for grasp stability, a necessary but insufficient condition for practical manipulation. A stable grasp on a functionally inappropriate part (e.g., a knife blade) renders the grasp useless or even dangerous for subsequent tasks.
- **Lack of Human-like Poses:** Policies learned through pure reinforcement learning often yield kinematically unnatural or awkward hand postures, even if physically viable. Human-like movements are crucial for seamless human-robot interaction and for efficiently performing tasks often designed with human ergonomics in mind.
- **Neglect of Affordance-Aware Positioning:** Robots need to understand *where* to grasp an object based on its intended use or potential hazards. This involves reasoning about affordances (what actions an object permits) and, critically, **negative affordances** (what regions should be avoided). Prior affordance prediction models are often task- or category-specific, limiting generalization.
- **Facilitating Downstream Manipulation:** The ultimate goal of grasping is to enable further manipulation. By ensuring grasps are human-like and affordance-aware, AffordDex aims to pre-position the hand and object in a way that inherently simplifies subsequent manipulation steps, such as opening a bottle or cutting with a knife.

In summary, the paper is motivated by the need to bridge the gap between purely stable, but potentially unnatural or functionally incorrect, robotic grasps and the kind of intuitive, safe, and task-oriented grasps that humans naturally perform.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AffordDex proposes a novel two-stage training framework to achieve its objectives, synergistically combining human motion priors with functional affordance constraints.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Human Hand Trajectory Imitating

The first stage focuses on instilling a strong prior for natural, human-like movement into the robotic hand.
- **Objective:** To learn a base policy ($\pi_H$) that captures the kinematic priors of natural human hand motions.
- **Approach:** Imitation learning is employed to pre-train $\pi_H$ on a large corpus of human hand motion data (specifically, the OakInk2 dataset, Zhan et al. 2024). This training constrains the policy to operate within a manifold of natural, human-like movements.
- **State Representation:** The input state ($S_H^t$) for this policy includes the robot's current state ($R_t$), the object's state ($O_t$), and a point cloud representation of the object ($P_t$).
- **Reward Function ($r_H$):** A custom reward function promotes both precise imitation and motion stability:
 - **Finger Imitation Reward ($r_H^{\text{finger}}$):** Encourages the dexterous hand's keypoints to closely track reference positions from the human hand dataset. It uses an exponential decay based on the squared Euclidean distance between corresponding keypoints on the dexterous hand and the MANO hand model, with hierarchical weighting for base vs. middle joints.
 - **Smoothness Reward ($r_H^{\text{smooth}}$):** Penalizes excessive power consumption by taking the absolute element-wise product of joint velocities and applied torques, encouraging energy-efficient and hardware-friendly movements.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Affordance-Aware Residual Learning

In the second stage, the pre-trained human motion prior ($\pi_H$) is refined to adapt to specific object interactions, guided by an understanding of object affordances and enhanced by a teacher-student distillation process. The weights of $\pi_H$ are frozen during this stage, and a lightweight residual module is trained.

- **4.2.1. Negative Affordance-aware Segmentation (NAA) Module:**
 - **Objective:** To identify and segment functionally inappropriate contact regions (negative affordances) on objects in an open-vocabulary manner.
 - **Challenge:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP and MLLMs like GPT-4V excel at image-level understanding but struggle with fine-grained spatial localization on non-textured 3D meshes for segmentation.
 - **Approach:**
 1. **Procedural Texturing:** Raw 3D meshes are first procedurally textured using TexPainter (Zhang et al. 2024c) to generate semantically plausible textures, making them interpretable by VLMs.
 2. **Multi-view Rendering:** The textured object is rendered from six cardinal directions to create a multi-view image set ($I$), providing a holistic visual representation.
 3. **GPT-4V Query:** GPT-4V (Achiam et al. 2023) is queried with these multi-view images to elicit a detailed textual description of the object's negative affordances (e.g., "The blade of a knife should not be touched").
 4. **VLM-Guided Segmentation as Classification:** Instead of direct segmentation by VLMs, the task is reformulated as a classification problem:
 - **SAM Mask Generation:** For each image in $I$, Segment Anything Model (SAM) (Kirillov et al. 2023) is prompted with a dense grid of points, generating an exhaustive set of candidate object-part masks ($M_i$). Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) refines these masks.
 - **CLIP Similarity Scoring:** For each candidate mask, a visually prompted image (where regions outside the mask are blurred) is created. CLIP (Radford et al. 2021) then computes a semantic similarity score between this prompted image and the textual negative affordance description (from GPT-4V).
 - **Selection and Projection:** The mask with the highest similarity score is selected as the final negative affordance segmentation. This 2D mask is then projected into 3D space to segment the corresponding negative affordance point cloud ($N_t$) on the object.
 - **Efficiency:** This NAA process is performed offline and once per object, taking about 160 seconds on an RTX 4090, which is deemed a reasonable one-time trade-off.

- **4.2.2. Teacher-Student Distillation:**
 - **Objective:** To refine the base policy with affordance awareness and transfer this knowledge from a privileged state-based teacher to a robust vision-based student policy.
 - **State-based Teacher Policy ($\pi_T$):**
 - **Inputs:** The teacher policy accesses privileged information, including the robot state ($R_t$), ground-truth object state ($O_t$), scene point clouds ($P_t$), and the predicted negative affordance point cloud ($N_t$).
 - **Learning:** A lightweight residual module ($R$) is trained via PPO (Schulman et al. 2017) to learn residual actions ($\Delta a_t$) that refine the base policy's actions: $a_t = \pi_H(S_T^t) + \pi_T(S_T^t)$.
 - **Reward Function ($r_T$):** A comprehensive reward function guides $\pi_T$:
 - **Grasp Reward ($r_T^d$):** Penalizes distance between the hand and the object.
 - **Goal Reward ($r_T^g$):** Penalizes distance between the object and a target goal.
 - **Success Reward ($r_T^s$):** Provides a bonus when the object reaches the goal.
 - **Negative Affordance Reward ($r_T^n$):** Critically, penalizes dexterous hand fingertips for approaching points within the predicted negative affordance region ($N_t$). This directly incorporates safety and functional correctness.
 - **Vision-based Student Policy ($\pi_S$):**
 - **Objective:** To enable the policy to operate with real-world inputs (vision-only, no privileged object state).
 - **Inputs:** Robot state ($R_t$), scene point clouds ($P_t$), and predicted negative affordance ($N_t$).
 - **Distillation:** The proficient state-based teacher policy ($\pi_T$) is distilled into the vision-based student policy ($\pi_S$) using DAgger (Ross et al. 2011), an imitation learning algorithm. This process trains the student to mimic the teacher's actions given the vision-based inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AffordDex demonstrates significant advancements in dexterous grasping across quantitative metrics and qualitative observations, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons

The performance of AffordDex was evaluated on the UniDexGrasp dataset (Xu et al. 2023) and the OakInk2 dataset (Zhan et al. 2024), measuring:
- **Success Rate (Succ):** The percentage of successful grasps where the object reaches a target goal.
- **Human-likeness Score (HLS):** Assesses the anthropomorphic quality of the grasp motion, obtained by prompting Gemini 2.5 Pro to analyze video sequences based on motion trajectory, velocity smoothness, and joint coordination. A higher HLS is better.
- **Affordance Score (AS):** Evaluates the functional correctness by penalizing contact with inappropriate object parts (negative affordances). A lower AS is better, indicating better avoidance of negative regions.

**Key results (Table 1):**

- **Overall Superiority:** AffordDex consistently achieves the highest success rates across all evaluation categories (seen objects, unseen objects from seen categories, unseen objects from unseen categories, and objects from the OakInk2 dataset) in both state-based and vision-based settings. For instance, in the state-based setting, it achieved 89.2% Succ on seen objects compared to 87.9% for UniDexGrasp++.
- **Remarkable Human-likeness:** AffordDex dramatically improves the HLS. For seen objects (state-based), it scored 8.6, significantly higher than UniDexGrasp's 6.9 and UniDexGrasp++'s 5.4. This validates the effectiveness of the Human Hand Trajectory Imitating stage.
- **Functionally Appropriate Grasps:** The Affordance Score (AS) for AffordDex is significantly lower (better) than all baselines. For seen objects (state-based), it achieved an AS of 4, compared to UniDexGrasp's 12 and UniDexGrasp++'s 28. This demonstrates the success of the Negative Affordance-aware Segmentation module in guiding the policy to safe and functionally appropriate contact regions.
- **Generalization:** AffordDex maintains its strong performance across unseen instances and entirely novel categories, showcasing its robust generalization capabilities beyond training data.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Observations

Qualitative comparisons (Figures 1 and 4) further reinforce the quantitative findings:
- **Natural Postures:** AffordDex generates grasps that appear much more natural and human-like compared to baselines, which often exhibit kinematically awkward configurations.
- **Safe and Functional Grasps:** Crucially, AffordDex correctly identifies and avoids negative affordance regions. The prominent example of grasping a knife safely by its handle, rather than its blade, illustrates the practical utility and safety benefits of the NAA module.
- **Diverse Grasps:** The method produces a diverse set of grasps, indicating adaptability to various object geometries while adhering to human-like and functional constraints.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies (Table 2) confirm the critical contribution of each proposed component:
- **Human Hand Trajectory Imitating (HTI):** Without HTI, the HLS significantly decreases (e.g., from 8.6 to 5.2 for state-based seen objects), confirming its role in ensuring natural, human-like movements.
- **Negative Affordance-aware Segmentation (NAA):** Omitting NAA leads to a much higher AS (e.g., from 4 to 22), proving its effectiveness in guiding the policy away from inappropriate contact regions and generating functionally sound grasps. The NAA module's VLM-guided classification approach is shown to be superior to naive GPT+SAM combinations for fine-grained segmentation (Figure 7).
- **Teacher-Student Distillation:** Without distillation, the vision-based policy's success rate decreases, highlighting the importance of leveraging privileged information from the teacher to train a robust student policy for real-world (vision-only) deployment.

#### 4.4. Extension to Other Grasping Methods

The proposed modules, HTI and NAA, demonstrate strong generalizability and can enhance other RL-based methods. When integrated into UniDexGrasp++ (Table 3), HTI improved human-likeness (HLS) and NAA boosted affordance appropriateness (AS), showcasing the modularity and broad applicability of AffordDex's components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AffordDex framework represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic dexterous grasping, with profound implications for the development of general-purpose embodied AI and practical robotic applications.

#### 5.1. Scientific Contribution

- **Novel Two-Stage Framework:** The paper introduces an innovative two-stage learning paradigm that effectively decouples and then synergizes the challenges of learning human-like motion priors and object affordance understanding. This structured approach provides a robust and generalizable solution.
- **Negative Affordance-aware Segmentation (NAA) Module:** The NAA module is a key methodological contribution. By reformulating fine-grained segmentation as a VLM-guided classification problem, it robustly identifies functionally inappropriate contact regions in an open-vocabulary manner. This moves beyond task- or category-specific affordance learning, offering a more universal approach to safety and functional correctness.
- **Integration of Human Priors:** The explicit incorporation of human hand motion data to instill natural kinematic priors significantly advances the "human-likeness" of robotic grasps, a dimension often overlooked in favor of pure stability.
- **Robust Generalization:** The demonstrated ability to generalize across seen objects, unseen instances, and entirely novel categories underscores the framework's versatility and potential for real-world deployment.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact

- **Enhanced Robotic Dexterity for Embodied AI:** By enabling robots to grasp objects naturally and functionally, AffordDex moves closer to the vision of general-purpose embodied agents that can interact intelligently and safely in complex human environments. This is crucial for tasks ranging from domestic assistance to industrial manipulation.
- **Improved Downstream Manipulation:** Grasps that are both human-like and affordance-aware inherently facilitate subsequent manipulation tasks. For example, grasping a tool by its handle immediately prepares it for use, reducing the need for re-grasping or complex re-orientation planning. This directly improves the efficiency and success rate of multi-step robotic tasks.
- **Increased Safety and Trust:** The explicit avoidance of negative affordances (e.g., sharp edges, hot surfaces) makes robotic manipulation inherently safer, both for the robot and for human co-workers. Natural, intuitive movements also foster greater trust and acceptance of robots in shared spaces.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's generalizability and vision-based policy design lay a crucial foundation for sim-to-real transfer, paving the way for deploying these advanced grasping capabilities on physical robots in diverse, unstructured settings. The modularity of HTI and NAA also allows for their integration into other existing grasping methods, boosting their performance.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge a limitation stemming from the NAA module's reliance on a fixed set of six rendered views for negative affordance prediction. This multi-view approach can sometimes fail to capture all functionally relevant parts on geometrically complex or concave objects due to occlusion, leading to imprecise segmentation.

Future work could address this by adopting volumetric-based affordance learning using implicit 3D representations. Such an approach would be inherently robust to viewpoint-specific occlusions, providing a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of object geometry and affordances. Furthermore, directly validating the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of AffordDex on physical dexterous robot hands would be a crucial next step towards real-world deployment.
