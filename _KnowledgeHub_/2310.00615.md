# Scene-aware Human Motion Forecasting via Mutual Distance Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> This work forecasts scene-aware 3D human motion by predicting a mutual distance representation: per-vertex signed distances from sampled body vertices to the scene surface, plus per-basis-point distances from fixed scene-independent points to the body surface. A GCN/DCT module predicts future distances, an RNN then predicts poses under consistency losses, with the scene encoded from an SDF volume, beating prior methods on four datasets.

## Problem

- Implicit scene encodings such as 2D images, point clouds, or single-object graphs give no explicit interaction constraint, so predicted motions show ghost motion and scene penetration artifacts.
- Explicit representations like human-scene proximity or eye gaze constrain only the global position or goal location, leaving local whole-body pose entirely unconstrained.
- Contact maps constrain only joints touching the scene, so free limbs like elbows remain unsupervised, and prior basis points were partly scene-dependent, hurting motion patterns.

## Method

- ==Mutual distance representation==: per-vertex signed distance from 67 sampled body vertices to the scene surface constrains whole-body pose, and its sign flags penetration inside the scene volume.
- Per-basis-point distance from 150 scene-independent points, sampled by ==spherical Fibonacci lattice== on a 2 m sphere anchored at the last observed root joint, resolves global-position ambiguity.
- Two-stage pipeline: ==DCT== plus ==GCN== residual predictor forecasts future distances from an ==SDF-volume== 3D CNN encoding, then an autoregressive ==RNN== predicts poses with vertex and basis consistency losses, followed by end-to-end fine-tuning.

## Results

- On GTA-IM mean path error drops to **72.0 mm** versus **83.4 mm** for the staged contact baseline; on PROX it reaches **162.6 mm** versus **189.6 mm**, with pose error **110.7 mm**.
- Given ground-truth interaction signals, mean GTA-IM path error is **22.4 mm** versus **40.0 mm** for ground-truth contact maps, and pose error **17.6 mm** versus **35.1 mm**.
- Ablations on HUMANISE seen scenes: **57.0 mm** motion-only and **52.6 mm** scene-only fall to **45.6 mm** with both distances; SDF beats point cloud **49.3**, occupancy **55.3**, BPS encoding **53.9**, end-to-end **51.5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interaction representations should constrain every body vertex, not just contacting joints, because dense signed distances supervise limbs that never touch the scene surface.
> - Signed distance alone is ambiguous across global placements, so pairing a body-centric field with a scene-centric one disambiguates local pose from global translation.
> - Choosing a scene encoding consistent with the supervision signal matters: SDF volumes beat point cloud and occupancy encodings, and staged training avoids the local minimum end-to-end training falls into.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Human motion forecasting is a research area with applications in domains such as autonomous driving, human-robot interaction, animation, and virtual/augmented reality. Traditional approaches to human motion prediction have primarily focused on modeling human motion in isolation, often neglecting the surrounding 3D environment. This oversight can lead to predicted motions that are unrealistic or inconsistent with the physical constraints of a scene, such as human limbs penetrating objects or standing in mid-air ("ghost motion").

More recent research efforts have begun to address "scene-aware" human motion prediction by incorporating scene context. These approaches can be broadly categorized into two types: implicit and explicit scene-human interaction modeling. Implicit methods integrate scene information (e.g., 2D scene images, 3D point clouds, or specific object encodings) as additional input, allowing the model to learn interactions without direct constraints. However, such methods often struggle with generating physically plausible motions and can still produce artifacts. Explicit methods, in contrast, attempt to represent human-scene interaction directly through specific constraints. Examples include using global proximity between human and scene, human gaze direction to infer target locations, or contact maps that specify interactions between a subset of human joints and the scene surface.

The present work identifies a limitation in existing explicit interaction models: they often constrain only partial aspects of human motion (e.g., global position, a few contact points) while leaving other parts of the body unconstrained. This can still result in unrealistic local poses or interactions. This paper positions itself within the explicit scene-aware human motion prediction landscape by proposing a novel, more comprehensive explicit representation of human-scene interaction designed to constrain the *entire body's motion* in both local and global contexts. By aiming to provide whole-body constraints, this research seeks to improve the realism and physical plausibility of forecasted human movements within complex 3D environments, thus advancing the state-of-the-art in scene-aware motion forecasting. It also draws parallels and distinctions with scene-aware human *synthesis* methods, noting that while synthesis focuses on generating static poses in scenes, forecasting requires dynamic interaction modeling over time.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to accurately forecast future 3D human motions that are coherent and physically plausible within a given 3D scene. This entails predicting not only the kinematic configuration of the human body (pose) but also its global translation and orientation relative to the environment, while ensuring it interacts realistically with scene geometry.

The motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in current scene-aware human motion forecasting techniques:
1. **Incomplete Human-Scene Interaction Modeling**: Existing methods that incorporate scene context often provide only implicit or partial explicit constraints on human-scene interactions. Implicit methods, which feed scene features to a model, may fail to guarantee valid interactions, leading to artifacts like "ghost motion" where the human body appears to float or penetrate scene objects.
2. **Limited Scope of Explicit Constraints**: While some works have explored explicit representations of human-scene interaction (e.g., global proximity, gaze, contact maps), these representations typically constrain only specific aspects of human motion. For instance, global proximity may only guide the overall human position, while contact maps might only provide detailed constraints for joints that are explicitly in contact with the scene, leaving the majority of the body unconstrained. This partial constraint can still result in unrealistic local poses or movements of non-contacting body parts (e.g., an unsupported elbow).
3. **Ambiguity in Global Pose Estimation**: Representations like per-vertex signed distances (which describe how close each body part is to the scene) can be ambiguous. The same per-vertex distances might correspond to a human mesh at multiple different global locations within a scene, making it challenging to predict unique and accurate global motion.

To address these motivations, the authors propose the following key objectives:
- **Develop a whole-body interaction representation**: Introduce a representation capable of simultaneously constraining both the local poses of the human body and its global motion in relation to the scene. This aims to prevent ghost motion and ensure all body parts move plausibly.
- **Resolve global pose ambiguity**: Supplement the local constraints with an additional mechanism that helps disambiguate global human positions that might otherwise appear similar based on local body-scene interactions alone.
- **Leverage efficient scene representation**: Explore and utilize a scene representation (Signed Distance Function volume) that is consistent with the proposed interaction representation and facilitates efficient computations, particularly for loss calculations during training.
- **Design a robust forecasting pipeline**: Construct a multi-stage prediction pipeline that effectively integrates the new interaction representation to forecast future human motion, ensuring consistency between the predicted interactions and the final predicted poses.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to significantly improve the realism, accuracy, and physical plausibility of 3D human motion predictions within diverse and complex 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed methodology addresses scene-aware 3D human motion forecasting by introducing a novel "mutual distance" representation of human-scene interaction and a two-step prediction pipeline.

**Problem Formulation**:
The task is to predict `U` future human poses `Y = [x_{T+1}, ..., x_{T+U}]` given `T` past human poses `X = [x_1, ..., x_T]` and a 3D scene `S`. Each pose `x_t` is a parametric representation (e.g., SMPL-X). The 3D scene `S` is represented as a Signed Distance Volume (SDF), where each voxel stores the signed distance to the nearest scene surface. This SDF representation is chosen for its consistency with the mutual distance representation and for efficient querying of signed distances.

**Mutual Distance Representation**:
This is a central contribution, designed to provide comprehensive constraints on whole-body motion. It comprises two components:

1. **Per-vertex Signed Distance (`d_t`)**:
 - For a given human pose `x_t`, a subset of `K` vertices `v_t` from the human mesh is selected.
 - `d_{kt}` is the minimum Euclidean distance from each `k`-th selected human vertex `v_{tk}` to the scene surface `∂S`.
 - The sign of `d_{kt}` indicates whether `v_{tk}` is inside (negative) or outside (positive) the scene volume `S`.
 - This component provides information about the detailed local pose of the whole body relative to the scene, including potential penetration.
 - **Limitation addressed**: By itself, it can be ambiguous as different global human positions might yield the same per-vertex signed distances.

2. **Per-basis Point Distance (`b_t`)**:
 - A set of `P` scene-independent basis points `p ∈ R^{P×3}` is defined. These points are sampled from a sphere centered at the root joint of the last observed pose, making them relative to the human's immediate environment rather than fixed scene points.
 - `b_{pt}` is the minimum Euclidean distance from each `p`-th basis point `p_p` to the human surface `∂H_t`.
 - This component primarily captures the global location and proximity of the human relative to its surroundings, addressing the ambiguity of the per-vertex signed distance.
 - **Comparison to prior work**: Differs from methods like PLACE by using scene-independent basis points, making it more suitable for modeling continuous motion.

By combining these two distances, the mutual distance representation (`D` for per-vertex, `B` for per-basis point) explicitly constrains both detailed local body poses and global human motion.

**Two-Step Prediction Pipeline**:
The model follows a sequential prediction process:

- **Step 1: Mutual Distance Prediction Module**:
 - **Input**: Past mutual distances (`D = [d_1, ..., d_T]`, `B = [b_1, ..., b_T]`), past human motion `X`, and the 3D scene `S`.
 - **Temporal Encoding**: Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is applied to the time series of each distance component (individual vertex distances and individual basis point distances). This transforms the sequence into a set of DCT coefficients, which are effective for representing smooth temporal data.
 - **Spatial Encoding**: Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) are used to capture spatial dependencies between different distance sequences (e.g., between different vertices or different basis points).
 - **Scene Encoding**: A 3D CNN (`G_s`) processes the SDF volume `S` to extract scene features.
 - **Motion Encoding**: A model (`G_x`) encodes past human motion `X`.
 - **Prediction**: GCN-based predictors (`G_d`, `G_b`) predict residual DCT coefficients, which are then inverse-transformed to obtain the future per-vertex signed distances (`ˆD`) and per-basis point distances (`ˆB`). The prediction covers the entire `T+U` sequence (past and future).
 - **Loss Function**: `ℓ_{dist}` calculates the average ℓ1 distance between the predicted (`ˆd_{kt}`, `ˆb_{pt}`) and ground-truth (`d_{kt}`, `b_{pt}`) mutual distances over all `T+U` frames and all vertices/basis points.

- **Step 2: Motion Forecasting Network**:
 - **Input**: Predicted future mutual distances (`ˆD`, `ˆB`), past human motion `X`, and the 3D scene `S`.
 - **Model**: An RNN-based auto-regressive model (`G_y`) is used to predict future human poses. It iteratively predicts the next pose `ˆx_{T+u}` based on the previous predicted pose `ˆx_{T+u-1}`, the corresponding predicted future mutual distances `ˆd_{T+u}` and `ˆb_{T+u}`, and encoded past motion (`G_x(X)`) and scene (`G_s(S)`).
 - **Loss Functions**: To ensure the predicted motion `ˆY` is consistent with the scene and the predicted mutual distances, several losses are combined:
 - `ℓ_{global}`: ℓ1 loss on global translation (`ˆt`) and orientation (`ˆr`) of the root joint.
 - `ℓ_{local}`: ℓ1 loss on local human pose parameters (excluding global translation/orientation).
 - `ℓ_{vertex}`: Consistency loss ensuring the per-vertex signed distances derived from the *predicted* human mesh (`˜d`) match the *predicted* mutual distance `ˆD`. This is computed by querying the SDF volume with the vertices of the predicted human mesh.
 - `ℓ_{basis}`: Consistency loss ensuring the per-basis point distances derived from the *predicted* human mesh (`˜b`) match the *predicted* mutual distance `ˆB`.
 - **Total Loss**: `ℓ_{motion}` is a weighted sum of `ℓ_{global}`, `ℓ_{local}`, `ℓ_{vertex}`, and `ℓ_{basis}`.

**Training Strategy**:
The networks are trained in a stage-wise manner initially for better convergence. The mutual distance prediction network and the motion forecasting network are trained separately. During the initial stage of motion forecasting training, ground truth mutual distances are used as input. After separate training, both networks are combined and fine-tuned end-to-end with the combined loss functions (`ℓ_{dist}` and `ℓ_{motion}`). This prevents the motion forecasting network from being misled by inaccurate distance predictions in early training phases.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research evaluates the proposed method on four datasets: GTA-IM, PROX, HUMANISE, and GIMO, and compares its performance against several state-of-the-art scene-aware human motion prediction baselines: ContAware, GIMO, and STAG.

**Quantitative Results**:
- **Overall Superiority**: The method consistently outperforms all baseline approaches across GTA-IM (synthetic), PROX (real-world), and HUMANISE (synthetic with seen/unseen scenes) datasets. This superiority is observed for both path error (L2 distance of translation) and pose error (Mean Per Joint Position Error, MPJPE) at various future prediction time steps (0.5s, 1.0s, 1.5s, 2.0s).
- **Path Prediction**: Particularly strong performance in path prediction, especially evident on the real-world PROX dataset, where the proposed method significantly reduces path errors compared to baselines.
- **Ground Truth Comparison**: When provided with ground truth mutual distances ("Ours w/GT"), the method achieves substantially lower path and pose errors than baselines provided with ground truth contact maps ("ContAware w/GT", "STAG w/GT"). This indicates that the proposed mutual distance representation is more effective in constraining human motion than prior contact map approaches.
- **Performance on HUMANISE (Seen/Unseen Scenes)**: The method maintains its leading performance on both seen scenes (scenes observed during training) and unseen scenes (scenes not observed during training), suggesting a degree of generalization capability.

**Qualitative Results**:
- Visual comparisons (Fig. 3) illustrate that the proposed method's predictions align more closely with the ground truth human motions compared to baselines.
- Baseline models, such as GIMO, are shown to exhibit artifacts like motion freezing or scene penetration. ContAware and STAG, while improved over pure motion-only models, still struggle with complex motions, suggesting their constraints are less comprehensive.

**Ablation Study (on HUMANISE dataset)**:
The ablation study systematically analyzes the contribution of different components of the proposed method:
- **Contribution of Mutual Distance**: Models trained without scene context ("Motion only") or with scene context but no mutual distance ("Scene only") show significantly higher errors in both path and pose, confirming the critical role of explicitly modeling human-scene interaction.
- **Individual Mutual Distance Components**: Both the per-vertex signed distance (`D`) and the per-basis point distance (`B`) individually contribute to improved performance. The greatest gains are achieved when both components are utilized, confirming their complementary roles in providing comprehensive whole-body and global constraints.
- **Scene Representation**: The use of SDF volume for global scene representation ("Ours") outperforms alternatives such as point cloud representation ("Ours w/ P") and occupancy representation ("Ours w/ O") across all metrics.
- **Basis Point Set Comparison**: The proposed scene-independent per-basis point distance ("Ours") yields better results than the Basis Point Set (BPS) encoding from PLACE ("Ours w/ BP S"), which uses partially scene-dependent basis points.
- **Training Strategy**: End-to-end training ("Ours e2e") without the initial stage-wise pre-training leads to worse performance, supporting the necessity of the proposed two-step training strategy to avoid local minima and improve convergence.
- Qualitative ablations (Fig. 4) visually demonstrate that mutual distance prediction helps forecast actions more accurately, such as a "stand-up" motion, by providing more effective constraints.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the proposed mutual distance representation, combined with an SDF volume scene representation and a two-step prediction pipeline, results in more accurate and plausible scene-aware human motion forecasts compared to existing methods.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of human motion forecasting and has potential impacts across various application domains.

**Advancement in Scene-Aware Motion Forecasting**: The primary significance lies in effectively addressing the challenge of predicting physically coherent human motions in complex 3D environments. By introducing a comprehensive explicit representation of human-scene interaction, the work mitigates common issues such as scene penetration and unrealistic body postures, which have limited the applicability of prior methods.

**Novel Interaction Representation**: The proposed "mutual distance" representation, comprising per-vertex signed distances and per-basis point distances, offers a robust and detailed way to encode human-scene interactions. This representation constrains both the local pose of the entire human body and its global translation and orientation relative to the scene, providing a more complete and less ambiguous interaction signal than previously explored methods. This methodological innovation could influence future research in interaction modeling for various human-centric computer vision tasks.

**Improved Accuracy and Realism**: The consistent and substantial performance improvements over state-of-the-art baselines across multiple datasets (both synthetic and real-world) demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed approach. The enhanced accuracy in path and pose prediction leads to more realistic and plausible forecasted motions, which is crucial for downstream applications.

**Robustness and Generalization**: The method's strong performance on unseen scenes in the HUMANISE dataset suggests a degree of generalization capability, indicating it can adapt to new environments beyond those seen during training. This is an important characteristic for real-world deployment.

**Methodological Contributions**: The adoption of an SDF volume for scene representation offers consistency with the mutual distance concept and allows for efficient querying, simplifying the calculation of consistency losses. The two-stage training strategy followed by fine-tuning also addresses convergence challenges often encountered in complex deep learning models, providing a practical blueprint for similar sequential prediction tasks.

**Potential Impact and Applications**:
- **Autonomous Driving**: More accurate prediction of pedestrian movements and intentions in complex urban environments can enhance safety and decision-making for self-driving vehicles.
- **Human-Robot Interaction**: Robots operating in shared spaces with humans can benefit from anticipating human actions, leading to more natural, safe, and efficient collaborative tasks.
- **Animation and Virtual/Augmented Reality**: The ability to generate realistic human motions that interact plausibly with digital environments can significantly enhance the immersion and quality of virtual characters in games, films, and AR/VR experiences.
- **Ergonomics and Design**: Understanding how humans interact with environments could inform the design of safer and more comfortable spaces and products.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The authors acknowledge a key limitation: the performance of motion prediction is reliant on the accuracy of the predicted mutual distances. Improving the mutual distance prediction module is identified as a direct avenue for future research. Another significant consideration, particularly for real-world deployments, is the absence of explicit physics modeling. Without considering factors like imbalanced forces, the model might predict motions that are kinetically unstable or lead to collisions in physical systems, despite appearing geometrically plausible. Integrating real-world physics constraints could be a crucial next step for deploying such models in safety-critical human-robot interaction or assistive technologies.
