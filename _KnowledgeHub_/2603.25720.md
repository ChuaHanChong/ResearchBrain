# R-C2: Cycle-Consistent Reinforcement Learning Improves Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The R-C^2 framework introduces cycle-consistent reinforcement learning to enhance multimodal reasoning in MLLMs by transforming cross-modal inconsistencies into a self-supervised reward signal. This approach leads to improved accuracy and significantly higher consistency in predictions across different data modalities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently exhibit a 'modality gap,' producing inconsistent predictions when processing the same information presented in different forms (e.g., image vs. text).
- Improving MLLMs traditionally relies on expensive, human-curated datasets for fine-tuning or struggles to define reliable reward signals for reinforcement learning in complex multimodal reasoning tasks.
- Existing self-improvement methods, such as majority voting, often fail in multimodal contexts due to prevalent disagreements between modalities, potentially reinforcing incorrect systematic biases.

## Method

- R-C^2 proposes a reinforcement learning framework that re-conceptualizes ==cross-modal inconsistency== as a valuable, label-free signal for self-supervised learning, moving from answer aggregation to answer verification.
- The core mechanism involves a multimodal cycle consistency check: a candidate answer is used to perform backward inference to reconstruct queries across modalities, which are then used for forward inference to reconstruct the original answer.
- Binary rewards are generated based on whether the reconstructed answers match the original candidate, enforcing consistency both within a single modality and across different modalities (e.g., text-to-image and image-to-text).

## Results

- R-C^2 consistently improved multimodal reasoning accuracy across seven benchmarks, achieving gains of up to +7.8 points in text accuracy and +7.3 points in vision accuracy on ScienceQA for the Qwen2.5-VL-**3B** model.
- The framework substantially increased the multimodal consistency ratio, demonstrating improvements of up to +12.5 points on A-OKVQA and +10.0 points on ScienceQA for the Qwen2.5-VL-**3B** model.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining both intra-modal (e.g., text-to-text) and inter-modal (e.g., text-to-image) cycle paths is crucial for optimal performance, and the method's self-bootstrapping capability allows it to use self-generated answers effectively for training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-modal inconsistency should be leveraged as a self-supervised signal for MLLM improvement, rather than being masked or ignored.
> - Enforcing cycle consistency across modalities forces the model to align its internal semantic representations, thereby mitigating the 'modality gap' without requiring external ground-truth labels.
> - Training with data exhibiting higher cross-modal disagreement acts as a form of 'hard example' mining, leading to more robust reasoning and tighter cross-modal alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has progressed significantly, enabling these models to process and reason across different data modalities such as text and images. MLLMs are increasingly deployed in applications requiring complex understanding, including multimodal document analysis, web user interface navigation, and autonomous agent systems. However, a prevalent challenge in these models is the "modality gap," where MLLMs frequently generate contradictory predictions when presented with the same underlying information in different forms—for instance, an identical webpage rendered as a screenshot versus its raw HTML source. This inconsistency can lead to a lack of robustness and reliability, which is critical in real-world deployments.

Previous research efforts to enhance MLLM reasoning have largely focused on two main strategies. One approach involves extensive fine-tuning on large, meticulously curated datasets. While effective to some extent, this method is resource-intensive, expensive to scale, and can be limited by the inherent biases and coverage of the datasets. Another avenue involves the application of reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. RL typically relies on external reward signals to guide model improvement. In domains with objectively verifiable answers, such as mathematics or code generation, constructing reliable reward functions is feasible. However, for complex multimodal reasoning tasks, obtaining definitive "correct" answers or granular step-level evaluations without human annotation is challenging, limiting the applicability of standard RL.

In response to the difficulty of acquiring ground-truth labels for complex multimodal answers, some self-improvement methods have emerged. These often utilize consensus mechanisms, such as majority voting, where a model generates multiple candidate answers and selects the most frequent one as a pseudo-label for training. However, these voting mechanisms present limitations. In multimodal contexts, when predictions from different modalities frequently disagree, simple majority voting can become unstable or arbitrary. This does not resolve the underlying conflict between modalities and can, in certain scenarios (termed "majority-is-wrong" cases), inadvertently reinforce incorrect systematic biases present in the model. The current work positions itself as an alternative to these approaches by proposing that cross-modal inconsistency itself is not merely a failure to be masked but a valuable signal for self-supervised learning, enabling MLLMs to autonomously align their internal representations without external labels or reliance on potentially flawed consensus.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the multimodal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs by addressing the inherent "modality gap" that causes inconsistent predictions across different sensory inputs. The authors aim to improve both the accuracy of reasoning and the cross-modal consistency of MLLM outputs.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations regarding the limitations of current MLLMs and existing training paradigms:
- **Modality-Invariant Failure**: MLLMs frequently fail to produce consistent answers for identical semantic content when it is presented in different modalities (e.g., as an image versus as text). This "modal-invariance" is crucial for robust perception and reasoning, and its absence leads to unreliable model behavior in practical applications. The paper highlights scenarios where MLLMs yield different answers for the same webpage when presented as a screenshot versus its raw HTML source.
- **Limitations of Data-Centric Approaches**: Traditional methods for improving MLLMs often depend on large-scale fine-tuning with expensive, human-curated datasets. These datasets are costly to construct and inherently limited in their capacity to scale model performance across the vast and varied landscape of multimodal reasoning tasks. The authors seek a more scalable and label-efficient alternative.
- **Challenges in Reward Signal Generation for RL**: While reinforcement learning offers a promising avenue for model improvement, its application to complex multimodal reasoning is hampered by the difficulty of obtaining reliable reward signals. Unlike domains such as mathematics or code, where correctness can be objectively verified, complex multimodal answers often lack clear, verifiable ground truth, making outcome-based rewards difficult to define and scale.
- **Flaws of Consensus-Based Self-Improvement**: Recent self-improvement techniques for RL aim to mitigate the need for human labels by using internal feedback, often relying on majority voting among multiple model rollouts to generate pseudo-labels. However, the paper argues that this approach is fundamentally flawed, especially in multimodal settings. When visual and textual predictions conflict (a common occurrence), majority voting can lead to unstable or arbitrary consensus, failing to resolve the underlying discrepancies and potentially amplifying systematic errors ("majority-is-wrong" failures).

Given these challenges, the core motivation is to transform cross-modal inconsistency from a perceived failure into a valuable, untapped resource for self-reward learning. By reframing this inconsistency as a signal, the authors propose a novel framework that encourages the model to autonomously identify, confront, and resolve its own internal multimodal conflicts, thereby driving improvements in reasoning and alignment without reliance on external annotations or unstable voting mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces R-C$^2$, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to improve multimodal reasoning by enforcing cross-modal cycle consistency. This approach aims to address the modality gap in MLLMs by leveraging internal consistency as a self-supervised reward signal.

**A. Framework Overview**
The core idea behind R-C$^2$ is to shift from answer-side voting to answer-side verification. Instead of starting with a query and aggregating conflicting answers, the framework begins with a candidate answer ($a_{orig}$) and assesses its logical self-consistency across modalities. This process involves a backward-forward inference cycle.

**B. Multimodal Cycle Consistency Mechanism**
As illustrated in the paper, given a candidate answer $a_{orig}$ and a sample with synchronized textual ($x_T$) and visual ($x_I$) views, R-C$^2$ evaluates its stability through four distinct reasoning paths:

1. **Backward Inference**: The MLLM performs backward inference to reconstruct two potential queries that could have elicited $a_{orig}$:
 - $\hat{q}_T$: A query inferred when conditioning on $a_{orig}$ and the textual view ($x_T$).
 - $\hat{q}_I$: A query inferred when conditioning on $a_{orig}$ and the visual view ($x_I$).
 This step effectively generates synthetic query-answer pairs reflecting the model’s internal understanding.

2. **Forward Inference (Cross-Modal Verification)**: Each reconstructed query ($\hat{q}_T$, $\hat{q}_I$) is then used to perform forward inference *across both modalities* to reconstruct the original answer. This results in four reconstructed answers:
 - $a_{tt}$: Original query from Text, forward inference via Text.
 - $a_{ti}$: Original query from Text, forward inference via Image.
 - $a_{it}$: Original query from Image, forward inference via Text.
 - $a_{ii}$: Original query from Image, forward inference via Image.

3. **Reward Generation**: For each of these four paths, a binary reward is generated:
 - $r = 1$ if the reconstructed answer ($a_{tt}$, $a_{ti}$, $a_{it}$, or $a_{ii}$) matches the original candidate answer $a_{orig}$.
 - $r = 0$ otherwise.

These cycle-consistency rewards are dense and label-free, removing the need for human-annotated query-answer pairs. The "same-modality" cycles (T→T, I→I) ensure internal stability of reasoning within each modality, while the "cross-modality" cycles (T→I, I→T) compel the model to resolve its modality gap and align semantic representations between modalities. This comprehensive 4-way evaluation provides a robust signal for learning.

**C. Training Pipeline**
The R-C$^2$ training pipeline follows an offline strategy, where the entire synthetic cycle dataset is pre-generated before the RL training begins for efficiency. It comprises three stages:

0. **Multimodal Data Preparation**: For datasets that natively provide aligned visual and textual views (e.g., webpages and their HTML), these are used directly. For image-only datasets, the MLLM itself is prompted to generate a semantic textual description, ensuring every sample has a coherent multimodal representation for the cycle.

1. **Constructing Cycles from Candidate Answers**: Training initiates by sampling candidate answers. These can be either predictions from the model's current policy (self-generated) or reference answers from a dataset (without requiring a labeled query-answer pair). For each candidate answer, the MLLM performs backward inference (conditioned on either the image or text modality) to infer a probable query. These inferred (query, answer) pairs form the synthetic data for cycle verification.

2. **Reinforcement Learning with Cycle Rewards**: The inferred query is then used for a forward pass (in the same or alternate modality) to obtain a reconstructed answer. A comparison between this reconstructed answer and the original candidate answer yields the binary cycle-consistency reward. This reward is then utilized in a GRPO (Generalized Policy Gradient) objective to update the MLLM's parameters, aligning the model towards responses that exhibit higher cycle consistency. The objective is formalized as $L_{GRPO} = E_h \log \pi_{\theta}(\hat{a}_i|x_i, q_i) \cdot \hat{A}(\hat{a}_i, a_i)$, where $\hat{A}$ is the normalized advantage based on the reward $r_i$.

**D. Implementation Details**
The authors utilized Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct as base MLLMs. Training employed a learning rate of 1e-6, a weight decay of 1e-2, and a KL penalty coefficient of 1e-2. During each update step, the model generated four rollouts per modality with a temperature of 1.0 and top-p of 0.95. Batch sizes varied (256 for long-input datasets, 1024 for others), with training typically lasting 100-150 steps, incorporating validation for early stopping.

**E. Datasets**
Evaluation was conducted across seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks: ScienceQA, ChartQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, A-OKVQA, MathVista, and a repurposed subset of Visual Web Arena (VWA) adapted into a multiple-choice question-answering task for consistent evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of R-C$^2$ demonstrated improvements across various multimodal reasoning benchmarks and MLLM scales.

**A. Multimodal Reasoning Accuracy**
R-C$^2$ consistently outperformed baseline models and majority-voting self-improvement methods across all evaluated datasets, utilizing both Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct models (Table 1).
- **Significant Gains**: On the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, R-C$^2$ achieved notable increases in both text and vision accuracy. For instance, on ScienceQA, it showed an improvement of up to +7.8 points in text accuracy and +7.3 points in vision accuracy. Similar gains were observed on ChartQA (+6.1/+2.0 points), MathVista (+6.0/+2.8 points), and A-OKVQA (+4.9/+2.7 points). The complex Visual Web Arena (VWA) benchmark also registered substantial improvements of +5.5 points in text accuracy and +4.2 points in vision accuracy.
- **Consistency Across Modalities**: The improvements extended to DocVQA and InfoVQA, confirming that the cross-modal reward benefits both modalities. The performance gains are attributed to R-C$^2$'s ability to resolve inter-modal conflicts and stabilize reasoning steps, which voting baselines frequently fail to address effectively due to inherent disagreements between modalities.

**B. Multimodal Consistency Ratio**
Beyond accuracy, R-C$^2$ significantly enhanced the consistency of predictions between image and text modalities (Table 2). The consistency ratio, defined as the proportion of samples where image- and text-based predictions agree, increased substantially:
- **Large Consistency Improvements**: For the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, consistency improvements reached up to +12.5 points on A-OKVQA, +10.0 points on ScienceQA, and +6.1 points on ChartQA.
- **Scalability with Model Size**: Even for the larger Qwen3-VL-8B model, R-C$^2$ continued to improve consistency by approximately 3-5 points across benchmarks. This indicates that the R-C$^2$ framework complements model scaling and remains effective as baseline accuracy increases, suggesting a deeper alignment of semantic interpretations across modalities.

**C. Qualitative Examples**
Visual comparisons presented in the paper (Figure 5) illustrate that R-C$^2$ yields answers that are both correct and consistent across modalities. In contrast, the base model and voting baselines often produced conflicting answers between modalities or exhibited spurious agreements on incorrect predictions, highlighting the efficacy of R-C$^2$ in mitigating the modality gap.

**D. Ablation Studies and Analysis**
Further analyses provided insights into the framework's design choices:
- **Importance of Multimodal Interaction (Table 3)**: An ablation study comparing single-modality cycles (T→T, I→I), cross-modality cycles (T→I, I→T), and a combination of both (Mixed) revealed that incorporating cross-modal interaction consistently outperformed single-modality baselines. The "Mixed" configuration, leveraging all four paths, yielded the highest accuracy and consistency, indicating that both intra-modal stability and inter-modal alignment are complementary and beneficial.
- **Ablation Study of Cycle Paths (Figure 6)**: Analysis of specific cycle paths showed that single-path supervision was insufficient, leading to lower performance. The incorporation of cross-modal paths (e.g., Text→Image) improved consistency by forcing the model to align semantic representations. The optimal performance was achieved when utilizing the full union of all cycle paths (Image + Text for both backward and forward inference), demonstrating that diverse cycle constraints enhance robustness.
- **Impact of Cross-Modal Disagreement Data (Figure 7)**: The study found that training on data with a higher ratio of cross-modal inconsistency (ranging from 0% to 50%) systematically improved both accuracy and consistency on ScienceQA. This suggests that exposing the model to more challenging, conflicting examples acts as a form of "hard example" mining, prompting the RL process to achieve tighter cross-modal alignment and more robust reasoning.
- **Source of Candidate Answers (Table 4)**: The research investigated whether the candidate answers used to initiate the cycle needed to be external or could be self-generated by the model. Results showed that using the model's own self-generated answers achieved performance comparable to or better than using dataset reference answers on web understanding tasks like VWA and A-OKVQA. This finding demonstrates the self-bootstrapping capability of R-C$^2$, implying its potential for scalable, label-free multimodal reinforcement learning without relying on externally provided ground truth.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "R-C$^2$: Cycle-Consistent Reinforcement Learning Improves Multimodal Reasoning" offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI.

**A. Redefining Cross-Modal Inconsistency**: The work re-conceptualizes cross-modal inconsistency not as a mere failure or a problem to be masked by aggregation methods like voting, but as a valuable and rich signal for self-supervised learning. This paradigm shift could influence future research directions in multimodal learning, moving towards frameworks that actively leverage internal model disagreements for improvement.

**B. Enhanced MLLM Robustness and Consistency**: By enforcing cycle consistency, R-C$^2$ directly addresses the fundamental modality gap in MLLMs. The demonstrated improvements in both reasoning accuracy and cross-modal consistency indicate that models trained with this framework are more reliable and produce more coherent outputs across different sensory inputs. This is crucial for building trustworthy AI systems that operate in complex, real-world environments where information can be presented in varied forms.

**C. Scalable and Label-Free Self-Supervision**: A key advantage of R-C$^2$ is its ability to generate dense, label-free reward signals. This significantly reduces the reliance on expensive, time-consuming human annotations or potentially biased synthetic data generation pipelines, which are common bottlenecks in training large multimodal models. The self-bootstrapping capability, where the model can generate its own candidate answers to initiate the consistency cycle, further enhances its scalability and autonomy. This makes R-C$^2$ a promising approach for developing advanced MLLMs without the prohibitive data acquisition costs typically associated with large-scale model training.

**D. Structural Consistency for Advanced Reasoning**: The findings suggest that advanced reasoning capabilities in MLLMs may emerge not solely from scaling up data volume, but from enforcing a deeper, structurally consistent understanding of the world. By compelling models to align their internal representations bidirectionally across modalities, R-C$^2$ encourages a more robust and grounded form of intelligence. This insight could guide the architectural design and training objectives for the next generation of multimodal foundation models.

**E. Practical Applications**: The improvements in multimodal reasoning and consistency have direct implications for a range of practical applications. For instance, in multimodal document understanding, agents could more reliably extract information from complex layouts that combine text, images, and charts. In web navigation, MLLMs could more accurately interpret web pages regardless of whether they are presented visually or as raw HTML, leading to more robust automated agents. Overall, R-C$^2$ contributes a methodology for developing more capable, dependable, and self-improving multimodal AI systems.
