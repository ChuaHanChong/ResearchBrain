# FunGrasp: Functional Grasping for Diverse Dexterous Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> FunGrasp introduces an end-to-end system for enabling dexterous robot hands to perform functional grasping from a single human demonstration. The system achieved a 74% real-world success rate for functional grasps on unseen objects and generalized across diverse robot hand morphologies, demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

## Problem

- Current dexterous grasping research often focuses on stable 'power grasps,' neglecting the task-specific 'functional grasps' crucial for diverse real-world applications.
- Effectively transferring human grasp poses from visual data to morphologically different robot hands, while preserving both posture and precise contact points, remains challenging.
- Achieving robust sim-to-real transfer for high-degree-of-freedom dexterous hands is difficult due to discrepancies in dynamics, limited motor precision, and lack of real-world ground truth data.

## Method

- A human-to-robot (H2R) grasp retargeting module optimizes robot hand poses to match human-like postures and precise contact points on objects from a single RGBD image, accounting for morphological differences.
- A reinforcement learning framework, trained in simulation, generates dynamic grasp control policies, leveraging a reward function that incentivizes target joint positions, accurate contact, and human-like hand postures.
- Robust sim-to-real transfer is achieved through privileged learning (reconstructing contact info from proprioception), system identification (optimizing sim dynamics parameters), domain randomization, and real-time gravity compensation.

## Results

- FunGrasp achieved an average real-world grasping success rate of 74% for one-shot functional grasping on 20 unseen daily objects, demonstrating generalization to diverse shapes and sizes.
- The system showed generalization across different robot hand morphologies, with simulated success rates of 75% for Shadow Hand, 81% for Faive Hand, and 85% for Allegro Hand, and qualitative success on real Allegro and Inspire Hands.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of key components: the H2R retargeting module achieved 85% success (vs. ~60% for baselines), privileged learning matched teacher policy performance, and both system identification and gravity compensation notably improved real-world success (75% with vs. 38% and 59% without).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-component loss function for human-to-robot grasp retargeting effectively translates human-like functional postures and precise contact points to diverse robot hands.
> - Privileged learning with an LSTM-based encoder can robustly reconstruct unobservable contact information (forces, states) from proprioception, enabling sim-to-real transfer without real-world contact sensors.
> - System identification and gravity compensation are critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap, significantly improving real-world grasping success rates for high-DoF dexterous hands.
