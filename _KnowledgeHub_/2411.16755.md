# FunGrasp: Functional Grasping for Diverse Dexterous Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> FunGrasp introduces an end-to-end system for enabling dexterous robot hands to perform functional grasping from a single human demonstration. The system achieved a 74% real-world success rate for functional grasps on unseen objects and generalized across diverse robot hand morphologies, demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

## Problem

- Current dexterous grasping research often focuses on stable 'power grasps,' neglecting the task-specific 'functional grasps' crucial for diverse real-world applications.
- Effectively transferring human grasp poses from visual data to morphologically different robot hands, while preserving both posture and precise contact points, remains challenging.
- Achieving robust sim-to-real transfer for high-degree-of-freedom dexterous hands is difficult due to discrepancies in dynamics, limited motor precision, and lack of real-world ground truth data.

## Method

- A human-to-robot (H2R) grasp retargeting module optimizes robot hand poses to match human-like postures and precise contact points on objects from a single RGBD image, accounting for morphological differences.
- A reinforcement learning framework, trained in simulation, generates dynamic grasp control policies, leveraging a reward function that incentivizes target joint positions, accurate contact, and human-like hand postures.
- Robust sim-to-real transfer is achieved through privileged learning (reconstructing contact info from proprioception), system identification (optimizing sim dynamics parameters), domain randomization, and real-time gravity compensation.

## Results

- FunGrasp achieved an average real-world grasping success rate of 74% for one-shot functional grasping on 20 unseen daily objects, demonstrating generalization to diverse shapes and sizes.
- The system showed generalization across different robot hand morphologies, with simulated success rates of 75% for Shadow Hand, 81% for Faive Hand, and 85% for Allegro Hand, and qualitative success on real Allegro and Inspire Hands.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of key components: the H2R retargeting module achieved 85% success (vs. ~60% for baselines), privileged learning matched teacher policy performance, and both system identification and gravity compensation notably improved real-world success (75% with vs. 38% and 59% without).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-component loss function for human-to-robot grasp retargeting effectively translates human-like functional postures and precise contact points to diverse robot hands.
> - Privileged learning with an LSTM-based encoder can robustly reconstruct unobservable contact information (forces, states) from proprioception, enabling sim-to-real transfer without real-world contact sensors.
> - System identification and gravity compensation are critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap, significantly improving real-world grasping success rates for high-DoF dexterous hands.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous robotic grasping and manipulation is a cornerstone of advanced robotics, crucial for enabling robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured environments, from industrial assembly to healthcare and household assistance. Historically, research in this area has faced numerous challenges, including the vast dimensionality of dexterous hands (high degrees of freedom), the complexities of hand-object interaction (contacts, friction), the need for precise control, and the significant sim-to-real gap that often prevents simulated successes from translating to physical robots.

Much of the prior work in dexterous grasping has focused on achieving stable "power grasps" – maximizing contact area and grip strength to ensure an object doesn't slip. While important, this often overlooks the "functional" aspect of grasping, where the specific task dictates a particular, often precise, interaction with the object (e.g., holding a mug by its handle for drinking vs. by its body for washing). Enabling robots to perform such task-specific functional grasps is a major unsolved problem.

The broader landscape includes several key areas that FunGrasp addresses or builds upon:
- **Dynamic Dexterous Grasping (RL-based):** Many recent works, including D-Grasp, UniDexGrasp, and GraspXL, leverage reinforcement learning (RL) to train policies for dynamic grasping in simulation. While these have shown impressive generalization in simulation across many objects or hands, they often lack robust real-world validation.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects/Categories:** A significant challenge is to train a policy that can generalize beyond the specific objects or categories it was trained on. Some methods achieve category-level generalization, but truly unseen object generalization remains difficult.
- **Human-to-Robot Grasp Retargeting:** Translating human manipulation knowledge to robots is a promising approach. Existing retargeting methods often struggle to simultaneously preserve human-like posture and precise contact points, leading to either unnatural robot grasps or inaccurate functional interactions.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Bridging the gap between simulation and real-world performance is a persistent hurdle. Techniques like domain randomization and system identification are common, but their effectiveness for high-DoF dexterous hands, especially for precise functional grasps, is still an active research area.

FunGrasp uniquely positions itself by explicitly addressing these intertwined challenges. As highlighted in Table I, it stands out by achieving:
1. **Functional Grasping:** Unlike many methods focusing on power grasps, FunGrasp prioritizes task-specific, human-like poses.
2. **Hardware Deployment:** It rigorously validates its approach on real dexterous hands, overcoming the sim-to-real challenge.
3. **Cross-Category Generalization:** It successfully performs one-shot functional grasping for unseen objects, moving beyond category-specific policies.
4. **Diverse Robot Hands:** The system is designed to generalize across different robot hand morphologies, a critical step towards universal manipulation.
5. **Diverse Poses:** It generates and controls a wide variety of task-specific grasping poses.

By integrating a novel human-to-robot grasp retargeting module, a robust RL framework for dynamic control, and advanced sim-to-real transfer techniques, FunGrasp aims to provide a comprehensive solution for achieving human-like functional dexterous grasping in real-world scenarios, thereby pushing the boundaries of what dexterous robots can achieve.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of FunGrasp is to enable dexterous robot hands to perform **functional grasping** – an essential human capability where the way an object is grasped is dictated by the specific task. The authors motivate this work by highlighting the limitations of current dexterous grasping research, which predominantly focuses on stable "power grasps" and often overlooks the task- and object-specific functional poses crucial for diverse real-world applications.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Functional Grasping Gap:** Humans instinctively adapt their grasps based on the intended task (e.g., holding a mug by its handle for drinking versus its body for washing). Current robot grasping often lacks this nuanced understanding, focusing instead on generic stable grasps. The primary motivation is to empower dexterous robot hands with such human-like functional grasping capabilities.
- **Leveraging Human Guidance:** Functional grasping is inherently guided by human intent and interaction. However, effectively transferring human grasp poses (obtained, for instance, from RGBD images) to robot hands is challenging due to significant morphological differences (finger count, DoF, knuckle sizes). A core objective is to develop an efficient and general method for this "human-to-robot" (H2R) grasp retargeting that preserves both human-like postures and precise contact points.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** For real-world deployment, a robot system must be able to handle novel objects without extensive re-training. Most existing methods train category-specific policies, limiting their applicability. FunGrasp aims for "one-shot generalization" to diverse, unseen object shapes and geometries.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While reinforcement learning has shown great promise in simulation, transferring these learned policies to physical robots remains a significant hurdle. Factors like limited motor precision, susceptibility to disturbances, and inaccuracies in simulated dynamics complicate real-world deployment. A crucial objective is to employ and refine techniques that ensure robust and reliable sim-to-real transfer for dexterous functional grasping.
- **Versatility Across Robot Hands:** Different dexterous robot hands have varying morphologies. The system aims to be versatile, capable of deploying policies across a variety of robot hand platforms, rather than being limited to a specific hand model.

In essence, FunGrasp seeks to create a practical, end-to-end system that can translate a single observation of human functional grasp (from an RGBD image) into a dynamic, functional grasp for a variety of dexterous robot hands, generalizing to novel objects and operating robustly in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FunGrasp is structured as a three-stage system designed to bridge the gap from human visual demonstrations to dynamic functional robot grasping in the real world.

#### 3.1. A. H2R Grasp Retargeting (Static Functional Grasp Retargeting)

This module addresses the challenge of transferring a human functional grasp reference (G_h) to a specific dexterous robot hand (G_r), accounting for morphological differences.
1. **Human Grasp Reference Acquisition:** Given a single RGBD image of a human grasping an object, the system utilizes off-the-shelf hand-object pose estimation models (e.g., FoundationPose for object pose, Metro for human hand pose) to obtain G_h, which includes human hand joint angles (q_h), global hand and object poses (T_h, T_o), and binary contact states (c). This assumes known object meshes.
2. **Initialization:** The robot hand pose G_r is initialized by aligning its fingertip positions and finger link directions with those of the human hand in the object's frame. For robot hands with fewer fingers or joints, non-existent parts (e.g., pinky finger) are simply removed.
3. **Optimization for Robot Grasp Reference:** The initialized G_r is then optimized using a combination of loss functions to ensure a stable, functional, and kinematically feasible grasp:
 - **Penetration Energy Loss (L_pen):** Prevents robot hand penetration into the object.
 - **Force Closure Loss (L_fc):** Encourages a stable grasp that resists external forces.
 - **Contact Position Loss (L_pos):** Ensures the robot hand maintains contact with the object at the precise desired points, crucial for functional grasping (e.g., specific finger holes).
 - **Joint Limit Loss (L_joints):** Keeps robot joint angles within their physical limits.
 - **Collision Loss (L_col):** Penalizes self-collisions of the robot hand and collisions with the table.
This optimization process generates a static robot grasp reference (G_r) that serves as a prior for the dynamic grasping phase.

#### 3.2. B. Dynamic Grasp Control

This module uses reinforcement learning (RL) to train a policy for dynamic grasping, guided by the static G_r from the retargeting module.
1. **Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The problem is formulated as an RL problem, leveraging a similar approach to but without wrist guidance to ensure safety and prevent overly rapid movements. RaiSim is used as the simulation engine, and PPO for RL training.
2. **State Space:** The robot observes its own proprioception (joint angles q_r, wrist pose T_r and velocity ̇T_r), the object's state (pose T_o and velocity ̇T_o), per-finger binary contact states (c) and contact forces (f), and the retargeted grasp reference (G_r). A feature extraction layer (ϕ) transforms these raw states into a more informative representation, including wrist-relative coordinates, object displacement, and distances to target joint/wrist positions.
3. **Action Space:** The policy outputs target finger joint angles and wrist 6D poses. These are then fed into PD controllers to compute joint torques, which drive the robot's movements.
4. **Reward Function:** A multi-component reward function incentivizes desired behaviors:
 - **Joint Position Reward (r_p):** Encourages the robot joints to move towards their target positions.
 - **Contact Reward (r_c):** Guides the robot hand to achieve and maintain desired contact positions on the object, with a dynamic weight to facilitate accurate contact.
 - **Safety Reward (r_s):** Penalizes undesired contact forces between the hand, itself, and the table.
 - **Pose Reward (r_q):** Encourages the robot's hand to maintain human-like postures by aligning current and target link directions in the object frame.

#### 3.3. C. Sim-to-Real Transfer

This crucial module employs several techniques to ensure policies trained in simulation translate robustly to real hardware.
1. **Privileged Learning:** To address the challenge of inaccessible ground-truth contact information (c and f) in the real world:
 - A "teacher policy" is trained in simulation using ground-truth contact information through RL.
 - A "student policy" is then trained to imitate the teacher's actions. This student policy includes an LSTM-based encoder that reconstructs contact information (ˆc_t, ˆf_t) solely from proprioceptive data (past state-action pairs). The MLP part of the student policy is initialized with the teacher's weights. Training involves both contact reconstruction loss (L_re) and action imitation loss (L_act).
2. **System Identification:** To account for discrepancies in actuator dynamics between simulation and reality:
 - An initial policy is pre-trained in simulation with rough estimates of joint stiffness and damping.
 - This policy is deployed on the real robot in an open-loop manner to collect command-state trajectories.
 - The CMA-ES method is used to optimize the simulation's joint stiffness and damping parameters by minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between simulated and real robot joint angles for the collected trajectories.
 - Finally, the pre-trained policy is fine-tuned in simulation using these optimized parameters.
3. **Domain Randomization:** Standard technique employed during RL training to enhance policy robustness by randomizing various physical parameters such as joint damping, PD controller gains, friction coefficients, object mass, table height, and hand state observations.
4. **Gravity Compensation:** The gravitational forces acting on each robot hand link are calculated in real-time using the Kinematics and Dynamics Library. These gravity-induced torques are then compensated for in the actuator commands, improving precision and trajectory adherence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate FunGrasp's capabilities in one-shot functional grasping for unseen objects, generalization across diverse robot hands, and the effectiveness of its sim-to-real transfer components.

#### 4.1. A. One-shot Functional Grasping of Unseen Objects

- **Evaluation:** Tested on 20 common daily objects unseen during training, using single RGBD images of human grasps to derive references. Each object had three human grasp references, and each reference was tested on two different object poses, totaling six grasps per object.
- **Results:** The system achieved an average grasping success rate of **74%** on unseen objects in the real world.
- **Observations:** FunGrasp demonstrated remarkable generalization to objects with diverse shapes, sizes, and masses, including a deformable loopy doll. It showed robustness against external disturbances, highlighting its closed-loop RL-based control. Some difficulties were noted for objects with highly unusual shapes, such as a hot glue gun. Qualitative results showcased a wide variety of human-like functional grasps (e.g., grasping scissors by finger holes, a mug by its handle).

#### 4.2. B. Generalization Across Diverse Robot Hands

- **Simulation Evaluation:** Quantitatively evaluated on three different robot hands: Shadow Hand, Faive Hand, and Allegro Hand, using the DexYCB dataset.
- **Results (Simulation):** The system consistently achieved high success rates across all tested hand models: **75% for Shadow Hand, 81% for Faive Hand, and 85% for Allegro Hand**. Simulated Distance (SimD.) and Contact Ratio (Con. R.) metrics also indicated stable and precise grasps. Allegro Hand showed slightly better performance, attributed to its larger knuckles facilitating easier grasping.
- **Real-World Evaluation:** Qualitatively demonstrated generalization on real Allegro and Inspire Hands.
- **Observations (Real-World):** Both robot hands successfully performed diverse human-like functional grasps, even for thin and small objects lying on the table. This is challenging due to potential hand-table collisions, indicating the effectiveness of the system in preserving precise postures and contacts from human references.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies in both simulation and real-world confirmed the efficacy of FunGrasp's key components.

- **H2R Grasp Retargeting:**
 - Compared against baselines: DexGraspNet (contact-rich but not functional/collision-aware) and Angle Reset (simple joint-to-joint mapping).
 - **Results (Simulation):** FunGrasp's retargeting module achieved **85% success rate** (vs. 65% for DexGraspNet and 62% for Angle Reset), with superior contact ratio and stability.
 - **Observation:** The baselines struggled with thin/small objects or produced inaccurate contacts/postures, underscoring the importance of FunGrasp's retargeting method that considers both precise contacts and human-like postures while avoiding collisions.

- **Privileged Learning:**
 - Compared against: policy without privileged information (w/o Priv. Info.), student policy trained from scratch (w/o Priv. Learn.), and the teacher policy (with GT Privileged Info.).
 - **Results (Simulation):** The full FunGrasp system (85% success) performed comparably to the Teacher Policy (85% success), significantly outperforming the policy without privileged information (61% success) and the student trained from scratch (40% success).
 - **Observation:** This validates that contact information is crucial for robust grasping and that the LSTM-based encoder effectively reconstructs this privileged information, enabling the student policy to achieve performance on par with the teacher.

- **System Identification & Gravity Compensation:**
 - Evaluated on real robots (Allegro Hand) against variants without system identification (w/o Sys. Id.) and without gravity compensation (w/o Grav. Comp.).
 - **Results (Real-World):** The full FunGrasp system achieved an average success rate of **75%**. Without gravity compensation, success dropped to **59%**. Without system identification, it fell significantly to **38%**.
 - **Observation:** These results strongly emphasize the critical role of both accurate joint dynamics models (provided by system identification) and real-time gravity compensation for robust sim-to-real transfer and successful real-world dexterous manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FunGrasp represents a significant step forward in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between human-centric functional grasping and its practical implementation on diverse robotic hardware. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Enabling Functional Grasping for Robots:** This is perhaps the most profound impact. By explicitly focusing on "functional grasping" rather than just stable "power grasps," FunGrasp allows robots to perform tasks in a more human-like, intuitive, and efficient manner. This is crucial for applications where specific object interaction points are critical for task success, such as using tools (scissors by handles), handling delicate items, or performing multi-stage manipulation sequences. This capability unlocks a vast array of new applications for dexterous robots in domestic, healthcare, and industrial settings.

2. **One-Shot Generalization from Human Demonstrations:** The ability to learn a functional grasp from a single RGBD image of a human demonstration and generalize it to unseen objects is highly impactful. This greatly reduces the need for extensive, time-consuming, and costly robot-specific data collection or manual programming for every new object. It makes the deployment of dexterous robots more flexible and adaptable to novel environments and tasks.

3. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer for Dexterous Hands:** The meticulous integration of privileged learning, system identification, domain randomization, and gravity compensation effectively addresses the notorious sim-to-real gap. This is a critical barrier for high-DoF dexterous manipulators, whose complex dynamics and precise control requirements often make real-world deployment challenging. FunGrasp provides a proven methodology for achieving robust transfer, paving the way for more reliable real-world robot systems.

4. **Versatility Across Diverse Robot Hands:** The system's design for generalization across different robot hand morphologies (e.g., Allegro, Inspire, Shadow, Faive Hands) is a major advantage. This means the underlying principles and trained policies are not tied to a single hardware platform, making the research more broadly applicable and accelerating the development of dexterous manipulation capabilities across the robotics community.

5. **Comprehensive System Integration:** FunGrasp offers an end-to-end pipeline, from perception of human intent to dynamic robot control and real-world execution. This holistic approach is valuable, as many previous works often focus on isolated components (e.g., only grasp synthesis or only sim-to-real).

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** Robots capable of functional grasping can better assist humans in daily tasks, handling objects with appropriate task-specific intentions.
- **Advanced Manufacturing and Assembly:** More precise and adaptable grasping can lead to automation of complex assembly tasks requiring nuanced object manipulation.
- **Healthcare and Rehabilitation Robotics:** Dexterous robots could assist in patient care, manipulating medical instruments or personal items with the required precision and care.
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** Handling a wider variety of objects, including irregularly shaped or delicate ones, becomes more feasible.
- **Research Acceleration:** The robust framework and detailed ablation studies provide valuable insights and a strong baseline for future research in functional grasping, sim-to-real transfer, and human-robot skill transfer.

A current limitation noted by the authors is the reliance on known object meshes for off-the-shelf pose estimation. Future work will likely focus on more integrated models that can directly utilize image inputs, further enhancing generalization and autonomy. Despite this, FunGrasp makes a substantial contribution by demonstrating a practical and robust solution for functional dexterous grasping, bringing human-like manipulation capabilities closer to real-world robot deployment.
