# ConTrack: Constrained Hand Motion Tracking with Adaptive Trade-off Control

> [!summary] Summary
> ConTrack is a constrained reinforcement learning framework for tracking human hand-object demonstrations on dexterous robot hands. It treats object-pose tracking as a hard constraint and spends leftover control authority on motion style, adapting the task-style mix online via a Lagrangian dual variable, and stabilizes long-horizon training with a reset library of policy-reachable simulator states. Mean progress reaches 0.899 versus 0.743 for the strongest baseline.

## Problem

- Kinematic and actuation gaps between human hands and robot hands make demonstrated joint motion and contact timing physically unrealizable, so purely geometric retargeting breaks under contact-critical schedules.
- Existing trackers use fixed hand-tuned reward weights balancing object tracking against motion style, requiring per-clip retuning and failing when signal scales shift across task phases.
- Long-horizon contact-rich rollouts train poorly: resetting to reference states yields physically inconsistent hand-object configurations, while always restarting from frame one wastes interaction budget.

## Method

- Constrained objective maximizes style fidelity subject to task return exceeding a fraction alpha of a running clip-specific normalizer, converted through ==Lagrangian relaxation== with a scalar dual variable.
- ==Online dual controller== mixes task and style advantages with weight given by a sigmoid of the dual variable, which ascends whenever normalized task return drops below the target ratio.
- ==Adaptive mid-trajectory reset library== stores the furthest-reaching policy-reachable simulator state per reference frame, sampling restarts with probability decaying in a per-frame survival ratio, plus link-level contact-prior rewards.

## Results

- Mean progress of **0.899** across GRAB, ARCTIC and DexterHand clips beats ManipTrans at **0.743**, SPIDER at **0.444** and DexMachina at **0.246**, at **5000** PPO updates per clip.
- Style fidelity held at **0.163 rad** finger error, **0.784** contact F1 and **0.018 m** contact-point error, with object errors of **0.026 m** and **0.272 rad** despite far higher completion.
- Ablations: adaptive mixing beats fixed 1:1 weights (**0.899** versus **0.868** progress); the reset library beats start-only resets (**0.700**) and uniform mid-clip resets (**0.727**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Casting the dominant objective as a constraint and optimizing secondary objectives inside it removes per-sequence reward weight tuning in multi-objective imitation.
> - Reset states for curriculum learning should be policy-reachable simulator snapshots, not reference kinematic states, whenever discontinuous contact dynamics make reference configurations physically inconsistent.
> - Reported failures were budget-limited, not structural: the hardest in-hand clip reached 94% success with 100,000 updates, so fixed-budget benchmarks understate method ceilings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen advancements through scaling robot data and developing general-purpose policies. Concurrently, a significant trend involves learning from human demonstrations, capitalizing on the availability of human data to supervise dexterous behaviors. However, a persistent challenge in transferring human demonstrations to robots, particularly for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, is the "kinematic gap." This gap arises from the morphological and actuation differences between human hands and robot end effectors, leading to a mismatch between demonstrated human motion and what a robot hand can physically execute.

Prior approaches to address this kinematic gap include:
- **Retargeting**: Optimization-based methods align human hand trajectories to robot hands by minimizing geometric errors, sometimes supported by rigid-body dynamics toolkits or vision-based teleoperation systems. Neural retargeting aims to improve scalability by learning fast mappings. However, purely geometric objectives often prove fragile when contact schedules become critical.
- **Physics-informed and Functional Retargeting**: More recent work incorporates physical feasibility and object-centric goals, prioritizing object motion over joint-level imitation. While these methods improve physical consistency, they often leave unresolved the question of how a system should autonomously balance task success (e.g., object trajectory) and motion style (e.g., joint movements, contact patterns) when the reference motion is not jointly realizable, particularly without extensive per-sequence manual tuning.
- **Learned Tracking Controllers**: These train neural policies to follow time-indexed references, but also face the challenge of managing the task-style trade-off.

In other domains, such as physics-based character control, tracking-centric formulations have reduced the need for manual reward engineering and improved scalability with diverse motion data. This paper draws inspiration from this shift. Furthermore, long-horizon training in reinforcement learning often benefits from curricula and exploration strategies that expand the reachable state space over time. However, applying direct mid-trajectory resets to reference states in dexterous hand-object interaction can lead to physically inconsistent configurations due to discontinuous contact dynamics.

The proposed ConTrack framework positions itself within this landscape by adopting a constrained reinforcement learning (RL) approach. It seeks to balance task tracking against imitation by treating task success as a primary constraint and optimizing for style fidelity within that constraint. This contrasts with common approaches that use fixed, hand-tuned reward weights, which can be brittle and require retuning as task difficulty or signal scale shifts across different clips or phases of a task. ConTrack's methodology, particularly its use of primal-dual updates for trade-off control, aligns with recent work in constrained RL aimed at learning stylistic behavior under task optimality constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the difficulties in transferring human dexterous manipulation demonstrations to robotic systems, specifically due to the kinematic gap between human and robot hands. This gap makes it challenging to accurately track long-horizon, contact-rich sequences in simulators, as a tracking policy must simultaneously maintain objects on their target trajectories, preserve demonstrated joint motion, and adhere to critical contact timing. Current methods frequently rely on manual reward tuning, which is labor-intensive, clip-specific, and prone to breaking under limited interaction budgets.

Given these challenges, ConTrack aims to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a Scalable Reinforcement Learning Framework for Reference Tracking**: The goal is to create an RL framework that can effectively track contact-rich hand-object interactions over long horizons in physics simulations, moving beyond the limitations of manual reward engineering.
2. **Introduce an Adaptive Task-Style Trade-off Mechanism**: A core objective is to allow the system to dynamically adapt the balance between achieving task success (object tracking) and preserving motion style fidelity (joint motion and contact patterns). This adaptation should occur online during training, eliminating the need for pre-defined, fixed reward weights or per-sequence tuning.
3. **Stabilize Long-Horizon Learning**: To overcome the instability associated with long-horizon tracking, particularly in contact-rich scenarios, an objective is to develop a robust mechanism for restarting rollouts. This mechanism should address the problem of physically inconsistent resets by leveraging policy-reachable simulator states.
4. **Enhance Performance in Dexterous Manipulation Tasks**: The framework aims to significantly improve success rates and object pose accuracy compared to existing methods, while concurrently preserving the fidelity of demonstrated joint motions and contact interactions.
5. **Demonstrate Real-World Feasibility**: A practical objective is to verify that the trajectories learned in simulation are executable on physical robot hardware, thereby bridging the sim-to-real gap for dexterous manipulation policies.

In essence, ConTrack seeks to provide a more autonomous, efficient, and robust solution for transferring complex human dexterous skills to robots by intelligently managing the inherent trade-offs and stability challenges in long-horizon, contact-rich scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ConTrack addresses the problem of constrained hand motion tracking by formulating object tracking as a primary constraint and dedicating the remaining optimization capacity to style fidelity. This approach is implemented through three main components: an online dual controller for adaptive task-style mixing, an adaptive mid-trajectory reset library for stable long-horizon learning, and contact priors to guide style objective.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Reward Formulation

The problem is modeled as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) for each reference clip. The simulator state includes robot joints, object poses, and velocities. The reference provides time-indexed targets for joint trajectories ($q_{ref,t}$), object poses ($p_{ref,t,o}$, $\bar{q}_{ref,t,o}$), and link-level contact annotations ($c_{ref,t,o,\ell}$, $y_{ref,t,o,\ell}$). The robot policy outputs residual joint displacements ($a_t$) that are added to the reference joint positions ($q_{ref,t}$), allowing corrective deviations while staying close to the reference.
The total reward $r(s_t, a_t)$ is decomposed into three components:
- $r_g$: Measures task success through object pose tracking.
- $r_s$: Measures style fidelity through hand kinematics and contacts.
- $r_p$: Penalizes high-frequency motion.
ConTrack's objective is to maximize style fidelity ($J_s(\pi)$) subject to a minimum level of task success, expressed as $J_g(\pi) \ge \alpha J_g^\star$, where $\alpha \in (0, 1]$ is a target task ratio and $J_g^\star$ is a clip-specific normalizer (running maximum of task return).

#### 3.2. Adaptive Task–Style Mixing (Online Dual Controller)

Recognizing that fixed reward weights are often insufficient for contact-rich tracking due to varying difficulties, ConTrack employs an online dual controller to adapt the task-style trade-off.
- **Lagrangian Relaxation**: The constrained optimization problem is converted into a Lagrangian relaxation: $L(\pi, \lambda) = J_s(\pi) - \lambda(\alpha - J_g(\pi)/J_g^\star)$, where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ is a scalar dual variable. A larger $\lambda$ increases pressure to satisfy the task constraint, while a smaller $\lambda$ prioritizes style.
- **Advantage Mixing**: The policy is updated using a mixed advantage $A_{mix} = w_{task}A_g + (1 - w_{task})A_s + A_p$. The mixing weight $w_{task} \in$ is derived from the dual variable using a logistic sigmoid function: $w_{task} = \sigma(\lambda)$.
- **Dual Variable Update**: $\lambda$ is updated using an online estimate of the task return $\hat{J}_g$: $\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \eta (\alpha - \hat{J}_g/J_g^\star)$, where $\eta > 0$ is a step size. If $\hat{J}_g/J_g^\star$ falls below $\alpha$, $\lambda$ increases, shifting optimization towards task tracking. Conversely, if task performance exceeds $\alpha$, $\lambda$ decreases, prioritizing style.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Mid-Trajectory Reset Library

To overcome the inefficiency of always starting from the first reference frame and the physical inconsistencies of direct reference state resets, ConTrack introduces an adaptive reset library.
- **Online Refresh**: For each reference frame $k$, the library stores a simulator state that is empirically reachable by the current policy. When an episode starting at $t_{start}$ successfully reaches $t_{end}$, the continuation length $\ell_k = t_{end} - k + 1$ is calculated for each frame $k \in [t_{start}, t_{end}]$. If $\ell_k$ exceeds the previously recorded maximum $\ell_{max,k}$ for frame $k$, the stored state at $k$ in the library is overwritten with the current simulator state, ensuring physical consistency.
- **Difficulty-Aware Sampling**: Reset frames are sampled probabilistically. A "survival ratio" $u_k = \bar{\ell}_k / (T - k)$ is maintained using an exponential moving average ($\bar{\ell}_k$), indicating how far the policy typically tracks from frame $k$ relative to the remaining horizon. Resets are sampled with probability $p(k) \propto \exp(-u_k / \tau)$, where $\tau$ is a temperature parameter. This strategy focuses training on segments where the policy frequently fails or struggles, pushing learning towards the current failure boundary.

#### 3.4. Contact Priors

Reference contact annotations are used as an auxiliary style prior to encourage realistic hand-object interactions.
- **Reward Terms**: The style reward ($r_s$) includes terms that reward agreement with reference contact events ($c_{ref,t,o,\ell}$) and penalize the distance between simulated and annotated object-local contact points ($y_{t,o,\ell}$ vs. $y_{ref,t,o,\ell}$) when both indicate contact.
- **Annotation Processing**: Dataset-level contact annotations (segment-level) are mapped to the robot's link set to provide specific link-level contact targets.

#### 3.5. Observation Space

ConTrack utilizes an asymmetric actor-critic architecture. The actor receives a compact, potentially noisy, state for feedback tracking (robot/object poses and velocities). The critic receives additional, uncorrupted signals including current poses and velocities, binary contact indicators, a short stack of future reference poses, reference contact flags, and a normalized phase scalar. This enriched critic observation helps reduce value-estimation variance and stabilize long-horizon training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ConTrack was evaluated against established baselines on a suite of dexterous manipulation tasks, categorized into three tiers of increasing contact complexity: GRAB (bimanual rigid-object), ARCTIC (articulated objects, multi-object contact), and DexterHand (single-hand in-hand rotation). All learning-based methods were trained for a fixed budget of 5000 PPO updates per clip. Evaluation metrics included task-related measures (progress, object position/rotation error) and style-fidelity measures (finger joint error, contact F1, contact point error).

#### 4.1. Baseline Comparison

Across the benchmark clips, ConTrack demonstrated robust performance:
- **Progress and Object Tracking**: ConTrack achieved a mean progress of 0.899 (fraction of clip completed successfully), significantly outperforming ManipTrans (0.743), DexMachina (0.246), and SPIDER (0.444). It maintained competitive object pose accuracy, with mean object translation error of 0.026 m and rotation error of 0.272 rad. While ManipTrans sometimes achieved slightly lower object pose errors (0.012 m, 0.207 rad), it did so with substantially lower progress and contact fidelity.
- **Style Fidelity**: ConTrack preserved joint and contact fidelity effectively, with a mean finger error of 0.163 rad, a contact F1 score of 0.784, and a contact point error of 0.018 m. These metrics were generally superior or comparable to the baselines, especially considering its higher progress rate. DexMachina, while strong in finger error and contact F1, showed limited progress. SPIDER struggled with object tracking and contact fidelity once contact dynamics became dominant.
- **Tier-Specific Performance**: ConTrack achieved near-complete progress on GRAB clips (average 0.996) and strong progress on ARCTIC clips (average 0.944). DexterHand clips proved more challenging within the fixed budget (average 0.733), particularly the "Ring" task.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments were conducted to assess the contribution of ConTrack's key components:
- **Adaptive vs. Fixed Task–Style Mixing**: Adaptive mixing consistently improved mean progress (0.899) and contact F1 (0.784) compared to variants with fixed mixing weights (e.g., Fixed task: 0.764 progress, 0.679 contact F1; Fixed 1:1: 0.868 progress, 0.701 contact F1). This indicated that the adaptive mechanism enables the policy to operate closer to the empirical Pareto frontier of task-style trade-offs.
- **Adaptive Mid-Trajectory Reset Library**: The adaptive reset library was shown to be crucial for long-horizon learning. It yielded a mean progress of 0.899, significantly higher than policies that always reset from the start (0.700 progress) or used uniform mid-clip resets (0.727 progress). Analysis of the reset distribution showed that the library dynamically adapted, initially focusing resets near the end of the clip and moving backward to earlier frames as the policy learned to track further, concentrating learning on challenging segments.
- **Contact Prior Reward**: Removing the contact event overlap term or the contact point distance term from the style reward led to reductions in progress, object accuracy, or contact fidelity. This suggested that both components of the contact prior provide complementary supervision, assisting in resolving contact dynamics that might not be fully captured by object pose errors alone.

#### 4.3. Real-World Feasibility

A feasibility study was conducted on a bimanual xArm7+xHand robot platform. Policy-predicted joint references streamed from the simulator were executed on the real hardware. This qualitative demonstration indicated that the learned trajectories are executable on physical systems.

#### 4.4. Failure Analysis for DexterHand Ring

The DexterHand Ring clip was identified as the most challenging under the fixed budget. An extended training budget (100,000 PPO updates) on this clip increased its success rate to 94%, suggesting that its difficulty was partly budget-related. Failure analysis revealed that terminations primarily occurred due to rotational pose-break conditions, concentrated around a specific rotation-dominant phase early in the clip, rather than gradual positional drift.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ConTrack introduces a framework that addresses several key challenges in robot learning from human demonstrations, particularly for dexterous and contact-rich manipulation:

- **Bridging the Kinematic Gap**: The work provides a method to translate physically infeasible human reference motions into executable robot trajectories, which is a critical step towards leveraging abundant human data for robot skill acquisition.
- **Automated Trade-off Control**: A significant contribution is the adaptive task-style mixing mechanism, which dynamically adjusts the balance between task success and motion fidelity online. This removes the necessity for manual, per-sequence reward tuning, a common bottleneck in multi-objective reinforcement learning. This automation enhances the scalability and applicability of the framework to diverse and complex manipulation tasks.
- **Stabilized Long-Horizon Learning**: The adaptive mid-trajectory reset library is pivotal for efficient training of long-horizon tasks. By refreshing the reset library with policy-reachable simulator states and sampling start frames based on learning difficulty, ConTrack ensures physically consistent resets and focuses learning on crucial failure boundaries, thereby accelerating the acquisition of complex sequential skills.
- **Enhanced Performance and Fidelity**: ConTrack demonstrates improved success rates and object pose accuracy compared to prior methods, while concurrently maintaining high fidelity to demonstrated joint motions and critical contact patterns. This balanced performance is essential for replicating human-like dexterous skills.
- **Real-World Transferability**: The successful execution of ConTrack-trained policies on a physical robot platform validates the framework's potential for real-world deployment. This feasibility study contributes to bridging the sim-to-real gap, an ongoing challenge in robotics.

**Potential Impact**:
The ability to autonomously learn and adapt complex dexterous manipulation skills from human demonstrations, without extensive manual tuning, could accelerate the development of robotic systems capable of performing intricate tasks in unstructured environments. This has implications for industries such as manufacturing, service robotics, and assistive technologies, where robots need to interact skillfully with objects and adapt to dynamic contact conditions. The framework's modularity and the insights gained from the ablation studies also provide directions for future research in constrained reinforcement learning and curriculum design for physically embodied agents.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The paper acknowledges several limitations, including that the online normalization for the task constraint does not strictly guarantee satisfaction, and the reliance on the availability and quality of contact annotations. Furthermore, performance on the hardest in-hand manipulation clips is still limited under fixed interaction budgets. Future work could explore stricter constraint satisfaction mechanisms, develop methods for learning contact priors implicitly, and investigate richer perception and tighter sim-to-real alignment to enhance robustness in real-world deployments.
