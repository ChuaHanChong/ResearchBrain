# Antagonistic Bowden-Cable Actuation of a Lightweight Robotic Hand: Toward Dexterous Manipulation for Payload Constrained Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> A robotic hand designed for payload-constrained humanoids, the Antagonistic Bowden-Cable-Driven Lightweight (ABCDL) system from KAIST, features remote actuation to achieve a 236g distal mass, 20 degrees of freedom with 15 motors, and successfully lifted 25 kg, enabling dexterous object manipulation.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots require hands with high dexterity, grasping force, and many degrees of freedom, yet must adhere to lightweight and human-scale size constraints.
- Traditional robotic hand designs often face trade-offs where minimizing distal mass conflicts with achieving high output force, backdrivability, or numerous DOFs.
- Existing cable-driven systems for anthropomorphic hands frequently suffer from bulky actuator modules, limited wrist compatibility, and nonlinearities like friction and hysteresis in tendon-sheath systems.

## Method

- A lightweight (236g distal mass) anthropomorphic hand is designed with remote actuation via 15 antagonistic Bowden cable pairs to minimize mass at the robot's end-effector.
- ==Rolling Contact Joints (RCJs)== are kinematically optimized to allow a single motor to control both flexion and extension for each joint by ensuring negligible cable length change.
- The system includes a modular remote actuator unit housing 15 Dynamixel servomotors, connecting to the hand via reinforced PTFE Bowden cables with optimized routing.

## Results

- The hand achieves 20 degrees of freedom with 15 motors, a distal mass of 236g, and maintains a 1:1 anthropomorphic scale to human hands.
- It demonstrated robust payload capacity by lifting a 25 kg dumbbell (over 100 times its own mass) in 10 consecutive trials and achieved fingertip forces up to **21.9** N.
- Kinematic consistency of the antagonistic Bowden-cable transmission was confirmed with an RMSE of 5 mm in fingertip trajectories under dynamic perturbations, and all Cutkosky grasp classes were successfully executed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Remote actuation through antagonistic Bowden cables effectively reduces distal mass on humanoid robot arms, thereby enhancing overall payload capacity and dynamic performance.
> - Kinematic optimization of rolling contact joint geometry enables single-motor, bidirectional control for each joint by maintaining a near-constant combined cable length in antagonistic pairs.
> - An anthropomorphic hand structure with an opposable thumb and precise cable routing allows for diverse and stable grasping capabilities across various object geometries and weights.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots necessitates robotic hands capable of human-level dexterity, characterized by high grasping force, rapid actuation speeds, multiple degrees of freedom (DOFs), and lightweight structures within human-like size constraints. The human hand, weighing approximately 0.4 kg, provides over 20 DOFs and grasping forces exceeding 400 N, along with backdrivability and payload capacity. Mechanically replicating these features involves inherent trade-offs, particularly balancing weight against output force and motion capability. Heavy hands reduce a robot arm's effective payload, while high gear ratios for torque can compromise backdrivability and compliance. Embedding numerous actuators in the palm to achieve high DOFs can also increase the lever arm to the wrist, complicating arm compatibility.

Various approaches have been explored to address these conflicts while retaining dexterity. Some designs integrate actuators directly at the finger joints or embed them in the palm, utilizing linkage-based transmissions. Cable-driven architectures have received considerable attention due to their potential for human-like dimensions, force generation, and high DOFs. Examples include the Biomimetic Hand, which mimics human tendon structures, the Anatomically Correct Testbed (ACT) Hand, utilizing spectra strings for high DOFs, and the SeoulTech Hand, employing antagonistic cable pairs for one-motor-per-DOF actuation. The CasiaHand demonstrated 15 DOFs with palm-integrated actuators. While tendon-driven hands can achieve high DOFs and human-scale dimensions, they often face challenges such as distal weight and limited wrist compatibility due to bulky actuator modules, which increase the lever arm and hinder integration with various robotic arms. Nonlinearities such as friction and hysteresis in tendon-sheath systems also require compensation.

The contemporary landscape of humanoid robotics, particularly in areas like imitation learning and large-scale demonstration datasets for Physical AI, increasingly emphasizes five-fingered anthropomorphic hands. This trend underscores a demand for advanced hand designs. This research positions itself by addressing the limitations of existing designs, specifically the trade-offs between lightness, high DOFs, and compatibility with humanoid platforms, proposing a solution aimed at achieving a higher level of compromise among these competing requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to develop a lightweight, anthropomorphic robotic hand capable of dexterous manipulation for payload-constrained humanoid robots. The motivation for this work stemmed from the critical need to resolve several inherent conflicts in robotic hand design that limit the practical applicability of humanoid robots in human-centered environments.

Specific objectives and motivations included:

- **Mitigating Distal Mass Limitations:** To enable humanoid robots to handle substantial payloads without significantly compromising the arm's overall payload capacity, a key motivation was to reduce the mass concentrated at the hand (distal mass). This was achieved by exploring remote actuation strategies.
- **Achieving High Dexterity and DOFs:** The hand needed to offer multiple degrees of freedom to perform complex manipulation tasks, approaching human-level dexterity, while maintaining anthropomorphic scale.
- **Enhancing Actuation Efficiency and Simplicity:** To overcome the complexity and bulk associated with individual actuators for each DOF, the design aimed to enable single-motor-per-joint control. This required a transmission mechanism that could provide bidirectional control with minimal loss and without requiring synchronization between multiple motors per joint.
- **Improving Joint Mechanism Robustness and Performance:** The motivation extended to developing a joint mechanism that could provide a wide range of motion, maintain low friction, and be robust without relying on complex bearings or linkages. This also involved addressing issues like unintended flexion in unidirectional cable-driven systems.
- **Ensuring Anthropomorphic Form and Function:** The design was driven by the need to replicate human hand proportions, joint arrangements, and critical functional aspects like thumb opposability and palm concavity, which are vital for versatile grasping.
- **Facilitating Fabrication and Maintenance:** Practical considerations such as low-cost fabrication, ease of assembly, and simplified maintenance were also motivating factors, leading to the exploration of 3D printing and modular designs.
- **Validating Robustness and Consistency:** A crucial motivation was to experimentally validate that the proposed hand could consistently perform dexterous tasks, withstand significant payloads, and maintain kinematic accuracy even under dynamic conditions typical of humanoid robot operation.

By addressing these motivations, the research aimed to provide a practical and deployable robotic hand solution that resolves many of the long-standing design challenges in anthropomorphic robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The researchers developed an Antagonistic Bowden-Cable-Driven Lightweight (ABCDL) Robotic Hand, integrating several design principles to achieve its objectives.

**Overall System Architecture:**
The proposed system consists of a lightweight hand assembly and a remote actuator module connected via Bowden cables. The entire hand, excluding the actuator module and Bowden sheaths, weighs 236 g. All hand components and the actuator module were fabricated from polylactic acid (PLA) using a conventional fused deposition modeling (FDM) 3D printer. Rubber tape was applied locally to finger and palm surfaces to compensate for PLA's low friction coefficient.

**Remote Actuation and Bowden Cable System:**
To minimize distal mass, the actuator module is designed for remote placement, such as within a humanoid robot's torso. This allows the hand to maintain anthropomorphic scale and dexterity while reducing the mass that the arm must move. Bowden cables are employed to transmit tensile forces from the remote actuators to the hand.
- **Cable Components:** The actuation cables consist of 0.75 mm stainless-steel wires with a nylon coating. These wires are housed within polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) tubes, which have an inner diameter of 1 mm and an outer diameter of 2 mm.
- **Reinforcement:** To prevent collapse or damage of the PTFE sheath under cable tension, each tube is reinforced with a close-fitting tensile spring (2 mm inner diameter, 3.2 mm outer diameter) wound around its exterior.
- **Routing:** A total of 30 Bowden cables, forming 15 antagonistic pairs, connect the hand and the actuator module. Cable guides are placed at regular intervals along the Bowden cable path to constrain excessive curvature, thereby mitigating friction losses and enhancing transmission efficiency and service life, particularly during movements like elbow flexion.

**Antagonistic Cable-Driven Rolling Contact Joints (RCJs):**
The hand utilizes Rolling Contact Joints (RCJs) for all finger joints, a design choice intended to emulate human anatomical joint mechanics while maintaining low friction and a wide range of motion without the need for additional bearings or pins.
- **RCJ Design Principles:** Each RCJ generates rotation through the rolling engagement of two curved surfaces. The geometry of these contact surfaces is optimized for specific motion trajectories and force-transmission characteristics.
- **Kinematic Optimization:** A key aspect of the RCJ design is its optimization for antagonistic cable actuation. The contact geometry is tuned such that the combined length change of an antagonistic cable pair remains negligible over the full range of motion (ROM) of the joint. This enables a single motor to actively control both flexion and extension without requiring a complex two-motor setup per joint for differential length control. Kinematic simulations determined the optimal rolling radius (r_i) such that the sum of length variations of the flexors (Δc_f) and extensors (Δc_e) satisfied |Δc_f + Δc_e| ≈ 0. This optimization resulted in residual length deviations of approximately 0.03 mm, which is considered negligible.

**Anthropomorphic Hand Structure:**
The hand design emphasizes anthropomorphic proportions and joint arrangements.
- **Finger Kinematics:** All fingers feature identical parameterized structures, with differences primarily in tendon routing for the thumb.
 - For the four fingers (index to little), ulnar and radial cable pairs are anchored to the proximal phalanx (PP) to constrain the two DOFs of the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint. Flexor-extensor cables pass through the middle phalanx (MP) and are fixed at the distal phalanx (DP), achieving underactuation between the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) and distal interphalangeal (DIP) joints.
 - For the thumb, cables pass through the metacarpal (MC) and are fixed to the PP, realizing underactuation between the carpometacarpal (CMC) flexion-extension and MCP joints. Parallel routing for the MCP and CMC cables effectively actuates a single DOF with two tendons, reinforcing torque generation at the basal joints.
 - Cable routing within each phalanx and knuckle is designed along smooth curves to ensure bending radii above the recommended minimum, thereby reducing resistance due to cable deformation.
- **Palm and Thumb Arrangement:** The fingers are arranged to maintain anthropomorphic placement and ranges of motion, with particular attention to thumb opposability. The oblique arch formed by the thumb and little finger in the human hand is mimicked through specific transformations and analysis of inter-finger interactions. The base of the thumb metacarpal is broadened to resemble the thenar eminence, enhancing contact surface during grasping.

**Actuator Unit Design:**
The actuator module utilizes 15 Dynamixel XC330-T288-T servomotors (Robotis Co.), each integrating a motor, encoder, and controller.
- **Modular Assembly:** The module is designed with a stackable architecture, where each layer accommodates three motors. Five layers are stacked for the experimental prototype.
- **Power and Communication:** Power lines are distributed in a tree-branch configuration per layer, while communication lines are daisy-chained across all layers.
- **Cable Anchoring and Winding:** A dedicated top layer is used for Bowden cable anchoring. Each cable is guided to a bobbin mounted on a motor shaft, with antagonistic pairs wound in opposite directions. The bobbin's three-part design (lower barrel, partition wall, upper barrel) allows for preloading adjustment during assembly. Flexion cables, typically under higher loads, are wound on the lower barrel to minimize bending moment on the motor shaft.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrated the performance, consistency, and manipulation capabilities of the proposed robotic hand.

**Performance Evaluation:**
- **Weight and Dimensions:** The hand assembly has a distal mass of 236 g (excluding remote actuators and Bowden sheaths). The total weight, including 0.9 m Bowden cables and the actuator module, is 1952 g. The hand maintains a 1:1 anthropomorphic scale with human hands (122 × 189 × 198 mm³ in anatomical posture). It features 20 DOFs actuated by 15 motors.
- **Fingertip Force and Speed:**
 - Thumb: Achieved a contact force of 21.9 ± 0.6 N and a tip speed of 193 mm/s.
 - Index–Little Fingers: Achieved a contact force of 18.0 ± 0.3 N and a tip speed of 208 mm/s.
 These forces exceed 18 N, as stated in the abstract.
- **Payload Capacity:** The hand demonstrated robust payload capacity by successfully lifting and holding a 25 kg dumbbell—over one hundred times its own mass—in 10 consecutive trials without failure using a power-wrap grasp.
- **Thumb Opposability:** The thumb opposability index (J) was calculated as 0.172. This index quantifies the shared workspace between the thumb and other fingers, with the index finger having the largest shared workspace (88.6 cm³), followed by the middle (69.6 cm³), ring (39.6 cm³), and little fingers (7.1 cm³). This value is reported as higher than those reported in some prior anthropomorphic designs.
- **Comparative Specifications:** Relative to other representative robotic hands (Table III), the proposed hand offers a combination of low hand weight (236g) for its anthropomorphic scale, high DOFs (20) with a reasonable number of actuators (15), and significant fingertip force (18 N). For instance, it is lighter than the CasiaHand (890g) and achieves higher fingertip force than the SeoulTech Hand (6N), while having comparable DOFs and actuator count.

**Antagonistic Bowden-Cable Actuation Consistency:**
The consistency and robustness of the antagonistic Bowden-cable actuation were assessed by comparing fingertip trajectories under two conditions: a static configuration with the actuator module fixed, and a perturbed configuration where the actuator module was intentionally shaken relative to the hand.
- Despite visible perturbations to the actuator module, the fingertip trajectories from both conditions remained closely aligned.
- The root-mean-square error (RMSE) of fingertip positions was approximately 5 mm. This deviation is attributed to both the imposed actuator perturbations and the inherent variability of underactuated cable-driven mechanisms. The results indicate that the antagonistic Bowden-cable transmission maintains consistent kinematic behavior within a bounded deviation, even under dynamic transformations between the actuator and the hand.
- The kinematic optimization of the rolling radius in the RCJs was validated, showing negligible residual deviation (approximately 0.03 mm) in the sum of antagonistic cable lengths over the full ROM, confirming the feasibility of single-motor antagonistic control.

**Object Manipulation:**
The hand's ability to manipulate objects was evaluated by reproducing all grasp classes of the Cutkosky taxonomy. The hand achieved natural grasp postures and stably held a diverse range of target objects, including a 1.075 kg bottle, a 0.8 kg hammer, a 7.56 kg stack of books (platform push), and smaller items like a drill bit and a golf ball. This demonstrated the effectiveness of the anthropomorphic design, the opposable thumb, and the robustness of the grasping capabilities across various object sizes and weights.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing the fundamental challenges of integrating high dexterity with lightweight construction in robotic hands. The key innovation lies in the synergistic combination of optimized rolling-contact joints and antagonistic Bowden-cable actuation, enabling remote actuation and substantial reduction of distal mass.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Payload Capacity for Humanoids:** By relocating the actuator module to a more proximal location (e.g., the torso), the design substantially mitigates the distal mass problem that limits the effective payload capacity of robot arms. This directly contributes to improving the practical applicability of humanoid robots in tasks requiring manipulation of heavier objects or dynamic arm movements.
- **Improved Dexterity and Anthropomorphic Fidelity:** The hand maintains human-scale dimensions and achieves 20 DOFs, supporting complex, dexterous manipulation tasks. The anthropomorphic design, including the opposable thumb and finger arrangement, allows for natural grasp postures and versatile object interaction, as validated through the Cutkosky taxonomy.
- **Simplified Actuation and Control:** The kinematic optimization of rolling-contact joints for antagonistic cable actuation enables a single motor to control each joint bidirectionally with negligible cable length deviation. This simplifies the actuator module and control scheme compared to systems requiring multiple motors per joint or complex synchronization mechanisms.
- **Robustness and Reliability:** Experimental validations confirmed the hand's robustness through its ability to lift payloads over one hundred times its own mass and maintain trajectory consistency under perturbed actuator-hand transformations. This indicates a reliable system suitable for dynamic and uncertain humanoid environments.
- **Cost-Effectiveness and Maintainability:** The use of 3D printing for most components, coupled with a modular design, suggests potential for low-cost fabrication, flexible customization, and ease of maintenance, which are important factors for widespread adoption.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as enhancing thumb fidelity through advanced carpometacarpal joint designs and exploring intrinsic muscle-inspired antagonistic routing, indicates a clear path for further expanding the hand's anthropomorphic fidelity and manipulation capabilities. Overall, this research demonstrates a feasible and practical solution for designing dexterous robotic hands that can overcome the payload constraints typically encountered in humanoid robot platforms.
