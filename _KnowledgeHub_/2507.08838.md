# wd1: Weighted Policy Optimization for Reasoning in Diffusion Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> wd1 introduces a weighted policy optimization approach for diffusion-based Large Language Models (dLLMs) that reformulates the reinforcement learning objective to require only a single likelihood approximation. This method achieves up to a 100% accuracy improvement on reasoning and planning benchmarks while reducing training costs by eliminating the need for supervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based Large Language Models (dLLMs) face challenges in applying reinforcement learning due to the intractability of their sequence-level likelihood functions.
- Existing RL methods for dLLMs require approximating likelihoods for multiple policies (current, old, reference), leading to high computational overhead.
- These multi-policy likelihood approximations, particularly in policy ratios used for importance sampling, introduce bias and training instability.

## Method

- wd1 reformulates the reinforcement learning objective by leveraging ==reverse-KL regularization==, deriving a ==weighted log-likelihood objective== that avoids explicit policy ratios.
- The approach significantly reduces computational overhead by requiring the approximation of only the ==current policy's likelihood==, unlike previous methods that need multiple policy likelihoods.
- It incorporates both ==positive and negative weights== derived from group-relative advantages, ensuring robust learning by amplifying beneficial samples and explicitly penalizing low-advantage ones.

## Results

- The method achieved up to a **100%** relative gain on planning tasks like Countdown (**51.2%** accuracy) and a **43%** relative gain on Sudoku (**25.2%** accuracy) compared to existing methods, without requiring supervised fine-tuning.
- Training costs were reduced by eliminating the two-hour supervised fine-tuning phase and achieving faster reinforcement learning training times (**82.16** seconds per global step vs. **103.5** seconds for baselines).
- It demonstrated superior sample efficiency with faster training reward increases and converged to shorter, more token-efficient output sequences on mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating the RL objective using reverse-KL regularization can bypass the need for explicit policy ratios, enabling stable policy optimization for dLLMs with intractable likelihoods.
> - Reducing the number of required likelihood approximations to only the current policy substantially boosts training efficiency and stability for dLLMs.
> - The explicit inclusion of negative weights for low-advantage samples is crucial for effective policy optimization, demonstrating the value of leveraging all training data.
