# wd1: Weighted Policy Optimization for Reasoning in Diffusion Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> wd1 introduces a weighted policy optimization approach for diffusion-based Large Language Models (dLLMs) that reformulates the reinforcement learning objective to require only a single likelihood approximation. This method achieves up to a 100% accuracy improvement on reasoning and planning benchmarks while reducing training costs by eliminating the need for supervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based Large Language Models (dLLMs) face challenges in applying reinforcement learning due to the intractability of their sequence-level likelihood functions.
- Existing RL methods for dLLMs require approximating likelihoods for multiple policies (current, old, reference), leading to high computational overhead.
- These multi-policy likelihood approximations, particularly in policy ratios used for importance sampling, introduce bias and training instability.

## Method

- wd1 reformulates the reinforcement learning objective by leveraging ==reverse-KL regularization==, deriving a ==weighted log-likelihood objective== that avoids explicit policy ratios.
- The approach significantly reduces computational overhead by requiring the approximation of only the ==current policy's likelihood==, unlike previous methods that need multiple policy likelihoods.
- It incorporates both ==positive and negative weights== derived from group-relative advantages, ensuring robust learning by amplifying beneficial samples and explicitly penalizing low-advantage ones.

## Results

- The method achieved up to a **100%** relative gain on planning tasks like Countdown (**51.2%** accuracy) and a **43%** relative gain on Sudoku (**25.2%** accuracy) compared to existing methods, without requiring supervised fine-tuning.
- Training costs were reduced by eliminating the two-hour supervised fine-tuning phase and achieving faster reinforcement learning training times (**82.16** seconds per global step vs. **103.5** seconds for baselines).
- It demonstrated superior sample efficiency with faster training reward increases and converged to shorter, more token-efficient output sequences on mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating the RL objective using reverse-KL regularization can bypass the need for explicit policy ratios, enabling stable policy optimization for dLLMs with intractable likelihoods.
> - Reducing the number of required likelihood approximations to only the current policy substantially boosts training efficiency and stability for dLLMs.
> - The explicit inclusion of negative weights for low-advantage samples is crucial for effective policy optimization, demonstrating the value of leveraging all training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) is undergoing significant development, with diffusion-based large language models (dLLMs) emerging as an alternative to traditional autoregressive (AR) models. AR models generate text sequentially, while dLLMs iteratively refine entire response sequences through a denoising process. This iterative approach has demonstrated advantages in inference efficiency, with some dLLMs achieving substantial speed-ups in generation while maintaining comparable quality to AR models. Open-weight dLLMs have also shown competitive performance on standard language benchmarks.

A critical area of research in LLMs involves fine-tuning these models using reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance their alignment and reasoning capabilities. This paradigm has been effective in improving AR models. However, applying RL to dLLMs presents specific challenges primarily due to the inherent intractability of their likelihood functions. Current methods, such as adaptations of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for dLLMs (referred to as diffusion-based GRPO or `d1`), rely on approximating these likelihoods for policy optimization. This reliance on approximations, particularly for computing policy ratios in importance sampling, introduces several issues. These include exponential amplification of approximation errors, high variance in RL objective estimation, and significant computational overhead, as likelihoods for the current, old, and reference policies must be estimated at each optimization step. These challenges become more pronounced with increased completion lengths and a higher number of diffusion steps. The present work addresses these limitations by proposing a novel policy optimization approach that aims to mitigate the reliance on explicit likelihood ratio approximations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the reasoning capabilities of diffusion-based large language models (dLLMs) through reinforcement learning (RL) while simultaneously addressing the computational and accuracy issues associated with existing RL fine-tuning methods for dLLMs.

The motivation stems from several identified challenges:
1. **Intractability of dLLM Likelihood Functions:** Unlike autoregressive (AR) models, the exact likelihood functions for dLLMs are intractable. This necessitates approximations, which introduces errors into the RL objective.
2. **Amplification of Approximation Errors:** Existing diffusion-based GRPO (d1) methods compute policy ratios using approximated log-likelihoods. This can lead to an exponential amplification of approximation errors, making the estimated RL objective less accurate and potentially unstable.
3. **High Variance in Policy Ratio Estimation:** The reliance on approximated likelihoods for importance sampling can result in high variance estimates of the policy ratio, further contributing to instability in RL training.
4. **Significant Computational Overhead:** Current approaches require approximating the likelihoods of three distinct policies—the current policy (π_θ), the old policy (π_old), and a reference policy (π_ref)—at every training step. This process is computationally expensive, exacerbating the training burden as sequence lengths and diffusion steps increase.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel approach with the following key objectives:
- **Develop a Ratio-Free Policy Optimization Method (`wd1`):** The core objective is to formulate an RL objective as a weighted log-likelihood that avoids explicit policy ratios, thereby eliminating the associated approximation errors and variance. This method aims to require only a single likelihood approximation for the current parametrized policy.
- **Establish Theoretical Soundness:** Formally demonstrate that the proposed `wd1` method can be interpreted as a combination of energy-guided discrete diffusion training and negative sample unlearning, providing a theoretical foundation for its operation.
- **Improve Reasoning Performance:** Empirically validate that `wd1` enhances the reasoning capabilities of dLLMs on various benchmarks, surpassing existing diffusion-based RL methods in accuracy.
- **Reduce Computational Cost:** Demonstrate that `wd1` requires lower computational resources (e.g., fewer FLOPs, NFEs, and training steps) compared to current baselines, making dLLM fine-tuning more efficient.
- **Extend to Stepwise Optimization (`wd1++`):** Develop an extended method that leverages intermediate completions generated during the denoising process of dLLMs to further boost performance and sample efficiency, aiming for state-of-the-art results on challenging math reasoning tasks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a more robust, efficient, and theoretically grounded framework for fine-tuning dLLMs using reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed `wd1` (weighted policy optimization for dLLMs), is designed to circumvent the issues of likelihood approximation and ratio computation in reinforcement learning for dLLMs. The approach is rooted in reformulating the RL objective as a weighted log-likelihood.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning as Weighted Log-Likelihood Maximization

The method begins by adopting a reverse-KL regularized policy optimization objective, which penalizes the deviation of the current policy π_θ from an old policy π_old, and also includes regularization with respect to a reference policy π_ref. The objective is expressed as:
max_θ E_{q,o} [A_π_old(q, o) - λD_KL(π_θ || π_old) - βD_KL(π_θ || π_ref)], where A_π_old is the advantage function, D_KL is the Kullback-Leibler divergence, and λ, β are regularization hyperparameters.
The optimal policy (π*) for this objective can be shown to have a closed form proportional to π_old^(λ/(λ+β)) * π_ref^(β/(λ+β)) * exp(A_π_old(q, ·)/(λ+β)).
Instead of directly optimizing the original objective, the authors propose minimizing the KL divergence between this optimal policy π* and the current policy π_θ, i.e., D_KL(π* || π_θ). This minimization is equivalent to a Weighted Log-Likelihood (WLL) loss function, which samples completions (o_i) from a geometric mixture policy π_ref_old (proportional to π_old^(λ/(λ+β)) * π_ref^(β/(λ+β))) and weights their log-likelihoods by a term proportional to exp(ψ * A_hat_i), where A_hat_i is a group-relative advantage and ψ is a scaling factor. This formulation inherently avoids explicit policy ratios.

#### 3.2. wd1: Fully Utilizing Completions

The initial WLL formulation had two limitations: it might not effectively utilize low-advantage (negative) samples due to vanishingly small weights, and it might paradoxically increase the likelihood of suboptimal samples if all samples in a batch yield identical, low rewards.
To address this, `wd1` introduces a modified objective that explicitly reinforces positive samples and penalizes negative samples. The `wd1` loss function is defined as:
L_wd1(θ) = E_{q,{o_i}} [ sum_i (-w+(q, o_i) + w-(q, o_i)) * log π_θ(o_i|q) ],
where w+ and w- are positive and negative weights, respectively.
- w+(q, o_i) = exp(ψ * A_hat_i) / sum_j(exp(ψ * A_hat_j))
- w-(q, o_i) = exp(-ψ * A_hat_i) / sum_j(exp(-ψ * A_hat_j))
The w- term acts as a penalty, minimizing the likelihood of low-advantage completions and generating negative gradients. This mechanism ensures that even when all completions in a group have similar rewards, the optimization process is guided, as w+ and w- terms balance.
For the practical implementation with dLLMs, the log-likelihood of completions, log π_θ(o|q), is approximated using a method similar to `d1` (Zhao et al., 2025). This involves approximating per-token log-likelihoods based on the clean token prediction from a fully masked response (x1) and a randomly masked prompt (q'), which is computationally efficient.

#### 3.3. wd1++: Stepwise Weighted Policy Optimization

To further enhance efficiency and performance, `wd1++` extends `wd1` by leveraging intermediate clean completions (x_0|l) generated at each denoising step (l) of the dLLM's iterative decoding process. Instead of using only the final predicted completion, `wd1++` expands the set of samples to include all intermediate clean predictions. The `wd1` loss objective is then averaged over these intermediate completions and denoising steps, effectively increasing the sample size and providing more granular feedback during training.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Insights

The paper provides a theoretical interpretation for `wd1`. The advantage-weighted log-likelihood (WLL component of `wd1`) is formally shown to be equivalent to training an energy-guided discrete diffusion model, where the energy function corresponds to the negative advantage. This leads to an Advantage-Weighted Denoising Cross-Entropy (AW-DCE) loss, which is a weighted form of the Denoising Cross Entropy (DCE) objective commonly used for dLLMs. Furthermore, the additional negative sample penalty term (w- ) in `wd1` is interpreted as a form of data unlearning, where the model minimizes the evidence lower bound (ELBO) on samples associated with lower advantage, effectively pushing the model away from undesirable outputs.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Model:** LLaDA-8B-Instruct (Nie et al., 2025a), fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).
- **Benchmarks:** GSM8K, MATH, Sudoku, and Countdown, covering various reasoning tasks.
- **Baselines:** `d1` (Zhao et al., 2025), Diffu-GRPO, and state-of-the-art methods like SDPO, TCR, and MDPO for comparative analysis.
- **Training Data:** Training splits from respective benchmarks; `wd1++` uses data subsampled from OpenR1.
- **Reward Functions:** Task-specific, multi-component reward functions are employed for each benchmark (e.g., XML structure, format, correctness for GSM8K; fraction of correctly filled cells for Sudoku).
- **Hyperparameters:** Key parameters like λ and β (for regularization) are set (e.g., β=0, λ=1 in practice), and ψ (for exponential weighting) is typically set to 1.
- **Evaluation:** Zero-shot pass@1 accuracy for different completion lengths (256, 512 tokens).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the LLaDA-8B-Instruct model demonstrate that `wd1` and its extension `wd1++` achieve improved performance and efficiency compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Superior Reasoning Ability

- **Comparison with d1:** `wd1` consistently outperformed the reproduced `d1` baseline, notably without requiring a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase, which `d1` utilizes.
 - On the Sudoku task (256 token length), `wd1` achieved 76.4% accuracy, representing a +58.8% improvement over `d1`.
 - For the Countdown task (256 token length), `wd1` reached 51.2% accuracy, a +16% improvement over `d1`.
 - On math problem-solving benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH500, `wd1` showed slightly higher accuracy.
- **State-of-the-Art with wd1++:** The extended method, `wd1++`, achieved state-of-the-art performance on challenging math benchmarks.
 - It reached 44.2% on MATH500 and 84.5% on GSM8K, outperforming concurrent RL for dLLM methods, including MDPO (43.4% on MATH500, 83.4% on GSM8K).

#### 4.2. Reduced Training Cost

- **Elimination of SFT:** `wd1` does not require a separate SFT stage, saving approximately two hours of initial training time, which is part of the `d1` pipeline.
- **Faster RL Training:** During the reinforcement learning phase, `wd1` exhibited lower average training time per step (81.16 sec/step) compared to `d1` (103.5 sec/step).
- **Lower Computational Load:** `wd1` demonstrated reduced FLOPs per training step (8.887 × 10^15 vs. 9.922 × 10^15 for `d1`) and fewer function evaluations (NFEs) for likelihood approximation. This efficiency stems from `wd1`'s avoidance of old policy likelihood approximations.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency (wd1++):** `wd1++` showcased rapid convergence, requiring 10 times fewer rollouts and substantially fewer training steps (e.g., 20 steps for best performance on MATH500/GSM8K) to achieve its superior results compared to baselines like MDPO (150 steps) and `d1` (7500 steps).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of SFT:** Ablations confirmed that SFT provided only marginal performance gains for `wd1`, indicating that `wd1` can effectively learn without this initial supervised phase.
- **Role of Negative Samples:** Removing the negative-weighted term (w-) from `wd1` (resulting in a WLL-like objective) significantly deteriorated performance (e.g., Sudoku accuracy dropped from 76.4% to 6.69%). This result underscores the importance of explicitly penalizing low-advantage completions for effective policy optimization.
- **Weight Balancing:** Experiments on the combined weight (cw) of positive and negative samples demonstrated that a balanced contribution (cw=0.5) was most effective. Deviations (e.g., cw=0.0 or cw=0.8) led to performance degradation, supporting the theoretical argument for balancing both terms.
- **ψ Coefficient:** Varying the ψ coefficient (scaling the exponential advantage weight) showed that while small values converged to similar rewards, an overly large ψ (e.g., 10.0) could negatively impact performance.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics

- `wd1` exhibited a faster increase in training reward compared to `d1`, suggesting higher sample efficiency.
- On math reasoning tasks, `wd1` converged to shorter average completion lengths than `d1`, implying improved token efficiency while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- On coding benchmarks (HumanEval and MBPP), `wd1` consistently showed improvements over the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of `wd1` and `wd1++` represents a development in the fine-tuning of diffusion-based large language models (dLLMs) using reinforcement learning (RL). This work directly addresses several limitations inherent in existing RL methods for dLLMs, particularly those stemming from the intractability of likelihood functions and the computational burden of likelihood approximations.

**Primary Significance:**
- **Addressing Likelihood Intractability:** The core contribution is the formulation of a ratio-free policy optimization objective. By reformulating the RL problem as a weighted log-likelihood, `wd1` minimizes the reliance on explicit policy ratios, thereby mitigating the exponential amplification of approximation errors and the high variance that characterize previous methods. This constitutes a more robust approach for models where exact likelihood computation is infeasible.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** `wd1` significantly reduces computational overhead by eliminating the need for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and requiring fewer likelihood approximations per RL training step. This efficiency is further amplified by `wd1++`, which leverages intermediate denoising steps and achieves state-of-the-art performance with considerably fewer rollouts and training steps. This aspect makes RL fine-tuning of dLLMs more scalable and accessible, especially for models with a large number of diffusion steps or longer sequence lengths.
- **Improved Reasoning Capabilities:** The empirical results demonstrate substantial accuracy gains on complex reasoning benchmarks such as Sudoku and Countdown, and state-of-the-art performance on mathematical reasoning tasks like MATH500 and GSM8K. These performance improvements suggest that `wd1` effectively steers dLLMs towards generating higher-quality, more accurate reasoning steps and final answers.
- **Novel Theoretical Interpretation:** The theoretical interpretation of `wd1` as energy-guided discrete diffusion training combined with negative sample unlearning provides insights into the underlying mechanisms. This connection to established concepts in diffusion models and data unlearning deepens the understanding of how RL can guide dLLM generation.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement of dLLM Research:** This work establishes a more efficient and effective paradigm for RL fine-tuning of dLLMs, potentially accelerating research and development in this emerging area. It provides a foundation for developing dLLMs with enhanced reasoning, factual accuracy, and alignment.
- **Broader Applicability:** The ratio-free, weighted log-likelihood approach is a general framework that might be applicable beyond dLLMs to other generative models with intractable likelihoods. This could foster innovation in various domains where RL is used to optimize complex generative processes.
- **Practical Deployment:** The reduced computational cost could facilitate the deployment of RL-tuned dLLMs in settings with limited resources or where rapid iteration is necessary. This could impact applications requiring high-quality, efficient text generation and reasoning.
- **Ethical Considerations:** As with any advanced LLM, improved reasoning capabilities carry potential ethical implications, including the generation of highly convincing but potentially biased or harmful content. Continued monitoring and development of robust alignment techniques remain important.

In conclusion, `wd1` offers a method for improving the reasoning capabilities of dLLMs while simultaneously addressing critical computational and theoretical challenges. Its effectiveness and efficiency contribute to making RL fine-tuning a more viable and powerful tool for dLLMs.
