# The SA-FARI Dataset: Segment Anything in Footage of Animals for Recognition and Identification

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed SA-FARI, the largest open-source dataset for multi-animal tracking (MAT) in wild environments, comprising 46 hours of video with over 940,000 manually verified segmentation masks across 99 species. Fine-tuning vision-language models like SAM 3 on SA-FARI significantly improved MAT performance, achieving a +32.9 cgF1 gain over the base model.

## Problem

- Existing multi-animal tracking (MAT) datasets for wild animals lacked sufficient scale, species diversity, geographical coverage, and high-quality, manually verified segmentation masks, hindering the development of generalizable MAT models.
- The increasing volume of data from camera traps and other in-situ sensors overwhelms human processing capabilities, creating a bottleneck for wildlife conservation efforts.
- Current datasets often focus on a limited number of species, short temporal windows, or rely on bounding box annotations rather than precise segmentation masks, leading to inaccuracies.

## Method

- The **SA-FARI** dataset was compiled from 11,609 camera trap videos (46 hours) collected over approximately ten years from 741 diverse sampling locations across four continents.
- The dataset provides 99 animal species categories, 16,224 individual masklet identities, and 942,702 exhaustive spatio-temporal segmentation masks and bounding boxes, meticulously annotated through a human-in-the-loop verification process.
- A rigorous train/test split strategy was implemented to maximize species diversity in the test set while ensuring no geographical overlap between training and testing camera trap locations.

## Results

- Fine-tuning SAM 3 on SA-FARI achieved the highest performance in species-specific tracking, with 46.9 cgF1, 68.1 pHOTA, and 84.6 TETA, representing a +32.9 cgF1 gain over the base SAM 3 model.
- Using a species-agnostic "animal" prompt, SAM 3 (SA-FARI) outperformed vision-only tracking methods, achieving 71.1 IDF1 and 83.7 HOTA, surpassing the closest competitor (MD+BoostSort++) by +23.9 IDF1.
- Performance analysis on test subsets showed that tracking large masklets was +29.2 pHOTA higher than small masklets (81.4 vs. 52.2), and challenging subsets (occlusions/motion) experienced reduced detection (pHOTA-Det 4.1 points lower) and association (pHOTA-Ass 9.3 points lower) accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The scale, diversity, and high-quality, manually verified segmentation masks of SA-FARI are crucial for training and evaluating generalizable multi-animal tracking models that can adapt to various wild animal populations and ecological contexts.
> - Incorporating domain-specific data from SA-FARI during training leads to substantial performance improvements for state-of-the-art vision-language models in MAT tasks.
> - Challenges persist in tracking small, heavily occluded, or rapidly moving animals, highlighting specific areas for future model development and targeted annotation efforts.
