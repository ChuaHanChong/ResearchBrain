# Position: Agentic Evolution is the Path to Evolving LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces "Agentic Evolution" as a new paradigm for Large Language Model (LLM) systems to autonomously adapt and improve in dynamic real-world environments. This framework, called A-EVOLVE, enables LLMs to continuously diagnose failures, strategically update their capabilities through verified, modular artifacts, and consistently enhance task performance, allowing smaller models to surpass larger static counterparts.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with the "train-deploy environment gap," leading to performance degradation in dynamic, open-ended real-world scenarios.
- Existing adaptation methods lack "strategic agency" to intelligently diagnose failures and decide what, when, and how to change, often risking catastrophic forgetting or accumulating unverified experience.
- Centralized retraining for continuous adaptation faces challenges with privacy constraints and high computational costs, especially for recurrent failures.

## Method

- Introduces ==Agentic Evolution==, defining it as a deployment-time ability for an LLM system to autonomously improve its capabilities through interaction without direct human intervention.
- Develops **A-EVOLVE**, a framework where an explicit ==evolver agent== diagnoses failures, plans targeted updates to a persistent, editable "artifact state" (knowledge, tools, validation assets), and commits them via a ==governance gate==.
- Separates system operation into a "Solve Phase" for task execution and an "Evolve Phase" for asynchronous capability improvement, ensuring dedicated compute for adaptation and relying on goal-oriented, autonomous, and compositional principles.

## Results

- **A-EVOLVE** achieved substantial improvements in task goal completion (TGC) and average passed tests (APT) across various LLM solver backbones, e.g., Claude Haiku 4.5 with A-EVOLVE reached **64%** TGC compared to **32%** for the vanilla solver.
- Smaller solver models augmented with **A-EVOLVE** matched or exceeded the performance of larger vanilla models; for instance, Claude Haiku 4.5 with A-EVOLVE outperformed a vanilla Claude Sonnet 4.
- Performance consistently improved with increased evolution-time compute (evolution steps) and larger evolver model sizes, with the `Verify` component identified as critical for preventing regressions and ensuring stable long-term adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating evolution as an agentic decision-making problem, rather than a fixed pipeline, allows for strategic, goal-directed, and autonomous adaptation to address underlying failure causes.
> - System improvements can be made through structured, modular, and verifiable artifacts, which amortize expensive reasoning into reusable, persistent capabilities.
> - A new "evolution-scaling hypothesis" suggests that the capacity for adaptation scales predictably with the compute allocated to the evolution process, offering a new axis for AI development complementary to training and inference compute.
