# Synthesizing Long-Term 3D Human Motion and Interaction in 3D Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical generative framework synthesizes long-term 3D human motion inside real 3D scenes. A PointNet-conditioned CVAE first samples plausible SMPL-X bodies at sub-goal positions; RouteNet then predicts translations and rotations between sub-goals and PoseNet fills in body and hand poses; a final geometry optimization enforces foot stability, collision, contact and smoothness. It beats improved motion-synthesis and affordance baselines on reconstruction, physical plausibility and human naturalness ratings.

## Problem

- Motion synthesis work models realistic dynamics but rarely conditions on scene geometry, so generated motion ignores collisions, contacts and affordances of the surrounding environment.
- Scene affordance research predicts only single static human poses or skeletons placed in a scene, barely addressing physically plausible motion sequences over time.
- Environment-aware motion methods rely on predefined objects, primitive motions or hand-crafted simple scenes, and key-frame in-betweening only covers transient motion with small position change.

## Method

- A ==conditional VAE== decoder, conditioned on a segmentation-pretrained **PointNet** scene feature plus shape, position and orientation, samples diverse static SMPL-X bodies at each sub-goal.
- **RouteNet** predicts inter-sub-goal translation and 6D rotation with a ==bi-directional LSTM== and scene feature; **PoseNet**, trained after RouteNet is frozen, predicts body and hand poses along that route.
- A gradient-descent optimization over mesh parameters combines a label-free stable-foot term, ==signed-distance-field== collision energy, ==Geman-McClure== contact energy and vertex smoothness, stitching short clips into long motion.

## Results

- On PROX, the full model reaches MPJPE **191.6 mm** and MPVPE **180.9 mm** without optimization, versus **226.2**/**217.6** for a single end-to-end BaseNet and **294.2**/**278.2** for the CVAE-plus-route baseline.
- Physical plausibility improves to **99.91** non-collision on PROX and **97.53** contact score on Matterport3D, exceeding both optimized baselines; neighbour vertex-to-vertex distance is **8.25** versus **21.78** and **21.05**.
- Mechanical Turk naturalness ratings favor the method at every horizon, **3.39**, **3.55** and **3.65** for 2, 4 and 6 seconds, against **2.90-3.26** for the strongest baseline and **3.60-3.92** pseudo-ground-truth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing long-horizon generation into sub-goal anchoring plus short-segment synthesis reduces prediction uncertainty and makes segment stitching trivial, since one segment's end equals the next's start.
> - Predicting global translation and rotation in the trajectory module, then conditioning pose on that trajectory, beats a single joint network, showing factorization helps sequence generation.
> - Post-hoc geometric optimization slightly raises reconstruction error yet substantially improves perceived naturalness and contact realism, so reconstruction metrics alone mislead for physical plausibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic 3D human motion is a recognized area of research with applications in virtual reality, video games, and human-robot interaction. Prior work in this domain has largely focused on synthesizing visually appealing and natural pose sequences. Approaches have included optimization-based statistical models and deep neural networks. However, a common limitation in many of these motion synthesis methods is the neglect of explicit modeling of human-scene interactions and scene affordances. The generated motions often do not account for the environment in a physically plausible manner, potentially leading to collisions or unnatural interactions with objects.

Concurrently, another line of research has investigated 3D scene affordance, focusing on understanding the "opportunities for interactions" that a scene provides. These studies typically address how static human poses or gestures relate to a 3D environment, such as determining where a person might stand or sit. However, these affordance reasoning methods generally do not extend to synthesizing dynamic, long-term human motion. They typically generate only single, static human poses, which limits their applicability for dynamic scenarios.

The present work identifies a gap between these two research areas: the synthesis of realistic, long-term 3D human motion that explicitly incorporates detailed human-scene interactions and scene affordance reasoning. Existing motion synthesis techniques often lack scene awareness, while affordance learning methods are typically confined to static poses. This paper aims to bridge this gap by integrating scene context and interaction constraints into a generative framework for dynamic human motion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize long-term 3D human motion that is both realistic and physically plausible, explicitly conditioning the motion on the structure and affordances of a 3D scene. The authors motivate this objective by highlighting two main challenges in current human motion synthesis:
1. **Generating realistic motion in the long-term:** Synthesizing extended sequences of natural-looking human movement poses a significant challenge due to the complexity and variability of human actions over time.
2. **Modeling human-scene interaction and affordance:** Integrating an understanding of how humans interact with their environment (e.g., avoiding obstacles, sitting on furniture) into motion generation is crucial for realism, yet it is often overlooked by existing methods.

To address these challenges, the authors propose to bridge the domains of human motion synthesis and scene affordance learning. The specific problem setting considered is the generation of human motion between a given start and an end position located far apart within a 3D scene. This requires not only generating smooth transitions but also ensuring that the intermediate actions and interactions with the scene are natural and physically coherent. The work is motivated by the need for more advanced and realistic character animations in applications such as virtual reality, video games, and human-robot interaction, where dynamic, context-aware human behavior is essential.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a hierarchical generative framework designed to synthesize long-term 3D human motion conditioned on 3D scene structure, followed by an optimization process to refine realism.

#### 3.1. Representation

- **Scene:** Represented as a mesh, with vertices (v_s) and faces (f_s). For neural network processing, scene point clouds (v_s) are used.
- **Human Body:** Modeled using modified sequential SMPL-X parameters. This includes translation (t ∈ R³), a 6D continuous rotation (r ∈ R⁶) for global orientation, body shape (β ∈ R¹⁰), body pose (p ∈ R³²), and hand pose (h ∈ R²⁴). The SMPL-X model is differentiable, which facilitates geometric constraints and realistic contact modeling.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Generative Framework

The framework operates in two levels to generate long-term motion between a distant start and end position:

##### 3.2.1. Static Human Body Synthesis on Sub-Goals

- **Goal:** To generate plausible human body poses at predefined sub-goal positions along the long trajectory, as well as at the start and end points. These sub-goals divide the long trajectory into shorter segments.
- **Model:** A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) is employed.
- **Inputs:** Body shape (β), a sub-goal's location (t) and orientation (r), and the scene point cloud (v_s).
- **Process:**
 - A PointNet extracts a 256-dimension feature (F_s) from the scene point cloud.
 - This scene feature is concatenated with {β, t, r} to form an integrated conditional feature (F_hs).
 - **Encoder:** Takes ground-truth human body parameters (M₀), concatenates them with F_hs, and outputs the mean (μ) and variance (σ²) of a Gaussian distribution for a latent code (z).
 - **Decoder:** Takes the sampled latent code (z) and the conditional feature (F_hs) to predict the desired human body parameters (SMPL-X).
 - **Training:** Uses a reconstruction loss against ground-truth body parameters and a KL-Divergence loss between the latent distribution and a standard Gaussian. Contact and collision constraints (motivated by) are also incorporated.
- **Inference:** Only the decoder is used. Latent codes are sampled from a standard Gaussian distribution, enabling the generation of diverse human bodies at the same sub-goal position.

##### 3.2.2. Motion Synthesis between Sub-Goals

- **Goal:** To generate short-term motion sequences between consecutive sub-goals.
- **Models:** Two sequentially connected networks: RouteNet and PoseNet.
 - **RouteNet (R):** Predicts the locations and orientations (the route) between two sub-goals.
 - **Inputs:** Start {t₀, r₀} and end {t_k, r_k} locations/orientations, and the scene point cloud (v_s).
 - **Architecture:** Uses a PointNet for scene features (F_rs) and a bi-directional LSTM to process start/end locations/orientations. These features are concatenated and passed through fully connected layers to output the intermediate route {t̂₁:k-1, r̂₁:k-1}.
 - **Loss:** `L1` distance between predicted and ground-truth {t, r} parameters.
 - **PoseNet (P):** Predicts human body poses for each location along the route generated by RouteNet.
 - **Inputs:** Start {p₀, h₀} and end {p_k, h_k} poses, the scene point cloud (v_s), and the predicted route {t̂₁:k-1, r̂₁:k-1} from RouteNet.
 - **Architecture:** Uses another PointNet for scene features (F_ps) and a bi-directional LSTM for start/end pose parameters. These features are concatenated with the route features and passed through fully connected layers to output intermediate pose parameters {p̂₁:k-1, ĥ₁:k-1}.
 - **Loss:** `L1` distance between predicted and ground-truth {p, h} parameters.
- **Process:** RouteNet is trained first, then its weights are fixed while training PoseNet. The generated SMPL-X parameters (including given β) form the mesh sequence for short-term motion. These short-term motions are then connected to form the long-term motion.

#### 3.3. Optimization

A geometric optimization process is applied to the generated motion sequences to enhance realism and physical plausibility, and to ensure continuity between concatenated short-term motions. This optimization adjusts the translation (t), global orientation (r), body pose (p), and hand pose (h) parameters.

- **Foot Location Constraints (E_foot):** Identifies a "stable" foot in sub-sequences of motion. The average location of the stable foot within each sub-sequence is calculated, and the foot is encouraged to remain close to this average, promoting stability. This differs from methods assuming a flat floor or predefined pivot feet.
- **Environment Constraints:**
 - **Collision (E_col):** Minimizes the intersection of the human mesh with the 3D scene surfaces. This is achieved by using the negative signed distance field (Ψ⁻_s) of the scene, encouraging body vertices to remain in the positive level-set.
 - **Contact (E_cont):** Encourages predefined body vertices (e.g., feet, posterior for sitting) to come into contact with the scene, simulating physical support. A Geman-McClure error function is used to downweight distant scene vertices.
- **Motion Smoothness (E_smooth):** Minimizes the `L2` distance between the vertices of consecutive human meshes (v_M_i and v_M_i+1), promoting smooth transitions between frames.
- **Combined Objective:** The optimization minimizes a weighted sum of these four error terms using gradient descent with the Adam optimizer. The optimization is performed in two stages, prioritizing environmental constraints and smoothness first, then improving motion plausibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using the PROX dataset for training and both PROX (unseen scenes) and MP3D datasets for evaluation.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

- **Reconstruction Error:** `L1` distance for translation (t), orientation (r), and pose (p). MPJPE (Mean Per Joint Position Error) and MPVPE (Mean Per Vertex Position Error) in millimeters. "Neighbour v2v distance" measures `L2` distance between input and generated start/end bodies, indicating connection quality for long-term motion.
- **Naturalness Metrics:**
 - **In-environment evaluation:** Non-collision score and a modified contact score to assess physical plausibility.
 - **Human evaluation:** Amazon Mechanical Turk users scored the naturalness of generated motions (1-5 scale) compared to baselines and pseudo-ground truth.

#### 4.2. Baselines and Ablative Studies

The proposed method was compared against adapted state-of-the-art baselines and several ablative variants:
- **Route+CVAE:** Combines the paper's RouteNet with a CVAE (inspired by) to generate a sequence of static bodies.
- **HSNMS:** A hierarchical motion synthesis method, adapted with SMPL-X and optimization for scene interaction.
- **Ablative baselines:**
 - `RouteNet(t)+PoseNet`: PoseNet generates pose without predicted route.
 - `RouteNet(t+r)+PoseNet`: RouteNet predicts both translation and rotation, PoseNet generates pose.
 - `BaseNet`: An end-to-end network predicting route and pose jointly.
 - Variants with/without scene information.
- Optimization (`w/ opt`) variants were also tested for baselines and the proposed method.

#### 4.3. Main Findings

- **Reconstruction Accuracy (Table 1):**
 - The proposed method generally achieved lower reconstruction errors (lower `L1` for translation, orientation, pose, and lower MPJPE/MPVPE) compared to the baselines (Route+CVAE, HSNMS).
 - Optimization (`w/ opt`) tended to slightly increase reconstruction error for all methods, but improved naturalness.
 - Ablative studies indicated that incorporating the predicted route into PoseNet improves performance, and RouteNet predicting both translation and rotation is more accurate.
 - Using scene information consistently improved synthesis accuracy across all ablative baselines.
- **Connection Quality (`Neighbour v2v distance`, Table 4):**
 - The proposed method demonstrated the lowest `neighbour v2v distance` (8.25 w/ opt, 7.31 w/o opt) among all methods, indicating better continuity between short-term motion segments for long-term synthesis.
- **In-Environment Naturalness (Table 2):**
 - The proposed method (Ours w/ opt) achieved a non-collision score of 99.91% on PROX and 99.30% on MP3D, and contact scores of 99.35% (PROX) and 97.53% (MP3D), performing comparably or superior to baselines.
 - The optimization process significantly improved the environmental adaptability (collision and contact scores) for all methods, including baselines.
- **Human Evaluation (Table 3):**
 - For 2-second, 4-second, and 6-second motions, the proposed method received the highest average naturalness scores (3.39 to 3.65) from human evaluators compared to baselines on both PROX and MP3D datasets.
 - The scores for the proposed method were close to the pseudo-ground truth (3.60 to 3.92), suggesting a high degree of perceived realism.
- **Qualitative Results (Figures 5, 6, 7, 8, 9):**
 - Visual comparisons showed that the proposed method produced more natural motions with consistent sitting down actions and better human-scene interaction, avoiding issues like feet penetrating the ground observed in baselines.
 - The optimization process visibly improved contact with surfaces (e.g., feet on the floor) and motion stability.
- **Generalization and Diversity (Figures 8, 9, 13, 14):**
 - The model demonstrated generalization ability to the unseen MP3D dataset, generating plausible motions even for challenging cases like jumping on a sofa.
 - The hierarchical framework, combined with CVAE for sub-goals, allowed for generating diverse long-term motions by sampling different latent variables for sub-goal bodies and varying body shapes (β).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a notable limitation in 3D human motion synthesis by integrating explicit modeling of human-scene interaction and affordance reasoning. The introduction of a hierarchical generative framework, combining deep learning models for pose and route generation with a geometric optimization process, contributes to the field by producing long-term 3D human motions that are demonstrated to be both realistic and physically plausible within complex 3D environments.

The use of the differentiable SMPL-X model for human representation, coupled with detailed geometric and physical constraints in the optimization stage, allows for more accurate collision avoidance and contact modeling. The hierarchical approach, which first establishes sub-goal poses and then interpolates motion between them, offers a method for managing the complexity of long-term motion synthesis and enhancing diversity.

The findings, supported by quantitative metrics and human evaluations, indicate that the proposed method surpasses previous approaches in generating natural motions with appropriate scene interactions. This work has potential implications for various applications, including:
- **Virtual Reality and Video Games:** Enabling more immersive and believable character animations that interact dynamically and realistically with virtual environments.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Providing tools for simulating and predicting complex human behaviors in shared spaces, which can inform robot navigation and interaction strategies.
- **Animation and Content Creation:** Offering automated or semi-automated tools for generating intricate human motion sequences, reducing manual effort for animators.

By demonstrating improved synthesis of long-term, scene-aware human motion, this research advances the capabilities for creating digital human surrogates that behave more consistently with physical reality and environmental context.
