# Neurosymbolic Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Neurosymbolic Diffusion Models (NESYDMS), a framework that integrates masked diffusion models with symbolic programs to learn expressive distributions over symbolic concepts and task outputs. The approach achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on high-dimensional reasoning tasks like visual path planning (97.40% on 30x30 grids) and demonstrates improved concept calibration for better reasoning shortcut awareness.

## Problem

- Standard probabilistic neurosymbolic predictors rely on a conditional independence assumption between extracted symbolic concepts, limiting their ability to model complex interactions and quantify uncertainty.
- This conditional independence leads to 'reasoning shortcuts' (RSs), where models achieve high output accuracy with incorrect underlying concepts, resulting in overconfident predictions and poor generalization to out-of-distribution data.
- Existing methods to overcome the independence assumption often face scalability issues, requiring exponential time or a large number of additional parameters, making them impractical for complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- The authors introduce ==Neurosymbolic Diffusion Models (NESYDMS)==, which leverage ==masked diffusion models (MDMs)== as neural concept extractors to explicitly model dependencies between symbolic concepts.
- A novel ==continuous-time variational lower bound (NELBO)== is derived, enabling scalable training by incorporating symbolic programs and decomposing the loss into concept unmasking, output unmasking (via Weighted Model Counts), and variational entropy terms.
- A sampling-based approach using the ==REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO) gradient estimator== is employed to approximate gradients for intractable components like WMC, coupled with self-normalised importance sampling for latent concept inference.

## Results

- NESYDMS achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on high-dimensional visual path planning tasks, reaching **9**7.40%** ± 1.23** on challenging 30x30 grids.
- The model demonstrated significantly improved concept accuracy and calibration (lower ECE) on reasoning shortcut (RS) benchmarks like RSBench, with an ECE of **4.18%** for MNIST Half compared to **36.81%** for prior state-of-the-art BEARS.
- On the real-world BDD-OIA (autonomous driving) task, NESYDMS showed better predictive performance (MF1 of **62.63%**) and improved concept calibration (ECE of **21.72%**) compared to independence-assuming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Masked diffusion models can effectively capture global dependencies between symbolic concepts while maintaining local tractability, allowing for scalable integration into neurosymbolic frameworks.
> - Explicitly modeling concept dependencies and incorporating entropy regularization in the loss function significantly improves reasoning shortcut awareness and the calibration of concept predictions.
> - A principled continuous-time variational lower bound can be formulated for neurosymbolic diffusion, providing a robust theoretical foundation for learning expressive, non-factorized distributions under hard symbolic constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of **Neurosymbolic AI (NeSy)**, a rapidly evolving field aiming to combine the perceptual strengths of neural networks with the reasoning capabilities of symbolic AI. Specifically, it addresses critical limitations within probabilistic NeSy predictors, which learn neural networks to extract high-level "concepts" from raw inputs, subsequently used in symbolic programs for reasoning and prediction.

The broader landscape of NeSy methods is diverse, encompassing approaches based on fuzzy logics, probabilistic logics, and neurosymbolic programming languages. A common architectural choice in many existing probabilistic NeSy predictors is the assumption of **conditional independence** between the extracted symbolic concepts given the input. While this assumption simplifies probabilistic reasoning and enables efficient computation using techniques like Weighted Model Counting (WMC) or knowledge compilation, it severely hampers the models' ability to:
1. **Model interactions and dependencies** between concepts.
2. **Properly quantify and express uncertainty** over alternative concept assignments.
3. **Generalize reliably** to out-of-distribution (OOD) data.
4. **Detect and mitigate "reasoning shortcuts" (RSs)**, where a model achieves high output accuracy by learning incorrect underlying concepts. RSs lead to overconfident predictions and can dramatically harm performance on unseen data.

Previous attempts to overcome the independence assumption often involve mixture models, probabilistic circuits, or probabilistic programming languages. However, these methods frequently face challenges with **scalability**, requiring exponential time for compilation or demanding many additional parameters, making them impractical for high-dimensional or complex reasoning tasks. Autoregressive models, while expressive, also struggle with the required marginalization over concepts in NeSy predictors.

"Neurosymbolic Diffusion Models (NESYDMS)" positions itself by recognizing that **discrete diffusion models**, particularly Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs), offer a unique advantage. They achieve expressiveness by modeling global dependencies through an iterative denoising process, yet crucially, each step of this process involves predicting a discrete distribution that *fully factorizes* (i.e., locally assumes independence). This "local independence for global dependencies" paradigm is key to integrating them into NeSy predictors without sacrificing scalability. By leveraging the local tractability of MDMs, NESYDMS aims to bridge the gap between scalable but limited NeSy methods and expressive but often intractable ones, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in reliable and generalizable neurosymbolic AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **overcome the inherent limitations of the conditional independence assumption** prevalent in standard neurosymbolic (NeSy) predictors. This assumption, while enabling computational efficiency, restricts these models from effectively modeling interactions and uncertainty among symbolic concepts, often leading to overconfident predictions and poor generalization, especially in out-of-distribution scenarios.

The core motivation stems from the critical problem of **reasoning shortcuts (RSs)**. As highlighted by prior work from the authors and others, NeSy predictors can "fail silently" by learning incorrect concepts while still achieving high accuracy on output labels. This ambiguity in concept assignments, when indistinguishable from input-output mappings alone, compromises the reliability and trustworthiness of AI systems. To address this, the authors contend that NeSy predictors **must express uncertainty over concepts** consistent with the data, allowing for informed user intervention, better trust calibration, or targeted data acquisition when the model is uncertain.

To achieve this, the specific objectives for NESYDMS are:
1. **Develop a new class of NeSy predictors** that utilizes discrete diffusion models to explicitly model dependencies between symbolic concepts.
2. **Enable scalable learning** for these expressive models by reusing the local independence assumption of diffusion processes at each step, thereby capturing global concept dependencies and uncertainty.
3. **Achieve state-of-the-art accuracy** among NeSy predictors on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks, particularly in complex, high-dimensional reasoning problems like visual path planning and rule-based autonomous driving.
4. **Demonstrate strong calibration** of concept predictions, indicating enhanced RS-awareness and improved reliability compared to existing methods.
5. **Formulate a principled loss function** for neurosymbolic diffusion that effectively incorporates symbolic programs and allows for scalable training, overcoming the intractability of marginalizing over all possible concepts.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for more reliable, interpretable, and robust AI systems that can not only make accurate predictions but also understand and communicate their own uncertainties regarding the underlying symbolic reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Neurosymbolic Diffusion Models (NESYDMS)" introduces a novel framework that integrates masked diffusion models (MDMs) with symbolic programs to learn expressive distributions over concepts and labels. The methodology is structured around several key innovations:

1. **Model Setup (MDM for Concepts and Outputs):**
 - NESYDMS employs MDMs to learn a conditional concept unmasking model, $p_\theta(\tilde{c}_0 | c_t, x)$, which predicts completely unmasked concepts ($\tilde{c}_0$) given an input image ($x$) and a partially masked concept representation ($c_t$). This neural network acts as the concept extractor.
 - A symbolic program, $\phi: [V_c]^C \to [V_y]^Y$, maps the extracted concepts ($\tilde{c}_0$) to the output labels ($\tilde{y}_0$).
 - The framework defines forward diffusion processes for both concepts ($q(c_t | c_s)$) and outputs ($q(y_t | y_s)$), which progressively mask dimensions.
 - A reverse process $p_\theta(y_s | c_s, y_t, x)$ is defined for outputs by reusing the concept unmasking model and marginalizing over concepts consistent with the (partially masked) output. A crucial aspect is the introduction of $\phi_{y_t}$, a modified program that respects the "carry-over unmasking assumption" by ensuring unmasked dimensions in $y_t$ remain fixed.

2. **Loss Function (Neurosymbolic ELBO - NELBO):**
 - The authors derive a novel continuous-time variational lower bound (NELBO) for the data log-likelihood $p_\theta(y_0 | x)$. This is a significant theoretical contribution, extending standard MDM ELBOs to non-factorized distributions, which is necessary for the output reverse process.
 - The NELBO consists of three components:
 - **Concept Unmasking Loss ($L_c$):** Analogous to the standard MDM unmasking loss, it encourages the model to reconstruct sampled concepts ($c_0$) from their partially masked versions ($c_t$). Since ground-truth concepts are latent, $c_0$ is sampled from a variational distribution.
 - **Output Unmasking Loss ($L_y$):** This term is a sum of multiple Weighted Model Counts (WMCs), one for each dimension of the output label $y_0$. It uses the concept unmasking model to weight concepts and ensures they are consistent with the true output via the symbolic program $\phi$. This loss is crucial for leveraging the symbolic knowledge.
 - **Variational Entropy ($L_{H[q]}$):** Maximizing the entropy of the variational distribution $q_\theta(c_0 | y_0, x)$ encourages the model to explore multiple concept assignments consistent with the observed output, thereby promoting reasoning shortcut (RS) awareness.

3. **Variational Posterior for Latent Concepts:**
 - To handle the latent nature of concepts ($c_0$), NESYDMS employs a variational distribution $q_\theta(c_0 | y_0, x)$, which shares parameters with the MDM.
 - Sampling from this posterior is challenging due to the hard symbolic constraint. The authors adapt a controlled generation method, approximating $q_\theta(\tilde{c}_0 | c_t, y_0, x)$ via a soft constraint $r_\beta(\tilde{c}_0 | y_0)$ (an exponential penalty for constraint violations) and using self-normalised importance sampling (SNIS) with $K$ samples. In practice, this amounts to sampling $K$ concepts and choosing the one that minimizes constraint violations.

4. **Loss Optimization and Scalability:**
 - Training involves gradient descent on the NELBO. The concept unmasking loss $L_c$ is tractable.
 - For the output unmasking loss $L_y$, which involves #P-hard WMC computations, the authors propose a **sampling-based approach** using the REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO) gradient estimator. This estimator approximates the WMC gradients by sampling multiple concepts and evaluating their consistency with $y_0$, allowing for a flexible trade-off between variance and computation. The decomposition of $L_y$ into a sum over output dimensions aids in structuring the search for consistent concepts.
 - The variational entropy term $L_{H[q]}$ is difficult to compute exactly. NESYDMS approximates it using either a conditional 1-step entropy (if a tractable constraint circuit for $\phi$ is available) or an unconditional 1-step entropy.
 - The indirect gradient stemming from sampling $c_0$ from the variational distribution is ignored to simplify optimization and mitigate high variance.

5. **Sampling and Inference:**
 - For making predictions (inferring $y$ from $x$), NESYDMS samples $L$ concept trajectories ($c_0^l$) from the trained MDM, applies the symbolic program $\phi$ to each, and then uses a **majority voting strategy** to determine the final output $\hat{y}$.
 - Concept sampling can be performed either with a first-hitting sampler (for smaller concept spaces) or a T-step time-discretization of the reverse process.

In essence, the methodology elegantly combines the iterative, locally independent nature of masked diffusion with gradient estimation techniques and variational inference to enable scalable, expressive, and RS-aware neurosymbolic learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of NESYDMS focuses on two key research questions: (RQ1) scalability to high-dimensional reasoning problems, and (RQ2) improvement in reasoning shortcut awareness compared to independent models.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Scalability of NESYDM

To assess scalability, NESYDMS was tested on tasks with high combinatorial complexity:

- **Multidigit MNIST Addition:**
 - This task involves summing two numbers, each comprising N MNIST digits. Concepts are the individual digits.
 - NESYDMS achieved competitive accuracy with state-of-the-art approximate NeSy methods like A-NeSI and exact methods (e.g., DEEPSOFTLOG, PLIA) for both N=4 and N=15 digits. For N=15, NESYDMS (77.29% ± 1.40) performed comparably to A-NeSI (76.84% ± 2.82) and PLIA (79.00% ± 0.73).
 - This demonstrates that NESYDMS's enhanced expressivity does not compromise performance or scalability on traditional NeSy benchmarks where concept dependencies are not a primary issue.

- **Visual Path Planning:**
 - This task requires predicting a minimum cost path on a grid (N×N) from a visual map, where concepts are discrete costs for each cell. The symbolic program is Dijkstra's algorithm.
 - NESYDMS significantly **outperformed all baselines** on the challenging 30×30 grid problem (combinatorial space of $5^{900}$), achieving an accuracy of **97.40% ± 1.23**. This is a substantial improvement over I-MLE (93.7% ± 0.6), EXAL (80.85% ± 3.83), and A-NeSI (17.13% ± 16.32, or 67.57% ± 36.76 with RLOO).
 - On the 12×12 problem, NESYDMS (99.41% ± 0.06) achieved state-of-the-art accuracy, comparable to A-NeSI + RLOO (98.96% ± 1.33) but with **much lower variance**, highlighting its reliability.
 - These results strongly support the claim that NESYDMS can scale to and effectively solve high-dimensional reasoning problems beyond the capabilities of current NeSy predictors.

#### 4.2. RQ2: RS-awareness of NESYDM

To evaluate RS-awareness, NESYDMS was tested on the RSBench dataset, which includes problems prone to reasoning shortcuts:

- **MNIST Half and MNIST Even-Odd:**
 - These are MNIST addition variations designed to make concept disambiguation impossible from input-output data alone, requiring models to express uncertainty to be RS-aware.
 - NESYDMS achieved significantly **better concept accuracy** (Acc$_c$) both in-distribution (e.g., MNIST Half: 71.16% vs. 43.26% for BEARS) and out-of-distribution (e.g., MNIST Half: 62.76% vs. 39.49% for BEARS) compared to both independence-assuming baselines (PNP$_{\perp \perp}$, SL$_{\perp \perp}$) and the RS-aware ensemble BEARS.
 - Crucially, NESYDMS demonstrated **much better concept calibration** (lower Expected Calibration Error, ECE$_c$), especially when using conditional entropy regularization. For instance, on MNIST Half, ECE$_{c,ID}$ was 4.18% for NESYDMS (conditional entropy) compared to 36.81% for BEARS and 69.40% for PNP$_{\perp \perp}$. This indicates that NESYDMS can properly model uncertainty over ambiguous concepts, a hallmark of RS-awareness. Output accuracy (Acc$_y$) remained high and competitive.

- **BDD-OIA (Rule-based Autonomous Driving):**
 - A real-world task where a model predicts car actions from dashcam images using rules and extracted concepts.
 - NESYDMS showed **better predictive performance on outputs (MF1$_y$)** (62.63% ± 0.53) than BEARS (60.80% ± 0.11).
 - It also significantly improved **concept performance (MF1$_c$)** and **calibration (ECE$_c$)** compared to PNP$_{\perp \perp}$ (e.g., ECE$_c$: 21.72% vs. 38.89%).

In summary, NESYDMS successfully balances high predictive accuracy with robust uncertainty quantification and concept calibration, proving its effectiveness in addressing reasoning shortcuts across various complex neurosymbolic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Neurosymbolic Diffusion Models (NESYDMS) represents a significant advancement in the field of Neurosymbolic AI, with profound implications for the development of more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Bridging Scalability and Expressivity:** NESYDMS is the first method to effectively integrate masked diffusion models as neural extractors within neurosymbolic predictors. This is a crucial breakthrough, as it simultaneously addresses the long-standing challenge of modeling complex dependencies between symbolic concepts (achieving expressivity) while maintaining computational tractability and scalability. Previous expressive methods often struggled with scaling to high-dimensional problems, while scalable methods typically made restrictive independence assumptions.
2. **Enhanced Reliability and RS-Awareness:** By explicitly modeling concept dependencies and quantifying uncertainty, NESYDMS directly tackles the critical issue of "reasoning shortcuts" (RSs). Its demonstrated superior calibration (lower ECE) means the model is better at recognizing and expressing its uncertainty when multiple concept assignments are consistent with the observed data. This leads to more reliable predictions and improved out-of-distribution generalization, which are vital for trust and robustness in real-world applications.
3. **Principled Probabilistic Framework:** The derivation of a novel continuous-time loss function (NELBO) that gracefully incorporates symbolic programs within the diffusion framework provides a strong theoretical foundation. This extends the applicability of diffusion models to complex, structured data governed by logical rules.
4. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Empirically, NESYDMS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on challenging, high-dimensional tasks like visual path planning (e.g., 97.40% on 30x30 grids with a $5^{900}$ combinatorial space), outperforming existing specialized methods. This confirms its practical utility and competitive edge.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Safer AI in Critical Applications:** The improved calibration and RS-awareness make NESYDMS highly suitable for safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving, medical diagnosis, and financial modeling, where silent failures due to incorrect reasoning are unacceptable. By being aware of its uncertainties, the system can flag ambiguous situations for human oversight or trigger active learning.
2. **More Interpretable and Explainable AI:** By providing a richer, more uncertain distribution over symbolic concepts, NESYDMS offers a deeper insight into the model's reasoning process. This can facilitate better debugging, auditing, and human understanding of complex AI decisions.
3. **Foundation for Future NeSy Research:** The work opens new avenues for research in several directions:
 - **Integration with Advanced Inference:** Exploring the combination of NESYDMS with more sophisticated exact inference routines (e.g., knowledge compilation for richer programs) to handle even more complex logical structures.
 - **Beyond Decomposable Outputs:** Developing methods to handle non-decomposable output spaces more efficiently, extending its current approximation capabilities.
 - **Hybrid Diffusion Models:** Extending NESYDMS to hybrid domains that involve both discrete symbolic concepts and continuous latent variables, leveraging recent advances in continuous constraint-guided generation.
 - **Variational Inference Refinements:** Investigating more sophisticated approximations for the variational entropy term and methods for the indirect gradient.
4. **Broader AI Ecosystem:** The novel theoretical extension of MDM NELBOs to non-factorized distributions could benefit diffusion model research beyond NeSy, enabling more expressive generative models for structured data.

In conclusion, NESYDMS represents a significant step towards creating AI systems that are not only intelligent but also trustworthy, reliable, and transparent, addressing some of the most pressing challenges in the pursuit of generalizable and interpretable artificial intelligence.
