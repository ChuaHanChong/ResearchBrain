# RynnValue: Scaling Robotic Value Foundation Models with Temporal Distance

> [!summary] Summary
> RynnValue is an open-source robotic value foundation model that predicts temporal distance, the directed cost-to-go from an observation to a language-specified goal, instead of preferences or normalized progress. Labels come from timestamps, so training scales to 7,000+ hours and ~3M instruction-conditioned clips. Random sampling, order shuffling, and value-isolation attention suppress shortcuts. It beats preference-supervised state of the art and raises real-world RL success rates.

## Problem

- General-purpose reward models anchor supervision to preferences, reference demonstrations, or local state comparisons, tying signals to specific trajectories that do not reuse across heterogeneous data.
- Normalized progress in a fixed interval is an intra-trajectory coordinate, not a goal-conditioned cost-to-go, so it misaligns with control-theoretic value across varying durations and embodiments.
- Multi-frame reward models exploit regular sampling intervals and presentation order as shortcuts, producing smooth value curves insensitive to regressions, failures, and other non-monotonic events.

## Method

- ==Temporal-distance supervision== with ==subtask segmentation== and ==cutoff relabeling== turns timestamps into dense preference-free labels over a mixture exceeding 7,000 hours and 3M instruction-conditioned clips.
- ==Value-isolation attention== blocks queries of different observations from attending to each other, while ==random temporal sampling== and ==temporal-order shuffling== break interval and position shortcuts.
- Dual distributional heads over 256 symlog bins with ==two-hot targets== predict absolute and signed relative temporal distance; ==potential-based shaping== converts negated distance into dense rewards.

## Results

- RynnValue-8B reaches **0.675** average Kendall's tau-a on the out-of-distribution ranking suite, above the preference-supervised **0.655** and far above the progress-only **0.292** counterpart.
- Real-world policy success rises from **52.5%** to **72.5%** online and **63.8%** to **82.5%** offline over the strongest reward baseline, versus **23.8%** for supervised fine-tuning.
- Ablations confirm shortcut suppression: removing order shuffling drops average to **0.189**, uniform sampling to **0.379**, and removing value-isolation attention to **0.482**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cost-to-go measured in time is a label-cheap, embodiment-agnostic supervision target; timestamps can replace expensive preference pairs without sacrificing ranking quality.
> - Architectural information barriers matter more than scale here: a 4B variant nearly matches 8B, while removing anti-shortcut designs collapses performance.
> - Task diversity reduces held-out error monotonically while within-task episode volume saturates early, so broadening task coverage beats collecting more of the same.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies frequently employs reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. However, a significant challenge in scaling these systems is the provision of effective reward supervision. Traditional approaches to reward design, such as hand-engineered rewards, often lack generalizability across a wide array of open-ended tasks. Sparse success signals, while providing clear task objectives, offer insufficient guidance for complex, long-horizon behaviors, leading to sample inefficiency in RL.

Existing general-purpose reward models typically rely on task-internal anchors. These include explicit human preferences for trajectory segments, reference demonstrations, or comparisons of local states. Such methods often tie supervision to specific trajectories or comparison sets, which limits their reusability and transferability across heterogeneous datasets encompassing diverse robot embodiments, environments, and task structures. A common alternative, normalized progress (typically scaled to), presents its own limitations. It functions as an intra-trajectory coordinate rather than a goal-conditioned cost-to-go, which is the standard notion of value in optimal control. This misalignment makes it difficult to maintain consistency across varying task durations, robot morphologies, and task compositions.

This research posits a re-framing of the problem from learning a reward model that scores trajectory-level anchors to developing a value foundation model that predicts a goal-conditioned cost-to-go as a unified, reusable interface. RynnValue positions itself within this emerging paradigm by proposing temporal distance as its scaling target. By estimating the directed temporal cost from an observation to a language-specified goal, the work aims to overcome the limitations of preference-based or progress-based supervision, providing a more scalable and consistent value signal for robot learning. This approach seeks to enable generalist policies to learn from large-scale, heterogeneous datasets more effectively, contributing to the broader goal of more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the bottleneck of reward supervision in scaling generalist robot policies. The authors identify several motivations for their approach:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Reward Models:** Current general-purpose reward models often rely on "task-internal anchors" such as human preferences, reference demonstrations, or normalized progress. These methods are frequently tied to specific trajectories or comparison sets, hindering their transferability and reusability across diverse robot embodiments and data sources. The authors specifically highlight that normalized progress, commonly used, is an intra-trajectory coordinate rather than a goal-conditioned cost-to-go. This makes it poorly aligned with the concept of value in control theory and challenging to maintain consistently across varying durations, embodiments, and task structures.

2. **Developing a Scalable and Unified Value Interface:** The core motivation is to shift from reward models scoring trajectory-level anchors to a "value foundation model" that predicts a goal-conditioned "cost-to-go." The proposed solution is to adopt **temporal distance**—the directed cost-to-go from an observation to a language-specified goal—as the scaling target. This metric aligns with the hitting-time cost-to-go under a minimum-time objective, providing clear directionality and task conditioning. A key motivation here is that temporal distance labels can be derived directly from timestamps, making them scalable to large, heterogeneous datasets without requiring costly manual preference annotations or task-specific progress normalization.

3. **Ensuring Reliability and Robustness at Scale:** Merely scaling data does not guarantee reliable temporal-value learning. In a multi-frame setting, models may exploit superficial cues (e.g., regular sampling intervals, observation order) rather than grounding predictions in task-relevant visual evidence. This could lead to learned values being insensitive to task regressions, failures, or non-monotonic events. This motivates the inclusion of specific architectural and training designs to suppress these "shortcuts," ensuring that predictions are based on visual semantics and task understanding.

4. **Facilitating Zero-Shot Generalization:** A significant motivation is to enable robotic policies to generalize to unseen tasks, embodiments, and viewpoints without additional fine-tuning. By training on a broad and diverse corpus of robot data, and by focusing on a fundamental concept like temporal distance, the aim is to instill a general understanding of task progress that transcends specific domain variations.

5. **Practical Application for Downstream RL:** The ultimate objective is for this value foundation model to serve as a practical reward interface for downstream policy learning. By converting temporal distance into dense rewards via potential-based shaping, the research aims to provide a signal that can accelerate and improve both online and offline reinforcement learning for generalist robot policies.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a more scalable, robust, generalizable, and theoretically grounded approach to reward specification for large-scale robot learning, moving away from task-specific heuristics towards a universal value interface.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RynnValue is designed as a language-conditioned visual value model built upon the RynnBrain architecture, which is pretrained on large-scale robot data to adapt its representations to embodied scenes, robot states, and language-conditioned manipulation tasks.

**Core Concept:** RynnValue estimates the temporal distance, defined as the remaining time from a given robot observation to a language-specified task completion. This "cost-to-go" formulation provides a unified, preference-free value target.

**Model Architecture:**
- **Inputs:** A language instruction (task instruction and system prompt), embodiment metadata, and a sequence of K observations (K=8 in the main setting).
- **Outputs:** Joint prediction of absolute temporal distance for each observation, signed relative temporal displacement between consecutive observations, and natural-language outputs for video analysis and task verification.
- **Grouped Temporal Queries:** To avoid information bottlenecks, each temporal prediction uses a group of N (N=8) repeated query tokens. Absolute-value query groups (V_i) are inserted for each observation, and relative-value query groups (R_i-1) are added before each subsequent observation. These tokens share respective query types (<value> or <relative_value>). The hidden states of these N tokens are concatenated to form a richer representation.
- **Continuous Temporal Readouts:** Two specialized distributional heads, `ValueHead` and `RelativeHead`, produce continuous absolute and relative temporal estimates. The absolute range ( seconds) and relative range ([-256, 256] seconds) are discretized into 256 symlog-spaced bins. Training uses two-hot targets over adjacent bins, converting regression into a stable classification problem.
- **Value-Isolation Attention:** A masking strategy is introduced to prevent temporal queries associated with different observations from attending to one another. This ensures that each temporal estimate is grounded in its corresponding visual observation and task instruction, rather than extrapolating from other value queries or sequence position.
- **Natural-Language Video Analysis and Task Verification:** A natural-language verification prompt is appended after all temporal queries. The model generates a natural-language description of the video, followed by task-matching and task-success judgments using the original LM head. This provides auxiliary semantic grounding without direct participation in temporal-value prediction.

**Data Preparation:**
- **Heterogeneous Data Mixture:** The model is trained on a large corpus exceeding 7,000 hours of robot, simulation, and egocentric data from diverse sources (e.g., AgiBot, EgoDex, Open X-Embodiment). This mixture comprises approximately 3 million instruction-conditioned clips across various embodiments, viewpoints, and task families.
- **Preprocessing and Temporal-Distance Relabeling:** Raw trajectories are converted into instruction-conditioned segments. Long demonstrations are split using native annotations or by retaining the full episode. A completion cutoff is assigned to each segment (defaulting to endpoint, with dataset-specific trimming for semantic completion). Temporal-distance labels are then directly derived from timestamps: remaining time to cutoff for observations before, and zero for observations at or after cutoff. Segment-level descriptions are generated by Qwen3-VL-27B to supervise the natural-language video analysis output.

**Training Recipe:**
- **Multi-Frame Sampling Strategy:**
 - **Random Temporal Sampling:** K=8 observations are sampled at irregular timestamps from each trajectory clip to prevent the model from exploiting fixed sampling intervals.
 - **Temporal-Order Shuffling:** The chronological order of sampled observations is perturbed. Half of the sequences are sampled independently, and the other half follow a forward-biased temporal walk with a 0.3 rewind probability. This breaks the correspondence between sequence position and temporal distance.
- **Instruction-Mismatch Augmentation:** For 10% of samples, the original instruction is replaced with a mismatched one. The language branch is supervised to predict "Match: No" and "Success: No." The absolute temporal-distance loss is masked for these samples, while the relative temporal loss is retained. This encourages the model to detect instruction-video inconsistencies.
- **Joint Training Objectives:**
 - **Absolute Temporal-Distance Loss (L_abs):** Cross-entropy over the symlog-binned absolute targets.
 - **Relative Temporal-Distance Loss (L_rel):** Cross-entropy over the symlog-binned relative targets.
 - **Natural-Language Loss (L_lang):** Standard token-level cross-entropy for autoregressive language generation.
 - The final objective is L = L_abs + L_rel + λ_lang * L_lang (with λ_lang = 2). Gradients from L_lang propagate through the frozen LM output projection to adapt backbone representations.

**Inference and Reward Interface:**
- **Chronological Inference:** During inference, observations are chronologically ordered, but value-isolation attention remains active to prevent inter-query group extrapolation.
- **Temporal-Distance Potential:** The continuous absolute temporal-distance estimate `v_t` is converted into a potential `Φ_t = -v_t` to align with the convention that higher values are better.
- **Reward Shaping:** For downstream RL, a dense reward `r'_t` is computed using potential-based shaping: `r'_t = κ (γΦ_{t+1} - Φ_t) + sparse_term`. A sparse completion term is added to provide a reliable final objective signal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RynnValue covered its intrinsic value quality, scaling properties, qualitative behavior, and utility as a reward interface for real-world policy learning.

**Benchmark Evaluation on RBM-EVAL-OOD:**
- RynnValue-8B achieved an average Kendall’s τ_a of 0.675 on the RBM-EVAL-OOD test suite, outperforming the fully preference-supervised state-of-the-art (Robometer, 0.655). The smaller RynnValue-4B also achieved 0.670.
- The model demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks, embodiments, and viewpoints.
- RynnValue variants achieved the best results on all six datasets among methods trained without explicit trajectory-level preference supervision, substantially improving upon prior preference-free methods (e.g., RoboReward-4B at 0.502 average τ_a).
- It significantly outperformed a progress-only Robometer ablation, which scored 0.292.
- RynnValue-8B ranked first on USC Franka, USC Koch, and USC Trossen datasets.

**Instruction-Trajectory Alignment:**
- An analysis of instruction-trajectory confusion matrices showed that RynnValue produced the clearest diagonal structure, achieving the highest normalized diagonal margin of 0.79. This indicates that the model effectively distinguishes between matched and mismatched instruction-trajectory pairs, grounding its temporal-distance estimates in the language-specified goal.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Temporal-order shuffling:** Removing this component resulted in the largest performance degradation, reducing the average Kendall’s τ_a to 0.189, highlighting its role in preventing reliance on sequence position shortcuts.
- **Uniform sampling:** Replacing random temporal sampling with uniform sampling reduced the average score to 0.379, suggesting that regular sampling intervals can be exploited as a shortcut.
- **Value-isolation attention:** Removing this architectural design decreased performance to 0.482, affirming its importance in grounding predictions in visual evidence rather than extrapolation from other value queries.
- **Natural-language supervision:** Removing the auxiliary natural-language supervision reduced the average score to 0.537, indicating its contribution to semantic grounding.
- **Relative temporal-distance supervision:** Removing this objective led to a score of 0.627, demonstrating its complementary role in improving shared visual representations by modeling local temporal changes.

**Scaling Analysis:**
- Scaling analysis demonstrated that **task diversity** yielded a consistently steeper reduction in mean absolute temporal-distance error on unseen tasks compared to increasing **episode volume** within fixed tasks. Episode volume gains saturated quickly, while task diversity continued to improve performance monotonically. This suggests that broad task coverage, rather than merely more data from existing tasks, is crucial for general-purpose temporal-value learning.

**Temporal-Value Curve Case Study:**
- Qualitative comparison with Robometer on a real-world trajectory showed that RynnValue responded more strongly to task regression (e.g., sharp potential decrease when the robot moved away from a productive state).
- RynnValue exhibited a steadier increase towards task completion, avoiding the longer plateaus and abrupt jumps observed in Robometer’s predictions.
- The model maintained sensitivity near task completion, reacting to late disturbances with an immediate potential decrease and subsequent recovery, avoiding premature reward saturation.

**Real-World Policy Learning:**
- **Online Reinforcement Learning:** RynnValue, converted into dense rewards, achieved an average success rate of 72.5% across four challenging manipulation tasks, outperforming Robometer (52.5%) and Sparse Reward (48.8%). Significant improvements were observed on tasks like "Steak Serving with a Spatula" (30 percentage points over Robometer) and "Bimanual Box Transfer" (35 percentage points over Robometer).
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning:** RynnValue achieved an average success rate of 82.5%, compared to Robometer’s 63.8% and the original Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) policy’s 23.8%.
- RynnValue-guided policies generally achieved task success with fewer action chunks, indicating improved execution efficiency.
- It enabled the successful solution of tasks (e.g., "Box-in-Drawer Placement" and "Bimanual Box Transfer") where the SFT policy recorded no successful executions.
- Performance gains were less pronounced in precision-sensitive tasks like "Box-in-Drawer Placement" where visual ambiguity made intermediate reward calibration challenging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic learning:

1. **Scalable and Preference-Free Reward Supervision:** The core contribution of RynnValue is the identification and empirical validation of temporal distance as a scalable and preference-free supervision target for robotic value models. This approach bypasses the need for costly and labor-intensive preference annotations or task-specific progress normalization, which are common bottlenecks in scaling robot learning to heterogeneous, large-scale datasets. By deriving labels directly from timestamps, RynnValue offers a path to more automated and efficient data utilization.

2. **Unified Value Foundation Model for Robotics:** RynnValue establishes the concept of a "value foundation model" for robotic manipulation. By predicting a directed, goal-conditioned cost-to-go, it provides a consistent and unified interface for understanding task progress across diverse embodiments, viewpoints, and task structures. This stands in contrast to previous approaches that often required context-specific adjustments, fostering a more generalizable and reusable knowledge representation.

3. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The model demonstrates robust zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks, embodiments, and viewpoints. This level of generalization is critical for developing truly generalist robot policies that can operate effectively in open-ended, dynamic environments without extensive retraining. The diverse training data and the method's focus on fundamental temporal semantics contribute to this capability.

4. **Improved Reinforcement Learning Performance:** RynnValue significantly enhances both online and offline real-world policy learning. Its ability to provide dense, well-calibrated rewards leads to higher success rates and more efficient task completion compared to state-of-the-art reward models and sparse reward baselines. This directly impacts the feasibility of training complex manipulation skills, particularly for tasks that are difficult to reward engineer.

5. **Robustness through Shortcut Suppression:** The introduction of specific designs like random temporal sampling, temporal-order shuffling, and value-isolation attention is significant. These mechanisms force the model to ground its predictions in task-relevant visual evidence rather than exploiting superficial correlations or temporal shortcuts. This increased robustness is crucial for reliable operation in real-world scenarios where failures and regressions are common.

6. **Interpretability via Natural Language:** The inclusion of natural-language video analysis and task verification capabilities provides an additional layer of interpretability. While not directly involved in temporal-value prediction, these outputs offer insights into the model's understanding of robot behavior and task semantics, which can be valuable for diagnosis and development.

Looking forward, the research outlines paths for extending RynnValue to broader temporal horizons, richer value semantics (e.g., incorporating energy or safety costs), and more diverse embodiments like dexterous hands and mobile manipulators. This suggests that the temporal distance framework could serve as a foundational component for developing increasingly capable and adaptable autonomous robotic systems.
