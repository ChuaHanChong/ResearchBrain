# Self-Distillation Zero: Self-Revision Turns Binary Rewards into Dense Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Distillation Zero (SD-ZERO) enables large language models to convert sparse binary rewards into dense, token-level self-supervision for reasoning tasks. The method trains a single model to self-revise from initial attempts and then distills this explicit revision capability into a more efficient, proactive generation style, yielding over 10% average accuracy gains on math and code benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods provide only sparse binary feedback, leading to computationally expensive and sample-inefficient training for complex reasoning tasks.
- Existing distillation methods require either an external, stronger teacher model or costly datasets of high-quality demonstrations, limiting their applicability.
- There is a need for a post-training approach that combines the broad applicability of binary rewards with the sample efficiency of dense supervision, without relying on external teachers or expensive expert data.

## Method

- **SD-ZERO** introduces ==Self-Revision Training (SRT)==: a single model first generates an initial response and then attempts to revise it based on a binary reward (correct/incorrect), retaining only successful revisions to form a training dataset.
- ==Self-Distillation==: The SRT-trained model is frozen as a 'teacher' and provides ==token-level probability distributions== to a 'student' model. The student is trained to minimize ==KL divergence== with the teacher's revision-informed distributions, internalizing the revision process into direct generation.
- The method uses specific control prompts and combines log-likelihood losses in SRT with KL-divergence minimization in Self-Distillation, allowing the model to autonomously transform sparse rewards into detailed internal guidance.

## Results

- **SD-ZERO** improved average accuracy by **10.5%** for Qwen3-4B-Instruct and **10.4%** for Olmo-3-7B-Instruct across math and code benchmarks, outperforming baselines like SFT, RFT, GRPO, and SDFT.
- The Self-Revision Training (SRT) phase alone boosted average accuracy by **7.8%** (Qwen3) and **9.2%** (Olmo) and increased explicit revision capability by **5.0%** on AIME24.
- Self-Distillation further enhanced performance and reduced response length by approximately **2x**, demonstrating successful internalization of revision behaviors into efficient, proactive generation while maintaining or exceeding accuracy.
- Analysis revealed ==token-level self-localization==, where the reviser concentrated dense KL rewards on a small fraction of mistake-relevant tokens in incorrect generations, effectively pinpointing errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language models can effectively generate their own dense, token-level supervision from sparse binary rewards by learning to explicitly self-revise, even from initially incorrect attempts.
> - The explicit self-revision training phase is critical for unlocking the model's ability to internalize error correction, as direct distillation from a base model is largely ineffective.
> - The self-distillation phase successfully transitions the model from overt, multi-turn revision to concise, proactive, and accurate single-pass generation, significantly improving inference efficiency and response length.
> - Iterative self-evolution, where an updated student model becomes the teacher, allows for sustained performance gains, suggesting a self-bootstrapping mechanism for continuous improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in post-training large language models (LLMs) for reasoning tasks in verifiable settings, such as mathematics and coding. Current methods generally fall into two categories, each with distinct advantages and limitations.

**Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** methods, a dominant approach, rely on binary rewards (e.g., whether a final answer is correct). While broadly applicable, this provides only sparse supervision, meaning the model receives feedback only on the overall outcome of a generated response, not on individual reasoning steps. This sparsity can make training sample-inefficient, as the model must explore and contrast numerous self-generated responses to discover effective reasoning paths.

**Distillation methods** offer an alternative by providing dense, token-level supervision. This typically involves using an external, stronger "teacher" model or requiring access to high-quality human-generated demonstrations. Such supervision guides the student model token-by-token, leading to more sample-efficient training. However, collecting high-quality external supervision can be costly, time-consuming, or sometimes unavailable, posing a practical limitation. Recent self-distillation methods (e.g., OPSD, SDFT, SDPO) aim to remove the external teacher requirement by generating demonstrations internally, but often still rely on filtering for high-quality, correct examples, which can be computationally intensive or depend on strong initial model performance.

This research, Self-Distillation Zero (SD-ZERO), positions itself by aiming to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It seeks to leverage the broad applicability of binary rewards while providing the sample efficiency of dense, token-level supervision. Critically, it does so without requiring an external teacher or costly high-quality demonstrations. SD-ZERO introduces a mechanism for a single model to generate its own dense supervision from its potentially incorrect attempts and their sparse binary rewards, thereby making the training process more self-contained and efficient. It draws parallels with Process Reward Models (PRMs) by generating localized, token-level feedback, but without needing a separate reward model or step-level annotations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SD-ZERO is to enable a single language model to convert sparse binary rewards into dense token-level self-supervision, thereby improving its performance on reasoning tasks in verifiable domains more efficiently than existing methods.

The motivation for this objective stems from the limitations of current post-training methodologies:

1. **Addressing Sparse Supervision in RLVR:** While RLVR is effective and widely applicable due to its reliance on simple binary correctness rewards, the sparse nature of this feedback makes training sample-inefficient. Models struggle to pinpoint which specific reasoning steps within a long sequence of tokens contributed to a correct or incorrect final answer. This requires extensive exploration and many self-generated responses, leading to high computational costs. The objective is to provide a richer, more informative signal that guides the model's learning process at a finer granularity.
2. **Mitigating Reliance on External Teachers or Costly Demonstrations in Distillation:** Distillation techniques provide dense, token-level supervision that is more sample-efficient. However, they traditionally depend on an external, stronger teacher model or a substantial dataset of high-quality, human-curated "gold" demonstrations. Acquiring or maintaining such resources is often expensive, time-consuming, or simply not feasible for many domains or research groups. Even modern self-distillation approaches often still necessitate strong initial model performance or extensive filtering to produce high-quality internal demonstrations. The motivation is to remove these dependencies, making dense supervision accessible even when such external resources are unavailable or prohibitive.
3. **Enhancing Training Sample Efficiency:** By transforming sparse binary rewards into dense, token-level supervision within a single model, the goal is to significantly reduce the number of model-generated responses required during training compared to RLVR methods. This directly translates to lower computational costs and faster convergence.
4. **Developing a Self-Evolving Training Mechanism:** The research aims to explore if the model, by improving its self-revision capabilities, can iteratively serve as a stronger teacher for itself, leading to continuous performance gains without external intervention. This would create a self-bootstrapping mechanism for improvement.

In essence, the central question motivating this work is: Can a model, by conditioning on its own initial (possibly incorrect) attempts and their sparse binary rewards, generate improved dense supervision for itself? SD-ZERO seeks to provide an affirmative answer by designing a training paradigm that enables this internal transformation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SD-ZERO employs a two-phase training methodology where a single language model plays two distinct roles: a Generator, producing initial responses, and a Reviser, improving upon those responses.

**Phase 1: Self-Revision Training (SRT)**

The first phase focuses on enhancing the model's capability to act as a reviser. This involves creating a dataset of "outcome-conditioned self-revision traces" and fine-tuning the model on them.

1. **Outcome-Conditioned Self-Revision Data Collection:**
 - For a given input question (`x`), the base model (`πθ`) generates an initial reasoning response (`y_init`).
 - A binary verifier assesses `y_init` for correctness, yielding a reward `r` (0 for incorrect, 1 for correct).
 - Based on `r`, a specific control prompt (`P_r`) is constructed:
 - If `r = 1`, `P_r` is set to "Let me rephrase the above solution."
 - If `r = 0`, `P_r` is set to "Wait, this response is not correct, let me start over."
 - The *same model* then generates a revised response (`y_revised`) conditioned on `x`, `y_init`, and `P_r`.
 - Only traces where `y_revised` is *correct* are retained, forming the `D_REVISION` dataset. This filtering ensures the model learns from successful revisions.

2. **Self-Revision Objective (`L_SRT`):**
 The model is fine-tuned on `D_REVISION` using a combined loss (`L_SRT`) consisting of two log-likelihood terms:
 - **Revision Loss (`L_revision`):** Trains the model to produce `y_revised` when explicitly conditioned on `x`, `y_init`, and `P_r`. This directly teaches the model to perform self-revision based on outcome and prior attempt.
 - **Generation Loss (`L_generation`):** Retains and improves the model's ability to generate a full correct response from scratch, conditioned only on `x`. Critically, the `y'` for this loss is defined as `[y_init, P_r, y_revised]`, encouraging the model to internalize the full self-correction trajectory as part of a complete solution.
 - The combination of these two losses (`L_SRT = L_revision + L_generation`) aims to improve both explicit self-revision and implicit generation of correct, comprehensive responses. The model after this phase is referred to as the SRT model (`π_θ_SRT`).

**Phase 2: On-Policy Self-Distillation via Revision Feedback**

This phase leverages the reviser capabilities acquired in Phase 1 to distill dense, token-level supervision back into the generator role, making responses more concise and direct.

1. **Student-Teacher Interaction:**
 - The student policy (`πθ`) is initialized with the SRT model (`π_θ_SRT`). Its parameters are continuously updated.
 - The teacher policy (`π_T`) is kept frozen as the SRT model (`π_θ_SRT`).
 - For each training example (`x`, `a`), the *student* model generates an on-policy response (`y_init`).
 - The binary reward `r` for `y_init` is computed.
 - A control prompt `P_r` is constructed as in Phase 1.
 - The *teacher* model (`π_θ_SRT`), conditioned on `x`, `y_init`, and `P_r`, provides token-level distributions for generating `y_revised`.

2. **Self-Distillation Objective (`L_Self-Distillation`):**
 - The student model is updated to match the teacher's next-token distributions. This is achieved by minimizing the KL divergence between the student's next-token distribution (`πθ(· | x, y_<t)`) and the teacher's next-token distribution (`π_θ_SRT(· | x, y_init, P_r, y_<t)`) for each token `t` in the generated response `y_init`.
 - This loss effectively transforms the sparse binary reward (which determines `P_r`) into dense, token-level feedback from the reviser (`π_θ_SRT`) to guide the generator (`πθ`). The goal is to internalize the revision behavior, allowing the model to produce stronger, more efficient responses directly, without explicit multi-turn self-correction at inference time.

**Iterative Self-Evolution:**
A notable aspect of SD-ZERO is its capacity for iterative self-evolution. Since the Self-Distillation phase also improves the model's revision capabilities, the updated student model can, in subsequent rounds, serve as an even stronger reviser (teacher) for further distillation. This "teacher synchronization" allows the model to continuously bootstrap its performance over multiple rounds of Self-Distillation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SD-ZERO method was evaluated on math and code reasoning benchmarks using Qwen3-4B-Instruct and Olmo-3-7B-Instruct models, consistently demonstrating performance improvements and novel behavioral characteristics. All comparisons were made under matched question sets and training sample budgets.

### 5. Performance Gains Over Baselines

- **SRT's Initial Strength:** Phase 1 (SRT) alone significantly improved performance. For Qwen3-4B-Instruct, it achieved a 7.8% average accuracy gain over the base model, and 9.2% for Olmo-3-7B-Instruct. This performance surpassed strong baselines like Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT), GRPO, and Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT) on the same data budget. SRT's effectiveness is attributed to learning from error correction, where it preserves incorrect attempts as context for revision, unlike RFT which discards them.
- **SD-ZERO's Cumulative Impact:** The full SD-ZERO pipeline (SRT + Self-Distillation) yielded total gains of 10.5% for Qwen3-4B-Instruct and 10.4% for Olmo-3-7B-Instruct over their respective base models. This included an additional 2.7% gain for Qwen3-4B-Instruct and 1.2% for Olmo-3-7B-Instruct from the Self-Distillation phase beyond SRT.
- **Comparison to Other Methods:** SD-ZERO outperformed baselines by at least 4.8% on average. Notably, it did so without requiring gold solutions (unlike SDFT, which performed much worse without them) and with a lower generation cost than GRPO in its self-distillation phase (single response per question vs. multiple rollouts).

### 6. Token Efficiency and Internalized Self-Revision

- **SRT Model's Lengthy Responses:** The SRT-trained model, while accurate, tended to produce excessively long responses, often exhibiting explicit self-revision behaviors (e.g., "Wait, this is wrong. Let me start over.").
- **Self-Distillation's Efficiency:** The Self-Distillation phase was crucial for refining this behavior. It enabled the model to internalize its revision capability, leading to the SD-ZERO model generating responses roughly half the length of the SRT model, and fewer tokens than all baselines, while achieving superior accuracy.
- **Evolution of Self-Revision:** Analysis showed that explicit self-revision keywords and response length initially increased during the SRT phase, indicating the learning of overt self-correction. During the Self-Distillation phase, these metrics steadily decreased, while accuracy continued to improve, suggesting a shift from explicit backtracking to more internalized, proactive reasoning where the model anticipates pitfalls and guides itself to the correct answer directly.

### 7. Iterative Self-Evolution through Teacher Synchronization

- SD-ZERO demonstrated the ability to iteratively improve its performance. After one epoch of Self-Distillation, synchronizing the teacher (reviser) with the updated student model led to further accuracy gains of at least 3% on OpenR1-Math. This indicated that the improved student model, having learned better revision, could serve as a stronger teacher, enabling a continuous self-improvement loop.

### 8. Token-Level Self-Localization

- The reviser model, despite receiving only a binary outcome reward, converted this into dense, localized token-level feedback. For incorrect responses, the KL divergence (representing "Token KL Reward") concentrated on a small fraction of tokens, indicating specific error localization. For correct responses, the KL reward was more uniformly distributed, suggesting preservation of the response. This demonstrates that the reviser can effectively identify faulty parts of a generation and redirect the model towards corrections.

### 9. Ablation Studies

- **Loss Terms in SRT:** Both `L_revision` and `L_generation` were found to be complementary and necessary. `L_generation` alone preserved strong generation but weak self-revision, while `L_revision` alone improved revision but at a cost to overall generation quality.
- **Necessity of SRT Phase:** Directly applying the Self-Distillation phase to the base model (without prior SRT) yielded only marginal improvements and did not enhance self-revision ability, confirming that the SRT phase is a prerequisite for unlocking effective revision.
- **Data Split:** Allocating more data to the Self-Distillation phase (after SRT has established revision capability) generally led to better final performance compared to prioritizing more data for SRT.

### 10. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Self-Distillation Zero (SD-ZERO) presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of large language model training:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Sparse and Dense Supervision:** SD-ZERO successfully demonstrates a method for transforming sparse binary rewards into dense, token-level supervision. This addresses a fundamental trade-off in LLM post-training, providing the broad applicability of simple correctness checks with the sample efficiency typically associated with more fine-grained feedback. This dual benefit can accelerate training and reduce computational resource requirements.
2. **Elimination of External Teachers and Costly Demonstrations:** A key impact is the removal of the dependency on external, stronger teacher models or high-quality human-annotated "gold" demonstrations. By enabling a single model to act as both generator and self-revising teacher, SD-ZERO makes advanced fine-tuning techniques more accessible and scalable. This could significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, particularly in resource-constrained environments or emerging domains where high-quality expert data is scarce.
3. **Enhanced Training Efficiency and Performance:** The method achieves substantial performance gains (over 10% average accuracy) across multiple benchmarks and model families, while maintaining comparable or lower sampling budgets than existing strong baselines. The shift from explicit self-revision to internalized, token-efficient generation means the final models are not only more accurate but also more efficient at inference time, producing shorter, more direct answers.
4. **Enabling Iterative Self-Evolution:** The demonstrated capability for iterative self-evolution through teacher synchronization suggests a path toward continually improving models without needing to re-engage with external data sources or human experts after initial setup. This bootstrapping mechanism could lead to more robust and autonomously improving AI systems.
5. **Insights into Model Behavior:** The analyses on token-level self-localization and the evolution of reasoning behaviors provide insights into how models can learn to identify and correct errors internally. The conversion of a scalar reward into a localized, two-sided token-level signal (penalizing errors while redirecting toward better alternatives) suggests a mechanism for internal metacognitive processes in LLMs.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The current study focuses on instruct models and verifiable domains. Future work could extend SD-ZERO to:
- **Thinking Models:** Applying the method to models that generate long, exploratory chains of thought (e.g., chain-of-thought models) poses challenges in distinguishing productive exploration from genuine errors.
- **Domains without Verifiable Rewards:** Extending SD-ZERO to domains lacking clear binary correctness signals is an open problem. Exploring meta-cognitive signals like consistency or self-correction as alternative reward mechanisms could be a promising direction.

In summary, SD-ZERO offers a novel and effective paradigm for LLM self-improvement, potentially broadening the applicability of distillation-based techniques and fostering the development of more autonomous and efficient learning systems.
