# Self-Distillation Zero: Self-Revision Turns Binary Rewards into Dense Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Distillation Zero (SD-ZERO) enables large language models to convert sparse binary rewards into dense, token-level self-supervision for reasoning tasks. The method trains a single model to self-revise from initial attempts and then distills this explicit revision capability into a more efficient, proactive generation style, yielding over 10% average accuracy gains on math and code benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods provide only sparse binary feedback, leading to computationally expensive and sample-inefficient training for complex reasoning tasks.
- Existing distillation methods require either an external, stronger teacher model or costly datasets of high-quality demonstrations, limiting their applicability.
- There is a need for a post-training approach that combines the broad applicability of binary rewards with the sample efficiency of dense supervision, without relying on external teachers or expensive expert data.

## Method

- **SD-ZERO** introduces ==Self-Revision Training (SRT)==: a single model first generates an initial response and then attempts to revise it based on a binary reward (correct/incorrect), retaining only successful revisions to form a training dataset.
- ==Self-Distillation==: The SRT-trained model is frozen as a 'teacher' and provides ==token-level probability distributions== to a 'student' model. The student is trained to minimize ==KL divergence== with the teacher's revision-informed distributions, internalizing the revision process into direct generation.
- The method uses specific control prompts and combines log-likelihood losses in SRT with KL-divergence minimization in Self-Distillation, allowing the model to autonomously transform sparse rewards into detailed internal guidance.

## Results

- **SD-ZERO** improved average accuracy by **10.5%** for Qwen3-4B-Instruct and **10.4%** for Olmo-3-7B-Instruct across math and code benchmarks, outperforming baselines like SFT, RFT, GRPO, and SDFT.
- The Self-Revision Training (SRT) phase alone boosted average accuracy by **7.8%** (Qwen3) and **9.2%** (Olmo) and increased explicit revision capability by **5.0%** on AIME24.
- Self-Distillation further enhanced performance and reduced response length by approximately **2x**, demonstrating successful internalization of revision behaviors into efficient, proactive generation while maintaining or exceeding accuracy.
- Analysis revealed ==token-level self-localization==, where the reviser concentrated dense KL rewards on a small fraction of mistake-relevant tokens in incorrect generations, effectively pinpointing errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language models can effectively generate their own dense, token-level supervision from sparse binary rewards by learning to explicitly self-revise, even from initially incorrect attempts.
> - The explicit self-revision training phase is critical for unlocking the model's ability to internalize error correction, as direct distillation from a base model is largely ineffective.
> - The self-distillation phase successfully transitions the model from overt, multi-turn revision to concise, proactive, and accurate single-pass generation, significantly improving inference efficiency and response length.
> - Iterative self-evolution, where an updated student model becomes the teacher, allows for sustained performance gains, suggesting a self-bootstrapping mechanism for continuous improvement.
