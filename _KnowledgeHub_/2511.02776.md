# XR-1: Towards Versatile Vision-Language-Action Models via Learning Unified Vision-Motion Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> The X Robotic Model 1 (XR-1) introduces Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC) to enable precise, versatile control for robots by jointly encoding visual dynamics and robotic motion. It achieves superior performance and strong generalization across various robot embodiments and manipulation tasks, demonstrating robust learning from heterogeneous data sources including human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack the precision required to map high-dimensional observations to fine-grained low-level actions for complex robotic manipulation.
- Difficulty in effectively learning from and transferring policies across heterogeneous data sources, including diverse robot embodiments and human demonstration videos without explicit action labels.
- Existing latent representation learning approaches for VLA often isolate vision from action, missing crucial multimodal alignment and complementary knowledge.

## Method

- Develops Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC) using a self-supervised, dual-branch VQ-VAE to jointly encode visual dynamics and robotic motion into a discrete, shared latent space.
- Employs a cross-modality alignment loss during UVMC learning to regularize visual codes with motion, enabling learning from data lacking explicit actions (e.g., human videos) and reducing task-irrelevant visual noise.
- Integrates UVMC into a three-stage training paradigm: UVMC learning, UVMC-guided VLA pretraining, and task-specific fine-tuning for robust and scalable policy learning.

## Results

- XR-1 consistently outperformed state-of-the-art VLA baselines, achieving superior success rates across over 120 diverse manipulation tasks on six distinct real-world robot embodiments.
- Demonstrated strong generalization to unseen scenarios, including novel objects and environmental variations, and rapid adaptation to 15 new tasks on an unseen robot embodiment (Tien Kung **2.0**) with 20-shot demonstrations.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC) and cross-modal alignment, yielding up to a **15%** absolute improvement in lightweight models and showing monotonic performance gains with increased pre-training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly encoding visual dynamics and robotic motion into a unified, discrete latent representation (UVMC) is effective for bridging the precision gap in VLA models and enhancing action generation accuracy.
> - A cross-modality alignment mechanism between vision and motion representations allows for robust learning from diverse data sources, including human videos, and facilitates transfer across different robot embodiments.
> - Developing embodiment-agnostic features that abstract away robot-specific details while preserving task semantics is essential for building generalist robotic agents capable of operating across varied platforms.
