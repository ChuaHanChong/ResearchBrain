# XR-1: Towards Versatile Vision-Language-Action Models via Learning Unified Vision-Motion Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> The X Robotic Model 1 (XR-1) introduces Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC) to enable precise, versatile control for robots by jointly encoding visual dynamics and robotic motion. It achieves superior performance and strong generalization across various robot embodiments and manipulation tasks, demonstrating robust learning from heterogeneous data sources including human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack the precision required to map high-dimensional observations to fine-grained low-level actions for complex robotic manipulation.
- Difficulty in effectively learning from and transferring policies across heterogeneous data sources, including diverse robot embodiments and human demonstration videos without explicit action labels.
- Existing latent representation learning approaches for VLA often isolate vision from action, missing crucial multimodal alignment and complementary knowledge.

## Method

- Develops Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC) using a self-supervised, dual-branch VQ-VAE to jointly encode visual dynamics and robotic motion into a discrete, shared latent space.
- Employs a cross-modality alignment loss during UVMC learning to regularize visual codes with motion, enabling learning from data lacking explicit actions (e.g., human videos) and reducing task-irrelevant visual noise.
- Integrates UVMC into a three-stage training paradigm: UVMC learning, UVMC-guided VLA pretraining, and task-specific fine-tuning for robust and scalable policy learning.

## Results

- XR-1 consistently outperformed state-of-the-art VLA baselines, achieving superior success rates across over 120 diverse manipulation tasks on six distinct real-world robot embodiments.
- Demonstrated strong generalization to unseen scenarios, including novel objects and environmental variations, and rapid adaptation to 15 new tasks on an unseen robot embodiment (Tien Kung **2.0**) with 20-shot demonstrations.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC) and cross-modal alignment, yielding up to a **15%** absolute improvement in lightweight models and showing monotonic performance gains with increased pre-training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly encoding visual dynamics and robotic motion into a unified, discrete latent representation (UVMC) is effective for bridging the precision gap in VLA models and enhancing action generation accuracy.
> - A cross-modality alignment mechanism between vision and motion representations allows for robust learning from diverse data sources, including human videos, and facilitates transfer across different robot embodiments.
> - Developing embodiment-agnostic features that abstract away robot-specific details while preserving task semantics is essential for building generalist robotic agents capable of operating across varied platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI), specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control. The long-term objective of Embodied AI is the development of generalist agents capable of executing diverse natural language instructions in real-world environments such as households and factories.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated the effectiveness of large-scale pre-training on internet-scale data for robust visual-semantic understanding. Building upon this, VLA models integrate action generation capabilities to ground perception and language into executable motor commands for robots.

Despite progress, existing VLA models encounter two primary challenges:
1. **Precision Gap:** The difficulty in mapping high-dimensional observations to precise low-level actions. Even minor errors, such as centimeter-level discrepancies, can lead to task failure in dexterous or contact-rich manipulation.
2. **Data Heterogeneity:** The challenge of transferring learned policies across diverse robotic embodiments (varying hardware and degrees of freedom) and effectively utilizing human demonstration videos, which often lack explicit action labels or visual alignment with robot actions.

Prior research has explored latent representations as intermediate abstractions to bridge the gap between high-dimensional observations and low-dimensional motor commands. These approaches typically fall into two categories:
- **Action-centric methods:** Encoding action sequences for motion modeling. These methods often require substantial amounts of costly labeled robotic data.
- **Vision-centric methods:** Encoding visual dynamics from unlabeled video data (e.g., human or web videos). While capable of capturing task semantics, these methods often isolate vision from action during representation learning, leading to a lack of explicit action grounding.

The authors observe that both existing approaches tend to treat vision and action in isolation, failing to fully leverage complementary multimodal knowledge or capture the necessary multimodal alignment. Inspired by human supramodal cognition, where heterogeneous sensory inputs are fused into abstract codes that preserve task semantics while abstracting embodiment-specific details, this work proposes a joint encoding of visual dynamics and motor control. XR-1 aims to address the identified limitations by introducing Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC) to capture this multimodal alignment, enabling cross-data exploitation and cross-embodiment control, and ultimately fostering more versatile and scalable VLA learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in achieving versatile and precise robotic control across diverse scenarios. The authors identify two fundamental challenges that existing approaches struggle with:

1. **Generating Precise Low-Level Actions:** Current VLA models face a precision gap when translating high-dimensional sensory observations into the fine-grained, low-level motor commands required for successful manipulation. Multimodal uncertainty exacerbates this problem, as even slight inaccuracies in action generation can lead to failure in tasks demanding dexterity or physical contact. The objective is to develop a representation that facilitates the generation of actions with sufficient accuracy to navigate these complexities.

2. **Bridging Domain Gaps Across Heterogeneous Data Sources:** The ability to learn from and generalize across varied data sources is crucial for building scalable robot policies. However, existing models struggle with the heterogeneity inherent in large-scale datasets, which comprise data from diverse robot embodiments (differing hardware, kinematics, and degrees of freedom) and human demonstrations. Human videos, in particular, often lack explicit action labels or precise visual alignment with robotic actions, making their direct utilization challenging. The motivation here is to create a framework that can effectively exploit this rich but disparate knowledge, enabling robust cross-embodiment transfer and leveraging human expertise.

To address these motivations, the authors propose X Robotic Model 1 (XR-1), centered on the concept of **Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC)**. The key objectives of introducing UVMC and the XR-1 framework are:

- **To create an intermediate representation (UVMC) that serves as a bridge between observations and actions.** This aims to abstract away the direct mapping from pixels to motor commands, thereby simplifying the control problem and enhancing precision.
- **To align multimodal dynamic information from heterogeneous data sources.** By jointly encoding visual dynamics and robotic motion into a shared latent space, UVMC is designed to capture complementary knowledge that unimodal representations miss. This alignment is intended to enable effective learning from both robotic demonstrations (with precise action labels) and actionless human videos, mitigating the data heterogeneity problem.
- **To achieve cross-data exploitation and cross-embodiment control.** The framework is motivated by the insight that humans process sensory inputs into "supramodal codes" that are abstract enough to generalize across different body parts while retaining task-relevant semantics. XR-1 seeks to emulate this by learning embodiment-agnostic representations that preserve task semantics, allowing policies to transfer efficiently to novel robot platforms and tasks.

Through this approach, XR-1 aims to provide a scalable and versatile solution for VLA learning, enhancing robustness and generalization capabilities in complex real-world robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The XR-1 framework is designed for versatile and scalable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) learning, structured around a three-stage training paradigm. Its core innovation is the Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC), a discrete latent representation learned through a dual-branch VQ-VAE.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Three-Stage Training Paradigm

1. **Stage 1: Self-supervised UVMC Learning:** A dual-branch VQ-VAE is trained on large-scale robot and human video datasets to learn UVMC. This stage focuses on creating a unified, discrete latent space that jointly encodes visual dynamics and robotic motion.
2. **Stage 2: UVMC-Guided Generalist Pretraining:** The learned UVMC knowledge is integrated into a VLM (Vision-Language Model) during large-scale pre-training on cross-embodiment robotic datasets. Learnable tokens in the VLM input are used to predict UVMC.
3. **Stage 3: Task-Specific Post-Training (Fine-tuning):** The VLA policy is further refined on task-specific datasets for deployment, adapting it to specific dynamics and improving success rates on downstream tasks.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Learning Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC)

The first stage involves training a dual-branch VQ-VAE to generate UVMC through self-supervision. This VQ-VAE comprises two complementary encoders (vision and motion) and corresponding decoders, all mapping their respective modalities into a shared discrete codebook.

- **Visual Dynamic Code Extraction:**
 - The **vision branch encoder** (`E_vis`) takes two frames, `c_t` and `c_t+h`, as input to capture temporal visual variations over `h` steps. It uses a SigLIP backbone (400M parameters) and a four-layer ViT-based visual dynamic module (32M parameters) to produce a latent code `z_vis`.
 - The **vision branch decoder** (`D_vis`) reconstructs the future frame `c_t+h` from `c_t` and `z_vis`, focusing `z_vis` on essential visual dynamics. This decoder is a 12-layer ViT-based network (94M parameters).
 - The objective is `L_vis`, combining `L1` reconstruction loss with VQ-VAE codebook and commitment regularization.

- **Robotic Motion Extraction:**
 - The **motion branch encoder** (`E_mo`) takes sequences of actions (`a_t:t+h`) and proprioceptive states (`m_t:t+h`) as input, outputting `z_mo`. It uses 1D causal strided convolutions for temporal compression and an 8-layer transformer encoder (34M parameters). It does not use raw images or instructions, ensuring the representation focuses purely on robotic dynamics.
 - The **motion branch decoder** (`D_mo`) reconstructs the action sequence `a_t:t+h` from `z_mo` and conditional inputs (`cd`, e.g., proprioceptive states `m`). In the main XR-1 variant, a Gemma-based autoregressive language model (300M parameters) is used.
 - The objective is `L_mo`, structured similarly to `L_vis`.

- **Unified Vision-Motion Codes:**
 - Both `z_vis` and `z_mo` are quantized to discrete entries (`z_e_vis`, `z_e_mo`) from a shared VQ-VAE codebook `e`. The codebook has `d` discrete entries of dimension `f`. The quantized codes are then concatenated to form `z_e_uvmc`.

- **Cross-Modality Alignment:**
 - A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence-based alignment loss (`L_align = D_KL(q(z_e_mo) || q(z_e_vis))`) is introduced. This regularizes visual codes to be consistent with their motion counterparts, mitigating task-irrelevant visual noise and grounding perception in motor dynamics. This mechanism also facilitates learning from human demonstrations where low-level actions are unavailable.

- **Final Training Objective for Stage 1:**
 - For robotic demonstrations, the total loss is `L_robot_total = L_vis + L_mo + L_align`.
 - For human demonstrations (lacking low-level actions), the objective simplifies to `L_human_total = L_vis`.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: UVMC-Guided Generalist Pretraining

After learning the UVMC, the VLA policy `π(·)` (comprising a VLM `F(·)` and an action head `H(·)`) is pre-trained.
- Learnable tokens (`t`) are introduced into the VLM's input. The VLM is trained to predict the `z_e_uvmc` using a mean squared error loss: `L_uvmc = ||F(l, o, t) - z_e_uvmc||^2_2`.
- Simultaneously, the action head is pre-trained on robot datasets using an action loss `L_act` (e.g., generative or autoregressive).
- The overall objective for this stage is `L = L_uvmc + L_act`. This joint training encourages the VLM to internalize structured vision-motion representations while performing large-scale action pre-training.

#### 3.4. Stage 3: Post-training for Deployment

In the final stage, the pre-trained VLA policy is fine-tuned on task-specific datasets using only the action loss `L_act`. This refinement adapts the generalist policy to downstream tasks. The framework's model-agnostic design allows compatibility with various VLA architectures.

#### 3.5. Data Collection and Implementation Details

- **Dataset:** A comprehensive dataset is curated, integrating Open-X, RoboMIND, Ego4D (first-person human activity videos), and XR-D (in-house data across multiple robot embodiments). Dataset-specific sampling weights are used to balance contributions.
- **XR-1 Model Instantiation:** The main XR-1 model is based on the design of π
0, using PaliGemma (SigLIP visual encoder and Gemma backbone) with an action head, totaling around 2.6 billion parameters. It is initialized from a publicly available PaliGemma checkpoint.
- **XR-1-Light Model Instantiation:** A lightweight variant, XR-1-Light, is built upon SwitchVLA, utilizing Florence-2 as the backbone (approximately 230 million parameters) for reduced computational cost. This variant skips the XR-D pretraining stage and is directly fine-tuned on task-specific data.
- **Training Resources:** UVMC pre-training and policy pre-training each consumed ~38,400 GPU hours on 80 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Fine-tuning per embodiment required ~576 GPU hours on 8 A100 GPUs.
- **Inference:** The system operates at an action chunk inference frequency of ~5 Hz and an action-level rate of ~200 Hz on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of XR-1 involved extensive real-world experiments comprising over 14,000 rollouts on six distinct robot embodiments across more than 120 diverse manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Real-World Robotic Tasks

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art Baselines:** XR-1 consistently outperformed strong VLA baselines, including π
0.5, π
0, RDT, UniVLA, and GR00T-N1.5.
 - **Dual-Arm UR-5e:** On 20 tasks, XR-1 achieved significantly higher success rates. For example, in "DUR-FindTapeBasket," XR-1 reached 85% success compared to π
0's 50%. Baselines often experienced 0% success on more challenging tasks, which the authors attribute to optimization conflicts without sufficient auxiliary supervision.
 - **Tien Kung 2.0 (Unseen during Pretraining):** When evaluated on a novel robot embodiment (Tien Kung 2.0), XR-1 maintained its performance advantage. For instance, in "TK2-MoveCupSauce," it achieved 70% success versus 60% for π
0
. This demonstrates the effectiveness of UVMC in encoding embodiment-agnostic dynamics, enabling knowledge transfer to new platforms.
 - **Other Robots:** XR-1 also demonstrated superior performance across other robot configurations, including Tien Kung 1.0, Dual-Arm Franka, AgileX Cobot Magic V2.0, and Single-Arm UR-5e, achieving substantial relative gains over the strongest baselines.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted on six Dual-Arm UR-5e tasks to analyze the contribution of XR-1's components and training strategies.

- **Lightweight Models (XR-1-Light):** The compact XR-1-Light (230M parameters) showed a +15% absolute improvement in average success rate (from 42.5% to 57.5%) when the UVMC module was integrated. This indicates that the proposed methodology provides substantial benefits even for computationally efficient architectures.
- **UVMC and Cross-Modal Alignment:** The inclusion of UVMC and the cross-modal alignment loss consistently improved performance, confirming their complementary importance for robust feature learning across tasks.
- **Scaling with Pretraining Data (Stage 1):** Increasing the Stage 1 pretraining dataset size from 1% to 100% resulted in a monotonic increase in average success rate (from 29.2% to 65.0%). The performance with 100% pretraining data was comparable to a model exclusively fine-tuned on downstream tasks (66.7%), suggesting that task-agnostic pretraining instills a powerful inductive bias.
- **Ego4D Contribution:** Including human video data from Ego4D in pre-training resulted in a 5.8% increase in average success rate, suggesting its positive impact.
- **Cross-Embodied Knowledge Transfer:** Learning similar tasks across different embodiments improved the success rate of individual embodiment skills by approximately 15%, validating that UVMC learns beneficial embodiment-agnostic features.

#### 4.3. Generalization Analysis

- **Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** XR-1 was evaluated on out-of-distribution conditions, including novel objects, dynamic and static distractors, illumination changes, and background variations. XR-1 consistently outperformed π
0
across all settings, demonstrating robustness and stable performance under environmental shifts not encountered during training.
- **Out-of-Box Evaluation:** After Stage 2 pre-training and without Stage 3 post-training, the XR-1-oob model achieved performance comparable to or better than some baselines that *had* undergone task-specific fine-tuning. This highlights UVMC's efficacy in aligning multimodal dynamics, enabling strong generalization with minimal task-specific supervision.
- **Fast Adaptation to New Tasks:** XR-1 demonstrated rapid adaptation to 15 unseen tasks on Tien Kung 2.0 (a novel embodiment) using only 20-shot demonstrations per task. It achieved significantly higher success rates compared to single-task baselines (ACT, Diffusion Policy), attributing this to large-scale pre-training and UVMC supervision.

#### 4.4. Additional Analyses

- **Visualization of Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC):**
 - **Frame-to-Frame Nearest-Neighbor Retrieval:** Cosine similarity analysis showed that motion codes (MC) retrieved visually consistent action phases from vision codes (VC) across different tasks and even different robot embodiments. This indicates UVMC's ability to learn semantic, embodiment-agnostic action representations.
 - **Task-to-Task Nearest-Neighbor Retrieval:** The mode of similarity distribution was highest for different-embodiment but similar-task pairs, suggesting that UVMC's features are primarily action-centric and invariant to embodiment.
 - **t-SNE Visualization:** Projection of UVMC embeddings confirmed semantic consistency and cross-embodiment alignment, with clusters forming based on task dynamics (e.g., "left-arm pick") rather than robot morphology.
- **Failure Analysis:** Baselines frequently exhibited failures due to optimization collapse, conflicting gradients, precision deficiencies, and coordination errors. XR-1 mitigated these issues through UVMC, which provides structured intermediate supervision. However, high-precision manipulation still presented challenges for XR-1.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of X Robotic Model 1 (XR-1) marks an advancement in the field of Embodied AI by addressing critical challenges in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) learning.

- **Addressing Key Limitations:** XR-1 directly confronts two fundamental issues in VLA models: the difficulty of generating precise low-level actions from high-dimensional observations and the inability to effectively exploit multimodal knowledge from heterogeneous data sources (diverse robots and human demonstrations). By providing a framework that explicitly targets these problems, the work contributes to overcoming significant bottlenecks in generalist robot learning.

- **Novel Contribution: Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC):** The core innovation of UVMC, a discrete latent representation that jointly encodes visual dynamics and robotic motion, is a significant methodological contribution. This concept of an embodiment-agnostic abstraction for aligning visual perception with motor control provides a structured way to represent multimodal information, moving beyond unimodal approaches that treat vision and action in isolation. The demonstrated effectiveness of UVMC in improving precision and enabling cross-modal knowledge transfer highlights its potential as a foundational component for future VLA models.

- **Robust and Generalizable Performance:** The extensive evaluation across six diverse robot embodiments and over 120 manipulation tasks, involving more than 14,000 real-world rollouts, substantiates XR-1's capabilities. Its consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art baselines, coupled with strong generalization to novel objects, environmental variations, and rapid adaptation to new tasks with limited data, indicates a substantial step towards more capable and flexible robotic agents. The ability to transfer knowledge to unseen robot embodiments further underscores the framework's robustness.

- **Scalability and Flexibility:** The three-stage training paradigm of XR-1 provides a scalable approach, leveraging large-scale datasets for representation learning and pre-training before task-specific refinement. Its model-agnostic design, demonstrated by successful instantiations with both large (PaliGemma-based) and lightweight (Florence-2-based) VLMs, suggests broad applicability and adaptability across various computational constraints and VLA architectures.

The significance of this work lies in its potential to advance the development of general-purpose robotic agents. By enabling robots to better understand and execute complex instructions, adapt to novel environments, and learn from diverse sources, XR-1 contributes to the long-term goal of robots seamlessly interacting with the physical world. The findings underscore the importance of multimodal alignment for embodied AI and suggest promising directions for future research into robust and versatile robotic intelligence.
