# CDP: Towards Robust Autoregressive Visuomotor Policy Learning via Causal Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> The Causal Diffusion Policy (CDP) model enhances visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation by explicitly conditioning action predictions on historical action sequences. It improves robustness against degraded visual observations and boosts real-time inference efficiency, consistently outperforming baseline diffusion models with success rate improvements ranging from 5% to 20% points in both simulation and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Diffusion Policy (DP) methods model actions independently, leading to distributional shift and error accumulation in sequential decision-making for robots.
- Real-world robotic deployments suffer from degraded observation quality (e.g., sensor noise, occlusions) and real-time inference constraints, which limit temporal context and cause task failures.
- Autoregressive action generation, while beneficial for temporal context, typically incurs significant computational costs, hindering real-time deployment.

## Method

- **CDP** develops a ==Causal Action Generation Module (CAGM)== to condition action prediction on historical action sequences, providing richer temporal context.
- ==Historical Actions Re-Denoising== was incorporated during training, perturbing historical actions with noise to build robustness against error accumulation and degraded observations.
- A ==Chunk-wise Autoregressive Inference== process with a ==Cache Sharing Mechanism== and ==Cyclic Temporal Embedding== was introduced to optimize computational efficiency for real-time robot control.

## Results

- CDP consistently outperformed baseline Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) by **5% to 20% points** in success rates across diverse 2D and 3D manipulation tasks in benchmarks like Adroit, DexArt, MetaWorld, and RoboFactory.
- The model demonstrated superior robustness to degraded observation data, maintaining high success rates even with noisy low-resolution point cloud inputs, unlike baselines that significantly declined.
- The Cache Sharing Mechanism reduced per-AR-step inference latency, exhibiting approximately linear scaling of speed-up with increasing historical data length, critical for real-time deployment on physical hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging historical action sequences provides essential temporal context, allowing policies to maintain performance and compensate for diminished spatial information from degraded visual observations.
> - Perturbing historical actions with noise during training makes the model more robust to error accumulation, improving its ability to handle noisy real-world data and long-horizon tasks.
> - A cache sharing mechanism significantly reduces the computational burden of autoregressive inference for transformer-based models, making complex causal diffusion policies practical for real-time robotic control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation tasks, such as grasping, placing, and assembly, are complex and exhibit high variability, making explicit programming challenging. Imitation Learning (IL) has emerged as an alternative, enabling robots to acquire skills by mimicking expert demonstrations. Among IL frameworks, Diffusion Policy (DP) has garnered attention for modeling action generation as a denoising diffusion process, facilitating smooth and multimodal trajectory prediction in visuomotor policy learning.

However, existing Diffusion Policy approaches typically employ a naive behavior cloning strategy. This strategy models actions independently, neglecting the sequential structure inherent in decision-making. Consequently, this can lead to issues such as distributional shift, where small prediction errors accumulate and push the robot into unstable or previously unobserved states. A significant challenge in real-world robotic deployments is the degradation of observation quality due to factors like sensor noise, occlusions, and hardware limitations. Concurrently, real-time inference constraints often restrict models to using instantaneous state and scene observations, thereby limiting access to richer temporal context. These practical limitations can lead to failures in critical subtasks, including object localization, grasp planning, and the execution of long-horizon tasks.

Recent advancements in diffusion models for robotics, such as 3D Diffusion Policy (3D DP), have incorporated richer representations like point clouds to enhance environmental perception. Despite these improvements, holistic generation approaches, which predict entire action sequences in a single pass, face inherent limitations regarding error propagation and the handling of long-range dependencies. This has prompted a shift towards token-wise incremental generation paradigms in robot policy learning, with notable examples including ICRT, ARP, and CARP, which aim to improve flexibility and robustness by breaking down action generation into smaller, manageable steps.

The integration of causal generation principles within diffusion models has also gained traction in sequence and video generation. Techniques like Diffusion Forcing, CausVid, and Ca2-VDM have focused on improving temporal coherence and computational efficiency by varying noise levels across tokens, integrating causal transformer architectures, and implementing efficient cache management.

This research builds upon these foundational developments by proposing a novel transformer-based diffusion framework designed to address the specific challenges of visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation. It integrates causal generation by explicitly conditioning action prediction on historical action sequences and incorporates a caching mechanism for efficient autoregressive inference. By doing so, this work aims to mitigate the adverse effects of degraded observations and real-time constraints, thereby enhancing the robustness and practicality of imitation learning in robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the practical limitations encountered when deploying Diffusion Policy (DP) in real-world robotic manipulation scenarios. Current DP implementations often suffer from degraded efficacy due to two main factors:
1. **Hardware limitations**: These can lead to a reduction in observation quality, manifesting as sensor noise, occlusions, or other data imperfections.
2. **Real-time inference constraints**: These restrict models to utilizing only instantaneous state and scene observations, precluding access to a comprehensive temporal context.

These limitations collectively hinder the robot's ability to reliably perform tasks, leading to failures in object localization, accurate grasp planning, and coherent execution of tasks over longer horizons.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Enhance Action Prediction via Historical Context**: A central objective is to improve the accuracy and coherence of action predictions by explicitly conditioning the diffusion model on historical action sequences. This approach aims to provide a richer temporal context, allowing the policy to reason over prior actions and their evolving states, rather than relying solely on instantaneous, potentially unreliable, visual observations.
2. **Improve Robustness to Degraded Observations**: The research seeks to develop a visuomotor policy that maintains performance even when faced with degraded input observation quality. By leveraging temporal continuity derived from historical actions, the model is intended to compensate for insufficient spatial constraint information in noisy or incomplete visual data.
3. **Optimize Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Inference**: Autoregressive inference, while beneficial for incorporating temporal context, typically incurs significant computational costs. Another objective is to mitigate this overhead to enable real-time deployment on physical hardware. This involves designing a mechanism that reduces redundant computations inherent in standard transformer-based policies.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between effective imitation learning in controlled environments and robust, practical deployment in real-world settings where observational quality is often compromised and real-time performance is crucial. The proposed solution aims to make visuomotor policy learning more reliable and applicable under realistic, imperfect operating conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Causal Diffusion Policy (CDP), is a transformer-based diffusion model designed to enhance visuomotor policy learning by leveraging historical action sequences and optimizing inference efficiency. The approach is structured around a Causal Action Generation Module and a Chunk-wise Autoregressive Inference process with a Cache Sharing mechanism.

**Causal Action Generation Module (CAGM)**

1. **Training Phase**:
 - **Action Sequence Division**: During training, an action sequence of length `L + M` is divided into Historical Actions ($\tilde{A}$, length `L`) and Target Actions ($A$, length `M`). $\tilde{A}$ serves as the causal condition, while $A$ are the actions to be predicted.
 - **Historical Actions Re-Denoising**: To bolster robustness against error accumulation during inference, $\tilde{A}$ is perturbed by introducing small-scale Gaussian noise ($N_\sigma \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$) to form $\tilde{A}_{perturb}$. This process encourages the model to focus on the coarse-grained temporal dynamics of historical actions rather than their precise values, thereby complementing degraded observations ($O$).
 - **Input and Objective**: The CAGM receives $\tilde{A}_{perturb}$ concatenated with Denoising Targets ($N$, sampled from $N(0,1)$). Its objective is to minimize the L2 distance between its predicted Target Actions and the ground truth $A$.
 - **Module Structure**: The CAGM consists of $P$ blocks, each performing a sequence of operations:
 - **Causal Temporal Attention (CTA)**: This layer integrates temporal dynamic context from $\tilde{A}$ into the Denoising Targets $N$, generating intermediate features $N_t$.
 - **Visual-Action Cross Attention (VACA)**: This layer incorporates spatial constraints from the degraded observation $O$ (encoded by `Enc(O)`) into $N_t$, producing $N_{ts}$. The denoising timestep embedding ($t$) is strategically placed in this layer to facilitate cache sharing of $\tilde{A}$ features.
 - **Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP)**: Further processes $N_{ts}$ to produce the block's output features $N_o$. Layer Normalization (LN) is applied after CTA, VACA, and MLP.
 - **Causal Temporal Attention Mask**: A customized attention mask is applied to ensure causality and compatibility with inference. It dictates that:
 - Denoising Targets $N$ can attend to all Historical Actions $\tilde{A}$.
 - Historical Actions $\tilde{A}$ are chunked; actions within a chunk are fully visible to each other, but not to actions in other chunks.
 - Denoising Targets $N$ are treated as a single chunk, attending to both $\tilde{A}$ and themselves.
 - Redundancy in the length of $N$ (only the first chunk-size actions are used as final predictions) enables the model to anticipate future context.

**Chunk-wise Autoregressive Inference with Cache Sharing**

1. **Chunk-wise Autoregressive Inference**:
 - In each autoregressive (AR-$k$) step, the model denoises Denoising Targets $N_k$ conditioned on Historical Actions $\tilde{A}_k$ to predict Target Actions $A_k$.
 - The generated $A_k$ are applied to the environment and then incorporated as part of the historical actions for the subsequent AR-$(k+1)$ step.
 - A window-sliding mechanism updates historical actions: the oldest "invalid" portion of $\tilde{A}_k$ is discarded, and $A_k$ is added to form $\tilde{A}_{k+1}$.

2. **Cache Sharing Mechanism**: This mechanism addresses computational redundancy in autoregressive inference.
 - Historical Actions $\tilde{A}_k$ are categorized into Cached Historical Actions (whose Key and Value representations were precomputed in prior AR steps) and Uncached Historical Actions.
 - Key ($K$) and Value ($V$) representations for cached portions of $\tilde{A}_k$ are directly reused.
 - For Uncached Historical Actions and Denoising Targets $N_k$, their Query ($Q$), Key ($K$), and Value ($V$) representations are computed using a QKV MLP layer. The $QKV$ for Uncached Historical Actions are then cached for sharing across all denoising timesteps.
 - These representations are concatenated to form the complete $Q(k, t)$, $K(k, t)$, and $V(k, t)$ for attention computation, which is then masked according to the causal temporal attention rules.

3. **Cyclic Temporal Embedding**: To handle the variable length of action sequences in autoregressive inference, a Cyclic Temporal Embedding mechanism is introduced. Instead of generating new embeddings, Denoising Targets utilize temporal embeddings that are cyclically shifted from the beginning of the sequence. During training, a random cyclic offset is applied to the temporal embedding sequence of each sample, promoting generalization across diverse temporal offsets during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results from extensive experiments conducted in both simulated and real-world environments.

**Simulation Experiments (Table 1, Fig. 4a, 4b, Table 4, 5, 6)**

- **Overall Performance**: CDP consistently outperformed baseline methods, Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), across a range of 2D and 3D manipulation tasks in the Adroit, DexArt, MetaWorld, and RoboFactory benchmarks. CDP demonstrated improvements in success rates ranging from approximately 5% to 20% points. This suggests that the causal generation paradigm, which leverages temporal dynamic context, enhances task success even when using identical visual encoders. The results indicate that CDP's reliance on both observations and historical actions contributes to its robustness in policy planning.
- **Robustness to Degraded Observation Data (Ablation Study)**: Experiments on the Lift Barrier task from RoboFactory assessed the impact of noisy low-resolution point cloud inputs. Figure 4a illustrates that CDP exhibited superior robustness to observation degradation compared to DP3. While DP3's success rate declined significantly with increased noise, CDP maintained a consistently high performance. This resilience is attributed to CDP's capacity to utilize historical action sequences, thereby compensating for diminished spatial constraints from degraded observations.
- **Efficiency of Cache Sharing Mechanism (Ablation Study)**: Evaluation on an RTX 4090D measured per-AR-step inference latency. Figure 4b demonstrates that the Cache Sharing Mechanism consistently reduced latency, with the acceleration becoming more pronounced as the length of Historical Data Pairs (Historical Actions and Observations) increased. This indicates an approximately linear scaling of speed-up with increasing historical data length.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis (Ablation Study)**:
 - **Noise Scale in Perturbed Historical Actions (Table 4)**: An intermediate noise scale (1/6) yielded the highest success rate, suggesting an optimal balance between creating robustness to error accumulation and preventing distortion of historical action structure.
 - **Chunk Size (Table 5)**: Larger chunk sizes, particularly 8, were found to be more effective, especially for complex tasks, leading to improved model performance.
 - **Length of Historical Actions and Denoising Targets (Table 6)**: Generally, longer sequences of Historical Actions were beneficial for model performance. The research also highlighted that optimal performance depended on well-matched combinations of Historical Actions length and Denoising Targets length.

**Real-World Experiments (Table 2, Fig. 5)**

- **Task Performance**: CDP was evaluated on three real-world tasks: "Collecting Objects," "Stacking Cubes," and "Push T." CDP consistently outperformed the baseline DP across all tasks in terms of grasping success rate, placing success rate (conditional on successful grasping), and overall task success rate.
- **Data Efficiency**: The model achieved high success rates across these tasks even when trained with a modest dataset of 50 expert demonstrations.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Figure 5 provides visual evidence of CDP's ability to generate effective trajectories in real-world scenarios, consistent with its strong performance in simulation.

Overall, the findings suggest that CDP's causal generation paradigm, which integrates historical action sequences, and its efficient autoregressive inference mechanism contribute to significantly improved accuracy, robustness, and practicality for visuomotor policy learning in robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Causal Diffusion Policy (CDP) introduces a methodological advancement in visuomotor policy learning that addresses critical challenges in deploying robotic systems in practical, real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness to Imperfect Conditions**: A primary contribution of CDP is its ability to counteract the degradation of observation quality arising from factors such as sensor noise, occlusions, and hardware limitations. By leveraging historical action sequences to provide temporal context, CDP can compensate for diminished spatial constraint information in noisy or incomplete visual inputs. This enhanced robustness is crucial for reliable robot operation outside of controlled laboratory settings.
2. **Improved Policy Coherence and Accuracy**: The causal-transformer diffusion framework allows the model to reason through temporal continuity, leading to more coherent and accurate action predictions. This sequential understanding of actions helps mitigate issues like distributional shift and error accumulation that often plague non-autoregressive or purely instantaneous observation-based policies.
3. **Feasibility for Real-Time Deployment**: The introduction of a Cache Sharing Mechanism is significant for practical applications. By reusing pre-computed key-value attention tensors, the mechanism substantially reduces the computational burden of autoregressive action generation. This optimization is essential for achieving the low-latency inference required for real-time control on physical robot hardware, making the complex causal modeling approach computationally tractable.
4. **Broader Applicability**: The comprehensive evaluations across diverse 2D and 3D manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world settings demonstrate the generalizability and effectiveness of CDP. This indicates its potential applicability to a wide array of robotic manipulation problems, from fine-grained dexterity to multi-object interaction.
5. **Advancement in Imitation Learning**: CDP pushes the boundaries of imitation learning by explicitly integrating temporal dynamics and addressing robustness concerns. It offers a more sophisticated approach to learning complex behaviors from demonstrations, moving beyond naive behavior cloning to capture the sequential nature of decision-making.
6. **Future Research Directions**: While demonstrating substantial progress, the paper identifies a limitation regarding tasks demanding "extremely long horizons." This explicit acknowledgment opens avenues for future research to extend CDP's capabilities to more complex, multi-stage, or prolonged robotic operations, further enhancing its impact on autonomous systems.

In conclusion, CDP represents a step towards making visuomotor policies more robust, efficient, and practical for real-world robotic manipulation. Its methodological innovations have the potential to facilitate wider adoption of imitation learning in industrial, service, and research robotics applications where environmental imperfections and real-time performance are key considerations.
