# Diffusion-Guided Multi-Arm Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT introduce DG-MAP, a closed-loop multi-arm motion planning framework that combines MAPF-inspired search with specialized conditional diffusion models to achieve scalable and data-efficient coordination. The framework demonstrates high success rates (over 90%) for up to eight robotic arms on goal-reaching tasks and 89% on a multi-arm pick-and-place task, outperforming baselines even when trained on significantly less data.

## Problem

- Multi-arm motion planning faces scalability issues ('curse of dimensionality') as the number of robotic arms and their degrees of freedom increase, making traditional sampling-based methods computationally prohibitive.
- Existing learning-based multi-arm planners often require extensive and diverse training data covering all higher-order interactions, which is costly and time-consuming to collect.
- Efficient and robust collision avoidance, particularly for inter-arm conflicts in dense shared workspaces, remains a significant challenge for multi-robot systems.

## Method

- A **Diffusion-Guided Multi-Arm Planner (DG-MAP)** framework is proposed, integrating a ==Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF)-inspired search== with two specialized ==conditional denoising diffusion models==.
- A ==single-arm diffusion model== generates feasible individual trajectories, while a ==dual-arm diffusion model== specifically handles pairwise collision resolution by conditioning an ego-arm's trajectory on a conflicting arm's state.
- The system operates within a ==closed-loop, receding horizon control== framework, allowing for continuous adaptation and re-planning by executing partial action sequences and updating the environment state.

## Results

- DG-MAP achieved average success rates **above 90%** across various task difficulties for 3 to 8 arms, substantially outperforming a comparable limited-data baseline which often dropped **below 10%** for 4+ arms.
- The framework was competitive with, and often surpassed, an extended-data baseline (trained on richer 3- and 4-arm interaction data), demonstrating relative improvements of up to **3.8%** on medium tasks and **3.3%** on hard tasks.
- In a four-arm pick-and-place simulation, DG-MAP achieved an **89%** success rate, outperforming both limited-data (**37.5%**) and extended-data (**71.4%**) baselines, showcasing its practical utility for collaborative tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the multi-arm planning problem into single-arm planning and pairwise conflict resolution, guided by specialized diffusion models, significantly enhances scalability and data efficiency.
> - Training diffusion models on lower-order interactions (single-arm and dual-arm data) allows generalization to larger teams of up to eight arms, reducing the need for extensive higher-order interaction datasets.
> - The hybrid approach, combining generative diffusion models for trajectory synthesis with a systematic search-based conflict resolution, provides robust collision avoidance and effective coordination in complex, shared workspaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-arm motion planning is a foundational challenge in robotics, crucial for enabling robotic systems to perform complex, long-horizon tasks that require multiple manipulators to operate efficiently and without collisions in shared workspaces. This area of research aims to address the coordination of robotic arms, which involves managing high-dimensional joint configuration spaces.

Traditional sampling-based motion planners (SMPs), such as Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT) and Probabilistic Roadmaps (PRM), face scalability issues when applied to multi-arm systems due to the "curse of dimensionality" as the number of arms and their degrees of freedom increase. Optimization-based planners, while capable of refining trajectories, are prone to local minima and require favorable initial conditions.

More recent approaches have explored learning-based methods. For single-arm planning, these methods can guide traditional planners. However, extending them to multi-arm setups typically necessitates extensive and expensive multi-arm training data and involves solving large, non-convex optimization problems. To improve scalability, some methods have integrated SMPs with Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) decomposition techniques, which construct individual roadmaps for each arm and coordinate them using MAPF-like solvers. Despite this, the computational cost of generating initial SMP roadmaps can still be a bottleneck with increasing arm numbers.

End-to-end learning-based approaches, such as Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), have also emerged. While MARL can train decentralized policies and demonstrate scalability, these policies often depend on diverse expert demonstrations from SMPs and may struggle to generalize to larger teams if trained primarily on simpler, lower-order interactions (e.g., two-arm interactions).

Diffusion models represent another line of work, offering generative capabilities and explicit constraint handling. Approaches like Multi-robot Multi-model planning Diffusion (MMD) integrate single-robot diffusion models within MAPF frameworks for collision-free navigation. However, effectively encoding complex multi-agent geometric constraints for articulated manipulators within diffusion models remains a challenge. Additionally, current end-to-end multi-agent diffusion policies often require centralized training with data covering the full team composition, which limits their scalability to unseen team sizes.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by seeking to enhance the scalability and data efficiency of learning-based multi-arm motion planning. It draws inspiration from MAPF's decomposition principles while leveraging the generative strengths of diffusion models. The key distinction is its structured approach to learning, focusing on specialized single-arm and dual-arm interaction models rather than an end-to-end policy for all team sizes or relying solely on traditional planners. This aims to overcome the data dependency of end-to-end learning and the scalability limitations of traditional methods when dealing with complex, high-dimensional multi-arm scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a scalable and data-efficient multi-arm motion planner capable of generating collision-free coordination for complex, long-horizon tasks in shared workspaces. The authors identify several motivations for this objective:

1. **Addressing Scalability Challenges:** Current multi-arm motion planning methods often face scalability issues due to the exponential growth of the state space as the number of arms increases. This "curse of dimensionality" makes traditional sampling-based planners computationally prohibitive and poses a significant hurdle for learning-based approaches that struggle to generalize to larger team sizes.
2. **Reducing Reliance on Large Training Datasets:** Existing learning-based models, especially end-to-end policies, typically require extensive and diverse training data that covers all relevant interaction scenarios, including higher-order (three or more arms) conflicts. Collecting such large datasets for complex multi-arm systems is costly and time-consuming. The authors aim to reduce this data dependency by focusing on learning from lower-order interactions.
3. **Enabling Efficient Collision Resolution:** Recognizing that a majority of collisions in multi-arm scenarios are pairwise, the work is motivated to develop a mechanism for efficient and effective conflict resolution between pairs of arms. This approach seeks to avoid the computational burden of planning in the full, high-dimensional joint space by focusing on the "conflict-resolution space."
4. **Developing a Closed-Loop Planning Framework:** For practical deployment, robotic systems require closed-loop control that allows for adaptation to dynamic environments and persistent collision avoidance. The paper is motivated to integrate the planning mechanism within a receding horizon control framework to achieve this.
5. **Leveraging Generative Models for Trajectory Synthesis:** Diffusion models have shown promise in generating complex data distributions and handling constraints. The authors are motivated to harness these generative capabilities to synthesize feasible single-arm and collision-avoiding dual-arm trajectories, thereby guiding the planning process.

Specifically, the key objectives are to:
- Propose a novel closed-loop multi-arm motion planning framework, DG-MAP, that integrates structured decomposition principles from MAPF with conditional diffusion models.
- Develop two specialized conditional denoising diffusion models: one for generating feasible single-arm trajectories respecting individual constraints, and another specifically for generating collision-avoiding trajectories for effective pairwise conflict resolution.
- Empirically validate the framework's scalability and effectiveness, particularly when trained on limited interaction data (single and dual-arm trajectories only), compared to alternative learning-based methods.
- Demonstrate the practical applicability of DG-MAP beyond simple goal-reaching tasks through evaluation on a multi-arm pick-and-place scenario.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Diffusion-Guided Multi-Arm Planner (DG-MAP) integrates a MAPF-inspired search framework with two specialized conditional diffusion models. The approach is designed to provide scalability and data efficiency for multi-arm motion planning.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The problem involves N manipulator arms, each with `d_i` degrees-of-freedom, operating in a system configuration space `Q_sys = x_N_i=1 Q_i`. The objective is to find a simultaneous, continuous path `τ` for all arms, from a start configuration `q_start` to a goal `q_final`. This path must satisfy boundary conditions (end-effectors reaching target poses within specified tolerances) and collision avoidance (each arm being collision-free with itself and static obstacles, and no inter-arm collisions between any distinct pair).

#### 3.2. Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) Principles

DG-MAP draws inspiration from MAPF, which decomposes multi-agent planning into single-agent problems with conflict resolution. Algorithms like Conflict-Based Search (CBS) use single-agent planners to propose individual trajectories and then iteratively resolve detected conflicts by imposing spatio-temporal constraints. DG-MAP adopts this decomposition, where arms initially plan independently, and conflicts are resolved through a search process.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Models

The framework utilizes Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs). These generative models learn to reverse a process that gradually adds Gaussian noise to data over K steps. By iteratively denoising a sample of pure noise, the model can generate a sample (e.g., a trajectory segment). The model `ϵ_θ` predicts the noise at each step, trained by minimizing the mean squared error between the true and predicted noise. This capability for modeling complex, multi-modal distributions makes diffusion models suitable for generating manipulator trajectories.

#### 3.4. DG-MAP Framework

The DG-MAP approach consists of two phases: offline training of specialized diffusion models and an online search-based planning strategy.

##### 3.4.1. Single-Arm Diffusion Model (ϵ_θ_1)

- **Purpose:** This model generates feasible single-arm trajectories, respecting individual arm constraints and environmental obstacles (excluding other arms). It serves as the initial trajectory proposer for each arm.
- **Training:** A shared set of model parameters (`θ_1`) is learned using expert demonstrations collected from single-arm BiRRT plans. This leverages the Diffusion Policy framework for closed-loop control.
- **Observations (`o_i`):** Each arm `i` receives its own observation history over `T_o` steps, including current joint configuration, end-effector pose, target end-effector pose, link positions, and fixed base pose.
- **Actions (`∆q_i`):** The model predicts a sequence of future delta joint actions (`∆q_i`) over a prediction horizon `T_p`.
- **Loss Function:** Standard diffusion objective minimizing the squared error between predicted and true noise.

##### 3.4.2. Dual-Arm Diffusion Model (ϵ_θ_2)

- **Purpose:** This model is specifically designed to generate feasible trajectories for an ego-arm to resolve pairwise conflicts, conditioned on the relevant states of a conflicting interacting arm.
- **Training:** This model (`θ_2`) is trained using expert demonstrations collected from dual-arm BiRRT plans.
- **Observations (`ˆo_i`):** The observation structure is designed to capture interactions. For an ego-arm `i` conflicting with arm `j`, `ˆo_i` is constructed by pairing transformed observations of arm `j` (mapped into arm `i`'s reference frame) with arm `i`'s own observations across the history window `T_o`.
- **Actions (`∆q_i`):** The model predicts delta joint action sequences for the ego-arm.
- **Loss Function:** Similar to `ϵ_θ_1`, minimizing the squared error between predicted and true noise.

##### 3.4.3. Diffusion-Guided Multi-Arm Planning (DG-MAP Search)

The trained diffusion models are integrated into a search framework inspired by MAPF:
- **Planner State:** The search uses nodes (N) representing tuples of selected plan indices `b = (b_1, ..., b_N)`. Each `b_i` points to a candidate delta-action sequence `∆q_b_i_i` for arm `i` over horizon `T_p`. A frontier set (F) acts as a min-priority queue, ordered by a cost function `g(N)`.
- **Initialization:** For each arm `i`, `B` diverse candidate delta-action sequences are sampled using the single-arm model `ϵ_θ_1` based on its observation history (`o_i`). The search begins with a root node `N_0` in F. A collision cache `C` stores pairwise collision results to avoid redundant checks.
- **Search Process:**
 1. The algorithm iteratively extracts the lowest-cost node `N` from F.
 2. It checks the plan combination `{∆q_N.b_i_i}` for collisions using `FindFirstCollision`.
 3. If no collision is found, a valid solution is returned.
 4. If a conflict `c = (i, j, ˆt)` is detected (between arm `i` and `j` at time `ˆt`), the earliest conflict time `t*` is updated.
 5. Successor nodes are generated for the conflicting arms `i` and `j` using two strategies:
 - **Rebranch (Algorithm 2):** Creates successors by trying alternative, pre-existing plans from `P` (initially sampled by `ϵ_θ_1`) for the conflicting arm. It avoids plans already identified as conflicting in ancestor nodes.
 - **Repair (Algorithm 3):** Uses the dual-arm model `ϵ_θ_2` to generate `B` new candidate action sequences for the conflicting ego-arm, specifically conditioned on the paired observation `ˆo_ego` to avoid the detected conflict with the other arm. These new sequences are added to `P`, and successors are created.
 6. All generated successors are added to F, allowing the search to explore alternatives based on their estimated costs, which combine action magnitude, goal proximity, and a large penalty for collisions.
- **Termination:** The search concludes when a collision-free node is found or a timeout/empty frontier condition is met. The best plan found is returned.

#### 3.5. Closed-Loop, Receding Horizon Control

DG-MAP operates within a closed-loop, receding horizon controller. At each step, it plans from the current state to generate action sequences `{∆q_b_i_i}` for horizon `T_p` and an earliest predicted collision-free duration `t* ≤ T_p`. Actions are executed for `t*` steps, the environment state is updated, and the planning process repeats. This allows for adaptation and persistent collision avoidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate DG-MAP's scalability, data efficiency, and performance against learning-based baselines across varying team sizes and task complexities.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Task:** Static single goal-reaching tasks for N = 3 to N = 8 manipulator arms (6 DoF each) in the PyBullet simulator.
- **Success Criteria:** All arms reach target end-effector poses within 0.03 units position tolerance and 0.1 radians orientation tolerance within 400 simulation steps.
- **Dataset:** 18,000 unique test scenarios with novel base and target poses, categorized into easy, medium, and hard difficulties based on maximum workspace intersection volume between arm pairs.
- **Baselines:** Two variants of the multi-arm motion planner from Ha et al.:
 - **Baseline-LD (Limited Data):** Trained only on single-arm and dual-arm interactions (up to 756.8M timesteps), comparable to DG-MAP's training data scope.
 - **Baseline-ED (Extended Data):** Trained on extended interaction data including three and four arms (up to 668.2M timesteps), representing a more data-intensive approach.

**Key Findings:**

**Q1 & Q2: Scalability and Comparison with Baseline-LD (Limited Data):**
- **Results (Table 1):** DG-MAP consistently achieved high average success rates above 90% across all task difficulties and team sizes (3 to 8 arms). In contrast, Baseline-LD, trained on similar limited interaction data, showed moderate success with 3 arms (41.2% average) but experienced significant performance degradation with more arms, often dropping below 10% and failing entirely on medium and hard tasks beyond 4 arms.
- **Conclusion:** This demonstrates DG-MAP's ability to scale effectively to a larger number of arms (up to 8) while being trained on only lower-order (single/dual-arm) interaction data, substantially outperforming a comparable learning-only baseline. The structured planning-guided coordination in DG-MAP addresses the scalability limitations of learning-based methods that rely solely on learned policies without explicit conflict resolution.

**Q3: Competitiveness with Baseline-ED (Extended Data):**
- **Results (Table 2):** Baseline-ED, trained on richer multi-arm interaction data (including 3- and 4-arm scenarios), maintained high success rates (over 85%) across all settings. However, DG-MAP consistently outperformed Baseline-ED, showing relative improvements up to 3.8% on medium tasks with 6 arms and 3.3% on hard tasks with 8 arms.
- **Conclusion:** This finding indicates that DG-MAP's combination of specialized diffusion models and MAPF-inspired planning provides robust scalability and efficiency. It can compete with, and often surpass, end-to-end learned policies that have benefited from more extensive and complex multi-arm training data, highlighting DG-MAP's data efficiency and the effectiveness of its structured approach.

**Q4: Application to Practical Tasks (Multi-Arm Pick-And-Place):**
- **Task:** A four-arm, 6-DoF pick-and-place simulation where UR5 robots collaboratively pick objects from the ground and deposit them into a central bin (Figure 4).
- **Results (Table 3):** DG-MAP achieved a success rate of 89% across 100 trials, outperforming both Baseline-LD (37.5%) and Baseline-ED (71.4%). DG-MAP also showed a lower average number of steps compared to Baseline-ED (5390 vs 6018).
- **Conclusion:** This demonstrates DG-MAP's practical utility and effectiveness in enabling scalable, collision-free multi-arm coordination in a dense, shared workspace, extending beyond simple goal-reaching tasks.

**Additional Findings (Appendix A.3 - Effect of value-based diffusion models):**
- An exploration using DiffusionQL-trained variants of DG-MAP (ϵ_QL_θ_1, ϵ_QL_θ_2) showed a slightly higher success rate (90.8% vs. 89.0%) and fewer steps (5254 vs. 5390) on the multi-arm pick-and-place task (Table 5).
- For general goal-reaching benchmarks (Table 6), the performance difference between the standard and DiffusionQL variants was minimal and inconsistent, typically less than 1% difference in average success rate. This suggests that the core benefit of DG-MAP stems from its structured planning framework and the generative capabilities of diffusion models, rather than a strong dependence on the specific diffusion training objective (behavioral cloning vs. Q-learning for rewards) in the general case.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DG-MAP, a framework that provides a scalable and data-efficient solution for multi-arm motion planning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

1. **Enhanced Scalability for Multi-Arm Systems:** DG-MAP effectively addresses the "curse of dimensionality" inherent in multi-arm planning by decomposing the problem. Its ability to achieve high success rates for up to eight arms, while being trained on only single and dual-arm interaction data, indicates a substantial step forward in managing the combinatorial complexity of multi-robot systems. This scalability is crucial for deploying robotic teams in increasingly complex industrial and service environments.

2. **Increased Data Efficiency in Learning-Based Robotics:** By leveraging specialized diffusion models for single-arm trajectory generation and, more distinctly, for pairwise collision resolution, DG-MAP reduces the reliance on large, diverse datasets containing higher-order multi-arm interactions. This data efficiency lowers the barrier to entry for training sophisticated multi-arm planners, as collecting comprehensive data for complex N-arm scenarios is resource-intensive. The method's performance, competitive with or exceeding baselines trained on richer data, highlights the effectiveness of this structured, data-efficient approach.

3. **Robust Collision Avoidance through Structured Decomposition:** The MAPF-inspired decomposition, combined with a dedicated dual-arm diffusion model for conflict resolution, provides a systematic and robust mechanism for avoiding inter-arm collisions. This contrasts with end-to-end learned policies that might struggle with generalization to unseen conflict scenarios or larger team sizes. The closed-loop, receding horizon control further enhances robustness by allowing continuous adaptation and re-planning.

4. **Practical Applicability:** The successful demonstration on a complex multi-arm pick-and-place task validates DG-MAP's utility beyond basic goal-reaching. This suggests its potential for application in real-world scenarios requiring collaborative manipulation, assembly, or logistics in shared workspaces, such as manufacturing lines or warehouses.

5. **Integration of Generative Models and Search-Based Planning:** The work effectively combines the strengths of modern generative models (diffusion models for trajectory synthesis) with established search-based planning principles (MAPF-like conflict resolution). This hybrid approach offers a path to developing intelligent planners that benefit from learning capabilities while retaining the explainability and robustness associated with explicit search.

The potential impact of DG-MAP includes accelerating the deployment of multi-robot systems in various domains, reducing the cost and time associated with training complex robot policies, and providing a robust framework for safe and efficient multi-arm collaboration. Future research could build upon this foundation by exploring morphology-agnostic representations, more complex collaborative tasks, or adaptive integration of higher-order interaction models when necessary.
