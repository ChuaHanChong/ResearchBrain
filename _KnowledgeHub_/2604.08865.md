# SPPO: Sequence-Level PPO for Long-Horizon Reasoning Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> SPPO (Sequence-Level PPO) presents an efficient and stable reinforcement learning algorithm for aligning Large Language Models on long-horizon reasoning tasks with sparse rewards. By reformulating reasoning as a Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit, SPPO achieves superior performance on mathematical benchmarks with a 5.9x training speedup over existing methods, while enabling reduced memory footprint through a decoupled critic.

## Problem

- Standard token-level PPO suffers from high bias and instability due to the "tail effect" when assigning credit in long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) tasks with sparse, delayed rewards.
- Existing stable, critic-free methods like GRPO incur significant computational overhead by requiring multiple response samples per prompt to reduce gradient variance, limiting training throughput.

## Method

- Introduces ==Sequence-Level PPO== (**SPPO**), which reformulates long-horizon reasoning tasks as a Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit, treating an entire response as an atomic action.
- A scalar value model is trained with Binary Cross-Entropy loss to estimate the probability of success for a given prompt, providing a stable, sequence-level baseline.
- The single, sequence-level advantage is uniformly propagated to all tokens within a generated sequence, effectively decoupling credit assignment from sequence length.

## Results

- SPPO consistently achieved the highest average performance on mathematical benchmarks, scoring **48.06**% for the **1.5B** model and **58.11**% for the **7B** model, outperforming GRPO (N=8) with single-sample efficiency (N=1).
- The decoupled critic strategy (**1.5B** critic for **7B** policy) not only maintained top performance, achieving an average score of **58.56**% for the **7B** model, but also significantly reduced VRAM usage.
- SPPO demonstrated a **5.9x** training speedup compared to GRPO, reaching peak performance faster due to its single-sample processing and stable optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The effectiveness of existing group-based methods implicitly demonstrates the structural robustness gained by modeling reasoning at the sequence level.
> - Estimating the scalar solvability of a problem is less complex than token-level generative reasoning, allowing for a smaller, decoupled critic model to align larger policies.
> - Uniformly applying a single sequence-level advantage to all tokens in a response circumvents the temporal credit assignment problem in sparse-reward, long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated increased proficiency in complex reasoning tasks, particularly when employing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a significant method for aligning these models with specific objectives, especially in settings involving verifiable rewards, such as mathematical problem-solving (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards, or RLVR).

Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a widely adopted algorithm for RL-based alignment of LLMs. However, the standard token-level PPO framework, which relies on a token-level critic and Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) for credit assignment, encounters structural incompatibilities in long-horizon CoT tasks. Specifically, the sparse and delayed nature of rewards in these tasks means that GAE must propagate signals across potentially thousands of tokens. This can introduce high bias and lead to instability, as the critic may "overfit" to semantic cues near the end of a sequence, causing the advantage signal to diminish prematurely for correct trajectories or failing to adequately penalize incorrect intermediate steps. This phenomenon, termed the "tail effect," undermines effective optimization.

In response to PPO's limitations, alternative approaches such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have been developed. GRPO mitigates the issues of token-level critic instability by eliminating the learned critic and instead deriving advantage signals from group-based statistical baselines. This approach implicitly re-frames the reasoning process as a Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit problem, treating an entire response as an atomic action, thereby bypassing the token-level noise. While successful in improving stability, GRPO introduces a new challenge: it relies on Monte Carlo outcomes for baseline estimation, which can result in high variance in the gradient signal. To counteract this variance, GRPO necessitates sampling multiple responses per prompt, leading to substantial computational overhead and bottlenecking training throughput.

This work builds upon the understanding that GRPO's efficacy stems from its implicit sequence-level modeling. It aims to develop an algorithm that explicitly formalizes this sequence-level perspective while addressing both the high-bias issue of standard PPO and the high-variance, computational inefficiency of multi-sampling critic-free methods like GRPO.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop Sequence-Level PPO (SPPO), a scalable algorithm that harmonizes the sample efficiency characteristic of standard PPO with the stability associated with outcome-based updates, specifically for long-horizon reasoning tasks in LLMs.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing PPO's Instability in Long-Horizon CoT:** Standard token-level PPO struggles with temporal credit assignment in long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) tasks, particularly when rewards are sparse and only available at the end of a sequence. The authors highlight the "tail effect," where the token-level critic overfits to the end of the sequence, causing the advantage signal for earlier, crucial steps to vanish, leading to unstable optimization.
- **Overcoming Computational Overhead of Critic-Free Methods:** While critic-free methods like GRPO improve stability by implicitly treating the entire response as an atomic action, they incur significant computational costs. Their reliance on sampling multiple responses per prompt to generate statistical baselines severely limits training throughput, making them resource-intensive.
- **Formalizing the Implicit Sequence-Level Bandit Insight:** The authors posit that GRPO's success originates from its implicit reformulation of reasoning as a Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit problem. This observation motivates an explicit re-framing of the problem to harness this structural robustness more efficiently.
- **Developing a Scalable and Resource-Efficient Alignment Framework:** The goal is to design an algorithm that provides a stable, sequence-level baseline using a generalizable scalar value model, enabling optimization stability without the need for computationally expensive multi-sampling. Furthermore, the research aims to explore architectures, such as decoupled critics, to reduce the memory footprint and make RLVR training more accessible.

In essence, the research seeks to resolve the trade-off between the high bias and instability of token-level PPO and the high computational cost of existing stable, group-based methods, thereby providing a more efficient and robust framework for aligning reasoning LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces Sequence-Level PPO (SPPO), which fundamentally re-conceptualizes the reasoning process for LLMs.

#### 3.1. Formulation: Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit (SL-CB)

SPPO explicitly reformulates the reasoning task from a token-level Markov Decision Process (MDP) to a Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit (SL-CB). In this formulation:
- The **context (S)** is defined solely by the static prompt ($s_p$).
- The **action (A)** is the entire generated response sequence ($a_{seq} = (y_1, \ldots, y_T)$), treated as a single, atomic unit.
- The **reward ($r(s_p, a_{seq})$)** evaluates the holistic correctness of the complete generated chain, typically a binary outcome (success/failure).

This SL-CB formulation collapses the time horizon to a single step (H=1), thereby circumventing the temporal credit assignment ambiguity inherent in token-level MDPs with sparse rewards. Instead of decomposing a sparse outcome into noisy token-level signals, the value function $V(s_p)$ directly estimates the scalar probability of success for a given problem prompt.

#### 3.2. SPPO: Sequence-Level Proximal Policy Optimization

SPPO integrates this SL-CB formulation into a PPO-like optimization framework.

- **Value Function and Advantage Estimation:**
 - A **scalar value model** $V_\phi(s_p)$ is trained to estimate the probability of success for a given prompt $s_p$. This contrasts with standard PPO's token-level value function, which predicts returns from intermediate states.
 - The **advantage** for a sampled sequence $a$ generated from prompt $s_p$ is calculated as $A(s_p, a) = R - V_\phi(s_p)$, where $R \in \{0, 1\}$ is the binary reward for the entire sequence. This formulation standardizes the advantage, amplifying the signal when the model is confident but incorrect, and reducing noise when the model is uncertain.
 - The value model $V_\phi(s_p)$ is optimized using a **Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss**: $L_V(\phi) = -E[R \log V_\phi(s_p) + (1 - R) \log(1 - V_\phi(s_p))]$. This loss ensures that $V_\phi(s_p)$ provides a calibrated estimate of the success probability, serving as a stable baseline.

- **Sequence-Level Policy Optimization:**
 - SPPO adapts the clipped surrogate objective of PPO. However, the crucial difference lies in the application of the advantage term. Unlike standard PPO, where each token $t$ receives a unique, time-dependent advantage $\hat{A}_t$ via GAE, SPPO propagates the *single, sequence-level advantage* $A(s_p, a)$ uniformly to all constituent tokens $t$ within the generated sequence $a$.
 - The objective function is defined as: $J_{SPPO}(\theta) = E_{s_p \sim D, a \sim \pi_{\theta_k}, t \in a} [ \min(r_t(\theta)A(s_p, a), \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1 - \epsilon, 1 + \epsilon)A(s_p, a)) ]$. Here, $r_t(\theta)$ is the probability ratio for token $t$, and $\epsilon$ is the clipping hyperparameter.
 - This uniform propagation means that if a reasoning chain is correct, every token contributing to it is equally reinforced; if it fails, every token is equally penalized. This mechanism effectively decouples the advantage signal from the sequence length, providing a solution to the temporal credit assignment problem in long CoT tasks.

#### 3.3. Decoupled Critic Strategy

Based on the hypothesis that estimating scalar solvability is less complex than generative reasoning, the authors investigate a "Decoupled Critic" strategy. This involves using a significantly smaller critic model (e.g., 1.5B parameters) to align a larger policy model (e.g., 7B parameters). This approach aims to reduce the memory footprint associated with RLHF training, where traditionally the critic model is of similar size to the policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B models, trained on DeepScaleR and DAPO-17K datasets, and evaluated on five mathematical benchmarks: AIME24/25, AMC23, MATH500, and Minerva Math, using Average@16 accuracy. Baselines included the Base Model, Standard PPO, ReMax, RLOO, and GRPO (N=8).

#### 4.1. Performance on Mathematical Benchmarks

- **SPPO's Superior Performance:** SPPO consistently achieved the highest average performance across the benchmarks for both 1.5B and 7B models. For the 1.5B model, SPPO recorded an average accuracy of 48.06, surpassing GRPO (N=8) which achieved 47.08. On the 7B model, SPPO reached an average of 58.11.
- **PPO's Instability:** Standard PPO generally struggled to improve upon the base model, confirming its instability in sparse-reward, long-horizon settings. This aligns with the "tail effect" described, where token-level credit assignment is ineffective.
- **Single-Sample Efficiency:** SPPO achieved its performance with single-sample efficiency (N=1), contrasting with GRPO's requirement for multiple samples (N=8), thereby demonstrating computational efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.

#### 4.2. Critic Decoupling

- The "Decoupled Critic" strategy, where a 1.5B critic was used to align a 7B policy, not only maintained performance but achieved the highest average score (58.56) among all configurations for the 7B model. This indicates that a lightweight critic can effectively guide a larger policy model. This strategy also significantly reduced the memory footprint, making RLVR training more resource-efficient.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Impact of Loss Function

- An ablation study showed that integrating Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss into standard token-level PPO ("PPO + BCE") did not replicate SPPO's performance and exhibited instability. This evidence suggests that SPPO's effectiveness is primarily attributed to its Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit formulation and the unified advantage signal, rather than the BCE loss in isolation.

#### 4.4. Scalability and Computational Efficiency

- **Training Speedup:** SPPO demonstrated superior training efficiency. For the 7B model, SPPO reached peak performance in approximately 22 hours, while group-based methods like GRPO required significantly longer to achieve comparable or lower scores, due to the bottleneck of generating multiple samples. SPPO achieved a 5.9x training speedup compared to GRPO.
- **Resource Efficiency (VRAM):** The decoupled critic strategy (7B policy with 1.5B critic) significantly reduced peak VRAM usage compared to symmetric actor-critic setups (7B policy with 7B critic) and other baselines. This suggests SPPO, especially with a smaller critic, can align large reasoning models under constrained GPU budgets.

#### 4.5. Value Model Analysis: Calibration and Correlation

- A correlation analysis on 200 held-out prompts showed a positive linear correlation (Pearson r = 0.642, Spearman r = 0.664) between the critic's predicted difficulty ($V(s_p)$) and the empirical success rate (AVG@k). This indicates the value model successfully learned to assess problem difficulty.
- The critic's predictions had a unimodal, quasi-normal distribution, contrasting with the bimodal (0 or 1) distribution of empirical task difficulty. This suggests a conservative prediction strategy, aggregating uncertainty rather than overfitting to binary extremes, while still providing a valid, variance-reducing baseline.

#### 4.6. Controlled Analysis: The RLVR Benchmark

- SPPO was evaluated on five classic control tasks (CartPole, MountainCar, Hopper, LunarLander, Pendulum) reconfigured with sparse, binary rewards (RLVR framework).
- SPPO consistently matched or outperformed Standard PPO across these environments. In long-horizon tasks like Hopper and MountainCar, where PPO failed to propagate sparse signals effectively, SPPO successfully converged by estimating sequence-level solvability. SPPO also showed improved stability and monotonic improvement in LunarLander and achieved superior precision alignment in CartPole.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Sequence-Level PPO (SPPO), an algorithm that addresses critical limitations in applying Reinforcement Learning to align Large Language Models for complex, long-horizon reasoning tasks with sparse, verifiable rewards.

The primary significance lies in SPPO's ability to **resolve the fundamental trade-off** between the high-bias and instability issues inherent in standard token-level PPO, and the high-variance and computational inefficiency associated with group-based, critic-free methods like GRPO. By explicitly reformulating reasoning as a Sequence-Level Contextual Bandit problem, SPPO offers a structurally stable approach that decouples the credit assignment problem from the sequence length.

**Key potential impacts include:**

- **Enhanced Stability and Efficiency for RLVR:** SPPO provides a robust and stable optimization framework for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) tasks. Its single-sample efficiency (N=1) translates to significantly higher training throughput and faster convergence compared to multi-sampling baselines, achieving a 5.9x speedup over GRPO in tested configurations. This makes the process of aligning LLMs for reasoning more practical and less time-consuming.
- **Increased Accessibility to LLM Alignment:** The demonstrated efficacy of a "Decoupled Critic" strategy, where a substantially smaller critic model can effectively align a much larger policy, offers a tangible path to **reduced memory footprint and GPU resource requirements**. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, enabling the alignment of powerful reasoning models even with constrained hardware resources.
- **A New Paradigm for Reasoning Alignment:** By shifting the perspective from token-level MDPs to sequence-level contextual bandits, SPPO offers a conceptual framework that may be more congruent with the outcome-oriented nature of many reasoning tasks. This can inspire further research into similar sequence-level approaches for other complex generative tasks.
- **Broader Applicability (within defined constraints):** The validation across diverse mathematical benchmarks and, importantly, in re-configured classic control environments demonstrates SPPO's generalized robustness in sparse-reward, long-horizon settings beyond just LLMs.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The authors acknowledge that SPPO is specifically tailored for tasks with verifiable outcomes, as its value function estimates prompt solvability based on ground truth. Extending this sequence-level bandit formulation to open-ended generation tasks, where objective ground-truth verifiers are absent, represents a direction for future research.

**Ethical Considerations:** The paper notes that while the study is conducted in controlled environments without human subjects or personal data, the improved capabilities of reasoning models could be misused (e.g., for harmful or deceptive content, bias propagation). The authors emphasize the need for responsible deployment with appropriate safeguards and oversight.
