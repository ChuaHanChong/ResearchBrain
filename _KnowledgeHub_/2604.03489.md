# Improving Feasibility via Fast Autoencoder-Based Projections

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast Autoencoder-Based (FAB) projections are introduced as a data-driven method for rapidly improving the feasibility of neural network predictions under complex, potentially nonconvex, operational constraints. This approach achieves high feasibility rates, often approaching 100%, with sub-millisecond inference times for constrained optimization problems and substantially reduces constraint violations in safe reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Enforcing complex, often nonconvex, operational constraints in learning and control systems is computationally expensive for exact projection methods.
- Existing constraint enforcement techniques often lack generality, struggling to efficiently handle diverse constraint types like disjoint or arbitrary continuous sets.
- There is a fundamental trade-off between guaranteeing strict constraint satisfaction and achieving high computational efficiency for real-time applications.

## Method

- A Fast Autoencoder-Based (**FAB**) approach is proposed, which learns a fast, ==data-driven==, approximate mapping from infeasible points to feasible ones by leveraging a structured latent representation.
- The core involves a neural network autoencoder with an encoder mapping to a simple convex latent subset (e.g., a hypersphere) and a decoder reconstructing feasible outputs from this subset.
- A two-phase training procedure is used: first, reconstructing feasible points; second, structuring the latent space via adversarial training with a discriminator, hinge loss, and Jacobian regularization to ensure points from the latent subset decode to feasible outputs.

## Results

- FAB achieved high feasibility rates (often near **100%**) for diverse nonconvex and disjoint 2D constraint sets and N-dimensional hyperspherical shells in constrained optimization.
- The method demonstrated sub-millisecond inference times (**0.4**-**0.8 ms**), which is 1-2 orders of magnitude faster than exact projection methods like FSNet and Homeomorphic Projection, for various constrained optimization problems.
- In safe reinforcement learning, PPO-FAB significantly reduced mean constraint violation costs (e.g., from 167.46 to 24.32 in SafetyPointGoal2-v0) and showed lower standard deviations in both cost and reward compared to standard and Lagrangian-based safe RL algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By structuring a simple, easy-to-project-onto latent space, an autoencoder can learn a one-shot, non-iterative approximation of complex, continuous constraint set projections.
> - Adversarial training, combined with reconstruction, hinge loss, and Jacobian regularization, is effective for inducing the autoencoder to map points into a latent subset that consistently decodes to the desired feasible region.
> - This learned feasibility mapping acts as a 'plug-and-play' module, enabling end-to-end training of base neural networks to account for rapid feasibility improvement during both training and inference.
