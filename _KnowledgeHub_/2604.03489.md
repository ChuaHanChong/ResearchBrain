# Improving Feasibility via Fast Autoencoder-Based Projections

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast Autoencoder-Based (FAB) projections are introduced as a data-driven method for rapidly improving the feasibility of neural network predictions under complex, potentially nonconvex, operational constraints. This approach achieves high feasibility rates, often approaching 100%, with sub-millisecond inference times for constrained optimization problems and substantially reduces constraint violations in safe reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Enforcing complex, often nonconvex, operational constraints in learning and control systems is computationally expensive for exact projection methods.
- Existing constraint enforcement techniques often lack generality, struggling to efficiently handle diverse constraint types like disjoint or arbitrary continuous sets.
- There is a fundamental trade-off between guaranteeing strict constraint satisfaction and achieving high computational efficiency for real-time applications.

## Method

- A Fast Autoencoder-Based (**FAB**) approach is proposed, which learns a fast, ==data-driven==, approximate mapping from infeasible points to feasible ones by leveraging a structured latent representation.
- The core involves a neural network autoencoder with an encoder mapping to a simple convex latent subset (e.g., a hypersphere) and a decoder reconstructing feasible outputs from this subset.
- A two-phase training procedure is used: first, reconstructing feasible points; second, structuring the latent space via adversarial training with a discriminator, hinge loss, and Jacobian regularization to ensure points from the latent subset decode to feasible outputs.

## Results

- FAB achieved high feasibility rates (often near **100%**) for diverse nonconvex and disjoint 2D constraint sets and N-dimensional hyperspherical shells in constrained optimization.
- The method demonstrated sub-millisecond inference times (**0.4**-**0.8 ms**), which is 1-2 orders of magnitude faster than exact projection methods like FSNet and Homeomorphic Projection, for various constrained optimization problems.
- In safe reinforcement learning, PPO-FAB significantly reduced mean constraint violation costs (e.g., from 167.46 to 24.32 in SafetyPointGoal2-v0) and showed lower standard deviations in both cost and reward compared to standard and Lagrangian-based safe RL algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By structuring a simple, easy-to-project-onto latent space, an autoencoder can learn a one-shot, non-iterative approximation of complex, continuous constraint set projections.
> - Adversarial training, combined with reconstruction, hinge loss, and Jacobian regularization, is effective for inducing the autoencoder to map points into a latent subset that consistently decodes to the desired feasible region.
> - This learned feasibility mapping acts as a 'plug-and-play' module, enabling end-to-end training of base neural networks to account for rapid feasibility improvement during both training and inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of enforcing complex, particularly nonconvex, operational constraints within learning and control systems. Such constraints are prevalent in real-world applications such as robotics, energy systems, and industrial automation, where compliance is critical for safety and system performance. Existing methods for constraint enforcement in learning-based systems broadly include penalty methods, differentiable projection and correction methods, and post-hoc repair algorithms.

Penalty methods convert constrained problems into unconstrained ones by adding terms that penalize constraint violations. While they incentivize feasibility, they do not guarantee it and can yield highly infeasible solutions. Differentiable projection and correction methods integrate exact solvers directly into neural networks, offering feasibility guarantees but often incurring significant computational expense or being specialized to specific constraint types. Post-hoc repair techniques aim to adjust model outputs to be feasible after initial prediction; however, these can also be computationally costly, specialized, or suffer from performance degradation due to a mismatch between training and testing conditions.

This research positions itself as a data-driven, amortized alternative to these traditional constraint enforcement techniques. It aims to provide a computationally efficient, approximate method for feasibility improvement, contrasting with exact projection methods that offer strict guarantees but at higher computational cost. The approach relates to amortized optimization, where machine learning models act as fast approximators for optimization problems, and safe reinforcement learning, which focuses on maximizing rewards while minimizing constraint violation costs. The use of adversarial training is inspired by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and adversarial robustness in deep learning, applying a similar minimax concept to structure latent representations for feasibility. The work is differentiated from prior homeomorphic projection methods by its one-shot, non-iterative projection mechanism and broader applicability to general continuous constraint sets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and generalizable method for improving the feasibility of predictions made by neural networks in the presence of complex, potentially nonconvex, operational constraints. This is motivated by several key challenges inherent in existing constraint enforcement approaches:

- **Computational Cost:** Many exact constraint enforcement methods, particularly those involving iterative solvers or differentiable projections, are computationally expensive. This high latency can be prohibitive for real-world systems that require rapid decision-making, such as real-time control applications. The paper aims to provide a fast alternative for scenarios where sub-millisecond inference times are critical.
- **Generality of Constraints:** Existing methods often struggle to efficiently handle general classes of constraints, especially those that are nonconvex or disjoint. Specialized solvers or formulations are often required for different constraint types, limiting the broad applicability of any single method. The research seeks a framework capable of approximating projections onto arbitrary continuous constraint sets.
- **Reliability vs. Efficiency Trade-off:** There is a recognized trade-off between the reliability of constraint satisfaction (i.e., strict guarantees of feasibility) and the computational efficiency of the method. While strict guarantees are essential in some safety-critical regimes, many applications can benefit significantly from "near-feasibility" or moving predictions closer to the feasible set, provided the computational overhead is low. The motivation is to explore this middle ground, offering a practical solution for latency-sensitive domains.
- **Integration with Learning Systems:** The goal is to develop a "plug-and-play" mechanism that can be easily appended to standard neural networks. This allows the base neural network to be trained end-to-end to account for the feasibility improvement procedure, potentially leading to better overall performance by balancing optimality and feasibility.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel data-driven approach based on autoencoders, aiming to learn a fast, approximate mapping from infeasible points to feasible ones by leveraging a structured latent representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Fast Autoencoder-Based (FAB) feasibility improvement, addresses parametric optimization problems where neural networks approximate a mapping from problem parameters ($x$) to decision variables ($y^\star(x)$) within a constraint set $C(x)$. The goal is to achieve low objective values, satisfy constraints, and ensure fast computation.

The core of the approach involves a neural network of the form $\hat{y}_\theta := \phi_x \circ N_\theta$, where $N_\theta$ is a standard feedforward network and $\phi_x$ is a differentiable feasibility improvement procedure. Instead of exact, often expensive, projections, FAB learns a fast, data-driven, approximate mapping for $\phi_x$.

The specific construction of $\phi_x$ utilizes an autoencoder:
$\phi_x := R_\psi \circ \phi_S \circ E_\gamma$

Here:
- $E_\gamma: I \rightarrow Z$ is an encoder with parameters $\gamma$, mapping from the input space $I := Y \times X$ (or $Y$ for input-independent constraints) to a latent space $Z \subseteq \mathbb{R}^k$.
- $R_\psi: Z \rightarrow Y$ is a decoder with parameters $\psi$, reconstructing the output.
- $S \subseteq Z$ is a specially structured, simple convex set (e.g., a hypersphere or simplex) that is easy to project onto.
- $\phi_S: Z \rightarrow S$ is a cheap, exact mapping to points in $S$ (e.g., closed-form projection for a ball or softmax for a simplex).

The central challenge is to train the autoencoder parameters $(\gamma, \psi)$ such that points in $S$ decode to feasible points within $C(x)$. This is achieved through a two-phase autoencoder training procedure:

#### 3.1. Two-Phase Autoencoder Training

1. **Phase 1: Constraint Set Reconstruction.**
 - **Objective:** To enable the autoencoder to accurately reconstruct points within the feasible set.
 - **Data:** A dataset $T_{\text{feas}}$ consisting exclusively of feasible points $(y^{(i)}, x^{(i)})$ where $y^{(i)} \in C(x^{(i)})$.
 - **Loss Function:** A standard L2 reconstruction loss is minimized:
 $L_{\text{recon}}(y, x) = \|y - R_\psi(E_\gamma(y, x))\|_2^2$
 - **Process:** The encoder $E_\gamma$ and decoder $R_\psi$ are jointly trained to minimize $L_{\text{recon}}$.

2. **Phase 2: Latent Space Structuring.**
 - **Objective:** To structure the latent space $Z$ such that points sampled from the simple latent subset $S$ consistently decode to feasible points in $C(x)$.
 - **Inspiration:** Draws from Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) training.
 - **Components:**
 - **Discriminator $D_\xi$:** A feedforward neural network that distinguishes between feasible and infeasible points, mapping from $I$ to an estimated probability of feasibility.
 - **Datasets:**
 - $T_{\text{disc}}$: A labeled dataset of both feasible and infeasible points, $((y^{(i)}, x^{(i)}), c^{(i)})$, where $c^{(i)}=1$ for feasible and $c^{(i)}=0$ for infeasible.
 - Samples $z^{(j)} \sim S$ from the latent subset.
 - **Training Losses:**
 - **Discriminator Loss $L_{\text{disc}}$:** Minimizes the negative log-likelihood on $T_{\text{disc}}$ to train $D_\xi$ to accurately classify points:
 $L_{\text{disc}}(y, x, c) = - (c \log D_\xi(y, x) + (1-c) \log (1 - D_\xi(y, x)))$
 - **Autoencoder Composite Loss $L_{\text{struc}}$:** The autoencoder is trained to minimize a combination of four terms:
 - **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{\text{recon}}$):** Same as in Phase 1, ensuring reconstruction fidelity.
 - **Hinge Loss ($L_{\text{hinge}}$):** Enforces structure by mapping feasible points to the interior of $S$ (e.g., a hypersphere) and infeasible points to its exterior. For $S := \{z : \|z\|_2 \le r\}$, it is defined as:
 $L_{\text{hinge}}(y, x, c) = c \cdot \text{ReLU}(\|E_\gamma(y, x)\|_2 - r) + (1-c) \cdot \text{ReLU}(r - \|E_\gamma(y, x)\|_2)$
 - **Latent Loss ($L_{\text{latent}}$):** Encourages points decoded from $z \in S$ to be feasible, using supervision from the discriminator:
 $L_{\text{latent}}(z) = - \log D_\xi(R_\psi(z))$
 - **Jacobian Regularization Term ($L_{\text{geom}}$):** Encourages uniform coverage of the feasible set by the decoder, computed as the variance of the log determinant of the Gram matrix of the decoder's Jacobian, $J_z = \nabla_z R_\psi(z)$.
 $L_{\text{geom}}(\hat{S}) = \text{Variance}_{z \sim \hat{S}} [\log \det(J_z J_z^\top + \epsilon I_k)]$
 - **Process:** $D_\xi$ and the autoencoder $(E_\gamma, R_\psi)$ are trained alternatingly.

#### 3.2. Leveraging the Trained Feasibility Mapping

Once trained, the encoder and decoder weights are fixed. The feasibility mapping $\phi_x$ is then appended to a base neural network $N_\theta$, forming $\hat{y}_\theta = \phi_x \circ N_\theta$. This combined network $\hat{y}_\theta$ can then be trained end-to-end for the specific amortized optimization or reinforcement learning task. The base network $N_\theta$ learns to adapt its outputs to leverage the autoencoder's mapping, aiming for optimal and feasible solutions. This forms a "plug-and-play attachment" for improving model feasibility during both training and inference, designed for speed.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details (from Appendix and Main Text)

- **Latent Space $S$**: A 0.5-radius hypersphere was chosen for experiments.
- **Hardware**: Experiments were conducted on a workstation with one NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.
- **Constrained Optimization**: 5 seeds, 300 problems per seed (18,000 problems total), 500 epochs for Phase 1, 150 epochs for Phase 2. Batch size 32, Adam optimizer. Hidden dimension 64 for autoencoder.
- **Safe RL**: FAB autoencoder trained on 100,000 state-action pairs (balanced safe/unsafe), 50 independent seeds for RL. Phase 1: 500 epochs, LR 0.001. Phase 2: 100 epochs, AE LR 0.0001, Discriminator LR 0.0002.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the FAB approach was evaluated on constrained optimization problems and safe reinforcement learning tasks, comparing it against both classical and machine learning-based baselines, as well as several ablations of the FAB method itself.

#### 4.1. Experiments on Constrained Optimization Problems

- **Problem Types:** Linear, quadratic, and distance minimization objectives with four classes of 2-dimensional nonconvex constraints (Blob with Bite, Concentric Circles, Star-Shaped, Two Moons) and 3-, 5-, and 10-dimensional hyperspherical shell constraints.
- **Baselines:** Projected Gradient Descent, Penalty Method, Augmented Lagrangian, Interior Point, Penalty Neural Network, FSNet, and Homeomorphic Projection.
- **Metrics:** Feasibility rates (%), inference time (ms), and optimality gap.
- **Key Results (Table 1, B.2-B.5):**
 - **Feasibility:** FAB generally achieved high feasibility rates, often approaching 100%, across diverse and challenging nonconvex or disjoint feasible sets. For instance, for the Two Moons constraint (Table 1), FAB achieved 96.8% feasibility, and with 2 or 3 decoder networks, it reached 100%. Similarly, for Blob with Bite and Concentric Circles, FAB achieved 100% feasibility (Tables B.2 and B.3). This performance was competitive with, or superior to, several baselines, especially ML-based ones.
 - **Computational Cost:** A significant finding was FAB's low computational cost, with sub-millisecond inference times (e.g., 0.4-0.8 ms for various problems and constraints), which was 1-2 orders of magnitude faster than exact feasibility methods like FSNet (3-5 ms) and Homeomorphic Projection (hundreds of ms). Classical methods like Projected Gradient Descent were also significantly slower (e.g., tens of ms).
 - **Optimality Gap:** While demonstrating high feasibility and speed, FAB's optimality gaps varied. It did not always achieve the lowest optimality gap, indicating a trade-off where improved feasibility and speed might sometimes come with a slightly less optimal solution value compared to slower, more exact methods.
- **Ablation Studies (Table 1):**
 - **Multiple Decoders:** FAB with 2 or 3 decoder networks (an ablation) achieved 100% feasibility for the Two Moons, Blob with Bite, and Star-Shaped constraint sets, often with slightly increased but still sub-millisecond inference times. This suggested that multiple decoders could improve performance for complex, disjoint constraint sets.
 - **Phase 1 Only:** Training with only Phase 1 (reconstruction loss) resulted in lower feasibility rates (e.g., 82.5% for Two Moons Quadratic, compared to 96.8% for full FAB), indicating the importance of the latent space structuring phase.
 - **No Hinge Loss/No Discriminator:** Removing the hinge loss or the discriminator from Phase 2 training significantly degraded feasibility performance (e.g., 28.4% feasibility for Two Moons Quadratic without hinge loss, and 75.1% without discriminator), confirming the importance of these adversarial training components for structuring the latent space.
- **Additional Ablations (Appendix C):**
 - **Incomplete Coverage:** FAB generally maintained high feasibility (often 90-100%) even when trained with only 10-75% coverage of the feasible set. For instance, the 10% coverage ablation often achieved 100% feasibility, suggesting that very low coverage might simplify the problem topologically. However, the optimality gaps varied, implying that while partial coverage can still lead to feasible solutions, the quality of these solutions (in terms of optimality) can depend on whether the optimal point is within the sampled region.
 - **Decoder Capacity:** Performance varied with different decoder widths and depths (W32-D2 to W128-D6 configurations), indicating that tuning these architectural parameters might be beneficial for specific problems.

#### 4.2. Experiments on Safety Gym (Safe RL)

- **Environments:** SafetyPointGoal2-v0 and SafetyPointPush2-v0 from the Safety Gymnasium benchmark.
- **Baselines:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), Lagrangian PPO (PPO-LAG), and Lagrangian TRPO (TRPO-LAG).
- **Method:** PPO with FAB projections (PPO-FAB).
- **Metrics:** Episode rewards (mean, std), episode costs (mean, std), and mean inference time (s).
- **Key Results (Tables 2 and 3):**
 - **Constraint Violation Costs:** PPO-FAB achieved the lowest mean constraint violation costs across both environments. For SafetyPointGoal2-v0, PPO-FAB had a mean cost of 24.32, significantly lower than PPO (167.46), TRPO (164.14), PPO-LAG (54.10), and TRPO-LAG (89.04). Similar trends were observed for SafetyPointPush2-v0.
 - **Reliability:** PPO-FAB also exhibited the lowest standard deviations in both cost and reward, indicating higher reliability and less variability in performance compared to other algorithms.
 - **Reward Performance:** This emphasis on safety often corresponded to a more conservative policy, resulting in lower mean episode rewards for PPO-FAB compared to unconstrained methods like PPO and TRPO. For example, in SafetyPointGoal2-v0, PPO-FAB had a mean reward of -1.12, while PPO achieved 13.26. It sometimes had lower rewards than Lagrangian variants as well.
 - **Inference Time:** The inference times for PPO-FAB (2.75 s for Goal, 3.71 s for Push) were comparable to or slightly higher than other RL methods, indicating that the integration of FAB did not introduce a prohibitive overhead in these environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Fast Autoencoder-Based (FAB) projections as a data-driven method for efficiently improving feasibility in learning and control systems subject to complex, nonconvex constraints. This method demonstrates the potential to address a gap in existing approaches, which often face trade-offs between computational cost, generality, and reliability.

The primary significance lies in its capacity to provide rapid, high-fidelity approximate projections. For constrained optimization problems, FAB consistently achieves high feasibility rates, often near 100%, across diverse and challenging constraint geometries, including disjoint sets. Crucially, it does so with inference times typically in the sub-millisecond range, which is significantly faster than many traditional solvers and other machine learning-based methods offering formal guarantees. This speed is a key differentiator, making FAB a viable option for latency-sensitive applications where real-time decision-making is essential.

In the context of safe reinforcement learning, FAB-augmented policies (PPO-FAB) demonstrate a marked reduction in constraint violation costs and improved reliability (lower standard deviations) compared to both standard and Lagrangian-based safe RL algorithms. This suggests a practical avenue for developing safer agents in environments with operational constraints, even if it leads to more conservative reward-seeking behavior in some cases.

The "plug-and-play" nature of the FAB autoencoder allows for its integration into various neural network architectures, offering a modular approach to constraint enforcement. This could lower the barrier for developers to incorporate feasibility considerations into new and existing learning systems.

However, the work acknowledges several limitations that point to future research directions. FAB does not provide hard feasibility guarantees, a characteristic that differentiates it from exact solvers and makes it less suitable for safety-critical domains demanding absolute constraint satisfaction. Its performance relies on the availability of a representative dataset of feasible and infeasible points, and the adversarial training process can be sensitive to hyperparameter tuning. The current evaluations are relatively small-scale, suggesting a need for larger-scale validation. Future work could focus on improving sample efficiency, distributional performance, interpretability of the learned mapping, and exploring adaptive co-training strategies or formal verification techniques to better understand and assess its behavior.

Overall, FAB represents a step towards practical, efficient, and broadly applicable constraint enforcement in machine learning, offering a middle ground between computationally expensive exact methods and less reliable penalty approaches. Its impact could be significant in domains where rapid approximate feasibility is sufficient and computational speed is a premium.
