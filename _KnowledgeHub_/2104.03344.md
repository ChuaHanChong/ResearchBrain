# OVANet: One-vs-All Network for Universal Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> OVANet proposes a framework for Universal Domain Adaptation that robustly classifies target samples into known categories or identifies them as unknown, eliminating the need for manual threshold setting or prior knowledge about unknown class ratios. The approach consistently demonstrates superior performance across various domain shift scenarios with a simpler design and fewer hyper-parameters.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks struggle to generalize to novel domains with differing data distributions (domain shift), particularly when target domains contain categories not present in source data.
- Universal Domain Adaptation (UNDA) requires models to classify target samples into shared known classes while rejecting unknown, target-private categories, without prior knowledge of the category shift type.
- Existing UNDA methods rely on manually set confidence thresholds, requiring labeled target data for validation or pre-defined ratios of unknown samples, which are impractical for real-world unsupervised settings.

## Method

- **OVANet** trains a set of ==one-vs-all (OVA) binary classifiers== for each source class, designed to distinguish a specific class from all others.
- It employs ==Hard Negative Classifier Sampling (HNCS)== during source training to learn fine-grained boundaries by penalizing the nearest negative classes, which helps define robust thresholds for unknowns.
- ==Open-set Entropy Minimization (OEM)== is applied to target data, adapting the OVA classifiers to increase confidence for either a known class or an inherent 'outlier' dimension, thereby learning to reject unknown samples without explicit labels.

## Results

- OVANet achieved higher H-scores on multiple Universal Domain Adaptation benchmarks including Office, OfficeHome, VisDA, and DomainNet, with improvements exceeding **10 points** on challenging datasets like VisDA over some baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robustness to varying degrees of 'openness' (number of unknown classes) and maintained stable performance across different architectures, while utilizing only one fixed hyper-parameter (λ=**0.1**) across all experiments.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Hard Negative Classifier Sampling and Open-set Entropy Minimization are critical for OVANet's superior performance, enhancing the separation between known and unknown samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging the inherent inter-class distances within the source domain, specifically through hard negative sampling, provides a principled basis for defining robust thresholds to detect out-of-distribution (unknown) samples.
> - Entropy minimization, when applied to open-set classifiers with an 'outlier' dimension, can effectively adapt these classifiers to the target domain by increasing confidence in either known or unknown classifications.
> - The combination of tailored one-vs-all classifiers and targeted entropy minimization enables a practical UNDA solution that avoids manual threshold tuning and unrealistic assumptions about target domain composition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid progress in deep neural networks has led to highly discriminative models for tasks like image recognition, provided vast amounts of labeled training data. However, these models often suffer from poor generalization when deployed in novel domains where data distributions differ from the training data—a phenomenon known as **domain shift**. Collecting new, annotated data for every novel domain is prohibitively expensive, leading to the development of **domain adaptation (DA)** techniques.

Traditionally, unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) assumes a "closed-set" scenario, meaning the source (labeled) and target (unlabeled) domains share the exact same set of categories. This assumption, however, frequently breaks down in practical applications. The research landscape has therefore expanded to address various forms of "category shift":
- **Open-set Domain Adaptation (ODA):** Where the target domain contains categories not present in the source domain ("unknown" classes).
- **Partial Domain Adaptation (PDA):** Where the source domain contains categories not present in the target domain ("source private" classes).
- **Open-Partial Domain Adaptation (OPDA):** A more general setting encompassing both ODA and PDA.

The most challenging and practical scenario is **Universal Domain Adaptation (UNDA)**, which is the focus of this paper. UNDA aims to provide a robust solution where the exact nature of the category shift (closed-set, ODA, PDA, or OPDA) is unknown *a priori*. In UNDA, the model must be capable of classifying target samples into known source categories while simultaneously identifying and rejecting "unknown" samples that belong to categories unseen during source training.

The core technical difficulty in UNDA and ODA lies in distinguishing these "unknown" samples from "known" ones without any explicit supervision for the unknown classes. Existing methods often grapple with this by:
- **Manually setting a confidence threshold:** This typically involves either validating the threshold using *labeled target data* (which violates the unsupervised assumption of UNDA) or relying on *prior knowledge* about the ratio of unknown samples in the target domain (which is often unavailable or inaccurate in practice).
- **Synthetically generating unknown instances:** This attempts to teach the model what "unknown" might look like, but requires complex generation processes and often still necessitates validation to tune the generation parameters, again leaning on unrealistic assumptions.

OVANet directly addresses these limitations. It positions itself as a practical and robust alternative by proposing a mechanism to *learn* the threshold for "unknown" sample rejection directly from the labeled source data and then adapt it to the target domain, entirely avoiding the need for manual tuning, validation on target labels, or prior knowledge of unknown ratios. This approach represents a significant step towards truly "universal" and deployable domain adaptation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a practical and robust method for Universal Domain Adaptation (UNDA) that overcomes the critical shortcomings of existing approaches, specifically the reliance on impractical manual threshold settings for identifying unknown categories.

The core motivations driving OVANet are:
1. **Addressing the "Unknown" Challenge in UNDA:** Deep networks struggle with novel domains, and a key challenge in UNDA is the presence of target-private or "unknown" categories. The goal is to build a model that can reliably categorize target samples into either one of the shared "known" labels or an "unknown" label.
2. **Eliminating Manual Thresholds:** Existing UNDA and open-set DA methods typically require manually setting a confidence threshold to reject unknown samples. This often involves:
 - Validation using labeled target samples, which is unrealistic in a truly unsupervised setting.
 - Pre-defining a ratio of unknown samples in the target, a piece of prior knowledge rarely available.
 - Synthesizing unknown instances, a complex process that still requires validation to ensure the synthetic data adequately represents real unknowns.
 The authors are motivated to create a solution that does not necessitate any of these impractical strategies, making UNDA truly deployable.
3. **Leveraging Inter-Class Distances:** The central hypothesis is that the minimum inter-class distance within the source domain can provide a valuable, learned threshold for discerning between known and unknown samples in the target. If a sample is too far from all known classes (beyond their minimum separation), it is likely unknown.
4. **Simplicity and Robustness:** The researchers aim for a solution that is significantly simpler in its design, requiring fewer hyper-parameters and demonstrating greater robustness to varying target domain characteristics (e.g., different proportions of unknown classes) compared to the more complex and often sensitive baseline methods.

In essence, OVANet seeks to provide a principled, data-driven mechanism for defining and adapting the boundary between known and unknown classes, thereby enabling more practical and reliable universal domain adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OVANet introduces a novel framework that trains specialized one-vs-all classifiers using source data and adapts them to the target domain. The methodology hinges on learning robust boundaries between known categories, which inherently provides a basis for detecting out-of-distribution (unknown) samples.

The approach involves three main components:
1. **One-vs-All (OVA) Open-set Classifiers and Hard Negative Classifier Sampling (HNCS):**
 - **Architecture:** OVANet employs a feature extractor ($G_\theta$) followed by two types of classifiers: a standard closed-set classifier ($C$) and a set of open-set classifiers ($O_j$). There are $|L_s|$ individual open-set binary classifiers, $O_j$, one for each class $j$ in the source label space $L_s$. Each $O_j$ is designed to output a 2-dimensional vector indicating the probability of a sample being an in-lier (belonging to class $j$) or an outlier (not belonging to class $j$).
 - **Motivation for HNCS:** Traditional OVA training, where all other classes are treated as negatives, can be inefficient, especially with many classes. "Easy" negatives (classes very dissimilar to the positive class) do not help in learning fine-grained decision boundaries necessary for identifying "unknowns" that might be subtly different.
 - **HNCS Mechanism:** For each labeled source sample $(x_s, y_s)$, OVANet trains two open-set classifiers:
 - The classifier $O_{y_s}$ is trained to identify $x_s$ as an in-lier for class $y_s$.
 - Simultaneously, the classifier for the *nearest negative class* (the class $j \neq y_s$ that is most similar to $y_s$) is trained to identify $x_s$ as an outlier for class $j$. This "hard negative sampling" forces the model to learn tight boundaries between closely related known classes, which then naturally extends to better differentiate unknown samples.
 - **Loss Function:** The open-set classification loss ($L_{ova}$) for a source sample $(x_s, y_s)$ is defined as the negative logarithm of the predicted probability for the positive class $y_s$, combined with the negative logarithm of the probability of $x_s$ being *not* in the hardest negative class.

2. **Open-set Entropy Minimization (OEM):**
 - **Problem:** Classifiers trained solely on source data may misclassify target samples due to domain shift. Standard entropy minimization for closed-set classifiers pushes unlabeled target samples towards *known* classes, which is undesirable for "unknown" samples.
 - **OEM Mechanism:** To adapt the open-set classifiers to the target domain, OVANet applies entropy minimization to *all* open-set classifiers for each unlabeled target sample ($x_t$). This means minimizing the entropy of the 2-dimensional output (in-lier/outlier) for each $O_j$.
 - **Advantage:** Because the OVA classifiers inherently possess an "outlier" (or "unknown") dimension, OEM allows the model to increase the confidence of target samples belonging to either a specific known class *or* being classified as unknown. This prevents known target samples from being misclassified and unknown target samples from being forced into known categories.
 - **Loss Function:** $L_{ent}(x_t)$ calculates the sum of binary cross-entropy losses for all open-set classifiers, pushing their outputs towards high confidence (either 0 or 1).

3. **Overall Learning and Inference:**
 - **Combined Loss:** The total training loss ($L_{all}$) combines the standard cross-entropy loss for the closed-set classifier ($L_{cls}$) on source data, the Hard Negative Classifier Sampling loss ($L_{ova}$) for open-set classifiers on source data, and the Open-set Entropy Minimization loss ($L_{ent}$) for open-set classifiers on target data. A single hyper-parameter, $\lambda$, balances the contribution of the target entropy minimization.
 - **Minimal Hyper-parameters:** A key advantage is that OVANet boasts only one unique hyper-parameter ($\lambda$), which simplifies training and makes it robust across different settings.
 - **Inference Procedure:** During testing, for an input sample, the closed-set classifier ($C$) first identifies the most probable known class. Subsequently, the *corresponding* open-set classifier ($O_j$) for that predicted known class $j$ is queried. The sample is classified as "known" if the open-set classifier's confidence score for being an in-lier is above a learned threshold (derived from the OVA training), and "unknown" otherwise.

This end-to-end learning approach with its novel components allows OVANet to dynamically learn robust thresholds for unknown detection, avoiding the manual or heuristic thresholding prevalent in prior UNDA literature.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OVANet underwent extensive evaluation across multiple benchmark datasets and various domain adaptation settings, consistently demonstrating superior performance and practical advantages over existing methods.

**1. Superior Performance Across Diverse Settings:**
- **Universal/Open-Partial DA (Office, OfficeHome, VisDA, DomainNet):** OVANet consistently achieved higher H-scores (harmonic mean of known and unknown accuracy) compared to strong baselines like UAN, CMU, DANCE, ROS, USFDA, and DCC. For instance, on the Office dataset, OVANet outperformed baselines by a significant margin. On more challenging datasets like VisDA and OfficeHome, OVANet showed more than 10-point improvements in H-score over some methods.
- **Robustness to Openness:** Experiments varying the number of unknown classes (e.g., Office, OfficeHome) illustrated OVANet's stable performance, whereas some baselines (e.g., ROS, DANCE) showed significant degradation when the ratio of unknown samples deviated from their assumed optimal.

**2. Simplicity and Hyper-parameter Robustness:**
- OVANet's design is notably simpler than baselines, featuring only one unique hyper-parameter ($\lambda$). The authors fixed $\lambda = 0.1$ across all experiments, and sensitivity analyses confirmed that OVANet's performance remains stable across a wide range of $\lambda$ values. This is a crucial practical advantage, as many existing methods require extensive, dataset-specific hyper-parameter tuning or manual threshold validation.

**3. Effectiveness of Core Components (Ablation Studies):**
- **Hard Negative Classifier Sampling (HNCS):** Ablation studies clearly demonstrated the importance of HNCS. Models trained without HNCS showed noticeable performance drops, especially on datasets with a large number of known classes (e.g., DomainNet). This confirms that forcing classifiers to learn boundaries against "hard negatives" is essential for defining effective thresholds against unknown samples.
- **Open-set Entropy Minimization (OEM):** OEM proved vital for adapting the learned open-set classifiers to the target domain. Comparing "OVANet w/o OEM" to the full OVANet, OEM consistently improved both the accuracy on known classes (Acc close) and the accuracy of rejecting unknown samples (UNK), as well as the AUROC score, which measures the separation quality between known and unknown.

**4. Clear Separation of Known and Unknown Samples:**
- Visualizations of anomaly score histograms showed that OVANet, even without OEM, effectively separates known and unknown target samples. The application of OEM further enhances this separation, leading to a more distinct boundary. This contrasts sharply with methods like DANCE, which failed to adequately separate these distributions.

**5. Impact of Number of Known Classes:**
- Interestingly, the accuracy of rejecting unknown samples generally improved or remained stable as the number of known classes increased. This observation aligns with the design of OVA classifiers and HNCS, as more known classes provide a richer set of "hard negative" examples, leading to more robust boundaries for unknown detection.

**6. Generalizability and Broader Applicability:**
- **Different Architectures:** OVANet's core idea proved effective when using different backbone networks (e.g., ResNet50 and VGGNet), indicating its architectural agnosticism.
- **Semi-supervised Learning:** OVANet's principles extended successfully to an open-set semi-supervised learning setting (using the NAbird dataset), outperforming baselines in identifying unknown categories without domain shift. This highlights the broad utility of learning an "unknown" concept.
- **Necessity of Both Classifiers:** The ablation showed that solely relying on open-set classifiers without the closed-set classifier significantly degrades performance because OVA classifiers are not intrinsically designed to distinguish *between* known classes effectively.

In summary, OVANet provides a strong, robust, and significantly simpler solution to Universal Domain Adaptation, outperforming complex baselines primarily due to its innovative method of learning unknown rejection thresholds directly from data rather than relying on unrealistic manual settings or prior knowledge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OVANet framework introduces a significant advancement in the field of Universal Domain Adaptation (UNDA) and open-set recognition, with substantial practical implications and potential for future research.

**1. Enhanced Practicality and Deployability:**
The most profound impact of OVANet lies in its ability to eliminate the requirement for manually setting a threshold to identify "unknown" samples. This fundamental shift from heuristic-based thresholding to a learned, data-driven approach removes a critical bottleneck that has hindered the practical deployment of UNDA systems. In real-world scenarios, where labeled target data is unavailable and the exact composition of unknown classes is unpredictable, OVANet offers a truly "universal" and robust solution. This makes it highly valuable for applications such as autonomous systems, medical diagnosis, and industrial inspection, where encountering unseen categories is common and misclassification can have severe consequences.

**2. Simplicity and Efficiency in Development:**
OVANet's design is remarkably simple, requiring only one hyper-parameter ($\lambda$) that is also robust to tuning. This significantly reduces the complexity of model development, experimentation, and deployment compared to previous methods that often involve multiple hyper-parameters, multi-stage training, or cumbersome validation processes. This ease of use can accelerate research and development in domain adaptation and open-set recognition.

**3. Robustness Across Diverse Scenarios:**
The extensive experimental evaluation demonstrates OVANet's consistent and stable performance across various degrees of category shift (openness), different domain discrepancies, and a wide array of datasets. This robustness ensures that the model can handle uncertainties in the target domain, making it a reliable choice for applications where domain shifts are common but unpredictable.

**4. Broader Applicability Beyond Domain Adaptation:**
The core methodology of learning a robust boundary for "unknown" samples through Hard Negative Classifier Sampling (HNCS) and adapting it via Open-set Entropy Minimization (OEM) is not limited to domain adaptation. The paper successfully demonstrates its effectiveness in a semi-supervised learning setting with open-set characteristics, suggesting its potential impact on:
- **Open-set Recognition:** Improving the ability of models to classify known instances while rejecting novel ones during testing.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection:** Providing a more robust mechanism for flagging data points that differ significantly from the training distribution.
These applications are crucial for building more trustworthy and safe AI systems that can identify when they are operating outside their trained competence.

**5. Foundation for Future Research:**
OVANet provides a powerful and simple baseline that can inspire further research into dynamic threshold learning, improved open-set classifier designs, and more sophisticated methods for leveraging inter-class distances. Its success underscores the value of focusing on the inherent structure of known classes to infer boundaries for unknowns.

In conclusion, OVANet represents a significant step forward in making Universal Domain Adaptation a truly practical and deployable technology. By prioritizing simplicity, robustness, and a principled approach to learning "unknown" boundaries, it addresses a critical challenge in machine learning and paves the way for more intelligent, adaptive, and trustworthy AI systems.
