# Make Tracking Easy: Neural Motion Retargeting for Humanoid Whole-body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanjing University, Huawei Technologies, and Horizon Robotics developed a Neural Motion Retargeting (NMR) framework that leverages a Transformer-based network and a novel physics-based data generation pipeline to translate human motion to humanoid robots, achieving zero joint jumps and 54% fewer self-collisions compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Traditional optimization-based motion retargeting suffers from non-convexity, leading to local optima and physically implausible robot motions like abrupt jerks and self-interpenetration.
- Existing methods propagate noise and errors from imperfect human motion data, resulting in unstable robot trajectories that are difficult for downstream control policies to handle.
- A lack of large-scale, high-quality, physically consistent human-robot motion data hinders the training of robust neural retargeting models.

## Method

- A multi-stage ==Clustered-Expert Physics Refinement (CEPR)== (CEPR) pipeline automatically generates a large dataset of physically consistent human-robot motion pairs using motion clustering and parallel reinforcement learning.
- A ==Transformer-based neural network== learns a direct mapping from human motion distributions to the robot's feasible motion manifold, employing full self-attention for global temporal context reasoning.
- A two-stage training scheme first pre-trains the network on broad kinematic data and then fine-tunes it on the physics-refined CEPR data, ensuring both wide motion coverage and dynamic feasibility.

## Results

- The NMR framework achieved zero joint jumps and reduced self-collisions by **54**% (**0.87**% of frames) compared to optimization-based baselines, demonstrating superior physical plausibility and motion smoothness.
- Downstream whole-body control policies trained with NMR-generated references showed higher success rates and lower mean per-joint pose errors, indicating more efficient and stable learning for complex tasks.
- NMR robustly filtered out abnormal jitters and twitching from noisy human motion inputs, producing smooth and stable robot trajectories, unlike prior methods which mechanically propagated these errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconceptualizing motion retargeting as learning a dynamic mapping between motion distributions effectively mitigates the inherent non-convexity and local optima issues of traditional optimization-based methods.
> - Leveraging a Transformer's global temporal context and physics-consistent training data enables the neural network to implicitly filter upstream noise and correct physically implausible elements from human motion inputs.
> - A hierarchical data generation pipeline that combines kinematic filtering with reinforcement learning for physics refinement can robustly create high-fidelity, dynamically feasible training data for neural retargeting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are progressing towards integration into complex environments, which necessitates the acquisition of diverse motor skills. A dominant paradigm in this field involves leveraging large-scale human motion data (e.g., video, motion-capture) to guide the training of robot motor control policies, often through imitation learning or reinforcement learning (RL). Within this pipeline, motion retargeting is a critical component that bridges the kinematic and dynamic embodiment gap between human demonstrations and robot execution.

Traditional motion retargeting methods, including inverse kinematics (IK)-based approaches and differential optimization schemes (e.g., GMR), primarily operate at a geometric level, seeking optimal joint configurations for individual frames. However, these methods face two primary limitations:
1. **Mathematical non-convexity**: Motion retargeting is an inherently non-convex optimization problem, making it prone to local optima. This leads to sensitivity to initialization, requiring extensive parameter tuning, and can produce physically infeasible artifacts such as abrupt joint movements, self-interpenetration, and foot sliding. Such artifacts can burden downstream controllers with the need to learn compensatory behaviors, potentially reducing stability.
2. **Noise propagation**: Human motion data, often derived from sources like SMPL-based estimations, can contain noise such as ground penetration or temporal jitter. Geometric optimization methods typically lack inherent awareness of physical plausibility and therefore tend to propagate these errors directly into the retargeted robot motion.

To address the limitations of optimization-based methods, data-driven approaches have emerged, particularly in character animation. These methods often learn shared latent spaces between different skeletal structures, enabling cross-skeleton retargeting without explicit paired data. In humanoid robotics, similar latent space alignment techniques have been explored for bridging the embodiment gap, sometimes using self-supervised methods to automate correspondence discovery. However, existing data-driven methods often inherit local optima artifacts from their optimization-based supervision and may still lack sufficient physical reasoning to filter out noise from source motion data.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a neural, data-driven framework that reframes motion retargeting from a static, frame-wise optimization problem to a dynamic mapping between motion distributions. It aims to directly learn the transformation from human motion to the robot's feasible motion manifold while implicitly addressing the issues of non-convexity, local optima, and noise propagation by grounding the learning process in physically consistent data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust motion retargeting framework that can effectively transfer diverse human motor skills to humanoid robots while overcoming the inherent limitations of conventional approaches. The motivation stems from two critical bottlenecks identified in existing retargeting pipelines:

1. **Mitigating Non-convexity and Local Optima**: Traditional optimization-based retargeting methods are formulated as per-frame optimization problems that are demonstrably non-convex. This mathematical property implies that gradient-based solvers are susceptible to getting trapped in local minima. Such failures manifest as physically implausible robot motions, including sudden joint jerks, self-collisions, and joint limit violations, requiring manual intervention or complex post-processing. The authors' Hessian analysis provides a formal demonstration of this non-convexity, highlighting that negative curvature arises from both the recursive trigonometric composition in forward kinematics and the nonlinearity of the logarithmic map on SO(3)/SE(3). This theoretical limitation motivates a fundamental shift from direct optimization to a learning-based paradigm that can circumvent these geometric traps.

2. **Suppressing Noise Propagation and Ensuring Physical Plausibility**: Source human motion data, particularly when estimated from videos (e.g., SMPL-based), often contains noise such as temporal jitter or ground penetration. Conventional geometric retargeting methods typically propagate these errors directly to the robot's joint space because they lack mechanisms for physical reasoning. This "garbage in, garbage out" dilemma necessitates that downstream control policies either learn to compensate for unphysical references or operate with reduced stability. The research aims to develop a system that not only maps motion but also implicitly filters out these inconsistencies, ensuring the generated robot motions are physically plausible and dynamically feasible for real-world execution.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose to reformulate the retargeting problem as a supervised learning task. This involves learning a direct, dynamic mapping from the human motion space to the robot's feasible motion manifold. However, this vision presents a "chicken-and-egg" dilemma: training a robust neural retargeter requires large-scale, high-quality robot motion data, which is difficult to obtain without an effective retargeting tool. This challenge motivates the development of a data generation pipeline that can reliably produce such physically plausible, paired human-robot motion data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a Neural Motion Retargeting (NMR) framework that learns a direct mapping from human SMPL motion sequences to feasible humanoid robot motions. This framework is built upon a hierarchical data pipeline and a Transformer-based neural network.

**A. Preliminary: Optimization-based Human to Humanoid Retargeting and Non-convexity Analysis**
The paper first provides an analysis of traditional optimization-based retargeting methods, which typically formulate the problem as a per-frame optimization to match human body part orientations and end-effector positions. The authors perform a Hessian analysis on a weighted surrogate cost function derived from the Lie algebra of the relative pose error. This analysis formally demonstrates that the Hessian of this objective can possess strictly negative directional curvature. This non-convexity is attributed to two distinct geometric sources:
1. **Curvature of forward kinematics**: Due to the recursive trigonometric composition in multi-joint chains, the mapping from joint angles to end-effector positions is inherently nonlinear.
2. **Nonlinearity of the logarithmic map on SO(3)/SE(3)**: The differential of the logarithm map itself varies nonlinearly with rotation angle, especially as rotation errors approach 180 degrees.
This non-convexity explains why gradient-based retargeting methods are sensitive to initialization and prone to converging to suboptimal solutions, motivating the shift towards a data-driven approach.

**B. Clustered-Expert Physics Refinement (CEPR)**
To obtain physically plausible motion data for training the neural network, a three-stage hierarchical data pipeline called Clustered-Expert Physics Refinement (CEPR) is introduced. This pipeline systematically transforms raw human motion into robot-feasible trajectories.

- **Step 1: Physics-Aware Human Motion Curation**: The raw SMPL dataset is initially filtered to remove motions semantically incompatible with robotic applications or those containing gross physical inconsistencies. This filtering identifies and removes motions with excessive jerk, a Center of Mass (CoM) outside its support base, or insufficient foot-ground contact (e.g., floating or penetration). This step ensures that the retained sequences are, in principle, executable by a robot.

- **Step 2: Kinematic Retargeting and Quality Filtering**: The curated human motion sequences are then processed by an optimization-based kinematic retargeting method (GMR) to obtain initial corresponding robot motions. While GMR aims to reduce artifacts, the non-convex nature of the underlying IK problem means the output quality is not universally guaranteed. To address this, a hard-threshold filtering pipeline is applied:
 - **Joint Continuity and Stability**: Motion segments with inter-frame joint velocities exceeding a hardware-specific saturation limit (e.g., 0.5 rad change per step) are discarded to prune abrupt, non-smooth jumps indicative of IK singularities.
 - **Geometric Self-Intersection Detection**: The MuJoCo collision detection engine is used to identify interpenetrations between the robot's geometric links. Sequences with a high fraction of self-intersecting frames (e.g., > 0.05) are rejected.
 - **Floating Foot Rectification**: Motions where the average elevation of the lowest foot point exceeds a threshold (e.g., > 0.10 m) are classified as "floating" and removed.

- **Step 3: Physics-based Humanoid Motion Refinement**: This is the core stage, aiming to transform kinematic references into dynamically consistent robot motion trajectories using a physics simulator and reinforcement learning (RL) policies.
 - **Motion Clustering**: To manage the diversity of human motions, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is used to extract motion features, and these features are then clustered using K-Means with cosine similarity. This partitions the motion library into behaviorally related clusters, allowing expert policies to specialize over homogeneous motion distributions and avoid distributional conflicts. TMR is leveraged for motion encoding, ensuring semantic alignment in the latent space.
 - **Expert Policy Training**: For each motion cluster, a separate RL tracking policy is trained in a massively parallelized physics simulation environment. The objective is to make the robot precisely replicate the target motion from the clustered set. The policy receives a comprehensive observation space, including reference motion states (joint positions, velocities, body positions, orientations, linear/angular velocities) and the robot's proprioceptive state, along with action history. A symmetric actor-critic framework with the PPO algorithm is used. The reward function primarily consists of tracking rewards, with minimal regulation rewards, and an adaptive standard deviation schedule is employed for tracking rewards, allowing the policy to learn coarse patterns before refining precision.
 - **Generating Physically Faithful Data Pairs**: Once the expert policies converge, they are rolled out in the simulator using their corresponding reference sequences. The resulting robot state trajectories are recorded and paired with the input SMPL sequences, creating approximately 30,000 "ground-truth" data pairs that are implicitly validated by physics simulation.

**C. Motion Retargeting Network**
The core neural network architecture directly maps human motion to humanoid sequences.
- **Motion Representation**: Distinct representations are used for human and humanoid motion to account for differences in joint structure and actuation. Human motion is represented by root linear velocities (XZ-plane), 6D root orientation, and local joint positions and velocities. Humanoid motion additionally includes joint degrees of freedom (DoFs).
- **Network Architecture**: As illustrated in Figure 2, the network comprises a 1D ResNet-based encoder to extract latent features from the human motion sequence. These features are then fed into a Transformer-based network, which uses full self-attention (rather than causal attention) to reason over the global temporal context of the entire input sequence. The output of the Transformer is decoded through upsampling and 1D-Conv layers to produce the final humanoid motion sequence. The network is optimized using an L1 loss between the predicted and target humanoid motion representations.

**D. Two-Stage Training Scheme**
A two-stage training strategy is implemented, corresponding to the two-tier data hierarchy:
- **Step 1: Kinematic Alignment with Large-scale Data (Pre-training)**: The network is initially pre-trained on the large-scale kinematic retargeting dataset generated in CEPR Step 2. This stage minimizes the regression loss between predicted robot joint angles and kinematic reference targets. Despite potential residual physical artifacts, this large and diverse dataset provides the network with a foundational embodiment mapping across a broad range of motion categories.
- **Step 2: Physical Grounding with CEPR Data (Fine-tuning)**: The pre-trained network is then fine-tuned using the approximately 30,000 physically consistent motion pairs generated in CEPR Step 3. Although smaller, this dataset carries strong physical feasibility signals verified through RL policy rollouts. Fine-tuning shifts the network's output distribution towards the robot's dynamically feasible motion manifold, enabling it to implicitly suppress physically infeasible components or noise from upstream SMPL-X inputs. This two-stage approach ensures both broad motion coverage and improved physical feasibility, addressing the limitations of training with either dataset alone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed Neural Motion Retargeting (NMR) framework was evaluated through diverse experiments on the Unitree G1 robot, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art baselines.

**A. Retargeting Quality and Physical Plausibility**
The retargeted motions were assessed using three physical plausibility metrics:
- **Joint Jump**: Number of frames where the maximum single-step joint angle change exceeds 0.5 rad.
- **Self-Collision**: Number of frames with non-hand body-segment contacts, detected via MuJoCo.
- **Joint Limit**: Number of frames where any joint is within 0.05 rad of its hardware boundary.

As shown in Table III, NMR achieved superior results across all metrics compared to GMR and PHUMA:
- **Joint Jump**: NMR exhibited zero joint jumps (0.00%), a significant improvement over GMR (56 jumps, 0.11%) and PHUMA (12 jumps, 0.02%). The ablation study (NMR w/o RL) showed 2 joint jumps (0.00%), indicating the importance of the physics refinement step.
- **Self-Collision**: NMR recorded 431 self-collision frames (0.87%), which is 54% fewer than GMR (947 frames, 1.91%) and substantially better than PHUMA (2456 frames, 4.95%).
- **Joint Limit Violations**: NMR reduced joint limit violations to 8339 frames (16.80%), approximately half of PHUMA (10580 frames, 21.32%) and significantly lower than GMR (21443 frames, 43.21%).

A visual comparison (Figure 5) further illustrated these improvements. For an "arm raise-lower" motion, GMR exhibited a sudden joint jump of approximately 1.5 rad within 0.2s due to converging to a local optimum near the shoulder joint limit. In contrast, both NMR and PHUMA generated smooth and continuous joint trajectories, with NMR achieving the most optimal motion smoothness while maintaining motion similarity. The ablation (NMR w/o RL) confirmed that the physics refinement (CEPR) is essential for producing hardware-feasible output.

**B. Impact on Downstream Whole-body Control Policies**
The quality of the retargeted motions directly influenced the efficiency and performance of downstream reinforcement learning (RL) tracking policies.
- **Training Efficiency**: Policies trained using NMR-generated references reached longer episode lengths and achieved higher rewards (Figure 4). This indicates that cleaner and more physically plausible reference motions lead to more efficient policy learning and faster convergence.
- **Tracking Accuracy**: End-to-end tracking performance was evaluated across short, medium, and long sequences using success rate, mean per-joint pose error (MPJPE), and world-frame MPJPE (W-MPJPE). As presented in Table IV:
 - NMR achieved the highest success rate and lowest MPJPE/W-MPJPE across all sequence lengths.
 - For short sequences, NMR had a success rate of 31/33, MPJPE of 0.040, and W-MPJPE of 0.237 m. In comparison, PHUMA showed a high W-MPJPE of 0.660 m on short sequences, suggesting its sequence-level optimization approach could distort transient or short motions.
 - Similar trends were observed for medium and long sequences, with NMR consistently outperforming baselines.

**C. Correction of Upstream Errors**
NMR demonstrated an ability to implicitly filter out abnormal jitters caused by upstream pose estimation errors in the original SMPL sequences (Figure 6). When the SMPL motion contained twitches or abnormal poses, GMR propagated these errors, leading to unstable robot motion. In contrast, NMR produced smoother, physically feasible robot motion trajectories. This robustness is attributed to two design choices:
1. **Physical-consistency filtering**: The training data for NMR is curated to be physically consistent, making noisy input samples effectively "out-of-distribution." Neural networks trained on clean data tend to generalize by producing continuous and plausible outputs for such inputs, rather than mechanically reproducing the noise.
2. **Bidirectional self-attention**: The Transformer-based network leverages global temporal context. This allows the network to implicitly interpolate and correct anomalous frames by reasoning about the normal motion information before and after the noisy segment, suppressing local noise propagation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Neural Motion Retargeting (NMR) framework addresses a critical challenge in humanoid robotics: bridging the kinematic and dynamic embodiment gap between human motion data and robot execution. By reformulating the retargeting problem as a learned distribution mapping rather than a frame-wise geometric optimization, the research offers a pathway to overcome fundamental limitations of traditional methods.

The **significance** of this work is multi-faceted:
1. **Enhanced Motion Quality and Physical Plausibility**: The primary contribution is the ability of NMR to generate robot motions that are significantly more physically plausible. The elimination of joint jumps, substantial reduction in self-collisions, and fewer joint-limit violations directly translate to motions that are safer, more stable, and less demanding on robot hardware. This improved quality addresses a long-standing issue in robot control where unphysical reference motions hinder robust policy learning.
2. **Robustness to Input Noise**: NMR's capacity to implicitly filter out noise and abnormalities from upstream human pose estimations (e.g., SMPL-based data) is a notable advantage. This characteristic enhances the practicality of using readily available, though often noisy, human motion data sources, reducing the need for extensive manual cleaning or sophisticated pre-processing.
3. **Accelerated Control Policy Learning**: The generation of high-fidelity, physically consistent reference motions by NMR has a direct and positive impact on downstream whole-body control policies. By providing cleaner and more reliable targets, NMR accelerates the convergence of these policies and leads to improved tracking accuracy and overall performance, as evidenced by higher rewards and success rates in RL training.
4. **Novel Data Generation Pipeline**: The Clustered-Expert Physics Refinement (CEPR) pipeline is a significant methodological contribution. By combining VAE-based motion clustering with massively parallel reinforcement learning experts, it provides a scalable and automated method for generating large-scale, high-fidelity, and physically validated human-robot motion pairs. This addresses the "chicken-and-egg" dilemma of requiring high-quality data to train neural retargeters, enabling data-driven approaches in this domain.
5. **Theoretical Foundation**: The detailed Hessian analysis of optimization-based retargeting formally establishes the inherent non-convexity of the problem, providing a strong theoretical motivation for the proposed data-driven shift.

The **potential impact** of this research is substantial for the advancement of humanoid robotics:
- **Wider Application of Human Demonstrations**: By making the transfer of human motor skills to robots more reliable and robust, NMR can facilitate the broader use of human demonstrations for training robots in diverse and complex tasks, from daily chores to specialized maneuvers like martial arts or dancing.
- **Reduced Development Time and Costs**: The automated generation of high-quality training data via CEPR and the improved efficiency of downstream control policy learning can significantly reduce the manual effort, expertise, and computational resources required to develop advanced robot behaviors.
- **Foundation for Real-World Deployment**: The generation of physically feasible and stable motions is critical for the safe and effective deployment of humanoid robots in real-world environments. NMR contributes to building a more reliable foundation for this transition.
- **Inspiration for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research, particularly in developing morphology-conditioned architectures that can generalize across different robot platforms without regenerating the entire data pipeline, further enhancing scalability and adaptability.

A current limitation noted by the authors is that the CEPR pipeline is morphology-specific. This means extending the framework to other robot platforms currently requires regenerating the data pipeline, highlighting a direction for future work in developing more generalized, morphology-conditioned retargeting architectures. Despite this, NMR represents a significant step towards scalable and robust human-to-humanoid motion transfer.
