# Meta-RL Induces Exploration in Language Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at EPFL, ETH Zurich, and Idiap Research Institute developed LAMER, a Meta-Reinforcement Learning framework for large language model agents, which teaches them to actively explore and adapt their policies in multi-turn tasks. This framework, using a cross-episode training objective and in-context self-reflection, consistently outperformed standard RL and prompting methods, achieving 11.8% to 19.3% absolute gains in pass@3 success rates across diverse environments and demonstrating enhanced generalization to harder and unseen tasks.

## Problem

- Large language model (LLM) agents struggle with active exploration in multi-turn, long-horizon tasks, frequently failing to discover optimal strategies in uncertain environments.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) trained LLM agents exhibit inefficient adaptation from trial-and-error experiences at test time, limiting their flexibility and performance in dynamic tasks.
- Sparse success signals in multi-turn environments make it challenging for agents to learn effective exploration-exploitation trade-offs within a single-episode RL framework.

## Method

- Introduced **LAMER** (LLM Agent with Meta-RL), a ==multi-episode== training framework where policy adaptation for each episode is conditioned on the accumulated history and reflections from prior episodes within a trial.
- Re-defined the return function to a cross-episode discounted return (G^(n)t), explicitly incentivizing exploration in early episodes to optimize for long-term cumulative rewards across an entire trial.
- Implemented an in-context policy adaptation mechanism via self-reflection, where the LLM agent generates textual reflections on past performance and formulates improved plans for subsequent episodes without gradient updates.

## Results

- LAMER consistently outperformed baselines across Sokoban, MineSweeper, and Webshop, achieving pass@3 success rates of **55.9%** (**11.8%** gain over GiGPO), **74.4%** (**19.3%** gain over GiGPO), and **89.1%** (**13.9%** gain over GiGPO), respectively.
- Demonstrated stronger test-time scaling, with larger performance improvements from pass@1 to pass@3 compared to baselines (e.g., **13.5%** `pass@3` gain over `pass@1` on Sokoban for LAMER vs. less than **5%** for baselines).
- Showed improved generalization to harder task variants (maintaining a **10%** performance gap over RL on harder Sokoban) and unseen tasks in ALFWorld (**23%** absolute gain on 'Cool' out-of-distribution task).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for a discounted cross-episode return effectively induces active exploration in LLM agents, leading to better long-term performance compared to single-episode reward maximization.
> - In-context self-reflection provides a computationally efficient and effective mechanism for policy adaptation in LLMs, leveraging their inherent few-shot learning capabilities without requiring gradient updates at test time.
> - Maintaining a higher level of trajectory diversity, a characteristic preserved by the Meta-RL approach, is crucial for balancing exploration and exploitation, allowing agents to better adapt and generalize.
