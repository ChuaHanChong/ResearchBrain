# Meta-RL Induces Exploration in Language Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at EPFL, ETH Zurich, and Idiap Research Institute developed LAMER, a Meta-Reinforcement Learning framework for large language model agents, which teaches them to actively explore and adapt their policies in multi-turn tasks. This framework, using a cross-episode training objective and in-context self-reflection, consistently outperformed standard RL and prompting methods, achieving 11.8% to 19.3% absolute gains in pass@3 success rates across diverse environments and demonstrating enhanced generalization to harder and unseen tasks.

## Problem

- Large language model (LLM) agents struggle with active exploration in multi-turn, long-horizon tasks, frequently failing to discover optimal strategies in uncertain environments.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) trained LLM agents exhibit inefficient adaptation from trial-and-error experiences at test time, limiting their flexibility and performance in dynamic tasks.
- Sparse success signals in multi-turn environments make it challenging for agents to learn effective exploration-exploitation trade-offs within a single-episode RL framework.

## Method

- Introduced **LAMER** (LLM Agent with Meta-RL), a ==multi-episode== training framework where policy adaptation for each episode is conditioned on the accumulated history and reflections from prior episodes within a trial.
- Re-defined the return function to a cross-episode discounted return (G^(n)t), explicitly incentivizing exploration in early episodes to optimize for long-term cumulative rewards across an entire trial.
- Implemented an in-context policy adaptation mechanism via self-reflection, where the LLM agent generates textual reflections on past performance and formulates improved plans for subsequent episodes without gradient updates.

## Results

- LAMER consistently outperformed baselines across Sokoban, MineSweeper, and Webshop, achieving pass@3 success rates of **55.9%** (**11.8%** gain over GiGPO), **74.4%** (**19.3%** gain over GiGPO), and **89.1%** (**13.9%** gain over GiGPO), respectively.
- Demonstrated stronger test-time scaling, with larger performance improvements from pass@1 to pass@3 compared to baselines (e.g., **13.5%** `pass@3` gain over `pass@1` on Sokoban for LAMER vs. less than **5%** for baselines).
- Showed improved generalization to harder task variants (maintaining a **10%** performance gap over RL on harder Sokoban) and unseen tasks in ALFWorld (**23%** absolute gain on 'Cool' out-of-distribution task).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for a discounted cross-episode return effectively induces active exploration in LLM agents, leading to better long-term performance compared to single-episode reward maximization.
> - In-context self-reflection provides a computationally efficient and effective mechanism for policy adaptation in LLMs, leveraging their inherent few-shot learning capabilities without requiring gradient updates at test time.
> - Maintaining a higher level of trajectory diversity, a characteristic preserved by the Meta-RL approach, is crucial for balancing exploration and exploitation, allowing agents to better adapt and generalize.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has progressed beyond conversational systems to autonomous decision-making agents capable of reasoning and interacting with environments in multi-turn, long-horizon tasks. A fundamental challenge in this domain is enabling these agents to actively explore their environment, acquire new knowledge, and adapt efficiently from trial-and-error experiences. Current reinforcement learning (RL) trained LLM agents frequently encounter difficulties in tasks requiring active exploration and often fail to adapt robustly.

Existing research has attempted to address the exploration limitation in LLMs. Some approaches involve training models offline to distill exploration strategies from diverse trajectories (Tajwar et al., 2025) or offline search traces (Gandhi et al., 2024). Other methods focus on learning to explore in-context to optimize test-time computation (Setlur et al., 2025). However, these efforts often focus on single-turn, non-agentic reasoning problems or rely on offline data, which primarily facilitates imitation rather than active, online exploration within interactive environments.

This paper positions itself within the broader landscape by introducing a Meta-Reinforcement Learning (Meta-RL) framework to address the limitations of LLM agents in active exploration and efficient adaptation in multi-turn, interactive settings. Meta-RL, which focuses on "learning to reinforcement learn," is a paradigm designed to enable rapid adaptation to new environments by discovering general strategies. The work extends the application of Meta-RL to LLM agents, specifically for multi-episode tasks where balancing exploration and exploitation is critical. By treating policy adaptation as an in-context process, the proposed framework leverages the intrinsic capabilities of LLMs for few-shot learning and adaptation without requiring gradient updates during testing.

The research also relates to the concept of optimizing test-time compute by amortizing training over multiple episodes, thereby enabling learned in-context policy adaptation for faster performance. Furthermore, it connects to the broader area of reasoning in LLMs, as language agents must integrate reasoning into their decision-making processes, especially when reflecting on past actions and planning future strategies. Unlike much of the reasoning work that targets single-turn problems (e.g., math, coding), this paper focuses on multi-turn agentic environments where continuous environmental feedback is available.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general Meta-RL framework, named LAMER (LLM Agent with Meta-RL), that enables large language model (LLM) agents to actively explore their environment and adapt their policies efficiently based on environmental feedback at test time.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current RL-trained LLM agents:
- **Struggling with Active Exploration:** While LLMs have advanced to become decision-making agents in multi-turn tasks, they often fail to engage robustly in active exploration, which is crucial for discovering optimal strategies in uncertain or novel environments. Unlike humans, who can systematically explore and adapt quickly, LLM agents typically require substantial external interventions to exhibit exploratory behaviors.
- **Inefficient Adaptation from Trial-and-Error:** Existing RL-trained agents frequently learn a fixed policy during training and subsequently struggle to adapt their behavior effectively from trial-and-error experiences at test time. This limits their flexibility and performance in dynamic or varied tasks.
- **Sparse Success Signals in Multi-Turn Tasks:** Many multi-turn, long-horizon tasks offer sparse feedback, often providing a clear success signal only at the end of an episode. This sparsity makes it challenging for agents to learn effective exploration-exploitation trade-offs within a single-episode RL framework.
- **Need for Principled Adaptation Mechanisms:** There is a need for robust and efficient policy adaptation mechanisms for LLM agents, particularly ones that can operate without computationally expensive gradient updates at test time, leveraging the in-context learning capabilities of LLMs.

To address these motivations, LAMER is proposed to:
- Introduce a multi-episode training regime where the agent learns to balance exploration and exploitation across multiple attempts at a task. This allows the agent to gather diverse experiences and informative feedback in early episodes and leverage this information for improved exploitation in later ones.
- Internalize a learning algorithm that explicitly incentivizes exploration for better long-term performance by maximizing cumulative rewards across episodes.
- Develop an in-context policy adaptation mechanism using self-reflection, enabling the agent to adjust its strategy based on past experiences and feedback without gradient updates, making it naturally suited for LLM architectures.

By leveraging Meta-RL principles, the authors aim to move towards LLM agents that can autonomously uncover information and refine their decision-making in new and challenging environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, LAMER (LLM Agent with Meta-RL), is designed to enable LLM agents to actively explore and learn from environment feedback through a Meta-RL approach. It comprises two primary components: a cross-episode training framework and an in-context policy adaptation mechanism via self-reflection.

#### 3.1. Cross-episode Training Framework

LAMER operates in a multi-episode structure, where each "trial" for a given task consists of *N* sequentially generated episodes, denoted as *T = (τ^(0), τ^(1)..., τ^(N-1))*. In this setup, the policy for episode *n*, π^(n)θ(·), is adapted based on the accumulated history of prior episodes and reflections, *H^(n) = (τ^(0)..., τ^(n-1), reflections)*. Episodes continue until a task is successfully completed or a maximum episode budget is reached.

To explicitly encourage exploration and optimize for long-term rewards across episodes, LAMER redefines the return function. Beyond the standard within-episode discounted return *g^(n)t*, it introduces a cross-episode discounted return *G^(n)t*. This *G^(n)t* aggregates the current within-episode return *g^(n)t* with the initial returns of all subsequent episodes in the trial, discounted by a cross-episode discount factor *γ_traj*:
`G^(n)t = g^(n)t + Σ_(m=n+1)^(N-1) (γ_traj)^(m-n) * g^(m)0`

The overall Meta-RL objective `J(θ)` is to maximize the expected discounted return of the entire trial, `E_(T~πθ) [G^(0)0]`. The parameter `γ_traj` is critical in controlling the exploration-exploitation trade-off: a smaller `γ_traj` emphasizes immediate rewards and encourages rapid exploitation, while a larger `γ_traj` promotes longer-term returns, thereby incentivizing more exploration in early episodes. This framework contrasts with standard RL training, where rollouts for a task are typically independent. In LAMER, each episode’s rollout is conditioned on previous rollouts within the same trial, allowing for adaptive behavior.

#### 3.2. In-context Policy Adaptation with Self-Reflection

Policy adaptation forms the inner loop of the Meta-RL process in LAMER. Instead of gradient-based updates, which can be computationally intensive for LLMs, LAMER employs a self-reflection mechanism for in-context policy adaptation. After each episode concludes, the agent is prompted to generate a textual reflection on its performance, identifying mistakes, inefficiencies, and formulating an improved plan for the subsequent episode.

This reflection, along with the history of past trajectories, is stored in an inter-episode memory *H^(n)*. The agent's policy for the next episode, `π^(n)θ(·)`, is then conditioned on this contextual memory, effectively `πθ(·|H^(n))`. This leverages LLMs' inherent in-context learning capabilities. Crucially, the self-reflection step itself is explicitly optimized during training, with its quality being reinforced by the rewards obtained in the subsequent episode. The memory content *H^(n)* can be managed to control context length, and the default configuration includes both history trajectories and reflections.

#### 3.3. Optimization

The Meta-RL objective defined by Eq. (5) is optimized using standard policy gradient methods. The gradient `∇θ J(θ)` is estimated by summing over policy gradients weighted by an advantage estimation `A^(n)t`, derived from the cross-episode return `G^(n)t`. The framework is compatible with algorithms such as PPO, GRPO, and GiGPO, with GiGPO used as the default optimizer in the experiments.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen3-4B is used for all main experiments, with additional validation on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct.
- **Environments:** Evaluation is conducted on four diverse, long-horizon tasks:
 - **Sokoban:** Fully observable, grid-based planning game.
 - **MineSweeper:** Partially observable, logical deduction game.
 - **Webshop:** Partially observable, text-based e-commerce task.
 - **ALFWorld:** Partially observable, text-based embodied environment simulating household activities.
- **Training Parameters:**
 - Default `γ_traj` = 0.6.
 - N = 3 episodes per trial for Meta-RL.
 - Group size of 8 for Meta-RL, and 24 for standard RL (to ensure equal total trajectories per gradient update).
 - A learning rate of 1e-6 with Adam optimizer.
 - Environment reward: 10 for success, 0 for failure.
 - Temperature: 1.0 for rollout, 0.7 for evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that LAMER, a Meta-RL framework for LLM agents, consistently improves performance, generalization, and test-time adaptation compared to established baselines.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison (Table 1)

LAMER consistently outperformed prompting-based baselines (Zero-shot, ReAct, Reflexion) and RL-training methods (PPO, RLOO, GRPO, GiGPO) across Sokoban, MineSweeper, and Webshop environments.
- **Sokoban:** LAMER achieved a 55.9% `pass@3` success rate, an 11.8% absolute gain over the strongest RL baseline (GiGPO's 44.1%).
- **MineSweeper:** LAMER reached a 74.4% `pass@3` success rate, marking a 19.3% absolute gain over GiGPO (55.1%).
- **Webshop:** LAMER demonstrated an 89.1% `pass@3` success rate, an improvement of 13.9% over GiGPO (75.2%).
The performance gains were also observed in `pass@2` across all environments and `pass@1` for Sokoban, indicating broad effectiveness.

#### 4.2. Stronger Test-Time Scaling (Table 1)

LAMER exhibited improved test-time scaling, with larger performance increments from `pass@1` to `pass@3` compared to baselines. For example, on Sokoban, LAMER's `pass@3` success rate was 13.5% higher than its `pass@1` rate, whereas RL and prompting baselines showed gains of less than 5%. Although LAMER sometimes started with slightly lower `pass@1` scores on MineSweeper and Webshop than the best RL baseline, it surpassed all baselines by `pass@2` and `pass@3`, suggesting an enhanced ability to learn and adapt from earlier attempts.

#### 4.3. Induction of Exploration (Figure 3)

Analysis of trajectory diversity revealed that while the base model produced the most diverse trajectories (highest entropy) but low success, RL-trained agents converged to more deterministic behaviors with reduced diversity. In contrast, LAMER trained with Meta-RL maintained a higher level of trajectory diversity than RL baselines, suggesting it effectively balanced exploration and exploitation.

#### 4.4. Generalization to Harder Tasks (Figure 4)

When evaluated on more challenging versions of Sokoban (more boxes) and MineSweeper (more mines), LAMER consistently outperformed RL-trained models across all difficulty levels. On the most difficult settings, LAMER maintained a 10% performance gap over RL on Sokoban and a 5% gap on MineSweeper, indicating superior generalization to harder variants of known tasks.

#### 4.5. Generalization to Unseen Tasks (Table 2)

In the ALFWorld environment, where models were trained on four in-distribution tasks (Pick, Look, Clean, Heat) and evaluated on two out-of-distribution tasks (Cool, Pick2), LAMER demonstrated improved generalization. While RL models performed well on in-distribution tasks, LAMER consistently surpassed them on both in-distribution and, notably, out-of-distribution tasks. For the out-of-distribution tasks, LAMER achieved a 23% absolute performance gain on 'Cool' and approximately 14% on 'Pick2' compared to the RL baseline.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Influence of Trajectory Discount Factor (γ_traj) (Figure 5):** The optimal `γ_traj` varied by environment. Intermediate values (e.g., 0.6) were optimal for Sokoban and Webshop, suggesting a balanced approach to immediate and long-term rewards. MineSweeper benefited from a larger `γ_traj` (e.g., 0.9), indicating that extended credit assignment aids strategic exploration in that environment.
- **Inter-episode Memory (Table 3):** The self-reflection mechanism in LAMER was crucial. Using only reflections in the inter-episode memory (vs. trajectory-only or both) resulted in the highest performance, with gains of 21.6% on Sokoban, 11.0% on MineSweeper, and 3.5% on Webshop compared to the trajectory-only configuration. This suggests that concise, focused guidance from reflections is highly effective for adaptation.

#### 4.7. Training Budget

While LAMER and standard RL used the same total number of trajectories for each gradient update (by adjusting group sizes), LAMER incurred approximately twice the training time due to the sequential generation of episodes within a trial, which limits parallelism compared to independent RL rollouts.

#### 4.8. Experiments on Different Architectures (Appendix D.1, Table 4)

LAMER's effectiveness was also demonstrated on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, confirming its general applicability across different LLM architectures and sizes, with `pass@3` gains of 14% on Sokoban and 24% on MineSweeper over GiGPO.

#### 4.9. Comparison to RL Baselines with Inter-episode Memory (Appendix D.2, Table 5)

Even when RL-trained agents were given access to inter-episode memory, LAMER still substantially outperformed them across all environments, highlighting that the benefits are attributable to the Meta-RL objective rather than merely providing memory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces LAMER, a Meta-RL framework for training LLM agents, representing a development in endowing language agents with enhanced exploratory and adaptive capabilities.

The significance of this work can be understood through several points:
- **Principled Approach to Exploration:** LAMER offers a principled Meta-RL approach to induce active exploration in LLM agents. By maximizing a discounted cross-episode return, it explicitly teaches agents when to explore and when to exploit, moving beyond single-episode immediate payoff maximization. This is a critical advancement over traditional RL methods that often lead to sub-optimal, less explorative behaviors in LLMs.
- **Enhanced Adaptation and Generalization:** The framework enables LLM agents to learn general explorative strategies during training, facilitating rapid, in-context policy adaptation at test time. This capability is demonstrated by LAMER's superior performance across diverse, long-horizon tasks, its improved generalization to harder task variants, and its notable ability to perform better on out-of-distribution tasks compared to existing RL methods. This suggests a step towards more robust and flexible LLM agents.
- **Improved Test-Time Efficiency:** The observed stronger test-time scaling of LAMER, where performance significantly improves with more attempts, indicates that the learned exploration strategies allow agents to effectively learn from prior mistakes and adapt within a limited number of trials. This is a practical benefit for real-world applications where rapid adaptation is crucial.
- **Leveraging LLM In-Context Learning:** The reliance on self-reflection for in-context policy adaptation effectively leverages the inherent capabilities of LLMs for few-shot learning and reasoning. This approach avoids the computational overhead of gradient-based adaptation at test time, making the framework more efficient for large models.
- **Foundation for Generalist Agents:** The demonstrated generalization to harder and unseen tasks suggests a potential pathway toward building more generalist LLM agents. Agents trained with LAMER exhibit a learned capacity to transfer knowledge and adaptive strategies to novel environments or task variations, which is a long-standing goal in AI research.

The potential impact of this research is substantial. It could lead to the development of more autonomous and intelligent LLM agents capable of navigating complex, uncertain, and dynamic environments with greater efficiency and success. This has implications for various applications, including advanced robotic control, complex decision-making systems, interactive gaming, and more sophisticated web navigation or task automation agents. Furthermore, the findings open new avenues for research, such as integrating LAMER with other advanced RL algorithms, exploring more sophisticated advantage estimation techniques, or refining memory management strategies to further improve computational efficiency and performance.
