# VTAO-BiManip: Masked Visual-Tactile-Action Pre-training with Object Understanding for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VTAO-BiManip pre-trains a masked visual-tactile-action transformer on human bimanual demonstrations, jointly reconstructing vision, tactile, future actions, and object 6-DoF pose/size, then feeds frozen features into a two-stage curriculum RL policy. On a dual-hand bottle-cap twisting task the method beats prior visual-tactile pretraining baselines by over 20% success rate in simulation and real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Prior visual-tactile pretraining for dexterous manipulation relies on single-hand, fixed-object setups and cannot capture inter-hand kinematic synergy needed for dynamic bimanual coordination.
- Existing methods lack explicit perception of object pose and size, causing incoherent hand movements when the manipulated object's state changes dynamically mid-task.
- Single-stage RL policies in prior work have no mechanism to sequence interdependent sub-skills, such as one hand stabilizing before the other initiates rotation.

## Method

- **VTAO** capture system: Hololens2 vision, tactile-motion gloves, and a 16-camera mocap rig jointly record 216 bimanual trajectories with synchronized hand motion, tactile, and 6-DoF bottle pose, retargeted to robot hands via ==DexRepNet==-style optimization.
- **Masked VTAO Transformer**: an ==encoder-decoder== pretrains on ==masked vision, tactile, and action tokens==, reconstructing current signals, predicting actions 5 steps ahead, and estimating object pose/size to force geometry-aware, cross-modal fusion.
- Two-stage ==curriculum RL==: ==PPO== first trains sub-skills with the bottle fixed to the table, then fine-tunes on a released, freely movable bottle for full collaborative bimanual unscrewing.

## Results

- VTAO-BiManip achieves **63±4%** seen and **51±7%** unseen success rate, surpassing single-modality and non-pretrained VTA-Scr baselines by over **20** percentage points.
- Ablations show removing left-hand action/tactile data drops success from **63%** to **46%** and **41%**, confirming bimanual pretraining data materially improves coordination.
- Action-prediction ablations show 5-step future prediction with 24x2 per-joint tokens outperforms no-prediction (**35%**), 1-step prediction (**44%**), and coarser tokenization (**31-50%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting future actions during masked pretraining, not just reconstructing current state, instills stronger action priors than standard imitation-style next-step prediction.
> - Object-state reconstruction as an auxiliary pretraining target, even when unavailable at deployment, teaches geometry-aware representations that improve downstream policy robustness.
> - Curriculum staging that first fixes environment complexity, then releases it, is a practical fix for RL exploration failure in high-dimensional multi-skill bimanual tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly dexterous bimanual manipulation, is a grand challenge in robotics, primarily due to the high degrees of freedom (DoFs) involved, the intricate coordination required between multiple manipulators, and the need for real-time adaptation to dynamic object states. This paper positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly advancing areas: multimodal pre-training, reinforcement learning (RL), and sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation tasks.

**Existing Limitations:**
The paper highlights that while significant progress has been made in single-hand dexterous manipulation, often leveraging human demonstrations and multimodal sensing (e.g., visual-tactile fusion as in their prior work), these methods typically rely on constrained task setups, such as objects being fixed on a table. Directly extending these to dynamic bimanual tasks, like twisting a bottle cap where the object can move, introduces "three irreducible challenges":
1. **Kinematic coordination limitation:** Single-hand methods lack the capability to capture and learn the inter-hand kinematic synergy crucial for bimanual tasks.
2. **Object-state decoupling:** Current methods often lack explicit perception of an object's 3D pose and size, making it difficult to adapt to dynamic changes in the object's state.
3. **Sub-skill composability:** Complex tasks require sequencing and coordinating multiple sub-skills (e.g., grasping, holding, rotating). Single-stage policies struggle with this temporal composition.

**Multimodal Pre-training for Robotics:**
The broader landscape of multimodal pre-training has seen success in integrating vision and language (e.g., CLIP, Ego4D), vision and actions (e.g., RT-2), and visuo-tactile modalities. However, the paper points out that pre-training on *action modalities* has primarily focused on simpler end-effectors, and the application of *visuo-tactile fusion* in bimanual tasks remains largely unexplored. This work distinguishes itself by integrating visual, tactile, *and action* modalities, coupled with explicit object understanding, into a masked autoencoder (MAE)-based pre-training framework specifically for bimanual manipulation.

**Robotic Bimanual Manipulation:**
Research in bimanual manipulation has explored demonstration data, multi-agent RL, and sim-to-real transfer. Most existing methods, however, predominantly rely on visual and proprioceptive (joint angle) modalities, with limited exploration of rich multimodal data, especially *tactile* sensing, in dual-dexterous-hand scenarios. Previous works on dual-dexterous manipulation often focus on specific tasks (e.g., handover, piano playing, twisting cylindrical containers with sparse visual keypoints). This paper explicitly states its "pioneering effort to apply multimodal sensing to dual-dexterous-hand manipulation," thereby filling a critical gap in the research landscape. By integrating vision, tactile, action, and object understanding, VTAO-BiManip offers a more holistic approach to understanding and executing complex bimanual tasks that mimic human capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable human-like bimanual dexterous manipulation in robotics** by addressing the inherent complexities of high DoF hands and their coordination. This overarching goal is broken down into specific technical objectives, largely motivated by the limitations of existing single-hand and less comprehensive bimanual manipulation techniques.

The core motivations stem from the "three irreducible challenges" identified in extending prior single-hand visual-tactile methods to dynamic bimanual tasks:

1. **To address kinematic coordination limitation and object-state decoupling:** The paper aims to introduce explicit **hand action and object-centric understanding** as complementary modalities during pre-training.
 - **Action Prediction:** By introducing hand joint angles as explicit kinematic observables and predicting future action sequences using masked multimodal inputs, the objective is to model inter-hand synergy. This allows the policy to dynamically coordinate bimanual motions, essential for tasks like adjusting one hand to counteract torque from the other.
 - **Object Understanding:** Augmenting the pre-training with object pose (6D) and size (3D) estimation forces the encoder to learn geometry-aware representations. This provides the model with explicit awareness of dynamic object state changes, enabling real-time adaptation during manipulation.

2. **To address sub-skill composability:** The paper aims to develop a **two-stage curriculum reinforcement learning (RL) framework**. This is motivated by the difficulty of training precise dual-hand manipulation with non-fixed objects from scratch, where exploration in high-dimensional bimanual action space often leads to zero success rates. The curriculum breaks down the complex task into foundational sub-skills (e.g., stable grasping, initial cap rotation) before progressing to full collaborative manipulation.

In summary, the key objectives and contributions are:
- Proposing **VTAO-BiManip**, a novel visual-tactile-action pre-training method with object understanding for bimanual dexterous manipulation, leveraging human demonstrations for pre-training.
- Developing a **curriculum reinforcement learning framework** to tackle the multi-skill learning challenge within a single, complex bimanual task.
- Demonstrating the effectiveness of VTAO-BiManip and the curriculum RL on a challenging bimanual bottle-cap twisting task in both simulated and real-world environments, achieving a significant performance improvement over existing visual-tactile pre-training methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VTAO-BiManip framework integrates a novel multimodal pre-training scheme with a curriculum reinforcement learning approach.

#### 3.1. A. VTAO Data Capture System and Dataset

The foundation of VTAO-BiManip is a custom data collection system designed to capture ego-centric human bimanual manipulation data, comprising **Vision (V), Tactile (T), Action (A), and Object (O)** information.
- **Vision:** Captured via Hololens2.
- **Tactile:** Acquired using two-layer acquisition gloves, with an inner layer containing 20 tactile sensors per hand (similar to).
- **Action & Object Pose:** A CHINGMU motion capture system with 16 cameras precisely records hand motion trajectories and 6-DoF object poses in the same coordinate system. The outer layer of the gloves has retroreflective markers for motion capture.
- **Data Alignment:** All modalities (visual at 30 Hz, tactile at 200 Hz, mocap at 1 kHz) are synchronized to the visual modality using local timestamps. A calibration procedure unifies motion capture and visual coordinate systems.
- **Human Trajectory Retargeting:** Human bimanual trajectories are retargeted to dexterous robotic hands (specifically, Shadow Hand) using a nonlinear optimization method based on DexRepNet, which preserves geometric similarity between human and robot hands.
- **VTAO Dataset:** The system collects 216 human bimanual manipulation trajectories with 26 different bottles, totaling over 61,000 temporally aligned multimodal frames. Each processed frame includes RGB image (224x224x3), bimanual binarized tactile signals (40 values), retargeted robotic joint angles (48 values for 24 joints per hand), and an object state vector (9 values for 6-DoF pose and 3D size).

#### 3.2. B. Masked VTAO Transformer for Pre-Training

The core of VTAO-BiManip is an MAE-based (Masked Autoencoder) framework pre-trained on the VTAO dataset, following an encoder-decoder architecture.
- **VTAO Encoder:** Takes a single frame input of Vision (V), Tactile (T), and current Action (A). Object information (O) is *excluded* from the input but is a target for reconstruction.
 - **Tokenization:** Visual input is divided into patches (196 tokens). Binarized tactile signals (40 values) are projected into latent tokens (40 tokens). Action (48 joint angles) is encoded into 48 action tokens.
 - **Concatenation and Masking:** All input tokens, along with a "fusion token" (k_fs) for global information and mask tokens (k_null) for object reconstruction, are concatenated. A MAE-style masking strategy is applied, randomly masking portions of visual (75%), tactile (50%), action (50%), and object mask tokens.
 - The concatenated and masked tokens are fed into a Transformer encoder, outputting latent representations (h_fs, h_v, h_c, h_a, h_o).
- **VTAO Decoder:** Reconstructs the original sensory modalities and predicts future information from the latent tokens.
 - **Reconstruction Objectives:**
 1. Reconstruct the original image (V).
 2. Reconstruct the binarized tactile signals (T).
 3. Reconstruct the current dual-hand actions (A) AND predict future actions for `p` steps (specifically `p=5`). This is a critical innovation for modeling inter-hand synergy.
 4. Reconstruct the bottle's 6-DoF pose and 3D size (O). This forces the encoder to learn geometry-dynamic representations, providing explicit object awareness.
 - **Loss Function:** A weighted sum of L2 losses for image, tactile, object, and action (current + future prediction) reconstruction. The weights are configured as W_img=1, W_tac=2, W_bot=5, W_act=2, emphasizing object and action understanding.

#### 3.3. C. Curriculum RL for Dexterous Bimanual Manipulation

A two-stage curriculum reinforcement learning framework is proposed to stabilize training for the complex bimanual bottle-cap twisting task.
- **Task Setup:** Bimanual bottle-cap unscrewing task using dual Shadow Hands (in simulation) or Leap Hands (in real world). The bottle body is initially at the table center, with the left hand in a pre-grasp position and the right hand above. Success is defined by the cap rotating more than 4 radians.
- **Problem Formulation:** Modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and solved using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.
- **State Space:** S = {V_obs, T_obs, P}, where V_obs is the ego-centric RGB image, T_obs is binarized tactile signals from both hands, and P is proprioception data (joint angles and velocities). Critically, the object state information (O) is *excluded* from the input state space during RL training to maintain real-world feasibility. Instead, the frozen pre-trained VTAO encoder generates a fusion token (h_fs) from (V_obs, T_obs, A_obs), which is combined with proprioception (P) via a linear layer to form the policy input.
- **Action Space:** The Shadow Hand has 24 DoFs. The right hand controls its initial position, while the left hand has 6 DoFs for free manipulation. This results in a 46-dimensional continuous action space.
- **Learning Curriculum and Reward Design:**
 - **Stage 1: Sub-skill Learning (Fixed Bottle):** The bottle body is fixed on the table. Rewards encourage the left hand to grasp the bottle (distance penalty, contact reward) and the right hand to unscrew the cap (rotation angle reward, rotation speed reward, approach reward). This stage reduces exploration complexity and builds foundational skills.
 - **Stage 2: Collaborative Learning (Released Bottle):** The bottle body is physically released, requiring bimanual cooperation.
 - Left hand rewards: Encourage hand approach to a target position, bottle stability (remaining near initial position), and upright maintenance.
 - Right hand rewards: Same as Stage 1, but now adapted to dynamic bottle movements.
 - The model from Stage 1 initializes Stage 2, which then trains for collaborative manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in the Isaac Gym simulation environment using 15 bottles from ShapeNet (10 for training, 5 for testing). The primary metric was the success rate (cap rotation > 4 rad).

#### 4.1. A. Effectiveness of Pre-training with Different Modalities

- **Multimodal Advantage:** Multimodal joint pre-training (VTA, VTAO) demonstrated significantly superior performance in downstream manipulation success rates compared to single-modality pre-training (V, T, A) and training from scratch (VTA-Scr). This validates the benefits of multimodal fusion.
- **Contribution of Action Prediction and Object Understanding:**
 - Improvements from VT (Visual + Tactile) to VTA (VT + Action prediction) and to VTO (VT + Object understanding) confirmed that both action prediction and object understanding substantially enhance visuo-tactile fusion.
 - **VTAO**, which combines all modalities (Vision, Tactile, Action, Object understanding), achieved the highest success rates, indicating complementary benefits between predicting future actions and explicitly understanding object states.
- **Importance of Object State Perception:** Proprioception-only configurations (Base, A) failed to train, while Base+Obj (proprioception + object understanding) achieved successful training. This highlights the critical role of object state perception for policies to dynamically adapt manipulation strategies.
- **Overall Performance:** While VTAO achieved the highest success rate (around 63% on seen bottles, 51% on unseen bottles), the overall performance was noted as "relatively low." This is attributed to the task's high complexity, large variations in object shapes/sizes, and the high-dimensional bimanual action space.

#### 4.2. B. Ablation Study

Comprehensive ablation studies validated key design choices:

- **Effectiveness of Bimanual Pre-training:** Using bimanual tactile and action data consistently led to higher success rates compared to using only right-hand data (e.g., removing left-hand action or tactile data). Even though the left hand primarily performs grasping and holding, its inclusion significantly enhanced overall coordination and success.
- **Action Prediction Horizon:**
 - Solely reconstructing the current frame's action (v5) was detrimental compared to the baseline without action addition (v1, which includes action as input but no prediction/reconstruction loss).
 - Predicting future actions improved success rates, with a 5-step prediction horizon showing the best performance. This confirms the importance of learning temporal dynamics for robotic policies.
- **Number of Action Tokens (N):** Treating each joint's angle as an individual token (24x2 for two 24-DoF hands) was more advantageous for VTA fusion than aggregating actions per hand or per finger, suggesting fine-grained action representation is beneficial.

#### 4.3. C. Real-World Experiment

The proposed method was deployed on a real-world platform using dual Leap Hands equipped with tactile sensors and a Unitree Z1 arm. Visual perception was from an ego-centric RGB camera. The paper qualitatively confirms that the method supports effective physical experimentation, with detailed demonstrations available in a submitted video. This demonstrates the sim-to-real transferability of the learned policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VTAO-BiManip represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for complex bimanual tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Multimodal Bimanual Manipulation:** The paper makes a pioneering effort by integrating visual, tactile, *and action* modalities with explicit object understanding for dual-dexterous-hand manipulation. This holistic approach addresses limitations of previous methods that often relied on a subset of these modalities or were restricted to simpler manipulators.
- **Enhanced Skill Transferability and Human-like Dexterity:** By pre-training on human VTAO demonstrations, the framework aims to imbue robots with more human-like bimanual manipulation capabilities. The masked autoencoding approach allows the model to learn robust, transferable representations from partial observations, which is critical for generalizing to novel scenarios and objects.
- **Addressing High-DoF Complexity:** Bimanual dexterous manipulation is inherently complex due to the exponential increase in DoFs and coordination challenges. VTAO-BiManip's multimodal fusion, especially the action prediction and object understanding, provides the policy with richer, more contextualized information, enabling it to better manage this complexity and dynamically coordinate two hands.
- **Robustness to Dynamic Environments:** The explicit object pose and size estimation during pre-training allows the robot to adapt its manipulation strategy in real-time to dynamic changes in the object's state (e.g., a tilting bottle). This is crucial for real-world applications where objects are rarely perfectly static.
- **Stabilizing Complex Reinforcement Learning:** The two-stage curriculum RL framework is a practical solution to stabilize training in high-dimensional, sparse-reward bimanual tasks. By breaking down the problem into manageable sub-skills, it accelerates learning and improves the chances of success, which can be directly applied to other complex multi-skill robotic tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future work, including scaling multimodal bimanual datasets across diverse scenes and tasks, extending the approach to broader manipulation scenarios beyond bottle-cap twisting, and exploring advanced methods for geometric object priors from vision models (potentially replacing motion-captured 3D bounding boxes with estimations from large models). This indicates a promising research direction that can lead to more generalized and autonomous bimanual robots.

In conclusion, VTAO-BiManip pushes the boundaries of dexterous robotic manipulation by providing a robust framework for learning complex bimanual skills. Its novel combination of multimodal pre-training with a structured learning curriculum offers a pathway towards more intelligent, adaptable, and human-like robotic capabilities, with significant implications for assembly, assistance, and manufacturing applications.
