# Flow-ERD: Agent-type Aware Flow Matching with Entropy-Regularized Distillation for Diverse Traffic Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NAVER LABS Corp. introduced Flow-ERD, a multi-agent traffic simulator combining agent-type aware flow matching with entropy-regularized distillation to achieve high closed-loop realism and diverse traffic behaviors. This approach secured the top rank in realism on the WOSAC benchmark and demonstrated superior diversity compared to existing methods, providing controlled balancing of these two critical simulation qualities.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving simulations require simultaneously realistic, closed-loop traffic behaviors and diverse, plausible future scenarios to robustly test planning policies.
- Current generative models for traffic simulation often exhibit a trade-off between realism and diversity, with benchmarks typically favoring realism against a single logged trajectory.
- Learning-based simulators face covariate shift in closed-loop operation, and existing mitigation strategies risk reducing the multi-modality of generated behaviors.

## Method

- The **Flow-ERD** framework integrates **Agent-Type Aware Flow Matching (AFM)** to generate continuous, kinematically consistent actions based on agent type (==holonomic== or ==non-holonomic transitions==).
- A unique training approach ensures action targets are ==transition-consistent==, minimizing the gap between training and closed-loop inference.
- **Entropy-Regularized Distillation (ERD)** is applied as a closed-loop fine-tuning strategy, adding an ==entropy regularizer== to the ==reverse-KL objective== to preserve multi-modality via a ==tempered target distribution==.

## Results

- Flow-ERD achieved the **top rank** in the **WOSAC Realism Meta Metric (RMM)**, demonstrating state-of-the-art closed-loop realism.
- The Agent-Type Aware Flow Matching (AFM) backbone significantly improved intrinsic diversity, scoring the **highest Cross-Pair Diversity (CPD)** among pretrained models and dominating the realism-diversity Pareto front.
- Entropy-Regularized Distillation (ERD) successfully fine-tuned the model for closed-loop stability, with the entropy temperature **β** providing direct control to balance realism with the preservation of diverse, intent-level multi-modality, including rare maneuvers like U-turns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agent-Type Aware Flow Matching (AFM) successfully resolves the tension between continuous motion expressiveness and physical realism by enforcing type-specific kinematic transitions for diverse action generation.
> - Entropy-Regularized Distillation (ERD) mitigates covariate shift in closed-loop simulation by tempering the target distribution, preventing mode collapse and preserving crucial behavioral diversity.
> - A controllable entropy temperature (β) in ERD allows for explicit balancing along the realism-diversity Pareto front, enabling simulators to be optimized for specific needs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traffic simulation is a foundational component for the development and validation of autonomous driving systems. These simulations enable controlled environments for testing autonomous vehicle (AV) planning policies and ensuring robustness before public deployment. For a simulator to effectively serve these roles, it must generate realistic behaviors that accurately imitate real-world traffic dynamics and exhibit closed-loop reactivity. Concurrently, it must also produce diverse scenarios, encompassing a range of plausible future behaviors for a given scene, to ensure the AV policy's resilience to varied conditions. This work addresses the challenge of achieving both realism and diversity jointly, rather than as competing objectives.

Existing research in learning-based multi-agent simulators, particularly those employing generative models, has made substantial advancements in realism, often driven by benchmarks such as the Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge (WOSAC). However, these benchmarks typically measure realism against a single logged ground truth future, which may not adequately capture the breadth of plausible behaviors or differentiate between models that merely fit the logged trajectory and those that generate diverse, yet realistic, alternatives. Consequently, diversity has often been acknowledged but not prioritized equally, sometimes managed through sampling hyperparameters or evaluated only qualitatively.

Current simulator designs present inherent trade-offs between realism and diversity. Next-token prediction (NTP) based methods, which discretize motion into a fixed vocabulary, tend to provide an inductive bias towards realistic, type-compatible motion due to their data-derived or rule-based primitives. However, this discrete vocabulary inherently limits the achievable diversity by collapsing fine-grained motions onto a coarse set of tokens. In contrast, continuous representation models, such as diffusion models, overcome the vocabulary bottleneck and are capable of modeling complex multi-modal distributions. Yet, this expressiveness does not inherently guarantee realism; continuous policies must implicitly learn valid motion, and occasional generation of type-incompatible predictions (e.g., lateral slip for wheeled vehicles) can accumulate errors in closed-loop simulations.

Furthermore, a common challenge in learning-based simulators is covariate shift. Models trained in an open-loop fashion, where conditioning contexts are drawn from logged data, often exhibit accumulating errors when deployed in a closed-loop setting where their own generated actions feed back into the system. Existing methods to mitigate covariate shift, such as augmenting data with log-proximal rollouts or using reinforcement learning to optimize realism scores, primarily pull generated rollouts closer to the recorded trajectory or the data manifold. While these approaches improve closed-loop stability, it remains unclear whether they do so by inadvertently narrowing the distribution of generated behaviors, thereby compromising diversity.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by introducing a multi-agent simulator designed to explicitly address the joint pursuit of realism and diversity. It aims to overcome the limitations of both token-based and continuous models by combining continuous generation with type-specific kinematic execution, and to mitigate covariate shift without sacrificing the inherent multi-modality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a multi-agent traffic simulator, named Flow-ERD, that simultaneously enhances both closed-loop realism and rollout diversity. This objective is motivated by several identified gaps and requirements in the field of autonomous driving simulation:

1. **Joint Realism and Diversity Requirement:** Autonomous driving systems necessitate simulations that are not only realistic—accurately reflecting real-world traffic behaviors and inter-agent reactions in a closed-loop manner—but also diverse, capable of exploring multiple plausible future scenarios. This diversity is crucial for ensuring the robustness of ego-vehicle planning policies by exposing them to a wider range of potential interactions. The paper emphasizes that these two properties must coexist, not operate as alternatives.

2. **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Prevailing benchmarks, such as the WOSAC, predominantly evaluate realism by comparing simulated trajectories against a single logged future. This evaluation method is insufficient for distinguishing models that merely replicate a specific recorded event from those that can generate a rich spectrum of plausible, yet varied, behaviors. This design encourages models to optimize for fidelity to a single ground truth, potentially leading to a lack of exploration of alternative valid futures.

3. **Architectural Trade-offs in Generative Models:**
 - **Token-based methods:** While effective at producing realistic and type-compatible motions by drawing from discrete vocabularies, these methods are inherently limited in their ability to represent fine-grained continuous variations, thereby bounding the attainable diversity. The motivation is to overcome this "codebook bottleneck."
 - **Continuous methods (e.g., diffusion models):** These models offer high expressiveness for multi-modal distributions, but they often struggle to implicitly learn valid, type-compatible motions. Generating physically implausible actions (e.g., lateral slip for vehicles) can lead to compounding errors in closed-loop simulations. The motivation is to leverage the expressiveness of continuous models while grounding them in physical realism.

4. **Addressing Covariate Shift without Sacrificing Diversity:** The deployment of open-loop trained models in closed-loop environments inevitably leads to covariate shift, causing errors to accumulate over time. Existing fine-tuning strategies to mitigate this shift often involve pulling rollouts toward observed data, which, while improving stability, may inadvertently narrow the distribution of generated behaviors by collapsing minority modes. The motivation is to develop a fine-tuning mechanism that reduces covariate shift while explicitly preserving and promoting multi-modality and diversity.

5. **Need for Explicit Diversity Metrics:** The absence of standardized, log-free diversity metrics makes it difficult to quantitatively assess a simulator's ability to generate varied futures. This paper is motivated to introduce such a metric to provide a more comprehensive evaluation alongside realism scores, enabling a clearer understanding of the realism-diversity trade-off.

In summary, the core motivation is to construct a generative simulation framework that intrinsically supports fine-grained, multi-modal, and kinematically valid agent behaviors, then fine-tune this framework to maintain its expansive behavioral repertoire under closed-loop conditions, all while providing a robust method for evaluating this joint performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Flow-ERD framework comprises two main components: Agent-Type Aware Flow Matching (AFM) as the generative backbone, and Entropy-Regularized Distillation (ERD) for diversity-preserving closed-loop fine-tuning.

**A. Agent-Type Aware Flow Matching (AFM)**

AFM is designed to leverage the multi-modal expressiveness of flow matching while ensuring that generated motions are kinematically consistent with agent types.

1. **Motivation and Design Rationale:** Token-based simulators are constrained by discrete motion primitives, limiting continuous variation. Continuous generators, while removing this bottleneck, may produce physically invalid motions in a closed-loop setting without type-aware execution. AFM addresses this by decoupling the generation of continuous kinematic actions from their physical execution, ensuring that only poses consistent with agent-type kinematics are fed back into the simulator's context.

2. **Kinematic Action-Space Flow Modeling:** For each agent *i* at time *t*, AFM models a metric kinematic action `a_i_t = (a_i_∥,t, a_i_⊥,t, a_i_ψ,t)`, representing local longitudinal, lateral, and heading increments. The flow model generates an *H*-step sequence of these continuous actions. During pretraining, the "clean endpoint" for the flow matching objective is a target action sequence `a_⋆_t:t+H-1`, derived from logged data.

3. **Type-Specific State Transition:** The core of AFM's realism enforcement is its agent-type-specific transition function, `F_c_i`, which maps an agent's current planar state `s_i_t = (p_i_t, ψ_i_t)` and a generated action `a_i_t` to its next state `s_i_t+1`.
 - **Holonomic Motion (PED):** For pedestrians, a holonomic displacement `Δp_hol` is applied, allowing both longitudinal and lateral movement in the agent's frame.
 - **Non-Holonomic Motion (VEH, CYC):** For vehicles and cyclists, a bicycle-style non-holonomic motion is used. The lateral displacement component (`a_i_⊥,t`) from the shared action vector is *not* used in the pose update for these types, enforcing a no-slip condition. The displacement `Δp_nh` depends on forward progress, heading change, and a `no-slip offset r_i = ρ_c_i * ℓ_i` (where `ℓ_i` is box length, `ρ_c_i` is a type-specific ratio).

4. **Training: Transition-Consistent Action Targets:** To supervise the action-space flow model, logged pose data must be converted into executable actions.
 - The no-slip offset ratio `ρ_c_i` for non-holonomic agents is estimated from logged turning intervals, aggregating robust statistics by agent type.
 - Target action sequences `a_i,⋆_t+k` are constructed iteratively. Starting from a previously executed state `~s_i_t`, the inverse transition `F^-1_c_i` is applied to recover the action needed to reach the next logged pose `s_i,⋆_t+k+1`. This action is then executed forward with `F_c_i` to obtain `~s_i_t+k+1`, which becomes the basis for the next action recovery. This process ensures that the training targets are consistent with the same type-specific transitions used at inference, minimizing the train-inference gap. Targets with large re-execution errors are filtered out for stability.

5. **Closed-Loop Inference:** During inference, AFM generates an *H*-step action sequence. The simulator then executes these actions using the type-specific `F_c_i` transitions. In a receding horizon manner, only the first *B* (where *B < H*) steps are committed, and their resulting poses are appended to the historical context for the next prediction cycle. This ensures that feedback to the model consists of kinematically valid, type-specific executed poses, rather than unconstrained, potentially invalid, predictions.

6. **Architecture:** The AFM architecture employs a SMART-style scene encoder to build temporal, map-agent, and agent-agent context features (`e_t`). The flow decoder takes `e_t`, a noisy *H*-step action sequence (`x_λ`), and the flow noise timestep (`λ`). It processes the sequence in *B*-step chunks, applying chunk-level self-attention with DiT-style scale-shift-gate conditioning. A step refiner then performs within-chunk self-attention, and an MLP velocity head outputs the action-space velocity field `v_θ(x_λ, λ, e_t)` for the flow matching objective.

**B. Entropy-Regularized Distillation (ERD)**

ERD fine-tunes the pretrained AFM backbone to mitigate covariate shift in closed-loop rollouts while explicitly preserving and enhancing diversity.

1. **Problem Formulation:** The goal is to align the closed-loop rollout distribution `p_CL_θ(τ | H_0)` with the data distribution `p_data(τ | H_0)`. A common approach is to minimize the reverse-KL divergence, `D_KL(p_CL_θ || p_data)`. However, reverse-KL is known to be mode-seeking, penalizing model rollouts in low-density data regions and potentially leading to mode collapse where probability mass concentrates on dominant modes, neglecting minority ones.

2. **Entropy Regularization:** To counteract mode collapse, ERD adds an entropy regularizer to the reverse-KL objective: `J_ERD(θ) = E_H0[D_KL(p_CL_θ || p_data) - γ Ent(p_CL_θ(τ | H_0))]`, where `γ ≥ 0` is the regularization weight.

3. **Tempered Target Distribution:** This objective is equivalent to minimizing an ordinary reverse-KL divergence against a tempered target distribution `p_β_data ∝ p_data(τ | H_0)^β`, where `β = 1 / (1 + γ) ∈ (0, 1]` is the temperature.
 - A smaller `β` (larger `γ`) flattens `p_β_data`, reducing the density contrast between modes and up-weighting minority modes, thereby relaxing the mode-dropping bias of reverse-KL.
 - `β = 1` (or `γ = 0`) recovers the standard reverse-KL, targeting `p_data` directly.

4. **Optimization via Distribution-Matching Distillation (DMD):** Since `log p_CL_θ` is intractable for autoregressive flow rollouts, ERD leverages Distribution-Matching Distillation (DMD) in an approach similar to Self-Forcing.
 - The KL gradient is expressed through the scores (`∇_x log p(x)`) of the action-sequence distributions.
 - The real data score is approximated by `β g_OL_θ0(x_λ, λ)`, where `g_OL_θ0` is the score of the frozen pretrained AFM backbone (which approximates `p_data` on its support). The `β` scaling implements the tempered target.
 - A "fake score" `g_CL_ϕ(x_λ, λ)` is trained on action sequences generated by the *current* closed-loop policy.
 - The generator `θ` is updated to align `g_CL_ϕ` with the tempered real score. This process, detailed in Algorithm 1, involves alternating phases of updating `ϕ` (fake-score parameters) and `θ` (generator parameters), ensuring `g_CL_ϕ` remains on-policy. The update for `θ` involves backpropagation through the flow-matching generation process.

By combining AFM's capacity for diverse yet kinematically realistic action generation with ERD's ability to fine-tune in a closed-loop context while explicitly preserving multi-modality, Flow-ERD aims to achieve state-of-the-art performance in both realism and diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Flow-ERD was conducted on the WOSAC 2025 test and validation splits, using both standard realism metrics and a novel diversity metric.

**A. Evaluation Metrics**

- **Realism:** Measured by the WOSAC Realism Meta Metric (RMM), which aggregates likelihoods over kinematic, interactive, and map-based statistics against a single logged future. Components include Kinematic (Kin.), Interactive (Int.), and Map scores. Additionally, minADE (minimum Average Displacement Error) per-object is reported for comparison.
- **Diversity:** A log-free metric called Cross-Pair Diversity (CPD) was introduced. Given *K* closed-loop rollouts for the same scenario, CPD calculates the average type-normalized pairwise distance between all unique pairs of rollouts. A higher CPD indicates more distinct generated futures for the same initial state. The authors emphasize that CPD should be compared at matched RMM values, as spurious variance from low realism could inflate diversity scores.

**B. Experimental Results**

1. **AFM Breaks the Backbone Realism–Diversity Trade-off:**
 - **WOSAC Test Split (Table I):** The AFM backbone achieved the highest overall RMM among continuous backbones and demonstrated competitive performance with strong tokenized backbones like TrajTok. Notably, AFM secured the best kinematic component score across all baselines, including fine-tuned models, indicating its effectiveness in reproducing type-specific kinematics without explicit fine-tuning.
 - **WOSAC Validation Split (Table II, Fig. 3):** AFM attained the highest CPD among all other pretrained baselines. Figure 3 illustrates that AFM dominates the realism–diversity Pareto front, outperforming other models (SMART, TrajTok, UniMM) even when their controllable parameters (e.g., top-k decoding, Gaussian noise scale) were swept. This suggests that AFM's advantage is not merely due to sampling strategy but stems from its intrinsic ability to generate more diverse, yet realistic, futures. The observed gap against UniMM indicates that flow-based modeling contributes significantly beyond just continuous representation.
 - **Ablation Study (Table II):** An ablation study on type-specific transitions confirmed their importance. An all-holonomic flow matching backbone yielded the highest raw CPD but the lowest kinematic score, attributed to type-incompatible lateral slip for wheeled agents. Conversely, an all non-holonomic backbone improved kinematic realism but over-constrained pedestrians, leading to lower RMM and CPD. AFM, by assigning appropriate holonomic or non-holonomic transitions, resolved both issues, achieving the best RMM and kinematic score among the ablations.

2. **ERD Improves Closed-Loop Realism Without Collapsing Diversity:**
 - **WOSAC Test Split (Table I):** Flow-ERD (AFM + ERD) achieved the best RMM overall among all baselines, including other fine-tuned models. This result was obtained with the vanilla reverse-KL objective (`β = 1.0`), as the leaderboard primarily evaluates realism.
 - **WOSAC Validation Split (Table II):** Even with `β = 1.0`, Flow-ERD maintained a higher CPD than all other fine-tuned baselines, suggesting that ERD effectively reshapes the probability mass within AFM's broad support rather than merely benefiting from generic fine-tuning. However, `β = 1.0` did show a reduction in CPD (`ΔCPD = 0.0174`) compared to the pretrained AFM backbone, consistent with the mode-seeking tendency of vanilla reverse-KL.
 - **Effect of Entropy Temperature `β` (Table II, Fig. 4):** Adjusting the entropy temperature `β` in ERD allowed direct control over the realism-diversity trade-off. Lowering `β` to 0.99 recovered most of the backbone's diversity (`ΔCPD = 0.003`) while retaining a substantial realism gain, outperforming other fine-tuned baselines that incurred larger CPD reductions. Further lowering `β` (e.g., to 0.95) increased diversity but at a steeper cost to realism, falling below the backbone's RMM. This indicates that `β` provides a mechanism to balance between maximizing realism (at `β = 1.0`) and preserving diversity.

3. **Preserved Diversity Reflects Intent-Level Multimodality:**
 - **Qualitative and Quantitative Intent Analysis (Fig. 5):** To confirm that the increased CPD represented meaningful behavioral diversity rather than noise, 64 rollouts were sampled across validation scenarios and ego-trajectory intents were classified.
 - Qualitatively, Fig. 5(a) shows that a baseline like SMART primarily concentrates on a dominant "straight" mode. Flow-ERD with `β = 1.0` diversified behaviors within existing intents, while lowering `β` to 0.99 (more entropy regularization) further recovered rare maneuvers such as U-turns. These behaviors were identified as semantically distinct and physically plausible, not merely noisy dispersion or closed-loop drift.
 - Quantitatively, the Shannon entropy of ego-maneuver intents increased monotonically with lower `β` (Fig. 5b), confirming that the preserved diversity corresponds to a broader range of meaningful behaviors. The slight RMM drop at lower `β` is explained by RMM's inherent bias towards the single logged ground truth, which may undervalue rare but plausible alternative modes. This highlights the inadequacy of RMM alone for assessing comprehensive simulator performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous driving simulation, primarily by addressing the long-standing challenge of jointly achieving high realism and rich diversity in multi-agent traffic environments.

1. **Advancement in Simulation Capabilities:** Flow-ERD demonstrates a method that achieves state-of-the-art realism on a prominent benchmark (WOSAC) while simultaneously providing superior diversity compared to existing approaches. This combined capability offers a more comprehensive and robust simulation platform for developing and validating AV planning policies.

2. **Bridging the Realism-Diversity Gap:** The paper effectively tackles the inherent trade-offs in generative models for traffic simulation. By coupling the continuous, multi-modal expressiveness of flow matching with agent-type-aware kinematic execution (AFM), it generates fine-grained and diverse behaviors without compromising physical realism or producing type-incompatible motions. This design choice represents an impactful advancement over both discrete token-based and naive continuous models.

3. **Robust Closed-Loop Fine-Tuning:** The introduction of Entropy-Regularized Distillation (ERD) provides a novel and effective strategy for mitigating covariate shift in closed-loop simulations. Crucially, ERD counteracts the mode-seeking tendency of standard distribution matching objectives, thereby preventing the collapse of generated behaviors onto dominant modes and explicitly preserving (or even enhancing) the simulator's multi-modality. This ensures that the benefits of diverse generation are retained even after fine-tuning for closed-loop stability.

4. **Promotion of Explicit Diversity Evaluation:** The paper's development and application of the Cross-Pair Diversity (CPD) metric highlight the necessity of log-free, quantitative measures for diversity. By demonstrating that high realism scores alone may not reflect true behavioral diversity, this work advocates for a more holistic evaluation framework that considers both realism and diversity along a Pareto front. This could influence future benchmark designs and encourage researchers to explicitly optimize for diverse outputs.

5. **Impact on Autonomous Driving Development:** More realistic and diverse simulations directly translate to more robust and safer autonomous driving systems. By exposing AV planning policies to a wider range of plausible, even rare, scenarios—including minority modes and complex interactions—Flow-ERD can help identify and mitigate potential failure points, leading to more resilient autonomous vehicles. This has the potential to accelerate the development, testing, and deployment of self-driving technology.

In conclusion, Flow-ERD represents a step forward in creating sophisticated traffic simulators that can generate both authentic and varied driving environments. Its methodology and the insights gained into the realism-diversity trade-off are likely to influence future research directions in generative models for complex multi-agent systems and their application in safety-critical domains.
