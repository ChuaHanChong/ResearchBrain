# RayRoPE: Projective Ray Positional Encoding for Multi-view Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> RayRoPE introduces a projective ray positional encoding for multi-view transformers that represents image patches as geometry-aware ray segments with unsupervised depth prediction, achieving SE(3) invariance, uniqueness, geometry-adaptiveness, and multi-frequency similarity. It demonstrates improved performance in novel-view synthesis, with a 15% relative LPIPS improvement over prior methods on CO3D, and more accurate stereo depth estimation.

## Problem

- Existing positional encodings for multi-view transformers struggle to simultaneously satisfy four critical desiderata: SE(3) invariance, uniqueness, geometry-adaptiveness, and multi-frequency similarity.
- Absolute positional encodings lack SE(3) invariance, causing attention operations to depend on arbitrary global coordinate systems.
- Camera-based relative encodings achieve SE(3) invariance but typically lack geometry-adaptiveness and multi-frequency capabilities, or compromise uniqueness when incorporating 2D pixel-based encodings.

## Method

- Represents each image patch as a ==geometry-aware ray segment== defined by the camera center and a 3D point (at a learned depth), with depth predicted unsupervisedly by a linear layer within each attention layer.
- Computes positional encodings in the local frame of the query camera using a ==projective operator==, then applies a multi-frequency ==Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)== to the 6D projected ray for SE(3) invariance and multi-frequency similarity.
- Models uncertainty in the predicted depths by having the linear layer also predict an uncertainty value, which is then used in an =='Expected RoPE' mechanism== to provide robustness against noisy depth estimations.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance in novel-view synthesis, with a **15%** relative improvement in LPIPS over PRoPE on the challenging CO3D dataset, and consistent gains in PSNR and SSIM across Objaverse and RealEstate10K.
- Demonstrated more accurate stereo depth estimation, outperforming baseline UniMatch and PRoPE variants across RGBD, SUN3D, and Scenes11 datasets by improving metrics like Abs Rel, Sq Rel, RMSE, and RMSE log.
- Ablation studies confirmed that geometry-adaptiveness, uncertainty modeling, and multi-frequency encoding are each critical components contributing to RayRoPE's superior performance in 3D tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed RayRoPE framework effectively fulfills all four critical desiderata for multi-view positional encodings: SE(3) invariance, uniqueness, geometry-adaptiveness, and multi-frequency similarity.
> - The model can implicitly learn geometrically plausible depth maps and associated uncertainties from the task objective (e.g., image reconstruction) without direct depth supervision.
> - Explicitly modeling depth uncertainty via the Expected RoPE mechanism is crucial for robust performance, particularly for high-frequency encoding components, as confirmed by ablation studies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision transformers have become a foundational component in various image processing applications, operating by treating image patches as 'tokens' and updating their representations through an 'attention' mechanism. A fundamental aspect of these models is the incorporation of positional encodings to convey spatial information, as the attention operation is inherently permutation-invariant. In single-image contexts, 2D pixel coordinates commonly define these encodings.

However, the application of transformers extends to multi-view scenarios, such as novel-view synthesis and multi-view stereo, where tokens originate from different posed images. In this multi-view setting, a simple 2D position encoding is insufficient due to the varying camera perspectives and underlying 3D geometry. The development of appropriate positional encodings for multi-view transformers is an active area of research.

Existing positional encoding schemes generally fall into two categories: absolute positional encodings (APE), which add fixed or learned embeddings to tokens, and relative positional encodings (RPE), which modify the attention mechanism to model relative positions between token pairs. Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) represent a prominent RPE approach, providing translational invariance in 1D and 2D contexts and becoming standard in various domains.

For multi-view transformers, prior methods have attempted to encode camera information using rays or camera matrices, often concatenating them with input features, akin to APE. More advanced relative positional encodings like CaPE, GTA, and PRoPE have aimed for SE(3) invariance (invariance to rigid transformations of the global coordinate system) by transforming attention features based on camera pose matrices. While these methods achieve SE(3) invariance, they generally face limitations. Specifically, they may not uniquely represent corresponding 3D points observed across different views, lack the ability to adapt to varying scene geometry, or do not support multi-frequency similarity in a geometrically grounded manner, as seen in hybrid approaches that combine camera-based RPEs with 2D RoPE on patch indices, thus breaking uniqueness.

This work directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel positional encoding mechanism, RayRoPE, designed to meet a comprehensive set of desiderata for multi-view attention, thereby advancing the capabilities of multi-view transformers in tasks requiring robust 3D understanding and synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective positional encoding mechanism for multi-view transformers that process tokens derived from a set of posed input images. The authors identify a specific set of four desirable properties that existing encoding schemes for multi-view attention do not simultaneously fulfill, and their motivation is to address this gap. These key desiderata are:

1. **SE(3) invariance**: The attention operation should be independent of the choice of a global coordinate system, meaning its output should only be affected by the relative spatial relationships between cameras, not their absolute positions or orientations in an arbitrary global frame. Prior methods like standard RoPE on 3D positions do not guarantee this.
2. **Uniqueness**: If the same physical 3D patch or point is observed across multiple input images, the positional encoding of its corresponding tokens should be identical, regardless of the specific image or its camera parameters. Many hybrid approaches that combine camera-based RPEs with 2D patch-index-based RoPE fail this, as the 2D indices (u,v) of a 3D point vary depending on the camera view.
3. **Geometry-adaptiveness**: The positional encodings should be sensitive to the underlying 3D scene geometry. Specifically, tokens that correspond to patches observing the same 3D point in space should exhibit higher positional similarity than tokens observing different 3D points, even if their 2D image coordinates or camera poses might otherwise suggest similar relative positions. Existing camera-based methods often encode positions independently of the actual scene depth.
4. **Multi-frequency similarity**: Analogous to successful 1D and 2D positional encodings (such as RoPE), the multi-view encoding should enable the computation of similarity in attention at various spatial 'frequencies.' This allows the model to reason about positional information at different scales of detail. Current camera-based RPEs typically do not intrinsically support multi-frequency encoding for camera information itself.

The motivation stems from the observation that the absence of these combined properties limits the performance and robustness of multi-view transformers in complex 3D vision tasks. By developing RayRoPE, the authors aim to provide a more principled and effective positional encoding that can enhance the ability of these models to reason about and synthesize 3D environments from multiple views.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RayRoPE is designed to satisfy the identified desiderata through a novel approach that integrates ray-based position representation, projection into the query camera frame, multi-frequency encoding with RoPE, and uncertainty modeling.

#### 3.1. Image Patch as Ray Segments

The core representation for an image patch's position in 3D is extended from a simple ray (defined by camera center `c` and ray direction `r`) to a ray segment `(c, p_d)`. Here, `p_d` is a 3D point located at a specific depth `d` along the ray emanating from the camera center `c` and passing through the patch center. This representation naturally ensures the "uniqueness" property, as a 3D point has a single representation regardless of which camera observes it.

To achieve "geometry-adaptiveness," the model is designed to *predict* the depth `d` for each token (patch) along its corresponding ray. This prediction is made without direct supervision for depth; instead, a single linear layer is added to each attention layer, projecting the input features to estimate `d`. These depth prediction layers are learned jointly with the main model. While the discussion primarily refers to one ray per patch, the practical implementation uses three rays (e.g., passing through three corners of a patch) to also encode patch orientation.

#### 3.2. Relative Encodings in Query Frame

To ensure "SE(3) invariance," RayRoPE computes positional encodings in the local coordinate frame of the query token's camera.
- **Projection**: Given a query camera matrix `P_i` and a global ray segment `x=(c, p_d)` (from any token, query or key), a projective operator `π(P_i, x)` transforms `x` into `~x = (T_i c, P_i p_d)`. `T_i c` represents the 3D camera center transformed into the query camera's own frame (which becomes the origin), and `P_i p_d` is the projection of the 3D point `p_d` onto the query camera's image plane, represented by pixel coordinates `(u,v)` and a disparity `1/d'`. This results in a 6D vector `~x` that compactly describes the projected ray.
- **RoPE Application**: Standard Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) (extended to a 6-dimensional input) is applied to this projected ray `~x`, yielding `ρ_D(~x)`. This step introduces "multi-frequency similarity," allowing the model to reason about spatial relationships at different scales.
- **Attention Mechanism**: The RayRoPE encoding `ρ_D(~x)` is incorporated into the attention calculation by applying it to the query (Q), key (K), value (V), and output (O) features. This mathematical formulation ensures that the attention score between any two tokens depends solely on their *relative* positions `~x_i - ~x_j` in the query camera frame, thereby guaranteeing SE(3) invariance. The attention process is implemented iteratively for each query camera, grouping tokens by their respective views.

#### 3.3. Modeling Uncertainty via Expected RoPE

Recognizing that the predicted depths `d` might be approximate, RayRoPE incorporates a mechanism to model uncertainty. Along with the depth `d`, the model predicts an uncertainty value `σ` for each token, also via a dedicated linear layer. This `σ` defines a range, effectively bounding the 3D point along the ray between `p_{d-σ}` and `p_{d+σ}`.

Instead of applying RoPE to a single, deterministic position, an *expected RoPE encoding* `E_~x[ρ_D(~x)]` is computed. This assumes that the projected position `~x` follows a uniform distribution within the range defined by `~x_min` (corresponding to `p_{d-σ}`) and `~x_max` (corresponding to `p_{d+σ}`). The expected SO(2) rotation for each component of the ray position is calculated analytically. For components with zero uncertainty, this simplifies to the regular RoPE. For larger uncertainties, the expected rotation becomes "smoothed out," preventing noisy high-frequency encodings. This approach also maintains the property that the relative position is preserved in the attention mechanism.

#### 3.4. Extension to Known Depths

RayRoPE can naturally integrate known depth information. For input views where ground-truth depth maps are available (e.g., RGB-D datasets), the predicted depth `d` for tokens from these views is simply replaced with the known depth `d_known`, and the uncertainty `σ` is set to zero. For views without known depth (e.g., target views in novel-view synthesis), the model continues to use its predicted `d` and `σ`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of RayRoPE was evaluated on two distinct 3D vision tasks: novel-view synthesis (NVS) and stereo depth estimation, demonstrating consistent improvements over baseline positional encoding methods.

#### 4.1. Novel-View Synthesis

- **Models and Datasets**: RayRoPE was integrated into LVSM (both a smaller variant with ~47M parameters and a larger ~150M parameter variant) and EscherNet (~800M parameters). Experiments were conducted on CO3D, Objaverse, and RealEstate10K (RE10K) for LVSM, and DL3DV for EscherNet.
- **Baselines**: Comparisons included the original Plucker raymap (LVSM's default), a naive "RoPE on rays" baseline (applying RoPE to global ray coordinates), GTA, and PRoPE (both advanced camera-based relative positional encodings). EscherNet comparisons included its original CaPE and PRoPE.
- **LVSM Results (Table 1)**: RayRoPE consistently outperformed all baselines across all three datasets and model scales. For instance, on RE10K, the large RayRoPE model achieved a PSNR of 28.31, LPIPS of 0.055, and SSIM of 0.876, surpassing the large PRoPE model (PSNR 27.77, LPIPS 0.059, SSIM 0.866). The results indicated that relative encodings generally surpassed absolute ones. The naive "RoPE on rays" baseline struggled with complex scenes (CO3D), underscoring the importance of SE(3) invariance.
- **EscherNet Results (Table 2)**: RayRoPE consistently improved performance over both the original EscherNet and its PRoPE-augmented variant across settings with 3, 6, or 9 reference views, with the performance gap widening as the number of reference views increased.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4, 11)**: Visual examples demonstrated that RayRoPE generated novel views with superior high-frequency details and better 3D consistency, even in challenging scenarios with limited overlap between reference and target views.

#### 4.2. Stereo Depth Estimation

- **Model and Datasets**: RayRoPE was applied to the cross-attention layers of UniMatch, a multi-view transformer model for depth estimation. Evaluation was performed on RGBD, SUN3D, Scenes11, and for out-of-distribution generalization, ScanNet.
- **Results (Table 3)**: RayRoPE consistently led to more accurate depth predictions, yielding lower Absolute Relative Difference (Abs Rel) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) compared to the original UniMatch and its PRoPE-augmented version, including on the unseen ScanNet dataset.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5)**: The improved quantitative metrics were supported by qualitative observations showing better geometric consistency and more accurate 3D reconstruction from the estimated depths.

#### 4.3. Ablations and Analysis

- **Ablation Study (Table 4)**: Several variants of RayRoPE were tested:
 - Removing uncertainty prediction (Variant ①: `w/o σ prediction`) led to performance degradation, particularly in datasets with larger pose variations (CO3D, Objaverse), indicating the robustness provided by uncertainty modeling.
 - Removing depth prediction (Variant ②: `p_∞ instead of p_d`) resulted in a significant performance drop, confirming the necessity of geometric adaptiveness.
 - Removing multi-frequency encoding (Variant ③: `w/o frequency`) also significantly reduced performance, similar to the limitations of prior camera-based methods.
 - Removing encoding on value and output features (Variant ④: `w/o v, o encoding`) also showed performance reduction, consistent with findings in related work.
- **Analysis on Emergent Depth (Figure 6)**: The predicted depths (`d`) and uncertainties (`σ`) demonstrated meaningful geometric information. In deeper network layers (Layers 5-6), a strong positive correlation was observed between depth prediction error and predicted uncertainty, indicating that the model assigned higher uncertainty to less reliable predictions. Furthermore, geometrically plausible depth maps emerged in deeper layers without any direct depth supervision, suggesting that RayRoPE's internal mechanisms effectively leverage depth for geometric understanding.
- **Generalization (Figure 7, Table 6, 7)**:
 - **Varying Reference Views**: RayRoPE maintained its superior performance when generalizing to a varying number of reference views (2, 4, 8, 16) at test time, even though it was trained with only two. Its advantage over baselines amplified with an increased number of reference views.
 - **Out-of-Domain**: When trained on CO3D and evaluated zero-shot on unseen domains (RE10K, Objaverse, and held-out CO3D categories), RayRoPE consistently outperformed baselines, indicating robustness to out-of-distribution scenes.
 - **Radial vs. Compound Pose Variations (Table 7)**: RayRoPE demonstrated larger performance gains for target views with purely "radial" pose variations (same viewing angles but different intrinsics/radius) compared to "compound" variations. This suggests that the multi-frequency encoding particularly benefits scenarios where rays from reference and target views overlap significantly, aiding in the transfer of fine-grained texture details.

#### 4.4. NVS with Known Depth

- **Results (Table 5)**: When provided with known ground-truth depths for reference views, RayRoPE seamlessly integrated this information, yielding more significant performance gains compared to its RGB-only counterpart and surpassing the improvements observed with PRoPE. This result highlighted RayRoPE's ability to effectively leverage available geometric information at the attention level.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RayRoPE represents a notable contribution to the field of multi-view computer vision and transformer architectures. Its significance stems from addressing a recognized gap in existing positional encoding schemes for multi-view attention, where no single method simultaneously fulfilled the desiderata of SE(3) invariance, uniqueness, geometry-adaptiveness, and multi-frequency similarity.

The consistent and substantial performance improvements demonstrated across diverse tasks (novel-view synthesis, stereo depth estimation), datasets, and model scales suggest that RayRoPE provides a more robust and principled way to embed spatial information in multi-view transformers. The method's ability to predict geometrically meaningful depths and associated uncertainties without direct supervision, and to integrate this information analytically, represents a novel mechanism for transformers to internally reason about 3D scene structure.

The strong generalization capabilities to varying numbers of reference views and out-of-domain scenes indicate that RayRoPE enhances the fundamental robustness of multi-view models. Its ability to effectively leverage known depth information further expands its applicability in settings where such data is available.

The potential impact of RayRoPE is broad, offering a direct improvement to the performance and geometric consistency of various multi-view transformer-based applications in 3D reconstruction, novel view rendering, and comprehensive 3D scene understanding. By enabling attention mechanisms to better account for relative camera poses, scene geometry, and multi-scale spatial relationships, RayRoPE contributes to the development of more capable and reliable 3D vision systems. The work also opens avenues for future research, such as extending uncertainty modeling to camera matrices or designing encodings for transformers processing unposed or mixed image inputs.
