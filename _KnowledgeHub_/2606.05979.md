# World-Language-Action Model for Unified World Modeling, Language Reasoning, and Action Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> The World-Language-Action (WLA) model introduces a unified embodied foundation model that integrates world modeling, language reasoning, and action synthesis using an autoregressive Transformer backbone. It demonstrated superior performance on complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in simulations and real-world scenarios, while achieving significantly reduced inference latency and the ability to learn novel tasks from action-free videos.

## Problem

- World-Action Models (WAMs) often focus on low-level visual prediction, hindering semantic reasoning and high-level generalization.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models excel at language understanding but frequently lack robust physical dynamics modeling due to insufficient explicit visual supervision.
- Existing approaches struggle to integrate both high-level semantic intentions and precise low-level physical dynamics within a single, efficient framework for embodied AI.

## Method

- A unified World-Language-Action (**WLA**) model is proposed, utilizing an autoregressive (AR) Transformer backbone, initialized with a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), to process multimodal inputs and generate multimodal outputs.
- The model predicts the "next state" through two complementary representations: high-level textual intentions (subtasks) and low-level physical dynamics (==latent actions==), managed by a ==World Expert== and an ==Action Expert==.
- An end-to-end joint training objective combines losses for action prediction, world modeling (future visual states), and language generation (textual subtasks), enabling implicit influence of world prediction on action generation without explicit future image predictions at test time.

## Results

- WLA-0 achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **56.5%** on the RMBench long-horizon bimanual manipulation benchmark and outperformed baselines on RoboTwin 2.0 (**92.94%**) and LIBERO (**98.6%**).
- It demonstrated an inference latency of approximately **40 ms** on an NVIDIA RTX 5090, reducing test-time latency by about 40 times compared to prior WAMs on real-world tasks.
- The model successfully learned novel tasks from action-free same-embodiment and cross-embodiment robot videos, nearly tripling baseline success rates for unseen tasks without embodied pretraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A comprehensive "next state" representation should integrate both high-level textual intention for abstract planning and low-level physical dynamics for fine-grained control.
> - An autoregressive Transformer backbone can effectively unify language reasoning (via textual subtask prediction) and physical interaction within a single, efficient embodied control loop.
> - Learning novel skills from diverse action-free robot videos, including cross-embodiment data, is feasible and can significantly reduce reliance on expensive, action-annotated datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied AI has seen a convergence of several paradigms, notably world models, world-action models (WAMs), and vision-language-action (VLA) models. This paper positions the World-Language-Action (WLA) model as an effort to integrate the strengths of these existing approaches while addressing their respective limitations.

**World Models (WMs) and World-Action Models (WAMs):**
World models aim to simulate physical dynamics, forming a basis for physical AI. More recently, WAMs have emerged by integrating these world models with embodied control. WAMs benefit from large-scale pretraining on egocentric videos, where the prediction of physical dynamics provides future-state priors to facilitate action prediction. However, current WAM methods typically focus on predicting the next visual state. This approach can burden models with low-level visual details and may restrict their capacity for semantic reasoning and high-level generalization.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:**
In parallel, VLA models leverage the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) to enable robots to follow complex textual instructions, perform high-level planning, and achieve behavioral steering. Language provides a natural interface for decomposing long-horizon tasks into structured subtasks and translating them into action sequences. Despite advances in language-guided control, existing VLA methods often face challenges in accurately capturing physical dynamics. The absence of explicit visual supervision for dynamics can lead to insufficient training signals and limit the model’s ability to interact with the physical world effectively.

**The Research Gap:**
The paper identifies a gap between WAMs, which excel at modeling physical dynamics but lack robust semantic reasoning, and VLAs, which demonstrate strong language understanding but struggle with precise physical dynamics. The WLA model is proposed to bridge this gap by unifying world modeling, language reasoning, and action synthesis within a single framework. The core insight is that a comprehensive representation of the "next state" should encompass both high-level textual intention and complementary low-level physical dynamics, allowing for both abstract planning and fine-grained control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a unified foundation model for physical AI that can concurrently manage multimodal inputs and produce multimodal outputs, thereby combining the strengths of World-Action Models (WAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLAs).

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Unified Multimodal Processing:** The model aims to process textual instructions, images, and robot states simultaneously. This contrasts with approaches that handle modalities sequentially or in isolation. The output should similarly be multimodal, including textual subtasks, subgoal images, and robot actions. This unified approach is motivated by the need for embodied agents to perceive, reason, and act in complex environments that naturally involve diverse information.
2. **Bridging High-Level Semantics and Low-Level Physics:** A central motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing WAMs (which focus on low-level visual prediction without strong semantic reasoning) and VLAs (which excel at language reasoning but may not robustly model physical dynamics). The paper proposes that the "next state" should include both:
 - **High-level textual intention:** This provides a compact, generalized, and abstract representation of future states, leveraging the capabilities of large language models for semantic understanding and planning. This allows for effective context management and long-horizon task decomposition.
 - **Low-level physical dynamics:** This captures the transitions between states, serving as a bridge between abstract intentions and fine-grained motion control. Unlike high-resolution visual states, these dynamics focus on the core information necessary for state transitions.
3. **Enabling Autoregressive Generation for Embodied Control:** The adoption of an autoregressive (AR) Transformer backbone is a deliberate design choice. Unlike bidirectional diffusion Transformers used in many WAMs, an AR Transformer natively supports text generation. This enables the model to predict textual subtasks as part of its next-state prediction, thereby integrating language reasoning directly into the control loop.
4. **Leveraging Heterogeneous Data:** The proposed WLA framework is designed to exploit diverse data sources, including image-text pairs, robot demonstrations, and egocentric videos. This flexibility is crucial for scaling up robot learning, especially through the use of cross-embodiment robot videos, even those lacking explicit action annotations. This expands the potential data pool beyond expensive, robot-specific action-annotated datasets.
5. **Achieving Efficient and Robust Robot Control:** The motivation extends to practical deployment, aiming for high inference efficiency and robust performance in real-world scenarios. This includes reducing test-time latency to enable real-time adaptation in dynamic environments and demonstrating robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) objects and scenarios. The ability to learn novel tasks from observation (action-free videos) further underscores the goal of creating more adaptable and generalizable robot policies.

In summary, the WLA model is motivated by the ambition to create embodied foundation models that are simultaneously capable of sophisticated language-based reasoning for complex tasks, accurate physical interaction with the environment, and efficient operation for real-time control, all while being trainable from diverse and scalable data sources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The World-Language-Action (WLA) model is designed as a unified foundation model for physical AI, capable of mapping multimodal inputs (images, text, robot states) to multimodal outputs (images, text, robot actions). The core of its methodology lies in an autoregressive (AR) Transformer backbone, which predicts the next state through two complementary representations: high-level textual intention and low-level physical dynamics.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The WLA architecture comprises three primary components:
1. **AR Transformer Backbone:** This is the central component, initialized with a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM). Unlike WAMs that typically use bidirectional diffusion Transformers, WLA’s AR Transformer is designed for sequential generation, facilitating textual outputs. It processes the current observation ($o_t$), a historical observation ($o_{t-h}$), the proprioceptive state ($q_t$), and the instruction ($\ell$), along with a memory buffer ($M$).
2. **World Expert ($f_{wm}$):** This component is responsible for predicting future visual states.
3. **Action Expert ($f_{act}$):** This component generates executable robot actions.

#### 3.2. Textual Intention Learning

For robot control, high-level intentions are conceptualized as textual subtasks derived from the original user instructions. The AR Transformer backbone ($f$) is trained to predict a contiguous subtask window $S_t = \{\hat{\ell}_k^t, \dots, \hat{\ell}_k^{t+n}\}$ that covers the upcoming action horizon $[t, t+n]$. This prediction is based on the current and historical visual observations, the main instruction, and a memory buffer $M$ which accumulates past subtasks, aiding in long-horizon task management. This leverages the VLM’s language modeling abilities and contextual representations.

#### 3.3. Physical Dynamics Modeling

To incorporate physical dynamics, WLA introduces a set of meta-queries ($Q$) that are appended to the AR backbone’s context. These meta-queries aggregate contextual information through causal attention, and their outputs define the physical dynamics $h_t$. This $h_t$ is conceived as a compact representation capturing core visual transitions, referred to as latent action.

The **World Expert** ($f_{wm}$) then uses this physical dynamics representation $h_t$ along with the representation of the current state $o_t$ (e.g., from the VLM's vision encoder) to predict the future visual state $o_{t+n}$. Notably:
- $f_{wm}$ predicts the VAE (Variational Autoencoder) feature representation of $o_{t+n}$, rather than raw pixels or high-level semantic features, allowing $h_t$ to remain compact.
- $f_{wm}$ is designed to predict only the target future frame $o_{t+n}$, not an entire video clip, based on experimental evidence that full-clip supervision can hinder training efficiency without performance gains.
- The World Expert can optionally predict depth maps alongside $o_{t+n}$ for enhanced spatial supervision.

The physical dynamics $h_t$ also serves as a conditioning signal for the **Action Expert** ($f_{act}$), which generates the $n$-step action chunk $a_{t:t+n}$ based on $h_t$ and the current proprioceptive state $q_t$. This design allows the world modeling objective to implicitly influence action generation via shared parameter learning during training, without requiring explicit future image predictions at test time. This is a departure from traditional "image-then-act" WAMs.

#### 3.4. Training Objective

The model undergoes end-to-end joint training using a combined loss function:
$L = L_{act} + \alpha L_{wm} + \beta L_{lang}$
- $L_{lang}$: A cross-entropy loss for the textual subtask generation, guiding the prediction of high-level intentions.
- $L_{wm}$: A flow-matching loss for the world modeling objective, supervising the World Expert's prediction of future visual states.
- $L_{act}$: A flow-matching loss for action prediction, guiding the Action Expert to produce executable robot actions.
Here, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are hyperparameters weighting the auxiliary world-modeling and language losses, respectively.

#### 3.5. Inference Optimizations

The paper proposes two modes for inference:

- **Efficient Mode:** By default, the World Expert ($f_{wm}$) is entirely disabled during inference. This is possible because the world prediction influences action generation through implicit parameter updates during training, not explicit conditioning at test time. To further improve deployment efficiency, WLA-0 incorporates several acceleration techniques:
 - **CUDA Graph Capture:** Replaces eager execution with pre-captured CUDA graphs to minimize Python dispatch and kernel-launch overheads.
 - **Operator Fusion:** Implements custom Triton kernels to fuse frequently adjacent operations, reducing launch overhead and intermediate memory traffic.
 - **Precomputation and Caching:** Invariant quantities (e.g., token embeddings, causal masks, RoPE tables, action-time encodings) are precomputed and cached.
 These optimizations collectively reduce inference latency to approximately 40 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090.

- **Test-Time Scaling (TTS) Mode:** When additional computation is available, WLA can activate this mode to enhance action prediction. In TTS, the model samples $K$ candidate action chunks. For each candidate, the World Expert predicts the corresponding future static frame. A separate value model then scores these imagined future states. WLA selects and executes the action chunk associated with the highest predicted value, enabling the rejection of potentially failing trajectories in an imagined space. The imagination horizon can be extended by recursively using predicted future frames as inputs. The value model is trained using rollouts from a fine-tuned WLA-0, associating discounted value labels with predicted future frames.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The WLA-0 prototype, with 2 billion active parameters, demonstrates performance across simulated and real-world environments, along with favorable inference efficiency and novel learning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Simulation Environment Evaluations

- **RoboTwin 2.0:**
 - WLA-0 achieved a 92.94% success rate on clean environments and maintained robustness under strong domain randomization, performing comparably to or exceeding state-of-the-art World-Action Models (WAMs).
 - It accomplished this with 2 billion active parameters during inference and without requiring embodied pretraining, in contrast to larger, pre-trained baselines.
 - An ablation study where the World Expert loss was removed ("$-L_{wm}$") resulted in a decreased average success rate (90.98% vs. 92.94% on clean scenes), indicating that the future-state prediction component effectively refines action generation.

- **LIBERO:**
 - WLA-0 achieved an average success rate of 98.6% across all four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), outperforming all evaluated WAM and VLA baselines.
 - The model demonstrated training efficiency, reaching strong performance after only 30,000 training steps.
 - Activating the Test-Time Scaling (TTS) mode with 6 candidates and an imagination horizon of 2 further improved the average success rate to 98.9%.
 - Preliminary experiments showed that single-frame future prediction for the World Expert ($o_{t+n}$) yielded higher success rates (98.2% average) compared to multi-frame prediction ($o_{t:t+n}$), suggesting that overly dense visual supervision might impede action learning.

- **RMBench:**
 - On RMBench, a benchmark for long-horizon, memory-dependent bimanual manipulation, WLA-0 achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of 56.5%.
 - This performance substantially surpassed Fast-WAM (13.3%) and nearly doubled Mem-0 (28.5%), highlighting the benefits of integrating language-level subtask reasoning and explicit memory tracking with WAM-style action generation.
 - An ablation ("$-L_{lang}$") without the language-based subtask prediction loss saw the average success rate drop significantly from 56.5% to 17.25%, confirming the importance of semantic-level textual subtasks for long-horizon progress tracking and action generation in memory-dependent tasks.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Evaluations on four real-world bimanual manipulation tasks (Unscrew Cap, Pack Object, Stack Cup, Dispose Trash) were conducted under standard, out-of-distribution (OOD) object, and OOD scenario settings.

- **Success Count Comparison:** WLA-0, trained from scratch, performed comparably to pre-trained baselines ($\pi_{0.5}$ and Motus). It demonstrated robust generalization across OOD settings despite lacking pretraining. WLA-0 achieved the highest success rate on the dynamic Dispose Trash task, which involved grasping objects and placing them into a rotating bin. This performance was attributed to its integration of historical context and future state prediction for accurate environmental modeling, combined with low inference latency.

- **Inference Efficiency Evaluation:** WLA-0 exhibited the lowest task completion time and inference latency on the Stack Cup task. It reduced inference latency by approximately 40 times relative to Motus and consistently outperformed the VLA baseline $\pi_{0.5}$. WLA-0 achieved about 40 ms inference latency on an RTX 5090, underscoring its suitability for real-time deployment in latency-sensitive control tasks.

#### 4.3. Learning New Tasks from Videos

Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, partitioning them into 45 seen and 5 unseen tasks, demonstrated WLA-0’s capability to learn novel skills from action-free videos.

- **Same-Embodiment Video Learning:** When provided with video supervision from unseen tasks under the same robot embodiment ("+Unseen Same-Emb. Video"), WLA-0 nearly tripled the baseline success rate, achieving the best overall results. It successfully acquired novel actions, such as the "beat" action in "Beat Block Hammer," solely from video observations, while the baseline struggled.

- **Cross-Embodiment Video Learning:** The model maintained a competitive success rate when using video supervision from unseen tasks performed by a cross-embodiment robot ("+Unseen Cross-Emb. Video"). This indicates WLA-0’s ability to align visual observations with actionable control knowledge and demonstrated robust cross-embodiment transfer and task generalization.

- **Human Egocentric Videos:** Attempts to learn new tasks from human egocentric videos did not yield positive results, attributed to the domain gap between real-world human videos and the simulation environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of World-Language-Action (WLA) models represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied AI, offering a unified framework that integrates world modeling, language reasoning, and action synthesis.

**Unified Framework for Embodied Intelligence:** The primary significance of WLA is its capacity to concurrently address three critical aspects of embodied intelligence: understanding the physical world (world modeling), interpreting and executing complex instructions (language reasoning), and performing physical interactions (action synthesis). By using an autoregressive Transformer backbone to predict both textual subtasks (semantic level) and physical dynamics (fine-grained), WLA moves beyond the limitations of World-Action Models (WAMs) that primarily focus on low-level visual prediction and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that may lack robust physical dynamics understanding. This unification suggests a path towards more comprehensive and versatile robot policies.

**Enhanced Performance in Complex Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate WLA-0’s ability to achieve strong multi-task performance and set new benchmarks on long-horizon, memory-dependent manipulation tasks like RMBench. This indicates that the integration of explicit language-based planning and memory mechanisms, alongside robust physical modeling, is effective for tasks requiring sequential reasoning, error correction, and long-term state tracking. Such capabilities are crucial for deploying robots in real-world scenarios that involve intricate procedures and dynamic decision-making.

**Improved Efficiency for Real-time Control:** The design of WLA-0 prioritizes inference efficiency. By decoupling the World Expert from the action generation at test time, and leveraging various acceleration techniques (CUDA Graph Capture, Operator Fusion, Precomputation and Caching), WLA-0 achieves low latency (~40 ms). This enables real-time adaptation in dynamic environments, a critical requirement for practical robot control where rapid reactions to environmental changes are essential. The significantly reduced inference latency compared to prior WAMs highlights its practical applicability.

**Scalable Learning from Heterogeneous Data:** A substantial potential impact lies in WLA-0's demonstrated ability to learn novel tasks from action-free robot videos, including those from cross-embodiment robots. This capability could dramatically reduce the reliance on expensive, action-annotated robot demonstration datasets. By leveraging passive video observations, WLA offers a promising direction for more scalable and data-efficient robot skill acquisition, potentially enabling robots to learn a broader range of skills from diverse and readily available video data.

**Steerability and Generalization:** The model's capacity for language-guided reasoning provides a natural interface for human interaction, allowing for more intuitive task specification and higher-level steering. Its robustness to out-of-distribution objects and scenarios in real-world experiments, despite lacking extensive pretraining, indicates improved generalization capabilities.

**Future Directions for Physical AI:** WLA contributes to the vision of developing generalist foundation models for physical AI. While limitations such as the domain gap with human egocentric videos exist, the approach lays groundwork for future research into better aligning diverse visual data sources and expanding evaluations across a wider range of robots and task domains. The concept of integrating semantic and physical understanding within a single, efficient framework could influence the design of future embodied agents, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous robots.
