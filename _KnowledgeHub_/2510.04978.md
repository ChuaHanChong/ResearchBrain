# Aligning Perception, Reasoning, Modeling and Interaction: A Survey on Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sun Yat-sen University and collaborators present a comprehensive survey of Physical AI, proposing a unified framework that delineates its progression through physical perception, reasoning, world modeling, and embodied interaction. The work analyzes existing deep learning systems' ability to leverage physics, identifying a persistent gap in causal understanding and outlining future directions for more robust AI.

## Problem

- Current AI systems lack genuine physical understanding, often struggling with basic physical reasoning tasks that humans easily perform.
- AI models primarily learn statistical correlations from data, failing to develop causal knowledge based on physical principles.
- The fragmented approach in existing literature examines physical understanding dimensions in isolation, hindering a holistic view of Physical AI progression.

## Method

- A comprehensive, structured literature review synthesizes over 300 papers on Physical AI, organized around a novel taxonomy.
- A hierarchical taxonomy is proposed, organizing physics-aware AI capabilities into four interconnected stages: physical perception, physics reasoning, world modeling, and embodied interaction.
- The survey systematically analyzes the interconnections and synergistic relationships between these stages, demonstrating a unified developmental pathway for physical AI.

## Results

- A systematic framework outlining the progression of physical AI through perception, reasoning, modeling, and interaction was established.
- Analysis revealed that current state-of-the-art AI models frequently exhibit modeling flaws and misinterpretations when tackling complex physics problems.
- The work identified an increasing trend towards hybrid "Neural Physics" approaches that integrate explicit physical laws with neural network learning capabilities to overcome limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical AI development follows a unified pathway from perception to reasoning, modeling, and embodied interaction, where each stage enhances the next.
> - Current AI models primarily leverage statistical correlations and pattern recognition, lacking genuine causal physical understanding.
> - Achieving robust physical intelligence requires AI systems to internalize the compositional and causal structure of physical laws rather than just statistical regularities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of artificial intelligence systems with a robust understanding of the physical world represents a significant and fundamental challenge in contemporary AI research. While specialized AI models have achieved high performance in tasks such as image classification, they frequently exhibit limitations in basic physical reasoning, a capability that humans acquire effortlessly from early childhood. This discrepancy suggests that current AI models often learn statistical correlations from extensive datasets rather than developing causal knowledge grounded in physical principles. This gap is particularly critical as AI systems are increasingly deployed in real-world scenarios, including autonomous vehicles and humanoid robotics, where physical comprehension is essential for safe and effective operation.

Existing literature reviews have addressed individual aspects of physical understanding within AI, such as world models or embodied intelligence, in isolation. This survey distinguishes itself by providing a unified perspective that connects these capabilities as an integrated developmental pathway. It focuses on how deep learning systems can leverage the laws of physics to solve problems in an end-to-end manner, rather than examining physics-inspired neural network architectures or the broader field of deep learning for physics research. The paper situates its analysis within a broader paradigm shift in physical AI, moving from disparate, task-specific solutions towards integrated architectures that aim to evolve from basic pattern recognition to sophisticated causal reasoning and counterfactual prediction. This approach is informed by recent advancements in graph neural networks, diffusion models, transformer architectures, physics-guided training methodologies, and particularly, the emergence of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that demonstrate sophisticated reasoning capabilities across multiple modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the observed disparity between human and AI capabilities in understanding the physical world. Humans naturally grasp complex physical interactions, whereas current AI models often fail at basic physical reasoning tasks. This limitation, often evidenced by models learning statistical correlations rather than causal knowledge, poses significant risks for AI deployment in real-world applications such as autonomous systems and robotics. Existing evaluation frameworks consistently highlight this deficiency, indicating a need for AI systems capable of structured understanding of physical laws.

Given this context, the key objectives of this research include:

- **Systematic Framework Development:** To establish a systematic framework that elucidates the progression of physical AI through four interconnected stages: physical perception, physics reasoning, world modeling, and embodied interaction. This framework aims to reveal how these capabilities form a unified developmental pathway.
- **Analysis of Interconnectedness:** To analyze how each stage of physical understanding—perception, reasoning, modeling, and interaction—enables and enhances the subsequent stages. For instance, perceptual grounding is hypothesized to support causal reasoning, which in turn unlocks predictive capabilities, with robust models ultimately driving genuine physical interaction.
- **Synthesis of Developments:** To synthesize advancements across various domains, including architectural innovations (e.g., GNNs, diffusion models, transformers), training methodologies (e.g., physics-guided loss functions, curriculum learning, reinforcement learning with physics-informed rewards), causal inference techniques, and embodied systems. The goal is to illustrate how a comprehensive physical understanding emerges from the cumulative integration across these progressions.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation AI:** To provide foundational insights and a structured perspective for the development of next-generation physical AI systems. This includes direct implications for fostering safe, generalizable, and interpretable AI deployments in critical areas like robotics, scientific discovery, and autonomous operations.
- **Comprehensive Taxonomy:** To present a three-tier taxonomy that systematically organizes relevant research. This taxonomy categorizes capabilities into hierarchical task structures and offers a detailed methodological analysis for each, distinguishing itself from prior surveys that have examined these dimensions in isolation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology primarily involved an extensive literature review and the synthesis of findings to construct a novel organizational framework for Physical AI.

**Literature Identification and Screening:**
The process adhered to PRISMA guidelines for systematic reviews, involving several stages of identification, screening, eligibility assessment, and inclusion.
- **Initial Retrieval:** An initial pool of 331,482 records was collected between January 2012 and March 2026 from OpenAlex (targeting over 100 relevant venues) and arXiv (covering cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.CV, cs.RO, cs.HC, cs.SY, and physics categories). This dual-source approach aimed for maximal coverage of both published and emerging research.
- **Automated Preprocessing and Screening:** Records underwent multi-stage automated filtering. This included identifier- and title-level deduplication (with a 0.90 similarity threshold), heuristic screening for title/abstract relevance, taxonomy-aligned topic matching (minimum topic score 0.1), abstract availability checks, year-adaptive citation thresholds, and a venue-quality filter (removing 39 blocked-venue records). Furthermore, DeepSeek-V3.2, an LLM, was employed to screen paper titles, abstracts, and main texts for alignment with the survey's defined capabilities and tasks. A manual audit of 500 records indicated an 88% agreement rate with human judgments, validating the LLM's utility for large-scale triage.
- **Manual Eligibility Assessment:** The remaining approximately 500 records underwent manual review of titles, abstracts, and full texts. Papers were assessed for substantive methodological, benchmark, or system-level contributions to the defined taxonomy. Exclusion criteria included being out of scope for Physical AI, lacking sufficient technical detail, primarily describing products or demos without reproducible methodological content, duplicating more appropriate archival versions, or an inability to reliably map to the taxonomy. Borderline cases were resolved through detailed full-text review of principal output, evaluation setting, and archival version.
- **Citation-based Supplementation:** Additional references were incorporated through manual citation chaining, benchmark-completion passes, and targeted supplementation of official technical reports for influential systems already discussed.
Ultimately, 325 studies were included in the review.

**Proposed Framework and Formalization:**
The core of the methodology is the development of a novel three-tier taxonomy organized around four interconnected capabilities: Physical Perception, Physics Reasoning, World Modeling, and Embodied Interaction.
- **Output-Centric Organization:** These capabilities are defined by the principal type of output an AI system produces when interacting with physical environments. This output-centric view ensures applicability across diverse model families.
- **Hierarchical Structure:**
 - **Capability Level:** The four main domains (Perception, Reasoning, Modeling, Interaction) represent broad categories reflecting cognitive progression from passive observation to active intervention.
 - **Task Families:** Each capability is decomposed into task families (e.g., Object Recognition, Spatial Perception under Physical Perception).
 - **Subtasks:** Representative subtasks are further detailed within each task family.
- **Formal Definitions:** Each capability is rigorously formalized within a partially observed controlled dynamical system context, using unified notation for latent physical state ($s_t$), observation ($o_t$), action ($a_t$), language instruction ($l$), query ($q$), interaction history ($h_t$), and task-relevant physical laws ($\kappa$). This formalization precisely delimits the scope of each capability:
 - **Physical Perception:** Infers current task-relevant physical states or structures ($x_t$) from observations ($p_{\theta}(x_t | h_t)$).
 - **Physics Reasoning:** Applies, composes, or discovers physical laws to solve physics tasks, producing answers, derivations, or discovered laws ($p_{\theta}(y_R_t, r_t, \kappa | h_t, q)$).
 - **World Modeling:** Constructs a physically coherent model for generation, reconstruction, prediction, or simulation, either of current states ($p_{\theta}(\hat{s}_t, \hat{o}_t | h_t, l)$) or future rollouts ($p_{\theta}(\hat{s}_{t+1:t+H}, \hat{o}_{t+1:t+H} | h_t, a_{t:t+H-1}, l)$).
 - **Embodied Interaction:** Selects executable interventions in a physical environment (${\pi_{\theta}}(a_t | s_t, l)$ or ${\pi_{\theta}}(a_t | h_t, l)$).
- **Separation of Concerns:** Architectural choices, benchmark suites, and application domains are treated as auxiliary descriptors rather than primary taxonomic axes to improve interpretability and avoid conflating objectives with implementation details.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents a detailed analysis of advancements and limitations across its four proposed capabilities, providing both positive and negative evidence for a progressive pathway toward integrated Physical AI.

**A. Progressive Pathway and Interdependency:**
The core thesis of the survey is that Physical AI develops through a progression of capabilities.
- **Positive Evidence:** Existing research demonstrates that later capabilities often build upon earlier ones. Multimodal systems like the ICML 2025 physics challenge solution and Physics Supernova show how perception of scene structure supports multi-step physical inference. World modeling approaches (PhysORD, LagNetViP, MoSim) indicate that predictive stability improves when dynamics are constrained by explicit physical structures. Furthermore, embodied interaction systems (SAIN, X-MOBILITY, DrivingGPT, OccLLaMA) leverage predictive latent states or internal rollouts to facilitate control, navigation, and planning.
- **Negative Evidence:** Despite these interdependencies, mastering one capability does not automatically guarantee generalization to subsequent stages. For example, models strong in synthetic video perception may struggle with human-grounded causal reasoning (CLEVRER-Humans). Similarly, partial interaction competence in simplified environments does not ensure robust multi-step intervention without strong predictive modeling (I-PHYRE). Current video generation and world models often achieve perceptual plausibility but underperform on physics benchmarks or real experiments, indicating a disconnect between visual convincingness and physical fidelity (PhysicsIQ, Morpheus). This suggests that while a progressive pathway exists, significant challenges remain in ensuring seamless integration and transfer of understanding.

**B. Findings within Each Capability:**

1. **Physical Perception:**
 - This stage transforms sensory data into structured physical representations, forming the foundation for higher-level understanding.
 - **Object Recognition:** Models show robust zero-shot detection and fine-grained classification (GPT-4V, Qwen3-Omni). However, recognizing physically anomalous configurations (Phys-AD) remains challenging.
 - **Spatial Perception:** Progress has been made in 2D spatial reasoning, but models often struggle with complex 3D spatial concepts, precise metric grounding, and cross-view consistency due to weak geometric grounding (MMSI-Bench).
 - **Intrinsic Property Estimation:** Inferring properties like mass, viscosity, rigidity, and optical characteristics from visual data is complex due to indirect observability. While material and texture identification show promise (Intrinsic Img Diff., Fabric Characterization), open-world generalization for mass and rigidity estimation remains fragile.
 - **Dynamic Estimation:** Captures how objects behave and interact over time. Graph-based methods (Interaction Networks, Neural Physics Engine) are prominent for inferring object relations and interaction structures. Recent work extends this to inferring dynamics from weaker visual evidence and preserving interaction structure using object-centric video models. Benchmarks like Physion++ highlight that models still struggle with online inference of latent physical properties and transfer to intervention-heavy scenarios.

2. **Physics Reasoning:**
 - This stage moves from merely identifying physical properties to understanding *why* phenomena occur, connecting abstract physical concepts with concrete observations.
 - **Symbolic Reasoning:** LLMs with Chain-of-Thought prompting, reinforcement learning, and multi-agent paradigms (Physics Supernova, LOCA-R) have improved performance on textbook and olympiad physics problems. However, performance remains brittle for multi-step derivations and out-of-distribution problems (PHYBench, GPQA), with external knowledge graphs aiding decomposition.
 - **Multimodal-grounded Reasoning:** Tasks requiring visual perception and diagram comprehension are addressed by models like Dynamic Concept Learner and TRACE. The main bottleneck is often the weak coupling between grounded perception and structured physical reasoning, leading to performance drops on vision-essential questions (SeePhys).
 - **Causal and Counterfactual Reasoning:** This involves inferring hidden mechanisms and predicting outcomes under interventions. Causal graph modeling, intervention-based learning, and differentiable physics approaches support mechanistic inference. However, models show a substantial gap compared to human causal judgments (CLEVRER-Humans), struggling with complex causal structures and principle-first reasoning (PhySense).
 - **Accelerate Physics Research:** Data-driven approaches like Symbolic Regression aim to discover physical laws, often incorporating unit consistency and domain priors. Multi-agent frameworks (AI Scientist, AtomAgents) automate hypothesis generation, experiment execution, and analysis. While feasible in narrow, tool-rich settings, robust open-ended scientific autonomy remains unsolved (ScienceAgentBench, SciCode).

3. **World Modeling:**
 - This capability enables AI systems to generate, understand, and predict the dynamics of simulated environments.
 - **Image Generation:** Physically Based Rendering (PBR) and neural rendering approaches (NeRFs, ENVIDR) produce visually convincing and physically consistent images, establishing a visual foundation for temporal modeling. Evaluation primarily focuses on fidelity and consistency.
 - **Video Generation:** Diffusion models (GPT4Motion, PhysGen, VLIPP) are progressively constrained by physics laws to produce visually coherent and physically plausible video sequences. However, a significant gap persists between visual fluency and physical consistency (PhysicsIQ, Morpheus).
 - **Scene Reconstruction:** Beyond static geometry, modern methods (PhyRecon, IDR, DriveDreamer4D) aim to reconstruct physical properties and dynamic behaviors, leading to physically accurate and visually realistic scenes. Evaluation is still largely dominated by geometric quality, with less standardization for physics-aware reconstruction.
 - **Physics-constrained Simulation:** The core challenge is the insufficient out-of-distribution physical reasoning of existing world models, which tend to rely on "case-based" rather than "rule-based" generalization. Methodologies include neuro-symbolic integration (SAIN), physics-structured neural ODEs (MoSim), and differentiable physics engines (FusionForce) to merge explicit physical computations with neural learning. These methods aim to improve counterfactual reasoning, novel interaction modeling, and generalization under new constraints.

4. **Embodied Interaction:**
 - This stage represents the critical transition from virtual simulation to active manipulation in the real world.
 - **Robotics:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (Gato, RT-1/2, π0, OpenVLA) bridge internal world representations with external physical reality. Challenges include generating continuous actions that respect physical constraints (Diffusion Policy, flow matching), achieving cross-platform generalization (Open X-Embodiment), and robust perceptual grounding from noisy real-world observations. World Action Models demonstrate potential for zero-shot policies and enhanced closed-loop control.
 - **Navigation:** LLM and VLM-based approaches (NavGPT, NavCoT) improve navigation precision and interpretability by incorporating common sense and physical knowledge. However, they are susceptible to hallucinations and representational bottlenecks when converting rich physical data to tokens. Integrating predictive world-model components (X-MOBILITY, TWIST) is crucial for simulating action outcomes and bridging the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** This domain integrates perception, prediction, planning, and control. It has evolved from rule-based to learning-based, world models (DrivingGPT, OccLLaMA), and hybrid approaches. World models are vital for addressing the "long tail" problem of rare, safety-critical edge cases by enabling "mental rehearsal" in learned simulations, thereby enhancing predictive autonomy and safety. Evaluation is multi-level, focusing on closed-loop planning, safety, and sim-to-real reliability.

**C. Discussion on Remaining Challenges:**
- **Sim-to-real Gap:** Current world models often prioritize perceptual plausibility over physical consistency, leading to fragility in real-world deployment. The survey emphasizes treating real-world feedback as an essential learning component, allowing interaction to supervise earlier perceptual and predictive stages.
- **Internalizing Laws of Nature:** A fundamental limitation is AI systems' reliance on pattern matching rather than internalizing compositional, causal physical structures. This hinders generalization to counterfactual scenarios. Future architectures must encode physical laws explicitly (e.g., differentiable physics engines, neuro-symbolic systems) or acquire intuition through active, embodied intervention to overcome inductive biases and achieve genuine physical understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey contributes a novel and systematic framework for understanding the progression of Physical AI, articulating it as a four-stage pathway from perception to reasoning, modeling, and embodied interaction. This framework offers a foundational perspective that helps to organize the vast and diverse research landscape, moving beyond fragmented analyses of individual capabilities.

The primary significance lies in its clear identification of the interdependencies and cumulative nature of these capabilities, suggesting that robust physical intelligence requires their tight integration rather than isolated development. By highlighting both the successes and the persistent challenges—particularly the "sim-to-real gap" and the limitation of pattern matching over genuine causal understanding—the survey provides a critical diagnosis of current AI capabilities.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Guiding Research Directions:** The proposed framework serves as a roadmap for future research, advocating for hybrid approaches that integrate physics-grounded architectures, physics-informed training methodologies, and symbolic reasoning within unified frameworks. This emphasis on integration and causal understanding is crucial for overcoming current limitations.
- **Enhancing AI Robustness and Safety:** By underscoring the need for AI systems to internalize the compositional and causal structures of physical laws, the survey points towards pathways for developing more robust, generalizable, and interpretable AI. This directly impacts the safety and reliability of AI applications in critical domains like robotics, autonomous driving, and industrial automation.
- **Advancing Scientific Discovery:** The discussion on accelerating physics research through AI agents capable of hypothesis generation and streamlined experimentation highlights the potential for AI to become a transformative tool in scientific inquiry, moving beyond data analysis to contribute to the discovery of novel physical laws.
- **Fostering Embodied Intelligence:** The detailed analysis of embodied interaction underscores the importance of real-world feedback and active manipulation for grounding AI's physical understanding, suggesting that interaction should not merely be a deployment target but a supervisory mechanism for learning.

In conclusion, this survey provides a structured and critical perspective on the current state of Physical AI, offering a conceptual foundation and clear directions for building next-generation AI systems that can genuinely perceive, reason about, model, and interact with physical reality.
