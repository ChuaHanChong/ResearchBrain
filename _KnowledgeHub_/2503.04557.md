# Learning Generalizable Language-Conditioned Cloth Manipulation from Long Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua and NUS developed a decomposition-based pipeline that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) for autonomously discovering basic cloth manipulation skills and for high-level task planning. The system demonstrated superior generalization to unseen multi-step tasks in both simulation and real-world robot experiments, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable objects like cloth presents challenges due to high-dimensional state spaces, complex non-linear dynamics, and the irreversibility of errors.
- Existing learning-based methods often learn task-specific policies that struggle to generalize effectively to novel or unseen multi-step manipulation tasks.
- There is a lack of universally defined basic skills or readily available single-step skill datasets specifically for cloth manipulation, creating a gap between high-level planning and low-level execution.

## Method

- Utilizes an LLM, specifically GPT-4o with few-shot and Chain-of-Thought prompting, to autonomously decompose long multi-step demonstrations into a dataset of language-conditioned 'pick' and 'place' basic skills.
- Trains a Transformer-based model (Model A) on the discovered basic skills, learning to infer precise 'pick' or 'place' target points on the cloth from current depth observations and single-step language instructions.
- Employs the same LLM as a high-level task planner to decompose new, unseen multi-step tasks into a sequence of the learned basic skills, which are then sequentially executed by Model A.

## Results

- GPT-4o (LLM mode) successfully decomposed complex multi-step demonstrations into basic skills with **100%** accuracy on all tested tasks.
- In simulation, the proposed method achieved 98-**100%** success rates on unseen multi-step cloth manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming baselines (0-**74%**).
- Real-world experiments demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer, achieving **100%** success on 'unseen' tasks like 'Half folding (vertical) Trousers' and 'Corner folding Square' with a Franka robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex, multi-step deformable object manipulation into a sequence of simpler, reusable basic skills significantly improves generalization.
> - Large Language Models can effectively serve a dual role in robotics: autonomously discovering basic skills from implicit information in long demonstrations and performing high-level task planning for novel sequences.
> - Learning policies for language-conditioned basic actions simplifies the learning problem and provides a robust framework for composing solutions to previously unseen manipulation challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects, particularly cloth, represents a significant challenge in robotics due to their high-dimensional state spaces and complex, non-linear dynamics. Early approaches to multi-step cloth manipulation relied on model-based methods, employing heuristic policies and task-specific grippers often based on physical models. These methods typically suffered from a lack of generalization capabilities and susceptibility to environmental distractions.

More recently, learning-based methods have advanced, enabling robots to learn actions directly from expert demonstrations. However, a prevalent limitation of many such methods is their tendency to learn task-specific policies, which struggle to generalize effectively to novel or unseen manipulation tasks. Some advancements have introduced language conditioning and semantic keypoints to improve generalizability for relatively short-horizon tasks, but these are often still constrained to task-specific action sequences.

The integration of foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has emerged as a promising direction in robotic manipulation. These models have been leveraged for various functions, including generating executable code, formulating high-level plans, and providing commonsense knowledge for classical planners. While these foundation models have demonstrated significant potential for generalization and semantic understanding, particularly for rigid objects, they often require fine-tuning for complex, multi-step tasks and are not yet robust enough for direct deployment in deformable object manipulation. Existing LLM-based approaches in robot manipulation often focus on recomposing primitive actions.

Skill discovery from demonstrations is another related research area, aiming to identify reusable and modular sub-skills from existing datasets. Traditional unsupervised methods, have faced limitations in their segmented understanding of skills, impacting reusability. Recent work has explored leveraging the semantic knowledge of LLMs for skill discovery, which enhances the semantic diversity of discovered skills and simplifies their utilization through natural language. However, a gap remains in the availability of single-step basic skill datasets specifically for cloth manipulation.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenge of generalizable multi-step cloth manipulation. It seeks to overcome the limitations of task-specific learning and the current shortcomings of foundation models in deformable object manipulation by introducing a novel decomposition-based pipeline. This pipeline leverages LLMs for autonomous basic skill discovery from existing long demonstrations and for high-level task planning, allowing for the composition of learned basic skills to address unseen multi-step cloth manipulation tasks. The approach aims to bridge the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level action execution in complex deformable object manipulation, building upon advancements in language conditioning and skill discovery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a learning method for generalizable cloth manipulation that can effectively perform on unseen multi-step manipulation tasks.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges inherent in multi-step cloth manipulation for robots:
1. **High-dimensional state spaces and complex dynamics:** The deformable nature of cloth results in high-dimensional state spaces and non-linear dynamics, making the cloth's state difficult to predict over multiple manipulation steps. Small variations in interaction can lead to significantly different cloth behaviors.
2. **Irreversibility of errors:** A single incorrect action during multi-step cloth manipulation can cause the cloth to crumple or enter an undesirable state that is difficult or impossible to recover from, necessitating precise action sequences.
3. **Limitations of existing methods:**
 - **Heuristic/model-based policies:** Early methods lacked generalization and struggled with environmental distractions.
 - **End-to-end imitation learning:** While showing advancements, most contemporary learning-based methods train task-specific policies that do not generalize to novel tasks or conditions.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a core insight: **decomposition**. Unlike rigid objects that have well-defined basic actions (e.g., grasping, moving, placing), cloth manipulation lacks a universally defined set of basic skills, leading to a significant gap between abstract task planning and concrete action execution.

Therefore, the specific objectives and motivations for the proposed pipeline are:
- **To autonomously discover basic skills from long demonstrations:** Motivated by the absence of readily available basic skill datasets for cloth manipulation, the research aims to leverage the language reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically break down complex, long demonstrations (e.g., "fold the t-shirt for storage") into elementary, generalizable "pick" and "place" instructions (e.g., "pick up the left sleeve").
- **To learn language-conditioned policies for these basic skills:** Once basic skills are identified, the objective is to train a robust model that can map current visual observations (depth images) and single-step language instructions to specific "pick" or "place" target points on the cloth. This aims to simplify the learning problem by focusing on simpler, reusable sub-tasks.
- **To compose learned basic skills to generalize to unseen tasks:** The final objective is to utilize an LLM-based high-level task planner. This planner would take a new, unseen multi-step task instruction and decompose it into a sequence of the learned basic skills. This composition mechanism, augmented by the LLM's commonsense knowledge, is intended to enable the robot to perform complex, unseen cloth manipulation tasks effectively, moving beyond the limitations of task-specific learning.

In summary, the motivation is to achieve generalizable multi-step cloth manipulation by transforming a complex problem into a sequence of manageable, reusable basic skills, enabled by the planning and reasoning abilities of LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology for learning generalizable language-conditioned cloth manipulation from long demonstrations is structured into three main stages: skill discovery, language-conditioned skill learning, and generalizable cloth manipulation through skill composition.

#### 3.1. A. Overall Framework

The framework begins by processing existing long demonstrations to autonomously identify and learn basic manipulation skills. These learned skills are then composed by an LLM-based planner to execute unseen multi-step tasks.

#### 3.2. B. Skill Discovery and Learning from Long Demonstration

1. **Decompose Long Demonstration through Large Language Model (LLM):**
 - **Input Data:** The method utilizes an existing dataset of language-conditioned cloth manipulation demonstrations, where each demonstration (D_i) comprises a sequence of depth images (I_i), corresponding long natural language instructions (L_i), and action steps (A_i). Each depth image I_ik (except the last) is associated with a "pick" and a "place" action.
 - **Objective:** The goal is to extract single-step "pick" and "place" language instructions (l_ij) from the implicit meaning within the longer, multi-step instruction (L_i).
 - **LLM Utilization:** A Large Language Model (LLM) is employed due to its strong language reasoning capabilities. The LLM is prompted with the long language instruction, its corresponding action steps, and a textual prompt.
 - **Prompting Strategy:** To enhance the reliability of the LLM's responses, few-shot prompting and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning are incorporated.
 - **Output Structure:** The LLM's responses are structured using predefined instruction templates such as "Pick up the {which} of the {cloth type}" and "Fold it to the {which}". "{cloth type}" refers to a list of fabric names (e.g., T-shirt, skirt), and "{which}" denotes common parts of the fabric (e.g., left sleeve, right collar).
 - **Result:** This process autonomously decomposes the long demonstrations, generating a language-conditioned basic skill dataset. This dataset associates each single-step "pick" or "place" instruction (l_ij) with the relevant depth image (I_ik) and action step (a_ij).

2. **Language-Conditioned Skill Learning:**
 - **Model Architecture (Model A):** A Transformer-based model, adapted from previous work, is designed to learn from the established basic skill dataset. This model is trained to infer target points for manipulation.
 - **Language Embedding:** Natural language instructions (l_t) are first encoded using a CLIP language encoder. These embeddings are then augmented with position and learnable embeddings to provide contextual information and adaptability.
 - **Depth Image Processing:** Depth images (o_t) are processed using a patch-based approach. Each image is divided into N patches, flattened into 1D vectors, and then projected linearly. These vectors are further augmented with position and learnable embeddings to retain spatial information and enhance adaptability.
 - **Transformer Encoder:** Modal-type embeddings are added to both language and visual inputs, which are then fed into a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder.
 - **Position Decoder:** The outputs from the ViT encoder are passed to a position decoder, which comprises alternating convolutional and upsampling layers.
 - **Output:** The decoder generates a pixel-level affordance heatmap. This heatmap indicates the probability or likelihood of each pixel being a suitable "pick" or "place" point. The model selects the pixel with the highest likelihood as the target point for the robot's action.
 - **Skill Execution (Low-level):** When Model A predicts a 2D interaction point (x_t = [px, py]ᵀ) in the pixel space, this point is converted into 3D camera coordinates (X_c_t = [x, y, z]ᵀ) using the RGB-D image (I_t) and camera intrinsic parameters (K). These 3D coordinates are then transformed to the robot's base frame (X_b_t) and used in conjunction with motion planning to compute the robot arm's trajectory (T_t).

#### 3.3. C. Generalizable Cloth Manipulation through Composition of Learned Skills

1. **High-level Planning through Large Language Model (LLM):**
 - **Input:** For an unseen multi-step task, a new high-level language instruction (L_new) is provided.
 - **LLM's Role:** The same LLM (GPT-4o in experiments) used for skill discovery is leveraged as a task planner. It takes L_new and decomposes it into a sequence of individual, actionable language instructions (l_1, l_2..., l_t), corresponding to the basic skills that have been learned.
 - **Prompt Structure:** The LLM employs the same prompt structure as in the skill decomposition phase.
 - **Benefit:** The LLM's ability to reason and integrate commonsense knowledge enables it to generate logical plans for novel combinations of actions, effectively breaking down abstract commands into a tangible sequence of learned basic skills.

2. **Skill Execution:**
 - **Sequential Action:** The sequence of decomposed single-step language instructions (l_t) generated by the LLM planner is fed sequentially into the trained Model A.
 - **Affordance Prediction:** For each step, Model A takes the current observation (o_t) and the specific single-step instruction (l_t) to predict the precise "pick" or "place" target point (p_t) on the cloth.
 - **Robot Trajectory Generation:** As described in skill learning, this 2D pixel-level target point is converted to 3D robot base frame coordinates, and motion planning is executed to guide the robot arm to perform the "pick" or "place" action. This process repeats for each instruction in the sequence until the multi-step task is complete.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of autonomous skill discovery, performance on seen and unseen multi-step cloth manipulation tasks, and sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

#### 4.1. A. Skill Discovery Experiments

- **Setup:** Various foundation models (GPT-4o, Deepseek R1, Qwen 2.5 Max) were evaluated for their ability to autonomously discover basic skills. GPT-4o was tested as both an LLM and a VLM (with image input). Three multi-step tasks of increasing complexity (4, 6, and 12 action steps) were used. Each task involved 10 trials, with human experts evaluating the success rate of skill decomposition.
- **Results:**
 - GPT-4o (LLM mode) demonstrated the highest success rate, achieving 100% on Task 1 ("fold the trousers from left to right") and Task 2 ("fold the right sleeve of the T-shirt inward, then fold the T-shirt from bottom to top"), and 100% on Task 3 ("Fold both sleeves inward, then fold the T-shirt top to bottom, then left to right").
 - Deepseek R1 achieved 100%, 80%, and 60% on Task 1, 2, and 3, respectively. Qwen 2.5 Max showed significantly lower performance (60%, 30%, 0%).
 - GPT-4o with image input (VLM mode) also performed well, achieving 100% on Task 1 and 2, but 70% on Task 3, indicating that for this specific task, the LLM-only approach was more effective.
- **Conclusion:** GPT-4o exhibited strong reasoning abilities for skill discovery in cloth manipulation, supporting its selection as the LLM for subsequent stages.

#### 4.2. B. Simulation Experiments

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted in an extended SoftGym benchmark, including 10 multi-step cloth manipulation tasks across five cloth categories (T-shirt, skirts, trousers, square, rectangle). Tasks were categorized into 5 "seen" (included in training) and 5 "unseen" (only for testing, requiring generalization). Baselines included Language-Deformable and Foldsformer. Each task involved 50 trials. Success was defined by an error of less than 0.025m between the policy's final result and an oracle's result.
- **Results (Table I):**
 - **Overall Performance:** The proposed method consistently outperformed both baseline methods on both seen and unseen tasks.
 - **Seen Tasks:**
 - The proposed method achieved high success rates, ranging from 92% to 100%.
 - Language-Deformable achieved 100% for most tasks but dropped to 62% for the "Sleeves folding & Half folding (horizontal) T-shirt" task.
 - Foldsformer generally performed worse, with success rates from 66% to 100%.
 - This indicates that learning basic skills, as done by the proposed method, is simpler and more robust than learning complex multi-step policies.
 - **Unseen Tasks:**
 - The proposed method demonstrated significantly higher generalization capabilities, achieving success rates ranging from 98% to 100%.
 - Language-Deformable showed limited generalization, achieving 0% success on all unseen tasks. Its generalization was constrained to simple spatial pattern changes, not to fundamentally different action sequences.
 - Foldsformer achieved higher success rates than Language-Deformable (26% to 74%) for unseen tasks, but it required complete sequential images from expert demonstrations as task specifications, providing additional sub-goal information.
 - In contrast, the proposed method did not require such additional information for unseen tasks, yet achieved substantially higher success rates, demonstrating its effectiveness in generalizing to novel, complex folding requirements through skill composition.

#### 4.3. C. Real Experiments

- **Setup:** Real-world experiments utilized a Franka robot platform with a Realsense RGB-D camera. Performance was evaluated on multiple types of cloth manipulation tasks, including both seen and unseen.
- **Metrics:** Task success was defined by a Mean Intersection over Union (MIoU) greater than 0.8 and a Window Recall (WR) less than 0.35. MIoU measured overlap between expert and robot manipulation outcomes, while WR quantified undesired edges and wrinkles. Success rate was also reported.
- **Results (Table III & Fig. 5):**
 - The method successfully transferred from simulation to the real world.
 - For "Seen Half folding (vertical) Square", it achieved 100% success with MIoU of 0.9153 and WR of 0.0134.
 - For "Unseen Half folding (vertical) Trousers", it achieved 100% success with MIoU of 0.9103 and WR of 0.0325.
 - For "Unseen Corner folding Square", it achieved 100% success with MIoU of 0.7970 and WR of 0.0301.
 - For the more complex "Unseen Sleeves folding & Half folding (vertical) T-shirt", it achieved 60% success with MIoU of 0.8978 and WR of 0.0189.
- **Conclusion:** The real-world experiments confirmed that the proposed method is effective for multi-step cloth manipulation and can generalize to unseen tasks in a physical environment, though increased task complexity (e.g., 12 steps for T-shirt folding) can still present challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation of deformable objects, particularly cloth, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Addressing Generalization in Deformable Object Manipulation:** The paper tackles the long-standing challenge of achieving generalizable multi-step cloth manipulation, which is crucial for deploying intelligent robots in unstructured environments like households. Current methods often falter when encountering unseen tasks, a limitation this work aims to overcome.
2. **Novel Decomposition-Based Pipeline:** The introduction of a novel pipeline that autonomously discovers basic skills from long demonstrations and composes them for unseen tasks is a departure from conventional end-to-end or task-specific learning approaches. This modularity enhances reusability and simplifies the learning problem.
3. **Effective Integration of Large Language Models:** The work effectively leverages the language reasoning and commonsense knowledge capabilities of LLMs for two critical aspects:
 - **Autonomous Skill Discovery:** LLMs are used to automatically decompose complex, multi-step human demonstrations into elementary "pick" and "place" skills, thereby addressing the lack of pre-defined basic skill sets for cloth manipulation.
 - **High-Level Task Planning:** LLMs act as a high-level planner, breaking down novel, abstract multi-step language instructions into sequences of learned basic skills, bridging the gap between human intent and robot actions.
4. **Demonstrated Superior Generalization:** The experimental results, especially on unseen tasks in both simulation and the real world, illustrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms existing baselines. This enhanced generalization capability is a step towards more adaptable and robust robotic systems that can handle variations in tasks and environments.
5. **Robustness and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful transfer of policies trained in simulation to real-world cloth manipulation tasks with high success rates indicates the practical applicability and robustness of the approach. This reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection and training, which is often costly and time-consuming.
6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The principles of decomposing complex tasks into basic, language-conditioned skills, and using LLMs for planning, could potentially be extended to other challenging deformable object manipulation tasks (e.g., cables, bags) or even complex rigid object assembly tasks, thereby broadening the scope of autonomous robot capabilities.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies limitations, such as performance degradation with increased action steps due to distribution shift in observations. This clearly indicates future research directions, like exploring more robust language-conditioned skill learning methods, potentially by integrating pretrained large vision models as feature encoders, thereby paving the way for continued advancements in the field.

In summary, this research offers a practical and effective paradigm for generalizable cloth manipulation, advancing the state of the art by intelligently integrating LLMs with skill learning and composition, and laying groundwork for more capable and versatile robotic agents.
