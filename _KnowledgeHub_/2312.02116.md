# GIVT: Generative Infinite-Vocabulary Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Generative Infinite-Vocabulary Transformers (GIVT) introduces a decoder-only architecture that directly generates real-valued vector sequences, thereby removing the need for discrete tokenization in generative transformers for visual data. GIVT achieves competitive image generation quality, outperforming VQ-based methods and some diffusion models, while demonstrating strong representation learning and applicability to dense prediction tasks.

## Problem

- Generative transformers typically rely on discrete tokenization (e.g., VQ-VAEs), which introduces non-continuous operations, requires complex differentiable approximations, and leads to issues like low vocabulary utilization and increased memory consumption.
- The discrete nature of vector quantization limits control over low-level image details, can cause information loss, and complicates the integration of multiple modalities.
- Existing two-stage generative transformer pipelines using VQ-VAEs often necessitate specialized auxiliary losses and optimization strategies, increasing training complexity.

## Method

- A two-stage approach replaces the ==VQ-VAE== with a standard continuous-latent "==beta-VAE==" (β-VAE) that uses a Gaussian encoder and prior to learn a continuous, real-valued latent space.
- The **GIVT** architecture modifies a decoder-only transformer to directly predict parameters of a continuous distribution (specifically, a ==k-mixture Gaussian Mixture Model== with channel-wise independence) for real-valued latent vectors, replacing the discrete vocabulary lookup and categorical output.
- The framework incorporates an ==invertible flow model== (adapter) for better alignment between the VAE and GIVT latent distributions and adapts continuous sampling techniques like variance scaling, beam search, and ==Distribution-Based Classifier-Free Guidance (DB-CFG)== for inference.

## Results

- **GIVT-MaskGIT** achieved a FID of **4.64** for 256x256 ImageNet (outperforming MaskGIT's **4.92**), and a larger **GIVT-Causal-L+A** variant achieved a FID of **2.59** (outperforming LDM-4-G's **3.60** and ViT-VQGAN-L's **3.04**).
- For 512x512 ImageNet, GIVT-MaskGIT achieved a FID of **4.86**, outperforming MaskGIT (FID **7.80**), and GIVT-Causal-L+A achieved a FID of **2.92** with DB-CFG, outperforming the best available DiT model (DiT-XL/2-G's **3.04**).
- Unconditional GIVT-Causal achieved **65.1%** linear probing accuracy on ImageNet, matching VIM+ViT with fewer parameters and shorter sequence length, and demonstrated competitive performance in panoptic segmentation (PQ **40.2** vs VQ-based UViM's **39.0**) and depth estimation within the UViM framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly modeling continuous, real-valued latent sequences with a transformer decoder is feasible and can surpass the performance of existing discrete tokenization methods for generative tasks.
> - A more regularized latent space from the β-VAE, even at the cost of some reconstruction fidelity, is beneficial for the downstream GIVT's generative performance and easier to model.
> - Employing a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with multiple components for predicting continuous distributions and utilizing advanced sampling strategies like DB-CFG are critical for achieving high-fidelity image generation with GIVT.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Transformers, initially dominant in natural language processing (NLP), have significantly impacted computer vision. Architectures like Vision Transformers (ViT) have become standard for discriminative vision tasks, including image classification, object detection, and segmentation, by processing images as sequences of patches. However, applying generative transformer decoders to image generation presents challenges due to their original design for discrete tokens from finite vocabularies, which contrasts with the continuous nature of image data.

The prevailing approach for generative modeling of visual data with transformers has been a two-stage process. This typically involves training a Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) to convert images into sequences of discrete tokens, followed by training a transformer decoder to model the distribution of these discrete latent tokens. Examples of this include VQ-GAN and MaskGIT, which have yielded substantial quality improvements in image generation. VQ-VAE-based tokenization also facilitates multimodal generative models by concatenating vocabularies from different modalities. Furthermore, VQ-VAEs are utilized for tokenizing label spaces in dense prediction tasks and for self-supervised learning from images.

Despite its advantages, the VQ-VAE approach has several inherent issues. The non-continuous nature of vector quantization necessitates differentiable approximations for gradient-based optimization. Small vocabularies can simplify latent modeling but reduce information content and control over low-level image details, impacting quality. Conversely, large vocabularies can lead to low vocabulary utilization and increased memory consumption due to large embedding matrices, often requiring advanced techniques like entropy losses or codebook-splitting to mitigate.

In parallel, latent diffusion models (LDMs) have also emerged as a prominent two-stage generative approach, where a diffusion model learns to denoise latents produced by a pre-trained autoencoder, offering competitive image generation quality. Other related works explore continuous outputs in NLP by predicting word embeddings with continuous distributions, although these still operate on a fixed, finite set of embeddings. Time-series modeling with transformers often uses regression losses or data discretization but typically does not predict continuous distributions for autoregressive generation.

This work addresses the limitations of discrete tokenization by proposing Generative Infinite-Vocabulary Transformers (GIVT), which aim to entirely remove quantization from generative transformers for visual data. GIVT represents a departure from VQ-based methods by enabling transformers to directly generate unquantized, real-valued vector sequences. This positions GIVT as a novel approach seeking to simplify the generative modeling pipeline, enhance image generation quality, and improve representation learning capabilities by directly embracing the continuous nature of visual latents, while still leveraging the sequential modeling power of transformers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a generative transformer decoder architecture capable of directly generating sequences of real-valued vectors, thereby eliminating the need for discrete tokenization and fixed vocabularies. This new architecture is termed Generative Infinite-Vocabulary Transformer (GIVT).

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges and limitations observed in existing transformer-based generative models, particularly those relying on Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAEs):

- **Addressing Quantization Difficulties:** Current VQ-VAE-based approaches introduce non-continuous operations that require complex differentiable approximations for stochastic gradient-based optimization. This often leads to documented issues such as low vocabulary utilization, which necessitate advanced mitigation techniques like entropy losses, codebook reinitialization, or dedicated optimization algorithms. By removing quantization entirely, GIVT aims to simplify the training process and overcome these inherent optimization challenges.
- **Enhancing Latent Representation Quality and Control:** The discrete nature of VQ can lead to information loss and limit control over low-level details in generated images. While a large vocabulary might offer more detail, it can exacerbate utilization issues. GIVT, by directly modeling continuous, real-valued latent sequences, seeks to preserve more information and offer finer control, potentially leading to better image generation quality and richer representation learning capabilities.
- **Reducing Model Complexity and Memory Footprint:** VQ-based methods often require large embedding matrices to represent the discrete vocabulary, consuming significant memory. This can be particularly problematic in multimodal contexts where multiple large vocabularies might be concatenated. GIVT's ability to directly consume and predict real-valued feature representations eliminates the need for such large lookup tables, offering a more memory-efficient solution.
- **Simplifying Training Pipelines:** GIVT's reliance on a standard β-VAE with a Gaussian prior and conventional deep learning techniques (e.g., standard cross-entropy loss) is motivated by the desire to streamline the two-stage generative process. This contrasts with the specialized auxiliary losses and optimization strategies often required for VQ-VAEs.
- **Leveraging Transformer Advancements:** The authors are motivated by the extensive advancements in scaling and inference efficiency for large language models. By adapting the core transformer architecture to continuous data, GIVT aims to directly benefit from these developments, making improvements in transformer technology readily transferable to visual data generation.
- **Facilitating Multimodal Modeling:** The limitations of discrete tokenization can complicate interleaved multimodal generative models. By adopting continuous latents, GIVT aims to provide a more flexible framework for combining different modalities, addressing a growing trend in generative AI.
- **Demonstrating Generalizability:** Beyond image generation, a key motivation is to demonstrate that the continuous latent modeling paradigm of GIVT can be effectively applied to other dense prediction computer vision tasks, such as panoptic segmentation and depth estimation, within frameworks like UViM. This aims to showcase the versatility and broader utility of the proposed approach.

In summary, the motivation behind GIVT is to develop a more straightforward, efficient, and performant generative transformer for continuous data by directly addressing the fundamental limitation of discrete tokenization in existing architectures, while maintaining compatibility with the two-stage VAE-transformer paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GIVT framework employs a two-stage approach, conceptually similar to VQ-VAE-based methods or latent diffusion models, but crucially avoids any quantization. It involves first learning a continuous latent space with a β-VAE and then modeling the distribution of these real-valued latent sequences using a GIVT.

#### 3.1. VAE Training (Stage 1)

1. **Continuous Latent β-VAE:** A standard continuous-latent β-VAE with a Gaussian encoder and prior is trained.
2. **Encoder (E):** For an input image `x`, the encoder predicts the mean (`μ`) and diagonal covariance (`σ`) of a multivariate normal distribution. A latent representation `z` is sampled from `N(μ, σ)` using the reparametrization trick.
3. **Latent Representation:** The latent `z` is spatially downsampled to dimensions `h × w` with a feature dimension `d`. For `H×W` input, `h=⌈H/16⌉` and `w=⌈W/16⌉`.
4. **Decoder:** The VAE decoder reconstructs the image from the sampled latent sequence.
5. **Loss Function:** The VAE is trained to minimize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO).
 - **KL-Term:** The KL-divergence between the approximate posterior and the Gaussian prior is computed in closed form. The hyperparameter `β` controls the regularization strength, influencing how closely `z` adheres to the Gaussian prior.
 - **Reconstruction/Likelihood Term:** For image generation, a mixture of Mean Squared Error (MSE), perceptual loss, and GAN loss is used. For dense prediction tasks, categorical cross-entropy is employed.

#### 3.2. GIVT Training (Stage 2)

A GIVT is trained to model the true latent distribution `p(z)` or `p(z|c)` (conditioned on a signal `c`, such as a class label). The `hwd` latent `z` is reshaped into an `hw`-length sequence of `d`-dimensional real-valued vectors, referred to as "soft tokens." Two key modifications are made to a standard decoder-only transformer architecture:

1. **Input Embedding:** The traditional finite-vocabulary lookup table is replaced with a single linear layer that projects the `d`-dimensional real-valued input vectors to the transformer's hidden dimension.
2. **Output Prediction:** Instead of predicting a categorical distribution over a finite vocabulary, GIVT predicts the parameters of a continuous distribution. Specifically, it uses a `k`-mixture Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM), assuming channel-wise independence for the mixture components (i.e., multivariate Gaussians with diagonal covariance). The GIVT model outputs `2kd + k` parameters per soft token (kd means, kd variances, and k mixture probabilities). Softmax is applied to mixture probabilities and softplus to variance parameters to ensure valid distributions.
3. **Loss Function:** GIVT is trained using the negative log-likelihood (NLL), which is equivalent to the cross-entropy loss, on the predicted distribution `˜p`. The loss is given by `L_T = P_c E_z [-log ˜p(z|c)]`.

GIVT models are trained in two configurations:

- **GIVT-Causal:** This variant uses a causal attention mask and teacher forcing, predicting each `d`-dimensional vector in the sequence conditioned on all preceding vectors. For class-conditional generation, a learned `[CLS]` vector is prepended to the input sequence. This is analogous to latent modeling in VQ-GAN.
- **GIVT-MaskGIT:** Similar to MaskGIT, a subset of the input sequence is randomly masked during training. For masked locations, the corresponding vectors in `z` are replaced with zeros, and learned `[MASK]` or `[UNMASK]` special vectors are concatenated in the feature dimension.

#### 3.3. Towards End-to-End Training: Adapters

To better align the latent distributions of the VAE and GIVT, a simple alternative to full end-to-end training is explored:

- **Invertible Flow Model:** A small invertible flow model, or "adapter," consisting of 8 convolutional, bijective iRevNet blocks, is used to map the VAE latent sequences to a new latent space of identical dimensions.
- **Joint Training:** GIVT is trained jointly with this adapter, modeling sequences in the transformed latent space.
- **Inference:** Samples from GIVT are first processed by the inverted adapter before being fed to the VAE decoder. The adapter adds negligible computational and parameter overhead.

#### 3.4. Inference

After training, GIVT samples are generated either sequentially (GIVT-Causal) or through iterative masked prediction (GIVT-MaskGIT), and then decoded into images by the VAE decoder. Standard sampling techniques are adapted for the continuous domain:

- **Temperature Sampling (Variance Scaling):** For continuous GMMs, this involves scaling the covariance matrices of the predicted Gaussian distributions.
- **Beam Search:** Applied similarly to discrete transformer decoders, maintaining multiple beams and selecting candidates with the highest cumulative log probability.
- **Distribution-Based Classifier-Free Guidance (DB-CFG):**
 - **Training:** GIVT is trained with an additional null class `∅` to learn the unconditional data distribution.
 - **Inference:** At each step, GIVT evaluates the conditional distribution (`˜p(z|c)`) and the unconditional distribution (`˜p(z|∅)`).
 - **Sampling:** Samples are drawn from an unnormalized density `p_CFG(z|c) ∝ ˜p(z|c)^(1+w) ˜p(z|∅)^(-w)` using rejection sampling. Proposal distributions are typically `N(μ_c, 2σ_c)`. This approach requires two forward passes per inference step.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** ImageNet1k for class-conditional and unconditional image generation (256x256 and 512x512). COCO Panoptic 2017 and NYU Depth v2 for dense prediction tasks.
- **VAE Configuration:** Uses a ResBlock-based architecture adapted from MaskGIT, removing the VQ layer. Latent dimension `d` (default 16) and KL weight `β` are tuned.
- **GIVT Configuration:** BERT-Large configuration (304M parameters) by default, also explored a larger "Large" backbone (1.67B parameters). GMM with 16 factorized components is typically used.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** FID, Precision, and Recall for image generation, following the ADM TensorFlow Suite. Panoptic Quality (PQ) and RMSE for dense prediction. Linear probing accuracy for representation learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding GIVT's performance in image generation, representation learning, and dense prediction tasks.

#### 4.1. Image Generation

- **Performance Comparison (256×256 ImageNet):**
 - **GIVT-MaskGIT:** Outperforms MaskGIT, its VQ-based counterpart, achieving a FID of 4.64 compared to MaskGIT's 4.92. With DB-CFG, FID further improves to 4.53.
 - **GIVT-Causal:** Without guidance, GIVT-Causal outperforms most diffusion and VQGAN baselines. With DB-CFG, GIVT-Causal achieves a FID of 3.35, outperforming VQGAN (FID 5.20) with a model that is over 4.5 times smaller (0.3B vs. 1.4B parameters). The larger GIVT-Causal-L+A variant achieves a FID of 2.59, outperforming LDM-4-G (FID 3.60) and ViT-VQGAN-L (FID 3.04), despite ViT-VQGAN-L having a 4x larger sequence length and 10x larger VAE.
- **Performance Comparison (512×512 ImageNet):**
 - **GIVT-MaskGIT:** Achieves a FID of 4.86, outperforming MaskGIT (FID 7.80) at the same inference cost.
 - **GIVT-Causal-L+A:** Achieves a FID of 2.92 with DB-CFG, outperforming DiT-XL/2-G (FID 3.04), which is reported as the best available DiT model.
- **Unconditional Image Generation:** GIVT-Causal-L achieves a FID of 11.02 for unconditional 256x256 ImageNet, which is lower (better) than the diffusion-based ADM (FID 26.20).
- **VAE Latent Space Impact:**
 - Increasing the KL regularization weight `β` in the VAE leads to a higher (worse) VAE reconstruction FID but a lower (better) sampling FID for the downstream GIVT model. This suggests that a more regularized (and thus easier to model) latent space, even at the cost of some reconstruction fidelity, is beneficial for GIVT's generative performance.
 - Increasing the number of GMM mixtures `k` from 1 to 16 significantly reduces the sampling FID, reaching a plateau at `k=16`.
- **Sampling Strategies:**
 - Variance scaling, beam search, and especially DB-CFG consistently lead to substantial improvements in FID across all model configurations. DB-CFG is identified as the most effective strategy.
 - The combination of an adapter and `k=16` mixtures results in compounding improvements in FID.
 - Analysis of predicted standard deviation during inference shows that conditional predictions generally have lower standard deviation, indicating higher certainty, and the standard deviation gradually decreases over inference steps.
- **GMM Configuration:** Using a 16-mixture GMM with factorized component densities (diagonal covariance) is found to be more effective than a single Gaussian with a full covariance matrix, which yielded only modest gains and made DB-CFG less tractable.
- **Sample Quality:** Generated samples are described as high-fidelity and coherent. Visualizations of GIVT-MaskGIT inference steps demonstrate gradual coherence improvement.

#### 4.2. Representation Learning

- **Linear Probing Accuracy:** Unconditional GIVT-Causal achieves a linear probing accuracy of 65.1% on ImageNet, matching VIM+ViT (ViT-VQGAN). This is achieved with a model that has over 2x fewer parameters and a 4x shorter sequence length, implying lower computational cost. GIVT-Causal is outperformed by encoder-decoder architectures like MAGE, which are typically better suited for representation learning.

#### 4.3. Panoptic Segmentation and Depth Estimation

- **Performance in UViM Framework:** When integrated into the UViM framework, a GIVT-based variant (using a β-VAE) demonstrates comparable performance to the VQ-VAE-based UViM baseline.
 - **Panoptic Segmentation (COCO Panoptic 2017):** GIVT-based UViM achieves a higher Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 40.2 compared to the VQ-based UViM's 39.0.
 - **Depth Estimation (NYU Depth v2):** GIVT-based UViM performs slightly worse, with an RMSE of 0.474 compared to the VQ-based UViM's 0.459.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Generative Infinite-Vocabulary Transformers (GIVT) marks a significant advancement in generative modeling with transformers, particularly for visual data.

**Key Significance:**

- **Pioneering Continuous Latent Modeling:** GIVT is presented as the first decoder-only model capable of directly generating sequences of real-valued vectors. This fundamentally shifts the paradigm from discrete token prediction to continuous distribution modeling within the transformer architecture.
- **Simplification of Generative Pipelines:** By eliminating vector quantization, GIVT circumvents the complex optimization challenges and specific mitigation techniques (e.g., entropy losses, codebook-splitting, reinitialization) that are often necessary for VQ-VAEs. This allows for the use of simpler, standard β-VAEs, streamlining the two-stage training process and potentially making generative model development more accessible and robust.
- **Enhanced Generative Performance:** GIVT demonstrates competitive or superior performance against established VQ-based generative models (VQGAN, MaskGIT) and strong latent diffusion models (LDM, DiT) in class-conditional image generation. This is often achieved with computational advantages, such as smaller model sizes or shorter effective sequence lengths, indicating improved efficiency.
- **Improved Representation Learning:** The method yields strong results in representation learning, matching or exceeding the performance of larger VQ-VAE-based models in tasks like linear probing, suggesting that continuous latent spaces learned by GIVT can capture rich and useful features.
- **Broader Applicability:** The successful application of GIVT to dense prediction tasks (panoptic segmentation, depth estimation) within the UViM framework demonstrates its generalizability beyond pure image generation. This indicates the potential for GIVT to be a versatile component in various vision tasks that benefit from continuous latent representations.
- **Synergy with NLP Advances:** By adapting the core transformer decoder architecture, GIVT is positioned to directly benefit from ongoing advancements in scaling and inference efficiency developed for large language models, providing a clear path for future improvements. The continuous nature also simplifies its integration into future multimodal interleaved modeling systems.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Facilitating Research in Continuous Latent Spaces:** GIVT could stimulate further research into direct continuous latent modeling with transformers, potentially leading to new architectures and training methodologies that leverage the intrinsic continuity of many real-world data types.
- **Applications Beyond Vision:** The authors highlight the potential for GIVT to be applied to other modalities that are inherently continuous, such as audio and time-series data, where current transformer approaches often rely on discretization or regression losses.
- **Foundation for Multimodal AI:** Its ability to handle continuous representations could make GIVT a foundational component for next-generation multimodal models that seamlessly integrate and generate content across text, images, audio, and other continuous data streams.
- **Efficiency Gains in Production:** The reduced memory footprint (by avoiding large embedding matrices) and potentially simpler training/inference pipelines could make GIVT-based models more efficient to deploy in resource-constrained environments or for large-scale applications.

The successful demonstration of a quantization-free generative transformer opens new avenues for generative AI, potentially simplifying model development, enhancing performance, and broadening the applicability of transformer architectures across diverse continuous data domains.
