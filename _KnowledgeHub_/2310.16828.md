# TD-MPC2: Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> TD-MPC2, a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm from UCSD, trains robust and scalable generalist agents for continuous control across 104 diverse tasks using a single set of hyperparameters. It achieves strong performance, outperforming prior methods on high-dimensional locomotion and manipulation, and shows consistent capability increases with larger models and datasets.

## Problem

- Existing generalist embodied agents often rely on large datasets of near-expert demonstrations, limiting data diversity and their applicability to high-dimensional continuous control.
- Traditional reinforcement learning algorithms are typically designed for single-task learning, suffer from high hyperparameter sensitivity, and lack robustness for diverse, multi-task scenarios.
- Prior model-based reinforcement learning methods, including TD-MPC, struggled with stable performance scaling and numerical instabilities when scaled to larger models and datasets.

## Method

- A ==model-based reinforcement learning== algorithm, **TD-MPC2**, is developed using an ==implicit (decoder-free) world model== that optimizes for predicting outcomes (returns) rather than raw observations.
- Robustness is enhanced through key innovations including ==discrete regression== for value and reward prediction, ==SimNorm== for latent state normalization, and an ==ensemble of Q-functions==.
- To enable generalist multi-task learning, the model incorporates ==learnable task embeddings== and ==action masking==, allowing a single set of hyperparameters to be used across diverse tasks with varying action spaces.

## Results

- TD-MPC2 achieved superior performance on **104** continuous control tasks across **four** diverse domains, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods with a single set of hyperparameters.
- A single **317M** parameter world model was successfully trained to perform **80 tasks** across multiple domains, demonstrating consistent capability increases with model and data scale.
- Pre-trained multi-task agents exhibit promising few-shot learning, showing approximately a **2x** improvement when finetuned to unseen tasks in low-data regimes compared to training from scratch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit world models focused on predicting outcomes (returns) are effective for control and can generalize better than generative models for continuous control tasks.
> - Algorithmic innovations, specifically discrete regression for value and reward prediction and SimNorm for latent states, are crucial for achieving robustness and stability across diverse continuous control tasks.
> - Scaling model and data size consistently improves agent capabilities, enabling the training of large generalist world models for embodied agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

TD-MPC2 positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas within artificial intelligence: **generalist AI**, **reinforcement learning (RL)**, and **model-based RL (MBRL)**, with a specific emphasis on **continuous control** and **scalability**.

The paper explicitly draws parallels to the recent successes of "generalist models" in natural language processing (e.g., GPT-3, Brown et al., 2020) and computer vision (e.g., MAE, He et al., 2022; Segment Anything, Kirillov et al., 2023). It identifies a crucial gap in extending this paradigm to **embodied agents** capable of diverse low-level control tasks across multiple embodiments and action spaces. Existing approaches to generalist embodied agents (e.g., Gato, Reed et al., 2022; RT-1, Brohan et al., 2023) are criticized for their reliance on large datasets of *near-expert demonstrations* for behavior cloning, which inherently limits data availability. Furthermore, these methods often rely on action space discretization, making scaling to high-dimensional continuous control difficult. TD-MPC2 aims to overcome these limitations by leveraging reinforcement learning, which is inherently designed to extract expert behavior from *uncurated* datasets containing mixed-quality data.

Within the **reinforcement learning** landscape, traditional RL algorithms (e.g., SAC, Lillicrap et al., 2016; Haarnoja et al., 2018) are typically designed for single-task learning and suffer from high sensitivity to hyperparameters, often requiring per-task tuning. TD-MPC2 directly addresses this long-standing issue by striving for an algorithm robust enough to use a single set of hyperparameters across a wide variety of tasks. This aligns with a growing trend in the RL community towards developing more robust and user-friendly algorithms (e.g., DreamerV3, Hafner et al., 2023; DrQ-v2, Yarats et al., 2021).

As a **model-based RL** algorithm, TD-MPC2 differentiates itself from "generative" world models (like DreamerV3) that rely on explicitly reconstructing future observations. Instead, it builds upon the concept of an "implicit (decoder-free) world model" introduced by its predecessor, TD-MPC. The core argument is that predicting raw future observations accurately over long horizons is challenging and not necessarily optimal for control; rather, a "maximally useful model" predicts *outcomes* (returns) conditioned on action sequences. This objective function design is a key distinguishing feature within the MBRL space, aiming for efficiency and better generalization.

Finally, the work directly contributes to the emerging field of **scalable RL**, where researchers are increasingly exploring whether larger models and larger datasets lead to improved capabilities in RL agents, similar to the scaling laws observed in large language models. While prior work has explored this (e.g., Baker et al., 2022; Kumar et al., 2023), TD-MPC2 specifically demonstrates consistent scaling of *continuous control* capabilities with model and data size, even on mixed-quality, multi-task datasets, which remains a significant challenge. By providing a scalable and robust algorithm, TD-MPC2 offers a critical building block for future generalist robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **scalable and robust model-based reinforcement learning algorithm for continuous control**, named TD-MPC2. This algorithm is designed to effectively learn "generalist world models" from large, uncurated multi-task datasets.

The key motivations driving this research stem from observed limitations in current approaches to embodied AI and reinforcement learning:

1. **Bridging the Gap for Generalist Embodied Agents:** Inspired by the success of large generalist models in language and vision, the authors seek to extend this paradigm to robotics. However, they identify that existing methods for generalist embodied agents often rely on the assumption of **near-expert trajectories for behavior cloning**. This severely restricts the amount and diversity of available data, making true generalization difficult. TD-MPC2 aims to overcome this by using an RL framework that can learn from **large, uncurated datasets** comprising behaviors of varying skill levels.
2. **Addressing the Lack of Scalable Continuous Control Algorithms:** A significant challenge is the scarcity of continuous control algorithms that can effectively consume and learn from massive, diverse datasets. Most current RL algorithms are designed for single-task learning and are sensitive to **per-task hyperparameter tuning**, lacking a principled method for hyperparameter selection. A truly generalist agent must be robust to variations across tasks (e.g., action space dimensionality, exploration difficulty, reward distributions). TD-MPC2 is specifically engineered to address this robustness challenge, aiming for stable performance with a **single set of hyperparameters across all tasks**.
3. **Improving upon TD-MPC:** The work is explicitly motivated by enhancing the original TD-MPC algorithm. While TD-MPC demonstrated strong empirical performance in single-task learning, it suffered from issues like performance *decrease* with naive scaling of model and data size, and sometimes numerical instabilities (e.g., exploding gradients). TD-MPC2's objective is to introduce a series of algorithmic and architectural improvements to rectify these shortcomings, enabling stable and effective scaling.
4. **Democratizing Reinforcement Learning:** The authors express a broader goal of "democratizing use of RL," acknowledging that historical RL algorithms have been notoriously sensitive and resource-intensive, often requiring large expert teams. By improving algorithmic robustness and providing open-source resources, TD-MPC2 aims to lower the barrier of entry for smaller teams and individuals, thereby accelerating broader research and application of RL.

In essence, TD-MPC2's core objective is to advance the state-of-the-art in model-based reinforcement learning by creating an algorithm that is not only highly performant in continuous control but also inherently robust and scalable, paving the way for more general-purpose embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TD-MPC2 builds upon its predecessor, TD-MPC, a model-based RL algorithm that performs local trajectory optimization (planning) in the latent space of a learned implicit (decoder-free) world model. The methodology revolves around a series of key algorithmic and architectural improvements designed to enhance robustness and scalability for multi-task continuous control.

#### 3.1. Implicit World Model Learning

Instead of learning a generative model that reconstructs observations, TD-MPC2 focuses on learning a "maximally useful model" that accurately predicts *outcomes* (returns) conditioned on action sequences. The world model comprises five key components:
- **Encoder (h):** Maps raw observations (s) to their normalized latent representations (z).
- **Latent Dynamics (d):** Models the forward dynamics in the latent space, predicting the next latent state (z') given the current latent state (z) and action (a).
- **Reward Function (R):** Predicts the reward (ˆr) for a given transition (z, a).
- **Terminal Value Function (Q):** Predicts the discounted sum of future rewards (ˆq), or return, from a given latent state and action.
- **Policy Prior (p):** Predicts an optimal action (ˆa) that maximizes Q, serving to guide the trajectory optimizer.

All components are conditioned on a learnable task embedding (e) for multitask scenarios, which is excluded for single-task settings for clarity.

#### 3.2. Model Objective

The h, d, R, and Q components are jointly optimized to minimize a combined loss function (L(θ)) based on data sampled from a replay buffer. This objective includes:
- **Joint-embedding prediction:** An L2 loss between the predicted next latent state (z't) and the stop-gradient encoded true next observation (sg(h(s't))). This implicitly aligns the latent space without explicit reconstruction.
- **Reward prediction:** Cross-entropy (CE) loss between the predicted reward (ˆrt) and the true reward (rt).
- **Value prediction:** Cross-entropy (CE) loss between the predicted value (ˆqt) and a TD-target (qt).
 - **Discrete Regression:** Crucially, both reward and value prediction are formulated as discrete regression problems in a log-transformed space, optimized using cross-entropy with soft targets. This makes the loss magnitudes independent of raw reward magnitudes, significantly improving robustness across diverse tasks with varying reward scales.
 - **Ensemble Q-functions:** An ensemble of five Q-functions is learned, and TD-targets are computed as the minimum of two randomly subsampled Q-functions, similar to Double Q-learning, to reduce overestimation bias. An exponential moving average (EMA) of the Q-functions is used for target computation.

#### 3.3. Policy Objective

The policy prior (p) is trained with a stochastic maximum entropy objective (Lp(θ)). This maximizes the expected return while encouraging exploration by maximizing the policy's entropy. The balance between value maximization and entropy is automatically tuned via moving statistics, enhancing robustness compared to fixed noise schedules.

#### 3.4. Model Predictive Control (MPC) with Policy Prior

TD-MPC2 uses a closed-loop control policy derived by planning with the learned world model. It employs Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) as a derivative-free optimizer. At each decision step, MPPI iteratively samples action sequences, evaluates their expected return by rolling out latent trajectories using the learned dynamics, reward, and value functions, and updates parameters of a time-dependent Gaussian distribution for actions.
- The expected return is bootstrapped beyond the planning horizon (H) using the learned terminal value function (Q), making it a truly "temporal difference" approach to MPC.
- The policy prior (p) guides sampling and accelerates convergence by providing an initial distribution for actions.
- Planning is warm-started by shifting the solution from the previous decision step.
- The implementation is simplified by removing momentum in MPPI and using uniform sampling from the replay buffer, while optimizing code for higher throughput.

#### 3.5. Training Generalist TD-MPC2 Agents

To enable multi-task learning across diverse domains, TD-MPC2 incorporates:
- **Learnable Task Embeddings (e):** All model components are conditioned on a fixed-dimensional, learnable task embedding. These embeddings are jointly trained with the rest of the model and are constrained (ℓ2-norm ≤ 1) to improve training stability and semantic coherence.
- **Action Masking:** To handle varying action spaces across tasks, all model inputs and outputs are zero-padded to the largest dimension. Invalid action dimensions are masked during policy prior prediction and planning, preventing erroneous influence on learning and exploration.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details and Hyperparameters

The paper emphasizes using a *single set of hyperparameters* across all 104 tasks. All components are implemented as MLPs with LayerNorm and Mish activations. A crucial architectural innovation is **SimNorm**, which normalizes the latent representation by projecting it into fixed-dimensional simplices via a softmax operation. This biases the representation towards sparsity without hard constraints, and is shown to be "essential to the training stability." Model scaling is achieved by varying MLP dimensions, latent state dimensions, encoder layers, and the number of Q-functions. Heuristics are used to automatically set the discount factor and initial seed steps based on episode length.

#### 3.7. Evaluation

The methodology is validated through extensive experiments on 104 continuous control tasks from DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2, and MyoSuite, encompassing diverse objectives, embodiments, and action spaces (up to 39 dimensions), including visual observations. Comparisons are made against state-of-the-art model-free (SAC, DrQ-v2, CURL) and model-based (DreamerV3, TD-MPC) baselines. Ablation studies systematically assess the contribution of each design choice.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on 104 diverse continuous control tasks demonstrate significant advancements offered by TD-MPC2:

1. **Superior Performance and Data-Efficiency:** TD-MPC2 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art model-free (SAC) and model-based (DreamerV3, original TD-MPC) methods across all four task domains (DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2, MyoSuite). This is achieved with a *single set of hyperparameters* used across all tasks, highlighting its remarkable robustness.
 - **High-Dimensional Tasks:** TD-MPC2 shows a substantial margin of improvement on particularly challenging tasks involving high-dimensional locomotion (e.g., Dog and Humanoid tasks from DMControl, with action spaces up to 38 dimensions) and complex multi-object manipulation (e.g., Pick YCB from ManiSkill2).
 - **Stability:** Unlike the original TD-MPC, which sometimes diverged due to exploding gradients, and DreamerV3, which occasionally experienced numerical instabilities, TD-MPC2 demonstrates consistent training stability across all benchmarks.
 - **MyoSuite Performance:** Strong results on MyoSuite, a benchmark for physiologically accurate musculoskeletal motor control, are noteworthy as the algorithm was not specifically tuned for this domain.

2. **Scalability of Capabilities with Model and Data Size:** A core finding is that TD-MPC2's agent capabilities *increase consistently* with both model and data size.
 - **Large-Scale Multi-task Agents:** The authors successfully trained a single 317M parameter world model to perform 80 tasks across multiple task domains, embodiments, and action spaces. This demonstrates unprecedented scaling for continuous control generalist agents.
 - **Scaling Law:** While not formally formulated, the normalized score appears to scale linearly with the logarithm of model parameters, suggesting continued performance gains with even larger models.
 - **Training Efficiency:** The 317M parameter model, though large, can be trained with "limited computational resources" (approximately 33 GPU days on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU).

3. **Impact of Algorithmic Innovations (Ablations):** Detailed ablation studies confirm the crucial role of TD-MPC2's specific design choices:
 - **SimNorm and LayerNorm:** The normalization techniques applied to the latent state (SimNorm) and within MLPs (LayerNorm) are shown to be essential for training stability and strong performance.
 - **Discrete Regression:** Formulating reward and value prediction as discrete regression in a log-transformed space significantly contributes to robustness.
 - **Q-function Ensemble:** Using an ensemble of Q-functions for TD-target computation meaningfully improves performance.
 - **Policy Prior:** The maximum entropy policy prior, guiding the planner, is critical for success.
 - **Normalized Task Embeddings:** Constraining the ℓ2-norm of the learned task embeddings improves multitask performance and results in more semantically coherent representations.

4. **Semantic Task Embeddings:** The learned task embeddings reveal intriguing semantic organization. Tasks that are dynamically similar (e.g., sharing embodiment or object types) cluster closely in the embedding space, even if their objectives differ (e.g., "Door Open" and "Door Close"). This suggests the model implicitly captures meaningful relationships between tasks based on their underlying physics and interaction patterns.

5. **Promising Few-Shot Learning Capabilities:** Pre-trained multi-task TD-MPC2 agents can be effectively finetuned to unseen tasks. In the low-data regime (20k environment steps), finetuning yields approximately a 2x improvement over training from scratch, indicating strong transfer learning potential.

6. **Applicability to Visual RL:** TD-MPC2, with a simple convolutional encoder, performs comparably to state-of-the-art visual RL methods (DrQ-v2, DreamerV3) on image-based DMControl tasks, without any hyperparameter changes, demonstrating its versatility across different observation modalities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TD-MPC2 represents a significant step forward in the field of reinforcement learning and embodied AI, with far-reaching implications:

1. **Advancing Generalist AI for Robotics:** The most profound impact lies in its contribution to building truly generalist embodied agents. By demonstrating that a single, robust RL algorithm can learn low-level continuous control across an unprecedented diversity of tasks, embodiments, and action spaces from *uncurated* data, TD-MPC2 directly tackles a major bottleneck in robot learning. This moves away from the impractical reliance on vast datasets of expert demonstrations, opening doors for more scalable and accessible data collection strategies.

2. **Breakthrough in RL Robustness and Scalability:** Historically, deep RL algorithms have been notorious for their sensitivity to hyperparameters and environmental specifics. TD-MPC2's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance across 104 diverse tasks with a *single set of hyperparameters* is a remarkable achievement. This inherent robustness significantly lowers the barrier of entry for applying RL, making it more practical for researchers and practitioners outside of large, resource-rich labs. The clear evidence of performance scaling with model and data size further suggests a path towards even more capable agents in the future, akin to the scaling laws observed in other deep learning domains.

3. **Validation of Implicit World Models:** The success of TD-MPC2 reinforces the efficacy of implicit, decoder-free world models, especially for control. By focusing on predicting outcomes and returns rather than raw observations, the approach proves efficient and effective, particularly for tasks with high visual variation where explicit reconstruction can be challenging and computationally intensive. This could encourage further research into control-centric model learning paradigms.

4. **Enabling Transfer Learning in Robotics:** The promising results in few-shot finetuning demonstrate the potential for pre-trained generalist world models to rapidly adapt to new, unseen tasks. This capability is critical for deploying robots in novel environments without needing to train from scratch, significantly reducing the data and computational resources required for new applications.

5. **Open Science and Community Acceleration:** The public release of 300+ model checkpoints, datasets, and code is a substantial contribution to open science. This generosity will enable researchers worldwide to build upon TD-MPC2, accelerate their own experiments, and foster collaborative development in scalable RL and embodied AI.

6. **Future Research Directions:** TD-MPC2 lays a strong foundation while simultaneously highlighting crucial open problems. It inspires future work on zero-shot generalization to unseen embodiments, more robust finetuning methods, integration with high-level vision-language models for cognitive tasks, and extending the framework to discrete action spaces. The paper also thoughtfully discusses the critical risks associated with powerful generalist models, such as reward misspecification, safety concerns with autonomous physical robots, and the concentration of power due to data costs, inviting the community to address these ethical and practical challenges proactively.

In summary, TD-MPC2 is a landmark contribution that pushes the boundaries of robust and scalable continuous control, offering a tangible path towards realizing highly capable and versatile generalist embodied agents.
