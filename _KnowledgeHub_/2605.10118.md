# Plan in Sandbox, Navigate in Open Worlds: Learning Physics-Grounded Abstracted Experience for Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The SAGE framework enables embodied agents to learn robust navigation capabilities from scratch, leveraging abstracted, physics-grounded experiences synthesized in a sandbox environment. It achieved state-of-the-art performance on embodied navigation benchmarks and demonstrated effective transfer to physical robot deployment.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation agents face data scarcity, lacking large-scale, aligned real-world vision and robot control datasets.
- The Sim2Real transfer problem arises from a modality gap between high-level VLM reasoning and low-level robot actuation, leading to brittle policies in physical environments.
- Reinforcement Learning approaches for embodied navigation suffer from sample inefficiency and the 'cold-start' problem without structured guidance or embodied priors.

## Method

- Introduces **SAGE**, a three-phase framework (==Genesis, Evolution, Navigation==) that generates, optimizes, and deploys embodied navigation policies.
- The Genesis phase leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in a ==physics-grounded sandbox== to synthesize diverse navigation tasks and explicit ==IF-THEN experience rules== from multi-view trajectories, stored in a vector database for retrieval.
- The Evolution phase optimizes the agent's policy using ==hybrid prompt-augmented sampling== with a time-varying injection probability and ==Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping (AAC)==, which applies aggressive upper clipping bounds to augmented samples and conservative bounds to standard ones, within a ==Homogeneous Group Advantage Estimation== (GRPO-style) framework.

## Results

- SAGE (Qwen3-4B) achieved a new state-of-the-art on **A-EQA** with **60.2%** LLM-Match Success Rate (SR†) and **47.2%** SPL†, outperforming baselines and GPT-4o.
- Demonstrated strong performance on **GOAT-Bench**, with SAGE (Qwen3-4B) reaching **64.8%** Success Rate and **44.9%** SPL, significantly surpassing previous methods.
- Successfully deployed the framework on a **physical robot** for real-world indoor navigation and Q&A tasks, showcasing its practical transferability from sandbox learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthesizing physics-grounded, abstracted experiences in a sandbox environment effectively mitigates data scarcity and the Sim2Real gap for embodied navigation agents.
> - An adaptive policy optimization strategy like Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping, combined with prompt augmentation, allows efficient distillation of VLM-generated semantic reasoning into robust control policies.
> - Decomposing complex navigation into VLM-selected subgoals (e.g., Frontier or Memory Nodes) that are executable by a geometric planner facilitates practical grounding of abstract intent into reliable robot behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied navigation has seen significant advancements, particularly with the emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that offer capabilities in open-world perception, semantic reasoning, and zero-shot generalization. Current embodied navigation agents primarily operate under two paradigms: goal-based navigation and Q&A-based navigation.

Despite the progress in VLMs, their application to embodied navigation faces challenges. Unlike passive vision-language tasks that benefit from large-scale datasets, embodied navigation suffers from a scarcity of aligned real-world vision and robot control data. Reinforcement Learning (RL) is often employed to adapt policies, but it frequently encounters the Sim2Real transfer problem due to a modality gap between the semantic reasoning space of VLMs and the continuous actuation space of robots. This gap can lead to brittle policies that struggle to ground high-level intent into robust robot control in real-world, noisy environments. Furthermore, RL agents can suffer from sample inefficiency and the cold-start problem without structured guidance or embodied priors.

This work addresses these challenges by proposing a novel generative experience-driven learning paradigm. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level VLM reasoning and low-level robot control by leveraging abstracted, physics-grounded experiences synthesized in a sandbox environment. This approach mimics human mental simulation and aims to improve data efficiency, transferability, and policy robustness for embodied navigation agents. The research positions itself within the growing area of self-evolving agents, where systems autonomously acquire data, assess performance, and update themselves, but focuses specifically on mitigating the data scarcity and Sim2Real problems inherent to embodied navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, SAGE (Sandbox-Abstracted Grounded Experience), that enables embodied agents to learn robust navigation capabilities from scratch, specifically addressing the limitations of current VLM-based and RL-based approaches in data-scarce, open-world environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges:
1. **Data Scarcity in Embodied Navigation**: Unlike passive vision-language tasks that have vast internet-scale datasets, embodied navigation lacks large, aligned real-world datasets for vision perception and robot control. This limits the ability of VLMs to fully realize their potential in interactive environments.
2. **Sim2Real Transfer Problem**: While simulators offer a cost-effective way to generate data, policies learned in photorealistic simulations often exhibit poor transferability to the real world. The substantial difference between the high-level semantic reasoning of VLMs and the continuous, noisy actuation space of robots creates a significant modality gap, making learned policies brittle when deployed in physical environments.
3. **Sample Inefficiency and Cold-Start Problem in RL**: Traditional RL approaches, while capable of learning control, are often sample-inefficient and struggle with the "cold-start" problem, especially when learning from scratch without prior guidance or structured knowledge.
4. **Bridging High-Level VLM Reasoning and Low-Level Robot Control**: There is a need to effectively distill the rich, high-level reasoning capabilities of VLMs into concrete, robust robot control policies. Existing methods often fail to adequately ground abstract intent plans into executable actions.

To address these, the authors propose an approach inspired by human cognitive processes—mental simulation. Instead of expensive real-world exploration, the idea is to allow VLMs to operate within a physics-grounded sandbox. This sandbox facilitates the synthesis of diverse tasks and the proactive distillation of embodied experiences, thereby transferring VLM's high-level reasoning into the robot's policy in a structured and efficient manner. The core motivation is to overcome data limitations and improve Sim2Real transfer by learning in an abstracted, yet physically consistent, environment, ultimately enabling more robust and generalizable embodied navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SAGE framework operates through three synergistic phases: Genesis, Evolution, and Navigation.

#### 3.1. Genesis Phase

This phase focuses on generating diverse, physics-grounded semantic environments and distilling multi-view trajectories into embodied tasks and experience rules.
- **Sandbox Environment Setup**: Large-scale indoor datasets like HM3D and InteriorGS are used to instantiate a physics-grounded, reward-free Markov Decision Process (MDP). The continuous space is parsed into discrete navigable nodes, and state transitions adhere to physical constraints (e.g., collision-free paths).
- **Task Synthesis**: An automated pipeline generates language-guided sandbox navigation tasks. For each task:
 - Start and goal pairs are randomly sampled.
 - A* algorithm computes an optimal geodesic trajectory.
 - The trajectory is discretized into keypoints. At each keypoint, multi-view observations (forward, left, right) are rendered. The forward view serves as the ground-truth action.
 - Detected objects are projected into a semantic scene graph.
 - A VLM synthesizes a natural language instruction and its corresponding answer based on the trajectory endpoint and visible entities, forming a task tuple `o = (I, τ*, a*, K)`.
- **Experience Rule Synthesis**: To extract explicit navigation knowledge, the VLM is prompted to articulate the rationale for choosing the ground-truth action over other frontiers at each step of the optimal trajectory. These are formulated as IF-THEN reasoning rules ("IF answering/searching [Task] AND observing [Scene Description] THEN prioritize this path.") and stored in a vector database `D_exp`.

#### 3.2. Evolution Phase

This phase optimizes the agent's policy using the synthesized experiences.
- **Reward Shaping**: A shaped reward function `r_ϕ` is defined as a weighted sum of format compliance (VLM output adhering to template) and semantic alignment (correct image selection and textual similarity to ground truth), penalizing errors.
- **Hybrid Prompt-Augmented Sampling**: To balance exploitation of known trajectories and exploration, a dynamic controller is introduced. It injects retrieved experience `K_ret` from `D_exp` into the input context with a time-varying probability `η_t`. `η_t` decreases as validation reward improves, guiding the agent from mimicking priors to autonomous exploration. The input context `x_t` includes the natural language instruction `I_t`, RGB observations `v_t`, and optionally `K_ret`.
- **Homogeneous Group Advantage Estimation**: A group-based relative policy optimization framework (similar to GRPO) is adopted. Advantages `A_i,j` are standardized strictly within homogeneous groups (augmented vs. standard samples) to prevent bias from potentially higher rewards of augmented samples.
- **Policy Update (Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping - AAC)**: The policy update mechanism differentiates constraint strictness. The importance sampling ratio `ρ` is used.
 - A conservative lower clipping bound `(1 - ϵ_std)` is universally applied to all samples to prevent policy collapse.
 - An asymmetric upper clipping threshold `ϵ_up(m_i)` is applied: `ϵ_exp` for augmented samples (`m_i = 1`) and `ϵ_std` for standard samples (`m_i = 0`), with `ϵ_exp >> ϵ_std`. This allows aggressive updates for high-reward augmented samples, accelerating experience absorption, while maintaining conservative updates for standard samples to preserve training stability. A KL divergence term prevents excessive deviation from the foundation model.

#### 3.3. Navigation Phase

This phase translates the internalized semantic priors into planner-executable subgoals for real-world deployment.
- **Perception**: The robot uses RGB-D observations to build a dynamic 3D scene graph and an occupancy map. These continuous data are abstracted into two discrete visual buffers:
 - **Memory Buffer `M_t`**: Stores images and nodes of unique objects/landmarks observed along the trajectory, providing historical context.
 - **Frontier Buffer `F_t`**: Contains candidate frontier images and nodes for exploration, generated by computing boundaries between free and unexplored space.
 - The robot's state representation `R_t` is constructed as `{I_query, M_t, F_t}`.
- **Experience Augmentation**: The most relevant experience `C_ret` is retrieved from `D_exp` based on current observations and the query. The VLM policy `π_θ` then selects an optimal subgoal `a*t` from `F_t ∪ M_t` by maximizing `π_θ(a | R_t, C_ret)`.
- **Planning and Actuation**:
 - If `a*t` is from `M_t`, it indicates a target identification or Q&A goal.
 - If `a*t` is from `F_t`, it signifies an exploration step.
 - The selected subgoal is translated into low-level robot control commands by a geometric planner (Habitat-Sim in simulation, ROS navigation stack in real-world). Movement is limited by a maximum step size (`δ_max = 1.0m`) until the target node is reached or proximity is met. This loop continues until the final goal is reached or max steps are exhausted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results on simulated embodied navigation benchmarks (A-EQA and GOAT-Bench) and includes evidence of transfer to physical indoor robot deployment.

- **Overall Navigation Performance**: SAGE demonstrates performance improvements across benchmarks.
 - On A-EQA, SAGE (Qwen3-2B) achieves a 53.21% LLM-Match Success Rate (SR†), surpassing the 3D-Mem (Qwen3-2B) baseline by 8.9% and even outperforming the 3D-Mem (GPT-4o) closed-source model (52.6%). It also significantly improves SPL† efficiency.
 - Scaling to 4B parameters, SAGE (Qwen3-4B) sets a new A-EQA SR† state-of-the-art at 60.2% and achieves 47.2% SPL†.
 - On GOAT-Bench, SAGE (Qwen3-2B) achieves 56.7% SR and 38.9% SPL, considerably outperforming the 3D-Mem (Qwen3-2B) baseline (46.4% SR, 20.3% SPL) and LLaVA-7B (49.6% SR, 29.4% SPL). The 4B model reaches 64.8% SR and 44.9% SPL.
 - In category-specific analysis on A-EQA, SAGE (Qwen3-4B) shows strong performance in complex reasoning categories like Spatial Understanding (53.2% SR†), Functional Reasoning (65.3% SR†), and Object State Recognition (69.6% SR†).

- **Impact of Sandbox Data**:
 - **Synthetic Data Composition**: A mixed strategy using both HM3D and InteriorGS datasets for sandbox environment generation yielded the highest performance (53.21% SR†), indicating the importance of scene diversity and texture variety for generalization.
 - **Scalability with Minimal Data**: Performance generally increased with more synthetic data. Even with only 12.5% of the available sandbox data, the model achieved a respectable 44.75% SR†, suggesting that the approach can mitigate the need for massive datasets.

- **Analysis on Evolution Phase**:
 - **Model-dependent vs. Experience-dependent**: A dynamic schedule for experience-injection probability (`η_t`) performed best, preserving experience guidance in early training and gradually annealing as validation performance improved. Fixed `η` values were suboptimal, either providing insufficient guidance or over-relying on retrieved experiences.
 - **Conservative vs. Aggressive Optimization**: The Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping (AAC) mechanism, with an optimally relaxed upper bound `ϵ_exp = 1.0` for augmented samples, balanced aggressive experience absorption with training stability. Overly conservative `ϵ_exp` led to underfitting, while overly aggressive values resulted in instability.
 - **Sparse Observation vs. Rich Context**: Inputting 4 visual frames (`v_t=4`) provided the best balance between reasoning capability and input efficiency, showing significant improvement over fewer frames but diminishing returns with more.

- **Ablation Studies**:
 - **Main Components**: Each component of SAGE contributed to performance improvements. Adding real-time experience retrieval (`+C_ret`) provided immediate zero-shot benefits. Sandbox-synthesized tasks (`+Task`) were a primary driver of capability. Integrating Experience Rules (`+Exp`) with Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping (`+AAC`) yielded consistent additional gains, demonstrating successful internalization of priors. The full SAGE framework, which combines optimized policy with real-time `C_ret`, showed synergistic effects.
 - **Navigation Phase**: Training with Genesis and Evolution phases (`+G.&E.`) boosted A-EQA SR† by 6.29% over the zero-shot baseline, confirming policy internalization. The full SAGE framework outperformed random experience injection, indicating that relevant experience retrieval during inference is critical for maximizing success.

- **Real-World Deployment**: The framework demonstrated practical efficacy through successful deployment on a physical robot. A computational cost analysis showed an average latency of approximately 2.7 seconds per navigation step, with VLM inference being the dominant factor. Qualitative examples illustrated the agent's ability to navigate and answer questions in real indoor environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SAGE framework presents a significant contribution to embodied AI, particularly in addressing challenges related to data scarcity and Sim2Real transfer for navigation agents powered by Vision-Language Models.

- **Mitigation of Data Scarcity**: By autonomously synthesizing diverse, physics-grounded tasks and experience rules in a sandbox environment, SAGE offers a scalable and cost-effective approach to generate structured training data. This reduces reliance on expensive and labor-intensive real-world data collection, making advanced embodied navigation more accessible.
- **Improved Sim2Real Transfer and Robustness**: The framework's emphasis on learning from abstracted, physics-grounded experiences, rather than solely photorealistic simulations, helps bridge the modality gap between high-level VLM reasoning and low-level robot control. This leads to more robust policies that exhibit encouraging transfer capabilities to physical robots in real-world environments.
- **Effective Integration of VLM Reasoning and Robot Control**: SAGE successfully distills the high-level semantic reasoning of VLMs into planner-executable subgoals. The decomposition of navigation into selecting task-relevant Frontier or Memory Nodes, executed by a geometric planner, provides a practical mechanism to ground abstract reasoning into reliable robot behaviors.
- **Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Stability**: The Evolution phase, with its hybrid prompt-augmented sampling and Asymmetric Adaptive Clipping mechanism, enables the policy to rapidly internalize beneficial priors while maintaining training stability. This is crucial for efficient policy optimization in complex environments.
- **Competitive Performance with Smaller Models**: The research demonstrates that, by internalizing sandbox-derived priors, smaller open-source models (e.g., Qwen3-2B) can achieve performance competitive with, and in some cases surpass, larger proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o) on specific benchmarks. This highlights the potential for developing performant embodied agents without requiring massive computational resources or reliance on closed-source APIs.
- **Foundational for Self-Evolving Agents**: The generative experience-driven learning paradigm contributes to the broader field of self-evolving AI agents by offering a mechanism for autonomous data acquisition and policy improvement in embodied tasks.

The potential impact of SAGE includes accelerating the development of more capable, autonomous, and general-purpose embodied agents for various applications such as assistive robotics, logistics, and exploration in complex indoor environments. However, the authors acknowledge limitations, including its current optimization for indoor environments, assumed quasi-static environments, reliance on reliable geometric planners, and limited scale of real-world validation. Future work could address these to further enhance the framework's applicability and robustness.
