# Model Merging in LLMs, MLLMs, and Beyond: Methods, Theories, Applications and Opportunities

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey of model merging techniques provides a novel two-phase taxonomy for classification and extensively reviews existing methods, their applications across large language models, multimodal models, and other machine learning subfields, alongside theoretical insights and future research directions.

## Problem

- The absence of a systematic and comprehensive review of model merging, despite its rapid evolution and increasing adoption across various ML fields.
- The need for efficient, often data-free, knowledge integration from multiple pre-trained machine learning models to avoid prohibitive computational costs and data requirements.
- Challenges in effectively combining the strengths of an increasing number of specialized, open-source model checkpoints.

## Method

- A systematic and comprehensive survey offering a novel, ==two-phase taxonomic framework== to classify model merging methods into =="Pre-Merging" and "During-Merging"== strategies.
- Extensive review of methodologies, including merger-friendly fine-tuning, ==weight alignment==, weighted merging, ==subspace methods==, optimization-based techniques, and routing-based approaches.
- A detailed analysis of model merging's applications across foundation models (LLMs, MLLMs, visual generative models) and over ten diverse machine learning subfields.

## Results

- The establishment of a novel, two-phase taxonomic framework successfully categorizes and clarifies diverse model merging techniques.
- Model merging is shown to be widely applicable, enhancing capabilities like human value alignment in LLMs, multimodal fusion in MLLMs, and style mixing in visual generative models, while also mitigating catastrophic forgetting in continual learning.
- Empirical results indicate that advanced merging techniques significantly outperform basic methods, but a performance gap often remains compared to individually fine-tuned expert models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model merging methods can be systematically categorized into two phases: "Pre-Merging" techniques that prepare models for combination, and "During-Merging" strategies for parameter aggregation.
> - Model merging acts as a versatile and efficient knowledge integration paradigm, applicable to foundation models (LLMs, MLLMs, visual generative models) and various ML subfields to enhance capabilities or address specific challenges.
> - Despite empirical advancements, the theoretical understanding of model merging remains constrained, lacking a unified framework, especially for heterogeneous cross-task merging.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of machine learning, especially with the advent of foundation models, faces a critical challenge: integrating diverse knowledge and capabilities efficiently without incurring prohibitive computational costs or requiring extensive raw training data. Model merging, as highlighted by this survey, emerges as a potent solution in this landscape.

The paper effectively contextualizes model merging by distinguishing it from related paradigms, such as single-task learning, multi-task learning, federated learning, and ensemble learning. While these approaches aim to facilitate knowledge fusion, they often suffer from limitations like high memory costs, the need for data access, multiple communication rounds, or lack of cross-task knowledge transfer. Model merging, in contrast, directly aggregates parameters of well-trained expert models, making it highly efficient, often data-free, and computationally inexpensive. This positions model merging as a particularly attractive paradigm for knowledge integration, especially in scenarios where data privacy is paramount or computational resources are limited.

The proliferation of pre-trained and fine-tuned model checkpoints in open-source repositories (e.g., HuggingFace, torchvision, timm) has further accelerated the relevance and development of model merging. As the community generates an ever-increasing number of specialized models, the need for methods to efficiently combine their strengths becomes critical.

Before this work, the literature lacked a systematic and comprehensive review of model merging techniques that could keep pace with its rapid evolution. Existing efforts, such as MergeKit and FusionBench, primarily served as benchmarks with limited coverage of methods. Other works, like Zheng et al. and the survey by Li et al., either treated model merging as a minor subsection or covered a narrower range of applications and recent advancements. This paper addresses this significant gap by offering an exhaustive overview, a novel taxonomy, and a broad discussion of applications and future directions, thereby serving as a foundational resource for the burgeoning field of model merging. It effectively consolidates disparate research efforts and provides a coherent framework for understanding and advancing the technology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is to bridge the significant gap in the literature regarding a systematic and thorough review of model merging techniques, particularly given their increasing prevalence across various machine learning fields and the rapid evolution of the domain. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive understanding of the available techniques, their advantages, limitations, and applications.

Specifically, the paper articulates three key objectives:

1. **Systematizing Existing Model Merging Methods:** The authors aim to introduce a novel taxonomic approach to classify and exhaustively discuss existing model merging methods. This involves organizing techniques into logical categories and subcategories to clarify their underlying principles and mechanisms.
2. **Exploring Applications Across Diverse Domains:** A major objective is to extensively cover the application of model merging techniques. This includes a detailed discussion of its use in large language models (LLMs), multimodal large language models (MLLMs), visual generative models, and more than ten other machine learning subfields (e.g., continual learning, multi-task learning, few-shot learning). This highlights the broad utility and impact of model merging.
3. **Identifying Challenges and Future Opportunities:** The survey seeks to highlight the remaining challenges in model merging and propose future research directions. This forward-looking perspective is crucial for guiding subsequent research efforts and fostering the continued development of the field.

By fulfilling these objectives, the authors intend for this survey to serve as a vital resource for researchers and practitioners, offering a structured understanding of model merging, illuminating its diverse applications, and pointing toward promising avenues for future innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and comprehensive approach to survey the vast landscape of model merging. Their methodology is structured to provide a clear and exhaustive understanding of the topic, encompassing theoretical foundations, practical methods, diverse applications, and future research directions.

The core of their approach is a **novel taxonomic framework** that categorizes model merging methods into two main phases:
1. **Pre-Merging Methods:** These techniques aim to create optimal conditions for merging before the actual parameter aggregation takes place. Subcategories include:
 - **Merger-Friendly Fine-Tuning:** Designing fine-tuning strategies (e.g., linearization fine-tuning, sharpness-aware fine-tuning, subspace fine-tuning) that yield models more amenable to merging by promoting properties like weight disentanglement or reduced parameter conflicts.
 - **Architecture Transformation:** Techniques to convert heterogeneous models (those with different architectures) into homogeneous ones, enabling direct parameter-level merging, often through knowledge distillation or layer addition.
 - **Weight Re-basin or Alignment:** Methods (e.g., optimal transport, heuristic neuron alignment, learning-based alignment) that permute or align the weights of models to place them in the same "basin" within the loss landscape, facilitating smoother interpolation and reducing interference.

2. **During-Merging Methods:** These focus on the actual strategies for combining multiple models into a single one, specifically addressing task conflict and interference problems. Subcategories include:
 - **Basic Merging Methods:** Simple approaches like weighted averaging or Task Arithmetic, which combine parameters or "task vectors" (difference between fine-tuned and pre-trained models).
 - **Weighted-based Merging Methods:** Techniques that assign different importance weights to models or parameters based on specific rules, often learned through optimization, evolutionary algorithms, or Bayesian methods (e.g., Fisher information, singular value decomposition).
 - **Subspace-based Merging Methods:** Approaches that project models into sparse or low-rank subspaces (e.g., by pruning parameters based on magnitude, sensitivity, or using SVD for low-rank approximation) to mitigate interference and preserve essential information.
 - **Optimization-based Merging Methods:** Methods that formulate an optimization objective, often data-free, to minimize inter-task interference or information loss by exploiting intrinsic properties of task vectors (e.g., orthogonality).
 - **Routing-based Merging Methods:** Dynamic methods that adaptively merge models or subsets of layers based on the input sample or task during inference, often employing gating or expert networks.
 - **Post-calibration-based Merging Methods:** Techniques applied after initial merging to correct biases or reduce knowledge loss in the merged model, such as representation surgery to align feature spaces.

Beyond this detailed methodological breakdown, the paper also dedicates sections to:
- **Theoretical and Experimental Analysis:** Discussing existing theoretical explanations for model merging (e.g., linear mode connectivity, flat minima, neural tangent kernel) and providing empirical comparisons through a review of various benchmarks and performance across different model architectures and tasks.
- **Comprehensive Application Review:** Systematically outlining how model merging is applied across foundation models (LLMs, MLLMs, Visual Generative Models) and over ten diverse machine learning subfields (continual learning, multi-task learning, federated learning, etc.), showcasing its utility in solving specific challenges.
- **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** Concluding with a discussion of open problems and promising research avenues to guide future work in the field.

The inclusion of a public GitHub repository (https://github.com/EnnengYang/Awesome-Model-Merging-Methods-Theories-Applications) further supports their approach by providing a dynamic and accessible list of relevant papers, enhancing the resourcefulness of the survey.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's comprehensive analysis yields several key findings across methodologies, applications, theoretical underpinnings, and empirical performance.

#### 4.1. Methodology Overview

The survey successfully establishes a novel, two-phase taxonomy for model merging: **Pre-Merging** and **During-Merging**. This classification system provides a clear framework for understanding the diverse strategies employed.
- **Pre-Merging methods** (merger-friendly fine-tuning, architecture transformation, weight re-basin/alignment) are crucial for setting up favorable conditions, ensuring models are compatible or operate in a shared "basin" within the loss landscape.
- **During-Merging methods** (basic, weighted, subspace, optimization, routing, post-calibration) represent the core parameter aggregation techniques. These range from simple averaging to sophisticated, data-free optimization schemes and dynamic, input-conditioned merging. The comparative summary in Table 2 highlights trade-offs between parameters, tuning-freeness, data requirements, merging patterns (static/dynamic), and weighting granularity, demonstrating that no single method is universally optimal.

#### 4.2. Application Overview

Model merging proves to be a versatile and efficient empowerment technique with widespread applicability:
- **Foundation Models:**
 - **Large Language Models (LLMs):** Merging enhances human value alignment (e.g., personalized preferences, safer alignment), facilitates detoxification by reducing harmful content generation, enables knowledge unlearning (e.g., removing copyrighted data), accelerates training by merging checkpoints, speeds up inference in MoE-based LLMs by expert merging, and improves reasoning efficiency by combining "slow-thinking" and "fast" models. It also effectively combines capabilities of expert LLMs into a unified model.
 - **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** Merging supports multimodal fusion, integrating knowledge from different modalities (e.g., text, image, audio, video) into a single model, and enables cross-modal knowledge transfer, especially from high-resource to low-resource modalities.
 - **Visual Generative Models:** Model merging is crucial for style mixing (e.g., combining LoRAs for content and artistic styles in diffusion models) and reducing training costs by interpolating intermediate training trajectories.
- **Machine Learning Subfields:**
 - **Continual Learning (CL):** Merging models for old and new tasks is shown to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and maintain new learning abilities without needing to cache old data or expand network architecture linearly.
 - **Multi-Task/Domain/Objective/Auxiliary Learning (MTL/MDL/MOO/ATL):** Model merging is an effective paradigm for knowledge transfer, allowing a single model to perform multiple tasks, adapt to various domains, optimize for conflicting objectives, or leverage auxiliary tasks without expensive joint training or negative transfer issues.
 - **Out-of-Distribution/Domain Generalization (OODG/DG):** Techniques like Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) and Model Soups, which are forms of model merging, improve generalization to unseen data or domains by converging to flatter minima or combining diverse expert models.
 - **Federated Learning (FL):** Model merging is fundamental for aggregating local models from clients into a global model, with methods ranging from simple weighted averages to permutation-invariant neuron matching.
 - **Zero-shot/Few-shot Learning (ZSL/FSL):** Merging pre-trained or parameter-efficient modules (e.g., LoRAs) significantly enhances cross-task generalization in low-resource settings, enabling capabilities like cross-lingual transfer and adaptive performance for unseen tasks.
 - **Adversarial Learning:** Model merging can be exploited for attacks (e.g., injecting backdoors via poisoned LoRAs) but also serves as a defense mechanism (e.g., mitigating poisoning attacks, enhancing robustness, or protecting intellectual property).

#### 4.3. Theoretical and Empirical Insights

- **Theoretical Analysis:** The theoretical understanding of model merging is still limited. Existing work explains merging through:
 - **Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC):** Suggesting that properly aligned model weights can be linearly interpolated along paths of low loss, particularly when models share initialization or tasks.
 - **Flat Minima:** Correlating flatter loss landscapes with better generalization, explaining why weight averaging can improve out-of-distribution performance.
 - **Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK):** Explaining how "approximately orthogonal" task vectors, derived from NTK feature space, can mitigate conflicts during cross-task merging.
 However, these theories often rely on specific assumptions (e.g., shared initialization, wide-network limits) and a unified, comprehensive theoretical framework for heterogeneous cross-task merging remains absent.
- **Empirical Comparisons:** Benchmarks like MergeKit, FusionBench, and MergeBench provide platforms for evaluating different merging strategies. The empirical results (Figure 6) consistently show:
 - Pre-trained models perform poorly, and individual models set the upper bound.
 - Simple merging methods (e.g., Weight Averaging, Task Arithmetic) often lead to significant performance degradation compared to individual expert models.
 - More sophisticated techniques, such as weighted merging, subspace-based methods, and optimization-based approaches (e.g., WUDI-Merging), generally achieve substantial performance improvements.
 - Dynamic merging methods (e.g., WEMoE) often achieve performance closest to the upper bound, albeit with increased inference costs.
 - Larger parameter models tend to be easier to merge.
 - Despite advancements, a noticeable performance gap persists between merged models and individually fine-tuned expert models across various domains, particularly for complex tasks or a large number of merged models.

#### 4.4. Remaining Challenges and Future Directions

The paper identifies seven critical areas for future research:
1. **Closing the Performance Gap:** Reducing the disparity between merged models and independent experts, especially for numerous or highly diverse tasks, under more relaxed fine-tuning conditions.
2. **In-depth Theoretical Analysis:** Developing a more comprehensive theoretical framework, especially for cross-task and heterogeneous model merging, to provide stronger guarantees and explanations.
3. **Trustworthy Model Merging:** Addressing intellectual property protection (active/passive defense) and mitigating poisoning attacks by developing robust merging schemes.
4. **Effective and Efficient Model Merging:** Designing methods that are performant while also being highly efficient in terms of memory and computational resources, ideally without requiring additional training or data.
5. **Merging Heterogeneous Models:** Developing techniques to merge models with different architectures efficiently and without costly architectural transformations.
6. **Merger-Friendly Fine-tuning Strategies:** Innovating fine-tuning procedures that inherently produce models more amenable to merging, minimizing subsequent performance degradation.
7. **Interdisciplinary Application:** Exploring new application scenarios and adapting model merging strategies across different subfields of machine learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a highly significant contribution to the machine learning community by providing the first truly comprehensive and systematic overview of model merging. Its detailed taxonomy, extensive review of applications, and forward-looking discussion of challenges and opportunities make it an indispensable resource for researchers and practitioners alike.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Standardization and Clarification:** By introducing a novel and thorough taxonomy, the paper brings much-needed order and clarity to a rapidly expanding and often fragmented field. This standardized framework will help researchers classify new methods, understand existing ones, and identify underexplored areas.
- **Accelerating Research and Development:** The exhaustive review of methodologies and applications provides a solid foundation for future research. It consolidates disparate knowledge, making it easier for new entrants to grasp the field and for experienced researchers to identify promising avenues. The explicit outlining of open challenges and future directions will undoubtedly guide the next generation of innovations in model merging.
- **Enhancing Model Efficiency and Capabilities:** Model merging offers a pragmatic solution to critical challenges in modern AI, particularly with large foundation models. Its ability to integrate knowledge without extensive retraining or data access directly translates to significant reductions in computational costs, memory footprint, and development time. This efficiency is crucial for the sustainable scaling of AI systems.
- **Addressing Practical and Ethical Concerns:** The survey highlights how model merging can be leveraged to address crucial practical issues, such as personalized model alignment, detoxification of LLMs, and knowledge unlearning, which are vital for building responsible and ethical AI systems. Its role in adversarial learning, for both attacks and defenses, also underscores its importance in model security and intellectual property protection.
- **Promoting Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** By showcasing the wide array of applications across various ML subfields and foundation models, the paper encourages cross-pollination of ideas and techniques, potentially leading to novel solutions inspired by successes in different domains.

In essence, "Model Merging in LLMs, MLLMs, and Beyond" is more than just a review; it is a foundational roadmap that systematizes a burgeoning field, underscores its profound practical implications, and articulates a clear vision for its future development. As an efficient and modular solution for model empowerment, model merging, as thoroughly elucidated in this survey, is poised to play an increasingly pivotal role in the landscape of artificial intelligence.
