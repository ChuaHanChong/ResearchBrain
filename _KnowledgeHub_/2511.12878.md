# Uni-Hand: Universal Hand Motion Forecasting in Egocentric Views

> [!summary] Summary
> Uni-Hand is a universal egocentric hand-motion forecasting framework that fuses RGB, point clouds, waypoints, and text via a dual-branch diffusion model, jointly predicting head egomotion and hand motion with target indicators for multi-joint 2D/3D waypoints plus contact states, achieving state-of-the-art forecasting and strong human-robot policy transfer.

## Problem

- Existing hand motion forecasting methods only predict coarse hand-center waypoints, ignoring finger/wrist joint motion and hand-object contact/separation timing needed for manipulation.
- Prior HMF pipelines rely mainly on 2D RGB video without metric-scale 3D perception or text interfaces, causing geometric ambiguity and no way to inject language instructions.
- Prior works encode past head egomotion into hand features but never explicitly forecast future head motion, so hand prediction lacks conditioning on incoming head-hand coordination.

## Method

- ==Dual-branch diffusion model== that concurrently denoises ==egomotion-forecasting (EMF)== latents via ==vanilla Mamba== and ==hand-motion-forecasting (HMF)== latents, explicitly coupling future head and hand movement.
- ==Hybrid Mamba-Transformer module (HMTM)== combining egomotion-aware Mamba blocks with a ==structure-aware Transformer== (voxel cross-attention) and ==task-aware Transformer== (text-embedding cross-attention) for denoising.
- ==VL-fusion module== with ==target indicators== (one-hot embeddings) letting one diffusion model forecast hand center, wrist, or finger joints in both 2D and 3D, plus an ==interaction-state decoder== for contact/separation timing.

## Results

- Uni-Hand achieves lowest or near-lowest ADE/FDE across EgoPAT3D-DT, H2O-PT, HOT3D-Clips, Ego4D, and CABH against MADiff, Diff-IP, EMAG, S-Mamba, and USST baselines in both 2D and 3D.
- On the new Hand-ALOHA-Transfer benchmark, Uni-Hand reaches **100%/90%/80%/80%/20%** success across five manipulation tasks versus S-Mamba's **40%/20%/0%/0%/0%**, with **0%** failure on harder tasks.
- Ablations show removing egomotion forecasting, target indicators, or task-aware Transformer each degrades ADE/FDE and success rate (e.g., TAT removal drops SR from **80%** to **40%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly forecasting future head egomotion, not just conditioning on past egomotion, provides useful spatiotemporal constraints for hand trajectory prediction.
> - Injecting task text embeddings only once into fused features is insufficient; repeated cross-attention injection through the denoising stack is needed to keep task specificity from washing out.
> - Predicted hand motion features transfer as generic affordances, boosting downstream action anticipation/recognition and robot policy execution beyond the forecasting task itself.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Predicting future human behaviors, particularly hand movements, is a critical area of research with applications in augmented reality, human-robot interaction, and assistive technologies. Prior work has explored hand trajectory prediction (HTP) in egocentric (first-person) views, where models receive past egocentric observations to forecast future hand waypoints. These methods primarily focus on predicting 2D or 3D positions of hand centers.

Existing HMF paradigms generally exhibit several limitations. Current approaches predominantly focus on predicting only hand centers, neglecting fine-grained joint movements and interaction states like hand-object contact or separation. Most methods rely solely on 2D egocentric video inputs, which can introduce geometric ambiguity in 3D motion forecasting and lack explicit integration of 3D spatial information or textual instructions for task awareness. Furthermore, while some models encode past head egomotion, they typically do not explicitly forecast future head movements, thereby potentially missing the synergy between head and hand motion in egocentric vision. Lastly, the evaluation of HMF models has largely been confined to trajectory error metrics, with limited validation of their applicability in diverse downstream tasks such as robotic manipulation or action recognition.

Against this background, Uni-Hand aims to advance the field by proposing a more comprehensive framework that addresses these limitations. It seeks to provide a universal solution for hand motion forecasting by integrating multi-modal inputs, enabling multi-dimensional and multi-target predictions, and demonstrating utility across multiple downstream applications. This work builds upon and extends previous diffusion-based models for hand motion analysis, as well as imitation learning techniques for human-robot policy transfer, by offering a more unified and versatile approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop Uni-Hand, a universal hand motion forecasting framework capable of handling multi-modal inputs, performing multi-dimensional and multi-target predictions, and providing multi-task affordances for downstream applications. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in the existing hand motion forecasting (HMF) literature:

1. **Insufficient Prediction Targets:** Current HMF paradigms primarily focus on forecasting the future center points of a hand's bounding box. This coarse-grained prediction overlooks the movement of individual hand joints (e.g., wrist, fingers) in 2D and 3D space, which are crucial for understanding fine hand poses and precise interaction. Additionally, existing methods do not typically predict the exact timings of hand-object contact and separation, which are vital for practical applications like human-robot manipulation that require "when to grasp" information. Uni-Hand aims to address this by forecasting specific joint waypoints and hand-object interaction states.

2. **Modality Gaps:** Many existing HMF approaches predominantly use 2D egocentric video as input, often neglecting metric-scale 3D spatial observations. This can lead to geometric ambiguity in 3D motion forecasting and may limit the physical plausibility of predicted interactions. Furthermore, the absence of textual modality interfaces prevents the explicit incorporation of human intentions or task instructions, creating a discrepancy between model optimization and language-conditioned real-world deployments. Uni-Hand is motivated to bridge these gaps by incorporating 2D RGB images, 3D point clouds, and text prompts as inputs.

3. **Lack of Prediction-Level Head Motion Conditioning:** Human hand motion is often kinetically coupled with whole-body movements, particularly head motion which provides visual context. Previous works have encoded past headset camera egomotion into hand state transitions to implicitly link head and hand features. However, they do not explicitly forecast future head motion to condition hand motion prediction. This limits the model's ability to comprehend future hand motion patterns in the context of anticipated hand-head cooperation. Uni-Hand aims to improve this by concurrently predicting human head and hand movements through a dual-branch diffusion model.

4. **Limited Validation in Downstream Tasks:** The evaluation of existing HMF frameworks often prioritizes reducing errors between predicted and ground-truth hand waypoints. While important, their practical utility for downstream applications has not been extensively explored. Robotic manipulation, in particular, can benefit from predicted human hand trajectories and interaction states for action planning and policy transfer. Similarly, predicted hand motion features could enhance performance in action anticipation and recognition tasks. Uni-Hand is motivated to establish novel benchmarks to comprehensively assess its real-world applicability in human-robot policy transfer and as a feature enhancer for action understanding tasks.

By addressing these limitations, Uni-Hand seeks to provide a more robust, versatile, and practically relevant solution for hand motion forecasting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Uni-Hand is designed as a universal framework for multi-dimensional and multi-target hand motion forecasting in egocentric views, leveraging multi-modal inputs and a dual-branch diffusion model.

#### 3.1. System Overview

Given past egocentric observations (2D RGB images and 3D point clouds), past hand waypoints, and a text prompt, Uni-Hand predicts future hand trajectories (2D or 3D waypoints for hand centers or specific joints) and future hand-object interaction states (contact/separation). The framework operates by first extracting features from these multi-modal inputs, then utilizing a dual-branch diffusion model to concurrently predict future hand and head motion latents, which are finally decoded into trajectories and interaction states.

#### 3.2. Multi-Modal Feature Extraction

- **Egomotion Encoder:** This module processes a sequence of past RGB images to calculate sequential homography matrices, representing headset camera egomotion. These homographies are then encoded into *past egomotion (EM) latents* using Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). For training, ground-truth future EM latents are derived similarly from future image frames; for inference, noisy future EM latents are sampled.
- **VL-Fusion Module:** This module fuses visual features, past waypoint features, and textual information to generate *hand motion (HM) latents*.
 - Pretrained GLIP is used to generate vision-language fusion features from past RGB images and a universal text prompt (e.g., "hand").
 - MLPs encode past hand waypoints into waypoint features.
 - For task-specific forecasting, CLIP generates *task-aware text embeddings* from explicit instructions (e.g., "push the block onto the cloth").
 - Crucially, *target indicators* (tiled one-hot embeddings representing the desired prediction target, e.g., hand center, wrist, thumb tip) are concatenated with the fused features. This allows the model to differentiate and predict various hand targets within a unified framework.
 - All these concatenated features are then fused by MLPs to produce past and (for training) future HM latents.
- **Voxel Encoder:** This module processes 3D point cloud observations to incorporate 3D structure awareness.
 - **Point Cloud Preprocessing:** MobileSAM is used to remove arm-related depth points, purifying the point clouds. These purified point clouds from past observations are transformed into a unified global coordinate system via visual odometry and then aggregated into dense voxel grids.
 - **Voxel Encoding:** A 3D convolutional network encodes these voxel grids into sparser *voxel patches*, which serve as 3D global context.

#### 3.3. Dual-Branch Diffusion Model

This core component is designed to capture the synergy between head and hand movements, concurrently predicting their future latents.
- **Hand-Head Motion Coordination:** The framework acknowledges that hand movements are often conditioned on head motion (for visual cues), and vice-versa, with humans tending to keep hands within egocentric views. Explicitly coordinating these motions is hypothesized to improve prediction accuracy.
- **EMF Diffusion (Ego-Motion Forecasting Diffusion):** This branch denoises future EM latents, conditioned on past EM latents. It uses vanilla Mamba blocks as the denoising model for computational efficiency, predicting future head motion trends without explicitly decoding them into homography matrices.
- **HMF Diffusion (Hand-Motion Forecasting Diffusion):** This branch forecasts future HM latents, conditioned on past HM latents and the future EM latents predicted by the EMF diffusion. It employs a novel *Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Module (HMTM)* as its denoising model:
 - **Stacked Egomotion-Aware Mamba (EAM):** Adapted from MADiff, EAM blocks incorporate the holistic sequence of past and *predicted future* EM latents into Mamba's state transitions via Motion-Driven Selective Scan (MDSS). This integrates head motion awareness into hand motion modeling.
 - **Structure-Aware Transformer (SAT):** This component uses multi-head cross-attention to incorporate the *voxel patches* (3D global context) into the HM latents. The HM latents act as the query, and voxel patches as key/value, enabling the model to understand 3D environmental layout and avoid collisions.
 - **Task-Aware Transformer (TAT):** For task-specific scenarios, TAT performs multi-head cross-attention between HM latents and tiled *task-aware text embeddings*. This continuously injects task information, enhancing the model's ability to generate hand motions compliant with specific instructions.
 The denoised future HM latents from HMF diffusion are then decoded by MLP-based Hand Trajectory Decoder and Interaction State Decoder into future hand waypoints and interaction states.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference

- **Partial Noising and Denoising:** The model employs partial noising/denoising, anchoring past latents and overriding them with initial values after each diffusion step.
- **Loss Functions:** Uni-Hand is trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of six loss functions:
 - `L_em_VLB`: Variational Lower Bound loss for EMF diffusion.
 - `L_hm_VLB`: Variational Lower Bound loss for HMF diffusion.
 - `L_dis`: Trajectory displacement loss (Euclidean distance).
 - `L_angle`: Trajectory angle loss (cosine similarity of motion vectors).
 - `L_reg`: Regularization term penalizing differences between pseudo-trajectory predictions (from GT latents) and ground truth.
 - `L_int`: Binary cross-entropy loss for interaction state prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Uni-Hand was evaluated across multiple public datasets and newly proposed benchmarks, demonstrating its capabilities in multi-dimensional, multi-target hand motion forecasting, and its utility in various downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Multi-Dimensional Hand Trajectory Prediction (Hand Centers)

- **Quantitative Performance:** Uni-Hand consistently demonstrated the lowest prediction errors (ADE and FDE) compared to state-of-the-art baselines across 3D and 2D hand center forecasting on EgoPAT3D-DT, H2O-PT, HOT3D-Clips, Ego4D, and the CABH benchmark (Tables 1-4).
 - On EgoPAT3D-DT (seen), Uni-Hand achieved 0.170 ADE and 0.336 FDE (3D) and 0.064 ADE and 0.109 FDE (2D), outperforming the next best baselines.
 - Similar improvements were observed on unseen data, H2O-PT, HOT3D-Clips (a small data volume dataset), and Ego4D, suggesting robustness and generalizability.
 - The consistent superiority across both 2D and 3D spaces indicates the effectiveness of Uni-Hand's multi-modal fusion.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visualizations (Figures 11-12) indicated that Uni-Hand's predictions for hand center waypoints exhibited better stability and directionality compared to baselines, suggesting a more accurate capture of hand motion patterns and intentions.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Multi-Target Hand Trajectory Prediction (Multiple Joints)

- **Quantitative Performance:** Uni-Hand also outperformed baselines in forecasting waypoints for multiple hand joints (wrist, thumb, index finger) in 3D space on H2O-PT, HOT3D-Clips, and the CABH-E (extended) benchmark (Table 5).
 - On CABH-E, Uni-Hand achieved 0.038 ADE and 0.046 FDE, significantly reducing errors compared to the second-best baseline.
 - The ablation study on target indicators demonstrated their critical role, with "Uni-Hand w/o X_tar" showing higher errors than the full Uni-Hand model (0.047 ADE vs. 0.038 ADE on CABH-E), confirming their effectiveness in distinguishing prediction targets.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visualizations (Figure 13) showed that Uni-Hand generated plausible joint waypoints that extended in correct directions, successfully guiding interaction with target objects.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Downstream Tasks

- **Human-Robot Manipulation Policy Transfer (Hand-ALOHA-Transfer Benchmark):**
 - **Prediction Accuracy:** On the validation set, Uni-Hand showed lower ADE and FDE for 3D trajectory prediction and lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for contact-separation transition timings compared to S-Mamba (Table 6).
 - **Real-World Success Rates:** In real-world robotic trials, Uni-Hand achieved significantly higher success rates across all five manipulation tasks (Push, Pick-and-Place, Harder Pick-and-Place, Language-Conditioned Pick-and-Place, Long-Horizon Pick-and-Place) (Table 6). For complex tasks like "Harder Pick-and-Place," "Language-Conditioned Pick-and-Place," and "Long-Horizon Pick-and-Place," Uni-Hand achieved success rates of 80%, 80%, and 20% respectively, while S-Mamba failed in all trials (0% SR).
 - This demonstrates Uni-Hand's ability to generate executable trajectories and gripper actions, supporting atomic skills, language-conditioned tasks, and long-horizon tasks.
- **Action Anticipation (Epic-Kitchens-55):**
 - Incorporating Uni-Hand's hand motion (HM) features into the vanilla RU-LSTM framework (RGB and flow branches) consistently increased Top-5 verb/noun/action accuracy across most anticipation times (Figure 15). This indicates that the predicted HM features effectively provide future activity information.
- **Early Action Recognition and Action Recognition (Epic-Kitchens-55):**
 - Adding Uni-Hand's HM features improved recognition accuracy for both early action recognition (partial observations) and action recognition (complete observations) (Table 7).
 - Further improvements were observed when HM features encompassed multi-joint motion information, validating the advantages of multi-target predictions for action recognition.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Camera Egomotion Forecasting:** Forecasting future headset camera egomotion improved hand prediction accuracy. Removing the EMF Diffusion ("Uni-Hand w/o EMF") led to higher ADE/FDE compared to the full Uni-Hand (Table 8, Figure 16), confirming the importance of modeling hand-head motion synergy.
- **Camera Egomotion Representation:** Using homography matrices for egomotion modeling yielded better HMF performance than using 6-DOF poses (SE(3)) (Table 8), suggesting homography is better suited for egocentric vision contexts where hands are constrained to 2D image planes.
- **Multi-Modal Inputs:** Incrementally adding past hand waypoints, RGB images, universal text prompts, and point clouds progressively enhanced forecasting performance (Table 9), validating the benefits of harmonizing multi-modal information.
- **Text Embedding Injection:** The Task-Aware Transformer (TAT) significantly reduced prediction errors and increased success rates in language-conditioned tasks (Table 10), demonstrating the effectiveness of continuous injection of task-aware text embeddings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Uni-Hand represents a notable advancement in egocentric hand motion forecasting, introducing several algorithmic innovations and demonstrating practical utility across diverse applications.

**Algorithmic Significance:**
- **Universal Framework:** Uni-Hand provides a unified framework capable of multi-dimensional (2D/3D) and multi-target (hand center, wrist, various finger joints) forecasting. This extends the scope of traditional HTP which typically focuses on hand centers.
- **Hand-Head Motion Synergy:** The novel dual-branch diffusion architecture is a significant contribution, explicitly coordinating and concurrently predicting future head egomotion and hand movements. This aligns better with human behavior planning and provides stronger spatiotemporal constraints for hand trajectory prediction.
- **Interaction-Aware Prediction:** By jointly predicting hand trajectories and hand-object contact/separation states, Uni-Hand moves beyond purely kinematic forecasting. This provides richer motion knowledge crucial for physical interaction.
- **Multi-Modal Integration:** The harmonious integration of 2D visual cues, 3D point cloud data (for global scene context and collision avoidance), past hand waypoints, and task-aware text prompts addresses long-standing modality gaps in egocentric HMF. The introduction of structure-aware and task-aware transformers specifically leverages these modalities.
- **Task-Conditioned Generation:** The injection of task-aware text embeddings into the diffusion denoising process allows the model to align hand motion generation with high-level task intentions, moving towards more intelligent and context-aware predictions.
- **Target Indicators:** The use of devised target indicators enables a single model to forecast heterogeneous hand targets within a unified denoising process, enhancing flexibility and efficiency.

**Practical Impact and Applications:**
- **Human-Robot Policy Transfer:** The most prominent impact is demonstrated through its strong performance in human-robot manipulation policy transfer. Uni-Hand's ability to generate accurate 3D end-effector trajectories and precise gripper actions (contact/separation states) from human demonstrations significantly facilitates robotic manipulation for atomic skills, language-conditioned tasks, and long-horizon operations. This contributes to the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
- **Embodied Intelligence:** By providing a deeper understanding of human hand-object interaction, Uni-Hand contributes to the broader goal of embodied intelligence, where agents can intelligently perceive and interact with their environments.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Assistive Technologies:** The improved accuracy and richness of hand motion forecasts can enhance AR applications that require predicting user interactions or support assistive technologies that anticipate user needs.
- **Action Understanding:** The demonstrated enhancement of action anticipation, early action recognition, and action recognition models by incorporating Uni-Hand's hand motion features indicates its potential to provide valuable semantic information for understanding human activities from egocentric videos.

**Future Research Directions:**
The release of code, pretrained models, and novel benchmarks for downstream tasks (CABH and HAT) will provide valuable resources for the research community, potentially inspiring further investigation into multi-target, interaction-aware, and task-conditioned generative models for human-object interaction. The authors' stated future plan to incorporate tactile sensing suggests a continued effort to integrate more sensory modalities for contact-rich human-robot interaction, pointing towards even more physically grounded predictions.
