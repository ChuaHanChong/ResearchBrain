# Cambrian-S: Towards Spatial Supersensing in Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NYU and Stanford introduce a "spatial supersensing" hierarchy for video-based Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and new VSI-SUPER benchmarks to reveal current models' limitations in genuine spatial and temporal reasoning. They develop Cambrian-S, a specialized MLLM trained on a large spatial dataset achieving state-of-the-art on VSI-Bench, and prototype a "predictive sensing" paradigm that leverages prediction error ("surprise") to robustly improve memory management and event segmentation in arbitrarily long videos.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs primarily offer semantic perception, treating video as discrete frames rather than continuous 3D world projections, limiting deeper visual-spatial and temporal reasoning.
- Existing video benchmarks often permit MLLMs to rely on linguistic priors or context expansion, failing to rigorously assess genuine spatial cognition or world modeling.
- Even leading MLLMs struggle with long-horizon spatial memory, continuous counting, and proactive understanding of dynamic environments from unbounded video streams.

## Method

- Propose a four-stage =="spatial supersensing" hierarchy== for video understanding, moving beyond semantic perception to predictive world modeling.
- Introduce ==VSI-SUPER==, a benchmark featuring ==Visual Spatial Recall (VSR)== and ==Visual Spatial Counting (VSC)== tasks, designed to challenge MLLMs on long-horizon spatial memory and cumulative reasoning in extended videos.
- Develop ==Cambrian-S==, a specialized video MLLM, and curate the VSI-590K dataset to enhance spatial understanding, alongside a =="predictive sensing" paradigm== leveraging "surprise" to optimize memory and event processing.

## Results

- Benchmarks like VSI-SUPER demonstrated that even advanced proprietary MLLMs like Gemini-2.5-Flash struggle with long-horizon spatial recall and continuous counting in extended videos.
- Cambrian-S achieved state-of-the-art performance on the VSI-Bench dataset, scoring **67.5%** and outperforming prominent open-source and proprietary models, showcasing its enhanced spatial cognition capabilities.
- The proposed predictive sensing paradigm, utilizing surprise-driven memory management and event segmentation, enabled Cambrian-S to maintain stable accuracy on VSR across arbitrary video lengths and significantly improve VSC performance compared to vanilla MLLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing MLLMs, despite large context windows, fall short on tasks demanding genuine spatial-temporal reasoning across long videos, often relying on linguistic shortcuts or brute-force context expansion.
> - Achieving "spatial supersensing" requires moving beyond scaling current MLLM paradigms; true understanding necessitates models that proactively build and update internal representations of the 3D world.
> - Leveraging "surprise" (prediction error in latent frame prediction) provides an effective mechanism for intelligent memory management and event segmentation, enabling more robust processing of unbounded video streams.
