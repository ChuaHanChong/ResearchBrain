# Cambrian-S: Towards Spatial Supersensing in Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NYU and Stanford introduce a "spatial supersensing" hierarchy for video-based Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and new VSI-SUPER benchmarks to reveal current models' limitations in genuine spatial and temporal reasoning. They develop Cambrian-S, a specialized MLLM trained on a large spatial dataset achieving state-of-the-art on VSI-Bench, and prototype a "predictive sensing" paradigm that leverages prediction error ("surprise") to robustly improve memory management and event segmentation in arbitrarily long videos.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs primarily offer semantic perception, treating video as discrete frames rather than continuous 3D world projections, limiting deeper visual-spatial and temporal reasoning.
- Existing video benchmarks often permit MLLMs to rely on linguistic priors or context expansion, failing to rigorously assess genuine spatial cognition or world modeling.
- Even leading MLLMs struggle with long-horizon spatial memory, continuous counting, and proactive understanding of dynamic environments from unbounded video streams.

## Method

- Propose a four-stage =="spatial supersensing" hierarchy== for video understanding, moving beyond semantic perception to predictive world modeling.
- Introduce ==VSI-SUPER==, a benchmark featuring ==Visual Spatial Recall (VSR)== and ==Visual Spatial Counting (VSC)== tasks, designed to challenge MLLMs on long-horizon spatial memory and cumulative reasoning in extended videos.
- Develop ==Cambrian-S==, a specialized video MLLM, and curate the VSI-590K dataset to enhance spatial understanding, alongside a =="predictive sensing" paradigm== leveraging "surprise" to optimize memory and event processing.

## Results

- Benchmarks like VSI-SUPER demonstrated that even advanced proprietary MLLMs like Gemini-2.5-Flash struggle with long-horizon spatial recall and continuous counting in extended videos.
- Cambrian-S achieved state-of-the-art performance on the VSI-Bench dataset, scoring **67.5%** and outperforming prominent open-source and proprietary models, showcasing its enhanced spatial cognition capabilities.
- The proposed predictive sensing paradigm, utilizing surprise-driven memory management and event segmentation, enabled Cambrian-S to maintain stable accuracy on VSR across arbitrary video lengths and significantly improve VSC performance compared to vanilla MLLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing MLLMs, despite large context windows, fall short on tasks demanding genuine spatial-temporal reasoning across long videos, often relying on linguistic shortcuts or brute-force context expansion.
> - Achieving "spatial supersensing" requires moving beyond scaling current MLLM paradigms; true understanding necessitates models that proactively build and update internal representations of the 3D world.
> - Leveraging "surprise" (prediction error in latent frame prediction) provides an effective mechanism for intelligent memory management and event segmentation, enabling more robust processing of unbounded video streams.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal AI, especially concerning MLLMs that combine vision and language, has seen rapid advancements. Current state-of-the-art MLLMs, exemplified by models like GPT-4o, Gemini-Pro, and various open-source initiatives (e.g., LLaVA), excel at "semantic perception"—identifying objects, describing scenes, and answering questions based on visual input. However, this paper critically argues that these systems are largely "reactive" and "task-driven," treating video as a collection of sparse, independent frames rather than a continuous, evolving projection of a 3D world. They often rely on brute-force context expansion or language priors, overlooking deeper visual-spatial and temporal reasoning.

This work directly addresses these limitations by proposing a shift towards "spatial supersensing." This concept moves beyond merely describing what is seen to actively constructing, updating, and predicting an implicit model of the 3D world from continuous sensory experience. This ambition aligns with a growing trend in AI research to develop more human-like intelligence, characterized by:

- **Embodied AI:** Creating agents that can interact with and understand the physical world, which inherently requires robust spatial and temporal reasoning.
- **World Models:** Building internal representations of the environment that allow AI systems to anticipate future states, plan actions, and learn from experience, drawing inspiration from cognitive science.
- **Beyond Scaling Laws:** Challenging the notion that simply scaling up model parameters, data, or context length will automatically lead to general intelligence, particularly for complex cognitive tasks. The paper explicitly states that scale alone is insufficient for spatial supersensing.

By deconstructing existing video benchmarks, the authors reveal that many primarily test linguistic understanding or semantic perception rather than true visual-spatial cognition or world modeling. This paper therefore positions itself as a foundational step in redirecting MLLM research towards more advanced, proactive, and ecologically valid forms of intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's key objectives are multifaceted, aiming to redefine, benchmark, and advance the capabilities of MLLMs in video understanding:

1. **Define and Propose a Hierarchy for Spatial Supersensing:** The authors aim to articulate a comprehensive framework for "spatial supersensing" in video, progressing beyond linguistic-only understanding. This hierarchy consists of four stages: semantic perception, streaming event cognition, implicit 3D spatial cognition, and predictive world modeling. This provides a roadmap for future AI development.
2. **Expose Limitations of Current MLLMs:** A primary motivation is to demonstrate that even cutting-edge MLLMs fall short when faced with tasks requiring genuine spatial and temporal reasoning over unbounded video streams. The paper argues that current models often rely on "linguistic shortcuts" or "brute-force context expansion" rather than true understanding.
3. **Introduce Novel Benchmarks for Spatial Supersensing:** To concretely measure progress towards spatial supersensing, the authors introduce **VSI-SUPER**, a two-part benchmark designed to be resistant to current MLLM paradigms:
 - **VSR (Visual Spatial Recall):** Tests long-horizon spatial observation and sequential recall of events in arbitrarily long videos.
 - **VSC (Visual Spatial Counting):** Evaluates continual counting across changing viewpoints and scenes in extended video streams, demanding cumulative reasoning.
4. **Push the Limits of the Current Paradigm:** The paper seeks to investigate how far performance can be pushed within the existing MLLM framework through meticulous data curation and advanced training strategies. This involves building a dedicated dataset and training a state-of-the-art model.
5. **Propose a New Paradigm: Predictive Sensing:** Recognizing the inherent limitations of the current approach, the authors aim to introduce and provide a proof-of-concept for "predictive sensing," where models actively anticipate sensory input and leverage "surprise" (prediction error) to drive memory, attention, and event organization.

The ultimate motivation is to bridge the gap between current reactive MLLMs and a more general, robust, and human-like multimodal intelligence that can truly perceive, remember, and reason about a continuous, evolving 3D world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-pronged approach, spanning theoretical definition, benchmark creation, model development, and a novel conceptual paradigm.

#### 3.1. Defining Spatial Supersensing

The authors define a four-stage hierarchy:
1. **Semantic Perception:** Naming and describing seen objects (current MLLM strength).
2. **Streaming Event Cognition:** Always-on sensing, memory across time, proactive responses.
3. **Implicit 3D Spatial Cognition:** Understanding video as projections of a 3D world, reasoning about objects, relations, and changes.
4. **Predictive World Modeling:** Creating internal models that anticipate future states, leveraging "surprise" to filter and organize information for learning and decision-making.

#### 3.2. Benchmarking Current Capabilities

The first step involved a diagnostic analysis of existing video MLLM benchmarks (e.g., VideoMME, EgoSchema, MVBench). By testing models under various input conditions (multiple frames, single frame, frame captions, blind test), the authors quantitatively show that many benchmarks can be solved using linguistic priors or minimal visual input, rather than requiring deep visual-spatial sensing.

#### 3.3. VSI-SUPER Benchmark Construction

To address the identified gaps, **VSI-SUPER** was introduced:
- **VSR (Visual Spatial Recall):** Generated by editing walkthrough videos to insert "surprising" objects (e.g., a teddy bear) at specific spatial locations. These edited clips are concatenated with other videos to form arbitrarily long (10-240 minutes) streams. The task requires recalling the sequential order of locations of these unusual objects, challenging long-horizon spatial memory.
- **VSC (Visual Spatial Counting):** Created by concatenating multiple room-tour videos. Models are tasked with continually counting a specific object type across changing viewpoints and scenes, with queries posed at multiple timestamps to simulate streaming. This emphasizes cumulative reasoning and generalization beyond short-term memory.

#### 3.4. Cambrian-S Model Development (Current Paradigm Exploration)

To investigate the limits of current MLLM designs, the authors developed **Cambrian-S**, a family of spatially-grounded video MLLMs (0.5B to 7B parameters).
- **Base Model:** An upgraded Cambrian-1 was used, featuring a SigLIP2-SO400m visual encoder, a Qwen2.5 instruction-tuned language model, and a simple two-layer MLP connector for computational efficiency.
- **VSI-590K Dataset:** A large-scale (590K QA pairs), spatially-focused instruction-tuning corpus was curated. It combined:
 - **Annotated Real Videos:** From 3D instance-level datasets like S3DIS, ScanNet, ARKitScenes, ADT, and ScanNet++ V2, with meta-information (object counts, bounding boxes, room dimensions) used to generate QA pairs.
 - **Simulated Data:** From embodied simulators like ProcTHOR and Hypersim, generating spatially grounded videos and QA pairs.
 - **Unannotated Real Videos:** Pseudo-annotated frames from YouTube room tours and robotic datasets (Open X-Embodiment, AgiBot-World) using object detection (Grounding-DINO), segmentation (SAM2), and 3D reconstruction (VGGT) to generate spatial QA pairs.
 - **Question Types:** Defined across 12 types in a spatiotemporal taxonomy (size, direction, count, distance, appearance order), including relative/absolute variants and different perspectives.
- **Training Pipeline:** A four-stage process: 1) Vision-Language Alignment, 2) Image Instruction Tuning (using an AnyRes strategy for high-resolution images), 3) General Video Instruction Tuning (on a large 3M-sample dataset, CambrianS-3M), and 4) Spatial Video Instruction Tuning (on VSI-590K mixed with general video/image data to prevent generalization loss).

#### 3.5. Predictive Sensing (New Paradigm Prototype)

As a proof-of-concept for the predictive world modeling stage, the authors introduced:
- **Latent Frame Prediction (LFP) Head:** A self-supervised two-layer MLP, trained jointly with the main MLLM objectives in Stage 4, to predict the latent representation of the *next* video frame.
- **Surprise Estimation:** During inference, "surprise" is quantified as the cosine distance between the LFP head's prediction and the actual latent feature of the incoming frame.
- **Case Studies:**
 - **Surprise-driven Memory Management (for VSR):** Frames with low "surprise" are compressed (2x downsampling of KV caches) before being pushed to a fixed-size long-term memory. When memory is full, the least surprising frames are dropped or merged. For queries, top-K relevant frames are retrieved based on cosine similarity.
 - **Surprise-driven Event Segmentation (for VSC):** High-surprise frames are used as "event boundaries." The model accumulates frame features in an event buffer; when a high-surprise event is detected, the buffer is summarized to produce a segment-level answer, and the buffer is cleared. All segment answers are aggregated for the final count.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several significant findings that underscore both the limitations of current approaches and the promise of the proposed paradigm shift:

- **Critique of Existing Benchmarks:** The diagnostic tests reveal that most current video MLLM benchmarks primarily assess linguistic understanding and semantic perception. Many tasks can be effectively solved using textual captions or strong language priors, with less reliance on genuine visual sensing. VSI-Bench and Tomato were identified as notable exceptions requiring more visual input.
- **VSI-SUPER Reveals Fundamental Gaps:**
 - **Gemini-2.5-Flash Limitations:** Even leading proprietary MLLMs like Gemini-2.5-Flash struggle severely on VSI-SUPER tasks. It reaches its context limit on two-hour videos despite a large token window and shows poor generalization in counting tasks, with predictions saturating at small constant values regardless of the actual object count. This robustly demonstrates that brute-force context expansion and large-scale proprietary models are insufficient for true spatial supersensing.
- **Cambrian-S Achieves SOTA Spatial Cognition (Current Paradigm):**
 - **VSI-Bench Performance:** Cambrian-S (e.g., 7B model) achieves state-of-the-art performance on VSI-Bench (67.5%), significantly outperforming all open-source models and surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro by over 16 absolute points. This indicates the effectiveness of the VSI-590K dataset and the refined training pipeline in enhancing spatial cognition within the current MLLM framework.
 - **Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** Cambrian-S shows strong generalization on challenging subtasks like "Route Planning" (which was not explicitly in VSI-590K training data), with performance scaling with model size.
 - **Robust Visual Reasoning:** Performance on the debiased VSI-Bench confirms that Cambrian-S learns visual reasoning rather than relying solely on language priors.
 - **Competitive General Capabilities:** This enhanced spatial reasoning does not come at the expense of general video understanding; Cambrian-S remains competitive on standard video benchmarks (e.g., Perception Test, EgoSchema).
 - **Limitations on VSI-SUPER:** Despite its strengths, Cambrian-S, when evaluated under the standard (non-predictive sensing) setup, still performs poorly on VSI-SUPER. Accuracy on VSR drops sharply with increasing video length (e.g., from 38.3% for 10 min to 6.0% for 60 min), and VSC tasks largely fail. This reinforces the argument that scaling data and model size alone within the current reactive framework is insufficient for continual spatial supersensing.
- **Predictive Sensing Demonstrates Promise (New Paradigm):**
 - **Superior VSR Performance:** Cambrian-S augmented with the surprise-driven memory management system substantially outperforms Gemini-2.5-Flash and the vanilla Cambrian-S on VSR. It maintains consistent accuracy and a stable GPU memory footprint across arbitrarily long video durations.
 - **Improved VSC Counting:** The surprise-driven event segmentation approach for VSC leads to significantly higher and more stable performance than Gemini-2.5-Flash, even in streaming evaluation settings. Cambrian-S's predicted counts show a stronger, more linear correlation with ground truth, indicating better generalization than Gemini's saturation.
 - **Effectiveness of "Surprise":** Using latent frame prediction error as the "surprise" signal consistently outperforms simpler baselines, such as adjacent-frame visual feature similarity, for both VSR and VSC tasks. This highlights the value of predictive models for understanding spatiotemporal dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant implications for the future direction of multimodal AI and our understanding of intelligence:

1. **Redefining Multimodal Intelligence:** The paper provides a compelling argument for shifting the focus of MLLM development from mere linguistic-centric perception to a more holistic "spatial supersensing" paradigm. This expanded definition, encompassing streaming event cognition, implicit 3D spatial cognition, and predictive world modeling, offers a clear developmental roadmap for more advanced AI.

2. **Challenging Current Benchmarking Practices:** By demonstrating how existing benchmarks often rely on linguistic shortcuts rather than true visual reasoning, the paper encourages the community to design more rigorous, ecologically valid, and "paradigm-resistant" evaluation methods. VSI-SUPER serves as a crucial tool to expose the shortcomings of current models in handling unbounded, continuous visual streams and complex spatial-temporal reasoning.

3. **Advancing Spatial Cognition in MLLMs:** The development of the VSI-590K dataset and the Cambrian-S model represents a substantial step forward in explicitly training MLLMs for spatial understanding. Cambrian-S's state-of-the-art performance on VSI-Bench and its ability to generalize to unseen spatial tasks establish a powerful new baseline and resource for researchers.

4. **Pioneering Predictive Sensing:** The most significant impact lies in the introduction and initial validation of "predictive sensing" as a new paradigm. By demonstrating that leveraging "surprise" (prediction error) can effectively manage memory and segment events in long videos, the authors open a promising avenue for building AI systems that are proactive, anticipatory, and learn from their environment in a more human-like cognitive manner. This moves beyond passive data processing towards active world modeling.

5. **Implications for Embodied AI and Real-World Applications:** The ability of an AI system to build and update internal world models from continuous sensory experience is fundamental for embodied agents operating in dynamic, unstructured environments. Predictive sensing, as prototyped in this paper, is a critical component for future robots, intelligent assistants, and autonomous systems that need to reason about, navigate, and interact with the physical world over extended periods.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** While acknowledging its limitations as a proof-of-concept, the paper acts as a strong catalyst, urging researchers to explore richer world models, more diverse and embodied scenarios, and deeper integrations between vision, language, and predictive learning. It lays the groundwork for overcoming the "scaling alone is insufficient" barrier, fostering research into more genuinely intelligent and adaptable AI systems.
