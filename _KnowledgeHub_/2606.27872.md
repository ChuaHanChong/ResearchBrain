# S$^2$-VLA: State-Space Guided Vision-Language-Action Models for Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> S2-VLA is a 2B-parameter VLA model that maintains a GRU-updated belief state tracking task progression, using it to dynamically gate fusion of visual, semantic-intent, and action-history attention branches. Despite compact size, it beats 7B-scale models, hitting 98.2% average on LIBERO and SOTA on SimplerEnv-Bridge.

## Problem

- Static feature fusion uses fixed weights across visual, language, and action representations, so models cannot adapt attention to different phases of task execution.
- Policy heads that attend only to compressed action-query embeddings discard fine-grained spatial detail needed for high-dexterity grasping and placement.
- Lack of a context-aware correction mechanism lets early-step decision biases propagate and amplify along the action chain in long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- ==State-Space Guided Adaptive Attention (SSGAA)== maintains a belief state via a ==GRU== update over recent actions and proprioception, generating dynamic gating weights per layer.
- Three parallel attention pathways, ==low-level visual cross-attention==, ==high-level intent cross-attention==, and ==action-sequence self-attention==, are fused by belief-guided ==softmax gating== at a single middle layer.
- **Belief-Driven VLA** paradigm conditions a Qwen3-VL-backed policy on the belief state end-to-end via ==action-prediction MSE loss==, without explicit phase supervision.

## Results

- Achieves **98.2%** average success on LIBERO across Spatial/Object/Goal/Long subsets, beating 7B models like OpenVLA-OFT (**97.1%**) and CronusVLA (**97.0%**).
- Reaches **78.1%** average success on SimplerEnv-Bridge with WidowX, outperforming MemoryVLA (**71.9%**) and CogACT-Large (**57.3%**) on pick-place and stacking tasks.
- Ablation shows gating only at layer 12 with belief-state guidance yields **96.4%** versus **95.0%** without gating and **95.8%** with a static-vector substitute for belief state.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive, phase-aware fusion of modalities matters more than parameter count for long-horizon manipulation, a 2B model can outperform 7B baselines.
> - Gating benefits concentrate at semantically rich middle transformer layers, gating at all layers or combining multiple layers degrades performance below the ungated baseline.
> - A belief state can emerge purely from action-prediction loss without explicit phase labels, implicitly encoding task stage and execution fidelity from action-perception history.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robotic systems capable of performing perception, reasoning, and action from open-ended instructions represents a significant area of research. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have facilitated progress in this domain. Within this context, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a framework for bridging high-level understanding with low-level control, enabling instruction-driven robotic manipulation.

Existing VLA research generally approaches action decoding through two paradigms: autoregressive token generation and parallel decoding with decoupled policy heads. Autoregressive models discretize actions into tokens and generate them sequentially, offering semantic unification but often incurring higher inference latency. Parallel decoding methods, in contrast, decouple representation from execution to enhance speed and precision. In this paradigm, VLMs primarily provide semantic representations or high-level planning signals, while a decoupled policy head handles concrete action generation, often through techniques like diffusion or flow-matching models, or ACT-style chunking. The present work operates within the parallel decoding paradigm, focusing on optimizing the feature fusion mechanism to leverage VLM perceptual capabilities for action generation.

A common limitation across many existing VLA models, particularly in the context of long-horizon manipulation tasks, is the reliance on static representation fusion mechanisms. These mechanisms typically employ fixed weights to combine visual, language, and action information throughout the task execution. This static approach can lead to a lack of adaptability, as the model cannot dynamically adjust its focus to the varying requirements of different task phases (e.g., precise localization during grasping versus semantic comprehension during overall task planning). Such static designs contribute to decision biases at critical steps, and in long-horizon tasks, these early biases can propagate and amplify, potentially leading to task failure. This research aims to address this propagation of cumulative error in long-horizon tasks by introducing an adaptive feature fusion mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the performance degradation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. This degradation is attributed to the cumulative error propagation stemming from static feature fusion mechanisms employed by conventional VLA models.

The motivation for this work is rooted in the observed limitations of current VLA approaches. Standard VLA models often utilize fixed weights to combine multimodal representations—visual observations, language instructions, and action sequences—throughout the entire task execution. This static fusion approach presents several challenges:
- **Lack of Phase-Adaptive Capability:** In a multi-step task, different phases demand different types of information emphasis. For instance, a grasping phase requires fine-grained spatial details for precise localization, while a planning phase necessitates a broader understanding of the overall semantic goal. A model with static fusion cannot dynamically shift its focus, leading to suboptimal decision-making.
- **Decision Biases:** The inability to adapt focus results in decision biases during critical steps. If the model cannot adequately prioritize spatial details during grasping or semantic intent during planning, it may make imprecise or misguided actions.
- **Cumulative Error Propagation:** In long-horizon tasks involving multiple sequential steps, initial errors or biases caused by static fusion can propagate and amplify over time. This compounding effect increases the likelihood of task failure, as early inaccuracies are not corrected by a context-aware mechanism.

To counteract these limitations, the authors propose a framework designed to enable phase-adaptive feature fusion. The central objective is to develop a mechanism that maintains temporal coherence and dynamically gates information from complementary sources—visual features for spatial perception, task intents for high-level planning, and temporal action sequences for execution consistency. This dynamic gating is intended to allow the model to adjust its attention based on the evolving requirements of different task stages, thereby mitigating error propagation and improving overall success rates in long-horizon robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose S$^2$-VLA, a framework incorporating a State-Space Guided Adaptive Attention (SSGAA) mechanism, designed to improve performance in long-horizon manipulation tasks by addressing the limitations of static feature fusion.

**Problem Formulation:**
Standard VLA policies map multi-view visual observations ($V_t$), natural language instructions ($L_t$), and proprioceptive feedback ($P_t$) directly to a future action sequence ($A_{t:t+K-1}$). This formulation is identified as lacking an explicit mechanism for temporal coherence, generating actions from static sensory snapshots and disregarding execution history, which contributes to error accumulation in long-horizon scenarios.

**S$^2$-VLA: Belief-Driven VLA Paradigm:**
To introduce temporal coherence, the S$^2$-VLA framework incorporates an internal belief state ($b_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_b}$). This belief state functions as a compact temporal context, summarizing task progression and historical information. It is recursively updated by integrating the recent action sequence ($A_{t-K:t-1}$) and its sensory feedback ($P_t$) using a learned update function ($f_\phi$), implemented as a lightweight Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU). This recurrent mechanism enables the compression of action-perception history into a dynamic representation of the task phase and execution quality. The policy then generates actions conditioned on this belief state: $A_{t:t+K-1} = \pi'_{\text{VLA}}(V_t, L_t, P_t | b_t)$. The belief state is learned end-to-end through action prediction loss, implicitly encoding task-phase information and execution fidelity.

**Framework Overview:**
The S$^2$-VLA framework integrates the belief state ($b_t$) to guide adaptive fusion of multimodal information. Its core is the SSGAA mechanism, which comprises three complementary attention pathways adaptively integrated via a dynamic gating network regulated by $b_t$. This design enables stage-aware and adaptive feature aggregation.

**Unified Input Construction:**
Input features are constructed as follows:
- Visual features ($F_t$) are extracted from multi-view images using a Vision Transformer (ViT).
- Language instructions ($L$) are tokenized.
- Learnable intent tokens ($A_{tokens}$) are embedded for task-specific context.
These are concatenated into a unified input sequence $X$, which is then processed through Transformer layers to generate hierarchical representations.

**State-Space Guided Adaptive Attention (SSGAA):**
The SSGAA mechanism consists of belief-guided dynamic gating and three parallel attention pathways:
1. **Action Sequence Self-Attention:** This branch operates on an action-sequence embedding, initialized as zero vectors and refined through self-attention within the SSGAA module. Its purpose is to model temporal dependencies across the predicted action steps, ensuring temporal coherence in motion trajectories.
2. **Low-Level Visual Cross-Attention:** This pathway provides precise spatial grounding. A visual vector ($C_{vis}$) is constructed from the hidden states of visual tokens. Cross-attention is performed using a query derived from the learnable action sequence representation, with $C_{vis}$ providing keys and values. This preserves fine-grained spatial details crucial for object manipulation.
3. **High-Level Intent Cross-Attention:** This branch facilitates semantic intent understanding. A semantic context vector ($C_{ite}$) is formed from the hidden states of learnable intent tokens. Cross-attention is applied, where the action-token representations, encoding distilled task semantics, serve as keys and values.

**Belief-Guided Gating:** At each layer, the belief state ($b_t$) dynamically modulates the outputs of these three branches. Soft gating weights ($g^{(l)}_{vis}, g^{(l)}_{ite}, g^{(l)}_{act}$) are computed by an MLP and Softmax function using $b_t$. These weights are then used to combine the outputs of the visual, semantic, and action self-attention branches: $H^{(l)} = g^{(l)}_{vis} \cdot O^{(l)}_{vis} + g^{(l)}_{ite} \cdot O^{(l)}_{ite} + g^{(l)}_{act} \cdot O^{(l)}_{act}$. The fused representation is then processed by a feed-forward network, providing the query for the next layer’s attention branches and also feeding into the current layer’s action sequence self-attention branch for iterative refinement.

**Parallel Action Decoding:** The final action prediction ($\hat{A}_{t:t+K-1}$) is generated by applying layer normalization and a linear projection to the output of the last SSGAA layer.

**Training and Inference Strategies:**
- **Unified Loss Formulation:** The training objective is an L2 action prediction loss, minimizing the squared difference between predicted and ground-truth actions: $L_{total} = L_{action} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \|a_{t+k} - \hat{a}_{t+k}\|_2^2$.
- **End-to-End Training and Inference:** The S$^2$-VLA model is trained end-to-end, optimizing all components including the Qwen3-VL backbone, belief update module, and SSGAA mechanism. During inference, the model operates in a recurrent loop: updating its belief state via GRU, computing adaptive gating weights, fusing features, predicting the next action, and executing it. This design aims for temporal consistency and dynamic attention shifting across modalities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, efficiency, and operational mechanisms of the S$^2$-VLA framework across simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

**Performance on Simulation Benchmarks:**
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** S$^2$-VLA achieved success rates of 98.4% on Spatial, 99.6% on Object, 98.4% on Goal, and 96.4% on Long-Horizon subsets, resulting in an average success rate of 98.2%. This performance surpassed multiple larger-scale baseline methods, including models with up to 8.5 billion parameters, demonstrating a capability to mitigate error accumulation in long-horizon tasks.
- **SimplerEnv-Bridge Benchmark:** On four manipulation tasks involving the WidowX robot, S$^2$-VLA achieved an average success rate of 78.1%, outperforming several established methods like MemoryVLA (71.9%) and CogACT-Large (57.3%).

**Real-World Experimental Evaluation:**
S$^2$-VLA was evaluated on an ALOHA dual-arm mobile manipulation platform across four complex tasks: pick-and-place, stacking, desktop organization, and utensil distribution. The system demonstrated superior success rates compared to baseline methods such as ACT and $\pi^0$-FAST, indicating effective real-world transfer capabilities for its SSGAA mechanism.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies were conducted on a 24-layer SSGAA action head to evaluate the necessity and configuration of dynamic gating and belief-state guidance.
- **Dynamic Gating Necessity:** Disabling gating resulted in 95.0% performance, while indiscriminate gating across all layers (Gate@All) slightly degraded performance to 94.4%. This suggested that selective application of gating is more effective.
- **Depth Sensitivity of Gating:** Single-layer gating experiments revealed that gating at middle layers (6, 12, 18) provided performance gains, with layer 12 achieving the highest success rate of 96.4% (a +1.4% improvement over no gating). Shallow and deep layers showed minimal or negative impact, indicating that intermediate layers, which capture semantically rich features, are more suitable for adaptive resource allocation.
- **Optimal SSGAA Configuration:** Combining multiple high-performing layers (6, 12, 18) performed worse (94.4%) than the best single-layer setting, suggesting that multi-point gating could introduce instability. The optimal configuration identified was single-layer gating at layer 12.
- **Belief-State Guidance Contribution:** Replacing the dynamic belief state ($b_t$) with a learnable static vector in the optimal Gate@12 configuration resulted in 95.8% success, lower than the full SSGAA (96.4%). This confirmed that the performance gains were not merely from gating but specifically from the dynamic modulation of gating weights by the evolving belief state, enabling phase-aware adaptation.

**Evaluation of QwenVL-Based Models:**
To ensure fair comparison and isolate architectural benefits, mainstream VLA policy heads were transplanted onto QwenVL family backbones (Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL). S$^2$-VLA, with its compact 2B parameter architecture, achieved the highest success rate of 96.4% on the LIBERO Long benchmark. This outperformed other 2B models (e.g., Qwen3-VL+Adapter-Pro at 94.4%) and larger 3B/4B models (e.g., Qwen3-VL+OFT at 93.8%), indicating that the SSGAA mechanism offered architectural advantages beyond parameter scaling for long-horizon tasks.

**Gating Interpretability Analysis:**
Visualization of gating weights during a representative long-horizon task ("put both the cream cheese box and the butter in the basket") provided insights into the SSGAA mechanism:
- **Visual Focus:** During phases requiring precise object grounding and spatial alignment (e.g., "Aim target," "Locate and move to basket"), the weight for low-level visual cross-attention increased, indicating reliance on fine-grained visual details.
- **Intent Focus:** At key decision points or subtask transitions (e.g., "Grip item," "Place item"), the weight for high-level Intent cross-attention showed clear peaks, demonstrating the model’s use of aggregated task representations for planning and stage transitions.
- **Action Sequence Focus:** During long-range, steady-state motion segments, action sequence self-attention remained dominant, contributing to smooth and temporally consistent trajectories.
This dynamic allocation of attention pathways is presented as the mechanism through which S$^2$-VLA enhances decision robustness and mitigates error propagation.

**Performance Efficiency Metrics:**
Inference efficiency comparisons showed that S$^2$-VLA achieved a throughput of 80.8 Hz, which exceeded other 7B models such as OpenVLA-OFT (71.4 Hz), CronusVLA (8.7 Hz), and TraceVLA (4.3 Hz).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The S$^2$-VLA framework introduces a novel approach to addressing a critical challenge in robotic manipulation: the performance degradation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in long-horizon tasks due to cumulative error propagation. By integrating a State-Space Guided Adaptive Attention (SSGAA) mechanism, this work offers a method for dynamically fusing multimodal information, which contrasts with the static fusion mechanisms prevalent in prior VLA models.

The primary significance of this work lies in demonstrating that adaptive feature fusion, guided by a belief state tracking task progression, can substantially improve robotic manipulation performance for complex, multi-step tasks. The achieved success rates on benchmarks like LIBERO and SimplerEnv, particularly in long-horizon scenarios, indicate an advancement in the robustness and reliability of VLA models. The outperformance of larger, 7B-scale models by a more compact 2B parameter S$^2$-VLA model suggests that architectural innovation focused on adaptive information processing can be more impactful than simply scaling model parameters. This finding contributes to the development of more efficient and deployable robotic systems.

The interpretability analysis of the gating mechanism offers insights into how the model dynamically prioritizes different information streams (spatial visual features, semantic task intent, temporal action sequences) according to the current phase of task execution. This phase-aware adaptation helps reduce early-stage biases and effectively mitigates the propagation and amplification of errors, which are common failure modes in extended robotic operations.

The potential impact of S$^2$-VLA extends to several areas:
- **Robust Robotic Systems:** The enhanced ability to handle long-horizon tasks with greater reliability could facilitate the deployment of robots in more complex environments, requiring multi-step sequential actions without human intervention.
- **Efficient Model Design:** The demonstration that a compact model can outperform larger alternatives through architectural innovation encourages the development of more parameter-efficient VLA models, potentially reducing computational costs and energy consumption.
- **Advancements in Adaptive Learning:** The concept of a belief state dynamically guiding attention mechanisms could be generalized to other areas of machine learning where sequential decision-making and context-aware processing are crucial.

Future work outlined by the authors includes exploring the architectural compatibility of SSGAA and extending the belief state modeling to other generative frameworks like Diffusion models or Flow Matching to enhance the modeling of complex multimodal action distributions. These directions suggest that the core ideas presented in S$^2$-VLA could contribute to broader advancements in embodied AI and generalist robot policies.
