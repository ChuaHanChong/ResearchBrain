# AnnotateAnything: Automatic Annotation of 3D Assets for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AnnotateAnything converts raw 3D assets into manipulation-ready assets by pairing a hierarchical vision-language annotation pipeline, which infers object semantics, parts, keypoints and affordances, with a massively parallel physics annotation pipeline that generates, optimizes and simulation-validates action labels. It produces 100M validated annotations over 18 atomic skills across 17,005 assets, lifting rollout data success from 24% to 86%.

## Problem

- Raw 3D asset collections encode only shape and appearance, omitting where to grasp, which parts move, and where insertion or hanging is feasible.
- Human annotation of semantic and physical interaction knowledge is costly, labor-intensive, hard to scale, and limited in the diversity of strategies produced.
- RL-based data generation avoids labeling but demands reward engineering, heavy training compute, and can yield behaviors misaligned with human interaction priors.

## Method

- **AnnotateAnything**'s ==hierarchical visual-language pipeline==: a VLM writes three-level asset and room descriptions, while native ==3D part segmentation== and ==FPS-plus-VLM keypoint selection== give grounded anchors.
- ==Unified action schema== keeps per-anchor, per-skill candidate banks, a one-to-many mapping from grounded anchors to function-conditioned executable candidates carrying poses, trajectories and feasibility metadata.
- ==Physics annotation pipeline==: skill-conditioned candidate generation, trajectory templates, ==energy-based optimization== over task, contact, collision, kinematic and smoothness terms, ==CUDA-parallel validation==, symmetry-aware augmentation.

## Results

- Processed **17,005** assets from **9** source families into **100M** validated annotations across **18** skills; **2,315** candidates per asset-skill pair give a **26.6%** validation pass rate.
- Human-rated annotation bundle quality reaches **90.9** overall versus **78.5** without 3D refinement and **69.6** for direct VLM prompting, with **91.9%** macro execution success.
- Rollout data success is **86.0%** against **61.8%** VL-only, **48.2%** geometry-only and **24.0%** unannotated; zero-shot real transfer scores **18/20** grasp, **12/20** long-horizon.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic reasoning alone is not executable: language priors need explicit 3D grounding plus simulation validation before becoming reliable, asset-specific action labels.
> - Keeping many validated candidates per anchor rather than one canonical label buys robustness, since planners can retry when randomized scenes block a solution.
> - Annotation quality dominates collection cost: attempts per success fall from 4.17 to 1.17, so offline label validation beats brute-force rollout retries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic agents necessitates large-scale and diverse datasets encompassing a variety of objects, robot embodiments, and tasks. Simulation environments are recognized as a scalable approach for generating such data. Recent advancements in asset collections and simulation benchmarks have provided geometric and visual substrates for data generation. However, a significant limitation persists: raw 3D assets typically only encode shape and appearance information. They lack the semantic, interactive, and physical knowledge crucial for robot learning, such as identifying graspable regions, articulating parts, or determining feasible insertion and hanging locations. This absence of actionable knowledge represents a bottleneck in leveraging large volumes of 3D assets for robot manipulation research.

Existing methods for generating manipulation annotations generally fall into two categories: human annotation or reinforcement learning (RL)-based approaches. Human annotation, while providing semantic and physical knowledge, is often costly, labor-intensive, difficult to scale, and limited in the diversity of actions it can capture. RL-based alternatives reduce manual labeling requirements but necessitate complex reward engineering, substantial computational resources for training, and may generate behaviors that do not align with human interaction priors.

This research addresses the identified bottleneck by proposing an automated framework to convert passive 3D assets into "manipulation-ready" assets. It positions itself as a complementary layer to existing simulation and asset creation paradigms, aiming to bridge the gap between static 3D geometry and the actionable knowledge required for robot learning. The work acknowledges the abundance of 3D content and generative models but highlights the scarcity of pipelines that transform these into interaction-ready annotations. By providing a unified mechanism for generating transferable task annotations, it aims to facilitate the population of simulators and benchmarks with diverse, scalable, and task-relevant information for skill-centric robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop "AnnotateAnything," a general automatic annotation framework capable of converting passive 3D assets into manipulation-ready assets that include structured, diverse, and executable manipulation labels. This overarching goal is motivated by the critical need for large-scale, diverse, and semantically rich robot data for training general-purpose robotic agents, a need that current raw 3D assets fail to meet due to their lack of actionable knowledge.

The specific motivations and challenges addressed by AnnotateAnything are detailed as follows:

- **C1: Incorporating human-prior reasoning and interaction understanding:** Raw 3D assets lack human interaction priors, which are essential for generating robot trajectories that are natural, safe, and likely to succeed. The framework aims to infuse human-prior reasoning into the automatic annotation process by inferring object semantics, interaction constraints, and 3D-grounded cues.
- **C2: Grounding abstract interaction intent in asset-specific geometry and kinematics:** Abstract interaction intents (e.g., "grasping a mug") must be translated into concrete, executable, asset-specific labels (e.g., specific grasp poses on a particular mug's handle). The physical properties and kinematics of each asset dictate feasible actions. AnnotateAnything seeks to convert high-level priors into executable manipulation parameters.
- **C3: Preserving diverse feasible solutions:** Annotations should not collapse to a single canonical solution but should represent the multiple valid ways a robot can interact with an object or scene. The framework aims to generate diverse feasible labels across various interaction strategies, tasks (e.g., grasping, dexterous manipulation, articulation, insertion), and robot embodiments (e.g., parallel-jaw grippers, dexterous hands, humanoids).
- **C4: Achieving scalability without per-asset manual design or task-specific engineering:** Generating annotations for a large number of assets, categories, and interactions requires a highly automated process that maintains geometric precision and interaction specificity without extensive manual intervention or task-specific engineering for each asset. The objective is to achieve scalability through a fully parallelized annotation pipeline accelerated by technologies like CUDA.

In addressing these challenges, AnnotateAnything aims to provide complementary language annotations for high-level reasoning, visual annotations for 3D understanding, and action annotations that specify executable manipulation parameters, thereby bridging the gap between passive 3D assets and actionable robot interaction knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnnotateAnything is designed as a unified framework structured around two complementary pipelines: a visual-language annotation pipeline and a physics-based action annotation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Visual-Language Annotation Pipeline

This pipeline injects human interaction priors and grounds them into asset- and scene-level annotations.

##### 3.1.1. Hierarchical Language Annotation

- **Asset-level:** For individual 3D objects, multi-view RGB observations are fed into a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen3-VL, to generate three levels of descriptions: a semantic phrase, a functional sentence, and a part-aware paragraph. These descriptions provide object identity, manipulable parts, and feasible interactions. This process also involves part-specific masked renderings to generate dense, part-aware descriptions grounded in visual evidence.
- **Room-level:** For room-scale scenes, selected room views and a labeled top-view occupancy map are queried using Qwen3.5-VL to produce a layout summary, a furniture-and-zone description, and dense scene context. These capture object relations, navigable regions, and task-relevant interaction areas. Automatic room-view rendering and rule-based scan-pose sampling are employed to ensure informative views.
- **Output Format and Filtering:** VLMs are prompted to produce structured JSON outputs, explicitly describing only visible evidence and marking uncertainties to reduce hallucination.

##### 3.1.2. Hierarchical Visual Annotation

- **Asset-level:** For each object, a fused RGB-D point cloud is reconstructed from multi-view observations. Semantic keypoints are annotated via VLM selection over Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) candidates. Part masks are obtained through Hunyuan3D-style native 3D decomposition using P3-SAM and X-Part, providing 3D anchors and part regions.
- **Room-level:** Multi-height occupancy maps are constructed from simulated LiDAR and ray-cast observations. Floor-plan and wall-structure annotations are derived by filtering out movable objects using VLM-assisted structural component selection.
- **Cross-level Composition and Consistency:** Object instances within room-scale scenes are individually annotated with object-centric information, which is then transformed back to the global frame and aligned with room-level annotations using shared identifiers.

#### 3.2. Physics-based Action Annotation Pipeline

This pipeline grounds the visual-language priors in robot embodiment and physical feasibility, converting them into structured, executable, and validated action labels.

##### 3.2.1. Unified Action Annotation Schema

Annotations are organized as a hierarchical candidate bank. Grounded interaction anchors ($H(A)$) from visual annotations (parts, keypoints, affordance, scene regions) are associated with functional affordances ($\phi(h)$). Compatible skills ($S(h)$) are inferred, leading to a candidate bank ($B_{h,s}$) for each anchor-skill pair. Each action candidate ($a^{(i)}_{h,s}$) includes skill type, associated object, anchor, functional affordance, concrete target ($x^{(i)}$), skill-specific parameters ($\theta^{(i)}$), optional trajectory ($\tau^{(i)}$), feasibility metadata ($v^{(i)}$), and diversity descriptor ($d^{(i)}$). This schema aims to preserve action diversity and functional consistency.

##### 3.2.2. General Physics-based Action Generation Pipeline

- **Candidate Target Generation:** Skill-compatible regions ($C_s$) are localized using visual-language grounding. Concrete targets are sampled from these regions via FPS (for objects/parts) or grid/2D FPS (for navigation meshes). These targets are filtered by geometric and embodiment constraints such as curvature, visibility, collision margins, reachability, and traversability.
- **Trajectory Generation:** For each retained target, an initial waypoint sequence ($\tau^{(i)}_0$) is generated based on the skill's constraint source:
 - **Object-property-conditioned:** Uses static geometry or garment keypoints for tasks like grasping, dexterous grasping, fling, and folding.
 - **Object-trajectory-conditioned:** Follows articulated motion for opening, closing, or rotating parts while preserving end-effector-part relations.
 - **Object-vector-conditioned:** Aligns actions with insertion, hanging, pouring, or placement directions.
 - **Scene-trajectory-conditioned:** Samples interaction-ready base poses on a navigation mesh and computes coarse A* paths.
- **Trajectory Optimization:** Initial trajectories are refined by minimizing a skill-dependent objective function, $E_s$, which includes terms for functional affordance consistency ($E_{func}$), task-specific goals ($E_{task}$), geometry adaptation ($E_{geom}$), contact stability ($E_{contact}$), collision avoidance ($E_{coll}$), robot kinematics ($E_{kin}$), and motion smoothness ($E_{smooth}$). This adapts templates to asset-specific and embodiment-aware requirements (e.g., dexterous contact, adaptive garment folds, DWB for navigation).
- **Physics Validation:** Optimized candidates undergo parallel physics simulation. For most object-centric skills, a "floating end-effector" (e.g., gripper or dexterous hand) tests contact, collision, and stability. For embodiment-dependent skills (e.g., garment folding), a full manipulator validates arm collision and entanglement. Robustness tests, including disturbances and varied gravity, are applied to assess stability. Skill-specific success criteria are used.
- **Physics-aware Augmentation:** To enhance diversity, validated candidates are augmented through local perturbations (sampling variations around anchors and parameters) and symmetry-aware augmentation (exploiting annotated object symmetries). Augmented candidates are re-checked for feasibility using lightweight geometric and physics constraints.

#### 3.3. Downstream Tasks

The generated annotations serve two main purposes:
- **Large-scale Robot Data Collection:** Annotations act as executable interfaces for an asynchronous parallel simulation data-collection system, enabling rollouts across diverse objects, tasks, and robot embodiments. An atomic-skill library and cuRobo-v2 planning are utilized with domain randomization.
- **Annotation-derived Downstream Applications:** Annotations are converted into supervision for tasks such as affordance and keypoint detection, robot reasoning VQA (derived from runtime grounded labels during rollouts), and 3D VLM instruction-tuning (using simulation assets, rollouts, and language annotations for dense grounding labels and spatial reasoning QA).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate AnnotateAnything's ability to convert raw 3D assets into high-quality language, visual, and action annotations for robot manipulation, assessing annotation quality, efficiency, executability, and utility for downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Annotation Coverage over Heterogeneous Assets

- The framework processed 17,005 assets from 9 source families, covering rigid objects, articulated objects, deformable/garment assets, and room-scale scenes.
- This process yielded 100 million physics-validated action annotations across 18 atomic skills.
- On an audited evaluation suite, the pipeline generated an average of 2,315 candidates per attempted asset–skill pair. Of these, 615 passed physics validation, and 538 were retained in the final annotation bank, corresponding to a 26.6% physics validation pass rate and a 23.2% retained-candidate rate.
- The macro-averaged physics validation pass rate was 40.6%, with an asset readiness rate (fraction of attempted pairs with at least one retained annotation) of 87.4%.

#### 4.2. Visual-Language Annotation Quality

Evaluation on a manually annotated subset showed the full AnnotateAnything pipeline achieved a mean overall human rating of 90.9 out of 100 for visual-language bundles.
- This score was composed of: Semantic Correctness (93.1), 3D Grounding Accuracy (89.4), Coverage (90.2), and Actionability (91.0).
- Ablation studies indicated that direct VLM approaches were competitive in semantics but weaker in 3D grounding and actionability. Affordance-only heuristics improved affordance localization but lacked reliable part and keypoint grounding. Removing 3D refinement weakened all three visual annotation types.
- Aggregate matching scores for generated part, affordance, and keypoint annotations showed the full pipeline achieved scores of 91.5 for parts, 89.0 for affordances, and 90.3 for keypoints.

#### 4.3. Physics-grounded Action Annotation Quality

The physics pipeline converted grounded visual-language priors into executable action banks.
- The full pipeline achieved a macro-averaged execution success rate of 91.9% for retained annotations, with a human trajectory quality score of 85.5.
- Annotation throughput was 198 annotations per minute (macro average).
- In terms of annotation-enabled rollout collection, the full pipeline achieved a macro-averaged data success rate of 86.0%, collecting 165 successful trajectories per hour, with an average of 1.17 attempts per successful trajectory.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that geometry-only sampling produced an execution success rate of 58.4% (vs. 91.9% for full pipeline), VL-only annotation achieved 67.5% (vs. 91.9%), and removing physics validation resulted in 63.2% (vs. 91.9%). These results indicate that both language reasoning and explicit 3D grounding, combined with physics-based optimization and validation, were critical for robust, executable annotations.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

Policies trained solely from AnnotateAnything's simulation rollouts, with domain randomization, exhibited zero-shot transfer capabilities to representative real-world tasks.
- Success rates over 20 trials included: Grasp (18/20), DexGrasp (15/20), Insertion (14/20), Open the Drawer (16/20), Close the Lid (17/20), and a Long-horizon Task (12/20).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnnotateAnything introduces a general automatic annotation framework designed to address a core bottleneck in scalable robot learning: the conversion of passive 3D assets into manipulation-ready assets with actionable knowledge. The integration of Vision-Language Model (VLM)-driven human-prior reasoning with physics-grounded optimization and scalable parallel validation represents a structured approach to generating diverse, executable, and asset-specific labels.

The significance of this work is evidenced by its demonstrated capabilities:
- **Enhanced Annotation Efficiency and Scale:** The framework processes a large volume of heterogeneous 3D assets, producing millions of validated action annotations across a broad spectrum of manipulation skills. This significantly reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming manual annotation or complex reward engineering typical of other approaches.
- **Improved Data Collection for Robot Learning:** By generating executable action annotations, AnnotateAnything directly supports large-scale simulation data collection. The system yields higher data success rates and collection efficiency compared to alternative annotation and data-generation pipelines, facilitating the training of more robust and general-purpose robotic policies.
- **Foundational for Downstream Applications:** The integrated visual-language-action annotations provide a rich source of supervision for various downstream tasks. This includes affordance and keypoint detection, enabling robots to perceive actionable regions more effectively. It also supports robot-oriented Visual Question Answering (VQA) and visual instruction fine-tuning for multimodal robot models, contributing to more grounded and reasoning-capable robotic systems.
- **Generalizability Across Embodiments and Tasks:** The unified framework and action schema support diverse objects, tasks (e.g., grasping, articulation, insertion, deformable manipulation, navigation), and robot embodiments (e.g., parallel-jaw, dexterous, bimanual, mobile manipulators, humanoids). This aims to reduce the need for task- or embodiment-specific annotation pipelines, fostering greater versatility in robot learning.
- **Validation of Annotation Quality:** Rigorous physics validation, including robustness tests and parallel simulation, ensures that the generated annotations are not only geometrically plausible but also physically executable, contributing to higher task success rates in both simulation and real-world zero-shot transfer.

The potential impact of AnnotateAnything lies in its contribution to lowering the barrier for creating diverse and high-quality robot learning datasets. By providing a scalable infrastructure to transform raw 3D content into actionable robot intelligence, it aims to accelerate research in general-purpose robotics, multimodal robot perception, and skill-centric learning, enabling the development of more capable and adaptable robotic agents.
