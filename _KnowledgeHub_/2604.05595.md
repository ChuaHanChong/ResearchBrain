# Uncovering Linguistic Fragility in Vision-Language-Action Models via Diversity-Aware Red Teaming

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced DAERT, a framework for generating diverse adversarial linguistic instructions to identify vulnerabilities in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The framework achieved a task success rate of 5.85% on target VLA models and demonstrated strong transferability of these linguistic fragilities across different VLA architectures and environments.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit linguistic fragility when faced with variations in instructions, posing safety and reliability challenges for real-world deployment.
- Existing red teaming methodologies for VLA models are limited by rigid heuristics or mode collapse in RL-based approaches, preventing comprehensive discovery of diverse vulnerabilities.
- A lack of scalable methods for systematically stress-testing VLA models against diverse linguistic inputs hinders the identification of critical safety blind spots.

## Method

- The Diversity-Aware Embodied Red Teaming (**DAERT**) framework formulates ==red teaming== as a ==reinforcement learning== problem, integrating a ==diversity-aware objective== to mitigate ==mode collapse==.
- An implicit ==diversity-aware actor-critic== mechanism, using a ==uniform-average successor value==, smooths the optimization landscape to maintain linguistic diversity in generated instructions.
- A ==physically-grounded, multi-gate, cascaded reward== mechanism ensures that adversarial instructions remain valid control signals, preserving action intention and executable validity.

## Results

- DAERT reduced the average task success rate of the π0 VLA model from **93.33%** to **5.85%** with adversarial instructions, outperforming other baselines in attack effectiveness.
- The framework simultaneously achieved the highest semantic diversity (CLIP Cosine Distance of **12.23**) and LLM-as-Judge score (**8.48**), effectively mitigating mode collapse observed in standard RL methods.
- Adversarial instructions generated by DAERT showed strong zero-shot transferability, reducing OpenVLA's success rate from **76.50%** to **6.25%** and achieving approximately **60%** attack performance on CALVIN and **82.0%** on SimplerEnv.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contemporary VLA models demonstrate significant fragility to linguistic variations, often relying on surface-level patterns rather than robust compositional understanding, leading to severe generalization gaps.
> - Promoting diversity in adversarial instruction generation is crucial for uncovering a broad spectrum of vulnerabilities and for ensuring the transferability of attack methods across different VLA architectures and environments.
> - Introducing compositional constraints and fine-grained behavioral requirements in instructions is more effective at exposing VLA model fragility than simple lexical substitutions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute corresponding actions by integrating visual observations. These models leverage pre-trained vision-language models and large-scale datasets, demonstrating capabilities in various benchmarks. However, a critical, yet under-explored, safety concern is their robustness to linguistic nuances, such as rephrased instructions or the inclusion of irrelevant context. This "linguistic fragility" poses substantial risks for real-world deployment where human-robot communication is inherently diverse and variable.

The broader research landscape recognizes red teaming as an essential methodology for identifying environmental scenarios that elicit catastrophic behaviors in embodied AI agents, thereby contributing to safer deployment. Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach for automated red teaming, aiming to uncover these vulnerabilities. Nevertheless, existing RL-based adversarial methods often suffer from mode collapse. This phenomenon causes the adversary to converge to a narrow set of trivial or repetitive failure patterns, failing to reveal the comprehensive landscape of meaningful risks and limiting the utility of the discovered vulnerabilities for model improvement.

Prior red teaming efforts specifically for VLAs are nascent. These approaches are often training-free, relying on rigid in-context learning or heuristic detection, which inherently restricts their adaptability across diverse tasks and scenarios. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a method that automatically generates a diverse set of challenging linguistic instructions, moving beyond the limitations of existing methods to comprehensively stress-test VLA models and uncover a broader spectrum of their linguistic vulnerabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and develop a novel framework, Diversity-Aware Embodied Red Teaming (DAERT), to systematically expose the vulnerabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to linguistic variations. This framework aims to uncover the full landscape of linguistic fragility, which is currently an under-explored safety concern.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations regarding VLA models and current red teaming methodologies:
- **Linguistic Fragility as a Safety Risk**: Despite their success in robotic manipulation, existing VLA models exhibit significant fragility when faced with linguistic variations, such as rephrased instructions or extraneous context. This sensitivity is a major safety and reliability challenge for real-world deployment, where human instructions are naturally diverse and unstructured.
- **Limitations of Current Red Teaming**: While red teaming is recognized as vital for identifying model vulnerabilities, existing approaches for VLAs are limited. Many are training-free, rely on rigid in-context learning, or employ heuristic detection. These methods lack adaptability across different tasks and often fail to generate a diverse set of adversarial instructions.
- **Mode Collapse in RL-based Red Teaming**: Automated red teaming approaches using reinforcement learning often suffer from mode collapse. Standard reward-maximizing RL algorithms tend to converge to a narrow set of highly effective but repetitive failure patterns. This over-optimization prevents comprehensive exploration of the instruction space, thus failing to reveal a wide range of potential risks.
- **Need for Comprehensive Vulnerability Assessment**: To ensure the safe and reliable deployment of embodied AI agents, there is a clear need for a scalable approach that can stress-test VLA models thoroughly, exposing critical safety blind spots related to linguistic robustness before these systems are introduced into real-world environments.

Therefore, the researchers aim to overcome these limitations by developing an automated method capable of generating diverse and effective adversarial instructions, thereby enhancing the evaluation of VLA model robustness and ultimately contributing to their safer development and deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research formulates embodied red teaming as a reinforcement learning (RL) problem with a specific focus on mitigating mode collapse to ensure the generation of diverse adversarial instructions.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Language-conditioned robotic tasks are modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). A VLA model's policy, π(a_t|s_t, l_task), generates actions a_t based on the current state s_t and a natural language task instruction l_task. The objective of embodied red teaming is to find a diverse set of attack instructions {l_i^attack} that induce execution failures (i.e., task success indicator 1_succ = 0). These instructions must be physically achievable, capability-aligned with the VLA's known skills, and linguistically plausible. The red teaming objective minimizes the VLA's success rate while maximizing instruction diversity, balanced by a hyperparameter λ.

This problem is transformed into an RL optimization problem where an attacker model (a VLM policy p_θ) learns to generate instructions l_attack. The objective is to maximize an expected reward R(π, l_attack), which encourages task failures, regularized by the entropy H(p_θ) to promote diverse generation. Standard RL algorithms, however, typically yield mode-seeking behavior, leading to a narrow set of outputs.

#### 3.2. DAERT Framework

To address the mode collapse issue, the researchers propose the Diversity-Aware Embodied Red Teaming (DAERT) framework, which incorporates a diversity-aware objective inspired by the Random Policy Valuation (ROVER) mechanism.

- **Implicit Diversity-Aware Actor-Critic**: DAERT avoids training a separate critic by using an implicit token-level Q-parameterization defined by the deviation of the current policy p_θ from a frozen reference policy p_θ_old. The Q-value is scaled by ρ for exploration. A target Q-value is constructed by combining the terminal reward with a uniform-average successor value, which smooths the optimization landscape and implicitly penalizes sharp probability peaks, thereby maintaining linguistic diversity.
- **Group Relative Training**: To stabilize training with sparse binary rewards (task success/failure), a group of 'n' rewritten instructions is sampled for each input. Their rewards are standardized relative to the group's average, making the reward signal more informative for learning. The final objective minimizes the Bellman error against the diversity-aware target Q-values.

#### 3.3. Physically-Grounded Reward Design

A multi-gate, cascaded reward mechanism is designed to ensure that generated adversarial instructions remain valid control signals, preserving action intention and executable validity, thus preventing "instruction drift" or "verbosity hacking."

- **Cascaded Action-Alignment Filtering**: This mechanism imposes three sequential constraints:
 - **Executable Format Gate (Structural Validity)**: Filters out instructions with degenerate artifacts (e.g., newline characters, meta-prefixes, non-English characters) that would cause pre-processing errors. Violation results in a fixed penalty (-0.2).
 - **Action-Intention Preservation Gate (Semantic Fidelity)**: Ensures the adversarial instruction l_attack still corresponds to the original physical target (l_task). This is enforced by using a CrossEncoder model to measure semantic similarity ϕ(l_task, l_attack). A hard retention threshold (τ_sem = 0.6) is applied, penalizing instructions that drift too far semantically.
 - **Concise Control Gate (Length Constraint)**: Prevents the attacker from generating overly verbose instructions that might cause failure due to context window overflow rather than linguistic misunderstanding. A penalty is applied if the instruction length exceeds L_max.
- **Constraint-Aware Reward Formulation**: The total reward function integrates these gates. It is defined as the adversarial execution feedback (1 for successful attack/VLA task failure, 0 otherwise) multiplied by a product of (1 - I_k) for each constraint 'k', where I_k is a violation indicator. If a constraint is violated, a specific penalty r_k is applied, weighted by the satisfaction of all preceding constraints, ensuring penalties are cascaded.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Target VLA Models**: Two state-of-the-art models, π0 and OpenVLA, were used as targets, with their weights frozen during experiments.
- **Benchmarks**: Primary training and evaluation were on LIBERO, a suite of language-conditioned robotic manipulation tasks. Generalization was assessed on CALVIN and SimplerEnv benchmarks, without additional training for cross-domain transfer.
- **Attacker Model**: Qwen3-VL-4B, a vision-language instruction generator, was fine-tuned as the attacker policy. It takes the original instruction and an initial RGB image as input.
- **Baselines**: Comparisons were made against Original Instructions (upper bound), Embodied Red Teaming (ERT) (GPT-4o based manual prompting), and GRPO (standard RL with identical reward function). An ablation of GRPO without KL divergence regularization was also included.
- **Implementation Details**: The VERL framework was used for optimization. Semantic rewards were computed using stsb-roberta-large. Training involved 100 steps, a group size of 6, batch size of 8, learning rate of 1 × 10−6, KL coefficient of 0.01, entropy coefficient of 0.001, and a DAERT temperature ρ = 1.0. A semantic similarity threshold of 0.6 and a maximum response length of 50 were enforced.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

Each task was evaluated by testing 10 rewritten instructions over 5 episodes, totaling 50 episodes per task. Performance was measured by:
- **Task Success Rate (Succ ↓)**: Average task success rate, with lower values indicating more effective attacks.
- **CLIP Cosine Distance (Cos ↑)**: Average pairwise cosine distance between CLIP embeddings of generated instructions, measuring semantic diversity in feature space.
- **LLM-as-Judge Score (LLM ↑)**: A holistic linguistic diversity score (1-10) evaluated by DeepSeek-R1, assessing lexical, syntactic, semantic, and stylistic variation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Target Model Selection and Language Dependency Analysis

A diagnostic test using a generic "no action" prompt revealed a substantial performance drop for π0, indicating its functional reliance on textual instructions. In contrast, π0.5 maintained high success rates, suggesting a vision-dominant policy. Consequently, π0 was selected for adversarial analysis to ensure a faithful evaluation of linguistic robustness.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results in LIBERO Benchmark

The frozen π0 policy exhibited an average success rate of 93.33% with original instructions. However, DAERT-generated adversarial instructions significantly reduced this to an average success rate of 5.85%. This indicates a severe generalization gap and suggests that current VLA models are highly dependent on surface-level linguistic patterns.
Comparing attack effectiveness and diversity:
- **Attack Effectiveness**: DAERT achieved the lowest overall task success rate (5.85%), outperforming ERT (65.50%), GRPO (20.45%), and GRPO w/o KL (11.35%).
- **Diversity**: DAERT simultaneously attained the highest semantic diversity (CLIP Cosine Distance of 12.23) and LLM-as-Judge score (8.48). GRPO and GRPO w/o KL suffered from mode collapse, showing lower diversity scores (7.05 and 5.28 for CLIP Cosine, 4.58 and 3.95 for LLM-as-Judge, respectively). This confirms that standard RL methods converge to narrow, repetitive adversarial patterns, whereas DAERT effectively explores a richer linguistic manifold.

#### 4.3. Transferability Across VLA Models

Adversarial instructions trained on π0 were tested against OpenVLA. OpenVLA's baseline success rate of 76.50% plummeted to 6.25% under DAERT-generated instructions. This significantly outperformed ERT (32.15%) and GRPO (17.00%), demonstrating that DAERT discovers universal linguistic vulnerabilities that transfer effectively across different VLA architectures and pre-training distributions.

#### 4.4. Direct Transfer to Other Benchmarks

- **CALVIN (3D-Diffuser Actor)**: DAERT, trained on LIBERO/π0, achieved a zero-shot cross-architecture transfer with approximately 60% attack performance (1 - success rate), significantly higher than GRPO and ERT. DAERT also maintained high semantic diversity, occupying an optimal region on the Pareto frontier for attack performance vs. diversity. This suggests DAERT captures fundamental, transferable semantic failure modes.
- **SimplerEnv (OpenVLA-7B)**: In a zero-shot cross-domain transfer to a different visual domain and robot embodiment, DAERT boosted attack performance to 82.0%. GRPO's performance (59.5%) surprisingly lagged behind ERT (69.2%), attributed to its overfitting to source-domain specifics without diversity constraints. This result underscores that diversity is crucial for transferability across substantial distributional shifts.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

- **Case Study**: ERT primarily relied on lexical substitution (e.g., "red milk carton"), which did not significantly degrade performance on robust tasks. DAERT, in contrast, introduced compositional constraints and fine-grained behavioral requirements (e.g., "orient it correctly," "without disturbing any other objects"), leading to a sharp performance drop. This indicates that increasing semantic complexity, rather than simple synonym replacement, effectively exposes the model's fragility.
- **Visual Execution Analysis**: Visualizations of agent rollout trajectories showed that DAERT-generated instructions with nuanced constraints induced disjointed motion planning, disrupted grasping primitives, and caused hesitation, leading to execution errors.
- **Semantic Space Exploration**: PCA visualization of instruction embeddings revealed that GRPO produced tight, dense clusters, indicative of mode collapse. ERT occupied a limited semantic pocket. DAERT, however, exhibited substantially broader variance, spanning wide regions of the embedding space and tracing a diverse diagonal trajectory, consistent with its diversity-aware objective.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

Removing the KL divergence term from GRPO (GRPO w/o KL) resulted in a more aggressive attacker but at the cost of severe mode collapse and reduced linguistic diversity. While the KL penalty stabilized generation and produced more varied instructions than GRPO w/o KL, its diversity remained well below DAERT, indicating that standard RL regularization alone is insufficient for broad adversarial search.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Significance

This research introduces DAERT, a novel automated, learning-based embodied red teaming framework designed to systematically evaluate the linguistic robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. A key contribution of DAERT is its integration of a diversity-aware reinforcement learning mechanism, which effectively mitigates the common problem of mode collapse in RL-based adversarial generation. This enables the framework to generate adversarial instructions that are both semantically consistent with the original task and highly effective in inducing VLA model failures, while maintaining high linguistic diversity.

The extensive experiments across diverse robotic benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN, SimplerEnv) and against state-of-the-art VLAs (π0, OpenVLA) demonstrate that DAERT significantly outperforms existing baselines in both attack success rate and coverage of failure modes. This work reveals deep-seated vulnerabilities in contemporary VLA architectures, suggesting that these models often rely on surface-level linguistic patterns rather than robust compositional understanding or true semantic grounding. The ability of DAERT to generate adversarial examples that exhibit strong zero-shot cross-domain transferability further highlights shared fundamental linguistic fragilities across different VLA models and environments.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The findings and proposed methodology of this research have several potential impacts:
- **Enhanced Embodied AI Safety**: By providing a scalable and effective framework for uncovering linguistic blind spots, DAERT contributes directly to improving the safety of embodied AI agents. Identifying these vulnerabilities before deployment is crucial for preventing unexpected and potentially dangerous behaviors in real-world human-robot interaction scenarios.
- **Improved VLA Generalization**: The diverse set of adversarial instructions generated by DAERT can serve as valuable augmentation data for training VLA models. Retraining or fine-tuning models with these challenging examples could lead to more robust policies that generalize better across a wider range of linguistic inputs, moving beyond reliance on narrow training distributions.
- **Standardized Benchmarking**: DAERT establishes a rigorous and reproducible methodology for benchmarking the linguistic robustness and generalization abilities of VLA models. This provides a standardized tool for researchers and developers to evaluate their models comprehensively, fostering progress in the field.
- **Shift in Development Paradigm**: The revelation that current VLAs are fragile to subtle linguistic variations may encourage a shift in the development paradigm, prompting greater focus on compositional understanding, semantic robustness, and diverse language grounding rather than solely optimizing for performance on canonical instructions.

#### 5.3. Future Directions

The authors suggest several avenues for future research. These include leveraging the proposed method for attacking more modern VLAs in increasingly complex and long-horizon manipulation tasks. Furthermore, future work can incorporate Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) evaluation pipelines to rigorously assess the validity and quality of generated adversarial data, potentially through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Finally, open-sourcing the full dataset of generated adversarial instructions is planned to foster transparency and facilitate broader community assessment, allowing external researchers to audit the attacks and contribute to a fair, open, and reproducible benchmark for future VLA model evaluations.
