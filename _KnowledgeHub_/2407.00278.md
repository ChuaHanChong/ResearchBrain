# PerAct2: Benchmarking and Learning for Robotic Bimanual Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Washington, Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, and NVIDIA introduced RLBench^2, a benchmark suite for bimanual robotic manipulation, and PerAct^2, a language-conditioned behavioral cloning agent. PerAct^2 demonstrated higher success rates on most bimanual tasks within this new simulation benchmark and showed qualitative transferability to a real-world humanoid robot.

## Problem

- A lack of a comprehensive, diverse, and reproducible simulated benchmark for robotic bimanual manipulation tasks.
- Challenges in developing learning-based policies for bimanual control due to the precise spatial and temporal coordination required between two arms.
- Existing methods often rely on less efficient multi-agent or fixed leader-follower architectures that struggle with complex bimanual tasks.

## Method

- Extended the existing RLBench framework to create **RLBench²**, a new open-source benchmark with ==13 diverse bimanual manipulation tasks== and 23 variations, designed for systematic evaluation.
- Proposed **PerAct²**, a ==language-conditioned behavioral cloning== agent utilizing a single ==Perceiver IO transformer== to implicitly learn bimanual coordination within a unified network.
- The agent processes multi-view RGB-D observations into a shared ==3D voxel grid==, integrates proprioception and language goals, and predicts ==6-DoF end-effector poses== and gripper states for both arms.

## Results

- PerAct² achieved the highest success rates in **9 out of 13** bimanual tasks, outperforming extended unimanual baselines like ACT, RVT-LF, and PerAct-LF.
- PerAct² demonstrated the fastest average training time (**54 hours**) compared to other methods, attributed to its efficient single-network architecture.
- Qualitative real-world experiments on the ARMAR-6 humanoid robot successfully demonstrated the robot-agnostic transferability of PerAct^2's policies for various bimanual tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single, end-to-end network architecture like PerAct^2 can effectively learn implicit coordination for bimanual tasks by sharing perceptual and proprioceptive information across both arms.
> - Representing observations as a 3D voxel grid and predicting 6-DoF end-effector poses allows for a robot-agnostic policy, enabling transferability to different hardware platforms.
> - Consistently low success rates across all evaluated methods underscore the inherent challenges in bimanual manipulation, particularly in achieving robust coordination, motion planning, and handling task complexities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly bimanual manipulation, presents significant challenges due to the requirement for precise spatial and temporal coordination between multiple robotic arms. While advancements have been made in grasping and unimanual manipulation, and several real-world bimanual robotic systems are emerging (e.g., Boston Dynamics' Atlas, Tesla's Optimus, Figure AI's Humanoid), the systematic study and evaluation of bimanual capabilities remain an underexplored area, especially concerning learning manipulation policies.

Existing research in bimanual manipulation often focuses on specific aspects such as explicit coordination constraints, task and motion planning (TAMP), leader-follower architectures using Dynamical Movement Primitives (DMPs), or learning separate coordination modules. Notable real-world efforts include learning from teleoperation. However, real-world experiments face reproducibility and variability issues, which hinder systematic assessment across a wide range of tasks.

The current landscape of simulated benchmarks for robotic manipulation, such as robosuite, ManiSkill2, Orbit, and HumanoidBench, typically offer a limited number of bimanual tasks, if any, or focus on other aspects like dexterous hand manipulation or whole-body control. RLBench, a widely accepted benchmark, provides a large diversity of unimanual tasks and automated dataset generation but lacks bimanual scenarios. This absence of a comprehensive, standardized, and reproducible simulated benchmark with high task diversity for bimanual manipulation is identified as a critical gap in the field.

This research directly addresses this gap by extending an established unimanual benchmark (RLBench) to specifically include bimanual manipulation tasks. Furthermore, it contributes to the development of learning-based policies for bimanual control by proposing a novel, language-conditioned behavioral cloning agent, PerAct$^2$, which integrates bimanual action prediction and coordination within a single network architecture. The work aims to provide a foundation for systematic evaluation and further research in bimanual robotic manipulation by offering both a benchmark and a baseline learning method.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent complexity of bimanual manipulation, which demands precise spatial and temporal coordination and a deep understanding of tasks, particularly in dynamic environments. Despite recent progress in unimanual robotic manipulation and the increasing deployment of complex bimanual robotic systems in real-world scenarios, the systematic investigation and learning of bimanual manipulation policies remain challenging. Real-world experiments, while valuable, suffer from issues of reproducibility and variability, making standardized assessment difficult. This highlights a critical need for a dedicated, diverse, and reproducible benchmark in a simulated environment.

The key objectives of this work are as follows:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive Bimanual Manipulation Benchmark:** The central objective is to extend the existing RLBench framework to create a simulated benchmark for bimanual manipulation. This extension, referred to as RLBench$^2$, aims to feature a large diversity of tasks that require a high degree of coordination and adaptability between two robotic arms. The motivation is to facilitate reproducible and systematic evaluation of novel methods and models for bimanual manipulation.
2. **Introduce a Diverse Set of Bimanual Tasks:** A specific objective is to implement and open-source a benchmark comprising 13 new bimanual manipulation tasks with 23 unique task variations. These tasks are designed to encompass a range of complexities, including prehensile and non-prehensile manipulation, and varying degrees of physical, spatial, and temporal coupling and coordination, in response to language instructions.
3. **Propose a Novel Learning Method for Bimanual Control:** The research aims to introduce a language-conditioned behavioral cloning agent, named PerAct$^2$, as an extension of the unimanual PerAct framework. The motivation for this is to develop a single network architecture that can efficiently integrate language processing with bimanual action prediction, implicitly learning coordination and addressing the limitations of approaches involving separate agents or fixed leader-follower roles.
4. **Provide Baselines and Initial Evaluations:** To kickstart the benchmark, the paper aims to extend several existing state-of-the-art unimanual learning-based methods (PerAct, RVT, ACT) to bimanual manipulation and evaluate their performance on the newly introduced tasks. This serves to establish baseline capabilities and highlight the challenges inherent in bimanual control, particularly the crucial role of coordination.
5. **Demonstrate Robot-Agnosticism and Real-World Transferability:** A further objective is to illustrate that the proposed PerAct$^2$ method is robot-agnostic, meaning its policy, which outputs a 6-DoF pose, can be readily transferred to different robot platforms with varying degrees of freedom. This is motivated by demonstrating qualitative experiments on a humanoid robot in a real-world setting.

Overall, the work is motivated by the desire to accelerate research in bimanual manipulation by providing both a standardized evaluation environment and an advanced learning methodology.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the challenge of bimanual manipulation through two main components: the development of a bimanual manipulation benchmark (RLBench$^2$) and the proposal of a novel learning method (PerAct$^2$).

#### 3.1. Benchmark Extension: RLBench$^2$

The authors extended RLBench, a robot learning benchmark suite, to accommodate bimanual manipulation. This extension was designed to retain RLBench's core functionalities, including task diversity, reproducibility, and the ability to generate training data with variations automatically.
- **Task Development:** 13 new bimanual manipulation tasks were introduced, offering 23 unique task variations. These tasks were categorized based on a bimanual taxonomy, considering factors such as temporal, spatial, and physical coupling, symmetry, and synchronicity. Examples include "push box" (requiring synchronous force application), "lift a ball" (non-prehensile, synchronous), "push two buttons" (simultaneous pressing), "handover an item" (coordinated object transfer), and tasks involving opening containers or manipulating deformable objects.
- **Task Description and Metrics:** Each task includes detailed descriptions, success metrics, lists of involved objects, and explicit outlining of coordination challenges (provided in Appendix A of the paper). This level of detail aims to standardize evaluation and understanding of task complexities.
- **Implementation:** The implementation required synchronization mechanisms to control both arms simultaneously within the simulation environment.

#### 3.2. Learning Method: PerAct$^2$

PerAct$^2$ is a language-conditioned behavioral cloning agent, an extension of the unimanual PerAct framework, designed to predict bimanual manipulation actions.

- **Input Representation:**
 - **Perception:** The model takes RGB-D camera images from multiple views (five cameras in simulation: front, left/right shoulder, left/right wrist) and merges them into a 3D voxel grid. This voxel grid (100$^3$ voxels with 0.01m size, covering 1.0m$^3$ workspace) represents both the observation and action space, offering a viewpoint-independent representation that addresses occlusion issues inherent in image-based methods.
 - **Proprioception:** Current joint states and velocities of both robot arms are provided as input.
 - **Language Goal:** A natural language instruction describing the task (e.g., "push the box to the red area") is tokenized and embedded.
- **Network Architecture:**
 - The core of PerAct$^2$ is a single Perceiver IO transformer. This architecture efficiently learns per-voxel and language features.
 - **Bimanual Integration:** Unlike naive approaches that would use two separate agents or a fixed leader-follower structure, PerAct$^2$ integrates bimanual control within a single network. It achieves this by splitting the latent space between the two arms and implementing a combined self-attention mechanism. This design allows for shared voxel representation and proprioception, facilitating implicit communication and coordination between the arms without predefined roles.
- **Action Inference:**
 - For each robot arm, the network outputs a discretized action tuple:
 - **6-DoF End-Effector Pose:** This includes a translation (identified as the voxel nearest the gripper center) and a rotation (quantified into discrete 5° intervals for each axis).
 - **Gripper State:** A binary value indicating whether the gripper should be open or closed.
 - **Collision Flag:** A binary parameter indicating whether the motion planner should use collision avoidance (e.g., for non-contact movements) or allow contact (e.g., for opening a drawer).
- **Expert Demonstrations and Keyframe Extraction:**
 - The model is trained using a dataset of expert demonstrations, where each demonstration consists of a sequence of simultaneously executed bimanual actions, paired with visual observations and a language goal.
 - **Keyframe Extraction:** A heuristic, extended for the bimanual case, identifies salient keyframes from continuous demonstrations. A keyframe is recorded if either arm's gripper state changes or if an arm's end-effector pose ceases to change (indicating trajectory completion). This discretizes the continuous actions into "next best actions" for behavioral cloning.
- **Training:**
 - The loss function is an extension of PerAct's, encompassing individual losses for translation, rotation, gripper state, and collision flag for both the right and left arms. Each component uses a softmax over predicted Q-values.
 - Data augmentation, including translation and rotation perturbations on the voxel grid and keyframes, is applied to enhance robustness.

#### 3.3. Baselines and Evaluation Setup

- **Baseline Methods:** The authors compared PerAct$^2$ against three other methods:
 - **ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers):** An image-based method that outputs joint positions.
 - **RVT-LF (Robotic View Transformer - Leader-Follower):** Two independent RVT networks arranged in a leader-follower architecture.
 - **PerAct-LF (Perceiver Actor - Leader-Follower):** Two independent PerAct networks in a leader-follower architecture.
- **Simulation Experiments:**
 - Two Franka Panda robots were used.
 - Noiseless RGB-D sensors (five cameras) with 256x256 resolution.
 - Each of the 13 tasks was trained individually with 100 demonstrations.
 - Training was conducted on NVIDIA A40 GPUs for up to 100k iterations, with batch sizes maximized to fit GPU memory.
 - Performance was evaluated based on task success rate (over 100 episodes) and average training time.
- **Real-World Experiments:**
 - Qualitative experiments were conducted on the ARMAR-6 humanoid robot to demonstrate robot-agnosticism.
 - A single Azure Kinect RGB-D sensor (1920x1080 resolution) was used, with a reduced voxel size of 50x50x50.
 - Demonstrations were recorded via kinesthetic teaching.
 - A Cartesian waypoint controller executed the predicted targets.
 - Four tasks were demonstrated, including synchronous and spatial coordination tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation primarily focuses on the task success rates in simulation and training efficiency, supported by qualitative real-world demonstrations.

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance

- **Task Success Rates (Table 3):**
 - PerAct$^2$ achieved the highest success rate in 9 out of the 13 bimanual tasks. For example, in "push two buttons," PerAct$^2$ achieved 47% success, significantly outperforming PerAct-LF (10%), RVT-LF (39%), and ACT (4%). Similarly, for "handover an item," PerAct$^2$ recorded 11% success, while other methods yielded 0-3%.
 - PerAct-LF demonstrated the highest success in 3 tasks: "put item in drawer" (27%), "sweep dustpan" (28%), and "lift tray" (14%).
 - RVT-LF performed best for "push box" (52% success).
 - ACT generally exhibited the lowest success rates across tasks, often failing completely.
 - The overall success rates for all methods remained relatively low, even for the top-performing ones, indicating the complexity of the bimanual manipulation tasks.
 - Image-based methods like ACT and RVT encountered difficulties due to task symmetry (robot arms being identical) and the high variation in spawned objects or generated motion planning paths, especially for ACT, which predicts joint angles rather than 6-DoF poses.
- **Average Performance and Training Time (Table 4):**
 - In terms of average task success rate across all tasks, PerAct-LF achieved the highest (17.5%), closely followed by PerAct$^2$ (16.8%). RVT-LF and ACT had lower average success rates (10.5% and 5.9%, respectively).
 - PerAct$^2$ demonstrated the fastest average training time (54 hours), which was notably quicker than PerAct-LF (89 hours), ACT (80 hours), and RVT-LF (231 hours). The reduced training time for PerAct$^2$ is attributed to its single network architecture and shared voxel representation.

#### 4.2. Discussion of Failure Types

Analysis of common failure modes provided insights into the challenges:
- **Collisions:** In tasks like "handover," robots often collided due to insufficient spatial awareness or timing errors.
- **Grasping Failures:** Misalignment of grippers led to objects not being securely grasped, or missing the object entirely (e.g., "pick up plate," "lift tray"). These issues were linked to insufficient demonstrations or errors in motion planning.
- **Temporal Dependencies:** Tasks requiring sequential actions, such as "put item in drawer" (opening the drawer before placing the item), failed if one sub-task was not completed correctly.
These failures highlight the need for more sophisticated motion planning and coordination strategies in bimanual manipulation.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Robot-Agnosticism:** The PerAct$^2$ framework was successfully integrated into the ARMAR-6 humanoid robot, demonstrating its ability to transfer to different hardware platforms due to its 6-DoF pose output, which is separated from the underlying controller.
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative experiments showed ARMAR-6 performing four bimanual tasks: "lifting a bowl," "pushing a chair," "storing away a tool," and "getting a coke." Three of these tasks required synchronous arm coordination, while one involved spatial coordination.
- **Challenges:** The authors noted that quantitative reproducibility in real-world scenarios is challenging due to hardware variability, object pose changes, and sensor noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical gaps in benchmarking and learning for bimanual tasks.

1. **Enabling Systematic Research:** The primary significance lies in the introduction of RLBench$^2$, an open-source benchmark specifically designed for bimanual manipulation. This benchmark, comprising 13 new tasks with 23 variations, provides a standardized and reproducible environment for systematically evaluating and comparing new bimanual manipulation methods. This is crucial for accelerating research, as the lack of such a diverse benchmark has previously hindered progress in this complex domain.
2. **Advancing Bimanual Learning Architectures:** The proposed PerAct$^2$ method offers a novel approach to learning bimanual manipulation policies. By employing a single Perceiver IO network to implicitly learn coordination between two arms, it moves beyond less efficient multi-agent or fixed leader-follower architectures. The architecture's ability to efficiently integrate language instructions and action prediction, alongside its shared voxel representation and proprioception, represents an advance in learning bimanual tasks.
3. **Providing Strong Baselines:** The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of PerAct$^2$ against extended state-of-the-art unimanual methods (ACT, RVT-LF, PerAct-LF). These benchmarks establish current performance levels and highlight the inherent difficulties of bimanual tasks, setting a clear challenge for future research to build upon.
4. **Demonstrating Robot-Agnosticism:** The qualitative real-world experiments on the ARMAR-6 humanoid robot successfully demonstrate that PerAct$^2$'s policy, by outputting 6-DoF end-effector poses rather than joint angles, is robot-agnostic. This indicates broad applicability across various robotic platforms, including humanoids, which could expand the utility of learned policies.
5. **Highlighting Future Research Directions:** The consistently low success rates across all evaluated methods underscore the significant remaining challenges in bimanual manipulation. This finding encourages the research community to focus on developing more robust and sophisticated solutions for complex coordination, handling deformable objects, improving motion planning strategies, and enhancing spatial and temporal awareness, potentially incorporating mobile manipulation.
6. **Efficiency Benefits:** The comparatively faster training time of PerAct$^2$ offers practical advantages, allowing researchers to iterate more quickly on model development and experimentation in a computationally intensive field.

Overall, this work lays foundational groundwork for future advancements in bimanual robotics, offering both a standardized testing ground and a novel, efficient learning methodology.
