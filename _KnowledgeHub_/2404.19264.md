# DiffuseLoco: Real-Time Legged Locomotion Control with Diffusion from Offline Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffuseLoco trains a single transformer-based DDPM policy on offline multi-skill locomotion datasets, using receding-horizon control and delayed inputs for real-time inference, achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of five diverse skills (bipedal walking, running, trotting, pacing, hopping) on quadrupedal and bipedal robots, edge-deployable.

## Problem

- Online RL for legged locomotion cannot scale to large models and datasets since it requires costly online rollouts on dynamic, high-DOF systems.
- Prior multi-skill RL approaches like AMP suffer mode collapse and have never combined agile bipedal walking with quadrupedal skills in one policy.
- Existing offline learning for locomotion is confined to simplified simulated Gym tasks or single-skill humanoid walking, unproven at scale or in diverse real-world skill sets.

## Method

- **DiffuseLoco**: a transformer-based ==DDPM== policy trained end-to-end via ==MSE noise-prediction loss== on source-agnostic state-action-goal tuples pooled from heterogeneous single-skill RL source policies.
- ==Receding horizon control== with delayed inputs: predicts action sequence from lagged state/goal/action history so denoising runs in parallel with control, enabling real-time inference above 30-50Hz on edge compute.
- Architecture uses separate MLP encoders for state and goal embeddings queried via transformer ==cross-attention== against noisy action tokens, retaining ==DDPM== (not ==DDIM==) sampling for accuracy.

## Results

- DiffuseLoco is the only method achieving **100%** stability across all five velocity/turn commands in real-world trials, versus AMP baselines failing at **0%** and **20%** stability for **0.7 m/s** forward.
- Against TF w/ RHC baseline, DiffuseLoco cuts velocity tracking error by **38.97%** on average and reduces body angular-velocity oscillation by **10.40%**.
- Ablations show DDIM sampling raises tracking error by **42-51%** versus DDPM, and removing domain randomization drops stability/robustness by **44.26%**, confirming both design choices matter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion policies avoid the mode collapse that plagues GAN-style multi-skill imitation (AMP), letting one model represent genuinely distinct gait modalities under identical commands.
> - Sequence-prediction training (denoising a horizon of actions) generalizes better than single-step autoregressive or reconstruction-loss objectives, which overfit offline trajectories.
> - Delayed-input receding horizon control is a general recipe for deploying large, slow-inference sequence models on high-frequency real-time control loops.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion control has seen significant advancements, largely driven by reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Prior state-of-the-art approaches primarily rely on *online* RL, where policies are trained through continuous interaction with a simulated environment. While online RL has enabled impressive single-skill agility (e.g., jumping, running, highly dynamic maneuvers), it faces inherent challenges:

- **Scalability:** Online RL struggles to scale to large models and diverse datasets due to the computational cost and time required for online rollouts.
- **Multi-skill Learning:** Integrating a diverse set of dynamic locomotion skills into a *single* coherent policy remains an open problem. Many existing multi-skill methods either train separate policies and require complex high-level coordination or use motion imitation which still needs well-trained expert policies and struggles with model expressiveness for diverse skills. Combining fundamentally different gaits like bipedal and quadrupedal locomotion in a unified policy is particularly challenging.
- **Data Reusability:** Online RL is data-inefficient; data gathered is often specific to a training run and difficult to reuse across different tasks or policy versions.

In contrast, *offline* learning, particularly from large-scale datasets, has revolutionized fields like computer vision and natural language processing (e.g., large language models, diffusion models for image generation). In robotics, offline learning has shown promise for manipulation and autonomous driving, domains characterized by lower-dimensional action spaces, lower replanning frequencies, and inherently more stable systems. However, its application to high-frequency, low-level control of *unstable, high-DOF* systems like legged robots has been limited to simplified simulations or single-skill tasks.

**Diffusion models** are a class of generative models that excel at modeling complex, multimodal data distributions. Their application in robotics is relatively new, mostly focusing on high-level trajectory planning, safe planning, or visuomotor manipulation tasks, which typically involve slower dynamics and less stringent real-time constraints. Applying diffusion models to *low-level, high-frequency* control of legged robots is a nascent area, presenting unique challenges due to the rapid dynamics and inherent instability.

DiffuseLoco positions itself at the intersection of these challenges. It aims to bridge the gap by:
- Leveraging the scalability and multimodal capabilities of diffusion models.
- Applying these to *offline learning* for legged locomotion.
- Enabling *real-time control* on physical robots with *diverse, multi-skill* policies.

This work directly addresses the limitations of current online RL approaches for multi-skill legged locomotion and extends the applicability of large-scale offline learning and generative models to a highly dynamic and challenging robotics domain. It represents a significant step towards developing more generalist legged robot controllers that can learn from vast, diverse datasets without continuous online interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DiffuseLoco is to develop a scalable framework for training multi-skill, diffusion-based policies for dynamic legged locomotion *exclusively from offline datasets*, enabling robust real-time control of diverse skills on physical robots.

The key motivations driving this research are:

1. **Overcoming Online RL Limitations for Legged Locomotion:** Current state-of-the-art legged locomotion controllers are predominantly trained using online reinforcement learning (RL). While effective for single skills, online RL struggles to scale to large models and datasets, making it challenging to train a single policy capable of performing a diverse range of dynamic locomotion skills. The online rollout requirement makes data collection expensive and difficult to reuse.
2. **Leveraging Offline Learning for Scalability:** Inspired by breakthroughs in computer vision and natural language processing, where scaling models and datasets through offline learning has led to superior performance and generalization, the authors seek to apply this paradigm to legged robotics. This allows for effective data reuse, simpler training schemes, and potentially more versatile policies.
3. **Harnessing Multimodality of Diffusion Models:** Legged locomotion involves diverse, often multimodal, behaviors (e.g., trotting, pacing, hopping, bipedal walking). Traditional behavior cloning methods often struggle to capture this multimodality and can suffer from mode collapse. Diffusion models, by their nature, are adept at modeling complex data distributions and generating diverse outputs, making them a suitable candidate for learning multiple, distinct locomotion skills from a varied dataset without explicit skill labeling.
4. **Enabling Real-Time Control and Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A core motivation is to ensure the learned policies are deployable in the real world on edge computing devices. This requires addressing challenges related to the inference speed of large models (like transformers used in diffusion models) and ensuring robustness against real-world uncertainties and dynamics. The goal is to achieve zero-shot transfer from simulation-trained policies to physical robots.
5. **Achieving Seamless Skill Transitions:** Beyond learning individual skills, the ability for a single policy to smoothly and freely transition between different locomotion skills, even those not explicitly demonstrated as transitions in the dataset, is a crucial step towards truly versatile robot autonomy.

In essence, DiffuseLoco aims to push the boundaries of learning-based legged locomotion by combining the strengths of large-scale offline learning with the generative power of diffusion models, tailored specifically for the demanding requirements of real-time, dynamic robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffuseLoco proposes a novel, scalable framework structured into three distinct stages: Data Source, Training, and Deployment.

**a) Data Source:**
The first stage involves compiling a diverse offline dataset of locomotion demonstrations.
- **Diverse Skill Collection:** The framework is versatile enough to utilize existing datasets or generate new ones from multiple "source policies." In this work, the authors use a combination of RL methods:
 - **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP):** For skills like trotting and pacing, by imitating reference motions from dog motion capture data.
 - **Central Pattern Generator (CPG) Guidance:** For periodic skills like hopping and bouncing, using nominal CPG curves.
 - **Symmetry-Augmented RL:** For agile bipedal walking on quadrupedal robots, leveraging symmetric gait patterns.
 - For bipedal robots (Cassie), demonstrations are collected from separately trained walking and running RL policies.
- **Source-Agnostic Data:** Despite varying underlying RL methods and their specific observation/action spaces, the critical step is to collect *source-agnostic* state-action-goal pairs. For legged robots, this typically means proprioceptive feedback (motor positions/velocities, base orientation/angular velocities) as states, and joint-level PD targets as actions.
- **Goal Randomization:** Goals (commands like desired velocity, base height, turning rate) are re-sampled periodically within episodes to ensure the policy learns to adapt to varying commands.
- **Dynamics Randomization:** Crucially, the dataset is diversified by applying significant domain randomization during data collection (e.g., varying motor PD gains, base mass, ground friction, and random base velocity changes). This is vital for promoting sim-to-real transfer robustness.

**b) Training:**
The DiffuseLoco policy is trained end-to-end on this aggregated offline dataset using a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) with a Transformer backbone.
- **DDPM for Multimodality:** DDPMs are chosen for their ability to model complex multimodal distributions, allowing a single policy to represent diverse locomotion skills and their variations without explicit skill labeling.
- **Input & Output:** The model takes a history of proprioceptive states, goals, and *previous actions* (`s_t-h:t`, `g_t-h:t`, `a_t-h-1:t-1`) and predicts a sequence of `n` future actions (`a_t:t+n`). Including previous actions helps with system identification and state estimation for legged locomotion.
- **Transformer Backbone:** An encoder-decoder Transformer serves as the denoising model ($\epsilon_\theta$). Separate MLP encoders transform robot I/O history and goal history into distinct embeddings. These embeddings, along with a one-hot diffusion step embedding, are queried by noisy action tokens via multiple Transformer decoder layers using cross-attention. Causal attention masks are applied. This architecture allows the model to assign different attention weights to dynamic states and static goals.
- **Training Objective:** The model learns to predict the noise added to the action sequence using a mean squared error (MSE) loss, effectively learning the reverse diffusion process.
- **Real-Time Control Considerations in Training:**
 - **Receding Horizon Control (RHC) during Inference:** While the model predicts a sequence of future actions, only the immediate action is executed. This allows for rapid replanning based on new state information, which is critical for unstable dynamic systems.
 - **Delayed Inputs:** To overcome the long inference times of large Transformer models, DiffuseLoco employs delayed inputs. The policy uses inputs from the *previous* timestep (`s_t-h-1:t-1`, `a_t-h-2:t-2`, `g_t-h-1:t-1`) to predict actions for the *current* timestep (`a_t:t+n`). This enables parallel computation, as the inference for the next action can begin before the very latest state `s_t` is available, ensuring actions are up-to-date while managing computational load.
 - **DDPM over DDIM:** Unlike many diffusion applications that use DDIM for faster inference (fewer steps), DiffuseLoco sticks with DDPM, even with more denoising iterations. This choice is based on empirical findings that DDPM yields more accurate and stable action outputs, which is crucial for the high-frequency control of unstable legged robots, preventing compounding errors and out-of-distribution scenarios.

**c) Deployment:**
The trained DiffuseLoco policy is transferred *zero-shot* to real robot hardware.
- **Inference Acceleration:** To meet real-time control frequencies (e.g., 30-50Hz) on edge computing devices, a specialized acceleration pipeline is utilized. This involves converting the model to ONNX format and then optimizing it with NVIDIA TensorRT, which refines the execution graph and compiles it for the target GPU. This optimization achieves significant speedup (e.g., 7X faster inference per denoising iteration), allowing the policy to run onboard.
- **Execution:** During deployment, the policy receives noisy action sequences, denoises them conditioned on real-time robot states and goals, and outputs a sequence of actions. Only the first action is applied via joint-level PD targets, following the RHC principle.

This comprehensive methodology allows DiffuseLoco to learn from diverse offline data, manage the complexity of multi-modal behaviors, and achieve robust real-time control on physical legged robots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DiffuseLoco demonstrates significant advancements in multi-skill legged locomotion control, validated through extensive real-world experiments and quantitative comparisons.

- **State-of-the-Art Multi-Skill Controller:**
 - **Quadrupedal Robots:** DiffuseLoco successfully learns and executes five diverse and agile locomotion skills—trotting, pacing, hopping, bouncing, and crucially, *bipedal walking*—within a *single policy* on a quadrupedal robot (Unitree Go1). This represents a leading effort, as prior RL methods have struggled to combine such varied and dynamic skills, especially stable bipedal walking with quadrupedal gaits, in a unified controller.
 - **Bipedal Robots:** The method is also extended to high-dimensional bipedal robots (Cassie in MuJoCo simulation), learning both walking and running skills within a single policy and demonstrating smooth transitions. However, zero-shot transfer to the real Cassie hardware proved challenging, highlighting a limitation for higher-dimensional, more complex systems.

- **Versatility with Diverse Data Sources:**
 - DiffuseLoco proves invariant to the specific methods used to generate the offline demonstrations. It successfully integrates data derived from disparate RL frameworks (AMP, CPG guidance, symmetry-augmented RL) into a cohesive policy, simplifying the process of combining diverse skill sets. This capability is critical for scaling locomotion learning.

- **Seamless Skill Transitioning and Modality Switching:**
 - The policy demonstrates the ability to transition freely and robustly between skills that were *not explicitly trained as transitions* in the dataset (e.g., hopping to bipedal walking to pacing).
 - It also showcases impressive modality switching within the same command, such as transitioning from trotting to pacing when a sudden braking event changes the contact sequence, demonstrating DiffuseLoco's capacity to learn and adhere to different modes present in the offline data.

- **Real-World Robustness:**
 - DiffuseLoco policies exhibit considerable robustness on real quadrupedal robots across various ground conditions (padded floor, bare floor, turf) and small terrain variations (e.g., stepping over a wooden board). Bipedal walking on a half-padded floor demonstrated robustness to significant differences in ground height, friction, and restitution forces on opposing legs.
 - While its robustness is acknowledged as subpar compared to *single-skill* state-of-the-art RL policies (which often benefit from extensive simulation randomization), DiffuseLoco shows potential for improvement by absorbing more diverse data, including potentially real-world data, highlighting its scalable nature.

- **Quantitative Performance Benchmarking:**
 - **Against RL Baselines (AMP, AMP w/ H):** DiffuseLoco significantly outperforms RL-trained AMP policies in real-world stability and velocity tracking. DiffuseLoco is the *only* method among tested baselines that reliably completes all challenging locomotion tasks without falling. AMP baselines suffer from "mode-collapse" in real-world deployment, leading to reduced performance at high speeds or for low-speed tasks where they oscillate instead of tracking commands.
 - **Against Non-Diffusion Behavior Cloning (TF, TF w/ RHC):** DiffuseLoco demonstrates superior stability and robustness.
 - Single-step prediction (TF) policies are unstable and fail on agile tasks.
 - Transformer with Receding Horizon Control (TF w/ RHC) performs better but struggles with more agile motions (e.g., 0.7 m/s forward), with stability drastically dropping. Analysis of training curves shows that TF w/ RHC tends to overfit the offline datasets, leading to less robust performance in out-of-distribution real-world scenarios.
 - DiffuseLoco, benefiting from its DDPM training, produces smoother and more stable motions (e.g., 10.40% less body oscillation and 38.97% less tracking error compared to TF w/ RHC), indicating DDPM's suitability for imitating locomotion tasks.

- **Ablation Studies Validating Design Choices:**
 - **Receding Horizon Control (RHC):** Essential for improving smoothness and consistency of the locomotion policy, outperforming single-step prediction.
 - **Sampling Techniques (DDPM vs. DDIM):** DDPM (with full denoising steps) is found to be superior for real-time control of legged robots compared to DDIM, which tends to produce noisier control signals leading to instability and higher tracking errors.
 - **Model Architecture:** A Transformer backbone significantly outperforms a CNN-based U-Net, producing more stable actions due to better handling of temporal data and separate attention for goal conditioning.
 - **Dataset Effects (Dynamics Randomization):** Training on datasets with diverse dynamics randomization significantly enhances real-world robustness and stability (e.g., 44.26% increase in robustness for challenging tasks).

In summary, DiffuseLoco delivers a robust, real-time, multi-skill locomotion controller for quadrupedal robots by effectively leveraging diffusion models and offline data. It addresses key limitations of prior RL and behavior cloning methods, setting a new benchmark for combining diverse dynamic skills in a single policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiffuseLoco represents a significant contribution to the field of legged robotics and the broader domain of learning-based control, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Pioneering Application of Diffusion Models in Low-Level Legged Control:** This work is among the first to successfully apply expressive diffusion models for *real-time, high-frequency, low-level* control of highly dynamic and unstable legged robots, specifically demonstrating zero-shot transfer to hardware. Previous applications of diffusion models in robotics were largely confined to high-level planning or manipulation tasks with inherently more stable dynamics. This expands the known utility of diffusion models into a new, challenging domain.

2. **Scalable Multi-Skill Locomotion Learning:** DiffuseLoco provides a scalable framework for learning a diverse array of agile locomotion skills within a *single unified policy*. By leveraging offline datasets, it bypasses the demanding online rollout requirements of traditional reinforcement learning, making it more data-efficient and flexible. The ability to absorb data from various sources (e.g., different RL algorithms, even those with varying internal structures) without requiring complex integration or expert policy access is a major step towards truly generalist robot controllers.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Performance over Baselines:**
 - **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The method demonstrates superior real-world stability and velocity tracking compared to multi-skill RL baselines (like AMP) that often suffer from mode-collapse and poor generalization to the real world. DiffuseLoco's DDPM training objective inherently promotes smoother and more coherent action sequences, which is vital for stable locomotion.
 - **Beyond Behavior Cloning:** It outperforms non-diffusion behavior cloning methods, showing that the generative capabilities of diffusion models lead to more robust and less overfitting policies for complex dynamics, even with the same model capacity.

4. **Feasibility of Large-Scale Offline Datasets for Legged Robots:** The work demonstrates that learning *exclusively from offline datasets* is feasible and highly effective for complex legged locomotion. This opens up possibilities for collecting and curating large, diverse datasets (potentially including real-world robot experience) to continuously improve policy robustness and skill repertoire without needing to re-train expert policies or perform expensive online interactions for every new skill.

5. **Path Towards Generalist Robot Policies:** By demonstrating the ability to combine fundamentally different locomotion types (quadrupedal and bipedal) and seamlessly transition between them, DiffuseLoco lays groundwork for truly general-purpose legged robots capable of adapting to a wide range of tasks and environments with a single control policy. This aligns with a broader trend in robotics towards generalist foundation models.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By learning from offline data, the framework could significantly reduce the time and computational resources required to develop new locomotion skills, as it minimizes the need for iterative online fine-tuning on expensive robot hardware.
- **Wider Applicability:** More versatile and robust legged robots could be deployed in a wider range of unstructured and dynamic environments, from disaster response to logistics and exploration.
- **Future Research Directions:** DiffuseLoco motivates future work on:
 - Scaling to even larger and more diverse datasets, including real-world experiences.
 - Developing control-specific sampling techniques for diffusion models to further accelerate inference without compromising stability.
 - Integrating multimodal inputs such as vision and language instructions to allow for higher-level task conditioning.
 - Exploring adaptation to different robot morphologies within a single policy.

In conclusion, DiffuseLoco represents a pivotal advancement, demonstrating that offline learning with diffusion models can unlock new levels of scalability, versatility, and real-world robustness for legged robot locomotion, paving the way for the next generation of agile and adaptable robotic systems.
