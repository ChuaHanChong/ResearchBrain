# PLED-VINS: A Point-Line Event-Based Visual Inertial SLAM for Dynamic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> KAIST researchers developed PLED-VINS, a point-line event-based visual-inertial SLAM system that achieves accurate state estimation in dynamic environments by leveraging an adaptive fusion of event-camera-derived temporal reliability and robust geometric consistency to suppress observations from moving objects.

## Problem

- Conventional visual SLAM systems assume static environments, leading to degraded accuracy when dynamic objects introduce erroneous geometric constraints.
- Frame-based cameras are susceptible to motion blur in high-speed or aggressive motion scenarios, making dynamic object handling challenging.
- Existing event-based SLAM frameworks largely assume static scenes, and current event-based dynamic handling methods often incur high computational costs, limiting real-time applicability.

## Method

- **PLED-VINS**, a monocular event camera-based visual-inertial SLAM, uses a ==sliding-window VIO backend== that incorporates both point and line features.
- It develops an ==Entropy-Recency Score Map== to quantify pixel-level temporal consistency from ego-motion compensated event streams, distinguishing static from dynamic features.
- The system adaptively fuses this temporal reliability with geometric reliability derived from a ==robust bundle adjustment==, applying ==motion-conditioned weighting== for line features.

## Results

- PLED-VINS achieved consistently lower **Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE)** on the VIODE dataset, outperforming other SLAM methods, particularly in high dynamic scenarios.
- The system recorded the lowest **Mean Position Error (MPE)** on the real-world DAVIS 240C dataset, demonstrating effective suppression of dynamic human features and real-time processing at **21.25 Hz**.
- An ablation study showed that integrating temporal reliability significantly improved the distinction between static and dynamic features, leading to better weight estimation for both points and lines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained temporal statistics from motion-compensated event streams (quantified by entropy-recency scores) can effectively distinguish static features from independently moving objects.
> - Adaptively combining event-based temporal reliability with geometric reliability from robust bundle adjustment provides a more comprehensive and resilient assessment of feature trustworthiness.
> - Dynamically adjusting the fusion of reliability cues, especially for line features based on their geometric observability and temporal consistency, improves robustness in monocular dynamic SLAM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental problem in robotics, enabling autonomous agents to build a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously tracking their own location within it. Most SLAM systems have traditionally been developed under the assumption of a static environment. This assumption is frequently violated in real-world scenarios, where moving objects like vehicles and pedestrians introduce erroneous geometric constraints, leading to degradation in state estimation accuracy. Addressing dynamic environments is therefore a significant challenge in the SLAM community.

Frame-based visual SLAM methods have attempted to handle dynamic scenes by identifying moving objects, often leveraging semantic segmentation (e.g., deep learning-based object detectors) or geometric consistency checks. However, these approaches face limitations. Semantic methods are typically restricted to predefined object classes and can be computationally intensive, while geometric methods can struggle in scenarios with aggressive motion, where motion blur in traditional camera frames can lead to incorrect feature associations and unreliable geometric constraints.

Event cameras represent an alternative sensing modality that addresses some of the limitations of frame-based cameras, particularly in low-light and high-speed motion conditions. Unlike frame-based cameras that capture intensity images at a fixed rate, event cameras asynchronously record per-pixel brightness changes, yielding a stream of "events" with high temporal resolution. This enables precise analysis of instantaneous motion and reduces sensitivity to motion blur. Despite these advantages, the majority of existing event-based SLAM frameworks have largely adopted the static environment assumption. While there have been attempts to integrate event-based motion segmentation into SLAM, these methods often involve high computational costs, limiting their real-time applicability for state estimation.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the capabilities of event cameras and the demands of dynamic SLAM. It addresses the challenge of robust state estimation in dynamic environments by developing a monocular event camera-based visual-inertial SLAM framework that efficiently incorporates dynamic information at the feature level, rather than relying on computationally expensive explicit object-level segmentation. The work explores the under-leveraged potential of event camera temporal dynamics to characterize feature reliability and integrate this information with geometric consistency in a unified optimization framework. This positions PLED-VINS as a contribution towards developing more robust and efficient SLAM systems for real-world applications where dynamic scene elements are ubiquitous.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust monocular event camera-based visual-inertial SLAM framework, termed PLED-VINS, that can achieve accurate state estimation in dynamic environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aimed at addressing key challenges in the current SLAM landscape:

1. **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** The fundamental motivation stems from the common failure of conventional visual SLAM systems when the static scene assumption is violated. Moving objects introduce outliers into geometric optimization, degrading the accuracy and consistency of camera pose and map estimates. The work seeks to mitigate this by developing mechanisms to identify and suppress unreliable observations originating from dynamic elements.

2. **Leveraging Event Camera Advantages:** Event cameras offer unique properties, such as high temporal resolution and low latency, which make them less susceptible to motion blur compared to standard frame-based cameras. However, most existing event-based SLAM frameworks still assume static scenes, and the full potential of event data for handling dynamic scenes remains underexplored. The objective is to effectively harness the fine-grained spatio-temporal information provided by event cameras to discern dynamic features.

3. **Efficient Dynamic Information Integration:** Existing approaches to dynamic SLAM, whether frame-based (semantic segmentation, geometric checks) or event-based (motion segmentation), often incur high computational costs. Semantic segmentation requires predefined object classes and significant processing, while event-based clustering methods can introduce substantial integration overhead, hindering real-time performance. A key motivation for PLED-VINS is to integrate dynamic information efficiently at the feature level, avoiding explicit object-level segmentation to enable real-time operation.

4. **Comprehensive Feature Reliability Estimation:** Current methods often lack a systematic approach to quantify the reliability of features in dynamic contexts. This is particularly challenging for monocular systems and for different feature types. The paper aims to propose a novel method for characterizing the reliability of *both* point and line features, drawing from both temporal statistics of event streams and geometric consistency. Line features, while providing stronger geometric constraints, are often difficult to handle robustly in monocular dynamic SLAM due to scale ambiguity and motion degeneracy.

5. **Adaptive Fusion of Multi-modal Reliability Cues:** Observations can be unreliable for various reasons (e.g., independent motion, poor tracking, occlusions). A single reliability metric may not be sufficient. The motivation is to develop a strategy that adaptively fuses complementary reliability cues derived from different sources—specifically, event-based temporal patterns and geometric consistency from bundle adjustment—to provide a more robust and comprehensive assessment of feature trustworthiness. This includes a specific consideration for line features where observability can vary with motion direction.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more resilient SLAM systems in dynamic real-world environments, specifically by designing an event-based monocular visual-inertial framework that can effectively and efficiently identify and down-weight dynamic observations through a novel, adaptively fused temporal and geometric reliability assessment for both point and line features.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PLED-VINS is a monocular event camera-based visual-inertial SLAM framework designed for dynamic environments. The system processes incoming event, IMU, and monocular image data, integrating them within a sliding-window Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) backend. Its core innovation lies in a multi-faceted approach to characterize and adaptively weigh the reliability of point and line features, thereby suppressing contributions from dynamic objects.

**System Architecture:**
The framework builds upon DynaVINS and incorporates several key modules:
1. **Preprocessing:**
 - **Motion Compensation:** Raw event streams are first compensated for the camera's ego-motion using IMU measurements. This process aligns events that originate from static scene structures, sharpening their spatial representation and enabling more accurate pixel-wise temporal analysis. Events from independently moving objects will generally remain spatially dispersed even after ego-motion compensation.
 - **Event Encoding:** Compensated events are encoded into a Time Surface (TS) via a Surface of Active Events (SAE). The TS represents the recency of events at each pixel.
 - **IMU Pre-integration:** IMU measurements are pre-integrated to provide relative motion estimates for the VIO backend.
2. **Feature Detection and Tracking:**
 - **Image Features:** Shi–Tomasi corners are detected and tracked using Kanade–Lucas–Tomasi (KLT).
 - **Event Features:** Point features are extracted from the TS using Arc*, and line features are detected using the Line Segment Detector (LSD). Line features are tracked using the line band descriptor.
3. **Temporal Reliability Estimation (Entropy-Recency Score Map):**
 This module quantifies the temporal consistency of event streams at a pixel level, aiming to distinguish events from static structures (consistent with ego-motion) from those generated by independently moving objects.
 - **Entropy Score:** For motion-compensated events within a fixed time window, the event stream is divided into *B* temporal bins. A probability mass function is calculated for each pixel based on event counts in these bins. The normalized Shannon entropy `h(x, y)` is computed from this distribution. A higher entropy indicates a more dispersed temporal distribution, suggesting inconsistency with a single rigid motion hypothesis.
 - **Recency Score:** To complement entropy, a recency score `q(x, y)` is defined. This score assigns higher values when event distributions are biased towards later bins, indicating recent activations that are characteristic of moving objects. A temporal decay rate `α` is used in its formulation.
 - **Entropy-Recency Score Fusion:** The entropy and recency scores are combined using a nonlinear symmetric fusion function. This fusion `s(x, y)` emphasizes pixels with jointly high entropy and recency (indicative of dynamic objects) while suppressing regions where only one score is elevated (e.g., highly textured static areas, or low-light regions with sparse but recent events).
 - **Feature Temporal Reliability:** For a point feature at `(x, y)`, its temporal reliability `w_EV_pt` is `1 - s(x, y)`. For a line feature, the reliability `w_EV_line` is computed by averaging the entropy-recency scores of pixels within a Gaussian-weighted "line band region" `Ω_ℓ` around the line.
4. **Geometric Reliability Estimation (Unified Point-Line Robust Bundle Adjustment):**
 Geometric reliability is estimated within a robust sliding-window VIO backend, extending DynaVINS to incorporate line features.
 - **Optimization Problem:** The system jointly optimizes the camera's state (poses, velocities, IMU biases) and 3D landmark positions, along with feature weights `w_pt` and `w_line`. The optimization minimizes a cost function comprising IMU pre-integration residuals, point reprojection residuals, and line reprojection residuals.
 - **Robust Loss Function:** A robust loss function `ρ` is employed for point and line residuals. This function includes terms for the squared residual weighted by `(w_j)^2`, a regularization term `λ_w(1 - w_j)^2` to penalize small weights, and a momentum term `λ_m(n_j(˜w_j - w_j))^2` to maintain weight stability over time.
 - **Residuals:** Point features use standard reprojection residuals (difference between observed and reprojected pixel coordinates). Line features use a residual based on the perpendicular distance of their endpoints to the reprojected 3D line.
 - **Geometric Weights:** The optimized weights `w_BA_pt` and `w_BA_line` from this robust BA serve as the geometric reliability measures.
5. **Adaptive Fusion of Temporal and Geometric Reliability:**
 The temporal `w_EV` and geometric `w_BA` reliability measures are adaptively combined to produce the final feature weights `w_pt` and `w_line` used in the subsequent BA iterations. This creates a recursive refinement loop.
 - **Point Feature Fusion:** The fusion coefficient `α_pt` for point features is determined by `α_0 * exp(-|w_EV_pt - w_BA_pt| / τ)`. This formulation reduces the contribution of temporal reliability (`w_EV_pt`) when it strongly disagrees with geometric reliability (`w_BA_pt`), limiting the impact of potentially erroneous temporal cues.
 - **Line Feature Fusion (Motion-Conditioned):** For line features, the fusion coefficient `α_line` is dynamically adjusted based on geometric observability and temporal consistency.
 - **Normal Flow Ratio (`p_n`):** This term quantifies the proportion of the line's tracking motion that is normal to the line itself. A higher `p_n` indicates stronger geometric constraints for the line residual. When geometric observability is high, `α_line` is reduced, increasing reliance on `w_BA_line`.
 - **Gradient Alignment Term (`r`):** This term measures the alignment between the mean temporal gradient (∇q) within the line band region and the line's 2D tracking motion direction. High alignment suggests that the temporal behavior is consistent with the estimated motion, thereby supporting the temporal reliability. When alignment is strong, `α_line` increases, giving more weight to `w_EV_line`.
 - The combined `α_line = 1 - p_n(1 - r)` adaptively modulates the influence of temporal reliability based on how well geometric constraints are defined and how strongly temporal evidence supports the motion.

This comprehensive methodology integrates event camera-specific temporal dynamics with robust geometric optimization, providing a refined, feature-level reliability estimation strategy for stable SLAM in dynamic environments.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experimental evaluation of PLED-VINS was conducted across three datasets: the simulated VIODE dataset, the real-world DAVIS 240C dataset, and the high-resolution DSEC dataset, comparing its performance against several contemporary feature-based SLAM and VIO methods.

**A. Evaluation on the VIODE Dataset:**
The VIODE dataset features simulated image-IMU sequences with increasing levels of dynamic objects (none, low, mid, high). Synthetic events were generated.
- **Quantitative Results (ATE):** PLED-VINS consistently demonstrated improved accuracy, achieving lower Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) across most sequences and dynamic levels compared to other methods. Notably, in the "high" dynamic sequences, PLED-VINS achieved the lowest ATE, indicating superior robustness in highly cluttered dynamic scenes.
 - Methods like PL-VINS, PL-EVIO, and ESVIO, which primarily rely on static-scene geometric assumptions, showed significant performance degradation in high dynamic scenarios.
 - DynaVINS, which uses motion agreement for dynamic suppression but lacks temporal information, consistently exhibited higher errors than PLED-VINS.
 - E2-VINS, which incorporates some temporal cues, also degraded in these challenging sequences, suggesting a less comprehensive exploitation of temporal information compared to PLED-VINS.
- **Qualitative Results:** Trajectory comparisons on sequences like `city_night_high` showed that PLED-VINS maintained closer alignment with the ground truth, particularly during early, curved segments where other methods exhibited deviations. This was attributed to the complementary temporal reliability mitigating initial residual distortions and stabilizing convergence under strong dynamics. The visualization of feature weights confirmed effective suppression of dynamic features while preserving static structures.

**B. Evaluation on the DAVIS 240C Dataset:**
This dataset provides real-world data including events, images, IMU, and ground-truth poses, featuring dynamic human subjects.
- **Quantitative Results (MPE):** PLED-VINS achieved the lowest Mean Position Error (MPE) in both `dynamic_translation` and `dynamic_6dof` sequences. This indicates its effectiveness in real-world scenarios with dynamic objects and sensor noise.
- **Qualitative Results:** The system effectively suppressed features associated with the moving human subject while robustly tracking static background features.
- **Runtime Performance:** PLED-VINS demonstrated an average processing frequency of 21.25 Hz at 240 × 180 resolution, suggesting real-time applicability.

**C. Evaluation on the DSEC Dataset:**
The DSEC dataset comprises high-resolution driving sequences with rich sensor data, but without ground-truth poses.
- **Qualitative Validation:** PLED-VINS was qualitatively evaluated on highly dynamic sequences like `zurich_city_01_e` and `zurich_city_01_f`. The framework successfully suppressed features belonging to moving vehicles (e.g., cars, trucks), preserving reliable features from the static urban background.
- **Trajectory Consistency:** The estimated trajectories exhibited plausible motion patterns consistent with driving scenarios and showed close agreement with a FAST-LIO reference trajectory, despite the challenging and highly dynamic nature of the outdoor environments.

**D. Ablation Study:**
An ablation study on the `parking_lot_high` sequence of the VIODE dataset investigated the contribution of the temporal reliability estimator.
- **Metrics:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE) between estimated weights and ground-truth dynamic labels (where static=1, dynamic=0), and a weight ratio `w_ratio` (mean(w_static) / mean(w_dynamic)).
- **Results:** Integrating temporal reliability (geometric + temporal) significantly reduced MAE and increased `w_ratio` for both point and line features compared to using geometric-only weighting. This indicates that the entropy-recency score map effectively improved the discrimination between static and dynamic observations. The improvement was particularly pronounced for line features, suggesting that temporal information provided crucial cues that geometric reliability alone might not fully capture for line dynamics.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that PLED-VINS enhances state estimation accuracy and robustness in dynamic environments by effectively leveraging and fusing temporal reliability derived from event streams with geometric reliability from robust bundle adjustment. Its performance surpassed methods that rely solely on geometric cues or employ less sophisticated temporal modeling, especially in high-dynamic scenarios.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The PLED-VINS framework represents a notable advancement in the field of Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) for dynamic environments, with several key aspects contributing to its significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Dynamic Scenes:** The primary significance of PLED-VINS lies in its ability to improve the robustness and accuracy of state estimation in environments where dynamic objects are present. By explicitly modeling and suppressing unreliable observations from moving elements, the system mitigates a fundamental challenge that often causes failure or significant drift in conventional SLAM systems. This makes SLAM more viable for real-world applications where dynamic scene elements are common.

2. **Novel Feature Reliability Estimation:** The introduction of the entropy–recency score map is a novel contribution. It provides a data-driven, event-centric method to quantify the temporal reliability of pixel locations, which is then extended to both point and line features. This moves beyond relying solely on geometric consistency or high-level semantic information, offering a granular and efficient way to leverage the unique temporal characteristics of event cameras for dynamic scene understanding.

3. **Comprehensive Utilization of Event Camera Data:** The work demonstrates a more comprehensive exploitation of event cameras' advantages. While event cameras are known for their high temporal resolution and low latency, their full potential in dynamically challenging SLAM scenarios has been largely under-explored. PLED-VINS shows how fine-grained temporal event statistics, particularly after ego-motion compensation, can be effectively translated into actionable reliability cues for SLAM, enhancing the capabilities of event-based systems.

4. **Adaptive Multi-Modal Fusion:** The adaptive fusion strategy, combining temporal and geometric reliability, particularly the motion-conditioned weighting for line features, is significant. It acknowledges that different cues provide complementary information and that their relative importance can vary based on sensing conditions and feature types. This intelligent fusion mechanism allows the system to flexibly adapt to different dynamic scenarios and improve overall performance.

5. **Practical Applicability for Autonomous Systems:** The demonstrated improvements in accuracy and robustness across simulated and real-world dynamic datasets suggest a strong potential impact on various autonomous systems. This includes autonomous vehicles navigating city streets, drones operating in complex environments, and mobile robots interacting with humans or other moving agents. More reliable SLAM contributes directly to safer and more effective navigation, mapping, and interaction capabilities for these systems.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The approach of modeling feature reliability at a granular level and adaptively fusing multi-modal cues opens up new avenues for future research. The authors themselves point to extending the reliability formulation to multi-motion hypotheses. This could lead to more sophisticated dynamic object tracking and segmentation, further refining the distinction between ego-motion consistent structures and independently moving objects, potentially even enabling predictions of dynamic object behavior within the SLAM framework.

In conclusion, PLED-VINS advances the state-of-the-art in event-based visual-inertial SLAM by providing a robust and efficient solution for dynamic environments. Its novel reliability estimation and adaptive fusion strategy demonstrate a deeper integration of event camera capabilities into the SLAM pipeline, with substantial implications for the development of resilient autonomous systems.
