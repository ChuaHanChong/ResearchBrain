# DiffuCoder: Understanding and Improving Masked Diffusion Models for Code Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Apple and The University of Hong Kong present DiffuCoder, an open-source 7B masked diffusion model for code generation, demonstrating performance competitive with autoregressive models. They introduce Coupled-GRPO, a 'diffusion-native' reinforcement learning algorithm that improves DiffuCoder's performance by 4.4% on EvalPlus, while also enhancing the model's non-autoregressive generation and parallelism.

## Problem

- Autoregressive LLMs, while dominant, are limited by their sequential left-to-right generation, which may not be ideal for tasks requiring global planning like code generation.
- Open-source masked diffusion models (MDMs) for code generation lacked a strong, reproducible testbed and a systematic understanding of their decoding behaviors.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for MDMs either showed marginal gains or compromised the models' non-autoregressive nature by relying on semi-autoregressive decoding.

## Method

- The authors developed and open-sourced **DiffuCoder**, a 7B ==masked diffusion== model for code, trained through a multi-stage adaptation pipeline from an autoregressive LLM.
- They systematically investigated dLLM decoding behaviors using novel 'autoregressive-ness' metrics (Local AR-ness@k and Global AR-ness@k) and analyzed the impact of sampling temperature.
- They introduced Coupled-GRPO, a 'diffusion-native' reinforcement learning algorithm that addresses variance in log-probability estimation using a novel coupled-sampling scheme based on antithetic variates.

## Results

- The **7B** DiffuCoder base model achieves competitive performance with leading autoregressive code LLMs of similar size on benchmarks like HumanEval and MBPP.
- Coupled-GRPO significantly boosts DiffuCoder-Instruct's performance by +**4.4%** on EvalPlus, demonstrating stable and efficient training compared to baseline RL approaches.
- Post-Coupled-GRPO training, DiffuCoder-Instruct shows reduced global autoregressive-ness and a smaller performance drop when decoding steps are halved, indicating enhanced parallelism and robustness to faster generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) for code generation do not decode purely autoregressively; they exhibit flexible, non-sequential generation patterns.
> - An 'entropy sink' phenomenon in conditional generation biases initial token recovery towards local context, contributing to an inherent autoregressive tendency.
> - Sampling temperature in MDMs uniquely diversifies both token choices and their generation order, providing a richer exploration space for reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) is currently dominated by **autoregressive (AR) models**, which generate text token by token from left to right. Models like OpenAI's GPT series, Meta's Llama, and Alibaba's Qwen have showcased remarkable capabilities across a wide range of tasks, including dialogue, reasoning, and code generation.

However, **masked diffusion models (MDMs)**, also known as Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs), have recently emerged as a compelling alternative. Unlike AR models, MDMs operate by iteratively refining an entire sequence in parallel, starting from a fully masked or noisy input and progressively denoising it to produce the final output. This parallel, iterative refinement mechanism intrinsically allows for **global planning of content**, a feature that is intuitively well-suited for tasks requiring non-sequential thought processes or back-and-forth refinement, such as image synthesis and, crucially, code generation. Writing code often involves jumping between different parts of a function, defining helper methods before the main logic, or refactoring, which aligns more closely with a global planning and iterative refinement paradigm than a strict left-to-right generation.

While commercial-scale dLLMs for code, such as Inception Labs' Mercury and DeepMind's Gemini Diffusion, have demonstrated performance comparable to leading AR code models and even offer faster generation speeds, the mechanisms of their open-source counterparts remain largely unexplored and poorly understood. Existing open-source dLLMs like LLaDA and Dream have shown promise, but their post-training efforts (e.g., using DPO or GRPO) have either yielded only marginal gains or relied heavily on "semi-autoregressive" (block-wise) decoding, which deviates from the core global planning benefit of diffusion.

This paper directly addresses this gap by:
1. **Providing a robust open-source 7B dLLM (DiffuCoder)** specifically trained for code generation, serving as a much-needed testbed for in-depth analysis.
2. **Systematically investigating the decoding behavior** of dLLMs, particularly for code, to understand how their generation differs from AR models and to quantify their "autoregressive-ness."
3. **Developing a "diffusion-native" reinforcement learning (RL) methodology** that harnesses the unique characteristics of dLLMs without resorting to AR-like compromises, thereby unlocking their full potential for code generation.

By offering both an open-source model and a refined RL training framework, this work aims to deepen the community's understanding of dLLMs and push the boundaries of their application in complex tasks like code generation, positioning them as viable and potentially superior alternatives to traditional AR models in certain domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to **demystify the decoding behavior of Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) and unlock their full potential for code generation**. The authors are motivated by the inherent advantages of dLLMs – their global planning and iterative refinement capabilities – which align well with the non-sequential nature of code writing, distinguishing them from the dominant autoregressive (AR) paradigm.

The specific key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **To develop and release a strong 7B-scale open-source Masked Diffusion Model (MDM) specialized for code generation, named DiffuCoder.**
- **Motivation:** While commercial dLLMs for code have shown promise, a comprehensive, openly available model with a detailed training recipe was lacking. Such a model is essential to serve as a reproducible testbed for systematic investigation and further development by the research community.

2. **To systematically investigate the intrinsic decoding behaviors of dLLMs and how they differ from AR models.**
- **Motivation:** Despite their intuitive fit for code, the exact mechanisms of dLLM generation, especially their level of "non-autoregressiveness," were poorly understood in open-source contexts. Existing dLLMs often resorted to semi-AR decoding, suggesting an unclear understanding of how to fully leverage their global planning capabilities. The authors sought to demystify how dLLMs decide their generation order and whether they can inherently adapt their causality.

3. **To introduce quantitative metrics for "autoregressive-ness" (AR-ness) in dLLM generation and analyze its evolution.**
- **Motivation:** To objectively measure and track how closely dLLM generation patterns resemble traditional left-to-right AR generation. This would help understand the impact of data modality (e.g., code vs. math) and different training stages on the model's inherent causality and parallelism. The observation of an "entropy sink" phenomenon further motivated understanding how local context biases initial generation.

4. **To understand the role of sampling temperature in dLLM generation diversity.**
- **Motivation:** In AR models, temperature primarily affects token choice diversity. The authors suspected that in dLLMs, temperature might also influence the generation *order*, creating a richer search space for post-training methods like RL. Uncovering this unique aspect of dLLM diversity was crucial for designing effective RL strategies.

5. **To design and implement a "diffusion-native" reinforcement learning (RL) framework, coupled-GRPO, for dLLMs.**
- **Motivation:** Previous RL efforts for dLLMs either showed marginal gains or relied on semi-AR decoding during rollouts, which compromises the non-autoregressive nature of diffusion. The authors aimed to overcome the challenges of variance and efficiency in Monte Carlo-based log-probability estimations inherent in dLLMs, thereby developing an RL method that genuinely respects and enhances diffusion's global planning principle. This framework would lead to improved performance without reintroducing AR biases.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aimed to provide deeper insights into the fundamental machinery of dLLM generation and offer an effective, principles-aligned RL training methodology to significantly boost their performance in complex tasks like code generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach combining model training, systematic behavioral analysis, and novel algorithm development.

#### 3.1. DiffuCoder Model Training Pipeline

The foundation of their investigation is **DiffuCoder**, a 7B-scale Masked Diffusion Model (MDM) specifically tailored for code generation. Its training follows a multi-stage pipeline, leveraging insights from current LLM training practices:

1. **Adaptation Pre-training (Stage I):** The model is adapted from **Qwen-2.5-Coder**, an existing autoregressive LLM, utilizing a "continual pre-training" strategy (similar to DiffuLLaMA). It is trained on a massive 400B-token corpus, primarily comprising code data from RefineCode and Stackv2, mixed with general text and math data (Fineweb, DCLM-pro). This stage aims to imbue the AR-initialized model with diffusion capabilities and code-specific knowledge. Early stopping was applied based on downstream validation to avoid performance degradation from lower-quality later data.
2. **Mid-training (Stage II):** This stage acts as an annealing phase, training on 16B tokens of higher-quality annealing code data (algorithmic corpus, synthetic data) from OpenCoder. This helps bridge the gap between initial pre-training and later post-training stages, further enhancing the base model's capacity.
3. **Instruction Tuning (Stage III):** DiffuCoder undergoes supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using 436K instruction tuning samples from OpenCoder. They employ a "classifier-free guidance SFT" technique, which involves using a conditional mask to ensure the diffusion process respects the input prompt. A progressive conditional mask strategy was used for the first epoch, transitioning from unconditional pre-training to conditional instruction-following.
4. **Reinforcement Learning (Stage IV - Coupled-GRPO):** The instruction-tuned DiffuCoder is further enhanced using a novel RL algorithm. The training dataset consists of 21K "hard samples" from Acecoder-87k, filtered for low average and high variance pass rates to target challenging problems.

#### 3.2. Decoding Behavior Analysis

To understand how dLLMs generate sequences, particularly how non-autoregressive their behavior is, the authors introduce two novel metrics and conduct extensive analyses:

1. **Autoregressive-ness (AR-ness) Metrics:**
- **Local AR-ness@k (Consecutive Next-Token Prediction):** Measures the ratio of predicted sequence spans where each token is an immediate successor of the previously generated token within a *k*-length range. This quantifies local sequential coherence.
- **Global AR-ness@k (Earliest Mask Selection):** Measures the tendency for the model to unmask one of the earliest *k* positions among all remaining masked tokens at each step. This quantifies a broad left-to-right filling strategy.
- **Analysis Scope:** These metrics are used to compare:
 - Different dLLMs (LLaDA vs. adapted models like Dream/DiffuCoder).
 - Different data modalities (math vs. code).
 - DiffuCoder's evolution across its multi-stage training.

2. **Entropy Sink Phenomenon:** The authors investigate the confidence scores of tokens recovered during the first diffusion step of conditional generation. They observe an "L"-shaped distribution, where tokens immediately adjacent to the prompt (prefix) receive disproportionately high confidence, leading to a causal bias.

3. **Generation Diversity Analysis:** They examine how sampling temperature affects both token choices and, uniquely for dLLMs, the *order* of token generation. This is quantified by analyzing `pass@k` accuracy and visualizations of decoding trajectories.

#### 3.3. Coupled-GRPO Algorithm Development

The paper tailors Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for dLLMs, addressing the challenge of accurate and efficient log-probability estimation in the iterative diffusion process:

1. **Problem Formulation:** The denoising diffusion process is reinterpreted as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), allowing for policy optimization. Reward is defined at the final denoising step based on code correctness (test case pass rate) and format.
2. **Baseline Limitations:** Existing approaches (e.g., d1) compute token probabilities by masking all completion tokens and performing a single forward pass at a fixed diffusion step (t=T), which can be biased (as high-entropy tokens on the left are updated aggressively) and less representative of the actual multi-step diffusion process. Randomly masking condition tokens, as in d1, was also found unstable for code.
3. **Novelty: Coupled-Sampling Scheme:** To overcome these issues, coupled-GRPO introduces a novel scheme that samples complementary mask noise. For each completion sequence, it picks pairs of timesteps `(t, ˆt)` such that `t + ˆt = T` (e.g., one partial mask and its complement). Every token in the sequence is masked in exactly one of these two complementary passes. This ensures:
- **Full Coverage:** Every token's log-probability is computed.
- **Realistic Context:** Tokens are evaluated under partial-masking, which is more representative of the diffusion decoding process.
- **Variance Reduction:** The authors theoretically prove that this coupled sampling acts as an application of **Antithetic Variates**, guaranteeing a reduction in the variance of probability estimations by ensuring a negative covariance between the coupled samples.
- **Computational Efficiency:** It only requires two additional forward passes per update compared to the full-mask baseline (for λ=1).
4. **Advantage Estimation:** They also consider leave-one-out (LOO) strategies for estimating the value baseline for advantage calculation, providing an unbiased estimate.
5. **Reward Design:** The reward function for RL combines a code correctness reward (pass rate on test cases, verified via an online sandbox E2B) and a code format reward (valid Markdown code block, Python syntax check), reflecting practical code generation requirements.
6. **Hyperparameters and Ablations:** They carefully tune GRPO parameters and conduct ablation studies comparing coupled-GRPO against baseline sampling methods (d1, full mask completion, decoupled sampling) and different rollout temperatures.

This comprehensive methodology allows the authors to not only build a high-performing dLLM for code but also deeply analyze its generative properties and develop a principled RL approach tailored to its unique characteristics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings regarding dLLMs for code generation and the efficacy of the proposed coupled-GRPO method:

1. **DiffuCoder's Base Performance:** The 7B DiffuCoder, after its adaptation pre-training and mid-training stages, achieves performance competitive with leading autoregressive code LLMs of similar size, such as Qwen2.5-Coder and OpenCoder, on standard benchmarks like HumanEval, MBPP, and BigCodeBench (completion tasks). This establishes DiffuCoder as a robust testbed for further analysis and improvement.

2. **Instruction Tuning Gap in dLLMs:** A key observation is that while AR models like Qwen2.5-Coder show substantial performance gains from instruction tuning (e.g., +9.1% average for Qwen2.5-Coder+SFT), dLLMs (LLaDA, Dream, DiffuCoder) demonstrate only marginal improvements after instruction tuning (e.g., DiffuCoder-Instruct gains +1.1%). This gap highlights a significant area for post-training improvement in dLLMs, motivating the need for effective RL.

3. **Distinct Decoding Behavior of dLLMs (AR-ness Analysis):**
- **Non-Purely Autoregressive Generation:** Unlike AR models which are 100% autoregressive, dLLMs do not always decode in a strict left-to-right manner. A considerable fraction of tokens are recovered out of typical sequential order, indicating a more flexible decoding pattern.
- **Inherent AR Structure:** Despite their flexibility, dLLMs (both trained from scratch and adapted from AR models) naturally capture some AR structure, with AR-ness metrics closer to 1 than 0, suggesting an inherent sequential bias in text data. Adapted dLLMs tend to exhibit stronger AR-ness, likely due to inheriting left-to-right dependencies from their AR pre-training.
- **Entropy Sink Phenomenon:** The first diffusion step in conditional generation reveals an "L"-shaped confidence distribution, where tokens immediately to the right of the given prefix (prompt) are assigned disproportionately high confidence. This "entropy sink" contributes to the observed non-trivial AR-ness, biasing the model towards local context.
- **Modality-Specific AR-ness:** Code generation exhibits lower mean and higher variance in global AR-ness compared to mathematical text generation. This suggests that for code, the model more frequently produces later tokens first, delaying the recovery of some early masked tokens, aligning with the "global planning" nature of code writing.
- **AR-ness Evolution During Training:** AR-ness patterns change across training stages. Initial exposure to high-quality data (e.g., first epoch of instruction tuning) can increase causal bias, but as training progresses on more tokens, task performance improves, and measured AR-ness tends to decline. This implies dLLMs learn to capture dependencies beyond a strict AR order.

4. **Impact of Sampling Temperature on Diversity:**
- For dLLMs, increasing the sampling temperature (e.g., from 0.2 to 1.2) not only diversifies token choices (leading to significant `pass@k` improvements) but also *critically* diversifies their generation order, making the decoding process more non-autoregressive. This unique property of dLLMs creates a much richer search space for RL rollouts compared to AR models, where temperature primarily affects token probabilities at fixed positions.

5. **Effectiveness of Coupled-GRPO:**
- **Significant Performance Improvement:** Coupled-GRPO significantly boosts DiffuCoder-Instruct's performance, achieving a +4.4% gain on EvalPlus and substantial improvements across other benchmarks, including BigCodeBench, with training on only 21K samples. This demonstrates the power of RL when properly aligned with diffusion principles.
- **Stability and Efficiency:** Unlike baseline GRPO variants (d1, full-mask completion, decoupled sampling) which exhibit unstable reward learning, coupled-GRPO demonstrates stable and effective training. The theoretical proof of variance reduction via antithetic variates is empirically validated.
- **Reduced AR Bias and Increased Parallelism:** After coupled-GRPO training, DiffuCoder-Instruct shows a further decrease in global AR-ness. More importantly, when decoding steps are halved (leading to a 2x speedup), the coupled-GRPO trained model experiences a smaller performance drop compared to the pre-RL instructed model (Figure 1c). This suggests that coupled-GRPO enhances the model's non-AR generation capabilities and increases its inherent parallelism, making it more robust to faster decoding schedules.
- **Optimal Temperature Shift:** RL fine-tuning shifts the optimal sampling temperature for evaluation from the base model's 0.2 to a slightly higher value (0.3-0.4), indicating that training sharpens per-token distributions, similar to observations in AR LLMs.

In summary, the authors successfully trained a competitive open-source dLLM for code and provided an unprecedented, systematic analysis of its decoding behaviors. They leveraged these insights to develop a novel, principled RL algorithm that not only significantly improves performance but also embraces and enhances the non-autoregressive nature of diffusion models, paving the way for faster and more flexible code generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the development and application of Large Language Models, particularly within the domain of code generation:

1. **Deeper Understanding of Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs):** The paper provides an invaluable, systematic analysis of dLLM decoding mechanisms. The introduction of local and global AR-ness metrics offers novel tools to quantify the degree of sequential generation, shedding light on how dLLMs inherently capture AR structures while maintaining non-AR flexibility. The identification of the "entropy sink" phenomenon provides a new lens to understand initial causal biases in conditional generation. These insights are crucial for guiding future architectural designs and training strategies for dLLMs, moving beyond a trial-and-error approach.

2. **Validation and Advancement of Diffusion for Code Generation:** By training and open-sourcing DiffuCoder, a 7B-scale dLLM competitive with state-of-the-art AR models, the authors firmly establish the viability of diffusion models for complex tasks like code generation. This serves as a significant contribution to the open-source community, providing a robust base model for further research and application. It supports the notion that dLLMs are not just an alternative but a potentially superior paradigm for tasks benefiting from global planning and iterative refinement.

3. **Pioneering Diffusion-Native Reinforcement Learning:** The development of **coupled-GRPO** is a major methodological breakthrough. It addresses a critical challenge in applying RL to dLLMs—the variance and efficiency of log-probability estimations—by leveraging the theoretically sound principle of antithetic variates. By avoiding reliance on semi-AR decoding, coupled-GRPO aligns RL with the intrinsic non-autoregressive nature of diffusion models. This framework demonstrates how to effectively fine-tune dLLMs for complex objectives (like code correctness) while enhancing their unique parallel generation capabilities. This approach can potentially be generalized to other complex reasoning and generation tasks in dLLMs.

4. **Enhancing Parallelism and Generation Speed:** The finding that coupled-GRPO not only improves performance but also reduces AR-ness and leads to a smaller performance drop when decoding steps are halved is highly impactful. This directly translates to faster generation speeds, a critical advantage for practical deployment. As commercial dLLMs like Mercury and Gemini Diffusion already tout speed advantages, this work provides open-source validation and a pathway to achieve similar efficiencies in academic and open-source models.

5. **Broadening the Scope of Generative AI:** The discovery that sampling temperature in dLLMs diversifies *both* token choices and their generation order is a fundamental insight. This unique property creates a richer search space for exploration during RL rollouts, distinguishing dLLMs from AR models and highlighting a previously underappreciated source of diversity. This understanding can inform sampling strategies across various dLLM applications.

In conclusion, this paper provides a comprehensive blueprint for understanding, training, and optimizing masked diffusion models for code generation. Its contributions not only advance the state of the art in code synthesis but also offer profound insights into the mechanics of dLLMs, laying a strong foundation for their future exploration in complex reasoning, planning, and highly parallel generation tasks across various domains.
