# Heracles: Bridging Precise Tracking and Generative Synthesis for General Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The Heracles framework introduces a hierarchical control architecture for humanoid robots, employing a state-conditioned diffusion middleware that allows for seamless transitions between precise motion tracking and generative synthesis of anthropomorphic recovery behaviors. The system achieves a 90.6% completion rate across diverse motions and a 90.0% completion rate for fall-and-recovery sequences on a Unitree G1 humanoid in both simulation and real-world tests.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid control methods like mimic-based trackers achieve high-fidelity motion but are brittle and fail non-anthropomorphically under severe environmental disturbances.
- Purely generative or unsupervised reinforcement learning methods, while robust to perturbations, lack the spatial and temporal precision required for strict task execution.
- Hybrid control architectures often fail to seamlessly integrate generative recovery with precise tracking, typically relying on explicit and rigid mode-switching mechanisms.

## Method

- A hierarchical control architecture is introduced, comprising a state-conditioned generative middleware and a low-level, general-purpose physics tracking policy.
- The generative middleware uses continuous conditional flow matching over a geometrically constrained residual space to synthesize short-horizon, dynamically feasible keyframe trajectories.
- A deep reinforcement learning-trained physics tracker executes actions based on modulated reference commands, which are either original references or middleware-generated trajectories.

## Results

- Heracles demonstrated a **90.6%** completion rate across diverse motions, outperforming baseline methods like VQ-VAE (**86.0%**) and MLP (**84.8%**).
- The framework achieved a **90.0%** completion rate for challenging fall-and-recovery sequences, surpassing the next best method (VQ-VAE) by over 20 percentage points, while also showing the lowest joint position and orientation errors.
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid validated the framework's ability to generalize across a variety of behaviors and perform robust omnidirectional fall recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating trajectory generation as conditional flow matching in a geometrically constrained residual space allows seamless transition between identity mapping for tracking and generative synthesis for recovery.
> - Techniques like noisy-state augmentation, kinematics-aware loss weighting, and a directional warm start are critical for improving robustness, handling pose-dependent geometry, and guiding trajectory generation.
> - An improved Finite Scalar Quantization (iFSQ) tokenizer for the low-level tracker effectively compresses motion signals and distills a structured motion taxonomy, enhancing tracking precision and codebook utilization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is progressing towards deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments. This transition necessitates advanced control architectures that can execute precise, goal-directed movements while exhibiting anthropomorphic adaptability to unforeseen disturbances. Current research predominantly focuses on two paradigms for general humanoid control:

1. **Mimic-Based Motion Trackers:** This approach, often powered by deep reinforcement learning (DRL), formulates control as minimizing kinematic deviation from a reference trajectory. Controllers like DeepMimic, Generalized Motion Tracker (GMT), SONIC, and OmniXtreme have achieved high-fidelity replication of diverse motion capture (MoCap) data, enabling zero-shot execution of agile skills. However, these systems are described as brittle under severe environmental perturbations, often leading to non-anthropomorphic failure modes and unrecoverable falls when pushed far from their reference. They struggle to synthesize coordinated, multi-step recovery actions. Recent attempts to integrate guided diffusion into tracking formulations (e.g., BeyondMimic) and language models for skill execution also exist, but tracking-first controllers generally require expensive test-time guidance for generation and predominantly produce rigid responses to out-of-distribution disturbances.

2. **Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning (URL) and Skill Discovery (Behavioral Foundation Models - BFMs):** These methods aim to equip robots with a diverse repertoire of motor skills without explicit reference trajectories, leveraging large-scale pre-training to capture broad behavioral priors. Examples include adversarial motion priors for full-size humanoids and frameworks like BFM-Zero, which use unsupervised RL to create objective-centric latent spaces for zero-shot tasks. While these policies exhibit physical compliance and robustness to perturbations due to their unconstrained exploration of state-action spaces, they generally struggle with the spatial and temporal precision required for strict task execution compared to dedicated mimic controllers.

A third area of **Motion Generation** focuses on synthesizing naturalistic human movements in the kinematic domain, often using diffusion models or large language models (e.g., Human Motion Diffusion Model, MotionGPT, HY-Motion). These models achieve anthropomorphism and diversity but operate without physical constraints, making their raw outputs unstable for direct physical robot deployment.

Hybrid architectures attempt to combine generative synthesis with tracking objectives, either by constraining kinematic generation with tracking formulations (e.g., COMET) or by distilling multipurpose motion priors for physical characters (e.g., ASE, VMP). However, the paper argues that these hybrid approaches often couple high-level generative priors loosely with low-level execution policies, preventing real-time reshaping of generative targets based on high-frequency physical state deviations. This often reverts to explicit state-switching mechanisms rather than seamless transitions between tracking and recovery.

Heracles aims to address the limitations of these existing paradigms by introducing a novel state-conditioned diffusion middleware that bridges precise motion tracking and generative synthesis within a closed-loop control architecture. It seeks to provide the exactitude of trackers with the generative adaptability of robust human recovery, enabling anthropomorphic resilience without explicit mode-switching.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a general-purpose humanoid control architecture that can execute commanded motions precisely while adaptably recovering from unpredictable environmental perturbations in an anthropomorphic manner. The motivation stems from the perceived limitations of current approaches in achieving this dual capability:

1. **Limitations of Pure Reference-Tracking:** The dominant paradigm of formulating motion control as a rigid reference-tracking problem, while effective in nominal conditions, leads to brittle, non-anthropomorphic failure modes under severe disturbances. When a robot is pushed significantly off its reference trajectory, a pure tracker often generates rigid, physically infeasible joint torques in an attempt to minimize immediate state error, frequently resulting in unnatural and unrecoverable falls. This "myopic" corrective strategy lacks the multi-step reasoning required for dynamic balance recovery.

2. **Limitations of Pure Generative Models:** Conversely, purely generative models, such as end-to-end behavior cloning, can produce natural, human-like movements but fundamentally sacrifice the spatial and temporal precision required for strict task execution. They often operate in an open-loop kinematic domain, ignoring crucial embodied parameters and leading to instability if directly implemented on physical robots.

3. **Limitations of Existing Hybrid Approaches:** While hybrid architectures aim to combine tracking and generation, they are often described as retaining structural deficiencies, failing to implicitly transition from nominal tracking to generative recovery under extreme conditions and relying on explicit state-switching mechanisms.

To overcome these limitations, the Heracles project proposes a novel state-conditioned diffusion middleware. The core motivations and objectives are:

- **To bridge precise motion tracking and generative synthesis:** Heracles aims to function as an intermediary layer between high-level motion commands and low-level physics trackers. It is designed to implicitly adapt its behavior based on the robot's real-time state, approximating an identity map for precise tracking when states align with references, and seamlessly transitioning into a generative synthesizer for anthropomorphic recovery when significant deviations occur.
- **To develop a generative control middleware paradigm:** This involves decoupling high-level intent generation from high-frequency physical execution, allowing generative priors to be injected directly into the control loop without disrupting low-level physics.
- **To enhance the overall control architecture for general-purpose tasks:** This includes improving the underlying physics tracker to retain high-fidelity tracking characteristics while ensuring seamless integration with the generative middleware.
- **To achieve robust anthropomorphic recovery and motion generalization:** The framework aims to demonstrate emergent, human-like recovery behaviors and generative adaptability under severe, out-of-distribution physical disturbances, ultimately elevating humanoid control from a rigid tracking paradigm to an open-ended, generative architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Heracles framework employs a hierarchical control architecture designed to decouple high-level intent generation from high-frequency physical execution. It comprises two main components: a state-conditioned generative middleware and a low-level, general-purpose physics tracking policy.

#### 3.1. System Overview and Problem Formulation

The system addresses the dual objective of high-fidelity motion tracking and robust physical recovery. When the robot's state (p_t) is close to the reference motion command (m_t), the system aims for optimal zero-shot tracking. When perturbations push the state into out-of-distribution (OOD) regions, the system aims to synthesize recovery trajectories. The generative middleware, operating at a lower planning frequency, learns an intermediary mapping function, *f*<sub>*θ*</sub>, that takes the current proprioceptive state (p_t) and the original reference (m_t) to predict a short-horizon, dynamically feasible keyframe trajectory (𝜏_t). This mapping is designed to approximate an identity transformation during nominal tracking and synthesize new, physically feasible transition trajectories for recovery under disturbances. The synthesized keyframes are then densified and fed to the low-level physics tracker. The tracking policy *π* then executes actions *a*<sub>*t*</sub> to maximize a discounted return, considering modulated reference commands (m'<sub>t</sub>) and high-level motion embeddings.

#### 3.2. State-Conditioned Generative Middleware via Flow Matching

The trajectory generation process is formulated as a conditional flow matching problem over a geometrically constrained residual space.

- **Geometric Residual Parameterization:** Instead of predicting absolute state coordinates, the middleware predicts residual trajectories relative to the current state (p_t). A static baseline (𝛽<sub>t,k</sub> = p_t) is anchored at the current proprioceptive state, and the model predicts the residual deviation (r_t). The final trajectory 𝜏_t is recovered as 𝛽_t + r_t. This parameterization allows the middleware to act as an identity map during nominal tracking and leverage the conditioning gap for synthesizing recovery trajectories during deviations.
- **Continuous Conditional Flow Matching:** Continuous flow matching is used to synthesize complex, multimodal distributions of these recovery residuals. The model is trained to regress an underlying continuous vector field (v̂) by minimizing a velocity matching objective. The conditioning vector (c_t = [p_t, m_t]) provides strict state-conditioning. Inference involves integrating the learned velocity field from noise to data using Euler steps.
- **Architecture and Kinematic Continuity:** The velocity field is parameterized by an AdaLN-modulated Transformer. Kinematic continuity between the real-time physical state and the synthesized trajectory is maintained by an inpainting constraint that pins the first residual token during integration, ensuring the trajectory starts from the current state.
- **Receding-Horizon Planning with Directional Warm Start:** The middleware predicts a fixed temporal window of motion (Δt = 0.2s) with 𝐾=8 keyframes. Long-horizon recovery is achieved autoregressively through successive replanning cycles at 25 Hz. During inference, the ODE solver is initialized with a directional motion prior, which is a linear interpolation toward the target, partially noised. This warm start provides an immediate directional estimate, allowing the learned velocity field to refine it into a natural trajectory with fewer ODE steps. The generated keyframes are densified via cubic spline interpolation for joint positions and spherical linear interpolation for root orientations.

#### 3.3. General-Purpose Physics Tracker

The low-level physics tracker, also trained with deep reinforcement learning, consumes the modulated reference commands.

- **Motion Tracking Formulation:**
 - **Observation Space (o_t):** Comprises the robot's proprioceptive state (p_t, including gravity vector, root angular velocity, joint positions and velocities, previous action), the kinematic motion reference trajectory (m_t, including reference root velocities, 6D root orientation error, target joint positions), and a discrete high-level motion embedding (z_d). The reference m_t is replaced by the densified middleware output m'_t during deployment.
 - **Action Space:** The policy outputs target joint positions, which are executed by a low-level Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller at high frequency.
 - **Rewards and Domain Randomization:** The reward function combines positive tracking terms (root position/orientation/velocity, body-link orientations, joint position matching) with regularization penalties (action jerks, joint-limit violations, undesired contacts). Comprehensive domain randomization (perturbing physical properties and command-level targets) is applied during training to enhance sim-to-real transferability.

- **General Motion Tracking Policy:** This policy uses a shared motion-latent representation via an encoder–quantizer–decoder structure.
 - **Improved Discrete Quantization (iFSQ):** Continuous latent features from a motion encoder are discretized into compact semantic tokens using an improved Finite Scalar Quantization (iFSQ). A sigmoid-based mapping is employed for bounding, improving bin utilization compared to standard tanh.
 - **Encoder-Decoder Architecture:** A motion encoder processes a 10-frame future motion window to produce a continuous embedding. After iFSQ discretization into tokens (z_d), a reconstruction decoder maps z_d back to the motion sequence for representation learning, while an action decoder fuses z_d with a 10-step proprioceptive history to produce control actions.
 - **Adaptive Motion Sampling:** To address optimization imbalance across a heterogeneous motion corpus, a continuous temporal-bin variant with smoothed difficulty propagation is used. This mechanism partitions the corpus into bins, estimates difficulty based on tracking scores, smooths difficulty across bins, and then mixes this with a uniform baseline to steer training towards challenging temporal regions.

#### 3.4. Training Paradigm for Unified Tracking and Generation

- **Dataset Construction:** Training tuples for the generative middleware are created from a diverse motion corpus using a receding-horizon sampling strategy. Keyframes are extracted for a fixed planning horizon, and the conditioning vector pairs the start state with the reference command at the segment endpoint. The residual supervision is against a static baseline, encoding motion increments. No near-zero-length segments are included to prevent trivial zero-residual bias.
- **Noisy-State Augmentation:** To bridge the gap between noisy physical state estimation during deployment and clean training references, asymmetric start-state perturbations are applied. Only the initial proprioceptive state is corrupted with channel-wise Gaussian noise, while the target reference remains clean, improving robustness to real-world discrepancies.
- **Kinematics-Aware Loss Weighting:** Each state dimension in the loss function is weighted by a pose-dependent Jacobian magnitude, approximating the diagonal of J<sup>T</sup>J. This accounts for pose-dependent lever-arm geometry, allowing the model to prioritize geometrically sensitive configurations.
- **State Representation and Model Variants:** Two state parameterizations are evaluated for the trajectory generator: a 38D configuration (29 joint positions, 3D root position, 6D root orientation) and a 35D variant (omitting global root position). The 38D variant enables autonomous global positional correction, while the 35D variant removes dependence on external localization, relying on proprioception and IMU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated eight model configurations on a Unitree G1 humanoid platform in IsaacLab (training) and MuJoCo (evaluation), using a held-out set of 101 unseen motion sequences covering diverse behaviors. Real-world deployment was also performed.

#### 4.1. Comparisons

- **Baselines:** MLP, Transformer (TokenHSI), VQ-VAE, SONIC.
- **Heracles Variants:** iFSQ<sub>BM</sub> (iFSQ tokenizer, BeyondMimic observations), iFSQ<sub>+H</sub> (iFSQ, proposed observations + height), iFSQ (iFSQ, proposed observations w/o height), Heracles (iFSQ tracker + state-conditioned trajectory generator).
- **Metrics:** Completion Rate (CR, fraction of frames with root height error < 0.3m and root orientation error < 1.2 rad), Joint Position Error, Root Height Error, Root Orientation Error (excl. yaw), Root Linear Velocity Error.

**Principal Findings:**
- **Robustness:** Heracles achieved the highest completion rate (90.6%), surpassing the best external baseline VQ-VAE (86.0%) by 4.6 percentage points and MLP (84.8%) by 5.8 points. Variants with the proposed observation design and iFSQ tokenizer consistently outperformed baselines in CR. The transition from BM to the proposed observation design (iFSQ<sub>BM</sub> → iFSQ) improved CR from 85.1% to 87.2%.
- **Tokenizer Effectiveness:** Comparing VQ-VAE and iFSQ<sub>BM</sub> (same policy/observation, different quantizer) showed iFSQ reduced joint-position error significantly (2.3013 to 1.4760 rad, a 35.8% reduction) while maintaining comparable completion rates, indicating superior codebook utilization of iFSQ.
- **Observation Design and Height Features:** iFSQ<sub>+H</sub> achieved the lowest height error (0.0271m) and orientation error (0.1539 rad) across all methods. iFSQ achieved the best linear-velocity tracking (0.1561 m/s). Removing explicit height features (iFSQ<sub>+H</sub> → iFSQ) improved joint error but degraded height and orientation control.
- **Trajectory Generator:** Heracles achieved the highest completion rate (90.6%), a 3.9% improvement over the standalone iFSQ tracker. It reduced root orientation error by 24.5% and height error by 20.0% compared to iFSQ, with a modest increase in joint-position error. This suggests the generator refines vertical and heading control.

Qualitative sim-to-sim evaluations on an out-of-distribution martial arts sequence showed MLP, Transformer, and SONIC collapsing early, VQ-VAE barely tracking, while iFSQ<sub>BM</sub> and iFSQ survived. Heracles tracked the full sequence accurately, demonstrating robustness. Real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 confirmed generalization across diverse behaviors like walking, running, kicking, and human-object interaction.

#### 4.2. Fall-and-Recovery Evaluation

A dedicated evaluation on fall-and-recovery sequences (lie-to-stand, prone-to-stand, stand-to-lie) was performed.
- **Performance Divide:** Reactive tracking baselines (MLP, Transformer, SONIC) showed low completion rates (<45%), indicating a general inability to execute fall-recovery motions. VQ-VAE showed comparatively better resilience (CR = 69.8%). iFSQ tracker variants without the generative middleware showed mixed results, with iFSQ<sub>+H</sub> achieving the highest CR among standalone trackers (52.7%) and the lowest height error.
- **Heracles' Role:** Heracles achieved the highest completion rate (90.0%), exceeding the second-best method (VQ-VAE) by 20.2 percentage points and representing a 104.5% relative improvement over MLP. It also achieved the lowest joint-position error (1.4114 rad) and orientation error (0.2427 rad) among all methods. This indicates the state-conditioned generative middleware is critical for maintaining coherent tracking through extreme state transitions during fall recovery.

Qualitative sim-to-sim comparisons on a lie-to-stand sequence showed Heracles successfully completing the full transition and accurately tracking root position, while baselines failed or only partially succeeded. Real-world experiments demonstrated omnidirectional fall recovery across varied initial fallen configurations (supine, lateral, prone), with the robot successfully rising towards different orientations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Architectural Analysis

Ablations on the trajectory generator's components, keeping the iFSQ tracker fixed, were conducted.
- **Directional Warm Start:** Removing the directional motion prior reduced completion rate by 3.8% and increased joint error by 22.3%, suggesting its importance for guiding the ODE solver towards the correct recovery heading.
- **Noisy-State Augmentation:** Removing asymmetric noise injection resulted in the most significant degradation, with CR falling to 78.6% (a 13.2% reduction), joint error increasing by 42.4%, height error almost doubling, and orientation error increasing by 58.3%. This highlights the importance of bridging the train-deploy distribution gap for robustness to sensor noise and tracking drift.
- **Kinematics-Aware Loss Weighting:** Removing Jacobian-based weighting reduced CR to 82.1% (a 9.4% reduction), and increased height and orientation errors by 57.1% and 48.6% respectively. This indicates the criticality of modeling pose-dependent lever-arm geometry for robust tracking and navigating complex transitions.
- **Learned Discrete Representation:** Visualization of the iFSQ codebook revealed emergent semantic clustering of motion tokens (e.g., walking, running, jumping, martial arts, dance, fall recovery), even without explicit category labels. This suggests iFSQ not only compresses motion signals but also distills a structured motion taxonomy.

These ablations collectively support the hierarchical architecture, indicating that the individual design choices contribute to robust performance, especially in bridging the train-deploy distribution gap and handling geometrically sensitive configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Heracles framework introduces a state-conditioned generative middleware that addresses a fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics: the dichotomy between precise kinematic tracking and robust physical recovery. This work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Unified Control Paradigm:** By integrating a continuous flow matching process within a closed-loop control architecture, Heracles provides a unified framework that can seamlessly transition between high-fidelity reference tracking under nominal conditions and generative synthesis of anthropomorphic recovery maneuvers during severe perturbations. This eliminates the need for explicit mode-switching, which is often brittle and limits real-time adaptability in previous hybrid approaches.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The system demonstrates enhanced robustness to out-of-distribution physical disturbances and challenging recovery scenarios (e.g., lie-to-stand transitions). The emergent human-like recovery behaviors, such as compensatory stepping and coordinated arm motions, are not hand-designed but arise from the learned distribution over physically plausible motion transitions, indicating a more general and flexible response mechanism.
3. **Improved Generalization:** The ability of the generative middleware to dynamically modulate reference signals based on real-time physical feasibility implies a shift from a reactive trajectory follower to an active trajectory synthesizer. This enables compositional generalization, where the model can recombine learned motion primitives into novel recovery strategies for previously unseen perturbations, which is critical for deployment in unstructured real-world environments.
4. **Architectural Insights:** The ablation studies highlight the importance of specific design choices within the generative middleware, such as directional warm start, noisy-state augmentation, and kinematics-aware loss weighting, for achieving robust performance. The findings support a frequency-separated hierarchical architecture, mirroring biological motor systems, where a lower-frequency generative planner guides a higher-frequency physics tracker.
5. **Foundation for General-Purpose Humanoids:** By addressing the limitations of both pure tracking and pure generative approaches, Heracles establishes a scalable foundation for deploying agile and resilient general-purpose humanoids. This could accelerate the transition of humanoid robots from structured laboratory settings to dynamic and unpredictable real-world applications where both precision and adaptable recovery are essential.

The work's demonstrated success in both simulation and real-world deployment on the Unitree G1 humanoid, particularly in omnidirectional fall recovery, suggests a step towards more capable and autonomous humanoid robots that can operate reliably in complex human environments.
