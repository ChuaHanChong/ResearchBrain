# SCDP: Learning Humanoid Locomotion from Partial Observations via Mixed-Observation Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Sensor-Conditioned Diffusion Policies (SCDP) enables humanoid locomotion control by distilling a generative trajectory planner from an expert policy, using only onboard proprioceptive sensors. This framework achieves robust velocity control and motion tracking in simulation and on a physical Unitree G1 robot without requiring privileged full-body state information.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid diffusion controllers rely on privileged full-body state information (e.g., global positions, velocities) during deployment.
- Obtaining privileged state information on real robots requires complex, often unreliable, and impractical state estimation systems.
- Performance of current controllers significantly degrades or fails when privileged inputs are unavailable, highlighting a gap in practical deployment under partial observability.

## Method

- **SCDP** employs a mixed-observation distillation strategy, training a diffusion model to generate future privileged state-action trajectories conditioned solely on historical onboard sensor data.
- Restricted denoising is introduced to infer pelvis linear velocity from historical context, avoiding direct velocity feedback, and context distribution alignment mitigates train-deploy observation mismatch.
- The framework utilizes full prefix attention within the context window to enhance the aggregation of historical information and facilitate inference of latent dynamics from partial observations.

## Results

- SCDP achieved high success rates (**99.8**-**100%**) in velocity-commanded locomotion tasks (e.g., perturbation recovery, waypoint navigation) using only onboard sensors, matching or exceeding privileged baselines.
- The framework demonstrated a **93%** success rate in motion reference tracking on the AMASS-walk test set, substantially outperforming behavior cloning (**31%**) and a mixed-observation baseline (**79%**).
- Successful deployment on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot showcased robust sim-to-real transfer and real-time locomotion capabilities at 50 Hz without external sensing or state estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mixed-observation training is fundamental for diffusion policies to infer global dynamics from partial observations, significantly improving performance over contextual-only baselines.
> - Representation alignment between sensor encoder and privileged state encoder proved detrimental to performance, suggesting that implicit learning of global state inference is more effective.
> - Context distribution alignment is critical for robust sensor-based control, ensuring consistency between training and deployment observation conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robot control has progressed from task-specific reinforcement learning (RL) controllers to more general motion reference tracking methods, enabling diverse whole-body behaviors for applications such as teleoperation and multi-skill locomotion. Recently, diffusion models have emerged as a promising approach for learning expressive control policies. These models can learn full distributions of expert behavior rather than deterministic action mappings, offering flexibility for task adaptation and demonstrating strong performance in visuomotor manipulation, bipedal locomotion, and physics-based humanoid control. Guided state-action diffusion approaches have further shown capabilities in look-ahead planning and multitask control within a unified framework.

However, a significant limitation of existing humanoid diffusion controllers is their reliance on "privileged full-body state information" during deployment. This includes data such as global position, orientation, base velocity, and rigid-body positions. Obtaining this information typically requires complex and often unreliable state estimation pipelines on real robots, particularly outside controlled laboratory environments. The absence of these privileged inputs has been observed to degrade performance substantially, leading to failures in even basic tasks. This dependence poses a practical challenge for deployment scenarios where only direct onboard proprioceptive sensing is reliably available. Learning under these conditions falls into the category of a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the policy must infer hidden global states from incomplete observations. Current diffusion-based controllers are not intrinsically designed to operate under such partial observability.

This work addresses this specific limitation by developing a diffusion-based distillation framework tailored for humanoid control that operates solely from onboard proprioceptive measurements. Unlike prior teacher-student transfer methods, which often distill reactive observation-to-action mappings for MLP-based policies, this research focuses on distilling a *generative trajectory planner* from offline datasets under partial observability. The proposed framework aims to enable such distillation without requiring privileged state information for the student policy, thereby distinguishing it from existing diffusion methods that assume full state access at deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust humanoid locomotion using only onboard sensors, thereby eliminating the necessity for explicit, and often unreliable, state estimation pipelines during deployment. This is achieved by distilling control policies from offline datasets into deployable diffusion policies.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges in current humanoid robotics:
- **Reliance on Privileged Information:** Contemporary humanoid control methods, especially those leveraging advanced techniques like diffusion models, fundamentally rely on privileged full-body state information (e.g., global positions, orientations, and velocities of all rigid bodies) for effective operation.
- **State Estimation Complexity and Unreliability:** On real robots, acquiring this privileged information necessitates sophisticated state estimation systems (e.g., motion capture systems, advanced IMU-GPS fusion), which are complex to set up, prone to errors, and generally impractical for real-world deployment outside controlled laboratory settings.
- **Performance Degradation under Partial Observability:** When these privileged inputs are unavailable, the performance of existing controllers degrades significantly, often leading to catastrophic failures, even for relatively simple locomotion tasks. This highlights a critical gap in deploying these advanced policies in practical, sensor-constrained environments.
- **Need for Implicit State Inference:** Operating with only onboard proprioceptive sensors (e.g., joint positions/velocities, base IMU data) introduces a partially observable environment. In such scenarios, the control policy must implicitly infer the necessary global state information to generate coherent and stable locomotion behaviors, a capability not inherent in current diffusion models.

To address these motivations, the research outlines several specific objectives:
- **Decouple Sensing from Supervision:** Develop a training methodology where the diffusion model conditions only on historical onboard sensor data while being supervised by privileged future state trajectories. This aims to enforce the model to learn an internal representation capable of inferring global body dynamics from partial observations.
- **Achieve Velocity Control without Direct Velocity Feedback:** Design mechanisms that enable accurate velocity control without relying on explicit base velocity measurements, which are often noisy or unavailable on hardware.
- **Prevent Train-Deploy Mismatch:** Implement techniques to mitigate distribution shifts between the training environment (which might use noisy or privileged data for context generation) and the deployment environment (where observations are clean and partially observable).
- **Enhance Latent Dynamics Inference:** Improve the model's ability to aggregate historical information effectively from partial observations to infer underlying motion dynamics.
- **Validate Performance:** Empirically validate the proposed framework on standard humanoid locomotion tasks, including velocity-commanded locomotion and motion reference tracking, in simulation and on a physical robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Sensor-Conditioned Diffusion Policies (SCDP) framework is designed to distill a diffusion-based controller from an expert RL policy to achieve whole-body humanoid control using only onboard sensing. The approach integrates four main components: a Multi-Motion Tracking Policy (MMP) as the expert demonstrator, the diffusion distillation framework itself, a specific data collection methodology, and a real-time deployment configuration.

**A. Multi-Motion Tracking Policy (MMP)**
The foundation of the distillation process is an RL-trained expert, the Multi-Motion Policy (MMP). This policy is trained for multi-motion tracking, utilizing a reward function and PD controller structured similarly to prior work. The MMP operates with a full state observation, `o_robot`, comprising anchor-centric, heading-aligned local positions, orientations, velocities of all rigid bodies, and previous actions. Additionally, a `o_task` observation provides future absolute and relative states for reference bodies over a look-ahead horizon. This expert policy, with its access to privileged information, generates the trajectories used for distilling the SCDP model.

**B. Mixed-Observation Distillation: Learning Global Dynamics from Partial Sensing**
The core innovation of SCDP is its mixed-observation distillation strategy.
1. **Problem Formulation:** The full state `s_t` includes proprioceptive measurements `o_t` and geometric information (local positions and velocities of rigid bodies) `{~p_b, ~v_b}`. During inference, the robot only has access to `o_t`, which comprises pelvis linear/angular velocities, joint positions/velocities, and gravity vectors. Crucially, `o_t` lacks geometric body information. The diffusion policy `π(τ_t | O_t, c_t)` generates future state-action trajectories `τ_t = [a_t, s_{t+1}..., s_{t+H}]` conditioned on a history of onboard observations `O_t = [o_{t-N}..., o_t]` and task commands `c_t`. The critical asymmetry is that while `O_t` consists of sensor observations during inference, `τ_t` includes privileged states `s_{t+1:t+H}` as supervision targets during training.
2. **Diffusion Model Formulation:** A Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) is employed to iteratively refine trajectory samples. The model predicts a clean trajectory `x_{0,θ}` from a noised trajectory `τ^k_t`, minimizing the `L_DDPM` loss. Independent noise schedules are used for state and action components.
3. **Restricted Denoising:** To address the unreliability of pelvis linear velocity (`v_pelvis`) feedback on hardware, restricted denoising is introduced. `v_pelvis` is excluded from the denoising inputs (`τ^k_t_res`) but remains in the supervision target (`τ_t`). This forces the model to infer `v_pelvis` from the historical context, promoting robustness without direct velocity feedback. The loss becomes `L_DDPM_res`.
4. **Context Distribution Alignment:** Prior diffusion methods often use `(s_noised, a)` pairs for context during training, which creates a distribution mismatch when the model encounters clean `(s, a)` context during inference. SCDP addresses this by providing `(s_noised, a_noised)` pairs in the training context, ensuring alignment between training and deployment conditions and maintaining causal relationships.
5. **Context-Aware Attention Masking:** Instead of applying strictly causal attention masks (preventing states from attending to actions but allowing future states) as in some prior works, SCDP enables bidirectional attention within the context window. Causal constraints are only maintained in the prediction horizon. This allows the model to aggregate historical information more comprehensively, which is designed to facilitate the inference of latent dynamics from partial observations.
6. **Representation Alignment (Exploration):** An experimental cosine-similarity alignment loss was tested to align sensor encoder output with privileged state encoder output. This component was ultimately excluded from the final SCDP model due to observed detrimental effects on performance.
7. **Conditioning:**
 - **Velocity Conditioning:** Desired pelvis velocities (`v_x_cmd, v_y_cmd, ψ_dot_cmd`) are incorporated as commands `c_t` via an MLP encoding and combined with timestep embeddings. A `L_cmd` loss minimizes the difference between `c_t` and the mean predicted velocity over the horizon.
 - **Motion Reference Conditioning:** For motion tracking, commands `c_t` include reference joint positions (`q_ref`), velocities (`q_dot_ref`), and relative orientation (`R_rel`).
8. **Complete Training Objective:** The overall objective combines the restricted denoising loss and the command tracking loss: `L = L_DDPM-res + λ_cmd L_cmd`.

**C. Datasets**
The MMP expert is trained on PHC-filtered AMASS walking motions (AMASS-walk), which are retargeted using Mink. For SCDP distillation, 65 diverse AMASS-walk clips are selected, mirrored to create 130 references. The expert then collects 40 rollouts per reference, incorporating stochastic actions, domain randomization, and push forces to generate a diverse dataset of approximately 5200 trajectories (around 750,000 timesteps at 50 Hz). For motion tracking evaluations, the full AMASS-walk training set is used, with a single rollout per trajectory.

**D. Training and Deployment Configuration**
The MMP is trained using PPO for 100,000 iterations in IsaacLab with 4096 parallel environments on an NVIDIA RTX 5090.
The SCDP diffusion model employs a six-layer Transformer encoder with four attention heads. Separate multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) encode actions, onboard observations, privileged states, and commands. The model predicts 16-step trajectories, but action losses are weighted to zero beyond 8 steps to prioritize near-term accuracy; only the first action is executed during inference before replanning. Training involves 1 million gradient updates, a batch size of 128, AdamW optimizer, and a cosine learning rate schedule.
For deployment, SCDP runs on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot at 50 Hz. The model is executed on a remote workstation (NVIDIA RTX 5090), achieving 105 Hz throughput via ONNX Runtime, enabling real-time control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SCDP's performance in simulation across velocity-commanded locomotion tasks (Perturbation Recovery, Joystick Control, Waypoint Navigation) and motion reference tracking, followed by real-robot deployment.

**A. Comparison with Baseline Methods**
The study compares SCDP against a classifier-guided diffusion baseline and behavior cloning (BC), with variations in observation modes (privileged `priv`, contextual-only `co`, mixed-observation `mo`) and `v_pelvis` feedback.
- **Performance under Partial Observability:** Contextual-only policies (`co`) demonstrated significant performance degradation, confirming the need for geometric information in standard diffusion distillation. In contrast, mixed-observation training (`mo`) largely resolved this dependency, achieving performance comparable to privileged baselines.
- **Velocity Control without Feedback:** The full SCDP model, which does not rely on `v_pelvis` feedback, achieved competitive success rates across all tasks (99.8% for Perturbation Recovery, 99.8% for Waypoint Navigation, 100% for Joystick Control). These scores matched or exceeded privileged methods, indicating that SCDP enables robust velocity control without explicit state estimation.

**B. Data Collection Strategy Ablation**
An ablation study on data collection revealed that:
- **Action Noise:** Moderate action noise (e.g., 0.12–0.18 standard deviation) is important for robustness. Policies without noise performed poorly (<33% success), while minimal noise improved success to >70%.
- **Push Perturbations:** Applying push perturbations alone significantly improved performance (from <33% to >90% success) without additional action noise.
- **Combination:** Combining moderate action noise with push perturbations yielded optimal results, exposing the policy to diverse recovery behaviors. Excessive action noise (0.24 standard deviation) was found to degrade stationary performance.

**C. Context Length Ablation**
The impact of context length on policy robustness without `v_pelvis` feedback was investigated.
- **SCDP Performance:** A four-step context was found to be sufficient for SCDP, while longer histories (16 steps) led to degraded performance, potentially due to compounding errors or distribution mismatch.
- **Ours_mo Performance:** The baseline `Ours_mo` showed inconsistent performance across context lengths, lacking a monotonic relationship between context length and success rate.

**D. Feature Design Ablation**
A systematic ablation of SCDP's key design components revealed their individual contributions:
- **Mixed-Observation Training:** This component was identified as fundamental; its removal resulted in complete failure (1.4% navigation success).
- **Representation Alignment:** Surprisingly, removing representation alignment led to the best performance (97.8%), suggesting that it interfered with the optimization process.
- **Context Distribution Alignment:** This feature was found to be critical for achieving sensor-based control without velocity feedback, with its removal dropping performance to 9.7%.
- **Full Prefix Attention and Restricted Denoising:** These components contributed to the overall reliability and robustness of the full method.
- **Combined Effect:** Mixed-observation training alone achieved 74.2% navigation success, while the collective integration of context distribution alignment, full prefix attention, and restricted denoising enabled the full SCDP method to match privileged-observation performance.

**E. Velocity Tracking Performance**
SCDP's velocity tracking fidelity and responsiveness were compared to the BeyondMimic baseline.
- **Linear Velocity:** SCDP demonstrated tighter linear tracking with reduced oscillations, although with a slight increase in lag (250–500 ms) during direction reversals.
- **Angular Velocity:** Angular velocity tracking performance was comparable between SCDP and BeyondMimic.

**F. Motion Reference Tracking Performance**
SCDP's ability to reproduce reference motions from the AMASS dataset was evaluated.
- **Success Rate:** SCDP achieved a 93% success rate on the AMASS-walk test set, significantly outperforming BC (31%) and the `Ours_mo` baseline (79%).
- **Position Error:** It reduced the global position error (MPJPE-G) from 473 (for `Ours_mo`) to 288. Local pose accuracy (MPJPE-L) remained comparable to the expert. The remaining global position error for SCDP is attributed to the expert's ability to correct global drift, which is not possible under partial observability for SCDP, which relies on kinematic reference alone.

**G. Real Robot Deployment**
The trained SCDP policy was successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, operating in a closed-loop at 50 Hz. This deployment demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer and reliable locomotion without external sensing or state estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a significant challenge in humanoid robot control: enabling advanced locomotion policies to operate effectively with only onboard sensors, thus removing the dependency on complex and often unreliable external state estimation systems.

The key significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Enhanced Practical Deployability:** By eliminating the need for privileged full-body state information, SCDP moves humanoid control closer to practical, real-world deployment scenarios where external motion capture or sophisticated state estimation pipelines are infeasible or undesirable. This could broaden the applicability of humanoids in various unstructured environments.
- **Robustness under Partial Observability:** The development of mixed-observation training, restricted denoising, and context alignment techniques demonstrates that diffusion models can effectively infer hidden global dynamics from incomplete, proprioceptive observations. This capability is crucial for autonomous operation in environments with sensor limitations.
- **Advancements in Diffusion-based Control:** This work contributes to the field of diffusion models in robotics by extending their utility to partially observable control problems, specifically for generative trajectory planning rather than just reactive policies. The proposed techniques offer generalizable solutions for adapting diffusion policies to real-world sensor constraints.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of SCDP opens several avenues for future work, including the exploration of sim-to-real fine-tuning methods that do not require privileged states, the development of drift-correction mechanisms for long-horizon motion tracking under partial observability, and the extension of these principles to more complex tasks like contact-rich manipulation and locomotion on challenging terrain.
- **Validation on Physical Hardware:** The successful deployment and robust performance on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot at 50 Hz validate the practical utility and sim-to-real transfer capabilities of the proposed framework, demonstrating its potential for real-world applications.
