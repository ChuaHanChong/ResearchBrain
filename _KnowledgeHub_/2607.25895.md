# HiFi-UMI: Learning Deployable Manipulation Policies from High-Fidelity UMI Data Alone

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed HiFi-UMI, a system for collecting high-fidelity, robot-free human manipulation demonstrations. Policies trained exclusively on data from this system achieve comparable real-robot deployment success rates to those trained with real-robot teleoperation, and large-scale pre-training on this data further improves performance and data efficiency.

## Problem

- Real-robot teleoperation for data collection is expensive, resource-intensive, and difficult to scale, hindering the creation of large datasets for robot learning.
- Existing robot-free data collection methods (e.g., UMI) suffer from fidelity issues like pose inaccuracies, asynchronous sensors, and limited camera fields of view, making them insufficient for direct policy deployment.
- A prevailing assumption exists that real-robot teleoperation data is indispensable for post-training to 'anchor' policies for reliable real-world deployment.

## Method

- Developed **HiFi-UMI**, a portable data-production system with co-designed hardware and software to capture robot-free manipulation data with high accuracy, microsecond-level synchronization, and ultra-wide camera fields of view.
- Implemented a ==six-stage data processing pipeline==, including ==offline stereo-inertial SLAM== for accurate trajectory reconstruction, ==simulation retargeting== for kinematic feasibility, and AI-assisted human verification for annotations.
- Evaluated three diverse policy backbones (==VLA== and ==WAM== families) by training them solely on HiFi-UMI data to test the feasibility of direct real-robot deployment without any real-robot teleoperation data.

## Results

- Policies post-trained exclusively on HiFi-UMI data achieved comparable or superior real-robot success rates to policies post-trained on in-domain teleoperation data, with aggregate differences within sampling noise across three backbones and four tasks.
- Pre-training StarVLA-QwenPI on **4,000 hours** of multi-task UMI data reduced held-out action error by **61%** and improved Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) action error on unseen tasks by **41%**, demonstrating continuous scaling benefits.
- UMI pre-training significantly boosted downstream performance, raising aggregate StarVLA-QwenPI success by **18.1 percentage points** and enabling comparable performance with **4x less** task-specific data for post-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sufficiently high-fidelity robot-free UMI data can eliminate the need for real-robot teleoperation data in post-training for deployable manipulation policies, challenging the 'real-robot anchor' assumption.
> - Large-scale pre-training on high-fidelity robot-free data leads to a more effective base model, exhibiting power-law scaling in action error reduction and improved transferability to unseen tasks.
> - The fidelity of robot-free data—specifically concerning trajectory accuracy, inter-gripper pose, synchronization, and comprehensive sensing—is crucial for bridging the human-to-robot transfer gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of deployable manipulation policies for robots is constrained by the availability of high-fidelity and scalable data. Historically, real-robot teleoperation has been the primary method for acquiring accurate, embodied trajectories. However, this approach is resource-intensive, requiring specialized robots, teleoperation rigs, and skilled operators, which limits its scalability. Large-scale initiatives like AgiBot World and RoboMIND highlight the significant effort and infrastructure needed for such data collection.

In response to these limitations, robot-free data collection methods, such as the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI), have emerged. UMI systems use handheld, instrumented grippers, allowing humans to collect manipulation demonstrations without a physical robot, offering a more cost-effective and scalable alternative. While UMI and its derivatives have facilitated the creation of large datasets, their fidelity has typically been insufficient for direct policy deployment. Common deficiencies include pose inaccuracies due to visual SLAM drift, reconstructed rather than native inter-gripper relative pose, asynchronous sensor streams, and limited camera fields of view.

These fidelity issues have generally relegated robot-free data to a pre-training role, with real-robot teleoperation data still deemed necessary for post-training to ground policies for real-world deployment. Previous attempts to minimize teleoperation, such as ActiveUMI and XRZero-G0, reduced the real-robot fraction but did not eliminate it. RDT2 achieved zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer but still incorporated real-robot data when deployment-grade performance on a specific arm was required. The prevailing view has been that real-robot data forms a necessary "anchor" for deployable policies.

This work addresses the question of whether enhancing the fidelity of robot-free UMI data can remove the necessity for this real-robot anchor in post-training. It positions itself in the "middle tier" of the data pyramid, aiming to elevate UMI data quality to serve not just as scalable pre-training material but also as sufficient supervision for direct policy deployment, thereby challenging the established data-pyramid hierarchy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether sufficiently high-fidelity robot-free Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) data can enable the learning of manipulation policies that are directly deployable on real robots, without requiring any real-robot teleoperation data for post-training. This is termed the "zero-robot post-training" hypothesis.

The motivation for this objective stems from the bottlenecks in robot learning data acquisition:
- **Cost and Scalability of Teleoperation:** Real-robot teleoperation, while yielding embodied and directly trainable trajectories, is expensive and difficult to scale. It demands dedicated hardware, robots, and skilled human operators for every hour of data collected, making large-scale data generation impractical for many research and development contexts.
- **Fidelity Limitations of Existing Robot-Free Data:** While robot-free UMI-style data offers scalability advantages, current practices result in data with fidelity deficiencies. These include:
 - **Pose Accuracy:** Reliance on online visual-inertial SLAM for pose recovery often leads to drift over long horizons and failures in challenging visual conditions.
 - **Inter-gripper Relative Pose:** Relative poses between grippers are typically reconstructed from co-visibility rather than natively measured, introducing errors crucial for coordinated bimanual tasks.
 - **Synchronization:** Sensor streams often rely on software or wireless alignment, leading to temporal misalignments.
 - **Field of View (FoV):** Limited camera views (e.g., single wrist-mounted fisheye cameras) result in blind spots and weak depth cues.
- **The "Real-Robot Anchor" Assumption:** Due to these fidelity limitations, robot-free data has primarily been relegated to pre-training, with the assumption that a small amount of real-robot teleoperation data is indispensable for post-training to ground the policy for deployment. This work explicitly challenges this assumption.

To address these issues and test its central hypothesis, the research articulates several primary contributions:
- **Development of HiFi-UMI:** To overcome the fidelity deficiencies, the authors developed HiFi-UMI, a portable data-production system with co-designed hardware and software. This system aims to achieve high accuracy in trajectory, inter-gripper pose, synchronization, and field of view.
- **Demonstrate Zero-Robot Post-Training:** To provide empirical evidence that HiFi-UMI data alone suffices for post-training, yielding policies deployable on real robots that match in-domain teleoperation performance.
- **Evaluate Benefits of Large-Scale Pre-training:** To assess whether pre-training on the same high-fidelity robot-free corpus can further improve data efficiency and the performance ceiling of downstream post-training.
- **Release of HiFi-UMI-2K Dataset:** To contribute a large-scale, high-fidelity, open-source dataset to the robot-learning community to facilitate further research in deployment-grade robot-free policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development of the HiFi-UMI system and its application to data collection, processing, and policy training and evaluation.

#### 3.1. HiFi-UMI Capture Device Design

The HiFi-UMI capture device is designed around four requirements to ensure deployment-grade robot-free data:
- **Pose Acquisition and Accuracy:** Instead of wrist-mounted VIO, a head-mounted stereo camera rig with an integrated IMU estimates the global camera trajectory using offline stereo-inertial SLAM. Each hand is localized relative to the head via rigidly attached fiducial marker cubes observed by the same head cameras. This approach provides globally consistent and accurate trajectories for both hands (3 mm end-effector accuracy) and measures inter-gripper relative pose natively. Offline SLAM allows for future observations in optimization, while a local-consistency constraint bounds global drift.
- **Gripper Morphology:** An asymmetric two-finger, full-palm glove-like gripper is used. This design, inspired by the human hand, allows for a natural force distribution and contact geometry, suitable for both precise small-object manipulation and firmer support for heavier objects, broadening task coverage.
- **Cameras and Sensors:** Each hand features two non-parallel wide-angle fisheye cameras (top and bottom) providing approximately 200° horizontal and vertical coverage. This ultra-wide view reduces occlusion. The device integrates six cameras (stereo head views + four hand views) along with IMUs on the head and hands, and high-precision encoders on the grippers. Crucially, a single shared GPIO hardware trigger provides microsecond-level temporal synchronization across all sensors, addressing a key source of action-label noise in prior systems.
- **Online Interaction and Quality Control:** The device incorporates real-time quality monitoring during recording. It detects issues like underexposure, motion blur, excessive motion, or hands leaving the FoV, providing immediate voice feedback to the operator for in-situ correction. Online temporal slicing allows operators to mark task and subtask boundaries during collection.

#### 3.2. Data-Quality Criteria

Data quality is ensured through hard constraints on sensors, trajectories, and annotations. This includes proper camera exposure, synchronized timestamps, accurate sensor readings, consistently defined 6-DoF trajectory coordinate frames, accurate reconstruction, robot executability of trajectories, and precise gripper width measurements. Annotation consistency and temporal accuracy are also checked. This design yields a 96% cumulative validity rate for raw captures, combining a 98% trajectory reconstruction success rate and a 98% simulation replay validity rate.

#### 3.3. HiFi-UMI Data Processing Pipeline

A six-stage pipeline converts raw captures into training-ready episodes:
1. **Data Collection and Upload:** Hardware synchronization, online quality warnings, human-in-the-loop subtask marking, and real-time cloud streaming.
2. **Trajectory Reconstruction and Automatic Cleaning:** Offline stereo-inertial SLAM with fiducial marker localization for head and hand trajectories. A local-consistency constraint is used instead of global loop closure to handle dynamic manipulation scenes. Abnormal trajectories are recomputed, and residual issues are automatically flagged.
3. **Simulation Retargeting:** Each reconstructed trajectory is validated by replaying it on the target robot embodiment in simulation to ensure kinematic and dynamic feasibility, discarding infeasible ones.
4. **AI-Assisted Annotation:** A multi-view annotation model leverages head and hand camera views to infer task progress, object interactions, and action boundaries, generating draft labels and metadata with confidence scores.
5. **Human Verification:** Human annotators review flagged samples and low-confidence AI annotations, correcting or refining labels and quality-control metadata.
6. **Data Analysis and Export:** Curated data is statistically analyzed for distribution over tasks, scenes, objects, and manipulation attributes. Training sets are assembled by balancing these attributes, including rare behaviors. Export converts data into formats with synchronized multi-view video, calibrated trajectories, gripper states, language annotations, and quality metadata.

#### 3.4. Dataset and Release

The system has collected over 20,000 hours of bimanual manipulation data. A representative 2,000-hour subset, HiFi-UMI-2K, is publicly released. This dataset includes 482,100+ episodes across 110+ scenes, each with synchronized six-view video, calibrated bimanual trajectories, gripper states, language annotations, and subtask boundaries. Faces in recordings are masked.

#### 3.5. Baselines and Training Setup

The study uses three policy backbones spanning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World-Action-Model (WAM) families:
- **StarVLA-QwenPI (VLA):** Based on Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct with a π-style conditional flow-matching DiT action head. Action chunks (H=20) are integrated via 8 Euler steps, executing H_exec=10 in a receding-horizon manner.
- **OpenPI-π_0.5 (VLA):** JAX implementation combining PaliGemma visual-language stream with Gemma continuous action expert. Uses continuous flow-matching.
- **LingBot-VA (WAM):** Causal WAM that predicts future visual states before recovering actions. Factorizes joint prediction into future video latents and an inverse-dynamics model decoding actions from predicted visual transitions. Uses continuous flow matching for joint training.

All backbones use a common physical action semantics: relative pose increments (∆T) expressed in the current end-effector frame for translation, Rotation6D for orientation, and absolute gripper opening. This results in 10 active physical channels per arm (3+6+1), 20 for bimanual tasks. Each backbone retains its native observation, temporal sampling, action tensorization, normalization, and deployment interface.

#### 3.6. Training Variants

- **Real-robot teleoperation post-training:** Policy post-trained on task-specific teleoperated data (300 trajectories, 3-7 hours) collected on the target robot in the evaluation scene. This serves as the conventional baseline.
- **UMI post-training:** Policy post-trained only on HiFi-UMI demonstrations (3,200 trajectories, 10-20 hours per task), collected by multiple operators across diverse environments, without any real-robot teleoperation data.
- **UMI pre-training + UMI post-training:** For StarVLA-QwenPI, the policy is first pre-trained on 4,000 hours of multi-task UMI data, then post-trained on task-specific UMI data.

#### 3.7. Deployment Protocol

Experiments use a stationary bimanual platform with two seven-joint Tianji Robotics Marvin M6 arms. The platform carries the same gripper and four wrist cameras as the HiFi-UMI device for a matched observation interface. Policies emit end-effector pose targets at 125 Hz. A standardized benchmark system is used for real-robot evaluations, with two evaluators (policy operator, scene operator) to reduce bias. Tasks include Stain Wiping, Shirt Folding, Remote Insertion, and Produce Sorting. 40 rollouts are conducted per task-policy pair, with binary task success as the primary metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from experiments designed to test the efficacy of HiFi-UMI data for direct policy deployment and pre-training benefits.

#### 4.1. UMI-Only Post-Training vs. Teleoperation-Based Post-Training

Across the evaluated settings, HiFi-UMI-only post-training achieved performance comparable to in-domain teleoperation post-training.

- **VLA Backbones (StarVLA-QwenPI and OpenPI-π_0.5):**
 - **Aggregate Comparison:** For StarVLA-QwenPI, UMI-post-trained policy achieved 51.3% success (82/160), while teleoperation-trained achieved 53.8% (86/160), a difference of -2.5 percentage points. For OpenPI-π_0.5, UMI post-training achieved 77.5% success (124/160), exceeding teleoperation post-training at 74.4% (119/160) by +3.1 percentage points. Pooled across both VLAs, UMI achieved 64.4% success (206/320) vs. 64.1% (205/320) for teleoperation. The differences were within sampling noise, showing no consistent advantage for teleoperation.
 - **Task-Level Comparison:** Performance differences varied by task. For instance, OpenPI-π_0.5 with UMI data achieved 85.0% success on Remote Insertion versus 77.5% with teleoperation, and 82.5% on Produce Sorting versus 75.0%. Conversely, teleoperation provided a modest advantage on Shirt Folding for both backbones.
 - **Generalization under Scene Shift:** UMI demonstrations were collected across diverse environments, while teleoperation data was from the evaluation scene. Despite this scene-level distribution shift, UMI-post-trained policies maintained comparable performance.

- **WAM Backbone (LingBot-VA):**
 - **Aggregate Comparison:** UMI-only post-training achieved an aggregate success rate of 56.9% (91/160), closely matching 57.5% (92/160) obtained with real-robot teleoperation post-training. The difference was -0.6 percentage points.
 - **Task-Level Comparison:** Variations were observed across tasks. UMI post-training showed slightly better performance on Stain Wiping (62.5% vs. 60.0%) and Produce Sorting (57.5% vs. 55.0%). Teleoperation post-training performed better on Remote Insertion (50.0% vs. 42.5%).
 - **Execution Tempo:** Qualitative observations suggested UMI-post-trained policies exhibited larger-amplitude, more continuous, and natural-looking motions, while teleoperation-trained policies often made incremental corrections.
 - **Ground-Truth-Video Analysis:** When action decoding was evaluated with ground-truth future video (bypassing video generation errors), the UMI-post-trained policy achieved a full-chunk XYZ RMSE of 24.33 mm and SO(3) error of 0.65° on held-out real-robot observations. These errors were comparable to the teleoperation-post-trained policy (21.64 mm, 0.46°) and generalized well to episode-disjoint UMI observations (21.13 mm, 0.88°). This indicated that both UMI- and teleoperation-trained pose decoders achieved similar accuracy, with the UMI-trained decoder generalizing across observation domains.

#### 4.2. Large-Scale UMI Pre-Training Benefits (StarVLA-QwenPI)

The study investigated the impact of pre-training StarVLA-QwenPI on 4,000 hours of multi-task UMI data.

- **Scaling on Held-out Data:** Held-out action error decreased by 61% over one pass through the 4,000-hour corpus. A power-law fit of L_heldout(S) = L_∞ + AS^-α showed an exponent α = 0.268 (R^2 = 0.993), indicating continuous improvement with increased UMI exposure.
- **Transfer to Unseen Tasks:** Pre-training led to a 41% reduction in mean Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) action error across ten unseen manipulation tasks. OOD error also followed a power-law trend (α = 0.095). Improvement rates varied by task family: utensil/tableware interactions improved fastest, granular transfer was intermediate, and cloth folding improved slowest, correlating with their representation in the pre-training mixture. This suggests OOD transfer depends more on coverage of interaction dynamics than on specific object appearance.
- **Benefits for Post-Training:** On the Remote Insertion task, a UMI-pretrained policy with only 800 task-specific episodes outperformed a Qwen-VL-initialized policy trained from scratch with 3,200 episodes. Overall, UMI pre-training raised aggregate StarVLA-QwenPI success by 18.1 percentage points across the four benchmark tasks, indicating improved data efficiency and a higher performance ceiling for downstream post-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HiFi-UMI research presents findings that challenge established assumptions in robot learning and offer implications for data-driven policy development.

#### 5.1. Breaking the "Real-Robot Anchor" Assumption

The most significant finding is the empirical demonstration that high-fidelity robot-free UMI data alone can serve as the sole source for post-training deployable manipulation policies. By achieving approximate parity with policies trained on real-robot teleoperation data across three diverse policy backbones (StarVLA-QwenPI, OpenPI-π_0.5, and LingBot-VA), this work suggests that the long-held necessity for a "real-robot anchor" in post-training may be linked to data fidelity rather than the robot-free nature of the data source itself. This implies that if robot-free data is sufficiently accurate in trajectory, inter-gripper pose, synchronization, and sensing, it can bridge the sim-to-real or human-to-robot gap for deployment.

#### 5.2. Enabling Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Collection

The HiFi-UMI system, through its co-designed hardware and software, provides a method for producing high-fidelity manipulation data at scale without requiring access to target robots during collection. This significantly lowers the cost and logistical barriers associated with collecting large, deployment-grade datasets. The ability to collect data in diverse, in-the-wild environments by multiple operators without instrumenting each site enhances the scalability and generalizability of the resulting policies, as demonstrated by the performance under scene shift.

#### 5.3. Advancing Foundation Models for Robotics

The work demonstrates that large-scale pre-training on HiFi-UMI data can yield a more effective base model. The observed power-law scaling of action error with increased UMI data exposure, coupled with improved transfer to unseen tasks and enhanced data efficiency for post-training, indicates that robot-free data can contribute to developing robust visual-motor priors for generalist robot policies. This suggests that the development of robot manipulation foundation models can be significantly accelerated by leveraging readily available, high-fidelity human demonstrations, rather than relying solely on costly robot-centric data. The analysis of OOD transfer rates across different task families further informs strategies for curating more effective pre-training mixtures, emphasizing the coverage of interaction dynamics over object categories.

#### 5.4. Contribution to the Research Community

The open-sourcing of HiFi-UMI-2K, a 2,000-hour, microsecond-synchronized, replayable, ultra-wide-FoV dataset, provides a valuable resource. This dataset can enable other researchers to explore and validate robot-free post-training methodologies, develop new policy architectures, and study data scaling laws without the overhead of collecting their own high-fidelity demonstrations. This resource supports the collective effort to reduce the data bottleneck in robot learning.

In summary, HiFi-UMI contributes to a paradigm shift in robot learning by demonstrating that high-fidelity robot-free data can facilitate direct policy deployment and scalable pre-training, moving beyond the traditional reliance on real-robot teleoperation for grounding manipulation skills. This has the potential to accelerate the development of more capable and widely deployable robotic systems.
