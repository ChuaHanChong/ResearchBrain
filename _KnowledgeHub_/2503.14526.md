# ReBot: Scaling Robot Learning with Real-to-Sim-to-Real Robotic Video Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> ReBot, developed by researchers at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, presents a "real-to-sim-to-real" framework for generating high-quality synthetic robot videos. This method scales real robot datasets and significantly enhances the performance and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action models like Octo and OpenVLA in both simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning, particularly with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, is limited by the high cost and scalability challenges of collecting diverse real-world robot data.
- Policies trained solely on simulated data suffer from a significant sim-to-real gap, failing to generalize effectively to the complexities of real-world environments and robot dynamics.
- Existing generative AI methods for robot video augmentation often produce visual artifacts, lack physical realism, and temporal consistency, rendering them unsuitable for reliable robot learning.

## Method

- **ReBot** introduces a ==real-to-sim-to-real== pipeline to scale real robot datasets, generating new robot manipulation videos from existing trajectories.
- Real-world robot actions and scene information are transferred to a high-fidelity simulation environment to replay trajectories with new, diverse simulated objects.
- The original robot and object are automatically segmented and ==inpainted== from real videos using ==GroundedSAM2== and ==ProPainter==, creating a clean background onto which simulated robot and object movements are merged for video synthesis.

## Results

- ReBot significantly improved video quality over a generative baseline, achieving an average temporal quality score of **93.0%** and an imaging quality score of **66.4%**, closely matching real video fidelity.
- In simulation, ReBot boosted Octo's average success rate by **7.2%** and OpenVLA's by **21.8%** for in-domain tasks, and also enhanced generalization across physical, semantic, and subject variations.
- In real-world experiments with a Franka Panda robot, ReBot increased Octo's average success rate by **17%** and OpenVLA's by **20%**, demonstrating substantial performance gains in practical robotic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding real-world actions and observations in a simulated environment before transferring back to a real background minimizes the sim-to-real gap effectively.
> - Automated segmentation and inpainting of real-world video backgrounds allow for seamless integration of diverse simulated objects without manual intervention.
> - Leveraging 3D simulation for object manipulation inherently preserves multi-view consistency, which is crucial for robust robot learning across different camera perspectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a central challenge in the field of robot learning: **data scarcity and the sim-to-real gap.** Rapid advancements in robot learning, particularly with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, have been significantly propelled by large-scale real robot datasets like Open X-Embodiment. These datasets enable policies to learn diverse tasks across various environments and robot embodiments. However, the inherent difficulty and immense cost associated with collecting real-world robot data limit its scalability, thereby restricting the generalizability of VLA models to new, unseen scenarios.

The existing landscape presents several approaches to overcome this data bottleneck:
- **Real-world Data Collection:** Methods like kinesthetic teaching and teleoperation have contributed to impressive VLA models (e.g., RT-1, RT-2). Yet, they remain resource-intensive and struggle to scale for vast diversity.
- **Simulated Data Collection:** Simulation environments (e.g., Isaac Sim, Habitat) offer a scalable and cost-effective alternative, allowing for rapid generation of large datasets in controlled settings. The major hurdle, however, is the **sim-to-real gap**, where policies trained purely on simulated data often fail to generalize effectively to the complexities and nuances of the real world. This gap exists in both action space (robot dynamics) and observation space (visual realism).
- **Scaling Real Robot Datasets with Generative Models:** Recent efforts have leveraged advancements in generative AI (e.g., text-to-image inpainting) to augment real robot datasets by synthesizing new scenarios or objects within existing video frames (e.g., ROSIE). While promising, these methods frequently suffer from **AI-generated artifacts**, visible imperfections, and a critical lack of physical realism and temporal consistency. Such distortions can introduce new domain gaps, making it difficult for VLA models to learn stable, continuous, and reliable robot actions. Moreover, generated images may not precisely adhere to instruction conditions, limiting their utility for targeted domain adaptation.

ReBot situates itself as a novel solution within this landscape by introducing a **"real-to-sim-to-real" paradigm.** This approach aims to synergistically combine the strengths of both real and simulated data while mitigating their individual weaknesses. It seeks to leverage the authenticity and fidelity of real robot trajectories to ground action and observation spaces, thereby minimizing the sim-to-real gap. Simultaneously, it exploits the scalability and control offered by simulation environments to diversify manipulated objects and scenarios, addressing the data scarcity issue. Crucially, ReBot differentiates itself from prior generative methods by ensuring physical realism and temporal consistency through its unique synthesis process, directly tackling the "last-mile deployment challenge" in robot manipulation where VLA models need to be effectively adapted to specific target domains. This positions ReBot as a significant step towards more generalizable and robust robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ReBot stems from the inherent limitations faced by current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robot learning, despite their promising paradigm of training policies directly on large real robot datasets. The core challenges addressed are:

1. **High Cost and Scalability of Real-World Data Collection:** While real robot datasets like Open X-Embodiment have driven significant progress, their collection is prohibitively expensive and resource-intensive, requiring considerable effort, specialized robots, and human teleoperators. This severely restricts the data scale, limiting the generalizability of VLA models to novel scenarios and target domains.
2. **The Sim-to-Real Gap for Simulated Data:** Simulated datasets offer a cost-effective and scalable alternative. However, the discrepancies between simulation and reality—both in visual observations and robot dynamics—create a significant sim-to-real gap. Policies trained on simulated data often fail to transfer effectively to real-world applications, rendering purely synthetic data less effective for advancing VLA capabilities.
3. **Limitations of Existing Generative Models for Data Augmentation:** Recent generative AI techniques can synthesize robot videos by inpainting new objects or scenarios. However, these methods frequently produce undesirable artifacts, such as visual imperfections, inconsistent textures, and a lack of physical realism and temporal continuity. Such generated "fake" data can introduce new domain gaps, making it challenging for VLA models to learn stable and reliable robot actions, and raising concerns about their trustworthiness and instructional adherence for domain adaptation.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of ReBot are:

- **To develop a novel, automated method for scaling real robot datasets:** This involves generating new, diverse robot manipulation videos from existing real-world trajectories.
- **To adapt pre-trained VLA models to specific target domains:** By generating relevant, high-quality synthetic data, ReBot aims to fine-tune VLA models for enhanced performance and generalization in new environments and with new objects.
- **To synthesize physically realistic and temporally consistent robot videos:** Unlike prior generative approaches, ReBot prioritizes fidelity to ensure that the augmented data is genuinely useful for robot learning, minimizing the introduction of new domain gaps.
- **To combine the advantages of both simulation and real-world data:** This means leveraging the scalability and controlled environment of simulation for object diversity while grounding the action and observation spaces in real-world data to maintain realism and minimize the sim-to-real gap.
- **To achieve a fully automated data generation pipeline:** Eliminating manual intervention in the data augmentation process is crucial for scalability and practicality.
- **To significantly enhance the performance and robustness of VLA models:** The ultimate goal is to demonstrate tangible improvements in VLA success rates, generalization capabilities (in-domain, out-of-domain, cross-embodiment), and robustness in both simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

By addressing these motivations and achieving these objectives, ReBot aims to provide a robust and scalable solution for advancing generalist robot policies and bridging the gap between large-scale datasets and real-world robot deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReBot introduces a novel real-to-sim-to-real pipeline for scaling real robot datasets. The framework integrates three core components to synthesize new, diverse, and realistic robot manipulation videos from existing real-world episodes:

**A. Real-to-Sim Trajectory Replay**
This initial stage focuses on transferring real-world robot actions and scene information into a high-fidelity simulation environment to enable object diversification.

1. **Scene Parsing and Alignment:**
 - **Digital Twin Construction:** Pre-built digital twins of the robot and cameras (matching those in real datasets) are positioned and oriented to align with the initial frame of a real-world video.
 - **Table Height Estimation:** To accurately reconstruct the scene, the metric depth from the initial video frame is used to create a 3D point cloud. GroundingDINO, a zero-shot object detection model, segments the "table" using a text prompt. Outliers are removed from the segmented table points, and their average height is set as the table height in simulation, ensuring accurate spatial alignment of the workspace.

2. **Trajectory Replay:**
 - **Object Diversification:** The core idea is to reuse the *real-world action sequence* to manipulate *new, simulated objects*.
 - **Object Placement:** The gripper's action sequence is analyzed to identify the start (gripper closes, `t_start`) and end (`t_end`) times of object manipulation. The gripper's 3D position at `t_start` (obtained by replaying the actions up to that point) is used to precisely place the new, simulated object in the same location as the original real object. Optionally, a container can be placed at the gripper's `t_end` position.
 - **Simulated Movement Recording:** The entire real-world action sequence is then replayed in the simulation environment with the new object, recording the robot's and object's simulated movements (`o_sim_t`). Because the digital twins are accurately aligned to the real scene, these simulated movements remain consistent with the real-world background later.

3. **Replay Validation:**
 - To ensure the quality of synthetic episodes, ReBot automatically validates if the new object was successfully manipulated during replay. This is done by monitoring the Cartesian distance between the object and gripper from `t_start` to `t_end`. Episodes where manipulation fails due to incompatibility between the real trajectory and new object shape are discarded, enhancing dataset reliability.

**B. Real-world Background Inpainting**
This stage prepares a "clean" real-world background, free of task-specific elements, for integration with the simulated movements.

1. **Object and Robot Segmentation:**
 - **Automated Segmentation:** GroundedSAM2 (a combination of GroundingDINO and SAM2) is used to automatically segment and track the original real robot and the object it manipulated.
 - **Robot Segmentation:** The robot is segmented using the text prompt "robot" on the `t_start` frame where it's typically most visible.
 - **Object Segmentation (Crucial Step):** Since direct text prompts for arbitrary objects are unreliable, ReBot leverages the object's estimated 3D position at `t_start` (from the Real-to-Sim step). This 3D position is projected onto the 2D image plane of `o_t_start` to provide a precise 2D point prompt for SAM2, enabling accurate segmentation of the original real object.
 - **Mask Propagation:** Once the initial semantic mask (robot + object) is obtained at `t_start`, SAM2 propagates this mask across all frames (`o_t`) of the original real video, generating a consistent mask for removal.

2. **Object and Robot Removal:**
 - **Video Inpainting:** The generated semantic masks are used by ProPainter, a state-of-the-art video inpainting model, to remove the original robot and object from the real video. This results in a "task-agnostic" real-world background (`o_real_t`) that maintains the realism of the original scene. Removing the real robot here ensures correct occlusions and interactions when the simulated robot is later integrated.

**C. Sim-to-Real Video Synthesis**
The final step integrates the components to produce the synthetic video.

- **Integration:** The robot and the manipulated object are extracted from the simulated movements (`o_sim_t`). These segmented elements are then merged onto the inpainted real-world background (`o_real_t`). This process creates new video frames (`o_prime_t`) where the robot and object movements are physically accurate (from simulation) but visually grounded in a real environment.
- **Language Instruction Update:** A new language instruction (`L'`) is automatically generated by replacing the names of the original object and container in the instruction (`L`) with those of the newly manipulated simulated object and its target container.
- **Synthetic Episode Creation:** The combination of the new video frames (`o_prime_t`), the original unchanged real-world actions (`a_t`), and the updated language instruction (`L'`) forms a complete synthetic episode (`τ'_j`). The preservation of real-world actions is a key advantage, as it minimizes the sim-to-real gap in the action space.

ReBot's end-to-end automation, from scene alignment to video synthesis and language instruction generation, ensures scalability without manual intervention, making it a powerful tool for large-scale data augmentation for robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReBot's effectiveness is demonstrated through comprehensive evaluations across video quality metrics, simulation benchmarks, and real-world robot experiments, consistently outperforming a state-of-the-art generative baseline, ROSIE.

**A. Evaluation of Video Quality**
- **Quantitative (VBench Scores):** ReBot significantly surpasses ROSIE in all video quality dimensions and achieves fidelity highly comparable to original real videos.
 - **Temporal Quality:** ReBot boasts exceptional scores: 99.2% for Motion Smoothness (even slightly better than real videos, potentially due to reduced motion blur), 92.2% for Background Consistency, and 87.7% for Subject Consistency. Its average temporal quality score is 93.0%, very close to the real videos' 96.1%. ROSIE, in contrast, struggles with temporal consistency, especially Subject Consistency (65.6%), indicating its inability to maintain consistent object appearance across frames.
 - **Imaging Quality:** ReBot achieves 66.4%, a 13.0% improvement over ROSIE (53.4%), and is only 3.7% lower than original real videos (70.1%). This highlights ReBot's ability to produce visually high-quality manipulated objects.
- **Qualitative Comparison:** Visual examples clearly show ROSIE's tendency to generate artifacts, flickering, and inconsistent objects, making it unsuitable for learning continuous robot actions. ReBot, however, produces realistic, physically plausible movements with excellent temporal consistency, mimicking real-world video attributes.
- **Multi-view Consistency:** A unique advantage of ReBot, stemming from its 3D simulation foundation, is its inherent preservation of multi-view consistency. When generating videos from multiple camera angles, the same object and its interaction are consistently represented across views, a critical feature for training robust VLA models that generalize across camera setups. Generative models like ROSIE lack this capability.

**B. Evaluation in Simulation Environment (SimplerEnv with WidowX robot)**
- **In-domain Performance:** ReBot consistently boosts the performance of both Octo and OpenVLA.
 - For Octo, ReBot improved the average success rate from 16.0% (zero-shot) to 23.2% (a 7.2% gain).
 - For OpenVLA, the improvement was even more dramatic, from 1.1% to 22.9% (a 21.8% gain), and the average grasp rate surged from 14.6% to 59.4%.
 - ROSIE showed poor performance across most tasks, often yielding 0.0% success rates, indicating its generated data's unsuitability for fine-tuning.
- **Generalization Performance:** ReBot significantly enhances the generalization capabilities of VLA models across different types:
 - For Octo, ReBot increased the average success rate from 6.5% to 26.4% across physical (unseen object sizes), semantic (unseen instructions), and subject (unseen objects) variations.
 - For OpenVLA, it boosted the average success rate from 0.7% to 11.1% and the average grasp rate from 15.6% to 66.8%, demonstrating strong improvements in adapting to novel scenarios.
- **Cross-embodiment Performance:** ReBot also proved effective in improving cross-embodiment transfer. When OpenVLA was finetuned with DROID data (Franka Panda robot) scaled by ReBot, its performance on different robots (WidowX and Google Robot) in SimplerEnv showed substantial gains, whereas ROSIE offered only marginal or no improvement. This highlights ReBot's ability to enable more precise and adaptable manipulation strategies across diverse robot platforms and object poses.

**C. Evaluation in Real-world Environment (Franka Panda robot)**
- ReBot delivered robust performance gains in real-world robotic tasks.
 - For Octo, ReBot increased the average success rate by 17% (from 8% to 25%). Notably, for tasks where Octo initially had 0% success (e.g., "put carrot in blue plate"), ReBot boosted the grasp rate to 40% and success rate to 20%.
 - For OpenVLA, ReBot improved the average success rate by 20% (from 25% to 45%).
 - ROSIE's performance in real-world settings was marginal, occasionally even decreasing success rates compared to the base VLA models, reaffirming its unreliability for real-world deployment.

In summary, the findings robustly confirm ReBot's ability to generate high-quality, actionable synthetic robot videos. This data effectively scales real robot datasets, leading to significant and consistent improvements in VLA model performance and generalization across various benchmarks and real-world deployment scenarios, vastly outperforming current generative augmentation techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReBot represents a significant advancement in robot learning by directly addressing critical bottlenecks in data acquisition and generalization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Novelty in Data Augmentation for Robotics:** ReBot is highlighted as the first real-to-sim-to-real approach for scaling real robot datasets and adapting VLA models to target domains. This novel paradigm effectively combines the high-fidelity of real-world data with the scalability and control of simulation, offering a superior alternative to purely generative methods that often introduce undesirable artifacts and inconsistencies.
2. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap Effectively:** A key contribution is ReBot's ability to minimize the notorious sim-to-real gap. By grounding both the action and observation spaces in real robot data (through real-world trajectory replay and background inpainting), ReBot ensures that the generated synthetic videos maintain physical realism and temporal consistency. This directly translates to VLA models learning more stable, continuous, and reliable robot actions that are directly transferable to the physical world, unlike policies trained on less realistic simulated or artifact-ridden generated data.
3. **Automated and Scalable Data Generation:** The fully automated nature of ReBot, requiring no manual intervention for setting up digital twins, segmenting objects, or performing video synthesis, is a major practical advantage. This automation makes it highly scalable, enabling the generation of large volumes of diverse synthetic data efficiently and cost-effectively, circumventing the prohibitive expense and labor of real-world data collection.
4. **Tangible Performance Boost for State-of-the-Art VLA Models:** The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate ReBot's ability to significantly enhance the performance and robustness of leading VLA models like Octo and OpenVLA. The substantial improvements in in-domain performance, generalization capabilities (across physical, semantic, and subject variations), and crucial cross-embodiment transfer, both in simulation and especially in real-world settings, underscore its practical utility. This means robots can learn more robust policies from less initial real data, adapting faster to new environments and tasks.
5. **Addressing the "Last-Mile Deployment Challenge":** A core objective of ReBot is to enable effective adaptation of VLA models to target domains, which is often the final hurdle in robot manipulation deployment. By generating contextually relevant and high-fidelity synthetic data, ReBot facilitates fine-tuning models specifically for new environments, objects, or task variations, thus making VLA models more readily deployable in diverse real-world applications.
6. **Inspiring Future Research:** The paper explicitly states that ReBot could serve as a valuable asset and inspire future research in real-to-sim-to-real approaches for robot learning. This opens up exciting avenues, such as:
 - **Extending to Diverse Data Settings:** Applying ReBot to a wider range of camera setups, robot embodiments, and environmental conditions could further benefit cross-embodiment and cross-task learning.
 - **Exploring More Complex Scenarios:** Moving beyond tabletop manipulation to more challenging tasks like deformable object manipulation, dexterous manipulation, or even multi-robot coordination, could broaden ReBot's real-world applicability.
 - **Synergistic Integration with Other Techniques:** ReBot could potentially be combined with other learning paradigms, such as reinforcement learning or active learning, to optimize data collection and policy improvement.

In essence, ReBot provides a robust, automated, and scalable solution to a fundamental bottleneck in robot learning. By producing high-quality synthetic data that bridges the gap between simulation and reality, it significantly pushes the boundaries of VLA model performance and generalization, paving the way for more capable and adaptable robots in real-world scenarios.
