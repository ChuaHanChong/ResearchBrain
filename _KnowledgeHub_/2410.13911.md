# GraspDiffusion: Synthesizing Realistic Whole-body Hand-Object Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspDiffusion generates realistic full-body human-object interaction images from a 3D object mesh: a diffusion model first synthesizes SMPL-X body pose jointly optimized with a MANO hand grasp, then this 3D pose conditions a latent diffusion image pipeline via multi-adapter spatial control and cross-attention mask injection, outperforming prior methods on image quality and grasp plausibility.

## Problem

- Diffusion image generators produce distorted or anatomically implausible hands and multi-arm artifacts because hands occupy small image regions yet have highly complex, variable pose distributions.
- Existing hand-object generation methods like Affordance Diffusion and HOIDiffusion only model explicit hand-object contact, ignoring implicit non-contact body relationships such as gaze and torso orientation.
- No large-scale paired image-3D pose dataset exists for full-body human-object interaction; prior datasets are either egocentric hand-only or lack realistic RGB image quality.

## Method

- **Full-Body Grasping Pipeline**: a ==diffusion model== generates SMPL-X body pose conditioned on object location and hand side, jointly optimized with a GrabNet-produced MANO hand grasp via ==palm-vertex alignment==.
- **Scene Generation Pipeline**: ==CoAdapter-style spatial encoders== inject rendered skeleton, joint depth map, and occluded object conditions into a frozen ==Stable Diffusion U-Net==, trained on a ==pseudo-3D annotation pipeline== (BLIP-2, GroundingDINO, HybrIK, ACR) that builds 25K full-scene and 15K hand-refinement pairs from HICO-DET, V-COCO, and BEHAVE.
- **Attention Injection Scheme**: a ==training-free cross-attention guidance== uses positive and negative semantic segmentation masks to focus generation on intended human/object regions and suppress wrong-hand interactions.

## Results

- On full-body HOI generation, GraspDiffusion achieves FID **22.88** versus ControlNet's **32.76** and Champ's **40.63**, with CLIPScore **0.767** versus **0.739** and **0.71** respectively.
- On hand-object generation, the method reaches **97.94** hand-contact accuracy versus **65.69** for Affordance Diffusion and **61.45** for HandRefiner, with FID dropping from ControlNet's **99.38** to **64.67**.
- 3D grasp pose evaluation shows contact ratio **0.909** and pose-valid error **0.111** versus COOP's **0.841** and **0.239**, and a user study found **92.4%** preference for plausibility and **96.4%** for context faithfulness over baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling explicit 3D pose synthesis from image synthesis lets a model enforce physical plausibility (contact, body orientation) that purely image-space conditioning cannot guarantee.
> - Freezing the base diffusion backbone and training only lightweight conditional adapters preserves compatibility with arbitrary personalized/stylized checkpoints at inference time.
> - Training-free attention masking from 3D-rendered segmentation can suppress systematic generation errors (wrong-hand contact) without any additional model training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly diffusion-based models, has made advancements in synthesizing high-quality visual content. However, generating realistic human-object interactions (HOI), especially involving intricate hand poses and full-body context, remains a challenge. Existing generative models often produce distorted hands, anatomically incorrect poses, or misunderstand the semantic and functional relationships (affordance) between humans and objects.

Previous attempts to address issues with hands in generated images include inpainting methods, which primarily focus on hand region refinement in scenarios where hands are not interacting with objects. Other methods, such as Affordance Diffusion and HOIDiffusion, generate images of single grasping hands but are limited to hand-centric views, lack a clear human identity, and fail to capture implicit non-contact relationships (e.g., body orientation, gaze relative to an object). Moreover, standard conditional image generation techniques like ControlNet often struggle with the complexity of HOI, leading to erroneous object creation or physically implausible interactions.

In the domain of 3D grasp synthesis, significant research has been conducted in robotics, computer vision, and graphics to create stable or plausible grasps for hands or whole human bodies. However, many existing datasets for human-object interaction are either hand-centric, lack comprehensive 3D annotations paired with high-quality images, or suffer from limited scalability and variability. This data scarcity poses a significant hurdle for training models that can understand and generate full-bodied HOI. Prior full-body grasp synthesis methods also exhibit limitations, such as generating distorted poses for objects far from the human, relying on specific coordinate systems that introduce ambiguity, or requiring extensive test-time optimization.

GraspDiffusion addresses these limitations by proposing a novel pipeline that explicitly targets the generation of full-bodied HOI images from 3D object information. It aims to bridge the gap between high-quality image synthesis and the accurate representation of complex human-object interactions, encompassing both explicit physical contact and implicit spatial relationships. The work also contributes by introducing a method for constructing a pseudo-3D interaction dataset to mitigate the lack of suitable training data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generative method capable of synthesizing realistic scenes of whole-body human-object interaction, given a 3D object and its relative position. The authors are motivated by several challenges observed in current generative models:

1. **Inability to Comprehend and Visualize Complex Interactions:** Despite advancements in generative models, they frequently fail to produce realistic hand-object interactions. This is attributed to the models' limited understanding of interaction semantics and the intricate anatomical structure of hands, which results in artifacts like distorted hands or incorrect body postures.
2. **Limitations of Existing Hand-Centric Approaches:** Current methods that focus on hand refinement or specific grasping tasks often operate in hand-centric views or do not account for the full human body. This limits their applicability in scenarios requiring a broader understanding of the interaction, including the spatial and orientational relationships between the human's body (e.g., face, torso) and the object. The paper emphasizes the need for a pipeline that captures human identity and implicit non-contact relationships.
3. **Ambiguity of Textual Prompts:** Relying solely on textual prompts for complex interactions can be ambiguous, making it difficult to precisely describe desired poses and object relationships. The authors propose using a 3D object input and its position to provide clearer conditioning.
4. **Lack of High-Quality, Large-Scale Datasets:** There is a scarcity of publicly available datasets that pair high-quality images of human-object interactions with accurate 3D pose annotations. This data gap hinders the training of robust models for full-bodied HOI generation.

To address these motivations, GraspDiffusion sets out to:
- Propose a novel two-stage generative pipeline that synthesizes realistic, full-bodied human-object interaction images.
- Leverage separate generative priors for body and hand poses, optimizing them into a joint grasping pose to ensure physical plausibility.
- Utilize this generated 3D pose as guidance for image synthesis, ensuring correct reflection of the intended interaction and promoting diversity in generated samples.
- Develop an annotation pipeline to construct a pseudo-3D interaction dataset from existing 2D image-based datasets, thereby overcoming the data scarcity issue.
- Be the first approach to generate full-bodied HOI images from object information that portrays both explicit (physical contact) and implicit (body language, gaze) interactions in a physically plausible manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraspDiffusion employs a two-stage pipeline to generate realistic human-object interaction images, starting from a 3D object mesh and its relative position to a human body.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The methodology builds upon **Diffusion Models** for generative tasks, specifically **Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs)** which operate in the latent space of a trained autoencoder. For 3D human and hand representation, the paper utilizes standard **3D Parametric Models**: **MANO** for hands (parameters for finger articulation, wrist translation, and global orientation) and **SMPL-X** for the full body (parameters for full-body pose, hand poses, root translation, and global orientation).

#### 3.2. Full-Body Grasping Pipeline

This initial stage generates a 3D full-bodied grasping pose (SMPL-X mesh) that realistically interacts with the input 3D object.
1. **Hand Grasp Generation:** Given a 3D object mesh, a conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) called GrabNet is used. GrabNet, separately trained for left and right hands, generates MANO parameters (finger articulation, wrist translation, global orientation) that form a plausible hand grasp around the object. It conditions on the object's Basis Point Set (BPS).
2. **Body Pose Generation:** A diffusion generative model is trained to synthesize SMPL-X body parameters (body pose, global orientation). This model is conditioned on the object's relative location (translation from the human root) and the intended contacting hand (left or right). The objective is to create a body pose that roughly positions the hand at the object's location while also reflecting appropriate implicit relationships such as head orientation, arm extension, and torso lean for a plausible interaction. The model is trained using a denoising diffusion loss (Equation 1).
3. **Joint Optimization for Alignment:** The finger articulation from the generated hand grasp (from GrabNet) is integrated into the body pose. To ensure accurate alignment of the 3D hand-object grasp with the full human body, an optimization process is applied. This optimization adjusts the rotation and translation of the MANO hand model, while preserving its finger articulation, to align the palm vertices of the MANO model with the corresponding palm vertices of the SMPL-X body model. This alignment is achieved by minimizing the L1 distance between these vertex sets (Equation 2). The optimized hand transformation also correctly positions the 3D object relative to the full-body grasp.

#### 3.3. Scene Generation Pipeline

This stage takes the generated 3D full-body grasping pose and synthesizes a realistic image.
1. **Spatial Condition Extraction:** Multiple geometric cues are rendered from the 3D full-body grasping output:
 - **Human Skeleton:** A 2D projection of SMPL-X body and hand joints, providing guidance for human proportions.
 - **Joint Depth Map:** A depth map generated from both the SMPL-X body and the object model, offering crucial depth information for the scene.
 - **Occluded Object:** A rendered image of the object, occluded by the human if applicable, with ambient lighting to preserve its appearance while allowing for relighting in the generated scene.
2. **Conditional Image Generation:** These three spatial conditions are fed into a latent diffusion model. The CoAdapter approach is employed, where each condition is processed by a separate adapter, and their features are combined via a weighted sum (Equation 3) before being injected into the diffusion model's U-Net. During training, the parameters of the base U-Net are frozen, and only the conditional adapters are optimized. This design choice allows for flexible style control (e.g., using LoRAs or finetuned Stable Diffusion models) during inference. The training uses a denoising diffusion loss (Equation 4).
3. **Hand-Object Refinement:** To further enhance the quality and accuracy of the hand-object interaction region, a dedicated refinement module is used. This module receives localized spatial conditions (hand skeleton, joint hand-object depth map, occluded rendered object) and a joint hand-object mask, similar to the full-body conditioning but focused on the hand region. These inputs guide separate hand refinement adapters to improve structural and appearance details.
4. **Attention Injection Scheme:** To mitigate erroneous interactions (e.g., the wrong hand interacting with the object), a training-free guidance method is introduced. Binary semantic segmentation masks of the posed human and object are rendered. These masks are down-sampled and injected into the cross-attention layers of the diffusion model as guidance. A "negative mask" is also generated, representing the object grasped by the *opposite* hand, and subtracted from the attention matrix. This encourages the model to focus attention on the intended interaction regions and discourages unintended interactions, by modifying the cross-attention computation (Fig. 6).

#### 3.4. Dataset Construction

Given the lack of large-scale 3D-annotated HOI datasets, the authors devised an annotation pipeline. They leveraged existing 2D interaction datasets like HICO-DET and V-COCO.
- Images were filtered for visible humans and identifiable hands.
- The BLIP-2 language model was used for Visual Question Answering to identify object types, followed by GroundingDINO for object localization and segmentation, and a state-of-the-art depth estimation model for object depth maps.
- For human 3D pose, HybrIK was employed to estimate SMPL-X parameters using annotated bounding boxes. Human skeleton information was obtained from Halpe or DWPose.
- ACR was used for 3D MANO hand parameter estimation.
- The BEHAVE dataset was also processed similarly.
This process yielded 25K joint interaction pairs for training the scene generation pipeline. For hand refinement, the DexYCB and RHD datasets, along with a cropped subset of HICO-DET, provided 15K hand-object interaction pairs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GraspDiffusion demonstrates its efficacy in synthesizing realistic whole-body human-object interactions through both quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments.

#### 4.1. Full-Body Generation Quality

Quantitative evaluation used Frechet Inception Distance (FID), Kernel Inception Distance (KID), and CLIPScore (for text-image alignment). GraspDiffusion was compared against a finetuned Latent Diffusion Model (LDM), ControlNet, and Champ.
- As shown in Table 1, GraspDiffusion achieved lower FID (22.88) and KID (5.55 × 10⁻³) scores compared to baselines, indicating higher image quality.
- It also obtained a higher CLIPScore (0.767), suggesting better alignment between the generated image and the intended interaction context.
- An ablation study showed that while the model without attention injection had slightly better FID/KID, the full model with attention injection had a substantially higher CLIPScore, indicating its effectiveness in ensuring adherence to the intended interaction.

#### 4.2. Hand-Grasp Generation Quality

For hand-centric images, metrics included FID, KID, and a "Hand Contact" score (measured using a hand-object detector). Comparisons were made with depth-based ControlNet, HandRefiner, and Affordance Diffusion.
- GraspDiffusion significantly outperformed all baselines (Table 2), achieving an FID of 64.67, KID of 4.36 × 10⁻³, and a Hand Contact score of 97.94%. This demonstrates superior image quality and accurate hand-object contact.

#### 4.3. 3D Pose Evaluation

The plausibility of generated grasping poses was assessed using a test set of 10 novel 3D objects, 10 human body shapes, and 64 random 3D positions for each pair. Metrics included Contact Ratio, Pose Valid Error (L2 loss of vertex reconstruction from VPoser), and Displacement. GraspDiffusion was compared with GOAL, FLEX, and COOP.
- GraspDiffusion exhibited superior performance across all metrics (Table 3), with a Contact Ratio of 0.909, Pose Valid Error of 0.111, and Displacement of 2.696. This indicates the model's ability to generate authentic and physically plausible grasping poses for objects in diverse and challenging positions, including those outside the original training distribution.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

The importance of each structural rendering condition (object rendering, human skeleton, joint depth map) was investigated.
- Removing any of these conditions resulted in increased FID scores (Table 4), confirming that the full set of conditions contributes to higher image quality. Object rendering showed the largest impact.
- The attention injection scheme, while marginally affecting FID/KID, substantially improved CLIPScore, confirming its role in ensuring that interactions are generated in the intended locations and contexts.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user study involving 28 participants compared GraspDiffusion images with those generated by ControlNets combined with HandRefiner.
- 92.4% of participants preferred GraspDiffusion images for realism and plausibility.
- 96.4% preferred GraspDiffusion for faithfully adhering to the given grasping context.

#### 4.6. Applications

GraspDiffusion can serve as a practical tool for AI practitioners in content creation (e.g., advertisements, illustrations, comic books).
- It supports integrating 3D reconstruction models, allowing the use of a single image input to recover a 3D mesh, thus alleviating the requirement for pre-existing 3D object models.
- The pipeline supports various personalized image domains and artistic styles (e.g., realistic, anime, pixel art) by allowing the use of different finetuned Stable Diffusion models during inference (Figure 9), demonstrating its versatility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GraspDiffusion represents a significant contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in the domain of human-object interaction. Its key significance lies in being presented as the first image generation pipeline that explicitly targets the synthesis of realistic, full-bodied human-object interactions from object information.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
- **Comprehensive Interaction Synthesis:** The method effectively captures both explicit human-object interactions (e.g., accurate hand-object contact, realistic grasps) and implicit relationships (e.g., human gaze, body orientation towards the object). This comprehensive understanding of interaction goes beyond hand-centric approaches, providing a more holistic and physically plausible depiction of human behavior.
- **Reduced Input Complexity:** By requiring only a 3D object mesh and its relative position as input, the pipeline simplifies the user interface compared to complex textual prompts or extensive pose guidance. The ability to integrate with 3D reconstruction models further reduces the barrier to entry by allowing single 2D image inputs for objects.
- **Improved Generative Quality and Plausibility:** Quantitative and qualitative results demonstrate that GraspDiffusion outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods in generating high-quality images with realistic, valid 3D grasping poses and faithful adherence to interaction context. This improves the overall utility of generative models for scenes involving humans and objects.
- **Novel Methodology:** The two-stage pipeline, which leverages separate generative priors for body and hand poses and then optimizes them jointly, along with the innovative cross-attention modulation scheme, provides a robust and flexible framework for HOI synthesis.
- **Dataset Construction Strategy:** The proposed annotation pipeline to create a pseudo-3D interaction dataset is a valuable contribution, addressing a critical data scarcity issue in this research area. It allows leveraging existing 2D resources to create richer 3D-aware training data.
- **Practical Applications:** The versatility of GraspDiffusion, including its support for various artistic styles and its capability to generate diverse human identities and scene contexts, makes it a practical solution for creative industries such as digital art, advertisements, illustrations, and comic book creation.

**Future Directions and Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge certain limitations, such as occasional discrepancies in skin texture between the body and refined hand regions, attributed to data imbalance. Future work aims to address this by constructing more balanced, high-quality interaction samples. They also plan to extend the pipeline towards scene-level generation involving multiple humans and objects, enhance control through user-definable text prompts, and explore the synthesis of zero-shot interaction motions using image-to-video diffusion models. The potential for generating synthetic datasets for interaction detection and video generation further highlights the broader impact of this research.
