# WorldEval: World Model as Real-World Robot Policies Evaluator

> [!summary] Summary
> Midea Group and East China Normal University researchers develop WorldEval, a world model-based evaluation framework that assesses robot manipulation policies by generating videos of task execution using Policy2Vec action encoding and automated LLM-based success detection, achieving 0.79 Pearson correlation with real-world performance across multiple robotic tasks while outperforming real-to-sim evaluation methods and demonstrating capability to identify unsafe policies before deployment, with results showing that video generation approaches can provide more reliable policy assessment than traditional simulation-based methods for real-world robotics applications.

## Problem

- Real-world robot policy evaluation is time-consuming, expensive, and potentially dangerous, creating a need for automated safety detection to identify hazardous behaviors before deployment
- Traditional simulation-based evaluation suffers from the reality gap, failing to accurately predict real-world performance
- Existing evaluation methods cannot scale to assess the growing complexity of modern robot manipulation policies

## Method

- Develop **WorldEval** framework that uses ==world models== to generate videos of policy execution for evaluation
- Introduce ==Policy2Vec== method that extracts ==latent action representations== from policy networks and injects them into video generation models
- Create an automated evaluation pipeline using large language models (Gemini-2.0) to classify task success and detect irregular or dangerous policy behaviors (==safety detection==) in generated videos

## Results

- Achieves **0.79** Pearson correlation coefficient with real-world performance across multiple manipulation tasks
- Outperforms real-to-sim evaluation methods in both MMRV (Mean Maximum Rank Violation) and correlation metrics
- Successfully identifies unsafe policies exhibiting hazardous behaviors before real-world testing, demonstrating cost-effective evaluation that can reduce reliance on expensive real-world testing protocols

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World model-based evaluation achieves stronger correlation with real-world performance than real-to-sim approaches, as video generation models can effectively simulate complex manipulation scenarios when properly conditioned on policy actions
> - Policy2Vec action encoding outperforms traditional methods like VQVAE and one-hot encoding for video generation
> - Fréchet Inception Distance can serve as a lightweight metric for rapid policy ranking on simple tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of robotics: the difficulty and expense of evaluating robot policies in real-world settings. It tackles this challenge by leveraging world models, a growing area of research that aims to create simulators capable of predicting future states based on actions.

- **Evaluation of Robot Policies:** The paper directly addresses the need for scalable and reliable evaluation methods in robotics. Traditionally, policies are evaluated either in simulation or in the real world. Simulation struggles with the "reality gap," where simulated environments fail to accurately represent real-world physics and sensor noise. Real-world evaluation is time-consuming, expensive, and potentially dangerous. This work sits within a trend of research exploring alternative evaluation methods that are faster, safer, and more reliable than traditional approaches. This includes SIMPLER and AutoEval which they compare their work to in the related works section.
- **World Models for Robotics:** The use of world models in robotics is not new, with previous research focusing on video prediction as an auxiliary task to improve manipulation performance or for robot navigation. However, this paper distinguishes itself by explicitly focusing on using world models as policy *evaluators*. This shift in perspective is significant because it allows researchers to leverage the power of world models for a different, but equally important, purpose. They note that their method differs because "instead of employing video prediction or a world model to improve robotic policies, our proposed WorldEval aims to train a world simulator capable of performing online evaluation of real robot policies."
- **Action Representation:** Representing robot actions in a way that is both informative and compatible with world models is a key challenge. The authors' Policy2Vec method addresses this by leveraging the latent action representations within the policy network itself. This approach is novel because it avoids the need for explicitly training action encoders or relying on potentially ambiguous action spaces. This is an important contribution to the broader discussion of action representation in robotics. This is also related to research into imitation learning for robotic control. In this field, vision-language-action models have attracted significant attention. The paper notes that "However, as model capabilities rapidly advance, evaluating real-world policies has become increasingly challenging. Traditional methods of real-world evaluation are not only highly time-consuming and labor-intensive but are also inadequate for assessing performance in novel environments or with unfamiliar objects. To address this limitation, this work proposes utilizing a world simulator for comprehensive and efficient policy evaluation."
- **Real-to-Sim vs. Real-to-Video:** The paper directly compares its approach to real-to-sim methods, which aim to transfer policies trained in simulation to the real world. The results demonstrate that WorldEval outperforms real-to-sim, suggesting that real-to-video may be a more effective approach for policy evaluation. This finding contributes to the ongoing debate about the best way to bridge the gap between simulation and reality in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** The primary objective is to develop a scalable, reproducible, and reliable method for evaluating real-world robot manipulation policies *before* deployment.
- **Motivation:**
 - The increasing complexity and generality of robot manipulation policies necessitates efficient and safe evaluation methods.
 - Real-world evaluation is time-consuming, expensive, and poses risks to the robot, operators, and environment.
 - Existing simulation-based methods often fail to accurately reflect real-world performance.
 - To develop a system that provides actionable feedback for iterative policy refinement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes WorldEval, a system that leverages a world model to evaluate robot policies based on generated videos. The core components of the approach are:

- **Policy2Vec:** A method for converting a video generation model into a world simulator by injecting latent action representations from the policy network into the generator. This enables the generator to produce videos that reflect the policy's intended actions.
 - Extract latent action embeddings from the policy model.
 - Fine-tune a pre-trained video generation model (WAN 2.1) to generate videos conditioned on these embeddings, along with language instructions and initial frames.
- **Automated Evaluation Pipeline:**
 - Generate videos of the policy executing a task in the world simulator.
 - Use a large language model (Gemini-2.0) to automatically classify whether the task was successfully completed in the generated video.
- **Comparative Evaluation:** Evaluate multiple policies using WorldEval and compare the results to real-world performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Strong Correlation with Real-World Performance:** The paper demonstrates a strong correlation between policy performance in WorldEval and real-world scenarios. This suggests that WorldEval can serve as a reliable proxy for real-world evaluation.
- **Outperformance of Real-to-Sim:** WorldEval significantly outperforms real-to-sim approaches in terms of MMRV (Mean Maximum Rank Violation) and Pearson correlation coefficient.
- **Policy2Vec Effectiveness:** The Policy2Vec method outperforms other action encoding methods (VQVAE, One-hot encoding).
- **Safety Detector:** WorldEval can identify unsafe policies that exhibit irregular or hazardous behaviors.
- **Cost-Effective Evaluation:** The Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) can serve as a lightweight metric for rapidly ranking policy models on simple tasks, reducing reliance on expensive success detectors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Scalable Policy Evaluation:** WorldEval provides a scalable and automated way to evaluate robot policies, enabling faster development and deployment.
- **Safer Robot Development:** By identifying unsafe policies before real-world deployment, WorldEval can help prevent accidents and damage.
- **Improved Policy Design:** The insights gained from WorldEval can inform iterative improvements in policy design.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By reducing the need for extensive real-world testing, WorldEval can significantly reduce the cost of robot development.
- **Broader Applicability:** The approach can be adapted to various robot embodiments, tasks, and environments.
- **Impact on Midea Group:** The research directly benefits Midea Group by providing a practical tool for evaluating and improving the robot policies used in their automation systems.

Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of robotics by offering a practical and effective method for evaluating robot policies using world models. The WorldEval framework has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of robots in a wide range of applications.
