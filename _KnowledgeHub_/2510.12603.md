# Reasoning in the Dark: Interleaved Vision-Text Reasoning in Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Interleaved Vision-Text Latent Reasoning (IVT-LR), a framework that performs multimodal reasoning entirely within an MLLM's latent space, without explicit intermediate steps. This approach achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on M3CoT and ScienceQA benchmarks while reducing inference latency by 3-8 times and requiring 9 times fewer autoregressive steps compared to explicit reasoning methods.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal reasoning methods often require labor-intensive annotations for explicit intermediate reasoning steps.
- Generating explicit reasoning steps leads to significant inference latency, hindering real-time applications.
- Prior latent reasoning efforts for MLLMs struggled to unify interleaved vision-text interaction truly within the latent space.

## Method

- The ==IVT-LR framework== introduces ==multimodal latent reasoning==, where both 'latent text' (hidden states) and 'latent vision' (dynamically selected image embeddings) interact implicitly.
- An ==attention-based mechanism== dynamically selects the most relevant visual features at each reasoning step, fusing them with latent textual information.
- A ==progressive multi-stage training strategy== incrementally replaces explicit reasoning steps with latent ones, using NLL loss supervision only on remaining explicit outputs and the final answer.

## Results

- IVT-LR achieved state-of-the-art accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline (Chain-of-Focus) by **7.5%** on M3CoT and **3.4%** on ScienceQA using a Qwen2-VL backbone.
- The method demonstrated a **~9x** reduction in autoregressive steps and was **3 to 8x** faster in inference latency (e.g., **0.65-0.67s** for IVT-LR vs. **2.09s-5.31s** for baselines).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both latent text and latent vision components are crucial, with their removal causing substantial accuracy drops (e.g., **19.63%** without latent text, **25.19%** without latent vision on M3CoT).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Truly unified, interleaved vision-text reasoning can be effectively performed implicitly within an MLLM's latent space, avoiding explicit token generation.
> - Latent reasoning not only drastically improves inference efficiency but can also lead to higher accuracy compared to methods relying on explicit rationales.
> - Dynamic attention analysis within the latent process reveals a sophisticated, adaptive shift from visual focus to textual refinement, indicating robust internal problem-solving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and rapidly evolving area within artificial intelligence: multimodal reasoning, particularly in the context of Large Language Models (LLMs) extended to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The research landscape in reasoning has seen significant advancements, initially in LLMs through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which allows models to break down complex problems into intermediate, explicit reasoning steps. This success has naturally led to an exploration of extending such reasoning capabilities to MLLMs, enabling them to process and reason over information from both text and images.

The evolution of multimodal reasoning can be broadly categorized:
1. **Text-only reasoning in MLLMs:** Early approaches primarily converted visual information into textual descriptions (e.g., captions) and then applied LLM-style textual reasoning. While effective to some extent, these methods lose rich visual context and can be limited by the expressiveness of text descriptions.
2. **Explicit Vision-Text Involved Reasoning:** Recognizing the limitations of text-only approaches, subsequent research focused on *interleaving* visual and textual information explicitly during the reasoning process. This involves models generating sequential steps that might include selecting image patches, referring to specific visual regions, sketching images, or even generating new images as part of their "thought" process. Examples include methods that generate visual rationales or dynamically zoom into image regions. While more powerful, these methods often require extensive, labor-intensive annotations for intermediate visual reasoning steps and introduce significant inference latency due to the generation of long explicit multimodal sequences.
3. **Latent Reasoning in LLMs:** A parallel and more recent trend in pure LLMs has been the development of "latent reasoning." This paradigm shifts from generating explicit, verbose textual reasoning steps to performing reasoning internally within the model's hidden (latent) states. This promises improved efficiency by avoiding lengthy token generation while retaining the benefits of multi-step reasoning.

This paper, "Reasoning in the Dark," positions itself at the intersection of explicit vision-text involved reasoning and emerging latent reasoning. It identifies a crucial gap: while latent reasoning has been explored for LLMs, and some multimodal latent approaches exist (e.g., "latent visual reasoning" focusing on visual "thoughts"), none have successfully unified *interleaved vision-text* reasoning fully within the *latent space*. Existing multimodal latent reasoning still tends to focus on single-modal latent reasoning, rather than dynamic interaction between latent text and latent vision.

Therefore, this work represents a significant leap forward by proposing the first framework (IVT-LR) that allows both textual and visual information to be reasoned with implicitly and interactively in the latent space. It seeks to combine the benefits of rich multimodal interaction (from explicit vision-text CoT) with the efficiency and reduced annotation requirements of latent reasoning. By operating "in the dark"—meaning without explicit, observable intermediate steps—it challenges the conventional wisdom that reasoning must always be linguistically transparent.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Reasoning in the Dark" stems from the inherent limitations of current state-of-the-art multimodal reasoning methods, particularly those that involve explicit reasoning steps:

1. **Labor-intensive Annotations:** Current vision-text involved reasoning methods often rely on heavily annotated datasets, where intermediate reasoning steps (e.g., selected image regions, generated visual rationales, textual descriptions linked to specific visual elements) need to be explicitly provided. This manual annotation process is time-consuming, expensive, and scales poorly for diverse and complex reasoning tasks.
2. **Significant Inference Latency:** Generating explicit reasoning steps—whether textual rationales, image patches, or new images—requires multiple autoregressive steps. These lengthy generation chains substantially increase inference time, making such MLLMs impractical for real-time applications or scenarios requiring rapid responses. The computational cost associated with generating and processing these long sequences is also a considerable bottleneck.

To address these critical challenges, the paper sets forth a clear set of objectives:

- **Introduce Multimodal Latent Reasoning:** The overarching objective is to develop a novel paradigm for multimodal reasoning that conducts intermediate steps entirely within the latent space of an MLLM. This aims to overcome the limitations of explicit generation while retaining or enhancing reasoning capabilities.
- **Leverage Multimodal Representation Potential:** The authors hypothesize that latent space offers a richer, more continuous, and flexible environment for representing and integrating diverse multimodal information during reasoning compared to discrete, explicit tokens or generated images. The objective is to unlock this potential for more effective reasoning.
- **Reduce Annotation Dependence:** By shifting reasoning into the latent space, the goal is to significantly lessen the reliance on costly, fine-grained vision-text interleaved annotations for intermediate reasoning steps, simplifying data collection and model training.
- **Improve Inference Efficiency:** A core objective is to drastically reduce the inference latency by minimizing the number of autoregressive steps. This means avoiding the explicit generation of lengthy multimodal rationales and instead performing reasoning through internal, implicit computations.

Specifically, the paper proposes **Interleaved Vision-Text Latent Reasoning (IVT-LR)**, with the objective of injecting both visual and textual information into the latent reasoning process. This involves designing a mechanism where each latent reasoning step combines implicit "latent text" (derived from previous hidden states) and "latent vision" (dynamically selected image embeddings), and developing a progressive training strategy to enable MLLMs to master this novel reasoning approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Interleaved Vision-Text Latent Reasoning (IVT-LR) method is designed to perform multimodal reasoning entirely within the latent space, combining implicit textual and visual information. The core components of the methodology include its unique approach to multimodal latent reasoning, an attention-based selection mechanism for visual features, and a progressive multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Latent Reasoning

IVT-LR redefines how intermediate reasoning steps are handled by an MLLM. Instead of generating explicit tokens or visual outputs, reasoning occurs through the model's internal representations:

- **Latent Text:** Borrowing from latent reasoning in LLMs, the textual modality bypasses explicit token prediction. At each reasoning step, the "latent text" is represented by the hidden state `h_hidden_{t-1}` from the previous step. This hidden state compactly encodes the accumulated reasoning logic and contextual information, preserving richer intermediate details in a continuous latent space compared to discrete text tokens.
- **Latent Vision:** This is a novel extension to incorporate visual information implicitly. For each reasoning step, IVT-LR dynamically selects a fixed number (`k`) of the most relevant visual features (image embeddings) from the complete set of visual embeddings `Z = [z_1..., z_J]` derived from a visual encoder. This selection is based on an **attention-based mechanism**, where the sum of attention weights across all layers of the model identifies the `k` image embedding positions with the highest cumulative scores. This ensures that the model focuses on the most informative parts of the image at each specific reasoning juncture.
- **Multimodal Latent Representation:** The selected `k` visual features (`z_selected_{t-1}`) are concatenated with the latent text (`h_latent_{t-1}`, which is the hidden state from the previous step). This fused representation `[h_latent_{t-1}, z_selected_{t-1}]` forms the input for the subsequent reasoning step. This allows the model to perform step-wise multimodal reasoning internally without generating intermediate reasoning sequences, directly influencing the model's continuous hidden states.

The overall input to the model at step `t` thus includes the initial question embeddings, any preceding explicit inputs, and the sequence of previously generated latent multimodal representations. The model then fuses these representations to compute the fused hidden state `e_fused_t`, which is then projected to predict the distribution over the next token (if an explicit token is required, such as the final answer).

#### 3.2. Progressive Multi-Stage Training Strategy

To effectively teach the MLLM to perform multimodal latent reasoning, IVT-LR employs a progressive, multi-stage training strategy inspired by methods that internalize Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning:

1. **Preprocessing:** Each multimodal reasoning trajectory in the training data is first segmented into a maximum of `N` intermediate reasoning steps, followed by the final answer. This segmentation standardizes the structure of reasoning. (The paper clarifies `N=4` meaning three core reasoning steps based on rationale length distribution).
2. **Stage 0 (Standard CoT Supervision):** The model is initially trained with traditional CoT supervision. In this stage, all `N` reasoning steps are explicitly generated (textually and visually, if applicable in the baseline CoT method), and the model is supervised on generating these explicit steps along with the final answer. This phase is crucial for grounding the model in symbolic reasoning and establishing a foundational understanding of reasoning logic.
3. **Progressive Latent Integration (Stages 1 to N):** In subsequent stages, the core of the progressive learning begins. At each new stage, one additional *explicit* reasoning step is incrementally replaced by a *latent* reasoning step, denoted by a special `<latent>` token. This replacement starts from the first reasoning step and moves forward. For example, in Stage 1, the first explicit reasoning step becomes latent; in Stage 2, the first *and* second explicit steps become latent, and so on, until all `N` intermediate steps are latent in the final stage.
4. **Loss Function and Supervision:** Training is optimized using Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss. Critically, supervision is applied *only* to the remaining explicit reasoning steps and the final answer. Latent reasoning steps and the initial question tokens are masked out from the loss calculation. This design ensures that the model is penalized only for errors in its observable output, while simultaneously encouraging it to develop effective internal (latent) mechanisms for the masked-out reasoning steps. This approach differs from typical knowledge distillation as it doesn't force a strict alignment between latent states and explicit rationales, granting the model flexibility to internalize reasoning efficiently.

#### 3.3. Inference Process

During inference, after the question and initial image inputs, `N` `<latent>` tokens are appended to the input sequence. This configuration ensures that all intermediate reasoning is conducted fully within the latent space, without generating any explicit reasoning steps. The model directly proceeds to generate the final answer after completing its internal latent reasoning process. The model's operations outside this latent reasoning phase (e.g., generating the final answer) still follow a purely linguistic generation mode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks, M3CoT and ScienceQA, using Qwen2-VL-7B and Chameleon-7B backbones, demonstrate the superior performance and efficiency of IVT-LR.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Reasoning Accuracy

- **Overall Superiority:** IVT-LR consistently achieved the highest accuracy on both M3CoT and ScienceQA datasets, across both Qwen2-VL and Chameleon backbones. This indicates its robust ability to perform complex multimodal reasoning.
- **Significant Gains over Baselines:**
 - On **M3CoT**, IVT-LR with the Qwen2-VL backbone scored 71.8%, outperforming the strongest baseline (Chain-of-Focus) by a substantial 7.5% (64.3% -> 71.8%). With the Chameleon backbone, it achieved 41.8%, a 5% improvement over Chain-of-Focus (36.5% -> 41.8%).
 - Similar performance boosts were observed on the **ScienceQA** benchmark, where IVT-LR with Qwen2-VL reached 94.6% (compared to 91.2% for Chain-of-Focus) and with Chameleon achieved 64.0% (compared to 61.2% for Chain-of-Focus).
- These results demonstrate that by integrating textual and visual information effectively in the latent space, IVT-LR can achieve stronger multimodal reasoning capabilities compared to methods relying on explicit reasoning steps.

#### 4.2. Reasoning Efficiency

A crucial advantage of IVT-LR is its significantly enhanced inference efficiency, addressing one of the core motivations of the work.

- **Fewer Autoregressive Steps:** IVT-LR achieved a remarkable reduction in the number of autoregressive steps required for inference. For both backbones, it required only 10-11 autoregressive steps, which is approximately a **9x reduction** compared to most baselines that often needed 77 to 185 steps (e.g., Chain-of-Focus required 185.7 steps on M3CoT with Qwen2-VL). This reduction is a direct consequence of performing reasoning in the latent space, bypassing the need for explicit token generation.
- **Lower Inference Latency:** The reduction in autoregressive steps translated directly into substantial speed improvements.
 - With the Qwen2-VL backbone, IVT-LR's average inference time was approximately 0.65-0.67 seconds, making it **3 to 8 times faster** than other baselines (which ranged from 2.09s to 5.31s).
 - A similar significant speedup was observed with the Chameleon backbone, with IVT-LR taking ~1.13-1.14s compared to 2.56s to 6.12s for baselines.
- While No-CoT achieved slightly lower latency (around 0.35s) by completely sacrificing deep reasoning, IVT-LR delivered state-of-the-art accuracy at an inference speed only marginally longer, showcasing superior efficiency in a high-accuracy setting.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both latent text and latent vision components:

- **Removing Latent Text:** This led to a substantial accuracy drop (e.g., 19.63% on M3CoT for Qwen2-VL), underscoring the importance of hidden states in providing a compact, continuous representation for internalizing multi-step reasoning and mitigating error accumulation.
- **Removing Latent Vision:** This resulted in an even larger accuracy decrease (e.g., 25.19% on M3CoT for Qwen2-VL), highlighting the critical role of incorporating the most informative visual cues for precise multimodal reasoning and enabling the model to focus on relevant image regions.
- **Removing Both Latent Parts:** The combined removal (`w/o the whole latent part`) also showed a significant drop, affirming that the synergy between latent text and latent vision is crucial for IVT-LR's performance.

#### 4.4. In-depth Analysis

- **Length of Latent Vision:** Increasing the number of selected image embeddings (`k`) per step for latent vision led to a steady increase in accuracy. This suggests that providing richer visual cues is beneficial, and the step-wise adaptive selection mechanism allows for targeted and cumulative coverage of essential visual details.
- **Stages of Latent Reasoning:** Accuracy improved progressively as more reasoning steps were conducted in the latent space (from 1 to 3 latent stages). This indicates that latent representations offer a more robust reasoning mechanism than explicit language, especially for structured reasoning tasks like science and mathematics, which benefited most from additional latent tokens.
- **Attention Shift over Step-wise Embeddings:**
 - **Dynamic Attention Ratio:** In latent reasoning, the attention ratio (visual part to textual part) showed a clear downward trend, indicating an initial focus on latent vision followed by a gradual shift to latent text for deeper textual reasoning. This dynamic adaptation reflects enhanced visio-linguistic perception. Explicit reasoning, in contrast, showed a largely static and lower attention ratio for visual information, suggesting difficulty in effectively leveraging visual features amidst abundant textual information.
 - **Rising Attention Focus:** Latent reasoning exhibited a progressively increasing attention focus (measured by inverse entropy), meaning the model's attention became more concentrated and refined over steps, converging on critical cues. Explicit reasoning, however, showed lower and less changing attention focus, implying a diffuse distribution of attention over redundant information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Reasoning in the Dark" paper introduces a highly significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with profound implications for the design and application of MLLMs.

**Key Significance:**

- **Pioneering Unified Multimodal Latent Reasoning:** IVT-LR is the first framework to achieve truly unified multimodal latent reasoning, where *both* textual and visual information are processed implicitly within the latent space. This moves beyond prior efforts that focused on single-modal latent reasoning or explicit multimodal interactions, opening a new paradigm for how MLLMs can "think."
- **Unprecedented Efficiency Gains:** By operating "in the dark" and avoiding explicit generation of intermediate reasoning steps, IVT-LR drastically reduces inference latency (3-8x faster) and the number of autoregressive steps (9x fewer). This breakthrough efficiency makes complex multimodal reasoning practical for real-world applications requiring rapid responses, such as real-time agents, interactive systems, and large-scale deployments, where explicit CoT methods are often prohibitively slow.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Accuracy:** Beyond efficiency, IVT-LR simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on challenging VQA benchmarks. This demonstrates that implicit, latent reasoning can be not only faster but also more effective than explicit, text-heavy rationales, potentially because it avoids the biases and error propagation inherent in language-based alignment and allows for richer, continuous representation.
- **Reduced Annotation Burden:** The latent nature of IVT-LR's reasoning processes inherently lessens the dependence on labor-intensive, fine-grained vision-text annotations for intermediate reasoning steps. This makes training more scalable and accessible, reducing a major bottleneck in multimodal AI development.
- **New Insights into MLLM Cognition:** The in-depth attention analysis provides valuable insights into the internal working mechanisms of MLLMs during latent reasoning. The observed dynamic attention shifts and rising attention focus reveal a sophisticated, human-like problem-solving pattern where the model adaptively prioritizes information and refines its focus, offering a deeper understanding of MLLM "cognition."

**Potential Impact:**

- **Catalyst for Future MLLM Architectures:** IVT-LR's success will likely inspire a new wave of MLLM architectures and training methodologies that prioritize latent reasoning. Future models might be designed from the ground up to leverage continuous hidden states for complex multimodal interactions.
- **Broader Applications:** The efficiency gains make sophisticated multimodal reasoning accessible for a wider range of applications, including:
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Faster decision-making in real-time environments (e.g., robotics, self-driving cars).
 - **Interactive AI:** More responsive virtual assistants and educational tools that can reason over visual content.
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Accelerated analysis of scientific diagrams and data in fields like medicine and material science.
- **Advancement in AI Interpretability (indirectly):** While latent reasoning is "dark," the attention analysis showcases that methods *can* be developed to probe and understand these internal processes. This could lead to new tools for interpreting complex implicit models.
- **Inspiration for Multimodal Planning and Decision-Making:** The authors themselves highlight the potential for extending this approach beyond pure reasoning to broader sequential multimodal tasks, such as planning and complex decision-making in dynamic environments. This could pave the way for more intelligent and adaptable AI agents.

In conclusion, "Reasoning in the Dark" represents a significant step towards building more efficient, perceptive, and less annotation-dependent vision-language models. By masterfully uniting the power of multimodal interaction with the efficiency of latent space reasoning, it not only pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities but also offers a compelling vision for the future of AI.
