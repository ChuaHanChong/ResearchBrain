# Geometric Action Model for Robot Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Geometric Action Model (GAM) repurposes a pretrained Geometric Foundation Model (GFM) as a unified backbone for language-conditioned robot manipulation, integrating 3D perception, future geometry prediction, and action decoding. This approach improves policy robustness to environmental changes and achieves significantly faster inference speeds, up to 55 times quicker than diffusion-based baselines.

## Problem

- Existing generalist robot policies, like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily operate on 2D image frames, leaving crucial 3D geometry implicit and limiting generalization to varied camera viewpoints or robot states.
- Geometric Foundation Models (GFMs), while powerful, have largely been underutilized in robot policies, serving mainly as static feature extractors rather than for temporal prediction or action generation.
- Developing robust and efficient generalist robot manipulation policies requires a unified understanding of language, visual appearance, scene geometry, robot state, and physical dynamics for effective operation in the 3D world.

## Method

- The Geometric Action Model (**GAM**) splits a pretrained GFM into an observation encoder and a deeper feature propagation section, inserting a causal transformer directly into the GFM's latent space.
- A ==causal future predictor== module within the GFM's architecture jointly forecasts future latent geometric states and robot actions, conditioned on past context, language instructions, and robot proprioceptive state.
- Predicted future geometry and action tokens are propagated through the remaining GFM layers and decoded by lightweight heads into executable action chunks and action-aligned future depth maps, enabling single-pass, real-time inference.

## Results

- GAM achieved competitive success rates on LIBERO (**97.6%**) and demonstrated superior robustness on the challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, with only a **12.1** percentage point performance drop compared to **16.1**-**60.9%** for baselines, especially under camera perturbations (**83.1%** success rate).
- Real-world evaluations on four manipulation tasks showed that GAM substantially outperformed baseline policies and maintained robust performance under out-of-domain camera perturbations, demonstrating successful simulation-to-real transfer of resilience.
- The model achieved low inference latency of approximately **6.9** ms per pass (~145 Hz), making it up to 55 times faster than diffusion-based baselines, and operates with a relatively compact model size of **1.4** billion parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deeply integrating 3D geometric priors by repurposing a GFM's full multi-layer structure for unified perception, temporal world modeling, and action prediction significantly enhances a robot policy's robustness to environmental variations.
> - Embedding a causal transformer within the latent space of a GFM allows for the joint prediction of future actions and geometry, fostering a tight interaction between action planning and spatial reasoning.
> - This architectural design facilitates highly efficient robot control, offering substantial reductions in inference latency and model size compared to contemporary foundation-model-scale robot learning systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the long-standing objective in robotics of developing generalist manipulation policies capable of following natural language instructions and interacting with diverse objects across various environments. This requires a unified understanding of language, visual appearance, scene geometry, robot state, and physical dynamics.

Recent advancements in this area have leveraged large-scale foundation models. Vision-language-action models (VLAs) adapt pre-trained vision-language models to map visual and linguistic inputs to robot actions. Similarly, video world-action models (WAMs) utilize pre-trained video generation models to jointly predict future frames and actions. While these approaches have demonstrated language-conditioned manipulation capabilities, they primarily operate on 2D image frames or 2D-derived latent spaces. This 2D focus leaves 3D cues such as depth, scale, and occlusion implicitly handled, which can limit generalization, especially in scenarios involving changes in robot initial state or camera viewpoint.

To overcome the limitations of 2D-centric methods, some research has incorporated 3D geometric information. Approaches have included learning policies directly from explicit 3D observations like point clouds, which can improve generalization but often require task-specific encoders. With the emergence of Geometric Foundation Models (GFMs) that infer dense 3D structures from multi-view images, some studies have attempted to transfer pre-trained geometric priors into VLA policies. This typically involves distilling selected GFM features into VLA backbones or attaching lightweight action heads to GFM features. However, these methods often use GFMs primarily as static feature extractors, without fully repurposing their multi-layer geometric structure for temporal prediction or action generation.

The Geometric Action Model (GAM) differentiates itself by directly repurposing a pre-trained GFM as a shared foundation for perception, temporal prediction, and action decoding. This integration allows GAM to jointly predict future 3D geometry and robot actions within the GFM's intrinsic geometric representation space, aiming to address the spatial ambiguities inherent in 2D-based foundation models and more thoroughly exploit 3D geometric understanding for robust manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a language-conditioned robot manipulation policy that exhibits improved accuracy, robustness, and efficiency compared to existing foundation-model-scale baselines. This goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of current approaches:

- **Addressing the 2D limitation of current models**: Existing vision-language-action (VLA) and video world-action models (WAM) primarily operate on 2D image data or 2D-derived latent spaces. This 2D perspective implicitly handles crucial 3D geometric information such as depth, scale, and occlusion. Consequently, these models often exhibit limited generalization capabilities, particularly when faced with changes in environmental conditions, camera viewpoints, or robot initial states, which are common in real-world scenarios requiring contact-rich manipulation. The objective is to explicitly integrate 3D geometric reasoning.
- **Leveraging Geometric Foundation Models (GFMs) more effectively**: While Geometric Foundation Models (GFMs) provide rich 3D priors by inferring dense 3D structures from multi-view images, current methods often utilize them as static feature extractors. This underutilizes the GFM's inherent multi-layer geometric structure, missing opportunities to integrate it deeply into the policy's temporal and action-generating processes. The motivation is to repurpose the GFM not just for static perception, but as an active substrate for dynamic world modeling and action prediction.
- **Unifying perception, temporal prediction, and action decoding**: A robust generalist manipulation model requires a cohesive understanding of language, visual appearance, scene geometry, robot state, and physical dynamics. The current fragmentation, where geometric understanding might be an auxiliary feature or a separate head, prevents a truly unified reasoning process. The objective is to combine temporal world modeling, latent feature-space prediction, and a geometric foundation-model substrate into a single shared-backbone architecture. This integration is hypothesized to inherently incorporate geometric world dynamics into robot policies.
- **Improving efficiency and real-world applicability**: Large foundation models can be computationally intensive, leading to high inference latency and large model sizes, which are challenges for real-time robot control. A more integrated and streamlined architecture that can achieve high performance with reduced computational cost and model size is a significant motivation for practical robotic deployment.

In summary, the research aims to build a more accurate, robust, and efficient robot policy by developing a novel architecture that directly embeds language-conditioned temporal world modeling and action decoding within a pre-trained GFM, thereby fully exploiting its rich 3D geometric priors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Geometric Action Model (GAM) is designed to repurpose a pre-trained Geometric Foundation Model (GFM) as a unified system for perception, future-state prediction, and action decoding. The methodology is structured into three sequential stages within the GFM's architecture, followed by a multi-task training strategy.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Geometric Foundation Models (GFM)

GAM builds upon existing GFMs, such as DA3-Giant or VGGT, which are feed-forward transformers mapping multi-view RGB images to dense 3D geometry. A GFM processes a sequence of V views, tokenizing each view into camera and patch tokens. These tokens are then processed by a stack of transformer blocks, employing both frame-wise (within-view) and global (cross-view) attention to fuse information. Dense geometry is typically decoded from intermediate hidden states using an auxiliary head like DPT.

#### 3.2. GAM's Three Sequential Stages

1. **Observation Encoder:**
 The initial stage of GAM repurposes the shallow layers of the pre-trained GFM. A split layer, denoted as $L_s$, divides the GFM transformer stack into an encoder and a decoder. The shallow layers, $E_{\le L_s} = f^{(L_s)} \circ \dots \circ f^{(1)}$, function as the observation encoder. For each timestep $t'$ within the policy's context window, this encoder takes multi-view RGB observations $o_{t'}$ and transforms them into a sequence of geometric latent states $Z^{(L_s)}_{t'}$. The selection of $L_s$ is critical; it must be deep enough to extract meaningful visual features but also shallow enough to ensure that subsequent predicted future states can be decoded into geometry by the GFM's depth heads.

2. **Causal Future Predictor:**
 A causal transformer, $g_{\phi}$, is inserted at the split layer $L_s$ to perform temporal prediction. This predictor forecasts future latent geometric states conditioned on current and past observations, as well as task instructions, proprioceptive states, and action history.
 - **Inputs to the Predictor**:
 - Latent geometric tokens $Z^{(L_s)}_{t'}$ from the observation encoder.
 - Proprioceptive states $s_{t'}$ (robot's joint configuration and end-effector pose) and previous actions $a_{t'-1}$ are embedded into tokens $p_{t'}$ and $q_{t'}$ using lightweight projection layers $\psi_s$ and $\psi_a$, respectively.
 - Natural language task instruction $\ell$ is encoded into language tokens $L_{\ell}$ using a pre-trained text encoder (e.g., T5).
 - **Token Block Formation**: A per-timestep token block $U_{t'} = [p_{t'}; q_{t'}; Z^{(L_s)}_{t'}]$ is formed.
 - **Processing**: The full input sequence $X = [L_{\ell}; U_{t'-H+1}; \dots; U_{t'}]$ is fed into the causal transformer. This transformer employs block-causal self-attention, a mechanism designed to incorporate past and present context while strictly preventing any future information leakage.
 - **Outputs from Predictor**: At the final layer of $g_{\phi}$, the hidden states corresponding to geometry slots predict future latent geometric tokens, $\tilde{Z}^{(L_s)}_{t'+1}$. Simultaneously, the hidden state from a designated previous-action slot is projected to produce a predicted next action token, $\tilde{a}_{t'}$. This joint forecasting of action and geometric latents within the same layer ensures a tight interaction between action planning and spatial representations. This design allows the pre-trained GFM to acquire language-conditioned temporal world modeling with minimal architectural modifications, as only $g_{\phi}$ needs to learn how to fuse the various input modalities.

3. **Feature Propagation and Action Decoding:**
 Following the causal future predictor, the predicted future geometric tokens $\tilde{Z}^{(L_s)}_{t'+1}$ and the action token $\tilde{a}_{t'}$ are propagated through the remaining deep layers of the GFM, $D_{>L_s} = f^{(M)} \circ \dots \circ f^{(L_s+1)}$. The single action token $\tilde{a}_{t'}$ is replicated for each view and appended to its corresponding geometry token sequence. The GFM's global attention layers in these deeper blocks are modified with an extended causal mask to maintain the integrity of future prediction.
 - **Action Decoding**: A lightweight action head $h_{act}$ aggregates the action tokens over the context window to regress the final executable action chunk $\hat{a}_{t'}$. These action chunks typically consist of a sequence of delta-pose or joint commands.
 - **Geometry Decoding**: The original GFM depth head $h_{depth}$ decodes the processed geometry tokens into action-aligned future depth maps $\tilde{D}_{t'+1}$. This repurposes the GFM's deep blocks, originally trained for static 3D reconstruction, to decode the world model's predicted future states.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

- **Training Objective**: GAM is trained end-to-end using a multi-task loss function: $L_{total} = \lambda_{act}L_{act} + \lambda_{feat}L_{feat} + \lambda_{depth}L_{depth}$.
 - $L_{act}$: An $\ell_1$ regression loss between the decoded action chunk $\hat{a}_{t'}$ and the expert action $a_{t'}$.
 - $L_{feat}$: Anchors the predictor $g_{\phi}$ to temporal geometric transitions by aligning the predicted future geometric tokens $\tilde{Z}^{(L_s)}_{t'+1}$ with the actual next frame's geometric tokens $Z^{(L_s)}_{t'+1}$, extracted from a frozen GFM.
 - $L_{depth}$: Grounds the predicted future geometry by supervising the decoded depth $\tilde{D}_{t'+1}$ against ground-truth future depth $D_{t'+1}$, using scale-invariant and gradient-matching penalties.
- **Implementation Details**: The model uses a context horizon $H=4$ for pre-training and $H=1$ for post-training. It predicts action chunks of $C=8$ steps. GAM is pre-trained on a large dataset of single-arm robot trajectories (RoboCasa365, MimicGen, OpenX-Embodiment), then fine-tuned on specific benchmarks.
- **Inference**: During inference, historical context is maintained online using key-value caching. This allows each step to process only the new observation and previous action in a single feed-forward pass, contributing to efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, robustness, and efficiency of the Geometric Action Model (GAM) across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance and Robustness in Simulation

- **LIBERO Benchmark**: GAM achieved competitive success rates on the standard LIBERO benchmark, matching or slightly exceeding other foundation-model-scale baselines where performance is often saturated.
- **LIBERO-Plus Benchmark**: On the more challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, which introduces controlled environmental perturbations, GAM consistently outperformed competing baselines. It demonstrated a notable improvement in the camera-perturbation setting, with a 9.7 percentage point increase in success rate. This gain suggests that GAM's end-to-end integration of geometric priors is effective for robust manipulation under varied viewing conditions.
- **Difficulty Levels**: Further analysis on LIBERO-Plus revealed that GAM maintains higher success rates than all baselines across every level of camera perturbation difficulty, including the strongest perturbations.
- **RoboCasa-Kitchen**: In the RoboCasa-Kitchen simulation benchmark, GAM achieved an average success rate of 69.4%, outperforming existing baselines, including Cosmos Policy.

#### 4.2. Performance in Real-World Scenarios

- **Real-World Tasks**: GAM was evaluated on four real-world manipulation tasks, including "Pick and Place" and "Stack milk and cube." It substantially outperformed baseline models such as $\pi_{0.5}$ and Spatial Forcing.
- **Out-of-Domain Robustness**: In real-world camera-perturbation settings (out-of-domain conditions), GAM maintained robustness, whereas other baselines exhibited a significant drop in performance. This indicates that the simulated robustness translates to physical execution.

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Model Size

- **Inference Latency**: GAM demonstrated the lowest inference latency among all baselines, requiring only 6.9 ms for a single feed-forward pass. This translates to an approximate control rate of 145 Hz, making it up to 55 times faster than the diffusion-based Cosmos Policy (382.4 ms). This speed is attributed to its single-pass prediction mechanism, which avoids the multi-step denoising inherent in diffusion policies.
- **Model Size**: GAM utilizes a total of 1.4 billion parameters, with approximately 983.2 million trainable parameters. This is substantially smaller than many VLM-based and video-diffusion-based baselines, such as OpenVLA-OFT (7B parameters) and $\pi_{0.5}$ (3.3B parameters). The compact size results from repurposing a pre-trained geometric backbone as the shared substrate.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Post-training Component Analysis**:
 - Pre-training was identified as crucial for robustness, with its omission severely degrading performance on LIBERO-Plus.
 - With a pre-trained backbone, removing the future-depth loss ($L_{depth}$) or future-feature loss ($L_{feat}$) had minimal impact, suggesting the geometric dynamics were already well-encoded. However, without pre-training, these losses provided strong geometric supervision and substantially improved robustness.
 - A context horizon of $H=1$ was found to be sufficient and more robust than longer histories, consistent with prior observations regarding spurious correlations from extended contexts.
- **Split Layer ($L_s$) Selection**: The optimal split layer for the causal future predictor was found to be $L_s=12$. Inserting the predictor too early ($L_s=0$) or too late ($L_s \ge 27$) led to significant performance degradation. This indicates that forecasted tokens require sufficient interaction through deep GFM layers to integrate properly into the 3D geometric prior.
- **Direct Action-Token Ablation**: Passing the action token through the deep geometric decoder provided an additional performance improvement, particularly on LIBERO-Plus Object tasks. This suggests that the GFM's deep layers contribute to refining the action representation, especially under camera perturbations.

#### 4.5. Attention Analysis

Visualizations of action token attention maps indicated that several intermediate GFM layers attend to task-relevant regions, with salient areas around manipulated objects and potential contact points. This finding qualitatively supports the utility of mid-level geometric representations for action decoding and refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Geometric Action Model (GAM) represents an advancement in robot policy learning by addressing limitations of existing approaches, particularly in integrating 3D geometric understanding and temporal reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization**: By directly repurposing a Geometric Foundation Model (GFM) as a unified backbone for perception, future state prediction, and action decoding, GAM demonstrates improved robustness to environmental perturbations, especially camera viewpoint changes. This is evident in its superior performance on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark and real-world experiments under out-of-domain conditions. This enhanced generalization is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unstructured real-world environments, reducing the need for extensive domain-specific data collection.

- **Improved Efficiency for Real-Time Control**: GAM achieves significantly faster inference speeds (up to 55 times faster than diffusion-based baselines) and a smaller model size. This efficiency makes it more viable for real-time robot control applications, where low latency is critical for reactive behavior and safe operation. The ability to operate at a high control frequency (approximately 145 Hz) could enable more dynamic and precise manipulation tasks.

- **Unified Architecture for Robot Intelligence**: The proposed architecture unifies typically disparate components—perception, world modeling, and action generation—within a single, shared geometric backbone. This integration allows for a more cohesive understanding of the physical world, where geometric dynamics are inherently linked to future actions and scene evolution. Such a unified approach could streamline the development of more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems.

- **Leveraging Pre-trained Geometric Priors**: The work effectively demonstrates how the rich 3D geometric priors learned by GFMs can be thoroughly exploited for robot control, moving beyond their use as static feature extractors. This indicates a promising direction for future research in robotics, where specialized foundation models can be adapted and integrated in novel ways to imbue policies with fundamental physical understanding.

- **Foundation for Future Research**: While GAM's language reasoning is currently bounded by a frozen text encoder, its modular design provides a clear path for future extensions. Integrating larger language models or external reasoning modules could further enhance its language understanding and commonsense capabilities. This research lays a foundation for developing more capable generalist robots that can operate effectively in complex, dynamic environments with natural language instructions.

In conclusion, GAM contributes to the development of more capable and deployable robot policies by offering a framework that is both robust to real-world variations and efficient for practical application, setting a precedent for leveraging geometric understanding in robotic learning.
