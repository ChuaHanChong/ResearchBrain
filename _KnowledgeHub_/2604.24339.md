# See Further, Think Deeper: Advancing VLM's Reasoning Ability with Low-level Visual Cues and Reflection

> [!summary] Summary
> ForeSight is an RL-trained interleaved multimodal reasoning framework that lets a VLM autonomously call low-level visual tools (Canny edges, zoom-in, color transform) and then verify its draft answer by masking the predicted region and re-inspecting the image. Trained with SFT cold start plus GRPO, ForeSight-7B lifts Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 29.68 IoU and 19.27 F1 on the new CG-SalBench odd-one-out benchmark.

## Problem

- Interleaved multimodal CoT methods like DeepEyes and DriveAgent-R1 give tools for region zooming or depth/detection, but ignore low-level cues such as edges and color contrast.
- State-of-the-art VLMs fail at seemingly trivial saliency tasks, missing obvious odd-one-out anomalies in color, shape, or orientation, because text-centric reasoning neglects fine-grained visual features.
- Existing VLM reasoning is open-loop: once an answer is produced it is never re-checked against visual evidence, and prior reflection like GThinker stays text-centric.

## Method

- **Low-level visual toolkit**: parameter-free ==Canny edge detection== and ==color-difference amplification== plus a bbox-parameterized ==Zoom-In tool==, invoked autonomously mid-reasoning with returned images interleaved into the chain of thought.
- ==Mask-based visual reflection==: the draft answer's box is masked out of the image and fed back; any remaining salient object signals an error and triggers a revised answer, up to two iterations.
- ==V2-CoT cold start== plus ==GRPO==: Qwen2.5-VL-72B annotates 1,425 tool-free and tool-dependent reasoning-and-reflection traces for SFT, then GRPO optimizes format, IoU, F1, tool-use, and verification-consistency rewards.

## Results

- On CG-SalBench, ForeSight-7B reaches **62.24 IoU** and **84.24 F1**, near Qwen2.5-VL-72B's **66.00** IoU, beating its **82.70** F1, and exceeding best closed-source grounding (Doubao-Seed-1.6, **47.66** IoU); GPT-5 scores only **7.85** IoU.
- Training ablation: tools alone or reflection alone give mixed results (F1 **57.19** and **45.96** versus **51.40**), but combining both jumps F1 to **84.24**; SFT+RL beats SFT-only (**72.34**) and RL-only (**74.94**).
- Inference-only tools or reflection on closed-source models yield inconsistent gains (max **+15.27** IoU); off-task transfer adds **+2.51** RefCOCO and **+37.8** MME but loses **0.97** on MMBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Classical low-level image operators like edge maps and color transforms remain useful perception aids for VLMs, recovering fine-grained cues that scaling alone fails to supply.
> - Visual self-verification can be done by perturbing the input, masking the claimed answer and re-looking, turning reasoning into a closed perception loop rather than text-only rethinking.
> - Tool access without training is unreliable; models must learn when to invoke tools and reflection, with components synergizing only when jointly trained via SFT cold start and RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shifted the focus from basic understanding to complex reasoning capabilities. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a key method for enhancing LLM and VLM reasoning, particularly in tool-augmented and reflective reasoning tasks. While RL integration has improved VLM performance on complex tasks, existing reasoning processes in VLMs are largely confined to the language modality. This text-only approach to visual tasks contrasts with human visual reasoning, which inherently involves both visual and language modalities, including the active acquisition of additional visual information when necessary.

Previous studies, such as the interleaved Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (i-MCoT) paradigm, have attempted to support step-by-step cross-modal reasoning. Examples include DeepEyes, which enables "thinking with images" through end-to-end RL, and DriveAgent-R1, which introduces a Hybrid-Thinking framework. However, these i-MCoT approaches have exhibited two main limitations: (1) an insufficient incorporation of low-level visual information and (2) the absence of an effective visual feedback mechanism.

Regarding the first limitation, research like SalBench has identified that state-of-the-art large VLMs perform inadequately in detecting salient, intuitive low-level visual anomalies, tasks often considered simple for humans. Studies such as VAT have demonstrated that the introduction of low-level visual information, including edge contours or binarized maps, can improve VLM performance on visual tasks. While DeepEyes and DriveAgent-R1 integrated image tools, their focus has primarily been on region scaling and high-level image understanding tasks, rather than leveraging fundamental low-level visual details.

Concerning the second limitation, most existing VLMs operate on an open-loop reasoning model, where answers are derived without subsequent reflection or adjustment based on visual feedback. This contrasts with human cognitive processes, which involve iterative self-examination and refinement. Although GThinker introduced a reflection mechanism guided by visual cues, its reasoning process remained predominantly text-centric, limiting truly interleaved multimodal reflection.

This work aims to address these identified limitations by proposing a framework that integrates low-level visual cues and a mask-based visual feedback mechanism, driven by reinforcement learning, to enable more human-like, closed-loop, and perceptually grounded reasoning in VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the reasoning ability of Vision-Language Models by equipping them with enhanced perceptual capabilities and reflective self-correction mechanisms. This overarching goal is driven by two specific limitations identified in existing VLM reasoning paradigms:

**Motivation 1: Addressing the Lack of Sufficient Low-Level Visual Information**
Existing VLMs often rely on textual reasoning for visual tasks, which can lead to the neglect of fine-grained visual features. This deficiency becomes more pronounced in extended reasoning processes and for tasks requiring detailed visual discrimination, such as identifying subtle anomalies. The SalBench dataset highlighted this problem by demonstrating that even advanced VLMs struggle with low-level perceptual tasks that are straightforward for humans. This gap arises because current VLM tools, even when integrated, often focus on higher-level understanding rather than fundamental visual characteristics like edges or color differences.
- **Objective:** To enable VLMs to "See Further" by introducing a set of low-level visual tools. These tools are designed to integrate essential fine-grained visual information directly into the reasoning chain, allowing the model to actively scrutinize visual details when necessary, thus mitigating the oversight of critical visual features.

**Motivation 2: Incorporating Effective Visual Feedback for Reflection**
Most current VLM reasoning operates in an open-loop manner, proceeding directly from reasoning to an answer without mechanisms for self-evaluation or dynamic adjustment based on visual evidence. Once an initial answer is generated, the model typically does not revisit or modify it, limiting its capacity for self-correction and iterative refinement. This unidirectional process restricts the model's intelligence compared to human reasoning, which frequently involves reflection and correction.
- **Objective:** To enable VLMs to "Think Deeper" by integrating a mask-based visual feedback mechanism into the reasoning process. This mechanism aims to transform the open-loop "reasoning → answer" flow into a closed-loop "reasoning → answer → visual feedback → reasoning → answer..." cycle. By leveraging visual feedback, the model can re-examine its initial answers, diagnose potential errors, and update its predictions, thereby enhancing accuracy and robustness.

**Overarching Objective:**
To propose a unified multimodal interleaved reasoning framework, named ForeSight, that combines these two mechanisms. This framework is driven by Reinforcement Learning (RL), allowing the VLM to autonomously decide when and how to invoke visual tools and engage in visual reflection, with the ultimate goal of maximizing the accuracy of the final answer.

**Evaluation Objective:**
To rigorously evaluate the proposed framework, the researchers also aimed to construct a novel dataset, Character and Grounding SalBench (CG-SalBench), based on the existing SalBench dataset. This new benchmark requires models not only to identify anomalous visual characteristics but also to provide precise bounding box localizations, thereby pushing the boundaries of saliency detection evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces ForeSight, a unified framework for multimodal interleaved reasoning designed to enhance VLM capabilities through low-level visual cues and reflection. The methodology involves a two-stage training paradigm and the development of a specialized evaluation dataset.

#### 3.1. ForeSight Framework Overview

ForeSight enables a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to dynamically interact with its visual input and reasoning process. Given a question and an image, the model performs textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. At each step, it autonomously decides whether sufficient information is available for an accurate answer. If not, it invokes specific low-level visual tools to process and enhance the image, then continues reasoning with the updated visual input. This iterative process continues until an answer is deemed correct or a maximum tool invocation limit is reached. A key component is a visual reflection mechanism, invoked after a draft answer is generated, to reassess the output and reasoning trajectory.

#### 3.2. Visual Tools and Visual Reflection

The framework integrates a visual toolkit to acquire low-level visual information and a visual reflection module for self-evaluation.

- **Visual Tools:**
 - **Canny Tool:** Implements the Canny edge detection algorithm to identify object boundaries and strong intensity gradients, providing explicit structural cues for contour-aware reasoning.
 - **Zoom-In Tool:** Allows the model to adaptively zoom into relevant image regions, extracting more fine-grained details when available visual information is insufficient.
 - **Color Tool:** Amplifies color differences within images to provide enhanced cues, improving the model's grounding capability by strengthening local image features.

- **Visual Reflection:** This module transforms the open-loop reasoning into a closed-loop process. After the VLM produces a `draft_answer` (containing predicted bounding box `I_ans` and answer `T_ans`), the visual reflection module masks the `I_ans` region on the original image `I` to create a `masked image I'(k)`. The VLM then re-evaluates its reasoning and the masked image to `verify` the `draft_answer`. If new salient regions are detected in the masked image, or if the initial answer is deemed incorrect, the model refines its prediction and generates an updated `T_ans(k)` and `I_ans(k)`, forming an iterative self-correction loop (maximum 2 iterations in experiments). This mechanism allows the model to leverage explicit visual signals for cross-validation and iterative refinement.

#### 3.3. V$^2$-CoT Data Construction for Cold Start (Stage 1: SFT)

To provide a foundational understanding for the VLM, a high-quality dataset, Visual Cues and Visual Feedback Chain (V$^2$-CoT), is constructed for supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This automated process involves three steps:
1. **Data Partitioning:** The CG-SalBench training data is divided into "tool-free" samples (where a Qwen2.5-VL-7B model performs well without tools) and "tool-dependent" samples (where a Qwen2.5-VL-32B model shows improved performance with tools). This partitioning aims to balance computational efficiency and reasoning reliability.
2. **CoT Annotation Generation:** A larger model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, is used to generate structured Chain-of-Thought annotations for both data types, with integrated visual reflection.
3. **Data Cleaning and Refinement:** Manual rule-based cleaning is applied to filter out low-quality annotated samples, resulting in a balanced and high-quality SFT dataset (596 tool-free and 829 tool-dependent samples). This dataset teaches the model when to use textual reasoning and when to invoke visual tools.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning for V$^2$-CoT (Stage 2: RL)

Reinforcement learning is employed to enable dynamic and autonomous tool utilization. The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is used. A comprehensive reward function `R(τ)` is designed to guide the model in generating structured, visually aligned, and semantically accurate reasoning trajectories `τ`:
- **Format Consistency Reward (R_fmt):** Encourages coherent inference trajectories.
- **IoU Reward (R_IoU):** Quantifies the Intersection over Union to enhance localization accuracy.
- **F1 Reward (R_F1):** Measures the F1 score between generated text and ground truth to improve recognition accuracy.
- **Tool Reward (R_tc):** Provides positive rewards for correct tool usage that leads to accurate answers, and negative rewards for tool overuse or invalid usage.
- **Verification Reward (R_ver):** Ensures the correctness of visual reflection by aligning the model's verification outcome with the discrepancy between the draft answer, final answer, and ground truth.

#### 3.5. CG-SalBench Dataset

A new dataset, Odd-One-Out Character and Grounding Saliency Benchmark (CG-SalBench), is constructed. This dataset extends the existing SalBench, which assesses low-level visual saliency perception. CG-SalBench requires models to identify the distinctive visual character of an "Odd-One-Out" object (e.g., color, orientation, size, shape) and provide its precise bounding box. It includes both synthetic (P3, 2589 images) and real-world (O3, 2001 images) data. The dataset is partitioned into a training set (3929 samples) and a test set (661 samples: 252 natural, 409 synthetic).

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The ForeSight model is built upon Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Training is performed on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs using a two-stage pipeline: SFT for 10 epochs on 1425 samples, followed by RL for 1 epoch on 3929 samples using GRPO. Bfloat16 precision, a maximum sequence length of 12800 tokens, and a maximum pixel count of 1003520 per image are used.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Metrics

Model performance on CG-SalBench is evaluated using:
- **IoU (Intersection over Union):** Primary metric for grounding quality (spatial overlap of bounding boxes).
- **Precision and Recall:** Used for Odd-One-Out Character Identification.
- **F1 Score:** Harmonic mean of Precision and Recall, providing a balanced measure of identification performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the ForeSight framework across various evaluation settings.

#### 4.1. Performance on CG-SalBench Benchmark

The ForeSight-7B model was compared against several state-of-the-art open-source and closed-source VLMs on the CG-SalBench dataset.
- ForeSight-7B achieved an IoU of 62.24% and an F1 score of 84.24%.
- Compared to its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ForeSight-7B showed substantial improvements, with a 29.68% increase in IoU and a 19.27% rise in F1 score.
- The 7B ForeSight model's IoU was close to that of the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (66.00%) and surpassed it in F1 score (84.24% vs. 82.70%). This indicates that the architectural design and training methodology effectively compensate for a smaller model size.
- ForeSight-7B also outperformed all other baseline models in grounding (IoU), including closed-source models like Doubao-Seed-1.6 (47.66%), with an IoU gain of over 14%.
- While some closed-source models like GPT-5 exhibited high F1/Recall, their IoU scores were considerably low (e.g., GPT-5 at 7.85%), suggesting poor localization capabilities. ForeSight-7B maintained high and balanced scores across all metrics (IoU, F1, Precision, Recall), indicating robust and accurate visual understanding.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study on Visual Tools and Visual Reflection

An ablation study investigated the contribution of visual tools and visual reflection components.
- **Inference-time-only evaluation:** Testing closed-source models by merely providing tools or reflection capabilities during inference, without specialized training, showed limited and inconsistent improvements in IoU and character identification metrics. For example, some models showed minor IoU gains (up to 15.27%), but others did not, and performance on F1/Precision/Recall was mixed. This suggests that simply making tools available is insufficient; explicit training on how and when to utilize them effectively is necessary.
- **Training component evaluation:** Training ForeSight-7B with different combinations of components confirmed their complementary roles.
 - Using only low-level tools during training improved F1 from 51.40% to 57.19%.
 - Using only visual reflection improved Recall from 84.25% to 93.18%.
 - Combining both low-level tools and visual reflection in training yielded the highest performance across all metrics (IoU: 62.24%, F1: 84.24%), demonstrating that tools enable active perception while reflection supports error correction.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Training Stages

The study also validated the two-stage training paradigm (SFT followed by RL).
- Compared to an SFT-only model (IoU 57.47%, F1 72.34%) and an RL-only model (IoU 52.80%, F1 74.94%), the full SFT + RL pipeline achieved the highest performance (IoU 62.24%, F1 84.24%). This result confirms that an initial "cold start" through SFT is crucial for establishing foundational skills, which are then effectively refined by reinforcement learning for optimal performance.

#### 4.4. Generalization Performance Analysis

To assess generalizability beyond the primary task, ForeSight was evaluated on additional grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) and Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks (MME, MMBench).
- ForeSight demonstrated improvements across all grounding benchmarks compared to a baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B* model, with gains of +2.51% (RefCOCO), +1.55% (RefCOCO+), and +2.03% (RefCOCOg).
- On the MME VQA benchmark, ForeSight showed an improvement of +37.8.
- A slight decline was observed on the MMBench test (-0.97%).
- These results suggest that the training paradigm enhances fundamental vision and complex reasoning capabilities, with benefits extending beyond basic perception grounding to visual reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses critical limitations in existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically the inadequate incorporation of low-level visual information and the absence of effective visual feedback mechanisms during reasoning. The proposed ForeSight framework offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

**Advancement in VLM Reasoning:** By integrating low-level visual tools, ForeSight enables VLMs to "See Further," allowing for the active acquisition and utilization of fine-grained visual cues. This capability is crucial for tasks requiring precise visual discrimination, where subtle details might otherwise be overlooked by text-centric reasoning processes.

**Introduction of Closed-Loop Reflection:** The incorporation of a mask-based visual feedback mechanism allows VLMs to "Think Deeper" by transforming the traditional open-loop reasoning into a closed-loop, self-correction cycle. This enables models to dynamically re-examine, verify, and update their initial answers based on visual evidence, mimicking human-like reflection and iterative refinement. This mechanism has the potential to significantly enhance the robustness and accuracy of VLM outputs.

**Competitive Performance with Smaller Models:** The study demonstrates that a 7B parameter model, when effectively trained with the ForeSight framework, can achieve performance comparable to or even surpassing much larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and leading closed-source systems on specific tasks like saliency detection and grounding. This suggests an alternative path to improving VLM capabilities beyond merely scaling up model size, offering a more resource-efficient approach.

**Autonomous Tool Use and Dynamic Reasoning:** The RL-driven framework empowers VLMs to autonomously decide when and how to invoke visual tools and reflection mechanisms. This moves beyond fixed reasoning paradigms, allowing the model to adapt its strategy based on the perceived complexity or ambiguity of the visual input, leading to more flexible and intelligent reasoning.

**New Benchmark for Saliency and Grounding:** The introduction of the CG-SalBench dataset provides a valuable new benchmark for evaluating low-level visual saliency perception combined with precise object grounding and character identification. This dataset can facilitate future research into fine-grained visual reasoning and the development of more perceptually aware VLMs.

**Implications for Human-Aligned AI:** The work highlights the importance of equipping AI models with human-inspired perceptual and cognitive processes. By integrating low-level visual perception and reflective self-correction, ForeSight contributes to the development of VLMs that exhibit more robust, accurate, and human-aligned visual intelligence.

**Potential for Broader Applications:** The observed generalization performance on standard grounding and VQA benchmarks suggests that the principles of ForeSight could be extended to a wider range of visual tasks. This could lead to improved performance in applications requiring detailed visual analysis, complex reasoning, and reliable decision-making in various real-world scenarios.
