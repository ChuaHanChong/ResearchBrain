# SoftNav: Injecting 3D Scene Tokens into VLMs for Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Zhejiang University researchers developed SoftNav, a method for embodied navigation that directly injects 3D scene entity tokens from a dedicated 3D scene encoder into a frozen vision-language model. This approach improves navigation success rates by 7.5 percentage points on HM3D-OVON and demonstrates robust zero-shot generalization across various navigation benchmarks and to real-world robotic platforms.

## Problem

- VLM-based embodied navigation suffers from an information bottleneck when rich, continuous 3D scene information is serialized into discrete text.
- Existing VLM approaches often lose critical geometric, semantic, and spatial-relational context by compressing 3D features into textual representations.
- The performance cost associated with text serialization in VLM-based navigation has largely remained unquantified.

## Method

- **SoftNav** directly injects entity-level 3D continuous representations (query embeddings from a ==PQ3D encoder==) into a VLM's hidden space as ==soft tokens==.
- It employs a lightweight ==MLP projector== to align the 768-dimensional query space of PQ3D with the VLM's 2048-dimensional hidden space, keeping large backbones frozen.
- Visual memory tokens, soft 3D tokens, and textual navigation prompts are combined into the VLM's input sequence for joint ==multimodal attention==.

## Results

- SoftNav achieved state-of-the-art results on HM3D-OVON, with a **74.2%** success rate and **33.9%** Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) on val_seen, an improvement of **7.5 pp** SR and **9.2 pp** SPL over text serialization baselines.
- The policy demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization, achieving **67.2%** SR on GOAT-Bench val_seen and **47.2%** sub-task SR on SG3D without retraining.
- It successfully transferred to a real-world Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, achieving a **63.3%** success rate in diverse environments, including outdoor scenes, without domain adaptation or fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Serializing rich 3D scene information into text for VLM consumption creates a significant "representation gap," causing a measurable performance penalty in navigation.
> - Direct, continuous embedding-level transfer of structured 3D scene information through soft tokens effectively preserves geometric, semantic, and spatial-relational context for the VLM.
> - A highly parameter-efficient training strategy, using only a lightweight projector and LoRA adapters, enables state-of-the-art performance and strong zero-shot generalization with minimal data and computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation, particularly goal-directed tasks where an agent must locate a specified target in an unfamiliar 3D environment, is a central challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics. The field has seen progress driven by two primary paradigms:
- **Modular approaches**: These decompose navigation into distinct perception and planning modules. Examples include methods that use semantic maps (e.g., SemExp, PONI) or unified 3D perception encoders (e.g., MTU3D with PQ3D) to process environmental information. These approaches often use specialized learned policies for decision-making.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based methods**: These leverage the reasoning capabilities of large pre-trained language models, often combined with visual information, for navigation tasks. Recent benchmarks have expanded goal specifications, moving from fixed object vocabularies (e.g., OVON) to multi-modal goals (e.g., GOAT-Bench) and scene-graph-grounded targets (e.g., SG3D), increasing the demand for robust 3D understanding integrated with VLM reasoning.

A significant challenge in VLM-based navigation is how 3D scene information is conveyed to the decision-making VLM. Existing approaches typically serialize structured 3D information into text formats, such as coordinates, scene graph strings, or natural language summaries (e.g., SayNav, SG-Nav, NavGPT, L3MVN). This approach introduces an information bottleneck, as continuous and rich 3D features are compressed into discrete textual representations, potentially losing critical geometric, semantic, and spatial-relational context. The performance cost of this text serialization has largely remained unquantified.

Concurrently, the broader field of cross-modal alignment has explored "soft-token injection," where outputs from one modality (e.g., 2D visual features) are projected directly into the hidden space of another model (e.g., a frozen language model) using lightweight trainable projectors. This approach has been successful in 2D vision-language tasks (e.g., Flamingo, LLaVA, BLIP-2) and, more recently, for 3D scene understanding using point cloud features (e.g., 3D-LLM, LEO). However, prior work has not applied soft-token injection to transfer structured, entity-level 3D scene encoder outputs for *online navigation decisions*. Methods like NaVid and EmbodiedGPT operate primarily on 2D visual information without explicit structured 3D scene representations. Dynam3D, a concurrent work, injects hierarchical 3D tokens but constructs them from 2D visual features projected into 3D space, rather than leveraging a dedicated 3D scene encoder, and focuses on instruction-following rather than open-vocabulary object-goal navigation with frontier-based waypoint selection.

SoftNav addresses this specific gap by quantifying the performance cost of text serialization in VLM-based navigation and proposing a direct, embedding-level transfer mechanism. It integrates a dedicated 3D scene encoder with a VLM through soft-token injection, aiming to retain the richness of 3D representations while leveraging the VLM's reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SoftNav research are:

1. **Quantify and address the "representation gap" in VLM-based embodied navigation**: The authors hypothesize that serializing continuous 3D scene information into discrete text for VLM consumption leads to a significant loss of information, creating a "representation gap" that hinders navigation performance. A key objective is to empirically measure this performance penalty through controlled ablation studies.
2. **Develop a method for direct, continuous 3D scene information transfer to VLMs**: Motivated by the hypothesized representation gap, the research aims to design an alternative mechanism to transfer 3D scene information at the embedding level directly into a VLM's hidden space. This aims to preserve the geometric, semantic, and spatial-relational richness that text serialization may discard.
3. **Enable efficient and transferable navigation with minimal training**: The objective is to achieve state-of-the-art navigation performance using a computationally efficient approach. This involves freezing the large pre-trained 3D encoder and VLM, training only a lightweight interface module (projector and LoRA adapters) on a small dataset. The goal is for the learned navigation policy to generalize effectively across diverse navigation benchmarks and transfer to real-world robotic systems without requiring further retraining or architectural modifications.
4. **Combine structured 3D perception with VLM reasoning**: The work seeks to synergize the strengths of dedicated 3D scene understanding (which provides structured entity-level representations of objects and frontiers) with the advanced reasoning capabilities of large vision-language models for robust goal-directed navigation in unseen environments.

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of current text-based VLM interfaces in embodied navigation by demonstrating that a direct, continuous embedding-level transfer of 3D scene information can lead to substantial performance improvements, particularly in complex and unseen environments, with minimal training overhead.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoftNav introduces a pipeline that injects entity-level 3D continuous representations directly into a Vision-Language Model (VLM)'s hidden space as soft tokens. The system operates with frozen 3D scene encoders and VLM backbones, minimizing trainable parameters.

**A. System Overview:**
The SoftNav pipeline processes streaming RGB-D observations through two parallel pathways:
1. **Visual Stream**: This pathway adopts a compression pipeline where a frozen DINOv3 backbone extracts patch features from each RGB frame. A CompressionHead with PatchMerger reduces these features into a fixed number of visual tokens (N_v = 15). A rolling buffer maintains a visual memory of the most recent K=16 frames, forming a compact representation of the agent's egocentric visual history.
2. **3D Scene Stream**: This stream utilizes a frozen PQ3D encoder to process accumulated RGB-D observations. PQ3D performs incremental 3D instance segmentation, maintains an object bank, and detects exploration frontiers from the occupancy map. It produces 768-dimensional query embeddings for each detected object and frontier, encoding geometric, semantic, and spatial-relational information.

These two streams converge with a textual navigation prompt within the VLM's hidden space.

**B. PQ3D 3D Scene Encoder:**
PQ3D, a 3D vision-language model adapted for embodied navigation, is employed as the core 3D scene encoder. It provides a unified representation by generating 768-dimensional embeddings for both objects and frontiers. These embeddings are designed to be semantically rich and are crucial for the soft-token injection mechanism. PQ3D remains frozen throughout the training process to preserve its pretrained representations.

**C. Soft-Token Injection:**
The core innovation is the direct injection of PQ3D's entity-level query embeddings into the VLM.
1. **Projector Design**: A lightweight, two-layer MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) with GELU activation and LayerNormalization is used to align the 768-dimensional query space of PQ3D with the VLM's 2048-dimensional hidden space. This choice is motivated by the small training dataset (∼1,200 samples), which makes high-parameter cross-attention prone to overfitting, and the premise that PQ3D embeddings are already semantically rich, requiring mainly geometric and distributional alignment.
2. **Scatter Injection and Token Ordering**: Special placeholder tokens are embedded into the VLM's text template. At the embedding level, these placeholders are replaced by the projected soft tokens. The input sequence to the VLM combines visual memory tokens, soft tokens (with frontier tokens preceding object tokens as a heuristic), and textual navigation prompt tokens. This ensures positional alignment and allows the VLM to jointly attend over all modalities.

**D. Frontier Decision:**
Instead of directly using the VLM's generated coordinate as the final navigation target, SoftNav snaps it to the nearest eligible frontier waypoint detected by PQ3D. This ensures that the selected target is always reachable and lies on the boundary between explored and unexplored space. A distance-ratio guardrail (r=2) prevents the selection of excessively distant frontiers. If no frontiers are detected, a rescue fallback mechanism re-triggers exploration from the agent's current position. A GreedyGeodesicFollower then executes local navigation to the selected frontier.

**E. Training Strategy:**
1. **Training Configuration**: The visual encoder (DINOv3), PQ3D 3D encoder, and the VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) are kept frozen. Only the MLP projector (∼4M parameters) and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters (∼13M parameters), applied to the VLM's attention and feed-forward layers, are trained.
2. **Cross-Architecture Knowledge Transfer**: The training data consists of approximately 1,200 supervised samples distilled from successful episodes of the MTU3D system on OVON training scenes. Since frontier coordinates are architecture-independent, the teacher's frontier selection at each decision step serves as the coordinate label for the VLM's autoregressive training objective. The VLM is trained to generate the target frontier coordinate string, with inference terminating upon generation of a coordinate delimiter.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several main findings regarding the performance of SoftNav, its generalization capabilities, and insights from ablation studies.

**A. Comparison with Prior Methods on HM3D-OVON:**
SoftNav demonstrates performance improvements on the HM3D-OVON benchmark for open-vocabulary object-goal navigation. It achieved success rates (SR) of 74.2% on val_seen, 68.3% on val_seen_synonyms, and 66.7% on val_unseen. These results surpassed prior state-of-the-art methods by substantial margins (e.g., +19.2 pp SR on val_seen, +23.3 pp on val_seen_synonyms, and +25.9 pp on val_unseen compared to MTU3D). SoftNav also achieved the highest Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) across all splits (33.9%, 28.6%, 25.7%), indicating improved path efficiency in addition to higher success rates. The largest gains were observed on val_unseen, suggesting robust performance even in novel environments due to the VLM's pretrained knowledge and the continuous 3D representations.

**B. Zero-Shot Generalization:**
A single SoftNav policy, trained solely on OVON, exhibited zero-shot transferability to other embodied navigation benchmarks without retraining or architectural modification:
- **GOAT-Bench**: For multi-modal lifelong navigation, SoftNav achieved 67.2% SR on val_seen and 64.2% on val_unseen, outperforming all reported baselines. While SR was higher, SPL was slightly lower than the strongest baseline (29.0% vs. 30.5% on val_seen), indicating a trade-off where the policy succeeded more often but sometimes took longer paths in novel layouts.
- **SG3D**: For scene-graph-grounded sequential navigation, SoftNav achieved 47.2% sub-task SR (s-SR), a significant improvement of +23.4 pp over the previous best, and 16.6% full-task SR (t-SR). SPL was 14.7% compared to the baseline of 16.5%.
These results suggest that the PQ3D query embeddings encode task-general navigation semantics that transfer across diverse task structures and goal modalities.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
1. **Representation Gap**: A controlled ablation study quantified the performance difference between soft-token injection and text serialization. Holding the 3D encoder and VLM constant, soft-token injection improved SPL from 24.7% to 33.9% (+9.2 pp, p<0.0001) and SR from 66.7% to 74.2% (+7.5 pp, p=0.028) on val_seen. Adding LoRA adapters to the Text-Hint baseline yielded only modest gains (+2.2 pp SPL, +1.6 pp SR), indicating that model adaptation alone does not fully bridge the gap. Further enriching the text channel with distance, direction, and confidence (Rich-Text variant) did not statistically improve performance on val_seen, supporting the conclusion that the gap is modality-driven rather than information-driven. Per-episode analysis showed SoftNav's superior SPL on shared successes and ability to solve more challenging episodes on val_seen. On val_unseen, SoftNav solved more unique episodes but with slightly longer paths on shared successes, suggesting that while soft tokens provide semantic benefits for success, optimal spatial planning in novel environments may require larger training sets. UMAP visualizations confirmed that raw and projected PQ3D embeddings retained clear semantic clustering, while text serialization destroyed this structure.
2. **Token Selection**: Experimenting with an Instruction-Conditioned Token Selector to select a subset of 3D tokens (K=32 or K=96) did not improve performance over injecting all 128 tokens. This suggests that the PQ3D query tokens are densely informative, and aggressive selection may discard useful information under the current training regime.

**D. Statistical Significance:**
Paired bootstrap results (N=10,000) for the core ablation demonstrated that the 95% confidence interval for SoftNav's SPL increase was entirely above zero. The lower bound of the 95% CI for SoftNav's SR (65.8%) exceeded the strongest prior result (55.0%) by +10.8 pp, indicating a robust performance advantage.

**E. Inference Efficiency:**
Each decision step took approximately 1 second on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, with PQ3D perception (∼0.45 s) and VLM inference (∼0.49 s) accounting for most of the computation. The lightweight MLP projector added a negligible 0.07 ms.

**F. Real-World Testing:**
SoftNav was deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot equipped with an RGB-D camera. The policy, trained exclusively in Habitat, transferred to real-world environments (corridor, hall, outdoor) without domain adaptation or fine-tuning. The agent succeeded in 19 out of 30 trials (63.3% SR), including 5/10 successes in an outdoor environment, which was outside the indoor training distribution. This confirms the sim-to-real transferability of the soft-token injection pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SoftNav research highlights a critical aspect of integrating 3D scene understanding with large vision-language models for embodied navigation: the modality of information transfer. The primary significance and potential impact are:

1. **Quantifying the Representation Gap**: By rigorously demonstrating and quantifying the performance penalty associated with serializing rich 3D information into text, the paper provides empirical evidence for a fundamental "representation gap." This insight is crucial for the design of future embodied AI systems, directing focus towards more effective continuous representation transfer rather than solely enhancing text-based prompts or increasing model capacity.
2. **Efficient and Effective Integration of 3D Perception and VLM Reasoning**: SoftNav introduces a method that efficiently bridges this gap by directly injecting entity-level 3D representations into the VLM's hidden space. The approach is highly parameter-efficient, requiring only approximately 17 million trainable parameters (MLP projector and LoRA adapters) and a small dataset (∼1,200 samples), while keeping the large 3D encoder and VLM frozen. This significantly reduces the computational cost and data requirements for training advanced embodied navigation agents.
3. **Enhanced Navigation Performance and Generalization**: The achieved state-of-the-art success rates and path efficiencies on HM3D-OVON, coupled with robust zero-shot generalization across diverse benchmarks like GOAT-Bench and SG3D, demonstrate the effectiveness of this direct embedding-level transfer. The policy’s ability to generalize to unseen goal types, task structures, and even out-of-distribution real-world environments without retraining signifies a step towards more versatile and robust embodied agents.
4. **Transferability to Real-World Robotics**: The successful zero-shot deployment on a physical quadruped robot, including partially novel outdoor environments, underscores the practical utility and robustness of the SoftNav pipeline. This indicates that policies learned in simulation using this paradigm can be directly applicable in the physical world, accelerating the development and deployment of embodied AI.
5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research into optimizing 3D encoders for soft-token injection, exploring more advanced token selection strategies, and scaling training datasets to further enhance spatial adaptation in novel environments. The modularity of the projector architecture allows for easy substitution of VLM backbones or 3D encoders as more efficient and capable models emerge, ensuring the approach's relevance in a rapidly evolving field.

In summary, SoftNav offers a novel and efficient method for integrating complex 3D scene understanding with advanced VLM reasoning, leading to high-performance, generalizable, and real-world-deployable embodied navigation policies.
