# Updating the standard neuron model in artificial neural networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers update the standard artificial neuron model with an Implicit Bias Neural Network (IBNN), inspired by cortical cells that include complex dendritic processing and backpropagating action potentials. This new model, maintaining the same number of parameters as standard ANNs, achieves higher expressivity, improved robustness to adversarial attacks, reduces the need for training data by 20-82%, enables faster learning by reaching peak accuracy in 18-25 epochs compared to over 53 epochs for standard models, and exhibits less memorization.

## Problem

- Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) still rely on a simplistic "point neuron model" from the 1950s, neglecting complex dendritic computation and backpropagating action potentials (bAPs) found in biological neurons.
- Current ANNs require vast amounts of training data, are highly susceptible to minor input perturbations (adversarial attacks), and tend to memorize training data, limitations not observed in living systems.
- Existing neuroscience-inspired models often remain within the standard deep learning framework, failing to capture the dynamic interaction between cell output and dendritic contributions involving bAPs.

## Method

- A new Implicit Bias Neural Network (**IBNN**) neuron model is introduced, directly inspired by recent neuroscience findings on cortical cells, incorporating non-linear dendritic processing and the effects of backpropagating action potentials (bAPs).
- The **IBNN** modifies the standard neuron's linear summation with an implicit bias term that models lateral interactions within a layer and self-feedback from the soma, formulated as a system of coupled implicit equations.
- The proposed **IBNN** model is designed to maintain the same number of trainable parameters as a standard neuron to isolate the performance benefits attributable solely to the updated neural unit.

## Results

- IBNNs consistently achieve higher accuracy with the same number of parameters, requiring 20-**82%** less training data for comparable performance across Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 image classification tasks.
- The updated neuron model demonstrates enhanced robustness against both white-box (PGD) and black-box (Pixle) adversarial attacks across various image datasets.
- IBNN networks exhibit faster learning, reaching peak validation accuracy in 18-25 epochs, significantly fewer than the 53+ epochs typically required by standard models, and show reduced memorization when trained on mislabeled data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Updating the fundamental neuron model with biologically plausible features like dendritic nonlinearities and bAP effects can enhance ANNs' capabilities, including expressivity and robustness, without increasing parameter count.
> - The implicit formulation of the IBNN inherently leads to improved robustness against input perturbations (e.g., smaller Jacobian norm) and allows for the approximation of complex, non-convex decision boundaries.
> - The mathematical properties of IBNNs, such as connections to energy functionals and potential for reduced loss function curvature, explain their observed benefits in learning efficiency and resistance to memorization.
