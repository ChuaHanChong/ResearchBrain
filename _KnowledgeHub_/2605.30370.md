# Updating the standard neuron model in artificial neural networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers update the standard artificial neuron model with an Implicit Bias Neural Network (IBNN), inspired by cortical cells that include complex dendritic processing and backpropagating action potentials. This new model, maintaining the same number of parameters as standard ANNs, achieves higher expressivity, improved robustness to adversarial attacks, reduces the need for training data by 20-82%, enables faster learning by reaching peak accuracy in 18-25 epochs compared to over 53 epochs for standard models, and exhibits less memorization.

## Problem

- Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) still rely on a simplistic "point neuron model" from the 1950s, neglecting complex dendritic computation and backpropagating action potentials (bAPs) found in biological neurons.
- Current ANNs require vast amounts of training data, are highly susceptible to minor input perturbations (adversarial attacks), and tend to memorize training data, limitations not observed in living systems.
- Existing neuroscience-inspired models often remain within the standard deep learning framework, failing to capture the dynamic interaction between cell output and dendritic contributions involving bAPs.

## Method

- A new Implicit Bias Neural Network (**IBNN**) neuron model is introduced, directly inspired by recent neuroscience findings on cortical cells, incorporating non-linear dendritic processing and the effects of backpropagating action potentials (bAPs).
- The **IBNN** modifies the standard neuron's linear summation with an implicit bias term that models lateral interactions within a layer and self-feedback from the soma, formulated as a system of coupled implicit equations.
- The proposed **IBNN** model is designed to maintain the same number of trainable parameters as a standard neuron to isolate the performance benefits attributable solely to the updated neural unit.

## Results

- IBNNs consistently achieve higher accuracy with the same number of parameters, requiring 20-**82%** less training data for comparable performance across Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 image classification tasks.
- The updated neuron model demonstrates enhanced robustness against both white-box (PGD) and black-box (Pixle) adversarial attacks across various image datasets.
- IBNN networks exhibit faster learning, reaching peak validation accuracy in 18-25 epochs, significantly fewer than the 53+ epochs typically required by standard models, and show reduced memorization when trained on mislabeled data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Updating the fundamental neuron model with biologically plausible features like dendritic nonlinearities and bAP effects can enhance ANNs' capabilities, including expressivity and robustness, without increasing parameter count.
> - The implicit formulation of the IBNN inherently leads to improved robustness against input perturbations (e.g., smaller Jacobian norm) and allows for the approximation of complex, non-convex decision boundaries.
> - The mathematical properties of IBNNs, such as connections to energy functionals and potential for reduced loss function curvature, explain their observed benefits in learning efficiency and resistance to memorization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) have their roots in the mid-20th century neuroscience concept of the "point neuron model," which assumes that individual neurons sum inputs linearly before a non-linear activation. This "standard deep learning (DL) framework" has remained largely unchanged in ANNs despite significant advancements in neuroscience that reveal more complex neural processes, particularly involving dendritic computation and backpropagating action potentials (bAPs).

Current ANN architectures, while powerful, face critical limitations not observed in living beings, such as requiring vast amounts of training data and being susceptible to minor input perturbations. There is a growing consensus that progress in artificial intelligence may necessitate moving beyond simply scaling up existing architectures and exploring "fundamentally new approaches" inspired by advanced neuroscience. Some recent works in the "NeuroAI literature" have begun to consider more realistic dendritic models, but many still fall within the standard DL framework, failing to fully capture the dynamic interplay between cell output and dendritic contributions facilitated by bAPs.

This research paper directly addresses these limitations by proposing an update to the fundamental building block of ANNs—the neuron—based on a more realistic model of cortical cells. By incorporating complex dendritic processing and bAPs, the authors aim to improve the expressivity, robustness, and learning efficiency of ANNs, aligning them more closely with biological intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from the conjecture that the "overly simplistic neuron model" adopted from 1950s neuroscience is at the root of many fundamental constraints and drawbacks observed in modern artificial neural networks (ANNs). While ANNs have become central to AI, they exhibit critical limitations, such as needing enormous training data and being highly vulnerable to input perturbations, which are not characteristic of living systems.

Neuroscience research has progressed significantly beyond the 1950s "point neuron model," demonstrating that dendrites engage in complex, non-linear computations and that backpropagating action potentials (bAPs) play an indispensable role in neural behavior and brain functions. Existing attempts in the NeuroAI community to incorporate more realistic dendritic models often remain within the "standard DL framework," which is inadequate for representing the dynamic interaction between cell output and dendritic contributions involving bAPs.

Therefore, the key objective of this paper is to:
1. **Introduce a new neuron model for ANNs:** This new model is directly inspired by a very recent neuroscience model of cortical cells that specifically considers the interaction between dendritic nonlinearities and bAPs.
2. **Demonstrate the advantages of ANNs built with this updated neuron model:** The authors aim to show, through theoretical analyses and experimental results, that simply by using a more realistic neural unit (referred to as Implicit Bias Neural Network or IBNN) – critically, "without augmenting the number of parameters" compared to a standard model (SM) ANN – the resulting networks offer significant improvements. These advantages are hypothesized to include increased expressivity, enhanced robustness, faster learning speed, and reduced memorization, thereby overcoming some of the fundamental limitations of current ANNs.

By updating the basic neural unit, the research seeks to leverage biological insights to advance the capabilities and overcome the inherent weaknesses of artificial intelligence systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves developing a new neuron model, conducting theoretical analyses to establish its properties, and performing empirical evaluations using image classification tasks.

#### 3.1. Updating the Neuron Model: Implicit Bias Neural Network (IBNN)

The paper first defines the standard neuron model (SM) in ANNs, where the output $u_i$ is `phi(y_i)` and `y_i` is a linear sum of inputs `x_j` minus a bias `b_i`. The proposed Implicit Bias Neural Network (IBNN) model extends this by introducing a new bias term inspired by biological dendritic non-linearities and bAPs.
The IBNN output $v_i$ is `phi(z_i)`, where `z_i` is defined as:
`z_i = sum(m_ij * x_j) - b_i - lambda * sum(w_ik * sigma(z_k - z_i))`.
Here, `lambda` is a scalar, `w_ik` is a linear filter, and `sigma` is a non-linear function. The first term (`sum(m_ij * x_j) - b_i`) represents the linear dendritic response, similar to the SM. The crucial second term `(-lambda * sum(w_ik * sigma(z_k - z_i)))` models the effect of non-linear dendrites, where `z_k` represents contributions from neighboring units in the same layer, and `z_i` emulates the bAP from the soma. This formulation makes `z_i` implicitly dependent on itself, leading to a system of coupled implicit equations. The authors note that the IBNN has the same number of trainable parameters as the SM when `lambda` and `w_i` are fixed and predefined.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analyses

The authors establish several theoretical properties for the IBNN model:

- **Existence, Uniqueness, and Energy Minimization:** By connecting Eq. (4) to an energy functional `E_lambda(z; y) = g(z; y) - f_lambda(z)`, the paper proves that solutions to the implicit equation exist and are unique under certain conditions (e.g., `sigma` being sigmoidal and `lambda` below a positive threshold T). This ensures that the model is well-defined.
- **Practical Computation of the Solution:** The solution to the implicit equation can be found using a gradient descent algorithm, which minimizes the energy functional. The library TorchDEQ is used for implementation.
- **Expressivity:**
 - The paper proves that a single-layer IBNN can approximate any given mapping with arbitrary small error, similar to the universal approximation power of SM ANNs.
 - It also argues that IBNNs are more expressive, meaning they need fewer parameters to approximate the same function. The first-order Taylor expansion of IBNN indicates that approximating a D-neuron IBNN layer with SM neurons would require D(D+2) neurons.
- **Robustness to Input Perturbations:**
 - **Local Perturbations:** By analyzing the Jacobian matrix, the authors demonstrate that the norm of the Jacobian for an IBNN is generally smaller than that of its SM counterpart (for `lambda <= 0`). This implies improved robustness against infinitesimal input changes.
 - **Global Perturbations:** Proposition 6 establishes that for `lambda <= 0` and a convex function S, the distance between IBNN outputs for two inputs is less than or equal to the distance between SM outputs for the same inputs, suggesting global robustness.
 - **Accuracy-Stability Tradeoff:** The paper notes that the formal mathematical proof showing SM networks are inherently subject to an accuracy-stability tradeoff does not directly apply to IBNNs due to their implicit and highly non-linear nature.
 - **Stealth Attacks:** The authors demonstrate that IBNNs are resistant to single-neuron stealth attacks, which are feasible on SM networks.

- **Resistance to Memorization:** The tendency of an ANN to memorize training data correlates with the curvature of the loss function. Mathematical analysis suggests that IBNN can achieve a smaller curvature, implying less memorization, given a proper choice of parameters `lambda` and `p`.

#### 3.3. Experimental Evaluation

The experimental evaluation compares IBNN-based ANNs with SM-based ANNs under various conditions, ensuring they have the same architecture and number of parameters to isolate the benefits of the proposed neuron model.

- **Network Architectures:** The experiments utilize "uniform convolutional networks (UCNs)" composed of sequential blocks of either SM or IBNN convolutional layers, followed by batch normalization and a fully-connected output head. ReLU is used as the activation function. For IBNN, uniform weights `w_ik` and a dendritic non-linearity `sigma(z) = tanh(pz)` with `p = 10` are used.
- **Datasets:** Three image classification datasets of increasing difficulty are used: Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10. A custom 2D dataset is also used for visualizing decision boundaries.
- **Training Details:** Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) is employed with a linear learning rate schedule. A warm-up training step on a SM-based surrogate model is incorporated for IBNN networks to ensure favorable initialization.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Less Training Data:** Assessed by training models with varying proportions of the training dataset.
 - **Robustness:** Evaluated against two types of adversarial attacks:
 - **White-box PGD (Projected Gradient Descent) attacks:** Generated using a SM-based surrogate model for IBNNs, as direct gradient calculation is difficult.
 - **Black-box Pixle attacks:** Directly applied to both SM and IBNN networks.
 - **Expressivity:** Examined by comparing accuracy across different numbers of channels (parameters).
 - **Learning Speed:** Compared by tracking the validation accuracy over epochs.
 - **Memorization:** Tested by corrupting training data with mislabeled samples and observing validation accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several advantages of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) updated with the proposed Implicit Bias Neural Network (IBNN) neuron model compared to standard neuron model (SM) networks of equivalent complexity.

- **Increased Expressivity:**
 - A toy 2D binary classification example demonstrates that IBNN can approximate non-convex decision boundaries, unlike SM networks which are limited to convex polygons (Figure 2). The parameters `lambda` and `p` control the curvature of these boundaries.
 - Across Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 datasets, IBNNs consistently achieve higher accuracy with the same number of trainable parameters, implying greater expressivity (Figure 6).

- **Reduced Training Data Requirement:**
 - IBNNs consistently require a fraction (20-82%) of the training data needed by SM networks to achieve comparable performance levels across the tested image classification tasks (Figure 3).

- **Enhanced Robustness to Input Perturbations:**
 - **Theoretical Basis:** The analysis of the Jacobian matrix shows that IBNN's output is less sensitive to infinitesimal input perturbations than SM networks, particularly when the parameter `lambda <= 0`. Furthermore, the formal proof of an inherent accuracy-stability tradeoff in SM networks does not directly apply to the IBNN formulation. The paper also demonstrates that single-neuron stealth attacks, possible on SM networks, are unfeasible on IBNNs.
 - **Experimental Evidence:** IBNNs demonstrate consistently higher robustness against both white-box adversarial attacks (Projected Gradient Descent, PGD) (Figure 4) and black-box adversarial attacks (Pixle) (Figure 5) across different image datasets.

- **Faster Learning:**
 - The results indicate that IBNN networks achieve the same classification performance as SM networks in significantly fewer learning steps. In all cases, IBNN reached its best validation accuracy in 18-25 epochs, while SM networks typically needed over 53 epochs to reach their peak (Figure 7).

- **Reduced Memorization:**
 - Mathematical analysis suggests that IBNN has a smaller curvature in its loss function with respect to the input, indicating less propensity for memorization.
 - Experiments involving mislabeled training data show that IBNN maintains a greater advantage over SM networks when data quality is compromised, suggesting IBNNs are less prone to memorizing noisy or incorrect samples (Figure 8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution by updating the fundamental building block of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs)—the neuron—with a more biologically plausible model inspired by recent advances in neuroscience. The standard "point neuron model" has been prevalent since the 1950s, but modern neuroscience has revealed the complexity of dendritic processing and the importance of backpropagating action potentials (bAPs). This paper proposes an "implicit bias neural network" (IBNN) that incorporates these features.

The key significance of this work lies in demonstrating that simply by updating the neural unit without increasing the number of parameters, ANNs can achieve a number of important advantages:
- **Improved Expressivity:** The ability to learn more complex, non-convex decision boundaries without needing more parameters is a significant step towards more efficient and powerful models.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** The demonstrated increased resistance to both local and global input perturbations, including adversarial attacks, addresses a critical limitation of current ANNs that hinders their deployment in safety-critical applications.
- **Reduced Training Data and Faster Learning:** The capacity to achieve good performance with less training data and in fewer epochs could dramatically accelerate AI development and reduce computational costs, making advanced AI more accessible.
- **Less Memorization:** Reduced memorization suggests better generalization capabilities, leading to more reliable models, especially when training data may contain noise or inaccuracies.

The approach aligns with a broader emerging consensus in the NeuroAI community that substantial progress in AI might require moving beyond mere scaling and incorporating more sophisticated principles from neuroscience. The IBNN formulation, by modeling internal dendritic-like nonlinearities and lateral interactions, represents a novel contribution in this direction.

**Future implications and research directions include:**
- **Trainable Parameters:** Exploring whether parameters like `lambda`, `w_i`, and the slope `p` of the nonlinearity `sigma` should be learned rather than predefined, which could further enhance expressivity.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Developing numerical techniques specifically tailored to IBNNs to reduce the computational complexity associated with solving the iterative implicit equations.
- **Full V1 Model Emulation:** Incorporating additional terms from the V1 model that inspired this work, potentially introducing non-monotonic internal nonlinearities for predicting time-varying phenomena.
- **Integration with Transformers:** Investigating the combination of IBNN's "independent representation" property with Transformer architectures, potentially leading to novel synergistic advancements.
- **Connections to Fundamental AI Concepts:** The model's mathematical form links it to energy models and causal modeling, which could open new avenues for understanding and developing more robust and intelligent AI systems.

Overall, this research suggests that a biologically inspired redesign of the fundamental neural unit can lead to tangible improvements in ANN performance across multiple critical dimensions, potentially paving the way for more robust, efficient, and intelligent AI systems.
