# BootsTAP: Bootstrapped Training for Tracking-Any-Point

> [!summary] Summary
> BootsTAP improves Tracking-Any-Point by bootstrapping a synthetically pretrained TAPIR model on 15 million unlabeled real video clips. A student network sees affine-warped, JPEG-degraded views and must reproduce an EMA teacher's tracks from any query point along the trajectory, filtered by cycle-consistency, while co-training on synthetic data. TAP-Vid-DAVIS rises from 61.3% to 67.4% Average Jaccard, Kinetics from 57.2% to 62.5%.

## Problem

- Ground-truth point tracks exist only in simulation; manual labeling is arduous and imprecise, and 3D sensing covers few scenarios, leaving a synthetic-to-real domain gap.
- Photometric self-supervision breaks under occlusion, lighting change and repeated texture, forcing complex priors such as explicit occlusion handling, smoothness terms and robust losses.
- Earlier semi-supervised correspondence work froze pseudo-labels or needed adversarial domain adaptation and target-domain fine-tuning, locking in teacher errors and yielding only small gains.

## Method

- ==Student-teacher bootstrapping==: an ==EMA teacher== labels raw clips, and the student reproduces those tracks under TAPIR's Huber position plus binary cross-entropy occlusion and uncertainty losses, with ==stop-gradient== blocking collapse.
- Two consistency views: time-varying frame-wise affine transforms with JPEG corruption and downscaling enforce ==equivariance==, while the student's query is resampled anywhere along the teacher trajectory to enforce query-equivalence.
- ==Cycle-consistency masking== discards trajectories whose student prediction misses the teacher query by over 4 pixels; multi-task training with separate Adam optimizers on Kubric prevents catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- BootsTAPIR sets state of the art on TAP-Vid strided evaluation: **66.2** Average Jaccard on DAVIS, **61.4** on Kinetics, **72.4** on RGB-Stacking, beating TAPIR and CoTracker.
- Ablations: removing affine transforms drops DAVIS Average Jaccard from **65.8** to **54.4**; a gradient-sharing Siamese variant collapses to **49.8** DAVIS and **29.6** Kinetics; Kubric-only longer training reaches **65.0**.
- Higher-resolution and longer-clip data lift DAVIS to **67.4** and RoboTAP query-first to **69.2**; evaluating at **512** pixels gives **70.2** DAVIS, and the causal model tracks **400** points at **30 fps**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-consistency alone, equivariance to spatial warps plus invariance to corruption and query choice, beats synthetic-only training without motion smoothness priors or architectural redesign.
> - Asymmetry is what prevents collapse: degrade only the student's view and freeze the teacher through an exponential moving average, since symmetric training finds trivial shortcuts.
> - Temporal context makes semi-supervised distillation work; 2- and 6-frame clips underperform 24-frame ones, explaining why earlier two-frame optical-flow attempts were judged unstable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tracking-Any-Point (TAP) is a crucial and foundational task in computer vision, aiming to precisely track arbitrary points on solid surfaces across video sequences. Unlike semantic segmentation or object detection, TAP provides dense, fine-grained motion and deformation information that is independent of high-level semantic categories. This makes it a highly general capability with wide-ranging applications in fields such as robotics (e.g., precise manipulation, imitation learning), 3D reconstruction and understanding, video generation and editing, and even improving general AI systems' physical and spatial reasoning abilities.

Historically, point tracking has been addressed by methods like optical flow, but these often struggle with long-range correspondences, occlusions, and large displacements. Recent advancements, particularly deep learning-based approaches, have shown significant progress. However, a major bottleneck for these models has been the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality ground-truth training data. Manually annotating point trajectories in real-world videos is notoriously tedious and imprecise, while 3D sensing methods offer limited data volume and specific scenarios. Consequently, state-of-the-art TAP models, including the immediate predecessor TAPIR, have heavily relied on synthetic datasets like Kubric and PointOdyssey. While these synthetic datasets provide perfectly labeled data, they inherently suffer from a "sim-to-real" domain gap, limiting the generalization performance of models to the complexities of real-world videos (e.g., diverse textures, lighting, motion blur, occlusions, and object types).

BootsTAP directly addresses this critical sim-to-real gap by introducing a novel self-supervised learning framework that leverages vast amounts of unlabeled, uncurated real-world video data. This work fits into a broader trend in computer vision towards self-supervised and semi-supervised learning, where models learn powerful representations or tasks without explicit human annotations, often by exploiting inherent consistencies or transformations within the data itself. Examples include contrastive learning for image representations, or photometric consistency for optical flow. However, applying these methods to the more complex, long-range TAP task without "trivial shortcuts" or degradation of performance has been a significant challenge. BootsTAP demonstrates that with careful design, self-consistency principles can be robustly applied to point tracking, thereby significantly advancing the generalization capabilities of TAP models beyond the confines of synthetic data. It represents a significant step towards building more robust and general-purpose visual perception systems for real-world applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **overcome the data scarcity challenge for Tracking-Any-Point (TAP) models by effectively leveraging large-scale, unlabeled real-world videos to improve tracking performance**. The authors aim to bridge the "sim-to-real" gap that arises from training state-of-the-art models primarily on synthetic datasets, which inherently limit their generalization to the diverse and complex conditions of real-world scenarios.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Enhancing Physical and Spatial Reasoning in AI:** Current generalist AI systems, despite advances in vision and language, still exhibit weaknesses in understanding physics and motion. Precise point tracking is seen as a fundamental building block for endowing models with a deeper comprehension of how objects move, deform, and interact in physical environments.
- **Expanding Application Domains:** A more robust and generalized TAP capability would unlock numerous applications in critical areas such as robotics (e.g., more precise control, better imitation learning), 3D reconstruction (e.g., generating high-quality assets from video), and advanced video manipulation (e.g., editing, generation).
- **Reducing Reliance on Costly Labeled Data:** Manual annotation of point trajectories is laborious and imprecise, making large-scale real-world ground truth data prohibitive. This work seeks to develop a method that can improve TAP models without incurring these high labeling costs.
- **Improving Generalization to Diverse Real-World Scenarios:** Models trained solely on synthetic data often struggle with the textureless regions, varied lighting, occlusions, and complex motions prevalent in real-world videos. The motivation is to enable models to learn from the true complexity of real data, leading to more robust and accurate tracking in practical settings.
- **Establishing a Robust Self-Supervised Framework:** The authors aim to demonstrate that simple, principled self-consistency signals, based on fundamental properties of point trajectories (equivariance to spatial transformations, invariance to query point/non-spatial augmentations), can be effectively harnessed in a student-teacher setup to boost performance without falling into "trivial shortcuts" that often plague self-supervised methods in complex vision tasks.

In essence, BootsTAP seeks to make point tracking models more practical, reliable, and broadly applicable by enabling them to learn from the real world, rather than being confined to the relatively controlled environments of synthetic datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BootsTAP introduces a self-supervised student-teacher framework designed to leverage unlabeled real-world videos for improving Tracking-Any-Point (TAP) models. The methodology builds upon the existing state-of-the-art TAPIR model, employing minimal architectural changes.

**Core Architecture and Initialization:**
The process begins with a strong "teacher" model, which is a TAPIR network pre-trained entirely using supervised learning on synthetic data (specifically, Kubric). This pre-trained model serves as the initialization for both the "teacher" and "student" networks in the bootstrapped training phase. To accommodate the learning from additional real-world data, the model's capacity is slightly increased by adding five 2D conv-residual layers to the backbone, roughly doubling the backbone's parameters. These new layers are initialized to the identity, following "zero init" principles.

**Student-Teacher Framework for Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):**
The core of BootsTAP is a self-supervised student-teacher setup, as illustrated in Figure 1 and 2 of the paper:
1. **Teacher Prediction:** Given an unlabeled real-world input video, the teacher model (whose weights `ξ` are an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student's weights `Θ`) processes the video to produce a set of point trajectories (`p_T`, `o_T`, `u_T`). These teacher predictions serve as pseudo-ground-truth labels.
2. **Student Input Degradation:** A "second view" of the video is generated for the student. This involves several degradations:
 - **Affine Transformations:** Frame-wise affine transformations (`Φ`) are applied, varying smoothly over time. These transformations resize each frame to a smaller resolution `r` and place it at a random `(h, w)` position on a black background. The resolution `r` and `(h, w)` vary linearly between start and end frame values, mimicking plausible camera motion and zooming. This enforces **equivariance to spatial transformations**, meaning the tracked points should transform consistently with the video.
 - **JPEG Corruption:** Random JPEG compression is applied to the video frames. This introduces non-spatial degradation, enforcing **invariance to non-spatial corruptions**, requiring the model to rely on higher-level features rather than low-level texture matching.
3. **Query Point Sampling for Student:** To enforce that "predictions should be invariant to the choice of query point along a given trajectory," the student's query point `Q2 = (q2, t2)` is not necessarily the same as the teacher's original query point `Q1 = (q1, t1)`. Instead, `Q2` is sampled from the teacher's predicted trajectory. Specifically, with 50% probability, `Q2` is the same as `Q1`; otherwise, `Q2` is randomly sampled from any visible point along the teacher's predicted trajectory. This `Q2` is then transformed by `Φ` to match the student's degraded video view.
4. **Student Prediction and Inverse Transformation:** The student network takes the degraded video (`X'`) and the transformed query point (`Q'2`) as input, predicting its own trajectory (`ˆp_S`, `ˆo_S`, `ˆu_S`). These predictions are then transformed back to the original coordinate space using the inverse affine transformation `Φ⁻¹`.
5. **Self-Supervised Loss (SSL):** The student's transformed predictions are compared against the teacher's pseudo-ground-truth using a modified TAPIR loss function (`ℓ_ssl`). This loss consists of a Huber loss for position, and binary cross-entropy (BCE) losses for occlusion and uncertainty. The uncertainty target `u_T` is set based on the distance between the teacher's and student's predictions, essentially teaching the student to predict its own accuracy relative to the teacher.
6. **Pseudo-Label Filtering (Cycle-Consistency):** To prevent training on potentially inaccurate teacher predictions, a cycle-consistency filter (`m_cycle`) is applied. The loss for a trajectory is ignored if the student's prediction at the original query time `t1` (`ˆp_S[t1]`) is too far from the teacher's original query point `q1`, or if the student predicts occlusion at `t1`. This ensures that only "reliable" teacher trajectories are used for supervision.
7. **Teacher Update:** The teacher's weights `ξ` are updated using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's weights `Θ`. This mechanism ensures that the teacher's predictions are generally more stable and accurate than the student's, acting as a robust source of supervision.
8. **Multi-Task Training:** To prevent catastrophic forgetting of the synthetic data knowledge and to maintain performance on diverse object types, the model is continuously co-trained on the original Kubric dataset using the standard supervised TAPIR loss, alongside the self-supervised loss on real-world videos. Separate Adam optimizers are maintained for each task, and gradients are applied with their respective learning rates.

**Training Data:** The real-world training data comprises 15 million 24-frame clips from publicly available online videos (e.g., lifestyle, one-shot videos), carefully filtered to exclude low-quality content, overlays, and shot boundaries to ensure continuous motion and reliable teacher signals.

This sophisticated yet principled approach allows BootsTAP to leverage the vastness of unlabeled real-world data, enabling the model to learn more robust and generalized features for point tracking than previously possible.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BootsTAP achieves significant state-of-the-art performance across the entire TAP-Vid benchmark, demonstrating the effectiveness of self-supervised learning on unlabeled real-world data for point tracking.

**Key Quantitative Results:**

- **Significant Performance Gains:** BootsTAPIR (the BootsTAP enhanced TAPIR model) substantially outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods, including its predecessor TAPIR and CoTracker, across all TAP-Vid datasets and metrics (Average Jaccard (AJ), <δx_avg, Occlusion Accuracy (OA)).
 - **TAP-Vid-DAVIS:** AJ improves from 61.3% (TAPIR) to 67.4% (BootsTAPIR+fix+snap+data), a 6.1 percentage point increase.
 - **TAP-Vid-Kinetics:** AJ improves from 57.2% (TAPIR) to 62.5% (BootsTAPIR+fix+snap+data), a 5.3 percentage point increase.
 - **TAP-Vid-RGB-Stacking:** AJ improves from 62.7% (TAPIR) to 77.4% (BootsTAPIR+fix+snap+data), a substantial 14.7 percentage point increase.
 - **RoboTAP (Query-First):** AJ improves from 59.6% (TAPIR) to 69.2% (BootsTAPIR+fix+snap+data), a remarkable 9.6 percentage point increase. This is particularly notable given RoboTAP's challenging nature with textureless and symmetric objects, and its domain gap from typical online videos.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** All reported numbers are from a single checkpoint, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization capabilities to datasets not seen during training.
- **Higher Resolution Performance:** When evaluated on 512x512 videos, BootsTAPIR+hires further boosts performance, reaching 70.2% AJ on DAVIS and 63.7% AJ on Kinetics, setting new records for these datasets at higher resolutions.
- **Causal Model Performance:** A causal variant of BootsTAPIR, suitable for online real-time applications, also shows significant improvements (e.g., 4.6% AJ on Kinetics and 3.0% AJ on DAVIS over causal TAPIR).
- **Perception Test:** BootsTAPIR achieves a 3.9 percentage point improvement over TAPIR on the challenging Perception Test validation set (59.6% vs 55.7% AJ).

**Qualitative Improvements:**

- **Robustness to Ambiguous Texture Cues:** BootsTAPIR shows improvements in tracking points in regions with ambiguous textures (e.g., dark jackets), suggesting it learns higher-level object shape and motion cues.
- **Improved Boundary Tracking:** The model performs better on points near object boundaries (e.g., dog's ears), which is crucial for precise segmentation and interaction understanding.
- **Reduced Over-Occlusion:** BootsTAPIR often correctly tracks points that TAPIR previously marked as occluded, even when still visible (e.g., a person's arm).
- **Performance on Textureless/Symmetric Objects:** On RoboTAP and RoboCAT-NIST, BootsTAPIR significantly enhances tracking smoothness and accuracy for textureless robotic grippers and gears, better handling large scale changes and shiny objects.

**Ablation Study Insights:**

- **Data Transformations are Crucial:** Removing affine transformations causes a massive performance drop (e.g., 54.4% AJ on DAVIS vs. 65.8% for base), highlighting their importance for enforcing equivariance and preventing overfitting. JPEG augmentations also provide a notable boost.
- **Query Point Strategy:** Sampling different query points along a trajectory (rather than always the original query point) generally improves position accuracy.
- **Student-Teacher Architecture is Key:** A "siamese" approach (backpropping to both student and teacher without EMA or stop-gradient) leads to a collapse in performance (49.8% AJ on DAVIS vs. 66.2% for full model), validating the necessity of the student-teacher setup to avoid trivial solutions.
- **Longer Clips Matter:** Using shorter video clips (2 or 6 frames) significantly degrades performance compared to 24-frame clips, suggesting that more temporal context is vital for effective self-supervised learning of long-range point tracking.
- **Data Volume:** Training on a small subset (1%) of real data still yields competitive results, indicating the efficiency of the method, although full data provides overall best performance.
- **"Snap-to-occluder" Bias:** A specific bugfix in TAPIR, and a subsequent "snap-to-occluder" technique, were found to interact with BootsTAPIR's performance, suggesting a learned bias towards tracking background in bootstrapped models, which could be an area for future work.

In summary, BootsTAP delivers substantial improvements in point tracking accuracy and robustness by effectively leveraging vast amounts of unlabeled real-world video, demonstrating the power of principled self-supervised learning to bridge the sim-to-real gap for foundational vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The BootsTAP research represents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision, particularly for the fundamental task of Tracking-Any-Point (TAP). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Foundation Models:** The most critical contribution is demonstrating a highly effective method for training robust TAP models using large-scale, unlabeled real-world video data. This directly addresses the long-standing "sim-to-real" gap, which has limited the deployment and generalization of models trained predominantly on synthetic datasets. By making models more adept at handling the complexities of the real world, BootsTAP pushes the frontier for building more practical and reliable visual foundation models.
- **Advancing Self-Supervised Learning for Complex Vision Tasks:** BootsTAP showcases that even for a highly precise and detailed task like point tracking, simple and principled self-consistency signals (equivariance to spatial transformations, invariance to query points and non-spatial corruptions) can be leveraged effectively within a student-teacher framework. This success provides a strong empirical basis for designing future self-supervised methods for other complex video understanding tasks, especially those where high-quality ground-truth annotation is prohibitive. It offers a solution to the "trivial shortcut" problem often encountered in such approaches.
- **Enhanced Capabilities for Downstream Applications:** The substantial improvements in point tracking accuracy and robustness, particularly on challenging real-world datasets like RoboTAP, have direct and far-reaching implications for various application domains:
 - **Robotics:** More accurate and generalized point tracking can significantly enhance robotic manipulation, allowing robots to better understand object deformation, track fine-grained features for precise control, and improve imitation learning from human demonstrations.
 - **3D Reconstruction and Scene Understanding:** Better point correspondences are fundamental for robust 3D reconstruction, enabling more accurate shape estimation, motion capture, and general scene understanding from monocular videos.
 - **Video Generation and Editing:** Precise tracking of arbitrary points can facilitate advanced video editing (e.g., rotoscoping, content replacement, stylization) and lead to more consistent and physically plausible video generation.
 - **General AI and Physical Reasoning:** By providing models with a better grasp of object motion and deformation, BootsTAP contributes to endowing AI systems with improved physical and spatial reasoning abilities, a recognized weakness of current state-of-the-art models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens up several avenues for future work, such as exploring more principled solutions for biases (e.g., towards background tracking), reducing computational training costs, and extending the framework to handle more complex scenarios like duplicated or rotationally symmetric objects. The release of models and checkpoints will undoubtedly stimulate further research and development in the community.
- **Practical Impact for Researchers and Developers:** The provision of publicly available models and checkpoints (in both JAX and PyTorch) lowers the barrier for other researchers and developers to integrate state-of-the-art point tracking into their own projects, accelerating innovation across various fields. The demonstrated real-time inference capabilities of the causal model further underscore its practical utility.

In conclusion, BootsTAP pushes the boundaries of point tracking by ingeniously combining the strengths of synthetic data pre-training with the vastness of unlabeled real-world videos. Its success validates a robust self-supervised learning paradigm, offering a powerful tool for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and real-world-ready AI systems across a spectrum of applications.
