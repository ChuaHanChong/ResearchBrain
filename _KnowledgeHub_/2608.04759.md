# Trace, Verify, and Correct: A Training-Free Framework for Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A training-free framework, "Trace, Verify, and Correct," enhances spatial reasoning in multimodal LLMs by systematically locating, verifying, and correcting perceptually unfaithful intermediate spatial judgments against visual input. It achieves an average accuracy improvement of 8.55 percentage points across 15 model–dataset settings and increases Spatial Evidence Faithfulness (SEF) by 8.97% on GQA.

## Problem

- Multimodal LLMs often produce spatial judgments in Chain-of-Thought reasoning that are inconsistent with the visual input.
- These "perceptually unfaithful" intermediate steps propagate errors, leading to lower final answer accuracy.
- Prior approaches primarily focus on overall output improvement, lacking explicit verification of intermediate spatial evidence against visual input.

## Method

- Develops a modular, training-free **"Trace, Verify, and Correct"** framework for MLLMs.
- Structurally represents spatial judgments from Chain-of-Thought as a ==Spatial Evidence Graph (SEG)== for explicit tracing and verification.
- Incorporates a ==Reliability-Aware Spatial Verification (SERA)== module to assess visual evidence quality (existence, localization, depth) before making verification judgments.
- Localizes the earliest reliably contradicted spatial evidence unit in the reasoning chain and prompts the MLLM to regenerate corrections.

## Results

- Achieved an average accuracy improvement of **8.55 percentage points** across 15 model–dataset configurations, with InternVL improving by **12.44 percentage points**.
- Demonstrated increased Spatial Evidence Faithfulness (SEF) from **82.12%** to **92.47%** on RealWorldQA (human evaluation) and improved SEF@100 by **8.97%** on GQA.
- Ablation studies confirmed the crucial contributions of both the Spatial Evidence Graph (SEG) and Reliability-Aware Spatial Verification (SERA) modules, with SERA improving accuracy by **3.92 percentage points** on average.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Perceptual unfaithfulness in MLLM spatial judgments is a systematic issue, not merely stochastic, necessitating explicit verification and independent visual evidence.
> - A strong positive correlation exists between the perceptual faithfulness of intermediate spatial evidence and the accuracy of MLLM final answers.
> - Both structured representation of spatial evidence (SEG) and reliability assessment of visual cues (SERA) are essential for robust, targeted spatial reasoning correction in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated progress across various visual-language tasks, including visual question answering, image understanding, and cross-modal reasoning. Despite these advancements, spatial reasoning within MLLMs remains a notable challenge. A recurring issue is that MLLMs may generate intermediate spatial judgments during Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning that are inconsistent with the visual input. These inconsistencies can lead to errors propagating through the reasoning chain, ultimately affecting the accuracy of the final answer.

Prior research has approached the improvement of MLLM spatial reasoning through two primary avenues:
1. **Training-stage enhancement:** These methods typically involve constructing spatial instruction data or integrating region- and depth-aware representations during the model training phase. This often requires access to model parameters and task-specific adaptation.
2. **Inference-time augmentation:** This category of methods enhances spatial reasoning by introducing auxiliary information such as depth cues, structured scene representations, or 3D priors at the time of inference.

A common characteristic of these existing methods is their "outcome-oriented" nature. They primarily focus on improving the overall output or the generation process without explicitly verifying the perceptual faithfulness of intermediate spatial evidence against the visual input. The present work identifies a gap in this landscape, proposing a "process-oriented" strategy. It aims to address the issue by explicitly locating, verifying, and correcting intermediate spatial evidence within the reasoning chain, thereby ensuring consistency with the visual input. This approach differentiates itself by focusing on the integrity of the reasoning steps themselves, rather than solely on the final answer or input enhancement. It contributes to the broader field of faithful reasoning and verification in MLLMs by specifically extending these concepts to spatial relations and by incorporating a mechanism to assess the reliability of the visual evidence used for verification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a modular and training-free framework for verifying and correcting spatial reasoning in MLLMs, aiming to enhance the perceptual faithfulness of their intermediate reasoning steps and consequently improve final-answer accuracy. The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations and observations in current MLLM capabilities:

- **Problem Statement:** MLLMs often produce intermediate spatial judgments that are inconsistent with the input image. This "perceptual unfaithfulness" allows errors to propagate through the CoT reasoning process, leading to incorrect final answers. This issue is particularly critical in high-stakes applications such as autonomous driving and robotics, where erroneous spatial judgments can have significant consequences.

- **Empirical Observations Supporting the Motivation:**
 - **Perceptually Unfaithful Reasoning and Lower Accuracy:** An analysis comparing reasoning chains generated by InternVL3.5-8B on RealWorldQA revealed a substantial difference in final-answer accuracy. Reasoning chains that were "fully faithful" (all spatial evidence units supported by the image) achieved 51.10% accuracy, whereas "unfaithful" chains (containing at least one contradicted evidence unit) achieved only 34.41% accuracy. This 16.69% difference was statistically significant (p < 0.001), indicating a strong association between perceptual faithfulness and final-answer accuracy. This finding directly motivated the investigation into whether improving faithfulness could enhance accuracy.
 - **Accumulation of Spatial Unfaithfulness:** Further analysis showed that "Spatial Evidence Faithfulness" (SEF) consistently decreases as the reasoning process progresses on datasets like RealWorldQA and GQA. This suggests that unfaithful spatial evidence can arise at various stages and accumulate, emphasizing the need for a mechanism to locate and address these errors throughout the reasoning chain.
 - **Unreliability of Untargeted Self-Revision:** Evaluation of a generic self-revision approach (where the MLLM is asked to reconsider its complete reasoning without explicit error localization or external visual evidence) yielded inconsistent results. While it improved InternVL's performance, it degraded Qwen and Llama. This indicated that MLLM perceptual unfaithfulness is not merely due to stochastic sampling but reflects systematic limitations, necessitating the introduction of independent, reliable visual evidence and explicit error localization for effective correction.

- **Key Challenges in Correcting Unfaithful Reasoning:**
 - **Difficulty in Extracting and Tracing Spatial Evidence Units:** Spatial judgments in free-form CoT can be implicitly expressed or distributed across interdependent reasoning steps. This makes it challenging to accurately identify individual evidence units and trace them back to their original source steps within the CoT for verification.
 - **Unreliable Verification Evidence:** The visual evidence required for verification (e.g., object existence, localization, geometric measurements) can be uncertain due to factors like object occlusion, cluttered backgrounds, ambiguous boundaries, or perception errors. Correcting reasoning based on unreliable evidence risks introducing new errors or altering valid judgments.

To address these motivations and challenges, the research aimed to develop a framework that could systematically extract and trace spatial evidence, assess the reliability of visual verification evidence, localize the earliest reliably contradicted evidence unit, and guide the MLLM to revise its reasoning accordingly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named "Trace, Verify, and Correct," is modular and training-free, designed to locate and rectify perceptually unfaithful spatial evidence units in MLLM reasoning. It operates in four main stages:

1. **Initial CoT Reasoning:**
 Given an image (I) and a question (q), an MLLM first generates an initial Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning chain and a final answer. This initial output serves as the input for the verification process.

2. **Spatial Evidence Graph (SEG) Construction:**
 To enable explicit verification and tracing, spatial judgments from the free-form CoT are converted into a structured representation called the Spatial Evidence Graph (SEG). This involves three steps:
 - **Extraction of Spatial Evidence Units:** The framework prompts an MLLM to parse each step (c_1..., c_n) of the initial reasoning chain and extract atomic spatial evidence units. These units fall into four categories: object existence (e.g., "apple is present"), absolute image position (e.g., "apple is at the center"), 2D relative spatial relations (e.g., "apple is to the right of the bowl"), and relative depth (e.g., "apple is closer than the bowl"). Each unit retains its source step (k_m), enabling traceability. Composite spatial statements are decomposed into atomic units.
 - **Mapping to Graph Structure:** These extracted units are organized into a graph G = (V, E, A). Visual entities (e.g., "apple," "bowl") and an image-reference node constitute the node set V. Directed edges in E represent spatial relations between entities or between an entity and the image-reference node. The attribute set A stores metadata for each unit, including evidence type, source step, and associated visual evidence. Object existence is an entity-node attribute, while positions and relations are directed edges.
 - **Association with Visual Evidence:** The framework binds relevant visual evidence to the SEG:
 - **Grounding DINO:** Used to provide candidate bounding boxes and confidence scores for each entity. An existence score p_yes(o) (image-level probability of entity presence) is computed. The top two raw grounding scores are retained for reliability assessment.
 - **Bounding Box Coordinates:** These are used for absolute position and 2D relative spatial relation units.
 - **SAM2 and Monocular Depth Estimator:** For relative-depth relations, SAM2 generates object masks, and a monocular depth estimator computes depth statistics within these masks. These depth measurements are linked to the corresponding depth-relation edges.

3. **Reliability-Aware Spatial Verification (SERA):**
 SERA addresses the challenge of unreliable visual evidence by assessing its quality before making verification judgments. This process has two main components:
 - **Entity-level Evidence Reliability Assessment:** For each visual entity `o`, SERA evaluates three aspects of its associated visual evidence:
 - **Existence Certainty (R_E(o)):** Calculated as `2 * |p_yes(o) - 0.5|`, reflecting how decisively an object's presence or absence is indicated.
 - **Localization Clarity (R_L(o)):** Measures the confidence of the top-ranked bounding box candidate and its score margin over the second-ranked candidate, indicating ambiguity in localization.
 - **Geometric-Measurement Stability (R_M(o)):** For non-depth units, it's 1. For relative-depth units, it assesses the sufficiency of the segmentation mask area and the consistency (low variance) of depth values within that mask.
 - **Aggregation and Reliability Tiers:** The three reliability components (R_E, R_L, R_M) are aggregated into an entity-level evidence reliability score `g(o)` using a weighted geometric mean. This score categorizes the evidence into three reliability tiers: HIGH (g(o) ≥ 0.75), MEDIUM (0.45 ≤ g(o) < 0.75), and LOW (g(o) < 0.45).
 - **Evidence-Unit-level Spatial Verification:** Each spatial evidence unit from the SEG is then verified against the visual evidence, considering its reliability tier:
 - **Object Existence Units:** Verified directly based on `R_E(o)` and `p_yes(o)`.
 - **Positional and Relational Units:** First, entity existence presuppositions (`J_E(o)`) are checked. If any involved entity is reliably `ABSENT` or `UNCERTAIN`, the unit is `CONTRADICTED` or `INCONCLUSIVE`, respectively. Geometric verification (using bounding box centers, normalized distances, depth statistics) is performed only if all involved entities are reliably `PRESENT` and in the `HIGH` reliability tier. Otherwise, the unit is marked `INCONCLUSIVE`.

4. **Targeted Revision & Answer Regeneration:**
 - **Error Localization:** After verification, the framework identifies the earliest spatial evidence unit in the reasoning chain that is reliably `CONTRADICTED` by the visual evidence.
 - **Verification Report:** A report is generated, highlighting this earliest contradicted unit, its source step, the verification result, and relevant precomputed visual judgments (e.g., Existence, Layout, Frame, Depth judgments). It also includes other `SUPPORTED` and `INCONCLUSIVE` judgments.
 - **Regeneration:** The original MLLM (acting as a "regenerator") is provided with the original image, question, initial reasoning, initial answer, and the comprehensive verification report. The model is prompted to revise the localized error according to the verified spatial judgments in the report, regenerate the affected reasoning from the identified source step, and produce a new final answer. If no reliable contradiction is found, the original reasoning and answer are retained. This process aims to prevent error propagation while preserving correct reasoning segments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the framework's effectiveness, faithfulness, and efficiency.

- **Overall Performance:** The proposed framework demonstrated consistent improvements across multiple MLLMs and datasets.
 - Across 15 model–dataset settings, the method achieved an average accuracy of 68.94%, which constitutes an 8.55 percentage point improvement over the average performance of compared baselines.
 - Specific accuracy gains were observed for different MLLMs: Qwen improved from 64.33% to 70.59%, InternVL from 56.11% to 68.55%, and Llama from 63.55% to 67.68%.
 - These results suggest that explicit verification and correction of intermediate spatial evidence are more effective than solely augmenting initial spatial information.

- **Reasoning Faithfulness:** The framework enhanced the perceptual faithfulness of MLLM reasoning.
 - Both human and automatic evaluations on subsets of RealWorldQA and GQA indicated an increase in Spatial Evidence Faithfulness (SEF), which correlated with improvements in final-answer accuracy. For example, SEF on RealWorldQA increased from 82.12% to 92.47% (human eval), accompanied by an accuracy increase from 45.00% to 57.00%.
 - While vanilla MLLMs showed a decline in SEF as reasoning progressed, the proposed method maintained consistently higher SEF throughout the reasoning process, improving SEF@100 by 8.97% on GQA.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Component Contribution:** Removing either the Spatial Evidence Graph (SEG) or Spatial Evidence Reliability Assessment (SERA) modules resulted in a decrease in average accuracy. Removing SEG reduced accuracy by an average of 2.92 percentage points, and removing SERA reduced it by an average of 3.92 percentage points across the tested backbones. This confirms that both structured evidence organization and reliability-aware verification are crucial for the framework's performance.
 - **SERA Components:** A fine-grained ablation of SERA's components (existence certainty, localization clarity, geometric-measurement stability) showed that each component contributed to the overall accuracy and SEF, with localization clarity having the largest individual impact.
 - **Threshold Sensitivity:** The default reliability thresholds (τh = 0.75, τl = 0.45) for SERA were found to achieve the optimal balance between correction coverage and evidence reliability, yielding the highest SEF and accuracy compared to looser or stricter settings.

- **Human Validation of Intermediate Components:**
 - **Spatial Evidence Extractor:** Human evaluation on randomly sampled CoT responses demonstrated the high quality of the extractor, achieving F1 scores of 91.99% on RealWorldQA and 91.72% on GQA for strict exact-match evaluation.
 - **Automatic Spatial Verifier:** The verifier achieved a 3-way accuracy of approximately 94.85% and a Macro-F1 score of 81.76%, with a decisive accuracy of 95.47% at 91.07% coverage, indicating strong agreement with human judgments.

- **Efficiency and Scope Analysis:**
 - **Inference Efficiency:** The framework incurred higher overall inference latency (approximately 2.81 times that of vanilla MLLM inference). However, the absolute runtime remained within seconds, and this overhead was associated with the largest observed accuracy gain (8.00 percentage points). The method operates without modifying the MLLM's underlying generation or decoding procedures.
 - **Transfer Beyond Spatial Reasoning:** The framework largely preserved or even improved performance on broader multimodal tasks, with a maximum performance decrease of 1.11 percentage points on ScienceQA and varied impacts on MathVista (e.g., Llama improved by 8.22 percentage points).

- **Complementarity with Inference-Time Spatial Augmentation:**
 Combining the proposed process-oriented spatial reasoning correction with an inference-time spatial augmentation method (SpatialPIN) yielded further performance gains. The combination achieved an average accuracy of 65.45% on RealWorldQA, outperforming SpatialPIN alone (56.95%) and the proposed method alone (64.75%). This suggests that enriching spatial information and verifying intermediate reasoning are complementary strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that addresses a fundamental challenge in Multimodal Large Language Models: the generation of perceptually unfaithful spatial reasoning. By focusing on the integrity of the intermediate reasoning steps, the work contributes to improving the reliability and trustworthiness of MLLMs in complex visual-language tasks.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Accuracy:** The framework consistently and substantially improves the final-answer accuracy of MLLMs across various spatial reasoning benchmarks by ensuring that intermediate spatial judgments align with visual input. This direct correlation between perceptual faithfulness and accuracy is a key contribution.
- **Novel Process-Oriented Approach:** Unlike many existing methods that primarily augment input or enhance outputs without scrutinizing the reasoning process, this work introduces a systematic, process-oriented verification and correction mechanism. This shift in focus allows for targeted interventions, preventing the propagation of errors from their earliest points in the reasoning chain.
- **Methodological Advancements:**
 - The **Spatial Evidence Graph (SEG)** provides a structured and traceable representation of spatial evidence within CoT, facilitating precise error localization and enabling detailed analysis of reasoning flows.
 - The **Spatial Evidence Reliability Assessment (SERA)** module offers a principled way to evaluate the quality of visual evidence (object existence, localization, and geometric measurements). This mechanism is crucial for preventing corrections based on uncertain or noisy visual information, thereby enhancing the robustness of the system.
- **Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Nature:** The framework's ability to operate without requiring retraining or fine-tuning of MLLM parameters makes it highly adaptable and practical for integration with various existing and future MLLM backbones. This broad applicability reduces the barriers to deployment.
- **Relevance to High-Stakes Applications:** Improved spatial reasoning faithfulness is particularly impactful for applications requiring high levels of precision and reliability, such as autonomous driving, robotics, and medical image analysis. In these domains, erroneous spatial judgments can lead to substantial safety and economic implications. The framework offers a step towards more dependable MLLM decisions in real-world interactive environments.
- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** The demonstrated complementarity with inference-time spatial augmentation techniques suggests that the framework can be combined with other methods to achieve even greater performance benefits, indicating its potential as a valuable component in a broader MLLM enhancement pipeline.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the reliance on explicit CoT and the focus on static-image QA, also open avenues for future research. This includes exploring methods for identifying implicit spatial evidence, extending the framework to dynamic environments (e.g., video understanding, embodied interaction), and evaluating its effectiveness with proprietary MLLMs.

Overall, this research provides a robust, interpretable, and effective approach to improving spatial reasoning in MLLMs, contributing significantly to the development of more accurate and reliable intelligent systems.
