# Eliciting Weak-to-Strong Generalization with On-Policy Reverse Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> On-Policy Reverse Distillation (OPRD) lets a strong student learn from a weaker RL-post-trained teacher without matching it. On student rollouts it takes the teacher's mean-centered logit shift relative to its pre-RL reference, projects the student's verifier-driven policy gradient onto that unit direction, and amplifies only that component. Stationary points of policy optimization are preserved; the 8B student reaches 51.91 math average, surpassing its 4B teacher and all baselines.

## Problem

- Conventional distillation makes the weak teacher's final policy the optimization target, imposing its capacity ceiling and inherited reference preferences on a higher-capacity student.
- Hybrid schemes that add reinforcement learning keep teacher matching as a separate loss, so policy matching competes with reward maximization instead of reinforcing it.
- Isolating the teacher's post-training policy change still fails if the student is trained to match that change, since it captures only weak-model-realizable improvements.

## Method

- ==Teacher policy shift==: mean-centered difference between post-RL teacher logits and its frozen pre-RL reference logits at each student-visited prefix, normalized to a unit direction, top-10 truncated.
- ==Directional gradient scaling==: decompose the verifier-driven token ==policy gradient== into projection onto the teacher direction plus orthogonal part, amplify the projection by one plus lambda, leave the orthogonal part untouched.
- ==Asymmetric alignment scaling==: positive-alignment branch active immediately, negative-alignment branch ramped over a warm-up horizon, keeping the map invertible so stationarity and nonnegative first-order alignment gain hold.

## Results

- Successive 4B-to-8B transfer: **51.91** math average and **55.18** Reasoning Gym average, beating the strongest baseline by **7.92** and **10.80** points respectively.
- Multi-teacher consolidation of four 4B specialists into one 8B student reaches **58.77** average, exceeding Mix-RL by **11.09** points and the specialist average by **14.12**.
- Strong-to-weak also improves, beating on-policy distillation by **3.79** points on AIME'24 and **29.20** on Knights & Knaves; lambda ablation saturates near **0.5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rescaling a learner's own gradient along a teacher-derived direction transfers guidance without ever making the teacher's policy an optimization target or performance ceiling.
> - A weak teacher at 29% task accuracy still bootstrapped a student to roughly 88%, so supervisor quality sets acceleration rate, not the student's attainable limit.
> - The method needs nonzero verifier advantage, so degenerate reward groups or reference policies carrying length bias can stall or distort the correction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of knowledge distillation (KD) for large language models (LLMs), specifically focusing on the "weak-to-strong generalization" paradigm. Traditional KD methods, such as On-Policy Distillation (OPD), involve transferring knowledge from a teacher model to a student model by optimizing the student to match the teacher's policy, often on student-generated sequences to mitigate distribution mismatch. This approach is effective when the goal is to align the student with a more capable teacher or to consolidate specific capabilities.

However, a key limitation arises in scenarios where the teacher model is weaker or has lower capacity than the student. In such "weak-to-strong" settings, simply optimizing the student to match the weak teacher can inadvertently impose the teacher's capacity limitations or its specific behavioral biases, thereby preventing the stronger student from achieving its full potential. This problem is particularly relevant for:
1. **Successive Model Transfer:** Utilizing a post-trained model from an earlier generation to supervise a larger, subsequent model. This aims to transfer prior post-training gains efficiently without repeating costly training from scratch.
2. **Multi-Domain Consolidation:** Integrating capabilities from multiple smaller, domain-specialized policies (teachers) into a single, unified foundation model. This allows for efficient iteration on reward functions and environments for individual domains.

Prior work in weak-to-strong generalization has explored various methods, including using weak labels, preferences, or fixed reasoning trajectories to supervise stronger students. Some methods refine student representations or extend coverage beyond the weak teacher, but often recover only a partial gap to strong supervision. Hybrid methods that combine teacher matching with reinforcement learning (RL) also exist, but often retain teacher matching as a separate objective, leading to potential competition with reward maximization. The present work positions itself by introducing a method that aims to exploit the *policy changes* learned by a weak teacher, rather than its final policy, to accelerate a stronger student's own reward-driven optimization, thereby enabling the student to surpass the teacher's performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method that enables stronger models to learn effectively from weaker supervisors and ultimately outperform them, a concept termed "weak-to-strong generalization." This objective is motivated by several practical and theoretical considerations in modern foundation model development:

- **Cost-Efficiency of Post-Training:** Developing frontier-scale language models involves extensive and expensive post-training processes (e.g., reinforcement learning from human feedback or verifiable rewards). Repeating these processes from scratch for every successive model generation or for integrating multiple specialized models becomes prohibitively costly and time-consuming. There is a need for efficient mechanisms to transfer and leverage post-training gains across models of different scales or specializations.
- **Limitations of Conventional Knowledge Distillation:** Standard knowledge distillation techniques, including on-policy distillation (OPD), are designed to optimize a student model to *match* the policy of a teacher. In a weak-to-strong setting, this presents a fundamental limitation: by matching the weak teacher's final policy, the student inherits not only the teacher's learned improvements but also its capacity constraints, inherent biases, and potentially suboptimal behaviors. This can create a "capacity ceiling" that prevents the stronger student from exploring and achieving higher performance levels that are within its capabilities but beyond the teacher's.
- **Ineffectiveness of Direct Policy Change Matching:** Even if one were to isolate the *change* in policy learned by the weak teacher during its post-training (its "policy shift"), directly optimizing the student to match this shift is also problematic. Such a shift reflects only the improvements realized by the *weak teacher* within its limited policy class. It does not encompass the full range of potentially better solutions accessible to a stronger student. Therefore, making this shift an independent optimization target would still impose a sub-optimal constraint on the student.
- **Competition in Hybrid RL-Distillation Methods:** Existing hybrid approaches combine teacher matching with reinforcement learning. While these methods provide dense teacher supervision alongside outcome-based optimization, the teacher-matching loss typically remains a distinct objective. This can create a tension or competition with the reward maximization objective, potentially hindering the student's ability to maximize rewards if the teacher's favored solutions do not align perfectly with higher verifier rewards for the stronger student.
- **Need for Accelerated Optimization Beyond Teacher Capacity:** The motivation is to find a mechanism that uses the weak teacher's post-training gains to *accelerate* the stronger student's own verifier-driven optimization, rather than defining the student's optimization target. This would allow the student to benefit from the teacher's guidance without being bounded by the teacher's specific policy or capacity.

In summary, the key objective is to facilitate efficient "reverse distillation" where post-training knowledge from weaker models can be effectively leveraged to bootstrap and accelerate the training of more capable students, enabling them to *surpass* their supervisors in performance, particularly in contexts of model succession and multi-domain integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, On-Policy Reverse Distillation (OPRD), aims to address the limitations of conventional distillation in weak-to-strong settings by using a weak teacher's policy shift to accelerate a stronger student's own reward-driven optimization. The approach is described as follows:

- **On-Policy Nature:** OPRD operates on rollouts generated by the student policy (`π_θ`), ensuring that supervision is provided at the prefixes the student actually visits during inference. This mitigates the distribution mismatch common in off-policy distillation.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) Foundation:** The student's primary optimization is based on RLVR, which uses rewards from programmatic verifiers (e.g., exact-answer checks, code execution). The token-level policy gradient (`g_t = A_t ∇_z_t log π_θ(y_t | s_t)`) drives the student towards higher rewards, where `A_t` is the advantage and `z_t` are logits at prefix `s_t`.
- **Teacher Policy Shift (Δ_t):**
 - **Definition:** To extract the specific policy change learned by the weak teacher during its post-training, OPRD calculates the mean-centered difference between the next-token logit vectors of the frozen RL-trained teacher (`z_T(s_t)`) and its frozen pre-RL reference policy (`z_ref_T(s_t)`).
 - **Formula:** `Δ_t := 𝒞(z_T(s_t) - z_ref_T(s_t))`, where `𝒞(v)` denotes mean-centering. This operation removes common offsets, focusing on relative token preference changes.
 - **Purpose:** `Δ_t` captures the alterations in the teacher's next-token preferences that resulted from its post-training, excluding aspects inherited from its initial state or constrained by its capacity.
 - **Guidance Direction (d_t):** Instead of directly optimizing towards `Δ_t`, OPRD uses only its unit direction: `d_t := Δ_t / ‖Δ_t‖_2`. This prevents the student from being limited by the magnitude or specific realization of the weak teacher's shift, focusing solely on the *direction* of improvement.
- **Gradient Scaling along the Teacher Direction:**
 - **Decomposition:** At each token `t`, the student's policy gradient `g_t` is decomposed into two components relative to `d_t`: a projected component `Proj_d_t(g_t) := u_t d_t` (where `u_t := d_t^⊤ g_t` is the alignment coefficient) and an orthogonal component `g_⊥_t := g_t - Proj_d_t(g_t)`.
 - **Transformation:** OPRD transforms the student's policy gradient `g_t` into `~g_t` by amplifying only the projected component: `~g_t := (1 + λ_t) Proj_d_t(g_t) + g_⊥_t`. The orthogonal component remains unchanged.
 - **Backpropagation:** The transformed gradient `~g_t` is used for backpropagation to update the student's parameters.
- **Learning Beyond the Weak Teacher (Theoretical Properties):**
 - **Stationarity Preservation:** The transformation `~g_t = (I + λ_t d_t d_t^⊤) g_t` is invertible. This implies that `~g_t = 0` if and only if `g_t = 0`. Consequently, OPRD preserves the stationary points of the original verifier-driven policy optimization objective. This is crucial because it means the student's ultimate performance is determined by the verifier objective and its own policy capacity, not bounded by the teacher's.
 - **Alignment Gain:** The inner product `⟨g_t, ~g_t⟩ = ‖g_t‖_2^2 + λ_t u_t^2 ≥ ‖g_t‖_2^2`. This shows that the transformed gradient retains the first-order progress of the original gradient and adds a non-negative "alignment gain" proportional to `λ_t u_t^2`. Greater alignment magnitude (`|u_t|`) leads to faster first-order progress.
- **Asymmetric Alignment Scaling:**
 - **Rationale:** Early in training, when student rollouts are weak, teacher-opposing (`u_t < 0`) components of `g_t` can be less reliable.
 - **Schedule:** The scaling coefficient `λ_t` is set asymmetrically:
 - `λ_t = λ` when `u_t ≥ 0` (positive alignment: amplifies components shared by teacher shift and verifier-driven gradient). This is active from the outset.
 - `λ_t = λ min(k/K_warm, 1)` when `u_t < 0` (negative alignment: amplifies verifier-supported departures from teacher direction). This is gradually ramped up over `K_warm` optimization steps.
 - **Effect:** This schedule prioritizes rapid teacher-aligned transfer early on while progressively strengthening verifier-supported departures, allowing the student to move beyond the weak teacher as its own rollouts become more reliable.
- **Practical Considerations:** A top-10 truncation under the student policy is applied to the teacher's policy shift (`Δ_t`) to focus the correction on the student's high-probability vocabulary region.

In essence, OPRD guides the student's reward-driven learning by amplifying its own policy gradient along directions that align with the weak teacher's learned policy shift, while ensuring that the student is not constrained to merely imitate the teacher.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across different experimental settings, demonstrating the effectiveness of On-Policy Reverse Distillation (OPRD) in eliciting weak-to-strong generalization.

### 5. Weak-to-Strong Distillation for a Successor Model (4B Teacher → 8B Student)

- **Performance Gains:** OPRD consistently achieves higher performance with fewer student updates compared to baseline methods (GRPO, OPD, KDRL).
 - On mathematical reasoning tasks (AIME'24, AIME'25, HMMT'25, OlympiadBench), OPRD achieves an average Mean@16 of **51.91%**, surpassing the strongest baseline (KDRL at 43.99%) by **7.92 percentage points**. The weak teacher's average performance was 38.66%.
 - On diverse logical reasoning tasks (Reasoning Gym: Knights & Knaves, Quantum Lock, String Manipulation, Countdown), OPRD achieves an average Pass@1 of **55.18%**, outperforming KDRL (44.38%) by **10.80 percentage points**. The weak teacher's average was 44.65%.
- **Acceleration and Beyond-Teacher Performance:** OPRD matches OPD's initial rapid improvement but, unlike OPD which plateaus near the weak teacher's performance, OPRD continues to improve, surpassing the weak teacher and eventually reaching GRPO's end-of-training performance substantially earlier.
 - It reaches weak-teacher performance with **33–67% fewer student updates** than GRPO.

### 6. Multi-Teacher Weak-to-Strong Distillation (Four 4B Specialists → One 8B Student)

- **Consolidation without Tradeoffs:** OPRD successfully consolidates capabilities from four domain-specialized weak teachers into a single stronger student model, outperforming all individual specialists.
 - The OPRD student achieves an average Pass@1 of **58.77%**, which is **11.09 percentage points higher than Mix-RL (47.68%)** and **14.12 percentage points higher than the average of the four specialist teachers (44.65%)**.
 - It reaches teacher-level performance with **55% fewer updates** than Mix-RL, indicating improved sample efficiency.
 - OPRD outperforms the corresponding specialist on all four tasks, suggesting joint improvement rather than cross-task interference.

### 7. Strong-to-Weak Distillation (8B Teacher → 1.7B/0.6B Student)

- **Generalizability of OPRD:** OPRD's effectiveness is not limited to weak-to-strong settings. In conventional strong-to-weak distillation:
 - On AIME'24 (8B teacher → 1.7B student), OPRD achieves **33.58% Mean@16**, outperforming OPD (29.79%) by **3.79 points**.
 - On Knights & Knaves (8B teacher → 0.6B student), OPRD achieves **49.40% Pass@1**, significantly surpassing OPD (20.20%) by **29.20 points**.
- This demonstrates that OPRD effectively combines verifier-driven policy optimization with teacher guidance regardless of the capacity ordering, and can overcome issues where standard OPD saturates.

### 8. Broader Comparison with Weak-to-Strong Methods

- OPRD consistently outperforms other weak-to-strong methods, including those relying on off-policy generations (W2SR-P, S2L-PO, OPSD) and those directly optimizing the weak policy delta (Direct-OPD, W2S-OPD).
 - OPRD achieves an average score of **60.81%** across AIME'24, Knights & Knaves, and String Manipulation, exceeding the strongest alternative (S2L-PO at 54.21%) by **6.60 points**.
 - It particularly outperforms methods directly optimizing the weak policy delta (W2S-OPD at 49.22% and Direct-OPD at 37.81%), indicating that *rescaling* the verifier gradient with the teacher shift is more effective than making the shift an independent optimization target.

### 9. Analysis of Teacher Guidance Design and Dynamics

- **Teacher Quality:** Better-trained weak teacher checkpoints lead to faster learning under OPRD, suggesting that more informative teacher shifts provide better guidance direction.
- **Guidance Direction:** The weak policy delta (difference between post-trained teacher and its reference) is found to be the most effective scaling direction compared to OPD teacher-matching gradients or OPSD self-distillation gradients.
- **Amplification Strength (λ):** Values of `λ > 0` consistently improve final performance over `λ = 0` (GRPO), with larger `λ` values (up to 0.5) yielding faster early gains.
- **Teacher Influence Over Time:** Early in training, the teacher guidance direction (`d_t`) and student policy gradient (`g_t`) exhibit substantial alignment. As training progresses, this alignment decreases towards orthogonality, indicating that the student gradient increasingly follows verifier-supported directions not captured by the teacher shift, and teacher guidance becomes less influential.

### 10. Discussion of Key Challenges and Mitigations

- **Vanishing Policy Gradients:** OPRD's teacher-guided correction is limited when the student's rollouts are so poor that most receive identical rewards, leading to vanishing verifier-driven policy gradients.
- **Length Bias:** Teacher shifts can sometimes encode reward-irrelevant biases, such as preferences for certain response lengths. Selecting a later teacher checkpoint as the *reference policy* (instead of the raw base model) can mitigate this bias by excluding early, sharp length transitions from the policy shift, leading to improved task performance (e.g., on Color Cube and Binary Matrix).

### 11. Student Behavior Under Teacher Guidance

- **Reasoning Paths:** OPRD enables students to move beyond the teacher's specific reasoning paths, allowing the stronger student to discover and follow its own, more direct solutions even when the teacher shift favors an alternative, valid detour.
- **Response Style:** OPRD students' response styles (across connectives, modality, grammar, punctuation, structure) remain closer to a natively GRPO-trained strong student than to the weak teacher. This suggests that OPRD accelerates the student's own optimization without forcing it to imitate the teacher's stylistic traits.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of On-Policy Reverse Distillation (OPRD) represents a significant advancement in the efficient and effective training of large language models, particularly in scenarios involving weak-to-strong generalization. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Efficient Knowledge Transfer from Weaker Models:** OPRD provides a practical and effective mechanism to transfer post-training gains from smaller or weaker models to larger, more capable ones. This addresses a critical limitation of traditional distillation methods, which tend to cap the student's performance at the teacher's capacity.
- **Cost-Effective LLM Development:** By allowing stronger models to inherit and build upon prior post-training efforts from weaker predecessors, OPRD offers a path to reduce the prohibitive computational and financial costs associated with retraining frontier-scale models from scratch for each successive generation or for consolidating domain-specific capabilities. This can accelerate the development cycle of LLMs.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that OPRD achieves higher performance with substantially fewer student updates compared to pure reinforcement learning (GRPO/Mix-RL) and conventional on-policy distillation (OPD/MOPD). This improvement in sample efficiency is crucial for large models where each training step is expensive.
- **Facilitating Multi-Domain Consolidation:** OPRD offers an effective strategy for integrating capabilities from multiple specialized models into a single, unified model without significant cross-task interference. This could lead to the development of more versatile and general-purpose foundation models from modular, efficiently trained specialists.
- **Generalizability Across Capacity Orderings:** The method's effectiveness in both weak-to-strong and strong-to-weak distillation scenarios indicates its broad applicability, suggesting it is a robust approach for combining verifier-driven optimization with teacher guidance regardless of relative model scales.
- **Advancing Weak-to-Strong Generalization Research:** OPRD contributes a novel algorithmic approach to the weak-to-strong generalization problem by emphasizing the *direction* of a teacher's policy shift to *rescale* a student's own gradient, rather than directly imposing the teacher's policy or policy change as an optimization target. This approach allows the student to surpass the teacher while maintaining its distinct reasoning and stylistic characteristics.
- **Potential for Recursive Self-Improvement:** The findings lay groundwork for future research into recursive self-improvement loops, where successive model generations could leverage OPRD to directly contribute their post-training policy shifts to accelerate the development of even more capable successors. This could enable an iterative process of model enhancement.
- **Insights into Teacher Guidance:** The analyses provide valuable insights into how teacher guidance operates, including the importance of teacher quality, the selection of the guidance direction, the dynamics of alignment between teacher and student gradients, and the management of potential biases like response length. These insights can inform the design of future knowledge transfer mechanisms.
- **Broader Applications:** While evaluated in mathematical and logical reasoning, the principles of OPRD could potentially extend to other domains involving verifiable rewards, such as code generation or complex agentic environments, where efficiently transferring learned behaviors is beneficial.
