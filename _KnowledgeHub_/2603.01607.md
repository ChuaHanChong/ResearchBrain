# CARE: Towards Clinical Accountability in Multi-Modal Medical Reasoning with an Evidence-Grounded Agentic Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> The CARE framework by Microsoft Research Asia and Yale University introduces an evidence-grounded agentic approach for multi-modal medical reasoning, enhancing clinical accountability by decomposing diagnostic tasks into specialized sub-modules and coordinating them. This framework achieved an average accuracy of 77.54% on VQA benchmarks, outperforming larger generalist models and showing improved generalizability on out-of-domain data.

## Problem

- Existing multi-modal medical visual language models (VLMs) operate as "black boxes," lacking transparency and verifiability, which deviates from established clinical diagnostic workflows.
- Monolithic VLM designs are prone to shortcut learning and confident hallucinations, particularly when relying on superficial cues rather than fine-grained, explicit visual evidence.
- Prior attempts at visual grounding often treat it as an isolated perception head or couple perception and reasoning within single generalist models, leading to data scarcity issues, error propagation, and computational intensity.

## Method

- CARE decomposes medical reasoning into a three-stage ==agentic workflow==: medical entity proposal, entity referring segmentation, and evidence-grounded visual question answering (EG-VQA).
- It employs specialized expert models for each sub-task, including a compact VLM fine-tuned with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for entity proposal and a SAM-style segmenter for precise pixel-level evidence.
- An agentic coordinator (CARE-Coord) dynamically plans tool invocation sequences, selects the most informative evidence views, and iteratively reviews the consistency between reasoning (Chain-of-Thought) and answers to mitigate hallucinations.

## Results

- CARE-Coord-B achieved an average accuracy of **77.54%** across four medical VQA benchmarks, surpassing the Lingshu-**32B** model by **5.2%**.
- The framework demonstrated parameter efficiency, with CARE-Flow-B (**10B** parameters) outperforming Lingshu-**32B** (**32B** parameters) by **2.6%** in average accuracy.
- Ablation studies confirmed that visual clues improved accuracy by **2.5%**, and the dynamic coordinator's iterative review process contributed a net positive of **1.79%** accuracy by successfully correcting wrong answers in **4.84%** of cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex medical reasoning into an evidence-grounded, multi-stage agentic workflow significantly enhances transparency and aligns AI operations with human clinical diagnostic methodologies.
> - Explicitly integrating high-quality, pixel-level visual evidence (e.g., zoom-in, masks) from specialized segmentation models directly into the reasoning pipeline improves diagnostic accuracy and robustness.
> - A dynamic coordinator capable of planning tool use and iteratively reviewing reasoning and answers effectively mitigates confident hallucinations and improves generalization to out-of-domain medical data.
