# Generating Human Interaction Motions in Scenes with Text Control

> [!summary] Summary
> TeSMo generates text-controlled, scene-aware human motion via diffusion models: a scene-agnostic text-to-motion model is pre-trained then fine-tuned with an added scene-aware branch (2D floor maps for navigation, 3D object BPS features for interaction), producing realistic navigation and object interactions preferred 71.9% over prior work.

## Problem

- Prior text-to-motion diffusion methods generate characters in isolation, ignoring scene geometry and object interactions entirely.
- Paired human-scene motion datasets with text annotations are scarce, limiting VAE/diffusion methods to small-scope, low-diversity interactions without text control.
- Reinforcement-learning approaches for scene interactions require tedious reward engineering and struggle with accurate goal-pose reaching due to error accumulation in autoregressive rollout.

## Method

- Two-stage navigation model (**TeSMo**): a ==root-trajectory diffusion model== conditioned on start/end goal poses and text, fine-tuned with a scene-aware branch that encodes a 2D floor map via ==ResNet-18==, then lifted to full-body motion via ==PriorMDM in-painting==.
- Object-driven interaction model: single-stage full-body diffusion model fine-tuned with an object-aware branch using ==Basis Point Sets== to encode object geometry and human-object distance relationships.
- ==Test-time guidance== objectives (goal-reaching and collision ==SDF losses==) perturb the denoised prediction at each step, plus new Loco-3D-FRONT dataset and augmented, text-annotated SAMP data with diverse object shapes for scene-aware fine-tuning.

## Results

- Navigation root-trajectory goal-reaching position error of **16.9cm** beats OmniControl (**122.6cm**) and GMD (**37.4cm**), with full-body motion FID of **20.465** competitive with baselines and best foot-skating ratio of **0.056**.
- Interaction model reduces object penetration ratio to **0.0611** versus DIMOS's **0.1076** and achieves lower goal-reaching position error (**14.45cm** vs **20.20cm**).
- User study of 30 raters over 70 videos preferred TeSMo's interactions **71.9%** of the time over DIMOS; ablations show single-stage training and alternative in-painting both degrade goal-reaching accuracy substantially.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling a strong scene-agnostic motion prior from a fine-tuned scene-conditioning branch, rather than training scene-awareness end-to-end, preserves text-following realism while adding environmental grounding.
> - Decomposing scene interaction into navigation (coarse, map-based) then interaction (fine, object-based) sub-problems lets each stage use the representation best suited to its spatial scale.
> - Synthetic data augmentation, placing real motions into diverse scenes/objects, can substitute for scarce paired human-scene-text datasets without sacrificing motion realism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic human movements, especially those interacting with 3D scenes and controllable by natural language, is a long-standing challenge and a highly active area in computer graphics, computer vision, and artificial intelligence. This work, TeSMo, addresses a critical gap in this landscape.

Historically, human motion synthesis has focused on various aspects:
- **Locomotion:** Generating walking, running, and other basic movements. Early methods included motion graphs and phase-functioned neural networks, but these often lacked adaptability to diverse environments.
- **Human-Object Interaction (HOI):** Synthesizing motions where humans manipulate or interact with specific objects (e.g., grasping, sitting). These approaches often relied on small, specialized datasets and struggled with generalization to novel objects or complex scenes.
- **Scene-Aware Motion Generation:** Efforts to make character movements responsive to the environment, such as navigating around obstacles. A major hurdle here has been the scarcity of high-quality datasets that pair 3D human motion with detailed 3D scene and object geometry, often leading to models with limited diversity or realism. Prior works sometimes used two-stage methods (path planning then full-body motion) or relied on noisy data from monocular RGB-D videos. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise for learning interaction motions from limited supervision but often requires tedious reward function design and struggles with generalization.

More recently, **Diffusion Models** have emerged as a powerful paradigm for generative AI, demonstrating remarkable capabilities in generating diverse and high-quality data across various domains, including images, video, and human motion.
- **Text-to-Motion:** Several diffusion models have successfully generated human motion from text prompts, allowing users to control stylistic aspects (e.g., "walk happily"). However, these methods primarily focus on characters in isolation, without considering environmental context.
- **Spatially Controllable Motion:** Some diffusion models have incorporated spatial constraints, such as desired joint positions or trajectories, but still mostly within scene-agnostic settings or for simpler, non-interactive scenarios.
- **Scene-Conditioned Diffusion:** A few diffusion-based approaches have begun to incorporate scene information, but these often deal with 2D trajectories on maps or suffer from the limitations of noisy training data for full 3D scenes. Others address single-object interactions but may lack text control.

TeSMo carves out a unique and important niche by **integrating text-conditioned control with comprehensive scene-awareness for both navigation and object interaction within complex 3D scenes**. It directly tackles the critical problem of dataset scarcity by proposing an innovative **pre-training and fine-tuning strategy**. By first leveraging large, scene-agnostic motion-capture datasets for learning robust motion priors and text correlations, and then fine-tuning with specifically augmented, scene-aware data, TeSMo achieves a level of realism, diversity, and controllability that surpasses previous methods. It addresses the "characters in isolation" limitation of many text-to-motion models and the "limited scope/diversity" issues of many scene-aware methods, positioning itself as a significant advancement towards truly intelligent and interactive virtual characters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a method that can generate realistic and diverse human movements that naturally interact with 3D scenes, while simultaneously offering granular control over the motion's style and intent through natural language text prompts. This addresses a fundamental need in various applications.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

1. **Integrate Scene-Awareness into Controllable Motion Generation:** The primary objective is to move beyond generating motions for isolated characters. For a character to exist plausibly in a virtual world, it must be able to perceive and respond to its environment. This means navigating around obstacles, interacting appropriately with objects (e.g., sitting on a chair, opening a door), and adapting its movements to the terrain. Prior text-to-motion models largely ignored this crucial aspect, producing motions that would often clip through or float above scene elements.
2. **Enable Text-Based Control for Scene-Aware Motions:** While some scene-aware motion generation methods exist, they often lack intuitive user control. Text prompts offer a powerful, high-level interface for animators, developers, and users to specify desired behaviors and stylistic nuances (e.g., "walk casually to the table," "sit down gracefully"). The goal is to combine the expressive power of text with the necessity of scene compliance.
3. **Address Data Scarcity Challenges:** A significant bottleneck in developing robust scene-aware human motion models is the lack of large-scale, high-quality datasets that comprehensively pair 3D human motion, detailed text descriptions, and corresponding 3D scene/object geometry. Existing datasets are either large but scene-agnostic, or scene-aware but small, lacking diversity, text annotations, or high-fidelity motion. TeSMo aims to overcome this by proposing clever data augmentation and training strategies.
4. **Decompose Complex Human-Scene Interactions:** Realistic interactions often involve a sequence of behaviors, such as navigating to an object and then performing an action with it. The objective is to modularize this complex task into manageable components (navigation and interaction) that can be individually tackled and then combined, allowing for more robust and generalizable solutions.
5. **Enhance Realism and Plausibility:** Beyond just generating motion, the goal is to produce motions that are physically plausible, avoid interpenetrations with objects, and appear natural to human observers. This includes accurate foot-ground contact and appropriate body-object contact during interactions.
6. **Increase Diversity of Generated Motions:** Generic motions are often insufficient. Applications require a range of motion styles and variations, even for the same basic action. Text prompts are leveraged to drive this diversity.

The motivation for achieving these objectives is driven by a wide array of applications:
- **Gaming and Film:** Character animators require powerful tools to author complex, realistic, and stylistically controllable movements for virtual characters in rich interactive environments, drastically reducing manual effort.
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Developing AI agents that can navigate and interact with the physical world requires models that can generate human-like behaviors, enabling more natural human-robot interaction and realistic simulations for training.
- **Virtual Reality and Simulations:** Populating virtual worlds with intelligent, interactive avatars that respond naturally to their environment and user commands enhances immersion and utility.
- **Digital Humans and Avatars:** Creating highly realistic digital doubles or avatars for various purposes, from virtual assistants to remote collaboration.

In essence, TeSMo is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of human motion generation towards truly intelligent, context-aware, and user-controllable virtual characters, bridging the gap between isolated movement and integrated, interactive behavior in 3D scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TeSMo's methodology is built upon a novel combination of denoising diffusion models, hierarchical decomposition, and strategic data augmentation to achieve text-controlled, scene-aware human motion generation. The core idea is to leverage the power of large-scale, scene-agnostic pre-training before fine-tuning with scene-specific information using an augmented control branch.

The approach decomposes the problem into two distinct but interconnected components: **Navigation Motion Generation** and **Object-Driven Interaction Motion Generation**. Both components utilize diffusion models with a shared underlying principle of augmented controllability.

#### 3.1. Background: Controllable Human Motion Diffusion Models

TeSMo builds on the success of diffusion models for motion generation. These models learn to iteratively denoise a sequence of poses, transforming random noise into a coherent motion sequence conditioned on various signals (e.g., text). The key insight adapted from image diffusion (ControlNet) is to augment a pre-trained, frozen base diffusion model with a new branch that takes in additional conditioning signals (like scene information). This allows the model to adapt to new tasks (scene-awareness) without forgetting its learned prior on realistic motion and text correlation. Test-time guidance is also employed to enforce specific objectives like collision avoidance and goal-reaching.

#### 3.2. Navigation Motion Generation

The first stage focuses on enabling characters to reach a goal location in a scene, avoiding obstacles, with text-controlled locomotion. This is designed as a hierarchical method:

1. **Root Trajectory Generation:**
 - **Representation:** The model generates a sequence of 2D pelvis positions (x, z) and orientation (θ), along with height (y), represented in absolute coordinates. This absolute representation is crucial for precise goal-reaching.
 - **Pre-training (Scene-agnostic):** A text-conditioned diffusion model is initially trained on locomotion sequences from the HumanML3D dataset. This large dataset allows it to learn diverse, realistic locomotion behaviors and their correlation with text prompts, but without any scene context. Goal-reaching constraints are emphasized by directly masking and providing clean start and end poses to the model during training.
 - **Scene-aware Fine-tuning:** An augmented control branch (similar to ControlNet) is introduced. This branch takes a 2D floor map `M` (a bird's-eye view of walkable regions extracted from the 3D scene geometry) as input. A ResNet encoder processes this map, and the resulting features are queried by the 2D pelvis positions of the noisy motion sequence. This branch is fine-tuned on top of the frozen pre-trained base model, allowing it to adapt motion generation to scene geometry.
 - **Scene-aware Training Data:** To facilitate scene-aware fine-tuning, the authors create **Loco-3D-FRONT**. This dataset is generated by "integrating" locomotion sequences from HumanML3D into diverse 3D environments from 3D-FRONT, carefully placing motions to avoid collisions. This augmentation allows for training on motions with corresponding text and scene context.
 - **Test-time Guidance:** To further improve scene compliance and goal accuracy during inference, collision avoidance guidance (using 2D SDF values from the floor map) and goal-reaching guidance (L2 error to the target pelvis pose) are applied to the predicted clean motion.
 - **Added Control with Trajectory Blending:** Users can optionally provide a 2D reference path (e.g., from an A* algorithm or drawn manually). The model's generated trajectory can then be smoothly blended with this input path, offering flexible control over the route while maintaining realism.
2. **Lifting to Full-body Poses:** The generated 2D root trajectory (with height and orientation) is then "lifted" into a full-body motion. For this, TeSMo leverages an existing, powerful text-to-motion in-painting model, PriorMDM. This model takes the dense 2D root trajectory as input and generates the full-body motion, retaining text-based stylistic control. This two-stage approach allows the root model to focus on global scene navigation and goal-reaching, while the in-painting model ensures detailed, realistic full-body movements.

#### 3.3. Object-Driven Interaction Motion Generation

Once the character has navigated near the target object, the second stage generates the specific interaction motion. Due to the intricate relationship between the body and object during interactions, this is designed as a single-stage full-body motion generation model.

1. **Interaction Motion Generation:**
 - **Representation:** This model uses an extended full-body pose representation, including absolute pelvis position/heading, root angular/linear velocities, root height, foot contacts, and local joint positions, velocities, and rotations. This rich representation is crucial for complex interactions.
 - **Pre-training (Scene-agnostic):** A diffusion model is pre-trained on a mix of HumanML3D and SAMP (Stochastic Scene-Aware Motion Prediction) datasets, initially without object information. Similar to navigation, it uses masking for start full-body pose and end pelvis goal to guide motion.
 - **Object-aware Fine-tuning:** A separate transformer encoder branch is augmented to incorporate 3D object information. The input to this branch includes:
 - **Object Geometry:** Represented using Basis Point Sets (BPS), which capture the distance from sampled points around the object to its surface.
 - **Human-Object Relationship:** Calculated as the minimum distance from each BPS point to any body joint at each timestep.
 These features are concatenated with the noisy pose representation and fed into the object-aware branch, which is fine-tuned on top of the frozen base model.
 - **Scene-aware Training Data:** The authors augment the existing SAMP dataset, which contains sitting/standing interactions. They diversify object geometry by randomly selecting chairs from 3D-FRONT that match contact points from original SAMP motions. Crucially, they extract specific sub-sequences of interactions (e.g., "walk then sit," "stand up from sitting") and manually annotate them with detailed text descriptions, increasing both skill diversity and text-conditioning capability.
 - **Test-time Guidance:** Similar to navigation, goal-reaching guidance (for the final pelvis pose) and collision avoidance guidance (using 3D SDF for the object and body vertices) are applied during inference to minimize interpenetration and improve goal accuracy.

In summary, TeSMo ingeniously combines pre-trained powerful generative models with targeted fine-tuning using carefully curated and augmented scene-aware datasets. The modular design, coupled with robust diffusion model architecture and test-time guidance, enables high-quality, text-controllable, and scene-compliant human motion generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TeSMo demonstrates its superior performance in generating realistic, diverse, and controllable human motions that interact plausibly with 3D scenes, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Navigation Motion Generation

- **Goal-Reaching and Collision Avoidance:** TeSMo achieves significantly better goal-reaching accuracy for the root trajectory compared to all baselines (GMD, OmniControl, TRACE, and even a 1-stage variant of its own). For instance, it records a root position error of 0.169m, substantially lower than OmniControl's 1.226m and GMD's 0.374m. Its collision ratio is low (0.031), indicating effective obstacle avoidance.
- **Full-Body Motion Quality (after In-painting):** When the generated root trajectory is lifted to full-body motion using PriorMDM, TeSMo maintains competitive performance across standard motion quality metrics. It achieves an FID score of 20.465, R-precision (top-3) of 0.376, and Diversity of 6.415, comparable to or better than other diffusion-based scene-agnostic models like GMD and OmniControl. Crucially, it exhibits the best foot skating ratio (0.056), signifying more physically plausible ground interaction.
- **Architectural Justification:** Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of TeSMo's two-branch (scene-agnostic pre-training + scene-aware fine-tuning) architecture. Training the full architecture from scratch ("Ours (1-stage train)") or using a single-branch design (like TRACE) leads to degraded goal-reaching and full-body motion quality, highlighting the benefits of the proposed hierarchical and fine-tuning strategy.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visually, TeSMo generates diverse locomotion styles (e.g., "a person walks like a zombie") that accurately navigate scenes, reach specified goals, and avoid obstacles, a significant improvement over baselines that often ignore the environment or fail to reach the goal precisely (Fig. 5).

#### 4.2. Object-Driven Interaction Motion Generation

- **Goal-Reaching Accuracy:** TeSMo significantly outperforms DIMOS, a state-of-the-art reinforcement learning approach for human-scene interaction, in goal-reaching accuracy for interaction motions. It achieves lower errors in pelvis position (0.1445m vs. 0.2020m), height (0.0120m vs. 0.1283m), and orientation (0.2410 rad vs. 0.4731 rad). This is particularly notable as DIMOS requires a full-body goal pose, whereas TeSMo only uses a pelvis goal.
- **Object Penetration:** TeSMo drastically reduces interpenetration between the human body and the interaction object. It shows a much lower average penetration value (0.0043 vs. 0.0193) and penetration ratio (0.0611 vs. 0.1076) compared to DIMOS, indicating more physically plausible interactions.
- **User Study Preference:** In a perceptual user study involving 30 individuals, TeSMo's generated interactions were overwhelmingly preferred (71.9% preference) over those produced by DIMOS, based on realism and lack of interpenetration.
- **Qualitative Performance:** TeSMo generates more realistic human-object interactions with reduced "floating" or "penetrating" issues when sitting on chairs. It also demonstrates the ability to generate diverse interaction styles (e.g., "sit down and stretch out his legs," "cross his arms") based on text prompts, a capability lacking in DIMOS (Fig. 6).

#### 4.3. Analysis of Capabilities

TeSMo demonstrates several advanced capabilities:
- **Diverse Text Control:** The model successfully generates a wide variety of motion styles and interaction nuances controlled by text prompts (Fig. 7, Top).
- **User-Defined Trajectory Control:** Users can guide navigation along predefined A\* paths, with a controllable blend scale to determine how closely the generated trajectory adheres to the input path while maintaining naturalness (Fig. 7, Middle).
- **Effective Test-Time Guidance:** The combined use of goal-reaching and collision avoidance guidance during inference significantly improves performance in both navigation and interaction, leading to more accurate goals and fewer collisions/penetrations (Table 3, Fig. 7, Bottom).

In summary, TeSMo presents a robust and effective solution for a challenging problem, offering significant improvements in realism, controllability, and diversity over existing methods, backed by both quantitative metrics and qualitative user preference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TeSMo represents a significant advancement in the field of human motion generation, particularly in its ability to combine complex scene understanding with flexible, text-based control. Its contributions extend beyond incremental improvements, offering a new paradigm for synthesizing intelligent and interactive virtual characters.

The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Highly Controllable and Context-Aware Digital Humans:** This is the first method to effectively combine text-conditioned control with detailed scene-aware human motion generation for both navigation and nuanced object interaction. This capability is foundational for creating truly intelligent digital humans and avatars that can dynamically adapt their behavior to rich 3D environments based on natural language commands.
2. **Bridging the Data Gap for Scene-Aware Motion:** One of the most critical contributions is TeSMo's strategy for addressing the scarcity of paired human motion-scene-text datasets. By pre-training on large scene-agnostic datasets and then fine-tuning with carefully augmented, smaller scene-aware datasets, the authors have devised a robust and scalable training methodology. The creation of **Loco-3D-FRONT** and the augmentation of **SAMP** with text annotations and diverse objects are valuable dataset contributions to the community, paving the way for future research.
3. **Advancing Diffusion Models for Embodied AI:** TeSMo demonstrates a powerful application of augmented diffusion models (akin to ControlNet) to the domain of 3D human motion. Its two-branch architecture and hierarchical decomposition provide a strong framework for integrating diverse conditioning signals (text, 2D maps, 3D objects) into complex generative tasks, setting a precedent for future embodied AI research.
4. **Transformative Impact on Creative Industries:**
 - **Gaming and Film Animation:** Animators can leverage TeSMo to rapidly prototype and generate complex character behaviors (e.g., "a person walks hesitantly to the chair and sits down with crossed arms") in intricate scenes, drastically reducing manual animation effort and speeding up content creation pipelines. This allows for greater artistic control at a higher level of abstraction.
 - **Virtual Reality and Metaverse:** The ability to populate virtual worlds with physically plausible and interactable characters that respond naturally to environmental cues and user commands will significantly enhance immersion and user experience in VR/AR applications and the emerging metaverse.
 - **Architectural Visualization:** Architects and designers can animate virtual walkthroughs with realistic human presence, testing the usability and flow of their designs.
5. **Enhancing Embodied AI and Robotics Simulations:** For training and evaluating embodied AI agents or robots, TeSMo can generate diverse human behaviors in simulated environments, which is crucial for developing robust human-robot interaction systems, autonomous navigation, and intelligent agent policies.
6. **Strong Baseline and Future Research Catalyst:** TeSMo establishes a new state-of-the-art, offering a robust baseline for future research in controllable human motion synthesis. Its modular approach for navigation and interaction, along with its guidance mechanisms, provides a fertile ground for extensions.

**Potential Future Work and Limitations:** The authors acknowledge several avenues for future improvement. A unified one-stage model for navigation that directly generates full-body motion could streamline the process and potentially address any disconnects between root trajectory and full-body in-painting. Expanding the scene representation beyond 2D floor maps to handle more complex 3D scene geometry would enable interactions like stepping over small objects. Furthermore, broadening the spectrum of modeled actions (e.g., lying down, touching), integrating interactions with dynamic objects, and exploring the synergy with Large Language Model (LLM) planners for high-level action sequencing represent exciting directions.

In conclusion, TeSMo represents a pivotal step towards truly intelligent and interactive virtual characters, addressing core challenges in data, model design, and control. Its impact is poised to be significant across research, creative industries, and the development of future embodied AI systems.
