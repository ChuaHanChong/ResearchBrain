# EmbodiedVSR: Dynamic Scene Graph-Guided Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Visual Spatial Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodiedVSR enhances spatial reasoning in embodied AI by integrating dynamic scene graph generation with physics-constrained chain-of-thought reasoning. This approach achieves improved performance on a new eSpatial-Benchmark and demonstrates robust capabilities in real-world robotic assembly tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with implicit spatial knowledge, leading to geometrically inconsistent reasoning in complex 3D environments.
- Existing MLLMs produce fragmented reasoning chains, causing error accumulation in long-horizon tasks requiring persistent environmental understanding.
- Current models often lack a strong understanding of physical constraints and dynamics necessary for precise object manipulation in embodied AI applications.

## Method

- Introduces **EmbodiedVSR**, a framework that generates and dynamically updates ==scene graphs== to create explicit, structured representations of spatial relationships.
- Integrates ==physics-constrained chain-of-thought reasoning==, leveraging the dynamic scene graph to guide the MLLM's reasoning process and ensure physically plausible steps.
- Developed a new comprehensive ==eSpatial-Benchmark== to evaluate embodied spatial reasoning across general scenes, robotic scenarios, and structured assembly tasks.

## Results

- **EmbodiedVSR** outperformed state-of-the-art baselines like **GPT-4o** and **Claude 3** across all components of the new eSpatial-Benchmark.
- The framework achieved substantial improvements in robotic-specific tasks, including a **18.4%** gain in "Arm Feasibility" and a **6.7%** gain in "Success Judgment" over GPT-4o on eSpatial-RoboMIND.
- In real-world robotic manipulation, EmbodiedVSR demonstrated **100%** accuracy in block assembly description and an **80%** success rate in robot operations for complex reassembly tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling spatial relationships through dynamic scene graphs provides structured information, overcoming the limitations of implicit spatial knowledge in MLLMs.
> - Guiding chain-of-thought reasoning with scene graphs ensures that each reasoning step is physically grounded and geometrically consistent.
> - The integration of these components enables zero-shot generalization to novel environments and tasks, reducing the need for domain-specific training.
