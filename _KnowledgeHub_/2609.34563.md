# Rethinking Latent Visual Reasoning: Grounding Latent Reasoning in Visual Evidence

> [!summary] Summary
> ReaLVR is a training add-on for latent visual reasoning in multimodal LLMs. During GRPO it supervises the model's own free-running latent tokens with visual evidence. Relevant-versus-mismatched image prototypes define what latents should preserve, and correct-versus-wrong answer attention defines where to supervise. Architecture and inference stay unchanged. It reaches a 63.7% five-benchmark average on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and scales to 235B backbones.

## Problem

- Latent visual reasoning supervises latent tokens with teacher-forced visual targets during SFT, but at RL and inference time the model generates free-running latents with no targets.
- Standard GRPO scores only generated text and replays sampled latents as fixed context, so no gradient tells latent tokens which visual evidence to keep or where.
- As a result, vanilla LVR latents barely react to answer-changing image edits: correct answers flip in 81-86% of edited pairs, yet LVR changes its prediction in only 6-13%.

## Method

- **On-policy latent regeneration**: alongside ==GRPO== replay, ReaLVR regenerates one current-model latent trajectory by feeding back hidden states and keeps gradients through the recurrence, so the evidence loss trains latent generation itself.
- **Visual-contrast margin loss**: a ==cosine hinge== pulls each latent toward a ==masked-mean ROI visual prototype== and pushes it away from the most similar prototype pooled from mismatched examples.
- **Answer-contrast routing**: correct and sampled wrong answers are teacher-forced after the shared latent span, and the positive gap in their attention to each latent sets ==detached per-position loss weights== above a uniform floor.

## Results

- On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ReaLVR averages **63.7%** across MMVP, BLINK, HRBench-4K/8K and MME-RealWorld, beating LVR-RL by **3.3 points**, Monet-RL by **1.5** and ILVR by **0.8**.
- Gains hold across model families and scales: **65.2%** on Qwen3-VL-30B, **+3.1** over LVR-RL on InternVL3-8B, **+2.6** on Gemma-3-12B, and gains over LVR-SFT on all three benchmarks tested at 235B.
- Ablations: supervising saved rollout latents instead of regenerated ones costs **1.8 points**, dropping negatives costs **1.6** and uniform routing costs **1.3**. Latent distance after image edits rises from under **0.0005** to **0.13-0.34**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-only rewards cannot assign credit to intermediate continuous states. Supervision has to reach the on-policy process that generates them, not saved rollouts replayed as fixed context.
> - The model's own wrong answers are a cheap baseline for credit assignment: subtracting the attention they share with the correct answer isolates the positions that carry answer-specific information.
> - Latent states can affect the answer yet be unreadable as text. Projecting every latent through the vocabulary head gives only a closing-tag token, so verbal probes understate what latents hold.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Latent visual reasoning (LVR) lets a multimodal model make intermediate computations in continuous hidden states rather than explaining every step in text. That makes it useful for questions about small visual details or spatial relationships. It also makes it hard to tell what evidence those states preserve. A final-answer reward says whether the answer succeeded, but it does not say which latent positions should preserve which visual evidence. The authors call this missing link the "latent evidence-credit gap." Their account of the gap is a hypothesis about supervision, not proof that GRPO alone causes weak visual use.

Their method, ReaLVR, adds that supervision to the model's own generated latent trajectory. It uses two comparisons. Correct answers are compared with model-generated wrong answers to decide *where* to supervise. Relevant image evidence is compared with mismatched evidence to specify *what* those states should preserve. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ReaLVR reaches **63.7%** five-task mean accuracy (±0.2 over three seeds), compared with **60.4%** (±0.2) for LVR-RL.

### 2. Why answer reward leaves a gap

A latent token is a continuous decoder state passed forward rather than a word. The answer-level reward can encourage an answer, but it does not directly identify which latent positions carried useful evidence.

ReaLVR first regenerates one current-model latent trajectory autoregressively. It retains gradients through the recurrence and generates this trajectory before any answer tokens are supplied. It then teacher-forces two kinds of answers after that same shared trajectory: the correct answer, and distinct, parseable model-generated wrong answers.

The method contrasts each answer type's average attention to each latent position. Positions read more strongly under the correct answer receive more supervision, and a uniform floor is retained. The weights are detached, so the evidence loss cannot reduce itself by simply lowering the routing weights.

The visual target supplies the "what." ReaLVR pools visual tokens from the annotated relevant region when one is available, and otherwise uses the whole image. It compares that prototype against mismatched prototypes and trains a margin that favors the relevant evidence over the hardest negative. This loss is added alongside GRPO, not substituted for its reward. The added branches are training-only, and the architecture and inference procedure stay unchanged.

(a) One real-world VQA example asks what is in front of an airplane's front wheel. LVR-7B answers "man," while ReaLVR answers "Unattended stroller." (b) Visual contrast specifies what to preserve, and answer contrast specifies where to supervise. (c) The top eight answer-attended latent tokens are replaced while other states are held fixed. Correct-answer probability falls from 67% to 63% for LVR-7B and from 70% to 59% for ReaLVR. This measures local answer dependence, not faithful reasoning.

### 3. Testing both "where" and "what"

The decisive mechanism test is a mass-matched 2×2 ablation. It crosses two choices:

- **Position allocation:** uniform (U) or answer-contrast (C).
- **Visual objective:** positive-only (+) or positive–negative (±).

Every arm has unit per-example routing mass. The full C± arm has a 63.6% five-task mean. U± reaches 62.5%, and C+ reaches 62.2%.

The arms also differ in how predictions respond to changed images. A strict correct flip means the prediction changes to the correct answer when the ground truth changes. C± has a strict paired correct-flip rate of 59.2%, versus 42.5% for U±. A false flip is a prediction change when the ground truth stays the same. False-flip rates are 7.2% for C± and 8.1% for U±.

The test supports the value of both components under matched coefficient mass. Coefficient-mass matching does not match active-hinge fraction, gradient magnitude, or training compute.

The benchmark comparison uses the same five-task mean. It is the unweighted average of task accuracy on MMVP, BLINK, HRBench-4K, HRBench-8K, and MME-RealWorld, not a pooled-item accuracy. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ReaLVR scores 63.7% (±0.2) and LVR-RL scores 60.4% (±0.2). ILVR-Stage2 is the strongest competing evaluated latent-reasoning row by this mean, at 62.9% (±0.2). These are benchmark accuracy comparisons, not evidence of fewer tokens, attempts, or inference time.

### 4. Evidence credit, not a visible rationale

ReaLVR's paired answer and visual contrasts turn "where" and "what" into training signals while leaving inference latent and unchanged. The evidence shows improved benchmark results and local answer dependence. It is not proof that the model's hidden computation is fully faithful.

The method also has practical boundaries. When region annotations are absent, the visual target is the whole image and is less spatially specific. The method also adds training-time computation.
