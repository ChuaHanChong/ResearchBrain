# Unsupervised Contrastive Learning Using Out-Of-Distribution Data for Long-Tailed Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University of Science and Technology and Chung-Ang University developed KDUP, a two-step framework for unsupervised contrastive learning on long-tailed datasets using out-of-distribution data. This method improved classification accuracy by up to 7.11% on CIFAR-10-LT and learned more balanced, well-separated embedding spaces.

## Problem

- Self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, while powerful, show significant performance degradation when applied to real-world long-tailed datasets due to inherent class imbalance.
- The high cost and impracticality of collecting and annotating sufficient in-domain data, particularly for rare 'tail' classes, hinders the application of SSL in many real-world scenarios.
- Traditional SSL struggles to learn robust representations for tail classes, leading to biased models that perform poorly on underrepresented categories.

## Method

- Introduces ==KDUP==, a two-step ==knowledge distillation== framework that strategically leverages abundant unlabeled out-of-distribution (OOD) data to address class imbalance.
- In Step 1, a 'guiding network' is pre-trained using in-domain (ID) data and OOD data sampled based on ==KL divergence-based clustering== and instance-wise 'tailness' scores, employing a pseudo semantic discrimination loss (L_PSD) and a domain discrimination loss (L_DD).
- In Step 2, a 'training network' is initialized from the guiding network and refined solely on ID data through knowledge distillation, utilizing a novel ==guided contrastive loss (L_GCL)== with force-controlling weights and an embedding space distillation loss (L_DL).

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art classification performance on long-tailed datasets, with up to **7.11%** higher overall accuracy on CIFAR-10-LT and **4.9%** on CIFAR-100-LT compared to prior methods like COLT.
- Produced demonstrably better-structured embedding spaces, characterized by improved class separability and compactness, as evidenced by higher Calinski-Harabasz Index and lower Davies-Bouldin Index scores, and clearer t-SNE visualizations.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of key components, including KL divergence-based clustering, the pseudo semantic discrimination loss, and the guided contrastive loss, to the overall performance gains and balancedness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating OOD data in a preliminary stage to establish a balanced and well-separated embedding space, followed by knowledge distillation to refine the model for in-domain data, yields superior representations for long-tailed distributions.
> - Dynamic and selective sampling of OOD data, guided by cluster-wise tailness scores derived from KL divergence clustering, effectively rebalances the representation learning process towards underrepresented classes.
> - A guided contrastive loss that weights attractive and repulsive forces based on similarities from a frozen guiding network enables effective transfer of robust, balanced representation knowledge to the final model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several critical and active research areas in machine learning and computer vision: self-supervised learning (SSL), contrastive learning, and long-tailed (class-imbalanced) learning, with a specific focus on leveraging out-of-distribution (OOD) data.

**Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** SSL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning robust visual representations without relying on human-annotated labels, significantly reducing the annotation burden for downstream tasks. Methods like SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) and MoCo (He et al., 2020) have demonstrated remarkable success on balanced datasets. This paper builds upon this foundation, aiming to extend the benefits of SSL to more challenging data distributions.

**Long-Tailed Learning:** Real-world datasets often exhibit a long-tailed distribution, where a few "head" classes have abundant samples, while many "tail" classes have very few. Standard deep learning models, including those trained with SSL, tend to perform poorly on these tail classes due to their underrepresentation. Addressing this imbalance is a major challenge, with existing supervised approaches ranging from re-sampling strategies (e.g., SMOTE) to advanced representation learning and prompt tuning methods. In the context of SSL, prior works have explored methods like re-weighted regularization (Liu et al., 2022), network pruning (Jiang et al., 2021b), and leveraging memorization effects (Zhou et al., 2022) to improve representation learning for tail classes.

**Leveraging Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) Data:** A key distinguishing factor of this work is its innovative use of readily available unlabeled OOD data to mitigate the class imbalance problem in SSL. This approach acknowledges that while collecting more in-domain (ID) data for tail classes is expensive, a vast amount of unlabeled OOD data is available online. Several recent works have explored auxiliary data for imbalanced learning: Jiang et al. (2021a) used external *ID* data, and Wei et al. (2022) assigned open-set noisy labels to OOD data. The most direct predecessor to this work is Bai et al. (2023), who also explored using external unlabeled OOD data for self-supervised long-tail learning (COLT).

**Distinguishing Contribution:** The authors explicitly differentiate their work from Bai et al. (2023) by proposing a novel **two-step framework**. While Bai et al. (2023) directly trained a network using both ID and OOD data for downstream tasks, Hoang et al. hypothesize that continuous use of OOD data might degrade accuracy, especially when generating accurate pseudo-labels for combined data is difficult. Their two-step approach first pre-trains a "guiding network" with carefully sampled OOD and ID data to establish a balanced and well-separated embedding space, and then *distills* this knowledge to a "training network" using only ID data. This strategic separation aims to harness the benefits of OOD data for balanced representation learning while ensuring the final model is optimally tuned for the target ID distribution. This methodology represents an advancement in how OOD data is integrated into SSL for long-tailed scenarios, moving beyond direct data augmentation to a more nuanced knowledge transfer strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust self-supervised learning (SSL) method capable of learning balanced and well-separated representations from inherently long-tailed datasets for subsequent downstream tasks like image classification.

The motivation stems from several critical observations and challenges:

1. **Real-World Data Imbalance:** The authors highlight that real-world data distributions are inherently imbalanced, with numerous object categories having vastly different frequencies. This "long-tailed" nature of data is a pervasive issue across various domains, from natural imagery to medical diagnostics, and severely impacts the performance and fairness of machine learning models.
2. **Limitations of Existing SSL on Imbalanced Data:** While traditional SSL methods have proven highly effective on balanced and carefully curated datasets, their performance significantly degrades when applied to long-tailed distributions. This is experimentally demonstrated by the authors using SimCLR on CIFAR-10/100 long-tailed subsets, showing lower overall accuracy and higher standard deviation in class-wise performance compared to balanced subsets. This limitation prevents the widespread adoption of SSL in many real-world applications where data imbalance is the norm.
3. **Cost and Difficulty of Data Annotation/Collection:** Acquiring large, labeled datasets for all classes, especially tail classes, is expensive, time-consuming, and often impractical. This reinforces the need for effective SSL methods that can learn from unlabeled data.
4. **Leveraging Abundant Unlabeled OOD Data:** Recognizing the impracticality of collecting more ID data for tail classes, the authors are motivated to explore the vast resources of unlabeled out-of-distribution (OOD) data readily available online. The hypothesis is that such data, if judiciously used, can help rebalance the representation space without incurring high annotation costs.
5. **Optimizing for In-Domain Performance:** While OOD data can help with balancing, directly training with a mix of ID and OOD data might not yield optimal representations for the target ID dataset, and generating accurate pseudo-labels for such combined data can be challenging. This motivates the proposed two-step approach, which aims to first leverage OOD data for initial balancing and then refine the representation specifically for the ID data.

In essence, the research aims to bridge the gap between the power of self-supervised learning and the reality of long-tailed data distributions by strategically incorporating OOD data through a novel two-stage knowledge distillation framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed KDUP (Knowledge Distillation in Unsupervised Contrastive Pre-training), is a two-step framework designed to learn balanced and well-separated representations from long-tailed datasets by leveraging unlabeled out-of-distribution (OOD) data.

**Step 1: Unsupervised Contrastive Pre-training (Learning a Balanced Embedding Space)**
This initial stage focuses on training an embedding network `f(·)` using a combination of in-domain (ID) long-tailed data and carefully sampled OOD data. The goal is to learn an embedding space that is both balanced across classes and exhibits good separability.

- **Sampling Out-Of-Domain Data:**
 - **In-Domain Sample Clustering:** To identify which OOD samples are relevant to tail classes, ID images are first clustered. Unlike standard k-means, the authors adapt a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence-based clustering method (Zhang et al., 2021). This method is preferred because it can better assign cluster centroids to tail samples, which are typically sparse in the embedding space, making it more effective for grouping rare classes. It uses Student’s t-distribution for soft assignments and refines centroids by minimizing KL divergence.
 - **Cluster-Wise Tailness Score Estimation:** For each ID sample, an "instance-wise tailness score" is computed based on the density of its K-nearest neighbors in the embedding space. Lower density (fewer close neighbors) indicates a higher probability of belonging to a tail class. These scores are updated with momentum (Eq. 5) for robustness. These instance-wise scores are then averaged within each cluster to obtain a "cluster-wise tailness score," indicating how likely a cluster represents tail classes.
 - **Sampling Budget Allocation & OOD Data Sampling:** A total sampling budget (`N_b`) for OOD data is allocated among the clusters based on their normalized cluster-wise tailness scores, giving more budget to clusters representing tail classes. OOD images whose embeddings are closest to the centroids of these high-tailness clusters are then sampled, forming the `D_OOD_s` dataset. This set of sampled OOD data is updated periodically (every `T` epochs) to adapt to changes in the embedding space.

- **Pre-training with Sampled OOD Data:** The embedding network `f(·)` is trained using a merged dataset (`D`) comprising ID data and the sampled OOD data (`D_OOD_s`).
 - **Pseudo Semantic Discrimination Loss (L_PSD):** Traditional unsupervised contrastive learning often treats all other instances in a mini-batch as negative samples, which can inadvertently push apart instances from the same semantic class. To address this, `L_PSD` considers not only augmented versions of an instance but also its `K_pos` nearest neighbors (within the same domain, either ID or OOD) as positive samples. This loss aims to increase similarities between instances and their pseudo-semantic positives while reducing similarities with negatives, thereby encoding semantic relationships and promoting better separability.
 - **Domain Discrimination Loss (L_DD):** This loss, adopted from Bai et al. (2023), leverages the known origin domain (ID or OOD) of each sample. It encourages samples from the same domain to be closer in the embedding space and samples from different domains to be farther apart. This helps disentangle domain-specific features.
 - **Total Contrastive Pre-training Loss (L_CPT):** `L_CPT = L_PSD + αL_DD`, where `α` balances the two components.

**Step 2: Knowledge Distillation in UCL (Optimizing for ID Data)**
After the first stage, `f(·)` is a pre-trained network with a balanced and well-separated embedding space, potentially noisy due to OOD data. This second stage aims to refine an optimal embedding network `g(·)` specifically for the ID dataset, by distilling knowledge from the frozen `f(·)`.

- **Network Initialization and Guiding:** A new embedding network `g(·)` is initialized using the weights of the pre-trained and *frozen* guiding network `f(·)`. The training then proceeds solely on the ID dataset `D_ID`.
- **Guided Sample Selection:** The frozen `f(·)` is utilized to select positive and negative samples for `g(·)`. For each ID instance `x_i`, a positive sample `x_p_i` is randomly selected from its `K_kd` nearest neighbors in `f(·)`'s embedding space. A negative sample `x_n_i` is randomly chosen from the farthest cluster from `x_i`'s cluster (cluster assignments are derived from `f(·)`'s embeddings). This guided selection ensures semantically meaningful positive/negative pairs.
- **Guided Contrastive Loss (L_GCL):** This novel loss uses the similarities from the guiding network `f(·)` (e.g., `w_pos_i = s_cos(z_i, z_p_i)`) to weight the attractive/repulsive forces in `g(·)`. Positive samples with higher `f(·)`-similarity are pulled more strongly, and negative samples with lower `f(·)`-similarity are pushed more strongly. This mechanism transfers the balanced and well-separated structure learned by `f(·)` to `g(·)`.
 - `L_GCL := (1 + w_pos_i)(1 - s_cos(y_i, y_p_i)) + (1 - w_neg_i)(1 + s_cos(y_i, y_n_i))`
- **Distillation Loss (L_DL):** To ensure `g(·)` maintains the overall embedding structure of `f(·)`, a distillation loss `L_DL` is used. It minimizes the squared difference between the cosine similarities of pairs of embeddings generated by `f(·)` and `g(·)` within a mini-batch.
- **Total Guided Learning Loss (L_GL):** `L_GL = L_GCL + βL_DL`, where `β` is a balancing hyperparameter.

The combined approach leverages OOD data for initial representation balancing and then refines this representation on ID data through a knowledge distillation process, yielding a network `g(·)` that is robust for long-tailed ID classification tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed KDUP method across various long-tailed datasets and evaluation protocols.

1. **Superior Classification Performance:**
 - **CIFAR-LT Datasets:** On CIFAR-10-LT, KDUP improved overall accuracy by 7.11% (with SimCLR as backbone) and 6.01% (with SDCLR) compared to the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) COLT method (Bai et al., 2023). Similarly, on CIFAR-100-LT, KDUP achieved 4.9% (SimCLR) and 3.8% (SDCLR) higher overall accuracies.
 - **ImageNet-100-LT and Places-LT Datasets:** For ImageNet-100-LT, KDUP achieved 1.61% (few-shot) and 2.19% (linear probing) higher overall accuracies than SimCLR+COLT. On Places-LT, improvements were 0.76% (few-shot) and 1.45% (linear probing).
 - **Balancedness:** In most cases, KDUP also achieved lower standard deviations (STD) across Many, Medium, and Few-shot classes, indicating better balancedness in performance across different class frequencies. Even in the CIFAR-100-LT SimCLR case where STD was slightly higher than COLT, the absolute STD value (0.79) was still very low, signifying excellent balance.

2. **Improved Embedding Space Quality (Visualization and Metrics):**
 - **t-SNE Visualization:** t-SNE projections of the CIFAR-10-LT dataset visually confirmed that KDUP learns representations with superior separability between classes and forms more compact clusters for each class compared to SimCLR and SimCLR+COLT.
 - **Clustering Metrics:** Quantitative metrics further supported this. KDUP yielded significantly higher Calinski-Harabasz Index (CHI) scores (e.g., 187.03 vs. 143.62 for CIFAR-10-LT) and lower Davies-Bouldin Index (DBI) scores (e.g., 2.21 vs. 3.38 for CIFAR-10-LT). Higher CHI and lower DBI indicate better-defined, more compact clusters with better separation, directly translating to more effective representation learning.

3. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Effectiveness:**
 - **Strong Baseline:** Incorporating KL-divergence based clustering and the proposed tailness score estimation alone improved SimCLR+COLT by 1.56% accuracy and 0.10 lower STD.
 - **Pseudo Semantic Discrimination Loss (L_PSD):** Adding `L_PSD` further boosted overall accuracy by 3.74% and reduced STD by 0.08.
 - **Knowledge Distillation (L_GL):** The full knowledge distillation stage, including guided sampling and guided weighting, contributed an additional 1.81% increase in overall accuracy and 0.09 lower STD.
 - **Guided Contrastive Loss (L_GCL) Analysis:** The ablation showed that directly using a typical contrastive loss in the KD stage deteriorated performance. Guided sampling (using `f(·)` to select positives/negatives) significantly improved accuracy by 5.87% (compared to typical loss). Further incorporating guided weighting (controlling attractive/repulsive forces) added another 1.04% improvement.
 - **Momentum Updates:** Momentum updates for tailness score estimation proved effective, especially for tail classes, improving overall accuracy by 1.13% and reducing STD by 0.23, which is attributed to robust score estimation for sparse tail samples.
 - **OOD Data vs. Augmented Data:** The use of genuinely OOD data for `D_OOD_s` consistently outperformed balancing clusters with augmented in-domain images, highlighting the value of diverse external data.

4. **Robustness to Hyperparameters:** The method demonstrated consistent superiority over baselines even with suboptimal hyperparameter values, suggesting its robustness.

5. **Efficiency:** The training and OOD sampling update times were reported, indicating the computational feasibility of the two-stage approach, with OOD sampling being infrequent enough not to significantly bottleneck the training.

In summary, the results convincingly establish KDUP as a new state-of-the-art method for unsupervised contrastive learning on long-tailed datasets. Its gains in both overall accuracy and balancedness, coupled with demonstrably better-structured embedding spaces, highlight the efficacy of its two-step knowledge distillation framework with strategically leveraged OOD data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed KDUP method represents a significant advancement in the field of self-supervised learning for challenging real-world data distributions. Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several key areas:

1. **Bridging the Gap for Real-World SSL:** The paper successfully addresses a critical limitation of traditional SSL methods, which primarily perform well on balanced datasets. By enabling robust representation learning on long-tailed distributions, KDUP brings SSL closer to practical applicability in domains where data imbalance is inherent and pervasive (e.g., medical imaging, rare species recognition, industrial defect detection). This expands the utility of SSL to a broader range of real-world scenarios.

2. **Effective and Efficient Use of OOD Data:** The method provides a novel and empirically validated strategy for integrating readily available unlabeled out-of-distribution data. Instead of simply augmenting the training set, the two-step framework with knowledge distillation offers a more sophisticated way to leverage OOD data for balancing representations without compromising optimization for the in-domain data. This approach opens avenues for future research into how diverse auxiliary datasets can be strategically utilized for robust model learning.

3. **Improved Representation Quality:** The core of the method lies in learning more balanced and well-separated embedding spaces. The quantitative improvements in classification accuracy (overall and across class frequencies) and qualitative metrics (t-SNE, CHI, DBI) confirm that KDUP produces higher-quality, semantically meaningful representations. These improved representations can significantly enhance the performance of downstream tasks, especially when labeled data for fine-tuning is scarce.

4. **Reduced Annotation Burden:** By providing a superior self-supervised framework for long-tailed data, the work indirectly contributes to reducing the heavy reliance on extensive human annotation. Representations learned by KDUP can be fine-tuned with minimal labeled data (as shown in few-shot learning experiments), making it more economical and feasible to develop AI systems for complex tasks.

5. **Novel Contributions to Contrastive Learning and Knowledge Distillation:** The specific algorithmic contributions, such as the KL divergence-based clustering for tail sample discovery, the momentum-updated tailness scores, the pseudo semantic discrimination loss, and particularly the guided contrastive loss with force-controlling weights and embedding space distillation, are technically novel. These innovations could inspire further research in designing more intelligent contrastive learning objectives and knowledge transfer mechanisms in SSL.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of KDUP suggests several potential directions for future work. For instance, exploring dynamic adjustments of `α` and `β` hyperparameters, investigating alternative OOD data sampling strategies, or applying similar two-stage distillation approaches to other SSL paradigms (e.g., generative models) could yield further improvements. The methodology could also be extended to other modalities beyond images, such as audio or text, where long-tailed distributions are common.

In conclusion, "Unsupervised Contrastive Learning Using Out-Of-Distribution Data for Long-Tailed Dataset" offers a robust and effective solution to a challenging problem, pushing the boundaries of self-supervised learning towards greater real-world utility and robustness against data imbalance. Its novel two-step approach and strong experimental validation mark it as an important contribution to the field.
