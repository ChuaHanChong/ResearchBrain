# SafeFlowMPC: Predictive and Safe Trajectory Planning for Robot Manipulators with Learning-based Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> SafeFlowMPC integrates a flow matching model with an online Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, enabling robot manipulators to perform safe and reactive trajectory planning in dynamic environments. This method guarantees continuous safety through a projection onto a safety manifold and terminal constraints, while learning complex behaviors from demonstrations, and demonstrating real-time performance and high success rates across various manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional robot motion planners lack flexibility, generalization, and real-time reactivity for dynamic, unstructured environments and human-robot interaction.
- Learning-based methods, while flexible, typically struggle to provide rigorous safety guarantees critical for real-world robotic deployment due to their 'black-box' nature.
- Designing explicit numerical objective functions for complex robotic tasks, such as human-robot handovers, is challenging, motivating the use of learning from demonstrations which often lacks safety integration.

## Method

- **SafeFlowMPC** combines a ==flow matching== model, which learns desired trajectory distributions from demonstrations, with an online Model Predictive Control (MPC) formulation.
- It projects proposed trajectories onto a 'safety manifold' using a non-convex optimization problem that enforces kinematic, collision avoidance, and crucial terminal safety constraints in real-time.
- A two-step training process for the flow model includes initial training on demonstrations and subsequent finetuning on a safety dataset, where all target trajectories are projected onto the safety manifold.

## Results

- SafeFlowMPC achieved high success rates (**86%** in static grasp, **82%** in reactive grasp) and zero obstacle collisions, outperforming purely learning-based methods (e.g., Behavior Cloning at **2%** success) and traditional MPC (**76%** success).
- The method maintained real-time planning times (average 62-63 ms per step) on a 7-DoF robot manipulator while adhering to all physical and collision constraints.
- In human-robot object handovers, SafeFlowMPC produced trajectories closest to human demonstrations (0.12m average distance) while ensuring terminal safety constraints, demonstrating its ability to learn complex, non-numerically defined task objectives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a learning-based flow model with an online optimization solver allows for combining the performance benefits of learned policies with rigorous, continuous safety guarantees.
> - The use of a terminal safety set, enforced by constraints within the MPC formulation, enables provable safety beyond the immediate planning horizon by ensuring a low-level controller can stabilize the system.
> - Employing a suboptimal, real-time iteration (RTI) scheme for solving the non-convex projection problem ensures computational efficiency crucial for real-time reactivity in dynamic environments.
