# cuNRTO: GPU-Accelerated Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> cuNRTO, a GPU-accelerated framework, addresses the high computational cost of Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization (NRTO) by re-architecting its core components for parallel execution. The framework achieves speedups of up to 139.60x over CPU-based NRTO while maintaining robust constraint satisfaction across various robotic tasks.

## Problem

- The existing Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization (NRTO) framework is computationally expensive, primarily due to its reliance on Interior Point (IP) methods for solving large Second Order Conic Programming (SOCP) constraints.
- This high computational cost limits NRTO's applicability to high-dimensional robotic systems or scenarios demanding real-time performance in safety-critical applications.
- Traditional robust control methods often struggle with either conservativeness (Tube-based MPC) or nonlinear state constraints (Min-Max DDP), while NRTO provides guarantees but lacks efficiency.

## Method

- **cuNRTO** introduces **NRTO-DR**, a methodology that accelerates the computationally demanding SOCP sub-problems within the NRTO's inner ==ADMM== loop using ==Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting==, leveraging GPU for parallel SOC projections and sparse linear solves.
- Proposes **NRTO-FullADMM**, a novel variant designed to fully integrate SOCP projections directly into a single, on-device ADMM loop, thereby eliminating nested DR layers and minimizing host-device communication for enhanced GPU utilization.
- Developed custom ==CUDA kernels== for efficient parallel SOC projection steps and utilized cuBLAS GEMM chains for feedback gain updates, enabling end-to-end GPU acceleration for the robust trajectory optimization process.

## Results

- NRTO-FullADMM achieved substantial speedups, including up to **139.60x** for unicycle and **66.89x** for quadcopter tasks with 5 obstacles, significantly reducing solve times compared to the CPU-based NRTO baseline.
- The framework demonstrated scalability to high-dimensional systems, achieving a **25.9x** wall-clock speedup for a 7-DOF Franka manipulator while satisfying complex joint, velocity, torque, and collision avoidance constraints.
- Robust constraint satisfaction was consistently maintained across various tasks, time horizons, obstacle configurations, and uncertainty levels, verified through Monte Carlo sampling and analysis of edge cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Restructuring robust trajectory optimization problems to fully leverage GPU architectures, particularly by integrating SOCP projections directly into an on-device ADMM loop, yields substantial computational speedups.
> - Minimizing CPU-GPU data transfers and maximizing GPU utilization through custom CUDA kernels for bottleneck operations like SOC projections are crucial for drastically improving the efficiency of robust optimization.
> - Significant algorithmic and hardware acceleration can be achieved without compromising the quality of the robust solution or its ability to satisfy constraints, as evidenced by comparable objective values and maintained feasibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Trajectory optimization is a foundational component in the motion planning and control of robotic systems, enabling the computation of dynamically feasible paths that minimize costs and satisfy constraints. Its applications span various domains, including manipulation, legged locomotion, aerial vehicles, and autonomous driving. However, the deployment of robotic systems in real-world environments necessitates accounting for uncertainty, particularly when constraints relate to safety-critical aspects like obstacle avoidance or actuator limits. Failure to incorporate uncertainty can lead to system failures.

Existing methods for handling uncertainty often model it as a stochastic disturbance, utilizing approaches such as chance-constrained optimization or covariance steering to provide probabilistic guarantees. However, some uncertainties, such as modeling inaccuracies or exogenous disturbances, are more appropriately modeled as deterministic, where disturbances are assumed to lie within a bounded set. For safety-critical applications, guarantees must hold for all possible realizations within this bounded set.

Robust control principles, which focus on worst-case guarantees over bounded uncertainty sets, have been adapted for trajectory optimization. Methods like Tube-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Min-Max DDP (Differential Dynamic Programming) have been developed. Tube-based MPC can be conservative due to the decoupling of nominal control and feedback gains. Min-Max DDP, while addressing adversarial disturbances, often struggles with nonlinear state constraints. Other approaches like DIRTREL handle robust nonlinear direct transcription under specific uncertainty assumptions. More recent research has leveraged Robust Optimization (RO) to address both nonlinear dynamics and state constraints. The Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization (NRTO) framework is one such approach, designed to handle nonlinearity in both dynamics and constraints under deterministic uncertainty.

A primary challenge with the NRTO framework is its computational expense. It converts robust constraints into Second Order Conic Programming (SOCP) constraints, which are solved using Interior Point (IP) methods. These methods, while effective, can be computationally intensive, limiting NRTO's application to high-dimensional systems or scenarios requiring real-time performance. This limitation has prompted the exploration of parallel computing architectures, particularly Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), to accelerate complex optimization tasks in robotics. While GPUs have been used to parallelize rigid body dynamics and gradients, and more recently for accelerating optimization loops in MPC (e.g., MPCGPU) and convex programs (e.g., ADMM-based QP solvers like OSQP), the NRTO framework, in its original form, is not inherently structured for efficient parallelization. This work seeks to address the computational bottleneck of NRTO by re-architecting its core components for GPU acceleration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work is to address the significant computational burden of the existing Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization (NRTO) framework. While NRTO provides a method for computing robust control policies for systems with nonlinear dynamics and state constraints under bounded deterministic uncertainty, its reliance on Interior Point (IP) methods for solving large Second Order Conic Programming (SOCP) constraints makes it computationally expensive. This high cost is a barrier to its widespread adoption in applications requiring rapid decision-making or for high-dimensional robotic systems.

The key objectives of this research are threefold, aiming to develop a GPU-accelerated framework named cuNRTO that enhances the computational efficiency of robust trajectory optimization:

1. **Introduce NRTO-DR**: Develop a methodology that solves the computationally demanding sub-problems within the NRTO framework using Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting. The motivation is to improve performance over existing IP methods by exploiting parallelism in SOC projections and leveraging sparse direct solves, thereby reducing matrix operations and computational cost with minimal changes to the original NRTO structure.
2. **Propose NRTO-FullADMM**: Develop a novel variant of the NRTO framework specifically designed to fully leverage GPU acceleration. This involves restructuring the inner Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) update blocks to directly integrate SOCP projections into the inner loop. The motivation is to eliminate the nested DR layer used in NRTO-DR and minimize host-device communication overhead, thereby improving scalability and overall speed.
3. **Develop GPU Implementations (cuNRTO)**: Implement both NRTO-DR and NRTO-FullADMM on GPUs using custom CUDA kernels for the SOC projection steps and cuBLAS GEMM chains for feedback gain updates. The objective is to validate the performance of these proposed methodologies through simulated experiments on various robotic models (unicycle, quadcopter, and Franka manipulator) and quantify the achieved speedups and maintained robust constraint satisfaction.

In essence, the motivation is to transform a theoretically sound but computationally limited robust optimization framework into a practical, high-performance tool suitable for complex, real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces cuNRTO, a GPU-accelerated framework that extends the Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization (NRTO) framework by introducing two dynamic optimization architectures, NRTO-DR and NRTO-FullADMM. The overall approach integrates successive linearization on the CPU (host) with accelerated inner-loop optimization on the GPU (device).

#### 3.1. Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization (NRTO) Framework

The baseline NRTO framework addresses robust trajectory optimization for discrete-time nonlinear systems with affine control policies. The system dynamics are $x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k) + d_k$, where $d_k$ represents an unknown disturbance within a bounded ellipsoidal set $U[\tau]$. Control policies are of the form $u_k = \bar{u}_k + K_k d_{k-1}$, involving feed-forward control $\bar{u}_k$ and feedback gains $K_k$. The objective is to minimize a cost function while satisfying robust state and control constraints ($g(x; \zeta) \le 0$, $h(u; \zeta) \le 0$) for all disturbances $\zeta \in U[\tau]$.

NRTO is a bi-level algorithm:
1. **Outer Loop (Successive Linearization)**: The nonlinear problem is iteratively linearized around a nominal trajectory, transforming the intractable robust constraints into a tractable form involving Second-Order Conic Programming (SOCP) constraints. A trust region constraint on control updates ($\|\text{F}_u \delta \hat{u}\|_2 \le r_{\text{trust}}$) is also imposed.
2. **Inner Loop (ADMM)**: The linearized subproblem, which can be infeasible, is solved using an Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM). The problem is formulated by introducing a slack variable $\tilde{p}$ to yield $p = \tilde{p}$ as the coupling constraint. The ADMM updates sequentially minimize an Augmented Lagrangian with respect to two blocks of variables: $\{k_v, \tilde{p}\}$ and $\{\delta \hat{u}, p\}$, followed by a dual update. The update for $\{k_v, \tilde{p}\}$ involves solving a SOCP problem, which is the most computationally intensive part in the original NRTO and is addressed by cuNRTO. NRTO-LE is an extension to handle linearization errors.

#### 3.2. NRTO-DR Framework

NRTO-DR aims to accelerate the solution of the computationally expensive SOCP subproblem (the $\{k_v, \tilde{p}\}$ update) within the inner ADMM loop of NRTO.

**Algorithmic Formulation (DR Splitting for SOCP)**:
The $\{k_v, \tilde{p}\}$ update problem is reformulated into a standard conic form $ \min_{\chi,s} \frac{1}{2} \chi^T P \chi + q^T \chi \quad \text{s.t.} \quad A\chi + s = b, s \in \mathcal{K}$, where $\chi = [k_v; \tilde{p}]$ and $\mathcal{K}$ is a product of SOCs. This problem is then solved using the Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting method. Each DR iteration involves:
1. **Proximal Step for Affine Set**: A projection onto an affine set, which reduces to solving a fixed sparse KKT system. This step is a quadratic programming (QP) problem.
2. **Reflection Step**: A linear transformation of the variables.
3. **Proximal Step for Conic Set**: A projection onto the product of SOCs. This step is separable across individual SOC constraints and has a closed-form solution for each.

The KKT matrix for the affine-set projection is constant within each inner ADMM iteration, allowing for one-time factorization and reuse of triangular solves. DR iterations terminate based on a fixed-point residual or maximum iteration count.

**GPU Implementation**:
The GPU implementation of NRTO-DR leverages the parallelism exposed by DR splitting:
- **Sparse KKT Solves**: One-time sparse factorization of the KKT matrix and per-iteration triangular solves are performed using `cuDSS`.
- **Parallel SOC Projections**: A custom CUDA kernel computes SOC projections. The logic for different projection cases is adapted from the SCS implementation. This step is highly parallelized, with each constraint block potentially handled by a dedicated warp.
- **Vector Operations**: All vector operations (reflection, updates, residuals) are handled by `cuBLAS` and kept on-device, minimizing data transfers.

#### 3.3. NRTO-FullADMM Framework

NRTO-FullADMM is a novel variant designed for even greater GPU acceleration by restructuring the inner ADMM loop of the NRTO framework. It aims to eliminate the nested DR layer and further reduce host-device communication.

**Algorithmic Formulation (Full ADMM Integration)**:
Problem 2 (Tractable Linearized Problem) is rewritten by introducing slack variable $\nu$ alongside $\tilde{p}$, yielding coupling constraints $p=\tilde{p}$ and $\hat{A}_j k_v + \hat{b}_j = \nu_j$ with $\|\nu_j\|_2 \le \tilde{p}_j$. The ADMM blocks are defined as:
1. **Block-1 Update** (for $\nu, \tilde{p}$): This step decouples across individual SOC constraints ($j$) and involves a direct projection onto a single SOC $\prod_{SOC}(\hat{y}, \hat{t})$, similar to the projection in NRTO-DR.
2. **Block-2 Update** (for $\delta \hat{u}, p, k_v$): This update decouples into two parts:
 - One part updates $(\delta \hat{u}, p)$ by solving a convex QP with fixed quadratic terms and constraints.
 - The other part updates $k_v$ using a closed-form solution derived from first-order optimality conditions, involving precomputed matrices $q, M, \bar{M}$.
3. **Dual Update**: Standard ADMM dual updates for the coupling constraints are performed.

The inner ADMM loop terminates based on primal and dual residuals.

**GPU Implementation**:
NRTO-FullADMM's inner ADMM loop runs entirely on-device to maximize efficiency:
- **On-device Data Layout**: Variables and constant matrices are stored in specific layouts (e.g., row-major blocks for $\nu, \lambda_\nu$) to optimize memory access and kernel execution.
- **Block-1 (Parallel SOC Projections)**: This involves two GPU steps:
 1. **Affine Evaluation**: `cuBLAS GEMM` calls compute inputs for the projections, exploiting structured factorizations to avoid dense matrix formation.
 2. **SOC Projection**: A CUDA kernel performs parallel SOC projections for each constraint block, optimized for warp-level execution and batched memory. This step is bandwidth-bound.
- **Block-2 (Linear Step)**:
 - The QP for $(\delta \hat{u}, p)$ is solved using preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) with a Jacobi preconditioner, computed once per outer iteration.
 - The $k_v$ update (a large linear system) is solved via PCG using matrix-free Hessian-vector products.
- **Dual Updates**: Vector `axpy`-style operations are performed on the GPU, with primal and dual residuals computed on-device for termination checks.

**Key Innovations over NRTO-DR**:
NRTO-FullADMM improves upon NRTO-DR by:
- Replacing the nested structure of NRTO-DR (ADMM outer, DR inner) with a single, fully integrated ADMM loop.
- Directly integrating parallel SOCP block projections into the inner ADMM updates, simplifying the algorithmic flow.
- Eliminating CPU-GPU data transfer bottlenecks by executing the entire inner ADMM loop on the GPU, leading to higher GPU utilization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The performance of cuNRTO, encompassing both NRTO-DR and NRTO-FullADMM, was evaluated through simulated experiments on unicycle, quadcopter, and Franka manipulator models.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware**: High-performance workstation with a 3.06 GHz 60-core Intel Xeon W-3500 CPU and an NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU, running Ubuntu 22.04 and CUDA 12.9.
- **Software**: Code compiled with g++ 11.4.0.
- **Baseline**: Original NRTO solver using MOSEK Interior Point optimizer with multi-threading on the CPU.
- **Metrics**:
 - **Constraint Satisfaction**: Verified via Monte Carlo sampling (1,000 i.i.d. disturbances) and 1,000 reproducible edge cases probing uncertainty boundaries. Reported as the fraction of successful rollouts. NRTO-LE, an extension for linearization error, was used to achieve 100% satisfaction in cases where baseline NRTO fell short.
 - **Wall-clock Time**: Measured using C++ `std::chrono::high_resolution_clock` for convergence to a feasible solution.
- **Sweep Studies**: Performance was analyzed by varying time horizon ($T$), uncertainty level ($\tau$), and number of obstacles (Obs) from a nominal setting ($T_0=30, \tau_0=0.05, O_0=0$). Timestep $\Delta k = 25$ ms.

#### 4.2. Performance Evaluation

**1. Horizon Sweep ($T$)**:
- **Unicycle**: At nominal $T_0=30$, NRTO-FullADMM reduced solve time from 32.137 s (NRTO) to 6.231 s, a 5.16x speedup. At $T=45$, it achieved 6.94x speedup (44.324 s to 6.387 s).
- **Quadcopter**: At nominal $T_0=30$, NRTO-FullADMM reduced solve time from 1327.390 s (NRTO) to 98.585 s, a 13.46x speedup. At $T=45$, it achieved 13.21x speedup (1749.458 s to 132.484 s).

**2. Obstacle Sweep (Obs)**:
- As the number of obstacles increased, the number of robust constraints and SOC projections grew, benefiting GPU implementations due to increased parallelism.
- **Unicycle**: At Obs=5, NRTO-FullADMM reduced solve time from 30423.523 s (NRTO) to 217.932 s, a **139.60x** speedup.
- **Quadcopter**: At Obs=5, NRTO-FullADMM reduced solve time from 13374.375 s (NRTO) to 199.935 s, a **66.89x** speedup.

**3. Uncertainty Sweep ($\tau$)**:
- As $\tau$ increased, the success rate of the baseline NRTO decreased, indicating increased conservativeness. However, with NRTO-LE, 100% constraint satisfaction was maintained across all $\tau$ values for both dynamics.
- NRTO-FullADMM consistently yielded the lowest wall-clock times among the compared methods across the uncertainty sweep.

#### 4.3. Source of Speedup

For the unicycle task with five obstacles, a detailed runtime breakdown showed:
- NRTO-DR spent significant time on host-device synchronization (43.5%) and linear solves (32.6%), with SOC projections accounting for 11.0%.
- NRTO-FullADMM, by keeping the inner ADMM loop entirely on-device, substantially reduced synchronization overhead and increased average GPU utilization from 34.9% to 86.5%. This indicates that the speedup is not solely due to parallel SOC projections but also to the algorithmic reformulation that minimizes CPU-GPU communication.

#### 4.4. Solution Quality

The proposed reformulations achieved significant speedups without compromising solution quality.
- Visualizations of terminal-state distributions for unicycle and quadcopter demonstrated robust constraint satisfaction for 2,000 disturbance realizations.
- Any observed variations in terminal-state distributions or linearization error were attributed to convergence towards distinct local minima, inherent to successive linearization's sensitivity to initialization, hyperparameters, and solver tolerances, rather than a degradation of robust feasibility.
- For the unicycle task, objective function components (J_u, J_kv) across NRTO, NRTO-DR, and NRTO-FullADMM differed by less than 0.5%, indicating comparable objective values.

#### 4.5. Real-World Robotarium Experiment

NRTO was deployed on a Robotarium unicycle robot to demonstrate its capability to compensate for real-world model mismatch.
- The disturbance set was estimated from open-loop rollout residuals.
- The affine disturbance-feedback policy ($u_k = \bar{u}_k + K_k d_k$) significantly reduced accumulated tracking error and successfully steered the robot into the target region.
- Executing the nominal control sequence alone resulted in substantial drift, leading to constraint violations.

#### 4.6. Franka Manipulator Experiment

The framework was evaluated on a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda manipulator, a high-dimensional system with $n_x=14$ and $n_u=7$.
- The task involved driving the end-effector to a goal region while satisfying joint limits, velocity limits, torque bounds, and collision avoidance with spherical obstacles.
- With fixed settings ($T=30, \tau=0.01, \text{Obs}=1$), NRTO-FullADMM achieved a 25.9x wall-clock speedup over NRTO (3948.523 s to 152.384 s), indicating scalability to higher-dimensional systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The cuNRTO framework significantly advances the practicality of Nonlinear Robust Trajectory Optimization (NRTO) for autonomous systems. The primary significance lies in its ability to overcome the substantial computational limitations of the original NRTO framework, which previously hindered its widespread application to complex and high-dimensional robotic tasks requiring real-time performance.

The introduction of NRTO-DR, which leverages Douglas-Rachford splitting, and especially NRTO-FullADMM, a novel ADMM variant with full on-device GPU execution, provides validated architectural solutions for accelerating robust control synthesis. The demonstrated speedups, reaching up to 139.6x over the baseline NRTO, directly translate to faster decision-making cycles, enabling the use of robust optimization in scenarios where it was previously infeasible due to time constraints. This is particularly relevant for safety-critical applications where guarantees against bounded deterministic disturbances are crucial.

The maintenance of robust constraint satisfaction across various tasks and parameter variations, while achieving substantial speedups, indicates that the computational efficiency does not come at the expense of solution quality or safety. The successful deployment on the Franka manipulator, a high-dimensional system, further highlights the scalability of the proposed methodology. The real-world Robotarium experiment provides empirical evidence that policies generated by cuNRTO can effectively compensate for real-world model mismatch and disturbances, reinforcing its potential for practical deployment.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Autonomy**: Robots can operate more safely and reliably in uncertain environments, leading to broader adoption in domains such as industrial automation, autonomous driving, and aerial navigation.
- **Real-time Robust Control**: The significant speedups pave the way for real-time robust Model Predictive Control (MPC) for highly nonlinear systems, offering stronger safety guarantees than many existing methods.
- **Complex Robotic Systems**: The framework's scalability to high-dimensional systems like manipulators means it can be applied to intricate tasks involving contact, dynamic obstacles, and complex motion profiles.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work establishes a robust and efficient computational foundation for further exploration in robust control.

Future work directions identified by the authors, such as extending cuNRTO to contact-rich manipulation and locomotion, leveraging learning-to-optimize frameworks (e.g., warm-starting, deep unfolding), and applying it to multi-agent swarms or heterogeneous uncertainties, suggest a broad impact on the future development of advanced robotic control systems.
