# Motion Prompting: Controlling Video Generation with Motion Trajectories

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Google DeepMind and partner institutions introduced "Motion Prompting," a framework for controlling video generation using spatio-temporal point trajectories. This method, built on the Lumiere model, enables fine-grained control over object and camera movements, achieving superior motion adherence and visual quality compared to baselines in both quantitative metrics and human evaluations.

## Problem

- Text prompts are inherently inadequate for conveying intricate nuances of motion in video generation, struggling with specifics like acceleration, exact trajectories, and timing.
- Existing motion conditioning techniques often lack flexibility, struggle with error accumulation (e.g., optical flow), or require specialized adapters for different motion types.
- Generating precise, user-defined dynamic content in videos remained challenging due to the difficulty of translating high-level intent into granular motion control.

## Method

- Introduced spatio-temporally sparse or dense point trajectories (motion prompts) as a flexible and expressive conditioning signal, encoding 2D coordinates and visibility flags for any number of points.
- Integrated this motion representation into the pre-trained Lumiere video ==diffusion model== via a **ControlNet** adapter, trained in a single stage using dense tracks extracted by **BootsTAP** from a large video dataset.
- Developed "motion prompt expansion" techniques to translate high-level user inputs (e.g., mouse drags, geometric primitives, depth-based camera paths) into detailed motion trajectories.

## Results

- "Motion Prompting" consistently outperformed baselines (Image Conductor, DragAnything) in quantitative appearance metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD) and achieved significantly lower End-Point Error for denser tracks on the DAVIS dataset.
- A human study showed strong preference for the proposed method, with win rates ranging from 73.7% to 80.5% across motion adherence, motion quality, and visual quality criteria.
- The framework enables a wide range of applications, including interactive image manipulation (e.g., animating hair), fine-grained object control (e.g., rotating a cat's head), realistic camera movements, and motion transfer between videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatio-temporal point trajectories provide a unified and robust representation for diverse motion control tasks, including object manipulation, camera control, and motion transfer.
> - Training on dense tracks enables strong generalization, allowing the model to perform effectively with varying numbers of tracks, localized tracks, and temporally sparse constraints during inference.
> - The system reveals emergent behaviors, such as realistic physics, indicating the underlying video model's learned "world prior" and offering a tool to probe its understanding of dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of diffusion models. Initial successes predominantly focused on text-to-video generation, where models synthesize videos from natural language descriptions. However, a significant limitation of text prompts is their inability to capture the subtle nuances of motion, acceleration, timing, and specific object trajectories. This paper directly addresses this critical gap, positioning itself at the forefront of research into **fine-grained, explicit motion control for video generation**.

Existing motion conditioning techniques have explored various signals:
- **Sparse motions:** Early works used limited keypoint movements.
- **Entity-centric controls:** Bounding boxes, segmentation masks, human poses, or camera poses offer control over specific elements.
- **Optical flow:** Represents dense motion fields, but struggles with error accumulation and occlusions.

"Motion Prompting" differentiates itself by introducing a highly flexible and comprehensive motion representation: **spatio-temporally sparse or dense point trajectories (also known as point tracks or particle video)**. This choice of representation is a key innovation, offering several advantages over prior methods:
1. **Flexibility:** It can encode any number of trajectories, from a single point to dense fields, enabling control over individual objects, camera movements, or global scene dynamics.
2. **Temporal Granularity:** It supports temporally sparse motion constraints, allowing for control over specific timeframes or emergent prediction where the model fills in the gaps.
3. **Occlusion Handling:** Unlike optical flow, point tracks naturally handle occlusions through visibility flags, crucial for realistic 3D motion.
4. **Unified Control:** It provides a single, consistent interface for diverse control tasks, including object manipulation, camera control, and motion transfer, without requiring specialized adapters for each motion type.

The paper leverages the **ControlNet** architecture, a well-established method for adding conditional control to pre-trained diffusion models without retraining the entire base model. This approach is highly efficient and capitalizes on the strong generative capabilities already present in models like Lumiere. By building on Lumiere, this work is directly extending Google DeepMind's own cutting-edge research in video diffusion models.

Compared to other recent works in motion-conditioned video generation (e.g., Tora, MotionCtrl, DragNUWA, Image Conductor, MCDiff), "Motion Prompting" claims a simpler, single-stage training recipe, yet demonstrates superior versatility and performance across a wide range of tasks. Many prior works often rely on complex multi-stage training, specialized losses, or elaborate data filtering pipelines. This simplicity, combined with strong generalization, suggests a more robust and broadly applicable solution.

Furthermore, the concept of **motion prompt expansion** parallels advancements in text-to-image generation where "prompt engineering" and "prompt expansion" are used to refine user intent. This mechanism aims to bridge the gap between simple user inputs and the detailed motion trajectory representation, making the powerful control accessible to a wider user base.

Ultimately, this research advances the state-of-the-art in controllable video generation, moving beyond high-level textual descriptions to intuitive, granular control over dynamic elements, paving the way for more sophisticated content creation tools and a deeper understanding of generative "world models."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Motion Prompting" stems from a fundamental limitation in current video generation models: their reliance on **text prompts, which are inherently inadequate for conveying the intricate nuances of motion**. While text excels at describing static scenes or high-level actions, it struggles with specifics like acceleration profiles, exact trajectories, timing, and synchronized movements. The authors posit that these subtleties are best expressed through the motion itself.

Building on this insight, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Flexible and Expressive Motion Conditioning Signal:** The core goal is to introduce a motion representation that can encode *any type of motion*—sparse or dense, object-specific or global scene, and temporally partial—to achieve comprehensive control over video content. Spatio-temporal point trajectories are identified as the ideal candidate.
2. **Train a General-Purpose Video Generation Model with Motion Conditioning:** The objective is to integrate this flexible motion representation into a pre-trained video diffusion model (Lumiere) using a ControlNet adapter, enabling it to follow diverse motion prompts.
3. **Bridge User Intent with Detailed Motion Control via "Motion Prompt Expansion":** Recognizing that manually designing complex motion trajectories is impractical, the authors aim to develop methods that translate simple, high-level user requests (e.g., mouse drags, conceptual rotations) into the detailed trajectory format required by the model.
4. **Demonstrate Versatile Applications:** Showcase the broad utility of their motion-prompted video generation across a variety of tasks, including detailed object control, realistic camera movements, motion transfer, and interactive image editing, all within a unified framework.
5. **Probe the "Video Prior" of Generative Models:** Investigate whether motion prompts can reveal emergent behaviors, such as realistic physics and world understanding, learned by the underlying video model, suggesting its potential for interacting with future generative world models.
6. **Quantitatively and Qualitatively Validate Performance:** Rigorously evaluate the proposed method against existing baselines using both objective metrics and human studies to confirm its effectiveness in motion adherence, motion quality, and visual fidelity.

In essence, the paper aims to empower users with unprecedented control over the dynamic aspects of generated videos, transforming the interaction from abstract linguistic descriptions to intuitive, direct manipulation of motion, thereby unlocking new creative possibilities and providing a tool for scientific inquiry into the capabilities of large generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of "Motion Prompting" is centered around a novel motion conditioning signal and a mechanism to integrate it into a pre-trained video diffusion model, complemented by practical strategies for user interaction.

#### 3.1. Motion Representation: Motion Prompts

The core of their approach is the use of **spatio-temporally sparse or dense motion trajectories (point tracks)**.
- **Definition:** A set of $N$ point trajectories of length $T$, where each point $p[n, t] = (x_n^t, y_n^t)$ tracks the 2D coordinate of the $n$-th track at time $t$.
- **Visibility:** Each track also has a visibility flag $v[n, t]$, indicating whether it's visible (1) or occluded/off-screen (0).
- **Advantages:** This representation is highly expressive, capable of capturing any number of points, object-specific or global scene motion, and handling occlusions, making it suitable for granular motion control.

#### 3.2. Architecture

The model is built on top of **Lumiere**, a pre-trained text-and-first-frame-conditioned video diffusion model from Google DeepMind, capable of generating 5 seconds of video (80 frames at 16 fps) at 128x128 resolution (which is then upscaled by Lumiere's spatial super-resolution model to 1024x1024).

To incorporate motion conditioning, they use a **ControlNet** adapter. ControlNet is an architecture that effectively adds conditional control to large pre-trained diffusion models by training a "copy" of the base model's encoder, whose weights are initialized from the base model and then fine-tuned with zero convolutions to smoothly integrate new conditioning signals.

#### 3.3. Conditioning Signal Encoding

The point tracks are encoded into a **space-time volume** $c \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times H \times W \times C}$.
- **Unique Embeddings:** Each track $p[n,:]$ is assigned a unique, random embedding vector $\phi_n \in \mathbb{R}^C$. These embeddings are drawn from a fixed pool and act as unique identifiers for each track.
- **Volume Population:** For every space-time location $(t, x_n^t, y_n^t)$ where a track is visible, its unique embedding $\phi_n$ is placed in the corresponding location in the conditioning volume: $c[t, x_n^t, y_n^t] = v[n, t]\phi_n$. All other locations in the volume are set to zero. If multiple tracks pass through the same location, their embeddings are summed.

#### 3.4. Data and Training

- **Dataset:** An internal Google dataset of 2.2 million videos, resized to 128x128 for the base model.
- **Track Extraction:** **BootsTAP**, an off-the-shelf point tracking method, is run on these videos to extract dense tracks (16,384 tracks per video), along with predicted occlusions. The dataset is used without any filtering, hypothesizing that diverse motions will lead to a more generalizable model.
- **Training Process:** The ControlNet adapter is trained in a single stage with the standard diffusion loss. During training, a random number of tracks (uniformly sampled between 1000 and 2000) are selected from each video to construct the conditioning signal. They use Adafactor optimizer with a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-4}$.
- **Observations:** The authors note "sudden convergence phenomena" similar to ControlNet, where the model rapidly transitions from ignoring the conditioning to full adherence within a few steps. They also highlight strong generalization to spatially localized tracks, varying numbers of tracks (more or fewer than trained), and tracks not starting from the first frame.

#### 3.5. Motion Prompt Expansion

To make motion prompts user-friendly, the paper introduces "motion prompt expansion"—a process to translate high-level user requests into detailed trajectories:
- **"Interacting" with an Image (Mouse Drags):** User mouse drags are converted into a grid of point tracks centered on the cursor. Users can control density, size, and even "pin" background areas with static tracks or make tracks persist after a drag. This enables tasks like manipulating hair or sand and drag-based image editing.
- **Object Control with Geometric Primitives:** Mouse motions are reinterpreted as manipulating a proxy geometric primitive (e.g., a sphere). By placing tracks over an object (e.g., a cat's head), users can achieve fine-grained control like rotations that are difficult with simple sparse drags.
- **Camera Control with Depth:** An off-the-shelf monocular depth estimator is used to create a 3D point cloud from the first frame. A desired camera trajectory is then used to re-project these 3D points back to 2D tracks for each frame, incorporating z-buffering for occlusion flags. Mouse inputs can also be translated into camera trajectories (e.g., fixing a point under the mouse while moving the camera).
- **Composing Motions:** Object and camera control tracks can be combined by adding displacement deltas, enabling simultaneous control (e.g., moving a dog's head while orbiting the camera).
- **Motion Transfer:** Tracks are extracted from a source video (e.g., using BootsTAP) and then applied to a different first frame image, transferring the motion style.

This comprehensive methodology, from robust motion representation to practical user interaction techniques, allows for a powerful and versatile framework for controllable video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive set of qualitative and quantitative results demonstrating the effectiveness and versatility of "Motion Prompting."

#### 4.1. Qualitative Demonstrations and Emergent Behaviors

The authors showcase a wide array of applications enabled by their unified model and motion prompt expansion:
- **"Interacting" with Images:** Simple mouse drags can animate objects (e.g., parrot's head, cow's head) or simulate complex physical interactions like tossing hair or sweeping sand. This reveals emergent behaviors such as realistic physics, indicating the model's learned understanding of the world. The ability to use temporally sparse tracks also enables "prediction" tasks, where the model completes future motion based on an initial prompt.
- **Drag-Based Image Editing:** A natural extension of interaction, allowing users to edit images by dragging specific points, with generated video showing the transformation.
- **Object Control with Primitives:** By mapping mouse input to geometric primitives (e.g., a sphere), users can achieve fine-grained control over object rotations (e.g., rotating a cat's head or a frog's eye), going beyond simple translations.
- **Camera Control with Depth:** Despite not being explicitly trained on camera poses, the model achieves compelling camera control (e.g., orbiting, arcing) by processing 2D tracks derived from 3D scene re-projections using a monocular depth estimator. User mouse input can also drive camera motion.
- **Composing Motions:** The ability to combine object and camera control tracks allows for simultaneous, coordinated movements, further extending the model's expressive power.
- **Motion Transfer:** Tracks extracted from a source video can be applied to an entirely different image, allowing for puppeteering effects (e.g., a person turning their head to animate a macaque) or even out-of-domain transfers (e.g., a monkey chewing motion applied to trees, creating a Gestalt common-fate effect where the "monkey" is only perceived in motion).
- **Model Probing and Limitations:** The paper highlights how motion prompts can be used to reveal the limitations and learned representations of the underlying video model. For instance, dragging a chess piece might result in a new piece spontaneously forming, indicating a less plausible outcome in the model's "world understanding."

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

The method was quantitatively evaluated on the **DAVIS video dataset** against two recent baselines: **Image Conductor** and **DragAnything**. Metrics used included:
- **Appearance metrics:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD.
- **Motion adherence metric:** End-Point Error (EPE), calculated as the L2 distance between conditioning tracks and estimated tracks from generated videos.

**Key Findings (Table 1):**
- **Superior Performance:** "Motion Prompting" consistently outperforms baselines in almost all appearance metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD) across various track densities (N=1 to N=2048).
- **Motion Adherence:** For denser tracks (N=512, N=2048), "Motion Prompting" achieves significantly lower EPE, indicating better adherence to the specified motion. For sparser tracks, DragAnything sometimes shows better EPE, but at the cost of visual quality (higher LPIPS, lower PSNR/SSIM), likely due to its latent warping module causing artifacts.
- The results strongly suggest that the proposed method generates videos that are not only more visually faithful but also more accurately follow the given motion instructions, especially with increasing detail in the motion prompt.

#### 4.3. Human Study

A human study (2AFC test) with 30 handcrafted inputs was conducted, comparing "Motion Prompting" against baselines on three criteria:
- **Motion Adherence:** Which video better follows the motion conditioning?
- **Motion Quality:** Which video has more realistic motion?
- **Visual Quality:** Which video has higher visual quality?

**Key Findings (Table 2):**
- "Motion Prompting" achieved **significantly higher win rates** across all three categories (ranging from 73.7% to 80.5%) when compared to both Image Conductor and DragAnything. This indicates a strong preference from human evaluators for the proposed method's ability to generate videos that are both visually appealing and accurately controlled.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablations on training track density (Sparse, Dense + Sparse, Dense) revealed that **training on dense tracks is most effective**, yielding the best performance even for sparse inference tracks. This suggests that dense training provides a richer signal, enabling the model to generalize better across different track densities.

In summary, the results highlight "Motion Prompting" as a highly effective and versatile framework for controllable video generation, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both quantitative metrics and human perception, while also revealing intriguing emergent capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Motion Prompting" framework represents a significant leap forward in the field of controllable video generation, with profound implications for content creation, human-AI interaction, and the scientific understanding of generative world models.

**1. Enhanced Control and Expressiveness:** By introducing spatio-temporal point trajectories as a flexible motion conditioning signal, the paper addresses a critical limitation of text-based video generation. This granular control allows creators to precisely dictate movements of objects, cameras, and even subtle physical dynamics, unlocking a new level of artistic and expressive freedom previously unattainable. The ability to handle both sparse and dense motion, and occlusions, makes the control highly versatile and robust.

**2. Intuitive User Interaction:** The concept of "motion prompt expansion" is a game-changer for usability. By translating simple user inputs like mouse drags or high-level geometric manipulations into complex motion trajectories, the framework makes advanced motion control accessible to a broader audience without requiring technical expertise in animation or tracking. This bridges the gap between user intention and the underlying generative model's capabilities.

**3. Broad Range of Applications from a Unified Model:** The work demonstrates a wide variety of applications—object control, camera control, motion transfer, and interactive image editing—all stemming from a single, unified trained model. This contrasts with approaches that require specialized models or fine-tuning for different control types, making "Motion Prompting" a more general and efficient solution.

**4. Emergent Behaviors and Probing World Models:** Perhaps one of the most exciting aspects is the observation of emergent behaviors, such as realistic physics, when interacting with the generated content. This suggests that the underlying video diffusion model (Lumiere) has learned a sophisticated "world prior"—an implicit understanding of physical laws and object interactions. Motion prompts thus become a powerful tool for *probing* these generative world models, allowing researchers to investigate what knowledge these models have acquired and where their limitations lie. This could pave the way for understanding and ultimately building more coherent and intelligent AI systems capable of simulating complex environments.

**5. State-of-the-Art Performance:** Quantitative evaluations and human studies confirm that the method not only provides novel control mechanisms but also outperforms existing baselines in terms of motion adherence, motion quality, and overall visual quality. This establishes "Motion Prompting" as a new benchmark in controllable video synthesis.

**6. Future Directions for Generative AI:** The ability to intuitively "interact" with generative outputs, as demonstrated with hair and sand manipulation, strongly hints at future possibilities for "generative world models" and embodied AI. Imagine interacting with virtual environments where objects respond realistically to user-defined motions, or where AI agents can learn complex motor skills by manipulating simulated physics. This research lays foundational groundwork for such interactive and intelligent systems.

In conclusion, "Motion Prompting" is a pivotal contribution that transforms how users can interact with and control video generation models. It moves beyond abstract textual descriptions to direct, expressive manipulation of dynamics, while simultaneously offering a unique lens through which to understand the emergent intelligence within powerful generative AI systems.
