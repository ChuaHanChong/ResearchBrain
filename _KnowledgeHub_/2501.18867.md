# UP-VLA: A Unified Understanding and Prediction Model for Embodied Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, UP-VLA, integrates multi-modal understanding with future visual prediction to enhance robotic control. The model achieved a 33% improvement on the CALVIN benchmark's ABC→D task over previous state-of-the-art and demonstrated improved generalization and precision on real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack precise low-level visual, spatial, and physical dynamic understanding essential for robotic manipulation.
- Visual pre-training methods (e.g., future frame prediction) excel at low-level visual patterns but typically lack the rich semantic and language reasoning capabilities of VLMs.
- There is a challenge in developing a single VLA model that can seamlessly handle both high-level semantic comprehension and accurate low-level physical action execution.

## Method

- **UP-VLA** proposes a ==unified autoregressive framework== based on an ==LLM backbone (Phi-1.5)==, incorporating both ==continuous vision encoders== for multi-modal understanding (MMU) and ==discrete vision encoders== for future visual prediction.
- It unifies MMU, future prediction, and action generation tasks into a single ==sequence modeling== problem, using a ==flexible attention mask== and a ==two-stage training strategy==.
- Action learning is enhanced through ==joint future observation prediction== and ==understanding-augmented prompting==, where the model's own MMU capabilities describe the scene to guide action generation.

## Results

- Achieved an average completion length of **4.08** on the CALVIN benchmark's ABC→D task, representing a **33%** improvement over the previous state-of-the-art.
- Demonstrated improved performance on real-world Franka-Emika Panda robot tasks, particularly in unseen semantic generalization and precise operations requiring detailed visual and spatial understanding.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both visual prediction capabilities and multi-modal understanding components are indispensable for achieving superior generalization and precision in embodied AI tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating both high-level multi-modal understanding and low-level future visual prediction is crucial for building robust and generalizable embodied agents.
> - Future visual prediction provides vital low-level visual and spatial understanding necessary for precise robotic control, while multi-modal understanding enhances semantic generalization.
> - A unified autoregressive training paradigm can effectively combine these diverse capabilities, leading to superior performance in complex manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotics has seen rapid advancements, with a central focus on developing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models capable of solving diverse tasks in open-ended environments. A prominent approach involves leveraging large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, and PaLM-E, to imbue VLA models with rich semantic knowledge and powerful reasoning abilities. Models like Google's RT-2 have demonstrated the potential of this paradigm by directly mapping language and visual inputs to robotic actions.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation within this VLM-centric approach: while VLMs excel at high-level semantic understanding (e.g., answering "What is on the table?"), their pre-training paradigms often neglect low-level visual features and understanding of physical dynamics. Previous research (e.g., Zheng et al., 2024a; Chen et al., 2024a; Wen et al., 2024) has highlighted VLMs' weaknesses in spatial understanding, perceiving precise details like distance and size, and grasping physical interactions. These aspects are paramount for precise robotic control tasks, which demand fine-grained visual and spatial information.

Concurrently, another line of research focuses on visual pre-training methods for robotics, often involving generative models for future frame prediction or video generation (e.g., GR-1, SuSIE). These methods excel at capturing low-level visual patterns and physical dynamics, demonstrating strong visual generalization. However, they typically lack the rich semantic understanding and language reasoning capabilities inherent in VLMs.

UP-VLA aims to bridge this gap. Instead of relying solely on one paradigm, it proposes a novel unified training approach that combines the strengths of both: high-level semantic comprehension from multi-modal understanding tasks and low-level spatial and dynamic understanding from future visual prediction. This positions UP-VLA as a significant step towards truly generalist embodied agents that can both understand complex linguistic instructions and execute precise physical actions in varied environments, addressing a fundamental challenge at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind UP-VLA stem from the identified limitations of existing VLA models:

1. **Address the deficiency of low-level visual and spatial understanding in VLM-based VLA models:** Current VLM-based VLA models, despite their strong semantic reasoning, often struggle with tasks requiring precise spatial information, understanding of object sizes/distances, and physical dynamics. This is a critical bottleneck for robotic manipulation, where fine-grained motor control and accurate perception of the physical environment are essential.
2. **Combine the strengths of multi-modal understanding and visual prediction:** The authors are motivated by the observation that while VLMs provide high-level semantic knowledge, visual prediction tasks (like future frame generation) are better at capturing low-level visual patterns and physical dynamics. The objective is to design a training paradigm that can effectively integrate both, allowing the model to leverage both semantic reasoning and precise visual understanding.
3. **Develop a unified model for embodied agents:** The ultimate goal is to create a single VLA model that can seamlessly handle high-level semantic understanding (e.g., Visual Question Answering), generate predictions about future visual states, and accurately produce robotic actions. This unification is expected to lead to more robust and generalizable policies for complex, open-world robotic tasks.
4. **Enhance generalization capabilities:** By integrating diverse training objectives, the authors aim to improve the model's ability to generalize to unseen objects, tasks, and environments in both simulation and real-world scenarios, a crucial aspect for practical robotic deployment.

In essence, the motivation is to answer the question: "Can a better training pipeline be developed to combine the strengths of both worlds, retaining semantic understanding while also emphasizing low-level features critical for control?" UP-VLA is presented as a direct attempt to answer this question positively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UP-VLA introduces a novel unified training paradigm for VLA models that integrates multi-modal understanding and future visual prediction objectives within a single autoregressive framework.

#### 3.1. Backbone Architecture

- **Large Language Model (LLM):** Phi-1.5 (Li et al., 2023c) serves as the underlying LLM, providing the core autoregressive sequence modeling capabilities.
- **Vision Encoders:**
 - **Continuous Vision Encoder (for MMU):** A CLIP-ViT (Radford et al., 2021) encoder projects images into the language embedding space via a connection layer. These continuous image features are used for multi-modal understanding tasks.
 - **Discrete Vision Encoder (for Future Prediction):** A VQ-GAN (Esser et al., 2021) is used to encode current visual observations into discrete tokens. This is crucial for treating images as sequences of tokens, enabling autoregressive prediction of future image tokens.
- **Decoder:** A discrete decoder reconstructs the predicted future observation images from the discrete tokens.

#### 3.2. Bridging Visual Prediction and Multi-modal Understanding

The core innovation lies in encoding different task types (MMU, future prediction, action generation) into a unified format that can be processed by the LLM backbone, utilizing a flexible attention mask.
- **Multi-modal Understanding (MMU):** Given an image-text pair (I, L), the continuous image tokens `u` are concatenated with language embeddings `l`. The attention mechanism is modified to allow image tokens to attend to each other. The objective is autoregressive prediction of the next language token, similar to Visual Question Answering (VQA).
- **Future Visual Prediction (PRE):** Given an image and instruction pair (O_t, L), the discrete image tokens `v_t` from the current observation are placed *after* the language tokens in the input sequence. This allows image tokens to attend to all preceding information (language and current image). A special token `PRE` denotes this task. The objective is to directly predict future discrete image tokens `v_{t+Δt}` at corresponding output positions.

#### 3.3. Enhancing Action Learning with Joint Prediction and Understanding

UP-VLA integrates action output directly with image prediction tasks and enhances it with scene understanding.
- **Joint Prediction:** During action learning, the model predicts both future observations and a sequence of actions simultaneously: (`O_{t+Δt}`, `A_{t:t+Δt}`) = `π_PRE_θ(O_t, L)`. The actions (`A`) are derived from the final layer features at designated action token positions.
- **Understanding-Augmented Prompting:** To leverage MMU capabilities for action tasks, the language instruction input `L` is expanded to `L'`. This expanded prompt includes:
 - Continuous image embeddings of the current observation: `E1(O_t')`.
 - A scene description generated by the model's own MMU capability: `π_MMU_θ(O_t, L_prompt)` (e.g., using "describe this image" as `L_prompt`).
 - The original language instruction `L`.
 This augmented prompt `L'` then guides the joint future prediction and action generation.
- **Policy Head:** A small policy head (MAP module + linear layer) converts the model's output features (`A_{t:t+Δt}`) into low-level robotic actions (e.g., relative position `a_pos` and end-effector status `a_end`).

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

The training is divided into two stages:
- **Stage 1: Prediction and Understanding Pretraining:** The model is pre-trained on mixed datasets:
 - **Bridge dataset (Walke et al., 2023):** ~25k robotic arm demonstrations for future visual prediction.
 - **LLava-tuning-665k (Liu et al., 2024):** ~665k image-text pairs for enhancing high-level multi-modal understanding.
 All LLM parameters are fine-tuned, while encoders are frozen.
- **Stage 2: Prediction with Action Tuning:** The model is fine-tuned on downstream embodied tasks (Calvin and real-world datasets) using the joint prediction-and-understanding action learning approach. Co-training with image-text pairs from Stage 1 continues to preserve MMU ability.

#### 3.5. Training Objective

The overall loss `L` is a weighted sum of three components:
- **Language Modeling Loss (L_MMU):** Cross-entropy loss for next language token prediction in MMU tasks.
- **Image Modeling Loss (L_PRE):** Cross-entropy loss for future image token prediction in visual prediction tasks.
- **Action Modeling Loss (L_ACT):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) for continuous action components (e.g., relative position) and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for discrete action components (e.g., end-effector status).

This comprehensive methodology allows UP-VLA to learn both semantic and low-level visual features, addressing the specific challenges of embodied AI by integrating diverse forms of knowledge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UP-VLA was extensively evaluated on both simulated (CALVIN benchmark) and real-world Franka-Emika Panda robot manipulation tasks, demonstrating significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation (CALVIN Benchmark)

- **Overall Performance:** UP-VLA achieved the highest performance on both ABC→D (out-of-distribution generalization) and ABCD→D (in-distribution multitask learning) tasks.
 - On ABC→D, UP-VLA scored an average completion length of **4.08**, a **33% improvement** over the previous SOTA (GR-1 at 3.06).
 - On ABCD→D, UP-VLA achieved **4.42**, outperforming GR-1 (4.21) and Robo-Flamingo (4.09).
- **Effectiveness of Visual Prediction:** Comparing VLA-based methods (e.g., RoboFlamingo, UP-VLA-RT-2) with prediction-based methods (e.g., GR-1, UP-VLA-phi-w/o-mmu), the results showed that prediction-based methods generally performed better in simulation tasks requiring visual generalization. UP-VLA's integration of visual prediction significantly boosted its performance (e.g., UP-VLA's 4.08 vs. UP-VLA-RT-2's 1.44 on ABC→D), confirming the value of this component.
- **Effectiveness of Multi-modal Understanding:** The ablation study with `UP-VLA-phi-w/o-mmu` (trained without MMU) showed a performance drop compared to full UP-VLA (3.13 vs. 4.08 on ABC→D). This indicates that injecting multi-modal understanding capabilities into the model significantly improves its generalization ability in new simulation scenarios.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Evaluation

- UP-VLA demonstrated significant improvements across various real-world manipulation tasks (seen, unseen, and precise operations).
- **Seen Tasks:** All methods based on the UP-VLA backbone (UP-VLA, UP-VLA-RT-2, UP-VLA-phi-w/o-mmu) outperformed RT-1 and Diffusion Policy, highlighting the superior multitasking capabilities of LLM backbones.
- **Unseen Semantic Generalization (New Objects):** `UP-VLA-RT-2` (VLM-based) outperformed `UP-VLA-phi-w/o-mmu` (prediction-based) on unseen objects, indicating that MMU capabilities aid semantic generalization. UP-VLA showed the best performance, proving effective alignment of multi-modal understanding with new objects and actions.
- **Precise Operations (Visual Details/Spatial Understanding):** For tasks like routing cables or grasping small, unseen objects, `UP-VLA-phi-w/o-mmu` (prediction-based) excelled over `UP-VLA-RT-2` (VLM-based), suggesting that visual prediction enhances spatial understanding and detail perception. Critically, UP-VLA performed best on these tasks, underscoring the combined benefit of its unified approach for precision.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Component Analysis)

- **Impact of Visual Prediction:** Removing the visual prediction task (`UP-VLA-w/o-Prediction`) caused a dramatic drop in Calvin performance from 4.08 to 1.44. Omitting pretraining on the Bridge dataset (`UP-VLA-w/o-Bridge-Pretrain`) also led to significant degradation (2.74). This confirms that strong visual supervision from prediction is crucial for generalization and understanding physical world dynamics.
- **Impact of Multi-modal Understanding:** Removing MMU tasks (`UP-VLA-w/o-MMU`) or the MMU-condition mechanism (`UP-VLA-w/o-MMU-Condition`) led to a decline in performance on *unseen* objects in real-world experiments, while often having comparable or even slightly better performance on *seen* tasks. This strongly supports that joint MMU training and using MMU to augment input prompts are vital for semantic generalization and preventing overfitting.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (VQA and Future Prediction)

- **Multi-modal Understanding:** UP-VLA effectively identified objects and estimated their relative positions in embodied scenes, crucial for action learning. However, some inaccuracies in specific object identification were noted, potentially due to data scale limitations.
- **Future Prediction:** The model accurately predicted robot arm and object positions based on instructions. A limitation observed was the model's tendency to use background colors from training datasets when predicting frames in new environments (e.g., Calvin D), suggesting room for improvement in visual generation generalization.

In summary, UP-VLA consistently outperforms baselines by effectively combining high-level semantic understanding with low-level visual and spatial reasoning, validated across diverse simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks. The ablation studies compellingly demonstrate that both components are indispensable for achieving superior generalization and precision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of UP-VLA represents a significant stride in the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, addressing a critical dichotomy in current model capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the High-Level/Low-Level Gap:** UP-VLA successfully tackles the long-standing challenge of integrating high-level semantic understanding (common in VLMs) with the fine-grained, low-level spatial and dynamic reasoning crucial for physical robot control. By unifying multi-modal understanding and future visual prediction, the model learns both *what* to do (from language and semantic context) and *how* to do it precisely (from visual dynamics and spatial details). This holistic approach leads to more capable and versatile robotic policies.

2. **Enhanced Generalization for Embodied Agents:** The impressive performance improvements, particularly in zero-shot generalization tasks on the CALVIN benchmark (33% over SOTA) and handling unseen objects and precise operations in real-world scenarios, highlight UP-VLA's superior generalization abilities. This is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments, where encountering novel situations is the norm. The model's ability to adapt to new scenes and objects without explicit re-training drastically reduces development and deployment costs.

3. **Unified Training Paradigm as a Blueprint:** The proposed training paradigm, which strategically co-trains an autoregressive model with distinct but complementary objectives, offers a valuable blueprint for future research in embodied AI. It demonstrates that fusing different forms of pre-training (vision-language and video prediction) can lead to emergent capabilities stronger than either component in isolation. This could inspire new architectures and training strategies for developing more robust foundation models for robotics.

4. **Improved Robotic Control Precision:** For tasks requiring high precision, such as grasping small objects or routing cables, UP-VLA's emphasis on low-level visual patterns through future prediction proves invaluable. This directly translates to more reliable and accurate robot manipulation, which is a bottleneck for many real-world applications in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and service robotics.

5. **Path towards Generalist Robots:** By enabling robots to understand complex instructions, perceive their environment in rich detail, and predict future states, UP-VLA contributes to the vision of truly generalist robots. These robots would not be limited to predefined tasks but could perform a wide array of activities in diverse settings, adapting to human commands and environmental changes.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** More reliable and generalizable robotic policies will reduce the need for extensive task-specific data collection and fine-tuning, accelerating the deployment of robots in various industries.
- **Safer and More Adaptive Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots with better scene understanding and predictive capabilities can interact more safely and effectively with humans and navigate complex environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** UP-VLA provides a strong baseline and methodology for exploring more advanced unified models, perhaps integrating other modalities like tactile feedback or leveraging even larger-scale internet data for more comprehensive pre-training.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (e.g., generalization in visual generation, potential for larger backbones), UP-VLA marks a significant step forward in the quest for intelligent, versatile, and precise embodied agents capable of navigating and manipulating the physical world under human instruction.
