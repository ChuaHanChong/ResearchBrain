# HIRI-ViT: Scaling Vision Transformer with High Resolution Inputs

> [!summary] Summary
> HIRI-ViT, a five-stage Vision Transformer architecture from HiDream.ai, introduces a principled approach to efficiently process high-resolution images. This architecture integrates specialized CNN-based operations with transformer blocks and a mixed normalization strategy, enabling it to achieve state-of-the-art performance on image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation benchmarks while maintaining manageable computational costs.

## Problem

- Traditional Vision Transformers face quadratic computational complexity with increasing input image resolutions due to the self-attention mechanism, making high-resolution processing prohibitively expensive.
- Standard ViT architectures often lose fine-grained details when downsampling high-resolution images, which are critical for dense prediction tasks like detection and segmentation.
- Achieving an efficient balance between performance and computational cost when scaling ViTs to very high resolutions remains a challenge for existing hierarchical or hybrid approaches.
- There was a need for a principled way to scale CNN+ViT hybrid backbones for high-resolution inputs while maintaining comparable computational overheads to lower-resolution models.

## Method

- ==HIRI-ViT== introduces a ==five-stage Vision Transformer architecture== that processes high-resolution inputs through progressive stages, with the first three stages focusing on high-resolution CNN-based operations and the last two on transformer blocks.
- A novel ==High-Resolution Stem (HR-stem)== and ==High-Resolution Block (HR-block)== are designed with a two-branch structure (high-resolution and low-resolution paths) to efficiently process fine details and fuse results.
- The model incorporates ==Inverted Residual Downsampling (IRDS)== between stages to better preserve information during resolution reduction by creating parallel paths that maintain different aspects of input features.
- It employs a ==mixed normalization strategy==, utilizing Batch Normalization in the initial high-resolution stages and Layer Normalization in the later low-resolution stages to optimize training stability and inference efficiency.

## Results

- Achieves **84.3%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with **448×**448 inputs at approximately **5.0 GFLOPs**, outperforming competing models and reaching **86.1%** at **768×**768 resolution.
- Consistently outperforms other backbones on COCO object detection and instance segmentation, with HIRI-ViT-L attaining **4**9.1%** box AP** and **4**4.0%** mask AP**.
- Demonstrates superior performance on ADE20K semantic segmentation, with HIRI-ViT-L achieving **5**1.3%** mIoU**.
- Establishes a new efficiency frontier for high-resolution vision models across GFLOPs, parameter count, and inference time, offering higher accuracy at comparable computational costs compared to state-of-the-art models like Swin and DaViT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A five-stage CNN+ViT hybrid architecture effectively scales Vision Transformers to high-resolution inputs by gradually reducing spatial dimensions while increasing feature dimensions.
> - Specialized two-branch processing within the High-Resolution Stem (HR-stem) and High-Resolution Block (HR-block) efficiently preserves fine-grained details and expands receptive fields at lower computational cost.
> - A mixed normalization strategy, using Batch Normalization for high-resolution stages and Layer Normalization for low-resolution stages, optimizes both training stability and inference efficiency.
> - Sophisticated downsampling through Inverted Residual Downsampling (IRDS) further contributes to preserving rich feature representations during resolution transitions between stages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have become increasingly popular in computer vision due to their strong performance across various tasks. However, as input image resolutions increase, traditional ViT architectures face significant computational challenges due to the quadratic scaling of attention mechanisms. The paper "HIRI-ViT: Scaling Vision Transformer with High Resolution Inputs" introduces a novel approach to effectively handle high-resolution inputs while maintaining reasonable computational costs.

The work from Ting Yao, Yehao Li, Yingwei Pan, and Tao Mei at HiDream.ai addresses a critical question: "Is there a principled way to scale up CNN+ViT hybrid backbones with high-resolution inputs while maintaining comparable computational overhead?" Their answer is HIRI-ViT, a five-stage Vision Transformer architecture specifically designed for high-resolution inputs that achieves state-of-the-art performance while keeping computational costs manageable.

### 2. Background and Challenges

Vision Transformers have demonstrated impressive capabilities in computer vision tasks, but they weren't originally designed with the inductive biases necessary for efficiently processing high-resolution images. Several challenges emerge when scaling ViTs to higher resolutions:

1. **Computational Complexity**: The self-attention mechanism in traditional ViTs scales quadratically with sequence length, making high-resolution inputs prohibitively expensive.

2. **Feature Representation**: High-resolution images contain fine-grained details that are important for tasks like detection and segmentation, but are often lost in standard downsampling approaches.

3. **Efficiency-Performance Trade-off**: Simply increasing resolution typically results in diminishing returns relative to computational costs.

Previous approaches have attempted to address these issues through hierarchical designs (Swin Transformer), multi-scale architectures (HRNet, HRViT), or CNN-Transformer hybrids (ConvNeXt). However, these methods still struggle to efficiently scale to very high resolutions.

### 3. The HIRI-ViT Architecture

HIRI-ViT introduces a comprehensive re-design of Vision Transformer architectures for high-resolution inputs. The architecture features:

1. **Five-Stage Design**: Unlike conventional four-stage ViTs, HIRI-ViT employs a five-stage architecture that allows for more gradual downsampling of high-resolution inputs.

2. **Resolution-Aware Stage Design**: The first three stages focus on processing high-resolution features with CNN-based operations, while the last two stages leverage transformer blocks for global semantic understanding.

3. **Hybrid Processing Strategy**: Different normalization strategies (Batch Normalization for high-resolution stages, Layer Normalization for low-resolution stages) are used to optimize both training and inference.

The overall architecture processes high-resolution inputs (e.g., 448×448) through a specialized stem, followed by five progressive stages that gradually reduce spatial dimensions while increasing feature dimensions.

### 4. Key Technical Innovations

HIRI-ViT introduces several technical innovations that enable efficient handling of high-resolution inputs:

#### 4.1. High-Resolution Stem (HR-stem)

The conventional stem in ViTs typically uses a 7×7 convolution with stride 4, which causes immediate loss of fine-grained information. HIRI-ViT replaces this with a two-branch structure:

- **High-Resolution Branch**: Uses depth-wise convolutions to process fine details with fewer parameters
- **Low-Resolution Branch**: Performs down-sampling and more extensive convolution operations on reduced-resolution features

This dual-branch structure maintains high-resolution information while limiting computational cost.

#### 4.2. High-Resolution Block (HR-block)

Traditional CNN blocks are replaced with a specialized HR-block that follows a similar two-branch philosophy:

The HR-block decomposes processing into high-resolution and low-resolution branches that operate in parallel, then fuses the results. This approach achieves the same receptive field as standard blocks but with lower computational cost.

#### 4.3. Inverted Residual Downsampling (IRDS)

To better preserve information during downsampling between stages, HIRI-ViT introduces a more sophisticated downsampling mechanism:

The IRDS approach creates parallel paths that maintain different aspects of the input features during downsampling, resulting in richer feature representations.

#### 4.4. Mixed Normalization Strategy

HIRI-ViT employs a hybrid normalization approach:
- Batch Normalization (BN) in the first three stages (high-resolution processing)
- Layer Normalization (LN) in the last two stages (low-resolution processing)

This mixed strategy optimizes both training stability and inference efficiency.

### 5. Performance Evaluation

HIRI-ViT was evaluated on several benchmark tasks including image recognition, object detection, and segmentation.

#### 5.1. ImageNet Classification

On ImageNet-1K classification, HIRI-ViT achieves 84.3% top-1 accuracy with 448×448 inputs at approximately 5.0 GFLOPs, outperforming competing models like iFormer-S (83.4% at 224×224 inputs). The results demonstrate HIRI-ViT's superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

When scaling to even higher resolutions (768×768), HIRI-ViT reaches 86.1% top-1 accuracy, demonstrating excellent scaling properties.

#### 5.2. Object Detection and Instance Segmentation

On COCO object detection and instance segmentation using Mask R-CNN, HIRI-ViT consistently outperforms other backbones:

- HIRI-ViT-S achieves 45.8% box AP and 41.3% mask AP
- HIRI-ViT-B reaches 47.7% box AP and 42.9% mask AP
- HIRI-ViT-L attains 49.1% box AP and 44.0% mask AP

These results surpass comparable models with similar computational costs.

#### 5.3. Semantic Segmentation

On ADE20K semantic segmentation with UperNet, HIRI-ViT also demonstrates superior performance:
- HIRI-ViT-S: 48.4% mIoU
- HIRI-ViT-B: 50.0% mIoU
- HIRI-ViT-L: 51.3% mIoU

### 6. Comparison with State of the Art

As shown in Figure 6, HIRI-ViT establishes a new efficiency frontier for high-resolution vision models across three key metrics:

1. **Computational Efficiency (GFLOPs)**: HIRI-ViT achieves higher accuracy at comparable computational cost.
2. **Parameter Efficiency**: The models maintain competitive performance with a reasonable parameter count.
3. **Inference Efficiency**: HIRI-ViT delivers faster inference times than alternatives at similar accuracy levels.

The HIRI-ViT family (S, B, and L variants) consistently outperforms other models including DaViT, Swin, MaxViT, and iFormer across a range of computational budgets.

### 7. Ablation Studies

The authors conducted comprehensive ablation studies to validate each component:

1. **Five-Stage Architecture**: Compared to the conventional four-stage design, the five-stage approach delivers 0.5% higher accuracy with similar computational costs, demonstrating more efficient processing of high-resolution inputs.

2. **Two-Branch Building Blocks**: The HR-stem and HR-block improve accuracy by 0.3% and 0.4% respectively, validating the parallel high/low-resolution processing approach.

3. **Inverted Residual Downsampling**: IRDS contributes a 0.2% improvement in accuracy, confirming its effectiveness in preserving information during resolution transitions.

4. **Normalization Strategy**: The mixed BN/LN approach provides a 0.2% accuracy improvement while reducing inference time by 15% compared to using only LN.

5. **EMA Distillation**: This training technique adds a further 0.3% to the final model's accuracy.

### 8. Impact and Applications

HIRI-ViT represents a significant advancement in high-resolution vision processing with several important implications:

1. **Computer Vision Tasks**: The architecture's ability to efficiently process high-resolution inputs makes it valuable for tasks requiring fine-grained details, such as medical image analysis, satellite imagery, and industrial inspection.

2. **Mobile and Edge Applications**: Despite handling high resolutions, HIRI-ViT's computational efficiency makes it suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

3. **Foundation Models**: The principles introduced could inform the design of more efficient vision foundation models capable of handling high-resolution inputs.

4. **Transfer Learning**: HIRI-ViT's strong ImageNet performance suggests it would serve as an effective pre-training backbone for downstream tasks.

### 9. Conclusion

HIRI-ViT introduces a principled approach to scaling Vision Transformers with high-resolution inputs while maintaining reasonable computational costs. Through its five-stage architecture, two-branch building blocks, and mixed normalization strategy, it achieves state-of-the-art performance across image classification, object detection, and segmentation tasks.

The research answers the fundamental question of how to efficiently scale ViT architectures to high resolutions, providing a 0.9% absolute improvement in ImageNet accuracy compared to previous approaches at similar computational budgets. HIRI-ViT establishes a new efficient frontier for high-resolution vision models and demonstrates that properly designed architectures can effectively leverage increased resolution without prohibitive computational costs.

As computer vision tasks increasingly demand higher resolution inputs for fine-grained understanding, HIRI-ViT provides a valuable architectural blueprint that balances performance, efficiency, and scalability.
