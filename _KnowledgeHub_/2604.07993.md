# HEX: Humanoid-Aligned Experts for Cross-Embodiment Whole-Body Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HEX is a Vision-Language-Action framework developed by Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics and collaborators, designed for full-sized bipedal humanoid robots to perform coordinated whole-body manipulation. It introduces a humanoid-aligned state representation and a Mixture-of-Experts Unified Proprioceptive Predictor to enable cross-embodiment learning and achieved an average 79.8% success rate across real-robot tasks, outperforming existing baselines in generalization and long-horizon scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often treat robot body parts independently, leading to uncoordinated and unstable behaviors on high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoid platforms.
- A lack of embodiment-aware predictive dynamics hinders robots from understanding and maintaining balance and posture during complex, coordinated whole-body movements.
- Current VLA models struggle with temporally grounded scene understanding, which is crucial for long-horizon planning and fast reactions in dynamic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **HEX** is a ==hierarchical VLA framework== comprising a high-level VLA policy for action generation and a low-level ==RL-based whole-body controller== for balance-preserving motion execution.
- A ==Unified Proprioceptive Predictor (UPP)== with a ==morphology-based Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== is introduced to model structured proprioceptive dependencies across body parts.
- A ==review-and-forecast paradigm== integrates a lightweight visual history query cache for temporal context and future state prediction for adaptive multimodal fusion.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **79.8%** across seven in-distribution real-robot tasks, outperforming baselines like GR00T N1.5 (**70.2%**) and ACT (**57.1%**).
- Demonstrated robust generalization with an overall average success rate of **61.8%** across eight variants of unseen scene variations, surpassing π0.5 (**44.3%**) and GR00T N1.5 (**41.0%**).
- Secured **100%** success in initial stages of a multi-stage 'long-horizon box convey' task and **53.3%** in the 'Place Box' stage, outperforming the strongest baseline π0.5 at **40.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling structured proprioceptive dependencies through a humanoid-aligned state representation and MoE design significantly enhances whole-body coordination and facilitates learning across heterogeneous robot embodiments.
> - Integrating a lightweight visual history summarization with future proprioceptive state prediction provides crucial temporal intelligence, improving performance in long-horizon and fast-reaction tasks.
> - Pretraining on a large, diverse dataset of humanoid trajectories (over 12M frames) improves optimization efficiency and sample efficiency during fine-tuning for specific tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics, particularly humanoid robotics, faces challenges in achieving complex manipulation that mirrors human capabilities. Humans naturally employ coordinated whole-body control for various tasks, but most existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robots primarily treat body parts as independent entities. This independent control paradigm complicates high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoid control, often leading to instability and difficulty in executing complex, coordinated movements.

Current approaches to humanoid whole-body manipulation generally fall into two categories. The first involves explicitly decomposed designs, where locomotion or navigation is handled by a separate policy from manipulation. While this decomposition simplifies control and learning, it heavily relies on manual task priors and interface designs, leading to fragility as task complexity increases and potential accumulation of errors across modules. This makes robust execution of tightly coupled behaviors, such as manipulation during locomotion, challenging.

A more recent trend involves hierarchical designs, where a high-level module generates task-relevant commands (e.g., arm and hand actions or hand-eye targets), which are then refined by a low-level whole-body controller into high-frequency, balance-preserving motions. In parallel, advancements in VLA models, leveraging large vision-language models, have introduced enhanced visual-semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities. These VLA models are increasingly adopted in humanoid systems to provide high-level planning. However, many existing VLA-based approaches for humanoids still predict actions over high-dimensional joints or latent commands without explicitly modeling the intricate interactions between body parts that are essential for maintaining shared balance and posture. Consequently, while these policies might capture the semantic intent of a task, they may struggle to produce coordinated whole-body behaviors, especially in scenarios requiring fast reactions or long-horizon planning where temporal consistency and whole-body coordination are critical.

Cross-embodiment learning is another significant area, focusing on transferring knowledge across robots with different morphologies. This includes learning from human videos or egocentric demonstrations (human-to-humanoid) or transferring policies/representations between heterogeneous robotic embodiments (robot-to-humanoid or cross-humanoid). Prior works in this domain have focused on generalizable pretrained policies or aligned control spaces.

The presented work, HEX, distinguishes itself by directly addressing the limitation of uncoordinated body part control in VLA models. It proposes a framework that explicitly models structured proprioceptive dependencies for humanoid whole-body control. Unlike prior approaches that focus primarily on semantic grounding or multimodal conditioning, HEX integrates VLA reasoning with a humanoid-aligned state representation and incorporates joint past-future temporal modeling. This aims to facilitate coordinated whole-body behavior. Furthermore, in the context of cross-embodiment learning, HEX introduces a compositional and humanoid-aligned proprioceptive modeling framework using a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design. This approach enables flexible encoding of heterogeneous trajectories from various embodiments into a shared latent space, avoiding the need to retrain a monolithic state encoder for each new joint configuration or missing body part.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while humanoid robots hold significant potential for operating in human environments, existing control paradigms fall short in enabling complex, coordinated whole-body manipulation. Humans perform such tasks effortlessly by coordinating their entire body, whereas robots struggle due to the independent treatment of body parts by most VLA models, leading to control challenges and instability in high-DoF systems. The lack of explicit modeling for how body parts interact through shared balance and posture means that even advanced VLA models may fail to produce coherent whole-body behaviors, particularly in dynamic or extended tasks.

The key objectives of the HEX framework are detailed as follows:

1. **Develop a Framework for Coordinated Whole-Body Manipulation:** The overarching goal is to enable full-sized bipedal humanoid robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that require the coordinated movement of their entire body, including arms, hands, waist, and legs, while maintaining dynamic balance.
2. **Introduce a Humanoid-Aligned Universal State Representation:** To facilitate scalable learning across a diverse range of heterogeneous humanoid embodiments (which can vary in kinematics, dynamics, and sensor configurations), HEX aims to create a structured, canonical state representation. This representation would allow different body parts and different robot platforms to be understood within a common framework.
3. **Model Whole-Body Coordination and Temporal Motion Dynamics:** A crucial objective is to capture the complex, interdependent dynamics of a humanoid robot's entire body. This involves learning from large-scale multi-embodiment trajectory data to understand how body parts coordinate and how motions evolve over time in the proprioceptive space.
4. **Efficiently Capture Temporal Visual Context:** The paper seeks to address the computational burden of processing long visual histories. An objective is to summarize past visual observations into compact, lightweight representations to provide temporal context without requiring repeated, costly encoding of historical images during inference. This is crucial for tasks where object motion, scene evolution, or partial observability make current observations insufficient.
5. **Adaptively Integrate Visual-Language Cues with Proprioceptive Dynamics:** HEX aims to develop a mechanism for action generation that intelligently combines high-level semantic understanding from visual-language inputs with the robot's internal sense of its body state and predicted future dynamics. This adaptive fusion is intended to produce actions that are not only task-relevant but also dynamically feasible and coordinated across the entire body.
6. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** A practical objective is to validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework on real-world humanoid manipulation tasks. This includes demonstrating superior task success rates and improved generalization capabilities, especially in challenging fast-reaction and long-horizon scenarios where coordinated whole-body dynamics and temporal consistency are critical.

By pursuing these objectives, HEX aims to bridge the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level coordinated physical execution in humanoid robots, moving closer to human-like embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HEX employs a hierarchical architecture designed for humanoid whole-body manipulation, comprising a high-level Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy and a low-level Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based whole-body controller.

**Overall Architecture:**
- The **high-level VLA policy** receives visual-language context and humanoid-aligned proprioceptive state as input. It then generates task-relevant actions for manipulation, which govern arm and hand behavior and serve as intermediate commands for the low-level controller.
- The **low-level controller** operates at a higher frequency, translating the high-level commands into balance-preserving and dynamically feasible whole-body motions for stable execution during locomotion and manipulation. Specific low-level controllers are instantiated: standing and walking controllers utilize a DeepMimic-style reference-tracking formulation, suitable for stable periodic motions, while a half-kneeling controller uses an AMP-style objective to encourage natural, contact-rich posture transitions.

**Components of the High-Level VLA Policy:**

1. **VLM with History Query Feature Cache:**
 - To efficiently incorporate temporal visual-language context without the computational cost of repeatedly encoding long image histories, HEX utilizes a lightweight history query feature cache.
 - At each timestep `t`, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) encodes the language instruction (L), the current visual observation (`V_t`), and a query token (`Q_t`).
 - The output query feature (`Q'_t`) serves as a compact summary of the current visual-language context. Instead of propagating the query token itself, a fresh query feature is generated at each timestep and stored in a fixed-length cache, `M_vl_t`, for a visual history window (τv=2).
 - This mechanism provides recent semantic context for subsequent modules by storing only compact query features, avoiding the repeated encoding of original images or full VLM activations.

2. **Unified Proprioceptive Predictor (UPP) with Morphology-based Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):**
 - This component addresses the challenge of modeling heterogeneous and high-dimensional proprioceptive states across different humanoid embodiments.
 - **Humanoid-Aligned Universal State Representation:** Proprioceptive inputs (joint positions, velocities, accelerations, tactile feedback) are organized into a fixed set of canonical body-part slots (e.g., left/right arms, hands, legs, head, waist, auxiliary "others"). Each available part is mapped into a shared latent space, and a learned missing-part token is inserted for absent parts, forming structured part latents (`P_t`). This allows prediction in a shared latent space.
 - **Spatio-Temporal Token Sequence:** The structured part latents are concatenated with learnable future query tokens (`N_t+1:t+τp`), where `τp` is the future prediction horizon. Positional embeddings are added to this sequence to create a shared tokenized representation over body parts and short-horizon time slots.
 - **Morphology-aware MoE:** Lightweight MoE modules are integrated at the input and output boundaries of a shared transformer backbone. After flattening the part-time token grid, each spatio-temporal token is routed by a learned top-k gate to a small subset of experts. A shared expert branch provides a common transformation. This design enables token-wise specialization for embodiment- and part-dependent variations, while the shared expert preserves reusable dynamics across embodiments.
 - **Shared Transformer Backbone:** Between the MoE modules, a transformer backbone models embodiment-agnostic temporal dynamics. It operates on the full part-time token sequence, using interleaved self-attention and visual-language-conditioned attention to capture intra-state dependencies and task-relevant contextual dynamics.
 - **Prediction:** Conditioned on the current proprioceptive latent (`P_t`), current visual-language features, and the history cache, UPP predicts future proprioceptive latents (`P'_t+1:t+τp`). These predictions capture short-horizon whole-body state evolution and coordinated changes across body parts, providing future-oriented cues for action generation.

3. **Action Expert with Adaptive Fusion:**
 - The action expert generates high-level actions by iteratively denoising action tokens (represented as `X_t`) under dual conditioning from both visual-language and proprioceptive features.
 - **Dual Cross-Attention:** The action hidden states serve as queries and are conditioned in parallel on VLM features (`H_vl_t`) and predicted proprioceptive features (`H_p_t`) from the UPP through two distinct cross-attention branches.
 - **Gated Fusion Mechanism:** A learned gate (`g_t`) adaptively modulates the contribution of the proprioceptive branch (`H_p_t`) to the visual-language branch (`H_vl_t`). The fused signal is then combined with the action states via a residual update. This adaptive fusion ensures that action generation is guided jointly by semantic context and future-oriented whole-body dynamics.
 - **Refinement:** After dual conditioning, the action expert applies self-attention and a feed-forward block to refine the action representation. The denoised features are then decoded into high-level actions for arm and hand control and downstream whole-body execution.

**Cross-Embodiment Training:**
- HEX is pretrained on a large corpus of over 12 million frames collected from seven humanoid embodiments across four diverse datasets, including Tienkung 2.0, Tienkung 3.0, Unitree G1/H1, AgiBot, and Leju humanoid.
- Training involves jointly optimizing an action-generation objective for the action expert and an auxiliary future-state prediction objective for the UPP.
- The action generation uses a flow-matching objective, predicting velocity targets from noisy action trajectories. The future-state prediction encourages UPP to model short-horizon proprioceptive evolution in the shared latent space.
- Both objectives operate on the shared body-part-aligned representation, making the training formulation extensible across heterogeneous humanoid embodiments. A staged optimization schedule is optionally used for stability, warming up UPP first before joint optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Experiments were conducted on real-world humanoid manipulation tasks using Tienkung 2.0 and Tienkung 3.0 platforms, comparing HEX against baselines including ACT, SwitchVLA, GR00T N1.5, and π0.5. The evaluation focused on task success rate, generalization, and practical behaviors.

**RQ1: Performance in Seen and Long-Horizon Scenarios:**

- **Seen Scenarios:** On seven in-distribution tasks (four on Tienkung 2.0, three on Tienkung 3.0), HEX achieved the highest overall average success rate of 79.8% (Table 1). While smaller models like ACT and SwitchVLA were competitive in trajectory-fitting, sometimes exhibiting smoother motions in reactive tasks, HEX demonstrated a superior balance between task success and motion quality among the large-scale models. This improvement was attributed to HEX's explicit future-state conditioning, which provides dynamic cues beyond current visual observations.
- **Long-Horizon Scenarios:** For a four-stage long-horizon box conveyance task, HEX outperformed all baselines across all stages (Table 2). Specifically, in the final "Place Box" stage, HEX achieved a 53.3% success rate, surpassing the strongest baseline (π0.5 at 40.0%) by approximately 15%. This indicated HEX's enhanced ability to sustain stable execution and reduce cascading errors over extended, multi-stage whole-body manipulation tasks.

**RQ2: Generalization Study:**

HEX was evaluated on eight generalization variants across four base tasks (Pose Mimic, Pouring, Box Carry, Kneel Pick), introducing distribution shifts like faster human motion, human interference, visual distractors, object-position changes, lighting variation, and dynamic scene changes.
- HEX achieved an average success rate of 61.8% across these generalization variants, significantly outperforming π0.5 (44.3%), GR00T N1.5 (41.0%), and SwitchVLA (22.4%) (Figure 6).
- In "Pose Mimic Fast," HEX matched the best performance (100%), and under "Pose Mimic Intervention" (distracting background person), HEX achieved 85.7%, demonstrating robustness to interference where other methods struggled to maintain poses.
- For "Pouring," HEX showed a substantial advantage, improving from 0% for baselines to 53.3% with visual distractors and 55.5% with bottle-position changes. Baselines frequently failed by pouring prematurely or getting stuck, possibly misinterpreting distractors.
- HEX also exhibited superior performance in "Box Carry" under unseen lighting (26.7%) and unseen surrounding objects (40.0%), and in "Kneel Pick" under dynamic object positions (33.0%) and unseen objects (100%).
- These results indicate that HEX generalizes more robustly across diverse distribution shifts.

**RQ3: Ablation Study:**

- **Pretraining:** Pretraining primarily enhanced optimization efficiency, leading to faster fitting, better initialization, and lower state and action losses during early training stages in single-task settings (Figure 7a). While the final converged success rates were similar with and without pretraining, pretraining significantly accelerated the learning process, achieving comparable success rates much earlier.
- **Model Components:** The individual components of HEX—the VLM history cache, the UPP, and the MoE design within UPP—each contributed to overall performance (Figure 7b). Performance consistently improved as these components were progressively introduced. The UPP demonstrated the most significant impact, with its removal leading to the largest performance degradation on both "Pouring" and "Box Conveying" tasks, highlighting the importance of structured proprioceptive modeling.

**RQ4: Other Analyses:**

- **Failure Analysis:** HEX exhibited fewer overall failures and a more concentrated failure distribution compared to baselines (Figure 8). In long-horizon tasks, where baselines showed distributed failures across grasping, turning, locomotion, and placement, HEX's failures were less pervasive, suggesting improved robustness to error propagation across sequential stages.
- **MoE Routing Pattern:** Analysis of the MoE routing patterns (Figure 9) revealed that routing before the transformer blocks was largely stable and indicative of persistent body-part specialization. In contrast, routing *after* the transformer blocks showed clear phase-dependent switching that aligned with semantic subtask boundaries (e.g., different experts selected for leg control during static support versus turning/locomotion). This suggests that post-transformer MoE enables more effective state-dependent specialization to address evolving control demands.
- **Latency:** On an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, HEX achieved the highest success rate (79.8%) with a latency of 73.34 ms (Figure 10). While ACT had the lowest latency but lower success, and GR00T N1.5 offered a favorable latency-accuracy trade-off among large models, HEX demonstrated strong effectiveness within a practical inference budget, providing the best task success rate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HEX framework represents a notable advancement in humanoid whole-body manipulation by addressing a fundamental limitation in existing VLA-style approaches: the insufficient modeling of interactions between different body parts under shared balance and posture constraints.

**Key Contributions and Novelty:**

- **First Whole-Body VLA Framework for Full-Sized Bipedal Humanoids:** HEX introduces a comprehensive VLA framework specifically designed to enable coordinated whole-body control on complex, full-sized bipedal humanoid robots.
- **Humanoid-Aligned Universal State Representation with Predictive Proprioceptive Modeling:** The development of a canonical, body-part-aligned state representation, coupled with a Unified Proprioceptive Predictor (UPP) incorporating a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design, allows for scalable learning across heterogeneous robotic embodiments. This compositional approach enables the model to adapt to different robot morphologies and sensor suites without extensive retraining.
- **Review-and-Forecast Paradigm:** The integration of a lightweight visual history summarization mechanism ("review") with future state prediction from the UPP ("forecast") and an adaptive multimodal fusion for action generation, provides a novel approach to incorporating temporal context and dynamic foresight.

- **Enhanced Coherence and Stability:** By explicitly modeling structured body-part interactions and incorporating future state predictions, HEX facilitates more coherent, stable, and coordinated whole-body action generation. This addresses a critical challenge in developing robust humanoid control policies.
- **Improved Performance in Complex Scenarios:** The demonstrated superior performance in real-world tasks, particularly those involving fast reactions and long horizons, highlights HEX's capability to handle complex and dynamic manipulation challenges where traditional methods may falter due to error accumulation or lack of coordination.
- **Robust Generalization:** The framework's ability to generalize effectively to unseen scene variations, including visual distractors, dynamic object changes, and human interference, indicates its potential for deployment in less constrained and more diverse real-world environments. This suggests a step towards more versatile and adaptable humanoid robots.
- **Scalable Learning from Diverse Data:** The cross-embodiment training methodology and the design of the UPP with MoE allow HEX to leverage large-scale trajectory datasets from various humanoid platforms. This scalability is crucial for developing generalist humanoid policies in the era of foundation models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** HEX's core components—particularly the humanoid-aligned state representation, predictive proprioceptive modeling, and adaptive multimodal fusion—provide a robust foundation and a blueprint for future research in embodied intelligence. It emphasizes the importance of understanding and explicitly modeling the internal dynamics and interdependencies of a robot's body for advanced manipulation.
- **Accelerating Humanoid Robot Deployment:** By enabling more reliable and coordinated whole-body manipulation, HEX contributes to the broader goal of deploying humanoid robots in complex human environments such as homes, schools, and workplaces, where dexterity and adaptability are paramount.
