# WanPE: Towards Cinematic Prompt Enhancement for Modern Text-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> WanPE is a 397B-parameter prompt enhancement model that rewrites text-to-video requests into director-level cinematic plans: a video-level overview plus timestamped shot-level descriptions of action, camera, lighting, dialogue and sound. It is trained by reverse construction from 1.05M real videos and refined with Semantic-Consistency GRPO, lifting human preference over raw prompts by 50.86 points at 30 seconds.

## Problem

- Prior prompt enhancers were built for early generators with short contexts, so they only added cosmetic visual detail rather than planning multi-shot cinematic structure.
- Forward rewriting from user request to richer description yields a synthetic prompt distribution, mismatched with the video-grounded captions downstream generators were actually trained on.
- Existing enhancers treat semantic preservation locally, so user-specified subjects, dialogue, camera and event order get omitted, altered or misbound as plans span many shots.

## Method

- ==Video-grounded reverse SFT==: caption 1.05M filtered real clips into hierarchical shot-level cinematic targets, then reconstruct a plausible user request from each caption with few-shot LLM prompting.
- **Semantic-Consistency GRPO**: group-relative policy optimization over ~15K human-written and failure-flagged prompts, rewarded by an LLM judge scoring nine dimensions such as style, dialogue, camera and spatial relations, with cross-shot consistency checked in each.
- **WanPEval**: 249 human-written requests spanning 5-30 seconds and intent/scene/shot granularities, scored by text-level semantic reward plus ~11K blind expert pairwise video comparisons with ==Bradley-Terry aggregation==.

## Results

- **WanPE**-397B reaches overall preference **50.61** on the 5-15 second subset, first among all systems, beating Seedance 2.0 by **7.19** points and raw prompts by **10.66-18.84**.
- On 30 seconds it lifts preference from **9.38** to **60.24**, competitive with Seedance 2.5's **59.76**; reverse-constructed SFT beats forward rewriting by **10.37** points.
- SC-GRPO adds **18.6-23.3** semantic-consistency points across 4B-397B, raising 397B perfect outputs to **85.5%** and downstream preference from **42.70** to **49.69**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning the enhancer's output distribution with the generator's training captions matters more than writing richer prose; reverse-construct supervision from real data.
> - Prompt-enhancement payoff scales with generation horizon: modest at five seconds, decisive at thirty, where unguided requests nearly always lose.
> - A text-only LLM reward for requirement preservation improves downstream video quality, so cheap textual RL works without video-level feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A short request can describe a whole story. In one example, a novice knight challenges a giant dragon, the dragon blocks his sword, treats him like an “itch scratcher,” flings him from the mountain, and settles contentedly on the summit. A coherent clip has to carry that encounter through each of these beats.

WanPE turns a request like this into a multi-shot plan before a generator renders the video. The plan can specify actions, camera, lighting, dialogue, and sound over time, and it aims to keep the requested events in order. The authors argue that this matters now because modern generators can follow longer, more complex instructions and produce videos up to 30 seconds. A brief request leaves much of that direction unspecified.

Project demo labeled “w/o PE”: Wan3.0 given the original knight-and-dragon request.

Project demo labeled “w/ WanPE”: the enhanced prompt lays out the request in five timed shots, from the summit challenge through the dragon resting alone.

The authors provide this pair to show what such a plan looks like. It is an example, not a controlled numerical finding.

### 2. From caption to screenplay

A common way to train an enhancer is to expand user prompts into longer descriptions. The authors reverse this. They start with real videos, caption the cinematic structure those videos actually contain, and then reconstruct compatible user requests from the captions. Their proposed rationale is that forward-written targets can differ from the captions on which video generators were trained.

The corpus contains about 1.05 million filtered clips, up to 30 seconds long, across ten content dimensions. Each caption has two levels: a video overview and timestamped shots. The shots describe composition, subjects, actions, lighting, camera, transitions, dialogue, music, and sound effects.

Training data therefore runs from video to plan to request. At inference, the direction reverses: the model receives only a request and produces a plan, with no real video to work from.

A second stage, SC-GRPO (Semantic-Consistency Group Relative Policy Optimization), checks whether the request's meaning survives across the plan. A text-only evaluator scores candidate plans on nine dimensions: style, subjects, actions, dialogue, sound, camera, lighting, spatial relations, and scene. It penalizes omissions, altered requirements, wrong bindings, ordering errors, contradictions, and cross-shot conflicts. This reward does not directly score the quality of generated videos.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

The main tests pass enhanced plans to the same Wan3.0 video generator. Sixty experts in screenwriting, directing, cinematography, and related fields judged the resulting videos blind in pairwise comparisons. They saw the request, and left–right order was randomized. The preference score S counts a win as one and a “both good” judgment as half a win, divided by valid comparisons. It is not a head-to-head win rate against one named opponent.

**Original request versus WanPE.** At 15 seconds, WanPE-397B scored an overall S of 49.43, compared with 30.59 for the original-request pipeline. At 30 seconds, the scores were 60.24 and 9.38. On that 30-second subset, the commercial system Seedance 2.5 scored 59.76. The authors describe WanPE as competitive at this length, which falls short of a demonstrated significant win.

**Reverse versus forward training.** This is the most direct test of the reverse construction, run under Wan3.0 on the WanPEval benchmark. The reverse-trained WanPE-397B-SFT scored an overall S of 49.86. Forward-target SFT scored 35.17, and forward rewriting scored 39.49. Forward-target SFT is the closer control: it uses the same reconstructed requests and the same training settings, but its targets are written forward. The authors interpret the gap as evidence that video-grounded targets provide more effective conditions. The result supports that interpretation in this setup, but it does not prove that every forward method would lose.

**Effect of SC-GRPO.** For WanPE-397B, SC-GRPO raised text semantic consistency from 75.5 to 97.6. Separately, under the same generator, expert preference S rose from 42.70 to 49.69. These are different metrics, so the pairing shows an association between better text consistency and higher video preference, not a sole cause.

Preference scores S by content category and overall. Exp1 compares the original request and WanPE-397B under Wan3.0, with Seedance 2.5 as the 30-second comparison. Exp2 compares forward and reverse alternatives under Wan3.0; the reverse-trained SFT row has the highest overall score.

### 4. A plan that holds together

WanPE's central idea is an enhancer that carries a request through a sequence of shots rather than simply making it longer. Under the tested generator, reverse-trained plans outscored both forward alternatives. The SC-GRPO comparison links stronger text consistency with higher video preference. All of these are expert preference scores under this protocol, not general measures of video quality.
