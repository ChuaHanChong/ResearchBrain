# Learning Context-Aware Neural ODE Dynamics for Adaptive Robotic Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of California, Berkeley and Aalto University developed a context-aware Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (NODE) dynamics model for adaptive robotic control, inferring hidden environmental factors from state-action histories. This model enables robust control in uncertain environments, achieving lower tracking errors than several model-based baselines in quadrotor simulations and real-world mobile robot/manipulator tasks, and demonstrating better generalization to out-of-distribution conditions than model-free methods.

## Problem

- Robotic systems frequently encounter variable and unobservable environmental factors (e.g., terrain, aerodynamics, payloads) in real-world deployments, hindering robust control.
- Traditional model-based control strategies struggle to adapt when precise dynamics models are unavailable or when crucial environmental factors change dynamically and are unobservable.
- Existing adaptive methods often rely on prior physics-based models or face limitations in computational efficiency and expressiveness for complex, unknown dynamics.

## Method

- A context-aware dynamics model based on Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODE) is introduced, designed to infer relevant environmental factors solely from historical state-action data.
- A two-phase training procedure is utilized: Phase 1 learns core system dynamics using 'privileged' environmental information, while Phase 2 trains an adaptive module to infer this environmental context from historical data using a 1D Convolutional Neural Network.
- The model integrates with a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) controller for action optimization and incorporates an online fine-tuning mechanism for continuous adaptation to distributional shifts.

## Results

- Achieved significantly lower tracking errors (e.g., **0.022**±**0.014** m RMSE for goal reaching in quadrotor simulation under spatially dissipating winds) and greater robustness compared to MLP, CaDM, and fixed NODE baselines.
- Demonstrated superior generalization to out-of-distribution wind conditions for a quadrotor, maintaining robustness up to **3x** disturbances beyond the training range, where a model-free baseline (DATT) failed at **1x**.
- Showed strong real-world adaptability on a Sphero BOLT robot navigating varying friction and a Fanuc manipulator pushing objects with unknown centers of mass, outperforming fixed baselines and being more sample-efficient and flexible than model-free approaches like RMA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the learning of context-aware dynamics into two distinct phases (privileged information for core dynamics, history-based inference for context) effectively disentangles complex system and environmental interactions.
> - Neural Ordinary Differential Equations can learn full system dynamics directly from data without requiring prior physics knowledge, providing smooth, continuous-time predictions suitable for model predictive control.
> - Inferring a latent environmental vector from high-dimensional historical state-action data, combined with online fine-tuning, enables robust and generalizable adaptation to varying and unseen environmental conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic systems operating in real-world environments frequently encounter variability in conditions such as terrain, aerodynamics, and payloads. Model-based control strategies require accurate dynamics models for effective long-horizon planning, yet these models often struggle with adaptability to unobservable or dynamically changing environmental factors. This research addresses the challenge of developing adaptive dynamics models that can infer hidden environmental factors from limited state-action histories, thereby enhancing control performance in uncertain conditions.

Previous efforts in adaptive robotics can be broadly categorized. Classical adaptive control and online estimation techniques offer computational efficiency but are limited by the accuracy of their nominal physics-based models and the prior specification of features. More recent approaches incorporating Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODE) variants, such as KNODE and Hamiltonian NODE, have focused on modeling residual dynamics when a nominal model is available, but generally still rely on such prior knowledge. The presented work departs from these by aiming to learn full system dynamics directly from data without requiring prior physics knowledge.

In model adaptation, a distinction is made between test-time adaptation, where model parameters are updated online, and train-time adaptation, where adaptability is encoded during training. Test-time methods, like meta-learning approaches adapting linear parameters or online gradient descent methods, can be computationally efficient but may have limited expressiveness or risk instability and overfitting. Train-time adaptation, using methods like in-context learning with transformers or Neural Process context encoders, integrates adaptability into the model's architecture. However, end-to-end train-time approaches can face challenges with learning complex, entangled system and environmental dynamics, especially with high-dimensional history inputs. The proposed method utilizes a two-phase training procedure that combines elements of train-time adaptation with a module designed to efficiently infer context from history, aiming to mitigate issues of complexity and high-dimensionality.

The concept of leveraging privileged environmental information during training has demonstrated utility in improving adaptive representation learning by disentangling environment-dependent factors. While effective, many existing methods employing this do not explicitly integrate with continuous-time modeling frameworks, which limits their compatibility with control strategies such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) that benefit from continuous dynamics.

Within the domain of dynamics modeling, Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODE) have shown promise for learning continuous-time dynamics, offering advantages like smooth dynamics and robustness to noisy data. However, most NODE-related applications in robotics have either focused on stationary settings or required prior system knowledge for adaptation. This paper contributes to the NODE literature by integrating it into an adaptive framework that learns full system dynamics without physics priors, while addressing adaptation to changing environments.

Finally, the work is situated in the context of model-free versus model-based control. Model-free approaches like Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) and DATT have demonstrated effectiveness in learning adaptive control policies directly. However, they typically suffer from sample inefficiency and reduced flexibility, as incorporating new objectives or constraints often necessitates retraining the policy. Model-based approaches, by explicitly modeling system dynamics, offer greater flexibility to incorporate new cost functions or constraints at test time. This research provides a model-based solution that aims to retain the benefits of explicit dynamics modeling while achieving adaptive capabilities comparable to, or exceeding, certain model-free methods in terms of robustness and sample efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges of deploying robotic systems in uncertain and dynamically changing environments. Such environments, characterized by variations in contact conditions, aerodynamic effects, external disturbances, or payloads, necessitate robust and adaptive control mechanisms. Traditional model-based controllers, which rely on precise dynamics models for predictive planning, often falter when faced with significant deviations from their nominal models or when crucial environmental factors are unobservable by onboard sensors. The inability of a system to infer these hidden environmental factors from limited data restricts its adaptability and overall performance.

Against this background, the paper sets out to achieve several key objectives:
1. **Develop a Context-Aware Dynamics Model:** The central objective is to create a dynamics model capable of inferring relevant environmental factors solely from historical state-action data. This inference should then enable the dynamics function to adapt its state predictions according to the identified environmental context.
2. **Leverage Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODE):** The work aims to utilize NODE to model the continuous-time dynamics of robotic systems. This is motivated by NODE's capacity to learn first-order derivatives and compute system states using numerical integrators, which aligns with the continuous nature of rigid-body dynamics and can yield smooth, physically consistent outputs, as well as robustness to noisy data.
3. **Facilitate Integration with Model Predictive Control (MPC):** The proposed dynamics model is intended to be compatible with MPC frameworks. MPC requires an accurate predictive model for optimizing future control actions over a finite horizon. An adaptive dynamics model that can mitigate model mismatch is crucial for maintaining MPC performance in varying environments.
4. **Achieve Adaptability Without Prior Physics Knowledge:** A significant objective is to learn the full system dynamics directly from data, removing the dependency on a known physics-based nominal model. This contrasts with many existing NODE-based adaptive control methods that rely on residual modeling with physics priors.
5. **Enhance Training Efficiency and Reduce Computational Overhead:** The research aims to improve training efficiency and reduce computational overhead by decoupling the learning of environmental conditions from system dynamics. This is achieved through an adaptive module that processes historical data to reconstruct a latent environmental vector, thereby reducing the input dimensions for the NODE.
6. **Demonstrate Robustness and Generalization:** The final objective is to validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach across diverse robotic platforms in simulation and real-world scenarios, demonstrating superior accuracy, robustness, and generalization capabilities compared to baseline methods under temporally and spatially varying environmental changes, including out-of-distribution conditions.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide a robust and flexible model-based control framework that can adapt to uncertainties and dynamic changes, ultimately improving the reliability and performance of robotic systems in complex real-world deployments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a context-aware dynamics model based on Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODE), designed to infer environmental factors from state-action histories. This model operates within a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, specifically using Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control for action optimization. The learning process is structured into a two-phase training procedure.

**A. Problem Formulation (MPC Context)**
The system dynamics are defined by a continuous-time ordinary differential equation: $\dot{x}_k = f(x_k, u_k, e_k)$, where $x_k$ is the system state, $u_k$ is the control input, and $e_k$ represents environmental factors. The next state $x_{k+1}$ is obtained by numerically integrating $f$ over a time step. MPC solves an optimization problem at each time step to find a sequence of future control actions ($u_k..., u_{k+H-1}$) that minimizes a cost function comprising stage and terminal costs, subject to system dynamics and input constraints. The proposed adaptive dynamics model aims to ensure the accuracy of the function $f$ in this MPC loop.

**B. Two-Phase Training Framework**

The learning of context-aware dynamics is decomposed into two distinct phases:

1. **Phase 1: Learning State Evolution with Privileged Information**
 - **Objective:** To learn the fundamental system dynamics ($f$) by providing direct access to complete environmental information ($e_k$), known as "privileged information."
 - **Components:**
 - **Environmental Encoder ($g$):** This module transforms the raw privileged environmental information ($e_k$) into a lower-dimensional latent vector ($z_k$). This step aims to distill the essential environmental characteristics into a compact representation.
 - **State Net:** This neural network models the derivative of the state, $\hat{\dot{x}}_k$, as a function of the current state ($x_k$), current action ($u_k$), and the latent environmental vector ($z_k$). It forms the core of the NODE dynamics function.
 - **Process:** The environmental encoder ($g$) and the State Net are trained end-to-end. The State Net takes $(x_k, u_k, z_k)$ as input and outputs $\hat{\dot{x}}_k$. A numerical integrator (e.g., Forward Euler or Runge-Kutta) then computes the predicted next state $\hat{x}_{k+1}$. The training objective is to minimize the difference between the predicted and true next states. This phase focuses on ensuring the NODE accurately captures the continuous-time dynamics under known environmental conditions, inherently producing smooth and physically consistent outputs.

2. **Phase 2: Inferring Environmental Factors from Historical Data (Adaptive Module Training)**
 - **Objective:** To learn to infer the latent environmental vector ($z_k$) solely from historical state-action data, as privileged information ($e_k$) is typically unavailable during deployment.
 - **Components:**
 - **Adaptive Module ($h$):** This neural network is designed to encode a history of state-action pairs, $\{(x_i, u_i)\}_{i=k-M}^{k-1}$, into a reconstructed latent environmental vector ($\tilde{z}_k$).
 - **Process:** After Phase 1 is complete, the weights of the learned State Net are frozen. The adaptive module ($h$) is then trained to predict $\tilde{z}_k$ by regressing it against the $z_k$ obtained from the environmental encoder ($g$) in Phase 1, using an MSE loss. This aims to ensure that $\tilde{z}_k$ captures the same environmental information as $z_k$. The approach of learning in the latent space ($z_k$) is chosen because it simplifies the alignment of high-dimensional historical data with raw environmental data, which may have distinct physical meanings and dimensionalities.

**C. Training Details**

- **Curriculum Learning:** To improve long-horizon prediction accuracy and mitigate issues like gradient explosion/vanishing, the NODE is trained using curriculum learning. The prediction horizon gradually increases from 1-step to H-step alignment.
- **Adaptive Module Architecture:** For handling high-dimensional historical data, a 1D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is employed within the adaptive module. This architecture is chosen for its ability to extract local temporal patterns and capture environmental changes regardless of their position in the sequence. State and action vectors are treated as separate channels to enhance feature extraction.
- **Online Fine-tuning:** To adapt to distributional shifts and unseen historical data during execution, an online fine-tuning mechanism is incorporated. As new observations $\{(x_k, u_k, x_{k+1})\}$ become available, the dynamics model parameters are updated on the fly to reduce prediction errors. Experience replay buffers are used to prevent catastrophic forgetting and balance exploration and exploitation.
- **MPC Implementation:** Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) is used as the underlying MPC framework to obtain optimal control actions, leveraging the learned context-aware NODE dynamics model for trajectory prediction. The paper notes that the same training and testing time step is used for NODE throughout the experiments.

This comprehensive methodology aims to develop a robust, adaptive, and physics-agnostic dynamics model that can effectively support model-based control in dynamic and uncertain robotic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed context-aware Neural ODE dynamics model was evaluated across various robotic platforms, including a quadrotor in simulation, and a Sphero BOLT robot and a Fanuc manipulator in real-world experiments. The results were compared against several baselines and ablations, including MLP-based dynamics, CaDM, meta-learning-based approaches, fixed NODE, and model-free methods like RMA and DATT.

**A. Quadrotor Navigation in Simulation**

1. **Environmental Variations:** Experiments were conducted under two challenging wind conditions: (i) time-varying winds fluctuating sinusoidally and (ii) a spatially dissipating wind field with additive Gaussian noise.
2. **Task Performance:** For both goal-reaching and path-tracking tasks, the proposed "Ours (Phase 2)" method consistently achieved lower tracking errors compared to most model-based baselines (MLP, CaDM, Meta-learning based, Fixed NODE).
 - For time-varying winds, "Ours (Phase 2)" achieved RMSEs of 0.031±0.016 m for goal reaching and 0.049±0.026 m for path tracking. "Ours (Phase 1)," representing the upper bound with privileged information, yielded even lower errors (0.010±0.004 m and 0.036±0.020 m respectively).
 - For spatially dissipating winds, "Ours (Phase 2)" achieved 0.022±0.014 m for goal reaching and 0.030±0.018 m for path tracking, again outperforming baselines.
 - MLP-based models and CaDM generally failed to adapt, resulting in errors greater than 0.2 m. Meta-learning and Fixed NODE showed better performance than these but still exhibited higher errors than the proposed method.
3. **Comparison with Model-Free Methods:** The proposed model-based approach achieved comparable performance to model-free methods like RMA and DATT in the specific tested scenarios. DATT showed strong performance (e.g., 0.051±0.008 m for goal reaching in spatially dissipating wind), but the paper notes fundamental differences in training pipelines and sample efficiency.
4. **Generalization under Distribution Shift:** Under increasing wind disturbance (scaling factor $\alpha$ beyond the training range), the proposed method exhibited significantly more graceful degradation than DATT, particularly in the goal-reaching task. While DATT failed at approximately $\alpha \approx 1$, the proposed method remained robust up to $\alpha \approx 3$. This indicates better generalization capability for out-of-distribution environments.
5. **Sample Efficiency and Inference Time:** The proposed method required 9M transition samples for dynamics learning, whereas DATT required 25M environment interactions for policy learning, suggesting superior sample efficiency. However, model-free methods like PPO demonstrated significantly faster inference times (approximately 1 ms per step) compared to the proposed method's 17 ms, highlighting a trade-off.
6. **Effectiveness of Online Fine-tuning:** Offline learning with 8 hours of training resulted in a path-tracking RMSE of 0.1649 ± 0.0221 m under wind disturbances exceeding 3 N. An additional 2 hours of online fine-tuning reduced this RMSE to 0.0646 ± 0.0153 m, demonstrating the benefit of continuous learning.

**B. Real-World Experiments**

### 5. Mobile Robot Platform (Sphero BOLT)

 - **Environment:** Sphero BOLT robot navigating on a pool table with randomized friction layouts (paper patches for low friction, table surface for high friction).
 - **Tasks:** Goal reaching and path tracking (circular and triangular paths).
 - **Performance:** The proposed method consistently achieved lower RMSE and smaller standard deviations across both friction layouts and all tasks compared to the Fixed NODE baseline.
 - For goal reaching, "Ours" achieved RMSEs of 0.766±0.303 cm (Layout 1) and 0.372±0.082 cm (Layout 2), significantly lower than Fixed NODE (2.127±1.233 cm and 2.725±1.176 cm).
 - Similar improvements were observed for circle and triangle tracking.
 - **Robustness:** The proposed model demonstrated greater robustness and repeatability under real-world uncertainty and handled surface boundary crossings more reliably, whereas the Fixed NODE often got stuck or lost track.
 - **Control Frequency:** The system's control frequency was limited to 15 Hz by Bluetooth communication latency, not by the computational cost of the proposed method (MPC + NODE forward simulation < 0.01 s per step, suggesting a potential rate exceeding 100 Hz).

### 6. Planar Pushing Platform (Fanuc Manipulator)

 - **Environment:** A Fanuc manipulator pushing a box with varying internal weight, leading to changes in the center of mass (CoM), which were unobservable by the camera.
 - **Task:** Pushing the box towards a goal position.
 - **Performance:**
 - The proposed "Ours" method achieved lower RMSEs than the Fixed NODE baseline in most scenarios, demonstrating the benefit of online adaptation for distribution shifts (e.g., 0.447±0.256 cm vs. 4.781±0.571 cm for in-distribution training CoM).
 - In "extreme" (unseen CoM offset) and "disturbance" scenarios (external perturbations), "Ours" significantly outperformed the model-free RMA approach, reducing errors by over 60% and 80% respectively.
 - "Ours" showed comparable accuracy to RMA in "training" and "moderate" regimes. RMA performed slightly better in "time-varying" settings, potentially due to favorable sampled weight locations aligning with its training distribution.
 - **Sample Efficiency and Flexibility:** The proposed model-based approach achieved its performance with approximately 40% fewer training samples than RMA. Furthermore, it offered greater flexibility, enabling collision avoidance in real-world deployments by incorporating an exponential barrier function without retraining, a capability that would require additional training for RMA.
 - **Reward Sensitivity:** The paper highlighted that model-free methods like RMA are sensitive to reward function design, with a simpler reward leading to a 3x performance degradation, contrasting with the more direct cost function design in model-based MPC.

These findings collectively indicate that the proposed context-aware Neural ODE dynamics model offers a robust and adaptive solution for robotic control in uncertain environments, exhibiting strong performance, generalization, and sample efficiency across diverse platforms and challenging scenarios.

### 7. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic control by introducing a context-aware Neural ODE dynamics model designed to enhance adaptability in uncertain and dynamically changing environments. The work's significance lies in several aspects:

Firstly, the development of a dynamics model that learns full system dynamics without requiring prior physics knowledge addresses a fundamental limitation in many adaptive control strategies. This broadens the applicability of model-based control to systems where accurate analytical models are difficult or impossible to obtain, or in scenarios where system parameters change unpredictably.

Secondly, the integration of Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODE) provides a robust framework for modeling continuous-time dynamics. This approach yields smooth state predictions and improved robustness to noisy or irregularly sampled data, which are common characteristics of real-world robotic operations. The continuous-time representation is inherently compatible with Model Predictive Control (MPC), facilitating the design of controllers that can plan over long horizons.

Thirdly, the proposed two-phase training procedure offers an efficient learning paradigm. By initially leveraging privileged environmental information to train the core dynamics model (Phase 1) and then training an adaptive module to infer environmental context from historical data (Phase 2), the method effectively decouples complex learning tasks. This structure, particularly the use of an environmental encoder to create a latent representation and a 1D CNN in the adaptive module, contributes to improved training efficiency and reduced input dimensionality, which can be critical for real-time deployment.

Fourthly, the demonstrated capabilities in diverse scenarios highlight the practical utility of the approach. Successful validation on a quadrotor in simulation under varying wind fields, and on real-world systems such as a Sphero BOLT robot navigating different ground textures and a Fanuc manipulator pushing objects with unknown centers of mass, indicates the method's versatility and robustness across different robotic platforms and environmental uncertainties. The results show consistent outperformance over non-adaptive and some adaptive baselines in terms of accuracy, robustness, and generalization to out-of-distribution conditions.

Finally, the work underscores the benefits of model-based approaches, such as superior sample efficiency compared to some model-free methods and greater flexibility in incorporating new objectives or constraints (e.g., collision avoidance) without requiring extensive retraining. While acknowledging the trade-off in inference speed compared to highly optimized model-free policies, the overall framework provides a strong foundation for reliable and adaptable robotic autonomy.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, enabling more resilient and autonomous robotic systems in applications ranging from mobile robots adapting to challenging terrains and weather conditions, to manipulators handling varied payloads and contact interactions in industrial or service settings. The ability to infer and adapt to hidden environmental factors dynamically can lead to safer, more efficient, and more reliable robot deployments in unstructured and uncertain environments. Future work addressing the sensitivity of NODE performance to mismatches between training and testing time steps could further enhance the practical utility and computational efficiency of the framework.
