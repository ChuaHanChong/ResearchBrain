# Accelerating Sampling-Based Control via Learned Linear Koopman Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces MPPI-DK, a control framework that integrates learned linear Deep Koopman Operator (DKO) dynamics into Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control to accelerate real-time optimization for complex nonlinear robotic systems. The approach replaces computationally intensive nonlinear dynamics with efficient linear propagation in a lifted space, achieving comparable control performance to classic MPPI with significant reductions in computational cost, especially with GPU acceleration.

## Problem

- Real-time control of complex nonlinear and high-dimensional robotic systems faces computational bottlenecks due to repeated online optimization and nonlinear dynamics propagation.
- Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, while robust to nonlinearities, is limited by the computational expense of repeatedly propagating full nonlinear system dynamics during trajectory sampling.
- Even data-driven deep neural network models for dynamics can incur substantial computational costs when repeatedly evaluated in sampling-based controllers.

## Method

- A Deep ==Koopman== Operator (DKO) framework is used to learn a linear representation of the nonlinear system dynamics in a higher-dimensional, lifted state space from observed data.
- The proposed MPPI-DK method integrates these learned linear DKO dynamics into the MPPI controller, replacing the true nonlinear dynamics for trajectory propagation.
- Trajectory rollouts are performed by propagating states in the lifted space using efficient matrix multiplications, with the actual state reconstructed via a linear output mapping, avoiding repeated evaluations of complex lifting functions.

## Results

- MPPI-DK demonstrated control performance comparable to classic MPPI with true system dynamics across simulations (pendulum balancing, surface vehicle navigation) and real-world hardware (quadruped robot reference tracking).
- On a Unitree Go1 quadruped robot, GPU-accelerated MPPI-DK achieved a lower per-step computation time of **8.8** ms compared to **11.7** ms for GPU-accelerated classic MPPI.
- MPPI-DK produced smoother control inputs and a slightly smaller final state tracking error (**0.0043** vs. **0.0072**) than classic MPPI on the quadruped, while maintaining a **100%** success rate across tested initial states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learned structured linear Koopman dynamics can act as effective computational accelerators within sampling-based control methodologies, significantly reducing the per-step computation time.
> - By computing the deep neural network lifting function only once per control step and propagating subsequent states linearly, MPPI-DK can leverage the efficiency of linear dynamics while maintaining compatibility with MPPI's stochastic sampling.
> - The MPPI-DK framework naturally benefits from parallel computation, particularly on GPU hardware, enabling substantial speedups crucial for high-frequency control tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The control of complex robotic systems with nonlinear and high-dimensional dynamics in real-time environments presents a persistent challenge in robotics. Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers a systematic framework for optimizing performance over a receding horizon while managing state and input constraints. However, applying MPC to highly nonlinear systems at high control frequencies often incurs substantial computational costs due to repeated online optimization and nonlinear dynamics propagation, which can limit its practical use in real-time robotic applications.

To address the scalability issues associated with highly nonlinear dynamics, Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control has emerged as an alternative. MPPI is an information-theoretic approach to stochastic optimal control that approximates the optimal control update through Monte Carlo trajectory sampling. This sampling-based structure naturally accommodates nonlinear dynamics and nonconvex cost functions, and its suitability for parallel computation has led to its application in areas such as aggressive autonomous driving, aerial robotics, and legged locomotion.

Despite these advantages, a primary limitation of MPPI is its reliance on the repeated propagation of nonlinear system dynamics during trajectory sampling. For systems with computationally intensive models or constrained onboard computational resources, this repeated forward simulation can restrict the achievable control frequency.

Recent research has explored the use of data-driven dynamics models as surrogates for exact nonlinear dynamics within MPC frameworks. Deep neural networks (DNNs) offer strong expressive capabilities for approximating complex dynamics. However, repeated forward evaluation of these learned models within sampling-based controllers can still impose significant computational costs, particularly if the learned models themselves are highly nonlinear.

An alternative approach to data-driven dynamics learning involves Koopman operator theory. This theory posits that nonlinear dynamical systems can be represented by linear dynamics in a lifted, higher-dimensional space of observable functions. Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) formalizes this concept using manually designed lifting functions, but its effectiveness is often contingent on the careful selection of these functions. To overcome this limitation, Deep Koopman Operator (DKO) methods employ deep neural networks to learn suitable lifting functions directly from data. DKO approaches have demonstrated empirical performance in approximating nonlinear dynamics and have been integrated with model-based controllers such as MPC or model-based reinforcement learning. A key characteristic of Koopman-based linear models is their ability to enable fast state propagation through matrix multiplications, which is a desirable feature for sampling-based control methods like MPPI.

While Koopman operator-based methods have been studied extensively for dynamics identification and prediction, their specific application as computational accelerators for real-time sampling-based control has been less explored. This paper aims to bridge this gap by integrating linear DKO dynamics into an MPPI control framework to enhance computational efficiency for complex nonlinear robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work is to enhance the computational efficiency of Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, particularly for systems characterized by complex nonlinear dynamics, without compromising control performance. The authors identify the repeated propagation of nonlinear system dynamics during MPPI's trajectory sampling as a significant computational bottleneck, which limits the achievable control frequency and scalability in real-time robotic applications.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Koopman-accelerated MPPI formulation:** The core objective is to integrate learned linear Deep Koopman Operator (DKO) dynamics into the MPPI control framework. This involves replacing the computationally expensive nonlinear dynamics models, traditionally used for trajectory propagation in MPPI, with a more efficient linear representation in a lifted space.
2. **Enable efficient sampling via lifted-state propagation:** The work aims to achieve substantial reductions in computational cost during trajectory rollouts by propagating lifted states using learned linear operators (matrix multiplications). This approach is designed to avoid the repeated re-evaluation of complex, potentially highly nonlinear, deep neural network lifting functions. The intent is to preserve compatibility with the stochastic sampling nature of MPPI and its ability to handle general nonconvex cost functions.
3. **Comprehensive evaluation and GPU acceleration:** A practical objective is to rigorously validate the proposed framework across various simulation and hardware platforms. This includes evaluating performance on tasks such as inverted pendulum balancing and surface vehicle navigation, and conducting real-world experiments on a quadruped robot for reference-tracking. The evaluation seeks to demonstrate favorable trade-offs between computational efficiency and control performance compared to classic MPPI with true nonlinear dynamics and MPC with learned models. Furthermore, the authors aim to show that the MPPI-DK framework can effectively leverage parallel computation, particularly on GPU hardware, to achieve significant speedups.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for a real-time optimal control strategy that combines the robustness of sampling-based methods with the computational advantages of data-driven linear dynamics approximations for challenging nonlinear robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper involves a two-stage approach: first, learning the Deep Koopman Operator (DKO) dynamics from observed data, and second, integrating these learned linear dynamics into an MPPI control framework, termed MPPI-DK.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The authors consider a discrete-time nonlinear system described by $x(t+1) = f(x(t), v(t))$, where $x(t)$ is the system state and $v(t) = u(t) + \delta u(t)$ is the control input subject to Gaussian noise $\delta u(t)$. It is assumed that these nonlinear dynamics can be represented by finite-dimensional linear Koopman dynamics in a lifted space, specifically:
$\hat{x}(t+1) = C^* (A^* g(\hat{x}(t), \theta^*) + B^* u(t))$
where $g(\cdot, \theta^*)$ is a Lipschitz continuous lifting function, typically parameterized by a Deep Neural Network (DNN), mapping the state to a higher-dimensional lifted space. $A^*, B^*, C^*$ are constant matrices and $\theta^*$ represents the DNN parameters.

The problem is twofold: (i) to estimate the DKO dynamics from state-input data generated by the true system, and (ii) to use the learned model to compute a finite-horizon control sequence that minimizes an expected cost, formulated as a finite-horizon stochastic optimal control problem.

**B. Dynamics Learning using DKO**
The DKO framework is employed to approximate the assumed linear Koopman dynamics. The system dynamics are represented in a lifted linear form:
1. Evolution in the lifted space: $g(x(t+1), \theta^*) = A^* g(x(t), \theta^*) + B^* u(t)$
2. Reconstruction from lifted space: $x(t+1) = C^* g(x(t+1), \theta^*)$

A dataset $D$ of observed state-input-next-state tuples $(x_i, v_i, x_i^+)$ is collected by applying control inputs to the true dynamical system. This dataset can be generated by uniformly sampled controls, an MPPI controller with a preliminary DKO model, or human demonstrations.

The DKO parameters ($A^*, B^*, C^*, \theta^*$) are obtained by solving a multi-variable optimization problem that minimizes a loss function $L_f$. This loss function consists of two terms:
1. A term approximating the lifted space evolution (predicting $g(x_i^+, \theta)$ from $g(x_i, \theta)$ and $u_i$).
2. A term enforcing the reconstruction of the next state ($x_i^+$) from its lifted representation ($C g(x_i^+, \theta)$).

The optimization problem is solved iteratively. At each iteration $k$, given an estimate $\theta_k$ for the DNN parameters, the matrices $A_k, B_k, C_k$ are determined analytically using pseudo-inverses of data matrices constructed from the dataset (e.g., $A_k, B_k = \bar{G}_k [G_k U]^\dagger$ and $C_k = \bar{X} \bar{G}_k^\dagger$). Subsequently, $\theta_{k+1}$ is updated via gradient descent using the determined $A_k, B_k, C_k$.

**C. MPPI Control (Classic and Proposed MPPI-DK)**
The classic MPPI controller solves the stochastic optimal control problem by performing Monte Carlo rollouts using the *true system dynamics*. For each of $N$ sampled trajectories, it propagates the state over a finite prediction horizon $T$, calculates a cumulative cost (cost-to-go), and then updates the nominal control sequence using a cost-weighted average of sampled perturbations.

The proposed **MPPI with Deep Koopman Operator dynamics (MPPI-DK)** modifies the classic MPPI by replacing the true nonlinear system dynamics with the learned linear DKO dynamics for trajectory propagation. The core algorithm is structured as follows:
1. **Initialization:** At each time step, the current state $x_t$ and its lifted vector $g(x_t, \theta^*)$ are initialized.
2. **Trajectory Sampling:** For each of $N$ sampled trajectories:
 - Zero-mean Gaussian noise perturbations ($\epsilon_s^n$) are sampled for the control inputs over the horizon.
 - Crucially, during the prediction horizon, the state $x$ and its lifted counterpart $g$ are propagated. The lifted state $g$ is updated using the linear Koopman dynamics: $g \leftarrow A^*g + B^*(u_{s-1} + \epsilon_{s-1}^n)$. The actual state $x$ is then reconstructed using the linear output mapping: $x \leftarrow C^*(A^*g + B^*(u_{s-1} + \epsilon_{s-1}^n))$. This means that once the initial lifted state $g(x_t, \theta^*)$ is computed, subsequent lifted states are propagated purely through matrix multiplications ($A^*g + B^*u$), avoiding recomputing the complex DNN $g(x, \theta^*)$ for every predicted state.
 - The cumulative cost for each trajectory is calculated based on the propagated states and control inputs.
3. **Control Update:** The nominal control sequence is updated using the standard MPPI cost-weighted average rule, similar to classic MPPI.
4. **Receding Horizon:** Only the first control input is applied to the system, and the remaining sequence is used as a warm start for the next optimization step.

This approach aims to reduce the computational burden of repeated dynamics evaluations by leveraging the efficiency of linear propagation in the lifted space. The framework is designed to be compatible with GPU acceleration for parallel trajectory sampling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluated the proposed MPPI-DK framework across three distinct scenarios: two numerical simulations (pendulum balancing and surface vehicle navigation) and one hardware experiment (quadruped robot reference tracking).

**A. Pendulum Balancing (Simulation)**
- **Task:** Swing up an inverted pendulum and stabilize it at the upright equilibrium.
- **Parameters Investigated:** The impact of DKO training data (uniformly sampled controls vs. expert demonstrations augmented data) and the architecture of the DNN lifting function $g$ (number of neurons in hidden layers, lifted state dimension).
- **Findings:**
 - **DNN Architecture:** Increasing the number of neurons in the lifting function's hidden layers resulted in faster convergence of the MPPI-DK controller to the goal state. This also led to more aggressive control inputs and trajectories that more closely matched those generated by classic MPPI using the true system dynamics.
 - **Lifting Dimension and Expert Data:** Increasing the lifted state dimension or augmenting the training dataset with expert demonstrations did not consistently yield improved control performance in this specific task.
 - **General Performance:** The MPPI-DK controller demonstrated control performance comparable to classic MPPI with access to the true system dynamics.

**B. Surface Vehicle Navigation (Simulation)**
- **Task:** Drive a simulated surface vehicle from a given initial state to a desired goal position.
- **Comparison:** MPPI-DK (on CPU and GPU), classic MPPI with true system dynamics, and an MPC controller based on the same learned DKO dynamics (MPC-DK).
- **Metrics:** Tracking error over time and per-step computation time.
- **Findings:**
 - **Tracking Performance:** MPPI-DK achieved tracking performance that was close to classic MPPI, which utilized the true system dynamics.
 - **Computational Efficiency (CPU):** When executed on a CPU, MPPI-DK showed lower per-step computation time (332.0 ± 7.7 ms) compared to classic MPPI (2041.7 ± 219.3 ms). However, MPC-DK (244.3 ms) was computationally less intensive than MPPI-DK (CPU).
 - **Computational Efficiency (GPU):** With GPU-enabled parallel sampling, MPPI-DK achieved significantly higher computational efficiency (17.9 ± 1.0 ms) than both MPC-DK (244.3 ms) and classic MPPI (2041.7 ± 219.3 ms). This demonstrated a substantial speedup from parallelization.

**C. Quadruped Robot (Hardware Experiments)**
- **Platform:** Unitree Go1 quadruped robot.
- **Task:** Reference-tracking to a desired goal pose.
- **Comparison:** MPPI-DK (GPU implementation) against classic MPPI with true robot dynamics (GPU implementation).
- **Metrics:** Per-step computation time, average tracking error, final state tracking error, control smoothness (quantified by the mean squared difference between successive control inputs), and success rate over 10 different initial states.
- **Findings:**
 - **Success Rate:** Both MPPI-DK and classic MPPI successfully completed the task for all 10 tested initial states, indicating robustness.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** MPPI-DK exhibited a lower per-step computation time (8.8 ± 0.1 ms) compared to classic MPPI (11.7 ± 0.1 ms), even with both methods leveraging GPU acceleration.
 - **Tracking Accuracy:** MPPI-DK achieved a slightly smaller final state tracking error (0.0043 ± 0.001) than classic MPPI (0.0072 ± 0.001). The average tracking error for MPPI-DK was 0.6577 ± 0.030, while for MPPI-True it was 0.5344 ± 0.002.
 - **Control Smoothness:** MPPI-DK produced smoother control inputs (20.6 ± 9.9) compared to classic MPPI (26.4 ± 13.3), which can be beneficial for robot wear and energy consumption.

In summary, the results indicate that MPPI-DK can achieve control performance comparable to classic MPPI using true dynamics, while significantly reducing computational cost, especially when utilizing GPU parallelization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper carries significance in the domain of real-time control for complex robotic systems, particularly concerning the practical deployment of advanced optimal control strategies.

#### 5.1. Significance

1. **Addressing MPPI's Computational Bottleneck:** The primary significance lies in addressing a fundamental limitation of classic Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control: the high computational cost associated with repeatedly propagating complex nonlinear system dynamics during trajectory sampling. By integrating learned linear Deep Koopman Operator (DKO) dynamics, the proposed MPPI-DK framework provides a method to accelerate this process.
2. **Validation of DKO for Sampling-Based Control:** The research demonstrates that learned structured linear DKO dynamics can function as effective computational accelerators specifically within sampling-based control methodologies. This extends the known applications of Koopman theory beyond dynamics identification and prediction to real-time control.
3. **Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** The findings indicate that MPPI-DK achieves control performance comparable to classic MPPI with access to true nonlinear dynamics, while substantially reducing computational cost. This favorable trade-off suggests a practical pathway for implementing advanced control on platforms with limited computational resources.
4. **Hardware Validation:** The successful deployment and validation on a physical quadruped robot (Unitree Go1) underscore the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed framework in real-world scenarios, moving beyond purely simulated environments.
5. **Leveraging Parallel Computation:** The observed significant speedups with GPU acceleration highlight the natural compatibility of the MPPI-DK framework with parallel computing architectures, further enhancing its appeal for high-frequency control tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Enhanced Real-Time Robotic Control:** The ability to execute MPPI-like performance with reduced computational demands can enable the real-time control of highly nonlinear and complex robotic systems (e.g., legged robots, agile aerial vehicles) in demanding applications that require rapid responses and aggressive maneuvers.
2. **Broader Applicability of MPPI:** By alleviating computational constraints, the MPPI-DK framework could broaden the applicability of MPPI to a wider range of robotic platforms and tasks where classic MPPI was previously unfeasible due to its computational burden.
3. **Data-Driven Control for Unknown Dynamics:** The approach removes the necessity for analytically derived system models, relying instead on data-driven dynamics learning. This is particularly impactful for systems where true dynamics are difficult or impossible to model accurately.
4. **Integration of Learning and Control:** This work contributes to the growing field of integrating data-driven learning techniques with model-based control strategies, offering a structured approach for leveraging machine learning to improve control system efficiency.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The demonstrated success opens avenues for future research in refining DKO learning algorithms for control, exploring adaptive DKO models for changing environments, and investigating the theoretical guarantees of such learned-model-based controllers.
