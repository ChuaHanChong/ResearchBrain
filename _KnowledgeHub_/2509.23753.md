# Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT) introduces a method for post-training large language models by integrating dynamic reweighting with KL divergence regularization. This approach consistently improves performance, achieving an average of +4.85 points over prior methods in mathematical reasoning and preventing instability in knowledge-intensive tasks, while maintaining the computational efficiency of supervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is efficient but often leads to memorization and lacks robust generalization for complex tasks.
- Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT) shows promise in reasoning but suffers from instability and performance degradation in knowledge-intensive tasks due to uncontrolled distributional shifts.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods offer superior generalization but are computationally expensive, prone to instability, and difficult to tune.

## Method

- The authors theoretically diagnose DFT's instability by characterizing it within the ==Reward-Weighted Regression== (RWR) framework and identifying progressive distributional drift.
- They propose Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT), which adds a lightweight KL divergence regularization term to DFT's objective, effectively anchoring the fine-tuned policy to a stable reference.
- ASFT-LoRA is introduced to compute the KL divergence with reduced memory footprint, making the method more practical for resource-constrained environments.

## Results

- ASFT consistently outperformed SFT and DFT across mathematical reasoning (e.g., +4.85 points average over DFT), medical knowledge, and code generation tasks, demonstrating broad applicability and enhanced generalization.
- The method maintained stable training dynamics and KL divergence, preventing the severe distributional drift observed in DFT on knowledge-intensive tasks.
- ASFT achieves performance comparable to advanced RL methods with only a **23.7%** increase in training time over SFT, and ASFT-LoRA reduces memory overhead, providing an efficient alternative.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DFT's reweighting provides a tighter lower bound on the RL objective than SFT, but its auxiliary distribution's unchecked drift causes instability and domain-specific failures.
> - KL divergence regularization effectively controls this distributional drift, allowing ASFT to maintain training stability and robust generalization without sacrificing tightness benefits.
> - The RWR framework offers a unified theoretical lens for understanding the trade-offs between SFT, DFT, and ASFT in terms of tightness and stability during post-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has necessitated sophisticated post-training techniques to align these powerful base models with human instructions and preferences, as well as to enhance their performance on specific tasks. The research landscape in LLM post-training is primarily dominated by two paradigms:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This method involves training LLMs on high-quality expert demonstrations using a maximum likelihood objective. SFT is computationally efficient and effective at rapidly acquiring desired behaviors. However, a significant drawback is its tendency to "memorize" surface patterns rather than developing robust, generalizable strategies, especially in complex reasoning tasks. It is often viewed as optimizing a loose lower bound on the true reinforcement learning objective.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Often implemented as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or its variants (e.g., Direct Preference Optimization - DPO), RL methods optimize outcome-based rewards by directly interacting with the model's own outputs. RL offers superior generalization and alignment with human preferences. However, it is substantially more computationally expensive, prone to instability (e.g., policy collapse), and difficult to tune in practice.

The current paper directly addresses the "fundamental trade-off" between SFT's efficiency and RL's generalization. It is situated within a growing body of work that seeks to bridge this gap by either making SFT more generalizable or making RL more stable and efficient.

Specifically, this work builds upon and critically analyzes **Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT)** (Wu et al., 2025a), a recent approach that reweights SFT objectives using token probabilities. DFT demonstrated promising results in certain reasoning domains by identifying and correcting a pathological reward structure in standard SFT that led to unbounded variance. However, the authors' preliminary experiments revealed DFT's limitations: domain-specificity (excelling in reasoning but unstable in knowledge-intensive tasks) and a lack of clear theoretical grounding for its design choices and instabilities.

The proposed **Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT)** method positions itself as an advancement in this lineage, offering a principled, theoretically grounded solution to DFT's shortcomings. It integrates concepts from reward-weighted regression (RWR) and importance sampling theory, which have deep roots in policy optimization and off-policy learning. By demonstrating that SFT, DFT, and ASFT can all be analyzed under the RWR framework, the authors provide a unifying theoretical lens for understanding these post-training methods. ASFT extends the idea of importance-weighted supervised objectives by adding a crucial "distributional anchoring" mechanism (KL regularization), linking it to trust-region methods in RL (e.g., TRPO, PPO) that constrain policy updates to maintain stability. This places ASFT at the forefront of efforts to develop efficient yet highly performant and stable LLM post-training algorithms, aiming for RL-comparable generalization with SFT-level computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT) stem from addressing the inherent limitations of existing Large Language Model (LLM) post-training paradigms, particularly the trade-off between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

The key motivations are:

1. **Overcoming SFT's Memorization and Limited Generalization:** Standard SFT, while efficient, tends to learn surface patterns from expert demonstrations, leading to memorization rather than robust, generalizable strategies. This limits its effectiveness, especially for complex reasoning tasks or out-of-distribution scenarios. The authors aim to enhance SFT's generalization capabilities without incurring the high costs of full RL.
2. **Addressing DFT's Instability and Domain Specificity:** Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT) emerged as a promising middle ground, showing empirical improvements in mathematical reasoning by adaptively reweighting SFT objectives. However, the authors' initial observations revealed that DFT suffers from significant instability in knowledge-intensive tasks and lacks a robust theoretical foundation for its efficacy and limitations. This instability, particularly in distributional shift, motivated a deeper theoretical investigation.
3. **Providing Theoretical Grounding for Adaptive Reweighting Methods:** A critical gap identified was the absence of a comprehensive theoretical understanding of DFT within a formal framework. The authors were motivated to provide a rigorous analysis of DFT using the reward-weighted regression (RWR) framework, which connects SFT and RL through importance sampling. This analysis aimed to explain *why* DFT works in some cases and *why* it fails in others.
4. **Developing a Principled and Stable Generalization Method:** Based on their theoretical insights into DFT's limitations (specifically, the lack of distributional anchoring leading to progressive drift), the authors were motivated to design a method that preserves the tightness benefits of adaptive reweighting while ensuring training stability. This led to the inclusion of a lightweight regularization mechanism.
5. **Achieving RL-Comparable Performance with SFT-Level Efficiency:** Ultimately, the overarching goal is to develop a post-training method that can achieve the generalization benefits often associated with RL, but with the computational efficiency and stability characteristic of SFT. This would make advanced LLM capabilities more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications and resource constraints.

In summary, the core objective is to theoretically diagnose and empirically solve the instability problems of advanced SFT techniques like DFT, leading to a new method (ASFT) that offers superior generalization, broader applicability, and greater stability while remaining computationally lightweight.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is two-fold: a principled theoretical analysis of existing methods followed by the proposal and empirical validation of a novel technique.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis of Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT)

The authors' approach begins by situating Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT) within the **Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) framework**, a theoretical lens rooted in importance sampling and policy optimization. This framework provides a principled connection between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) objectives.

1. **SFT as an RL Lower Bound:** The analysis first re-establishes that standard SFT optimizes a lower bound on the RL objective, particularly in sparse reward settings. This bound, however, can become loose as the fine-tuned policy diverges from the reference (pre-trained) distribution.
2. **Generalizing with Auxiliary Distributions:** The RWR framework allows for the construction of tighter bounds on the RL objective by introducing an auxiliary distribution, `q(τ)`. The choice of `q(τ)` is critical for both the tightness of the bound and the stability of optimization.
3. **DFT's Auxiliary Distribution:** The key theoretical contribution is proving that DFT's objective is mathematically equivalent to choosing a *specific* auxiliary distribution:
 `q(τ) = (π_ref(τ | D+) sg[p_θ(τ)]) / E_τ∼π_ref(·|D+)[sg[p_θ(τ)]]`
 where `sg[·]` is the stop-gradient operator. This precise characterization of DFT within RWR is crucial.
4. **Proof of Tightness:** The authors rigorously prove that this specific auxiliary distribution used by DFT yields a *strictly tighter lower bound* on the RL objective compared to standard SFT, provided the policy assigns non-uniform probabilities to demonstrations (i.e., `Var(p_θ(τ)) > 0`). This explains DFT's observed empirical gains in certain reasoning tasks.
5. **Uncovering Distributional Drift:** Despite the tightness, the analysis reveals a critical flaw: DFT's auxiliary distribution `q(τ)` is not "anchored" to a stable reference. As training progresses, `q(τ)` becomes increasingly concentrated on trajectories where the current policy `p_θ(τ)` assigns high probability. This creates a positive feedback loop, leading to progressive divergence from the initial reference distribution (`π_ref`) and undermining the validity of the RWR framework's assumptions. This "distributional drift" is identified as the root cause of DFT's instability and domain-specific effectiveness.

#### 3.2. Proposed Method: Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT)

To address DFT's distributional drift while retaining its tightness benefits, the authors propose **Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT)**.

1. **KL Regularization:** ASFT augments DFT's reweighting objective with a lightweight KL divergence regularization term. This term constrains the fine-tuned policy (`π_θ`) to remain within a "trust region" of a fixed reference policy (`π_base`, typically the pre-trained model). The objective function is:
 `L_ASFT(θ) = L_DFT(θ) + λ E_s [D_KL(π_θ(· | s)∥π_base(· | s))]`
 where `λ` controls the strength of the anchoring.
2. **Theoretical Guarantees:** This design is theoretically sound because the KL term does not alter the lower-bound structure derived for DFT, thus preserving its tightness advantages. Simultaneously, it provides explicit variance control, preventing the uncontrolled distributional drift and exponential variance growth that destabilize pure DFT. The anchoring mechanism stabilizes training by keeping the policy close to a known distribution.
3. **Practical Implementation:** ASFT is implemented at the token level, distributing sequence-level weights across tokens, a common and computationally efficient practice in LLM training. The method adds minimal computational overhead compared to standard SFT, primarily due to the KL divergence calculation.
4. **Computational Efficiency Considerations and ASFT-LoRA:** Recognizing that the KL term requires keeping `π_base` in memory, potentially doubling GPU memory, the authors also propose **ASFT-LoRA**. This variant leverages low-rank adaptation's parameterization (`ΔW = BA`) to compute the KL divergence using a single model instance by dynamically switching between `W_base` and `W_base + BA` during forward passes, significantly reducing memory footprint while maintaining the anchoring mechanism.

In essence, the approach is a systematic blend of theoretical insights (RWR framework) and practical algorithm design (KL regularization, LoRA integration) to create a robust and efficient LLM post-training method.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT) demonstrates its consistent superiority across diverse tasks and model scales, confirming the theoretical insights regarding stability and tightness.

#### 4.1. Consistent Outperformance Across Domains

- **Mathematical Reasoning:** ASFT consistently outperforms both SFT and DFT. On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (NuminaMath CoT, Math500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, AIME 2024, AMC 2023), ASFT achieved an average improvement of +4.85 points (18.6%) over DFT and a substantial +17.89 points (142%) over the base model with 100k training samples. Its gains were particularly pronounced on challenging benchmarks like AMC23, reflecting better generalization.
- **Medical Knowledge Grounding:** In knowledge-intensive tasks (MedMCQA, MMLU-medical, MedQA), DFT exhibited severe performance degradation due to its instability (e.g., -2.19 points average drop at 10k samples). In contrast, ASFT not only avoided this but achieved significant gains, outperforming the base model by +10.65 points (33.9% relative improvement) at 10k scale, maintaining robust advantages with larger datasets. This highlights ASFT's ability to maintain stability and effectiveness across domains where DFT falters.
- **Code Generation:** ASFT's generality was further validated on code generation benchmarks (HumanEval, HumanEval+, MBPP, MBPP+). Fine-tuning LLaMA-2-7B with ASFT resulted in the highest average score (27.0%), notably improving HumanEval and HumanEval+ while being competitive on MBPP. This confirms that the anchoring mechanism generalizes effectively to different types of tasks and data.

#### 4.2. Training Stability and Distributional Control

- **KL Divergence Stability (Figure 1):** The empirical results visually confirm that DFT exhibits severe distributional drift (high KL divergence from the base model) during training, especially in medical knowledge tasks. ASFT, by contrast, maintains stable KL divergence, demonstrating the effectiveness of its KL anchoring mechanism in preventing uncontrolled shifts and ensuring training stability.
- **Ablation Study on KL Regularization:**
 - **Forward vs. Reverse KL:** Comparing `D_KL(Q || P)` (reverse KL, used in ASFT) with `D_KL(P || Q)` (forward KL) showed that reverse KL leads to stable convergence and consistent improvements. Forward KL, conversely, tended to encourage mode-covering and resulted in less stable performance.
 - **Regularization Coefficient (λ):** An optimal range for `λ` around 0.1 was identified, with too small values leading to under-anchoring (instability) and too large values leading to over-regularization (limiting model flexibility).

#### 4.3. Robustness to Hyperparameters and Scaling Analysis

- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** ASFT demonstrated strong robustness and low sensitivity to key training hyperparameters such as learning rate and batch size. It consistently outperformed baselines across a wide range of configurations, indicating its practical applicability.
- **Scaling Across Model Sizes (Figure 3, Table 5):** Experiments conducted with various model sizes (LLaMA-2-7B/70B, Qwen2.5-7B/32B/72B) confirmed that ASFT's improvements remain robust as models scale. It consistently outperformed Base, SFT, and DFT across all model sizes, demonstrating effective and stable adaptation even in low-resource settings.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Reinforcement Learning Methods

- **Slightly Underperforming Advanced RL (Table 2):** While ASFT substantially outperforms SFT-based approaches, it still falls slightly short of advanced, full reinforcement learning methods like DAPO (ASFT average 42.03 vs. DAPO average 42.25 on medical benchmarks). This validates the theoretical framework, suggesting that ASFT achieves a tighter *lower bound* on the RL objective, but RL can still achieve slightly higher performance by directly optimizing the reward function.
- **Enhanced Initialization for RL:** Crucially, ASFT provides a superior initialization point for subsequent RL fine-tuning. Training with DAPO *after* ASFT (ASFT + DAPO) yielded consistent gains over SFT + DAPO (44.10 vs. 40.24 average) and even surpassed standalone DAPO (44.10 vs. 42.25). This indicates that ASFT's distributional stability creates a more robust policy foundation for further RL optimization.

#### 4.5. Computational Efficiency and Memory Management

- **Computational Overhead:** ASFT introduces minimal computational overhead compared to standard SFT, with approximately 23.7% increase in training time (0.648 hours vs. 0.524 hours for SFT on LLaMA-2-7B). This is substantially more efficient than full RL approaches like GRPO (51.24 hours).
- **Memory Efficiency with ASFT-LoRA:** Full-parameter ASFT requires doubling GPU memory (88.02GB vs. 38.96GB for SFT) due to needing `π_base` in memory. ASFT-LoRA reduces this to 40.70GB (comparable to SFT) with only a 7.3% training time overhead, demonstrating a practical compromise for resource-constrained environments while maintaining meaningful performance gains over SFT.

In conclusion, the main findings highlight ASFT as a highly effective, stable, and computationally efficient post-training method that addresses critical limitations of both SFT and DFT, offering a strong balance between generalization and practicality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT) presents a significant contribution to the field of large language model (LLM) post-training, with substantial theoretical and practical implications. Its significance stems from effectively addressing a core trade-off in modern LLM development and offering a robust, efficient solution.

#### 5.1. Bridging the SFT-RL Gap with Theoretical Rigor

The most profound significance of ASFT lies in its ability to bridge the long-standing gap between the efficiency of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and the generalization capabilities of Reinforcement Learning (RL). By grounding its analysis and design within the Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) framework, the paper provides a **principled theoretical understanding** of why certain SFT-like methods (like DFT) work and where they fail.

- **Clarity on DFT's Mechanics:** The theoretical analysis precisely characterizes DFT's auxiliary distribution, proving its ability to achieve tighter RL lower bounds than SFT. This provides the first rigorous explanation for DFT's empirical success in specific domains.
- **Diagnosis of DFT's Instability:** More importantly, the identification of "distributional drift" as the fundamental cause of DFT's instability and domain-specific failures is a critical theoretical insight. This diagnosis paves the way for a targeted solution.
- **The Power of Anchoring:** ASFT's proposed KL regularization, termed "anchoring," is a theoretically sound mechanism to control this drift without sacrificing the tightness benefits. This demonstrates that combining the adaptive weighting of DFT with trust-region-like constraints leads to both stronger theoretical guarantees and practical gains.

This work reinforces the idea that **principled theoretical analysis can directly lead to practical algorithmic improvements**, offering a clear roadmap for developing more effective LLM post-training methods.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Generalization and Stability with Minimal Overhead

Practically, ASFT offers several significant advantages:

- **Superior Performance:** ASFT consistently outperforms both standard SFT and the prior state-of-the-art DFT across a wide range of tasks including mathematical reasoning, medical knowledge grounding, and code generation. This broad empirical success indicates its general applicability.
- **Robustness and Stability:** Unlike DFT, which showed severe instability in knowledge-intensive tasks, ASFT maintains stable training dynamics and performance across diverse domains and scales. This stability is crucial for reliable deployment and reduces the effort required for hyperparameter tuning.
- **Computational Efficiency:** ASFT achieves performance comparable to advanced RL methods but with computational overhead akin to SFT. This makes advanced fine-tuning techniques significantly more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources, democratizing access to powerful LLM capabilities. The ASFT-LoRA variant further enhances memory efficiency, addressing a critical bottleneck in large model training and deployment.
- **Improved RL Initialization:** The finding that ASFT provides a superior initialization point for subsequent RL fine-tuning (outperforming even standalone advanced RL methods like DAPO) suggests a valuable multi-stage training strategy. ASFT can serve as a strong pre-alignment step, providing a stable policy foundation for further reward-based optimization.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact and Future Directions

The potential impact of ASFT is multifaceted:

- **Broader Accessibility of Advanced LLM Capabilities:** By offering a high-performance, stable, and efficient fine-tuning method, ASFT can accelerate the development and deployment of specialized LLMs across various industries (e.g., healthcare, education, finance, scientific research) that require accurate reasoning and knowledge injection.
- **New Avenues for Research:** The RWR framework provided by the authors offers a systematic lens for analyzing existing and designing new post-training methods. This could inspire further research into different auxiliary distribution choices, novel regularization techniques, and hybrid SFT-RL approaches with stronger theoretical underpinnings.
- **Ethical Deployment:** The increased stability and robustness of ASFT contribute to more predictable model behavior, which is crucial for ethical AI deployment. While the authors correctly highlight the need for continued validation and safeguards, a more stable training process reduces the likelihood of unexpected or undesirable behaviors stemming from uncontrolled distributional shifts.

In conclusion, ASFT represents a significant step forward in optimizing LLM post-training. By providing a clear theoretical explanation for existing methods' strengths and weaknesses and offering a robust, efficient solution, it pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with SFT-level computational resources, making advanced LLM generalization more attainable and reliable.
