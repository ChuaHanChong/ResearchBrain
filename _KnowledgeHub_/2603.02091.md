# Learning from Synthetic Data Improves Multi-hop Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforcement Learning fine-tuning on rule-generated synthetic data significantly enhances multi-hop reasoning in Large Language Models. Models trained on fictional, semantically simple data acquire a transferable ability to compose knowledge, resulting in relative F1 score improvements ranging from 56% to 131% on real-world benchmarks.

## Problem

- High-quality, verifiable training data for complex LLM reasoning, especially multi-hop tasks, is scarce and expensive to obtain.
- Existing methods like human annotation or LLM-generated data for RL fine-tuning face scalability, cost, and verification challenges.
- The potential of simple, rule-generated synthetic data to teach generalizable reasoning skills for real-world scenarios remained largely unexplored.

## Method

- The authors propose using ==rule-generated== synthetic data for Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning to enhance multi-hop reasoning in LLMs.
- They fine-tuned various LLMs (0.6B to 4B parameters) using the GRPO algorithm on four distinct, rule-generated synthetic datasets (e.g., PhantomWiki, GSM-∞).
- Performance was evaluated on five diverse real-world multi-hop reasoning benchmarks to assess the transfer of learned reasoning skills.

## Results

- RL fine-tuning on synthetic data consistently improved relative F1 scores on real-world multi-hop reasoning benchmarks by **56%** to **131%** for models like Qwen3-**0.6B**.
- The learned "knowledge composition" skill generalized to held-out synthetic test sets, including out-of-domain complexities, and transferred across different LLMs and synthetic data types.
- Performance on real-world benchmarks scaled positively with increased synthetic training data, and outcome-only rewards also improved the correctness of intermediate reasoning steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can learn "knowledge composition," a generalizable and domain-independent reasoning skill, even from semantically simple, fictional synthetic data.
> - Rule-generated synthetic data presents a scalable, free, and verifiable resource for continuously improving LLM reasoning capabilities.
> - Reinforcement Learning fine-tuning is more effective than Supervised Fine-Tuning in imparting transferable reasoning skills from synthetic data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs): the scarcity and cost of high-quality, verifiable training data, especially for complex reasoning tasks. The broader research landscape for LLMs is marked by an insatiable demand for data to improve capabilities across various domains like math, coding, and multi-hop reasoning.

Current approaches to enhance LLM reasoning often rely on Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning, particularly Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). However, the paper highlights three primary limitations:
1. **Human-annotated datasets:** These are high-quality but small and expensive to curate, hindering scalability (Villalobos et al., 2024; Muennighoff et al., 2023).
2. **LLM-generated data:** While scalable, it is prone to hallucinations, costly to generate, and faces verification challenges. It also risks contamination with pretraining corpora (Su et al., 2025).
3. **LLM-based verifiers:** These are often inaccurate and slow, making them unsuitable for real-time RL reward signal generation.

The paper positions itself as a "radical alternative" to these prevailing methods by advocating for **rule-generated synthetic data**. This contrasts sharply with approaches that use LLMs to generate synthetic data (Abdin et al., 2025; Trinh et al., 2024; Ruan et al., 2025). Historically, rule-generated synthetic datasets have been used more for *evaluation* of specific reasoning capabilities in isolation (Mirzadeh et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2024) rather than for *training* models to generalize to real-world tasks. The utility of such semantically simple data for teaching generalizable skills useful in knowledge-intensive real-world contexts has been "underexplored" (Yu et al., 2024; Mizrahi et al., 2025).

By demonstrating that RL fine-tuning on rule-generated synthetic data can teach "knowledge composition"—a fundamental and generalizable reasoning skill—that transfers to real-world benchmarks, this work proposes a paradigm shift. It offers a scalable, free, and verifiable data resource that could alleviate the data bottleneck, thereby transforming how LLMs are trained for reasoning. This aligns with a growing interest in leveraging synthetic data to target specific reasoning skills and overcome test-set leakage issues prevalent in traditional benchmarks (Allen-Zhu, 2025; Zhao et al., 2025a).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate a cost-effective and scalable alternative for enhancing the multi-hop reasoning capabilities of LLMs: **Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning on rule-generated synthetic data**.

The motivation stems from the significant limitations of current RL fine-tuning approaches for LLMs, particularly for complex tasks like multi-hop reasoning:
- **Data Scarcity and Cost:** High-quality, verifiable data, essential for effective RL fine-tuning (especially RLVR), is scarce and expensive to acquire through human annotation or generate via frontier LLMs.
- **Verification Challenges:** LLM-generated synthetic data, while scalable, often lacks ground truth and suffers from hallucinations, making verification difficult and costly. LLM-based verifiers themselves are often slow and inaccurate.
- **Need for Generalizable Skills:** The researchers aim to determine if LLMs can learn fundamental, domain-independent reasoning skills (specifically, "knowledge composition" for multi-hop reasoning) from data that is semantically simple, entirely fictional, and devoid of real-world knowledge or linguistic complexity.

The fundamental research question driving this work is: "Can models develop general reasoning capabilities solely from synthetic data, without relying on real-world knowledge or linguistic complexity?" Answering this question positively would "unlock rule-generated synthetic data as a viable resource for scaling LLM reasoning," which has "transformative implications." The paper seeks to demonstrate that this "synthetic-to-real transfer" is not merely superficial but reflects genuine skill acquisition, leading to more grounded reasoning processes and improved performance across diverse real-world benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic empirical approach involving RL fine-tuning of various LLMs on diverse rule-generated synthetic datasets and evaluating their performance on real-world multi-hop reasoning benchmarks.

#### 3.1. Language Models Used

Four LLMs were chosen to cover a range of model families and parameter sizes (0.6B to 4B parameters), controlling for differences in pre-training and post-training strategies:
- Qwen3-0.6B
- Qwen3-1.7B
- Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct
- Phi-4-mini-reasoning

#### 3.2. Synthetic Training Datasets (Rule-Generated)

The core of the methodology lies in the use of rule-generated synthetic data, which is semantically simple, fully verifiable by construction, free to produce at arbitrary scale, and allows precise control over question complexity. Four distinct datasets were used, each probing different reasoning styles:
- **PhantomWiki (Gong et al., 2025):** Designed for synthetic multi-hop reasoning. It generates Wikipedia-like documents about fictional people and multi-hop question-answer pairs using a context-free grammar and logic programming. Questions require retrieval and knowledge composition. Configured to require 1-9 hops, focusing on immediate family/friend relations and "Who is the..." / "What is the..." questions.
- **GSM-∞ (GSM-Infinite; Zhou et al., 2025):** Generalizes grade school math word problems (GSM8K) to infinite scale. It builds random computation graphs and converts them to word problems via templates, studying transfer from arithmetic to knowledge-intensive multi-hop reasoning. Questions involve 2-20 arithmetic operations.
- **ReasoningGym (Stojanovski et al., 2025):** An open-source RLVR library. Two environments were used:
 - **RG-Family (Family-Relationships):** Infers relationships between individuals in a randomly generated family tree.
 - **RG-Knights (Knights-Knaves):** Requires identifying truth-tellers from liars based on logical statements.
 These datasets probe logical deductive reasoning for transferability.

Crucially, all synthetic datasets were based on fictional worlds with **zero factual overlap** with real-world benchmarks, ensuring that any performance transfer indicates learning of general reasoning skills rather than memorization.

#### 3.3. RL Fine-tuning Algorithm

The **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO; Shao et al., 2024)** algorithm, a variant of PPO, was used. GRPO estimates the advantage term using a group of completions for each prompt. The authors used the `GRPOTrainer` implementation from Hugging Face TRL v0.21.0, noting specific hyperparameters and resource usage (4 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, vLLM colocation, FlashAttention-2).

#### 3.4. Prompt and Reward Design

- **Prompt Structure:** LLMs were fine-tuned for in-context reasoning. Prompts included evidence (problem statement or PhantomWiki articles), an instruction to output the final answer within `<answer>...</answer>` tags (popularized by DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3), and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) examples (3 for GSM-∞, 11 for PhantomWiki, 11 custom for RG-Family/Knights).
- **Reward Function:** The model's prediction from the `<answer>...</answer>` tags was compared to ground truth.
 - For GSM-∞, RG-Family, and RG-Knights: Binary reward based on exact match.
 - For PhantomWiki: F1 score reward due to potential multiple answers.
Training involved 10K samples per dataset, shuffled randomly.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Benchmarks (Real-World Multi-Hop Reasoning)

Five diverse real-world datasets were used, all in their "distractor versions" to test in-context reasoning with non-relevant information:
1. **HotpotQA (Yang et al., 2018):** 2-hop questions, combining information from Wikipedia paragraphs.
2. **2WikiMultihopQA (Ho et al., 2020):** 2-hop dataset, grounded in Wikidata, with compositional, inference, comparison, and bridge-comparison categories.
3. **MuSiQue (Trivedi et al., 2022):** 2-4 hop questions, bridging single-hop questions, focused on compositional reasoning.
4. **CounterfactualQA (CofCA) (Wu et al., 2025a):** A rewritten subset of HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, and MuSiQue, designed to remove factual knowledge memorization.
5. **SynthWorlds-RM (Gu et al., 2025):** Higher-complexity dataset (2-6 hops) with constraints curated from Wikidata.
Performance was evaluated using F1 scores on 500 subsampled questions from each test set.

#### 3.6. Ablation Studies

- **Format-only training:** To disentangle improved reasoning from mere answer formatting, models were fine-tuned with a binary reward only for using the `<answer>...</answer>` tags correctly.
- **SFT vs. RL:** Compared Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on ground-truth solution traces (from GSM-∞) with RL fine-tuning to assess which method teaches transferable knowledge composition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings demonstrating the efficacy of RL fine-tuning on rule-generated synthetic data for improving LLM multi-hop reasoning:

1. **Consistent Synthetic-to-Real Transfer:** RL fine-tuning on rule-generated synthetic data remarkably improved performance on all real-world multi-hop reasoning benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue, CofCA, SynthWorlds-RM). For the Qwen3-0.6B model, relative F1 score improvements ranged from 56% to 131% (Figure 2). This transfer was consistent across different synthetic data generators (PhantomWiki, GSM-∞, RG-Family, RG-Knights) and different model families and sizes (0.6B to 4B parameters), with smaller models benefiting the most. Notably, even models previously trained on synthetic data (Qwen3, Phi-4-mini-reasoning) showed further improvements. This is despite the synthetic training data containing no factual knowledge relevant to the real-world benchmarks and being semantically simple.

2. **Knowledge Composition as a Generalizable Skill:** The performance transfer is attributed to models learning "knowledge composition"—the elementary skill of integrating information across multiple steps—as a domain-independent skill, rather than merely memorizing or surfacing pretraining knowledge. This was confirmed by evaluating models on held-out synthetic test sets that shared no factual overlap with training data and included out-of-domain (OOD) question complexities. As fine-tuning progressed, performance improved across all complexity levels, including OOD difficulties, indicating that models generalized the skill beyond what they were trained on (Figure 3).

3. **RL Fine-tuning Beyond Answer Formatting:** An ablation study demonstrated that the observed performance improvements were not solely due to learning the correct output format. While `Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` showed substantial improvement from format-only training (indicating it learned "reward hacking" for formatting), `Qwen3` and `Phi-4-mini-reasoning` (which already produced correct formatting) did not improve with format-only training. This indicated that their synthetic-to-real transfer specifically stemmed from learning knowledge composition (Table 1).

4. **SFT Fails to Teach Transferable Knowledge Composition:** A comparison with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on ground-truth solution traces from GSM-∞ revealed a critical difference. While SFT improved performance on the synthetic GSM-∞ task, it showed no performance improvement on the real-world HotpotQA benchmark. In contrast, RL fine-tuning yielded improvements in both synthetic and real-world tasks. This suggests that SFT can overfit to the synthetic task, failing to teach the fundamental, transferable knowledge composition skill that RL fine-tuning successfully imparts (Table 2).

5. **Grounded Generations through Outcome-Only Reward:** The study found that RL fine-tuning, despite using only outcome-based rewards (final answer correctness), also improved the underlying reasoning process. As fine-tuning progressed on synthetic data, models generated reasoning traces that contained a higher proportion of correct intermediate answers for real-world tasks like MuSiQue and CofCA (Figure 4). This indicates that learning to compose knowledge led to more grounded and accurate intermediate reasoning steps.

6. **Performance Scales with Synthetic Data:** Performance on real-world benchmarks scaled monotonically with the number of synthetic training samples, with no signs of overfitting. As training steps increased (equivalent to more synthetic data exposure), the F1 scores on all real-world benchmarks steadily improved (Figure 5). This highlights rule-generated synthetic data as a viable and scalable resource for continued improvement in LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a highly significant contribution to the field of Large Language Models and AI reasoning, with transformative implications:

1. **Overcoming the Data Bottleneck:** The most immediate impact is providing a scalable, free, and verifiable alternative to expensive human-annotated or hallucination-prone LLM-generated data. As the demand for high-quality reasoning data outstrips supply, rule-generated synthetic data emerges as a crucial resource for continuous LLM training and improvement, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and sustainable.

2. **Demonstrating Generalizable Skill Acquisition:** By showing "synthetic-to-real transfer" from entirely fictional and semantically simple data to complex, knowledge-intensive real-world tasks, the paper provides strong evidence that LLMs can learn truly generalizable, domain-independent reasoning skills like "knowledge composition." This counters the hypothesis that RL fine-tuning primarily surfaces memorized pretraining knowledge and suggests a deeper form of learning is occurring. This insight is crucial for developing more robust and intelligent AI systems.

3. **Enhanced Understanding of LLM Learning:** The findings illuminate how LLMs learn. The success of RL fine-tuning over SFT in fostering transferability suggests that the active exploration and reward-based signal of RL are particularly effective for bootstrapping fundamental reasoning skills. Furthermore, the observation that outcome-only rewards improve intermediate reasoning steps (grounded generations) is a valuable insight into the internal mechanisms of LLM reasoning development.

4. **Facilitating Targeted Skill Development:** Rule-generated synthetic data, with its precise control over complexity and isolation of specific reasoning styles, offers a powerful tool for diagnosing and improving particular LLM capabilities. This can lead to more efficient and focused training regimes, enabling researchers to teach specific skills (e.g., causal reasoning, counterfactual inference, analogical thinking) in isolation before integrating them into broader systems.

5. **New Avenues for RL Fine-tuning Scaling:** The demonstration of monotonic performance scaling with increased synthetic data points to a new axis for scaling RL fine-tuning. This, alongside algorithmic and architectural improvements, could pave the way for LLMs with unprecedented reasoning abilities. The work challenges researchers to explore the interaction between synthetic and knowledge-intensive real-world data and how model initialization influences RL fine-tuning.

In conclusion, this research spotlights rule-generated synthetic data as an underutilized yet immensely powerful resource. It offers a practical and theoretically significant pathway to developing LLMs that can reason more effectively and efficiently across diverse, real-world challenges, ultimately accelerating progress towards more capable and intelligent AI.
