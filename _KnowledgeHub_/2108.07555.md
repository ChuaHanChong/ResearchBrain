# Revisiting State Augmentation methods for Reinforcement Learning with Stochastic Delays

> [!summary] Summary
> Delay-Resolved RL formalizes constant-delay and stochastic-delay MDPs and proves each reduces to an equivalent undelayed MDP with a simplified reward that preserves the optimal policy. The information state concatenates the latest observed state with a zero-padded buffer of pending actions; feeding it to a deep Q-network (DRDQN) reaches near-optimal reward under delays up to ten with almost no extra compute.

## Problem

- Classical delayed-MDP theory keeps a conditional-expectation cost costing O(d|S|^2) computation, and tabular augmented states grow exponentially with delay, so augmentation was considered intractable.
- Most earlier delayed-RL algorithms assume constant, known delays; memoryless action-history methods handle action delay only and need the delay value supplied a priori.
- Model-based and forward-model approaches must estimate transition dynamics, compounding prediction error over long or stochastic delays while adding training cost and trajectory storage.

## Method

- Formal ==CDMDP== and ==SDMDP== definitions, with a lemma showing action and observation delays are functionally equivalent and a theorem reducing delayed MDPs to undelayed MDPs sharing the optimal policy.
- ==Information-state== construction: last observed state concatenated with the buffer of actions taken since that observation, zero-padded to a fixed maximum length so stochastic delays need no per-step delay input.
- **Delay-Resolved Deep Q-Network** (**DRDQN**) feeds that information state into a neural function approximator instead of a table, shrinking input size to |S| + d|A|, linear in delay.

## Results

- On W-Maze, tabular delay-resolved Q-learning degrades at large action delays from exploding state space, while the DQN version holds near-optimal reward (about **0**) through delay **10**.
- Across Acrobot, MountainCar and CartPole (**1 million** steps, **10** runs), DRDQN converges near optimal (about **200** on CartPole, about **-100** on Acrobot) for constant and uniform **0-10** stochastic delays.
- DRDQN runtime nearly matches vanilla DQN, whereas forward-model Delayed DQN runs far longer as delay grows and underperforms on MountainCar and CartPole with inaccurate models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State augmentation is not doomed: neural function approximation converts an exponentially sized information state into a linear-size input, reviving a technique dismissed as intractable.
> - Action and observation delays are interchangeable for decision making, so a single delay-agnostic algorithm covers both without knowing the per-step delay value.
> - Delay hurts most when dynamics are stochastic; as transitions become deterministic the penalty vanishes, so predictive models pay off least exactly where delays are random.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, particularly those based on the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, typically operate under two core assumptions that restrict their applicability to real-world scenarios: the immediate availability of the system's current state (no observation delay) and the instantaneous effect of an agent's action on the system (no action delay). However, many practical systems, such as remote control, robotic systems, distributed computing, and medical decision-making, inherently involve significant and often stochastic delays in observations and actions. The presence of such delays is known to degrade the performance of RL agents, potentially leading to suboptimal behavior or system instability.

Existing approaches to address delays in RL include:
- **Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs):** While a generalization of MDPs, solving POMDPs without estimating hidden states can lead to suboptimal policies. State augmentation is often employed to restore the Markov property, but this typically results in an exponentially increasing state space with delay length, making tabular methods intractable.
- **Model-based methods:** These approaches attempt to predict the current state in a delayed environment by estimating transition probabilities. However, they are computationally expensive, particularly for large state spaces or stochastic delays, and susceptible to inaccuracies in learned models.
- **Memory-less methods:** Some approaches aim to handle constant delays without state augmentation by exploiting prior information about the delay, but these are often limited in scope.
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) with augmented states:** Recent advances in DRL have enabled the practical implementation of augmented state approaches by using deep neural networks as function approximators, which can handle higher-dimensional input spaces more effectively than tabular methods. Examples include Real-Time Reinforcement Learning (RTRL) for single-step action delays and methods using partial trajectory sampling for random delays.
- **Forward models:** Approaches like Delayed DQN predict the current undelayed state using forward models, but these require additional computational resources for model training and can suffer from compounding errors with larger delays or inaccuracies in the learned models.
- **Delay Correcting Actor Critic (DCAC):** This method for random delays uses augmented states and delay values to convert off-policy trajectories to on-policy, but it relies on complete information about delay values at each step.

This work contributes to the landscape by formally defining MDPs with constant and stochastic delays (CDMDPs and SDMDPs) and demonstrating that they can be transformed into equivalent standard MDPs with a simplified cost structure. This simplification, combined with state augmentation and deep neural networks, aims to provide a computationally efficient and robust model-free framework, called Delay-Resolved RL, that can handle both constant and stochastic delays without requiring explicit knowledge of delay values at each step or complex model learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are:

1. **Formalizing Delayed MDPs:** To formally define Constant Delay Markov Decision Processes (CDMDPs) and Stochastic Delay Markov Decision Processes (SDMDPs). This involves clearly articulating how the environment dynamics and agent's observations are affected by fixed or variable delays in actions and observations.
2. **Establishing Equivalence to Undelayed MDPs:** To demonstrate that MDPs with either deterministic or stochastic delays can be converted into equivalent standard (undelayed) MDPs. This theoretical foundation is crucial for applying existing RL algorithms to delayed settings.
3. **Simplifying the Cost Structure:** A key objective is to show that this equivalence transformation leads to a significantly simplified cost structure in the equivalent MDP. Traditional state augmentation methods often result in a complex cost structure involving conditional expectations, which makes their computation prohibitively expensive (scaling as O(d|S|^2) for delay `d` and state space `|S|`). The motivation is to reduce this computational burden while preserving optimal policies.
4. **Developing a Model-Free Delay-Resolved RL Framework:** Based on the formal equivalence and simplified cost structure, the authors aim to develop a model-free RL framework. This framework would address the limitations of model-based approaches (computational expense, model inaccuracy) and enable efficient learning in delayed environments.
5. **Addressing State-Space Explosion:** In delayed environments, state augmentation leads to an exponentially growing information state space (e.g., S x A^d). This makes tabular RL methods intractable for even moderate delays. A critical objective is to leverage deep neural networks as function approximators to manage this high-dimensional information state, allowing computational complexity to scale linearly with delay (O(|S| + d|A|)) rather than exponentially.
6. **Achieving Near-Optimal Performance with Minimal Overhead:** The ultimate motivation is to design an algorithm, Delay-Resolved Deep Q-Network (DRDQN), that can achieve near-optimal rewards in environments with both constant and stochastic delays in actions and observations, while also minimizing computational overhead compared to established delayed RL algorithms. This aims to enhance the practicality and real-world applicability of RL in dynamic systems where delays are inherent.
7. **Demonstrating Equivalence of Action and Observation Delays:** To formally and empirically establish that, from the agent's perspective, action delays are functionally equivalent to observation delays, implying that a single framework can address both types of delays.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work centers on transforming delayed MDPs into equivalent undelayed MDPs by augmenting the state space, coupled with a specific modification to the reward structure that simplifies computation. This forms the basis for a model-free deep reinforcement learning algorithm.

**Formalization of Delayed MDPs:**
The paper begins by formally defining:
- **Constant Delay Markov Decision Process (CDMDP):** An MDP augmented with a state space `S × A^(d_o + d_a)`, where `d_o` is observation delay and `d_a` is action delay. The "information state" `I_t` includes the latest observed state `s_{t-d}` and the sequence of actions taken since then `(a_{t-d}, ..., a_{t-1})`.
- **Stochastic Delay Markov Decision Process (SDMDP):** In an SDMDP, the time between successive observations is random. The information state `I_t` for an SDMDP is augmented to include the last observed state `s_{t-d_o}`, the time instant `k` it was first observed, and the sequence of actions `(a_{t-d_o}, ..., a_{t-1})`. To handle varying lengths of this action sequence, a "no-action" (`∅`) is introduced to pad the action buffer to a maximum allowable length `n+1`.

**Theoretical Equivalence and Simplified Cost Structure:**
The core theoretical contributions establish the equivalence between delayed and undelayed MDPs:

1. **Lemma 1 (Equivalence of Action and Observation Delays):** This lemma formally demonstrates that CDMDPs with observation delay `d` and no action delay are functionally equivalent to CDMDPs with action delay `d` and no observation delay. This implies that a unified approach can address both types of delays, reducing complexity. The proof relies on showing that the optimal policies for MDPs with original and modified cost structures are equivalent when conditioned on the information state.
2. **Theorem 1 (Equivalence of SDMDP and Undelayed MDP):** This theorem extends the concept to stochastic delays, proving that an SDMDP can be reduced to an equivalent undelayed MDP with a simplified cost structure. The key is to transform the original expected reward `E_π [ Σ γ^(m-t) r(s_m, a_m) | I_t ]` into a modified expected reward `E_π,D [ Σ γ^(m-t) r(s_{m-D}, a_{m-D}) | I_t ]`. While the optimal *rewards* may differ, the *optimal policies* for the original and modified MDPs are shown to be equivalent. This simplification is critical because computing the conditional expectation `E[r(s_t, a_t)|I_t]` is computationally expensive, whereas the modified reward directly depends on states and actions observed `D` steps in the past, simplifying calculations.

**Delay-Resolved Deep Q-Network (DRDQN) Algorithm:**
The paper proposes DRDQN, a model-free reinforcement learning algorithm built upon these theoretical equivalences:
- **State Augmentation:** The primary modification to a vanilla DQN is the state augmentation. Instead of the state being just the current observation, it becomes an "information state" `I_t`.
 - For **constant delays**, `I_t` is formed by concatenating the current observed state with a fixed-length history of pending actions.
 - For **stochastic delays**, `I_t` includes the most recent observed state, the time instant it was observed, and a history of pending actions. To handle variable delay lengths, a maximum allowable dimension for `I_t` is set, and the action buffer is zero-padded with "no-action" tokens (`∅`) when the actual delay is less than the maximum.
- **Function Approximation:** To circumvent the exponential increase in state space that plagues tabular methods (`O(|S|*|A|^d)`), DRDQN utilizes deep neural networks as function approximators. By concatenating the action buffer with the state observation as input to the neural network, the input space scales linearly with delay (`O(|S| + d|A|)`), making it computationally tractable.
- **Q-Learning Updates:** The Q-learning updates proceed as in a standard DQN, but operating on the augmented information state `I_t`. The simplified reward structure ensures that the target Q-values can be estimated efficiently.

**Experimental Setup:**
The DRDQN algorithm is benchmarked on a variety of environments:
- **W-Maze:** A 7x11 grid world used to illustrate the exploding state space issue and the benefit of neural networks over tabular methods.
- **OpenAI Gym Environments:** Acrobot-v1, MountainCar-v0, and CartPole-v0, modified to incorporate both constant and stochastic observation and action delays.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against vanilla DQN, a memoryless delay-Q agent, and Delayed DQN which uses forward models.
- **Metrics:** Average rewards over 1 million training steps (across 10 runs) and runtime are used to assess performance and computational overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded both theoretical and empirical findings regarding the handling of stochastic delays in Reinforcement Learning.

**Theoretical Findings:**
- **Formal Definitions of Delayed MDPs:** The paper successfully formalized Constant Delay Markov Decision Processes (CDMDPs) and Stochastic Delay Markov Decision Processes (SDMDPs), providing a structured framework for analyzing environments with various types of delays.
- **Equivalence of Action and Observation Delays (Lemma 1):** It was theoretically demonstrated that action delays are functionally equivalent to observation delays from the perspective of an agent making optimal decisions. This implies that a unified approach can address both scenarios.
- **Equivalence of SDMDP to Undelayed MDP with Simplified Cost (Theorem 1):** The work established that an SDMDP can be reduced to an equivalent undelayed MDP where the reward structure is significantly simplified. Instead of computing a complex conditional expectation `E[r(s_t, a_t)|I_t]`, the equivalent MDP's reward depends on a past state and action `r(s_{m-D}, a_{m-D})`. This simplification is critical for computational efficiency, as it maintains the same optimal policy despite potentially altering the absolute optimal reward.

**Empirical Findings:**
- **Mitigation of Exploding State Space:**
 - On the W-Maze environment, tabular Q-learning with state augmentation performed well for smaller delays but exhibited significant performance degradation and increased computational expense for larger delays due to the exponential growth of the state space (`S × A^d`).
 - In contrast, when using neural networks as function approximators (DRDQN), the algorithm effectively handled the augmented state. By concatenating the action buffer with the state as input, the computational complexity scaled linearly with delay (`|S| + d|A|`), leading to near-optimal performance across a wider range of delays (Figure 5).
- **Performance with Constant and Stochastic Observation Delays:**
 - The DRDQN algorithm demonstrated robust performance across Acrobot-v1, MountainCar-v0, and CartPole-v0 environments with both constant and stochastic observation delays.
 - For constant delays up to 10 steps, DRDQN converged to near-optimal policies, achieving average rewards comparable to or better than vanilla DQN in undelayed settings (Figure 7).
 - For stochastic delays (uniformly sampled between 0 and 10), DRDQN also maintained near-optimal performance, demonstrating its ability to generalize across varying delay values without requiring explicit delay information at each step.
- **Performance with Constant Action Delays:**
 - DRDQN performed consistently well in environments with constant action delays, often outperforming or matching the performance of baselines like Delayed DQN (which uses forward models) and vanilla DQN (Figure 8).
 - On Acrobot-v1, DRDQN achieved performance similar to Delayed DQN for large delays.
 - On MountainCar-v0 and CartPole-v0, Delayed DQN sometimes struggled due to challenges in learning accurate forward models, leading to worse performance compared to DRDQN, which does not require model learning.
- **Equivalence of Action and Observation Delays (Empirical Verification):**
 - Empirical tests on Acrobot-v1 confirmed Lemma 1, showing that DRDQN achieved similar asymptotic reward values for both action delays and observation delays across various delay magnitudes (Figure 10).
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - DRDQN exhibited minimal computational overhead. Its runtime was comparable to that of vanilla DQN, even for large delays (Figure 9a and 9b).
 - In contrast, Delayed DQN, which relies on learning and repeatedly applying forward models, incurred significantly longer runtimes, especially as delays increased. This demonstrates a key advantage of the model-free DRDQN approach in terms of computational resources.

Overall, the main findings indicate that the proposed Delay-Resolved RL framework, specifically DRDQN, provides a theoretically grounded and empirically effective approach to address both constant and stochastic delays in RL, achieving high performance with reduced computational cost compared to certain model-based or model-learning baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several contributions that have implications for the development and application of Reinforcement Learning algorithms in real-world settings.

**Theoretical Contributions:**
- **Formal Framework for Delayed MDPs:** The formal definitions of CDMDPs and SDMDPs provide a clearer conceptual foundation for studying and solving RL problems with delays.
- **Simplified Cost Structure for Equivalence:** The derivation showing that delayed MDPs can be transformed into equivalent undelayed MDPs with a simplified cost structure is a significant theoretical advancement. This simplification removes the need for complex and computationally intensive conditional expectation calculations (O(d|S|^2)) often encountered in traditional state augmentation approaches, making these problems more tractable for model-free methods.
- **Unified Treatment of Delays:** Lemma 1, demonstrating the functional equivalence of action and observation delays, simplifies the problem space. It suggests that a single underlying framework can address both types of delays, streamlining algorithm design.

**Algorithmic and Practical Impact:**
- **Enabling Model-Free RL in Delayed Environments:** By providing a computationally efficient means to convert delayed problems into standard MDPs, the Delay-Resolved RL framework extends the applicability of powerful model-free DRL algorithms, like DQN, to environments with inherent delays. This is crucial as many real-world systems are too complex for accurate model learning.
- **Addressing State-Space Explosion with DRL:** The use of deep neural networks to manage the augmented information state effectively addresses the "exploding state space" problem that renders tabular methods infeasible for even moderate delays. The linear scaling of input space with delay (O(|S| + d|A|)) makes the approach practical for larger delays.
- **Robustness to Stochastic Delays:** The DRDQN algorithm's ability to perform near-optimally under both constant and stochastic delays, without requiring explicit knowledge of delay values at each step, enhances its versatility. This is particularly relevant for real-world scenarios where delays are often unpredictable.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The DRDQN algorithm demonstrates significantly lower computational overhead compared to model-based or forward-model-based approaches, achieving comparable or superior performance in less training time. This makes it a more scalable and resource-friendly option for real-time applications.

**Potential Future Applications:**
The framework lays a foundation for future work in several practical domains where delays are critical:
- **Robotics:** Accounting for actuator and motor delays, enabling more precise and responsive control in real-time robotic systems.
- **Supply Chain Management:** Optimizing decision-making in the presence of shipping and information delays.
- **Medical Systems:** Improving decision support systems where diagnostic results or treatment effects may be significantly delayed.
- **Network Control:** Designing robust control strategies for communication networks with varying transmission delays.

In summary, this work advances the theoretical understanding of delayed RL problems and offers a practical, computationally efficient, and robust model-free deep reinforcement learning solution. By overcoming key limitations of existing methods, it broadens the scope for applying powerful RL techniques to a wider range of real-world problems characterized by various forms of stochastic and constant delays.
