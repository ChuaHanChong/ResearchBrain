# Training Agents Inside of Scalable World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind's Dreamer 4 introduces a scalable and efficient world model that enables learning complex control tasks by reinforcement learning entirely within imagination. It achieves the first-ever offline acquisition of diamonds in Minecraft, reaching a 0.7% success rate while performing real-time interactive inference at 21 frames per second on a single GPU.

## Problem

- Prior world models struggled to accurately predict complex object interactions and game mechanics in highly dynamic and open-ended environments like Minecraft.
- Large generative models, while powerful, were often computationally too expensive for real-time, iterative imagination training needed for efficient reinforcement learning.
- Learning sophisticated, long-horizon behaviors from fixed, offline datasets remained a major challenge due to the lack of interactive exploration and feedback.
- Agents needed to efficiently leverage vast amounts of unlabeled video data to learn general world knowledge and action conditioning with minimal labeled examples.

## Method

- **Dreamer 4** utilizes a three-phase training approach: world model pretraining, agent finetuning with task-conditioned policy/reward heads, and imagination training using reinforcement learning.
- A novel architecture combines a ==causal tokenizer== for efficient video compression with an interactive dynamics model (world model) built on an ==efficient transformer architecture==.
- Key innovations in the dynamics model include ==shortcut forcing== with ==x-prediction== and ==x-space loss== for stable, high-quality, and fast long-duration rollouts, requiring only K=4 sampling steps per frame.

## Results

- Dreamer 4 is the first agent to obtain diamonds in Minecraft purely from offline data, achieving a **0.7%** success rate in 60-minute episodes, significantly outperforming prior state-of-the-art offline agents.
- The world model accurately predicts complex Minecraft interactions and operates at **21 FPS** on a single H100 GPU, surpassing Minecraft's tick rate and prior world models in fidelity and context length (9.6 seconds).
- It learns accurate action conditioning from just **100 hours** of labeled data (out of **2541 hours** total) and generalizes this knowledge to visually distinct Minecraft dimensions (Nether/End) with high fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Imagination-based reinforcement learning with a scalable world model can effectively learn complex, long-horizon behaviors from purely offline datasets, even in challenging environments like Minecraft.
> - High-fidelity world models capable of simulating precise object interactions can achieve real-time interactive inference, making them practical for iterative agent training.
> - Action conditioning can be learned with high data efficiency, predominantly from unlabeled videos, demonstrating a powerful form of action generalization to visually novel environments.
> - Specific architectural choices like x-prediction, x-space loss, ramp loss weighting, and efficient transformer components (e.g., factorized attention, GQA) are critical for robust and efficient generation in world models.
