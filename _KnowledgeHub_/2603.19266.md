# Probing to Refine: Reinforcement Distillation of LLMs via Explanatory Inversion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Arizona State University, University of Virginia, and University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill developed ExGRPO, a reinforcement distillation framework that enables smaller language models to acquire robust reasoning capabilities from larger teachers. The method achieved an average accuracy of 82.54% for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 67.19% for Gemma-7B-it on held-in reasoning datasets, showing an average improvement of 2.94% and 1.79% respectively over state-of-the-art distillation baselines, while also enhancing generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Distilled large language models (LLMs) often suffer from superficial pattern memorization, leading to limited generalization and failures on out-of-distribution (OOD) data or inverse problems (reversal curse).
- Existing knowledge distillation (KD) methods, primarily based on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), struggle to instill a deep conceptual understanding of reasoning processes in student models.
- Current data augmentation techniques for distillation often encourage learning new directional mappings rather than fostering a genuine grasp of underlying principles.

## Method

- Introduced **Explanatory Inversion** (EI), a cognitive science-inspired method to generate diverse 'explanatory probes' that systematically deconstruct and challenge the teacher's reasoning.
- Curated a high-quality training dataset from EI-generated probes using consistency and rejective filtering to focus on impactful learning examples.
- Developed Explanatory GRPO (ExGRPO), a reinforcement distillation framework that combines SFT warm-up with an adapted Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to guide student models through multi-turn explanatory dialogues, incentivized by a novel 'Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus'.

## Results

- ExGRPO improved average accuracy by **4.55%** for Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct and **20.39%** for Gemma-**7B**-it over their zero-shot performance on 8 held-in reasoning datasets, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art distillation baselines.
- Demonstrated superior generalization on out-of-distribution datasets, outperforming RevThink by an average of **4.89%** (Gemma-**7B**) and **3.28%** (Qwen2.5-**7B**), and showed improved robustness against the 'reversal curse' (e.g., +**3.82%** for Qwen on GSM8K-Reversal).
- Achieved higher data sample and token efficiency, with ExGRPO using only **10%** of the data sometimes surpassing SFT trained on the full dataset, indicating more impactful learning per token and sample.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted 'explanatory probes' can compel student models to articulate the underlying logic and principles of reasoning, moving beyond surface-level imitation.
> - Integrating reinforcement learning with a specific 'Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus' effectively guides student models to internalize coherent, multi-step reasoning processes from teacher models.
> - A synergistic approach, combining initial supervised fine-tuning for stability with subsequent reinforcement learning for refinement, is crucial for distilling robust and generalizable reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a persistent challenge in the field of large language model (LLM) research: effectively distilling complex reasoning capabilities from large, computationally intensive teacher models into smaller, more efficient student models. Existing knowledge distillation (KD) methods frequently lead to student models that primarily memorize superficial patterns rather than acquiring a deep conceptual understanding. This limitation manifests as subpar generalization, particularly when exposed to out-of-distribution (OOD) data or when confronted with "reversal curse" scenarios, where models fail on inverse problems despite mastering forward ones.

Prior research in KD for LLMs has focused on various aspects, including aligning logits or hidden states, and more recently, distilling reasoning capabilities by training students on teacher-generated outputs like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales. Some data augmentation techniques, such as "reverse thinking" methods like RevThink, attempt to mitigate generalization issues by creating symmetric samples (A-to-Q reasoning). However, these approaches often still encourage learning another directional mapping rather than fostering a genuine grasp of underlying principles.

This paper positions itself as an advancement beyond these limitations by identifying that generalization deficiencies are amplified in distilled LLMs. It proposes a framework that shifts the focus from mere imitation or mechanical reversal to instilling a deeper conceptual understanding and coherent reasoning process. It also integrates reinforcement learning (RL) into the distillation process, moving beyond its traditional role in post-training, to guide students towards internalizing complex reasoning structures revealed by the teacher.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel distillation framework that enables smaller language models to acquire robust and generalizable reasoning capabilities from larger LLM teachers, moving beyond superficial pattern memorization. The authors identify the following specific motivations and objectives:

- **Address Superficial Pattern Memorization:** Distilled models often exhibit limited generalization because current supervised fine-tuning (SFT) based distillation methods encourage students to mimic fixed teacher outputs. This leads to memorization of patterns that are fragile under distribution shifts, exemplified by the "reversal curse." The motivation is to foster a deeper, conceptual understanding of underlying logic.
- **Improve Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** A core limitation of existing distilled LLMs is their poor out-of-distribution generalization. The research aims to enhance the student models' ability to apply learned reasoning to novel or perturbed tasks, rather than failing when task structures or data distributions deviate slightly from training.
- **Instill Deeper Conceptual Understanding through Explanatory Probes:** Drawing inspiration from cognitive science, the authors motivate the development of "Explanatory Inversion (EI)." The objective of EI is to generate targeted "explanatory probes" that compel the student model to articulate the *why* and *how* behind an answer, forcing it to engage with the underlying logic, assumptions, and principles, rather than just memorizing input-output pairs or fixed reasoning sequences.
- **Promote Coherent Reasoning Processes with Reinforcement Learning:** To overcome the intrinsic limitations of SFT, which does not inherently encourage exploration or the development of generalizable strategies, the authors aim to leverage reinforcement learning. Specifically, the objective is to design a novel RL algorithm, Explanatory GRPO (ExGRPO), that guides the student model to internalize the complex reasoning structures revealed by the teacher through EI. This is achieved not only by rewarding final answer correctness but also by explicitly rewarding the student for maintaining a coherent reasoning process across a sequence of explanatory probes.
- **Enhance Training Efficiency:** The framework also seeks to improve sample and token efficiency, allowing student models to achieve higher performance with less training data or fewer computational resources.

In summary, the work is motivated by the recognized generalization failures of distilled LLMs and aims to construct a distillation paradigm that emphasizes deep conceptual understanding and coherent multi-step reasoning through a synergistic combination of cognitively-inspired data augmentation and structured reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, ExGRPO, is a three-stage process designed to distill reasoning capabilities from large language models (LLMs) into smaller student models.

#### 3.1. Explanatory Inversion (EI): Crafting Probes for Deeper Reasoning

The initial stage involves generating a diverse set of "explanatory probes." Given an original question-answer pair `D(Q) = (Q, A, R_T)` where `R_T` is the teacher's Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, EI applies *N* (set to 10 in this paper) distinct categories of transformation rules. These rules `F = {f_1..., f_N}` are inspired by cognitive science and systematically deconstruct, challenge, and explore the underlying logic, assumptions, and principles within `R_T`. Examples of rules include:

- **R1. Counterfactual Scenario Generation:** Creates probes exploring outcomes if a premise were altered (e.g., "How would entailment change if 'most' rather than 'all' survived?").
- **R2. Explanatory Challenge:** Poses questions demanding justification for specific logical transitions (e.g., "Explain the logical steps connecting 'all survived' to 'no children were killed'").

This process yields up to *N* augmented data tuples `D_cand(Q) = F(D(Q)) = {(Q_aug_i, A_aug_i, R_aug_T,i)}_i=1^N`.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Data Curation

A high-quality dataset `D_EI` is curated from the EI-generated probes through a two-step filtering process:

1. **EI Consistency Filter:** For each candidate tuple `d_k = (Q_aug_k, A_aug_T,k, R_aug_T,k)`, this filter ensures that the augmented probe and its teacher-generated reasoning do not detract from the original problem's solution. A tuple passes if the teacher model, when prompted with `Q_aug_k` and its reasoning/answer, still correctly predicts the original answer `A` for `Q`.
2. **Rejective Filtering:** This step removes original questions `Q` (and their associated probes) that are deemed "too easy" or "too hard" for a baseline student model (`S_base`) to learn effectively. "Too easy" means `S_base` correctly answers all EI-consistent probes for `Q`. "Too hard" means `S_base` correctly answers fewer than a predefined threshold `τ_hard` of the probes. This aims to create a training set where learning from EI probes is most impactful.

The final dataset `D_EI` comprises all filtered tuples `(Q_aug_k, A_aug_T,k, R_aug_T,k)`.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Cold Start

The student model, with parameters `θ`, is initially fine-tuned on the curated `D_EI` dataset for `P` epochs. For each tuple `(Q_aug_i, A_aug_T,i, R_aug_T,i)`, the target output `T_i` is the concatenation of the teacher's reasoning and answer `[R_aug_T,i || A_aug_T,i]`. The SFT objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood loss of generating `T_i` given `Q_aug_i`. This stage serves as a warm-up, providing initial alignment with teacher insights and preparing the student model for the diverse reasoning challenges generated by EI.

#### 3.4. Stage 3: Reinforcement Distillation via Explanatory GRPO (ExGRPO)

Following SFT, the student model's policy `π_θ` is refined using the ExGRPO algorithm, which adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to leverage structured interactions from EI.

#### 3.4.1. Interaction Protocol with Randomized Explanatory Probe Sampling

For each original question `Q` in an ExGRPO training batch, a *k*-turn explanatory dialogue is constructed:

- **Probe Selection:** *k* distinct EI rule types are randomly sampled (e.g., *k*=5) to generate a sequence of *k* augmented probes `(Q_aug_1..., Q_aug_k)`.
- **Sequential Interaction:** These probes are presented one by one. For each turn, the student receives the current probe `Q_aug_j`, with the dialogue history forming the context. The student generates reasoning and an answer. Each intermediate turn is supervised using an auxiliary SFT loss (`L_SFT-aux`).
- **Final Response Generation:** After *k* turns, the student is prompted with the original question `Q` (enriched by the dialogue context) to produce its final reasoning and answer. This sequence constitutes a **Full Dialogue (Scenario A)**.
- For comparison in reward calculation, a **Partial Dialogue (Scenario B)** is also generated for a subset of instances, involving only `k'` probes (`k'` < `k`) before answering `Q`. A group of `G` trajectories is generated for each scenario.

#### 3.4.2. Rule-Based Reward Design for ExGRPO

Each trajectory `g` receives a rule-based reward:

- **Outcome Reward (`R_outcome`):** `1` if the student's final answer to `Q` matches the ground truth `A`, `0` otherwise. This is the base reward: `R_base = R_outcome`.
- **Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus (`r_dsu`):** This novel bonus rewards the student if engaging in the full *k*-turn dialogue (Scenario A) leads to better average `R_base` outcomes than a partial `k'`-turn dialogue (Scenario B). Specifically, `r_dsu = δ` (a positive bonus value) if `P_full > ν · P_partial` (where `P_full` and `P_partial` are average `R_base` for Scenario A and B, respectively, and `ν ≥ 1.0` is a margin), and `0` otherwise. This incentivizes effective use of the entire probe sequence.
- **Total Augmented Rule-Based Reward (`R_total`):** For Scenario A trajectories, `R_total = R_base + r_dsu` if `R_outcome = 1` and `r_dsu` is awarded, otherwise `R_total = R_base`.

#### 3.4.3. Advantage Computation and Policy Update

Advantages `U^(g)` are computed by normalizing `R_total^(m)` within the group. The policy `π_θ` is updated using the ExGRPO objective `L_ExGRPO(θ)`, which is a clipped surrogate objective that incorporates importance sampling and a KL regularization term `βD_KL(π_θ || π_ref)` to prevent large policy shifts from a reference policy (the SFT-trained student model).

#### 3.4.4. Imitation-Based Policy Regularization (`L_SFT-aux`)

To guide student reasoning during the EI turns, an auxiliary SFT loss `L_SFT-aux` is used. It encourages imitation of the teacher's intermediate responses `R_aug_T,j` during the *k*-turn dialogue. This loss is combined with or alternated with `L_ExGRPO`.

#### 3.4.5. Theoretical Guarantee

Theorem 3.1 states that if `r_dsu` is applied under the specified condition, the ExGRPO policy update with clipped importance sampling ensures that the expected `R_outcome` of the new policy is greater than or equal to that of the previous policy, with strict inequality when `r_dsu` is positive for a non-zero-probability set of trajectories.

#### 3.5. Inference Procedure

During inference, the trained student model generates reasoning and the final answer for an input `Q` in a single pass. The multi-turn probing dialogues, random rule sampling, and reward computations are exclusively training-time mechanisms, not impacting inference latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the ExGRPO framework yields substantial improvements across various metrics and tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Significant Accuracy Gains:** On an average of 8 held-in reasoning datasets, ExGRPO achieved 82.54% accuracy for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 67.19% for Gemma-7B-it.
 - For Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, this represented an average 4.55% improvement over its zero-shot performance and a 2.94% improvement over the state-of-the-art distillation baseline (On-Policy Distillation).
 - For Gemma-7B-it, the improvement was more pronounced, yielding an average 20.39% increase over its zero-shot performance and a 1.79% improvement over the state-of-the-art baseline.
- **Teacher Model Enhancement:** The EI-based data augmentation alone improved the Gemini-1.5-Pro teacher’s zero-shot performance by 2.22%, suggesting that structured reasoning tasks through EI can benefit even powerful LLMs.
- **Consistent Superiority:** ExGRPO consistently outperformed standard knowledge distillation (e.g., Distill Step-by-Step) and strong data augmentation strategies (e.g., RevThink) across various datasets (Commonsense, Math, Tabular, NLI, Logical Reasoning).

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation study investigated the contribution of SFT warm-up, the `L_SFT-aux` regularization, and the `r_dsu` bonus:

- **Importance of SFT Warm-up:** Reinforcement Learning from a "cold start" (without SFT warm-up) with only `R_base` led to significant performance degradation (e.g., -62.00% for Qwen, -34.46% for Gemma), indicating that SFT is crucial for stabilizing RL in distillation.
- **Effectiveness of `L_SFT-aux`:** Adding the imitation-based `L_SFT-aux` to cold-start RL recovered substantial performance (e.g., +9.53% for Qwen, +31.15% for Gemma), demonstrating its role in guiding intermediate reasoning.
- **Critical Role of `r_dsu`:** The `r_dsu` bonus, which rewards coherent use of the multi-turn explanatory dialogue, provided an additional performance boost (e.g., +1.31% for Qwen, +5.39% for Gemma) on top of SFT and `L_SFT-aux`. This indicates that `r_dsu` effectively incentivizes deeper internalization of reasoning.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics

During ExGRPO training, both the average base outcome reward (`R_base`) and the Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus (`r_dsu`) showed synchronized upward trends and stabilization. This suggests that the policy successfully learned to achieve correct outcomes by effectively leveraging the structured explanatory probes.

#### 4.4. Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Datasets

ExGRPO demonstrated superior generalization capabilities on four held-out OOD datasets (BoolQ, OpenbookQA, e-SNLI, GSM8K-Reversal):

- **Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** For Gemma-7B, ExGRPO outperformed RevThink by an average of 4.89%. For Qwen2.5-7B, it surpassed RevThink by an average of 3.28%.
- **Reversal Curse Mitigation:** Notably, on GSM8K-Reversal (a dataset specifically designed to test the reversal curse), ExGRPO showed strong individual gains (e.g., +3.82% for Qwen), indicating improved robustness against this type of generalization failure.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Study

- **Data Sample Efficiency:** ExGRPO consistently achieved higher accuracy than standard SFT across all training data fractions. In several cases (e.g., SQA, CSQA), ExGRPO with only 10% of the data surpassed SFT trained on the full dataset, indicating more effective learning from limited, EI-augmented data.
- **Token Efficiency:** ExGRPO outperformed the general trendline of baseline methods when comparing average test accuracy against average training token count per sample. This suggests that the richer interactions fostered by EI and the RL framework lead to more impactful learning per token.

#### 4.6. Case Study

A case study on the MATH dataset illustrated that while a base model might fail on complex reasoning by repeating template-like statements, EI probes reformulate the task into explanation-seeking questions. These probes, coupled with ExGRPO’s rewards for coherent reasoning across dialogue turns, guided the student model to recover the correct logic and solution, demonstrating a shift from pattern following to true constraint reasoning.

#### 4.7. Compatibility with Open-Source Teacher Model

Experiments using Llama-3-70B-Instruct as an open-source teacher model confirmed ExGRPO's robustness. Distilling into Gemma-7B with Llama-3 still yielded an average improvement of 18.5% over the zero-shot baseline. Performance was comparable to that achieved with Gemini-1.5-Pro, indicating that the gains arise primarily from the ExGRPO algorithm itself rather than exclusive reliance on a proprietary teacher.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel and effective framework for distilling robust reasoning capabilities from large language models into smaller, more efficient student models. The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations in Distillation:** The paper pioneers the identification and systematic mitigation of amplified generalization limitations and superficial pattern memorization in distilled LLMs. By moving beyond simple imitation, it fosters a deeper conceptual understanding essential for reliable AI.
- **Novel Methodological Contributions:**
 - **Explanatory Inversion (EI):** This cognitively-inspired data augmentation technique provides a systematic way to generate targeted "explanatory probes," compelling student models to articulate underlying logic rather than merely memorizing outputs. This is a significant advancement over prior data augmentation methods.
 - **Explanatory GRPO (ExGRPO) with Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus:** The integration of a tailored reinforcement learning algorithm into the distillation process, coupled with a novel reward mechanism, is a core contribution. The Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus explicitly rewards coherent reasoning across multi-turn explanatory dialogues, effectively guiding the student toward internalizing complex reasoning structures.
- **Empirical Validation:** Extensive evaluations across 12 datasets, covering diverse reasoning tasks, consistently demonstrate significant improvements in reasoning capability, robustness, and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. The results show substantial gains over both zero-shot baselines and state-of-the-art knowledge distillation methods, particularly for weaker student models.
- **Efficiency Improvements:** The framework offers notable data sample and token efficiency, allowing distilled models to achieve strong performance with less training data and more effective use of computational resources, which is crucial for practical deployment.
- **Reproducibility and Accessibility:** The demonstration of compatibility with open-source teacher models (Llama-3-70B) indicates that the method is not dependent on proprietary technologies, making it more widely accessible and reproducible within the research community.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted. By enabling smaller models to perform complex reasoning with greater reliability and efficiency, it can significantly lower the computational and financial barriers to deploying advanced AI capabilities. This could expand the use of sophisticated LLMs in resource-constrained environments and democratize access to AI for applications in education, scientific research, and various industries. Furthermore, the focus on deeper conceptual understanding rather than rote memorization represents a step towards developing more explainable and trustworthy AI systems.

**Ethical Considerations:** The authors acknowledge potential risks, including the propagation of biases and factual inaccuracies from teacher models, and the potential misuse of more powerful and accessible reasoning models for generating misinformation. They advocate for continued research into bias detection and mitigation, thorough fairness and safety evaluations, and responsible development practices to ensure the positive societal impact of this research direction.
