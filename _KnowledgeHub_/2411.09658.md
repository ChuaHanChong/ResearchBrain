# Motion Before Action: Diffusing Object Motion as Manipulation Condition

> [!summary] Summary
> A new module, Motion Before Action (MBA), enables robots to infer future object 6D pose sequences and use this predicted motion to guide action generation. Integrating MBA into diffusion-based policies improves manipulation performance by an average of 14.2% over Diffusion Policy and 6.2% over 3D Diffusion Policy in simulations, and enhances robustness and learning efficiency across various real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies often rely on direct visual observation-to-action mapping, leading to an over-reliance on memorized environmental features.
- This over-reliance results in poor generalization when objects exhibit significant pose variations in complex, real-world scenarios.
- Existing object-centric methods, particularly flow-based approaches, can suffer from a "vision-motion gap" and heuristic transformations, limiting adaptability and precision.

## Method

- A **Motion Before Action** (MBA) module is proposed, employing a cascaded diffusion process to decouple object motion inference from action generation.
- The first diffusion model predicts future object 6D pose sequences (p(M|O)) directly from visual observations.
- A second diffusion model then generates robot action sequences (p(A|M, O)), explicitly conditioned on both the original observations and the predicted object motion. This module is designed as a plug-and-play component, integrating seamlessly with existing diffusion-based visuomotor policies.

## Results

- Integrated MBA policies demonstrated improved performance in simulations, achieving an average **14.2%** higher success rate over Diffusion Policy (DP) and **6.2%** over 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) across 57 tasks.
- MBA-augmented policies exhibited higher learning efficiency, reaching peak task success rates earlier, and showed increased robustness with reduced standard deviation in task execution across various benchmarks.
- In real-world settings, MBA consistently outperformed baselines in challenging tasks such as Cut Clay, Put Bread into Pot, Open Drawer, and Pour Balls, showing enhanced precision and effective guidance for complex 6-DoF movements and object interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly reasoning about future object 6D pose sequences, analogous to human cognitive processes, can provide intrinsic guidance for robot action generation.
> - Utilizing object 6D pose as a direct representation for object motion reduces ambiguity and leverages mathematical consistency with robot end-effector poses, unlike indirect flow-based methods.
> - A cascaded diffusion model can effectively capture the probabilistic distributions of both object motion and robot actions, allowing for robust generation of diverse and complex sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robotic imitation learning, particularly focusing on visuomotor policies for manipulation tasks. It addresses a recognized limitation in many existing robot policies that predominantly rely on direct observation-to-action mapping. These policies often develop an overreliance on memorizing environmental features, which can hinder their generalization capabilities when encountering significant object pose variations in real-world scenarios.

The paper contrasts its approach with established imitation learning methods, including behavior cloning and the various action generation strategies (single-step, multi-step, unimodal, and multimodal action heads). It specifically highlights diffusion models as a robust method for capturing the diversity and complexity of action sequences, a characteristic it also applies to object motion sequences. The proposed MBA module is designed to integrate with existing policies that utilize diffusion action heads, positioning it as an enhancement rather than a standalone policy.

Furthermore, this work engages with the area of object-centric manipulation learning. Prior approaches in this domain have focused either on scene understanding through structured object representations or on action-oriented learning, such as affordance learning or object flow-based methods. The paper critically evaluates flow-based methods, noting that while they utilize object motion indirectly, the translation from visual flow to robot actions can introduce ambiguity due to a "vision-motion gap" and often depends on heuristic transformations, which may limit adaptability. By contrast, this research proposes using object 6D pose as a direct and mathematically consistent representation for object motion, thereby aiming to reduce training demands and enhance interpretability. It differentiates itself from a contemporaneous work (Spot) that infers robot actions heuristically from object trajectories by emphasizing the generation of actions directly conditioned on object motion. This distinction underscores the paper's contribution to improving the robustness and kinematic consistency of robot manipulation through a more direct and reasoned approach to object dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from observations in human physiology, which indicate that humans effectively process complex object motion information to execute actions. In contrast, many current robot manipulation policies primarily map visual observations directly to actions. This can lead to an overreliance on environmental cues, where models may memorize observation features rather than inferring and reasoning about the underlying object motion patterns. This memorization often results in poor generalization when objects exhibit significant pose shifts, a common challenge in real-world robotic applications.

To address these limitations and enhance robot manipulation capabilities, the authors established several key objectives:

1. **Endow Robots with Human-like Reasoning**: The central objective is to equip robots with the ability to infer future object motion from visual observations. This capability is intended to allow the robot to derive intrinsic information (such as object poses and motions) from a scene, facilitating an observation-to-action mapping that aligns more closely with human cognitive processes.
2. **Guide Action Generation with Object Motion**: Following the inference of object motion, the research aims to use this predicted motion as a explicit condition to guide the generation of robot action sequences. This decoupled approach is hypothesized to lead to more accurate and goal-driven action predictions.
3. **Establish Mathematical and Physical Consistency**: The authors recognized that representing object motion using 6D pose aligns mathematically with the robot's end-effector pose, suggesting a shared distribution. They aimed to leverage this consistency, along with the physical kinematic relationships between object and robot poses, to facilitate learning.
4. **Develop a Flexible and Integrable Module**: A practical objective was to design a module, named Motion Before Action (MBA), that could be seamlessly integrated as a plug-and-play component into existing robotic manipulation policies, particularly those employing diffusion action heads. This modular design seeks to enhance the versatility and efficiency of various robotic manipulation contexts without requiring a complete overhaul of established policies.
5. **Improve Robustness and Generalization**: By incorporating explicit object motion reasoning, the research aimed to improve the robustness and kinematic consistency of the policy's observation-to-action mapping, thereby enhancing its ability to generalize across diverse manipulation tasks and environments, especially those involving articulated, soft, or rigid bodies, tool use, and complex 6-DoF movements.
6. **Accelerate Policy Learning**: The authors also investigated whether providing object pose information, considered a more learnable and easily encoded feature representation, could lead to more efficient policy learning and faster convergence during training.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond mere visual memorization in robot learning by integrating explicit object motion reasoning, drawing inspiration from human cognition, and providing a flexible architectural enhancement for existing diffusion-based robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the Motion Before Action (MBA) module, designed to enable robots to concurrently reason about object pose and robot action from observations. This is achieved by modeling the joint conditional distribution `p(M, A|O)`, which is decoupled into two sequential processes: `p(M|O)` for object motion generation and `p(A|M, O)` for robot action generation guided by the sampled motion and observations.

The MBA module is structured as a cascade of two diffusion processes:

**A. Object Motion Generation (`p(M|O)`)**
The first stage focuses on predicting future object pose sequences.
- **Object Representation**: Object motion `m_t` is represented as a 9D vector, concatenating 3D translation and 6D rotation (using a continuous representation such as 6D rotation to avoid singularities), ensuring alignment with robot action representations used in baseline policies.
- **Diffusion Process**: Given current observation features `O_t` (encoded by the existing policy's perception module), a diffusion model generates the object pose sequence `M_{t:t+T_m}` for `T_m` future steps. This process begins by sampling noise from a Gaussian distribution, and a denoising network, `ε_ϕ(M_k, O, k)`, progressively removes this noise over `K` diffusion steps to recover a clean object pose sequence `M_0`.
- **Training**: The denoising network `ε_ϕ` is trained using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss. It learns to predict the noise added to a ground-truth object trajectory `M_0` (obtained from a Motion Capture (MoCap) system during demonstration collection) at a random diffusion step `k`.

**B. Robot Action Generation under Object Motion Guidance (`p(A|M, O)`)**
The second stage generates robot actions, conditioned on both observations and the predicted object motion.
- **Action Representation**: Robot action `a_t` is defined as a 10D vector, combining 3D end-effector translation, 6D end-effector rotation, and 1D gripper width. A diffusion process is used to generate the action sequence `A_{t:t+T_a}` for `T_a` future steps.
- **Conditional Diffusion**: The action noise prediction network, `ε_φ(A_k, M, O, k)`, receives both the observation features `O` (from the original policy's perception module) and the *predicted* object pose features `M` (from the first MBA module) as joint conditions. These features are concatenated to guide the denoising process. The network progressively denoises a noisy action sequence `A_k` to recover `A_0`.
- **Feature Encoding**: Given the physical and mathematical similarities between object poses and robot end-effector poses, a similar encoding network (MLP layers) is used for their feature representation. The object pose sequence is unfolded and processed to yield the object pose feature vector `M`.
- **Training**: The action denoising network `ε_φ` is trained with an MSE loss, predicting the noise added to ground-truth action sequences `A_0` obtained from expert demonstrations.

**C. Execution Process**
During inference:
1. The robot's current observation `O_t` is encoded.
2. The MBA module's first diffusion process predicts an object pose sequence `M_{t:t+T_m}` based on `O_t`. Importantly, this step does not require a MoCap system during deployment.
3. The predicted object pose sequence `M` is then used, alongside the observation features `O_t`, to condition the second diffusion process within MBA, which generates the robot action sequence `A_{t:t+T_a}`.
4. The robot executes `T'_a` steps of the predicted action sequence (where `T'_a ≤ T_a`).
5. The environment state and observations are updated, and the cycle repeats.
The paper notes that `T_m ≥ T_a` is maintained to adhere to kinematic principles.

**D. Demonstrations and Baselines**
- **Demonstrations**: For training, the system uses scripted policies for MetaWorld, VRL3 for Adroit, and PPO for DexArt, with demonstration success rates ranging from 72.8% to nearly 100%. The number of demonstrations varies by benchmark (10 for Adroit/MetaWorld, 100 for DexArt), and 50 demonstrations for each real-world task (teleoperated with haptic devices). MoCap systems are used to collect ground-truth object motion data during demonstration collection.
- **Baselines**: MBA is integrated into representative 2D and 3D diffusion-based policies: Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) for simulation, and these plus RISE (a SOTA real-world policy) for real-world experiments. This "plug-and-play" integration allows for direct performance comparison against the vanilla baselines. All methods use the same expert demonstrations and training steps for fairness.

The methodological approach emphasizes the explicit modeling of object motion as an intermediate reasoning step, drawing an analogy to human cognition, and leveraging the capabilities of cascaded diffusion models for probabilistic generation of both motion and actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental results from both simulated and real-world environments to evaluate the effectiveness of the MBA module.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
The evaluation involved 57 tasks across three benchmarks: Adroit, DexArt, and MetaWorld (categorized into Easy, Medium, Hard, and Very Hard difficulty levels). MBA was integrated into Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) baselines.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness (Q1)**: MBA consistently demonstrated improved performance across most benchmarks. On average, MBA-augmented policies achieved a 14.2% increase in success rate over DP and a 6.2% increase over DP3. A general reduction in the average standard deviation of task execution was observed, indicating increased robustness. Significant improvements were noted in tasks with higher difficulty levels, such as MetaWorld Very Hard, where DP3 w. MBA improved from 49.0% to 86.8%, and DP w. MBA from 26.6% to 79.8%. The authors attribute this to MBA's accurate prediction of object pose for localization (for stationary objects) and the strong correlation between predicted object pose sequences and robot actions (for moving objects), providing robust guidance.
- **Accelerated Policy Learning (Q2)**: Learning curves indicated that policies integrated with MBA exhibited higher learning efficiency. They typically reached peak task test success rates at earlier training steps and maintained a more stable success rate at higher levels. This efficiency is attributed to object pose information providing a more learnable and easily encoded feature representation.

**B. Real-World Experiments**
Four challenging real-world tasks were used: Cut Clay (tool-use, contact-rich), Put Bread into Pot (soft body manipulation), Open Drawer (articulated object, high precision), and Pour Balls (6-DoF task). MBA was integrated into RISE, DP, and DP3 baselines.
- **Cut Clay**: RISE w. MBA showed notable improvements in cutting (70% to 90%) and separation (30% to 55%) stages, indicating effective guidance in multi-stage 6-DoF tasks requiring tool motion prediction. However, it still exhibited some redundant knife-grasp attempts, hypothesized to be due to error amplification for thin objects.
- **Put Bread into Pot**: RISE w. MBA outperformed vanilla RISE by 15% in success rate and reduced grasp attempts, demonstrating robustness in handling soft object deformation and accurate localization. Policies with higher base error rates still led to more attempts, suggesting error accumulation from the visual backbone.
- **Open Drawer**: MBA-augmented policies consistently outperformed baselines in grasping and pulling stages, confirming enhanced performance in tasks requiring precise object localization and fine manipulation in complex setups.
- **Pour Balls**: MBA achieved over 15% higher success rate compared to RISE and 30% compared to DP. It also poured balls with greater precision, indicating its ability to accurately capture the relationship between object pose variations and actions in intricate 6-DoF tasks involving non-cubic objects.

**C. Comparative Experiment to Flow-based Methods**
MBA was compared against ATM, a representative flow-based method, on three 2D policy-fit tasks.
- MBA generally outperformed ATM across most metrics (e.g., Put Bread into Pot success: 40% for ATM vs. 45% for MBA; Open Drawer success: 5% for ATM vs. 30% for MBA). This supports the hypothesis that using 6D pose as a direct motion representation provides higher quality action generation compared to 2D point flow, particularly in fine-grained or high-DoF manipulation tasks where the "vision-motion" gap is more pronounced.

**D. Inference Speed**
While effective, MBA introduced increased computational cost. DP with MBA required 197.50 ms inference time (excluding visual backbone processing), compared to 95.98 ms for vanilla DP and 105.85 ms for ATM. This is attributed to the computationally intensive denoising steps in the cascaded diffusion processes.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that integrating MBA significantly improves the performance, robustness, and learning efficiency of existing diffusion-based robotic manipulation policies across a wide spectrum of tasks in both simulated and real-world environments, validating its core hypothesis about reasoning over object motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Motion Before Action" (MBA) research introduces a paradigm for robotic imitation learning that holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning for Robotic Manipulation**: By explicitly inferring future object motion (6D pose sequences) as an intermediate step before generating robot actions, MBA imbues robotic policies with a form of human-like reasoning. This shift from direct observation-to-action mapping to a more structured "motion-before-action" process represents a conceptual advance that could make robotic behaviors more intelligent and robust.
2. **Improved Generalization and Robustness**: The consistent performance gains demonstrated across diverse and challenging simulated and real-world tasks, including articulated object manipulation, soft and rigid body handling, tool use, and 6-DoF tasks, indicate a substantial improvement in the generalization capabilities and robustness of robotic manipulation policies. This is particularly impactful for real-world deployment where objects and environments are dynamic and unpredictable.
3. **Flexible and Modular System Design**: MBA's design as a plug-and-play module for existing diffusion-based policies is a practical contribution. This modularity allows for easy integration into current and future policy architectures, potentially broadening its applicability and impact across the robotic learning community without requiring extensive re-engineering of baseline systems.
4. **Addressing the "Vision-Motion Gap"**: By employing 6D object pose as a direct representation for motion, the work effectively addresses limitations observed in some flow-based methods, specifically the "vision-motion gap." This direct representation reduces ambiguity and facilitates clearer kinematic relationships between perceived objects and executed actions, leading to more precise and effective manipulation.
5. **Accelerated Learning Efficiency**: The observed acceleration in policy learning and increased stability during training suggest that object pose information provides a more structured and informative signal for neural networks. This could lead to more efficient development cycles for robotic skills, reducing the data and computational resources required for policy training.
6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work highlights several avenues for future research, including:
 - **Improving Inference Efficiency**: Addressing the increased computational cost of the cascaded diffusion process is crucial for real-time applications.
 - **Reducing MoCap Dependency**: Developing methods to acquire ground-truth object pose supervision without expensive MoCap systems (e.g., using generative models or foundation 6D pose estimators) would democratize its adoption.
 - **Scalability to Complex Scenarios**: Extending MBA to handle varying numbers of objects, highly deformable objects whose 6D pose is not easily trackable, and long-horizon, multi-stage tasks would further broaden its applicability.
 - **Broader Policy Integration**: Exploring its integration with non-diffusion action heads and leveraging diverse demonstration data (e.g., human videos or web data) could further enhance its generalizability.

In conclusion, MBA represents a significant step towards enabling robots to reason about object dynamics in a more human-like manner, leading to more capable, robust, and efficient manipulation policies. Its modularity and demonstrated improvements across varied tasks position it as a valuable contribution to the field of robotic imitation learning, with the potential to influence the development of more general-purpose and adaptable robotic systems.
