# Learning Structural Latent Points for Efficient Visual Representations in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces a pretraining framework for robotic manipulation that learns "structural latent points," a hybrid visual representation, by integrating a point-wise latent VAE (PL-VAE) with a lightweight 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) renderer. This system improves task success rates by up to 12 percentage points on RLBench and 5 percentage points on ManiSkill2 compared to baselines, and reduces pretraining GPU time by up to 60 times.

## Problem

- Existing 2D visual representations are insufficient for robots operating in 3D environments, leading to inefficiency and brittleness.
- Implicit 3D representations (e.g., NeRF-based) are computationally intensive and lack explicit structural cues interpretable by embodied agents.
- Explicit 3D representations (e.g., 3DGS-based) suffer from loss of fine-grained structural information upon downsampling, limiting their generalization capabilities.

## Method

- A pretraining framework that learns "structural latent points," a hybrid visual representation combining expressive implicit fields with explicit structural priors.
- A Point-wise Latent Variational Autoencoder (PL-==VAE==) is inserted into a Point ==Transformer== v3 (PTv3) autoencoder's latent space to regularize point-wise features and coordinates towards a Gaussian prior.
- A streamlined, lightweight 3DGS-based differentiable rendering pipeline performs feature and depth splatting for multi-view supervision, rather than full photorealistic image rendering.

## Results

- Achieved a Mean Success Rate of 0.56 and Mean Rank of 1.50 on RLBench, outperforming all baselines, and a Mean Success Rate of 0.64 on ManiSkill2, demonstrating robust generalization.
- Significantly reduced pretraining computational cost, achieving up to 60 times faster GPU time and requiring substantially less hardware compared to previous methods.
- Successfully deployed on a real-world robotic platform, achieving an **84%** success rate on the "place mandarin" task, confirming practical utility and transferability of learned representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Regularizing latent points with a PL-VAE creates a compact, smooth, and geometry-aware representation that captures coarse structural tendencies, enhancing robustness and generalization.
> - Prioritizing feature splatting in the rendering pipeline focuses model capacity on learning geometry-aware features, making them more informative and transferable for downstream tasks.
> - The hybrid representation effectively combines the expressiveness of implicit fields with the structural priors of explicit ones, addressing limitations of purely implicit or explicit methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence, particularly robotic perception and manipulation, has advanced through the use of general-purpose feature extractors that compress raw sensory inputs into compact latent representations. Early approaches directly feeding raw sensory data into policy networks proved inefficient and brittle, leading to the adoption of 2D feature extractors. However, for robots operating in a 3D physical world, 2D representations are often insufficient, prompting the development of 3D-aware pretraining frameworks.

These 3D-aware methods typically leverage multi-view observations and differentiable 3D rendering mechanisms, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). NeRF-based approaches create fully implicit representations by aggregating multi-view features into a volumetric field, which is then rendered via ray casting. While expressive, these implicit representations lack explicit structural cues and are computationally expensive. Conversely, 3DGS-based methods generate explicit point-like structures, which naturally encode geometric information and offer high rendering efficiency. However, their capacity is resolution-constrained, and downsampling can lead to a loss of fine-grained structural information, limiting their generalization capabilities.

A common challenge across existing pretraining paradigms is performance degradation when transferring to downstream tasks with significant scene variations. This occurs because the projected point clouds of novel scenes may lie outside the manifold learned during pretraining, resulting in suboptimal representations.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a pretraining framework that learns a hybrid representation, termed "structural latent points." This approach aims to combine the expressiveness characteristic of implicit representations with the structural priors provided by explicit ones, while also enhancing generalization across diverse environments. By introducing a probabilistically regularized latent space and a streamlined rendering pipeline, the research seeks to offer an efficient and robust alternative for learning visual representations in robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient pretraining framework that learns robust 3D-aware visual representations for robotic manipulation, addressing the shortcomings of current implicit and explicit 3D representation learning methods. The motivation stems from several key observations regarding existing approaches:

1. **Limitations of Implicit Representations:** NeRF-based methods, while highly expressive and capable of capturing complex scene details, represent scenes implicitly. This means they do not provide explicit structural information that can be readily interpreted or utilized by downstream embodied agents for tasks requiring geometric understanding. Furthermore, the reliance on ray casting for rendering makes NeRF-based pretraining computationally intensive, hindering scalability.
2. **Limitations of Explicit Representations:** 3DGS-based methods generate explicit point-like structures, which inherently encode interpretable geometric information beneficial for manipulation tasks. However, these methods are often constrained by resolution, meaning that to maintain lightweight policy networks, point-like splats are frequently downsampled. This downsampling can lead to the loss of fine-grained structural details, limiting their representational capacity and generalization performance.
3. **Generalization Challenges across Domains:** A significant hurdle for current 3D-aware pretraining frameworks is their reduced performance when transferring to downstream tasks whose scenes differ substantially from the pretraining domain. The learned latent manifolds may not generalize well to out-of-distribution inputs, resulting in weaker task performance.
4. **Need for a Hybrid Approach:** The researchers identified a gap where neither purely implicit nor purely explicit representations fully met the requirements for robust and generalizable robotic manipulation. There was a need for a representation that could leverage the expressiveness of implicit fields while retaining structural information, ideally in a compact and flexible form.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this work are:

- To propose a novel hybrid representation, "structural latent points," that combines the expressiveness of implicit representations with the structural priors of explicit ones.
- To implement this representation by integrating a point-wise latent variational autoencoder (PL-VAE) into the latent space of a point-cloud autoencoder, specifically a Point Transformer v3 (PTv3). This integration aims to regularize point-wise features and coordinates toward a Gaussian prior, resulting in a compact latent space that preserves coarse structural tendencies rather than precise geometry.
- To develop a streamlined and computationally efficient 3DGS-based rendering pipeline for self-supervised pretraining. This pipeline is deliberately lightweight to ensure that the primary representational capacity and learning burden are placed on the front-end latent module, fostering more informative and transferable features.
- To empirically demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves consistent gains in task success, sample efficiency, and robustness to viewpoint and scene variations compared to existing strong baselines on standardized robotic manipulation benchmarks (RLBench, ManiSkill2) and real-robot platforms.
- To conduct ablation studies to confirm the critical contribution of each proposed component, particularly the PL-VAE and the geometric reasoning module, to the overall performance and generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for learning structural latent points comprises two main components: a point cloud encoding and decoding pipeline for extracting structured latents, and a 3DGS-based differentiable rendering pipeline for multi-view supervision.

**A. Main Architecture of the Pretraining Framework**

The initial stage involves encoding raw point clouds and extracting a structural latent representation. A Point Transformer v3 (PTv3) encoder first processes the raw point cloud (P_raw) to produce a sparse feature point cloud (P_sparse). Uniquely, a **Point-wise Latent Variational Autoencoder (PL-VAE)** is then inserted into the latent space of the PTv3 autoencoder. The PL-VAE takes P_sparse as input and outputs a reconstructed sparse point cloud (ˆP_sparse), which is subsequently fed into the PTv3 decoder to restore the point cloud resolution, yielding a dense feature point cloud (P_dense). This PL-VAE step is designed to enforce a compact and robust representation, enhancing generalization.

The second component integrates a streamlined 3DGS-based rendering pipeline to impart 3D awareness to the learned features. This pipeline consists of a geometric reasoning module (G_θ), a feature-splatting module, and a reconstruction module (Rec_θ).
The geometric reasoning module (G_θ) predicts point-wise Gaussian properties (e.g., opacity, scale, rotation, color) from the dense feature points (P_dense). This step links the PTv3 latent space with local geometry.
Instead of rendering both features and colors, the feature splatting renderer (R_v) performs feature and depth splatting, generating rendered feature maps (ˆF_v) and depth maps (ˆD_v) for a given camera view. This design choice aims to focus model capacity on learning geometry-aware features rather than photorealistic image fidelity.
Two projectors (H_c, H_s) then decode the splatted feature maps into semantic features (ˆF_s_v) and color images (ˆI_v). This shared latent feature space for semantic and appearance cues simplifies the pipeline and strengthens feature coupling.
Additionally, an auxiliary reconstruction module is included to regress the original 3D coordinates and colors of each point from the decoded dense features (P_dense). This provides direct supervision early in training, stabilizing convergence and guiding features towards a well-behaved latent space.

**B. Point-wise Latent VAE (PL-VAE)**

The PL-VAE distinguishes itself from traditional VAEs by operating on both the feature and coordinate dimensions of the latent sparse feature points (P_sparse).
Its encoder extracts both global (f_global) and local (f_i) descriptors from P_sparse using a PointNet backbone and attention pooling. For each point, these descriptors are concatenated to form a joint representation (h_i). This joint representation is then used by two separate Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to predict the mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) for Gaussian distributions over both the feature dimensions (μ_f_i, σ_f_i) and coordinate dimensions (μ_p_i, σ_p_i) for each point.
Latent variables (z_vae = (z_f_i, z_p_i)) are sampled from these distributions using the reparameterization trick. This process regularizes the features and coordinates towards a Gaussian prior, creating a compact and smooth latent representation where point positions approximate structural tendencies rather than precise geometry. This approximate encoding aims to reduce sensitivity to noise and fine-grained variations, thereby enhancing generalization. The PL-VAE decoder then reconstructs ˆP_sparse from z_vae, integrating it into the overall framework for end-to-end optimization.

**C. Effective Representation for Downstream Tasks**

For downstream embodied tasks, the reparameterized latent representation z_vae, derived from the PL-VAE, serves as the direct input to a policy network (e.g., Action Chunking Transformer, ACT). This representation, being compact, smooth, and regularized towards a Gaussian prior, is designed to capture high-level structural regularities, promoting robustness and generalization.

**D. Optimization Strategies**

The framework's optimization involves a total loss function (L_total) composed of four terms, with an annealing weight w(t) that prioritizes reconstruction early in training and shifts focus to rendering alignment later.

1. **Rendering Alignment Loss (L_render)**: An L1 loss that measures the difference between rendered outputs (ˆI_v, ˆD_v, ˆF_s_v) and their ground-truth counterparts (I_v, D_v, F_s_v) for RGB images, depth maps, and semantic feature maps. Ground-truth semantic maps are obtained using E-RADIO.
2. **Auxiliary Reconstruction Loss (L_recon)**: An L1 loss that reconstructs the raw point cloud (P_raw) from the dense feature points (P_dense), guiding the representation towards basic appearance and geometry.
3. **PL-VAE Latent Reconstruction Loss (L_vae)**: An L1 loss designed to reconstruct the sparse feature points (P_sparse) from the sampled latent variables (z_vae), balancing the reconstruction of point positions and features with specific weights.
4. **PL-VAE KL Divergence Loss (L_KL)**: This term enforces that the predicted Gaussian posteriors for both feature and coordinate dimensions remain close to a standard Gaussian prior, fostering a smooth and well-behaved latent space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed pretraining framework was evaluated through extensive experiments on simulated benchmarks (RLBench, ManiSkill2) and a real-world robotic platform, using ScannetV2 as the pretraining dataset for all compared methods. The performance was assessed using metrics such as Success Rate (S.R.), Mean S.R., and Mean Rank for downstream manipulation tasks, with an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) policy.

**A. Evaluation on Simulation Benchmarks**

- **RLBench (Table I)**: The method achieved a Mean Success Rate of 0.56 and a Mean Rank of 1.50, outperforming all other baselines. Notably, 3D-aware pretrained methods consistently outperformed models trained from scratch, and point-based pretraining generally yielded stronger results than multi-view image pipelines. The variant of the proposed method without the PL-VAE (referred to as "No.VAE") showed a Mean S.R. of 0.49 and Mean Rank of 3.56, indicating the significant contribution of the PL-VAE component.
- **ManiSkill2 (Table II)**: Across six rigid and soft body tasks, the proposed method consistently delivered the most robust performance, with a Mean S.R. of 0.64 and a Mean Rank of 1.33. This result underscores the generalization capabilities of the pretraining approach on diverse manipulation tasks. Similar to RLBench, the "No.VAE" variant exhibited reduced performance (Mean S.R. 0.59, Mean Rank 3.92), confirming the impact of the PL-VAE.

**B. Further Analysis and Ablation Studies**

- **Ablation of the Geometric Reasoning Module (Table III, Fig. 2)**: Removing the geometric reasoning module and assigning constant values to Gaussian point properties resulted in noisy and inaccurate rendered depth maps, particularly at object boundaries. In contrast, the complete method produced smoother, more complete, and accurate depth maps, even outperforming Lidar ground truth in some areas. Downstream task performance significantly improved with the inclusion of the geometric reasoning module, demonstrating its importance for clean and reliable geometric information. For instance, the S.R. on "CloseJar" increased from 0.24 to 0.48.
- **Ablation of the Point-wise Latent VAE Module (PL-VAE) (Tables I, II)**: Eliminating the PL-VAE led to substantial performance drops across all evaluated tasks. While still outperforming a PTv3-from-scratch baseline, the "No.VAE" variant offered only a limited advantage over other 3D-aware methods, confirming that the PL-VAE is crucial for learning compact, well-regularized structural latents that enhance generalization.
- **Ablation of Feature Splatting (Fig. 3)**: Qualitative analysis via PCA visualizations showed that features predicted by the proposed feature-only rendering approach exhibited clearer semantic separability, with distinct objects and boundaries being more easily distinguishable compared to other methods. This observation supports the design choice to prioritize feature splatting for representation learning.
- **Complexity Analysis (Table IV)**: The proposed method demonstrated significantly improved training efficiency, reducing aggregate GPU time by 4.6, 24, and 60 times compared to VC-1, SPA, and PonderV2, respectively. It also required substantially less hardware (1x A800 compared to 80x A100 for SPA), simplifying reproduction and making large-scale pretraining more accessible.

**C. Evaluation on Real-world Experiments**

- The pretrained representation encoder was successfully deployed on a real-world robotic platform, utilizing an Orbbec Femto Bolt camera and an AgileX Piper robotic arm. Experiments were conducted across six diverse manipulation tasks using an ACT policy trained on 40 expert demonstrations.
- **Real Robot Task Success Rate (Table V)**: The model consistently outperformed baselines such as Pointnet, PonderV2, and its scratch-trained variant ("Ours (scratch)"). For example, on the "place mandarin" task, the method achieved an 84% success rate, compared to 56% for Pointnet and 72% for PonderV2. These results confirm the practical utility and transferability of the learned representations to real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in learning efficient visual representations for robotic manipulation by introducing a novel 3D pretraining framework centered on "structural latent points." The key significance lies in its ability to fuse the benefits of both implicit and explicit scene representations, a challenge that existing methods have addressed with limited success.

The proposed Point-wise Latent Variational Autoencoder (PL-VAE), by regularizing point features and coordinates towards a Gaussian prior, generates a compact, smooth, and geometry-aware latent representation. This approach effectively captures coarse structural tendencies, enhancing robustness to noise and variations in viewpoints and scene configurations, which is critical for real-world robot deployment. The resulting representation exhibits improved generalization capabilities, as evidenced by consistent performance gains across diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks.

Furthermore, the design of a streamlined and lightweight 3DGS-based rendering pipeline contributes significantly to the efficiency of the pretraining process. By focusing on feature-only splatting and incorporating auxiliary reconstruction losses, the framework reduces computational overhead and hardware requirements, making large-scale pretraining more accessible and scalable. This efficiency allows the representational capacity to be concentrated in the latent module, fostering the learning of more informative and transferable features.

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method consistently achieves superior task success, sample efficiency, and robustness compared to strong existing baselines on benchmarks like RLBench and ManiSkill2. The successful deployment and validation on a real-world robotic platform further underscore the practical applicability and transferability of the learned representations beyond simulated environments.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for embodied AI and robotics. By providing a more generalizable, efficient, and robust pretraining paradigm, it can accelerate the development of robotic systems capable of performing complex manipulation tasks in dynamic and unstructured real-world settings with reduced reliance on extensive task-specific data. This could lead to advancements in areas such as industrial automation, service robotics, and assistive technologies.

The identified limitation, regarding the potential trade-off between generalization (achieved through sampling) and the precise representation of object geometry in scenarios demanding extreme accuracy, suggests a clear direction for future research. Exploring hybrid 3D representations that intelligently combine deterministic and probabilistic components could further enhance both precision and generalization, broadening the applicability of such frameworks to an even wider range of highly sensitive robotic tasks.
