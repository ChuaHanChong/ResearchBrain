# Native Video-Action Pretraining for Generalizable Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> LingBot-VA 2.0 introduces a video-action foundation model specifically engineered for embodied robot control, utilizing a native causal architecture and a semantic visual-action tokenizer. This approach achieves a 14.0 percentage point improvement in average success rate on the RoboTwin benchmark and an end-to-end inference speedup of 6.5x, enabling real-time closed-loop control at up to 225 Hz asynchronous execution.

## Problem

- Existing video-action models repurpose generic video generative models, leading to inadequate representations for robot control where visual details overshadow semantic and physical structure.
- First-generation video-action models suffer from slow inference due to high-dimensional video tokens and iterative denoising, hindering real-time closed-loop robot control.
- Pretraining signals from generic web videos do not inherently teach how actions change the world, limiting the scalability of control knowledge acquisition without expensive robot data.

## Method

- A ==semantic visual-action tokenizer== creates a shared latent space for observations and actions by incorporating ==semantic alignment objectives== and ==latent action tokenization== from unlabeled web video.
- The model employs a native causal pretraining paradigm for its entire stack, including a ==causal diffusion transformer (DiT)== with a sparse ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== backbone, to preserve and acquire broad priors for embodied control.
- **Foresight Reasoning**, an asynchronous inference scheme, predicts future visual outcomes and actions while the robot executes current actions, combined with ==diffusion distillation== and hardware acceleration for a 6.5x speedup.

## Results

- LingBot-VA 2.0 demonstrated improved success rates on real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming both VLA baselines and its predecessor, LingBot-VA 1.0.
- Achieved a **93.6%** average success rate on the RoboTwin bimanual manipulation benchmark, improving by **14.0** percentage points over VLA baselines and showing robust generalization across clean and domain-randomized settings.
- Demonstrated a **6.5x** end-to-end inference speedup, increasing asynchronous control frequency from **35 Hz** to **225 Hz**, enabling real-time, reactive manipulation on physical robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Building video-action models natively for embodiment, starting with a causal architecture and semantic tokenizer, is more effective than adapting generic video generative models for robot control.
> - A shared semantic latent space for observations and actions, guided by a teacher model and an inverse/forward dynamics approach, can extract control-relevant information from unlabeled web videos.
> - Techniques like Multi-chunk Prediction (MCP) and Human-Robot Co-training (HCT) are crucial for efficient optimization, learning trajectory-level dynamics, and leveraging diverse data sources to enhance generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work operates within the field of generalist robot manipulation, specifically focusing on the emerging paradigm of video-action models. Historically, robot control has often relied on reactive vision-language-action (VLA) policies, which directly map observations to actions based on language instructions. More recent advancements have introduced video-action models, such as LingBot-VA and DreamZero, which aim to improve sample efficiency and generalization by jointly predicting scene evolution and corresponding actions, thereby grounding control in physical dynamics.

This research highlights a specific limitation within the current generation of video-action models: their foundational components are largely repurposed from generic video generative models designed for digital content creation. These models typically utilize reconstruction-oriented VAEs and bidirectional video-diffusion backbones, with action modules subsequently integrated for robotics. The authors argue that this adaptation leads to inherent inadequacies when applied to physical environments, motivating a shift toward models specifically designed for embodied control. LingBot-VA 2.0 is positioned as a development to address these foundational mismatches and limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a video-action foundation model, LingBot-VA 2.0, that is "built from the ground up for embodiment," rather than adapting existing video generative models. The authors identify several motivations and specific limitations of current approaches that LingBot-VA 2.0 aims to overcome:

1. **Representation Inadequacy:** Existing video generative models prioritize appearance-based pixel reconstruction. Their latents preserve visual detail but may lack sufficient semantic and physical structure relevant for robot control. When action modules are attached separately, world states and actions may reside in poorly aligned latent spaces, hindering instruction following and action precision. LingBot-VA 2.0 seeks to create a shared semantic latent space for observations and actions.

2. **Inference Speed for Closed-Loop Control:** High-dimensional video tokens and iterative denoising processes in first-generation video-action models can result in slow inference. This latency is problematic for real-time closed-loop control, where robots require high-frequency updates. The objective is to achieve high-frequency inference suitable for real-time operation.

3. **Scalability of Pretraining Signal for Control:** Generic video objectives from web-scale video data do not inherently teach how actions reshape the world. Consequently, the action signal in previous video-action models remains tied to expensive robot data, limiting the acquisition of control knowledge during pretraining. The goal is to develop a pretraining signal that scales with web video to enable the model to acquire control knowledge before downstream adaptation.

4. **Structural Mismatch in Temporal Dynamics:** A fundamental architectural conflict exists between the bidirectional attention used in many video generative models and the strictly causal, forward-in-time nature of closed-loop control. Adapting a bidirectional backbone to a causal one through finetuning on scarce robot data risks "catastrophic forgetting" of the broad priors learned during web-scale pretraining. The paper's explicit motivation is to pursue a "native route" by pretraining the entire stack—including the tokenizer and a causal diffusion transformer (DiT)—from scratch in a causal form to preserve and natively acquire these broad priors.

To address these motivations, LingBot-VA 2.0 incorporates four core design principles: a semantic visual-action tokenizer, a causal pretraining paradigm, a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) backbone for efficiency, and an enhanced asynchronous inference scheme called Foresight Reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LingBot-VA 2.0 is developed as a two-stage process: first, training a semantic visual-action tokenizer, and second, pretraining a causal video-action model on the resulting shared latent space. The overall architecture also integrates a hierarchical planning system.

#### 3.1. Semantic Visual-Action Tokenizer (Stage 1)

This tokenizer aims to create a shared semantic latent space for observations and actions.
- **Visual Tokenization with Semantic Alignment:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) autoencoder encodes video clips into visual latents. The encoder uses full spatial attention within frames and causal attention across frames. The reconstruction loss (L_rec) combines pixel, perceptual, and adversarial terms. Additionally, a semantic alignment objective (L_align) matches temporally pooled visual latents to features from a frozen Perception Encoder (a teacher model), promoting compact and semantically rich representations beyond pixel fidelity.
- **Latent Action Tokenization:** Building on the frozen visual tokenizer, an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) are trained without explicit action labels. The IDM predicts a compact latent action (ℓ_t) from two consecutive visual latents (z_t, z_t+1). The FDM reconstructs the next latent (z_t+1) from the current latent (z_t) and the predicted latent action (ℓ_t), incorporating a transport map and a residual. A reverse transport mechanism ensures backward consistency. This bottlenecked latent action token (a_t ≡ ℓ_t) captures control-relevant changes, enabling action-relevant supervision from unlabeled web video.

#### 3.2. Video-action Model Pretraining and Inference (Stage 2)

The second stage pretrains a causal video-action model on the semantic visual-action latent space.
- **Hierarchical Planning:** A high-level Vision-Language Model (VLM) planner decomposes long-horizon goals into structured subtask contexts. This planner operates asynchronously at a lower frequency (e.g., ∼2 Hz) and provides conditioning signals (structured JSON with `done`, `instruction`, `generation_instruction`, `local_scene_description`) to the low-level policy. Training data for the planner is constructed from segment-level annotations of robot demonstrations using "boundary-crossing sampling." The VLM backbone is LoRA fine-tuned with a frozen vision tower to adapt to this schema.
- **Architecture: Mixture-of-Experts Video Stream:** The low-level policy uses a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) structure where video and action modalities are handled by separate experts ({v_vid_θ}, {v_act_θ}) sharing a causal self-attention mechanism. The video expert, which carries the primary modeling burden for visual dynamics, employs a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routed layer in each causal DiT block, while the action expert remains dense. The MoE design (N_e=128, k=8) utilizes group-limited top-k routing and a load-balancing strategy (Loss-Free Balancing) to maintain balanced expert utilization and expand model capacity without sacrificing efficiency.
- **Multi-chunk Prediction (MCP):** To counter the "myopic supervision" problem of standard teacher-forced training, MCP is introduced as an auxiliary objective. It trains lightweight modules attached to the main DiT to predict several future latent chunks (K=3) simultaneously (next_1, next_2, next_3) from the main model's representation. This provides dense temporal supervision, encouraging the model to learn trajectory-level dynamics rather than just short-term visual continuity. Gradients from these future-chunk denoising tasks flow back to the backbone.
- **In-context Learning (ICL):** To enhance task specification beyond language, the model supports video in-context learning. Human demonstration videos, semantically aligned but not necessarily sharing viewpoint or object instances, are encoded as in-context latents (z_icl). This provides explicit temporal and procedural guidance, particularly for rare-object or complex tasks. The robot policy is conditioned on z_icl for all prediction steps.
- **Human-Robot Data Co-training (HCT):** To leverage diverse and scalable egocentric human manipulation videos (D_H) alongside robot data (D_R), a co-training strategy is employed. The action-space and motion gaps are addressed by mapping human hand poses into a unified robot action space (6-DoF root pose + scalar gripper opening) using a map Φ. Embodiment-specific action encoders (E_d) and decoders (P_d) are maintained around a shared world model (v_vid, v_act, causal attention), allowing both domains to share dynamics learning while isolating low-level action parameterization.
- **Training Recipe:** A multi-task co-training recipe optimizes all objectives (Text-to-Image (T2I), Text-to-Video (T2V), Text-Image to Video-Action (TI2VA), ICL, HCT) jointly with a coarse-to-fine schedule. Early training emphasizes T2I and T2V for broad priors, while late training focuses on embodied control tasks (TI2VA, ICL, HCT).
- **Foresight Reasoning:** This asynchronous inference scheme addresses the serial bottleneck in real-time control. While the robot executes the current action chunk (a_t), the prediction stream imagines the visual outcome (ˆz_t+1) using a forward-dynamics pass (FDM_θ) and predicts the next action chunk (a_t+1). To prevent drift, the rollout is re-grounded when a real observation (z_t+1) arrives, overwriting the imagined latent in the KV cache. A dedicated forward-dynamics grounding loss (L_FDM) is added during post-training to supervise this role.

#### 3.3. Post-training and Acceleration

- **Diffusion Distillation:** The post-trained policy's denoising steps (e.g., 5 for video, 10 for action) are reduced to a few-step consistency model (e.g., 2 steps for each expert) using a consistency distillation objective (L_CD), speeding up inference.
- **Inference Acceleration:**
 - **Model Level:** Low-precision compiled execution (ONNX-based TensorRT build, FP8 quantization) optimizes individual DiT forward passes.
 - **Sequence Level:** Long-horizon attention optimization uses paged/ragged KV-cache management and FlashInfer-based attention plugins to efficiently handle historical tokens.
 - **System Level:** Runtime overhead reduction amortizes host-side preparation, memory allocation, and synchronization costs.

#### 3.4. Data Recipe

The training leverages a multi-stage data recipe:
- **General image/video data:** Web-scale corpora from LingBot-Video for T2I and T2V pretraining.
- **Robot data:** A comprehensive mixture of public, semi-public, and internal robot demonstrations (e.g., AgiBot, RoboMind, OXE), totaling thousands of hours. All data are reprocessed with a unified annotation pipeline using Qwen3.5-397B for language prompts.
- **Ego-centric human data:** Thousands of hours of first-person manipulation videos, annotated with 6-DoF hand root poses and finger joint angles. These are converted to the robot gripper action space and used for HCT.
- **In-context learning data:** A large-scale human-robot dataset (50,000+ samples from 10+ robot datasets) is curated using a VLM (Gemini-3.1-Pro) to transform robot video frames into human manipulation contexts and synthesize corresponding human videos, which are then filtered for quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Real-world Deployment

LingBot-VA 2.0 demonstrates improvements in real-world manipulation tasks compared to baselines.
- **Performance:** Achieves higher success rates and task progress rates on tasks such as Fruit Sorting, Pen Collection, Drawer Tidying, and Plate Handover.
- **Comparison:** Outperforms the VLA baseline π0.5, particularly on continuous manipulation tasks requiring longer-horizon visual grounding. It also shows gains over its predecessor, LingBot-VA 1.0, attributed to causal video-action pretraining.

#### 4.2. In-context Learning (ICL) Demonstration

Qualitative demonstrations indicate LingBot-VA 2.0's ability to utilize visual demonstrations as task context for unseen tasks without parameter updates. The model successfully transfers action procedures from human reference videos to robot manipulation for new object compositions, arrangements, and instances (e.g., placing a calabash, different cups, or a pear into various plates).

#### 4.3. Simulation (RoboTwin)

LingBot-VA 2.0 achieves competitive performance on the RoboTwin bimanual manipulation benchmark.
- **Success Rate:** Achieves the best performance (93.6% average success rate) on both clean (93.8%) and domain-randomized (93.4%) settings.
- **Generalization:** Improves the average success rate by 14.0 percentage points over the VLA baseline π0.5 and by 1.4 percentage points over LingBot-VA 1.0. The small gap between clean and randomized evaluations (0.6 percentage points) indicates robust control under visual and physical variations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Semantic Visual-Action Tokenizer:** Replacing the reconstruction-oriented WAN2.2 VAE with the proposed semantic visual-action tokenizer consistently improves RoboTwin performance, with greater gains observed for longer prediction horizons. This suggests that the semantic tokenizer provides more useful state information for world-action modeling.
- **Multi-chunk Prediction (MCP):** MCP improves optimization efficiency, leading to faster convergence and higher final task success rates on RoboTwin at both 12 and 50 frames per second (fps). For instance, at 50 fps, the MCP variant shows a 29.7 percentage point improvement over the baseline after 5k training steps and achieves a 2.3x training speedup to match the baseline's 45k-step accuracy in 20k steps.
- **Inference Acceleration:** The cumulative application of acceleration techniques results in a 6.5x end-to-end speedup. The asynchronous control frequency is improved from 35 Hz to 225 Hz, with individual steps contributing: consistency distillation (927 ms to 466 ms), low-precision compiled execution (466 ms to 369 ms), long-horizon attention optimization (369 ms to 272 ms), and runtime overhead reduction (272 ms to 142 ms).

#### 4.5. Real-world Demonstrations

Qualitative rollouts on tasks like Desk Tidying, Conveyor Belt, Chip Picking, and Air Hockey illustrate various capabilities. These demonstrations highlight the model's ability for long-horizon planning, interaction with moving objects, fine-grained manipulation without tactile sensors, and fast, reactive control in dynamic environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LingBot-VA 2.0 represents a significant step towards developing generalizable robot control systems by proposing a video-action foundation model specifically engineered for embodied interaction. By departing from repurposed components and building a native, causal video-action stack, the research addresses fundamental limitations in representation, inference speed, and pretraining signal scalability that have constrained prior models.

The primary impacts of this work include:
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The shared semantic visual-action representation, combined with scalable self-supervision from web videos and human-robot co-training, enables few-shot adaptation to new tasks and, in some cases, zero-shot execution without extensive re-collection of robot data. This capability could reduce the cost and effort associated with deploying robots in novel environments or for new tasks.
- **Real-time Closed-Loop Control:** Foresight Reasoning, consistency distillation, and comprehensive inference acceleration techniques allow LingBot-VA 2.0 to operate at high frequencies (up to 225 Hz asynchronous execution) on real robots. This addresses a critical bottleneck for practical real-world deployment, enabling reactive and precise manipulation.
- **Improved Long-Horizon and Fine-Grained Manipulation:** The emphasis on trajectory-level dynamics through multi-chunk prediction and the re-grounding mechanism of Foresight Reasoning, coupled with hierarchical planning, contribute to more consistent long-horizon behavior and high-precision manipulation, surpassing previous baselines.

The paper outlines future directions such as tighter joint training of the planner and policy, incorporating interactive or reinforcement signals to sharpen the latent-action space, and further scaling the pretraining corpus and backbone to broaden embodiment coverage. These aspects suggest a continued trajectory toward more robust and versatile embodied foundation models.
