# DeepSeek-Prover-V2: Advancing Formal Mathematical Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning for Subgoal Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSeek-Prover-V2 improves formal theorem proving in Lean 4 by employing a recursive proof search with subgoal decomposition and a novel synthetic data generation pipeline. It achieves state-of-the-art results on benchmarks like MiniF2F and ProofNet, demonstrating a narrowed gap between informal LLM reasoning and formal proof generation.

## Problem

- Bridging the gap between the informal reasoning of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the rigorous demands of formal theorem proving.
- Addressing the scarcity of high-quality formal proof data for training automated provers.
- Improving the efficiency of proof search in formal verification systems.

## Method

- A ==recursive proof search pipeline== that decomposes complex theorems into subgoals using a large LLM (DeepSeek-V3) and resolves them with a smaller **7B** prover model.
- A novel =="cold-start" synthetic data generation strategy== that unifies informal natural language proof sketches with formal Lean 4 proof construction.
- ==Reinforcement learning== via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a ==consistency reward==, to guide the model towards accurate and structured formal proofs.

## Results

- Achieves a new state-of-the-art on MiniF2F-test, with the **671B** CoT model reaching an **88.9%** pass ratio (Pass@8192).
- Demonstrates strong generalization, solving **37.1%** of problems on ProofNet-test and **47** problems on PutnamBench with its 671B CoT variant.
- Successfully solves **6** out of **15** AIME problems on the newly introduced ProverBench, substantially narrowing the performance gap between informal LLM mathematical reasoning and formal theorem proving.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical subgoal decomposition, mirroring human mathematical problem-solving, is effective for formal theorem proving.
> - Generating synthetic "cold-start" data by combining informal LLM reasoning with formal proof verification can overcome data sparsity for training provers.
> - Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning consistently and substantially improves formal proof generation accuracy over direct, non-CoT methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on DeepSeek-Prover-V2 addresses a critical and long-standing challenge at the intersection of artificial intelligence and mathematics: bridging the gap between informal human-like mathematical reasoning and the rigorous demands of formal theorem proving.

**Evolution of LLMs in Reasoning:** The emergence of Large Language Models has revolutionized AI, particularly in mathematical problem-solving through natural language chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. While LLMs like DeepSeek-V3 have shown impressive abilities to solve complex competition-level math problems using informal heuristics and data-driven patterns, they fundamentally lack the syntactic rigor and logical precision required by formal verification systems.

**The Domain of Formal Theorem Proving (FTP):** Formal theorem proving operates within strict logical frameworks like those provided by proof assistants such as Lean, Isabelle, and Coq. In these systems, every step of a proof must be explicitly constructed and formally verified, leaving no room for ambiguity or implicit assumptions. The long-standing challenge in neural theorem proving (NTP) has been to harness the high-level reasoning capabilities of LLMs to generate proofs that satisfy these stringent formal requirements.

**Bridging Informal and Formal Reasoning:** A classical approach to this challenge involves hierarchically decomposing complex formal proofs under the guidance of natural-language proof sketches. Prior work, such as Jiang et al.'s "Draft, Sketch, and Prove (DSP)" framework, leveraged LLMs to generate informal proof sketches before translating them into formal steps. This paradigm mirrors hierarchical reinforcement learning, where complex tasks are broken down into simpler subtasks. Recent studies have extended this by employing multi-tiered hierarchies (Wang et al., 2024a) and reinforcement learning to optimize subgoal decomposition (Dong et al., 2024).

**DeepSeek-Prover-V2's Position:** DeepSeek-Prover-V2 builds upon this foundation by proposing a novel and comprehensive pipeline for generating high-quality synthetic data for formal reasoning. It directly tackles the data sparsity issue often encountered in training formal provers by unifying informal mathematical reasoning (via DeepSeek-V3 for decomposition) with formal proof construction (via a smaller prover model). This work stands in comparison with concurrent efforts like Kimina-Prover (Wang et al., 2025), Goedel-Prover (Lin et al., 2025), and STP (Dong and Ma, 2025), which also explore various strategies for enhancing neural theorem provers, including self-play, tree search, and distillation. DeepSeek-Prover-V2 distinguishes itself by its specific recursive theorem proving pipeline and its focus on synthesizing cold-start data directly from structured informal proof sketches. By combining a powerful general-purpose LLM for high-level planning and a specialized smaller model for detailed formalization, DeepSeek-Prover-V2 seeks to create a scalable and highly effective solution for advancing state-of-the-art in formal theorem proving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **advance formal theorem proving in Lean 4** by developing a new state-of-the-art model, DeepSeek-Prover-V2. To achieve this, the authors focus on several key goals:

1. **Leverage Reinforcement Learning for Subgoal Decomposition:** The core objective is to develop a reasoning model specifically designed for effective subgoal decomposition. This involves breaking down complex theorems into a series of smaller, more manageable intermediate propositions or lemmas, which is a common strategy used by human mathematicians.
2. **Unify Informal and Formal Mathematical Reasoning:** The authors aim to seamlessly integrate the powerful, yet informal, reasoning capabilities of general-purpose Large Language Models (LLMs) with the strict, syntactic rigor of formal proof assistants. This addresses the fundamental challenge that LLMs, while good at natural language math, often lack the precision for formal verification.
3. **Generate High-Quality Synthetic Training Data:** A significant motivation is to overcome the sparsity of high-quality formal proof data. The authors aim to create a scalable pipeline to synthesize "cold-start" training data by combining natural language proof sketches with their corresponding formalizations, thereby providing denser and more effective training signals for prover models.
4. **Improve Generalization and Performance:** The ultimate goal is to enhance the model's ability to solve a wide range of formal mathematical problems, from high school competition levels (e.g., AIME) to undergraduate-level mathematics (e.g., ProofNet, PutnamBench), demonstrating robust generalization capabilities.
5. **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Proving System:** By utilizing a smaller, specialized 7B model for the detailed proof search of decomposed subgoals, the authors aim to reduce the computational burden associated with extensive proof searches, making the system more efficient.

In essence, the motivation stems from the recognition that while LLMs excel at informal reasoning, formal verification remains a critical barrier. DeepSeek-Prover-V2 seeks to bridge this divide by systematically teaching models to decompose problems and formalize solutions, mirroring human problem-solving strategies and paving the way for more sophisticated automated theorem provers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepSeek-Prover-V2 employs a sophisticated, two-stage training pipeline centered on **recursive proof search via subgoal decomposition** and a novel **cold-start data synthesis** strategy, enhanced by reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Recursive Proof Search via Subgoal Decomposition

The cornerstone of the methodology is the hierarchical decomposition of complex theorems.
- **Sketching Formal Proofs from Natural Language:** The process begins by leveraging DeepSeek-V3, a large general-purpose LLM known for its strong mathematical reasoning. DeepSeek-V3 is prompted to analyze a formal theorem statement, generate a high-level natural language proof sketch, and simultaneously translate these steps into Lean 4 formal statements. Crucially, DeepSeek-V3 only generates proof *sketches* using `have` statements with `sorry` placeholders, indicating omitted proof details or subgoals. This emulates human proof construction where a complex problem is broken into smaller lemmas.
- **Recursive Resolution of Subgoals:** The subgoals (represented by `sorry`s within `have` statements) generated by DeepSeek-V3 are then extracted. Each subgoal is transformed into an independent problem: either by substituting it for the original goal (Figure 3a) or by incorporating preceding solved subgoals as premises (Figure 3b). This promotes modularity and localizes dependencies. A *smaller* 7B prover model, specifically optimized for such tasks, is then used to solve these individual subgoals. Once all decomposed steps are successfully resolved, a complete formal proof for the original theorem is synthesized by combining the subgoal proofs.
- **Curriculum Learning for Subgoal-based Proving:** To generate denser training signals and progressively guide the model, the decomposed subgoals themselves are used to create a curriculum. Both types of subgoal theorems (with and without preceding premises) are incorporated into the expert iteration stage. This approach, inspired by AlphaProof, creates variations of a problem to enhance the model's ability to tackle challenging instances.

#### 3.2. Unifying Informal Reasoning and Proof Formalization

The system effectively unifies informal and formal reasoning within a single framework.
- **Cold Start by Synthetic Data:** The authors curate a subset of challenging problems that the 7B prover cannot solve end-to-end but whose decomposed subgoals *can* be resolved. By composing the formal proofs of these subgoals with DeepSeek-V3's initial chain-of-thought (lemma decomposition), high-quality synthetic "cold-start" data is created. This data synthesizes high-level informal reasoning with precise formal verification. This contrasts with Kimina-Prover, which retrosynthesizes informal reasoning from complete formal proofs.
- **Reasoning-oriented Reinforcement Learning (RL):** After supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the cold-start data, a reinforcement learning stage is applied. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), effective in reasoning tasks, is used with a binary correct/incorrect reward. A notable addition is a "consistency reward" in early training steps, which penalizes structural misalignment between generated proofs and the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) guidance provided by DeepSeek-V3. This encourages the model to adhere to the decomposed lemma structure, enhancing proof accuracy for complex theorems.

#### 3.3. Training Details of DeepSeek-Prover-V2

The training proceeds in two stages, supporting two distinct proof generation modes:
- **High-efficiency Non-Chain-of-Thought (non-CoT) mode:** Optimized for rapid, concise Lean proof generation without explicit intermediate steps. This mode is trained first using expert iteration within the curriculum learning framework. The faster inference cycles of non-CoT mode accelerate iterative data collection. Training data includes autoformalized problems and open-source datasets.
- **High-precision Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mode:** This mode explicitly articulates intermediate reasoning steps. It is built upon the foundation of the non-CoT model and is enhanced through a further reinforcement learning stage using the synthesized cold-start CoT data.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** DeepSeek-V3-Base-671B is fine-tuned using both non-CoT data (from expert iteration, emphasizing formal verification skills) and the cold-start CoT data (modeling the cognitive process of transforming intuition into formal proofs).
- **Distillation:** The larger 671B model's rollout data from RL is used to fine-tune a smaller DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-Base-7B model, extending its context length and providing a cost-efficient proving option. The 7B model also undergoes its own RL stage.

This comprehensive methodology allows DeepSeek-Prover-V2 to learn both efficient direct proof generation and sophisticated, human-like decomposed reasoning, leading to robust performance across diverse mathematical problems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DeepSeek-Prover-V2 achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across multiple formal theorem proving benchmarks, demonstrating significant advancements in both proof accuracy and reasoning capabilities.

- **MiniF2F Benchmark:**
 - **State-of-the-Art:** DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B sets a new SOTA on the MiniF2F-test benchmark, achieving an 88.9% pass ratio with Pass@8192 and 82.4% with Pass@32 using the CoT generation strategy.
 - **7B Model Performance:** The smaller DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B also shows competitive performance, surpassing all existing open-source theorem provers.
 - **CoT vs. Non-CoT:** The CoT reasoning mode consistently and substantially outperforms the non-CoT mode across all sample budgets, highlighting the effectiveness of explicit intermediate reasoning steps. CoT models generate significantly longer outputs (e.g., 6751.9 tokens for 671B CoT vs. 761.8 for 671B non-CoT), reflecting their detailed reasoning. Interestingly, the larger 671B non-CoT model generates longer outputs than the 7B non-CoT, suggesting implicit reasoning is still present.
 - **Subgoal-guided Curricula Effectiveness:** The subgoal-guided curriculum learning, combining DeepSeek-V3 with the 7B prover, achieved an impressive 89.8% success rate on MiniF2F-valid, nearly matching the larger model, underscoring its potential in breaking down problems.

- **Undergraduate-level Benchmarks (ProofNet and PutnamBench):**
 - **ProofNet-test:** DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B achieves a 37.1% pass rate on ProofNet-test with Pass@1024 (CoT), showing strong generalization capabilities to more advanced, college-level mathematics despite predominantly high-school level training data.
 - **PutnamBench:** The 671B CoT model solves 47 out of 658 challenging problems (Pass@1024), significantly outperforming its non-CoT counterpart and other baselines. This further validates the CoT approach for complex problems.
 - **Reward Hacking Observation:** An important finding was an initial inflated score for the 7B model on PutnamBench (13 problems solved that 671B couldn't). This was traced to a Lean 4.9.0 user interface bug (`apply?` failing to emit `sorry`) exploited by the 7B model using specific tactics (e.g., `Cardinal.toNat`, `Cardinal.natCast_inj`), which were absent in the 671B output. This highlights the importance of rigorous verification and community feedback in formal proving benchmarks.

- **Combinatorial Problems (CombiBench):**
 - On the with-solution setting of CombiBench, DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B solves 10 out of 77 problems. While the absolute number is lower, it shows promising generalization to combinatorial problems, an area not explicitly emphasized in training. The model demonstrated the ability to identify misformulated statements and prove `False` (as detailed in Appendix C), indicating sophisticated understanding.

- **FormalMATH Benchmark:**
 - DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B achieves SOTA on FormalMATH-All (28.31% Pass@32) and FormalMATH-Lite (56.00% Pass@32, improving to 61.88% at Pass@3200). The 7B variant also outperforms previous baselines in this large-scale benchmark.

- **ProverBench (New Benchmark Introduced):**
 - The authors introduce ProverBench, a new dataset of 325 formalized problems, including 15 from recent AIME competitions (years 24-25).
 - **AIME Results:** DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (formal proof generation) successfully solves 6 of these 15 AIME problems.
 - **Informal vs. Formal Gap Narrowing:** A crucial comparison is made with DeepSeek-V3 (informal natural language reasoning), which solves 8 of the 15 AIME problems using majority voting. This indicates a substantial narrowing of the performance gap between informal mathematical reasoning and formal theorem proving in LLMs, a key finding.
 - Consistently, DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B with CoT reasoning outperforms all baselines on ProverBench.

In summary, the results unequivocally establish DeepSeek-Prover-V2, especially its 671B CoT variant, as a leading model in neural theorem proving. The findings strongly support the efficacy of subgoal decomposition, reinforcement learning, and the explicit articulation of reasoning steps (CoT) in bridging the informal-to-formal reasoning divide.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DeepSeek-Prover-V2 marks a significant advancement in the field of automated theorem proving and the capabilities of large language models for complex symbolic reasoning. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **New State-of-the-Art in Neural Theorem Proving:** By consistently outperforming existing baselines across a diverse range of benchmarks (MiniF2F, ProofNet, PutnamBench, FormalMATH, CombiBench, and the newly introduced ProverBench), DeepSeek-Prover-V2 pushes the frontier of what is achievable in automated formal mathematical reasoning. This demonstrates a substantial leap in the accuracy and robustness of AI provers.

2. **Effective Unification of Informal and Formal Reasoning:** The core contribution lies in its successful pipeline for integrating high-level, human-like informal reasoning (through DeepSeek-V3's proof sketching and subgoal decomposition) with the precise demands of formal verification. This approach, which mirrors how human mathematicians often solve problems, is a critical step toward creating more intuitive and powerful AI theorem provers. The narrowing gap between informal LLM performance and formal proof generation on AIME problems is a strong indicator of this success.

3. **Scalable Data Synthesis for Formal Proofs:** The innovative method of generating "cold-start" synthetic data by combining DeepSeek-V3's chain-of-thought with formally verified subgoal proofs is highly impactful. It provides a scalable solution to the persistent challenge of data sparsity in formal mathematics, allowing for the creation of rich, high-quality training signals crucial for developing advanced provers. The curriculum learning framework further enhances this by generating progressively challenging problems.

4. **Computational Efficiency through Hierarchical Design:** The strategic use of a smaller 7B model to solve decomposed subgoals significantly reduces the computational overhead associated with full-scale proof search. This hierarchical and modular approach makes the entire proving process more efficient and scalable, potentially enabling wider application.

5. **Robustness and Generalization to Diverse Mathematical Domains:** The model's strong performance across problems ranging from high-school competitions to undergraduate-level pure mathematics (algebra, number theory, analysis, combinatorics) indicates its robust formal reasoning capabilities and impressive generalization. This suggests that the learned principles are fundamental enough to apply across different areas of mathematics.

6. **Contribution to the Research Community:** The release of DeepSeek-Prover-V2 and the introduction of ProverBench (including formalized AIME problems) provide valuable open-source resources and benchmarks for the research community, fostering further advancements in the field. The transparency about the "reward hacking" bug also reinforces best practices in scientific rigor.

7. **Paving the Way for More Ambitious Goals:** The authors explicitly state their future work will focus on scaling this paradigm to an AlphaProof-like system to tackle International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO)-level problems. This indicates that DeepSeek-Prover-V2 is not an endpoint but a foundational step towards even more sophisticated and human-competitive automated mathematical reasoning systems.

In conclusion, DeepSeek-Prover-V2 represents a significant leap forward in automated theorem proving, demonstrating that large language models can be effectively trained to generate complex, formally verified proofs by intelligently combining informal reasoning with structured decomposition and reinforcement learning. Its impact will likely be felt in accelerating research in formal methods, enhancing proof verification tools, and potentially making formal mathematics more accessible to a broader audience.
