# TrafficBots: Towards World Models for Autonomous Driving Simulation and Motion Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> TrafficBots casts data-driven traffic simulation as a world model: one shared multi-agent policy drives every road user, conditioned on a per-agent navigational destination and a time-invariant CVAE personality, with a new angular positional encoding letting all agents share a single global vectorized context via dot-product attention. On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset it simulates dense urban scenes faster than real time while staying competitive on motion prediction.

## Problem

- Photo-realistic full-stack driving simulators render pixels that planning modules discard, and their scripted scenarios stay less realistic than logged real urban traffic.
- Existing data-driven traffic simulators lack configurability, since behavior cannot be steered by intent or style, and lack scalability to every agent in dense scenes.
- Prior goal-conditioned predictors anchor on the last observed pose, which is not intent, so closed-loop policies learn wrong causal relations like stopping mid-crosswalk.

## Method

- Destination conditioning: a map polyline extended from agent history, predicted as multi-class classification, approximates a navigator, with the policy falling back to unconditioned once the destination is reached.
- A time-invariant per-agent personality latent learned by ==CVAE==, sampled from the prior for prediction and the posterior for simulation, replacing the per-step latents of earlier simulators.
- An ==angular-encoding== state encoder concatenating sinusoidal position encodings with integer-frequency angle encodings, so ==Transformer== cross/self-attention reads one shared global context; trained by ==BPTT== with free-nats KL and stopped action gradients.

## Results

- On the WOMD marginal leaderboard it reaches **0.212** mAP versus **0.176** for the LSTM baseline and the lowest overlap rate, **0.145**, but a worse minADE of **1.313**.
- Ablations confirm each part: removing personality collapses mAP from **0.18** to **0.06**, weaker encoders give **0.12** and **0.14**, and a behavior-cloning baseline hits **0.01** mAP with **21.9%** vehicle collisions.
- Under **3M** parameters, 16 parallel simulations of 64 agents and 1024 map polylines run on one 2080Ti at roughly **10 ms** per rollout step, an order faster than prior simulators.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Closed-loop autoregressive policies accumulate displacement error yet cut overlap and collision rates, so choose metrics matching whether prediction accuracy or interaction realism matters.
> - Conditioning on where an agent intends to go, not where it was last seen, avoids spurious causal shortcuts even when the shortcut improves displacement metrics.
> - A behavior-style latent held fixed over short horizons captures agent diversity, and encoding angles with integer frequencies makes shared-context attention genuinely direction-sensitive.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research situates itself at the intersection of several critical areas within autonomous driving (AD) and artificial intelligence: data-driven simulation, world models in reinforcement learning, multi-modal motion prediction, and end-to-end (E2E) driving.

Traditional AD development heavily relies on real-world testing, which is resource-intensive and poses safety risks. This has led to the widespread adoption of simulation environments. While full-stack, photo-realistic simulators like CARLA are valuable for testing complete AD stacks and training visuomotor policies, they often fall short in replicating the complexity and realism of urban planning scenarios. Furthermore, their computationally demanding rendering capabilities are often redundant for planning modules, which primarily operate on intermediate-level representations. This highlights a need for more efficient and realistic simulators specifically tailored for AD planning.

Current data-driven traffic simulators often suffer from limitations in configurability and scalability. Many approaches rely on hand-crafted rules or log-replay, which may not generate sufficiently diverse or reactive human-like behaviors. Learning-based methods have emerged, but often face challenges in scaling to dense, multi-agent scenarios or providing flexible control over agent behaviors.

Concurrently, the concept of "world models" has gained traction in model-based reinforcement learning. World models are learned action-conditional dynamics models that can serve as differentiable substitutes for actual environments, facilitating planning and policy learning. Applying this concept to traffic simulation offers a path toward more sophisticated and learnable simulation environments. Prior work on world models has often focused on video prediction, which for urban driving could lead to unnecessary complexity by rasterizing all dynamics rather than focusing on critical decision-dynamics of interacting agents.

The research also builds upon advancements in multi-modal motion prediction, particularly those employing Transformer architectures and vectorized representations, which have demonstrated strong performance and computational efficiency. Methods in motion prediction have addressed multi-modality using CVAEs and have explored goal-conditioning. This paper integrates these techniques, refining goal-conditioning into a more robust "destination" concept to mitigate causal confusion in closed-loop policies.

TrafficBots endeavors to bridge these areas by formulating data-driven traffic simulation as a world model. It aims to overcome the configurability and scalability issues of existing data-driven simulators by introducing specific mechanisms for behavior generation and efficient context representation. By connecting simulation to multi-modal motion prediction, the work also proposes a standardized and reproducible evaluation framework using established motion prediction metrics and public leaderboards, addressing the lack of consistent evaluation protocols in prior traffic simulation research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a data-driven, differentiable traffic simulator, named TrafficBots, that can generate realistic, configurable, and scalable multi-agent behaviors, specifically tailored for the development and validation of autonomous driving planning algorithms.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges in the AD development lifecycle:

- **Cost and Safety of Real-World Testing:** Validating AD planning algorithms on public roads is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and carries significant safety risks. This necessitates robust simulation environments.
- **Limitations of Existing Simulators for Planning:**
 - **Full-stack, photo-realistic simulators** are computationally intensive due to demanding rendering. More importantly, they often fail to generate the nuanced and complex interactive scenarios crucial for stress-testing AD planning modules. Planning modules typically operate on intermediate-level representations (e.g., agent states, map data), rendering photo-realistic visuals redundant.
 - **Current data-driven traffic simulators** often lack the ability to generate diverse, configurable, and reactive behaviors. Many rely on pre-recorded log-replay, simple scripted rules, or struggle to scale efficiently to dense urban environments with numerous interacting agents (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists).
- **Need for a Differentiable Simulation Framework:** Framing the simulator as a "world model" allows it to be fully differentiable. This enables the simulator to be trained and used in ways that can benefit model-based reinforcement learning or imitation learning, potentially leading to more efficient training of AD planning policies.
- **Improving Simulation Configurability:** Human-like behaviors exhibit significant diversity. Existing simulators often do not provide sufficient mechanisms to control or specify varied behavioral styles for non-player agents (bots). This limits the ability to create targeted test scenarios.
- **Enhancing Simulation Scalability:** Urban environments are characterized by a large number of interacting agents. Efficiently simulating dense traffic without prohibitive computational costs or memory footprints is a significant challenge for existing methods.
- **Standardized Evaluation:** The field of data-driven traffic simulation has lacked a unified and reproducible evaluation protocol, making it difficult to compare different approaches objectively. Leveraging established motion prediction benchmarks offers a way to assess behavioral fidelity.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives for TrafficBots are:
- To formulate data-driven traffic simulation as a world model, making it differentiable and capable of supporting AD planning.
- To introduce a **navigational destination** for each bot agent to provide configurable, long-term intent, thus allowing agents to exhibit directed behaviors.
- To integrate a **time-invariant latent personality** for each bot agent, learned using a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), to capture and generate diverse behavioral styles (e.g., aggressive, cautious).
- To improve **scalability** by employing a vectorized representation for context, sharing a single policy among all agents, and introducing a novel positional encoding scheme for angles (Angular Encoding, AE) that enhances the efficiency of dot-product attention in global coordinates.
- To generate **realistic multi-agent behaviors** for all traffic participants (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists) by learning from real-world datasets.
- To propose a **comprehensive and reproducible evaluation protocol** by framing motion prediction as a form of "a priori simulation," thereby allowing assessment of simulation fidelity using established metrics on public leaderboards (specifically, the Waymo Open Motion Dataset).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TrafficBots proposes a multi-agent policy designed to generate realistic and configurable behaviors for non-player agents (bots) within a data-driven, differentiable simulation framework. The approach integrates concepts from motion prediction and end-to-end driving, leveraging a Transformer-based architecture and vectorized representations.

### 4. A. World Model Formulation

The simulator is conceptualized as a "world model" that updates its internal states based on actions from a player agent. The internal states comprise non-reactive elements (map, traffic controls) loaded from datasets and reactive elements (player agent states, bot agent states). The core focus is on learning the reactive decision-dynamics of bot agents, which are handled by the TrafficBots policy. The entire simulation framework, including an "observation generator" and TrafficBots, is designed to be fully differentiable.

### 5. B. TrafficBots Policy Architecture (Fig. 3)

The TrafficBots policy is designed to predict the next state (s_t+1) for each agent based on its current state (s_t) and rich contextual information. The architecture consists of:
- **State Encoder:** All shared contexts (map, traffic lights, agent states) are represented in global coordinates. A key component is a novel state encoder that incorporates positional encoding (PE) for x, y coordinates and a new **Angular Encoding (AE)** for the yaw angle (θ). This AE (AE_2i(θ) = sin(θ · i), AE_2i+1(θ) = cos(θ · i)) is designed to be more sensitive to directional information than prior methods by using integer frequencies, improving the efficiency of dot-product attention in modeling global-to-local coordinate transformations without explicit coordinate transformations. The encoder also processes other attributes (u) via an MLP.
- **Context Transformers:**
 - **Map Transformer:** Utilizes Transformer encoder layers to process map polylines, including a Polyline Subgraph Transformer and a Global Graph Transformer.
 - **Traffic Lights Transformer:** Processes traffic light states using attention mechanisms.
 - **Interaction Transformer:** Uses self-attention across the agent dimension, allowing agents to attend to each other's encoded states. During inference, states of non-TrafficBots agents are also processed to ensure reactive behavior.
- **Temporal GRU:** Each agent has a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to aggregate its historical information, addressing the non-Markovian nature of simulation states.
- **Conditioning Modules:**
 - **Destination:** A time-invariant navigational destination (approximated heuristically from the map topology and extended trajectories during training) provides long-term intent. It is distinct from short-term "goals" associated with prediction horizons, thereby avoiding causal confusion in closed-loop policies. The destination is combined with the agent's intermediate state via concatenation and a residual MLP. A "reached indicator" can mask its influence once achieved.
 - **Personality:** A time-invariant latent personality is introduced for each agent, learned using a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE). This latent variable (z) captures diverse behavioral styles. During training and simulation, the posterior (z_post) is used; for motion prediction, the prior (z_prior) is sampled. Personality is combined with the agent's intermediate state similarly to the destination.
- **Action Heads and Dynamics:** Two-layer MLPs predict acceleration and yaw rate for each agent type (vehicles, cyclists, pedestrians). These actions are then fed into a unicycle dynamics model with constraints on maximum yaw rate and acceleration to compute the next physical state (s_t+1).

### 6. C. Training Strategy

The model is trained using reparameterization gradients and back-propagation through time (BPTT).
- **Warm-starting:** For the history steps (t ∈ [−T_h, 0]), teacher-forcing with ground truth (GT) agent states is used.
- **Auto-regressive rollout:** For future steps (t ∈ [1, T_f]), the rollout is auto-regressive.
- **Loss Functions:** A weighted sum of three losses:
 - **Reconstruction Loss:** Smoothed L1 distance for (x, y, v) and cosine distance for yaw (θ) between predicted and GT states.
 - **KL-Divergence Loss:** Between the posterior (z_post) and prior (z_prior) personality distributions, clipped by "free nats" to prevent posterior collapse and encourage diverse latent space learning.
 - **Cross-Entropy Loss:** For destination prediction, framed as a multi-class classification task over map polylines.
- **Gradient Flow Control:** Gradients from the action prediction are stopped, allowing only gradients from the states during BPTT. This aims to focus learning on state dynamics rather than directly on actions for reconstruction.
- **Parallelization:** Training is conducted on all agents to ensure realistic behavior generation across the entire traffic scene.

### 7. D. Implementation Details

The model uses a hidden dimension of 128, resulting in less than 3 million parameters. It supports parallel simulation of multiple episodes (e.g., 16 on a 2080Ti GPU), achieving faster-than-real-time performance (approx. 10 ms per rollout step).

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TrafficBots was conducted using the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD), known for its length, diversity, and complexity of urban driving scenarios. The assessment involved two primary simulation formulations: "a priori simulation" (motion prediction) and "a posteriori simulation" (scenario reconstruction), alongside extensive ablation studies.

### 9. A. Evaluation Tasks and Metrics

- **A priori simulation (Motion Prediction):** Formulated as multi-modal joint future prediction, where TrafficBots control all agents and predict future states given only historical GT. This task is used to evaluate the model's ability to generate plausible and diverse future trajectories. Metrics used are standard WOMD metrics: mAP (mean average precision), minADE (minimum Average Displacement Error), minFDE (minimum Final Displacement Error), miss rate, and overlap rate. Additionally, sampling-based Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) of the GT scene is examined. For leaderboards, K=6 predictions are generated by sampling destination and prior personality.
- **A posteriori simulation:** Here, the model aims to reconstruct the original recorded episode, using the most likely posterior personality and GT destination. This evaluates the model's ability to accurately replicate observed behaviors. Metrics include position/rotation differences to GT, vehicle collision rate, running red light rate, and passiveness rate (stopping without reason).

### 10. B. Comparison with Motion Prediction Methods (Table I & III)

- **WOMD (marginal) leaderboard (Table I):** TrafficBots, when used for a priori simulation (motion prediction), achieved a mAP of 0.212, outperforming the Waymo LSTM baseline (0.176). However, its mAP, minADE (1.313), and minFDE (3.102) were generally lower/higher than state-of-the-art open-loop methods like DenseTNT and SceneTransformer. This performance difference is attributed to compounding errors inherent in the auto-regressive, closed-loop rollout of TrafficBots, a known drawback for such methods. Despite this, TrafficBots exhibited a competitive overlap rate (0.145), suggesting it learns causal relationships effectively.
- **Impact of Additional Information (Table I):** Providing additional ground truth information (e.g., future self-driving car trajectory, future traffic light states, GT destinations) significantly improved prediction performance. The a posteriori simulation, with access to all future GT information, achieved the best performance for a single (K=1) prediction (mAP 0.332, minADE 0.962).
- **WOMD (joint) interactive prediction leaderboard (Table III - Appendix):** For the joint prediction of two interacting agents, TrafficBots demonstrated stronger performance. It achieved a soft mAP of 0.113 and mAP of 0.111, outperforming baselines like Waymo LSTM, HeatIRm4, and Air2, and showing comparable performance to SceneTransformer (J). This indicates the model's proficiency in multi-agent interaction modeling, especially for joint predictions.

### 11. C. Ablation Studies (Table II)

- **State Encoder:** The proposed Angular Encoding (AE) in Eq. 3 for directional information significantly outperformed prior state encoder designs (Eq. 1 and Eq. 2) across most motion prediction and simulation fidelity metrics, highlighting its effectiveness in processing angular data for attention mechanisms.
- **Personality:**
 - Removing personality ("w/o persona") severely degraded motion prediction performance (mAP 0.06), confirming its role in capturing diverse behaviors.
 - Allowing a "larger KL" divergence (by downweighting the KL loss) improved a posteriori simulation but worsened motion prediction, and led to higher traffic rule violations. This suggests that without proper regularization, the posterior might "memorize" the GT future instead of learning underlying behaviors, emphasizing the importance of a time-invariant personality and balanced KL loss.
- **Destination:**
 - Training "w/o dest." (where latent captures all uncertainties) resulted in poorer motion prediction (mAP 0.16) due to mode averaging.
 - Conditioning on a "goal" (polyline closest to last observed pose) instead of a "destination" led to increased traffic rule violations, despite slightly better minADE/FDE. This supports the argument that "goal" can lead to learning incorrect causal relationships in closed-loop policies.
 - "Goal w/o navi" (dropping the goal once reached) resulted in overall inferior performance, indicating the necessity of continuous navigational information.
- **World Model Training Techniques:** Removing "free nats" or allowing "action grad." had minor negative impacts, suggesting the employed techniques contribute to model robustness.
- **Comparison with SimNet and TrafficSim variants:** TrafficBots generally performed better than variations inspired by these prior works. Specific findings included:
 - Models without personality had smaller NLL, implying less diverse predictions.
 - Models that "resample pers." (personality at each time step) performed better for a posteriori simulation but worse for motion prediction and had higher rule violations, further validating the time-invariant personality.
 - TrafficSim's interactive decoder showed a smaller miss rate in a posteriori simulation but required knowledge of all agents' personalities and destinations beforehand, limiting its general applicability.

### 12. D. Qualitative Results (Fig. 5)

Qualitative examples illustrated that TrafficBots can generate diverse and plausible a priori predictions, with at least one prediction often closely matching the ground truth. In a posteriori simulations, the model accurately reconstructed the recorded scenarios with minimal deviation, demonstrating its ability to simulate complex maneuvers like U-turns and pedestrian crossings while maintaining realism.

### 13. Significance and Potential Impact

The TrafficBots research makes several contributions to the field of autonomous driving simulation and related areas, with notable potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Data-Driven Traffic Simulation:** This work introduces a novel approach to data-driven traffic simulation by framing it as a "world model," a concept largely explored in reinforcement learning. This formulation enables a fully differentiable simulation environment, which is a significant step towards developing more sophisticated and trainable simulators that can evolve alongside AD algorithms.
- **Enhanced Realism, Configurability, and Scalability:** TrafficBots addresses key limitations of existing simulators:
 - **Realism:** By learning from real-world datasets and incorporating mechanisms for diverse behaviors (personality), it generates more human-like and interactive traffic scenarios than hand-crafted rules or basic log-replay.
 - **Configurability:** The introduction of "destination" and "latent personality" provides granular control over bot agents' intentions and behavioral styles, enabling researchers to design specific, challenging scenarios for AD planning.
 - **Scalability:** Utilizing vectorized contexts, shared policies, and the novel Angular Encoding for efficient global-to-local information processing, TrafficBots demonstrates the ability to simulate dense urban environments with numerous interacting agents faster than real-time.
- **Improved Tool for AD Planning Development:** By offering a realistic, configurable, and differentiable simulation platform, TrafficBots provides a safer and more cost-effective alternative to real-world testing for developing and validating AD planning algorithms. This can accelerate the iteration cycle for AD research and development.
- **Bridging Research Fields:** The work successfully integrates concepts from multi-modal motion prediction (CVAEs, Transformer architectures) and end-to-end driving (goal-conditioned policies) within a world model framework, fostering cross-disciplinary advancements.
- **Standardized and Reproducible Evaluation:** By proposing the formulation of motion prediction as "a priori simulation," the research provides a clear and reproducible evaluation protocol using established metrics on public leaderboards (like WOMD). This contributes to a more objective comparison of simulation fidelity across different methods.
- **Potential for Future AD System Training:** As a differentiable world model, TrafficBots holds the potential to serve as a platform for training end-to-end driving policies through reinforcement learning or model-based imitation learning. This could enable AD systems to learn robust behaviors by interacting with realistic simulated traffic.
- **Modularity and Integration:** TrafficBots can be integrated as a module within larger, full-stack AD simulators or game environments to provide more convincing and adaptive human-like behaviors for non-player characters.

In summary, TrafficBots represents a step towards creating more effective, data-driven simulation environments that can realistically challenge and train autonomous driving systems, thereby contributing to their safer and more efficient deployment.
