# AutoDesign: Meta-Harness Optimization for Long-Horizon Agentic Design

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoDesign is a meta-harness optimization framework where an outer-loop optimizer recursively rewrites a design harness (context, tools, runtime, orchestration, evaluation) surrounding a fixed LLM, using rollout trajectories and an acceptance gate. Instantiated for paper-to-poster generation, it beats commercial design agents and lifts diverse coding-agent/model pairs by 5-19.6 points.

## Problem

- Multimodal design systems rely on cycles of generation, critique, and revision but treat human-aligned feedback as transient per-response signal, never accumulating it as reusable system capability.
- Prior agentic-memory methods store reflections or skills across attempts but stop short of updating the harness that repeatedly produces outputs, so gains do not persist across tasks.
- Existing paper-to-poster benchmarks isolate single aspects like layout, extraction, faithfulness, or visual quality, lacking a joint protocol for evidence preservation, dense communication, and rendered usability.

## Method

- **Meta-harness optimizer**: an outer loop that runs the current harness on training tasks, evaluates trajectories, and proposes one bounded update to a single functional component per iteration via a planner-then-code-editor coding agent.
- ==Acceptance gate==: promotes a candidate harness only when it improves the training objective while not degrading a held-out development-set objective, guarding against overfitting the harness to seen tasks.
- **DesignHarness**: the evolved inner-loop system with paper ingestion, an editable-HTML designer, a rule-based validator plus ==VLM visual critic==, and a finalization stage that keeps the best valid candidate across up to 12 refinement attempts.

## Results

- On the 100-paper PosterBench Main Track, AutoDesign scores **78.32**, exceeding closed-source Claude Design by **7.45** points and OpenDesign by **8.87** points under matched Claude Code/Claude 4.8 configuration.
- Attaching DesignHarness across **seven** coding-agent/model pairs raises average PosterBench-mini score from **54.99** to **67.39**, with gains ranging **5.0** to **19.6** points, e.g. DeepSeek V4 Pro rises **34.73** to **54.29**.
- In a system-blind human study of **936** pairwise judgments, AutoDesign attains the highest Bradley-Terry win probability at **64.0%** (95% CI 55.2-77.8%), and benchmark-human agreement rises from **51.9%** to **74.4%** as score gaps widen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing the surrounding harness while freezing model weights is a distinct, cheap lever for capability gains, separate from and complementary to model post-training.
> - A train-gain-plus-dev-no-regression acceptance gate is a simple, generalizable safeguard against a self-editing system overfitting to its own optimization traces.
> - Restricting each optimization step to one functional component keeps credit assignment interpretable, a design principle transferable to any recursively self-improving agent system.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of transforming diverse multimodal information into structured, human-facing artifacts such as webpages, slides, posters, and videos. This process involves extracting relevant evidence, reasoning over heterogeneous information, planning intermediate steps, and iteratively refining outputs based on feedback. The paper positions this as a long-horizon agentic task, acknowledging its complexity in real-world workflows and the traditional reliance on human feedback.

Existing multimodal design systems often attempt to align with human design principles through cycles of generation, critique, and revision. At the response level, feedback typically revises the current output, while some agentic systems retain reflections, skills, or task experience across attempts. However, a gap is identified where these systems treat individual human-aligned feedback as transient signals rather rather than accumulating it as reusable design knowledge for persistent system-level improvement. The paper highlights an unresolved question regarding how to convert multimodal evidence, structural constraints, feedback, and human preferences into persistent, design-aligned capabilities of the production system itself.

AutoDesign introduces meta-harness optimization as a method to address this gap. It differentiates itself from related work that focuses on optimizing individual components or declarative systems (e.g., TextGrad, DSPy, GEPA) or searching for code- or graph-represented workflows (e.g., STOP, GPTSwarm, ADAS, AFlow). Instead, AutoDesign aims to update a *reusable design harness* itself from rollout records and evaluation outcomes. This aligns with recent research that treats operational "harnesses" as optimization targets distinct from the underlying model weights, contributing to the broader field of self-improving agents and adaptive AI systems. The work instantiates this framework by focusing on the specific task of academic paper-to-poster generation, which necessitates condensing complex, multimodal scientific information into a legible and visually coherent format while preserving traceable evidence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a framework, AutoDesign, that transforms a static design harness into a recursively improving system for human-aligned multimodal design. This framework aims to enable an agentic system to continuously optimize its own design harness based on evaluations grounded in human preferences.

The motivation stems from several identified problems and gaps in current multimodal design paradigms:

- **Problem of Static Design Systems**: Traditional multimodal design systems deliver isolated outputs or require individual edits, lacking the capacity for persistent, human-aligned production. A robust system should preserve source evidence, effectively communicate dense content, and produce editable, usable artifacts.
- **Lack of Reusable Design Knowledge**: Existing agentic systems, while capable of self-reflection or skill acquisition, tend to treat human feedback as transient signals. Unlike human designers who continuously learn from successes and failures, these systems do not systematically accumulate design knowledge to persistently enhance their underlying capabilities. This leads to inefficient re-learning or manual interventions for recurring design challenges.
- **Limited Evaluation Benchmarks**: For specific complex tasks like academic paper-to-poster generation, existing benchmarks often inspect isolated aspects such as layout, extraction accuracy, faithfulness, or visual quality. There is a need for a comprehensive evaluation protocol that jointly assesses crucial aspects like evidence preservation, dense scientific communication, and the rendered usability of the artifact as a whole.
- **Need for Aligned Capabilities**: The core motivation is to convert disparate design elements—multimodal evidence, structural constraints, various forms of feedback, and human preferences—into stable, design-aligned capabilities that are integrated into the production system itself.

To address these, AutoDesign's key objectives are:
1. To frame human-aligned design generation as a *meta-harness optimization problem*, where the system itself recursively improves its design harness.
2. To instantiate and evaluate this framework on a challenging, long-horizon task: academic paper-to-poster generation.
3. To introduce a comprehensive benchmark, PosterBench, that covers the joint requirements of source fidelity, dense scientific communication, and rendered usability for this task.
4. To develop an executable system, DesignHarness, that aligns with human design priors and can autonomously produce high-quality, editable conference posters.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoDesign is structured around a meta-harness optimization framework comprising two nested feedback loops: an inner loop for artifact generation and an outer loop for harness optimization.

#### 3.1. Design Harness (Inner Loop)

The inner loop represents the design harness (H), which transforms a multimodal source (e.g., an academic paper) into a human-facing artifact. It operates iteratively, generating and revising an artifact based on critic feedback. The harness is formally decomposed into five functional components to enable systematic optimization and credit assignment:
- **Context and Memory**: Manages source material, prompts, skills, reusable assets, and persistent state.
- **Tools and Specifications**: Defines tools and editable artifact specifications for layout, typography, and provenance.
- **Execution Runtime**: Provides the workspace and runtime for authoring, rendering, validating, and exporting artifacts.
- **Orchestration**: Handles task routing, attempt budgets, loop control, candidate selection, fallback mechanisms, and finalization.
- **Evaluation and Feedback**: Incorporates rule-based validation, model-based critique, and localized feedback for revision.

The inner loop is initialized with a minimal scaffold consisting of a designer module (M_design) for generation and revision, and a critic module (M_critic) for evaluation and feedback provision. Artifacts are maintained as editable HTML files, allowing for localized code edits rather than full regeneration.

#### 3.2. Meta-Harness Optimizer (Outer Loop)

The outer loop is responsible for improving the design harness across multiple tasks. Each iteration involves four stages:

1. **Rollout**: The current design harness (H_t) is executed on a training dataset (D_train). Each execution generates an artifact and an execution trajectory (τ_t), which records the sequence of actions, states, and revisions.
2. **Evaluation**: An evaluator (R_meta), instantiated as a coding agent and initialized from human-annotated reference artifacts, assesses the quality of each generated artifact. R_meta combines rule-based checks for measurable properties with Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based judgments for perceptual properties. This R_meta is distinct from the final PosterBench evaluation protocol and remains fixed during autonomous optimization.
3. **Update Proposal**: A meta-harness optimizer (P), implemented as a coding agent, analyzes H_t, the collected trajectories (τ_t), evaluation scores (s_t), and an optimization record (L). P functions in two roles:
 - **Planner**: Analyzes recurrent failures from trajectories and scores, synthesizing findings into a structured update plan. The plan specifies failure modes, the harness component to be modified, and intended changes.
 - **Code Editor**: Implements these changes within the selected harness component, producing a candidate updated harness (H'_t+1). A critical constraint is that each outer-loop iteration is restricted to modifying exactly one of the five functional components of the design harness, facilitating interpretable credit assignment.
4. **Acceptance Gate**: The candidate harness (H'_t+1) is accepted and promoted to H_t+1 only if its performance on the training set (J_train) improves and its performance on an independent development set (D_dev) does not decline. The development set serves as a guard against overfitting. If the conditions are not met, H_t is retained. The optimization record (L) stores details of each iteration, supporting reproducibility and informed future proposals.

#### 3.3. Human-in-the-Loop (Optional)

AutoDesign allows for optional human intervention. Users can provide natural language guidance to the planner to redirect the search or inject heuristics, particularly if autonomous optimization stagnates. Human input can also be used to revise the R_meta evaluator if systematic biases are observed.

#### 3.4. The Optimized DesignHarness

The meta-harness optimization process yields DesignHarness, an executable system exemplified by its application to academic paper-to-poster generation. Its architecture, derived from the optimization, includes:
- **Paper Ingestion**: Extracts metadata, section outlines, key passages, figures, and tables from the input paper, retaining source provenance. These are organized into a content brief and a medium-specific artifact plan.
- **Artifact Generation and Revision**: A coding agent (the designer module) generates or revises editable HTML artifacts. These can be rendered into previews (PNG, PPTX, MP4) for visual critique. The system permits up to 12 refinement attempts.
- **Validation and Finalization**:
 - **Rule-based validator**: Applies deterministic checks for safety, provenance links, layout overflows, and typographic constraints. Provides localized diagnostics for violations.
 - **Critic VLM**: Inspects rendered previews for compliance with design context, layout, readability, and aesthetics.
 - Feedback from both sources is consolidated and passed to the designer for revision. If a candidate passes all blocking checks, it proceeds to finalization, which includes rendering adjustments, typesetting, and inlining assets. Fallback mechanisms are employed if the attempt budget is exhausted.

#### 3.5. PosterBench Evaluation Protocol

To evaluate the systems, PosterBench was introduced as a comprehensive benchmark:
- **Benchmark Datasets**: Includes a 100-paper Main Track spanning five disciplines and PosterBench-mini, a 10-paper subset for controlled evaluations.
- **Evaluation Rubric**: A seven-dimensional rubric assesses Faithfulness, Coverage, Density, Visual Evidence, Layout, Readability, and Aesthetics.
- **Scoring**: A weighted rubric score (R_rubric) is calculated, followed by the application of strict, record-level ceiling penalties (C_layout, C_viability, C_failure, C_gate) for severe issues, to produce the final R_poster score. The overall score is the mean of these capped scores.
- **Evaluator Design**: PosterBench combines rule-based programmatic checks (for spatial properties, OCR text, numeric grounding, render integrity) with VLM-based judgments (for visual evidence use, readability, and aesthetics). Importantly, PosterBench is a *frozen, external evaluator*, distinct from the R_meta used in the meta-harness optimization loop.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AutoDesign involved quantitative benchmarks, ablation studies, cost-performance analysis, and human evaluation.

- **Meta-Harness Optimization Trajectory**: The autonomous optimization process demonstrated a substantial improvement in the design harness's performance. The score of a generated poster for a representative paper increased from an initial 49.00 to a plateau of 80.88. Subsequent human guidance further improved the score to 88.39, indicating that human intervention could redirect the search and enhance system performance beyond autonomous limits. Over a period of 7 days, the optimization involved 224 subagents, recorded at least 123 recursive iterations, and accumulated 54 harness updates.

- **Performance on PosterBench Main Track**: AutoDesign achieved the highest PosterBench Score of 78.32 on the 100-paper Main Track when configured with DesignHarness, Claude Code, and Claude 4.8. This score surpassed the closed-source commercial system Claude Design by 7.45 points (70.87) and OpenDesign by 8.87 points (69.45) under matched configurations. When using DesignHarness with Codex and GPT-5.5, AutoDesign scored 77.97.

- **Effect of DesignHarness (Ablation Studies)**: Integrating the learned DesignHarness consistently improved performance across various coding agent-model configurations on PosterBench-mini (10-paper subset). The average PosterBench Score increased from 54.99 to 67.39 (+12.40 points). Individual gains ranged from 5.01 to 19.56 points. For example, with the native Codex-GPT-5.5 configuration, the score rose from 75.87 to 81.46 (+5.59 points). With Claude Code and Kimi K2.7, the score increased from 57.20 to 70.12 (+12.92 points). The largest gain was observed for DeepSeek V4 Pro, increasing from 34.73 to 54.29 (+19.56 points).

- **Autonomous Operation Efficiency**: In a fully autonomous long-horizon loop, DesignHarness completed a poster generation run by executing 253 tool calls and 11 editing turns within approximately 40 minutes, at a cost of under $3. This process resulted in an output quality deemed comparable to average conference posters in human evaluation.

- **Cost-Performance Trade-off**: An analysis on PosterBench-mini revealed a Pareto frontier for cost versus performance across seven AutoDesign model configurations. LongCat-2.0 achieved a score of 55.13 at an estimated cost of $0.27 per poster. Doubao Seed 2.1 Pro scored 71.83 at approximately $2.75. Claude 4.8 scored 74.56 at $7.63, and GPT-5.5 achieved the highest performance at 81.46, costing $10.02. This indicated that Doubao could achieve 88% of GPT-5.5's score at 27% of its cost.

- **Human Evaluation**: A system-blind human study involving 11 reviewers and 936 responses demonstrated that AutoDesign achieved the highest human preference among the evaluated systems. Its Bradley–Terry preference estimate was 64.0% (with a 95% confidence interval of 55.2–77.8%) of beating a randomly sampled alternative. In head-to-head comparisons, AutoDesign was preferred over Claude Code in 61.3% of cases, OpenDesign in 63.1%, and Claude Design in 67.6%.
 - **Benchmark-Human Alignment**: The study also indicated a positive correlation (r = 0.34, 95% CI: [0.22, 0.44]) between a poster’s PosterBench Score and its aggregated human preference. Furthermore, the probability of human agreement with the PosterBench-preferred poster increased with the score margin: from 51.9% for 0–3-point score gaps to 74.4% for gaps of at least 20 points.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AutoDesign framework and its instantiated DesignHarness represent advancements in agentic AI for multimodal design, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Paradigm Shift in Agentic Design**: The work introduces meta-harness optimization, a method that allows a design system to recursively improve its own operational harness rather than just refining individual outputs or optimizing model weights. This contributes a new approach to building more adaptive and continuously learning agentic systems, enabling them to accumulate human-aligned design priors and experience over time.

- **Enhanced Multimodal Output Generation**: For the specific task of academic paper-to-poster generation, DesignHarness demonstrates the ability to autonomously produce high-quality, editable, and source-grounded conference posters. This capability can significantly reduce the manual effort and time required for creating such artifacts, making scientific communication more efficient and accessible. The ability to produce editable outputs facilitates direct use and localized revisions, addressing a common practical need.

- **Comprehensive Evaluation Standard**: The introduction of PosterBench provides a multi-dimensional and rigorously defined evaluation protocol for paper-to-poster generation. By combining programmatic checks with VLM-based judgments across dimensions like faithfulness, coverage, density, and aesthetics, and validating it through human studies, PosterBench offers a robust benchmark that can guide future research and development in multimodal design.

- **Cost-Effective High-Quality Production**: The analysis of cost-performance trade-offs indicates that high-quality multimodal design outputs are achievable at varying cost points. For instance, achieving a notable score with models like LongCat-2.0 at a low cost per poster suggests increased accessibility of advanced design automation to a broader range of users and institutions.

- **Generalizability and Future Expansion**: While validated for paper-to-poster generation, the underlying agentic design pattern of AutoDesign is presented as adaptable to other multimodal output types, including presentation slides, webpages, and conference videos. The paper suggests that shared context construction, preference memories, and repair histories could provide a foundation for reusing experience across different media, pointing towards a future of general multimodal-in, multimodal-out agentic design systems.

- **Research Directions in Agentic AI**: The work identifies open research problems such as optimizing component selection for updates and evolving adaptive evaluators. It also highlights the potential for model-harness co-evolution, where long-horizon trajectories and repair outcomes could provide execution-time supervision for both harness adaptation and future model post-training, thus contributing to the discourse on separating and integrating learning signals in complex AI systems.
