# The One RING: a Robotic Indoor Navigation Generalist

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Washington and the Allen Institute for AI developed The One RING, a generalist robotic policy for indoor navigation that achieves zero-shot transfer to diverse, unseen robot embodiments. Trained exclusively in simulation using large-scale embodiment randomization, the policy demonstrated an average 78.9% success rate on real-world robots and adapted navigation strategies based on inferred physical characteristics.

## Problem

- Most existing robotic navigation policies are designed for specific robot configurations, leading to significant performance degradation or failure when deployed on different hardware.
- Developing specialized policies for each new robot embodiment is costly and time-consuming, hindering rapid deployment and scalability.
- Previous cross-embodiment approaches often rely on limited real-world datasets or struggle with robust generalization to truly novel or out-of-distribution robot designs.

## Method

- ==Developed RING==, an embodiment-agnostic policy, by training it entirely in simulation across 1 million distinct, randomly sampled robot body and sensor configurations within the AI2-THOR environment.
- Utilized a deep learning architecture with a frozen pre-trained visual encoder, a goal encoder, and a transformer state encoder, combined with a causal transformer decoder for explicit memory modeling over time.
- Employed a two-stage training paradigm: initial large-scale imitation learning on expert trajectories followed by on-policy reinforcement learning finetuning using DD-PPO.

## Results

- Achieved an average zero-shot success rate of **72.1%** across five unseen simulated embodiments, an absolute improvement of **16.7%** over the CHORES-S benchmark.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer to four distinct real-world robotic platforms (Stretch RE-1, LoCoBot, Unitree Go1, RB-Y1) with an average success rate of **78.9%**, often outperforming specialized policies.
- Successfully generalized to human participants acting as navigation 'embodiments', achieving a **73.3%** average success rate and showcasing adaptive behavior such as navigating under furniture based on inferred robot size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale embodiment randomization in simulation is an effective method for achieving robust zero-shot generalization to diverse and unseen real-world robot platforms.
> - The policy can implicitly infer its own embodiment characteristics from visual inputs and environmental interactions, adapting navigation strategies without explicit body parameter knowledge.
> - Combining imitation learning with reinforcement learning provides a strong foundation for generalist navigation, enabling high zero-shot performance and efficient adaptation to specialized policies.
