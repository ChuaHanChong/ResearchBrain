# UniETP: Unifying Environments for Generalizable Embodied Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> UniETP provides a unified framework for embodied task planning by integrating four diverse 3D simulators under a consistent interface and establishing a benchmark with 536 complex task instances across five evaluation splits. The work demonstrates that supervised fine-tuning on its dataset significantly improves model performance and generalization, with proprietary models generally achieving the highest scores while revealing challenges in instance grounding and long-horizon planning.

## Problem

- Existing embodied task planning (ETP) simulators and benchmarks suffer from fragmentation, using distinct observation formats, action spaces, and evaluation protocols.
- This isolation complicates comprehensive model evaluation and hinders the scalability of training data, making it difficult to develop generalizable embodied agents.
- Models trained for one specific environment often lack transferability to others, limiting their applicability across diverse scenarios.

## Method

- Developed **UniETP**, a ==two-layered framework== that unifies four mainstream embodied simulators (AI2-THOR, VirtualHome, Habitat, BEHAVIOR) through a consistent Python-based environment interface.
- Established a standardized ==evaluation protocol with three modes (M1, M2, M3)== that systematically vary scene prior information, action granularity, and perceptual demands.
- Created a comprehensive benchmark dataset of 536 task instances, generated from 138 templates across ==five evaluation splits== (Atomic, Compositional, Logical, Linguistic, Instance Grounding), along with a larger 9,000-task training set.

## Results

- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on UniETP's training set significantly improved the performance of smaller models (e.g., Qwen3.5-4B), outperforming larger pretrained models and demonstrating improved generalization to unseen simulators and task types.
- Performance consistently decreased as the number of required action steps increased, underscoring limitations in long-horizon planning for current VLM-based agents.
- An ablation study confirmed the critical importance of binary action success indicators, while textual failure feedback had a minor impact, suggesting agents struggle to infer action success solely from visual observations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprietary models, particularly Gemini variants, generally achieve the highest performance on UniETP tasks, while open-source embodied foundation models underperform general-purpose VLMs of similar size.
> - Reducing scene prior information and restricting action granularity (M1 to M2) leads to a substantial and consistent performance drop across all models, indicating significant challenges in planning and object discovery.
> - Instance Grounding tasks pose the greatest difficulty for current agents, with even top-performing models reaching only about 50% success, highlighting struggles with fine-grained visual discrimination and reliance on object categories over specific instance attributes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence is increasingly adopting hierarchical architectures, where high-level modules are responsible for task understanding and planning, while low-level controllers manage fine-grained motor execution. Embodied Task Planning (ETP) is a subfield that addresses the high-level decision-making process, requiring an agent to interpret natural language instructions and generate a sequence of atomic actions within an interactive environment. This area is considered crucial for enabling embodied agents to accomplish complex, long-horizon tasks, especially with the advancement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

A variety of interactive 3D simulators, such as AI2-THOR, VirtualHome, Habitat, and BEHAVIOR, have been developed or repurposed for ETP research. These simulators serve as foundational environments for numerous benchmarks and datasets, including ALFRED, ALFWorld, VisualAgentBench, LoTa-Bench, and PARTNR, which define evaluation protocols and task specifications.

Despite these developments, a critical challenge in the ETP landscape is the fragmentation across environments. Existing simulators and the benchmarks built upon them typically utilize distinct observation formats, action spaces, and evaluation protocols. This isolation complicates comprehensive model evaluation, hinders the scalability of training data, and impedes the transferability of agents designed or tuned for one dataset to another. The varying characteristics of simulators in terms of scene scale, visual appearance, physical rules, and object interactions mean that bridging these gaps could enable models to be trained and evaluated on a more diverse set of tasks, thereby fostering improved generalization ability in embodied agents. UniETP is presented as an initiative to address this fragmentation by integrating multiple simulators under a unified framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to address the challenge of isolation and fragmentation that exists across various simulators and datasets used in embodied task planning (ETP). The researchers identified that the disparate interfaces, observation and action spaces, and evaluation protocols of existing environments complicate comprehensive model evaluation and hinder the development of generalizable embodied agents.

To achieve this overarching goal, UniETP outlines several specific objectives:

- **Unified Environment Interface**: A core motivation is to integrate four commonly-used, yet distinct, embodied simulators—AI2-THOR, VirtualHome, Habitat, and BEHAVIOR—into a single, consistent environment layer. This unification aims to standardize the observation and action spaces, allowing agents to interact with diverse environments through a common interface, thereby decoupling agent design from simulator-specific implementations.
- **Standardized Evaluation Protocol**: The project aims to establish a unified evaluation system capable of assessing agents against complex task goals. This includes supporting multi-granularity observations and actions, as well as providing mechanisms to systematically adjust task difficulty, which can facilitate a more nuanced understanding of agent capabilities.
- **Enhanced Task Diversity and Complexity**: A significant objective is to construct a new dataset that not only combines tasks from multiple simulators but also introduces greater diversity and complexity across several dimensions. This includes:
 - **Task Logic**: Incorporating complex logical structures in task goals, such as universal quantification, temporal ordering, and numerical comparisons, moving beyond simple goal-state-based evaluations.
 - **Instruction Understanding**: Designing tasks with linguistically challenging instructions that may involve complex references, aliases, contextual descriptions, distractors, or intricate syntactic structures.
 - **Instance-level Grounding**: Requiring agents to identify and interact with specific object instances based on visual attributes or spatial relations, rather than merely relying on category-level requirements.
- **Comprehensive Model Benchmarking**: The researchers aimed to conduct extensive experiments on the proposed benchmark to evaluate the embodied planning capabilities of a wide range of current models, including proprietary, open-source, and embodied foundation models. This is motivated by the need to identify current performance bottlenecks and limitations of VLM-based agents in interactive, long-horizon scenarios.
- **Facilitate Generalization**: Ultimately, the work is motivated by the desire to enable the development of embodied agents with stronger generalization abilities. By providing a standardized, diverse, and scalable benchmark, UniETP seeks to foster training methodologies that are more robust to variations in environments and tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniETP is designed as a two-layered framework: an environment layer for simulator unification and a task layer for diverse task generation.

#### 3.1. The Environment Layer

The environment layer focuses on unifying four mainstream embodied simulators (AI2-THOR, VirtualHome, Habitat, BEHAVIOR) through a Python-based environment interface. This interface abstracts away simulator-specific implementations, providing a consistent interaction framework for agents.

- **Unified Observation Space**: UniETP defines a standardized observation space, `o_uni_t = (l_t, g, f_t)`, comprising three components:
 - **Local View (`l_t`)**: Primarily an egocentric RGB image. Optionally, a list of bounding boxes (bboxes) for visible objects, each associated with an object ID and pixel coordinates, can be provided to separate visual perception from higher-level reasoning.
 - **Global Information (`g`)**: This component is configurable to control task difficulty. It can include lists of rooms, static objects (e.g., furniture), and/or movable objects in the scene. Access to more global information simplifies exploration and planning.
 - **Feedback (`f_t`)**: A binary signal indicating the success or failure of the previous action. For common failure types (e.g., invalid arguments, out-of-reach objects), a short explanatory message is included.
- **Unified Action Space**: The action space, `A_uni`, employs a parameterized formulation (action type + arguments) to avoid combinatorial growth. It merges over 30 action categories, aligning equivalent semantics across simulators, categorized into:
 - **Navigation Actions**:
 - *Movement*: Basic locomotion (move, rotate).
 - *Reaching*: Turning and moving close to a visible object.
 - *Teleportation*: Turning and moving close to any specified object or room in the scene. These granularities affect object search and scene exploration difficulty.
 - **Manipulation Actions**:
 - *Atomic Actions*: Non-decomposable primitive operations.
 - *Composite Actions*: High-level operations realized by multiple atomic steps (e.g., `heat with (potato, microwave)`). Composite actions allow control over planning abstraction.
 - **`stop` Action**: Allows the agent to signal task completion, rather than waiting for an external oracle. Episodes also terminate if maximum steps (H) or maximum failed steps (H') are reached.
- **Unified Scene Graph (USG) and Evaluation Logic**: A simulator-agnostic USG represents the environment, with nodes for objects and rooms (including ID, category, properties, states, location) and edges for spatial relations (e.g., `on`, `inside`, `grasping`). The USG is constructed from privileged simulator information and updates after each action. It is not exposed to the agent but serves as the foundation for task evaluation and data generation. Task goals (`L`) are formalized as logic formulas over USG traces, incorporating first-order predicates, Boolean connectives, quantifiers, temporal operators, and counting aggregates.

- **Evaluation Protocol**: UniETP defines three evaluation modes (M1, M2, M3) with varying difficulty:
 - **M1**: Provides full global information (all rooms/objects), all action categories (movement, reaching, teleportation to any object/room, atomic/composite manipulation), with arguments specified by object/room IDs.
 - **M2**: Provides partial global information (rooms, static objects only), restricts teleportation to rooms/static objects, removes composite manipulation actions, and uses ID-based arguments.
 - **M3**: Provides minimal global information (rooms only), restricts teleportation to rooms, removes composite manipulation, and requires object-targeted actions (except teleportation) to use bounding box-based arguments. The local view only provides an image, without bboxes.

#### 3.2. The Task Layer

The task layer supports the generation of diverse and complex task instances.

- **Task Generation Pipeline**:
 - **Task Templates**: Manually designed (138 types), each including a task name, a parameterized goal schema, domain constraints (e.g., `A` must be openable for `open one <A>`), and initial state constraints.
 - **Commonsense Affordance Knowledge Base (AKB)**: Built for each simulator, listing object categories, states, functionalities, and feasible spatial relations. Used in conjunction with domain constraints to instantiate task templates with concrete object categories.
 - **Task Grounding**:
 - A simulator is selected, and a scene is initialized. Initial state constraints are enforced by adding/configuring task-relevant objects.
 - A scripted expert policy (`π*`) attempts task completion to validate feasibility, retaining only completable tasks.
 - A Large Language Model (LLM) generates natural-language instructions (`I`) based on the task template, goal formula, and instantiated scene.
 - This process yields `τ = <I, s_i_0, L, E_i>`, an annotated, grounded task instance.
- **Evaluation Dimensions (Splits)**: The benchmark tasks are categorized into five splits to test specific agent capabilities:
 - **Atomic**: Single predicate goals, basic object state/relation understanding.
 - **Compositional**: Conjunctions of multiple atomic tasks, requiring long-horizon planning.
 - **Logical**: Tasks with universal quantification, temporal ordering, counting, and numerical comparisons.
 - **Linguistic**: Complex natural language instructions with indirect references, aliases, distractors, or complex syntax.
 - **Instance Grounding**: Requires selecting specific object instances based on visual attributes or spatial relations.
- **Benchmark Statistics**: The current UniETP version integrates AI2-THOR, VirtualHome, Habitat, and BEHAVIOR, covering 170 scene layouts. Scenes average 7 rooms and 210 objects from various categories. The action space contains 38/32/32 action types for M1/M2/M3, respectively. The benchmark dataset includes 536 task instances generated from 138 templates, validated by an expert policy. A larger training set of 9,000 tasks is also generated for model fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The study benchmarks a range of models, including proprietary models (GPT, Gemini, Claude), popular open models (Qwen, InternVL, Gemma), and embodied foundation models (RoboBrain, EmbodiedBrain, Vlaser). A ReAct-style pipeline is employed for closed-loop interaction. Inputs to the VLM include action space description, instruction, global information, current local observation (image and optional bboxes), and interaction history. Models generate optional "thinking" and actions in JSON format. Maximum steps (H) are 50/10 for M1/M2 and 150/30 for M3 (total/failed steps). Task success rate (SR) is the primary metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments on UniETP reveal several insights into the performance and limitations of VLM-based embodied agents.

- **Overall Model Performance**: Proprietary models generally achieved the highest performance, with Gemini-er-1.6 demonstrating the strongest average score in M1 and Gemini-3.1-pro in M2. Among open-source VLMs, a correlation was observed between increased model size and improved performance. Medium-sized open models, such as Qwen3.6-27B, showed performance relatively close to some closed-source counterparts. In contrast, existing open-source embodied foundation models generally underperformed general-purpose VLMs of comparable or smaller sizes, suggesting that their specialized fine-tuning may not transfer effectively to UniETP's closed-loop, multi-turn, and long-horizon evaluation setting.

- **Difficulty Across Evaluation Modes**: A substantial and consistent performance drop was observed across all models when transitioning from M1 to M2. This indicates that M2, with its reduced scene prior (no global information on movable objects) and more primitive actions (no composite actions, restricted teleportation), presents a considerably more challenging planning problem. For M3, evaluated only on the Atomic split with a subset of closed-source models, a further increase in difficulty was noted. This highlights the additional challenges introduced by requiring detailed navigation decisions and object grounding via bounding boxes from image-only observations.

- **Performance Across Task Splits**:
 - **Atomic** tasks were consistently the easiest for models.
 - **Compositional** tasks led to a clear performance degradation, indicating that handling compound goals and longer horizons significantly increases planning difficulty.
 - **Logical** and **Linguistic** splits did not show a consistent, proportional decrease in performance relative to the Compositional split, suggesting that for zero-shot VLM-based agents, understanding complex logical relations or intricate linguistic descriptions may not be the primary bottleneck.
 - **Instance Grounding** was identified as the most challenging split. Even the highest-performing models achieved only about 50% success in M1, indicating that current agents struggle with fine-grained visual discrimination and often rely on object categories rather than specific instance attributes or spatial relationships.

- **Performance Across Simulators**: VirtualHome proved to be the easiest domain within the benchmark, primarily attributed to fewer interactable objects and a smaller set of executable action categories. In M1, AI2-THOR presented the highest difficulty, likely due to its richer object states and more diverse tool affordances. However, under M2, performance on Habitat and BEHAVIOR dropped substantially, a factor attributed to their larger scene scales which necessitate extensive exploration and object discovery for movable target objects.

- **Impact of Horizon**: Performance consistently decreased as the number of required action steps increased, demonstrating that models become less reliable for tasks demanding longer planning horizons and larger interaction contexts. M2 episodes often reached the maximum step limit, frequently due to failures in object discovery.

- **Ablation Study on Action Feedback**: An ablation study using Qwen3.6-27B showed that removing textual failure feedback had a minor impact on overall performance. This was attributed to stronger models inferring causes of failure, while weaker models struggled to recover regardless of the feedback. Conversely, removing the binary action success indicator resulted in a noticeable performance drop. This suggests that current agents face difficulties in autonomously inferring action success purely from visual observations, underscoring the importance of explicit post-verification mechanisms in embodied systems.

- **Training and Generalization Analysis**: Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the UniETP training set significantly improved the performance of a smaller model (Qwen3.5-4B), outperforming larger pretrained models. This validates the effectiveness of the data generation and supervision pipeline. Furthermore, models fine-tuned with data that excluded a specific simulator (e.g., AI2-THOR) or a subset of task templates still demonstrated improved generalization to these unseen environments or task types. These results suggest that unifying simulator interfaces and providing diverse training data can contribute to stronger generalization capabilities in embodied agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of UniETP addresses a notable limitation in embodied task planning research: the fragmentation and lack of interoperability across diverse simulation environments. By providing a unified interface that integrates four prominent simulators (AI2-THOR, VirtualHome, Habitat, BEHAVIOR), UniETP offers a standardized platform for developing and evaluating embodied agents. This standardization is significant because it allows researchers to train and test models in a consistent manner across environments that vary in scale, visual realism, physical properties, and interaction complexities.

The detailed evaluation protocol, with its three modes (M1, M2, M3), enables fine-grained assessment of agent capabilities by systematically controlling the availability of scene priors, action granularity, and perceptual demands. This facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of what an agent "knows" and "sees," disentangling core planning abilities from perception and exploration challenges. Such a structured evaluation is crucial for identifying specific bottlenecks in agent performance.

The automatic task generation pipeline is a substantial contribution, allowing for the creation of a diverse and scalable dataset. By incorporating task templates that emphasize complex logic, intricate linguistic instructions, and instance-level grounding, UniETP pushes beyond simpler task definitions common in prior benchmarks. The findings highlight specific areas of difficulty for current VLM-based agents, particularly in instance grounding and long-horizon planning, thereby pointing to concrete directions for future research in spatial modeling, fine-grained visual recognition, and robust reasoning under uncertainty.

The empirical results demonstrating that supervised fine-tuning on UniETP's diverse dataset significantly improves model performance and generalization across simulators and task types underscore the framework's potential to foster more robust and transferable embodied agents. This addresses the critical need for agents that can operate effectively in varied real-world scenarios rather than being confined to specific simulated environments.

In summary, UniETP provides a valuable resource for the embodied AI community. It not only offers a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating current models but also establishes a framework that can accelerate the development of generalizable embodied agents by reducing fragmentation and enabling systematic analysis of their capabilities and limitations. The open-source availability of code and data further amplifies its potential impact by encouraging wider adoption and collaborative research.
