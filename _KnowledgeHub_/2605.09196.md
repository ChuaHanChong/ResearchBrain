# RigidFormer: Learning Rigid Dynamics using Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MIT and Meta developed RigidFormer, a Transformer-based model that simulates multi-object rigid-body dynamics directly from point clouds, demonstrating competitive accuracy and improved efficiency compared to mesh-based methods while enabling controllable integration step sizes.

## Problem

- Traditional physics engines often lack accurate meshes or calibrated physical parameters, limiting their fidelity in real-world scenarios where only point cloud data might be available.
- Existing learning-based simulators frequently rely on mesh-based inputs, leading to high computational costs that rapidly increase with geometric resolution and hinder efficiency for mesh-free representations.
- Current models struggle with efficient, stable, and long-horizon contact dynamics from low-dimensional rigid motion, especially when dealing with discontinuous SE(3) parameterizations or maintaining rigidity.

## Method

- RigidFormer employs an object-centric Transformer design, encoding each object from point cloud inputs into a compact token to model interactions primarily among these tokens, significantly improving computational efficiency.
- It utilizes a small, fixed set of anchors per object for state updates, combined with Anchor-Vertex Pooling (AVP) to incorporate fine-grained local geometry and Anchor-based Rotary Positional Embedding (ARoPE) for geometry-aware attention.
- The model integrates differentiable rigid projection via Kabsch alignment to enforce rigidity and ensure stable long-horizon simulations, while also conditioning predictions on temporal discretization for controllable step sizes.

## Results

- RigidFormer achieved competitive or superior accuracy on MOVi benchmarks, notably improving over HopNet from 0.176 m/17.91° to 0.161 m/15.33° (translation/orientation error) on MOVi-B at 100 frames.
- It demonstrated robust generalization across datasets, varying point resolutions, and with partial point cloud inputs (e.g., stable rollouts with 25% masked points), maintaining accuracy with larger integration step sizes.
- The model achieved significant efficiency gains, running at 23.9 FPS on MOVi-B (8x faster than FIGNet, 101x faster than HopNet), and scaled stably to large scenes with up to 217 objects while also showing potential for controllable articulated-body simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rigid-body dynamics can be efficiently modeled at an object level using compact object tokens, avoiding redundant vertex-level interactions and leveraging the low-dimensionality of rigid motion.
> - Enforcing rigidity through a differentiable projection mechanism significantly improves simulation stability and accuracy over long horizons by precisely preserving intra-object distances.
> - Geometry-aware positional embeddings (ARoPE) and local feature pooling (AVP) enable effective attention and contact reasoning from point cloud inputs, providing robustness to varying point counts and partial observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of physical simulation is crucial for applications in robotics, computer graphics, and embodied artificial intelligence. Traditional physics engines offer high fidelity when accurate meshes and calibrated physical parameters are available. However, these prerequisites are often absent in real-world scenarios, where object geometries may be incomplete (e.g., point clouds) and contact properties difficult to determine. This limitation motivates the development of learning-based simulators, particularly those capable of handling mesh-free representations.

Many existing learning-based simulators for rigid-body dynamics, such as MeshGraphNets (MGN), FIGNet, and HopNet, rely on mesh-based inputs, requiring explicit edge and face connectivity. These methods typically operate at the vertex, edge, or facet level, leading to computational costs that increase rapidly with geometric resolution. This characteristic limits their inference efficiency and applicability to inputs like point clouds, which lack inherent connectivity. Other approaches, such as SDF-Sim, use learned signed distance functions for shape representation, which can reduce collision-handling bottlenecks but necessitate an additional shape learning phase. Prior point-based dynamics models, like those by Kim and Fuxin or Whitney et al., have explored hierarchical point cloud representations or disentangled visual and physical states, but some have shown degraded accuracy in contact-rich environments. Furthermore, works like SE3-Nets and 6-PACK utilize keypoint-based representations primarily for pose estimation or tracking in manipulation tasks, rather than for long-horizon contact dynamics simulation.

RigidFormer addresses the gap in efficient, mesh-free modeling of high-fidelity multi-object rigid-body dynamics from point cloud representations. It seeks to overcome the computational and connectivity limitations of mesh-based approaches by shifting reasoning to the object level and incorporating mechanisms for rigidity enforcement and variable time step integration, aiming for robust performance in scenarios where mesh data is unavailable or impractical.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce RigidFormer, a Transformer-based model designed to learn mesh-free multi-object rigid-body dynamics from point cloud inputs, with the added capability of controllable integration step sizes. The development of RigidFormer is motivated by several observations regarding the nature of rigid-body motion and the limitations of current learned simulation approaches:

1. **Efficiency of Object-Level Reasoning:** Rigid bodies respond to impulses as coherent entities. Current vertex-centric simulators, which propagate interaction effects across individual surface vertices through local message passing, introduce substantial computational overhead that is not aligned with the global nature of rigid motion. RigidFormer aims to reduce this inefficiency by adopting an object-centric representation, encoding each object into a compact token and modeling interactions primarily among these object tokens. This approach seeks to significantly improve computational efficiency compared to vertex-level interactions, particularly as the number of vertices increases.

2. **Low-Dimensionality of Rigid Motion:** Despite potentially comprising thousands of points, rigid motion is fundamentally low-dimensional, described by 6 degrees of freedom (3 for translation, 3 for rotation) per object. Directly updating all vertices is computationally expensive and redundant, while directly regressing rotations and translations can be prone to errors and instability due to discontinuities in common SE(3) parameterizations. To address this, RigidFormer proposes using a small, fixed number of "anchors" per object for state updates. These anchors enable efficient and geometry-aware dynamics updates.

3. **Geometry-Aware Attention without Vertex-Level Cost:** Accurate prediction of accelerations during contact strongly depends on local geometry around contact points. While an object-centric approach reduces overall computational load, retaining fine-grained, contact-relevant geometric cues is essential. RigidFormer addresses this by proposing Anchor-Vertex Pooling (AVP), which enriches anchor features with local vertex information without requiring dense vertex-level interactions. Additionally, a novel Anchor-based Rotary Positional Embedding (ARoPE) is introduced to inject object geometry into the Transformer's attention mechanism. ARoPE is designed to respect the unordered nature of objects and anchors, ensuring object-token permutation equivariance and anchor-order invariance for robust generalization.

4. **Controllable Temporal Discretization:** The ability to control the integration step size (∆t) within a single model offers flexibility. Larger ∆t can improve long-horizon accuracy by reducing autoregressive error accumulation through fewer updates, while smaller ∆t can capture finer temporal details when necessary. RigidFormer conditions its dynamics prediction on the temporal discretization, allowing the same model to operate across different step sizes.

In summary, the key motivations are to create an efficient, stable, and scalable mesh-free framework for multi-object rigid-body dynamics modeling that operates on point cloud inputs, capable of controlling simulation fidelity and generalizing across diverse scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RigidFormer processes a system of *M* rigid objects, where *M* can vary, by learning a model *f_θ* that predicts the next state *x_t+1* from two consecutive observations *x_t-1, x_t* and a time-step *∆t*. The approach is object-centric and Transformer-based, detailed as follows:

1. **Input Feature Construction:** For each object *i* at time *t*, vertex-level input features *h_t^(i)* are constructed. These features concatenate four types of information for each vertex: (1) a 3D displacement vector to the nearest point on another object or the ground plane (*d_t*), (2) a 3D per-step position increment (*v_t = x_t - x_t-1*) serving as a discrete velocity surrogate, (3) a 3D reference-offset (*r_t = x_t - x_ref*) relative to the first frame, and (4) 3D physics parameters (mass *m*, friction *μ*, restitution *ϵ*) broadcast to all vertices of the object.

2. **Object-Centric Interaction Modeling:**
 - **Point Encoder:** A PointNet-inspired encoder, *Enc_θ*, processes the per-vertex features *h_t^(i)* for each object *i*. This encoder utilizes hierarchical feature extraction to aggregate multi-scale geometry (global and three subsampled levels) into a fixed-dimensional object embedding *o_t^(i) ∈ R^D* (where D=768). The encoder is shared across all objects to promote generalization and is robust to variable vertex counts. This object-level tokenization significantly reduces the sequence length for the subsequent Transformer, improving efficiency.
 - **Transformer Decoder:** The object embeddings *O_t = [o_t^(1)..., o_t^(M)]* are concatenated with *N_r = 16* learned register tokens. A stack of *L* Transformer blocks then processes this sequence. Each block involves:
 - Residual self-attention, where interaction updates are modulated by a query-conditioned sigmoid gate (gated attention) to attenuate noisy or weakly relevant interaction reads, which aids long-horizon stability.
 - FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) conditioning on a temporal code *c = (s, s^2)*, where *s ∝ ∆t*. This allows the model to adapt its features for different temporal discretizations.
 - A residual feed-forward network (FFN).
 - The output of this stage is a set of updated object-level tokens *{Z_t^(L)}* that summarize scene context and object interactions.

3. **Anchor-based State Advance:**
 - **Anchor Selection:** For each object, a small set of *N_a = 4* anchors is selected using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS). These anchors serve as compact representations of the object's rigid dynamics.
 - **Anchor Queries and Cross-Attention:** Anchor queries are formed by extracting features at anchor locations and projecting them to *Q_t ∈ R^(M N_a)×D*. Each anchor query then attends to a small set of multi-scale outputs from the decoder layers via cross-attention. This allows anchors to retrieve cross-object interaction context from various levels of object representation.
 - **Anchor-Vertex Pooling (AVP):** To incorporate fine-grained, local contact geometry without dense vertex interactions, an AVP module is attached. It aggregates per-vertex encoder features around each anchor using a learnable isotropic distance kernel. This module is invariant to vertex ordering and rigid transforms. The pooled feature is processed by an MLP and concatenated to the anchor query, enriching it with collision-aware local context.
 - **Acceleration Prediction:** The network predicts a per-anchor acceleration *a_t^(i,k)*.
 - **Verlet Integration:** Candidate anchor positions *q_t+1_hat* are computed using Verlet integration based on the predicted accelerations.
 - **Differentiable Rigid Projection:** To enforce rigidity and improve long-horizon stability, the system recovers a rigid transform (*R^(i), t^(i)*) for each object. This is achieved by aligning the object's reference anchors *q_ref^(i,k)* to the candidate anchors *q_t+1_hat^(i,k)* using Kabsch alignment. The implementation uses RoMa for robust differentiability through the SVD-based alignment process.
 - **Full-Resolution Point Update:** The computed rigid transform is then broadcast to all vertices of the object to obtain their next positions: *x_t+1^(i,v) = R^(i)x_ref^(i,v) + t^(i)*. This ensures that intra-object distances are preserved exactly.

4. **Anchor-based Rotary Positional Embedding (ARoPE):** To enable geometry-aware attention while maintaining efficiency and generalization, ARoPE is used. For each object *i*, a shared 3D rotary anchor map *ψ_ω(·)* is applied to each of its *N_a* anchors. The resulting per-anchor phase descriptors are then mean-pooled to create a single ARoPE descriptor for the object. This mean-pooling makes the descriptor invariant to anchor reindexing, while the descriptor itself depends on world-frame positions, capturing object centroid and shape extent. ARoPE is applied to the query and key vectors in attention, rotating them based on these descriptors. The overall object Transformer remains equivariant to object-token permutations as no sequence-index positional embeddings are used.

5. **Training Objectives:** The model is trained using a combined objective that utilizes Smooth L1 losses for both position and acceleration. These losses are computed for the selected anchors, both before (raw) and after (rigid) the differentiable Kabsch alignment. The total loss *L* is a weighted sum of these four terms (*λ_pos=10*, *λ_acc=1*). The model is optimized using AdamW with a learning rate schedule including linear warmup and cosine decay, and gradient norm clipping. Data augmentation techniques, including random Z-axis rotations and object permutations, are applied to enhance generalization and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RigidFormer's performance was evaluated across various dimensions, including accuracy, generalization, scalability, and efficiency, primarily using the MOVi (Multi-Object Video) datasets (MOVi-A, MOVi-B, MOVi-Sphere) and a custom WreckingBall dataset.

**Accuracy Comparison:**
- On standard MOVi benchmarks, RigidFormer demonstrated competitive or superior performance compared to prior state-of-the-art mesh-based methods like HopNet, FIGNet, and MGN, despite only using point cloud inputs. It achieved the best orientation error in all reported columns and the best or second-best translation error in most columns across different prediction horizons (50, 75, 100 frames).
- Specifically, on MOVi-B at 100 frames, RigidFormer improved over HopNet (the strongest prior baseline) from 0.176 m/17.91° to 0.161 m/15.33°. It also significantly reduced long-horizon translation drift compared to point-based VPD and Transformer-based HCMT.
- RigidFormer's performance, particularly with larger step sizes (e.g., 0.029 m/1.51° at step size 10), surpassed SDF-Sim without requiring prior SDF fitting.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
- **Cross-Dataset Generalization:** RigidFormer consistently improved over FIGNet and remained competitive with HopNet when trained on one MOVi variant and tested on another. Using larger step sizes further reduced long-horizon errors across all transfer splits, with orientation reasoning showing robust transfer.
- **Point Resolution Generalization:** The model, trained with varying point counts ({128, 256, 512, 1024}), maintained stability and accuracy when evaluated on an unseen test-time resolution of 768 points across different rollout horizons and step sizes.
- **Partial Point Cloud Inputs:** RigidFormer produced stable rollouts with accurate inter-object contacts and low long-horizon drift even when 25% of an object's points were randomly masked at test time. This indicates a robustness to partial observations without specialized retraining.

**Step-Size Conditioning:**
- Larger step sizes (e.g., ∆t=10) consistently resulted in better long-horizon performance across all MOVi datasets. This is attributed to fewer autoregressive updates required to cover the same physical horizon, which reduces the accumulation of errors. The model successfully adapted to and leveraged different temporal discretizations.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Anchor-based Rotary Positional Embedding (ARoPE):** ARoPE demonstrated superior performance compared to standard sinusoidal, learned absolute, OBB, PCA, and SE(3) positional embeddings, achieving the best or tied-best position error in eight of nine settings and the best orientation error in most, confirming its effectiveness in encoding geometry for attention.
- **Gated Attention:** The sigmoid gating mechanism substantially improved translation accuracy and long-horizon stability, notably reducing position error at 100 steps across various step sizes. It also improved orientation error in most configurations.
- **Differentiable Kabsch Alignment:** Employing differentiable rigid projection consistently reduced both position and orientation errors at longer horizons, indicating that enforcing rigidity through this mechanism and allowing gradient flow contributes to improved rollout stability.
- **Number of Anchors:** Using 4 anchors per object provided a favorable trade-off between quality and efficiency, outperforming 3 anchors and remaining competitive with 8 anchors while reducing computational cost.
- **Randomized FPS Anchors:** RigidFormer exhibited robustness to the specific choice of FPS anchors when they were randomly re-sampled during training and evaluation, suggesting that it learns intrinsic geometric features rather than relying on fixed anchor identities.

**Scalability, Controllability, and Efficiency:**
- **Large-Scale Simulation:** RigidFormer demonstrated scalability on the WreckingBall dataset, handling scenes with up to 216 cubes (217 objects total) stably, achieving low RMSEs. This validates the efficiency of the object-level interaction and anchor-based state advance.
- **Controllable Articulated-Body Simulation:** A preliminary extension showed RigidFormer's applicability to articulated characters (e.g., ASE Humanoid, Unitree G1 robot) under directional control. By treating body parts as interacting object-level components and using FiLM conditioning for commands, the model produced coherent whole-body motion that followed specified headings with low errors.
- **Runtime Performance:** RigidFormer achieved a runtime of 23.9 FPS on MOVi-B, demonstrating significant speedups (8× over FIGNet, 101× over HopNet). This efficiency is primarily attributed to its object-centric Transformer design, which processes compact object-level states rather than dense vertex-level structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RigidFormer represents a contribution to the field of learning-based physical simulation by offering an efficient, scalable, and mesh-free approach to modeling multi-object rigid-body dynamics from point cloud data. This work addresses a critical limitation of many existing learned simulators that depend on explicit mesh connectivity, which is often unavailable from real-world perception systems.

The significance of RigidFormer stems from several key aspects:
- **Bridging Perception and Simulation:** By directly operating on point cloud inputs, RigidFormer provides a more natural interface between modern perception systems, which often yield point-based representations, and dynamic scene modeling. This can facilitate tasks in robotics, such as planning over object interactions and embodied AI, where real-time simulation from raw sensor data is advantageous.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** The object-centric Transformer design, coupled with anchor-based state advance, significantly reduces the computational complexity compared to vertex-level interaction models. This allows RigidFormer to scale to scenes with a larger number of objects while maintaining high inference speeds, making it suitable for complex dynamic environments.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The incorporation of differentiable rigid projection ensures that intra-object distances are precisely preserved, promoting long-horizon stability. Furthermore, Anchor-based Rotary Positional Embedding (ARoPE) provides a geometry-aware attention mechanism that generalizes across diverse object counts and shapes, even to unseen point resolutions. The demonstrated robustness to partial point cloud inputs further expands its practical applicability.
- **Versatility and Controllability:** The ability to operate with controllable integration step sizes provides flexibility for various applications, allowing for efficient long-horizon predictions (large steps) or detailed contact analysis (small steps). Its preliminary extension to command-conditioned articulated bodies suggests a broader applicability beyond independent rigid objects, opening avenues for simulating complex robotic systems or character animation.

The potential impact of RigidFormer is broad, particularly in areas requiring efficient and robust physical reasoning:
- **Robotics:** Improved simulators can facilitate model-predictive control, reinforcement learning for manipulation, and training of embodied agents in virtual environments.
- **Computer Graphics:** Faster and more stable rigid-body simulations can enhance realism and efficiency in animation, game development, and virtual reality.
- **Scientific Computing:** The approach could inspire new methods for simulating physical systems, especially those with complex geometries or where traditional meshing is problematic.

While the current formulation relies on object labels and can face challenges with highly occluded inputs, this work lays a foundation for future research into more robust perception-to-simulation pipelines, mixed rigid–deformable dynamics, and adaptive time-stepping strategies, contributing to the development of more versatile and capable learned physics engines.
