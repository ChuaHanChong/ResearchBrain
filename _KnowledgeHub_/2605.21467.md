# DelTA: Discriminative Token Credit Assignment for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China and Ant International introduced DELTA, a framework that enhances reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) in large language models by assigning token-level credit based on a novel "discriminator view" of policy updates. DELTA reweights token-gradient terms to amplify discriminative signals, consistently improving mathematical reasoning scores by up to 3.79 points on various LLM backbones and generalizing to code generation and out-of-domain tasks.

## Problem

- A granularity mismatch exists in Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), where a single response-level reward is used for token-level policy updates.
- The implicit token selection mechanism in standard RLVR is not explicitly guided, leading to diluted discriminative signals due to shared patterns in both high- and low-reward responses.
- The precise mechanism by which scalar response-level rewards translate into specific token-level probability changes in LLMs remains underexplored.

## Method

- Introduced a "discriminator view" of RLVR updates, showing that ==policy gradient== updates implicitly act as a linear discriminator over token-gradient vectors.
- Proposed **DelTA** (Discriminative signal-guided Token Credit Assignment), a method to reshape the RLVR discriminator by adaptively reweighting token-gradient terms.
- **DelTA** estimates token coefficients by comparing each token's gradient direction to dynamically refined positive and negative side-wise centroids, giving higher influence to discriminative tokens.

## Results

- DelTA consistently outperformed strong RL baselines on seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks, achieving average improvements of 3.26 points (Qwen3-8B) and 2.62 points (Qwen3-14B) over the strongest baselines.
- The method demonstrated generalization by improving code generation performance (weighted average score from 47.7 to 49.5) and showing gains on out-of-domain tasks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the discriminative opposite-side comparison and showed that DelTA's coefficients effectively identify tokens with high learning value, with low computational overhead (approx. **10.2%** of first training step's wall-clock time).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLVR policy gradient updates define an implicit linear discriminator that determines which token probabilities are increased or decreased based on their token-gradient vectors.
> - Standard RLVR's simple advantage-weighted averaging for side-wise centroids can dilute discriminative signals, as common task-agnostic patterns appear in both good and bad responses.
> - By adaptively reweighting token-gradient terms based on their discriminative signal, the effective side-wise centroids can be made more contrastive, leading to more targeted policy updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a significant paradigm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) across various domains, including mathematics, code generation, and formal problem solving. RLVR optimizes LLMs using response-level rewards, such as the correctness of a final answer, without requiring dense, step-by-step process annotations.

A fundamental challenge within RLVR is the granularity mismatch between the reward signal and the policy update mechanism. While rewards are typically scalar and apply to an entire response, policy updates are accumulated through token-level terms. Prior research indicates that RLVR induces sparse and localized changes in token-level distributions, with substantial probability shifts concentrated on a small subset of tokens, suggesting an implicit token-level selection mechanism not directly specified by the response-level reward.

Existing approaches to address this granularity mismatch include token-level or step-level reweighting, the use of process reward models, learned value functions, or auxiliary token-selection signals such as entropy or future influence.

This work contributes to the broader landscape by introducing a "discriminator view" of RLVR updates. This perspective aims to explain the implicit token selection inherent in RLVR by showing that the policy-gradient update direction acts as a linear discriminator over token-gradient vectors. The proposed method, DelTA, builds upon this view to directly reshape the induced discriminator. Unlike methods relying on external dense rewards, value estimates, or auxiliary token-selection rules, DelTA derives token coefficients directly from the positive-negative discriminative signal induced by the update itself, offering an alternative approach to refine token credit assignment under sequence-level supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) is effective for improving LLM reasoning, the precise mechanism by which response-level rewards translate into token-level probability changes remains underexplored. The core problem is the "granularity mismatch" where a single scalar advantage applies to an entire response, yet the policy updates occur at the token level. This leads to the central question: "which token probabilities are increased or decreased by an RLVR update, and what determines these changes?"

The authors propose a "discriminator view" to explain this implicit token selection. They analyze that an RLVR update, traditionally seen as a parameter-space movement, concurrently defines a token-level decision rule. This rule determines whether a candidate token's probability is increased or decreased by comparing its token-gradient direction with aggregated reference directions derived from positive- and negative-advantage responses. In essence, the RLVR update functions as an implicit linear discriminator in the space of token-gradient vectors.

However, a limitation in standard sequence-level RLVR objectives is identified: the side-wise centroids (aggregate directions) that form this discriminator are typically constructed by simple advantage-weighted averaging of token-gradient vectors. In reasoning tasks, both high-reward and low-reward responses often share common structural elements, such as formatting tokens or problem-specific entities. These shared, high-frequency patterns can dominate both the positive and negative side-wise centroids. Consequently, the induced discriminator tends to emphasize these task-agnostic commonalities, thereby "diluting sparse yet discriminative directions" that could better differentiate higher-reward responses from lower-reward ones. This mismatch between within-side summarization and between-side discrimination compromises the effectiveness of the policy update.

The key objective of this work is to address this limitation. The authors propose DelTA (Discriminative signal-guided Token Credit Assignment) with the motivation of reshaping the RLVR discriminator. DelTA aims to estimate token coefficients that amplify token-gradient directions more characteristic of their respective advantage side (positive or negative) and downweight shared or weakly discriminative directions. By doing so, the method seeks to make the effective side-wise centroids more contrastive, thereby refining the RLVR update direction for more effective token-level credit assignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes a "discriminator view" of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) updates, particularly using DAPO (Deep Adaptive Policy Optimization) as a representative critic-free group-relative framework. This view posits that a policy-gradient update defines an implicit linear discriminator over token-gradient vectors, determining which token probabilities are increased or decreased.

**Discriminator View of RLVR Updates:**
The local policy update direction ($\Delta\theta$) around the old policy ($\theta_{old}$) is analyzed. For a sequence-level RLVR objective, the update can be expressed as an advantage-weighted aggregation of sampled-token gradients. Specifically, $\Delta\theta$ is proportional to the difference between the sum of advantage-weighted token-gradient vectors from positive-advantage responses and the sum of absolute advantage-weighted token-gradient vectors from negative-advantage responses. This can be re-written as $\Delta\theta \propto M_+ \bar{\mu}_+ - M_- \bar{\mu}_-$, where $M_+$ and $M_-$ are the total positive and negative advantage masses, and $\bar{\mu}_+$ and $\bar{\mu}_-$ are the normalized aggregate directions (centroids) for positive and negative sides, respectively.
The local change in a candidate token's log-probability is proportional to the inner product of its token-gradient vector with $\Delta\theta$. This means a token's probability is increased if its gradient aligns more with $\bar{\mu}_+$ than $\bar{\mu}_-$, and decreased otherwise. In this sense, the RLVR update implicitly acts as a linear discriminator.

**Limitation of Standard RLVR:**
Under standard sequence-level RLVR, $\bar{\mu}_+$ and $\bar{\mu}_-$ are calculated as simple advantage-weighted averages (or weighted least-squares centroids) of token-gradient vectors. This approach can be suboptimal for discrimination. High-frequency patterns, such as formatting tokens or common entities, appear in both high- and low-reward responses. Their token-gradient directions can pull both $\bar{\mu}_+$ and $\bar{\mu}_-$ towards these common structures, reducing the contrast between the two centroids and diluting the unique directions that distinguish between higher- and lower-reward responses.

**DelTA: Discriminative Signal-Guided Token Credit Assignment:**
To address this, DelTA reweights token-gradient terms in the RLVR surrogate objective to reshape the induced discriminator and make the side-wise centroids more contrastive. DelTA operates in three main steps per rollout batch:

1. **Initialization of Side-wise Centroids:** The process begins by computing initial positive ($\mu^{(0)}_+$) and negative ($\mu^{(0)}_-$) reference directions. These are standard advantage-weighted averages of token-gradient vectors from their respective sides, as defined in conventional RLVR.
2. **Alternating Refinement:** DelTA performs `K` stop-gradient refinement iterations (with `K=1` in the main experiments). In each iteration `k`:
 - **Estimate Discriminative Token Scores ($\alpha^{(k)}_{i,t}$):** For each token-gradient vector $v_{i,t}$, a soft discriminative score $\alpha^{(k)}_{i,t}$ is calculated. This score is higher when $v_{i,t}$ is closer to its own side's current centroid ($\mu^{(k)}_+$ for positive advantage, $\mu^{(k)}_-$ for negative advantage) than to the opposite side's centroid. The calculation uses an entropy-regularized assignment problem, resulting in a sigmoid function applied to a squared-distance margin:
 $\alpha^{(k)}_{i,t} = \sigma \left( \frac{\|v_{i,t} - \mu^{(k)}_- \|^2_2 - \|v_{i,t} - \mu^{(k)}_+ \|^2_2}{\gamma^{(k)}_+}\right)$ for positive advantage tokens, and symmetrically for negative. $\gamma^{(k)}_{\pm}$ are side-specific adaptive temperatures, dynamically estimated from the empirical standard deviations of the squared-distance differences, helping to scale the assignment.
 - **Update Centroids:** Based on these scores, new centroids ($\mu^{(k+1)}_+$ and $\mu^{(k+1)}_-$) are computed as *score-weighted* averages of the token-gradient vectors from their respective sides. This process gives higher influence to token gradients that were deemed more discriminative by $\alpha^{(k)}_{i,t}$.
3. **Final Coefficient Application:** After `K` iterations, final raw scores $\alpha^{\star}_{i,t}$ are computed using the refined centroids. These are then mapped to a bounded range using a linear function: $\lambda_{i,t} = \lambda_{min} + (\lambda_{max} - \lambda_{min})\alpha^{\star}_{i,t}$. The default range is $[0.8, 1.2]$. For zero-advantage responses, $\lambda_{i,t}$ is set to $\lambda_{min}$.
 Finally, these $\lambda_{i,t}$ coefficients are used to reweight a self-normalized RLVR surrogate objective. The objective becomes:
 $J_{DelTA}(\theta) = E \left[ \frac{1}{\sum_{i,t} \lambda_{i,t}} \sum_{i,t} \lambda_{i,t} \cdot \text{min} \left( r_{i,t}(\theta) \hat{A}_i, \text{clip}(r_{i,t}(\theta), 1-\epsilon_{low}, 1+\epsilon_{high}) \hat{A}_i \right) \right]$.
 This reweighting effectively reshapes the side-wise centroids in the policy gradient update, amplifying discriminative directions.

**Practical Implementation:**
Due to the computational cost of full-parameter gradients for LLMs, DelTA employs a layer-restricted LM-head gradient proxy: $(1 - p_t(y_t)) h_t$, where $h_t$ is the final hidden state and $p_t(y_t)$ is the probability of the generated token $y_t$. This proxy is solely for stop-gradient coefficient estimation; the full policy parameters are still optimized. The coefficients $\lambda_{i,t}$ are computed once per rollout batch and held fixed across subsequent optimization steps within that batch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DelTA was conducted across multiple settings, demonstrating its effectiveness and generalization capabilities.

**Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks:**
DelTA was primarily evaluated on seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AIME26, HMMT25 (Feb.), HMMT25 (Nov.), HMMT26 (Feb.), Brumo25) using two backbone models: Qwen3-8B-Base and Qwen3-14B-Base.
- **Performance:** DelTA consistently outperformed all strong same-scale RL baselines (DAPO, DAPO w/ FT, SAPO, FIPO).
 - On Qwen3-8B-Base, DelTA achieved an average score of 28.40, improving by 3.26 points over the strongest baseline (SAPO, 25.14).
 - On Qwen3-14B-Base, DelTA achieved an average score of 39.91, improving by 2.62 points over the strongest baseline (FIPO, 37.29).
- **Statistical Significance:** Under an evaluation-run-level significance test (one-sided Mann-Whitney U test, p < 0.05) with 16 repeated evaluation runs, DelTA significantly outperformed the strongest same-scale baselines at both model scales.

**Generalization Across Tasks, Backbones, and Domains:**
- **Code Generation:** DelTA improved performance on code generation benchmarks (HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench), increasing the weighted average score from 47.7 (DAPO) to 49.5.
- **Other Model Architectures:** DelTA remained effective on the Olmo3-7B-Base backbone, improving the average mathematical reasoning score from 19.01 (DAPO) to 22.80 (a 3.79-point gain).
- **Out-of-Domain Evaluation:** DelTA showed gains on out-of-domain benchmarks GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro. On Qwen3-8B-Base, the weighted average improved from 58.87 (DAPO) to 62.38. On Qwen3-14B-Base, it improved from 66.77 (DAPO) to 68.40.

**Training Dynamics:**
Analysis of training dynamics on Qwen3-8B-Base showed that DelTA sustained reward improvement and reached a higher final reward, whereas DAPO plateaued and slightly degraded. DelTA maintained longer responses with lower entropy, indicating more stable and confident long-reasoning behavior, in contrast to DAPO's shift towards shorter responses with rising entropy.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Opposite-Side Comparison Necessity:** A variant that considered only "within-side" centrality (without comparing to the opposite centroid) performed worse than both DelTA and the DAPO baseline, confirming the importance of the discriminative, opposite-side comparison.
- **Utility of Coefficients ($\lambda_{i,t}$):** Training DAPO using only the top 50% tokens ranked by DelTA's $\lambda_{i,t}$ coefficients outperformed both full-token DAPO and random 50% token selection. Conversely, training on the bottom 50% tokens led to performance collapse. This suggests that $\lambda_{i,t}$ effectively distinguishes tokens with high learning value from potentially harmful or uninformative ones.
- **Component Necessity:** Ablation studies on five design choices (adaptive $\gamma$, entropy regularizer, $\lambda$-normalization, range mapping, and refinement iterations) demonstrated that each component contributed positively to DelTA's overall performance. The largest performance drop occurred when refinement iterations were removed (`K=0`).
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** DelTA exhibited relative robustness to the coefficient range $[\lambda_{min}, \lambda_{max}]$. However, increasing the number of refinement iterations (`K`) beyond `K=1` consistently reduced performance, suggesting that a single refinement step is sufficient.

**Computational Overhead:**
The computational overhead introduced by DelTA, with `K=1` refinement iteration, involves approximately two additional actor forward passes for coefficient estimation per rollout batch. Empirically, this accounted for approximately 10.2% of the first training step's wall-clock time in the experimental setup, which is considered modest given that rollout generation typically dominates RLVR training time.

**Token Weight Analysis:**
Qualitative analysis of high- and low-weight tokens indicated that DelTA tended to assign larger coefficients to tokens related to reasoning processes (e.g., "scaffold", "prime", "=y", "forward"), while lower coefficients were assigned to more background-like or entity-specific tokens (e.g., "Seat", "domain", "players"). This aligns with DelTA's design to amplify discriminative, side-specific signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs) by addressing the fundamental challenge of token-level credit assignment under sequence-level verifiable rewards.

**Conceptual Advancement:** The introduction of a "local discriminator view" provides a novel analytical framework for understanding the internal mechanisms of RLVR. By characterizing the policy-gradient update as an implicit linear discriminator over token-gradient vectors, the work offers a mechanistic explanation for how token probabilities are selectively influenced by a response-level reward signal. This perspective highlights a limitation in standard RLVR, where shared, high-frequency token patterns can dilute the discriminative power of the side-wise centroids, thus weakening the policy update.

**Methodological Innovation:** DelTA represents a novel approach to improve token credit assignment without relying on external dense rewards, process reward models, or auxiliary token-selection heuristics. Instead, it leverages the inherent discriminative structure within the RLVR update itself. By adaptively reweighting token-gradient terms based on their discriminative signal (i.e., how much more characteristic they are of their own advantage side than the opposite side), DelTA systematically enhances the contrastiveness of the effective side-wise centroids, leading to more targeted policy updates.

**Empirical Effectiveness and Generalization:** The consistent and statistically significant performance improvements demonstrated across diverse benchmarks are noteworthy. DelTA’s superiority over strong RLVR baselines in mathematical reasoning, coupled with its generalization to code generation, different LLM backbones (Qwen3 and Olmo3), and out-of-domain evaluation tasks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), suggests that the proposed method addresses a fundamental aspect of RLVR training that is not specific to a particular task or model architecture. The observed improvements in training dynamics, leading to more stable and confident long-reasoning behavior, underscore its practical utility.

**Diagnostic Capabilities:** The analysis of DelTA's coefficients ($\lambda_{i,t}$) reveals that they capture meaningful learning signals, effectively differentiating between token-gradient directions that contribute positively to policy improvement and those that are uninformative or potentially detrimental. This inherent diagnostic capability could aid researchers in better understanding and debugging RL training processes.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced LLM Reasoning:** By improving the efficiency and effectiveness of RLVR, DelTA can contribute to developing more capable LLMs, particularly in domains requiring robust reasoning and verifiable outputs like mathematics, scientific problem-solving, and code generation.
- **Broader Applicability of RLVR:** The method's generalization across various tasks and models suggests that the "discriminator view" and the DelTA approach could be foundational for improving RLVR across a wider spectrum of applications where verifiable feedback is available.
- **Future Research:** The work opens avenues for further research into more sophisticated token-gradient proxies, alternative methods for shaping the induced discriminator, and broader evaluation in complex multi-turn or tool-use scenarios. The relatively modest computational overhead also suggests that such methods could be scaled effectively.

**Limitations and Broader Impacts:** The paper acknowledges limitations, including the use of a layer-restricted token-gradient proxy for scalability, which could be further refined. It also notes that the empirical evaluation, while diverse, could be expanded to other RLVR settings. The computational overhead, though modest, remains a consideration for very large-scale deployments. The authors explicitly address broader impacts, recognizing that more capable reasoning models may have dual-use applications and emphasizing the importance of responsible deployment practices, including evaluation, monitoring, and task-specific safeguards for high-risk applications.
