# DelTA: Discriminative Token Credit Assignment for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China and Ant International introduced DELTA, a framework that enhances reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) in large language models by assigning token-level credit based on a novel "discriminator view" of policy updates. DELTA reweights token-gradient terms to amplify discriminative signals, consistently improving mathematical reasoning scores by up to 3.79 points on various LLM backbones and generalizing to code generation and out-of-domain tasks.

## Problem

- A granularity mismatch exists in Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), where a single response-level reward is used for token-level policy updates.
- The implicit token selection mechanism in standard RLVR is not explicitly guided, leading to diluted discriminative signals due to shared patterns in both high- and low-reward responses.
- The precise mechanism by which scalar response-level rewards translate into specific token-level probability changes in LLMs remains underexplored.

## Method

- Introduced a "discriminator view" of RLVR updates, showing that ==policy gradient== updates implicitly act as a linear discriminator over token-gradient vectors.
- Proposed **DelTA** (Discriminative signal-guided Token Credit Assignment), a method to reshape the RLVR discriminator by adaptively reweighting token-gradient terms.
- **DelTA** estimates token coefficients by comparing each token's gradient direction to dynamically refined positive and negative side-wise centroids, giving higher influence to discriminative tokens.

## Results

- DelTA consistently outperformed strong RL baselines on seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks, achieving average improvements of 3.26 points (Qwen3-8B) and 2.62 points (Qwen3-14B) over the strongest baselines.
- The method demonstrated generalization by improving code generation performance (weighted average score from 47.7 to 49.5) and showing gains on out-of-domain tasks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the discriminative opposite-side comparison and showed that DelTA's coefficients effectively identify tokens with high learning value, with low computational overhead (approx. **10.2%** of first training step's wall-clock time).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLVR policy gradient updates define an implicit linear discriminator that determines which token probabilities are increased or decreased based on their token-gradient vectors.
> - Standard RLVR's simple advantage-weighted averaging for side-wise centroids can dilute discriminative signals, as common task-agnostic patterns appear in both good and bad responses.
> - By adaptively reweighting token-gradient terms based on their discriminative signal, the effective side-wise centroids can be made more contrastive, leading to more targeted policy updates.
