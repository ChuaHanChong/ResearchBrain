# KungfuBot2: Learning Versatile Motion Skills for Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> KUNGFUBOT2 introduces VMS (Versatile Motion Skills), a unified whole-body controller that enables humanoid robots to learn a broad repertoire of diverse and dynamic human motions within a single policy. Developed by researchers from TeleAI, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and East China University of Science and Technology, this framework achieved a 92.68% success rate on test motions and demonstrated robust real-world performance on the Unitree G1 robot for complex, long-duration skills like martial arts and dance.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid control policies struggled with limited expressiveness, failing to robustly represent diverse and dynamic skills within a single network.
- Achieving a balance between fine-grained local motion fidelity and overall global stability in motion tracking was challenging, often leading to drift or instability.
- Strict, step-wise matching to reference motions often resulted in brittle policies that accumulated errors and failed during long-horizon or complex sequences.

## Method

- A two-stage teacher-student reinforcement learning paradigm was used, training a teacher policy with full state information and distilling it into a proprioception-only student policy for real-world deployment.
- ==An Orthogonal Mixture==-of-Experts (OMoE) architecture was introduced, utilizing an orthogonality constraint to encourage experts to learn distinct, non-overlapping representations for diverse motion skills.
- A hybrid tracking objective, augmented with a segment-level tracking reward, was implemented to improve robustness against transient errors by allowing the robot to align with candidate reference states within a future temporal window.

## Results

- VMS consistently outperformed baseline methods (ExBody2, GMT) across all tracking error metrics and achieved a higher success rate of **92.68%** on the test dataset in simulation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the OMoE architecture led to lower tracking errors and expert activation analysis demonstrated effective skill specialization and adaptive expert usage.
- The segment-level tracking reward significantly improved long-horizon motion stability, allowing the Unitree G1 robot to perform complex, minute-long sequences like Tai Chi and Shaolin Kungfu reliably in the real world.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forcing orthogonality between expert features in a Mixture-of-Experts architecture effectively disentangles distinct motion skill representations, enhancing policy expressiveness and generalization.
> - Relaxing rigid step-wise tracking with a segment-level reward, which considers a future temporal window, dramatically improves a policy's robustness and stability for executing long-horizon and complex motions.
> - Combining global root tracking with local keybody tracking in a hybrid objective is crucial for simultaneously achieving overall spatial coherence and fine-grained motion style fidelity in humanoid control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is rapidly advancing, driven by progress in reinforcement learning, motion capture technology, and high-fidelity physics simulations. Traditionally, humanoid control relied on model-based approaches, which, while precise for specific tasks, demanded extensive modeling and struggled with generalization and unmodeled dynamics. Learning-based methods emerged as a powerful alternative, initially focusing on task-specific reward engineering for locomotion or basic actions. However, generating natural, human-like motions remained a challenge.

The paradigm shifted with the advent of motion tracking, where robots learn directly from human motion data (Mocap). Early works like DeepMimic pioneered learning individual, dynamic skills. Subsequent research, including the group's prior work "KungfuBot" and "ASAP", focused on achieving high-fidelity imitation of single, highly dynamic motions and addressing the sim-to-real gap. While successful, these approaches often required training separate policies for each skill, limiting the robot's versatility and scalability.

A critical next step, and the focus of KUNGFUBOT2, is to develop a *single, unified whole-body controller* capable of learning a *broad repertoire* of diverse and dynamic human motions. Several recent works, such as OmniH2O, ExBody2, TWIST, CLONE, GMT, and UniTracker, have explored this direction. However, KUNGFUBOT2 identifies key limitations in these existing multi-skill policies:
1. **Limited Policy Expressiveness:** Many approaches rely on a single Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) network, which struggles to encapsulate the diverse dynamics of varied motion skills without forming overlapping and inefficient representations.
2. **Imbalance between Local and Global Tracking:** Achieving both fine-grained motion style (local fidelity) and overall spatial coherence (global stability) is challenging. Purely local tracking can lead to error accumulation and global drift, while global tracking can be prone to long-horizon instability when the reference becomes difficult to follow.
3. **Robustness for Long-Horizon Sequences:** Strict step-wise matching to reference motions can be brittle, causing policies to fail when facing transient inaccuracies, external disturbances, or slightly infeasible reference states over extended periods.

KUNGFUBOT2 directly addresses these challenges, positioning itself at the forefront of research aiming to create truly versatile and robust general-purpose humanoid controllers. It builds upon the success of prior motion tracking methods but significantly enhances the policy architecture and tracking objectives to handle the complexity and diversity required for a universal skill set.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the KUNGFUBOT2 project is to develop **VMS (Versatile Motion Skills), a unified whole-body controller that enables humanoid robots to learn diverse and dynamic behaviors within a single policy.** This is seen as a fundamental step toward realizing general-purpose humanoid robots capable of imitating a broad spectrum of human actions, from stable locomotion to agile and complex maneuvers.

The motivation stems from several critical observations and unmet needs in the current state of humanoid control:
- **Demand for Versatility:** Humanoid robots are envisioned for roles requiring a wide range of human-like skills. Current methods, often requiring task-specific policies, are not scalable for such versatility. A single, general policy is essential.
- **Leveraging Mocap Data:** The availability of large-scale human motion capture (Mocap) datasets offers a rich resource for learning natural and expressive behaviors. The goal is to fully leverage this data to instill versatility.
- **Addressing Policy Expressiveness:** Learning highly distinct motion skills (e.g., walking, kicking, dancing) within a single neural network policy often leads to poor generalization and instability due to conflicting or blended representations. There's a need for architectures that can better disentangle and specialize these skills.
- **Balancing Tracking Objectives:** Existing tracking objectives often struggle to simultaneously maintain local motion fidelity (how precisely the robot performs specific joint movements or key body poses) and global trajectory consistency (how well the robot follows the overall path and maintains stability). A more robust approach is needed to reconcile these.
- **Ensuring Long-Horizon Stability:** For practical applications, robots must perform actions reliably over extended periods. Rigid, step-wise tracking can be prone to failure accumulation and sensitivity to minor deviations, necessitating a more forgiving and robust tracking mechanism for long sequences.

By tackling these core challenges, KUNGFUBOT2 aims to create a foundational controller that can serve as a robust, low-level behavioral primitive for higher-level planning and decision-making in humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KUNGFUBOT2 introduces VMS, a comprehensive framework built upon a two-stage teacher-student reinforcement learning paradigm, incorporating novel architectural and reward design elements to achieve versatile and stable whole-body control. The target platform for real-world deployment is the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, controlling 23 degrees of freedom.

1. **Motion Dataset Curation:**
 - **Source:** Begins with the publicly available AMASS Mocap dataset (SMPL format).
 - **Retargeting:** Human motions are retargeted to the Unitree G1 skeleton using a differentiable inverse kinematics (IK)-based method, ensuring end-effector trajectory alignment while respecting joint limits. This yields over 13,000 sequences.
 - **Filtering:** An "oracle policy" is trained on the full set to evaluate and filter out motions that are infeasible for the humanoid (e.g., stair climbing) or contain corrupted frames, resulting in a curated dataset of 9,770 high-quality, humanoid-compatible motions (30.41 hours).

2. **Two-Stage Learning Paradigm:**
 - **Teacher Policy (π_tea):** Trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in simulation, with access to full state information, allowing it to learn optimal control policies.
 - **Student Policy (π_stu):** Distilled from the teacher policy via behavior cloning (BC). This student policy relies only on proprioceptive observations available at deployment (partial observability), making it suitable for real-world transfer.

3. **Policy Objective and State Space Design:**
 - **Hybrid Tracking Targets:** To achieve both expressive motion style and spatial alignment, VMS combines:
 - **Global Root Tracking:** Minimizes deviations of the robot's root position and rotation from the reference world-frame trajectory.
 - **Local Keybody Tracking:** Aligns the positions and orientations of key body parts (head, hands, elbows, knees, ankles) relative to the root between the robot and reference. This captures the local motion style.
 - **State Spaces:**
 - **Teacher State (s_tea):** Includes full proprioceptive state (joint pos/vel, root lin/ang vel), keybody positions/orientations, root position/rotation tracking errors, and previous action. The goal (g_tea) provides a preview of future reference joint states and keybody poses (H timesteps).
 - **Student State (s_stu):** A proprioceptive-only state, augmented with a short history (K timesteps) of past joint positions/velocities, root angular velocity, root rotation error, and previous actions. The goal (g_stu) provides a preview of future reference joint states and relative keybody poses.

4. **Orthogonal Mixture-of-Experts (OMoE) Architecture:**
 - **Motivation:** To overcome the limitations of a single MLP in representing diverse skills, VMS introduces OMoE for the teacher policy.
 - **Mechanism:** Consists of multiple expert networks and a router network. Experts map the concatenated state and goal to feature vectors. An orthogonality constraint (approximated via Gram-Schmidt process) is imposed on these features, forcing experts to learn distinct and non-overlapping skill representations. The router dynamically computes weights to combine the orthogonalized expert outputs, which are then passed through a shared output head to produce the action. This design allows for efficient skill specialization and flexible composition.

5. **Segment-level Tracking Reward:**
 - **Motivation:** To address the brittleness of strict step-wise trajectory matching, especially for long-horizon or challenging motions.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of penalizing deviations from a single reference state at timestep *t*, the agent is rewarded based on the *minimum discrepancy* between its current state and a set of candidate reference states within a short future temporal window (horizon H). This allows the robot to "catch up" or find the most feasible alignment point, improving robustness against transient errors and slight deviations, balancing local style and global consistency. Separate segment-level rewards are applied for global root position and local keybody positions.

6. **Sim-to-Real Transfer Techniques:**
 - **Domain Randomization (DR):** During both teacher and student training, physical parameters (base mass, friction, motor strength, default joint positions) and external forces (robot pushes) are randomized to improve policy robustness to real-world uncertainties.
 - **Noised Reference State Initialization (RSI):** Gaussian noise is injected into sampled reference root and joint states during initialization, enhancing the policy's resilience to imperfect intermediate states and environmental variations in the real world.
 - **History Encoding:** A convolutional network encodes a short temporal history of proprioceptive states into a latent embedding. This embedding is trained to approximate privileged information about randomized physical parameters, effectively capturing environment dynamics. The frozen history encoder is then used in the student policy to provide robust input during real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments extensively validate VMS in both simulation and real-world, addressing four key research questions:

- **Q1: VMS Outperforms Baseline Methods:**
 - On both training and test datasets, VMS consistently achieves significantly lower tracking errors across all metrics (Mean Per Keybody Position Error E_mpkpe, Mean Per Joint Position Error E_mpjpe, Mean Global Root Position Error E_pos, Mean Root Linear Velocity Error E_vel) compared to strong baselines, ExBody2 and GMT.
 - VMS demonstrates superior local tracking fidelity while also effectively reducing global root position errors, contrasting with baselines that might achieve high success rates but incur larger global drift (e.g., ExBody2 and GMT with root velocity objectives).
 - VMS achieves a higher success rate (92.68% on the test set) compared to baselines.

- **Q2: OMoE Enables Effective Learning and Generalization of Versatile Motion Skills:**
 - **Quantitative:** Ablation studies show OMoE leading to the lowest tracking errors, outperforming standard soft Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and a single MLP of equivalent parameter size.
 - **Qualitative (t-SNE visualization):** T-SNE plots of expert output features reveal that OMoE produces more dispersed and well-separated feature clusters, indicating that the orthogonality constraint successfully encourages experts to learn distinct and specialized skill subspaces. In contrast, standard MoE exhibits significant overlap, suggesting redundancy.
 - **Expert Activation Analysis:** Examining expert activation frequencies across different motion categories (walking, squatting, kicking, dancing) confirms that VMS adaptively allocates expert usage based on motion complexity and type. For instance, walking activates a small subset of experts, while dancing shows a more distributed pattern, demonstrating effective skill decomposition and flexible composition.

- **Q3: Hybrid Tracking Objective Augmented with Segment-level Reward Improves Long-Horizon Motion Performance:**
 - **Quantitative:** Ablation studies comparing VMS with "VMS-Global" (pure global tracking) and "VMS-Step-wise" (strict step-wise tracking) clearly show VMS's superiority. VMS-Global performs poorly on the test set with low success rates (60.52%) due to error accumulation, while VMS-Step-wise is more stable but still underperforms VMS. VMS maintains low errors and high success rates across metrics.
 - **Qualitative (Motion Visualizations):** Visual examples highlight the robustness of the segment-level reward. For a "running-to-turn" motion, VMS achieves a smooth transition where global tracking fails due to accumulated errors, and step-wise tracking rigidly adheres and collapses. For a "side kick," VMS maintains balance by slightly compromising kick height, whereas global tracking falls, and step-wise tracking loses balance post-kick.
 - **Performance vs. Motion Duration:** VMS demonstrates remarkable robustness for long-duration motions, maintaining consistently lower errors compared to VMS-Global and VMS-Step-wise, which degrade rapidly as motion duration increases.

- **Q4: Strong Real-World Performance:**
 - VMS is successfully deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot.
 - Real-world experiments showcase its capability to perform a broad range of dynamic, versatile, and stable motions, including various locomotion styles (walking, marching, running), athletic movements (racket swings, ball throws), expressive dances (Charleston), highly dynamic actions (punches, kicks, jumping kicks), and minute-long composite martial arts sequences (Tai Chi, Shaolin Kungfu). The project page and supplementary videos confirm its real-world effectiveness.

- **Downstream Tasks (Generalization):**
 - **Text-to-motion Generation:** VMS demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization by successfully tracking diverse reference motions generated from text descriptions (using the MLD model), showcasing its potential as a universal low-level controller for high-level planners.
 - **Finetuning on Extreme Motions:** VMS adapts well to challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) motions (e.g., lying down and getting up, spinning kip-up) with minimal fine-tuning, highlighting its practicality for edge cases and specialized applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in KUNGFUBOT2 represents a significant leap forward in the field of humanoid whole-body control, particularly in its ability to instill versatility, dynamism, and long-horizon stability into a single, unified policy.

**Scientific Contribution:**
- **Advancing Unified Control:** VMS pushes the state-of-the-art for learning versatile motion skills from large-scale Mocap datasets within a single policy, a long-standing challenge in humanoid robotics.
- **Novel Policy Architecture:** The introduction of the Orthogonal Mixture-of-Experts (OMoE) architecture provides a principled way to enhance policy expressiveness and disentangle diverse skill representations, overcoming a major limitation of prior MLP-based approaches. This contribution could influence future multi-task reinforcement learning architectures in robotics.
- **Robust Tracking Objectives:** The hybrid tracking objective, combined with the innovative segment-level tracking reward, addresses the critical problem of balancing local motion fidelity with global stability. By relaxing rigid step-wise matching, it offers a more robust and forgiving approach to long-horizon motion execution, which is crucial for real-world reliability.
- **Comprehensive Validation:** The extensive validation in both simulation and real-world, including rigorous ablation studies and generalization tests on downstream tasks, provides strong evidence for the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework.

**Technological Impact and Potential Applications:**
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Humanoids:** VMS provides a scalable and robust foundation for building truly general-purpose humanoid robots. Its ability to learn a vast repertoire of skills within one policy simplifies control architecture and deployment.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots capable of performing a wide range of human-like motions will be more intuitive and engaging for human interaction, potentially transforming fields like personal assistance, entertainment, and telepresence.
- **Agile and Dynamic Robots:** The demonstrated capability to execute highly dynamic and complex motions, including martial arts and dance, opens doors for humanoids to perform in demanding physical environments or complex tasks requiring high agility.
- **Bridging AI and Robotics:** The success in text-to-motion generation demonstrates VMS's potential to act as a crucial low-level controller for higher-level AI planners, allowing natural language commands to translate directly into complex physical actions. This is a vital step towards more intelligent and autonomous robots.
- **Industrial and Commercial Potential:** The affiliation with China Telecom's TeleAI suggests an interest in practical applications. The robust sim-to-real transfer and ability to handle diverse skills make VMS highly relevant for future robotic solutions in logistics, manufacturing, and service industries where adaptability is key.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
1. **Lack of Visual Perception:** VMS currently lacks visual perception capabilities, which limits its ability to understand and interact with complex scenes and dynamic environments. Integrating vision would enable more autonomous and adaptive behaviors.
2. **Reliance on Mocap Dataset Quality:** The performance is heavily reliant on the scale and quality of the Mocap dataset. Uneven coverage of certain skills in the dataset could affect generalization to those less represented. Future work could explore methods to augment datasets or learn from less structured human demonstrations.

Addressing these limitations would further enhance the autonomy and versatility of humanoid robots controlled by frameworks like VMS, paving the way for a new generation of highly capable and adaptive machines.
