# TD-JEPA: Latent-predictive Representations for Zero-Shot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta developed TD-JEPA, a latent-predictive method for zero-shot reinforcement learning that learns policy-conditioned, multi-step latent dynamics from offline, reward-free data. The system achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across 65 tasks, particularly excelling with pixel-based observations, and significantly improved sample efficiency for adaptation.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning faces challenges with sample efficiency and generalization across diverse tasks, especially when dealing with high-dimensional observations like pixels.
- Existing latent-predictive representation learning methods often focus on one-step dynamics or single-policy training, limiting their applicability to zero-shot scenarios with offline, reward-free datasets.
- The need for pre-training agents on unsupervised data to solve a wide range of new tasks without additional learning at test time, which requires representations that universally support value estimation.

## Method

- Introduced **TD-JEPA** (==Temporal Difference Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture==), which adapts latent-predictive learning to a ==temporal-difference framework== for multi-step, policy-conditioned dynamics modeling.
- Employs ==asymmetric state (ϕ) and task (ψ) encoders==, trained with a novel ==TD-based loss==, to learn mutually predictive representations from off-policy, reward-free transitions.
- Distills ==zero-shot policies parameterized by task embeddings==, enabling agents to directly optimize for any linear reward function in the learned latent space at test time.

## Results

- **TD-JEPA** consistently matched or outperformed state-of-the-art zero-shot baselines across **65 tasks from 13 datasets** in both ExoRL and OGBench suites.
- The method exhibited particularly strong performance when learning from **pixel-based observations**, effectively addressing a long-standing challenge in unsupervised and zero-shot RL.
- Learned TD-JEPA representations led to substantial gains in **sample efficiency** for fast adaptation in downstream tasks, enabling rapid learning even when pre-trained encoders were **frozen**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly modeling policy-conditional successor measures through multi-step latent prediction is crucial for effective generalization in zero-shot reinforcement learning, outperforming methods focused solely on behavior policy dynamics.
> - Separating control-relevant features (state encoder) from task-defining features (task encoder) into distinct, mutually predictive representations generally yields more effective and specialized learning.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates that TD-JEPA's objective implicitly approximates successor measures and prevents representation collapse, unifying it with successor features and other unsupervised RL objectives.
