# TD-JEPA: Latent-predictive Representations for Zero-Shot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta developed TD-JEPA, a latent-predictive method for zero-shot reinforcement learning that learns policy-conditioned, multi-step latent dynamics from offline, reward-free data. The system achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across 65 tasks, particularly excelling with pixel-based observations, and significantly improved sample efficiency for adaptation.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning faces challenges with sample efficiency and generalization across diverse tasks, especially when dealing with high-dimensional observations like pixels.
- Existing latent-predictive representation learning methods often focus on one-step dynamics or single-policy training, limiting their applicability to zero-shot scenarios with offline, reward-free datasets.
- The need for pre-training agents on unsupervised data to solve a wide range of new tasks without additional learning at test time, which requires representations that universally support value estimation.

## Method

- Introduced **TD-JEPA** (==Temporal Difference Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture==), which adapts latent-predictive learning to a ==temporal-difference framework== for multi-step, policy-conditioned dynamics modeling.
- Employs ==asymmetric state (ϕ) and task (ψ) encoders==, trained with a novel ==TD-based loss==, to learn mutually predictive representations from off-policy, reward-free transitions.
- Distills ==zero-shot policies parameterized by task embeddings==, enabling agents to directly optimize for any linear reward function in the learned latent space at test time.

## Results

- **TD-JEPA** consistently matched or outperformed state-of-the-art zero-shot baselines across **65 tasks from 13 datasets** in both ExoRL and OGBench suites.
- The method exhibited particularly strong performance when learning from **pixel-based observations**, effectively addressing a long-standing challenge in unsupervised and zero-shot RL.
- Learned TD-JEPA representations led to substantial gains in **sample efficiency** for fast adaptation in downstream tasks, enabling rapid learning even when pre-trained encoders were **frozen**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly modeling policy-conditional successor measures through multi-step latent prediction is crucial for effective generalization in zero-shot reinforcement learning, outperforming methods focused solely on behavior policy dynamics.
> - Separating control-relevant features (state encoder) from task-defining features (task encoder) into distinct, mutually predictive representations generally yields more effective and specialized learning.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates that TD-JEPA's objective implicitly approximates successor measures and prevents representation collapse, unifying it with successor features and other unsupervised RL objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a core challenge in reinforcement learning (RL): learning effective state representations that generalize across diverse tasks and support efficient policy optimization. It situates itself at the intersection of **representation learning**, **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, and **zero-shot reinforcement learning (ZSRL)**.

Historically, RL has struggled with sample efficiency and generalization, particularly in high-dimensional observation spaces (e.g., pixels) and when faced with new tasks. Learning good state representations, which abstract raw observations into a compact and informative latent space, is crucial for mitigating these issues. A promising direction in this regard is **latent-predictive representation learning**, often referred to as self-predictive learning or, more broadly, Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA). This paradigm involves learning an encoder ($\phi$) and a predictor ($P$) such that the predictor can accurately estimate the latent representation of a future state ($\phi(s')$) given the current latent state ($\phi(s)$), without needing to reconstruct the raw state.

Existing latent-predictive methods in RL have primarily served as auxiliary losses to enhance performance in specific settings like reward-based learning (Schwarzer et al., 2021), behavior cloning (Lawson et al., 2025), or curiosity-driven exploration (Guo et al., 2022). They have also been applied to **unsupervised RL** by learning latent world models for goal-reaching (Assran et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025; Sobal et al., 2025) or state encoders for task definition (Jajoo et al., 2025). However, a common limitation of these approaches is their focus on one-step dynamics, single-task/single-policy training, or reliance on on-policy data.

The field of **zero-shot reinforcement learning** (ZSRL) aims to pre-train agents on unsupervised data to solve a wide range of new tasks without any additional learning at test time. A prominent framework for ZSRL is based on **successor features** (Barreto et al., 2017), which decouple rewards from environmental dynamics, allowing a policy to be evaluated for any linear reward function in a given feature space. Methods like Forward-Backward (Touati & Ollivier, 2021), HILP (Park et al., 2024), and RLDP (Jajoo et al., 2025) build upon this concept.

TD-JEPA makes a significant contribution by bridging these areas. It proposes a novel instantiation of latent-predictive representations that is inherently **multi-step** and **policy-conditioned**, leveraging **Temporal Difference (TD) learning** for **off-policy, reward-free** data. Unlike prior work, latent prediction in TD-JEPA is not merely an auxiliary objective; it is the *core* mechanism for learning all components necessary for ZSRL, including explicit state and task encoders and parameterized policies. This allows TD-JEPA to directly distill zero-shot policies that can optimize *any* reward function at test time, entirely in latent space. The theoretical analysis further unifies latent-predictive representations with successor features and other unsupervised RL methods, placing it within a broader theoretical context of learning generalizable dynamics representations. This work addresses critical gaps by enabling robust representation learning from static, reward-free datasets, a crucial step toward building more general and adaptable AI agents, especially for complex real-world applications like robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of TD-JEPA is to develop a robust, unsupervised reinforcement learning algorithm capable of learning generalizable state representations for zero-shot task execution. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims to address key limitations of existing approaches:

- **Learning Representations Predictive of Long-Term Latent Dynamics:** Traditional latent-predictive methods often focus on one-step prediction, which may not capture the full complexity of long-term environmental interactions necessary for value estimation and policy optimization across diverse tasks. TD-JEPA aims to learn representations that inherently encode these multi-step dynamics.
- **Policy-Conditioned Learning:** Most prior representation learning in RL is policy-agnostic or tied to a single behavior policy. TD-JEPA seeks to learn representations and predictors that are explicitly conditioned on a family of parameterized policies, enabling them to generalize across different behaviors.
- **Leveraging Offline, Reward-Free Data:** A major practical motivation is to learn effectively from large, readily available offline datasets that lack explicit reward signals. This reduces the need for expensive online interaction and human-labeled rewards, which are common bottlenecks in many real-world RL applications.
- **Enabling Zero-Shot Optimization:** The ultimate goal is to enable agents to solve novel tasks defined by *any* reward function at test time, without further fine-tuning. This requires learning representations that universally support value estimation and policy synthesis for a wide span of tasks.
- **Addressing Representation Collapse:** A known challenge in self-supervised learning, including latent prediction, is representation collapse (where all inputs map to similar trivial latents). A key motivation is to design an objective that inherently avoids this issue while learning meaningful representations.
- **Improving Performance in Challenging Settings:** Specifically, the authors aim to improve zero-shot RL performance in complex, high-dimensional observation spaces like pixels, which have proven difficult for existing unsupervised RL methods.
- **Unifying Theoretical Understanding:** Beyond empirical performance, the work aims to provide theoretical insights that connect temporal difference latent prediction to successor features and other forms of unsupervised RL, contributing to a more coherent understanding of these representation learning paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TD-JEPA proposes a novel, fully latent-predictive approach for zero-shot unsupervised RL. Its methodology can be broken down into several interconnected components:

- **Temporal Difference Latent-Predictive Representation Learning:** The core innovation lies in adapting latent-predictive learning, which typically focuses on one-step prediction or Monte Carlo objectives, to a temporal-difference (TD) framework.
 - **From Monte Carlo to TD:** Initially, the concept is framed as a Monte Carlo Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (L_MC-JEPA, Eq. 5), where a predictor $T_\phi(\phi(s), a, z)$ aims to predict the latent representation of a future state $\phi(s_+)$ visited by a policy $\pi_z$. This objective, however, requires on-policy sampling from successor measures, making it unsuitable for offline data.
 - **The L_TD-JEPA Loss (Eq. 7, 9):** To overcome this, the authors introduce a novel TD-based loss. This loss leverages the Bellman equation for successor features, allowing it to be estimated from single-step, off-policy transitions $(s, a, s')$ available in an offline dataset. The loss encourages $T_\phi(\phi(s), a, z)$ to be predictive of $\psi(s') + \gamma T_\phi(\phi(s'), a', z)$, effectively bootstrapping the prediction of long-term latent dynamics. This off-policy capability is critical for unsupervised learning from static datasets.

- **Asymmetric State and Task Representations:** TD-JEPA trains distinct encoders to serve different purposes:
 - **State Encoder ($\phi: S \to R^{d_\phi}$):** Embeds raw states into a latent space capturing low-level dynamical information relevant for control.
 - **Task Encoder ($\psi: S \to R^{d_\psi}$):** Embeds raw states into a latent space that defines the range of possible reward functions. This separation allows for flexibility in the dimensionality and content of each representation, recognizing that control-relevant features might differ from task-defining features.
 - **Symmetric Prediction:** To learn these distinct representations, two predictive losses are used: $L_{TD-JEPA}(\phi, T_\phi, \psi)$ for $\phi$ and $T_\phi$ (predicting in $\psi$'s space), and $L_{TD-JEPA}(\psi, T_\psi, \phi)$ for $\psi$ and $T_\psi$ (predicting in $\phi$'s space). This ensures that the representations are mutually predictive.

- **Zero-Shot Policy Distillation:**
 - **Parameterized Policies ($\pi_z$):** A family of policies, parameterized by $z \in R^{d_\psi}$ (from the task embedding space), are learned.
 - **Policy Optimization:** The policies $\pi_z(\phi(s))$ are trained to maximize $T_\phi(\phi(s), z, a)^\top z$. Since $T_\phi(\phi(s), z, a)$ approximates successor features for $\psi$, this objective aims to find policies that are optimal for rewards that can be linearly expressed in the $\psi$ space.
 - **Test-Time Inference:** Given a new reward function $r$, a corresponding task vector $z_r$ is obtained by linearly regressing $r$ onto the learned task embeddings $\psi(s)$. The agent then executes the policy $\pi_{z_r}$, achieving zero-shot generalization.

- **Theoretical Analysis:** The paper provides theoretical guarantees for an idealized tabular setting with linear predictors:
 - **Non-Collapse:** With proper initialization, the covariance matrices of $\phi$ and $\psi$ remain constant, preventing representation collapse.
 - **Successor Measure Factorization:** The learned encoders recover a low-rank factorization of the successor measures of the trained policies, while the predictors approximate successor features in the latent space.
 - **Policy Evaluation Error Bound:** The approach minimizes an upper bound on the policy evaluation error for any reward, directly supporting its utility for zero-shot optimization.
 - **Gradient Matching Argument:** A novel theoretical result shows that TD-JEPA's objective implicitly optimizes an approximation of the successor measure, unifying it with other unsupervised RL and representation learning methods.

- **Practical Implementation (Algorithm 1):**
 - **Target Networks:** Uses exponentially moving average (EMA) target networks ($\phi^-, \psi^-, T_\phi^-, T_\psi^-$) for stability, a common practice in deep RL.
 - **Covariance Regularization:** Applies an orthonormality regularization loss to $\phi$ and $\psi$ (L_REG) to explicitly prevent representation collapse and encourage diverse features.
 - **Actor Loss:** An actor loss based on the predicted successor features guides policy learning.

This comprehensive methodology allows TD-JEPA to learn rich, generalizable representations from offline, reward-free data, which can then be directly leveraged for zero-shot policy optimization across a wide spectrum of tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of TD-JEPA across a diverse set of environments and tasks yielded several key findings:

- **State-of-the-Art Zero-Shot Performance:** TD-JEPA consistently matches or outperforms state-of-the-art zero-shot baselines across 65 tasks from 13 datasets in both the ExoRL (locomotion, navigation) and OGBench (navigation, manipulation) suites. This demonstrates its broad applicability and effectiveness across different domain types and data coverage levels.
 - **Superiority in Pixel-Based RL:** Notably, TD-JEPA exhibits particularly strong performance when learning from pixel-based observations. This is a crucial finding, as learning effective representations from high-dimensional visual inputs has historically been a significant bottleneck for unsupervised and zero-shot RL methods.
 - **Consistent Top Performance:** While some baselines perform well in specific configurations, TD-JEPA is among the most consistently high-performing algorithms across the entire spectrum of evaluated problems, as evidenced by probabilities of improvement metrics.

- **Impact of Multi-Step, Policy-Aware Dynamics Modeling:** The ablation studies highlight the importance of TD-JEPA's design choices:
 - Directly modeling *policy-conditional successor measures* via multi-step prediction (as in TD-JEPA) proved more beneficial on average compared to methods (like BYOL* or BYOL-γ*) that approximate one-step or multi-step dynamics of only the *behavioral* policy. This underscores that learning dynamics relevant to the diverse *zero-shot policies* is key for generalization.

- **Advantage of Separate State and Task Representations:** The decision to train distinct state ($\phi$) and task ($\psi$) encoders, despite the potential for increased complexity, generally leads to improved empirical performance compared to a symmetric variant that uses a single, shared encoder. This suggests that separating control-relevant (state) features from task-defining (task) features allows for more effective and specialized representation learning.

- **Efficacy for Fast Adaptation and Downstream Tasks:** Beyond zero-shot performance, TD-JEPA's learned state representations demonstrated significant utility for fast adaptation:
 - **Sample Efficiency Gains:** Initializing agents with pre-trained TD-JEPA policies and critics, followed by fine-tuning (both offline and online), resulted in substantial gains in sample efficiency compared to training from scratch. This indicates the high quality and transferability of the learned representations.
 - **Effectiveness of Frozen Representations:** In many cases, freezing the pre-trained state encoder while fine-tuning the rest of the agent was sufficient to enable rapid adaptation, sometimes even outperforming full fine-tuning. This suggests that the representations capture robust, task-agnostic features highly suitable for various downstream learning problems, even in challenging visual domains.

These findings collectively establish TD-JEPA as a robust and highly effective method for learning generalizable representations in unsupervised, zero-shot RL settings, particularly excelling where visual inputs and diverse tasks demand sophisticated representation learning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TD-JEPA represents a significant advancement in the fields of reinforcement learning and self-supervised representation learning, with broad implications and potential impact:

- **Catalyst for Generalist RL Agents:** By effectively learning policy-conditioned, multi-step latent dynamics from reward-free, offline data, TD-JEPA offers a crucial building block for developing truly generalist RL agents or "behavioral foundation models." Such agents could pre-train on vast amounts of unlabelled interaction data and then solve an unbounded set of tasks with minimal or no additional fine-tuning.
- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization:** The ability to perform zero-shot optimization for *any* reward function in latent space, particularly from high-dimensional pixel inputs, pushes the boundaries of RL generalization. This capability is paramount for real-world applications where task specifications are dynamic or rewards are sparse/difficult to define.
- **Leveraging Unlabeled Data:** TD-JEPA's proficiency in learning from offline, reward-free transitions unlocks the potential of vast archives of unlabeled interaction data, such as robot teleoperation logs or public video datasets. This drastically reduces the data annotation burden and allows for pre-training in scenarios where online interaction is costly, dangerous, or impractical.
- **Theoretical Unification:** The theoretical analysis, particularly the "gradient matching" argument, provides a deeper and more unified understanding of latent-predictive representation learning. By connecting TD-based self-prediction to successor features and other unsupervised RL objectives, it offers a more coherent framework that could guide future theoretical and algorithmic developments in the field.
- **Impact on Robotics and Embodied AI:** The strong empirical performance on locomotion, navigation, and manipulation tasks, especially with pixel-based observations, positions TD-JEPA as a highly relevant method for robotics and embodied AI. Robots often rely heavily on visual sensors, and the ability to generalize across tasks and adapt quickly from pre-trained visual representations is a long-standing challenge that TD-JEPA helps to address.
- **Efficient Transfer Learning:** The demonstration of fast adaptation, where pre-trained representations accelerate downstream learning significantly (even when frozen), highlights TD-JEPA's value as a foundation for efficient transfer learning. This means learned representations can be readily reused for various subsequent tasks, reducing the overall training time and computational cost.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly points to exciting future work, such as exploring learning objectives compatible with asymmetric successor measures (beyond the simplifying symmetry assumption in theory) and benchmarking on large-scale, real robotic datasets. These directions underscore the practical relevance and ongoing research potential stemming from TD-JEPA.

In summary, TD-JEPA marks a substantial step toward more autonomous and general-purpose reinforcement learning systems. Its novel combination of temporal difference learning with policy-conditioned latent prediction, supported by robust theoretical underpinnings and strong empirical results, lays a fertile ground for the next generation of adaptable AI agents.
