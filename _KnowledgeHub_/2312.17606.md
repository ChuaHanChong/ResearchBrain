# Adaptive Control Strategy for Quadruped Robots in Actuator Degradation Scenarios

> [!summary] Summary
> Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed Adapt, an adaptive control strategy that allows quadruped robots to maintain stable locomotion despite sudden joint actuator degradation. This approach uses a reinforcement learning teacher-student framework with transformer networks, demonstrating successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and outperforming baseline methods in handling challenging "uncontrollable" joint failures.

## Problem

- Quadruped robots face reliability issues due to joint actuator degradation or failure, leading to loss of stability and mobility in real-world applications.
- Traditional fault-tolerant control methods often require extensive domain knowledge, rely on pre-defined schemes, or struggle with adaptability to unforeseen or 'uncontrollable' fault scenarios where a joint becomes freely movable.
- Learning-based approaches for fault tolerance face challenges in sample efficiency, stability, and successful transfer from simulation to real-world robots.

## Method

- Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed Adapt, an adaptive control strategy utilizing a teacher-student framework based on ==reinforcement learning== and transformer networks.
- Twelve specialized teacher policies are initially trained, each handling a specific joint actuator degradation scenario using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with domain randomization and Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP).
- A unified transformer-based student policy (Adapt) is then trained via offline knowledge distillation from the teacher policies, learning to adapt to a wide range of degradation conditions.

## Results

- In simulation, Adapt achieved performance comparable to specialized teacher policies and significantly outperformed baseline methods (Single Policy, MLP) across various joints and degradation rates.
- The learned control policy demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical Unitree A1 robot, maintaining stable locomotion in real-world joint degradation scenarios where baseline methods failed.
- Detailed analyses confirmed Adapt's ability to adjust gait patterns, contact forces, and center of gravity projection, enabling stable compensation for degraded joints and maintaining higher rewards and survival times, particularly at severe degradation rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Adapt framework's transformer architecture effectively models temporal dependencies and inter-joint relationships, enabling robust adaptation to diverse degradation scenarios.
> - The robot adapts to joint failures by dynamically shifting its center of gravity away from the affected leg and adjusting contact forces and gait patterns to maintain balance.
> - Knowledge distillation from multiple specialized teacher policies allows a single student policy to generalize across various and severe joint degradation types, including challenging 'uncontrollable' free-moving joint faults.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Quadruped robots have gained significant attention for their ability to navigate complex environments, but their reliability can be compromised by actuator failures in real-world applications. When joint actuators degrade or fail, these robots often lose stability and mobility, limiting their practical use in critical applications such as search and rescue, exploration, or industrial inspection.

In response to this challenge, researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University have developed an innovative adaptive control strategy that enables quadruped robots to maintain effective locomotion even when experiencing sudden joint actuator degradation. The approach, called Actuator Degradation Adaptation Transformer (Adapt), utilizes a teacher-student framework based on reinforcement learning and transformer networks.

The figure above illustrates how a quadruped robot transitions from normal locomotion to adaptation after experiencing sudden joint degradation. The red and blue dots indicate affected and supporting legs, respectively, showing how the robot shifts to a three-legged support gait to maintain stability.

#### 1.1. Research Context

Robotic systems in real-world environments face numerous challenges that can lead to hardware failures. For quadruped robots specifically, joint actuator degradation represents a significant concern that can compromise mobility and task performance. Traditional approaches to fault-tolerant control typically rely on:

1. Model-based methods that require extensive domain knowledge
2. Pre-defined control schemes for specific failure modes
3. Manual tuning for different robot configurations and environments

These approaches often struggle with adaptability, particularly when confronted with unforeseen fault scenarios. Learning-based methods offer a promising alternative but face challenges in sample efficiency, stability, and sim-to-real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The proposed research addresses a specific gap in the field: developing robust adaptive control for the "uncontrollable" fault scenario, where a joint becomes freely movable rather than locked. This scenario is particularly challenging as it creates unpredictable dynamics that are difficult to compensate for using traditional control methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Methodology

The researchers formulate the quadruped robot control problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), with:

- **State space (S)**: Includes the robot's base velocity, angular velocity, orientation, joint positions, and velocities
- **Action space (A)**: Target joint positions
- **Actuator degradation rate (d)**: A parameter that quantifies the degree of degradation for each joint
- **Reward function**: Encourages forward motion, stability, and appropriate gait patterns while penalizing undesirable behaviors

The methodology consists of two main phases:

1. **Teacher policy training**: Twelve specialized teacher policies are trained, each handling a specific joint actuator degradation scenario. These use the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm with domain randomization and Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) to guide natural locomotion patterns.

2. **Student policy training**: A transformer-based student policy (Adapt) learns to adapt to various degradation scenarios by distilling knowledge from the teacher policies.

#### 3.2. Adapt Framework

The Adapt framework represents the core innovation of this research, illustrated in the following diagram:

The framework consists of two main components:

1. **Online Training (Teacher Policies)**:
 - Twelve teacher policies (π^i) are trained using PPO
 - Each specializes in handling degradation in a specific joint
 - Training incorporates environmental rewards (r_env) and style rewards (r_style) from an AMP discriminator
 - The AMP discriminator helps ensure natural motion patterns by comparing robot movements to reference motion data

2. **Offline Distillation (Student Policy)**:
 - Data collection from teacher policies creates a diverse dataset of states (s), actions (a), and degradation rates (d)
 - The Adapt transformer processes this data to learn a unified policy
 - Training uses mean squared error loss (L_mse) between student and teacher actions
 - The trained model can be deployed directly on real robots without additional fine-tuning

The transformer architecture enables Adapt to effectively model the temporal dependencies in robot motion and the relationships between different joints, facilitating better adaptation to degradation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Adapt framework represents a significant advancement in fault-tolerant control for quadruped robots. By leveraging reinforcement learning and transformer networks, it enables robots to maintain effective locomotion despite joint actuator degradation, relying solely on internal sensors.

Key contributions and impacts include:

1. **Addressing the uncontrollable fault scenario**: The research focuses on the more challenging scenario where a joint becomes freely movable rather than locked, which is less explored in previous work.

2. **Reducing reliance on domain expertise**: The learning-based approach reduces the need for manual tuning and predefined control schemes, making robust control more accessible.

3. **Enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer**: The successful deployment on a physical robot without fine-tuning demonstrates the practicality of the approach for real-world applications.

4. **Providing insights into adaptation strategies**: The detailed analyses of gait patterns and center of gravity shifts provide valuable insights into how robots can effectively adapt to hardware failures.

This research contributes to the development of more reliable and resilient quadruped robots for applications in challenging environments such as disaster response, exploration, military operations, and industrial inspection. The ability to maintain functionality despite hardware failures is critical for autonomous systems operating in environments where human intervention is limited or impossible.
