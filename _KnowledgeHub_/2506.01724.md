# Active Learning via Vision-Language Model Adaptation with Open Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Active Learning with Open Resources (ALOR) enhances active learning by integrating open-source Vision-Language Models with their vast pretraining datasets. The framework significantly improves data efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art performance with approximately 7.2% higher accuracy over baselines by adopting contrastive tuning and a novel tail-first sampling strategy.

## Problem

- Minimizing the high costs and efforts associated with manual data labeling for specific downstream tasks in active learning.
- Existing VLM-based active learning methods underutilize the massive open pretraining datasets (e.g., LAION-400M) of VLMs and often assume prompt tuning is the most suitable adaptation strategy, potentially limiting performance.
- Addressing inherent biases and long-tailed distributions within real-world and retrieved data, which can hinder fair and robust model performance.

## Method

- Introduces ==Active Learning with Open Resources (ALOR)==, a framework integrating ==Retrieval-based Data Augmentation (RDA)== and ==Tail First Sampling (TFS)==, built on ==Contrastive Tuning (CT)== identified as superior to Prompt Tuning, Linear Probing, and Finetuning.
- Employs RDA to augment limited labeled data with relevant examples from VLM pretraining datasets via efficient string-matching and filtering, with hard-capping to balance retrieved data.
- Proposes TFS, an active learning strategy that prioritizes selecting highly uncertain examples from underrepresented "tail" classes identified through pseudo-labeling.

## Results

- Retrieval-based Data Augmentation (RDA) consistently boosted performance, achieving **1.7 times** higher accuracy in Round-0 and over **8%** accuracy gains in subsequent rounds for all active learning methods.
- Tail First Sampling (TFS) outperformed active learning baselines under prompt tuning, while Contrastive Tuning (CT) proved the superior VLM adaptation approach across low-data regimes and subsequent rounds.
- The full ALOR framework, combining CT, RDA, and TFS, achieved state-of-the-art performance with average accuracy gains of approximately **+7.2%** over strong baselines across five benchmark datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Open pretraining data of Vision-Language Models is a valuable, underutilized resource that can universally and significantly boost active learning performance.
> - Contrastive Tuning (CT) is a more effective VLM adaptation strategy for active learning than Prompt Tuning (PT), challenging existing assumptions about lightweight adaptation in limited-data scenarios.
> - Explicitly addressing data imbalance by prioritizing sampling from underrepresented 'tail' classes, guided by insights from retrieved data, is critical for improving overall model robustness and performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of **Active Learning (AL)** and **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, two highly active areas in machine learning. Its primary contribution lies in bridging a crucial gap in how these fields traditionally interact, particularly concerning the utilization of readily available open data.

**Active Learning (AL)** is a well-established subfield of machine learning focused on efficiently obtaining labeled data by strategically selecting the most informative unlabeled samples for annotation. This is motivated by the high cost and effort of manual data labeling, especially in domains requiring expert knowledge. Traditional AL methods often rely on metrics like uncertainty (e.g., entropy) or diversity (e.g., core-set selection) to identify valuable samples.

**Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP, have revolutionized computer vision by demonstrating remarkable zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities across various visual tasks. These models are typically pretrained on vast web-scale datasets of image-text pairs, enabling them to learn powerful multimodal representations. While VLMs offer strong foundational abilities, achieving peak performance on specific downstream tasks usually requires further adaptation, often through finetuning on task-specific labeled data.

The current research landscape for VLM-based AL methods primarily focuses on leveraging the *open-source nature of VLMs* themselves. However, a significant oversight, as identified by this paper, is the **underutilization of the *open data* (especially the massive pretraining datasets) that these VLMs were trained on.** Existing VLM-based AL approaches typically employ **prompt tuning (PT)** for model adaptation, assuming that this lightweight method minimizes the risk of overfitting to the limited labeled data characteristic of AL scenarios. This paper directly challenges this assumption, rigorously comparing PT with other VLM adaptation strategies.

Furthermore, **Retrieval-based Data Augmentation (RDA)** has gained traction in related fields like few-shot learning and natural language processing (e.g., Retrieval-Augmented Generation for LLMs) to enhance model performance by dynamically retrieving relevant information from large external data sources. However, its application within the context of active learning, particularly leveraging VLM pretraining data, has been largely unexplored.

This work critically advances the field by:
1. **Introducing the comprehensive exploitation of open resources (both open-source VLM and its pretraining data) into AL**, thereby creating a new paradigm for data-efficient learning.
2. **Rigourously re-evaluating VLM adaptation strategies for AL**, challenging the long-held reliance on prompt tuning.
3. **Proposing a novel AL sampling strategy (Tail First Sampling)** that directly addresses the implicit biases and long-tailed distributions inherent in real-world (and retrieved) data, a crucial aspect often overlooked in AL discussions but critical for fair and robust model performance.

By integrating these components, the paper positions itself as a significant contribution to developing more effective, efficient, and potentially fairer active learning systems that fully harness the power of modern foundation models and their underlying open data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives of this research stem from a clear motivation to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of active learning, particularly when leveraging the advanced capabilities of Vision-Language Models. The authors identify several critical challenges and opportunities:

1. **Reduce Data Labeling Costs:** The fundamental motivation for active learning is to minimize the expensive and time-consuming process of data annotation, which often requires specialized domain expertise. The authors aim to develop an AL framework that significantly reduces the amount of labeled data required to achieve high model performance.

2. **Fully Leverage Open Resources with VLMs:** While recent AL methods have begun to incorporate open-source VLMs, they have largely neglected a vast, publicly available resource: the web-scale open data used for pretraining these VLMs (e.g., LAION-400M). The key objective here is to demonstrate how strategically retrieving and utilizing this "open data" can profoundly augment task-specific labeled data and boost AL performance. This moves beyond merely using the pretrained VLM to actively exploiting its foundational training data.

3. **Rigorously Evaluate VLM Adaptation Strategies in AL:** A prevailing assumption in VLM-based AL literature is that prompt tuning (PT) is the most suitable adaptation method, particularly for limited labeled data, as it directly utilizes pretrained parameters and purportedly reduces overfitting. The authors motivate a comprehensive comparative study of popular VLM adaptation approaches—including Linear Probing (LP), Finetuning (FT), and Contrastive Tuning (CT)—against PT to determine which method genuinely offers superior performance in AL settings.

4. **Address VLM Biases and Data Imbalance:** The authors observe that the retrieved open data, when filtered for task-relevant classes, often exhibits naturally imbalanced or long-tailed distributions. This imbalance reflects inherent biases within the VLM itself, acquired during pretraining on such data, and also mirrors the imbalanced nature of real-world task-specific data. A crucial objective is to develop a simple yet effective active learning strategy that explicitly addresses these imbalances by prioritizing the sampling of data from underrepresented, "tail" classes, thereby improving model robustness and fairness across all categories.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of VLM-based active learning by systematically exploring untapped data resources and challenging established methodological assumptions, ultimately leading to a more powerful and equitable data acquisition paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Active Learning with Open Resources (ALOR)**, a novel framework designed to enhance active learning by integrating open-source Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with their extensive pretraining datasets. ALOR comprises three key components: Retrieval-based Data Augmentation (RDA), a rigorous evaluation of VLM Adaptation Approaches, and a new sampling strategy called Tail First Sampling (TFS).

#### 3.1. Retrieval-based Data Augmentation (RDA)

The first critical step in ALOR is to augment the limited, task-specific labeled data with relevant examples retrieved from the VLM's pretraining dataset.
- **Open Data Source:** The method primarily uses LAION-400M, a large-scale open dataset of image-text pairs, as the VLM's pretraining data, and OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 as the VLM.
- **Retrieval Mechanism:** Instead of computationally expensive feature-matching across hundreds of millions of images, the authors adopt an efficient string-matching approach. For given task-specific class names, relevant pretraining texts (captions) are identified. Their corresponding images are then fetched.
- **Filtering:** Outlier images are filtered out based on feature similarities between the class names and the retrieved images, ensuring higher relevance.
- **Data Capping:** To manage the potentially vast and highly imbalanced retrieved data, a capping strategy is employed. The number of per-class retrieved examples is capped (e.g., to 500), and further analysis shows that forcing a more balanced distribution through "hard-capping" (limiting maximum examples per class) yields better results than ratio-based capping.
- **Augmentation:** This retrieved data is then incorporated into the labeled data pool alongside the initially labeled task-specific examples.

#### 3.2. Tail First Sampling (TFS) for Active Learning

A key insight derived from RDA is that the retrieved data (and by extension, likely the real-world unlabeled data) often exhibits long-tailed or imbalanced distributions across task-relevant classes. This imbalance implies inherent biases within the pretrained VLM. To address this, TFS is proposed as an "embarrassingly simple yet effective" active learning strategy.
- **Pseudo-labeling:** In each AL round, the currently adapted VLM is used to generate pseudo-labels for the entire unlabeled data pool.
- **Rarest Class Identification:** TFS identifies the "rarest class" (most underrepresented) within the *current combined labeled dataset* (which includes both task-specific human-labeled data and the RDA-retrieved data).
- **Uncertainty-based Selection:** From the subset of unlabeled examples that were pseudo-labeled as this rarest class, TFS selects the example with the highest prediction uncertainty (e.g., maximum entropy).
- **Oracle Labeling:** This selected example is then sent to an oracle (human annotator) for accurate labeling.
- **Data Integration:** The newly labeled example is added to the overall labeled data pool for subsequent VLM adaptation.
The rationale is that prioritizing data from underrepresented classes, especially uncertain examples within those classes, helps the model to reduce its bias and improve performance on these difficult categories, ultimately boosting overall accuracy.

#### 3.3. VLM Adaptation Approaches in Active Learning

ALOR systematically evaluates various VLM adaptation strategies, challenging the prevalent use of Prompt Tuning (PT) in existing VLM-based AL literature.
- **Linear Probing (LP):** Only a linear classifier layer is trained on top of a frozen VLM visual encoder.
- **Finetuning (FT):** The parameters of the VLM's visual encoder are updated, and a linear classifier is learned.
- **Prompt Tuning (PT):** A set of learnable context vectors (prompts) are trained and concatenated with text inputs, while the VLM's visual and text encoders remain frozen. This is the *de facto* approach in current VLM-based AL.
- **Contrastive Tuning (CT):** Both the VLM's visual and text encoders are updated using a contrastive loss, similar to the original VLM pretraining objective. This method essentially continues the VLM's pretraining objective but on task-specific data.
The adapted VLM (using any of these methods) is then used to infer pseudo-labels and uncertainties for the next round of active learning.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **VLM & Open Data:** OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 and its pretraining data LAION-400M are utilized.
- **Benchmarks:** Five standard fine-grained image classification datasets are used: Semi-Aves, Food101, FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford Cars, and OxfordPets.
- **AL Protocol:** Experiments are conducted over 7 rounds (Round 0 to Round 6). Round 0 starts with one labeled image per class. In subsequent rounds, a budget of K (number of classes) images is selected and labeled.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Accuracy and Macro-F1 score are reported, averaged over three random runs for statistical robustness.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against strong AL baselines including Random, Entropy, Coreset, BADGE, PCB+BADGE, ALFA-Mix, and LoGo.
- **Implementation Details:** Specific learning rates for backbone and classifier, temperature updates, optimizer (AdamW for finetuning, SGD for prompt tuning), batch size, and weight decay are detailed.

The ALOR framework, particularly its emphasis on retrieving and intelligently sampling from open data, coupled with a thorough re-evaluation of adaptation techniques, represents a comprehensive approach to advancing active learning in the age of foundation models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings that collectively demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed ALOR framework and challenge existing paradigms in VLM-based active learning:

1. **Retrieval-based Data Augmentation (RDA) Universally Boosts AL Performance:**
 - Experiments clearly show that incorporating task-relevant data retrieved from the VLM's pretraining dataset (LAION-400M) significantly enhances the performance of *all* compared active learning methods.
 - In the very first round (Round-0), methods augmented with RDA achieve a remarkable **1.7 times higher accuracy** compared to their counterparts without RDA.
 - Across subsequent rounds, RDA consistently leads to **over 8% accuracy gains** for each method, underscoring its profound impact on jumpstarting and sustaining AL performance.
 - The study on capping strategies for retrieved data reveals that a "hard-capping" approach, which actively promotes a more balanced distribution among retrieved examples per class, yields superior results compared to merely proportionally reducing them.

2. **Tail First Sampling (TFS) Outperforms Prior AL Methods:**
 - When VLM adaptation is performed using prompt tuning (the prevailing method in current AL literature), the proposed Tail First Sampling (TFS) strategy consistently **outperforms all other state-of-the-art AL baselines**.
 - Analysis of per-class accuracy over AL rounds shows that TFS effectively addresses the learning disparities across classes. Specifically, it demonstrates a **quick improvement in accuracy for underrepresented classes**, which is a direct consequence of its mechanism to prioritize sampling data from these "tail" categories. This confirms the value of using RDA-revealed imbalanced distributions to guide sampling.

3. **Contrastive Tuning (CT) is the Superior VLM Adaptation Approach for AL:**
 - A rigorous comparison of VLM adaptation methods (Linear Probing, Finetuning, Prompt Tuning, and Contrastive Tuning) reveals that **Contrastive Tuning (CT) consistently outperforms all others.** This holds true both in the initial low-data regime (Round-0) and across all subsequent AL rounds, irrespective of whether RDA is used or which specific AL method is applied.
 - CT's superior performance, even without RDA, is a critical finding, as it **challenges the widespread assumption that Prompt Tuning is the optimal or safest adaptation strategy** for VLMs in limited-data AL scenarios. The results suggest that continuing the contrastive learning objective, even with limited task-specific data, is more effective for leveraging the VLM's powerful representations.

4. **ALOR (CT + RDA + TFS) Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - The final proposed method, ALOR, which combines Contrastive Tuning for VLM adaptation, Retrieval-based Data Augmentation, and Tail First Sampling, achieves **state-of-the-art performance** across all five benchmark datasets.
 - ALOR significantly surpasses previous best methods, demonstrating average accuracy gains of approximately **+7.2%** over strong baselines like PCB+BADGE and LoGo. This highlights the synergistic benefits of integrating all three components.

In summary, the key findings underscore that actively leveraging VLM pretraining data and adopting contrastive finetuning are critical for advancing active learning. The proposed TFS strategy, built upon insights from retrieved data distributions, further optimizes this process by addressing inherent model biases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Active Learning via Vision-Language Model Adaptation with Open Data" carries substantial significance and potential impact across both academic research and practical applications of machine learning.

1. **Re-shaping VLM-based Active Learning Paradigms:**
 - The most significant impact is challenging the conventional wisdom in VLM-based active learning. By demonstrating that not only the open-source VLM but also its vast **pretraining dataset is a crucial, underutilized resource for AL**, the paper opens a new avenue for research and development. Future AL methods leveraging VLMs will likely be compelled to consider methods for integrating and adapting external open data.
 - The decisive finding that **Contrastive Tuning (CT) significantly outperforms Prompt Tuning (PT)** as a VLM adaptation strategy for AL is a critical paradigm shift. This directly questions the long-held assumption in the literature that lightweight PT is superior for limited-data scenarios. This may lead to a re-evaluation of VLM adaptation techniques across various data-efficient learning tasks beyond AL.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Robustness in Data Annotation:**
 - ALOR's substantial performance gains (e.g., +7% accuracy) signify a major leap in active learning efficiency. This translates directly to **reduced data labeling costs and faster model development cycles** for real-world applications.
 - By actively addressing the naturally imbalanced distributions of classes through Tail First Sampling (TFS), ALOR contributes to **more robust and potentially fairer models**. This is particularly vital in applications where certain classes are inherently rare or expensive to acquire, ensuring that the model does not disproportionately neglect them.

3. **Broader Applicability to Specialized Domains:**
 - The problem of costly data labeling is most acute in highly specialized fields, such as medical imaging, scientific discovery, environmental monitoring, or fine-grained recognition of rare species, where domain expertise is essential for annotation. ALOR provides a powerful tool to accelerate the adoption of machine learning in these areas, potentially leading to faster breakthroughs and more effective solutions.

4. **Advancing Open Science and Reproducibility:**
 - The paper's emphasis on utilizing open-source VLMs (OpenCLIP) and publicly available pretraining datasets (LAION-400M), coupled with the release of their open-source code, aligns perfectly with principles of open science. This fosters transparency, reproducibility, and allows the community to build upon their findings.

5. **Future Research Directions:**
 - The authors explicitly outline limitations, such as potential domain gaps between retrieved and task-specific data, reliance on potentially biased pseudo-labels for TFS, and further improvements to VLM adaptation techniques. These clearly articulated points provide a fertile ground for future research, stimulating further innovation in areas like domain adaptation for retrieved data, bias mitigation in pseudo-labeling, and advanced hybrid sampling strategies for AL.

In conclusion, this research provides a comprehensive and impactful framework that not only pushes the state-of-the-art in active learning but also fundamentally alters our understanding of how to effectively leverage the full spectrum of open resources with Vision-Language Models. Its implications extend to making AI more accessible, efficient, and robust across a wide array of demanding applications.
