# SocialNav: Training Human-Inspired Foundation Model for Socially-Aware Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> SocialNav introduces a hierarchical "brain-action" foundation model for socially-aware embodied navigation, unifying high-level social norm comprehension with low-level trajectory generation through a multi-stage training pipeline and a novel reinforcement learning framework. The model achieved an 86.1% success rate and 82.5% distance compliance rate on a new benchmark, outperforming baselines.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation research primarily focuses on shortest paths and collision avoidance, overlooking social awareness crucial for acceptable agent deployment in human environments.
- Existing methods, including Vision-Language Models, often lack a direct connection between high-level social reasoning and low-level action generation, or struggle to generalize social norms beyond explicit demonstration data.
- A scarcity of large-scale, multimodal datasets exists that integrate both cognitive knowledge (e.g., social rules, contextual reasoning) and diverse action-oriented demonstrations for training socially-aware agents.

## Method

- Develops **SocialNav**, a hierarchical "==brain-action==" foundation model comprising a VLM "Brain Module" for high-level semantic reasoning and an "Action Expert" for end-to-end trajectory generation using conditional flow matching.
- Introduces the large-scale, heterogeneous SocNav Dataset (over 7 million samples) and the high-fidelity SocNav Benchmark for training and evaluating socially-aware agents in diverse, photorealistic environments.
- Proposes a three-stage training pipeline: initial pre-training for general navigation, fine-tuning with high-quality real-world data, and explicit social rule alignment using Socially-Aware Flow Exploration GRPO (SAFE-GRPO).

## Results

- Achieved an **86.1%** Success Rate (SR) and **82.5%** Distance Compliance Rate (DCR) on the SocNav Benchmark, significantly outperforming the second-best method (CityWalker) by +**38.3%** SR and +**46.4%** DCR.
- Demonstrated superior performance on the open-loop CityWalker benchmark with an overall Maximum Average Orientation Error (MAOE) of 7.8, indicating improved generalization and adherence to human social walking norms.
- Successfully deployed on a Unitree Go2 robot in real-world environments, achieving an average Success Rate of **85.0%** across diverse scenes (e.g., street crossing, office park), validating its practical effectiveness in real-time operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical "brain-action" architecture effectively integrates abstract social norm understanding from a VLM with precise, low-level trajectory generation, enabling contextually appropriate navigation.
> - The inclusion of cognitive activation data (e.g., social traversability annotations, Chain-of-Thought explanations) during VLM training is critical for instilling robust social understanding, which guides effective policy optimization in reinforcement learning.
> - Flow-based reinforcement learning (SAFE-GRPO), with a carefully designed norm-aware reward function, effectively aligns agent policies with human social conventions, addressing the limitations of pure imitation learning in complex social contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation research has primarily focused on optimizing for shortest paths and collision avoidance. Existing methods, such as GNM, ViNT, NoMaD, and CityWalker, have demonstrated capabilities in these areas. However, a significant gap remains in addressing social awareness and compliance with human norms, which are crucial for the safe and acceptable deployment of autonomous agents in shared human environments. For instance, a robot designed for efficiency might take shortcuts through restricted areas, leading to socially inappropriate behaviors.

Previous efforts in social navigation have explored handcrafted cost functions or, more recently, employed Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for high-level reasoning. However, these VLM-based approaches often lack a direct connection to low-level action generation. The application of flow matching (FM) in vision-and-language-action (VLA) models has enabled the modeling of multimodal action distributions, but these models typically operate within an imitation learning paradigm. This limitation prevents them from acquiring the causal understanding necessary to robustly adapt to novel or dynamic social situations that are not explicitly present in demonstration data.

The SocialNav project addresses these limitations by proposing a hierarchical foundation model that integrates high-level social norm comprehension with low-level, socially compliant trajectory generation. It attempts to bridge the gap between abstract social reasoning and concrete robot actions. By introducing a novel reinforcement learning framework (SAFE-GRPO) that is flow-based and explicitly rewards socially compliant behaviors, the work expands upon foundational research like GRPO and Flow-GRPO, which combine generative models with online reinforcement learning for human preference alignment. The authors contribute a new large-scale, multimodal dataset and a high-fidelity benchmark to support the development and evaluation of socially-aware navigation models, thus pushing the boundaries of embodied AI towards more human-aligned and contextually intelligent agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a foundational model, termed SocialNav, for socially-aware embodied navigation. This model is designed to possess a dual capability: understanding high-level social norms and subsequently generating low-level, socially compliant trajectories in diverse environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in current embodied navigation systems:
- **Societal Integration of Agents:** As autonomous agents become increasingly integrated into everyday social environments, their navigation capabilities must extend beyond mere operational efficiency and safety. Compliance with established social norms is identified as an essential prerequisite for real-world deployment, particularly for applications like robotic guide dogs or service robots operating in public spaces.
- **Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Current navigation methods predominantly focus on geometric path planning and collision avoidance. While effective for reaching goals and avoiding physical obstacles, these approaches often overlook the implicit social rules governing human environments. This oversight can lead to trajectories that are geometrically optimal but socially disruptive or inappropriate, such as jaywalking, traversing landscaped areas, or failing to yield in crowded spaces.
- **Disconnection Between Reasoning and Action:** While some advanced systems incorporate VLMs for high-level semantic understanding, this reasoning is frequently decoupled from the generation of low-level, robot-executable actions. A coherent framework that seamlessly integrates cognitive understanding of social norms with precise trajectory planning is largely absent.
- **Data Scarcity for Socially-Aware Training:** The development of models capable of nuanced social navigation requires rich, multimodal data that encapsulates both cognitive knowledge (e.g., explicit social rules, contextual reasoning) and diverse action-oriented intuition (e.g., human demonstrations of socially acceptable movements). Such comprehensive datasets are currently limited.
- **Inadequacy of Pure Imitation Learning:** Standard imitation learning, while effective for behavior cloning, often fails to capture the underlying causal structure of normative conduct. Even when social priors are implicitly present in demonstration data, imitation learning struggles to generalize these principles to novel or dynamic social situations, limiting the robustness and adaptability of the agent.

To address these motivations, the research outlines specific objectives:
- To design a hierarchical "brain-action" architecture that unifies high-level social norm understanding with the generation of norm-compliant trajectories.
- To construct a large-scale, heterogeneous dataset (SocNav Dataset) comprising both cognitive activation signals (e.g., Chain-of-Thought explanations, social traversability predictions) and diverse expert trajectories from various sources (internet videos, simulations, real robots).
- To propose a multi-stage training pipeline that progressively injects general navigation skills, refines them with high-quality data, and then explicitly aligns the policy with social rules using a novel reinforcement learning framework, Socially-Aware Flow Exploration GRPO (SAFE-GRPO).
- To establish a high-fidelity evaluation platform (SocNav Benchmark) that blends physics simulation and photorealistic rendering in complex social scenes, enabling a comprehensive assessment of both fundamental navigation capabilities and social compliance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SocialNav framework employs a multi-faceted approach involving a hierarchical model architecture, a specially constructed large-scale dataset and benchmark, and a multi-stage training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

SocialNav adopts a hierarchical "brain-action" design, integrating two tightly coupled modules:
- **The Brain Module:** This component functions as the cognitive core, implemented as a Vision-Language Model (VLM). Its role is to perform generative, autoregressive textual reasoning to infer environmental semantics relevant to navigation. It can produce three types of interpretable outputs:
 - **Socially traversable regions:** Delineated as polygons, identifying areas like sidewalks, crosswalks, or stairs.
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning:** Step-by-step textual explanations for navigational decisions.
 - **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** Responses to free-form questions to enhance scene understanding.
 This module is designed to provide semantic and interpretable insights, forming the basis for socially responsible navigation. Qwen2.5-VL (3B) is utilized as the VLM.
- **The Action Expert:** This module specializes in end-to-end trajectory generation. It leverages conditional flow matching to model a distribution of actions. The action expert is conditioned on latent semantic features extracted from the last-layer features of the VLM. This mechanism allows for the generation of efficient and socially compliant trajectories by decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control, while preserving a strong semantic connection. A Diffusion Transformer is used for the Action Expert, with 12 layers, 12 attention heads, and a hidden dimension of 1536. Trajectory denoising is performed iteratively for 5 steps during inference.

#### 3.2. SocNav Dataset and Benchmark

To support the training and evaluation of socially-aware agents, the authors introduce the SocNav Dataset and SocNav Benchmark.

**SocNav Dataset:** This large-scale, heterogeneous dataset comprises over 7 million training samples and is structured into two main components:
- **Expert Trajectories Pyramid (ETP):** A three-layer hierarchical dataset covering diverse indoor and outdoor urban scenes.
 - **D_video (2.0 million samples):** Pseudo-trajectories derived from public urban exploration videos. A pipeline involving 3D reconstruction (π3), metric scale alignment (MoGe), and pseudo-trajectory synthesis generates these.
 - **D_sim (1.7 million samples):** Trajectories from high-fidelity simulated scenes. This includes SocialGS, a new dataset of 3,400 real-world scenes (4.5 km²) reconstructed via 3DGS covering social environments, and SocCity, a 3.37 km² dynamic urban scene in Isaac Sim with simulated vehicles and pedestrians. Trajectories are generated on manually annotated traversable road networks, encompassing standard and recovery scenarios.
 - **D_real (340K samples):** High-quality trajectories collected from real-world robots from various public datasets (SCAND, Huron, Recon, CityWalker teleoperation data). These provide ground-truth metric accuracy and physical realism for fine-tuning.
- **Cognitive Activation Dataset (CAD, D_cog):** This dataset integrates non-trajectory auxiliary tasks to instill a semantically grounded understanding of environmental and human-centric norms.
 - **Social Traversability Recognition (1.2 million samples):** Manually annotated polygons over socially traversable regions (e.g., sidewalks) in first-person images, directly training the model to distinguish acceptable paths.
 - **Navigation Chain-of-Thought (CoT, 825K samples):** Generated by prompting Qwen2.5VL-72B with first-person images and templates, providing step-by-step textual rationales for navigation decisions.
 - **General Visual Question Answering (VQA, 1 million samples):** Curated from existing datasets to maintain general-world knowledge and spatial reasoning capabilities.

**SocNav Benchmark:** This unified, high-fidelity evaluation platform combines the physics simulation of Isaac Sim with photorealistic rendering from 3DGS. It is built upon 9 new large-scale social scenes (73K m²) captured and reconstructed using 3DGS, including parks, streets, offices, and a campus. Evaluations use a standardized Unitree Go2 robot model with a consistent locomotion policy. Each 3DGS scene is converted into a mesh for accurate collision feedback in Isaac Sim, and digital humans are randomly introduced to simulate dynamic collisions. For rigorous benchmarking, 10 start-goal pairs at 20m and 100m distances are sampled per scene, creating 20 evaluation cases per scene.

#### 3.3. Training Methodology

A three-stage training pipeline is proposed to progressively instill general navigation priors and social compliance:
- **Stage 1: Pre-training for General Navigation Ability:** The full model is trained end-to-end using AdamW (batch size 192, learning rate 5 × 10⁻⁵ for 3 epochs on 96 H20 GPUs). This stage utilizes the ETP datasets (D_video and D_sim) to activate the VLM's navigation capability and train the flow model to predict low-level waypoints. The CAD (D_cog) is concurrently used to improve the VLM's reasoning and decision-making through CoT and VQA tasks, and to equip it with traversable region prediction abilities.
- **Stage 2: Fine-tuning with High-Quality Real-World Data:** The model is fine-tuned on high-quality expert trajectories from real-world robots (D_real). During this stage, the VLM is frozen, and only the action expert is optimized (batch size 256, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁵ on 32 H20 GPUs). This approach aims to reduce the sim-to-real gap while preserving the VLM’s semantic and social reasoning.
- **Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning for Social Rule Alignment (SAFE-GRPO):** This stage explicitly aligns the policy with human social conventions using SAFE-GRPO, the first flow-based reinforcement learning framework for embodied navigation. The action expert is optimized on expert trajectories from SocCity within D_sim (rollout batch size 128, learning rate 5 × 10⁻⁷ on 16 H20 GPUs).
 - **Exploration:** The deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE) of the flow policy is converted into a stochastic differential equation (SDE) to encourage diverse yet semantically grounded exploration. Randomness is introduced during flow integration, but the VLM's semantic conditioning (Z_VLM) remains fixed, guiding the exploration.
 - **Reward Function:** A comprehensive reward function `R = R_social + λ_expert R_expert + λ_smooth R_smooth + λ_eff R_eff` is designed.
 - `R_social`: The primary reward derived from a semantic occupancy map (M_occ), encouraging the agent to maintain safe clearance from non-traversable areas, thereby promoting social compliance.
 - `R_expert`: Promotes consistency with expert trajectories.
 - `R_smooth`: Enforces natural motion continuity.
 - `R_eff`: Rewards efficient progress toward the goal.
 This reward structure ensures effective navigation while adhering to social norms.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

- **Open-loop:** Maximum Average Orientation Error (MAOE).
- **Closed-loop:** Success Rate (SR), Route Completion (RC), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) for navigation performance. For social compliance, Distance Compliance Rate (DCR) and Time Compliance Rate (TCR) are introduced, measuring the proportion of distance and time spent in socially traversable regions during successful episodes.
- **Baselines:** GNM, ViNT, NoMaD, and CityWalker, retrained for point-goal tasks as needed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results across open-loop, closed-loop, and real-world evaluations.

#### 4.1. Results on CityWalker Benchmark (Open-Loop)

On the open-loop CityWalker benchmark, SocialNav consistently outperformed baseline methods across all critical scenarios and in the overall mean Maximum Average Orientation Error (MAOE). SocialNav (Full) achieved an overall MAOE of 7.8, which is lower than the previous state-of-the-art CityWalker (11.5), indicating improved generalization, robustness, and closer alignment with human social walking norms. Notably, SocialNav showed significant gains in "Turn" and "Crossing" scenarios, where social layout and semantic understanding are particularly crucial.

#### 4.2. Results on SocNav Benchmark (Closed-Loop)

On the newly introduced SocNav Benchmark, which comprises unseen environments, SocialNav demonstrated significant advancements in both navigation performance and social compliance.
- **Navigation Performance:** SocialNav (Full) achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 86.1%, a Route Completion (RC) of 91.2%, and a Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 77.4%. These figures represent substantial increases over the second-best method, CityWalker, with improvements of +38.3 in SR, +26.5 in RC, and +32.7 in SPL, respectively.
- **Social Compliance:** SocialNav (Full) attained a Distance Compliance Rate (DCR) of 82.5% and a Time Compliance Rate (TCR) of 82.9%. These rates are more than double those of CityWalker (DCR: 36.1%, TCR: 36.6%), indicating a marked improvement in adhering to social norms. Qualitative visualizations confirmed that SocialNav consistently chose paths adhering to sidewalks and designated walkways, unlike baselines that frequently took shorter but socially inappropriate routes through restricted areas.
- **Effect of Model Design:** An ablation study comparing SocialNav* (trained only with Imitation Learning on D_real) against other baselines trained similarly, showed that SocialNav* significantly outperformed them, demonstrating the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of its core model architecture.

#### 4.3. Results on Real-World Deployments

SocialNav (Full) was deployed on a Unitree Go2 robot in real-world environments (street crossing, office park, shopping mall). It achieved an average Success Rate of 85.0%, substantially outperforming NoMaD (50.0%) and CityWalker (62.5%). In the challenging street crossing environment, SocialNav successfully completed 18 out of 20 trials. The system operated at over 5 Hz, enabling real-time navigation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the impact of data components and training stages:
- **Effect of Data Composition:**
 - Adding **D_video** (internet trajectories) to the Imitation Learning (IL) pipeline yielded gains in navigation performance (SR +11.7, RC +6.4, SPL +7.8) and social compliance (DCR +4.9, TCR +7.9), attributed to diverse human motion patterns.
 - Adding **D_sim** (simulated expert recovery trajectories) further improved robustness (SR +5.5, RC +1.2, SPL +7.7) and achieved moderate gains in social compliance.
 - Incorporating **D_cog** (Cognitive Activation Dataset) led to a significant increase in social compliance rates (DCR +8.4, TCR +10.2) and overall navigation metrics, confirming the criticality of social cognitive knowledge for internalizing social rules.
- **Effect of RL Training (SAFE-GRPO):**
 - Applying **SAFE-GRPO** (Stage 3) on the full dataset significantly improved social compliance (DCR from 78.2% to 82.5%, TCR from 78.4% to 82.9%) and enhanced navigation performance, achieving the highest SR (86.1%) and RC (91.2%). This validates the efficacy of flow-based RL with norm-aware rewards.
 - **The Crucial Role of D_cog in RL:** When SAFE-GRPO was applied without cognitive priors (D_cog), social metrics worsened compared to IL-only training. This indicates that the VLM's high-level social understanding from D_cog is essential for the RL agent to properly align its policy optimization with human social norms.
 - **Trade-offs in Path Efficiency:** A minor drop in SPL (from 79.4% to 77.4%) was observed after RL training. This reflects a trade-off where SAFE-GRPO prioritizes socially compliant behaviors (e.g., following walkways) over purely shortest-path efficiency, aligning with real-world navigation norms.
- **Ablation on SAFE-GRPO Reward Functions (Supplementary):** Deactivating the R_social component of the SAFE-GRPO reward function led to a notable decrease in DCR and TCR, confirming its critical role in discouraging socially risky shortcuts and promoting compliant behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SocialNav research represents an advance in the field of embodied navigation by explicitly addressing the critical dimension of social awareness, which has largely been overlooked by approaches prioritizing purely geometric efficiency. The introduction of SocialNav, a hierarchical "brain-action" foundation model, provides a scalable and generalizable framework for developing socially intelligent navigation systems.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Enhanced Robot Integration in Human Environments:** By enabling robots to understand and adhere to social norms, SocialNav facilitates safer, more acceptable, and less disruptive integration of autonomous agents into public spaces, such as streets, offices, and shopping malls. This is crucial for gaining public trust and wider adoption of robotic systems in human-centric applications.
- **Novel Architectural and Training Paradigms:** The hierarchical "brain-action" architecture effectively decouples high-level cognitive reasoning from low-level action generation, while maintaining a strong semantic link. This design principle may inform future embodied AI architectures for complex tasks. Furthermore, the multi-stage training pipeline, culminating in the Socially-Aware Flow Exploration GRPO (SAFE-GRPO) framework, introduces a novel method for instilling social compliance through flow-based reinforcement learning with norm-aware rewards, addressing the limitations of pure imitation learning in complex social contexts.
- **Creation of Foundational Resources:** The development of the large-scale, multimodal SocNav Dataset and the high-fidelity SocNav Benchmark provides valuable resources for the research community. The dataset, with its combination of diverse expert trajectories and cognitive activation data (including Chain-of-Thought explanations and social traversability annotations), addresses data scarcity and can accelerate future research in socially-aware navigation and embodied AI more broadly. The benchmark offers a robust platform for evaluating models with a blend of realistic physics and photorealistic rendering in varied social scenes.
- **Significant Performance Improvements:** The reported substantial gains in both navigation performance and social compliance over state-of-the-art methods, validated across open-loop, closed-loop, and real-world deployments, demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the SocialNav framework. These quantitative improvements, coupled with qualitative evidence of human-like navigation behaviors, indicate a meaningful progression towards truly socially intelligent agents.
- **Increased Interpretability and Trust:** The Brain Module's capability to generate Chain-of-Thought explanations and predict socially traversable regions offers enhanced interpretability of robot decisions. This transparency can be vital for debugging, understanding robot behavior in critical situations, and fostering greater trust between humans and autonomous systems.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The work identifies clear directions for future exploration, such as extending the reinforcement learning paradigm to capture a broader range of context-dependent human conventions and integrating Vision-Language Models for richer, more adaptive reward signals. This suggests a roadmap for further advancements in human-aligned AI.

Overall, SocialNav represents a step towards creating embodied agents that can navigate complex, dynamic social environments not just efficiently, but with genuine social awareness, paving the way for more harmonious human-robot co-existence.
