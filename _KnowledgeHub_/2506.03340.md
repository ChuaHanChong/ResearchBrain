# Seeing the Arrow of Time in Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Texas at Austin introduced ArrowRL, a reinforcement learning strategy to imbue Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) with "Arrow of Time" (AoT) awareness, allowing them to discern temporal directionality in videos. The method, alongside a new benchmark called AoTBench, significantly improves LMMs' ability to distinguish between forward and reversed video sequences and reason about event causality, achieving up to a +21.0% gain on challenging temporal tasks.

## Problem

- Current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) exhibit a profound insensitivity to the "Arrow of Time" (AoT), failing to distinguish between forward and backward video playback and often giving identical descriptions for temporally reversed events.
- Existing video question-answering (VQA) benchmarks often do not adequately probe genuine temporal understanding, allowing models to achieve high performance even with shuffled or reversed video frames, masking their lack of AoT awareness.
- LMMs' internal "world models" lack grounding in the fundamental reality of temporal progression and causality, limiting their ability to achieve truly deep, human-like video comprehension.

## Method

- Developed **ArrowRL**, a novel reinforcement learning-based post-training strategy, utilizing ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) to instill AoT awareness in LMMs.
- Introduced a unique reward structure for ArrowRL, which includes a "Reverse Reward" that maximizes dissimilarity between responses to forward and reversed videos, dynamically weighted to focus on AoT-sensitive samples.
- Created AoTBench, a new multi-faceted benchmark comprising sequence direction classification, directional caption matching, and a curated set of AoT-sensitive VQA questions, for rigorous evaluation.

## Results

- ArrowRL consistently and substantially improved LMM performance on AoTBench tasks, with gains of up to +**21.0%** on Sequence Direction Classification and +**9.2%** on AoT-sensitive VQA for various base models.
- ArrowRL-enhanced models generalized strongly, showing significant performance gains (e.g., +10.8 points on Vinoground, a +**65.9%** relative gain) on existing VQA benchmarks identified as more temporally sensitive.
- The training strategy preserved or slightly improved performance on general video benchmarks less sensitive to temporal order, confirming that AoT specialization does not compromise broader video understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art LMMs demonstrate a "universal failure mode" in fundamental AoT perception tasks, performing at near-chance levels when asked to classify video direction or generate temporally distinct descriptions.
> - A "Temporal Divergence Score (TDS)" can effectively quantify an LMM's sensitivity to temporal directionality within existing benchmarks, identifying questions that genuinely require AoT understanding.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically with a reverse reward mechanism, is an effective strategy to train LMMs for AoT awareness, leading to improved performance without degrading general video understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly computer vision and natural language processing, has seen rapid advancements with the advent of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). These models integrate visual perception with the powerful generative and interactive capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), enabling them to process and reason about multimodal content, especially video. However, this paper identifies a critical, yet largely overlooked, deficiency in current LMMs: their profound insensitivity to the **Arrow of Time (AoT)**.

Historically, temporal understanding in video has been explored through self-supervised learning pretext tasks, such as classifying forward/reverse video sequences or predicting video order. These early efforts treated temporal understanding primarily as a vision-only problem, focusing on physical cues and visual dynamics. With the rise of LMMs, the landscape shifted towards language-aware video understanding, where semantic meaning is deeply intertwined with temporal progression. While many LMMs aim for general temporal understanding, Xue et al. argue that AoT sensitivity—the ability to perceive and utilize temporal directionality—is a fundamental and overlooked prerequisite for achieving truly deep video understanding.

The paper posits that despite significant progress in other areas, state-of-the-art LMMs still struggle with basic AoT-related tasks. For instance, they fail to distinguish between forward and backward video playback and often produce identical descriptions for events whose semantic meaning is reversed by temporal direction (e.g., "igniting" vs. "extinguishing"). This highlights a critical gap where LMMs' internal "world models" lack grounding in the fundamental reality of temporal progression and causality, unlike human cognition.

Furthermore, the authors critically analyze existing video question-answering (VQA) benchmarks. Previous research has identified a "single-frame bias" where models can often answer questions correctly using only static image content. This work extends that critique, revealing that even multi-frame questions in many benchmarks often lack sensitivity to *temporal order*. Models can achieve high performance even when video frames are shuffled or reversed, indicating that these benchmarks do not adequately probe genuine temporal understanding. This paper systematically evaluates this problem, addressing a gap in prior evaluation studies by providing a rigorous analysis of AoT sensitivity at both the benchmark and model levels. By proposing a targeted training strategy (ArrowRL) and a dedicated evaluation benchmark (AoTBench), this research aims to push LMMs beyond superficial pattern recognition towards a more human-like, temporally aware understanding of video content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **instill Arrow of Time (AoT) awareness into Large Multimodal Models (LMMs)**, thereby enabling them to achieve a deeper and more genuinely temporal understanding of video content.

The motivation for this critical endeavor stems from several key observations and deficiencies:

1. **Fundamental Human Capability:** Humans possess an innate grasp of the AoT, effortlessly perceiving the sequential flow of events and decoding temporal directionality. This sensitivity is crucial not just for identifying physical irreversibility but for interpreting the causal structure and semantic meaning embedded in any progression of events. Equipping intelligent systems with this capability is considered essential for achieving true video comprehension.
2. **LMMs' Temporal Insensitivity:** Despite rapid advancements in other areas of multimodal understanding, current LMMs exhibit a profound temporal insensitivity. They struggle with basic AoT tasks, such as differentiating forward from backward video playback, and often fail to recognize crucial semantic distinctions arising from temporal direction (e.g., describing both an object being picked up and an object being placed down with the same generic "arranging" description).
3. **Deficiencies in Existing Benchmarks:** The observed failures are not solely due to model limitations but also to inadequacies in existing evaluation benchmarks. Many multi-frame video question answering (VQA) questions, while requiring multiple frames, do not depend on the *temporal order* of those frames. This allows models to succeed even with shuffled or reversed video, masking their fundamental lack of AoT understanding. The paper motivates a systematic study to quantify this "AoT insensitivity" across popular benchmarks.
4. **Grounding Internal World Models:** The authors argue that instilling AoT sensitivity is crucial for grounding LMMs' internal world models in the fundamental reality of temporal progression and causality. Without this, LMMs risk remaining at a superficial level of pattern recognition, incapable of the human-level reasoning required for complex dynamic environments.
5. **Advancing Beyond Single-Frame Bias:** While the "single-frame bias" in video benchmarks has received attention, this work identifies and addresses a distinct, yet equally critical, limitation concerning temporal directionality. The aim is to move beyond merely requiring multiple frames to demanding an understanding of *how* events unfold over time.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out with three specific objectives: first, to provide a critical analysis of existing benchmarks and models concerning AoT perception; second, to introduce ArrowRL, a novel reinforcement learning strategy to instill AoT awareness; and third, to develop AoTBench, a new multi-faceted benchmark for rigorous evaluation of this crucial capability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper addresses both the evaluation gap and the training deficiency related to AoT understanding in LMMs. It comprises a systematic analysis of existing benchmarks, a novel reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm, and a new dedicated benchmark.

#### 3.1. Temporal Sensitivity Analysis of Existing Benchmarks

To quantify the AoT sensitivity of existing benchmarks, the authors first select a pool of "evaluator" LMMs (LLaVA-OV-7B, LLaVA-Video-7B, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B) based on their overall accuracy and their difference in performance between forward and reversed video on a challenging temporal benchmark (TVBench).

A key innovation is the **Temporal Divergence Score (TDS)**. For a given MCQ sample, the TDS measures the KL divergence between the LMM's first-token probability distribution over answer choices when presented with the forward video (p_i) versus the reversed video (˜p_i). A high TDS indicates that the model's prediction is highly sensitive to the video's temporal direction, suggesting that the sample genuinely requires AoT understanding. This metric is more fine-grained than simply comparing accuracy, as it captures shifts in confidence or ranking of alternatives. The authors apply TDS across eight popular VQA benchmarks, identifying that benchmarks like TVBench, Vinoground, and TempCompass are more temporally sensitive, while others like VITATECS show lower sensitivity.

#### 3.2. ArrowRL: Learning the Arrow of Time

ArrowRL is a novel reinforcement learning-based post-training strategy designed to equip LMMs with AoT understanding. It leverages **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an algorithm that refines model outputs by comparing and ranking multiple generated responses for a single prompt.

The core of ArrowRL lies in its **innovative reward structure**, which integrates two complementary objectives for each candidate response (o_i) generated by the policy LMM (π_θ) for a given question (q = (v, l) with target response o_⋆):

1. **Target Fidelity Reward (r^fid_i):** This reward encourages alignment between the candidate response o_i and the ground truth target response o_⋆. For MCQ tasks, it's a binary accuracy score (1.0 if correct, 0.0 otherwise). For open-ended QA and captioning, an auxiliary LLM (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct) is used as a judge to compute a semantic similarity score between 0 and 1.
2. **Reverse Reward (r^rev_i):** This is the central component for instilling AoT sensitivity. It maximizes the dissimilarity between a forward-conditioned candidate response o_i and a response ˜o generated by the same LMM when presented with the *reversed* video (˜v). The reward is defined as `1 - Similarity(o_i, ˜o)`. This design penalizes generic or static responses that fail to account for temporal directionality, thereby compelling the model to attend to the dynamic and directional details of the forward video. It's crucial that ˜v is used *only* for reward computation, not for improving responses to reversed videos.

**Dynamic Weighting:** To handle cases where semantic distinctiveness between forward and reverse video is not relevant (e.g., static scenes), a dynamic weighting scheme is introduced. The overall reward is `r_i = r^fid_i + α_i * r^rev_i`. If the similarity between the reverse response ˜o and the target o_⋆ exceeds a threshold γ (meaning the sample is not highly AoT-sensitive), the reverse reward is disabled by setting α_i = 0. Otherwise, α_i takes a default hyperparameter value α.

The LMM is then optimized using the GRPO objective, maximizing the normalized rewards (advantages) while regularizing against a reference policy (π_ref). ArrowRL is applied directly to pre-trained LMMs (LLaVA-OV-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) without an intermediate supervised fine-tuning step. Training data is carefully curated to emphasize AoT-aware scenarios, including MCQ sequence direction classification (from UCF101), open-ended QA (filtered LLaVA-Video-178K for high temporality), and video captioning (RTime dataset providing distinct captions for forward/reverse videos).

#### 3.3. AoTBench: Addressing Temporal Evaluation Gaps

To rigorously evaluate LMMs' AoT perception, the authors develop **AoTBench**, a new multi-faceted benchmark with three distinct tasks:

1. **Sequence Direction Classification:** This task requires LMMs to classify whether a video segment is played forward or in reverse, based on visual cues. Videos are sourced from ReverseFilm and UCF101, selected for their strong temporal properties.
2. **Directional Caption Matching:** This task assesses the model's ability to link video dynamics with corresponding textual descriptions that are semantically directional. Utilizing the RTime benchmark, which provides videos with distinct human captions for forward and reverse playback, two MCQ sub-tasks are formulated:
 - **Video-to-Text (V2T):** Given a video, choose the correct caption (forward vs. reverse).
 - **Text-to-Video (T2V):** Given a caption, choose the correct video sequence (forward vs. reverse).
3. **AoT-sensitive VQA:** This component is curated from existing benchmarks. Using the per-sample TDS calculated earlier, the top 200 high-TDS questions from each of eight source benchmarks are selected, resulting in a subset of 1,800 VQA samples that demand robust AoT understanding.

AoTBench provides a comprehensive tool to diagnose and benchmark LMMs' capabilities in perceiving and utilizing the Arrow of Time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results strongly validate the effectiveness of ArrowRL in enhancing temporal perception in LMMs and highlight the critical need for dedicated AoT understanding.

**Performance on AoTBench:**
- **Critical Deficiencies:** The results on AoTBench reveal severe limitations in existing LMMs' AoT perception. Almost all open-source models exhibited a "universal failure mode" on visually-focused tasks like **Sequence Direction Classification**, achieving near-chance level accuracy (e.g., 50.0% on ReverseFilm and 51.6% on UCF101), indicating they produce identical responses regardless of video direction. Proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro), while slightly better, still performed far below their general VQA capabilities.
- **ArrowRL's Impact:** ArrowRL consistently and substantially improved performance across all three base LMMs and all AoTBench tasks. For example, Qwen2-VL-7B saw gains of +19.1% on ReverseFilm and +21.0% on UCF101. Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieved a +9.2% gain on AoT-sensitive VQA. This demonstrates that ArrowRL successfully instills AoT awareness, enabling models to overcome previously observed temporal insensitivity.

**Generalization to Existing Temporal Benchmarks:**
- ArrowRL not only excels on the newly designed AoTBench but also generalizes strongly to several existing VQA benchmarks identified as more temporally sensitive (TempCompass, TVBench, Vinoground). For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieved a +10.8 point gain on Vinoground (a +65.9% relative gain). These results underscore that improving fundamental AoT sensitivity translates into broader performance enhancements for general temporal perception tasks.

**Performance on General Video Benchmarks:**
- To ensure that specializing in AoT understanding does not degrade general video comprehension, ArrowRL-enhanced LLaVA-OV-7B was evaluated on five benchmarks less sensitive to temporal order. The results showed that ArrowRL either preserved or slightly improved performance on these general benchmarks, indicating that the enhancement of temporal perception comes without sacrificing broader video understanding capabilities.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Reverse Reward's Necessity:** Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the reverse reward. Removing it (setting α = 0) resulted in performance degradation below that of the base LMM, highlighting its importance for fostering AoT sensitivity.
- **Dynamic Weighting's Benefit:** The dynamic weighting mechanism (using γ) was also shown to be beneficial. Disabling it (setting γ = 1) yielded less improvement than the proposed dynamic approach, which effectively modulates the reverse reward's contribution based on a sample's inherent AoT sensitivity.
- **Training Data and RL:** ArrowRL significantly outperformed a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baseline trained on the same data, demonstrating the efficacy of the RL approach. Furthermore, using the curated high-temporality post-training data provided a notable performance gain.
- **Inference Frame Rate Robustness:** Despite training with a fixed number of frames (16), ArrowRL's performance gains remained consistent across varying inference frame rates (1-4 FPS) on Vinoground, showcasing its robust generalization.

**Qualitative Results:**
- Qualitative examples visually demonstrated ArrowRL's impact. Enhanced models were able to correctly answer temporally challenging VQA questions and generate distinct, temporally accurate captions for forward and reversed sequences, unlike the base models which often failed to capture temporal progression or were influenced by language biases.

In summary, the main findings confirm that current LMMs largely lack fundamental AoT awareness, and ArrowRL provides an effective and robust solution to instill this crucial capability, leading to significant performance gains on both targeted and general temporal video understanding tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant importance for the advancement of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and video understanding, offering both fundamental insights and practical contributions.

**1. Addressing a Fundamental Gap:** The most significant contribution of this work is its explicit identification and systematic addressing of the "Arrow of Time" (AoT) problem in LMMs. By demonstrating that current state-of-the-art LMMs struggle with even basic temporal directionality, the authors highlight a fundamental weakness that has largely been overlooked amidst rapid advancements in other aspects of multimodal perception. This work firmly establishes AoT awareness as a crucial, previously unexplored, prerequisite for genuine video comprehension.

**2. Novel Training Methodology (ArrowRL):** The introduction of ArrowRL provides a practical and effective solution for instilling AoT sensitivity. Its unique reverse reward mechanism, which encourages divergence between responses to forward and reversed videos, is an ingenious way to leverage inherent temporal signals for targeted learning. The success of ArrowRL, applied as a post-training strategy and without requiring an intermediate supervised fine-tuning step, demonstrates its efficiency and broad applicability to existing pre-trained LMMs. This approach offers a complementary path to architectural or data innovations, focusing on refining core perceptual capabilities through reinforcement learning.

**3. Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark (AoTBench):** The development of AoTBench is a critical contribution to the research community. By meticulously curating tasks like sequence direction classification, directional caption matching, and AoT-sensitive VQA (derived from high-TDS samples from existing benchmarks), AoTBench provides the first dedicated and multi-faceted tool for rigorously assessing LMMs' AoT perception. This benchmark will serve as an invaluable resource for future research, enabling clearer diagnosis of temporal understanding capabilities and guiding the development of more robust models. The Temporal Divergence Score (TDS) is also a valuable fine-grained metric for evaluating temporal sensitivity.

**4. Broader Performance Gains:** A key finding is that improving AoT awareness does not lead to specialization at the cost of general understanding. Instead, ArrowRL not only excels on AoT-specific tasks but also significantly boosts performance on standard video VQA benchmarks that demand temporal reasoning, while preserving performance on general video tasks. This suggests that AoT sensitivity is not a niche capability but a foundational element critical for overall temporal perception, advancing LMMs beyond superficial pattern matching.

**5. Towards Human-Level Video Understanding:** By grounding LMMs' internal "world models" in the fundamental reality of temporal progression and causality, this research moves these models closer to human-level reasoning abilities in dynamic environments. This enhanced understanding of "how events unfold over time" is crucial for real-world applications requiring nuanced interpretation of processes, actions, and narratives.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Reliable AI Systems:** LMMs with stronger AoT awareness will be more reliable in applications where temporal accuracy is paramount, such as video surveillance, content moderation, video summarization, and human-robot interaction.
- **Enhanced Generative AI:** A deeper understanding of temporal causality could lead to more coherent and physically plausible video generation, editing, and manipulation tools.
- **Improved Assistive Technologies:** For applications like assistive technology for visually impaired individuals or elderly care, LMMs could provide more accurate and contextually rich descriptions of ongoing events.
- **Scientific and Educational Tools:** Better temporal reasoning could benefit scientific simulations, historical video analysis, and interactive educational content.

While acknowledging limitations such as focusing on short videos and relying on LLM judges for reward calculation, this work lays a strong foundation. Future research could extend these methods to long videos, integrate with explicit temporal reasoning chains (e.g., Chain-of-Thought), and explore how time is encoded in neural representations beyond language. Ultimately, "Seeing the Arrow of Time" is a crucial step towards LMMs that genuinely comprehend the dynamic visual world.
