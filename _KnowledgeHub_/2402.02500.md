# Point Cloud Matters: Rethinking the Impact of Different Observation Spaces on Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Lab and collaborating universities re-evaluated the role of observation spaces in robot learning, proposing that 3D point clouds offer superior performance and generalization compared to 2D RGB or RGB-D images for contact-rich manipulation tasks. They introduced OBSBench, a new benchmark that systematically demonstrates point cloud methods consistently achieve higher success rates and better zero-shot generalization to environmental variations across 19 diverse robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning research often prioritizes policy design, neglecting the critical impact of the observation space on performance and generalization.
- Traditional 2D RGB images, while simple, inherently struggle to capture the 3D nature of robot actions and environments, leading to limited generalization capabilities.
- A comprehensive and fair comparison of different observation modalities (RGB, RGB-D, point clouds) within a unified, standardized framework was lacking, and existing 3D techniques were often complex or relied on dense voxel representations, hindering systematic adoption.

## Method

- Developed OBSBench, a novel, comprehensive benchmark that integrates two modern robot simulators (ManiSkill2, RLBench) and 125 diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks, with standardized pipelines for various encoders (ResNet, ViT, SpUNet, PointNet), PVRs (R3M, VC-1, MultiMAE, PonderV2), and policy baselines (ACT, Diffusion Policy).
- Systematically compared RGB, RGB-D, and point cloud observation spaces across diverse scenarios including training from scratch, leveraging PVRs, evaluating zero-shot generalization, and assessing sample efficiency.
- Conducted ablation studies to uncover optimal design choices for point cloud utilization, such as sampling strategies and the importance of color and coordinate information.

## Results

- Point cloud-based methods consistently achieved the highest mean success rates and best mean ranks across 19 diverse tasks, outperforming other modalities by up to 76.92% in mean success rate with Diffusion Policy.
- Point clouds showed superior zero-shot generalization to camera view changes (e.g., 5° and 10° shifts) and various visual changes (lighting, noise, background color), indicating better robustness in dynamic environments.
- Ablation studies revealed that post-sampling point clouds and incorporating explicit 3D coordinate information (alongside color) are crucial design choices, and real-world experiments on a low-cost robot arm corroborated this superiority for tasks like Reach Cube, Pick Cube, and Fold Cloth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The observation space is a critical bottleneck in robot learning: explicit 3D representations, particularly point clouds, are more effective than 2D/2.5D modalities, and depth-only data or simple RGB-D image stacking is generally insufficient for robust performance.
> - Pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) generally enhance robot learning, with point cloud PVRs like PonderV2 being remarkably efficient, achieving strong gains with orders of magnitude less pre-training data.
> - Point cloud methods consistently demonstrate superior zero-shot generalization capabilities to changes in camera viewpoint and visual environment compared to image-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is rapidly advancing, with a predominant focus on developing sophisticated policy designs to enable robots to perform complex tasks. However, this paper challenges a long-standing implicit assumption in the field: that the choice of observation space is a secondary concern to policy architecture. Traditionally, 2D RGB images have been the go-to observation modality due to their simplicity and the extensive progress in 2D computer vision foundation models. However, the inherent 3D nature of robot actions and environments poses a significant challenge for 2D visual inputs, often leading to limited generalization capabilities, especially under varying lighting conditions, camera viewpoints, and object appearances.

While the importance of 3D information has been gaining recognition, with methods like CLIPort and Perceiver-Actor integrating some form of 3D data, a comprehensive and fair comparison of different observation modalities (RGB, RGB-D, and point clouds) within a unified framework has been lacking. Existing 3D techniques are often complex or rely on dense voxel representations, making systematic comparison difficult.

This research addresses this critical gap by providing **OBSBench**, the first extensive benchmark designed specifically for evaluating and comparing the effectiveness of various observation spaces in robot learning. By standardizing pipelines across different encoders, policies, and simulators, the authors provide a much-needed controlled environment to rigorously test the impact of visual modalities. The work repositioning the observation space as a potential "bottleneck" alongside policy design prompts a fundamental re-evaluation of current robot learning paradigms, suggesting that a robot's perception of its 3D environment is as crucial as its action-planning capabilities. It aims to guide the community towards more robust and generalizable robotic models by illuminating the strengths and weaknesses of different observation spaces, particularly highlighting the underappreciated potential of 3D point clouds.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the observation that while robot learning researchers often prioritize policy design, the underlying observation space can critically influence a robot's performance and generalization ability. The distinct characteristics of different visual modalities (RGB, RGB-D, point cloud) can become a bottleneck, limiting a robot's capacity to generalize in dynamic and intricate settings due to partial observability.

The key objectives of this study are:

1. **Systematically Explore Observation Space Influence:** To thoroughly investigate how various observation spaces (RGB, RGB-D, and point cloud) impact robot learning performance, especially in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
2. **Develop a Standardized Benchmark (OBSBench):** To introduce OBSBench, a novel and comprehensive benchmark comprising two modern robot simulators (ManiSkill2 and RLBench) and 125 diverse tasks. This benchmark includes standardized pipelines for various encoders, pre-trained visual representations (PVRs), and policy baselines to enable fair and consistent comparisons across modalities.
3. **Evaluate Performance with and Without Pre-training:** To assess the performance of each observation space when models are trained from scratch, and also when utilizing state-of-the-art pre-trained visual representations (PVRs).
4. **Analyze Zero-Shot Generalization Capabilities:** To rigorously evaluate the zero-shot generalization of different observation spaces to critical environmental variations, including camera viewpoints, lighting conditions, and visual appearance changes.
5. **Investigate Sample Efficiency:** To understand how efficiently different observation spaces can learn tasks, particularly in scenarios with limited training data.
6. **Uncover Optimal Point Cloud Design Choices:** To explore critical design decisions for effectively utilizing point cloud observations, such as sampling strategies and the integration of color and coordinate information.
7. **Provide Insights for Future Robotic Model Design:** To offer valuable insights and guidance for the design of more generalizable and robust robotic models by quantifying the impact of observation space.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and empirical approach, centered around the development and utilization of their novel benchmark, OBSBench.

**A. OBSBench Development:**
- **Simulators:** OBSBench integrates two widely-used and representative robot simulators: ManiSkill2 (using SAPIEN physics engine) and RLBench (using CoppeliaSim physics engine).
- **Tasks:** It comprises 125 contact-rich manipulation tasks. For the extensive experiments, a diverse and representative subset of 19 tasks from both simulators was selected, focusing on distinct skills and non-trivial performance outcomes.
- **Demonstrations:** Ground-truth demonstrations are provided for all tasks and modalities, supporting behavior cloning.
- **Standardized Pipelines:** The benchmark provides standardized pipelines built using Hydra and PyTorch Lightning for consistent experimentation across different components.

**B. Observation Spaces and Encoders:**
The study focuses on three main observation spaces, each paired with relevant encoders:
- **RGB Images:** ResNet50 and ViT-B.
- **RGB-D Images:** ResNet50 (channel-wise stacked), ViT-B (channel-wise stacked), and MultiViT-B (designed for multi-modal input with distinct projection layers for RGB and depth).
- **Point Clouds:** SpUNet34 (sparse convolutional network) and PointNet (popular point-based network).
- **Depth Only:** ResNet and ViT are also used with depth-only input to evaluate the raw depth modality impact.

**C. Feature Extraction:**
- **2D/2.5D Encoders:** Final layer features for ResNet, [CLS] token for ViT and MultiViT. 2D cosine position embeddings are incorporated for spatial context.
- **3D Encoders:** Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is used to select 2048 seed points from the point cloud. K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) is then used to form 16-neighbor clusters around each seed. These clusters undergo linear projection and pooling, resulting in features that are then augmented with positional embeddings based on the 3D coordinates of the seed points.

**D. Policy Networks:**
Two state-of-the-art behavior cloning policy networks are implemented:
- **Action Chunking Transformer (ACT):** Utilizes a conditional VAE for generative modeling over action sequences, employing action chunking and temporal ensembling.
- **Diffusion Policy (DP):** Models observation and action spaces via a diffusion process, generally using a UNet architecture for stability.

**E. Pre-trained Visual Representations (PVRs):**
The study evaluates the impact of PVRs specific to each modality:
- **RGB:** R3M (for ResNet50, trained on Ego4D human interaction videos), VC-1 (for ViT-B, trained with masked auto-encoding on diverse video/image datasets).
- **RGB-D:** MultiMAE (for MultiViT-B, masked autoencoding across RGB, depth, semantics on ImageNet-1K).
- **Point Cloud:** PonderV2 (for SpUNet34, trained with differentiable neural rendering, focusing on geometry and appearance cues).

**F. Experimental Setups and Metrics:**
- **Tasks:** 19 diverse tasks from ManiSkill2 and RLBench.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success Rate (S.R.), Mean S.R. (average across tasks), and Mean Rank (average ranking of methods per task).
- **Generalization Studies:**
 - **Camera View Changes:** Zero-shot evaluation by shifting the camera vertically and horizontally by 5° and 10°.
 - **Visual Changes:** Varying lighting intensity, introducing visual noise (ray tracing samples), and altering background colors on the StackCube task.
- **Sample Efficiency:** Experiments conducted on RLBench tasks with reduced training data (10 and 25 out of 100 trajectories).
- **Point Cloud Design Decisions:** Ablation studies on sampling strategies (pre-sampling vs. post-sampling), importance of color vs. coordinate information, and a novel "pointmap" format (RGB + coordinate images).
- **Real-World Validation:** Limited real-world experiments using a low-cost robot arm with RGB-D cameras on three tasks (Reach Cube, Pick Cube, Fold Cloth) to corroborate simulation findings.

The methodology emphasizes fair comparison by controlling variables, using standardized pipelines, and evaluating across a broad range of scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical studies conducted on OBSBench revealed several key insights:

1. **Point Clouds Outperform Other Modalities (Q1):** Point cloud-based methods consistently achieved the highest mean success rates and best mean ranks across all 19 diverse tasks. This superiority held true whether models were trained from scratch or utilized pre-trained visual representations (PVRs), and was observed across both ACT and Diffusion Policy implementations. For example, with Diffusion Policy, SpUNet and PointNet outperformed the best other modality by 53.85% and 76.92% in mean success rate, respectively.
2. **Explicit 3D Representation is Crucial; Depth-Only is Insufficient (Q1):** Despite providing geometric information, depth-only data or RGB-D images (stacked channel-wise or processed separately by MultiViT) generally showed constrained or even degraded performance compared to RGB or explicit point cloud representations. This is attributed to the high variability and unstable distribution of depth data, especially the significant difference between background and foreground depth values. This underscores the necessity of explicit 3D structures.
3. **Point Cloud PVRs are Highly Efficient (Q2):** While PVRs generally improved performance, **PonderV2**, a point cloud PVR, achieved significant performance gains. Remarkably, PonderV2 used significantly less pre-training data (orders of magnitude smaller, from K to M) compared to other PVRs like R3M, VC-1, and MultiMAE. This efficiency is linked to its multi-view rendering pretext task, which enriches it with crucial geometric knowledge.
4. **Superior Zero-Shot Generalization for Point Clouds (Q3):**
 - **Camera View Changes:** All methods suffered from camera view changes, but point cloud methods (both scratch-trained and PVR-leveraged) demonstrated notable resilience. This suggests that image-based models are overly dependent on specific training views and struggle with 3D inference from 2D.
 - **Visual Changes:** Point cloud methods generally exhibited better generalization to visual changes (lighting, noise, background color). Specifically, SpUNet showed robustness to foreground visual changes, while PointNet excelled in generalizing to camera view changes. The inclusion of both coordinate and color information in point clouds contributes to this robustness.
5. **Point Clouds Not Significantly More Sample Efficient (Q4):** The study found that point cloud observation spaces did not offer a significant advantage in sample efficiency when trained with limited data. However, PVRs consistently improved performance in these few-shot learning scenarios, with PonderV2 again showing notable enhancement despite its smaller pre-training dataset size.
6. **Optimal Point Cloud Design Choices (Q5):**
 - **Post-sampling:** Performing FPS sampling on the feature map *after* the encoder ("post-sampling") significantly enhanced performance compared to pre-sampling, as it maintains better local information.
 - **Coordinate Information is Key:** Removing coordinate features from point clouds resulted in a larger performance drop than removing color information, proving the critical importance of explicit 3D structures. Using both color and coordinate information yielded the best results.
 - **Pointmap Potential, but Limitations:** A novel "pointmap" format (stacking RGB with explicit coordinate information) consistently outperformed RGB-only and RGB-D methods. However, it still lagged behind pure point clouds, especially with diffusion policies, likely due to its 2D-confined neighborhood locality compared to point clouds' 3D locality and insufficient research into specialized pointmap encoders.

**Additional Ablations and Real-World Experiments:**
- **Encoder Architecture:** Running RGB and RGB-D data through point cloud networks (as "plain point clouds") further supported the claim that point clouds are superior.
- **Coordinate Frame:** Using the end-effector (EE) frame for point clouds, as opposed to the world frame, sometimes enhanced performance depending on the task and network.
- **Diffusion Policy Variants:** Point cloud methods consistently performed best across both UNet and Transformer variants of the diffusion policy.
- **Real-World Validation:** Limited real-world experiments on Reach Cube, Pick Cube, and Fold Cloth tasks aligned with the simulation findings, showing point clouds to be superior to RGB and RGB-D.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant contribution to the field of robot learning by fundamentally challenging the prevailing focus on policy design over observation space choice. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Shifting Research Paradigm:** By rigorously demonstrating that observation space can be a bottleneck in robot learning, the paper advocates for a paradigm shift, encouraging researchers to give equal, if not greater, attention to how robots perceive their environments in 3D. This could lead to a broader exploration of diverse sensing modalities and their optimal integration.
2. **Empowering 3D Perception for Robotics:** The consistent and strong performance of point cloud methods, particularly their superior generalization capabilities, firmly establishes 3D point clouds as a highly valuable and potentially superior observation modality for intricate robotic tasks. This insight will encourage increased adoption and development of point cloud-based perception systems in robot learning.
3. **Introducing a Foundational Benchmark (OBSBench):** The creation of OBSBench is a crucial contribution. By providing a standardized, comprehensive, and open-source framework (including tasks, simulators, encoders, PVRs, and policies), it enables fair and reproducible comparisons. This will accelerate research by allowing the community to benchmark new methods against a common standard, fostering innovation in both observation space design and policy learning.
4. **Guiding Design Choices for Point Cloud Models:** The detailed ablation studies on point cloud design choices (post-sampling, coordinate vs. color information, pointmap format) provide practical, actionable insights for developers. The finding that post-sampling and explicit 3D coordinate information are critical offers clear directions for building more effective point cloud encoders and representation learning strategies.
5. **Leveraging Efficient Pre-training:** The discovery of PonderV2's effectiveness despite requiring significantly less pre-training data highlights the potential for highly efficient 3D pre-trained models. This is particularly impactful for robotics, where large-scale 3D datasets are often scarcer than their 2D counterparts, suggesting that intelligent pre-training strategies can overcome data limitations.
6. **Enhancing Robot Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstration that point clouds yield better zero-shot generalization to varying camera views and visual conditions is profoundly significant. Generalization and robustness are paramount for deploying robots in real-world, unstructured environments. This work lays the groundwork for developing robotic systems that can adapt more seamlessly to unforeseen changes.
7. **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly points towards future research avenues, such as leveraging larger-scale 3D datasets (e.g., RH20T, DL3DV-10K) for improved robustness, exploring dynamic sampling techniques, and integrating multi-modal sensing (e.g., tactile input). This provides a roadmap for advancing the field.

In the long term, by contributing to more robust and generalizable robotic systems, this research holds the potential to accelerate the deployment of autonomous robots in complex real-world applications, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and assistive robotics. However, the authors also acknowledge the importance of considering ethical implications, emphasizing the need to study how to prevent robots from causing harm in daily life as their capabilities advance.
