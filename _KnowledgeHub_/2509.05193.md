# Shift Before You Learn: Enabling Low-Rank Representations in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces temporally shifted successor measures in reinforcement learning, demonstrating that an inherent low-rank structure, crucial for efficient learning, emerges more naturally in these shifted versions than in standard ones. The work provides theoretical finite-sample performance guarantees for estimating these representations and empirically shows improved goal-reaching performance in various maze environments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms frequently assume or impose low-rank structures on components like successor measures to manage complexity, but the fundamental reasons for this structure's emergence in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) are not well understood.
- Standard successor measures often lack an intrinsic low-rank structure, making them challenging to approximate efficiently with low-rank methods.
- There is a need for theoretical justification and finite-sample guarantees for low-rank representations in RL that connect to the underlying dynamics of MDPs.

## Method

- Introduces the concept of a 'k-shifted ==successor measure==' (Mπ,kM_{\pi,k}Mπ,k​), which effectively reweights transitions to emphasize long-term behavior and induce a low-rank structure.
- Develops a novel metric called 'spectral recoverability' to quantify the approximation and estimation errors of low-rank matrix representations, considering both singular values and singular vector coherence.
- Provides theoretical finite-sample performance guarantees for the entry-wise estimation of low-rank approximations of the shifted successor measures, scaling as O(n/ϵ2)O(n/\epsilon^2)O(n/ϵ2) under low-rank conditions.

## Results

- Empirical results demonstrate that increasing the temporal shift consistently sharpens the spectrum of successor measures, making them more amenable to low-rank approximation in various maze environments.
- Using shifted successor measures improves goal-reaching performance and sample efficiency in goal-conditioned RL tasks, with moderate shifts often yielding optimal performance for both oracle and learned representations.
- Theoretical analysis establishes that the proposed framework provides the first finite-sample performance guarantees for entry-wise estimation of low-rank approximations of shifted successor measures, showcasing improved statistical efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard successor measures (k=0k=0k=0) generally do not possess spectral recoverability, meaning they are not inherently low-rank in a way that facilitates accurate entry-wise approximation.
> - A low-rank structure naturally emerges in the shifted successor measure (Mπ,kM_{\pi,k}Mπ,k​) for sufficiently large temporal shifts (kkk), as the system dynamics effectively 'forget' initial short-term transitions.
> - The amount of shift (kkk) required for low-rank emergence is quantifiable and directly linked to the local mixing properties of the underlying Markov chain, formalized through novel Type II Poincaré inequalities.
