# Shift Before You Learn: Enabling Low-Rank Representations in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces temporally shifted successor measures in reinforcement learning, demonstrating that an inherent low-rank structure, crucial for efficient learning, emerges more naturally in these shifted versions than in standard ones. The work provides theoretical finite-sample performance guarantees for estimating these representations and empirically shows improved goal-reaching performance in various maze environments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms frequently assume or impose low-rank structures on components like successor measures to manage complexity, but the fundamental reasons for this structure's emergence in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) are not well understood.
- Standard successor measures often lack an intrinsic low-rank structure, making them challenging to approximate efficiently with low-rank methods.
- There is a need for theoretical justification and finite-sample guarantees for low-rank representations in RL that connect to the underlying dynamics of MDPs.

## Method

- Introduces the concept of a 'k-shifted ==successor measure==' (Mπ,kM_{π,k}Mπ,k), which effectively reweights transitions to emphasize long-term behavior and induce a low-rank structure.
- Develops a novel metric called 'spectral recoverability' to quantify the approximation and estimation errors of low-rank matrix representations, considering both singular values and singular vector coherence.
- Provides theoretical finite-sample performance guarantees for the entry-wise estimation of low-rank approximations of the shifted successor measures, scaling as O(n/ϵ2)O(n/ϵ2) under low-rank conditions.

## Results

- Empirical results demonstrate that increasing the temporal shift consistently sharpens the spectrum of successor measures, making them more amenable to low-rank approximation in various maze environments.
- Using shifted successor measures improves goal-reaching performance and sample efficiency in goal-conditioned RL tasks, with moderate shifts often yielding optimal performance for both oracle and learned representations.
- Theoretical analysis establishes that the proposed framework provides the first finite-sample performance guarantees for entry-wise estimation of low-rank approximations of shifted successor measures, showcasing improved statistical efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard successor measures (k=0k=0k=0) generally do not possess spectral recoverability, meaning they are not inherently low-rank in a way that facilitates accurate entry-wise approximation.
> - A low-rank structure naturally emerges in the shifted successor measure (Mπ,kM_{π,k}Mπ,k) for sufficiently large temporal shifts (kkk), as the system dynamics effectively 'forget' initial short-term transitions.
> - The amount of shift (kkk) required for low-rank emergence is quantifiable and directly linked to the local mixing properties of the underlying Markov chain, formalized through novel Type II Poincaré inequalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) often relies on structural assumptions, particularly low-rank representations, to manage the complexity of environment dynamics and achieve statistical efficiency. This approach has been applied to various RL components, including Q-functions, transition kernels, graph Laplacians, and successor representations. While empirically successful, the fundamental reasons for the emergence and learnability of low-rank structures in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) remain open questions.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by scrutinizing the implicit low-rank assumption. It deviates from prior work that directly assumes or imposes low-rank structure on key matrices (e.g., transition kernels or Q-functions) or relies on specific function approximation classes. Instead, it proposes that an inherent low-rank structure materializes dynamically, specifically in "shifted" successor measures. This perspective offers a novel theoretical justification for the use of low-rank models in RL by connecting it to the mixing properties of underlying Markov chains. The work contributes to the theoretical understanding of representation learning in RL, particularly for reward-free and goal-conditioned settings, by providing finite-sample performance guarantees for estimating these shifted representations. It also draws connections to matrix completion techniques and functional inequalities in Markov chain theory, bridging these disparate fields.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research is to address fundamental questions regarding the prevalence and utility of low-rank structures in MDPs:
- **Why does low-rank structure arise in MDPs?** The authors observe that while modern RL algorithms frequently assume an underlying low-rank structure for quantities like the successor measure, the successor measure itself may not be approximately low-rank. This discrepancy motivates a deeper investigation into conditions under which such structures truly emerge.
- **Under what conditions do low-rank representations yield accurate and learnable models?** Beyond mere existence, the practical value of low-rank representations depends on their ability to be accurately learned from limited data and to support effective policy learning.

To address these, the paper introduces and investigates the concept of a "shifted successor measure." The core hypothesis is that by introducing a temporal shift, which effectively reweights transitions to emphasize long-term behavior and filter out short-term noise, the successor measure can become more amenable to low-rank approximation. This idea is motivated by the intuition that long-term dynamics are more stable and structured than immediate transitions.

Specifically, the key objectives are:
1. **Introduce and formalize "spectral recoverability"**: To quantitatively assess the approximation and estimation errors associated with low-rank representations, the paper aims to define a new metric, spectral recoverability. This metric is intended to blend classical notions of coherence and nuclear norm, providing a suitable quantity for controlling errors in entry-wise norms.
2. **Demonstrate the lack of low-rank structure in standard successor measures**: Provide theoretical evidence that standard successor measures do not inherently possess spectral recoverability, thus justifying the need for the proposed shifting mechanism.
3. **Prove the emergence of low-rank structure in shifted successor measures**: Show that sufficiently large temporal shifts can induce spectral recoverability, making the shifted successor measures approximately low-rank.
4. **Provide finite-sample performance guarantees**: Develop theoretical bounds for the entry-wise estimation of a low-rank approximation of the shifted successor measure from sampled entries. These guarantees aim to quantify the statistical efficiency of the proposed approach.
5. **Connect shifting to local mixing properties**: Establish a theoretical link between the required shift amount and the local mixing characteristics of the underlying Markov chain. This objective aims to provide a principled way to select the appropriate shift parameter. This involves introducing a new class of functional inequalities called "Type II Poincaré inequalities."
6. **Empirically validate theoretical findings**: Conduct experiments, particularly in goal-conditioned RL, to demonstrate that shifting the successor measure indeed leads to improved performance and validates the theoretical predictions regarding low-rank structure and estimation accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper's methodology combines theoretical analysis with empirical validation.

**A. Theoretical Framework:**
1. **Shifted Successor Measures**: The core innovation is the definition of the *k-shifted successor measure*, $M_{\pi,k} := P_{\pi}^k (I - \gamma P_{\pi})^{-1}$. This matrix captures the discounted future state occupancy distribution starting $k$ steps into the future, given an initial state-action pair and a policy $\pi$.
2. **Spectral Recoverability**: The authors introduce *spectral irrecoverability* $\xi(M) := \max_{x \in [n]} \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i \psi_i(x)^2$ (and its inverse, spectral recoverability) as a key quantity. This metric is designed to capture both the low-rank structure (via singular values $\sigma_i$) and the coherence of singular vectors ($\psi_i(x)$), which are critical for entry-wise matrix approximation and estimation.
 - **Justification for Shifting**: Proposition 1 demonstrates that standard successor measures ($k=0$) lack spectral recoverability, providing a theoretical basis for the proposed shift.
3. **Finite-Sample Performance Guarantees (Theorem 1)**: The paper provides theoretical bounds for the entry-wise estimation error of a rank-$r$ approximation of the shifted successor measure, denoted $[ \hat{M}_{\pi,k} ]_r$. The estimation error is shown to depend on:
 - **$E_{estim}$**: A term related to the sample complexity, controlled by factors such as the ratio of the largest to the $r$-th singular value ($\sigma_1 / \sigma_r$), the gap between singular values ($\sigma_r - \sigma_{r+1}$), the maximum shift and discount factor ($\max(k, (1-\gamma)^{-1})^2$), the ratio of probability measures ($\nu$ used for SVD vs. $\nu_{\pi,inv}$ invariant measure), and the number of observed transitions ($Z_{s,a}$).
 - **$E_{approx}$**: A term related to the approximation error of the true matrix, controlled by $\sqrt{\sigma_{r+1} \xi(M_{\pi,k})}$, highlighting the role of spectral recoverability.
 - The proof strategy for Theorem 1 involves a "leave-one-out" analysis, extending concentration results for the empirical transition matrix $\hat{P}$ from spectral norm to the desired $L_{2,\infty}$ norm for the shifted successor measure.
4. **Local Mixing Phenomena and Type II Poincaré Inequalities (Theorem 2, 3, 4, Proposition 2)**:
 - To explain *when* low-rank structure emerges, the authors connect it to the mixing properties of the underlying Markov chain. They propose that while full mixing (leading to rank-1 behavior) might require large $k$, "local mixing" can induce approximate low-rank structure for smaller $k$.
 - A novel class of functional inequalities, *Type II Poincaré inequalities*, is introduced. These inequalities provide bounds on $\lambda \|f\|_2^2 \le E_{PP^\dagger}(f,f) + C\lambda \|f\|_1^2$, where $E_{PP^\dagger}$ is the Dirichlet form.
 - Theorem 2 shows that if such an inequality holds, the $L_{2,\infty}$ norm of $P^k$ (and thus $M_{\pi,k}$) decays exponentially with $k$, reaching a constant $C$.
 - Theorem 3 relates this constant $C$ to the coherence of the singular vectors and the ($r+1$)-th singular value, providing a quantitative link between mixing and low-rank approximation.
 - Proposition 2 demonstrates how local Type II Poincaré inequalities on partitioned state spaces can be combined to derive a global one, allowing for the analysis of decomposable Markov chains (e.g., 4-room environments).
 - Theorem 4 applies this to the 4-room environment, showing that the required shift $k$ is much smaller than the global mixing time and enables an accurate low-rank approximation ($rank=4$) to be learned.

**B. Empirical Validation:**
1. **Environment**: Experiments are conducted in discrete maze environments (Medium PointMaze, U-maze, Large-maze, 4-room environment), allowing for tabular MDPs where true successor measures can be computed.
2. **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Spectrum Decay**: Plots of singular values $\sigma_i$ of $M_{\pi,k}$ to visually assess how shifting affects the inherent low-rank structure.
 - **Goal-Conditioned RL Performance**: Policies are learned based on (shifted, low-rank approximated) successor measures in goal-conditioned RL. Performance is measured by "accuracy" (probability of reaching the exact goal) and "relaxed accuracy" (reaching within two steps of the goal).
3. **Comparison of Ground-Truth and Learned Measures**: The evaluation is performed for both oracle (ground-truth) $M_{\pi,k}$ and those learned via Temporal Difference (TD) learning (and model-based estimation in Appendix H.4).
4. **Sample Efficiency**: The impact of the shift parameter on data requirements for achieving a given accuracy is evaluated by varying the number of trajectories.
5. **Role of $\nu$-SVD**: In settings with non-uniform data collection, the paper investigates the effect of using $\nu$-weighted SVD as theoretically proposed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings across both theoretical and empirical domains:

**A. Theoretical Findings:**
1. **Spectral Recoverability**: The paper successfully introduces "spectral irrecoverability" ($\xi(M)$) as a metric for controlling entry-wise approximation and estimation errors. It is formally shown that the $L_{2,\infty}$ approximation error $||M - [M]_r||_{2,\infty}$ is bounded by $\sqrt{\sigma_{r+1} \xi(M)}$ (Lemma 1).
2. **Lack of Initial Low-Rank Structure**: Proposition 1 demonstrates that standard successor measures ($k=0$) do not generally exhibit low spectral recoverability, implying they are not intrinsically low-rank in a manner conducive to accurate entry-wise approximation. This justifies the necessity of shifting.
3. **Emergence of Low-Rank Structure with Shift**: The paper proves that a low-rank structure *naturally emerges* in the *shifted* successor measure ($M_{\pi,k}$) for sufficiently large $k$. This is linked to the system dynamics "forgetting" initial transitions.
4. **Finite-Sample Guarantees**: Theorem 1 provides the first finite-sample performance guarantees for the entry-wise estimation of a low-rank approximation of the shifted successor measure. The estimation error $||[\hat{M}_{\pi,k}]_r - M_{\pi,k}||_{2,\infty}$ is bounded by terms including the newly introduced spectral recoverability, the singular value gap, and factors related to data scarcity and the discount factor. Under certain conditions, the sample complexity scales as $O(n/\epsilon^2)$, improved from $O(n^2/\epsilon^2)$ without structure.
5. **Type II Poincaré Inequalities**: The introduction of Type II Poincaré inequalities (Theorem 2) provides a novel theoretical tool for Markov chains. These inequalities quantify the amount of shift ($k$) required for effective low-rank approximation and estimation, relating it to the decay of high-order singular values of the shifted measure (Theorem 3).
6. **Connection to Local Mixing**: The required shift $k$ is linked to the local mixing properties of the underlying dynamical system, providing a theoretical basis for selecting the shift parameter (Proposition 2, Theorem 4). This shows that a low-rank approximation suitable for capturing "coarse" structures (e.g., distinguishing between rooms in a maze) can be achieved with a relatively small shift, much smaller than the global mixing time.

**B. Empirical Results:**
1. **Sharpened Spectrum with Shift**: Experiments consistently show that increasing the shift $k$ sharpens the spectrum of the successor measure (i.e., accelerates singular value decay), making it more amenable to low-rank approximation. This is observed in various maze environments (Figure 4b, 7b, 8b).
2. **Improved Performance in Goal-Conditioned RL**:
 - For *oracle* (ground-truth) successor measures, introducing a temporal shift *improves goal-reaching performance*, especially when combined with low-rank approximation. Moderate shifts tend to yield peak performance, suggesting an optimal balance of information (Figure 4c-d, 7c-d, 8c-d).
 - For *TD-learned* successor measures, shifting can still improve performance, but excessive shifts can degrade performance due to compounding estimation errors for longer horizons (Figure 4e-f, 7e-f, 8e-f). A moderate shift (e.g., $k=3$ or $k=5$) often provides the best trade-off.
3. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency**: Moderate shifts lead to improved sample efficiency, allowing satisfactory performance with fewer trajectories compared to non-shifted measures. This confirms the practical utility of the shifting mechanism for learning (Figure 4g-h, 7g-h, 8g-h).
4. **Robustness Across Environments**: The findings regarding spectral sharpening and improved performance with moderate shifts are consistent across different maze environments (Medium PointMaze, U-maze, Large-maze).
5. **Impact of $\nu$-SVD**: While $\nu$-weighted SVD results in different low-rank reconstructions compared to standard SVD under non-uniform data collection, goal-reaching performance remains largely consistent (Figure 9, 10).
6. **Model-Based vs. TD Estimation**: Model-based estimation of shifted successor measures is generally more robust to larger shifts compared to TD-learning, maintaining higher accuracy. This is attributed to the model-based approach leveraging all transitions to estimate $P_\pi$ (Figure 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work provides a novel theoretical foundation for understanding and leveraging low-rank structures in reinforcement learning, with several significant implications:

1. **Theoretical Justification for Low-Rank Models**: By demonstrating that a low-rank structure *emerges* in shifted successor measures due to underlying Markov chain dynamics, the paper provides a more rigorous theoretical justification for the use of low-rank models in RL, moving beyond heuristic assumptions. This could guide the principled design of more robust and statistically efficient RL algorithms.
2. **Improved Statistical Efficiency**: The finite-sample guarantees for shifted successor measures suggest that this approach can lead to significantly improved statistical efficiency, particularly for estimating complex value functions in reward-free or goal-conditioned settings. The $O(n/\epsilon^2)$ sample complexity, when low-rank structure is present, is a substantial improvement over methods that do not exploit such structure.
3. **Principled Parameter Selection**: The connection between the shift parameter $k$ and the local mixing properties of the MDP offers a theoretical basis for selecting $k$, potentially reducing the reliance on extensive hyperparameter tuning. This could be particularly impactful in complex environments where intuitive choices for $k$ are not readily available.
4. **Advancements in Goal-Conditioned RL**: The empirical results demonstrate clear performance improvements in goal-conditioned RL when using shifted successor measures. This suggests that the approach could lead to more effective and sample-efficient algorithms for learning universal policies capable of reaching diverse goals, a critical area for real-world RL applications.
5. **New Tools for Markov Chain Analysis**: The introduction of Type II Poincaré inequalities broadens the toolkit for analyzing Markov chains, potentially finding applications beyond RL in areas concerned with the transient and long-term behavior of stochastic processes.
6. **Foundation for Universal Representations**: The conceptual framework, particularly the idea of separating short-range (high-rank) from long-range (low-rank) dynamics, could be extended to other universal representations in RL, such as Forward-Backward models, potentially improving their estimation and generalization properties.
7. **Bridging Theory and Practice**: The paper attempts to bridge the gap between theoretical understanding of matrix approximation and practical RL performance, showing that theoretical insights into matrix properties (like spectral recoverability) can translate into tangible improvements in policy learning.

Overall, this research opens new avenues for both theoretical inquiry and algorithmic development in reinforcement learning, particularly for understanding and exploiting the intrinsic structure of MDP dynamics.
