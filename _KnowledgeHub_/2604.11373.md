# Minimal Embodiment Enables Efficient Learning of Number Concepts in Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers demonstrate that minimal embodiment, through basic visuomotor coordination on a non-humanoid robot, enables efficient learning of abstract number concepts. This approach significantly improves counting accuracy and data efficiency while spontaneously developing biologically plausible representations and dynamics similar to those observed in human cognition.

## Problem

- Understanding how intelligent systems acquire abstract numerical concepts from sensorimotor experience.
- Determining if sensorimotor coupling acts as a structural prior for learning or merely provides additional information.
- Investigating whether the benefits of embodiment generalize to non-humanoid robotic platforms beyond anthropomorphic forms.

## Method

- A Franka Emika Panda robot was used to perform a sequential counting task on colored balls, collecting ==egocentric RGB images== and robot joint positions.
- An embodied neural network architecture combining an ==AlexNet visual encoder==, an ==MLP motor encoder==, and a ==two-layer LSTM== was developed with dual objectives: ==counting prediction== and ==auxiliary motor prediction==.
- Systems neuroscience analysis techniques (==Grad-CAM==, ==number-selective unit analysis==, ==Representational Similarity Analysis==, and ==jPCA==) were applied to interpret internal representations and dynamics.

## Results

- Embodied models consistently outperformed vision-only baselines, achieving **96.8%** accuracy with only **10%** of training data compared to **60.6%** for vision-only models.
- Shuffling motor labels during training, which disrupted visuomotor correspondence while retaining the motor objective, had **negligible impact** on counting accuracy, supporting the structural prior hypothesis.
- Analysis revealed number-selective LSTM units with logarithmic tuning and neural population activity dominated by rotational dynamics (**100%** variance explained by skew-symmetric component), where terminal phase angle linearly encoded the count (r = **0.974**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimal embodiment enhances abstract concept learning primarily by introducing a structural prior via an auxiliary motor objective, which regularizes internal representations, rather than by directly providing task-relevant sensorimotor information.
> - Embodied learning facilitates the spontaneous emergence of biologically plausible internal representations, including logarithmic number-selective units, a mental number line organization, and rotational dynamics encoding numerical magnitude.
> - The robot's numerical learning trajectory, acquiring small numbers before larger ones and transitioning from 'subset-knower' to 'cardinal-principle knower', mirrors human cognitive development patterns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the broader interdisciplinary landscape of cognitive science and artificial intelligence, specifically addressing the fundamental question of how abstract concepts are acquired by intelligent systems through sensorimotor experience. Numerical cognition, the ability to perceive, represent, and manipulate quantities, serves as a tractable domain for this investigation.

The paper builds upon a foundation of research suggesting the embodied nature of numerical cognition in humans. This includes observations of children using finger counting to develop numerical abilities, and neuroimaging studies showing overlapping activation in the intraparietal sulcus for number representation and spatial attention, with arm kinematics influencing numerical judgments. These findings highlight a computational question: whether sensorimotor coupling provides a structured prior for learning or simply additional information.

Previous computational modeling efforts have explored different aspects of numerical cognition. Some work has shown that number-selective neurons can emerge from visual statistics in static images using hierarchical generative models and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Other studies have examined counting as a sequential process, demonstrating that recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks can exhibit learning trajectories that resemble children's developmental stages.

Cognitive developmental robotics has previously integrated these approaches by grounding counting in embodied interaction. For example, research has demonstrated that pointing gestures can facilitate counting acquisition in humanoid robots, and that proprioceptive information from robot hands can improve digit recognition and the formation of a uniform number line. Notably, some of this work suggested that embodiment might offer benefits beyond task-relevant information, even when motor signals were not aligned with visual input. This raised the question of whether these effects depend on humanoid morphology or reflect more general principles of embodiment.

This paper extends this line of inquiry by utilizing a non-humanoid robotic platform and systematically investigating the role of visuomotor correspondence. It aims to determine if embodiment's benefits are due to its informational content or its capacity to impose structural constraints, and whether these benefits generalize beyond human-like forms. By employing analytical methods from systems neuroscience (such as jPCA and Representational Similarity Analysis, RSA), the study seeks to characterize the internal representations and dynamics that emerge in artificial systems during embodied numerical learning, thereby bridging machine learning, developmental robotics, and systems neuroscience.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to investigate the computational principles by which minimal embodiment enables the efficient learning of abstract number concepts in a robotic system. This overarching goal was driven by several specific motivations and key questions:

1. **Understanding Abstract Concept Acquisition:** A central motivation stemmed from the fundamental challenge in cognitive science and artificial intelligence concerning how intelligent systems acquire abstract numerical concepts from sensorimotor experience. The paper sought to contribute to this understanding by using numerical cognition as a tractable domain for studying embodiment.

2. **Evaluating the Role of Embodiment (Structural Prior vs. Information Source):** The research was motivated by a key computational question: does sensorimotor coupling provide a structured prior that shapes learning dynamics and representation, or does it merely offer additional information channels that improve performance? The authors aimed to dissociate these two hypotheses.

3. **Generalizing Embodiment Effects Beyond Humanoid Morphology:** Previous work in developmental robotics had shown benefits of embodiment in humanoid robots (e.g., iCub with finger counting). A specific objective was to determine whether these embodiment effects generalize to a non-humanoid platform, such as a Franka Panda manipulator, thereby exploring if the critical factor is human-like form or general principles of sensorimotor interaction.

4. **Investigating Data Efficiency:** The researchers aimed to assess whether embodied learning can improve data efficiency, enabling artificial systems to learn complex abstract concepts with less training data compared to disembodied or vision-only approaches.

5. **Characterizing Biologically Plausible Representations and Dynamics:** A significant motivation was to examine if a robot learning to count through embodied interaction would spontaneously develop internal representations and neural dynamics that are aligned with observations in human and primate numerical cognition. This involved applying analytical methods from systems neuroscience, such as jPCA and RSA, to interpret the emergent internal states of the neural network. The specific aim was to look for:
 - Number-selective units with logarithmic tuning.
 - Mental number line organization in representational space.
 - Rotational dynamics encoding numerical magnitude.
 - Learning trajectories that mirror children’s developmental progression (e.g., from subset-knowers to cardinal-principle knowers).

6. **Bridging Disciplines and Informing AI Design:** The study was motivated by the desire to bridge machine learning, developmental robotics, and systems neuroscience. By demonstrating that artificial neural networks can develop interpretable, human-aligned representations through embodied interaction, the research aimed to point toward more efficient, robust, and interpretable AI systems, as well as offer insights into the role of embodiment in children’s early numerical learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a combination of robotic interaction, neural network modeling, and systems neuroscience analysis techniques to investigate embodied numerical learning.

#### 3.1. Robotic Platform and Data Collection

- **Robot:** A Franka Emika Panda 7-DOF robotic manipulator was used.
- **Sensors:** Equipped with a wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D435i RGB-D camera to capture egocentric visual data.
- **Task:** The robot performed a sequential counting task, visiting colored balls (diameter: 4 cm) arranged in its 90×60 cm workspace. For each counting trial, the robot sequentially moved to interact with each ball.
- **Data:** At each step of the counting sequence, an egocentric RGB image (640×480 pixels) and the robot’s 7-DOF joint positions were recorded.
- **Dataset:** Comprised 1,188 counting sequences for numerosities 1 to 10. The distribution of sequences followed a Zipf-like pattern, with smaller numbers (1-4) being more frequent. Ball positions were randomized across trials.
- **Data Splits:** A fixed validation set of 242 samples was used. Training subsets of 10% (94 samples), 50% (472 samples), and 100% (946 samples) were created to evaluate data efficiency.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The core model was an embodied neural network designed for sequential processing and dual task objectives.

- **Visual Encoder:** An AlexNet CNN was used to process visual inputs. Features were extracted from the final convolutional layer and subjected to global average pooling to yield a 256-dimensional visual feature vector. Two variants were tested: one with randomly initialized weights and another with ImageNet-pretrained weights.
- **Motor Encoder:** A multilayer perceptron (MLP) encoded the robot's 2-DOF joint angles (joints 1 and 6, representing primary reaching motions) into a 256-dimensional motor feature vector.
- **Recurrent Module:** The visual and motor feature vectors were concatenated to form a 512-dimensional input. This combined input was fed into a two-layer Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, each layer having 512 hidden units, with dropout (p=0.3) applied between layers.
- **Output Heads:**
 - **Counting Head:** A linear layer applied to the final LSTM hidden state, followed by a 10-way softmax activation, to predict the total count (1-10).
 - **Motor Prediction Head:** A linear layer that outputted the 2-DOF joint positions at each timestep, providing an auxiliary training signal throughout the sequence.

#### 3.3. Baseline Models

Two types of vision-only baselines were developed to isolate the contribution of motor information. These models received only visual input and lacked the motor encoder and motor prediction objective.

- **Single-Image Classifier (V1, V3):** An AlexNet encoder followed by a two-layer MLP. This model predicted the count solely from the final image of each counting sequence, without temporal information. V1 used random initialization, V3 used ImageNet pretraining.
- **Sequence Pooling Classifier (V2, V4):** This model processed the full image sequence. Visual features from each frame (via AlexNet) were independently extracted and then aggregated using mean pooling. The pooled feature vector was then fed into the same MLP classifier as the single-image baseline. V2 used random initialization, V4 used ImageNet pretraining.

#### 3.4. Training Regimen

- **Loss Function:** A combined loss function was used: $L = L_{count} + \lambda L_{motor}$.
 - $L_{count}$ was a cross-entropy loss computed at the final timestep for the count prediction.
 - $L_{motor}$ was a mean squared error (MSE) loss computed at every timestep for the joint position prediction.
 - $\lambda$ was set to 1 for embodied models (enabling motor supervision) and 0 for vision-only baselines (disabling motor supervision).
- **Optimization:** Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and weight decay of $10^{-5}$.
- **Parameters:** Batch size was 32, and models were trained for 250 epochs.
- **Experimental Variants:** Embodied models were trained under various conditions by crossing visual initialization (random vs. ImageNet-pretrained) and motor loss status (enabled vs. shuffled). Specific model labels (e.g., E1-E12, V1-V4) denote these configurations.
- **Curriculum Learning:** For embodied models, three curriculum strategies were implemented:
 - **Easy-to-hard:** Samples ordered by numerosity from 1 to 10.
 - **Hard-to-easy:** Samples ordered by numerosity from 10 to 1.
 - **Random:** Samples shuffled without systematic ordering.
- **Shuffle Experiment:** To test the "structural prior" hypothesis, a manipulation was introduced where, within each training batch, the joint position labels were randomly permuted across samples. This maintained the motor prediction objective but eliminated any meaningful correspondence between visual observations and motor states.

#### 3.5. Neural Analysis Techniques

Analytical methods from systems neuroscience were applied to interpret the internal workings of the trained models.
- **Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM):** Used to visualize which image regions the visual encoder attended to during counting, assessing visual attention patterns.
- **Number-Selective Units Analysis:** Individual LSTM hidden units were analyzed for their response patterns across different numerosities. Tuning curves were generated, and a selectivity index was calculated to identify units preferring specific numbers. Logarithmic scaling and distributions of preferred numerosities were examined.
- **Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA):** Pairwise Euclidean distances between mean LSTM activation patterns for each numerosity were computed to construct Representational Dissimilarity Matrices (RDMs). These RDMs were then correlated (Spearman's ρ) with a theoretical numerical distance matrix to assess the presence and structure of a "mental number line." The scaling of neural distance with numerical magnitude (Weber's Law) and ratio was also investigated.
- **jPCA (Joint Principal Component Analysis):** Applied to LSTM hidden states to characterize the temporal dynamics of neural population activity during counting. This technique fits a linear dynamical system ($\dot{X} = M X$) and decomposes the dynamics matrix $M$ into rotational (skew-symmetric) and non-rotational components. It was used to identify and quantify rotational dynamics and how they encoded counting progress, including rotation quality and phase encoding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings regarding the benefits of minimal embodiment for numerical concept learning in robots.

#### 4.1. Embodied Learning Enhances Counting Performance and Data Efficiency

- **Superior Accuracy:** Embodied models consistently outperformed vision-only baselines across all training data fractions (10%, 50%, 100%). For instance, with only 10% of training data, a pretrained embodied model (E9) achieved 96.8% accuracy, significantly higher than the pretrained vision-only baseline (V3) at 60.6%.
- **Interaction with Visual Pretraining:** ImageNet pretraining enhanced the performance of embodied models but resulted in a substantial *decrease* in performance for vision-only models (a negative transfer effect of approximately 15 percentage points in low-data regimes). This suggests that motor grounding can counteract biases introduced by visual representations optimized for general object recognition.
- **Distinct Learning Trajectories:** Embodied models exhibited a two-phase learning trajectory: an initial latent period (20-50 epochs) of low performance, followed by a sharp transition to rapid improvement and high accuracy. In contrast, vision-only models showed rapid early improvement that quickly plateaued.
- **Focused Visual Attention:** Grad-CAM analysis revealed that embodied models maintained focused attention on target objects across convolutional layers and numerosity sizes. Vision-only models, especially with pretraining, often showed diffuse attention or attention allocated to background regions, particularly for small numerosities or when struggling to encompass multiple objects.
- **Structured Representational Geometry:** Principal Component Analysis (PCA) of visual encoder outputs showed that pretrained embodied models developed linearly organized representations resembling a "mental number line" in PCA space. Vision-only models, conversely, exhibited U-shaped representational geometries, which may be suboptimal for magnitude discrimination.
- **Sequential Number Acquisition:** Per-number analysis revealed that embodied models acquired numbers sequentially (1→2→3→...), mirroring the developmental progression of children from "subset-knowers" to "cardinal-principle knowers." Vision-only models, however, displayed an "Approximate Number System" (ANS)-like pattern, showing partial accuracy across the entire numerical range from early training, indicative of imprecise magnitude estimation rather than discrete counting.

#### 4.2. Motor Signals Function as Structural Priors Rather Than Information Sources

- **Double Dissociation:** A key experiment involved shuffling motor labels during training, disrupting veridical visuomotor correspondence while retaining motor prediction as an auxiliary objective. This manipulation had negligible effects on counting accuracy and the characteristic two-phase learning dynamics. However, it substantially impaired motor prediction performance, demonstrating a clear double dissociation.
- This finding suggests that embodiment improves counting not by providing task-relevant sensorimotor information directly, but by introducing a structural prior through the auxiliary motor objective. This prior regularizes representation learning, encouraging the network to develop structured internal states.

#### 4.3. Learning Trajectory Mirrors Human Numerical Development

- **Curriculum Effects:** Curriculum strategies had modest effects on final counting performance, with random curriculum generally yielding the best results, followed by easy-to-hard, and hard-to-easy performing worst, particularly in low-data regimes.
- **Curriculum-Invariant Developmental Pattern:** Despite variations in training order, embodied models consistently acquired small numbers (1-4) before larger numbers. This small-to-large progression occurred regardless of whether the curriculum was easy-to-hard, hard-to-easy, or random. This suggests that the difficulty gradient from small to large numbers reflects intrinsic computational constraints of the counting task, rather than merely an artifact of training data presentation order, aligning with observations in human child development.

#### 4.4. Biologically Plausible Representations and Dynamics Emerge Spontaneously

- **Number-Selective Units:** Analysis of LSTM hidden unit activations revealed the spontaneous emergence of number-selective units. These units exhibited peaked responses to specific numerosities and showed logarithmic tuning, similar to neurons observed in primate intraparietal cortex. Both positive (activity increasing with numerosity) and negative (activity decreasing) detectors displayed logarithmic scaling ($R^2 \approx 0.90$).
- **Mental Number Line Organization:** Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA) showed that the model’s internal representations were organized along a continuous dimension resembling a mental number line. Neural distances between adjacent numbers decreased systematically with magnitude (logarithmic fit $R^2 = 0.45$), consistent with Weber’s law and the compressed representation of number in biological systems. Neural distance also scaled with numerical ratio rather than absolute difference ($R^2 \approx 0.85$). This number line organization was robust across different training conditions, including shuffled visuomotor correspondence and limited data.
- **Rotational Dynamics During Counting:** jPCA analysis revealed that rotational dynamics completely dominated the neural population activity in the LSTM hidden states (100% of fitted dynamics variance explained by the skew-symmetric component). Trajectories for different numerosities followed similar rotational paths, but terminated at distinct phases. The terminal phase angle in jPCA space linearly encoded the final count value with high correlation (e.g., $r = 0.974$ at 100% data, with a slope of 30.6$^\circ$/count). The primary rotation frequency aligned with the numerical range (8-10 counting steps), suggesting that the network implemented counting as a single rotational cycle through neural state space, where the phase of rotation encoded counting progress. This rotational structure was also robust to visuomotor shuffling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of abstract concept learning in artificial systems and has several potential impacts across multiple fields.

#### 5.1. Advancing Embodied Cognition Theory

The study provides empirical evidence that even "minimalist embodiment"—involving basic visuomotor coordination on a non-humanoid platform, without complex anthropomorphic features like fingers—can significantly enhance the learning of abstract concepts such as number. A key finding is the double dissociation between motor prediction and counting accuracy under shuffled visuomotor conditions. This suggests that the auxiliary motor objective acts as a *structural prior* that regularizes learning dynamics and representation, rather than merely supplying task-relevant information. This clarifies a previously hypothesized mechanism in embodied cognition.

#### 5.2. Bridging AI, Robotics, and Neuroscience

The work demonstrates the value of applying analytical methods from systems neuroscience (e.g., jPCA, RSA) to interpret the internal representations and dynamics of artificial neural networks. The spontaneous emergence of biologically plausible features such as logarithmic number-selective units, mental number line organization, and rotational dynamics in a robotic system suggests shared computational principles between biological and artificial intelligence in numerical cognition. This convergence offers a computational framework for simulating human numerical cognition and testing cognitive theories in a controlled environment.

#### 5.3. Implications for AI System Design

The findings indicate that embodied learning can lead to:
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** Embodied models achieved high accuracy with significantly less training data than vision-only baselines, suggesting a path toward more data-efficient AI.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The structured representations and learning dynamics fostered by embodiment appear to contribute to more robust performance and better generalization.
- **Interpretability:** The emergence of human-aligned, interpretable representations and dynamics within the model's "neural" activity provides insights into how abstract concepts can be learned and represented in complex systems. This contrasts with some conventional deep learning approaches that may be less transparent.

#### 5.4. Insights into Human Cognitive Development

The observation that the robot’s learning trajectory parallels children’s developmental progression (from subset-knowers to cardinal-principle knowers, and the curriculum-invariant acquisition of small-before-large numbers) provides a computational model for understanding fundamental constraints on human numerical learning. This suggests that these developmental patterns may reflect general computational challenges rather than solely human-specific biological constraints.

#### 5.5. Potential Practical Applications

- **Embodied Mathematics Tutoring:** The principles derived from this research could inform the design of interactive, embodied learning tools for children, potentially enhancing mathematics and science education by leveraging sensorimotor grounding. Robots trained with this approach could serve as effective educational aids.
- **Safety-Critical Industrial Applications:** Developing AI systems with a more robust and interpretable understanding of quantity could be relevant for industrial applications requiring precise enumeration or quantitative reasoning, where reliability and explainability are critical.
- **Interventions for Learning Difficulties:** Understanding the role of sensorimotor grounding in numerical concept development could inform interventions for dyscalculia and other learning difficulties, by focusing on embodied strategies to support conceptual acquisition.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The study opens avenues for future research, including exploring embodiment in other abstract domains (e.g., spatial reasoning, language acquisition), systematic comparisons across different robot morphologies and sensor modalities, scaling the approach to larger numerosities and arithmetic, and conducting more direct comparisons between model activity and neural recordings.
