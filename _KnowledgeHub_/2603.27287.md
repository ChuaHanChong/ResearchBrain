# Uni-World VLA: Interleaved World Modeling and Planning for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Uni-World VLA introduces a unified Vision-Language-Action model for autonomous driving that tightly interleaves future world state prediction and ego-vehicle trajectory planning within a closed-loop feedback mechanism. This approach, incorporating monocular depth, achieved a top Predictive Driver Model Score of 89.4 and a Fréchet Video Distance of 141.8 on the NAVSIM benchmark, demonstrating improved adaptive decision-making and visual fidelity.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving systems using world models often employ 'open-loop imagination,' where future scene predictions are static and do not dynamically adapt to the ego vehicle's evolving actions.
- This 'frozen hallucination' leads to outdated visual evidence for planning in dynamic, interactive scenarios, hindering adaptive decision-making.
- Prior world models primarily rely on RGB inputs, which can lack sufficient geometric cues for high-fidelity, long-horizon scene and motion prediction.

## Method

- **Uni-World VLA** employs a unified ==autoregressive== VLA model that tightly ==interleaves future frame prediction and trajectory planning== in a step-wise, closed-loop fashion.
- It integrates ==monocular depth maps==, extracted by **Depth Anything 3**, into historical visual inputs through specialized ==depth encoders== and ==cross-attention fusion==.
- The model is trained with joint supervision on future visual token generation (using ==Dynamic Focal Loss==) and trajectory prediction (L1 loss), ensuring temporal consistency.

## Results

- **Uni-World VLA** achieved the highest Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of **89.4** on the NAVSIM benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art methods, including multi-sensor approaches.
- The model demonstrated competitive video generation quality, achieving a Frechet Video Distance (FVD) of **141.8** for 4-second future rollouts.
- Ablation studies confirmed that interleaved future-frame generation and monocular depth integration significantly improve both planning performance (e.g., increased Ego Progress and Time-To-Collision) and visual fidelity of predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A continuous, interleaved feedback loop between world state prediction and ego-vehicle action planning is essential for adaptive decision-making in dynamic driving environments, overcoming limitations of static future rollouts.
> - Integrating monocular depth information significantly improves the geometric understanding and temporal consistency of predicted future scenes, crucial for robust long-horizon planning.
> - Optimizing the frequency and historical context of visual input (contextual and dynamic tokens) is critical for balancing detailed scene semantics with fine-grained motion cues, leading to better planning and generation performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving research has seen advancements with the emergence of multi-modal large models (MLLMs) and generative models, giving rise to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and world models. This work positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing limitations in how these paradigms are typically integrated for autonomous driving tasks.

**Existing Paradigms and Their Limitations:**
Historically, autonomous driving systems have ranged from end-to-end approaches relying on imitation learning to more recent VLA-based methods that leverage semantic understanding for trajectory prediction, and world models that forecast environmental evolution based on learned physical dynamics. A common observation is that these approaches are often developed in isolation, leading to a lack of effective knowledge sharing and integration between environment modeling and trajectory planning.

Prior attempts to unify world modeling and trajectory prediction have generally fallen into two categories:
1. **"Predict-and-plan" Paradigm:** In this approach, world modeling and planning are integrated within a single autoregressive architecture. However, the tasks often remain functionally decoupled. World modeling focuses on high-fidelity next-frame prediction conditioned on actions, while planning maps visual observations to control outputs without explicitly leveraging the learned dynamics of the world model.
2. **"Predict-then-plan" Paradigm:** This sequential approach first predicts future scenes and then generates the ego vehicle’s trajectory conditioned on these predicted states. A key limitation here is the implicit assumption of a stationary environment, which does not accurately reflect the continuous interactions and non-stationary nature of real-world traffic.

A significant common limitation across both paradigms is the concept of "open-loop imagination" or "frozen hallucination." When a world model generates a multi-second future rollout, it typically conditions this rollout on an initial intent or plan. This leads to a static prediction of the future that does not account for the continuous adjustments the ego vehicle might make in response to earlier predicted observations. Consequently, visual evidence from later parts of the prediction horizon may become outdated, as it does not reflect the dynamic interaction between the ego-vehicle's evolving plan and the environment's reaction. This can hinder adaptive decision-making in complex, rapidly evolving traffic scenarios such as unprotected left turns or merging.

**Uni-World VLA's Contribution to the Landscape:**
Uni-World VLA directly addresses these limitations by introducing an "interleaved world modeling and planning" paradigm. Instead of separate or sequentially cascaded prediction and planning, this work proposes a tightly coupled, step-wise feedback loop. It alternates between predicting future world states and planning ego-vehicle actions, allowing decisions to be continuously refined based on newly imagined observations. This closed-loop interaction is designed to enable more adaptive and robust decision-making compared to prior open-loop methods. Furthermore, the incorporation of monocular depth information aims to enhance the geometric understanding of the environment, a cue often limited in prior RGB-only world models, thereby improving long-horizon scene prediction. This work therefore represents an advancement towards more integrated and responsive VLA driving systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of tightly interleaving future frame prediction and trajectory planning for autonomous driving. This objective is motivated by several identified shortcomings in existing approaches.

**Motivation for Interleaved Prediction and Planning:**
Current world-model-based autonomous driving systems often predict future scenes as a distinct step before planning actions. This sequential "predict-then-plan" or functionally decoupled "predict-and-plan" approach leads to a critical limitation: open-loop imagination. In highly dynamic and interactive urban driving scenarios, such as making an unprotected left turn or merging into traffic, environmental conditions can evolve rapidly. If a world model generates a multi-second prediction (e.g., a 4-second rollout) based on an initial planned intent, this prediction effectively constitutes a "frozen hallucination" of the future. The visual evidence generated within this rollout does not dynamically update to reflect any subtle adjustments the ego vehicle might make early in the planning horizon (e.g., slight braking or steering at 0.5 seconds). Consequently, when the planner relies on visual information from later in the rollout (e.g., the third second), that information may already be outdated and inconsistent with the evolving ego-vehicle's actions. This lack of continuous feedback between predicted observations and planned actions can lead to suboptimal or unsafe decision-making.

The core motivation is to overcome this "frozen hallucination" problem by establishing a closed-loop interaction. By interleaving future scene prediction and ego-vehicle action planning step-by-step, the model can continuously condition planning decisions on the most recently imagined future observations. This allows the planning process to be more adaptive and responsive to the evolving environment, refining its decisions in real-time within the imagined future.

**Motivation for Depth Integration:**
A secondary objective and motivation is to enhance the fidelity and geometric consistency of future scene predictions, particularly over longer horizons. Many existing world models primarily rely on RGB visual inputs, which can sometimes lack sufficient geometric cues for precise environmental understanding and accurate motion prediction. Monocular depth information, which provides geometric cues about the scene structure, can offer complementary spatial context. By integrating monocular depth maps into the historical visual inputs, the researchers aim to provide stronger geometric priors for the world modeling component, thereby improving the quality and temporal consistency of long-horizon future frame predictions, which in turn could lead to more robust planning.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more adaptive and context-aware decision-making in autonomous driving, moving beyond open-loop predictive models to a tightly coupled, interactive system that benefits from continuous feedback and enhanced geometric understanding of the environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Uni-World VLA framework is designed as a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that performs interleaved world modeling and trajectory planning. The methodology incorporates a multimodal large language model (LLM) backbone, a novel interleaved generation paradigm, and an integrated depth fusion strategy.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture and Interleaved Generation

The core of Uni-World VLA is a unified autoregressive architecture based on Show-o, which is a Phi-1.5-based multimodal LLM. This model operates by alternately generating future visual tokens and action tokens. The process is step-wise: after predicting a future world state, the model plans an ego-vehicle action based on that predicted state. This interaction forms a closed-loop prediction and planning process.
The model is configured to generate up to N=8 future frames, corresponding to a 4.0-second prediction horizon, with a temporal interval of 0.5 seconds per frame. This step-wise interaction allows the planning decisions to be continuously updated and conditioned on the evolving environment.

#### 3.2. Inputs and Tokenization

The model's inputs consist of both historical ego-centric image frames and auxiliary ego-state information.
- **Visual Data:** A sequence of M historical ego-centric image frames (e.g., It-M..., It-1) is provided. To capture different aspects of the environment, the historical video stream is partitioned into two modalities:
 - **Contextual Tokens:** Derived from initial high-resolution frames (256x448), these provide detailed scene semantics and structural information.
 - **Dynamic Tokens:** Sampled at a higher frequency (10 Hz) and lower resolution (128x224), these capture fine-grained motion cues and short-term temporal variations.
 Each raw image is encoded into a sequence of discrete visual tokens using a MagVIT-v2 encoder, which is a dual-branch tokenizer with separate codebooks.
- **Ego State Information:** Auxiliary data at the current time t, including ego vehicle velocity, acceleration, and the high-level driving command, is collected and concatenated into "Ego Tokens."
- **Textual Prompts:** System and user text prompts are incorporated to provide conversational context and specify the task objective.
The entire input stream is constructed as a chat-style context: [System Prompt | Dynamic & Contextual Tokens | User Prompt | Ego Tokens]. The LLM then consumes this mixed token stream for autoregressive generation.

#### 3.3. Interleaved Frame-Action Generation and Output

The model autoregressively generates an interleaved sequence of future scene tokens (ˆd_t+k) and action tokens (ˆa_t+k).
- **Visual Prediction:** At each step, the model predicts dynamic tokens for a future frame (ˆd_t+k) conditioned on all previously generated dynamic tokens and action tokens (p_θ(d_t+k | ˆd_≤t+k-1, ˆa_≤t+k-1)). These tokens are then decoded by the MagVIT-v2 decoder into the corresponding RGB frame (ˆI_t+k), conditioned on contextual tokens (c_t+2⌊k/2⌋) for visual guidance.
- **Action Planning:** Immediately after generating a future frame's visual tokens, the model infers the ego vehicle's predicted position (ˆa_t+k) at that timestamp, conditioned on all previously generated visual and action tokens, including the just-predicted frame (p_θ(a_t+k | ˆd_≤t+k, ˆa_≤t+k-1)). These action tokens are passed through an MLP head to obtain the ego positions, forming the planned trajectory.
This alternating generation establishes a continuous interaction, allowing decisions to be refined based on newly predicted observations and aiming to reduce error accumulation.

#### 3.4. Depth Integration Strategy

To provide complementary geometric cues, monocular depth information is incorporated.
- **Depth Estimation:** Depth maps (D) are extracted from input images (I) using Depth Anything 3, a state-of-the-art monocular depth estimation model.
- **Feature Encoding:** The extracted depth maps are resized to match the resolutions of the contextual and dynamic visual branches (256x448 and 128x224). These are then fed into two specialized depth encoders: a context-depth-encoder (CDE) and a dynamic-depth-encoder (DDE), both based on MagVIT-v2.
- **Cross-Attention Fusion:** The visual token embeddings (E_q,c and E_q,d), which serve as queries, are fused with the key (D_k) and value (D_v) representations generated by their respective depth encoders using a cross-attention mechanism. This process generates fused feature embeddings (E_fused,c and E_fused,d) that combine visual appearance with geometric information, which are then fed into the VLM model.

#### 3.5. Training Objectives

The model is trained with joint supervision on both future visual token generation and trajectory prediction.
- **Visual Prediction Loss (L_dyn):** The model outputs logits over the discrete MagVIT-v2 vocabulary. To emphasize temporally varying image regions and address token invariance across frames, Dynamic Focal Loss is employed. This loss applies a dynamic weight (ω) based on whether a token has changed from the previous frame, giving more importance to dynamic regions.
- **Trajectory Prediction Loss (L_traj):** An L1 loss is used to supervise the predicted ego positions (ˆa_t+1:t+N) against the ground truth.
- **Overall Loss (L):** The final training objective is a weighted sum of L_dyn and L_traj.
- **Attention Masking:** During training, a causal attention mask is used, allowing tokens to attend to all previous tokens and all tokens within the current frame, facilitating coherent spatial and temporal dependencies while preserving causality.

#### 3.6. Inference Process

During inference, future frames and actions are generated step-by-step in an autoregressive manner. A KV-cache is leveraged to store key and value representations from previous steps, enabling the LLM to only compute attention for newly generated tokens and thus improving efficiency. The generated visual tokens from a previous step are appended to the context to condition the subsequent generation.

#### 3.7. Implementation Details

- **Dataset:** NAVSIM, a high-fidelity simulated driving benchmark, is used for all experiments.
- **Initialization:** The model is initialized from Policy World Model (PWM), which was fine-tuned from Show-o.
- **Tokenizer:** MagVIT-v2, with a high-resolution branch (256x448, 448 contextual tokens) and a low-resolution branch (128x224, 28 dynamic tokens).
- **Depth Encoder Initialization:** Weights of CDE and DDE are initialized using MagVIT-v2 weights.
- **Training Strategy:** A two-stage progressive paradigm is adopted for depth fusion training. Stage 1 pre-trains CDE/DDE with action-free video prediction (5 epochs, frozen foundation model). Stage 2 conducts multi-modal joint training (16 epochs, frozen CDE/DDE, unfrozen fusion/foundation model) using Scheme E for supervision.
- **Resources:** Training is performed on 32 NVIDIA H20 GPUs with a batch size of 3, using the front-view camera as input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the Uni-World VLA framework in both closed-loop planning and video generation.

#### 4.1. Closed-Loop Planning Performance

Evaluated on the NAVSIM test split, Uni-World VLA achieved a **Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 89.4**, which was the highest among the compared state-of-the-art methods (Table 1). PDMS is a comprehensive metric reflecting safety, comfort, and forward progress.
- **Efficiency and Safety:** The model attained the best Ego Progress (EP) with 83.2 and Time-To-Collision (TTC) with 96.4. These scores indicate improved efficiency in forward movement and enhanced safety during execution.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Uni-World VLA demonstrated superior overall performance compared to other world model methods, including ResWorld (PDMS 89.0), which utilized multi-sensor inputs (camera + LiDAR). This suggests that the proposed single-view camera-only design with interleaved generation and depth fusion is effective. Other baselines, such as DrivingGPT, WoTE, Epona, and PWM, showed strong performance on individual metrics but did not match the combined PDMS score of Uni-World VLA. The model maintained high comfort (100.0) and drivable area compliance (96.7).

#### 4.2. Video Generation Quality

For 2-Hz short-horizon video generation, Uni-World VLA achieved a **Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 141.8** on the NAVSIM 4s/2Hz protocol (Table 2).
- **Competitive Fidelity:** This FVD score is competitive with, and in some cases surpasses, other driving world models, including SVD, GenAD, and DrivingGPT.
- **Balanced Performance:** The results indicate that Uni-World VLA maintains high visual generation quality concurrently with strong closed-loop planning performance, suggesting an effective balance between scene prediction fidelity and planning reliability.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Visualizations of predicted future frames and corresponding Bird's-Eye View (BEV) trajectories (Figure 4, 7, 8) indicate that Uni-World VLA produces:
- **Temporally Consistent Visual Dynamics:** The generated future frames show coherent evolution of the scene.
- **Smooth and Safe Trajectories:** The planned trajectories adhere to lane geometry and account for surrounding agents, appearing stable and aligned with feasible driving behaviors.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Pretraining, Future Frames, and Depth (Table 3):**
 - **Pretraining:** Provided a significant performance improvement, increasing PDMS from 82.1 to 88.2.
 - **Future-frame generation:** Further enhanced performance, raising PDMS from 88.2 to 89.2, suggesting that explicit modeling of future world states provides beneficial context for trajectory planning.
 - **Depth Integration:** Contributed additional gains, increasing DAC to 96.7, EP to 83.2, and notably improving video quality by reducing FVD from 164.2 to 141.8. Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5) illustrate that depth fusion helps preserve clearer spatial layouts and geometric cues, particularly over longer horizons and during turns, contributing to smoother and more coherent predictions.
- **Effect of Alternative Generation Schemes (Table 4):**
 - Different interleaved generation schemes were evaluated. **Scheme E (2Hz aligned interleaving)**, which directly aligns frame and action generation with the 2 Hz evaluation frequency (F→A alternation), achieved the best PDMS of 89.2. This indicates that matching the generation frequency with the planning and evaluation protocol is important for temporal consistency and planning quality. Other schemes, such as high-frequency alternation or sliding windows, led to performance drops, suggesting that frequency mismatches or conflicting supervision signals can be detrimental.
- **Effect of Historical Visual Information (Table 5):**
 - Using **2.0 seconds of both contextual and dynamic historical tokens** yielded the best overall performance (PDMS 89.2, FVD 164.2). This suggests that a combination of high-resolution scene semantics (contextual tokens) and fine-grained motion cues (dynamic tokens) over a sufficient historical window is optimal.
 - Reducing the history to 1.0 second or using only contextual tokens resulted in slightly lower PDMS, while using only dynamic tokens significantly degraded both planning and generation metrics (PDMS 81.7, FVD 203.6), underscoring the importance of comprehensive visual context.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the interleaved world modeling and planning approach, augmented with monocular depth integration and optimized generation schemes, leads to improved autonomous driving performance, marked by higher safety, efficiency, and fidelity in future scene prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Uni-World VLA framework presents a significant advancement in the integration of world modeling and planning for autonomous driving, with several potential impacts.

### 6. Addressing "Frozen Hallucination" and Enhancing Adaptive Decision-Making

The primary significance lies in its departure from open-loop prediction paradigms by introducing tightly interleaved world modeling and planning. This closed-loop feedback mechanism directly addresses the "frozen hallucination" problem, where static future predictions can become inconsistent with the ego vehicle's evolving actions. By continuously refining planning decisions based on newly imagined observations, Uni-World VLA enables more adaptive and responsive behavior in dynamic and interactive traffic scenarios. This could lead to smoother, more natural, and ultimately safer interactions with other road users and the environment.

### 7. Improved Robustness and Safety in Complex Scenarios

The competitive closed-loop planning performance, particularly the highest Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) and improved Time-To-Collision (TTC) and Ego Progress (EP), suggests that Uni-World VLA contributes to more robust and safer autonomous driving systems. The ability to better reason about scene dynamics and ego-motion throughout the prediction horizon is crucial for navigating complex urban environments, such as unprotected turns, merging, or reacting to sudden changes in traffic flow.

### 8. Enhanced World Modeling with Geometric Cues

The integration of monocular depth information is a notable enhancement, providing stronger geometric cues to the world model. This leads to higher fidelity and temporal consistency in long-horizon future frame predictions, as evidenced by the improved Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and qualitative results. Accurate geometric understanding is foundational for many driving tasks, including object detection, collision avoidance, and path planning, and its robust incorporation into generative world models could significantly boost their predictive power.

### 9. A Promising Direction for Scalable VLA Systems

The unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model architecture demonstrates the potential for developing scalable driving systems that seamlessly integrate perception, prediction, and planning. By treating visual and action outputs as interleaved tokens within a single autoregressive framework, the model can learn shared representations and dependencies between these modalities. This unified approach could simplify architectural complexity and facilitate end-to-end learning for autonomous driving.

### 10. Implications for Future Research

This work opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated feedback loops between prediction and control. It highlights the value of multimodal inputs beyond RGB, particularly depth, for enriching environmental understanding. Further exploration could involve incorporating other sensor modalities (e.g., LiDAR, radar data) into similar interleaved architectures, investigating different temporal granularities for interaction, and extending the framework to handle multi-agent interactions more explicitly within the closed-loop imagination. The emphasis on matching generation frequency with the evaluation protocol also provides a practical lesson for designing future interactive models.

In conclusion, Uni-World VLA's methodology offers a step towards more intelligent and context-aware autonomous driving systems by fostering a dynamic interplay between a vehicle's understanding of its future environment and its planned actions. This has the potential to translate into more reliable and safer automated vehicles capable of handling the intricacies of real-world driving.
