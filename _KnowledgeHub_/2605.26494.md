# The MiniMax-M2 Series: Mini Activations Unleashing Max Real-World Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> MiniMax developed the M2 series, a family of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models designed for agentic tasks, demonstrating that models with approximately 10 billion activated parameters can achieve competitive performance against leading frontier models. The M2 series integrates agent-driven data pipelines, a scalable reinforcement learning system called Forge, and early self-evolution capabilities to enhance real-world intelligence.

## Problem

- Managing efficiency and cost bottlenecks associated with ultra-long contexts required for agentic tasks during both training and inference.
- Effectively addressing the intrinsic complexity and high-stakes nature of real-world deployment scenarios for advanced LLMs, such as software engineering and knowledge-intensive office automation.

## Method

- Introduced the **MiniMax-M2** series, a family of 62-layer ==Mixture-of-Experts== (MoE) decoder-only Transformers, designed for "mini activations" (9.8 billion activated parameters per token) while maintaining high capacity.
- Developed agent-driven data pipelines for agentic coding, collaborative work, reasoning, and general knowledge, emphasizing high-fidelity training trajectories with verifiable rewards or credible feedback signals.
- Engineered Forge, a scalable agent-native ==reinforcement learning== (RL) system, capable of integrating both white-box and black-box agents within a unified training loop using a novel Clipped Importance Sampling Policy Optimization (CISPO) algorithm.

## Results

- The M2.7 model achieved competitive performance against leading closed-weight frontier models (e.g., Claude Opus 4.6, GPT 5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro) across agentic coding, cowork, and reasoning benchmarks, including a 56.2 on SWE-bench Pro and **66.6%** medal rate on MLE Bench Lite.
- Showcased consistent improvement across the M2 series progression (M2 to M2.5 to M2.7), with absolute gains up to +33.8 points (BrowseComp), particularly in domains where new agentic task families were introduced.
- Demonstrated early self-evolution capabilities in the M2.7 checkpoint, enabling autonomous debugging, modification of its own agent scaffold, and a **30%** performance gain on internal evaluations for ML-engineering tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving competitive, frontier-tier performance in agentic domains is possible with significantly lower active parameter counts per token when combined with specialized data and training methodologies.
> - High-quality, verifiable agent-driven data pipelines, including techniques like Agent-as-a-Verifier (AaaV) and Terminal-Gym, are crucial for building robust real-world agentic capabilities.
> - A decoupled and robust RL infrastructure, employing advanced scheduling (windowed-FIFO) and computational optimizations (prefix-tree merging, MTP-based speculative decoding), is vital for scaling agentic learning effectively and efficiently.
