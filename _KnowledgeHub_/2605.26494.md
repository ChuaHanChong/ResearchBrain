# The MiniMax-M2 Series: Mini Activations Unleashing Max Real-World Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> MiniMax developed the M2 series, a family of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models designed for agentic tasks, demonstrating that models with approximately 10 billion activated parameters can achieve competitive performance against leading frontier models. The M2 series integrates agent-driven data pipelines, a scalable reinforcement learning system called Forge, and early self-evolution capabilities to enhance real-world intelligence.

## Problem

- Managing efficiency and cost bottlenecks associated with ultra-long contexts required for agentic tasks during both training and inference.
- Effectively addressing the intrinsic complexity and high-stakes nature of real-world deployment scenarios for advanced LLMs, such as software engineering and knowledge-intensive office automation.
- Frontier model development remains heavily dependent on resource-intensive human-in-the-loop iteration for debugging training runs and refining agent scaffolds, slowing the pace of model improvement.

## Method

- Introduced the **MiniMax-M2** series, a family of 62-layer ==Mixture-of-Experts== (MoE) decoder-only Transformers, designed for "mini activations" (9.8 billion activated parameters per token) while maintaining high capacity.
- Developed agent-driven data pipelines for agentic coding, collaborative work, reasoning, and general knowledge, emphasizing high-fidelity training trajectories with verifiable rewards or credible feedback signals.
- Engineered Forge, a scalable agent-native ==reinforcement learning== (RL) system, capable of integrating both white-box and black-box agents within a unified training loop using a novel Clipped Importance Sampling Policy Optimization (CISPO) algorithm.

## Results

- The M2.7 model achieved competitive performance against leading closed-weight frontier models (e.g., Claude Opus 4.6, GPT 5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro) across agentic coding, cowork, and reasoning benchmarks, including a 56.2 on SWE-bench Pro and **66.6%** medal rate on MLE Bench Lite.
- Showcased consistent improvement across the M2 series progression (M2 to M2.5 to M2.7), with absolute gains up to +33.8 points (BrowseComp), particularly in domains where new agentic task families were introduced.
- Demonstrated early self-evolution capabilities in the M2.7 checkpoint, enabling autonomous debugging, modification of its own agent scaffold, and a **30%** performance gain on internal evaluations for ML-engineering tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving competitive, frontier-tier performance in agentic domains is possible with significantly lower active parameter counts per token when combined with specialized data and training methodologies.
> - High-quality, verifiable agent-driven data pipelines, including techniques like Agent-as-a-Verifier (AaaV) and Terminal-Gym, are crucial for building robust real-world agentic capabilities.
> - A decoupled and robust RL infrastructure, employing advanced scheduling (windowed-FIFO) and computational optimizations (prefix-tree merging, MTP-based speculative decoding), is vital for scaling agentic learning effectively and efficiently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) is evolving from basic dialogue systems to advanced agents capable of long-horizon, multi-step workflows. These agentic tasks encompass a range of complex activities such as writing and deploying production code, navigating the internet, operating various tools, and generating structured professional documents. This transition introduces two significant challenges: managing the formidable efficiency and cost bottlenecks associated with ultra-long contexts required for agentic tasks during both training and inference, and effectively addressing the intrinsic complexity and high-stakes nature of real-world deployment scenarios, such as software engineering and knowledge-intensive office automation.

This research contributes to the frontier of LLM development by introducing the MiniMax-M2 series, a family of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models. MoE architectures are a known strategy for scaling model capacity while controlling the computational resources required per token. The MiniMax-M2 series explicitly positions itself against other leading closed-weight frontier models (e.g., Claude Opus, GPT, Gemini), aiming to achieve competitive performance in agentic domains with a significantly lower activation footprint. A notable aspect of this work is its focus on "mini activations" for "maximum real-world intelligence," alongside the early demonstration of a self-evolution capability, which aims to reduce human intervention in model development and positions the work at the cutting edge of autonomous AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the MiniMax-M2 series is to develop highly capable language models for real-world agentic deployment, driven by the principle that "mini activations can unleash maximum real-world intelligence." This overarching goal is motivated by the dual challenges inherent in deploying LLMs for complex, long-horizon tasks: the high computational cost and inefficiency of ultra-long contexts, and the necessity to address intrinsically complex and high-stakes tasks in production environments.

To achieve this, the research sets out three key objectives:

1. **Develop Agent-Driven Data Pipelines:** The project aims to design and implement high-fidelity, large-scale data pipelines specifically tailored for agentic coding, collaborative work (cowork), reasoning, and general knowledge tasks. A critical aspect of this objective is to ensure that each training trajectory is accompanied by corresponding static/runtime environments, verifiable rewards, or credible feedback signals. The motivation here is that elevating the quality and credibility of reward signals, through executable verification or judge-model evidence checking, is essential for unlocking the full potential of the base model in agentic settings.

2. **Engineer Forge: A Scalable Agent-Native RL System:** A central objective is to build a robust and scalable reinforcement learning (RL) system named Forge, designed for general-purpose agentic learning. This system is intended to seamlessly integrate both white-box (internally visible) and black-box (API-only) agents within a unified training loop. The motivation behind Forge is to overcome the engineering tensions in large-scale RL, such as achieving high system throughput, maintaining training stability, and ensuring agent flexibility. This involves architectural decoupling of training, inference, and agent components, coupled with robustness-first algorithmic designs, meticulous reward systems, and efficiency-enhancing mechanisms like windowed-FIFO scheduling, prefix-tree merging, and inference optimization.

3. **Advance Towards Self-Evolution:** The project aims to demonstrate an early operational form of self-evolution within the M2.7 checkpoint. This involves enabling the model to autonomously triage failed training runs, modify its own agent scaffold across various tasks and experiments, and engage in multi-round self-improvement on representative ML-engineering tasks. The motivation here is to automate and significantly reduce one of the most resource-intensive human-in-the-loop bottlenecks in the development of frontier models, thereby accelerating the iterative refinement process.

These objectives collectively aim to translate a mini-activation computational footprint into competitive, frontier-tier performance across critical agentic benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MiniMax-M2 series is developed using a multi-faceted approach encompassing architectural design, data curation, supervised fine-tuning, and a specialized reinforcement learning system.

#### 3.1. Pre-Training Architecture

The flagship M2 is a 62-layer decoder-only Transformer based on a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture. It features 229.9 billion total parameters, with 9.8 billion activated per token. The model employs a hidden dimension of 3,072, a vocabulary size of 200,064, and is pre-trained on 29.2 trillion tokens with a native context window of 192K. Key architectural choices include:
- **Attention**: Full multi-head attention is used across all layers, with Grouped Query Attention (GQA) employing 48 query heads and 8 key-value heads. Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) are applied throughout.
- **Mixture-of-Experts**: The MoE feed-forward layer contains 256 fine-grained experts, with 8 experts activated per token. Sigmoid gating with learnable expert-specific bias terms is used for routing, enhancing load balancing and reducing reliance on auxiliary losses. Ablation studies indicated fine-grained experts improved performance on reasoning and coding tasks.
- **Multi-Token Prediction (MTP)**: An MTP module is incorporated during pre-training (K=1), expanded to K=3 during continued pre-training via weight copying. This module provides richer training signals and enables multi-step speculative decoding during inference, consistently improving performance across benchmarks, particularly on reasoning-heavy tasks.

#### 3.2. Pre-Training Data

The pre-training corpus is diverse, including web documents, academic literature, books, programming code, and structured question-answering content. Document quality is assessed using model-based reward scoring and auxiliary classifiers, with a balanced sampling strategy that upweights high-quality content. The data mixture upsamples code, mathematics, and STEM content. A multi-stage training procedure progressively extends the context window from 8K to 192K tokens, using high-quality code concatenation, long-form PDF documents, and thematic document packing.

#### 3.3. Post-Training Data Collection

The bulk of M2's real-world capability is built through an agent-native post-training pipeline that co-evolves from M2 through M2.5 to M2.7, focusing on agentic coding, agentic cowork, reasoning-intensive tasks, general conversation, and role-play.
- **Agentic Coding**:
 - **SWE-scaling pipeline**: An agent-driven pipeline uses public GitHub data to generate diverse, verifiable software engineering tasks (bug fixes, feature additions, performance optimizations). It involves PR collection and filtering, agent-synthesized Docker environments, PR tagging, test-based verifiable reward construction, model-based task validation, and task augmentation (e.g., bug injection, commit merging).
 - **Application Development (AppDev) pipeline**: An expert-in-the-loop system generates tasks for building complete applications. Experts create meta queries and system prompts. An Agent-as-a-Verifier (AaaV) framework performs rejection sampling by deploying generated applications in sandboxed environments and evaluating them against expert-defined rubrics across execution, interaction, and visual aesthetics layers.
 - **Terminal-Gym**: An automated data synthesis pipeline converts Stack Overflow programming scenarios into verifiable terminal tasks. It generates structured task schemas, evolves query difficulties, and synthesizes robust Docker environments, with difficulty calibration to focus on challenging instances.
- **Agentic Cowork**: This track covers deep search, office tasks, financial analysis, and slide generation. Tasks are instantiated in real, runnable workspaces, trajectories are distilled from strong teacher models, and acceptance uses artifact-aligned verification signals and multi-axis rubrics.
 - **Deep Search**: Uses a guide-and-rewrite synthesis strategy to control task difficulty and requires answers to be grounded in retrieved evidence.
 - **Knowledge-Worker Office Tasks**: Builds on the GDPval benchmark, using hierarchical synthesis from occupational categories to generate concrete tasks with supporting documents and varying query specificity.
 - **Financial Analysis and Spreadsheet Operations**: Employs evidence-driven task synthesis (executing financial tools and reverse-deriving tasks) and a workbook-walk synthesis (agent performs atomic spreadsheet operations). Acceptance involves deterministic value-level matching and rubric-based judging.
 - **Slide Generation and Editing**: Uses parallel streams for end-to-end deck creation and localized editing instructions. Acceptance combines execution success, agent-judged functional correctness, layout aesthetic checks, and visual scoring.
- **Reasoning-Intensive Tasks**: A scaling-driven approach is used, expanding unique problems (query-side scaling), generating multiple solution paths per query (response-side scaling), and optimizing data mixture ratios (training-side scaling). Rigorous quality assurance is applied at every stage.
- **General-Purpose Conversation and Writing**: This track focuses on broad-coverage conversational data, including writing, Q&A, and multi-turn dialogue, with an emphasis on long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. It includes both tool-augmented and tool-free samples.
- **Role-Play and Persona Coherence**: Treated as a distinct track, formalized as long-horizon conditional generation. Training data is synthesized via large-scale self-play between expert models, with quality control measures like dispersion sampling and Best-of-N filtering.

#### 3.4. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

SFT is applied to instill "interleaved thinking" behavior, where reasoning traces are combined with intermediate actions and observations within a unified trajectory. High-quality interleaved-thinking trajectories are generated through large-scale rejection sampling against domain-specific reward signals and multi-stage data cleaning.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Forge System

The Forge RL system is central to post-training optimization.
- **RL Algorithm**: The LLM is modeled as a policy, and the external context, memory, and agent state transitions are treated as the environment. Training uses Clipped Importance Sampling Policy Optimization (CISPO) with an asymmetrically clipped importance sampling ratio and a stop-gradient operator for stability. Reward design is composite, including dense Process Rewards for behavioral patterns, Task Completion Time Rewards for efficiency, and Reward-to-Go with a trajectory-level baseline to reduce variance.
- **Mixed-Domain RL Training**: Training simultaneously draws data from reasoning, coding, agent, and general domains across multiple stages. Domain mixing ratios, context length, and difficulty distributions are systematically adjusted per stage to prevent catastrophic forgetting and negative transfer.
- **RL Infrastructure (Forge System)**: Organized into three decoupled modules:
 - **Agent Side**: Encapsulates agent implementations and orchestrates environment interactions as a pure trajectory producer.
 - **Middleware Abstraction Layer**: Includes a Gateway Server for standardized communication and a Data Pool for distributed asynchronous trajectory storage.
 - **Training and Inference Side**: Comprises a Rollout Engine for high-throughput generation and a Train Engine for policy gradient computation and weight synchronization.
 - **White-Box and Black-Box Agent Support**: The system supports both transparent (white-box) and opaque (black-box) agent paradigms through a unified Gateway abstraction.
 - **Windowed FIFO Scheduling**: A hybrid scheduling strategy balances throughput and distributional consistency by allowing greedy fetching within a sliding window while enforcing strict ordering across window boundaries.
 - **Prefix Tree Merging**: Restructures training computation for multi-turn trajectories by merging shared prefixes, leading to up to 40x speedup and reduced memory consumption without approximation error.
 - **Inference Acceleration**: Utilizes MTP-based speculative decoding (co-trained with RL policy), heterogeneous prefill-decode disaggregation, and a global L3 KV cache pool for maximizing generation throughput.

#### 3.6. Agentic Mechanism

- **Interleaved Thinking**: This principle dictates that the model alternates between producing natural-language reasoning tokens and action tokens (tool invocations). The full reasoning state from previous turns is preserved as context for subsequent turns, enabling continuous plan revision and self-correction. Ablation studies confirmed its impact on multi-step reasoning tasks.
- **Self-Evolution**: The M2.7 checkpoint demonstrates early self-evolution capabilities via a "Model Iteration System." This system enables M2.7 to autonomously execute tasks, profile runs, diagnose anomalies, debug code, and modify its own agent scaffold. The RL team uses a dual-loop workflow where humans set goals and review decisions, while the agent performs autonomous analysis and iteration. This recursive capability allows the model to improve its own infrastructure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The MiniMax-M2 series, particularly the M2.7 checkpoint, demonstrates competitive performance across a range of agentic coding, agentic cowork, and reasoning & knowledge benchmarks, despite activating significantly fewer parameters per token (~10B) compared to other frontier models.

#### 4.1. Performance Against Frontier Baselines

The M2.7 model achieves performance that approaches or matches leading closed-weight frontier systems such as Claude Opus 4.6, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT 5.4, and Gemini 3.1 Pro.
- **Agentic Coding**: M2.7 scores 56.2 on SWE-bench Pro, 76.5 on SWE-bench Multilingual, and 57.0 on Terminal-Bench 2.0. It achieved the top score among compared models on Multi-SWE-bench at 52.7. On NL2Repo, M2.7 reached 39.8, a 13-point increase from M2.5. For MLE Bench Lite, M2.7 attained a 66.6% medal rate, a 15-point improvement over M2.5 and tying Gemini 3.1 Pro.
- **Application Development**: M2.7 reached 55.6 on VIBE-Pro, demonstrating parity with leading baselines. On the more challenging HyperTask suite, it scored 67.6, an 8-point improvement over M2.5.
- **Cowork — Search and Deep Research**: M2.7 achieved 77.8 on BrowseComp, 75.2 on Wide Search, and 64.3 on the internal RISE benchmark. RISE showed a substantial 14-point gain from M2.5 (50.2).
- **Cowork — Agent, Office, and Workspace**: M2.7 posted 50.0 on GDPval-AA and 46.3 on Toolathlon. For Excel-grounded operations, it scored 63.3 on MEWC v2 and 57.0 on Finance Modeling Pro. On MM Claw, M2.7 reached 62.7. This category showed the largest within-series gains from M2.5 to M2.7, including +15.0 on GDPval-AA, +13.5 on MEWC v2, and +23.2 on Finance Modeling Pro.
- **Reasoning and Knowledge**: M2.7 demonstrated strong performance on reasoning-heavy benchmarks without tools, scoring 94.2 on AIME 2026, 89.8 on GPQA-Diamond, 76.0 on IFBench, and 72.0 on AA-LCR. On broader knowledge benchmarks, it achieved 81.8 on MMLU-Pro, 47.0 on SciCode, and 28.0 on HLE.

#### 4.2. Within-Series Progression

Analysis of the M2 series progression (M2 to M2.5 to M2.7) across eleven benchmarks indicates consistent improvement in every metric. Absolute gains ranged from +11 points (AA-LCR, GPQA-Diamond) to +33.8 points (BrowseComp). The largest gains were observed in domains where new task families were introduced into the data pipelines (deep search, tool use, autonomous ML engineering), aligning with the investment in agentic data. Reasoning benchmarks showed steady progress. This trend suggests that the combined strategy of agentic data, the Forge RL system, and self-evolution contributes to a stable and continuous improvement trajectory.

#### 4.3. Case Study: Self-Evolution on MLE Bench Lite

The MLE Bench Lite evaluation directly supports the claim of self-evolution capabilities. M2.7, acting as an independent ML engineer, showed clear cumulative improvement over 24-hour iterative trials, achieving a 66.6% medal rate and tying Gemini 3.1 Pro. Qualitatively, M2.7 autonomously debugged its own training scaffold, modified configuration files, and iterated through hundreds of rounds, leading to a 30% performance gain on in-house evaluations. This demonstrates the model's ability to drive its own iterative development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MiniMax-M2 series represents a notable advancement in the development of capable and efficient large language models for agentic applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized across several dimensions:

1. **Efficiency and Performance Frontier**: The demonstration that models with "mini activations" (approximately 10 billion activated parameters per token) can achieve competitive performance with frontier systems that activate an order of magnitude more parameters is a significant finding. This addresses critical efficiency and cost bottlenecks associated with deploying LLMs for complex, long-horizon tasks, making high-capability agents more feasible for large-scale, high-availability production environments. This could democratize access to advanced AI capabilities by reducing the computational overhead.

2. **Advancement in Agentic AI Capabilities**: The M2 series significantly pushes the boundaries of LLM agentic capabilities across diverse, real-world domains, including production-grade software engineering, full-stack application development, deep web search, office automation, financial analysis, and complex reasoning. By excelling in benchmarks that require environment-grounded, multi-step reasoning and interaction, this work facilitates the migration of LLMs from conversational tools to autonomous agents capable of performing sophisticated workflows, thereby expanding the practical utility of AI.

3. **Innovative Methodologies for Agent Training**:
 - **Agent-Driven Data Pipelines**: The systematic development of high-fidelity, large-scale agentic data pipelines, complete with executable workspaces and artifact-aligned, verifiable rewards, provides a robust foundation for training highly capable agents. Techniques like Agent-as-a-Verifier (AaaV) and Terminal-Gym offer novel approaches to generating and validating training data, which is crucial for agent reliability and generalization.
 - **Forge RL System**: The introduction of Forge, a scalable, agent-native reinforcement learning system, is a key methodological contribution. Its architectural decoupling, support for white-box and black-box agents, windowed-FIFO scheduling, prefix-tree merging, and inference optimizations represent significant progress in overcoming the inherent challenges of throughput, stability, and flexibility in large-scale agent RL training.
 - **Interleaved Thinking**: The adoption and demonstrated efficacy of interleaved thinking, where reasoning and action are intertwined and the full reasoning state is persistently carried forward, is important for developing agents that can plan, act, and reflect effectively over long horizons, enhancing self-correction and sample efficiency.

4. **Early Steps Towards Autonomous AI Development (Self-Evolution)**: The operationalization of self-evolution in M2.7, enabling the model to autonomously debug training runs, modify its own agent scaffold, and improve its infrastructure, marks a foundational step towards more autonomous AI development. This capability has the potential to significantly reduce human-in-the-loop bottlenecks, accelerate the iterative development cycle of frontier models, and ultimately lead to AI systems that can independently enhance their own performance and infrastructure.

In summary, the MiniMax-M2 series presents a cohesive framework for developing efficient and highly capable agentic LLMs. Its contributions in architectural design, data engineering, RL systems, and self-evolution mechanisms lay a pathway for future AI systems that are not only more intelligent in real-world tasks but also more autonomous in their own development and refinement.
