# SEAgent: Self-Evolving Computer Use Agent with Autonomous Learning from Experience

> [!summary] Summary
> SEAgent introduces a self-evolving framework for Computer Use Agents (CUAs) to autonomously learn and adapt to unfamiliar software environments without human supervision. This framework leverages a novel World State Model for precise step-level rewards and a specialist-to-generalist training strategy, achieving a 34.5% success rate on OS-World benchmarks, a 23.2% absolute gain over a DPO-based baseline.

## Problem

- Current Computer Use Agents (CUAs) heavily rely on costly, human-curated datasets, limiting their adaptability to new or specialized software.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for agents often struggle with sparse and delayed reward signals across multi-step interactions.
- Directly training a single generalist agent for computer use typically leads to suboptimal performance compared to specialized agents.

## Method

- **SEAgent** proposes an ==autonomous exploration framework== with a ==self-evolving curriculum==, enabling CUAs to learn from experience and continuously generate tasks without human oversight.
- A novel ==World State Model (WSM)==, based on a fine-tuned LVLM, processes full-trajectory context to provide high-quality, ==step-level reward signals== for reinforcement learning.
- A ==specialist-to-generalist training strategy== first trains individual CUA specialists, then distills their knowledge into a generalist model, which is further refined via RL across multiple software environments.

## Results

- The fine-tuned World State Model achieved state-of-the-art trajectory judgment, significantly narrowing the gap with commercial models like GPT-4o and improving precision by **+7.5%** over a baseline on AgentRewardBench.
- **SEAgent**'s specialist-to-generalist strategy raised the OS-World success rate from a baseline of **11.3%** to **34.5%** (**+23.2%** absolute), outperforming both an ensemble of individual specialists (**32.2%**) and direct generalist training (**30.6%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the World State Model as the reward source and the superiority of ==Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)== combined with adversarial imitation for improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An LVLM-based World State Model, when fine-tuned on full trajectory context, can generate precise, step-level reward signals, which are crucial for effective reinforcement learning in complex interactive tasks.
> - Autonomous curriculum generation, driven by the WSM's feedback, allows agents to progressively learn and master increasingly complex tasks in unfamiliar software environments without human intervention.
> - A multi-stage specialist-to-generalist training strategy can effectively create a generalist agent that not only generalizes across diverse software but also outperforms the collective performance of individual specialists.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Computer Use Agents (CUAs) has gained significant traction with the rapid advancements in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). Current CUAs leverage LVLMs' perception and reasoning to interpret screen inputs and operate computers via keyboard and mouse. However, the existing paradigm primarily relies on extensive, costly human-curated datasets, often derived from demonstrations or video tutorials. This reliance presents a critical bottleneck when dealing with novel, specialized, or frequently updated software for which human annotations are scarce or non-existent.

SEAgent directly addresses this limitation by shifting the focus from supervised learning on human-labeled data to **autonomous learning from experience**. This aligns with the broader vision in AI research, particularly articulated by figures like David Silver and Richard S. Sutton, who advocate for a new generation of agents capable of acquiring superhuman capabilities predominantly through experience.

Within the landscape of **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs/LVLMs**, previous scalable training efforts primarily used Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), analogous to imitation learning. While effective, SFT is data-hungry. Recent advancements, like DeepSeek-R1, have explored RL with verifiable rewards (e.g., Group Relative Policy Optimization, GRPO). However, in agentic scenarios like computer usage, feedback is often sparse and delayed across multi-step interactions. Prior RL approaches for agents typically fine-tune critic models or use Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from filtered interaction data, often struggling with precision and reward density. SEAgent distinguishes itself by introducing a novel **World State Model** that processes full-trajectory context to provide high-quality, step-level reward signals, a substantial improvement over existing reward models.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the challenge of **generalization across multiple software applications**. Directly training a generalist agent often leads to suboptimal performance compared to specialized agents. SEAgent proposes a **specialist-to-generalist training strategy**, which is a unique contribution that aims to combine the benefits of deep domain expertise with broad applicability, a crucial step towards more versatile and robust AI agents. This strategy resonates with recent discussions on building specialized generalist AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of SEAgent is to **enable Computer Use Agents (CUAs) to autonomously explore unfamiliar software environments and evolve into experts without relying on human supervision or costly human-curated datasets.**

The primary motivations driving this research are:
1. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Novel Software:** Current CUAs struggle with new, specialized, or updated software due as their training heavily depends on human-labeled data, which is unavailable in such scenarios.
2. **Transitioning to Experiential Learning:** The authors argue for a necessary shift towards agents that learn predominantly from their own interactions and experiences, moving beyond the limitations of pre-collected, static datasets.
3. **Enhancing Agent Adaptability and Robustness:** By enabling self-evolution, CUAs can continuously adapt to dynamic software environments, acquire new skills, and become more robust to changes and novel challenges.
4. **Improving Reward Signal Quality:** Existing reward models for CUAs often suffer from low precision and sparse reward signals, hindering effective reinforcement learning. A key motivation is to develop a more accurate, fine-grained, and dense reward mechanism.
5. **Achieving Generalization:** While individual software mastery is valuable, the ultimate goal is to develop agents capable of operating across a multitude of applications. This necessitates a strategy to effectively generalize learned skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEAgent proposes an agentic self-evolving framework built upon three primary components that interact in an iterative, curriculum-driven manner:

#### 3.1. Autonomous Exploration with Self-Evolving Curriculum

This paradigm facilitates continuous learning and task generation. It operates in sequential phases:
- **Task Initiation:** The World State Model (M_state) densely captions the initial GUI of unfamiliar software. The Curriculum Generator (M_task) uses these captions to create an initial set of basic tasks (I₀) and an initial software guidebook (U₀).
- **World State Judgment:** As the Actor Model (π) executes tasks, M_state evaluates each step of the trajectory, providing precise feedback (J_p, indicating correctness, redundancy, or failure step) and detailed GUI state change descriptions (C_p). M_state is a fine-tuned LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B based World State Model) that takes the *entire trajectory* of states and actions as input, classifying actions as correct (a_T) or failure (a_F).
- **Task Self-Evolving:** Based on M_state's feedback (J_p, C_p), the Curriculum Generator (M_task, a powerful LLM like Qwen2.5-72B) updates the software guidebook (U_p+1) and generates a progressively more diverse and challenging set of tasks (I_p+1). This establishes a curriculum learning approach where tasks evolve from simple to complex.
- **Autonomous RL Training:** The Actor Model's policy (π_p) is updated using reinforcement learning, guided by M_state's evaluations and action-specific verifiable functions (R_verifier). The updated policy (π_p+1) is then used in the next phase.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning from Experience

The policy optimization leverages the step-level reward signals from the World State Model:
- **Adversarial Imitation for Failure Action Punishment:** For incorrect actions (a_F) leading to failure or redundant loops, a contrastive log-ratio loss (similar to the negative part of DPO) is used to explicitly penalize and encourage divergence from these behaviors.
- **Verifiable Rewards for Correct Action Encouragement:** For correct actions (a_T), Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is employed, specifically **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. Rewards (r) are defined based on action type similarity and distance-based metrics (e.g., L1 distance for clicks, IoU for drag/select, BLEU for text input). This provides dense, action-specific reward signals.
- **Combined Loss:** The final training loss is a weighted combination of the GRPO loss for positive actions and the Adversarial Imitation loss for negative actions: L(π(θ)) = L_GRPO + γL_AI.

#### 3.3. From Specialist to Generalist Training Strategy

To overcome the challenge of suboptimal direct generalist training, SEAgent adopts a multi-stage approach:
1. **Specialist Training:** Individual CUA specialists are first trained via the SEAgent framework on distinct software environments, allowing each to master a specific application.
2. **Distillation:** Successful trajectories and their reasoning traces from these specialists are collected and used to supervised fine-tune (SFT) a new base model (UI-TARS-7B), creating an initial generalist model.
3. **Generalist Refinement:** This distilled generalist model is then further optimized using the full SEAgent reinforcement learning pipeline across *multiple* software environments. This approach aims to imbue the generalist with better reasoning, planning abilities, and transferable software-specific knowledge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on five professional software applications from OS-World and additional benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of SEAgent:

- **World State Model (WSM) Performance:**
 - The fine-tuned World State Model, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-sourced models for trajectory judgment, significantly narrowing the gap with commercial models like GPT-4o.
 - It showed improved precision (+7.5% over baseline) in evaluating CUA trajectories on AgentRewardBench and OS-World, particularly when conditioned on the *entire process of screenshots* rather than just the last state.
 - Co-training with screenshot change descriptions further enhanced judgment accuracy, demonstrating the importance of fine-grained visual understanding.
 - This reliable, step-level reward signal from WSM is crucial for effective reinforcement learning in the self-evolving system.

- **Overall SEAgent Performance and Self-Evolution:**
 - SEAgent achieved a significant improvement in success rate on OS-World, increasing from a baseline of 11.3% (UI-TARS-7B-DPO) to 34.5%. This represents a 23.2% absolute gain.
 - The Curriculum Generator successfully generated progressively more challenging tasks without human intervention, adapting to the agent's evolving capabilities across different training phases (as illustrated in Figure 4).

- **Specialist vs. Generalist Strategies:**
 - **Specialized RL (SEAgent):** Agents trained as specialists for individual software achieved a combined success rate of 32.2%, outperforming previous specialized RL methods like DigiRL (21.8%) and WebRL (21.8%). This highlights the power of SEAgent's RL framework and WSM.
 - **Direct Generalist RL (SEAgent):** Directly training a generalist agent across all software yielded a success rate of 30.6%, indicating that while functional, it underperforms compared to the ensemble of specialists.
 - **Specialist-to-Generalist Strategy (SEAgent):** This novel strategy achieved the best overall performance with a success rate of 34.5%. It surpassed both the ensemble of individual specialists (32.2%) and direct generalist training (30.6%), validating its efficacy in developing a stronger, transferable generalist CUA.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - The ablation study confirmed the critical role of the World State Model as the reward source, with significant performance gains observed compared to using a base model for rewards.
 - Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) proved superior to simple Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or behavior cloning, suggesting that RFT encourages more diverse and generalized task planning under verifiable rewards.
 - The inclusion of adversarial imitation for penalizing failure actions further contributed to performance gains, emphasizing the importance of learning from mistakes.

- **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Generalization (from Supplementary Material):**
 - When applied to truly novel, out-of-domain scientific software from the ScienceBoard benchmark, SEAgent delivered substantial improvements (e.g., from 0.0% to 10.59% on GrassGIS, 4.85% to 11.52% on Celestia) for UI-TARS-1.5, strongly validating its core claim about self-evolution in unfamiliar environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Significance

SEAgent represents a significant advancement in the field of Computer Use Agents by proposing a novel, self-evolving framework that transcends the limitations of human-labeled data dependency. Its key contributions are:
1. **Autonomous Experiential Learning Paradigm:** It successfully demonstrates how CUAs can autonomously explore novel software, learn through iterative trial-and-error, and progressively master tasks without human supervision. This is a crucial step towards more truly intelligent and adaptable agents.
2. **Robust World State Model:** The development of a fine-tuned LVLM capable of precise, step-level trajectory assessment based on full historical context is a breakthrough in reward signal generation for complex, multi-step agent tasks. This addresses a major challenge in applying RL to real-world environments.
3. **Effective Reinforcement Learning Strategy:** The combination of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for successful actions and Adversarial Imitation for failure actions provides a powerful and balanced learning mechanism for refining agent policies.
4. **Specialist-to-Generalist Training:** The proposed strategy offers a practical and effective pathway to build generalist agents that not only generalize across diverse software but can even surpass the collective performance of specialized agents. This is vital for creating versatile, real-world AI assistants.
5. **Curriculum Learning from Scratch:** The Curriculum Generator's ability to autonomously generate increasingly challenging tasks based on agent performance and an evolving software guidebook creates a scalable and self-improving learning loop.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Reduced Development Costs:** By eliminating the need for extensive human data annotation, SEAgent can drastically reduce the cost and time associated with developing CUAs for new software, making AI agent deployment more agile and widespread.
- **Enhanced Software Accessibility and Productivity:** CUAs powered by SEAgent could significantly improve accessibility for users with disabilities or those less familiar with complex software, automating tasks and providing intelligent guidance. This could boost productivity across professional and personal computing.
- **Broader Applicability of AI Agents:** The ability to adapt to novel software opens doors for CUAs in highly specialized domains (e.g., scientific research, creative design, niche enterprise software) where human experts are currently indispensable for data collection.
- **Inspiration for Embodied AI:** The principles of autonomous exploration, self-evolving curricula, and robust reward modeling in virtual software environments can directly inspire and inform the development of embodied agents operating in physical and real-world settings.
- **Ethical Considerations:** As with any powerful AI, there are potential negative impacts. The autonomous nature of SEAgent could be misused for malicious automation (e.g., spam, unauthorized data extraction) or could inadvertently learn and amplify biases present in software UIs or initial training data. Mitigation strategies involving controlled deployment, behavior filters, and robust ethical safeguards in the World State Model will be crucial.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Reward Signal Reliance:** The system still relies on a "GUI-Judge" (the World State Model) for reliable reward signals, rather than directly learning from sparse environmental rewards in highly complex, open-ended tasks.
- **Task Complexity and Duration:** While tested on relatively complex software, the evaluated tasks are currently relatively simple, often requiring less than 20 human expert steps. Adapting the system to achieve hours-long workflows in more challenging software remains an open direction.

Future work could focus on:
- Learning from truly sparse reward signals in more complex environments.
- Scaling the system to handle more extensive and intricate workflows.
- Exploring hybrid approaches for task generation, combining the strengths of reverse instruction generation with guidebook-based exploration.
- Further enhancing the robustness and generalization capabilities of the World State Model and the agent's policy for even broader applicability.
