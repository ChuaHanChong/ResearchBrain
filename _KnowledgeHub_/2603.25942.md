# Reinforcing Structured Chain-of-Thought for Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Summary-Driven Reinforcement Learning (SDRL) offers a single-stage reinforcement learning framework that improves multimodal large language models' video understanding by introducing a Structured Chain-of-Thought, enhanced with self-supervised mechanisms for factual grounding and reasoning diversity. This approach achieved state-of-the-art results across seven VideoQA benchmarks and produced more concise explanations, overcoming limitations like "thinking drift" and "weak temporal reasoning" without requiring costly supervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) applying Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for video understanding struggle with "thinking drift" (unconstrained, irrelevant intermediate reasoning) and "weak temporal reasoning" (lack of fine-grained temporal awareness).
- Current Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and SFT+Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods demand expensive, manually annotated CoT data and complex multi-stage training pipelines.
- SFT's next-token prediction objective can lead to rigid, token-level imitation of expert demonstrations, which may limit model generalization to new scenarios.

## Method

- Proposed ==Summary-Driven== Reinforcement Learning (**SDRL**), a single-stage Reinforcement Learning framework that extends GRPO, eliminating the need for Supervised Fine-Tuning.
- Implemented a Structured Chain-of-Thought (`Summarize 
 Think 
 Answer`) to guide reasoning, anchoring temporal action order within the "Summarize" phase.
- Incorporated self-supervised Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK) to ensure factual grounding in summaries and Dynamic Variety of Reasoning (DVR) to encourage diverse reasoning paths based on group accuracy.

## Results

- SDRL, an RL-only framework, achieved state-of-the-art performance across seven public VideoQA benchmarks, surpassing both SFT-only and SFT+RL baselines, with a +**9.5%** accuracy gain on VSIBench.
- The approach reduced average output token length, indicating more concise and efficient reasoning, while enhancing overall task accuracy and producing more temporally precise summaries.
- Self-supervised Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK) improved summary quality by +**9.43%** sBERT score and +**3.37%** BLEU score, proving more beneficial than ground-truth supervision for larger models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective Chain-of-Thought in video understanding benefits from a structured approach that explicitly captures key actions and their temporal order, enhancing reasoning fidelity.
> - Self-supervised mechanisms can effectively enforce factual consistency in generated summaries and dynamically encourage diversity in reasoning paths, circumventing the need for expensive human CoT labels.
> - A single-stage, RL-only framework integrating structured reasoning and self-supervision can achieve superior performance and efficiency compared to multi-stage SFT+RL pipelines for video understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in open-ended reasoning for video understanding. This capability is often amplified by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which introduces intermediate reasoning steps to enhance interpretability and logical inference. However, realizing the full potential of CoT typically necessitates high-quality reasoning data for training.

A significant development in this field involves the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL), such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to improve MLLMs' complex reasoning abilities. By optimizing models based on verifiable outcome rewards (e.g., correctness of the final answer), RL offers a scalable training approach without requiring extensive CoT labels. Despite these advantages, outcome-driven RL in complex video tasks exhibits limitations. These include "thinking drift," where intermediate reasoning steps are unconstrained, leading to verbose or visually irrelevant generations, and "weak temporal reasoning," where MLLMs often represent video as aggregated frame embeddings, thereby overlooking fine-grained temporal dependencies crucial for video tasks.

An alternative or complementary strategy involves Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on expert demonstrations, often integrated into a multi-stage SFT+RL pipeline. SFT is employed to instill specific reasoning behaviors. While SFT can impart valuable reasoning, its next-token prediction objective may enforce rigid, token-level imitation, potentially limiting model generalization and inducing bias beyond the training data. Furthermore, most SFT+RL approaches demand costly annotations of CoT and involve time-consuming, multi-stage training processes, which could constrain the base model's intrinsic reasoning potential.

This research addresses a gap concerning the need for efficient, structurally supervised, and temporally grounded training methodologies that bypass the dependence on costly SFT and mitigate the issues of thinking drift and weak temporal comprehension in RL-based video understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work is to overcome the identified limitations within existing MLLM training paradigms for video understanding. Specifically, the authors aimed to address:

- **Thinking Drift and Weak Temporal Comprehension:** MLLMs, even when enhanced by RL techniques like GRPO, often suffer from unconstrained reasoning that leads to irrelevant or unstable intermediate thought processes and a lack of precise temporal awareness in video tasks.
- **Costly and Multi-Stage Training:** Existing SFT+RL methods for CoT generation require expensive manual annotation of reasoning paths and involve complex, multi-stage training pipelines, which are resource-intensive and potentially limit the model's adaptability.
- **Fixed Reasoning Paths and Limited Generalization:** SFT-based approaches can impose rigid, token-level imitations of expert demonstrations, which may constrain the model's ability to generalize to novel scenarios or develop more flexible reasoning strategies.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a Novel Single-Stage RL Framework:** Introduce Summary-Driven Reinforcement Learning (SDRL), a framework designed to enhance MLLMs' video understanding without requiring prior SFT, thereby simplifying the training process.
- **Enhance Temporal and Factual Reasoning Fidelity:** Implement a Structured Chain-of-Thought format (`Summarize → Think → Answer`) to explicitly mandate correct temporal action order in the "Summarize" stage, acting as a structural anchor for subsequent reasoning.
- **Integrate Self-Supervised Mechanisms:** Introduce two complementary self-supervised mechanisms within the GRPO objective:
 - **Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK):** To enforce factual grounding by reducing KL divergence among generated summaries, promoting alignment with factual visual content.
 - **Dynamic Variety of Reasoning (DVR):** To promote exploration and prevent excessive uniformity by dynamically modulating thinking diversity based on group accuracy.
- **Balance Alignment and Exploration:** Design the framework to effectively balance the need for factual alignment in summaries with the encouragement of diverse reasoning paths in the thinking stage, supervising both the final answer and the intermediate reasoning process.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed single-stage, RL-only framework across various public VideoQA datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Summary-Driven Reinforcement Learning (SDRL), is a single-stage RL framework that extends GRPO to incorporate a Structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm. This framework is designed to enable robust video reasoning without the need for supervised instruction tuning (SFT).

**Structured CoT for Top-down Reasoning:**
The authors argue that effective CoT in video understanding requires capturing key actions and their correct temporal order. This is supported by an analysis showing that correct final predictions correlate with higher CoT-to-ground-truth similarity. Based on this, they introduce a `Summarize → Think → Answer` structure.
- **Summary Generation:**
 - If ground-truth (GT) temporal labels are available, summaries are deterministically constructed.
 - In the absence of GT, the model utilizes self-exploration, facilitated by a prepended `<summary>` tag, to extract high-level temporal cues.
- For an input `x = (Video, Question)`, the MLLM `π` generates a group of `G` sampled outputs `O_g = {o_g,t}_T_t=1`. Each output `O_g` is segmented into `Summary (S_g)`, `Thinking (P_g)`, and `Answer (Y_g)` segments.

**Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK):**
CVK is designed to enforce visual fidelity and factual grounding in the summary segment.
- **Group Consistency of Summarization:** The core assumption is that summaries generated from fixed visual content should exhibit strong semantic consistency.
- **Consistency Anchor (ˆS):** This serves as a reference for aligning group members.
- **Similarity/Dissimilarity Metric:**
 - **GT-Supervised Summary Alignment:** When GT summaries (`S_gt`) are available, `ˆS = S_gt`. A composite similarity metric `Sim(S_g, ˆS) = α·sBERT(S_g, S_gt) + β·BLEU(S_g, S_gt)` is used. This similarity score augments the original answer-based reward `R_g` into `R'g = γ_1 R_g + γ_2 Sim(S_g, ˆS)`.
 - **Self-Supervised Summary Alignment:** To avoid costly GT annotations, `ˆS` is dynamically derived. High-quality summaries are identified based on binary correctness of the final answer (`R_C_g`). A position-wise consistency center `s_C_t` is computed by aggregating token representations of these high-quality summaries. Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence `D_t = (1/G) Σ D_KL(s_g,t || s_C,t)` quantifies position-wise inconsistency. This divergence is converted into a `Summary Token Weight (ω_S_t)` (`1 - λ · (D_t - D_min) / (D_max - D_min)`), where lower consistency yields a smaller weight.

**Dynamic Variety of Reasoning (DVR):**
DVR encourages diversity in the `<think>` stage to prevent excessive uniformity in reasoning paths.
- **Diversity Weight (ω_d_g,t):** Directly proportional to the entropy `H_g,t` of the token distribution at each position in the Thinking segment.
- **Dynamic Modulation:** The base diversity weight `ω_d_g,t` is reweighted by `(1 - A)`, where `A` is the group accuracy (fraction of correct answers in the group). This ensures that the diversity incentive is strongest for groups with low overall accuracy and minimized for highly accurate groups, preserving stable reasoning paths. This yields the `Dynamic Diversity Weight (ω'_d_g,t)`.

**Structured Policy Objective:**
The final policy objective `J_total(θ)` maximizes an expected augmented reward, integrating structural consistency and diversity constraints with GRPO-specific policy regularization terms. The structured GRPO objective `J_SCoT_grpo(θ)` maximizes a token-wise weighted advantage. The `Token-Wise Weights W_g,t` modulate policy update strength: `ω_S_t` is applied to the Summary segment, and `ω'_d_g,t` to the Thinking segment.

**Dataset Construction (EventFlowQA):**
To facilitate training for effective video understanding, a new dataset, EventFlowQA, was introduced. This dataset (53K QA pairs) focuses on intricate action sequencing and temporal causality.
- **Pipeline:** Automated three-stage process:
 1. **Source Data Selection:** Utilizes action annotations from YouCook2, COIN, and EgoExo4D.
 2. **Template Design:** Defines 15 temporal logic templates covering action-level (e.g., precedence, duration) and video-level (e.g., action ordering, long-term dependency) reasoning.
 3. **LLM Generation:** An LLM is used to paraphrase templates for linguistic diversity, and then instantiate questions with real action labels, deriving ground-truth answers based on temporal logic. Ground-truth CoT is deterministically synthesized from human annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the SDRL framework.

**Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods (Table 2):**
- SDRL (RL-only) achieved reported state-of-the-art performance across seven public VideoQA benchmarks, surpassing both SFT-only and SFT+RL pipelines.
- On video reasoning benchmarks, SDRL outperformed the SFT+RL baseline (VideoRFT\*) by 4.2% on NExT-GQA, 1.3% on MMVU, and 0.7% on VideoMMMU.
- SDRL demonstrated consistent gains on general video understanding benchmarks, with improvements of 2.8% on MVBench and 0.3% on TempCompass.
- Compared to other single-stage RL methods (e.g., VideoChat-R1, TW-GRPO), SDRL consistently achieved higher accuracy across all metrics.
- The approach yielded accuracy improvements of up to +6.3% on VSIBench and +9.5% on VSIBench (when trained on EventFlowQA) compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, even under different training data settings.
- When trained on EventFlowQA (20% the size of the Video-R1 RL training set), SDRL (Ours†) achieved superior performance on TempCompass, suggesting high data efficiency.

**Analysis of Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK) (Tables 1, 3, 4):**
- **Ground-Truth Supervision vs. Self-Supervision:** For the larger 7B model, self-supervision yielded a greater accuracy gain (+11.91%) compared to GT supervision (+6.19%) on EventFlowQA. This suggested that strict GT alignment with limited human summaries might over-constrain optimization and cause catastrophic forgetting in larger models. Conversely, the smaller 3B model benefited more from GT supervision (+3.01%) than self-supervision (+2.40%), indicating a stronger reliance on explicit guidance for smaller architectures.
- **Metric for Consistency Constraint (GT Supervision):** Combining BLEU and sBERT scores (46.71%) was more effective for enforcing summary consistency than using sBERT alone (46.32%) or BLEU alone (43.85%), indicating complementary benefits of semantic and lexical signals.
- Self-supervised CVK improved summary quality, increasing BLEU score by +3.37% and sBERT score by +9.43% compared to the baseline (Table 4), while also enhancing overall task accuracy.
- Training dynamics showed a monotonic improvement and stabilization of summary quality (measured by (BLEU + sBERT)/2) without evidence of mode collapse or factual degradation.

**Analysis of Dynamic Variety of Reasoning (DVR) (Table 1):**
- **Necessity of Diversity:** Dynamically applying diversity (52.22% with GT, 56.10% with self-supervision) consistently outperformed static diversity application (49.13%, 55.78%). This indicated that indiscriminately enforcing diversity could degrade performance, while dynamic modulation, which focuses exploration on uncertain groups, was more effective.
- **Effective Diversity Metric:** Entropy regularization consistently yielded higher accuracy and more stable performance than KL divergence for promoting diversity in the thinking stage, suggesting better preservation of semantic diversity while stabilizing reasoning.

**Efficiency Analysis (Figure 6):**
SDRL generated a significantly shorter average output token length across six video benchmarks compared to baseline MLLMs (Video-R1 and VideoRFT). This indicates that SDRL promotes conciseness and efficiency by pruning redundant reasoning steps, leading to high-quality and direct explanations.

**Qualitative Visualization (Figure 5, 9-12):**
Qualitative examples demonstrated that SDRL produced more concise, grounded, and temporally precise summaries compared to other RL-based methods like VideoChat-R1. This led to more accurate answers and reduced "thinking drift," where intermediate thoughts contradict the final outcome. SDRL consistently maintained grounding by referencing key visual cues, avoiding detours from option-driven overanalysis observed in baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses several critical challenges in advancing MLLMs for video understanding. By introducing Summary-Driven Reinforcement Learning (SDRL), the authors demonstrate a viable single-stage, RL-only training paradigm that obviates the need for costly Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and its associated limitations.

The key significance lies in:

- **Mitigation of Core Limitations:** SDRL directly addresses issues such as "thinking drift" and "weak temporal comprehension" prevalent in existing RL-based MLLMs by imposing structural supervision on the reasoning process.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** By eliminating the requirement for human-annotated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data and streamlining the training into a single RL stage, SDRL offers a more efficient and scalable solution compared to multi-stage SFT+RL pipelines. This has practical implications for reducing development costs and time.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Fidelity and Interpretability:** The Structured CoT format (`Summarize → Think → Answer`) provides a mechanism for explicit temporal grounding and factual alignment. The self-supervised mechanisms, Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK) and Dynamic Variety of Reasoning (DVR), ensure that both factual accuracy in summaries and diverse exploration in reasoning are balanced, leading to more robust and interpretable decision-making.
- **Data Efficiency:** The development of the EventFlowQA dataset, specifically designed for intricate action sequencing and temporal causality, complements SDRL by providing a targeted benchmark for training and evaluation. The results demonstrating superior performance with a smaller dataset highlight the data efficiency of the approach.

This work is anticipated to pave the way for future research into single-stage, data-efficient, and structurally robust reasoning frameworks for general MLLMs. Its contributions could lead to more reliable and transparent AI systems capable of understanding complex dynamic visual information.
