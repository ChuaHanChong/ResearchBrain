# Reinforcing Structured Chain-of-Thought for Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Summary-Driven Reinforcement Learning (SDRL) offers a single-stage reinforcement learning framework that improves multimodal large language models' video understanding by introducing a Structured Chain-of-Thought, enhanced with self-supervised mechanisms for factual grounding and reasoning diversity. This approach achieved state-of-the-art results across seven VideoQA benchmarks and produced more concise explanations, overcoming limitations like "thinking drift" and "weak temporal reasoning" without requiring costly supervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) applying Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for video understanding struggle with "thinking drift" (unconstrained, irrelevant intermediate reasoning) and "weak temporal reasoning" (lack of fine-grained temporal awareness).
- Current Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and SFT+Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods demand expensive, manually annotated CoT data and complex multi-stage training pipelines.
- SFT's next-token prediction objective can lead to rigid, token-level imitation of expert demonstrations, which may limit model generalization to new scenarios.

## Method

- Proposed ==Summary-Driven== Reinforcement Learning (**SDRL**), a single-stage Reinforcement Learning framework that extends GRPO, eliminating the need for Supervised Fine-Tuning.
- Implemented a Structured Chain-of-Thought (`Summarize 
 Think 
 Answer`) to guide reasoning, anchoring temporal action order within the "Summarize" phase.
- Incorporated self-supervised Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK) to ensure factual grounding in summaries and Dynamic Variety of Reasoning (DVR) to encourage diverse reasoning paths based on group accuracy.

## Results

- SDRL, an RL-only framework, achieved state-of-the-art performance across seven public VideoQA benchmarks, surpassing both SFT-only and SFT+RL baselines, with a +**9.5%** accuracy gain on VSIBench.
- The approach reduced average output token length, indicating more concise and efficient reasoning, while enhancing overall task accuracy and producing more temporally precise summaries.
- Self-supervised Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK) improved summary quality by +**9.43%** sBERT score and +**3.37%** BLEU score, proving more beneficial than ground-truth supervision for larger models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective Chain-of-Thought in video understanding benefits from a structured approach that explicitly captures key actions and their temporal order, enhancing reasoning fidelity.
> - Self-supervised mechanisms can effectively enforce factual consistency in generated summaries and dynamically encourage diversity in reasoning paths, circumventing the need for expensive human CoT labels.
> - A single-stage, RL-only framework integrating structured reasoning and self-supervision can achieve superior performance and efficiency compared to multi-stage SFT+RL pipelines for video understanding.
