# SoftMimic: Learning Compliant Whole-body Control from Examples

> [!summary] Summary
> SoftMimic enables humanoid robots to learn and execute whole-body compliant behaviors, allowing them to adapt interactively to external forces while maintaining balance and motion style. This is achieved through a novel data augmentation strategy that generates compliant motion examples for a reinforcement learning policy, validated via sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Current reinforcement learning-based motion imitation methods produce "stiff" control, leading to brittle, unsafe, and unpredictable robot behaviors during unexpected physical contacts.
- Directly learning compliant behaviors with reinforcement learning is challenging due to exploration difficulties, complex reward shaping, and issues with feasibility constraints.
- The lack of controllable whole-body compliance hinders humanoid robots from safely operating in human-populated or uncertain environments and limits their generalization capabilities.

## Method

- **SoftMimic** introduces a "learning-from-examples" strategy, combining offline ==Compliant Motion Augmentation (CMA)== with reinforcement learning.
- CMA generates a large dataset of kinematically feasible and stylistically consistent compliant trajectories using an Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver that produces spring-like deviations from reference motions.
- A reinforcement learning policy is then trained to imitate these augmented compliant trajectories, inferring external forces and displacements solely from proprioceptive feedback and a commanded stiffness.

## Results

- SoftMimic policies demonstrated improved task generalization, successfully picking up objects of varying sizes (20cm to 50cm) and handling misalignments, unlike stiff baselines.
- The approach significantly enhanced safety by reducing maximum contact forces by nearly half in disturbance handling scenarios, and accurately controlled interaction forces based on commanded stiffness (40 N/m to 1000 N/m).
- The benefits, including compliance, generalization, and disturbance handling, were successfully transferred from simulation to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating a dataset of pre-authored compliant motions via IK-based data augmentation simplifies the reinforcement learning task, making it more tractable than direct RL exploration.
> - The IK solver's hierarchical optimization allows for fine-grained control over the whole-body styling of compliant responses, enabling different coordination strategies.
> - By training on augmented trajectories, the RL policy can maintain balance and overall motion style while responding to external forces, enabling controllable departure from the original reference motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of humanoid robotics, motion imitation learning, and compliant control, addressing a critical limitation in current state-of-the-art humanoid control strategies.

**Current Landscape and Limitations:**
Recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) combined with high-fidelity physics simulations and robust legged hardware have enabled impressive progress in humanoid robot motion imitation. Methods like DeepMimic have successfully trained policies for tracking complex human motions, leading to dynamic and visually appealing robot behaviors. However, these methods typically incentivize "stiff" control. In a stiff controller, any deviation from the reference motion is aggressively corrected, treating unexpected physical contacts (e.g., brushing a table, misjudging object location, interacting with a person) as errors. This leads to brittle, unsafe, and unpredictable behavior, where the robot can exert large, uncontrolled forces, risking damage to itself, the environment, or humans. This lack of compliance is a fundamental barrier to deploying humanoids safely alongside people and generalizing skills to varied, unperceived environments.

**Traditional Analytical Approaches:**
For decades, analytical approaches to compliant control, such as hybrid position/force control and task-space impedance/admittance control, have been developed, particularly for fixed-base robot arms. These methods aim to prescribe desired motion-wrench relationships, often ensuring passivity for safe interaction. Extending these to high-Degree-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoid robots with floating bases and intermittent contacts is significantly more complex, requiring sophisticated models and formulations like operational-space control. While these analytical methods have demonstrated precise force control and back-drivability on highly engineered hardware (e.g., DLR's TORO humanoid), they are often model-dependent and can be challenging to scale to the complexity of whole-body human-like motions or to environments with significant uncertainty.

**Recent Data-Driven Compliant Control:**
More recently, RL has been explored for directly learning compliant behaviors. Initial work demonstrated success in simulation but often relied on perfect state information. Other efforts have shown that end-to-end policies can learn to apply accurate task-space forces on legged manipulators or mimic specific dynamic models (e.g., spring-mass-damper templates). However, these approaches often focus on satisfying simpler locomotion tasks while controlling force interaction, rather than reconciling wide-range impedance control with high-fidelity *whole-body motion mimicry* on real hardware. A unified framework for achieving both, capable of learning from human demonstrations, remained an open challenge.

**SoftMimic's Contribution and Niche:**
SoftMimic bridges this critical gap by combining the strengths of data-driven learning with insights from analytical compliant control. It moves beyond purely stiff motion tracking to enable user-specified, controllable compliance across the robot's whole body. The key innovation lies in its "learning-from-examples" strategy, where compliant behaviors are *pre-authored* via an Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver and then used to augment the training data for an RL policy. This allows the policy to learn a wide range of compliant responses without suffering from the exploration challenges, reward conflicts, or feasibility issues inherent in direct RL optimization for compliant behavior. By demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 humanoid, SoftMimic provides a practical and scalable solution for creating humanoids that are both agile and safely interactive, paving the way for more robust and versatile real-world deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SoftMimic is to enable humanoid robots to safely and effectively perform a vast range of tasks in dynamic, unstructured, and human-populated environments by introducing **compliant whole-body control**.

The core motivations stem from the inherent limitations of existing motion imitation approaches:
- **Brittle and Unsafe Behavior:** Current RL-based motion imitation policies, while generating impressive dynamic motions, are inherently stiff. They treat any deviation from a reference motion as an error, leading to aggressive corrections and the generation of large, uncontrolled forces when unexpected contacts or disturbances occur. This makes them brittle, prone to failure, and potentially dangerous, particularly in real-world scenarios involving human interaction or unforeseen environmental variations.
- **Lack of Generalization:** Stiff controllers struggle to generalize from a single, precisely defined reference motion to variations in a task (e.g., picking up objects of slightly different sizes or in slightly different locations). The inability to adapt to minor discrepancies limits their real-world utility.
- **Barrier to Human-Robot Co-existence:** The aggressive nature of stiff controllers prevents safe and gentle physical interaction with humans, relegating humanoids to isolated operational environments. Achieving compliance is crucial for humanoids to operate alongside people.
- **Challenges of Direct Compliant Learning:** Directly learning compliant behaviors using standard reinforcement learning poses significant hurdles:
 - **Exploration Difficulty:** A purely stiff tracking policy often represents a strong local optimum, making it difficult for RL agents to discover nuanced compliant responses.
 - **Complex Reward Shaping:** Designing reward functions that effectively balance interaction forces with whole-body style and stability for a high-DoF humanoid is non-trivial, as these objectives can conflict.
 - **Feasibility Constraints:** Many desired compliant responses may be kinematically or dynamically infeasible depending on the robot's configuration, leading to training for impossible tasks and hindering learning.
 - **Incompatibility with Training Techniques:** Common RL training accelerants like early termination and reference state initialization are difficult to apply when the robot is intentionally deviating from a reference motion to comply with external forces.

To overcome these challenges, SoftMimic proposes to not merely minimize tracking error, but to **controllably depart from the reference motion in response to external forces, according to a user-specified stiffness**. The key is to achieve this while maintaining balance, posture, and the overall style of the motion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoftMimic addresses the challenges of learning compliant whole-body control through a novel **learning-from-examples strategy** that combines offline data augmentation with reinforcement learning.

**Core Approach: Compliant Motion Augmentation (CMA) + Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
Instead of directly training an RL policy to discover compliant behaviors, SoftMimic first *generates* a dataset of desired compliant motions. An RL policy is then trained to imitate these generated behaviors.

**A. Compliant Motion Tracking (CMT) Formulation:**
The desired compliant behavior is mathematically defined: for an interacting link, its desired pose is a spring-like deviation from its original reference pose (q_ref), proportional to the external wrench (force and torque) and inversely proportional to a user-commanded stiffness (K_cmd). This means the link behaves like a virtual spring around the reference.
An instantaneous Inverse Kinematics (IK) objective then finds a whole-body configuration (q̂) that achieves this desired compliant link pose while remaining as close as possible to the original reference motion (q_ref) for the rest of the body. The "closeness" metric (d) is crucial and defined using a mixture of keypoint error, joint position error, foot placement consistency, and center-of-pressure maintenance.

**B. SoftMimic: Reinforcement Learning for CMT:**
The compliant whole-body control problem is framed as an RL task:
- **Observation Space:** The policy observes the robot's proprioceptive state (joint positions, velocities, base state), a history of past observations, the original (non-compliant) reference posture (q_ref) including future horizon, and the *commanded stiffness* (K_cmd). Notably, the policy *does not directly observe external forces or displacements*, forcing it to infer these from its proprioceptive sensing.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs joint-space position targets for a low-level PD controller. This allows for torque control by modulating position errors.
- **Reward Function:** The agent is rewarded for two main objectives:
 1. **Reference Motion Tracking:** A DeepMimic-style reward, but critically, it tracks the *augmented compliant trajectory* (q_aug) for the interacting parts, not the original q_ref.
 2. **Spring-like Compliance:** Rewards based on matching the desired spring-like force and torque relationships, as well as position and rotation tracking for the interacting link, all relative to the desired compliant pose.
- **Command Sampling and Force Field Dynamics:** During training episodes, a motion clip, a desired robot stiffness, and an external force profile are sampled. External forces are simulated as a 'force field' pulling a selected robot link towards a moving setpoint, with a force proportional to distance and a randomized environment stiffness (K_env).
 - **Stiffness Bounds:** Feasible stiffness ranges (40 N/m to 1000 N/m for translational, 0.1 to 10 Nm/rad for rotational) are estimated based on robot sensing noise and control accuracy, guiding the sampling range.
 - **Log-Uniform Sampling:** Both robot and environment stiffnesses are sampled from a log-uniform distribution to ensure balanced exploration of both highly compliant and very stiff regimes.
 - **Velocity-based Event Sampling:** Force events are sampled with a probability proportional to the interacting link's velocity, simulating more realistic, dynamic collisions.
- **Early Termination and Reference State Initialization:** By using the *augmented compliant posture (q_aug)* as the reference for episode termination and initialization, the framework overcomes the problem of compliant deviations prematurely ending episodes or creating inconsistent starting states, which often destabilizes RL training.

**C. Compliant Motion Augmentation (CMA) Details:**
This offline data generation stage is critical for providing the RL policy with a "teacher" for compliant behaviors. It uses a differential IK solver (Mink with DAQP) to create a large dataset (D_aug) of feasible and stylistically consistent compliant trajectories.
- **Task Hierarchy for IK Solver:** The IK solver optimizes a weighted sum of objectives with varying priorities:
 1. **Compliant Interaction (High Priority):** Achieve the desired spring-like pose (p_i,des, R_i,des) for the perturbed link.
 2. **Foot Placement (High Priority):** Maintain consistency of stance feet with the reference contact schedule.
 3. **CoM Stabilization (Medium Priority):** Ensure Center of Mass (CoM) stability and moment compensation, allowing necessary body shifts.
 4. **Keypoint Posture (Low Priority):** Preserve the original motion's style by tracking key links (torso, elbows).
 5. **Joint Posture (Very Low Priority):** Regularization to resolve kinematic redundancies by staying close to q_ref.
- **Feasibility Validation and Rejection Sampling:** If the IK solver fails to find a solution or if the solution violates hard feasibility constraints (e.g., maximum link tracking error, max stance foot displacement, max CoM tracking error, force/torque limits), the event's magnitude is iteratively scaled down. If it still fails below a minimum threshold, the event is rejected entirely. This ensures that D_aug contains only kinematically achievable and well-behaved compliant motions, simplifying the subsequent RL learning task.

**D. Motion Data, Training Details, Baselines:**
- **Motion Data:** Uses AMASS and LAFAN1 datasets for various motion clips (standing, walking, box-pick, dance).
- **Training:** PPO algorithm with standard hyperparameters. Offline data generation is highly efficient, producing 40 minutes of augmented data from a 1-minute clip in about 1 minute wall-clock time.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Stiff Baseline:** A high-performance motion imitation controller rewarded *only* for rigid tracking of q_ref, but exposed to the same external force perturbations during training. This evaluates the emergent behavior of standard tracking.
 - **no-aug Ablation:** Trained with the same spring-like compliance reward as SoftMimic, but *without* the augmented dataset D_aug. It uses q_ref for its tracking reward, initializations, and termination conditions (modified to allow compliance without immediate penalty). This tests the efficacy of reward shaping alone for compliance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SoftMimic demonstrates significant improvements in safety, generalization, and controllability for humanoid robots, validated through both simulation and real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid.

- **Compliance Improves Task Generalization:**
 - A single SoftMimic policy, trained on a reference motion for picking a 20cm box, successfully generalized to pick up boxes of varying widths (from 20cm to 50cm) in simulation and on real hardware (Fig. 6). The compliant policy maintained a consistent, gentle squeezing force, adapting to the object size, whereas the stiff baseline generated large, unpredictable force spikes that could damage the box or robot. This robustness also extended to handling misaligned boxes without prior knowledge.

- **Compliance Improves Disturbance Handling and Safety:**
 - In challenging scenarios involving unexpected contact (e.g., T-pose by a wall, walking past an obstacle, misjudging a box's location), the compliant policy operating at low stiffness significantly reduced the maximum contact forces exerted on the environment (by nearly half compared to the stiff baseline). This directly translates to enhanced safety for the robot and its surroundings (Fig. 4).
 - The commanded stiffness directly modulated the interaction forces. A low stiffness resulted in gentle interactions and significant deviation from the reference motion, while a high stiffness led to strong resistance and potentially destructive forces (e.g., toppling a tower of blocks, Fig. 5).

- **Sim-to-Real Validation:**
 - The benefits of SoftMimic, including generalization, disturbance handling, and safety, successfully transferred from simulation to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating its practical applicability (Fig. 1).

- **Stiffness Adherence and Accuracy:**
 - The SoftMimic policy accurately tracked the commanded translational stiffness across a wide log-uniform range (40 N/m to 1000 N/m), exhibiting predictable force-displacement relationships (Fig. 3). The stiff baseline, in contrast, maintained an approximately constant effective stiffness of around 500 N/m, regardless of external forces.
 - Policies trained with Compliant Motion Augmentation (CMA) achieved significantly lower position and force errors in compliant interactions, particularly at low stiffness, compared to the `no-aug` ablation (Fig. 8). CMA resulted in a 50% reduction in displacement error at low stiffness, highlighting the crucial role of the augmented dataset.

- **Data Shaping Controls Behavior and Style:**
 - A key finding is that the offline IK-based data augmentation allows for fine-grained control over the *style* of compliant response. By adjusting cost terms within the IK solver (e.g., adding a pelvis orientation cost), different whole-body coordination strategies (e.g., squatting versus bending) could be pre-defined and were successfully reproduced by the learned policies (Fig. 7). The `no-aug` ablation, relying solely on reward shaping, produced unpredictable emergent postures, underscoring the value of explicit data-driven styling.

- **Preservation of Motion Quality:**
 - Despite learning a rich repertoire of compliant behaviors, SoftMimic maintained competitive motion tracking accuracy in unperturbed free-space conditions, comparable to the state-of-the-art stiff baseline (Table I). While there was a minor, expected increase in tracking error (e.g., 5.04° joint error vs. 2.04° for box pick), this is considered an acceptable trade-off for the significantly enhanced versatility and safety. Training SoftMimic to convergence generally required modestly more steps than stiff motion tracking (Fig. 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SoftMimic framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, offering solutions to several long-standing challenges and paving the way for more versatile, safe, and robust robot deployments.

- **Enhanced Safety and Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling predictable and controllable compliance, SoftMimic dramatically reduces the risk of damage during unexpected contacts. This is crucial for the safe deployment of humanoids in shared spaces with humans, moving closer to the vision of assistive and collaborative robots. The ability to command different stiffness levels allows the robot to adapt its interaction behavior from gentle cooperation to firm resistance.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The method significantly enhances a robot's ability to generalize skills from limited examples to varied environmental conditions and task parameters (e.g., different object sizes, misalignments, disturbances). This reduces the need for extensive, task-specific training data or complex, error-prone perception modules, making robot learning more efficient and applicable in diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Novel Methodology for Learning Complex Behaviors:** The Compliant Motion Augmentation (CMA) strategy is a key methodological contribution. It transforms the difficult problem of direct RL exploration of compliant behaviors into a more tractable imitation learning problem. This approach allows researchers to precisely "author" desirable whole-body compliant styles and filter out infeasible behaviors offline, greatly simplifying and stabilizing the subsequent RL training.
- **Bridging the Gap Between Imitation and Interaction:** SoftMimic effectively merges high-fidelity, dynamic motion imitation with sophisticated physical interaction capabilities. This is a crucial step for humanoids, which need to both perform complex human-like actions and interact safely and effectively with their environment.
- **Practical Applicability:** Validation on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot confirms the method's effectiveness in transferring learned behaviors from simulation to hardware, increasing its immediate relevance for practical robotic applications.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens up several exciting avenues for future research:
 - **Dynamic Stiffness Selection:** Exploring how to dynamically adjust stiffness based on task context (e.g., high stiffness for lifting a heavy box, low stiffness for handing an object to a person).
 - **Foundational Controllers:** Developing compliant controllers that can track large-scale motion datasets or real-time teleoperation inputs.
 - **Advanced Data Augmentation:** Incorporating dynamics into the IK solver for more physically plausible compliant motions, addressing workspace coverage gaps, and generating augmented data for simultaneous multi-link interactions.
 - **Whole-Body Compliance:** Extending compliant behavior to wrenches on any link of the robot's body for fine-grained stiffness control across the entire surface.

In essence, SoftMimic is a foundational step towards building truly adaptable, safe, and intelligent humanoid robots capable of operating effectively and robustly in the complex, contact-rich environments of the real world.
