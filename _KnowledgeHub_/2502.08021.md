# Model Selection for Off-policy Evaluation: New Algorithms and Experimental Protocol

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents hyperparameter-free algorithms for model selection in Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) within offline Reinforcement Learning. It introduces LSTD-Tournament, a model-free selector that achieves an improved statistical convergence rate of n^-1/2, alongside a new experimental protocol for robust evaluation, which demonstrates LSTD-Tournament's strong performance over newly developed model-based selectors.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning pipelines require model selection for candidate policies, which relies on accurate Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE).
- Many effective OPE algorithms (e.g., FQE) introduce their own hyperparameters, creating a 'chicken-and-egg problem' where OPE algorithms themselves require tuning.
- Existing OPE methods suffer from limitations like high variance (Importance Sampling) or introduce new hyperparameter tuning challenges (model-based methods), hindering robust model selection.

## Method

- Developed **LSTD-Tournament**, a novel ==hyperparameter-free== model-free selector, by leveraging LSTDQ principles and designing a specific feature map to address the 'double-sampling problem'.
- Proposed new model-based selectors, including a regression-based method that automatically constructs helper function classes from candidate models and a novel 'sign-flip' selector.
- Introduced a new experimental protocol using 'lazy evaluation' via Monte-Carlo rollouts and Q-caching to stably and controllably evaluate OPE selectors, avoiding the instabilities of prior FQE-based methods.

## Results

- LSTD-Tournament consistently achieved lower OPE error and greater stability compared to various model-free baselines and the newly developed model-based selectors.
- A naive model-based approach, minimizing ℓ2 model prediction loss, consistently exhibited poor performance, particularly in stochastic environments with varying noise levels.
- Experiments demonstrated that increasing data coverage generally improved the performance of most OPE selectors, while greater misspecification led to higher OPE errors for all methods without significant differentiation between them.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LSTD-Tournament provides a theoretical advancement with an improved statistical rate of n^-1/2 for OPE error, offering more accurate selection compared to previous methods with n^-1/4 rates.
> - Empirical evaluation revealed that the model-free LSTD-Tournament generally outperformed more complex, theoretically grounded model-based selectors, suggesting that indirect use of model information through Q-functions can be more effective in certain contexts.
> - The new experimental protocol enabled systematic studies, demonstrating that OPE model selection is most challenging when candidate models exhibit an intermediate level of difference (a 'down-U curve') from the true environment.
