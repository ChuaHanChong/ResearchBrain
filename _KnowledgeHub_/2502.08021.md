# Model Selection for Off-policy Evaluation: New Algorithms and Experimental Protocol

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents hyperparameter-free algorithms for model selection in Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) within offline Reinforcement Learning. It introduces LSTD-Tournament, a model-free selector that achieves an improved statistical convergence rate of n^-1/2, alongside a new experimental protocol for robust evaluation, which demonstrates LSTD-Tournament's strong performance over newly developed model-based selectors.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning pipelines require model selection for candidate policies, which relies on accurate Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE).
- Many effective OPE algorithms (e.g., FQE) introduce their own hyperparameters, creating a 'chicken-and-egg problem' where OPE algorithms themselves require tuning.
- Existing OPE methods suffer from limitations like high variance (Importance Sampling) or introduce new hyperparameter tuning challenges (model-based methods), hindering robust model selection.

## Method

- Developed **LSTD-Tournament**, a novel ==hyperparameter-free== model-free selector, by leveraging LSTDQ principles and designing a specific feature map to address the 'double-sampling problem'.
- Proposed new model-based selectors, including a regression-based method that automatically constructs helper function classes from candidate models and a novel 'sign-flip' selector.
- Introduced a new experimental protocol using 'lazy evaluation' via Monte-Carlo rollouts and Q-caching to stably and controllably evaluate OPE selectors, avoiding the instabilities of prior FQE-based methods.

## Results

- LSTD-Tournament consistently achieved lower OPE error and greater stability compared to various model-free baselines and the newly developed model-based selectors.
- A naive model-based approach, minimizing ℓ2 model prediction loss, consistently exhibited poor performance, particularly in stochastic environments with varying noise levels.
- Experiments demonstrated that increasing data coverage generally improved the performance of most OPE selectors, while greater misspecification led to higher OPE errors for all methods without significant differentiation between them.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LSTD-Tournament provides a theoretical advancement with an improved statistical rate of n^-1/2 for OPE error, offering more accurate selection compared to previous methods with n^-1/4 rates.
> - Empirical evaluation revealed that the model-free LSTD-Tournament generally outperformed more complex, theoretically grounded model-based selectors, suggesting that indirect use of model information through Q-functions can be more effective in certain contexts.
> - The new experimental protocol enabled systematic studies, demonstrating that OPE model selection is most challenging when candidate models exhibit an intermediate level of difference (a 'down-U curve') from the true environment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) focuses on learning policies from pre-collected data without further interaction with the environment. This paradigm is relevant for applications where online interaction is costly or dangerous. A critical challenge in offline RL, as in any machine learning pipeline, is the need for holdout validation and model selection. After multiple training algorithms or hyperparameter settings produce candidate policies, a primary task is to select the most performant policy among them.

Estimating the performance of a new target policy using data collected by a different behavior policy is known as Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE). Existing OPE methods generally fall into two categories:
1. **Importance Sampling (IS)-based methods**: These offer unbiasedness guarantees but suffer from variance that can grow exponentially with the horizon, limiting their practical applicability beyond short-horizon scenarios like contextual bandits.
2. **Model-based or Value-function-based methods (e.g., Fitted-Q Evaluation - FQE)**: These approaches aim to mitigate the exponential variance issue but often introduce their own set of hyperparameters (e.g., neural architecture, learning rates, regularization). This creates a "chicken-and-egg" problem: if OPE algorithms are used to tune the hyperparameters of policy training, then who tunes the hyperparameters of the OPE algorithms themselves?

This paper addresses this less explored "chicken-and-egg problem" by focusing on **model selection for OPE algorithms themselves**. Specifically, it investigates methods to select between candidate value functions (in a model-free setting) or candidate dynamics models (in a model-based setting) to accurately evaluate a target policy. The work aims to provide selection algorithms that are themselves hyperparameter-free, building on and extending prior work, such as BVFT, which offered a framework for converting certain OPE methods into selectors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work is to provide robust and practical solutions for model selection in Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) within offline Reinforcement Learning (RL). The paper identifies a significant gap in the current research landscape: while OPE is widely used to select between candidate policies, the problem of selecting among different OPE instances (e.g., OPE algorithms with varying hyperparameters or different underlying models) remains largely under-investigated. The core objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the "Chicken-and-Egg Problem" of OPE Hyperparameter Tuning:** Many effective OPE algorithms, such as FQE and model-based methods, introduce their own hyperparameters. If the goal of OPE is to aid in selecting policies by estimating their values, and this process itself requires tuning, an infinite regress of tuning problems emerges. The paper explicitly aims to develop selection algorithms that are hyperparameter-free to circumvent this issue.

2. **Develop Novel Model-Free Selectors with Theoretical Guarantees:**
 - **Challenge:** Directly estimating the Bellman error (a natural metric for evaluating Q-functions) is complicated by the "double-sampling problem," which makes naive squared TD error estimates biased in stochastic environments. Existing solutions often introduce additional function approximation or helper classes, which in turn require their own hyperparameters.
 - **Objective:** Propose a new model-free selector, named LSTD-Tournament, that can select among candidate value functions. This selector aims to build upon the principles of BVFT, which handles the double-sampling problem without additional function approximation, but with improved statistical properties.
 - **Motivation:** Achieve better statistical rates (n^-1/2 compared to n^-1/4 of prior work) and provide a simpler, more straightforward implementation. The underlying idea is to leverage the properties of LSTDQ (Least-Squares Temporal Difference Q-learning) as a base algorithm that satisfies the required criteria for conversion into a selector.

3. **Develop Novel Model-Based Selectors with Theoretical Guarantees:**
 - **Context:** In scenarios where structural knowledge of system dynamics allows for building candidate simulators, the task becomes selecting the best model from a set to evaluate a target policy.
 - **Challenge:** Naive model prediction losses (e.g., ℓ2 loss on next-state prediction) often exhibit biases in stochastic environments and depend on the choice of distance metric.
 - **Objective:** Introduce new model-based selectors that address these limitations. This includes adapting existing regression-based ideas to automatically construct helper function classes from the candidate models and proposing a new "sign-flip" selector.
 - **Motivation:** Explore whether leveraging explicit model information (beyond just induced Q-functions) can lead to improved performance compared to model-free approaches in the model-based setting.

4. **Propose a New, Controlled Experimental Protocol for Empirical Evaluation:**
 - **Critique of Existing Protocols:** Prior methods for evaluating OPE selectors often rely on FQE to generate candidate Q-functions. This approach can suffer from unstable training, lack of precise control over the quality and type of candidate functions, and difficulty in systematically evaluating model-based methods.
 - **Objective:** Develop an experimental protocol that allows for stable generation and better control of candidate value functions and dynamics models.
 - **Motivation:** Facilitate robust and systematic empirical evaluation of both model-free and model-based selectors, enabling detailed studies into factors like misspecification, data coverage, and the "gap" between candidate models. The protocol uses "lazy evaluation" and Monte-Carlo rollouts to compute Q-values without complex optimization, alongside computational efficiency measures like Q-caching and bootstrapping.

In summary, the paper is motivated by the need for principled, hyperparameter-free model selection within OPE, aiming to provide theoretically sound and empirically validated algorithms along with a robust experimental framework for their assessment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper centers on defining and solving the model selection problem for Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE), developing novel algorithms for both model-free and model-based settings, and establishing a new experimental protocol for their rigorous evaluation.

#### 3.1. Model Selection Problem Formulation

The paper considers a scenario where multiple OPE instances (e.g., algorithms with different hyperparameters) produce candidate value functions or dynamics models. The goal is to select the best one based on an offline dataset `D` sampled from a behavior policy `π_b`.

- **Model-free setting:** Given a set of candidate Q-functions `Q = {Q_i}_{i∈[m]}`, the task is to select `Q̂` such that `E_{s∼d_0}[Q̂(s, π)] ≈ J(π)`. For theoretical analysis, the paper assumes realizability (`Q_π ∈ Q`), but empirically tests misspecification (`Q_π ∉ Q`).
- **Model-based setting:** Given a set of candidate MDPs `M = {M_i}_{i∈[m]}` (differing from `M*` primarily in transition dynamics `P_i`), the task is to select `M̂` such that `J_M̂(π) ≈ J(π)`. Realizability (`M* ∈ M`) is assumed for derivations. Model-free methods can be applied here by converting each `M_i` into its corresponding `Q_{π,Mi}`.

#### 3.2. New Model-Free Selector: LSTD-Tournament

The approach to model-free selection addresses the "double-sampling problem," which complicates direct estimation of the Bellman error, `E_μ[(Q_i - T_π Q_i)^2]`. A naive empirical squared TD error `E_μ[(Q_i(s,a) - r - γQ_i(s',π))^2]` is a biased estimate in stochastic environments.

The proposed **LSTD-Tournament** builds on the principles of BVFT, which provides a general recipe for converting special "base" OPE methods into selectors. The key insight is to use LSTDQ as the base algorithm.

1. **LSTDQ Review:** LSTDQ learns `Q_π` assuming it is linearly realizable by a feature map `ϕ: S × A → ℝ^d` (i.e., `Q_π(s,a) = ϕ(s,a)^T θ*`). It estimates the linear coefficient `θ*` by solving `Aθ* = b`, where `A = E_μ[ϕ(s,a)ϕ(s,a)^T - γϕ(s,a)ϕ(s',π)^T]` and `b = E_μ[ϕ(s,a)r]`. The algorithm estimates `A` and `b` empirically (`Â`, `b̂`) and computes `θ̂ = Â^-1 b̂`.
2. **LSTD-Tournament Feature Design:** For selecting between two candidates `{Q_i, Q_j}`, the method constructs a 2-dimensional feature map: `ϕ_{i,j}(s,a):= [Q_i(s,a), Q_j(s,a)]^T`. This design ensures that `Q_π` is linearly realizable within this feature space, with `θ*` being either `^T` (if `Q_π = Q_i`) or `^T` (if `Q_π = Q_j`).
3. **Loss Function and Tournament:** The "loss" for a candidate `Q_i` (representing `θ =^T` in `ϕ_{i,j}`) is derived as `∥A_{i,j}^T - b_{i,j}∥_∞`, which simplifies to `max_{k∈{i,j}} |E_μ[Q_k(s,a)(Q_i(s,a) - r - γQ_i(s',π))]|`. To extend to `m` candidates, a tournament procedure is adopted: the loss for `Q_i` is defined as `L(Q_i; {Q_j}_{j∈[m]}, π):= max_{k∈[m]} |E_μ[Q_k(s,a)(Q_i(s,a) - r - γQ_i(s',π))]|`. The algorithm empirically estimates this loss and selects the `Q_i` that minimizes it.
4. **Theoretical Guarantees:** Theorem 2 provides a statistical guarantee for LSTD-Tournament, showing an `n^-1/2` rate of convergence for the error `|J(π) - E_{s∼d_0}[Q̂_i(s,π)]|`, a improvement over BVFT's `n^-1/4`.
5. **Variants:** The paper mentions variants for feature design, such as `ϕ_{i,j} = [Q_i/c_i, (Q_j - Q_i)/c_{j,i}]`, which can offer improved numerical stability.

#### 3.3. New Model-Based Selectors

For the model-based setting, the paper develops methods that leverage explicit access to candidate models `M_i`, aiming to outperform model-free methods by utilizing this additional information.

1. **Naïve Baseline:** A simple approach is to minimize the model prediction loss `E_{(s,a,s')∼μ, s̃∼P(·|s,a)}[d(s', s̃)]`. The paper notes its bias towards deterministic systems and dependence on the distance metric `d(·,·)`.
2. **Regression-based Selector (based on Antos et al. and Zitovsky et al.):** This method addresses the double-sampling issue by using regression to estimate `T_π Q_i`. The key idea is that `T_π f` is the minimizer of `E_μ[(g(s,a) - r - γf(s',π))^2]` over all functions `g`.
 - **Innovation:** In the model-based setting, the set of regression functions `G_i` for estimating `T_π Q_i` can be automatically constructed as `{T_{π,M_j} Q_i: j ∈ [m]}`. If `M* ∈ {M_i}`, then `T_π Q_i ∈ G_i`, ensuring realizability for the regression problem.
 - **Procedure:** `bg_i` is determined by empirically minimizing the regression loss over `G_i`. The loss for `Q_i` (derived from `M_i`) is then `E_D[(bg_i(s,a) - Q_i(s,a))^2]`.
 - **Theoretical Guarantees:** Theorem 3 provides a bound on the OPE error for this selector, incorporating a concentrability coefficient.
3. **Sign-flip Selector:** A novel selector that utilizes the `G_i` class but replaces the squared loss with an absolute value variant: `max_{g∈G_i} E_μ[sgn(Q_i - g)(Q_i(s,a) - r - γQ_i(s',π))]`. Theorem 4 provides its theoretical guarantee.

#### 3.4. New Experimental Protocol

The paper proposes a novel experimental protocol to empirically evaluate selectors, aiming for stability and control that previous FQE-based methods lacked.

1. **Experiment Unit Definition:** Each unit specifies:
 - A groundtruth model `M*`.
 - A list of candidate models `M = {M_i}_{i∈[m]}` (e.g., by varying environment parameters like gravity or noise).
 - A behavior policy `π_b` and offline sample size `n`.
 - A set of target policies `Π = {π_1..., π_l}`.
2. **Data Generation:** An offline dataset `D` of size `n` is sampled from `M*` using `π_b`.
3. **Lazy Evaluation of Q-values via Monte Carlo:** This is a core innovation. Instead of training complex RL algorithms (like FQE) to produce `Q_π,Mi`, the protocol directly computes the specific `Q_π,Mi(s,a)` and `Q_π,Mi(s',π)` values needed by the selectors for each `(s,a,r,s')` tuple in `D`. This is done by averaging multiple Monte-Carlo rollouts in `M_i` starting from the relevant `(s,a)` or `s'` states. This avoids unstable neural-net optimization and offers direct control over the quality of `Q_π,Mi` estimates. Model-based selectors requiring `(T_{π,Mj} Q_{π,Mi})(s,a)` also use Monte-Carlo simulation by switching models during rollouts.
4. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Q-caching:** Estimated Monte-Carlo Q-values are cached to avoid redundant computation, making it efficient to test new selectors or subsets of candidate models.
 - **Bootstrapping:** Used to account for dataset randomness by sampling multiple datasets with replacement from `D`, reporting mean performance with confidence intervals. This reuses cached Q-values efficiently.
5. **Environment Setup (Gym Hopper):** Experiments instantiate the protocol in Gym Hopper, introducing stochastic noise and varying gravity constants to create diverse `M_i` environments. DDPG is used to generate target and behavior policies.

This comprehensive methodology allows for both theoretical development of novel, hyperparameter-free selectors and their rigorous, controlled empirical assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of the proposed algorithms and protocol was conducted in the Gym Hopper environment, parameterized by gravity and noise levels.

#### 4.1. Performance of Model-Free Selectors

- **LSTD-Tournament Performance:** The proposed LSTD-Tournament method demonstrated robust and consistent performance across various experimental settings. It generally achieved lower OPE error compared to other model-free baselines and often exhibited greater stability.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **BVFT:** Its performance frequently aligned with that of TD-square, and in some cases, it was not competitive. The authors suggest this might be due to the data size not being sufficient for the discretization required by BVFT.
 - **TD-square:** This method, which directly squares the TD error, showed variable performance, sometimes performing reasonably but other times less effectively.
 - **Average Bellman Error:** This method, which can be viewed as a simpler version of LSTD-Tournament, generally underperformed LSTD-Tournament, indicating the benefit of the more sophisticated discriminator construction.
 - **Naïve Model-Based (`mb_naive`):** This baseline, which uses the ℓ2 model prediction loss, consistently exhibited poor performance, particularly in high-noise environments when the candidate models had varying levels of stochasticity. This empirical observation aligns with the theoretical prediction regarding its bias towards deterministic systems.

#### 4.2. Performance of Model-Based Selectors

- **LSTD-Tournament Outperformance:** A significant finding was that LSTD-Tournament, a model-free selector, generally outperformed the newly developed, more complex model-based selectors (regression-based and sign-flip selectors). This was unexpected, as the model-based methods leverage additional explicit model information.
- **Model-Based Selector Behavior:** While the model-based selectors generally addressed the catastrophic failures seen with the naive model-based method, they did not surpass the performance of LSTD-Tournament. This suggests that the indirect use of model information through Q-functions (as in LSTD-Tournament's construction) can be more effective or robust in these settings.

#### 4.3. Subgrid Studies: Gaps and Misspecification

The new experimental protocol allowed for targeted investigations into specific factors:

- **Gaps (Similarity of Candidate Models):** Experiments varied the "gap" (difference) between candidate simulators and the groundtruth. The results showed a "down-U curve" for OPE error as a function of gap size. This indicated that model selection is most challenging when candidate models have an intermediate level of difference from the true environment; if they are too similar, any selection works, and if they are too dissimilar, distinguishing the good ones is easy.
- **Misspecification (Groundtruth Not in Candidate Set):** Studies explored scenarios where the true environment `M*` was not present in the candidate set `M`. While increasing misspecification generally led to higher OPE errors for all methods, there were no "interesting" differentiations in sensitivity between the various selectors.

#### 4.4. Data Coverage

- **Impact of Behavior Policy:** Experiments varying the ratio of "target data" (from the target policy) to "off-policy data" (from a policy with poor coverage) demonstrated that most methods performed better with higher data coverage. This confirms theoretical predictions about the critical role of data coverage in the difficulty of OPE tasks.

#### 4.5. LSTDQ Family Variants and Rollout Number

- **Variant Comparison:** Among the LSTD-Tournament variants (vanilla, normalized, normalized_diff), the "normalized_diff" version, used in the main experiments, demonstrated generally strong empirical performance.
- **Rollout Sensitivity:** Investigating the effect of the number of Monte-Carlo rollouts (`l`) for Q-value estimation revealed that while a small number of rollouts could sometimes yield reasonable performance, more rollouts were typically beneficial for reducing variance and improving the accuracy of the loss estimates and thus the final OPE error.

In summary, LSTD-Tournament emerged as a performant and reliable selector. The new experimental protocol proved effective in generating controlled environments for evaluating selectors and provided insights into their behavior under various conditions, including the unexpected result of model-free methods outperforming sophisticated model-based ones in this context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) by addressing a critical, yet under-investigated, aspect: model selection for Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) algorithms themselves. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Addressing a Foundational Problem:** The paper directly tackles the "chicken-and-egg problem" in offline RL, where the hyperparameters of OPE algorithms themselves require tuning if they are to be used for policy selection. By proposing hyperparameter-free selectors, the work offers a principled pathway to break this circular dependency, enhancing the robustness and deployability of offline RL systems in real-world applications where ground-truth environments are unavailable.

2. **Theoretical Advancements in Model Selection:**
 - **LSTD-Tournament:** The development of LSTD-Tournament provides a new model-free selector with a `n^-1/2` statistical rate of convergence, which is an improvement over previous approaches like BVFT (`n^-1/4`). This represents a theoretical step forward in ensuring more accurate selection with finite data.
 - **Model-Based Selectors:** The introduction of new regression-based and sign-flip model-based selectors, complete with theoretical guarantees, expands the toolkit for model selection in settings where structural knowledge of dynamics is available. Although empirically outperformed by LSTD-Tournament in the presented experiments, these methods contribute novel theoretical insights into leveraging model information.

3. **Improved Experimental Methodology:** The proposed experimental protocol offers a significant advancement in the empirical evaluation of OPE selectors.
 - **Controllability and Stability:** By using "lazy evaluation" via Monte-Carlo rollouts from varied groundtruth environments, the protocol bypasses the instabilities and lack of control associated with training OPE algorithms (like FQE) to generate candidate Q-functions. This allows for precise control over the quality and characteristics of candidate functions/models.
 - **Systematic Studies:** The protocol enables systematic investigations into factors such as the "gap" between candidate models, the level of misspecification, and the impact of data coverage. This capability facilitates deeper understanding of selector behavior and limitations, which is crucial for advancing the field.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Features like Q-caching and bootstrapping make the protocol computationally feasible, allowing for extensive benchmarking and exploration of new algorithms.

4. **Practical Implications for Offline RL Deployment:**
 - **Reliable OPE:** By providing more reliable and hyperparameter-free OPE model selection, the work contributes to more trustworthy estimation of policy performance from offline data. This is crucial for applications in healthcare, robotics, and other high-stakes domains where policies must be thoroughly vetted before deployment.
 - **Simpler Implementations:** LSTD-Tournament's comparative simplicity and robust empirical performance suggest it could be a practical tool for practitioners facing OPE model selection challenges.

5. **New Research Directions:** The paper identifies several open questions:
 - **LSTDQ's Coverage Assumption:** The analysis highlights limitations and a lack of comprehensive understanding regarding the `σ_min(A)` coverage assumption in LSTDQ, inherited by LSTD-Tournament. This calls for fundamental theoretical investigation that could directly improve the guarantees and understanding of LSTD-Tournament.
 - **Realism of Candidate Functions:** While offering control, the current protocol's method of generating candidate Q-functions (e.g., varying gravity uniformly) might not perfectly reflect the types of errors produced by real-world learning algorithms. This opens avenues for generating more realistic error patterns.
 - **Alternative Instrumental Variables:** Exploring different designs for instrumental variables in LSTDQ could lead to further theoretical and practical improvements.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the robustness and trustworthiness of offline RL by providing theoretically sound algorithms and a rigorous experimental framework for OPE model selection. Its impact lies in improving the fundamental components necessary for deploying RL in complex, data-driven applications.
