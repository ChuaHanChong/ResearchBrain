# Context as Memory: Scene-Consistent Interactive Long Video Generation with Memory Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> The 'Context-as-Memory' framework facilitates scene-consistent interactive long video generation by treating all previously generated frames as a memory bank, retrieving relevant context based on camera field-of-view overlap. This method produces videos with enhanced scene consistency, achieving a PSNR of 20.22 and LPIPS of 0.3003 for ground truth comparison, and generalizes well to open-domain scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing interactive long video generation methods lack long-term scene consistency, failing to maintain coherent environments when revisiting previously seen locations.
- Models typically use only a limited window of recent frames, leading to a 'memory' deficit for maintaining spatial and temporal coherence over extended durations.
- Directly incorporating all historical frames into each generation step is computationally impractical and can introduce noise from irrelevant information.

## Method

- A **Context-as-Memory** framework that stores generated frames directly as ==memory== and concatenates retrieved relevant frames as conditions for a ==Diffusion Transformer==.
- A ==Memory Retrieval Module== uses known ==camera trajectories== and ==Field of View (FOV) overlap== to efficiently identify and select truly relevant historical frames.
- Further filtering strategies, such as selecting ==non-adjacent frames== from continuous overlaps, are employed to reduce redundancy and optimize the context fed to the model.

## Results

- Achieved superior scene consistency and memory, with a PSNR of **20.22** and LPIPS of **0.3003** against ground truth, and PSNR of **18.11** and LPIPS of **0.3414** for history context comparison, significantly outperforming baseline methods.
- Demonstrated improved generation quality, recording FID of **107.18** and FVD of **821.37** in ground truth comparison, indicating more realistic and perceptually similar video outputs.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the FOV-based memory retrieval and non-adjacent frame selection, showing substantial performance gains over random context selection or limited historical windows.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating the entire history of generated frames as a memory bank, when efficiently managed, can effectively address the problem of scene inconsistency in long video generation.
> - Robust memory capabilities can be achieved through simple yet effective designs, like direct frame storage and concatenation with an intelligent retrieval mechanism, without relying on complex 3D reconstruction.
> - Leveraging camera pose information for FOV-based overlap detection provides an efficient and accurate method for retrieving relevant context, significantly improving generation quality and consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Context as Memory" directly addresses a critical and widely acknowledged limitation in the rapidly advancing field of video generation: the lack of long-term scene consistency and "memory" in interactively generated videos.

**Rapid Advancements in Video Generation:** Recent years have witnessed explosive progress in video generation models (e.g., OpenAI's Sora, Runway's Gen-1/Gen-2, DeepMind's Veo, Kuaishou's Kling, Google's Genie). These models, often based on diffusion transformers, demonstrate impressive capabilities in generating high-quality, short-to-medium duration video clips from text prompts. They are increasingly seen as foundational for "world models" that can simulate dynamic environments.

**Emergence of Interactive Long Video Generation:** A crucial next step for these models is to enable *interactive* and *long video generation*, where users can control camera movements or agent actions in a streaming manner. This is vital for applications in gaming (e.g., procedural world generation, interactive narratives), simulations (e.g., autonomous driving, virtual training), and creating truly explorable virtual worlds.

**The "Memory" Problem – A Key Bottleneck:** Despite these advances, existing interactive long video generation methods largely suffer from a critical flaw: their inability to maintain *scene consistency* over extended durations, particularly when the camera revisits a previously generated area. The paper vividly illustrates this with the "Oasis" example, where turning left and then immediately right results in an entirely different scene. This "memory" deficit means that while models can generate videos of extended *duration*, they lack *coherence* in their depiction of a persistent virtual world. This is primarily because most models condition only on a limited window of recent frames, losing information about temporally distant scenes.

**Comparison with Existing Approaches:**
- **Limited Context Windows:** Methods like Diffusion Forcing (DFoT) rely on fixed-size windows of recent frames, which is insufficient for long-term memory.
- **Hierarchical Compression:** Approaches like FramePack attempt to compress historical context but often lose significant information from older frames due to aggressive compression strategies.
- **3D Reconstruction-based Methods:** Some works explore building explicit 3D representations from generated videos and re-rendering, but these are often limited by the accuracy and speed of 3D reconstruction, especially in continuously expanding scenes where accumulated errors become problematic. Moreover, these tend to be 3D generation methods that borrow from video generation, rather than enhancing video generation with memory.
- **WorldMem:** A related effort, WorldMem, attempts memory via cross-attention but has been validated on shorter video lengths (around 10 seconds) in specific scenarios (Minecraft).

**Addressing the Gap:** This paper directly targets this "memory capability" gap by proposing a method that allows generative models to explicitly remember and leverage previously generated visual content over long durations, without resorting to complex 3D reconstruction. It positions itself within the evolving landscape of "context learning for video generation," emphasizing that *all history context* can serve as memory, provided it is efficiently managed and retrieved.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to achieve **scene-consistent interactive long video generation** by endowing generative models with robust **memory capabilities**.

The primary motivation stems from the significant limitations of current state-of-the-art video generation models, particularly in interactive scenarios requiring prolonged engagement:

1. **Lack of Scene Consistency in Long Videos:** Existing methods, while capable of generating lengthy videos with frame-to-frame continuity, consistently fail to maintain scene consistency over long durations. This is most evident when the camera revisits a previously seen location, only to render a completely different scene (the "Oasis" problem). This inconsistency severely limits their utility for applications requiring a persistent virtual environment, such as realistic simulations, open-world gaming, or building "world models."
2. **Insufficient Utilization of Historical Context:** The authors identify that this memory deficit arises because models typically condition new frame generation on only a limited number of preceding frames (e.g., a fixed window). This short-term context is inadequate for long-term spatial and temporal coherence.
3. **The "All History as Memory" Hypothesis:** The central hypothesis motivating this work is that if a generative model could actively reference *all previously generated frames*—treating them as a continuous memory bank—it could select and replicate relevant content, thereby maintaining scene consistency.
4. **Practical Challenges of "All History":** While intuitive, the idea of processing *all* historical frames is impractical due to:
 - **Enormous Computational Overhead:** Incorporating potentially thousands of frames into each generation step is computationally prohibitive.
 - **Computational Wastefulness:** Most historical frames would be irrelevant to the generation of a specific new frame.
 - **Noise Introduction:** Irrelevant historical frames could actively hinder generation by adding noise or confusing the model.
5. **Motivation for Memory Retrieval:** To overcome these practical challenges, the authors are motivated to develop a mechanism, termed "Memory Retrieval," that can intelligently and efficiently select only the *truly relevant* frames from the vast pool of historical context to guide the generation of new frames. This aims to strike a balance between leveraging long-term memory and managing computational resources.
6. **Simplicity and Effectiveness:** A secondary motivation is to achieve this goal with "simple yet effective designs," avoiding the complexity of additional external control modules or explicit 3D reconstruction pipelines, thereby making the approach more integrated and efficient.

In essence, the paper aims to transform video generation models from producing merely *long* sequences to creating *coherent and consistent* long sequences that remember their past, unlocking their potential for truly interactive and immersive experiences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "Context-as-Memory" framework tackles scene-consistent interactive long video generation by explicitly leveraging historical generated frames as a memory source, coupled with an intelligent retrieval mechanism.

#### 3.1. Core Idea: Context-as-Memory

The central tenet is to treat all previously generated frames as a memory bank. When generating a new frame, relevant frames from this memory are retrieved and used as conditions. The method is built upon two "simple yet effective designs":

1. **Storage Format:** Instead of extracting features, embeddings, or performing complex 3D reconstructions, the generated context frames are directly stored in their original frame format. This simplicity avoids potential information loss or errors introduced by intermediate processing steps.
2. **Conditioning Method:** The retrieved context frames are directly incorporated into the input of the generative model by concatenating them with the frames to be predicted along the frame dimension. This approach allows the pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to learn from the context without requiring external control modules like adapters or cross-attention. During attention computation within the DiT blocks, context latents participate equally with noisy predicted latents. Critically, only the predicted latents are updated, while the context latents remain unchanged. Positional encodings (using RoPE) are adapted to handle the variable length of the combined input.

#### 3.2. Base Model and Camera Conditioning

The work builds upon a full-sequence text-to-video latent diffusion model. This model consists of:
- **Causal 3D VAE:** Compresses video frames temporally and spatially into latent representations.
- **Diffusion Transformer (DiT):** A transformer-based diffusion model for generating these latents. It includes spatial (2D) attention, spatial-temporal (3D) attention, cross-attention (for text prompts), and FFN modules. The use of 3D self-attention is highlighted for effective spatiotemporal token processing.
The base model is augmented with **camera control mechanisms** (inspired by ReCamMaster [Bai et al. 2025]), allowing interactive video generation guided by user-provided camera trajectories. Camera poses (rotation R, translation t) are encoded via an MLP and added to the model's feature channels as a condition. This camera conditioning is crucial for the Memory Retrieval module.

#### 3.3. Memory Retrieval Module

This is the critical component for managing the vast historical context efficiently. Its purpose is to select a small number of *truly relevant* frames from the entire history to condition the current generation, addressing the computational and noise issues of using all history.

- **Known Camera Trajectories:** Since the video generation is camera-controlled, the camera poses for all previously generated context frames are known. This eliminates the need for an additional camera pose estimator.
- **FOV (Field of View) Overlap-based Search:** The core of retrieval is a rule-based approach that determines co-visibility between historical frames and the current frame to be generated.
 - **Mechanism:** Given that camera movement is restricted to a 2D plane (XY plane) with rotation only around the z-axis, FOV overlap is simplified. It involves checking the intersection of rays (representing the left and right boundaries of the FOV) shooting from the camera origins of two frames.
 - **Filtering:** Cases where intersection points are too near or too distant from the cameras (indicating no actual or very small overlap) are filtered out.
 - **Limitations:** Acknowledged that this 2D simplification might not cover all complex scenarios or occlusions perfectly, but is effective for significantly reducing candidate frames.
- **Further Filtering Strategies (if FOV yields too many frames):**
 - **Non-adjacent Selection:** To reduce redundancy from highly similar consecutive frames, only one frame is randomly selected from a group of continuous overlapping frames. This is found to be highly effective.
 - **Far-space-time Selection:** Optionally, a few context frames that are furthest apart spatially or temporally can be selected to supplement long-term information. This was found to have a relatively minor impact in ablations.
 - **Context Size Limit:** A maximum number of retrieved context frames (e.g., k=20) is defined.

#### 3.4. Data Collection

To train and validate the method, a new dataset was collected using **Unreal Engine 5**, as existing datasets with camera poses are typically short.
- **Content:** 100 long videos (7,601 frames each at 30 fps), featuring 12 diverse scene styles (visually realistic, stylized, indoor/outdoor).
- **Camera Trajectories:** Smoothed B-spline curves are generated from random points in 3D scenes, with constrained movement distances and rotation angles in the XY-plane.
- **Annotations:** Precise camera extrinsic and intrinsic parameters are recorded for each frame. Captions are annotated by a multimodal LLM every 77 frames.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference Procedures

- **Training:** A segment is randomly selected from a long ground truth video. `k-1` context frames are retrieved from the remaining frames using Memory Retrieval (pre-computed overlaps). The first frame of the prediction sequence is added for continuity. A 10% chance exists to use only the recent context frame, simulating generation starts. The diffusion model is trained with context latents and camera poses.
- **Inference:** For each new video segment to be predicted, `k-1` context frames are searched from *previously generated* frames using FOV-based Memory Retrieval. The most recently generated frame is added. The model then infers the new latent and decodes the frames.

This comprehensive approach combines a robust base generative model with a novel, efficient memory mechanism that prioritizes relevance through camera pose analysis, all supported by a purpose-built dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate "Context-as-Memory" demonstrate its superior performance in achieving scene-consistent interactive long video generation, both quantitatively and qualitatively.

1. **Superior Memory Capabilities:**
 - **Quantitative Metrics (PSNR↑, LPIPS↓):** Context-as-Memory significantly outperforms all baseline methods in PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio) and LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity). Higher PSNR and lower LPIPS indicate better pixel-wise and perceptual similarity to ground truth or consistent reappearance of past scenes, directly reflecting stronger memory.
 - For Ground Truth Comparison, Context-as-Memory achieves PSNR 20.22 and LPIPS 0.3003, compared to DFoT's PSNR 17.63, LPIPS 0.4528, and FramePack's PSNR 17.20, LPIPS 0.4757.
 - Even more critically, for History Context Comparison (comparing newly generated frames when revisiting a scene with previously *generated* frames of that scene), Context-as-Memory still leads with PSNR 18.11 and LPIPS 0.3414, against DFoT's PSNR 15.70, LPIPS 0.5102, and FramePack's PSNR 15.65, LPIPS 0.4947. This metric strongly validates the ability to maintain consistency over generated long videos.
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** Visual comparisons clearly show that Context-as-Memory produces videos where scene elements, textures, and structures remain consistent even after significant camera movements and returns to previous viewpoints. In contrast, other methods exhibit noticeable scene inconsistencies, with objects changing appearance or entire environments being regenerated differently.

2. **Improved Generation Quality:**
 - **Quantitative Metrics (FID↓, FVD↓):** Beyond memory, Context-as-Memory also achieves the best generation quality, with lower FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) scores. Lower FID and FVD indicate that the generated videos are more realistic and perceptually similar to real videos.
 - Context-as-Memory achieves FID 107.18 and FVD 821.37 in Ground Truth comparison, compared to the next best DFoT with FID 112.96 and FVD 897.87.
 - **Reasoning:** The authors attribute this to two factors: (1) sufficient and relevant context provides stronger conditional guidance, reducing generation uncertainty, and (2) using earlier, relatively "cleaner" generated frames as context helps minimize error accumulation in subsequent frames.

3. **Effectiveness of Memory Retrieval Strategy:**
 - **Ablation Study (Table 3):** This study validates the design choices for Memory Retrieval.
 - "Random" context selection surprisingly outperforms DFoT and FramePack, suggesting that having *some* access to global context (even if random) is better than being limited to only recent frames.
 - "FOV+Random" (FOV filtering + random selection from remaining) significantly improves performance over "Random," demonstrating the effectiveness of FOV-based filtering in identifying relevant frames.
 - "FOV+Non-adj" (FOV filtering + selecting only one frame from continuous sequences) shows further significant gains, confirming that removing redundant adjacent frames is crucial for selecting truly *useful* context.
 - "FOV+Non-adj+Far-space-time" (adding selection of spatially/temporally distant frames) provides a minor, albeit positive, additional boost.
 - **Conclusion:** The combination of FOV-based overlap detection and non-adjacent frame selection is highly effective in filtering out irrelevant and redundant information, thereby enhancing the probability of selecting valuable context for memory.

4. **Impact of Context Size:**
 - **Ablation Study (Table 2):** As expected, increasing the context size generally improves memory capabilities (higher PSNR, lower LPIPS), as more useful information becomes available.
 - **Trade-off:** However, this comes at the cost of increased computational overhead and slower generation speed. A context size of 20 was found to offer a good balance between performance and speed for this specific setup.

5. **Generalization to Open-Domain Scenarios:**
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 6, 8, 9):** The method successfully generalizes to open-domain scenarios. Using diverse internet images as initial frames, the model is able to generate long videos with "rotate away and rotate back" trajectories, maintaining good memory consistency in the generated content.
 - **Reasoning:** This is attributed to the diverse training dataset (Unreal Engine 5 scenes) enabling generalizable context utilization skills and the strong generative priors learned by the base model during its pre-training on various data types.

In summary, the main findings conclusively demonstrate that "Context-as-Memory" effectively addresses the memory problem in long video generation, leading to visually coherent and high-quality outputs, and showing promising generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Context-as-Memory" research represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for interactive video generation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Truly Persistent Virtual Worlds:** The most profound impact lies in its ability to confer "memory" to generative video models. By allowing models to consistently remember and reproduce previously seen environments, this work moves beyond merely generating long sequences to creating truly persistent and explorable virtual worlds. This is a critical step towards realizing comprehensive "world models" that can simulate reality or complex environments.

2. **Unlocking New Interactive Applications:**
 - **Advanced Gaming:** This technology could revolutionize game development by enabling dynamic, procedurally generated open worlds where players can explore vast landscapes, revisit locations, and find them consistently rendered, without pre-rendered assets for every viewpoint. This could lead to more immersive and infinite gaming experiences.
 - **Realistic Simulations:** For fields like autonomous driving, robotics, or architectural visualization, the ability to generate long, consistent, and interactive simulations is invaluable. Engineers and designers could "fly through" virtual cities or buildings, making changes and experiencing persistent environments without breaks in continuity.
 - **Creative Tools:** Artists and creators could have more powerful tools to sculpt dynamic narratives and visual experiences, allowing for free-form camera movements and interactive storytelling within consistently generated scenes.

3. **Simplicity and Efficiency as a Paradigm:** The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D reconstruction — a computationally intensive and error-prone process for dynamic, expanding scenes — is not the only path to achieving memory in video generation. The "simple yet effective designs" of direct frame storage and concatenation, coupled with an intelligent retrieval mechanism, offer a more streamlined and potentially scalable alternative. This minimalist approach could inspire similar solutions in other generative tasks.

4. **Advancing "Context Learning":** The work highlights the critical role of "long context" in video generation. By proposing an effective method for retrieving and utilizing relevant historical context, it contributes to the broader research direction of making generative models more context-aware and capable of handling extended sequences of information.

5. **New Data Resource for Research:** The creation and release (or description of) a new long video dataset with precise camera annotations using Unreal Engine 5 is a valuable contribution. Such datasets are essential for training and benchmarking future memory-aware video generation models, accelerating research in this specific sub-field.

6. **Paving the Way for Future Research:** By identifying and partially addressing current limitations (e.g., static scenes, complex occlusions, error accumulation, base model scale), the paper clearly outlines crucial directions for future work. This sets a roadmap for improving memory capabilities in more dynamic and complex open-domain scenarios, eventually moving towards free, extended navigation from any given image.

In essence, "Context as Memory" doesn't just improve existing video generation; it lays foundational groundwork for a new generation of interactive AI experiences where digital worlds possess genuine visual coherence and a sense of permanence, mirroring the real world more closely.
