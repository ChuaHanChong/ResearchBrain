# Uncertainty-quantified Rollout Policy Adaptation for Unlabelled Cross-domain Temporal Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Queen Mary University of London and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab developed Uncertainty-quantified Rollout Policy Adaptation (URPA) for unlabelled cross-domain temporal grounding, enabling models to adapt to new domains using only 100-200 unlabelled target videos. URPA quantifies prediction uncertainty from policy rollouts to generate reliable pseudo-labels, achieving strong performance compared to methods requiring full target datasets.

## Problem

- Advanced temporal grounding methods (e.g., GRPO) critically rely on ground-truth labels for reward computation, making them impractical for unlabelled real-world target domains.
- Models trained on a source domain suffer significant performance degradation due to domain shift when applied to unlabelled target domains, limiting their generalization capabilities.
- Full-scale adaptation on large target video datasets is computationally expensive and time-consuming, hindering real-time or resource-constrained applications.

## Method

- Develops ==Uncertainty-quantified== Rollout Policy Adaptation (**URPA**) to adapt a source-trained model to an unlabelled target domain using only a small subset of target videos.
- Generates pseudo-labels by averaging predictions from multiple GRPO rollouts and quantifies their reliability using the standard deviation of predicted timestamps as epistemic uncertainty.
- Implements a confidence-aware reward function that weights pseudo-label-based temporal Intersection over Union (tIoU) by an exponential decay of the uncertainty, prioritizing more reliable pseudo-labels.

## Results

- URPA, using only **100**-200 unlabelled target videos, consistently improved temporal grounding performance across six cross-domain tasks, achieving competitive or superior results compared to state-of-the-art full-dataset unsupervised domain adaptation methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of source pre-training and demonstrated that both relaxed tIoU and uncertainty-quantified reward weighting are crucial for effective unlabelled target adaptation, each contributing to performance gains.
- The method showed robustness to hyperparameter choices for reward scaling and identified an optimal number of rollouts (G=8 for **200**-shot adaptation) for balancing performance and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The standard deviation of predicted timestamps across multiple GRPO policy rollouts can reliably approximate Bayesian predictive (epistemic) uncertainty, providing a formal basis for uncertainty quantification in reinforcement learning for self-supervised learning.
> - Utilizing relaxed ground-truth intervals during source training and target adaptation helps mitigate annotation biases and the impact of noisy pseudo-labels, improving generalization.
> - Combining explicit format rewards with uncertainty-weighted temporal accuracy rewards enables effective and robust policy adaptation in unlabelled cross-domain settings with minimal target data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video Temporal Grounding (TG) is a fundamental task in vision-language research, aiming to precisely locate specific video segments that correspond to natural language descriptions. This task is critical for high-level applications such as activity detection and embodied human-computer interaction.

The broader landscape of temporal grounding has seen advancements through various approaches. Initially, methods often relied on proposal-based techniques, generating candidate segments before matching them with queries, or proposal-free approaches that directly predicted temporal boundaries. Recent developments have incorporated Vision-Language Models (VLMs) due to their capabilities in capturing holistic video semantics. However, VLMs frequently encounter difficulties with fine-grained temporal localization, leading to suboptimal performance in TG.

To address VLM limitations, some studies have applied Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to enhance alignment between video and query pairs. However, these methods can overfit to redundant contextual information, hindering precise grounding, as relevant segments typically constitute a small portion of a video. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) post-training has been introduced to encourage explicit intermediate reasoning steps, but this approach is often dependent on expensive, manually annotated prompts, which restrict its scalability across different domains and tasks.

More recently, Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO) has reframed temporal grounding as a policy learning problem, utilizing reinforcement learning to directly optimize segment predictions. GRPO has demonstrated strong performance in in-domain settings by circumventing the need for hand-crafted prompts. However, GRPO's reliance on ground-truth temporal boundaries for reward computation presents a significant limitation in real-world scenarios where annotations are scarce or unavailable. Furthermore, GRPO-based models exhibit considerable performance degradation when applied to different domains due to distribution shifts, indicating poor generalization capabilities.

Beyond annotation constraints, the practical deployment of temporal grounding models faces challenges related to the scale of video data. Target domains often comprise thousands of videos, which are expensive to store and computationally intensive to process for full-scale adaptation. This introduces prohibitive latency, making existing adaptation methods unsuitable for real-time or on-device applications.

This paper addresses these issues by proposing a data-efficient approach to unlabelled cross-domain temporal grounding. It positions itself by focusing on adapting a model, initially trained on a labelled source domain, to a target domain using only a limited number of unlabelled videos from the target. This approach aims to bridge the gap between strong in-domain performance (like that achieved by GRPO) and the practical demands of real-world deployment, where labels are scarce, data is vast, and adaptation must be efficient. The work specifically contributes to Test-Time Domain Adaptation (TTDA) by developing a backward-based method that operates with sparse target data, contrasting with methods requiring continuous, per-sample adaptation or full-set target data. It also draws inspiration from uncertainty estimation techniques, particularly those related to Bayesian neural networks like MC Dropout, to enhance the reliability of self-supervised learning in unlabelled environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a Data-Efficient Unlabelled Cross-domain Temporal Grounding method. This method aims to enable a model, initially trained on a labelled source domain, to adapt effectively to an unlabelled target domain using only a small number of unlabelled videos from the target domain.

The specific motivations driving this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Overcoming Label Dependency in GRPO:** Current advanced temporal grounding methods, particularly those leveraging reinforcement learning like Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO), critically depend on ground-truth temporal boundaries to compute reward signals. This reliance makes them impractical for real-world scenarios where target domain annotations are often unavailable or prohibitively expensive to acquire. The objective is to enable GRPO-based grounding in an unlabelled setting.

2. **Mitigating Domain Shift:** Models trained on a specific source domain frequently experience significant performance degradation when deployed in a target domain due to differences in data distribution (domain shift). This issue affects the generalization capabilities of temporal grounding models, including GRPO. The research aims to improve cross-domain generalization without requiring target labels.

3. **Addressing Computational and Storage Overhead:** Video datasets are typically large, making full-scale adaptation on entire target domains computationally expensive and time-consuming. Storing and processing thousands of target videos for adaptation introduces substantial latency, rendering such approaches unsuitable for real-time or resource-constrained applications. A key motivation is to reduce these overheads by adapting with only a sparse subset of unlabelled target videos (e.g., K=100-200), thereby facilitating practical deployment.

4. **Enhancing Pseudo-Label Reliability:** In the absence of ground-truth labels for the target domain, self-learning approaches often rely on pseudo-labels generated by the model itself. These pseudo-labels can be noisy and uncertain, leading to suboptimal adaptation. A critical motivation is to explicitly quantify the uncertainty associated with these pseudo-labels, allowing the model to differentiate reliable supervision from unreliable signals and focus on the former during adaptation. This is inspired by methods like MC Dropout, adapting the concept to GRPO's rollout mechanism.

5. **Enabling Real-time Test-Time Adaptation:** The goal is to develop a lightweight adaptation mechanism that can operate efficiently at test-time with minimal latency. This contrasts with traditional domain adaptation methods that require extensive pre-adaptation on large datasets or continuous adaptation for every test input, which can be computationally intensive.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a practical, efficient, and robust temporal grounding solution that can generalize across domains and operate effectively with minimal, unlabelled target data, thereby making advanced policy optimization methods viable in diverse real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Uncertainty-quantified Rollout Policy Adaptation (URPA), is designed for data-efficient unlabelled cross-domain temporal grounding. It involves two main phases: source model training and target adaptation.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The problem is defined with a labelled source domain $D_s = \{V^s_i, I^s_i\}_{i=1}^N$ and a target domain with a small number of unlabelled videos $D_t = \{V^t_i\}_{i=1}^K$, where $K \ll N$. A distribution shift exists such that $P_s \neq P_t$. The model is initially pre-trained on $D_s$ and then adapted at test-time using the limited unlabelled videos from $D_t$.

#### 3.2. Remark on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO, a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), is employed as the backbone for policy learning. Given a query, the model generates $G$ candidate rollout responses $o = \{o_1, \ldots, o_G\}$ through policy sampling. Rewards $r = \{r_1, \ldots, r_G\}$ are computed by comparing candidates with a reference (ground truth during source training). Normalized advantages $A_i = (r_i - \text{mean}(r)) / \text{std}(r)$ are used to optimize the policy $\pi_\theta$, encouraging beneficial deviations. A KL-divergence regularization term $D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{ref})$ constrains excessive policy changes. While effective in-domain, GRPO's reliance on ground truth for reward signals limits its direct application to unlabelled cross-domain settings.

#### 3.3. Source Model Training

The primary goal of source training is to imbue a VLM (specifically, Qwen2.5-7B in this work) with temporal reasoning and grounding capabilities. This involves designing specific reward functions:

- **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** This reward encourages the model to generate structured outputs, adhering to a "think first, then answer" template using `<think>...</think>` and `<answer>...</answer>` tags. It is a binary reward, yielding 1 if the output matches the format and 0 otherwise. This is intended to stimulate explicit reasoning chains.

- **Accuracy Reward ($R_{tIoU}$):** To guide the model toward precise temporal localization, an accuracy reward based on the temporal Intersection over Union (tIoU) is used. To mitigate annotation biases and improve generalization across domains, the ground-truth interval $I^s = [\tau^s_{start}, \tau^s_{end}]$ is relaxed. Both boundaries are extended by a fixed proportion $\alpha$ (set to 0.1) of the event duration, creating $\tilde{I}^s_{gt}$. The $R_{tIoU}$ is then computed as the tIoU between the predicted interval $I^s_{pred}$ and this relaxed ground truth $\tilde{I}^s_{gt}$.

- **Overall Supervised Reward ($R_s$):** The total reward for source domain training is a weighted sum: $R_s = 0.5 \times R_{format} + 0.5 \times R_{tIoU}$. This phase equips the model with foundational spatio-temporal reasoning, though domain shift necessitates target adaptation.

#### 3.4. Target Adaptation (URPA)

For target adaptation, the model leverages a small number of $K$ unlabelled videos from the target domain. The lack of ground-truth labels and the limited data necessitate a self-learning approach that quantifies reliability.

- **Pseudo Label Generation:** For each unlabelled target video $V_t$, the policy model samples $G$ stochastic rollout responses. Each response includes predicted start and end timestamps. These individual predictions, while potentially noisy, are aggregated to form a pseudo label. The pseudo start and end timestamps, $\hat{\tau}^t_{start}$ and $\hat{\tau}^t_{end}$, are computed by averaging the predicted timestamps across the $G$ rollouts. A small margin relaxation, identical to that used in source training (Eq. 4), is then applied to these pseudo labels to generate $\tilde{I}^t_{gt}$ for robustness.

- **Uncertainty Quantification:** To assess the reliability of these pseudo labels, the method estimates prediction uncertainty. Analogous to Monte Carlo Dropout, where variance across sub-network outputs approximates uncertainty, URPA uses the standard deviation of the predicted timestamps across the $G$ rollouts as a proxy for epistemic uncertainty. The uncertainty $u$ is calculated as the sum of standard deviations for start and end timestamps: $u = \sigma(\{(p^t_{start})^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^G) + \sigma(\{(p^t_{end})^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^G)$.

- **Confidence Score and Weighted Reward:** The uncertainty $u$ is then converted into a confidence score $c$ using an exponential decay function: $c = \exp(-\gamma u)$, where $\gamma$ is a hyperparameter controlling sensitivity to uncertainty. This confidence score is used to weight the contribution of the pseudo labels during reward computation. The temporal grounding reward ($R_{tIoU}$) is scaled by this confidence score, while the format reward ($R_{format}$) remains unweighted. The overall target adaptation reward is: $R_s = 0.5 \times R_{format} + 0.5 \times R_{tIoU} \times c$. This confidence-aware reward function allows the model to prioritize more reliable pseudo labels, thereby improving the robustness of test-time adaptation.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) demonstrating that the empirical standard deviation derived from multiple GRPO rollouts converges to the Bayesian predictive standard deviation. This convergence quantifies epistemic uncertainty. The proof outlines that as the number of rollouts $G \to \infty$ and GRPO training reduces the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the GRPO policy and the Bayesian predictive distribution ($\varepsilon \to 0$), the estimator $\hat{\sigma}_G(x)$ converges in probability to $\sigma_*(x)$, the Bayesian predictive standard deviation. This establishes a formal basis for using rollout variance as a measure of epistemic uncertainty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted across three temporal grounding datasets (TACoS, ActivityNet Captions, Charades-STA) in six cross-domain configurations, using a Qwen2.5-7B backbone. The findings demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of URPA.

#### 4.1. Experimental Results (Table 1)

- **Data-Efficient Adaptation Performance:** URPA, utilizing only 100 or 200 unlabelled target videos for adaptation, consistently improved the performance of the source-pretrained model.
- **Comparison with Full-Dataset UDA Methods:** URPA achieved competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) methods that typically require adaptation on the entire unlabelled target domain (often thousands of videos).
 - Specifically, for the `TACoS → ActivityNet`, `TACoS → Charades`, and `ActivityNet → Charades` tasks, URPA with 200-shot adaptation outperformed all compared full-set UDA methods in terms of R@0.5. For example, on `TACoS → ActivityNet`, URPA (200-shot) achieved 41.83 R@0.5, surpassing UDA-TSL (33.54 R@0.5). On `ActivityNet → Charades`, URPA (200-shot) achieved 65.12 R@0.5, compared to UDA-TSL (60.26 R@0.5).
- **Comparison with Domain Generalization Baselines:**
 - The base Qwen2.5-7B model showed very low performance when directly applied to target domains (e.g., 15.18 R@0.5 on `Charades → ActivityNet`), indicating its lack of inherent temporal localization capabilities.
 - Models trained only on the source domain (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B + GRPO or + URPA Source Training only) showed significantly better performance than the base model (e.g., 35.61 R@0.5 for GRPO and 36.46 R@0.5 for URPA Source Training on `Charades → ActivityNet`).
 - URPA's source training phase (with its soft accuracy reward) generally yielded slightly better results than standard GRPO source training, suggesting an improved initial state for adaptation.
 - The addition of data-efficient target adaptation with URPA further improved these source-trained models, demonstrating the value of target-specific fine-tuning even with limited data.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (Table 2 on Charades → ActivityNet)

- **Necessity of Source Training:** Direct target adaptation with URPA (100-shot) without source pre-training yielded poor results (2.67 R@0.3), confirming that source training provides essential task-specific knowledge.
- **Impact of Relaxation and Uncertainty Quantification:**
 - Using pseudo labels directly for adaptation without relaxed tIoU and uncertainty quantification resulted in low performance (58.58 R@0.3).
 - Applying the relaxed tIoU (Eq. 4) to pseudo labels significantly improved results (63.72 R@0.3), indicating that soft label learning mitigates label noise effects.
 - Further incorporating the uncertainty-quantified reward weighting mechanism (Eq. 10) yielded additional improvements (64.61 R@0.3), confirming the effectiveness of GRPO-based uncertainty estimation in guiding adaptation.

#### 4.3. Parameter Analysis (Table 3 on Charades → ActivityNet)

- **Reward Scaling Factor ($\gamma$):** Performance remained relatively stable across different values of $\gamma$. In the 100-shot setting, $\gamma=10$ yielded the best R@0.5 (40.13), suggesting robustness to this hyperparameter.
- **Number of Rollouts ($G$):** For 200-shot adaptation, performance peaked at $G=8$ rollouts (42.57 R@0.5). Increasing $G$ beyond 8 did not lead to consistent gains and incurred higher computational and memory costs, indicating an optimal balance for efficiency and effectiveness.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3)

- Qualitative examples demonstrated that the adapted model using URPA produced more accurate temporal groundings compared to the model before adaptation. In instances where the pre-adaptation model failed to localize correctly, URPA enabled the capture of relevant behavioral patterns and precise predictions in the target domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of video temporal grounding, particularly in addressing practical limitations for real-world deployment.

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Annotation Costs:** The work introduces a data-efficient unlabelled cross-domain temporal grounding approach that overcomes the critical dependency on large, labelled datasets in target domains. By demonstrating effective adaptation with only 100 or 200 unlabelled target videos, it substantially reduces the prohibitive costs associated with manual annotation, making temporal grounding more feasible for diverse, unannotated real-world video collections.

2. **Enabling Efficient Cross-Domain Generalization:** The proposed Uncertainty-quantified Rollout Policy Adaptation (URPA) effectively mitigates the performance degradation caused by domain shift. This enables models, initially trained on a source domain, to generalize robustly to unseen target domains, which is a common challenge in computer vision. Its ability to achieve competitive or superior performance compared to full-dataset unsupervised domain adaptation methods, using significantly less target data, highlights its efficiency.

3. **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for Unlabelled Settings:** URPA extends the applicability of reinforcement learning-based methods like GRPO to unlabelled environments. By generating pseudo-labels from GRPO rollouts and quantifying their uncertainty, the method provides a novel framework for self-supervised learning without requiring explicit ground-truth reward signals in the target domain. This expands the utility of policy optimization in scenarios where labels are unavailable.

4. **Novel Uncertainty Quantification for Rollout Policies:** The theoretical demonstration that rollout standard deviation approximates Bayesian predictive variance provides a strong foundation for using this metric to quantify epistemic uncertainty. This novel application of uncertainty quantification within a reinforcement learning context allows the model to intelligently weight pseudo-labels, focusing on more reliable supervision and thereby improving adaptation robustness. This mechanism could inspire similar approaches in other domains where policy rollouts are used.

5. **Practical Deployment and Real-time Capabilities:** The data-efficient nature of URPA, requiring only a small target sample and performing lightweight gradient updates, significantly reduces computational overhead and latency. This makes the method suitable for real-time applications and on-device deployment, where resources are often constrained, and rapid adaptation to new data distributions is necessary.

In summary, URPA provides a robust and efficient solution for cross-domain temporal grounding in label-scarce environments. Its methodological innovations in uncertainty-quantified self-learning and data-efficient adaptation have the potential to broaden the deployment of advanced video understanding systems across various practical applications.
