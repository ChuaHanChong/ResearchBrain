# Hyperparameter Trajectory Inference with Conditional Lagrangian Optimal Transport

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Cambridge developed Hyperparameter Trajectory Inference (HTI), a method leveraging Conditional Lagrangian Optimal Transport (CLOT) to create surrogate models that dynamically adapt neural network behavior at inference time. This approach, which learns continuous output distribution dynamics from sparse observations, reduced the computational adaptation time for reinforcement learning policies from 38 to 11 GPU hours in cancer therapy simulations and achieved superior performance in various tasks for unseen hyperparameter settings.

## Problem

- Neural network hyperparameters are typically fixed at design, yet optimal settings can change post-deployment due to evolving user preferences or environmental conditions.
- Retraining neural networks for dynamic hyperparameter adjustments is often computationally expensive and impractical for real-time, inference-time adaptation.
- Existing trajectory inference methods often do not adequately account for conditioning variables influencing dynamics, and prior neural optimal transport approaches use fixed cost functions.

## Method

- Introduces Hyperparameter Trajectory Inference (**HTI**), which learns a neural network's conditional output distribution changes with hyperparameters by constructing a surrogate model.
- Proposes a Conditional Lagrangian ==Optimal Transport== (CLOT) method that jointly learns a conditional Lagrangian function (kinetic and potential energy terms) and associated optimal transport maps and geodesics.
- Incorporates inductive biases for dense data traversal (via a potential energy term) and least-action principles (via a learned kinetic energy term with an expressive neural metric), all conditioned on input data using FiLM layers.

## Results

- Achieved the lowest Negative Log-Likelihood and Circle Distance in synthetic conditional trajectory inference tasks, accurately reconstructing complex conditional probability paths.
- Reduced computational adaptation time for reinforcement learning policies from **38** to **11** GPU hours in cancer therapy simulations, yielding the highest average reward at held-out hyperparameter values.
- Demonstrated superior performance with the lowest Mean Squared Error in quantile regression and lowest Wasserstein Distance in generative modeling for unseen hyperparameter settings across diverse applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The learned potential energy term guides inferred paths to traverse dense regions of the data space, ensuring path feasibility and meaningfulness.
> - An expressive neural parametrization for the kinetic energy term's metric (learning both rotation and eigenvalues) effectively captures complex, non-Euclidean hyperparameter-induced dynamics.
> - A joint min-max optimization procedure allows for the efficient approximation of continuous neural network output distributions across a hyperparameter spectrum from sparsely observed ground-truth distributions.
