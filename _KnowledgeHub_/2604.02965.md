# Open-Loop Planning, Closed-Loop Verification: Speculative Verification for VLA

> [!summary] Summary
> SV-VLA, a framework from Southeast University and Nanyang Technological University, combines infrequent heavy VLA planning with continuous lightweight verification to improve robot control efficiency and robustness. On the LIBERO benchmark, it achieved a 90.9% success rate with a 2.17x speed-up, recovering 11.4% success over open-loop chunking methods.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models incur high inference latency, making them difficult to deploy as high-frequency closed-loop robot controllers.
- Open-loop action chunking improves efficiency by predicting action sequences but suffers from error accumulation and lack of adaptation to environmental changes due to stale observations.
- Standard speculative decoding from Natural Language Processing is not directly applicable to embodied control because future action validity depends on dynamic environmental interactions, not just static context.

## Method

- Introduced **SV-VLA** (==Speculative Verification for VLA Control==), a framework that decouples low-frequency ==macro-planning== from high-frequency lightweight ==verification==.
- A heavy VLA model acts as a ==Macro-Planner==, generating long action chunks and a concise ==planning context feature== at planning boundaries (e.g., task start or replanning events).
- A lightweight ==Verifier== continuously monitors execution, comparing planned macro actions with real-time reference actions based on current observations and the planning context, triggering replanning only when deviations exceed a threshold.

## Results

- **SV-VLA** achieved an average success rate of **90.9%** across LIBERO benchmark tasks, an **11.4%** improvement over a baseline open-loop chunking method (**79.5%**) with a comparable chunk size of 64.
- The framework demonstrated a **2.17x** inference speed-up compared to a closed-loop baseline (OpenVLA-OFT K=8), effectively balancing control robustness with computational efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the planning context feature and real-time current observations are crucial for the verifier's effectiveness, and the replanning mechanism is essential for recovering from execution errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing embodied control involves a combination of infrequent, computationally expensive planning and continuous, inexpensive verification.
> - The dynamic nature of embodied environments necessitates closed-loop verification, where future action validity is assessed against real-time observations, unlike static context-based verification in NLP.
> - A compact planning context feature extracted from the heavy VLA, combined with real-time visual observations, enables a lightweight verifier to effectively assess the consistency of an ongoing plan.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a significant development in embodied artificial intelligence, facilitating robot control through multimodal observations and natural language instructions. Models such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi_0$ leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining combined with robot demonstration data to exhibit zero-shot transfer capabilities and enhanced semantic understanding in complex, multi-step manipulation tasks within open-world environments. This represents an advancement over conventional task-specific policies trained on limited robotic data.

However, a principal challenge for the practical deployment of VLA models is their inference efficiency. Traditional closed-loop action prediction, where each action is generated based on the latest observation, allows continuous environmental adaptation but incurs substantial inference latency due to the sequential execution of the model at every timestep. To address this, action chunking methods predict a short sequence of future actions for open-loop execution, thereby reducing computational cost. Nevertheless, this approach is susceptible to error accumulation and environmental changes, as actions are executed based on potentially stale observations without incorporating real-time feedback.

A related technique, speculative decoding, originating in Natural Language Processing (NLP), has been explored to accelerate action token generation in VLA. This method typically uses a lightweight draft model to propose candidate actions, which are then verified by a full VLA model. In NLP, speculative decoding benefits from parallel verification because the validity of future tokens depends solely on the pre-generated textual context. However, its direct application to embodied control is limited because the validity of future actions in robotics depends on future observations that are dynamically generated by executed actions and environmental interactions, which are not available in advance. Prior VLA efforts using speculative decoding have largely maintained an open-loop verification paradigm, basing verification on a fixed, current observation rather than updated environmental feedback, thus failing to fully address the closed-loop responsiveness required for real-time robotic control.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing a framework that seeks to combine the efficiency benefits of action chunking with the robustness of closed-loop control. It aims to overcome the limitations of open-loop action chunking and the inherent incompatibility of traditional speculative decoding with the dynamic nature of embodied environments. The work attempts to bridge the gap between computationally intensive, accurate closed-loop control and efficient but less robust open-loop chunked execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and reliability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot control in dynamic environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations within the current state of VLA research and deployment:

1. **High Inference Latency of Large VLA Models:** Modern VLA models are characterized by their large parameter scales, which result in substantial inference latency. This latency makes them challenging to deploy as high-frequency closed-loop controllers, where actions must be continuously adapted based on real-time observations. The sequential decision-making process inherent in closed-loop control exacerbates this issue, as the large model must be invoked repeatedly at every control timestep.

2. **Limitations of Open-Loop Action Chunking:** While action chunking mitigates inference latency by predicting a sequence of future actions for open-loop execution, it introduces significant vulnerabilities. Actions within a chunk are executed without continuous feedback from the environment. This leads to issues such as:
 - **Reliance on Stale Observations:** Later actions in a chunk are based on observations from the initial planning step, which may no longer be accurate as the environment evolves.
 - **Error Accumulation:** Small deviations in early actions can compound over the execution of a chunk, leading to significant trajectory drift and task failures.
 - **Sensitivity to Environmental Changes:** The open-loop nature prevents the robot from reacting to unexpected perturbations, object displacements, or changes in the task state that occur during chunk execution.

3. **Inapplicability of Standard Speculative Decoding to Embodied Control:** Speculative decoding, a technique successful in accelerating Large Language Model (LLM) inference, is not directly transferable to embodied control in its standard form. In NLP, future token validity is context-dependent and can be verified in parallel from a static, drafted sequence. In robotics, however, the validity of future actions is contingent on future observations, which are generated dynamically by the execution of prior actions and environmental transitions. Consequently, action verification in embodied control requires sequential interaction with the environment and cannot be parallelized in the same manner. Existing VLA methods attempting speculative decoding often compromise by performing verification under stale observations, failing to incorporate the necessary closed-loop feedback for robust control.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a framework that combines the efficiency of long-horizon planning with the robustness of closed-time verification. The key insight is to decouple the computationally intensive planning process from the continuous monitoring and verification process. This aims to allow the VLA model to operate efficiently by generating macro action chunks (open-loop planning) while ensuring responsiveness and adaptability to dynamic environments through a lightweight, high-frequency verification mechanism (closed-loop verification) that can trigger replanning only when necessary.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Speculative Verification for VLA Control (SV-VLA), is designed to achieve efficient and adaptive Vision-Language-Action control by decoupling low-frequency macro planning from high-frequency lightweight verification.

The core principle of SV-VLA is that embodied control should involve infrequent but expensive planning, combined with continuous but inexpensive verification.

#### 3.1. System Architecture and Components

SV-VLA consists of two main components: a heavy VLA model acting as a Macro-Planner, and a lightweight Verifier module.

- **Heavy VLA Model (Macro-Planner):**
 - **Function:** This component is responsible for low-frequency, long-horizon planning. It is invoked only at specific "planning boundaries"—either at the beginning of a task or when a replanning event is triggered.
 - **Input:** At step $T_0$, the heavy VLA receives the current visual observation ($I_0$), the natural language instruction ($L$), and the proprioceptive state ($s_0$).
 - **Output:** It produces two main outputs:
 - An **action chunk ($A_{macro}$)**: A sequence of $K$ future actions, $[a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{K-1}]$, which represents a speculative open-loop prediction of future actions.
 - A **planning context feature ($F_0$)**: A compact, high-level representation summarizing the multimodal intent and scene context at the planning boundary. This feature is extracted from an intermediate Transformer layer (specifically, the second-to-top layer) of the heavy VLA.
 - **Operational Aspect:** During both deployment and the dedicated verifier training phase, the weights of the heavy VLA remain frozen. This ensures compatibility with existing pre-trained VLA models and focuses adaptation on the lightweight verifier.

- **Lightweight Verifier Module:**
 - **Function:** This component operates at a higher frequency than the macro-planner, continuously monitoring the execution state and assessing the validity of the pre-planned actions.
 - **Input:** At any subsequent step $t \in \{1, 2, \dots, K-1\}$ after the initial planning, the verifier takes the latest visual observation ($I_t$) and the fixed planning context feature ($F_0$) generated by the heavy VLA.
 - **Process:**
 1. The updated visual observation ($I_t$) is processed by a lightweight vision backbone ($\phi_{vit}$, e.g., ViT-Tiny) to extract a visual feature ($E_t$).
 2. The visual feature ($E_t$) is then concatenated with the planning context feature ($F_0$) and passed through a fully connected (FC) layer to create a fused representation ($Z_t$).
 3. This fused representation ($Z_t$) is fed into a lightweight verification head (e.g., an attention mechanism) to predict a **reference action ($a'_t$)**. This $a'_t$ is not directly executed but serves as a closed-loop estimate of what the action *should be* at the current state, consistent with the original high-level plan.
 - **Operational Aspect:** The verifier is designed to run at or near the control frequency, having a significantly lower inference latency ($T_{verify}$) compared to the heavy VLA ($T_{heavy}$).

#### 3.2. Deviation-based Replanning Mechanism

This mechanism dictates when to accept pre-planned actions and when to trigger replanning.

- **Discrepancy Measurement:** At each execution step $t$, the verifier calculates the discrepancy between the currently planned macro action ($a_t$) from $A_{macro}$ and its predicted closed-loop reference action ($a'_t$). The L1 distance is used for this comparison:
 $E_t = \text{norm}(\|a'_t - a_t\|_1)$
 where $\text{norm}(\cdot)$ scales the distance to a range based on the action space. $E_t$ quantifies how much the original macro action has diverged from an action locally consistent with the current visual observation.

- **Binary Execution Rule:** A predefined safety threshold ($\tau \in (0, 1)$) governs the decision:
 - **Acceptance:** If $E_t \le \tau$, the current state is considered compatible with the initial plan. The robot executes $a_t$.
 - **Rejection and Replanning:** If $E_t > \tau$, the discrepancy indicates a significant deviation from the original plan. In this scenario:
 - The remaining actions in the current chunk ($[a_t, \dots, a_{K-1}]$) are discarded.
 - The heavy VLA is immediately invoked to replan from the current state ($I_t, s_t$). This incurs an additional call to the heavy VLA. The first action of this newly generated chunk, denoted $\bar{a}_t$, is executed.

- **Efficiency Characterization:**
 - **Best Case:** If verification consistently accepts actions, the entire chunk $A_{macro}$ is executed. The per-step inference cost approaches $C_{min_{step}} = \frac{C_{VLA}}{K} + C_{verify}$, which is similar to standard open-loop chunking.
 - **Worst Case:** If replanning is triggered at every step, the system effectively performs step-wise execution. The per-step cost approaches $C_{max_{step}} = C_{VLA} + C_{verify} \approx C_{VLA}$, as $C_{verify}$ is negligible.
 SV-VLA operates between these bounds, adapting its frequency of heavy VLA calls based on execution reliability.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

The training of SV-VLA focuses solely on the lightweight verifier module, while the heavy VLA model remains frozen.

- **Objective:** The verifier learns to produce real-time reference actions that assess the local validity of the macro actions.
- **Data Generation:** For each timestep $t$ within a macro chunk, the verifier is trained using the current observation ($I_t$), the planning context feature ($F_0$), and the ground-truth action ($\hat{a}_t$) for that timestep.
- **Loss Function:** An L1 regression loss is used to train the verifier:
 $L_{verify} = \frac{1}{K-1} \sum_{t=1}^{K-1} \|a'_t - \hat{a}_t\|_1$
 This objective guides the verifier to predict actions ($a'_t$) that align with both the original high-level plan (through $F_0$) and the latest environmental state ($I_t$).
- **Benefits:** This training strategy ensures compatibility with existing pre-trained VLA models and minimizes additional computational overhead, as only the lightweight component requires training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the LIBERO benchmark, which includes LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Spatial, and LIBERO-Object task suites, designed to evaluate robot manipulation and policy generalization. The primary metrics were task success rate and inference speed-up relative to a baseline. The heavy VLA model used was OpenVLA-OFT with a chunk size of 64.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison (Efficiency vs. Robustness)

- **Baseline Performance:**
 - `BASE (K=8)`: Achieved the highest average success rate (96.0%) but had the lowest inference speed-up (1.00x), indicating frequent, expensive heavy VLA calls.
 - `BASE (K=64)` (Open-loop): Demonstrated the highest average speed-up (3.15x) but suffered a substantial drop in average success rate to 79.5%, particularly severe on LIBERO-Object (77.2%) and LIBERO-Spatial (68.0%). This confirms the trade-off of naive action chunking, where efficiency comes at the cost of robustness.

- **Speculative Decoding Comparison:**
 - `Speculative Decoding` (chunk size 4): Achieved an average success rate of 81.7% with a 1.36x speed-up. While providing some inference benefits, it did not resolve the execution mismatch in embodied control, performing verification under stale observations.

- **SV-VLA Performance:**
 - SV-VLA (K=64) achieved an average success rate of 90.9% and a 2.17x speed-up. This indicates a more favorable balance between efficiency and robustness compared to both `BASE (K=64)` and `Speculative Decoding`.
 - Specifically, SV-VLA significantly improved the average success rate over `BASE (K=64)` by 11.4% (from 79.5% to 90.9%).
 - Across individual task suites, SV-VLA consistently outperformed `BASE (K=64)`: LIBERO-Goal (94.4% vs 93.2%), LIBERO-Object (95.3% vs 77.2%), and LIBERO-Spatial (83.0% vs 68.0%).
 - Compared to `Speculative Decoding`, SV-VLA achieved higher success rates on LIBERO-Goal (94.4% vs 74.4%) and LIBERO-Object (95.3% vs 85.0%) with generally larger speed-ups. On LIBERO-Spatial, `Speculative Decoding` had a slightly higher success rate (85.8% vs 83.0%) but with a lower acceleration (1.28x vs 2.49x).

#### 4.2. Inference Time Breakdown

Analysis of inference-time composition revealed the following:
- **Cost Discrepancy:** The average macro-planner call took 1.373 seconds, while a lightweight verifier call took only 0.081 seconds. This substantial cost difference motivated the design of sparse heavy-model calls and frequent lightweight verification.
- **Call Statistics per Episode:**
 - `BASE (K=8)`: 14.5 macro-planner calls, 0 verifier calls, 15.9 seconds average time, 96.00% success.
 - `BASE (K=64)`: 4.2 macro-planner calls, 0 verifier calls, 5.7 seconds average time, 79.47% success.
 - `SV-VLA (K=64)`: 6.7 macro-planner calls, 13.3 verifier calls, 8.8 seconds average time, 90.90% success.
- SV-VLA significantly reduced expensive macro-planner calls compared to `BASE (K=8)` by executing longer chunks and replanning only when necessary. Despite adding 13.3 inexpensive verifier calls, the total inference time per episode (8.8s) was considerably lower than `BASE (K=8)`. Compared to `BASE (K=64)`, SV-VLA incurred a moderate increase in inference time (8.8s vs 5.7s), but this overhead led to a substantial improvement in control reliability (from 79.47% to 90.90%).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablations on LIBERO-Spatial demonstrated the contribution of key SV-VLA components:
- **Importance of Context Feature ($F_0$):** Removing $F_0$ from the verifier (relying only on current observation) reduced success from 83.0% to 73.7%. This indicates the utility of the high-level planning context for maintaining plan consistency.
- **Importance of Current Observation ($I_t$):** Removing $I_t$ from the verifier (relying only on $F_0$) caused a larger success drop to 63.7%. This highlights the critical role of real-time visual feedback for detecting execution drift and scene changes.
- **Importance of Replanning:** The `w/o replanning` variant, which executes verifier-predicted actions without invoking the heavy VLA for replanning, performed poorly with only 15.5% success. This confirms that lightweight verification alone is insufficient for recovery from invalid trajectories; the replanning mechanism is crucial for error correction.
- **Sensitivity to Threshold ($\tau$):**
 - $\tau=0.1$: 83.1% success, 2.17x speed-up, 28.0 executed steps before replanning.
 - $\tau=0.2$: 83.0% success, 2.49x speed-up, 37.2 executed steps before replanning.
 - $\tau=0.4$: 77.4% success, 2.60x speed-up, 40.5 executed steps before replanning.
 A smaller $\tau$ led to more frequent replanning and slightly lower speed-up but maintained robustness. A larger $\tau$ resulted in longer open-loop execution and higher speed-up but reduced robustness. This demonstrates $\tau$ as a tunable parameter for balancing reliability and efficiency.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

A qualitative example (picking up a bowl and placing it on a plate) illustrated SV-VLA's functionality:
- SV-VLA detected a failed grasp during execution, interrupted the current chunk, and triggered replanning from the latest observation. This enabled the robot to successfully re-attempt the manipulation.
- `BASE (K=64)`, lacking correction mechanisms, continued executing the entire chunk after the failed grasp, leading to task failure due to uncorrected error accumulation.
- `BASE (K=8)` remained robust through frequent replanning but incurred significantly higher computational costs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SV-VLA, a framework that addresses a critical challenge in the deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: balancing inference efficiency with control robustness in dynamic environments. The proposed method introduces a novel approach that differentiates it from existing action chunking strategies and direct adaptations of speculative decoding from Natural Language Processing.

The significance of SV-VLA lies in several aspects:

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Robustness Gap:** The work directly tackles the fundamental trade-off between the computational efficiency of open-loop action chunking and the robustness of closed-loop control. By combining a heavy VLA model for infrequent macro-planning with a lightweight verifier for high-frequency, real-time observation-based verification, SV-VLA provides a practical solution to maintain both high task success rates and reduced amortized inference costs. This is particularly relevant for real-world robotic applications where both factors are crucial.

2. **Novel Application of Speculative Principles:** Unlike prior speculative decoding approaches for VLA that largely operated under open-loop assumptions, SV-VLA introduces "Speculative Verification." This distinction is critical because it acknowledges the dynamic nature of embodied environments, where future action validity depends on actual environment interaction and updated observations, not just static context. This reinterpretation of speculative methods for robotics provides a pathway for accelerating VLA inference while preserving closed-loop responsiveness.

3. **Practical Deployment Enablement:** The framework's design allows for the preservation of existing VLA models, as the heavy VLA macro-planner remains frozen. Only the lightweight verifier module requires training, minimizing the burden of adapting pre-trained foundation models for improved real-time performance. This makes SV-VLA a readily deployable solution that can leverage the capabilities of current state-of-the-art VLA models.

4. **Adaptive Control Strategy:** The Deviation-based Replanning mechanism offers an adaptive control strategy. It avoids unnecessary computationally expensive replanning when execution proceeds as planned, but promptly intervenes when deviations occur. This dynamic adjustment of replanning frequency, influenced by a tunable threshold, provides flexibility for practitioners to balance risk tolerance with efficiency requirements for different tasks.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for the field of embodied AI and robotics:

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By enabling more reliable and efficient VLA-based control, SV-VLA could facilitate the deployment of robots in more complex, dynamic, and less structured environments, moving closer to general-purpose robot assistants.
- **Reduced Operational Costs:** The improved inference efficiency can lead to lower computational resource requirements in deployment, making advanced VLA capabilities more accessible.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a robust baseline and methodology for further research into hybrid planning-verification architectures. The identified limitations, such as fixed discrepancy thresholds, suggest clear avenues for future work, including adaptive triggering strategies and the integration of local corrections by the verifier.
- **Broader Impact on VLA Development:** As VLA models continue to grow in complexity, solutions like SV-VLA that address practical deployment challenges will be essential for their widespread adoption and continued development.
