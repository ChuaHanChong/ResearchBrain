# Zetta $ζ$: An Efficient Closed-Loop Embodied Harness for Self-Evolving Physical Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Zetta is a closed-loop embodied harness that keeps a base VLA/WAM policy frozen while evolving code-based runtime critics and recovery skills through three timescale-separated loops (action-frequency governance, rollout-batch proposal, validation-gated update). Paired with Z-Infra rollout infrastructure, it reaches 90.8% LIBERO-Pro and 93.6% RoboCasa success with 11.1x inference speedup.

## Problem

- Embodied agent harnesses remain open-loop, following fixed skills and reflecting only after episode completion, unable to govern execution as physical states change.
- Post-hoc reflection cannot test alternative actions online, struggles with credit assignment over full trajectories, and lacks precise failure-moment state access.
- Ad-hoc debugging of physical failures overfits repair to single instances, corrupting the action distribution and degrading generalization to unseen seeds.

## Method

- ==Critic-Governed Action Loop== runs learned ==code-based critics== at action frequency, generating structured failure proposals adjudicated by a fixed ==Orchestrator Agent== before invoking recovery skills.
- ==Top-Down Hierarchical Causal Diagnosis== traverses Evaluation, Critic, State, Planning, Recovery, Parameter layers to localize the minimal-effective repair layer, avoiding over-parameterized fixes.
- ==Validation-Gated Skill Update== consolidates seed-specific patches via a ==Generalization Agent== into a versioned harness package, gated by held-out evaluation before admission to skill memory.

## Results

- Success scales from **34.5%** to **90.8%** on LIBERO-Pro and from **73.6%** to **93.6%** on RoboCasa across evolution rounds, with the base policy left entirely frozen.
- Zero-shot skill transfer lifts pick-and-place tasks from **64%** to **84%** macro-average and articulated-interaction tasks from **64%** to **80%**, without target-task adaptation.
- Z-Infra raises valid rollout throughput **20.6x**, from **1.7** to **35.1** episodes per minute, and cuts inference latency **91%** versus the RPent baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frozen-policy governance shows execution reliability can scale by accumulating cheap runtime mechanisms rather than retraining large models.
> - Rollout throughput becomes the rate-limiter of self-evolution, so infrastructure decoupling agent logic from hardware directly accelerates learning speed.
> - Sharp non-monotonic 'aha moment' jumps in success indicate that early fixes can overfit until the true physical bottleneck mechanism is isolated.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of physical intelligence is progressing along two main complementary paths to achieve scalable and reliable robot control. The first path focuses on scaling end-to-end policy models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs), by training them on large-scale demonstration datasets. While these models have demonstrated capabilities in semantic understanding, their deployment in real-world scenarios faces challenges due to data scarcity, brittleness under dynamic real-world conditions, and the cascading effect of minor execution errors in long-horizon tasks. The gap between demonstration and reliable real-world execution persists.

The second path involves employing large language models (LLMs) as embodied agents to orchestrate various components, including policy models, code, tools, and control primitives. This approach aims to facilitate learning through self-exploration in diverse environments. However, existing embodied agent harnesses typically operate in an open-loop manner. Once execution commences, these agents tend to follow fixed skills or pre-planned trajectories without continuously adapting to the evolving robot-environment state. Reflection and learning usually occur only after an episode is completed (post-hoc reflection). This episodic reflection has inherent limitations: it cannot govern execution in real-time, struggles with credit assignment over entire trajectories, and often lacks precise state information at the moment of failure, making experience reuse difficult and adaptation to changing dynamics poor. Real-time physical interaction demands decision-making at millisecond-level latencies, a frequency beyond the current capabilities of large agentic models.

Zetta addresses this identified gap by introducing a closed-loop embodied harness that enables online self-evolution of code-based runtime critics and recovery skills. Unlike prior work that relies on episodic reflection or fixed policies, Zetta aims to facilitate continuous learning and adaptation during physical execution, thereby closing the semantic-physics gap and allowing for sustained improvement in success rates without modifying the base policy model. This work extends the capabilities of embodied agents beyond simple orchestration to include active, real-time governance and learning from self-exploration, positioning it within the domain of self-evolving agents for continuous physical intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a closed-loop learning paradigm for embodied agents during physical execution, allowing them to self-evolve and improve their reliability and task success over time. This is motivated by several limitations observed in current approaches to robotic control:

1. **Lack of Real-time Execution Governance:** Existing embodied agent harnesses largely operate in an open-loop fashion. They execute a sequence of actions or skills and only reflect on the outcome once an entire episode concludes. This post-hoc reflection is insufficient for dynamic physical environments where robot-environment states change rapidly, requiring immediate decisions and interventions. Large agentic models cannot provide decisions at the necessary high frequency (millisecond level) for real-time physical interaction.
2. **Ineffectiveness of Post-Hoc Reflection:** When reflection only occurs after an episode's completion, agents cannot test alternative actions to verify the correctness of their reflections. Assigning credit or blame for an entire trajectory of failure is difficult, and precise state information at the moment of failure is often lost. This diminishes the reusability and effectiveness of learned experiences for subsequent learning.
3. **Brittleness of Frozen Foundation Models:** While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World-Action Models (WAMs) show promise for general robot control, they are often brittle in real-world deployment due to the scarcity of diverse training data and the inherent variability of physical dynamics. Minor disturbances or deviations can cascade into complete task failures because these models lack mechanisms for real-time error correction.
4. **Bottleneck of Manual Debugging:** Relying on human experts to manually diagnose and patch individual failure instances across a wide range of tasks and initial conditions is resource-intensive and does not scale to general-purpose manipulation. There is a need for automated, systemic approaches to identify and resolve root causes of failures.
5. **Limited Rollout Throughput:** The iterative nature of self-evolution implies that the rate of learning is directly tied to the speed at which interaction data (rollouts) can be generated. Existing rollout infrastructures are often designed for standard reinforcement learning workloads and may not efficiently handle the heterogeneous compute demands (VLA inference, perception models, diverse simulators, tool calls) of self-evolving embodied agents.

To address these motivations, Zetta aims to:
- Introduce **code-based runtime critics** that operate at action frequency to continuously monitor physical execution and trigger interventions when deviations occur, thereby enabling closed-loop governance.
- Develop a system that **self-evolves** these critics and corresponding recovery skills online, maintaining the base policy models in a frozen state to preserve their generalization capabilities.
- Implement a **multi-timescale learning framework** with three coordinated loops: an action-frequency governance loop, a rollout-batch level candidate optimization loop, and a validation-gated skill update loop.
- Create a specialized **rollout infrastructure (Z-Infra)** that decouples agent logic from heterogeneous execution resources, significantly increasing rollout throughput and accelerating the self-evolution process.
- Optimize the evolvable harness (critics, recoveries, tools) to **maximize task success rates** while ensuring generalizability across different task instances and transferability of learned skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Zetta framework is designed to enable deployment-time evolution of embodied agents through a dual-agent governance and evolutionary architecture, supported by a specialized rollout infrastructure.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Dual-Agent Governance and Evolutionary Architecture

The system conceptualizes robotic manipulation as an authority-constrained governance and evolution process.

- **Fixed Runtime Components:**
 - **Action Policy ($\pi$):** A frozen, pre-trained VLA or WAM model that generates low-level actions ($a_t = \pi(s_t, g; \theta)$). Its parameters ($\theta$) remain invariant.
 - **Orchestrator Agent ($A_{orch}$):** A fixed multimodal reasoning component responsible for auditing real-time evidence, approving mode transitions (e.g., from VLA execution to recovery), and maintaining consistent decision logic throughout evolution.
- **Evolvable Harness (H):** This component is the target of the evolutionary process and is defined as $H = \{C, R, T\}$.
 - **Runtime Critic (C):** A set of high-frequency monitoring functions that scan the trajectory ($\tau_{0:t}$) to generate structured proposals ($P_t = \langle e_t, \hat{\sigma}_t \rangle$), indicating evidence of failure ($e_t$) and a suggested execution mode ($\hat{\sigma}_t$).
 - **Recovery Playbook (R):** A structured library of strategies mapped to specific causal failure mechanisms, providing actionable options for intervention.
 - **Heterogeneous Toolset (T):** A collection of executable tools and operators (e.g., planners, grasp detectors) that can be generated, instantiated, selected, and refined during evolution to support critics and recovery logic.
- **Authority Hierarchy:** During execution, the Orchestrator Agent ($A_{orch}$) uses the real-time proposal from $C$, along with $R$, $T$, and task knowledge $K$, to determine the final operational mode ($\sigma_t$). This ensures evidence-driven decision-making, where interventions are only permitted if $A_{orch}$ validates the evidence.
- **Offline Optimization: Evolutionary Agents ($A_{evo}$):** These agents drive offline refinement by iteratively improving the harness components ($H^{(k+1)} \leftarrow A_{evo}(D_{fail}^{(k)}, H^{(k)})$ based on failed rollout data. $A_{evo}$ extracts causal mechanisms, generates repair patches, and abstracts local experiences into versioned harnesses. The objective is to maximize the expected task success rate $J(H)$ while keeping $\pi_{VLA}$ and $A_{orch}$ invariant.

#### 3.2. Three Time-Scale Loops for Self-Evolution

Zetta realizes self-evolution through three timescale-separated loops:

1. **Critic-Governed Action Loop (High-Frequency):**
 - Operates at the action frequency, with runtime critics ($C$) continuously monitoring the physical execution state.
 - When a critic detects a deviation or potential failure (e.g., object slippage, collision), it generates a structured proposal ($P_t$).
 - If the Orchestrator Agent ($A_{orch}$) validates the evidence, it invokes a corresponding recovery skill from $R$, temporarily overriding the base policy $\pi$.
 - Control is returned to $\pi$ only after the recovery has resolved the failure and the physical state has been verified to be stable (VLA Re-entry Contract).

2. **Rollout-Batch Candidate Optimization Loop (Batch Level - Phase I & II):**
 - **Phase I: Empirical Failure Profiling and Baseline Establishment:**
 - Large-scale rollouts of the initial action policy $\pi$ are conducted across a development seed set $D_{dev}$.
 - Deterministic scheduling and execution protocols ensure reproducibility and distinguish infrastructure failures from policy failures. Invalid rollouts are rerun.
 - Multi-dimensional evidence (state, action, semantic milestones $\mu_t$, physio-auxiliary signals $\phi_t$) is acquired and stored.
 - Rollouts are categorized: Successful Reference Index ($I_{succ}$) for nominal behavior, and Failed-Seed Manifest ($M_{fail}$) with the First Missing Milestone ($m^*$) to localize failure stages.
 - **Phase II Stage 1: Failure Clustering and Causal Diagnosis:**
 - Failed seeds in $M_{fail}$ are clustered based on their Earliest Observable Divergence (EOD) from successful trajectories.
 - A medoid seed ($seed_{med}$) is selected for each cluster for in-depth causal diagnosis by a Diagnosis Agent ($A_{diag}$).
 - A strict single-view grounded observation protocol is enforced for multimodal models to ensure reasoning consistency.
 - $A_{diag}$ performs a Top-Down Hierarchical Causal Diagnosis across diagnostic layers (e.g., Evaluation, Critic, State, Planning, Recovery, Parameter) to pinpoint the root cause ($L^*$) at the highest possible abstraction level. This minimizes intervention.
 - A Repair Candidate Specification is output for each validated mechanism.
 - **Phase II Stage 2: Critic-Guided Harness Repair and Validation:**
 - A Repair Agent ($A_{repr}$) implements a minimal harness patch ($H_{patch} = \{C^*, R^*, T^*\}$) targeting the identified $L^*$.
 - This involves enhancing critics, drafting recovery playbooks, and adapting/synthesizing tools.
 - A crucial **VLA Re-entry Contract ($\Psi(s_t)$)** is embedded in recovery logic, defining strict conditions (failure cleared, physical state stability) for returning control to the base VLA policy.
 - The patch undergoes rigorous closed-loop validation on the original medoid seed, requiring successful task completion and proper adjudication of all interventions.

3. **Validation-Gated Skill Update Loop (Iteration Level - Phase III):**
 - **Phase III: Harness Consolidation, Packaging and Generalization:**
 - A Generalization Agent ($A_{gen}$) consolidates validated, seed-specific patches into a unified, robust governance harness ($H_{merged} = \{C_{merged}, R_{merged}, T_{merged}\}$). This involves Critic Unification (logical OR on triggers, dynamic thresholding), Recovery Integration (generalizing action sequences), and Toolset Expansion (including newly synthesized specialized physical skills).
 - The merged harness is packaged into a portable, standardized file structure (e.g., SKILL.md for logic, tools/ for executable implementations, plans/ for playbooks, params/ for configuration).
 - **Rigorous Generalization Validation:** $H_{merged}$ is subjected to Historical Regression (100% success on original failure cluster) and Held-Out Evaluation on a strictly isolated dataset $D_{held-out}$ to confirm broader applicability and calculate success rate increment ($\Delta SR$).
 - A Dynamic Transition Protocol ensures that if $H_{merged}$ fails on $D_{held-out}$, the failed seed is reclassified to $D_{dev}$, and a new held-out set is used for final validation before closing the iteration.

#### 3.3. Z-Infra: Embodied Agent Rollout Infrastructure

To support and accelerate the self-evolution process, Zetta introduces Z-Infra, an infrastructure designed for efficient, large-scale parallel rollouts of self-evolving embodied agents.

- **Challenges Addressed:** Resource Heterogeneity (diverse compute for simulation, policy, perception, primitives) and Execution Dynamism (unpredictable tool invocation, bursty inference demands).
- **Key Idea: Decoupled Abstraction Layers:** Agent logic is fully separated from hardware resource management, allowing agents to interact with a virtual rollout interface while Z-Infra handles worker allocation, request routing, batching, and hardware mapping.
- **System Architecture:** Three layers.
 - **Control Plane:** Single entry point, unified API, request routing, global session registry, worker health monitoring.
 - **Environment Worker Layer:** Manages lifecycle of simulation environments (session abstraction: CREATE $\to$ RUNNING $\to$ TERMINATED). Supports diverse environment families via declarative registration (init, reset, step, obs_schema). Implements a resource-sharing group for MuJoCo-based environments, amortizing model compilation and rendering context setup, using fork primitives and an optimized C++ controller.
 - **Rollout Worker Layer:** GPU-resident model serving for VLAs and perception models. Features a Scheduler for managing inference requests (compatibility grouping, FCFS dispatch, asynchronous result delivery). Utilizes Model Partitioning for VLA/WAMs (VLM and Action Expert processes, CUDA IPC for activations) and Quantization Runtime (module-specific quantization) to enhance inference throughput and memory efficiency.
- **Implementation:** Built on Ray for distributed execution, using Ray actors for workers and bounded Ray Channels for communication.

This comprehensive methodology allows Zetta to continuously improve embodied agent performance by systematically identifying, diagnosing, repairing, and generalizing physical execution failures through a closed-loop, data-driven evolution cycle, underpinned by a high-throughput execution infrastructure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on LIBERO-Pro and RoboCasa benchmarks demonstrate that Zetta achieves improved task success, scalable performance, zero-shot skill transfer, and accelerated execution.

- **Overall Task Success:** Zetta significantly improves task success rates compared to baseline frozen VLA policies.
 - On **RoboCasa**, the macro-average success rate across 18 atomic tasks increased from 73.56% (Pure VLA, GR00T) to 93.56% with Zetta, representing an absolute gain of 20.00 percentage points. Improvements were observed across all tasks, with notable gains in contact-rich or long-horizon manipulation tasks.
 - On **LIBERO-Pro**, the overall macro-average success rate across 40 task-setting pairs improved from 32.00% (Pure VLA, $\pi_{0.5}$) to 71.13% with Zetta, an absolute gain of 39.13 percentage points. Specifically, for Goal (T) tasks, success rose from 31.0% to 92.5%; for Goal (S) tasks, it increased from 38.0% to 89.0%. On the more challenging LIBERO-10 settings, Zetta improved (T) from 50.0% to 63.0% and (S) from 9.0% to 40.0%. Zetta improved or matched the baseline performance on every task-setting pair without additional VLA training.

- **Physical Intelligence Scaling:** Success rates consistently scale with increased self-exploration experience and cumulative evolution iterations.
 - On **LIBERO-Pro**, for Goal-T, the average success rate increased from 31.0% (Pure VLA) to 92.5% after four rounds of cumulative Critic–Recovery additions. For Goal-S, it increased from 38.0% to 89.0% over the same period.
 - On **RoboCasa**, the 18-task macro-average success rate increased from 73.56% to 93.56% through four cumulative global repair rounds. These gains were achieved by accumulating validated execution knowledge within the harness, not by increasing model capacity or fine-tuning VLA weights.

- **Zero-shot Skill Transfer Capability:** Learned critic-recovery mechanisms demonstrate zero-shot transferability to similar, unseen tasks.
 - **Pick-and-Place (PnP) Tasks on RoboCasa:** Mechanisms (pre-grasp alignment, re-grasp after loss, stable placement) discovered during evolution on `PnP-Stove` were applied zero-shot to `PnP-Sink`, `PnP-Cabinet`, and `PnP-Toaster`. This resulted in an increase in the macro-average success rate over these transfer tasks from 64% to 84%, indicating that general pick-and-place principles were learned.
 - **Articulated Interaction Tasks on RoboCasa:** Skills (target localization, collision-aware approach, stable-contact) evolved on `TurnOffStove` were transferred zero-shot to `TurnOnSinkFaucet`, `OpenCabinet`, and `TurnOnMicrowave`. The transfer tasks' macro-average increased from 64% to 80%. These results suggest that the framework learns reusable mappings from physical failure states to robust recovery behaviors rather than task-specific trajectories.

- **Physical Intelligence “Aha” Moments:** The system exhibited discontinuous jumps in success rates when critical physical bottlenecks were identified and resolved, transitioning from stagnant to high performance.
 - On **LIBERO-Pro Goal-T2** (placing a bottle in a bowl), success rose abruptly from 15% to 95% after identifying and addressing grasp retention as the decisive bottleneck.
 - On **LIBERO-Pro Goal-T8** (placing a bottle on a plate), success increased from 10% to 60% after implementing a recovery that monitored the full contact–grasp–retention sequence.
 - On **LIBERO-Pro Goal-S6** (putting cream cheese on a bowl), success jumped from 5% to 90% after resolving the lack of semantic progress during the pre-contact approach phase.
 - On **RoboCasa `TurnOnElectricKettle`**, success increased from 88% to 94% by recognizing the need for simple End-Effector (EEF) re-alignment. Similar jumps were observed for `SlideDishwasherRack` (76% to 94%) and `CloseToasterOvenDoor` (82% to 96%) when key physical bottlenecks were addressed.

- **Z-Infra Performance Evaluation:** The dedicated rollout infrastructure demonstrated efficiency and scalability.
 - **Inference Speedup:** Zetta achieved an 11.1x inference speedup, reducing inference latency by 91% compared to RPent.
 - **Rollout Throughput:** Z-Infra increased valid rollout throughput from 1.7 to 35.1 episodes/min, representing a 20.6x improvement, directly accelerating the harness evolution loop.
 - **Latency Under Load:** Z-Infra maintained controlled latency growth under high concurrency, with per-episode latency increasing from 39s at concurrency 8 to 95s at concurrency 64. In contrast, baselines exhibited explosive degradation (e.g., Ours w/o Z-Infra from 34s to 112s at concurrency 16) or inherently high overhead (RPent from 392s to 513s).
 - **Throughput Scaling:** Z-Infra enabled near-linear throughput scaling, reaching 35.1 episodes/min at concurrency 64. At concurrency 16, Z-Infra achieved 7.7x higher throughput than Z-Harness w/o Z-Infra and 12.8x higher than RPent. Baselines failed to scale beyond concurrency 16 due to Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors.

These results indicate that Zetta's closed-loop self-evolution, supported by the efficient Z-Infra, provides a viable method for enhancing the reliability and capabilities of embodied agents in physical interaction tasks without requiring modification of the underlying frozen policy models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Zetta, a closed-loop embodied harness, and Z-Infra, its supporting infrastructure, which together represent a development in self-evolving physical intelligence. The work introduces a methodology that addresses several limitations of current embodied AI systems and offers implications for the future of robot autonomy.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Embodied Learning

Zetta provides a mechanism for enabling closed-loop learning in physical execution, departing from the prevalent open-loop, post-hoc reflection approaches. By evolving code-based runtime critics and recovery skills online, Zetta allows embodied agents to continuously govern their execution and adapt to rapidly changing robot-environment states. This shift from episodic reflection to real-time intervention and learning could make robotic systems more robust and adaptable in dynamic, unstructured environments.

#### 5.2. Scalability of Physical Intelligence

The findings demonstrate that physical execution reliability can scale through the cumulative acquisition of compact, reusable runtime mechanisms, rather than solely relying on larger models or extensive fine-tuning of foundation policies. Zetta's ability to significantly improve task success rates on challenging benchmarks (LIBERO-Pro and RoboCasa) without modifying the frozen base VLA policies suggests a practical path for enhancing the performance of existing foundation models in deployment. This approach can extend the utility of current VLAs by providing a robust "harness" that addresses their brittleness under real-world conditions.

#### 5.3. Generalizable and Transferable Skills

The observation of zero-shot transfer capabilities for learned critic-recovery mechanisms across similar tasks is significant. It indicates that Zetta learns general physical principles (e.g., grasp stability, effective approach geometry) rather than task-specific trajectories. This suggests that the scalable unit of physical intelligence might be a reusable mapping from observable physical failure states to robust recovery behaviors. Such generalizable skills could reduce the need for extensive task-specific data collection and training, accelerating the development of versatile robots.

#### 5.4. Infrastructure for Self-Evolving Agents

Z-Infra is presented as the first rollout infrastructure specifically designed to support self-evolving embodied agents. By efficiently decoupling agent logic from heterogeneous execution resources, it addresses the unique computational demands of embodied AI, including parallel VLA inference, tool calls, and diverse simulation environments. The demonstrated improvements in rollout throughput and controlled latency growth (20.6x faster rollouts, 11.1x inference speedup) directly accelerate the iterative self-evolution process. This infrastructure is a foundational component for scaling embodied learning and reflection in complex, dynamic scenarios.

#### 5.5. Potential for Real-World Deployment

The ability of Zetta to robustly handle failures and continuously improve performance in simulation suggests its potential for application in real-world robotics. The current focus on freezing the base policy and evolving a lightweight harness aligns with the practical constraints of deploying large foundation models on physical robots. Future work extending Zetta and Z-Infra to real robots, bridging the sim-to-real gap, and integrating real-robot environments as first-class workers, could unlock widespread adoption of general-purpose robots in diverse applications. This could lead to more reliable, autonomous, and continuously improving robotic systems capable of performing long-horizon tasks in human environments.
