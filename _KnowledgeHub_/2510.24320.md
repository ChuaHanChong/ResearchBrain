# Critique-RL: Training Language Models for Critiquing through Two-Stage Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Critique-RL, developed by Fudan University and ByteDance Seed, introduces a two-stage reinforcement learning framework to train language models for critiquing other LLM outputs without strong supervision. This method jointly optimizes a critic's discriminability and helpfulness, leading to actor models achieving up to a 9.02% accuracy gain on in-domain tasks and 5.70% on out-of-domain tasks.

## Problem

- Existing methods for training LLM critique models heavily depend on expensive, hard-to-scale human or oracle supervision for critique data annotation.
- Prior reinforcement learning approaches for training critics using indirect reward signals often fail to jointly optimize both the critic's discriminability (accuracy in judging correctness) and helpfulness (quality of feedback).
- Many prompt engineering methods for critiquing implicitly assume an external oracle verifier during testing, failing to address the critical need for a critic to autonomously determine output correctness.

## Method

- **Critique-RL** employs a two-player ==actor-critic== reinforcement learning framework where a critic model assesses an actor's response and guides its refinement.
- A novel two-stage RL strategy is introduced: Stage I explicitly optimizes the critic's discriminability using direct, rule-based reward signals, and Stage II then enhances helpfulness using indirect rewards while maintaining discriminability with regularization terms.
- The framework uses online RL to train the critic without stronger supervision and without requiring an oracle verifier at test time, addressing limitations of previous methods.

## Results

- Critique-RL achieved up to a **9.02%** average accuracy gain on in-domain reasoning tasks and **5.70%** on out-of-domain tasks for Qwen2.5-**7B** actor models.
- The method significantly outperformed other online RL baselines, showing an average improvement of 5.11 points in actor accuracy and 12.69 points in critic discriminability over Retroformer for Qwen2.5-**7B**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both RL stages and the direct discriminability reward, demonstrating that the two-stage design is critical for achieving high performance in both discriminability and helpfulness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Indirect reward signals, commonly used in prior RL-based critic training, are insufficient to jointly optimize a critic's discriminability and helpfulness, often leading to critics that are either overly conservative or aggressive.
> - Explicitly disentangling and sequentially optimizing discriminability before helpfulness, with appropriate regularization, is crucial for developing robust and effective critique models.
> - An autonomous critique-refinement loop trained through this two-stage RL can significantly improve actor LLM performance while also being more compute-efficient than parallel sampling methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses the critical challenge of "scalable oversight" for large language models (LLMs), particularly for complex tasks like reasoning, coding, and decision-making where human supervision is difficult, expensive, or prone to errors. The paper positions itself within the broader context of improving LLMs by training auxiliary "critiquing language models" (or critics) to assess and provide feedback on the outputs of primary "actor" LLMs.

Historically, the development of such critique models has followed a few main trajectories, each with its limitations:
- **Stronger Supervision:** Many existing methods rely on extensive, high-quality critique data annotated by "stronger supervisors" (e.g., humans or even more powerful LLMs like GPT-4o). While effective, this approach is inherently costly, labor-intensive, and difficult to scale, posing a significant bottleneck for continuous LLM improvement. Moreover, such supervisor-labeled data might not always align with the distribution of a learner model's outputs.
- **Prompt Engineering:** Another line of work leverages prompt engineering to elicit critiquing and self-reflection abilities directly from LLMs. However, these methods often implicitly assume the availability of an "oracle verifier" during the testing phase to determine the correctness of responses. This allows the critique model to focus solely on generating helpful feedback, bypassing the crucial aspect of accurately *discriminating* between correct and incorrect outputs. In real-world scenarios without such an oracle, these methods often encounter performance plateaus.
- **Self-Improvement Methods:** Some recent efforts explore models that self-critique and self-correct through iterative self-training, moving away from external human annotation. While promising, Critique-RL differentiates itself by adopting a "two-player paradigm" with a distinct, separated critic model.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs:** RL has become fundamental in LLM post-training, notably with RLHF for alignment. More recently, RL has been applied to enhance LLM performance in reasoning, coding, and decision-making, including improving self-reflection and self-correction. Within this RL landscape, some approaches like Retroformer (Yao et al., 2024) and CTRL (Xie et al., 2025) use indirect reward signals (e.g., based on the actor's final output correctness) to optimize a critique model's *helpfulness*. However, a critical gap identified by this paper is that these methods tend to overlook the joint optimization of the critic's *discriminability* (the ability to accurately judge correctness) alongside its *helpfulness* (the ability to provide constructive feedback). This leads to critics that are either overly conservative or aggressive, ultimately hindering overall performance.

Critique-RL directly addresses these limitations by proposing an online RL approach that develops critiquing language models *without stronger supervision* and *without relying on an oracle verifier at test time*. Its core contribution to the landscape is a novel two-stage RL strategy designed to *jointly and explicitly optimize both the discriminability and helpfulness* of the critique model, resolving the conflict identified in previous indirect reward-based RL methods. This positions Critique-RL as a significant advancement in creating autonomous, reliable, and scalable LLM oversight mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop highly effective critiquing language models that can accurately assess and provide constructive feedback on the outputs of other LLMs, specifically for complex reasoning tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the shortcomings of existing approaches:

1. **Overcoming Stronger Supervision Dependency:** The most significant motivation is to bypass the reliance on expensive and hard-to-scale "stronger supervisors" for annotating critique data. Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for critique models necessitates human-curated or oracle-generated critiques, which is a major bottleneck for large-scale, continuous LLM improvement.
2. **Eliminating Oracle Verifier Assumption:** The paper aims to develop critics that do not require an "oracle verifier" during testing. Many prior prompt-engineering methods, while eliciting helpful feedback, assume an external mechanism to ascertain correctness, allowing the critique model to avoid the harder task of discrimination. Critique-RL seeks to train models that can *both* determine correctness *and* provide feedback autonomously.
3. **Addressing the Discriminability-Helpfulness Imbalance in RL:** A crucial motivation arises from the empirical observation that existing RL-based methods using indirect reward signals for training critics (like Retroformer and CTRL) fail to develop "satisfactory" models. While these methods might improve the critic's *helpfulness* (i.e., its ability to provide constructive feedback that leads to better actor performance), they significantly neglect or even impair its *discriminability* (i.e., its ability to accurately determine whether an original response is high-quality or not). This imbalance leads to critics exhibiting undesirable behaviors—either being "overly conservative" (not correcting errors effectively) or "overly aggressive" (incorrectly suggesting changes to correct answers), ultimately resulting in marginal performance gains for the actor.
4. **Enabling Scalable and Adaptable Oversight:** The ultimate motivation is to contribute a robust and generalizable method for scalable oversight. By training a critique model that can independently assess and guide an actor model, the research aims to pave the way for more autonomous LLM self-improvement systems that can adapt to new tasks and models without continuous manual intervention.

To achieve these, Critique-RL sets out to:
- Propose a novel online RL approach that operates on a two-player actor-critic paradigm.
- Explicitly analyze and identify the deficiency of indirect reward signals in jointly optimizing discriminability and helpfulness.
- Introduce a "two-stage optimization strategy" to first reinforce discriminability with direct rule-based rewards (Stage I) and then enhance helpfulness while preserving discriminability using indirect rewards and regularization (Stage II).
- Demonstrate substantial performance improvements across various tasks and models, and show strong generalization capabilities to out-of-domain and open-ended tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Critique-RL introduces a novel two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) approach to train language models for critiquing, operating within a "two-player interaction framework" consisting of an actor model and a critique model.

#### 3.1. The Two-Player Interaction Framework (Preliminaries)

The framework is a `response-critique-refinement` loop:
1. **Actor's Initial Response:** Given a question `x`, the actor model (`π_θ`) generates an original response `y`. An oracle reward function (`r_oracle(x, y)`) verifies `y`'s correctness.
2. **Critic's Critique:** The critique model (`π_φ`) takes `(x, y)` and produces a critique `c`. This critique should include an assessment of correctness (discriminability) and natural language feedback (helpfulness).
3. **Actor's Refinement:** Based on `c`, the actor model generates a refined response `y'`. This refined response is also evaluated by `r_oracle(x, y')`.
Rewards for the critic (`r_c`) are then designed using these `r_oracle` signals. The critic is optimized using policy gradient methods, maximizing `E[r_c]`, with a KL divergence term to regularize against the initial SFT model.

#### 3.2. Motivating Findings: Insufficiency of Indirect Reward Signals

Before proposing Critique-RL, the authors conducted preliminary experiments using various indirect reward signals for the critic, common in prior work like Retroformer and CTRL. These signals included:
- `r_refine`: Rewards based on the correctness of the *refinement* response (`r_oracle(x, y')`).
- `r_Δ`: Rewards based on the *difference* in correctness between original and refined responses (`r_oracle(x, y') - r_oracle(x, y)`).
- `r_correction`: A more structured reward favoring correction of incorrect responses, penalizing incorrect changes.

The key finding was that while these indirect rewards sometimes improved the actor's refinement accuracy, they consistently *failed to effectively optimize the critic's discriminability*. Specifically, they could either lead to an "overly conservative" critic (good at preserving correct answers, poor at correcting incorrect ones, resulting in low `Δi→c` and `Acc@Refine`) or an "overly aggressive" critic (good at correcting incorrect answers, but high `Δc→i`, turning correct answers into incorrect ones). The analysis revealed that during RL training, the discriminability (e.g., `Acc@Dis`) for either originally correct or originally incorrect responses would degrade, indicating a fundamental optimization bottleneck. This critical insight motivated the need for a more explicit approach to discriminability.

#### 3.3. Critique-RL's Two-Stage Approach

To address the identified challenges, Critique-RL employs a novel two-stage RL process:

**Stage I: Optimizing Discriminability through Direct Reward Signals**
- **Goal:** To explicitly teach the critique model to accurately distinguish between correct and incorrect original responses.
- **Method:** A direct, rule-based reward function `r_dis` is introduced. `r_dis(x, y, c) = 1` if the critic's judgment `f(x, y, c)` (extracted from its natural language critique) perfectly matches the ground-truth oracle correctness `r_oracle(x, y)`; otherwise, it's `0`.
- **Optimization Objective:** The critic (`π_Stage-I_φ`) is updated to maximize `E[r_dis(x, y, c) - βKL(π_SFT_φ || π_Stage-I_φ)]`. The KL regularization helps maintain proximity to the initial SFT model.
- **Outcome:** This stage effectively and stably improves the critic's discriminability for both originally correct and incorrect responses, overcoming the imbalance observed with indirect rewards. The resulting `π_Stage-I_φ` model serves as a robust discriminator.

**Stage II: Optimizing Helpfulness while Maintaining Discriminability**
- **Goal:** To enhance the quality of the critic's natural language feedback (helpfulness) without compromising the discriminability gained in Stage I.
- **Method:** This stage builds upon `π_Stage-I_φ` as its initialization. The reward function now incorporates both helpfulness and discriminability terms:
 - `r_refine`: The primary reward for helpfulness, based on the correctness of the actor's *refinement* (`r_oracle(x, y')`). This encourages the critic to provide feedback that leads to successful corrections.
 - `β1 * r_dis(x, y, c)`: The direct discriminability reward from Stage I is re-introduced as a regularization term. This ensures that the helpfulness optimization does not degrade the critic's ability to judge correctness.
 - `β2 * KL(π_Stage-I_φ || π_Stage-II_φ)`: A KL divergence term regularizes the current model (`π_Stage-II_φ`) against the discriminability-optimized model from Stage I (`π_Stage-I_φ`). This prevents significant drift from the well-discriminating policy.
- **Optimization Objective:** The critic (`π_Stage-II_φ`) is updated to maximize `E[r_refine + β1*r_dis(x, y, c) - β2*KL(π_Stage-I_φ || π_Stage-II_φ)]`.
- **Outcome:** This two-pronged approach (helpfulness via `r_refine`, discriminability maintenance via `r_dis` and KL regularization) leads to critics that are both accurate in their assessments and effective in guiding actor refinement, preventing the conservative or aggressive behaviors observed previously.

**Implementation Details:**
- Experiments use Qwen2.5 series (3B, 7B) as primary models, with additional evaluations on Llama3.2-3B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct.
- Tasks include mathematical reasoning (MATH, GSM8K, AQUA as in-domain; SVAMP, TheoremQA as out-of-domain) and summarization (CNN/DailyMail).
- Actor models are first fine-tuned to generate responses and refine them based on critiques. Critic models are initialized via SFT before RL.
- Standard policy gradient with KL regularization (similar to RLOO, a PPO variant) is used.
- Evaluation metrics include `Acc@Refine` (actor's refinement accuracy), `Δ` (accuracy improvement), `Δc→i` (correct to incorrect change rate), `Δi→c` (incorrect to correct change rate), and `Acc@Dis` (critic's discrimination accuracy).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted demonstrate the significant effectiveness and advantages of Critique-RL across various models and tasks.

1. **General Performance Improvement by Critique Models:** The introduction of critique models consistently boosts the actor's reasoning performance. Even the SFT baseline, which fine-tunes with critique data, showed a substantial 7.34% accuracy gain on MATH for the Qwen2.5-3B model compared to an actor without a critic. This reaffirms the value of critique-based scalable oversight.

2. **Superiority of Online RL-based Methods:** The study found that online RL-based methods generally outperform fine-tuning-based approaches (SFT and STaR). Critique-RL, in particular, showcases higher potential and adaptability, outperforming SFT by an average of 7.11 points on accuracy across three in-domain datasets for the 3B model. Notably, SFT and STaR sometimes led to negative performance impacts on tasks like AQuA, whereas Critique-RL consistently delivered positive improvements.

3. **Critique-RL Outperforms Baselines in Both Helpfulness and Discriminability:**
 - **Discriminability (`Acc@Dis`):** Critique-RL significantly surpasses other baselines. For example, on GSM8K, it outperforms CTRL by 5.31 points (3B model) and 6.36 points (7B model). This directly validates the effectiveness of Stage I's discrimination-focused reward shaping.
 - **Final Accuracy (`Acc@Refine`):** Thanks to its joint optimization, Critique-RL consistently achieves the highest `Acc@Refine`. For the Qwen2.5-7B model, it shows an average improvement of 5.11 points in accuracy and 12.69 points in discriminability over Retroformer across three datasets. Overall, it delivers a **9.02% gain on in-domain tasks** and a **5.70% gain on out-of-domain tasks** for Qwen2.5-7B.

4. **Effective Iterative Refinement and Training:**
 - **Iterative Refinement:** When applied iteratively (multiple critique-refinement steps for a single problem), Critique-RL demonstrates consistent and measurable accuracy gains, indicating its robust ability to guide successive improvements.
 - **Iterative Training:** Iteratively re-training Critique-RL through its two stages further enhances performance, with a second iteration on Qwen2.5-3B for MATH improving accuracy by 2.40 points and discriminability by 3.68 points compared to the first iteration.

5. **Ablation Study Validates Two-Stage Design:**
 - Removing either Stage I or Stage II results in a significant performance drop, confirming that both discriminability and helpfulness optimization are crucial and interdependent.
 - Ablations on Stage II's reward design highlight the necessity of maintaining discriminability: removing `r_dis` and KL regularization in Stage II leads to a substantial decrease in both `Acc@Refine` and `Acc@Dis`.
 - The choice of `r_refine` for helpfulness in Stage II slightly outperforms `r_Δ` or `r_correction`, suggesting its direct alignment with the desired outcome.

6. **Enhanced Helpfulness Even with Oracle Verifier:** Even in scenarios where an external oracle verifier is available (bypassing the need for the model's discrimination), Critique-RL still outperforms other methods in terms of helpfulness. This suggests that optimizing discriminability also implicitly contributes to better feedback quality, emphasizing the importance of joint optimization.

7. **Compute Efficiency and Scalability:**
 - Critique-RL significantly increases the performance ceiling and shows a more sustained upward trend with increased inference compute (e.g., using majority vote with `K` samples).
 - Critique-RL's `K×` response-critique-refinement sampling is more compute-efficient than `3K×` parallel sampling, making it a highly practical approach. For instance, on Qwen2.5-7B for GSM8K, Critique-RL's `Pass@1` even surpasses SFT's `Pass@64`.

8. **Strong Generalization Across Models and Tasks:**
 - **Out-of-Domain Tasks:** Critique-RL shows strong generalization to unseen tasks like SVAMP and TheoremQA, delivering significant performance improvements.
 - **Different Model Architectures:** It proves effective on various backbone models, including Llama3.2-3B, the strong reasoning model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, and even the larger Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (demonstrating weak-to-strong generalization), consistently achieving higher discriminability.
 - **Open-Ended Tasks:** For open-ended summarization (CNN/DailyMail), where reward signals are obtained from an external reward model, Critique-RL effectively optimizes discriminability and improves summary quality, demonstrating its broad applicability beyond rule-based reasoning tasks.

9. **Benefits over Actor-Only RL:** Comparing Critique-RL with actor-only RL methods (Directly RL, SCoRe) shows that the actor-critic paradigm with a dedicated critique model offers superior accuracy, discriminability, modularity (transferable critic), and compute efficiency.

10. **Qualitative Analysis and Robustness:** Qualitative examples confirm that Critique-RL-trained critics can accurately identify errors and provide detailed, constructive suggestions, leading to correct refinements, unlike SFT models or Stage I-only critics which might provide low-quality suggestions despite correct discrimination. Furthermore, the sensitivity analysis on hyperparameters (`β`, `β1`, `β2`) shows that the method is not overly sensitive to these values, indicating robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Critique-RL represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model training and scalable oversight, with several profound implications:

1. **Enabling Scalable Oversight without Stronger Supervision:** The most immediate impact is providing a practical and effective method for training LLM critics that do not rely on expensive human annotations or powerful oracle LLMs for critique data generation. This addresses a critical bottleneck in deploying and continuously improving LLMs for complex, real-world tasks. By democratizing the training of critique models, it can accelerate research and development in self-improving AI systems.

2. **Enhanced LLM Reliability and Performance:** By explicitly and jointly optimizing both discriminability and helpfulness, Critique-RL creates more intelligent critics that can accurately identify errors and provide high-quality, actionable feedback. This directly translates to more reliable and higher-performing actor LLMs, particularly for challenging reasoning tasks where mistakes can propagate. The significant performance gains across various benchmarks (up to 9.02% in-domain, 5.70% out-of-domain) validate its capability to push the boundaries of LLM accuracy.

3. **Solving a Core RL Optimization Challenge:** The paper's identification of the "discriminability-helpfulness conflict" in indirect reward-based RL is a crucial conceptual contribution. The proposed two-stage RL framework offers a principled solution to this conflict, providing a blueprint for designing more effective reward functions and training strategies for complex multi-faceted agent capabilities. This insight can influence future RL research beyond critique models.

4. **Modular and Transferable AI Components:** The two-player actor-critic paradigm, combined with the effective training of a standalone critique model, promotes modularity. A well-trained critic can be potentially transferred to supervise different or even stronger actor models (weak-to-strong generalization), enhancing the flexibility and reusability of AI components. This reduces the need for retraining entire systems and facilitates specialization.

5. **Improved Computational Efficiency:** The finding that the critique-refinement loop is more compute-efficient than brute-force parallel sampling methods (e.g., `K×` critique-refinement outperforming `3K×` parallel sampling) has significant practical implications. It suggests a more resource-efficient pathway to achieve high performance in LLMs, which is crucial given the growing computational demands of state-of-the-art models. This could make advanced LLM capabilities more accessible and sustainable.

6. **Broader Applicability to Diverse Tasks:** Critique-RL's demonstrated effectiveness on both highly structured mathematical reasoning problems and open-ended summarization tasks highlights its versatility. This suggests that the core principles of jointly optimizing discriminability and helpfulness are broadly applicable, potentially extending to other domains where nuanced feedback and error detection are critical, such as code debugging, scientific discovery, or complex decision-making processes.

7. **Inspiring Future Research:** By providing a stable and effective method for training robust critique models, this work opens new avenues for research into advanced LLM agents, meta-learning for critique generation, and human-in-the-loop systems where AI-generated critiques could further augment human oversight capabilities. It pushes towards a future where LLMs can genuinely understand, evaluate, and improve their own outputs with minimal external intervention.

In conclusion, Critique-RL delivers a powerful and principled approach to developing critiquing language models. Its ability to train effective critics without costly supervision, resolve key RL optimization challenges, and demonstrate robust performance and generalization capabilities marks a significant step forward for scalable oversight and the development of more autonomous and reliable LLM systems.
