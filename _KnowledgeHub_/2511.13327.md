# ZeroDexGrasp: Zero-Shot Task-Oriented Dexterous Grasp Synthesis with Prompt-Based Multi-Stage Semantic Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> ZeroDexGrasp presents a framework for zero-shot task-oriented dexterous grasp synthesis, leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for multi-stage semantic reasoning combined with geometric optimization. This approach generates physically plausible and semantically aligned grasps for diverse, unseen objects and task instructions, outperforming baselines in accuracy and stability.

## Problem

- Existing task-oriented dexterous grasping methods struggle with generalization to novel objects and tasks due to their heavy reliance on costly, manually labeled training data.
- A significant gap persists between high-level natural language task instructions and the precise, low-level robotic actions required for high-degree-of-freedom dexterous hands.
- Ensuring both semantic alignment with task instructions and physical feasibility for complex multi-finger grasps remains a major challenge in open-set scenarios.

## Method

- The framework employs ==prompt-based multi-stage semantic reasoning== using ==MLLMs== to infer initial hand contact regions (part- and point-level), global hand position, grasp type, and rotation based on task instructions and object visuals.
- It utilizes a ==geometry-guided verification step== to filter physically implausible initial hand orientations before MLLM inference for rotation.
- A ==contact-guided grasp refinement== component optimizes the MLLM-inferred initial poses through an ==energy minimization process==, ensuring physical feasibility, stability, minimal penetration, and high-fidelity semantic alignment.

## Results

- ZeroDexGrasp achieved significantly reduced interpenetration (P.Vol. of **3.322 cm³** and P.Dep. of **0.358 cm**) compared to the best baseline (P.Vol. **7.846 cm³**, P.Dep. **1.437 cm**), indicating higher physical plausibility.
- Demonstrated superior semantic alignment, with a Part Accuracy of **90.075%** and Semantic Contact Ratio of **83.831%**, nearly doubling the performance of existing methods in zero-shot scenarios.
- Successfully validated in real-world experiments using a Kinova Gen3 arm with Inspire FTP and RoboSense Papert dexterous hands, executing generated grasps on physical objects from single RGB-D inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grasp properties can be effectively decoupled: global hand positions and grasp types are primarily driven by high-level task semantics, while fine-grained finger configurations are influenced by local object geometry.
> - Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can serve as powerful zero-shot reasoning engines, inferring abstract semantic components of a grasp by leveraging their broad understanding of commonsense knowledge and spatial relationships.
> - Combining MLLM-driven semantic inference with a subsequent geometry-guided physical optimization is crucial to bridge the gap between abstract task instructions and concrete, physically plausible dexterous hand poses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly task-oriented dexterous grasping, is at the intersection of robotics, artificial intelligence, and computer vision. Historically, dexterous grasping has been a challenging problem due to the high degrees of freedom (DoFs) of multi-finger hands and the intricate contact mechanics involved. The paper "ZeroDexGrasp" addresses a critical limitation of existing approaches: their struggle to generalize across diverse, unseen objects and task instructions without extensive, costly labeled training data.

**Evolution of Grasping Research:**

- **Early Approaches (Stability/Diversity-focused):** Initial research in dexterous grasping primarily focused on generating stable grasps or a diverse set of grasps for known objects. These methods often relied on analytical models, heuristic rules, or geometric feature matching.
- **Data-Driven and Task-Oriented Grasping:** More recently, the field shifted towards data-driven approaches, where large datasets of human or robotic grasps were collected and used to train models. Task-oriented grasping emerged as a subfield, aiming not just for stable grasps, but for grasps that facilitate downstream manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping a mug by its handle to drink, rather than by its base). These methods typically relied on "object-semantic-grasp" data pairs, often requiring type-specific labels or language instructions to condition generative models.
- **Limitations of Data-Driven Approaches:** Despite progress, these methods faced significant limitations:
 - **Data Scarcity:** Manual data collection for dexterous grasping is laborious and expensive.
 - **Limited Generalization:** Models trained on finite datasets struggled to generalize to novel object categories or open-set scenarios, where new objects and tasks are constantly encountered.
 - **Bridging Semantics and Action:** A significant gap exists between high-level task instructions (e.g., "pour water") and the precise low-level robotic actions (e.g., specific finger joint angles and hand pose).

**Emergence of Large Language and Vision Models (LLMs/VLMs/MLLMs):**

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have revolutionized many AI domains. In robotics, these models offer unprecedented capabilities in semantic understanding, spatial reasoning, and commonsense knowledge. Researchers have begun exploring how to leverage LLMs and MLLMs to enhance robotic grasping, particularly for task-oriented and zero-shot scenarios.

- **LLM-enhanced Grasping:** Some works encode task instructions using LLMs and condition diffusion models on text and point clouds. SemGrasp finetunes MLLMs for semantic grasp generation. However, these still often require finetuning or are constrained by limited datasets.
- **Generalization Strategies:** Other efforts focus on improving generalization by integrating large vision-language models with grasp controllers (Dexgraspvla), using generalizable affordance representations (Afford-DexGrasp), or retrieving generalizable priors (G-dexgrasp). While promising, many still require expensive hand-specific data and training.
- **Zero-Shot Grasping:** This subfield aims to generate feasible grasps for unseen objects or tasks without additional training. For low-DoF grippers (e.g., two-finger grippers), foundation models and methods leveraging visual foundation models to identify semantic feature fields or task-relevant contact regions have shown success. However, extending these directly to high-DoF dexterous hands is challenging due to the complexity of ensuring reasonable global hand poses and physically plausible finger configurations.

**ZeroDexGrasp's Unique Contribution:**

ZeroDexGrasp positions itself directly to address the challenge of **zero-shot, task-oriented dexterous grasp synthesis for open-set objects**, specifically targeting high-DoF dexterous hands. It distinguishes itself by:

1. **Holistic Semantic-to-Grasp Reasoning:** Unlike methods that focus only on contact parts, ZeroDexGrasp offers a more comprehensive approach to align high-level semantics with global hand properties (position, orientation, grasp type) and local finger configurations.
2. **Zero-Shot Generalization via MLLMs:** It fully leverages the intrinsic capabilities of MLLMs for semantic understanding and spatial reasoning in a truly zero-shot manner, without requiring task-specific or object-specific training data. This is a significant departure from prior works that still involved finetuning or extensive labeled data.
3. **Prompt-Based Multi-Stage Reasoning:** The paper introduces a novel prompt-based multi-stage semantic reasoning pipeline, which is key to bridging the gap between abstract instructions and concrete grasp parameters.
4. **Integration of Refinement:** It combines MLLM-driven initial grasp inference with a geometry-guided optimization phase, ensuring both semantic alignment and physical feasibility, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

In essence, ZeroDexGrasp pushes the boundaries of robotic grasping by demonstrating how advanced MLLMs, combined with structured reasoning and refinement, can enable highly generalizable and intelligent dexterous manipulation in previously unseen scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of synthesizing high-quality, task-oriented dexterous grasps for **open-set objects and tasks in a zero-shot manner**. This means the robot should be able to generate effective and appropriate grasps for objects and task instructions it has never encountered during any training phase, solely based on its understanding of the task and the object's visual properties.

The motivation for this ambitious goal stems from two fundamental limitations of existing task-oriented dexterous grasping methods:

1. **Lack of Generalization:** Most current approaches heavily rely on costly, manually labeled data (e.g., "object-semantic-grasp" pairs) to achieve semantic alignment between task instructions and grasp poses. This reliance fundamentally restricts their ability to generalize to novel object categories, shapes, or task variations in real-world, open-set environments. Collecting exhaustive datasets for every conceivable object and task is practically impossible.
2. **Gap Between Abstract Semantics and Concrete Actions:** There is a significant conceptual divide between a high-level natural language task instruction (e.g., "grasp the handle and press the start button") and the precise, anthropomorphic multi-finger configurations and global hand poses required to execute that task. Bridging this gap effectively, especially for high-DoF dexterous hands, without explicit training examples for every new scenario, is a major challenge.

To overcome these challenges, the authors were guided by several key observations:

- **Decoupled Grasp Properties:** Human grasping behavior suggests that the global properties of the hand (its overall position, orientation relative to the object, contact points, and general grasp type) are primarily driven by the semantic requirements of the task and object affordances. In contrast, the fine-grained joint configurations of the fingers are more directly influenced by the local geometry of the object at the contact points. This observation suggests a multi-stage reasoning process could be effective.
- **MLLM Capabilities:** Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated powerful capabilities in semantic understanding, spatial reasoning, and leveraging commonsense knowledge from vast datasets. These capabilities make MLLMs ideal candidates for inferring the global, semantically-guided properties of a grasp in a zero-shot fashion, as they can "reason" about unfamiliar objects and tasks based on their broad pre-training.

Therefore, the core motivation is to harness the reasoning power of MLLMs to infer the high-level semantic components of a grasp, and then combine this with a robust physical refinement mechanism to generate dexterous grasps that are not only task-aligned but also physically feasible and human-like for any given novel object and task instruction. The goal is to move towards truly generalizable and intelligent robotic grasping systems that can adapt to the complexities of unstructured environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ZeroDexGrasp proposes a novel zero-shot task-oriented dexterous grasp synthesis framework that synergistically integrates Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with a contact-guided grasp optimization process. The framework takes a natural language task instruction ($S$) and a target object ($O$) as input and outputs a set of dexterous hand grasp parameters ($G = \{T, R, \theta\}$, representing translation, rotation, and joint angles). The methodology is structured around two main components: prompt-based multi-stage semantic reasoning and contact-guided grasp refinement.

#### 3.1. Prompt-Based Multi-Stage Semantic Reasoning

This component leverages MLLMs to infer initial grasp configurations and object contact information from the given task and object semantics. It operates on discretized semantic representations to bridge the gap between abstract instructions and concrete grasp parameters.

1. **Part-level and Point-level Contact Inference:**
 - **Goal:** To identify specific 3D regions on the object for whole-hand contact, functional finger contact (thumb and index), and precise contact keypoints.
 - **Approach:**
 - **Visual Prompting (Set-of-Mark - SoM):** The input object image is processed by an advanced image segmentation model (like SAM) to divide it into distinct, numbered regions. This "Set-of-Mark" image ($I_{SoM}$) is then fed to an MLLM (e.g., GPT-4.1).
 - **MLLM Inference:** The MLLM infers 2D masks for whole-hand contact ($M_{2d_{hand}}$) and functional finger contact ($M_{2d_{func}}$) by identifying and selecting the relevant numbered part IDs based on the task instruction.
 - **Point-level Contact:** For tasks requiring finer precision, the MLLM is guided to infer specific 2D contact points ($P_{2d_{func}}$) for the functional fingers by sampling and annotating candidate points on the contour of the functional finger contact mask. This is a conditional step for efficiency.
 - **3D Representation Extraction:** The inferred 2D masks and points are back-projected into 3D space using camera parameters to obtain {M$_{3d_{hand}}$, M$_{3d_{func}}$, P$_{3d_{func}}$}. A feature-based classification strategy using PartField is employed to cluster unlabeled points near the 2.5D hand contact region into 3D part-level contact representations.

2. **Initial Hand Position and Grasp Type Inference:**
 - **Goal:** To determine the initial relative 3D position of the hand ($T'$) and the general type of grasp ($G$) suitable for the task.
 - **Approach:**
 - **Relative Hand Position:** An MLLM (e.g., Gemini-2.5) is prompted with a discrete set of textual candidate hand approach directions (D$_{text}$, including cardinal and diagonal directions like "front," "behind," "above," "front-left"). The MLLM selects the most semantically aligned direction ($D^{*}_{text}$), which is mapped to a 3D direction vector ($\vec{D}^{*}$). The initial hand position $T'$ is then calculated by casting a ray from the center of the 3D hand contact region along $\vec{D}^{*}$ to intersect the object's expanded convex hull.
 - **Grasp Type:** The MLLM selects the most appropriate grasp type ($G$) from a predefined set of common grasp types ($G_{set}$). This choice provides the initial finger joint configurations ($\theta'$) that align with common human grasping patterns for the given task.

3. **Imagination-based Initial Hand Rotation Inference:**
 - **Goal:** To determine the optimal initial global rotation ($R'$) of the hand, defined by its palm direction ($\vec{D}_{palm}$) and finger direction ($\vec{D}_{finger}$).
 - **Approach:**
 - **Palm Orientation:** The optimal palm orientation ($\vec{D}^{*}_{palm}$) is initially set to oppose the approach direction ($\vec{D}^{*}$) inferred earlier, representing the common sense of "grasping from."
 - **Finger Orientation (Imagination-based Prompting):** To determine the finger direction, the hand is placed at the initial position $T'$ with a natural extended finger configuration. Multiple candidate hand states are generated by rotating the hand around the $\vec{D}^{*}_{palm}$ axis. For each candidate, two images (hand-object and hand-only) are rendered and fused into a composite view. These composite views form an "imagination-based visual prompt" ($I_{imag}$) for the MLLM. The MLLM evaluates this set of images and selects the optimal rotational component ($R'$) that best aligns with the task semantics and object affordances.

#### 3.2. Geometry-Guided Verification and Contact-Guided Grasp Refinement

This component takes the MLLM-inferred initial grasp pose ($G' = (\theta', T', R')$) and contact information ({M$_{3d_{hand}}$, M$_{3d_{func}}$, P$_{3d_{func}}$}) and refines it to ensure physical feasibility, stability, and high-fidelity semantic alignment.

1. **Geometry-Guided Verification:**
 - **Imagined Hand Rotation Filtering:** Before MLLM inference for rotation, candidate hand rotations are filtered if the functional finger directions do not align sufficiently with the local surface normals of the object's contact region. This prevents MLLMs from considering physically implausible orientations.
 - **Point-level Contact Validation:** For point-level contact inference, the identified points are validated using force-normal consistency (i.e., the force direction between thumb and index finger should approximately align with the surface normals). Invalid points trigger a re-inference.

2. **Contact-Guided Grasp Refinement (Optimization):**
 - **Goal:** To optimize the initial grasp pose to ensure effective hand-object contact, minimize penetration, maximize force closure, and respect joint limits, all while maintaining semantic alignment.
 - **Energy Minimization:** A set of optimization energy terms are defined and minimized using an Adam optimizer:
 - **Functional Finger Contact Energy ($E_{func\_cont}$):** Encourages functional fingers to reach the inferred point-level contact keypoints (or part-level region).
 - **Non-functional Finger Contact Energy ($E_{unf\_cont}$):** Promotes overall hand contact consistency with the inferred hand contact region. Both are weighted by a hand contact probability map.
 - **Hand Contact Map Energy ($E_{hand\_cmap}$):** Further encourages hand vertices to be on the object surface in relevant areas.
 - **Penetration Energy ($E_{pen}$):** Minimizes interpenetration between the hand and the object.
 - **Self-Penetration Energy ($E_{spen}$):** Prevents the hand from penetrating itself.
 - **Force-Closure Energy ($E_{fc}$):** Encourages a stable grasp by promoting opposing contact forces.
 - **Joint Limit Energy ($E_{pip}$):** Ensures that finger joint angles remain within physical limits.
 - **Output:** The optimization process yields a final dexterous grasp pose that is both semantically aligned with the task instruction and physically feasible.

The entire methodology demonstrates a sophisticated blend of high-level semantic reasoning using cutting-edge MLLMs and low-level geometric and physical optimization, making it highly robust for zero-shot task-oriented dexterous grasping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ZeroDexGrasp demonstrates its superior performance in zero-shot task-oriented dexterous grasp synthesis, particularly in terms of physical plausibility and semantic alignment for diverse and unseen objects and tasks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison with Baselines

The framework was quantitatively compared against several task-oriented dexterous grasp synthesis baselines: GraspTTA†, AffordPose†, and DexGYS. Two variants of ZeroDexGrasp were evaluated: "Ours*" (MLLM selects the best rendering viewpoint) and "Ours" (manual viewpoint selection). The test set comprised over 50 unseen object categories, 200 object instances, and 280 task instructions, specifically designed to assess zero-shot generalization.

**Key Quantitative Results (Table I):**

- **Physical Quality and Stability:**
 - **Penetration Depth (P.Dep.) and Volume (P.Vol.):** ZeroDexGrasp achieved a significant reduction in interpenetration, with Ours showing a P.Vol. of 3.322 cm³ and P.Dep. of 0.358 cm, which is over 50% lower than the best baseline (AffordPose†, P.Vol. 7.846 cm³, P.Dep. 1.437 cm). This indicates that the generated grasps are much more physically realistic and do not excessively penetrate the object.
 - **Simulation Success (Sim.Suc.):** ZeroDexGrasp demonstrated competitive stability (63.101% for Ours), slightly outperforming AffordPose† (54.862%) and significantly better than other baselines.
 - **Simulation Displacement (Sim.Dis.):** While not the absolute best, ZeroDexGrasp's displacement values were comparable to baselines, and the authors note that higher displacement in some cases might be linked to higher interpenetration in other methods.

- **Semantic Alignment:**
 - **Part Accuracy (Part Acc.):** ZeroDexGrasp achieved a remarkable Part Accuracy of 90.075% (Ours), nearly double that of the best baseline (AffordPose† at 53.172%). This metric directly confirms that the hand makes contact with the *correct, task-relevant* object parts.
 - **Semantic Contact Ratio (S.C.Ratio):** Similarly, the Semantic Contact Ratio was 83.831% for Ours, again significantly higher than baselines. This indicates that a much larger proportion of finger vertices are in contact with the semantically correct part of the object.
 - **Perceptual Score (P.Score):** Both MLLM scoring and user studies consistently rated ZeroDexGrasp's generated grasps higher (4.00/3.85 for Ours) compared to baselines, reinforcing the perception of human-like and task-aligned grasps.

These results unequivocally establish ZeroDexGrasp's superior performance, particularly in zero-shot scenarios, highlighting its strong generalization capability for unseen objects and task instructions.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Figure 5 provides compelling visual evidence of ZeroDexGrasp's ability to synthesize highly semantically aligned and physically feasible grasps across a diverse range of objects and complex task instructions. Examples include:
- "Could you give this wine bottle a little twist?" -> Grasping the neck of the bottle in a twisting orientation.
- "Make sure to hold the handle of the coffee machine tightly." -> Firmly grasping the ergonomic handle.
- "I'd appreciate it if you could gently press the trigger to fire the pistol." -> Index finger positioned on the trigger, thumb and other fingers grasping the handle.
- "Grab the milk carton from the table." -> A stable power grasp around the carton.
The qualitative results showcase the system's robust understanding of object affordances and task requirements, leading to intuitive and correct grasping behaviors.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to ascertain the contribution of each key component of the ZeroDexGrasp framework (Table II, Figure 6).

- **"W/O Contact" (Without Contact Inference):** Removing part- and point-level contact reasoning led to a significant drop in semantic alignment (Part Acc. 53.518%, S.C.Ratio 54.598%). While simulation stability slightly improved (possibly due to grasping near the object center), penetration control decreased. This confirms that contact inference is crucial for guiding grasps to task-relevant semantic regions; without it, grasps may be geometrically feasible but semantically incorrect (e.g., grasping a badminton racket by the head instead of the handle).
- **"W/O Initial" (Without Initial Grasp Pose Reasoning):** Removing the MLLM-driven inference for initial hand position, rotation, and grasp type (initializing hand at contact region center with random rotation) severely degraded performance across all metrics. Semantic alignment plummeted, and physical plausibility suffered (P.Vol. 4.569 cm³, P.Dep. 0.440 cm). Qualitatively, the hand exhibited unreasonable orientations and positions (e.g., inverted hand on a badminton racket, severe penetration on a spray bottle). This highlights the critical role of MLLMs in providing strong, semantically aligned initial guidance for global hand properties.
- **"W/O Refine" (Without Contact-Guided Grasp Refinement):** This variant used only the initial grasp poses inferred by MLLMs, without the subsequent optimization. While semantic alignment remained relatively strong (Part Acc. 73.940%, S.C.Ratio 71.963%), physical plausibility was severely compromised, with high penetration (P.Vol. 11.443 cm³, P.Dep. 1.585 cm) and low simulation success (43.135%). This demonstrates that MLLM-inferred initial poses capture the semantic intent well, but the contact-guided refinement stage is indispensable for achieving physically feasible, stable, and high-fidelity dexterous grasps.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

The paper also presents real-world experiments using a Kinova Gen3 arm equipped with an Inspire FTP dexterous hand and a RoboSense Papert Hand (Figure 7). By reconstructing 3D object models from single-view RGB-D images and aligning them with the real scene, ZeroDexGrasp successfully generated and executed grasps on physical robots. This validates the practical applicability and robustness of the framework, demonstrating its potential for real-world robotic systems.

In summary, the main findings confirm that ZeroDexGrasp effectively addresses the challenge of zero-shot task-oriented dexterous grasping by strategically combining MLLM-based semantic reasoning with geometry-guided refinement. It significantly outperforms existing methods in generalization, semantic alignment, and physical plausibility, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ZeroDexGrasp represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for dexterous grasping. Its primary significance lies in its ability to achieve **zero-shot generalization** for task-oriented dexterous grasps across diverse and unseen objects and tasks. This addresses a long-standing bottleneck in robotics, moving beyond the traditional reliance on costly, task-specific, and object-specific labeled datasets.

The potential impact of this research is substantial and far-reaching:

1. **Enabling Truly Generalizable Robotic Systems:** By eliminating the need for extensive prior training on every new object or task, ZeroDexGrasp paves the way for more adaptable and autonomous robots. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic environments such as homes, hospitals, and logistics warehouses, where encountering novel items is common.
2. **Bridging the Semantic Gap:** The framework effectively demonstrates how Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can serve as powerful reasoning engines, translating high-level, abstract natural language instructions into concrete, low-level robotic actions. This capability is vital for intuitive human-robot interaction, allowing users to instruct robots using natural language without complex programming.
3. **Advancing Dexterous Manipulation:** Dexterous hands, with their high degrees of freedom, offer human-like manipulation capabilities but are notoriously difficult to control. ZeroDexGrasp makes these complex manipulators more practical by providing a robust method for generating appropriate grasps without extensive hand-specific training, thereby unlocking the full potential of multi-finger robotic hands.
4. **Foundation for Future AI-driven Robotics:** The integration of MLLMs with structured semantic reasoning and physical optimization establishes a novel paradigm for robotic intelligence. This work contributes to the growing trend of leveraging large foundation models in robotics, suggesting future directions for incorporating commonsense knowledge and general intelligence into physical agents.
5. **Applications Across Industries:**
 - **Household Robotics:** Future domestic robots could perform a wider array of tasks, grasping unfamiliar objects (e.g., a new kitchen gadget, a child's toy) based on verbal instructions.
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** Robots could handle a greater variety of package shapes and sizes, improving efficiency and flexibility in sorting and packing.
 - **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Customizable and adaptable manipulation tasks could be performed by robots, especially in scenarios with high product variability.
 - **Healthcare and Assistive Robotics:** Robots could assist individuals with daily living tasks, handling personal items or tools that vary greatly in design and function.
6. **Inspiration for Further Research:** The explicit acknowledgment of limitations, such as the instability and potential hallucinations of current MLLMs, provides clear avenues for future research. Enhancing robustness through stronger spatial grounding, real-time feedback mechanisms, and more sophisticated MLLM architectures will be crucial next steps, inspiring continued innovation in this domain.
7. **Contribution to Human-Robot Interaction:** By generating anthropomorphic and semantically aligned grasps, ZeroDexGrasp contributes to a more natural and intuitive experience for humans interacting with robots, fostering greater trust and acceptance of robotic assistants.

In conclusion, ZeroDexGrasp is a landmark paper that pushes the boundaries of robotic dexterity and intelligence. Its innovative fusion of MLLM-driven semantic reasoning and physical grasp refinement provides a powerful framework for zero-shot task-oriented dexterous grasping, promising to accelerate the development of highly adaptable, general-purpose robots capable of operating effectively in complex, open-world environments.
