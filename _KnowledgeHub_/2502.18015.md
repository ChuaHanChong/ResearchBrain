# $\texttt{SPIN}$: distilling $\texttt{Skill-RRT}$ for long-horizon prehensile and non-prehensile manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SPIN, a framework from KAIST, combines planning and imitation learning to tackle long-horizon prehensile and non-prehensile robotic manipulation tasks. It achieved 93-98% task success rates in simulation and 80-90% on a real Franka robot across three complex domains by distilling robust plans from a Skill-RRT planner, aided by noise-based data filtering and learned skill-bridging "connectors".

## Problem

- Robotic systems struggle with long-horizon tasks that require complex sequences of prehensile and non-prehensile skills, often involving transient object repositioning and contact-rich interactions.
- Existing methods face challenges with "state gaps" where the termination state of one skill is not directly compatible with the initiation state of the next, risking object disturbance.
- Online planning algorithms are too computationally expensive for real-time deployment, while end-to-end learning methods suffer from reward sparsity and data inefficiency in complex, long-horizon scenarios.

## Method

- SPIN utilizes a two-stage framework: a simulator-based ==Skill-RRT== planner generates diverse skill plans, which are then distilled into a reactive policy using imitation learning with a Diffusion Policy.
- Goal-conditioned "connectors" are trained via reinforcement learning to robustly bridge state gaps between different pre-trained manipulation skills, minimizing object disturbance during transitions.
- A noise-based data filtering mechanism replays Skill-RRT plans with perturbations, selecting only high-quality, robust trajectories for the imitation learning dataset to enhance policy resilience.

## Results

- SPIN achieved simulation success rates of **95%** (Card Flip), **93%** (Bookshelf), and **98%** (Kitchen), outperforming various RL and PAMDP baselines which showed 0-**78%** success.
- The distilled SPIN policy reduced online computation time from 79-121 seconds (for Skill-RRT) to an average of **2.68**-**3.02** seconds, enabling real-time operation.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Franka Research 3 robot demonstrated high real-world success rates of **85%** (Card Flip), **90%** (Bookshelf), and **80%** (Kitchen).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly addressing skill transition "state gaps" with learned, contact-aware connectors is crucial for robustly chaining diverse prehensile and non-prehensile manipulation actions.
> - Combining the exploratory power of RRT-based planning with the efficiency of imitation learning enables solving long-horizon tasks while overcoming limitations of both pure planning and end-to-end learning.
> - Filtering planning solutions based on their robustness to noise during replay significantly improves the quality of demonstration data, leading to learned policies with higher success rates and better zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
