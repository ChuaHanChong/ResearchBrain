# Automatic Data Curation for Self-Supervised Learning: A Clustering-Based Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meta FAIR developed an automatic, clustering-based pipeline to curate large, balanced datasets specifically for self-supervised learning. This method, applied to web-scale image, text, and satellite data, demonstrated improved model performance, robustness, and fairness compared to models trained on raw or less rigorously curated data.

## Problem

- Uncurated, large-scale datasets, like internet crawls, exhibit a 'long-tail distribution' of concepts, leading to biased representations when used for self-supervised learning.
- Current data curation methods for self-supervised learning are either manual and labor-intensive, ad-hoc, or retrieval-based, limiting scalability, diversity, and generalizability.
- A lack of principled, automatic methods to curate large, diverse, and most importantly, *balanced* datasets for pre-training large foundation models.

## Method

- A **hierarchical k-means** clustering algorithm is applied iteratively, clustering centroids from previous levels, coupled with a ==resampling-clustering== step to progressively flatten the distribution of concept centroids.
- A ==top-down hierarchical sampling== strategy ensures balance not only among the highest-level concepts but also among sub-concepts at all intermediate levels of the hierarchy.
- The method leverages pre-computed embeddings (e.g., ==DINOv2== for images, ==SBERT== for text) to define the semantic space for unsupervised clustering and subsequent balanced sampling.

## Results

- Models trained on automatically curated image datasets from Meta FAIR significantly outperform those trained on raw data across various benchmarks, demonstrating superior out-of-distribution generalization and robustness.
- Language models trained on curated text data show consistent and substantial improvements in common sense reasoning and world knowledge tasks.
- Self-supervised models for satellite imagery trained on curated data achieve a **20%** relative improvement in the R2 metric on average for canopy height estimation and exhibit improved fairness metrics on sensitive datasets like Dollar Street.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vanilla k-means clustering exacerbates data imbalance by creating more clusters in dense regions, necessitating a hierarchical approach to progressively approximate a uniform concept distribution.
> - Balancing concepts across multiple levels of granularity, rather than just the highest, is critical for improved model generalization and robustness, achieved through hierarchical sampling.
> - The quality of initial embeddings and the use of k-means++ initialization are crucial for the effectiveness and stability of the automatic data curation process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the critical intersection of **self-supervised learning (SSL)** and **large-scale data management**. Self-supervised learning has become the cornerstone of modern state-of-the-art machine learning systems, from large language models (LLMs) to foundational visual encoders. Its promise lies in its ability to leverage vast amounts of unlabeled data, circumventing the costly and time-consuming process of human annotation required by supervised learning.

However, the paper highlights a crucial, often overlooked, challenge: while SSL removes the need for *labels*, it does not remove the need for *high-quality data*. Previous research has consistently shown that training on raw, uncurated data, such as internet crawls, often leads to suboptimal performance. This is primarily due to the "long-tail distribution of concepts" inherent in such data, where a few dominant concepts are heavily over-represented, while a multitude of rarer concepts are under-represented. This imbalance biases learned representations, hindering their universality and generalization capabilities.

Existing data curation strategies for large-scale SSL have typically fallen into two categories:
- **Manual or Heuristic Curation:** This involves significant human effort, often filtering data based on expert knowledge or pre-defined rules (e.g., Wikipedia-based filtering for text). This approach is expensive, non-scalable, and often ad-hoc.
- **Retrieval-Based Curation:** Methods like that used for DINOv2 involve retrieving images similar to a small, manually labeled "seed" dataset. While effective, this approach can inadvertently limit diversity and ignore vast ranges of concepts not present in the seed, making it less generic and potentially task-dependent.
- **Data Pruning and Active Learning:** These related fields aim to reduce dataset size or strategically select data for annotation. However, they often require label information or are focused on cost reduction rather than distribution correction in an unsupervised setting.

This paper directly addresses the limitations of these existing methods. It posits that a truly effective large-scale SSL dataset must be **large, diverse, and, critically, balanced**. Its contribution lies in proposing a principled, generic, and *automatic* solution for achieving this balance in an unsupervised manner. By developing a method that can infer and correct concept distributions from raw data without any human labeling or task-specific seeds, the authors push the frontier of scalable and robust AI system development. This work is highly relevant to the ongoing efforts to train ever-larger foundation models, where data quality at scale is a paramount concern.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **automatic and principled pipeline for curating high-quality datasets specifically for self-supervised pre-training**. The authors aim to overcome the inherent limitations of traditional, manual, or ad-hoc data curation methods, which are costly, time-consuming, and ultimately restrict the scalability of dataset sizes for burgeoning large-scale self-supervised models.

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical observations in the current landscape of machine learning:

- **Subpar Performance of Uncurated Data:** Despite the promise of SSL to leverage vast unlabeled data, empirical evidence consistently shows that training models on randomly collected internet data often yields unsatisfactory results. This is attributed to the "long-tail distribution" of concepts, where certain concepts (e.g., "dogs" or "websites" in images) are overwhelmingly present, leading to biased features that favor these dominant categories while neglecting rarer ones.
- **Limitations of Current Curation Practices:** While state-of-the-art LLMs and vision models benefit from carefully curated data, the curation processes themselves are often "ad hoc." For instance, LLMs might be trained on filtered internet data to match the distribution of high-grade sources like Wikipedia, and vision models might retrieve images based on similarity to manually labeled seeds. These methods are not generic, can be labor-intensive, and may not fully exploit the vast diversity of raw internet data.
- **Defining "High-Quality" for SSL:** The authors rigorously define the necessary characteristics of a good pre-training dataset for SSL: it must be **large, diverse, and balanced**. The first two are relatively straightforward to achieve by leveraging massive web archives. However, achieving **balance**—having a roughly uniform distribution of concepts—in an unsupervised setting without explicit labels is the core challenge this work tackles. They argue that this balance is essential to prevent models from learning biased representations and to enable them to learn truly universal features.
- **Towards Generic and Principled Curation:** The ultimate motivation is to design a generic curation algorithm that is agnostic to downstream tasks and application specifics. This would allow inferring valuable properties and creating reliable training datasets from completely uncurated sources, a critical step for future advancements in unsupervised pre-training and foundation models.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide a scalable, unsupervised, and effective solution to the fundamental problem of ensuring data quality and conceptual balance for training increasingly large and robust self-supervised models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology revolves around a novel **hierarchical k-means clustering approach coupled with a balanced sampling strategy** to curate large, diverse, and balanced datasets from uncurated data pools.

#### 3.1. The Problem of Imbalance in Uncurated Data

The authors first establish that raw, web-scale data typically exhibits a long-tailed distribution of concepts. They empirically demonstrate that models trained on artificially imbalanced ImageNet subsets suffer significant performance drops, underscoring the importance of data balance. The challenge is to achieve this balance without human annotations.

#### 3.2. Leveraging Embeddings

The method relies on pre-computed embeddings of all data points. For images, they use features extracted from a DINOv2 ViT-L model trained on ImageNet1k. For text, SBERT (all-mpnet-base-v2) is used, and for satellite images, DINOv2-reg ViT-L embeddings trained on raw satellite data are employed. The quality of these base embeddings is critical, as they serve as the "semantic space" where clustering occurs.

#### 3.3. Limitations of Vanilla K-means

The paper explains why a simple application of k-means clustering is insufficient for rebalancing. Theoretically (drawing on Zador's work), k-means centroids asymptotically follow a distribution proportional to p^(d/(d+2)), where 'p' is the data distribution's density and 'd' is the dimension. In high dimensions, this means k-means forms *more* clusters in high-density areas (dominant concepts) and fewer in low-density areas (rare concepts), thus perpetuating the imbalance rather than correcting it.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical K-means with Resampling

To overcome this limitation, the authors propose a **hierarchical k-means** algorithm. The key insight is based on a derived lemma: if a distribution Q is generated from a distribution P such that its density q is proportional to p^t (where t is a scalar between 0 and 1), then Q is closer to a uniform distribution U than P is (in terms of KL divergence). Since k-means centroids follow p^(d/(d+2)), applying k-means successively (T times) yields centroids following p^((d/(d+2))^T). As T increases, this exponent approaches zero, and the distribution of centroids approaches a uniform distribution.

The algorithm proceeds as follows (Algorithm 1):
1. **Iterative Clustering:** K-means is applied `T` times in a hierarchical manner. The first k-means clusters the raw data. Subsequent k-means operations cluster the centroids obtained from the previous level. This builds a tree structure where leaves are data points and internal nodes are centroids/clusters at different levels of granularity.
2. **Resampling-Clustering:** To allow for a sufficient number of iterations (`T`) without drastically reducing the input size for subsequent k-means, a "resampling-clustering" step is introduced. After computing clusters at level `t`, `r_t` points closest to the centroid are sampled from *each* cluster to form a reduced set `R`. The next k-means is then applied to `R` (instead of all centroids from the previous level). This ensures that the distribution of points input to k-means in subsequent levels is closer to uniform, helping the centroids converge faster to a uniform distribution over the data support. This resampling step can be repeated `m` times at each level.
3. **Choice of Cluster Numbers:** The number of clusters `k_t` at each level `t` is chosen intuitively, typically with larger numbers at lower levels (finer sub-concepts) and smaller numbers at higher levels (broader concepts).

#### 3.5. Sampling from Hierarchical Clusters

Once the hierarchical clustering is complete, a balanced subset of data is formed:
- **Flat Sampling (Baseline):** Uniformly samples a fixed number of data points (leaves) from the lowest-level clusters corresponding to the highest-level centroids.
- **Hierarchical Sampling (Proposed):** This is a top-down approach. Given a target size for a higher-level sub-tree, it recursively determines the number of points to sample from its child sub-trees. This ensures balance not only among the highest-level concepts but also among sub-concepts at all intermediate levels of the hierarchy.
- **Intra-cluster Sampling:** Within the lowest-level clusters, the authors compare sampling randomly ("r"), points closest to the centroid ("c"), or points furthest from the centroid ("f").

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

The methodology is extensively validated across three distinct data domains:
- **Web-based Images:** A large custom dataset of 743 million unique images, filtered for safety and duplicates. Evaluation uses DINOv2-reg (ViT-g and ViT-L) on various benchmarks: ImageNet classification (in-domain and OOD variants), long-tailed classification (iNaturalist), retrieval (Oxford, Paris), fine-grained classification, dense prediction (segmentation, depth estimation), and fairness (Dollar Street).
- **Text Data:** Two Common Crawl-based datasets (641M and 789M documents), varying in their initial level of curation. 7B parameter language models are trained and evaluated on common sense reasoning and world knowledge tasks.
- **Satellite Images:** An 18 million image pool of satellite patches. Models are trained for tree canopy height estimation and evaluated on NEON and other out-of-distribution datasets.

The ablation studies carefully analyze the influence of the number of hierarchical levels, sampling strategies, k-means initialization (k-means++ vs. random), number of clusters, resampling steps, and the quality of base embeddings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments across diverse data domains consistently validate the effectiveness of the proposed automatic data curation pipeline.

#### 4.1. Simulated Data Validation

Experiments on 2D simulated data visually and quantitatively confirm the theoretical premise. Hierarchical k-means with multiple levels and resampling steps effectively flattens the distribution of cluster centroids, making them approximate a uniform distribution over the data support. This outperforms vanilla k-means, DBSCAN, and Agglomerative Clustering, which tend to over-cluster dense regions.

#### 4.2. Web-Based Images (DINOv2)

- **Hierarchical K-means Efficacy:** Visual analysis (Fig. 4) shows that hierarchical k-means successfully forms larger clusters for dominant concepts, distributing clusters more equally across concept sizes than vanilla k-means. Empirically, increasing the number of hierarchical levels (e.g., from 1 to 2, or 2 to 3) leads to significant performance gains on downstream benchmarks, especially for robustness, out-of-distribution generalization, and long-tailed recognition (Table 3a).
- **Sampling Strategy:** The proposed **hierarchical sampling** consistently outperforms flat sampling (Table 3b), underscoring the importance of balancing concepts at all levels of granularity. Among intra-cluster sampling methods, random sampling ("r") generally works best, followed closely by "closest to centroid" ("c"), while "furthest from centroid" ("f") performs poorly.
- **K-means++ Initialization:** Using k-means++ for initialization is critical. It leads to substantially better performance (e.g., +18.7 on ImageNet-A, +6.7 on iNaturalist2018) compared to random initialization (Table 3c), emphasizing its role in achieving a robust and balanced clustering.
- **Sensitivity to Hyperparameters:** The method shows some sensitivity to the number of clusters and resampling steps. While more clusters can lead to finer partitions and better performance on some tasks (e.g., iNaturalist), the gains can saturate, and computational cost increases. Similarly, resampling steps are crucial for performance, with 10 steps offering a good balance (Table 3d, 3e).
- **Influence of Base Embeddings:** The quality of the initial embeddings used for clustering is paramount. Embeddings from a DINOv2 trained on ImageNet22k (a larger, manually curated dataset) yield better curated datasets than those from ImageNet1k or raw data, highlighting the need for good initial representations (Table 3f).
- **Comparison to Baselines (Table 5a & 5b):**
 - **Raw Data:** Features trained on the automatically curated dataset ("4r") significantly outperform those trained on the raw data pool across most classification and retrieval benchmarks, demonstrating improved robustness and generalization.
 - **Manually Curated (ImageNet22k):** "4r" features are highly competitive with ImageNet22k, performing comparably on ImageNet validation sets and often superiorly on out-of-distribution (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch, ObjectNet) and some retrieval tasks. This is a key finding, as "4r" is automatically generated, unlike ImageNet22k which involves substantial human effort.
 - **Retrieval-based (ImageNet1k-ret):** While "ImageNet1k-ret" excels on ImageNet-centric tasks, "4r" demonstrates better generalizability to diverse and out-of-distribution benchmarks, showcasing the limitation of seed-dependent retrieval.
 - **Dense Prediction:** The raw data pool shows a slight edge on some dense prediction tasks, likely due to a higher concentration of relevant concepts (e.g., indoor scenes) that are down-sampled in the balanced curated set. However, the performance drop is small compared to gains elsewhere.
- **Fairness (Table 5c):** Features from the curated dataset ("4r") lead to narrower performance gaps across different income levels and geographic regions on the Dollar Street dataset compared to raw data or even ImageNet22k, suggesting an improvement in fairness by mitigating biases.
- **Qualitative Evaluation:** The hierarchical clustering produces coherent concepts and sub-concepts (e.g., "food" breaking down into "dessert" or "main dish"), confirming its ability to capture meaningful structure in the data.

#### 4.3. Text Data (LLMs)

Applying the curation pipeline to two Common Crawl-based text datasets ("ccnet-1" and "ccnet-2") leads to consistent and significant improvements in LLM performance on common sense reasoning and world knowledge benchmarks (Table 6). This highlights that even for pre-filtered text corpora, further balancing through this method yields benefits.

#### 4.4. Satellite Images

For canopy height estimation from satellite imagery, models trained on the automatically curated dataset show significant improvements on all benchmarks (Table 7), achieving a 20% relative improvement in the r2 metric on average, with the largest gains on out-of-distribution aerial data. This demonstrates the method's generality across diverse data types and tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in the field of large-scale machine learning, particularly for self-supervised learning. Its potential impacts are far-reaching:

- **Enabling Scalability for Foundation Models:** The primary significance lies in providing a principled, automatic, and scalable solution to data curation, a major bottleneck in the development and training of increasingly massive foundation models (LLMs, large vision models). By removing the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and ad-hoc manual curation, this method opens the door to truly leveraging the vast, unstructured data available on the internet.
- **Improved Model Robustness and Generalization:** The empirical results conclusively demonstrate that models trained on automatically curated datasets exhibit superior robustness to out-of-distribution data and better generalization across a wider range of downstream tasks. This is crucial for deploying AI systems in real-world scenarios, which often encounter data variations not seen during training.
- **Mitigation of Data Biases:** By explicitly addressing the long-tail distribution problem and striving for concept balance, the proposed method inherently helps to mitigate biases present in uncurated web data. The improved fairness metrics on the Dollar Street dataset underscore this positive impact, leading to AI systems that might be more equitable in their performance across diverse demographics.
- **Domain Agnostic and Generic Applicability:** The method's core strength is its generality. It only requires the ability to extract meaningful embeddings from the raw data, making it applicable across various modalities (images, text, satellite imagery) and domains, as robustly demonstrated in the paper. This broadens its utility far beyond specific computer vision tasks.
- **Advancing Unsupervised Learning:** This work represents a significant step towards truly unsupervised pre-training. While it currently relies on pre-trained feature extractors (which themselves might have been trained on curated data like ImageNet1k), it reduces the *direct* human intervention in preparing the large-scale pre-training dataset itself. Future work could explore methods to make the initial feature extraction less reliant on manually curated seeds.
- **Broader Impact on Data Management:** The proposed hierarchical k-means approach, with its ability to generate diverse and representative data subsets, has potential applications beyond SSL data curation. It could be valuable in other data management tasks such as active learning (selecting informative samples for annotation) or data pruning (removing redundant samples), where a balanced and representative sample is desired without labels.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This paper lays a strong foundation for future research into data-centric AI, especially in understanding and manipulating data distributions for optimal model performance. It encourages a shift from merely accumulating vast amounts of data to intelligently structuring and balancing it.

In conclusion, this research offers a compelling solution to a fundamental challenge in modern AI, paving the way for more efficient, robust, and potentially fairer large-scale self-supervised learning systems.
