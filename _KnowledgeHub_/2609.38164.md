# Rho: A Foundation for Efficiently Adaptable VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Rho is Microsoft's family of open-weights 5B-parameter VLAs for bimanual manipulation. It pairs a physically grounded Phi-family vision-language backbone with a 542M flow-matching action expert. Its recipe splits training into multi-embodiment pretraining, single-robot embodiment midtraining, and task finetuning, and adds an internal latent policy for online correction. Midtraining halves the task data needed, and Rho beats pi0.5, GR00T-N1.7 and MolmoAct2 in simulation and on three physical robots.

## Problem

- Existing VLAs and world-action models still have low zero-shot success on unseen tasks and environments, so data-hungry task adaptation remains the main bottleneck to practical deployment.
- Pretraining gives broad but shallow exposure to many embodiments, so finetuning must teach the target robot's kinematics, cameras and action conventions alongside the task, which inflates data needs.
- Offline finetuning only covers states seen in small demonstration sets, while updating a full VLA online is computationally costly and risks eroding capabilities inherited from pretraining.

## Method

- **Phi-Phy** backbone: a 4.68B Phi-family VLM with a ==SigLIP 2 NaFlex== encoder, grounded on robotics VQA data and co-trained 9:1 with robot batches at 0.02 loss weight.
- Flow-matching action expert: a 12-block, 2,048-wide transformer with 128-dim ==grouped-query heads== and shared ==adaLN-single== modulation, cross-attending to one mid-layer backbone representation, 542M parameters in total.
- **Regularized progressive adaptation**: multi-embodiment pretraining on ==chunk-relative delta end-effector actions==, then single-robot embodiment midtraining, offline task finetuning, and ==FlowDAgger== online training of an internal observation-conditioned latent noise policy.

## Results

- On RoboTwin 2.0 held-out tasks, midtraining halves the finetuning data needed for equal success; on physical FR3 Duo plug insertion, success rises from **60.0%** to **86.7%** after two midtraining epochs.
- Finetuned Rho reaches **73.4%** average success on RoboEval, **6.3 points** above the next-best baseline, and **97.9%** average on LIBERO, including **95.9%** on LIBERO-Long.
- On FR3 Duo, Rho averages **80.0%** success versus **50.0%** for GR00T-N1.7 and **46.7%** for pi0.5; 15 corrected episodes lift test-tube assembly from **30%** to **70%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating embodiment adaptation from task adaptation pays off: broad multi-task data on the target robot builds reusable reachability knowledge that helps most on edge-of-distribution configurations.
> - Action-expert shape matters more than raw size: 128-dim heads and width drive precision-task success, while shared adaLN, grouped-query attention and fewer blocks cut parameters by two-thirds without loss.
> - Steering only a frozen flow policy's initial noise is a cheap online adaptation surface that preserves capabilities, and a tenth of the offline demonstration budget fixes known weak regions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Learning a bimanual manipulation task from a limited demonstration set is harder than it looks. The demonstrations may have to teach a robot policy both *how this robot moves and sees* and *what to do*. Rho separates those lessons. A shared model is first familiarized with one robot across many tasks, and then finetuned for a specific task. The authors call this staged recipe "regularized progressive adaptation."

The payoff shows up when task data is scarce. On five held-out tasks in a simulation benchmark, with 12.5% of the finetuning set, mean success on the Easy protocol rose from 28.1% for pretrained-only Rho to 40.6% for midtrained Rho.

Success rate on five held-out RoboTwin 2.0 tasks for pretrained-only Rho (purple) and midtrained Rho-RoboTwin (red), at 12.5%, 25%, 50%, and 100% of the finetuning dataset. Each checkpoint is adapted for 40k steps and evaluated on 50 episodes per task. Bars show means, with error bars over four Easy and three Hard seeds. Hard adds distractors and varies scene conditions. These are simulation results.

### 2. Learn the robot, then the job

Rho is a vision-language-action model (VLA): it uses visual observations and language instructions to generate robot actions. Its training proceeds in three stages:

1. A physically grounded vision-language foundation and multi-embodiment robot pretraining produce Rho-base.
1. Broad single-embodiment midtraining then exposes the model to a target robot's camera views, controls, kinematics, and motions across many tasks.
1. Downstream offline finetuning specializes that checkpoint to a particular task.

Midtraining is intended to familiarize the model with the platform, not to master any one task. The report releases midtrained variants for YAM Box, UR AI Trainer, and FR3 Duo.

### 3. Does midtraining save task data?

The controlled test compares two starting points under identical task training. Rho-base and Rho-RoboTwin are each adapted for 40k steps on the same nested fractions of demonstrations for five dual-arm UR5e tasks. Those tasks were excluded from midtraining. Both models are then evaluated on the same held-out tasks.

"Success" is the benchmark's binary task-success predicate, averaged across the five tasks. The Easy setting keeps the training backgrounds, lighting, and table height, without added clutter.

The authors report that, up to performance saturation, the midtrained model generally reached comparable success using about half as much task data, in both Easy and Hard. This is an approximate pattern rather than an exact two-to-one conversion: the plotted 25%-versus-50% bars are close, not identical. The benefit was greatest at low data and narrowed as the task-data set grew.

A physical check points the same way. On FR3 Duo plug insertion, the comparison held task data fixed and used 50k task-adaptation steps. Success was 18/30 (60.0%) without midtraining and 26/30 (86.7%) after two FR3 midtraining epochs. According to the authors, much of the gain appeared on plug configurations near the edge of the task-training distribution.

The authors hypothesize that broad exposure helps the policy model reachability and motion geometry. That is their interpretation. The experiment measures the outcome, not that mechanism alone. The midtrained model also receives additional robot-task training and compute, so these comparisons do not isolate "learning kinematics" as the sole mediator.

### 4. Learn the platform first

Rho's data-efficient task learning comes from giving the task policy a robot-familiar starting point. The controlled data-efficiency result comes from a simulation benchmark on one target embodiment and five held-out tasks. The physical check covers one FR3 Duo plug-insertion task over 30 trials. Together they support the value of the staged recipe in the tested settings, not universal generalization.

The authors release the base and robot-specific checkpoints as an open-weights resource: [Rho model collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/microsoft/rho).
