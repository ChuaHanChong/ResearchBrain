# Why Fine-Tuning Encourages Hallucinations and How to Fix It

> [!summary] Summary
> This research redefines supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-induced hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) as factual forgetting, analogous to catastrophic forgetting. It demonstrates that localized interference from semantically overlapping representations drives this phenomenon and proposes parameter freezing and self-distillation as mitigation strategies, reducing factual forgetting by approximately 80% (from 15% to 3% decline in accuracy) while enabling new knowledge acquisition.

## Problem

- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) frequently leads Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate factually incorrect statements (hallucinations) by causing them to forget previously known information.
- This SFT-induced factual forgetting, distinct from pre-training knowledge gaps, poses a significant barrier to LLM reliability and trustworthiness.
- The underlying mechanisms driving this specific type of factual degradation during SFT were not well understood, hindering the development of targeted solutions.

## Method

- The study reframes SFT-induced hallucinations as 'factual forgetting' within the continual learning paradigm, allowing for the application of established mitigation techniques.
- Parameter freezing is proposed as a 'stability-first' strategy, demonstrating that updating only non-factual knowledge-related parameters (e.g., specific attention components) prevents new fact acquisition and preserves existing knowledge.
- Self-distillation, a continual learning technique, is applied to enable models to acquire new factual knowledge while substantially minimizing the forgetting of pre-existing knowledge.

## Results

- Standard SFT consistently caused a ~15 percentage point decline in accuracy on held-out factual knowledge (D_Held) as new facts were learned.
- Freezing specific attention parameters effectively prevented new fact acquisition while maintaining high factual stability, preserving ~**93%** accuracy on held-out known facts.
- Self-distillation reduced factual forgetting from ~**15%** to approximately **3%** degradation on held-out facts, providing an ~**80%** mitigation of forgetting while allowing new factual knowledge to be learned.
- Synthetic experiments demonstrated that forgetting was highly sensitive to semantic similarity, with semantically related new facts causing substantial accuracy drops, whereas unrelated keys induced only 0-**4%** forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT-induced hallucinations are primarily a result of factual forgetting that occurs when LLMs learn new facts, rather than a general consequence of fine-tuning or global capacity limitations.
> - Localized interference among semantically overlapping representations is identified as the main mechanism driving factual forgetting, not merely the displacement of old facts by new ones due to finite model capacity.
> - Continual learning strategies, specifically parameter freezing for stability and self-distillation for balanced plasticity and stability, offer effective and principled ways to mitigate factual forgetting in LLMs.
