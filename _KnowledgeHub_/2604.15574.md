# Why Fine-Tuning Encourages Hallucinations and How to Fix It

> [!summary] Summary
> This research redefines supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-induced hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) as factual forgetting, analogous to catastrophic forgetting. It demonstrates that localized interference from semantically overlapping representations drives this phenomenon and proposes parameter freezing and self-distillation as mitigation strategies, reducing factual forgetting by approximately 80% (from 15% to 3% decline in accuracy) while enabling new knowledge acquisition.

## Problem

- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) frequently leads Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate factually incorrect statements (hallucinations) by causing them to forget previously known information.
- This SFT-induced factual forgetting, distinct from pre-training knowledge gaps, poses a significant barrier to LLM reliability and trustworthiness.
- The underlying mechanisms driving this specific type of factual degradation during SFT were not well understood, hindering the development of targeted solutions.

## Method

- The study reframes SFT-induced hallucinations as 'factual forgetting' within the continual learning paradigm, allowing for the application of established mitigation techniques.
- Parameter freezing is proposed as a 'stability-first' strategy, demonstrating that updating only non-factual knowledge-related parameters (e.g., specific attention components) prevents new fact acquisition and preserves existing knowledge.
- Self-distillation, a continual learning technique, is applied to enable models to acquire new factual knowledge while substantially minimizing the forgetting of pre-existing knowledge.

## Results

- Standard SFT caused a ~15 percentage point decline in held-out factual accuracy as new facts were learned, but self-distillation cut this forgetting to ~**3%**, an ~**80%** mitigation while still learning new facts.
- Freezing specific attention parameters effectively prevented new fact acquisition while maintaining high factual stability, preserving ~**93%** accuracy on held-out known facts.
- Synthetic experiments demonstrated that forgetting was highly sensitive to semantic similarity, with semantically related new facts causing substantial accuracy drops, whereas unrelated keys induced only 0-**4%** forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT-induced hallucinations are primarily a result of factual forgetting that occurs when LLMs learn new facts, rather than a general consequence of fine-tuning or global capacity limitations.
> - Localized interference among semantically overlapping representations is identified as the main mechanism driving factual forgetting, not merely the displacement of old facts by new ones due to finite model capacity.
> - Continual learning strategies, specifically parameter freezing for stability and self-distillation for balanced plasticity and stability, offer effective and principled ways to mitigate factual forgetting in LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the development and deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs): the phenomenon of hallucinations. Hallucinations, defined as the generation of factually incorrect or ungrounded statements, pose a significant barrier to the reliability of LLM applications. Recent studies have identified supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as a contributor to these errors, specifically causing models to produce incorrect answers to questions they previously answered correctly based on their pre-training knowledge. This phenomenon is distinct from hallucinations arising from knowledge gaps in pre-training or inference-time errors.

This research integrates insights from the field of continual learning, which studies how neural networks acquire new information sequentially without forgetting previously learned knowledge (a concept known as catastrophic forgetting). The paper reinterprets SFT-induced hallucinations as a form of factual forgetting that arises from the stability-plasticity tradeoff inherent in sequential model training. While continual learning has proposed various mitigation strategies for knowledge degradation, their application to LLM factual hallucinations has been less explored. This work bridges these areas by examining whether established continual learning techniques can mitigate SFT-induced factual forgetting in LLMs, thereby reducing hallucinations. Furthermore, it investigates the underlying mechanisms driving this forgetting, connecting it to representational interference rather than global capacity limitations or general behavioral shifts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and mitigate supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-induced hallucinations in large language models. The motivation stems from the observation that SFT, a standard practice in LLM development, can inadvertently cause models to forget pre-existing factual knowledge, leading to the generation of incorrect statements. This degradation of previously acquired knowledge during SFT is interpreted by the authors as a form of factual forgetting, analogous to catastrophic forgetting observed in continual learning.

The specific objectives of the study are:

- **Reframe SFT-induced hallucinations as factual forgetting:** The authors hypothesize that hallucinations occurring after SFT, particularly regarding facts known prior to fine-tuning, are a manifestation of knowledge degradation due to the stability-plasticity tradeoff during training. This reframing positions the problem within the established framework of continual learning.
- **Determine if continual learning strategies can mitigate factual forgetting:** The research explores whether techniques from the continual learning literature can be effectively applied to reduce SFT-induced hallucinations. This involves testing methods designed to preserve previously acquired knowledge while allowing for new learning.
- **Develop and evaluate mitigation strategies:**
 - **Controlling factual plasticity:** Investigate if reducing the model's ability to acquire new facts (factual plasticity) through methods like parameter freezing can preserve existing knowledge (factual stability) and reduce hallucinations, particularly in scenarios where new fact acquisition is not desired.
 - **Enabling factual learning without forgetting:** Explore methods that allow models to acquire new factual knowledge *and* retain pre-existing knowledge simultaneously, addressing the stability-plasticity dilemma. Self-distillation is proposed and evaluated for this purpose.
- **Investigate the mechanisms underlying SFT-induced hallucinations:** The study aims to understand *why* SFT causes factual forgetting. It tests three hypotheses:
 1. **Capacity limitations:** Suggests that new facts displace old ones due to finite model capacity.
 2. **Behavior cloning:** Proposes that SFT encourages a general tendency to generate answers, potentially ungrounded, irrespective of knowledge.
 3. **Localized interference:** Hypothesizes that new facts corrupt existing ones when they share overlapping representational structures. By disentangling these potential drivers, the research seeks to provide a mechanistic explanation for factual forgetting.

Through these objectives, the research aims to transform the understanding of SFT-induced hallucinations from an inevitable side effect into a tractable problem that can be addressed using principled continual learning approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach involving controlled experiments, comparative analysis of training strategies, and mechanistic investigations.

#### 3.1. Controlled Experimental Setup

The study adopted and reproduced the experimental setting of Gekhman et al. (2024) to disentangle task learning (acquiring the QA format) from factual learning (acquiring specific facts).

- **SLiCK Method:** To assess the model's pre-existing knowledge, the SLiCK method was applied. This method categorizes factual relations into HighlyKnown, MaybeKnown, WeaklyKnown, and Unknown based on a model's consistent performance across multiple randomized few-shot prompting configurations. For the main experiments, only HighlyKnown and Unknown examples were retained to clearly isolate factual learning and forgetting.
- **Dataset Splits:**
 - **D_Known:** A subset of HighlyKnown facts used to teach the QA task format without introducing new factual content.
 - **D_Unk:** A subset of Unknown facts used to measure the model's ability to acquire new factual knowledge (factual plasticity).
 - **D_Held:** A disjoint subset of HighlyKnown facts reserved for evaluation, directly quantifying the stability of previously acquired knowledge and SFT-induced factual hallucinations (factual stability).
 - **D_train:** Formed as D_Known ∪ D_Unk. The "Only Known" condition used D_Known only, without D_Unk.

#### 3.2. Data

The **ENTITYQUESTIONS** dataset (Sciavolino et al., 2021), comprising QA pairs from Wikipedia relational triplets (e.g., birthplaces, spouses), served as the primary benchmark. SLiCK classification was performed using 20 evaluation runs per question with three few-shot exemplars. Relations with at least 30% HighlyKnown examples were retained. From these, 8,000 D_Known and 8,000 D_Unk examples were sampled from the training split, and D_Held was drawn from the development split containing HighlyKnown facts from the same relations.

#### 3.3. Models

Experiments were conducted with several non-reasoning LLMs: QWEN 2.5 (1.5B and 8B parameters) and LLAMA 3.1 (8B parameters). QWEN 2.5-1.5B was the primary model for reported figures and representative results, with consistent qualitative trends observed across all models.

#### 3.4. Training Procedure

Models were fine-tuned with a learning rate of 5 × 10⁻⁵ on D_train, using either a 50/50 ratio of Known and Unknown facts or the "Only Known" setting. Performance was evaluated separately on D_Known (task learning), D_Unk (factual plasticity), and D_Held (factual stability) throughout training.

#### 3.5. Mitigation Strategies

- **Parameter Freezing:** To assess controlling factual plasticity, experiments involved freezing different parameter groups. Subsets of parameters (θ_S) were updated, including:
 - Attention layers only
 - FFN layers only
 - Full model (standard SFT baseline)
 - Fine-grained ablations of individual attention projections (q, k, v, o) and FFN sub-components (gate+up, down) were also performed.
- **Self-Distillation:** A continual learning technique (Li & Hoiem, 2017) was applied. The model was first trained on D_Known for one epoch to acquire the QA task format, and this snapshot was frozen as the teacher model (θ_i). Subsequent SFT on D_train (D_Known ∪ D_Unk) used an objective combining standard next-token prediction loss (L_task) with a distillation loss (L_distill). L_distill penalizes KL divergence between the student model's output distribution (θ) and the frozen teacher's output distribution (θ_i), using a temperature parameter (τ = 0.5) and distillation weight (λ = 1).

#### 3.6. Mechanism Investigation

To understand the origin of factual forgetting, a synthetic setting was created, varying representational overlap and scale.

- **Synthetic Facts:** The P17 Wikidata relation (Location → Country) was used. Entity names (keys) were constructed in two ways:
 - **Semantic Keys:** Formed by recombining tokens from real location names (e.g., "Bergadena" from "Bergamo" and "Pasadena") to resemble existing entities and activate overlapping representations.
 - **UUID Keys:** Random identifiers (e.g., "Loc_fcfb46ee") with no syntactic similarity to known entities, intended to occupy disjoint representational neighborhoods.
 Values were real country names. The number of synthetic facts was scaled from 10³ to 10⁶.
- **Comparison of Regularization:** Self-distillation was compared against L2 regularization (λ∥θ - θ_i∥₂²) to differentiate its effect from generic weight movement.
- **Drift Analysis:** Multiple metrics were used to track changes in internal representations and output distributions for held-out entities:
 - **Hidden-state drift (RD):** Average cosine distance between held-out entity representations (final token of the key entity at layer 14) before and after fine-tuning.
 - **Neighborhood structure (NS):** Measures changes in pairwise geometric structure among held-out entity representations.
 - **Mean targeted drift (MTD):** Measures whether held-out representations drift toward or away from new-entity representations.
 - **Output-distribution drift (Rank-ρ):** Spearman rank correlation between logit rankings for unknown-fact inputs and held-out inputs.
 - **Neighborhood token ratio (NTR):** Measures overlap in the top-k answer candidate sets for new-fact and held-out queries.
 - **Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD):** Quantifies differences in the full probability shape of output distributions.
- **Output Distribution Target Ablation:** Variants of distillation (Full, Top-k, Random-k) were tested to determine if constraining the entire output distribution or specific high-probability regions was essential.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding SFT-induced hallucinations, their mitigation, and their underlying mechanisms.

#### 4.1. SFT-Induced Hallucinations as Factual Forgetting

Standard SFT on a mixture of known and unknown facts (D_Known ∪ D_Unk) consistently led to a two-stage learning pattern. Initially, models rapidly learned the QA task format and achieved high accuracy on D_Known and D_Held. However, as training progressed and the model acquired new factual knowledge (increasing D_Unk accuracy), performance on D_Held systematically declined by approximately 15 percentage points. This decline saturated once learning on D_Unk converged. In contrast, when D_Unk was excluded from training ("Only Known" condition), D_Held performance remained stable. This confirmed that the acquisition of new factual information, rather than fine-tuning itself, was the causal driver of increased factual hallucinations, which were reframed as factual forgetting.

#### 4.2. Mitigation Strategy 1: Controlling Factual Plasticity via Parameter Constraints

Freezing specific parameter groups was shown to control factual plasticity and, consequently, reduce hallucinations.
- **Attention vs. FFN:** Training only the FFN layers resulted in similar dynamics to standard SFT, with new facts acquired and significant forgetting on D_Held. However, updating only attention parameters (or specific attention projections like `k`, `v`, or `o`) prevented new fact acquisition (D_Unk remained near chance levels) while preserving task learning (high D_Known) and maintaining high factual stability (D_Held remained close to its pre-trained level, ~93%). This suggests that in scenarios where new fact acquisition is undesirable, selective parameter freezing can be an effective mitigation.

#### 4.3. Mitigation Strategy 2: Self-Distillation for Simultaneous Factual Learning and Stability

Self-distillation, a continual learning technique, enabled models to acquire new facts while substantially reducing factual forgetting.
- The self-distillation approach, using a teacher model frozen after one epoch of task-format learning, reduced D_Held degradation to approximately 3%, compared to the 15% decline observed under regular SFT. Concurrently, D_Unk acquisition followed a similar learning trajectory to standard SFT, indicating that new factual knowledge was effectively learned. This demonstrates self-distillation as a strategy for fine-tuning scenarios requiring both factual learning and factual stability.
- Hyperparameter ablations for self-distillation revealed that freezing the teacher model after one epoch of task-format learning (i=1) yielded the best stability. A distillation weight (λ=1) provided a balance between efficient factual acquisition and forgetting mitigation. Lower temperatures (τ=0.5) led to better D_Held stability.
- The effectiveness of self-distillation generalized across models, providing approximately 80% mitigation of forgetting in Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, and showing even greater benefit for "MaybeKnown" facts, which are less robustly encoded and thus more susceptible to forgetting.

#### 4.4. Mechanism of Forgetting: Localized Interference and Semantic Overlap

The investigation into the mechanisms behind SFT-induced hallucinations supported a structural interference account.
- **Semantic Overlap Drives Forgetting:** In controlled synthetic experiments, forgetting was highly sensitive to the semantic similarity of new entities. Fine-tuning on semantic (name-like) keys caused D_Held to grow sharply with the number of new facts, leading to substantial drops at 10⁶ synthetic facts. In contrast, UUID-based keys (syntactically unrelated) induced minimal forgetting (0–4%) across all scales, largely insensitive to dataset size. This asymmetry suggests that interference among overlapping semantic representations, rather than global capacity limitations or general behavioral shifts, is a primary driver of forgetting.
- **Self-Distillation Mitigates Interference:** Self-distillation on semantic keys showed a hidden-state drift (RD) trajectory similar to SFT on UUID keys, stabilizing at around 5%. This contrasts with SFT on semantic keys, which showed RD increasing to 11%. This indicates that self-distillation effectively prevents representational drift of held-out facts, thereby mitigating interference caused by semantically overlapping entities.
- **Output-Level Interference:** Further drift metrics consistently supported this. Neighborhood structure (NS) showed less reorganization under self-distillation. Mean targeted drift (MTD) indicated that held-out and synthetic representations diverged under SFT but remained stable with self-distillation. Output-distribution drift (Rank-ρ) and neighborhood token ratio (NTR) demonstrated that SFT caused new-fact and held-out queries to converge or diverge in output space, respectively, while self-distillation preserved their separation.
- **Importance of High-Probability Region:** Ablations on the distillation objective revealed that constraining the teacher's top-k high-probability tokens (Top-k distillation) was as effective as full self-distillation, whereas constraining random tokens was not. This suggests that the "dark knowledge" or informative relational structure within the high-probability region of the output distribution is crucial for preventing interference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several contributions with implications for LLM development and deployment:

- **Reconceptualization of Hallucinations:** By reframing SFT-induced hallucinations as a form of factual forgetting within the continual learning paradigm, the work provides a new theoretical lens for understanding this critical LLM failure mode. This perspective moves beyond viewing hallucinations as an inherent and untraceable defect, instead suggesting a mechanistic and potentially solvable problem.
- **Practical Mitigation Strategies:** The study proposes two distinct and complementary strategies for reducing SFT-induced hallucinations:
 - **Parameter Freezing:** Demonstrated as effective for "stability-first" scenarios where preserving pre-trained knowledge is paramount and new factual acquisition is unnecessary (e.g., alignment fine-tuning, domain adaptation without new facts). This offers a low-cost, readily implementable solution.
 - **Self-Distillation:** Provides a method for "plasticity-and-stability" scenarios, enabling LLMs to acquire new factual knowledge efficiently while substantially minimizing the forgetting of existing knowledge. This is crucial for applications requiring ongoing knowledge updates or domain adaptation with novel factual content. The observed reduction in factual forgetting from ~15% to ~3% represents a practical improvement.
- **Mechanistic Understanding of Forgetting:** The detailed investigation into the origins of forgetting, through synthetic data experiments and comprehensive drift analysis, provides evidence that localized interference among semantically overlapping representations is a primary driver. This contradicts simpler explanations like global capacity limits or generic behavioral cloning. This mechanistic insight can guide the development of more targeted and effective anti-forgetting techniques in the future.
- **Leveraging Continual Learning:** The work successfully transfers established principles and techniques from the continual learning literature to address a contemporary challenge in LLMs. This encourages further interdisciplinary research between these fields, potentially unlocking more robust and stable LLM learning paradigms.
- **Improved LLM Reliability:** By offering methods to reduce factual hallucinations, the research directly contributes to enhancing the trustworthiness and reliability of LLMs in real-world applications, where factual accuracy is often critical. This could broaden the safe deployment of LLMs in sensitive domains.
