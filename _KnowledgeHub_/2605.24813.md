# Manifold-Constrained MPPI: Real-Time Sampling-Based Control Under Hard Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> Manifold-Constrained MPPI (MC-MPPI) is a real-time sampling-based control framework for robotic systems subject to hard, manifold-based equality constraints, combining latent-space planning with a VAE and a single-step Quadratic Program for precise constraint enforcement. The framework achieved a 95% success rate in real-world dynamic obstacle avoidance tasks with a 14-DoF dual-arm system, maintaining constraint violations below 0.007.

## Problem

- Robotic systems requiring strict satisfaction of hard kinematic equality constraints, such as in closed-chain manipulation, cannot be reliably controlled by standard sampling-based methods like MPPI due to their reliance on soft penalties.
- Existing MPPI variants for constraint handling are primarily designed for inequality constraints or provide only soft safety guarantees, making them unsuitable for hard, manifold-based equality constraints which define a zero-measure subspace.
- Gradient-based optimal control methods, while capable of satisfying constraints, often incur high computational costs and are susceptible to local minima when dealing with complex nonlinear equality constraints in high-dimensional spaces.

## Method

- A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is pre-trained to learn a low-dimensional, continuous latent space representation of the nonlinear constraint manifold, allowing efficient generation of near-feasible configurations.
- ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (**MPPI**) performs trajectory optimization within this learned latent space, using constant-innovation rollouts to efficiently explore and generate near-feasible control sequences.
- A single-step Quadratic Program (QP) is implemented at the execution stage to precisely correct the residual approximation errors from the VAE, projecting the robot's state exactly onto the constraint manifold in real time while preserving task objectives.

## Results

- MC-MPPI consistently maintained hard equality constraints with an average violation norm of **0.0066** ± **0.0007**, significantly outperforming Latent MPPI (**0.0199**) and Vanilla MPPI (**0.0314**) which failed.
- The framework achieved a **95%** success rate (38/40 trials) in real-world dynamic obstacle avoidance tasks with a 14-DoF closed-chain dual-arm robot while strictly enforcing manifold constraints.
- MC-MPPI demonstrated real-time performance, with the MPPI planning stage operating at 100 Hz (avg **7.098** ms) and the QP execution stage at 500 Hz (avg **1.277** ms).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the constrained optimal control problem into latent-space planning and execution-level correction effectively integrates the robustness of sampling-based methods with guaranteed hard constraint satisfaction.
> - Performing MPPI in a low-dimensional, learned latent space (parameterizing the constraint manifold) overcomes the inefficiency of directly sampling sparse feasible regions in the high-dimensional configuration space.
> - A computationally efficient, single-step QP can robustly enforce hard constraints by using the accurate linearization provided by the near-feasible reference generated from latent-space planning, avoiding iterative projections.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control frequently encounters scenarios where systems are subject to strict kinematic constraints. Such constraints, exemplified by closed-chain manipulation tasks, necessitate control strategies that can guarantee their satisfaction in real time, as even minor violations can lead to system damage or operational failure.

Traditional approaches to enforcing kinematic constraints often involve gradient-based model predictive control (MPC) solvers, such as Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP), Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP), and Iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR). These methods are recognized for their mathematical rigor in handling nonlinear constraint satisfaction. However, they typically operate directly within the full configuration space, where the feasible set for equality constraints is a thin, nonlinear submanifold. This can lead to limitations, including sensitivity to local minima in non-convex problems and computational costs that scale unfavorably with the number of constraints and the planning horizon.

In contrast, Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control offers a derivative-free, sampling-based alternative. MPPI is known for its inherent robustness to local minima and its ability to leverage parallel computation, particularly with modern GPU hardware, for real-time trajectory optimization in high-dimensional systems (e.g., STORM framework). A significant limitation of standard MPPI, however, is its reliance on cost-based soft penalties for constraints. This approach treats constraint violations as additive cost terms rather than strict boundaries, which means it cannot provide guarantees of hard-constraint satisfaction. This limits its applicability in safety-critical tasks requiring absolute physical feasibility.

Efforts to integrate constraint handling within the MPPI framework have explored two primary directions:
1. **Proactive safety filtering methodologies:** This includes using Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) to define safe sets, often formulated as soft penalties or combined with local gradient-based repair steps (e.g., Shield-MPPI). These methods, while improving safety, commonly share the limitation of not fully satisfying hard constraints and may exhibit suboptimal safety behaviors in highly constrained environments.
2. **Constraint-aware sampling strategies:** More recent approaches (e.g., π-MPPI, SCP-MPPI, CSC-MPPI) aim to modify sampled control sequences through techniques like quadratic programming-based projections, Stein Variational Gradient Descent, or iterative primal-dual updates. These strategies are primarily designed for inequality constraints (e.g., obstacle avoidance, joint limits) and are not directly applicable to manifold-based nonlinear equality constraints. Equality constraints confine the system to a lower-dimensional subspace of zero Lebesgue measure, making standard inequality-based filters computationally intensive and numerically unstable when trying to force high-dimensional samples onto such structures.

This paper positions its proposed Manifold-Constrained MPPI (MC-MPPI) framework as a solution to address this specific gap: enabling real-time, sampling-based control for hard, manifold-based equality constraints, which has been a challenge for existing MPPI variants and computationally intensive for gradient-based methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time sampling-based control framework that can effectively and reliably enforce hard, manifold-based equality constraints, while retaining the computational advantages of Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI).

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in controlling complex robotic systems, particularly those involved in tasks like closed-chain manipulation:

- **Necessity for Hard Constraint Satisfaction:** Robotic systems in tasks such as dual-arm object grasping are subject to strict kinematic constraints (e.g., maintaining a rigid relative pose between end-effectors, ensuring object flatness). Violations of these constraints, even if marginal, can result in significant issues, including excessive internal forces, damage to the manipulated object, or a complete loss of stability and control. Therefore, a control method that can *guarantee* hard constraint satisfaction is required.
- **Limitations of Standard MPPI for Hard Constraints:** While MPPI offers advantages such as derivative-free optimization and robustness to local minima, its conventional formulation relies on soft penalties within the cost function to handle constraints. This approach treats constraint violations as undesirable but allows them, which means it cannot provide any formal guarantee of hard-constraint satisfaction. This fundamentally restricts its applicability to safety-critical tasks where physical feasibility must be maintained at all times.
- **Inadequacy of Existing MPPI Constraint Handling for Equality Constraints:** Prior attempts to integrate constraints into MPPI, whether through safety filtering (e.g., Control Barrier Functions) or constraint-aware sampling (e.g., projection-based methods), have primarily focused on inequality constraints or soft safety guarantees. These methods are generally not well-suited for manifold-based equality constraints, which define a lower-dimensional subspace with zero Lebesgue measure. Applying such methods to equality constraints typically leads to severe computational bottlenecks and numerical instability due to the difficulty of correcting high-dimensional samples to lie precisely on such complex geometric structures.
- **Computational and Robustness Issues with Gradient-Based Methods:** Gradient-based optimal control methods, though mathematically rigorous for constraint satisfaction, can be computationally expensive, particularly for long planning horizons or a large number of constraints. They are also prone to getting stuck in local minima in non-convex optimization landscapes. Operating directly in the high-dimensional configuration space for manifold constraints further exacerbates these issues.
- **Need for Real-Time Performance:** For dynamic robotic tasks, the control framework must operate at high frequencies (e.g., 100 Hz) to enable reactive behavior and stable manipulation in changing environments. This demands a computationally efficient approach.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes a novel approach that decouples the constrained optimal control problem into two stages:
1. **Latent-space planning:** To allow efficient sampling for *near-feasible* trajectories by mapping the high-dimensional, constrained configuration space to a lower-dimensional, unconstrained latent space.
2. **Execution-level correction:** To resolve the inevitable approximation errors from the latent-space model and *guarantee* exact constraint satisfaction.

This two-stage strategy aims to preserve MPPI's computational benefits and robustness while overcoming its fundamental limitation regarding hard constraint enforcement for manifold-based equality constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Manifold-Constrained MPPI (MC-MPPI) framework utilizes a hierarchical control architecture, comprising an upper-level planner and a lower-level execution module. This structure decouples the problem of handling manifold-based equality constraints into distinct planning and correction stages.

#### 3.1. A. Planning Stage

The planning stage focuses on generating near-feasible trajectories in a computationally efficient manner by operating in a learned low-dimensional latent space.

1. **Manifold Learning via Variational Autoencoder (VAE):**
 - **Problem:** Robotic systems with `n` degrees of freedom (DoF) subject to `l` kinematic equality constraints, defined by `h(q) = 0`, confine the system to a nonlinear constraint manifold `M` of intrinsic dimension `m = n - l`. Direct sampling in the high-dimensional ambient space `R^n` to find configurations on `M` is highly inefficient as feasible samples occur with near-zero probability.
 - **Solution:** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is employed. The VAE is pre-trained to learn a continuous, low-dimensional latent representation of the constraint manifold `M`. The decoder component of the VAE, `ψ_θ: R^m → R^n`, provides a nonlinear mapping that approximately parameterizes `M`.
 - **Purpose:** This allows trajectory optimization to be performed in the unconstrained, lower-dimensional latent space `R^m`, enabling the generation of structurally near-feasible configurations.

2. **Latent Space Control Formulation:**
 - **Dynamics:** Instead of direct control in the joint space `R^n`, control is applied to the latent space `R^m`. The latent control input `u_t ∈ R^m` is interpreted as a latent-space velocity (`˙z_t`). Latent state evolution is modeled using first-order Euler integration: `z_t+1 = f_z(z_t, ~u_t) = z_t + ~u_t Δt`.
 - **Stochasticity:** Similar to standard MPPI, perturbed latent control inputs `~u_t` are generated by adding Gaussian noise `δu_t` to a nominal control sequence `u_t`: `~u_t = u_t + δu_t`, where `δu_t ∼ N(0, Σ)`.
 - **Objective:** The stochastic optimal control problem is defined over a finite horizon `T` to minimize an expected cost function `J`, which includes terminal and stage costs `c(z_t), ϕ(z_T)` and a control effort penalty `u_t^T R u_t`.

3. **MPPI in Latent Space:**
 - **Cost Evaluation:** Costs (e.g., end-effector tracking error, collision avoidance, joint limits) are typically defined in the joint or operational space. To evaluate these from latent samples `~z`, each predicted latent state `~z_t+1` along a sampled trajectory is decoded into the joint space using the pre-trained VAE decoder: `~q_t+1 = ψ_θ(~z_t+1)`. The trajectory cost `S^(k)` for each sample `k` is then evaluated using these decoded joint configurations.
 - **Sampling Strategy:** To ensure temporal continuity and computational efficiency, the framework adopts a single-instance sampling strategy. A single noise vector `δu^(k)` is sampled once for each trajectory `k` and applied uniformly across the entire prediction horizon (`~u_t^(k) = u_t + δu^(k)`). This contrasts with standard MPPI, where `δu_t^(k)` is sampled at each time step, and is aimed at producing smoother decoded trajectories given the nonlinearity of `ψ_θ`.
 - **Optimization:** Importance weights `w^(k)` are computed for each sampled trajectory based on its cost `S^(k)`. The optimal latent control sequence `U*` is then updated as the importance-weighted average of the perturbed sequences. The optimal latent state `z*` is propagated, and finally, the optimal joint configuration `^q* = ψ_θ(z*)` is obtained by decoding `z*`. This `^q*` serves as the near-feasible reference for the execution stage.

#### 3.2. B. Execution Stage

The execution stage addresses the "manifold mismatch" – the residual approximation error from the VAE decoder that causes `^q*` to be only *near-feasible* rather than exactly on `M`.

1. **Single-Step Quadratic Programming (QP):**
 - **Objective:** To find the optimal next-state configuration `q*` that lies *exactly* on the constraint manifold `M`, while also preserving task relevance. This is achieved through a single-step Quadratic Program.
 - **QP Formulation:**
 ```
        min_(˙q, q) ||˙q - ˙q_ref||^2 + w_task ||J_task ˙q - ˙x_task*||^2
        s.t. q = q_c + ˙qΔt
             J_h ˙q = -αh(q_c)
             ˙q_min ≤ ˙q ≤ ˙q_max
        ```
 - `q_c`: Current joint configuration.
 - `˙q_ref = (^q* - q_c)/Δt`: Reference velocity derived from the MC-MPPI planning solution, driving `q` towards `^q*`.
 - `˙x_task* = -K_p,task e_task(q_c)`: Desired task velocity to regulate operational-space error `e_task` (e.g., end-effector pose), weighted by `w_task`. This term ensures task relevance is maintained.
 - `q = q_c + ˙qΔt`: Integrates velocity `˙q` to yield the next configuration `q`.
 - `J_h ˙q = -αh(q_c)`: This is the linearized equality constraint. `J_h` is the constraint Jacobian evaluated at `q_c`. The term `-αh(q_c)` actively drives the constraint residual `h(q_c)` to zero at a rate `α`, thus ensuring `q*` lands on `M`. Crucially, because `^q*` from the planning stage is already *near-feasible*, the linearization of `h(q)=0` around `q_c` is accurate enough for a *single-step* QP to achieve exact constraint satisfaction, avoiding iterative projections.
 - `˙q_min, ˙q_max`: Joint velocity limits derived from joint position limits.
 - **Output:** The solution `q*` from the QP is the truly feasible and task-aligned configuration, which is then passed to a low-level controller for execution on the robot. A fallback strategy is in place if the QP becomes infeasible, though this was not triggered in experiments.

This two-stage methodology allows MC-MPPI to combine the benefits of MPPI (derivative-free, robust exploration) with guaranteed hard constraint satisfaction through a computationally efficient, single-step correction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of MC-MPPI involved a 14-DoF closed-chain dual-arm system, both in simulation (MuJoCo) and on real hardware. The system was subject to 8 equality constraints: a 6-D closed-chain term for relative end-effector pose and a 2-D tray flatness term. The VAE was trained with a 6-D latent space (`m = n - l`). The MC-MPPI planner used 200 samples, a 30-step prediction horizon, and a 0.01s control time step. Success criteria included maintaining the bimanual grasp and achieving position/orientation errors below 0.01m/0.01rad. Constraint violation was quantified by `||h(q)||`.

#### 4.1. A. Validation of Hard Constraint Satisfaction

This experiment compared MC-MPPI against two baselines: Latent MPPI (MC-MPPI without the QP execution stage) and Vanilla MPPI (standard MPPI in joint space with soft penalties for `h(q)`). The task involved moving a tray between two poses.

- **MC-MPPI:** Successfully converged to the target in 7.92 seconds. It consistently maintained the equality constraint, with an average violation norm of 0.0066 ± 0.0007, keeping it below the 0.01 threshold. This preserved both the closed-chain and tray flatness constraints.
- **Latent MPPI:** While initially reducing tracking error smoothly, it failed at 3.41 seconds. The residual manifold mismatch from the VAE decoder accumulated, leading to an average violation of 0.0199 and a peak of 0.0226, causing the bimanual grasp to break. This indicates that VAE-based planning enables meaningful exploration near the manifold but is insufficient for *hard* constraint satisfaction without correction.
- **Vanilla MPPI:** Failed earliest at 2.97 seconds, showing an average constraint violation of 0.0314 and a peak of 0.0820. It barely reduced the tracking error. This highlights the difficulty of finding feasible candidates in the full configuration space and the inadequacy of soft penalties for hard constraints.

**Finding:** These results confirm the necessity of MC-MPPI's two-stage design. The VAE-based planning stage provides near-feasible trajectories, while the QP-based execution stage is crucial for eliminating residual manifold mismatch and enforcing precise hard constraint satisfaction in real time.

#### 4.2. B. Obstacle Avoidance under Static Environments

This experiment assessed MC-MPPI's ability to navigate static obstacles while maintaining constraints, and specifically investigated the impact of constant-innovation latent rollouts (single-instance sampling). The task involved moving the tray between poses while avoiding two spherical obstacles.

- **MC-MPPI with constant-innovation rollouts (using Eq. 14):** Rapidly identified a collision-free path, converging to the target in 7.60 seconds. It maintained an average constraint violation of 0.0069 ± 0.0003.
- **MC-MPPI with standard per-step sampling:** Exhibited prolonged stagnation near the obstacles, requiring 79.25 seconds to converge, with an average constraint violation of 0.0067 ± 0.0010.

**Finding:** Constant-innovation rollouts significantly improved exploration efficiency in the latent space. This sustained directional exploration is beneficial for manifold-based equality constraints, where feasible configurations near obstacles are sparse. Additionally, this strategy produced temporally smoother trajectories, as the nonlinear VAE decoder can amplify high-frequency fluctuations from per-step noise into jerky joint motions.

#### 4.3. C. Obstacle Avoidance under Dynamic Environments

This experiment validated MC-MPPI's reactiveness on the real 14-DoF closed-chain dual-arm hardware in environments with dynamic obstacles. The system transported a tray between randomized poses while a spherical obstacle traversed the workspace.

- **Success Rate:** 38 out of 40 trials were successfully completed, yielding a 95% success rate. The failures occurred when insufficient lateral clearance prevented evasion.
- **Constraint Maintenance:** MC-MPPI successfully avoided moving obstacles while maintaining an average constraint violation of 0.0067 ± 0.0010, demonstrating the preservation of hard constraints during aggressive evasive maneuvers.
- **Real-time Performance:** The MPPI planning stage operated with an average cycle time of 7.098 ± 0.729 ms (max 9.025 ms), sustaining 100 Hz replanning. The QP execution stage averaged 1.277 ± 0.020 ms (max 1.369 ms), enabling 500 Hz reference tracking.

**Finding:** The decoupled two-stage architecture allowed MC-MPPI to achieve stable real-time control (100 Hz planning) on complex hardware in dynamic environments, reliably maintaining hard equality constraints and exhibiting reactive obstacle avoidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Manifold-Constrained MPPI (MC-MPPI) framework presents a notable advancement in real-time control for robotic systems subject to hard, manifold-based equality constraints.

Its primary significance lies in addressing a critical limitation of traditional sampling-based optimal control methods, particularly MPPI: the inability to provide guaranteed satisfaction for hard constraints, especially those defining zero-measure manifolds. By decoupling the problem into latent-space planning and execution-level correction, MC-MPPI successfully integrates the robustness and computational efficiency of derivative-free, sampling-based optimization with the precision required for strict constraint adherence.

The framework's dual-stage approach offers several advantages:
- **Leveraging Latent Space for Efficiency:** The use of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to learn a low-dimensional representation of the constraint manifold allows MPPI to sample in an unconstrained latent space. This overcomes the inherent difficulty and inefficiency of sampling directly in a high-dimensional configuration space where feasible solutions for equality constraints are extremely sparse. This enables the efficient generation of *near-feasible* candidate trajectories.
- **Guaranteed Hard Constraint Satisfaction:** The execution-level Quadratic Programming (QP) controller provides a mechanism to precisely correct the minor approximation errors (manifold mismatch) from the VAE. The ability to perform this correction in a single, computationally efficient QP solve, due to the accurate linearization afforded by the *near-feasible* reference, is a key contribution that ensures real-time operation and hard constraint satisfaction.
- **Enhanced Exploration and Smoothness:** The adoption of constant-innovation latent rollouts in the planning stage has been demonstrated to improve exploration efficiency in the learned latent space, which is particularly beneficial for navigating complex environments with sparse feasible regions. This strategy also contributes to the generation of temporally smoother joint trajectories.
- **Practical Applicability:** The validation on a complex 14-DoF closed-chain dual-arm system, encompassing both simulation and real-world dynamic obstacle avoidance scenarios, demonstrates the framework's practical applicability and robust real-time performance (100 Hz planning, 500 Hz execution). This indicates its potential for deployment in industrial or service robotics applications where highly constrained and reactive manipulation is required.

The potential impact of MC-MPPI is multifaceted. It broadens the applicability of MPPI to a wider range of safety-critical robotic tasks that demand absolute constraint satisfaction, such as collaborative robotics, dexterous manipulation, and surgical robotics. By providing a reliable method for incorporating hard equality constraints without sacrificing the benefits of sampling-based control, it could foster the development of more robust and versatile autonomous systems.

The identified future research directions, such as the direct integration of inequality constraints into the latent sampling paradigm and the use of Conditional VAEs for enhanced generalization, suggest that the MC-MPPI framework has potential for further expansion and adaptation to an even broader spectrum of constrained robotic challenges.
