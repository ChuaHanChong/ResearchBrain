# DiT4DiT: Jointly Modeling Video Dynamics and Actions for Generalizable Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> DiT4DiT introduces an end-to-end Video-Action Model that couples a video Diffusion Transformer with an action Diffusion Transformer, leveraging intermediate denoising features from video generation to condition action prediction. The method achieved state-of-the-art success rates of 98.6% on LIBERO and 50.8% on RoboCasa-GR1, demonstrating over 10x higher sample efficiency and superior zero-shot generalization compared to VLM-based baselines.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, based on static image-text data, struggle to learn complex spatiotemporal dynamics and physical interactions from limited robotic data.
- Existing generative video models, while capable of synthesizing future frames, have not been fully integrated as end-to-end foundational backbones for direct robotic policy learning.
- Learning low-level physical interactions and temporal state transitions in robotics is data-intensive and computationally expensive, limiting scalability and sample efficiency.

## Method

- **DiT4DiT** presents a unified cascaded framework that couples a video ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== with an action ==Diffusion Transformer==.
- It employs a ==dual flow-matching== mechanism with an asymmetric ==tri-timestep scheme== for video generation, feature extraction, and action prediction.
- Intermediate hidden activations from the video DiT's ==denoising== process are repurposed as rich visual tokens to condition the action DiT, providing stable inputs without full video generation during control.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average success rates of **98.6%** on the LIBERO benchmark and **50.8%** on the RoboCasa-GR1 benchmark, surpassing VLM-based and parameter-matched baselines.
- Demonstrated over **10x** higher sample efficiency and up to **7x** faster convergence for policy learning in simulation compared to semantic-centric baselines.
- Exhibited robust zero-shot generalization to unseen objects, categories, and quantity variations in both simulation and real-world tasks on a Unitree G1 robot, outperforming other methods that collapsed in such scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generation, by inherently capturing motion priors and causal structures, serves as a more effective and sample-efficient proxy objective for robotic policy learning compared to semantic-centric baselines.
> - Extracting features from specific intermediate denoising steps of a video generative backbone provides stable and generalized action priors, balancing semantic content with pixel-level details.
> - Jointly optimizing video dynamics and action prediction through decoupled timesteps for different modalities allows for synergistic learning of visual understanding and inverse dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper operates within the evolving field of robot learning, specifically at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and generative video models.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and GR00T, have emerged as a significant paradigm for generalist robot learning. These models leverage large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are pre-trained on extensive image-text datasets, to transfer semantic priors to embodied control tasks. While VLAs demonstrate capabilities in understanding diverse instructions and semantic concepts, a notable limitation is their foundation in static image-text data. This architecture often necessitates substantial action-labeled data during downstream policy training to learn low-level physical interactions, temporal state transitions, and implicit physics. The burden of acquiring such data can hinder scalability and sample efficiency.

In parallel, generative video models (VGMs) have advanced significantly, capable of synthesizing temporally coherent and physically plausible future video frames. These models naturally encode rich spatiotemporal structures, motion priors, and implicit physical dynamics by learning from internet-scale video datasets. Recent research has begun to explore the integration of video models into robotics, often by using them to generate additional training data or by extracting latent representations for inverse dynamics models. However, these approaches are frequently multi-stage, indirect, and do not fully integrate video generation as a foundational backbone for end-to-end policy learning.

This paper addresses the gap by proposing a method that directly integrates video generative models into an end-to-end robotic control framework. It posits that video generation itself can serve as a robust pre-training objective, offering a superior foundation for learning low-level robotic control policies compared to static VLM-based approaches. By directly coupling video dynamics with action prediction, the work aims to enhance sample efficiency, convergence speed, and generalization capabilities in robot control, particularly for tasks requiring an understanding of continuous physical interactions over extended horizons.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding the development of DiT4DiT are multifaceted:

1. **Address Limitations of Existing VLA Models:** Current VLA models predominantly inherit backbones pre-trained on static image-text data. This design means that the complex spatiotemporal structure and physical dynamics crucial for robotic manipulation must be learned from comparatively limited action-labeled robotic data during downstream policy training. This process is often data-intensive and computationally expensive. The authors aim to overcome this "physical blindness" by leveraging models that intrinsically capture real-world dynamics.

2. **Integrate Generative Video Models as a Foundational Backbone:** While generative video models excel at synthesizing realistic and physically plausible future frames, their full potential as foundational models for robot control remains largely unexplored in an end-to-end manner. Previous attempts to use video models in robotics have often been multi-stage or indirectly applied, failing to fully exploit the rich spatiotemporal representations learned during video generation for direct policy learning. A core motivation is to determine how video generative models can be integrated as a principled, end-to-end backbone for robust action policies.

3. **Investigate Video Generation as an Effective Proxy Objective for Policy Learning:** The research hypothesizes that video generation, by forcing a model to learn rich motion priors and causal structures, can serve as a more effective and sample-efficient training objective for robotic policy learning compared to semantic-centric baselines (e.g., object grounding or VLM-centric latent feature modeling). The authors seek to empirically validate this hypothesis, demonstrating that learning to predict future visual dynamics can implicitly teach a robot the necessary physical understanding for control.

4. **Develop an End-to-End Joint Video-Action Model:** To achieve the integration of video dynamics and action prediction, the authors aim to design a unified framework that simultaneously optimizes both video generation and action inference. This objective involves creating an architecture where the policy is grounded directly in the generative visual dynamics, rather than relying on decoupled or sequential learning stages. The goal is to establish a coherent training paradigm that streamlines the learning process and improves the synergy between visual dynamics and control.

5. **Enhance Sample Efficiency, Convergence Speed, and Generalization:** A significant motivation is to improve the practical aspects of robot learning. By leveraging the implicit physics encoded in video generation, the authors aim to develop policies that require substantially less training data, converge faster, and exhibit superior zero-shot generalization to novel objects, categories, and environmental conditions, both in simulation and real-world deployments. This objective addresses the high data demand and limited adaptability often observed in traditional VLA systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiT4DiT is presented as an end-to-end Video-Action Model (VAM) that couples a video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with an action Diffusion Transformer in a unified cascaded framework. Its core novelty lies in using intermediate denoising features from the video generation process to condition action prediction, facilitated by a dual flow-matching objective with decoupled timesteps and noise scales.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Flow Matching

The framework utilizes Flow Matching (FM), a generative modeling technique designed to regress a time-dependent velocity field vθ(x, τ). This field transports samples along a probability path from a noise distribution to the data distribution. The interpolation path xτ is defined as a linear combination of clean data x0 and pure Gaussian noise z, with τ ranging from 0 (clean data) to 1 (pure noise). The target velocity field v∗(xτ, τ) is the time derivative (z - x0). Training minimizes the L2 distance between the predicted velocity field vθ and the target velocity. During inference, sampling involves solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) by integrating vθ from τ=1 to τ=0, typically using a first-order Euler discretization.

The problem is formulated as predicting future video dynamics (o_t+1 ~ p_v(· | o_t, l)) and subsequently predicting actions (a_t ~ p_a(· | o_t, H(o_τ_v_t+1))) by reversing these sampled video dynamics. H denotes the extraction of hidden states from the intermediate future frame o_τ_v_t+1 at flow step τ_v. The training objective is to model the joint probability distribution p_va(o_t+1, a_t | o_t, l).

#### 3.2. Dual-DiT Architecture

The DiT4DiT architecture consists of two main components:

- **Video DiT:** This module uses Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B as its initialization, which includes a causal video VAE and a video diffusion transformer. The VAE compresses high-dimensional pixel-space observations (current o_t and future o_t+1) into compact latent representations (z_0_t, z_0_t+1). The video DiT, conditioned on language instructions, is then trained to predict the velocity field for future frame generation. Crucially, instead of using the final denoised video output, DiT4DiT repurposes the video DiT as a feature extractor. It employs a forward hook mechanism to intercept intermediate hidden activations at a specific flow timestep τ_f, converting the generative process into rich visual tokens. This process is formalized as h_τ_f_t = H(v_video_θ(z_τ_f_t+1, τ_f | z_0_t, l)).

- **Action DiT:** Adapted from GR00T-N1, this dedicated action diffusion transformer decodes the visual representations into continuous robot control commands. It operates as a separate flow-matching model comprising transformer blocks with Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) for timestep injection and cross-attention layers to attend to the visual features h_τ_f_t extracted by the video backbone. The input sequence includes proprioceptive state embeddings, noisy action trajectories, and learnable "future tokens." The network predicts the velocity vector field of the action sequence, which is then integrated numerically during inference to synthesize the final trajectory.

#### 3.3. Joint Training of Video and Action

To enable simultaneous modeling of latent representations for both video and action, DiT4DiT introduces a **Dual Flow-Matching mechanism** with an **asymmetric tri-timestep scheme**:

1. **Video Timestep (τ_v):** For the video generation module, τ_v is sampled uniformly from U at each training step. This ensures the model learns the full denoising trajectory across all noise levels, essential for synthesizing future frames.

2. **Feature Extraction Timestep (τ_f):** For extracting intermediate representations h_τ_f_t, a *fixed, deterministic timestep* τ_f is used. This provides a stable and consistent input signal to the downstream action module. Fixing τ_f allows selecting a specific "operating point" of the backbone, where features are consistently informative (e.g., emphasizing global structure in early stages or fine-grained details in later stages).

3. **Action Timestep (τ_a):** For the action DiT, τ_a is drawn from a Beta distribution (τ_a = 1 - σ, where σ ~ Beta(α, β)). This biased sampling strategy allocates more training capacity to critical control phases, allowing the action decoder to independently learn optimal inverse dynamics while being conditioned on the stable visual features from τ_f.

The overall training objective is a joint flow-matching loss (L_total):
L_total = L_action + λ * L_video
- **L_action:** Optimizes the action DiT to predict the velocity for transforming noisy action a_τ_a_t to target action a_0_t, conditioned on the robot's proprioceptive state s and the hidden features h_τ_f_t from the video backbone.
- **L_video:** Optimizes the video DiT to predict the velocity for transporting the current observation z_0_t and language goal l toward the future latent state z_0_t+1.
The text encoder and visual VAE are frozen during fine-tuning, focusing updates on the DiT modules to adapt them to the target domain.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, DiT4DiT allows for both video generation and action prediction through a decoupled sampling procedure:

- **Video DiT Sampling:** To synthesize future visual dynamics, the current observation o_t is encoded to z_0_t. Starting from Gaussian noise, the video model iteratively updates the latent (N_v steps) by predicting the velocity field conditioned on z_0_t and the language goal l. The final latent is decoded back to pixels, yielding the predicted future frame o_t+1.

- **Action DiT Sampling:** For action prediction, a single forward pass through the video backbone is performed at the *fixed feature-extraction timestep τ_f*, using a new noise latent. This step extracts the stable hidden representation h_τ_f_t. The action trajectory is then initialized from noise and iteratively refined (N_a steps) by the Action DiT, which predicts the action velocity field conditioned on h_τ_f_t and the robot’s proprioceptive state s, ultimately yielding the predicted action a_t. This single-pass feature extraction for action avoids the computational bottleneck of full multi-step video generation during real-time control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DiT4DiT was conducted across various benchmarks, including simulation (LIBERO, RoboCasa-GR1) and real-world (Unitree G1 robot), focusing on performance against state-of-the-art VLM-based policies, advantages over parameter-matched baselines, and zero-shot generalization.

#### 4.1. Validation of Video Generation as a Scaling Proxy

The paper first validates its core hypothesis: video generation is an effective proxy task for robot control. Compared to object-level grounding and FLARE-style latent feature modeling, the video-dynamics objective:
- **Sample Efficiency:** Achieves significantly higher data efficiency (up to > 10×) on the RoboCasa-GR1 tabletop benchmark.
- **Convergence Speed:** Converges much faster (up to 7×) to high-performance policies.
- **Performance:** Yields consistently better downstream manipulation success rates, demonstrating a more robust scaling behavior with increased data volume.

#### 4.2. Comparison Against State-of-the-Art Policies

- **LIBERO Benchmark (Simulation):** DiT4DiT (trained from scratch) achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **98.6%**, surpassing previous VLA models pre-trained on large-scale action datasets.
 - It demonstrated high success rates in LIBERO-Object (99.6%) and LIBERO-Goal (98.6%).
 - Showed a strong advantage in the challenging LIBERO-Long suite (97.6%), indicating robust performance in extended-horizon tasks.
 - Outperformed the direct parameter-matched baseline, Qwen3DiT (96.6% average), across all categories.

- **RoboCasa-GR1 Tabletop Benchmark (Simulation):** DiT4DiT achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **50.8%**.
 - This result significantly surpassed established policies like GR00T-N1.5 and GR00T-N1.6 by 9.0 and 10.0 absolute percentage points, respectively.
 - Demonstrated a **14.6% absolute improvement** over the parameter-matched Qwen3DiT baseline (36.2%), validating the superiority of video priors.
 - Achieved the highest success rate in 16 out of 24 tasks, with notable gains in tasks requiring precise spatial coordination (e.g., CanToDrawerClose: 74.0% vs. 56.0%).

- **Real-world Unitree G1 Tasks:** DiT4DiT showed dominant and robust performance, outperforming both the pre-trained GR00T-N1.5 and the parameter-matched Qwen3DiT baseline.
 - Qwen3DiT experienced a near-total collapse in real-world settings, often failing with 0% success rates.
 - DiT4DiT maintained a consistent lead over GR00T-N1.5 (which used significantly more pre-training data), particularly in high-precision tasks like Arrange Flower (75% vs. 25%) and Stack Cup (60% vs. 25%).
 - Excelled in extended-horizon tasks (Drawer Interaction: 90%, Box Packing: 50%), attributed to the temporal consistency from the tri-timestep mechanism.

#### 4.3. Generalization Capability

DiT4DiT exhibited strong zero-shot generalization under severe distribution shifts in both simulation and real-world environments.
- **Simulated Object Substitution:** When trained on a single object category (bottles) and evaluated zero-shot on unseen objects (can, cup, milk, wine) in RoboCasa tasks, DiT4DiT achieved 54.5% success in ToDrawerClose tasks (22.5% higher than Qwen3DiT), 34.0% in ToCabinetClose, and 30.5% in ToMicrowaveClose, demonstrating superior adaptability.
- **Real-world Zero-shot Scenarios:** On the Unitree G1 robot, DiT4DiT showed dominant zero-shot transfer in category generalization (unseen cups/vases), object substitution (packing corn instead of eggplant), and quantity variation (stacking four cups instead of three).
 - Qwen3DiT completely collapsed in these real-world scenarios (e.g., 0% for Arrange Flower).
 - DiT4DiT achieved 70% success in Arrange Flower (Category), significantly outperforming Qwen3DiT (0%) and GR00T-N1.5 (10%).
 - Maintained 50% success in Stack Cup (Number), indicating robustness to distractors and scene clutter. This implies that generative video representations provide a physics-aware understanding invariant to surface-level visual changes.

#### 4.4. Ablations

- **Choice of Feature Extraction Layer:** Performance peaked when extracting hidden states from layer 18 of the Video DiT. Early layers lacked actionable semantics, while final layers became overly specialized for pixel-level reconstruction, leading to performance degradation. Averaging features across all layers was competitive but not optimal.
- **Denoise Steps for Hidden Features:** A single denoising step for feature extraction yielded the highest performance, with success rates monotonically degrading as the number of steps increased. This suggests that excessive denoising forces hidden states to over-commit to pixel details, losing generalized action priors.
- **Joint vs. Decoupled Training:** A t-SNE visualization of latent features showed that joint training (compared to decoupled training) induced smoother temporal flows within task clusters, reflected by a nearly twofold improvement in silhouette score. This confirms that joint optimization embeds a continuous, physics-aware temporal progression, aiding long-horizon execution.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis

- **Trainable Parameters:** DiT4DiT has 2.2B trainable parameters, making it comparable to Qwen3DiT (2.3B) and smaller than GR00T-N1.5 (2.7B), indicating that performance gains are not due to larger model size.
- **Deployment Frequency:** DiT4DiT operates at 6Hz on the physical robot (on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU). While slower than Qwen3DiT (9Hz) and GR00T-N1.5 (13Hz), this is expected for a video-generative backbone that extracts temporally grounded features. The authors note that caching pre-extracted LLM features can further improve effective deployment frequency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DiT4DiT signifies a notable advancement in generalizable robot control by establishing video generation as an effective and data-efficient scaling proxy for policy learning. The work addresses critical limitations of traditional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that primarily rely on static image-text priors, which often struggle with the complex spatiotemporal dynamics inherent in robotic manipulation.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Faster Convergence:** DiT4DiT demonstrates over 10 times higher sample efficiency and up to 7 times faster convergence compared to semantic-centric baselines. This represents a substantial practical improvement, as it reduces the immense data collection and training time typically required for advanced robotic policies. Lower data requirements can democratize access to advanced robot learning techniques, making them more feasible for researchers and practitioners with limited resources.

2. **Superior Generalization and Robustness:** The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa-GR1) and shows strong zero-shot generalization capabilities in real-world settings. Its ability to adapt to unseen objects, category variations, and changes in object quantity, without additional training, indicates a more robust and physics-aware understanding of the environment. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world scenarios where encountering novel situations is common.

3. **Principled Integration of Video Dynamics:** By extracting intermediate denoising features from the video generation process to condition action prediction, DiT4DiT provides a principled, end-to-end approach to integrating rich spatiotemporal priors into robot control. This contrasts with previous multi-stage or indirect methods, offering a more coherent framework where the generative visual world inherently scaffolds the execution of complex robotic actions. The decoupled yet jointly optimized dual flow-matching objective is a novel contribution that facilitates this synergy.

4. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** The findings strongly suggest that modeling video dynamics provides a more effective foundation model backbone for robotic manipulation than current static image-text based VLAs. This paradigm shift could lead to the development of more generalizable embodied agents that possess an intrinsic understanding of physical interactions and causal structures, making them better equipped for complex, long-horizon tasks.

5. **Practical Real-world Deployment:** Despite a trade-off in control frequency compared to some baselines, DiT4DiT operates at a rate suitable for real-time closed-loop physical execution on a humanoid robot (Unitree G1). Its strong performance in real-world tasks, even with a smaller pre-training data volume than some large-scale models, highlights its practical applicability and the efficiency of its learning mechanism.

In conclusion, DiT4DiT's success in leveraging video dynamics for robot control offers a compelling pathway toward building highly capable, data-efficient, and generalizable robotic systems. It validates the hypothesis that generative video models can provide fundamental representations for robust policy learning, paving the way for more sophisticated and adaptable embodied intelligence.
