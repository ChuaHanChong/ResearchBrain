# Point-It-Out: Benchmarking Embodied Reasoning for Vision Language Models in Multi-Stage Visual Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> The Point-It-Out (PIO) benchmark offers a structured evaluation for Vision-Language Models, assessing embodied reasoning through precise visual grounding with pixel-level outputs. It reveals current VLM limitations in fine-grained object part localization and complex task-driven visual trace prediction, highlighting the necessity of dedicated grounding data for precise spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) benchmarks for embodied reasoning primarily use indirect evaluation methods (e.g., multiple-choice, language plans), failing to assess fine-grained visual grounding and precise spatial planning.
- Current evaluation methods do not fully close the perception-action loop, as they often stop at language-based planning without requiring VLM outputs to be grounded back into the visual space for actionable commands.
- Benchmarks for pixel-level grounding in robotic scenarios are often limited in scope or confined to specific environments, lacking coverage for complex spatiotemporal and affordance reasoning across diverse real-world embodied tasks.

## Method

- A novel benchmark, **Point-It-Out** (PIO), is introduced to directly assess embodied reasoning through precise visual grounding, requiring models to output pixel-level bounding boxes, points, or 2D trajectories.
- PIO employs a three-stage hierarchical evaluation protocol: Referred Object Localization (S1), Task-Driven Grounding (S2), and Task-Driven Visual Trace Prediction (S3), progressively increasing in complexity.
- The benchmark includes over 600 human-annotated question-answer pairs curated from diverse embodied AI datasets (e.g., RT-1, Ego4D, BDD100K) covering robot manipulation, household, kitchen, and driving scenarios.

## Results

- Models fine-tuned with grounding supervision (e.g., RoboRefer, MoLMO-7B-D, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen-2.5-VL) consistently achieve higher performance in S1 and S2 tasks compared to more general VLMs like GPT-4o and Claude-3.7.
- All evaluated models show a clear performance drop when asked to localize 'object parts' in S1, and 'affordance' and 'contact prediction' remain the most challenging subcategories in S2, with top models scoring below 0.5 and 0.4 respectively.
- Generalist models such as Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-o3 outperform models strong in S1/S2 on visual trace prediction (S3), generating more reasonable trajectories and reaching target objects effectively, suggesting their stronger integrated planning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct grounding supervision during training is crucial for achieving high precision in spatial reasoning tasks, as models explicitly trained with such data consistently outperform general-purpose VLMs in S1 and S2.
> - VLMs universally struggle with specific embodied skills, particularly fine-grained object part localization, inferring object affordances, and predicting precise contact points, indicating limitations in advanced spatial and interaction understanding.
> - Strong performance in single-target grounding (S1 and S2) does not directly translate to success in multi-step visual trace prediction (S3), implying a need for integrated training that combines precise grounding with temporal and multi-step planning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in interpreting visual inputs and responding to language instructions, making them relevant for embodied artificial intelligence (AI) systems such as robotic manipulation, navigation, and autonomous driving. These models leverage the world knowledge and instruction-following abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), grounding them in visual perception.

However, the evaluation of VLMs for embodied reasoning (ER) has primarily relied on indirect assessment methods. Existing benchmarks for embodied reasoning typically employ multiple-choice questions (MCQs), require closed-set skill selection from predefined actions, or focus on generating high-level language-based plans. For example, some benchmarks ask models to select the best trajectory from a given list or provide textual plans for tasks. These approaches offer partial insights into a VLM's capabilities but often overlook a critical aspect: the precise grounding of model outputs back into the visual space. This limitation means that current evaluations may not fully assess a model's capacity for fine-grained visual grounding and precise spatial planning, which are essential for physical interaction in the real world. Without pixel-level grounding, the interpretability and actionability of VLM outputs in practical embodied tasks remain a challenge, hindering the completion of the full perception-action loop.

This research introduces the Point-It-Out (PIO) benchmark to address this gap by directly evaluating embodied reasoning through precise visual grounding. It extends beyond traditional referring expression comprehension (REC) datasets, which primarily focus on basic object-level localization in everyday scenes, by incorporating nuanced forms of grounding critical for task-related understanding, such as affordance grounding and visual trace prediction across diverse embodied scenarios. PIO aims to provide a more comprehensive and interpretable assessment of VLM capabilities for physical interaction by requiring models to generate pixel-level outputs, thereby bridging the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level physical actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a novel benchmark, Point-It-Out (PIO), designed to systematically and precisely assess the embodied reasoning (ER) abilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) through direct visual grounding. The authors aim to provide a more granular and actionable evaluation than currently available methods.

The motivation for developing PIO stems from several limitations identified in existing VLM benchmarks for embodied reasoning:

- **Inadequate Evaluation of Fine-Grained Grounding:** Current benchmarks often evaluate ER using indirect methods such as multiple-choice questions or by requiring language-based planning. While these formats can assess input understanding and high-level reasoning, they fail to examine a VLM's capability for fine-grained visual grounding and precise spatial planning, which are critical for effective interaction in the physical world. For instance, knowing *which* trajectory is correct in a multiple-choice setting does not prove a model can *generate* that precise trajectory.
- **Incomplete Perception-Action Loop:** Many existing benchmarks stop at language-based planning or skill selection, overlooking the crucial step of grounding these outputs back into the visual space. This omission means they do not fully assess whether a VLM can truly complete a perception-action loop—from understanding a task to identifying where and how to act in the visual environment. Precise visual grounding is presented as an essential component for closing this loop, making VLM outputs interpretable and actionable for real-world robots.
- **Limited Scope of Existing Grounding Benchmarks:** While some benchmarks explore localization in robotic scenarios (e.g., simple object localization or vacant space detection), they are often confined to specific environments or limited in the complexity of grounding tasks. There is a perceived lack of benchmarks that offer pixel-level grounding for a broad spectrum of embodied tasks requiring advanced spatial, temporal, and affordance reasoning across diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Need for Interpretable and Actionable Interfaces:** For VLMs to serve as robust backbones for embodied AI systems, their outputs must be not only correct but also precisely aligned with the visual environment to guide actions. The ability to generate pixel-space bounding boxes, points, or trajectories directly assesses this alignment, providing a clear measure of a VLM’s practical utility.
- **Decomposition of Embodied Reasoning Complexity:** The authors hypothesize that embodied reasoning is a hierarchical process. Therefore, a benchmark is needed that can progressively evaluate different levels of visual grounding complexity, from simple object identification to complex spatiotemporal action planning. This hierarchical approach aims to provide fine-grained insights into where specific VLM capabilities may be strong or weak.

By addressing these motivations, the PIO benchmark seeks to advance the development of VLMs capable of more precise, interpretable, and effective embodied reasoning in various real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Point-It-Out (PIO) benchmark is designed to systematically evaluate the embodied reasoning abilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) through precise visual grounding. The methodology encompasses a hierarchical evaluation protocol, diverse data curation, candidate model selection, and specific evaluation metrics.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Evaluation Protocol (Three Stages)

PIO proposes a three-stage hierarchical framework, progressively increasing in complexity:

- **S1: Referred Object Localization:**
 - **Definition:** This stage assesses the VLM's ability to identify and localize specific objects in a scene as explicitly referenced by a language instruction. It is akin to referring expression comprehension (REC).
 - **Subclasses:** Includes "object without ambiguity" (single object), "object with constraints" (e.g., spatial, color, material cues), and "object part" (localizing specific parts of an object).
 - **Importance:** Represents a fundamental capability for any language-guided embodied policy, as models must first localize an object before interacting with it. Outputs are typically bounding boxes or points.
- **S2: Task-Driven Grounding:**
 - **Definition:** This stage moves beyond explicit references, challenging the VLM to determine which object or part is relevant for a given task and pinpointing where to interact. The entity to be localized may not be explicitly mentioned in the instruction, requiring reasoning about object affordances and task context.
 - **Subclasses:** Encompasses "affordance" (recognizing action-relevant locations like handles or buttons), "contact" (identifying precise contact points), "recommendation" (suggesting objects based on a need, e.g., "I'm hungry"), and "safety" (identifying safe areas or potential hazards).
 - **Importance:** Highlights embodied visual grounding's distinction from standard computer vision, requiring an understanding of physical interaction. It is crucial for general-purpose manipulation policies. Outputs are typically points or bounding boxes.
- **S3: Task-Driven Visual Trace Prediction:**
 - **Definition:** This most complex stage assesses the VLM's ability to generate a coarse 2D visual trace (a sequence of 2D points) that outlines how a multi-step task should be completed. It integrates object understanding (S1) and affordance reasoning (S2) with temporal planning.
 - **Importance:** Demands a complete understanding of how to act, beyond fixed interaction spots, and provides a human-interpretable, high-level intermediate representation for robot guidance and policy learning. The input includes the task and the robot end-effector's initial 2D position.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Curation and Data Sources

- **Data Collection:** PIO includes over 600+ human-annotated question-answer (QA) pairs.
 - **S1 and S2:** 501 QA pairs (approximately 230 for S1, 270 for S2) were collected from five datasets: Where2Place, Ego4D–EPIC-Kitchens, BDD100K, AgiBot, and RT-1. Each dataset contributed around 50 images and corresponding QAs to ensure diversity across visual contexts and embodied challenges.
 - **S3:** 100 questions targeting visual trace prediction tasks for robotic arms were extracted from AgiBot, DROID, and RT-1 datasets. For S3, the starting 2D position of the robot arm is provided as guidance.
- **Scenarios Covered:** The benchmark encompasses four critical domains for embodied intelligence:
 - **Robot Manipulation:** From RT-1, DROID, and AgiBot.
 - **Household Environments:** From Where2Place.
 - **Kitchen Activities:** From EPIC-Kitchens.
 - **Driving Scenes:** From BDD-100K.
- **Annotation Process:** Human annotators manually generated polygon-based segmentation masks, natural language questions, and assigned appropriate stages/subclasses for each datapoint using standard tools (RoboFlow). GPT-4o was employed to rewrite prompts for clarity and formality, aiming to minimize ambiguity and potential biases in language descriptions.

#### 3.3. Candidate Models

Over ten state-of-the-art VLMs were evaluated, categorized by their output formats (bounding boxes or points) and training focus:

- **Bounding Box Output Models (Black-box & Closed-source):** GPT-4o/4o-mini, GPT-o3/o4-mini, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Flash/Pro, Qwen-2.5-VL-32B-Instruct. These models were prompted to output a single bounding box.
- **Point Output Models (Fine-tuned):** MoLMo-7B-D (general-purpose pointing), RoboPoint (fine-tuned on robotic data). These models were prompted to output one or more points.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

- **S1 and S2 (Bounding Box / Point Grounding):**
 - A normalized Intersection over Union (IoU) metric was proposed to ensure fair comparison between models outputting bounding boxes and those outputting points, especially for irregular ground-truth masks.
 - The normalized IoU is calculated as: `IoU(predicted_bounding_box/points, ground_truth_mask) / IoU(tightest_bounding_box_enclosing_mask, ground_truth_mask)`. This normalization accounts for the inherent difficulty of covering irregular shapes with rectangular boxes, improving fairness over biased point-in-mask or raw IoU evaluations.
- **S3 (Visual Trace Prediction):**
 - **Human Ratings:** Annotators scored generated trajectories on a 1-5 scale, assessing overall direction, keypoint coverage, and task feasibility.
 - **GPT-o4-mini Based Assessments:** An automated evaluation using GPT-o4-mini was employed, guided by predefined criteria similar to human ratings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of over ten state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on the PIO benchmark revealed several findings regarding their embodied reasoning capabilities, particularly in precise visual grounding.

**Finding 1: The Importance of Grounding Supervision for S1 and S2 Performance.**
Models explicitly trained or fine-tuned with grounding supervision, such as RoboRefer, MoLMO-7B-D, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Qwen-2.5-VL, consistently demonstrated higher performance in S1 (Referred Object Localization) and S2 (Task-Driven Grounding) compared to more general-purpose VLMs like GPT-4o and Claude-3.7. This indicates that direct exposure to and training on grounding data are crucial for achieving precision in spatial reasoning tasks. Among the general-purpose models, GPT-o3 achieved better results than its smaller variants (GPT-o4 mini, GPT-4o mini), suggesting that enhanced reasoning capabilities contribute to improved grounding even without explicit grounding fine-tuning, though still lagging behind specialized models. Gemini-2.5 Pro also significantly outperformed other Gemini variants and slightly surpassed RoboPoint. Despite strong language-based embodied reasoning in prior benchmarks, GPT-4o and Claude-3.7 showed limitations when precise visual grounding was required.

**Finding 2: Specific Embodied Skills Pose Significant Challenges Across All Models in S1 and S2.**
A clear performance degradation was observed across all models when transitioning from S1 to S2, indicating increased complexity in task-driven grounding.
- **S1 Challenges:** Models exhibited a noticeable drop in accuracy when localizing "object parts." Even top-performing models like Qwen and MoLMO scored below 0.5 in this subcategory, suggesting difficulties with fine-grained spatial understanding beyond whole objects. However, models showed less performance decline on tasks requiring simpler language reasoning, such as localizing "objects with restrictions" in S1.
- **S2 Challenges:** In S2, "affordance" and "contact prediction" remained the most challenging subcategories for most models. While "recommendation" tasks were comparatively easier, likely relying more on language-level reasoning, even the best-performing model in affordance (MoLMO) scored below 0.4. These results suggest that VLMs struggle with inferring interaction points and understanding how objects can be physically engaged to complete tasks.

These findings highlight that while some models can handle basic object localization and high-level reasoning, their abilities for fine-grained object part localization, affordance prediction, and contact point identification are still limited. These are identified as crucial skills for VLMs acting as grounded agents.

**Finding 3: S1 and S2 Capabilities are Necessary but Not Sufficient for S3 (Visual Trace Prediction).**
The evaluation of S3 revealed that strong performance in S1 and S2 does not directly translate to success in visual trace generation, which requires integrating single-target grounding into coherent, multi-step planning.
- Models like MoLMO and Qwen, despite their high performance in S1 and S2, underperformed in S3. This suggests that their strength lies in isolated grounding tasks but they struggle with the temporal coherence and multi-step reasoning required for generating complete action trajectories.
- In contrast, generalist models such as Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-o3 (which slightly outperformed GPT-4o) generated more reasonable trajectories. These models were better able to follow correct overall directions and reach target objects effectively. The authors attribute Gemini-2.5-Pro's strong S3 performance to the inclusion of embodied and grounding data in its pre-training. This suggests that strongly pre-trained VLMs can excel at complex tasks involving both grounding and planning, even without explicit fine-tuning on 2D trajectory data.

This finding provides a guideline for model users, suggesting that models strong in S1/S2 are suitable for tasks not requiring action generation (e.g., pick-and-place based on points). For training robot policies, however, models that perform well on S3, even if weaker on S1/S2, might be better candidates due to their integrated grounding and planning capabilities. The results collectively underscore the need for targeted data and training strategies to improve VLMs' grounding-aware capabilities across all stages of embodied reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the Point-It-Out (PIO) benchmark holds several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of embodied artificial intelligence and Vision-Language Model (VLM) development:

- **Addresses a Critical Evaluation Gap:** PIO directly confronts the limitations of existing embodied reasoning benchmarks that largely rely on indirect evaluation methods (e.g., multiple-choice questions or high-level language plans). By requiring precise pixel-level visual grounding (points, bounding boxes, trajectories), PIO provides a more rigorous and realistic assessment of a VLM's ability to operate in the physical world, completing the perception-action loop. This fills a crucial gap for developing truly actionable and interpretable VLM interfaces for robots.
- **Offers Fine-Grained Insights into VLM Capabilities:** The hierarchical structure of PIO (S1: Referred Object Localization, S2: Task-Driven Grounding, S3: Visual Trace Prediction) allows for a granular analysis of VLM strengths and weaknesses. This decomposition provides specific diagnostic information, moving beyond aggregated scores to pinpoint where models excel (e.g., basic object localization) and where they struggle (e.g., object parts, affordance prediction, temporal planning). Such fine-grained insights are invaluable for targeted model improvement.
- **Guides Future VLM Development:** The findings from PIO highlight specific areas where current VLMs are deficient, such as fine-grained object part localization, reasoning about object affordances, predicting contact points, and generating coherent multi-step visual traces. This empirical evidence can guide researchers in developing new architectures, training methodologies, and data collection strategies to enhance these critical embodied reasoning skills. For instance, the observation that models fine-tuned for grounding excel in S1/S2 but struggle in S3 suggests a need for integrated training that combines precise grounding with temporal planning.
- **Establishes a Challenging Benchmark for Progress:** PIO provides a new, challenging benchmark that can drive progress in VLM research. The performance gaps between current state-of-the-art models and human-level performance, particularly in S3, indicate significant room for improvement, encouraging the development of more sophisticated models capable of complex embodied tasks.
- **Informs Model Selection and Deployment:** The benchmark offers practical guidelines for users and developers. For tasks requiring precise single-target interaction, models strong in S1 and S2 are preferable. For applications involving multi-step planning and action generation, models demonstrating robust S3 performance (even if slightly weaker in S1/S2) are more suitable. This differentiation helps in selecting appropriate VLM backbones for specific robotic policies and embodied AI systems.
- **Promotes Data-Centric AI for Embodied Systems:** The results underscore the "importance of grounding data when precise spatial reasoning is required." This finding reinforces the need for collecting and leveraging diverse, high-quality, pixel-level annotated datasets for training embodied VLMs, specifically targeting aspects like object parts, affordances, and full motion trajectories.
- **Facilitates Interpretability of Embodied Policies:** By requiring models to output visual groundings and traces, PIO makes VLM-driven embodied policies more interpretable. Users can directly see *where* a model intends to act and *how* it plans to move, which is critical for debugging, trust, and human-robot collaboration.

In summary, PIO represents a step towards a more comprehensive and actionable evaluation of VLMs for embodied AI, pushing the field towards models that can not only understand but also precisely act within complex physical environments.
