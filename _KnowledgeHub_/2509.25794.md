# Point-It-Out: Benchmarking Embodied Reasoning for Vision Language Models in Multi-Stage Visual Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> The Point-It-Out (PIO) benchmark offers a structured evaluation for Vision-Language Models, assessing embodied reasoning through precise visual grounding with pixel-level outputs. It reveals current VLM limitations in fine-grained object part localization and complex task-driven visual trace prediction, highlighting the necessity of dedicated grounding data for precise spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) benchmarks for embodied reasoning primarily use indirect evaluation methods (e.g., multiple-choice, language plans), failing to assess fine-grained visual grounding and precise spatial planning.
- Current evaluation methods do not fully close the perception-action loop, as they often stop at language-based planning without requiring VLM outputs to be grounded back into the visual space for actionable commands.
- Benchmarks for pixel-level grounding in robotic scenarios are often limited in scope or confined to specific environments, lacking coverage for complex spatiotemporal and affordance reasoning across diverse real-world embodied tasks.

## Method

- A novel benchmark, **Point-It-Out** (PIO), is introduced to directly assess embodied reasoning through precise visual grounding, requiring models to output pixel-level bounding boxes, points, or 2D trajectories.
- PIO employs a three-stage hierarchical evaluation protocol: Referred Object Localization (S1), Task-Driven Grounding (S2), and Task-Driven Visual Trace Prediction (S3), progressively increasing in complexity.
- The benchmark includes over 600 human-annotated question-answer pairs curated from diverse embodied AI datasets (e.g., RT-1, Ego4D, BDD100K) covering robot manipulation, household, kitchen, and driving scenarios.

## Results

- Models fine-tuned with grounding supervision (e.g., RoboRefer, MoLMO-7B-D, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen-2.5-VL) consistently achieve higher performance in S1 and S2 tasks compared to more general VLMs like GPT-4o and Claude-3.7.
- All evaluated models show a clear performance drop when asked to localize 'object parts' in S1, and 'affordance' and 'contact prediction' remain the most challenging subcategories in S2, with top models scoring below 0.5 and 0.4 respectively.
- Generalist models such as Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-o3 outperform models strong in S1/S2 on visual trace prediction (S3), generating more reasonable trajectories and reaching target objects effectively, suggesting their stronger integrated planning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct grounding supervision during training is crucial for achieving high precision in spatial reasoning tasks, as models explicitly trained with such data consistently outperform general-purpose VLMs in S1 and S2.
> - VLMs universally struggle with specific embodied skills, particularly fine-grained object part localization, inferring object affordances, and predicting precise contact points, indicating limitations in advanced spatial and interaction understanding.
> - Strong performance in single-target grounding (S1 and S2) does not directly translate to success in multi-step visual trace prediction (S3), implying a need for integrated training that combines precise grounding with temporal and multi-step planning capabilities.
