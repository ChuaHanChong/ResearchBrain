# RECALL: Recovery Experience Collection for Active Lifelong Learning in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RECALL is an active continual-learning pipeline for autoregressive vision-language-action models. It rolls out a pi0-FAST policy on LIBERO-10, uses the INSIGHT token-level uncertainty predictor to flag high-uncertainty states, resets to them and records expert recovery demonstrations, then folds that data back via replay mixing or EWC. Uncertainty-guided recovery data lifts overall success from 59.8% to 72.4%, but recovery data alone causes catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- Passive imitation fine-tuning only triggers after the policy already fails, making adaptation reactive, time-consuming and unsafe on real robots.
- Start-state recollection gives no guidance on where supervision helps, so demonstrator effort is wasted on substeps the policy already handles well.
- Uncertainty-guided aggregation was studied on small task-specific policies, not large pretrained VLAs, and prior VLA uncertainty work stops at asking for help.

## Method

- Active collection framework applies INSIGHT's ==token-level uncertainty head== at every step of a pi0-FAST rollout; predicted help states are ==reset in simulator== and relabelled with ==expert recovery trajectories== to task completion.
- Two collection regimes: ==online== takes one demonstration from the first uncertain state per rollout, ==offline== takes every uncertain state. Matched ==passive start-state datasets== control for task distribution and data volume.
- Continual-learning integration compares ==replay mixtures== (full, LIBERO-10, targeted) against ==new-only fine-tuning==, ==elastic weight consolidation== with a ==diagonal Fisher penalty==, and a low constant learning rate of 2.5e-8.

## Results

- Strong INSIGHT online recovery reaches **72.4%** overall success versus **60.2%** for matched passive collection and **59.8%** baseline (**p=0.0001**); collected-task success rises from **32.0%** to **49.2%**.
- Online collection (**72.4%**) matches dense offline collection (**68.4%**, **p=0.166**) despite far fewer demonstrations, while new-only fine-tuning collapses to **28.4%** overall and **0.4%** collected-task success.
- Low learning rate (**62.8%**) and EWC at lambda=**1e12** (**61.4%**) hold retained tasks near **91%** but underfit recovery data; targeted replay drops retention from **90.0%** to **83.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Uncertainty-flagged states are informative but distributionally narrow: state selection alone is not active learning, integration must also preserve prior competence.
> - Correcting from the first uncertain state supervises the remainder of the trajectory too, so dense per-state collection buys little extra performance.
> - Replay coverage, not replay volume, governs retention; parameter regularization cannot compensate for prior-task behaviors that the replay buffer omits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in the fine-tuning of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models within the field of robotic manipulation. Recent advancements in VLA models, such as RT-1, RT-2, Gemini Robotics, π0-FAST, and π0.5, have demonstrated broad manipulation capabilities. However, these models typically require further fine-tuning when deployed to new robots, environments, or task distributions.

Current practice for fine-tuning often relies on passive imitation learning. This approach involves an expert repeatedly collecting demonstrations, fine-tuning the model, and evaluating performance, triggering further data collection only after policy failures. This method presents several inefficiencies: it necessitates policy failure before data collection, provides limited guidance on the quantity or most useful locations for new data, and can lead to redundant effort by demonstrators on already well-learned task segments.

This research aims to transition from passive to an active, continual learning paradigm for VLAs. Active learning in robotics, which involves identifying states or trajectories where additional demonstrations are most informative, has been explored in interactive imitation learning methods like DAgger and HG-DAgger. Uncertainty-guided active learning further refines this by querying experts when the policy is uncertain. However, much of this prior work has focused on smaller, task-specific policies, often in domains like driving or classical imitation learning, rather than large, pre-trained VLAs. The paper builds on recent advancements in uncertainty quantification for autoregressive VLAs, specifically leveraging INSIGHT, which uses token-level probability features to predict when a robot requires assistance.

Furthermore, the paper integrates concepts from continual learning, a field concerned with enabling models to acquire new knowledge without experiencing catastrophic forgetting – a phenomenon where training on new data degrades performance on previously learned tasks. Catastrophic forgetting has been observed in large language models, suggesting a similar vulnerability in large pre-trained sequence models like VLAs. Common continual learning solutions, such as replay-based methods (mixing new data with prior examples) and regularization-based methods (constraining parameter changes important for prior behavior, e.g., Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)), are explored within this context. The present work empirically studies these techniques specifically for autoregressive VLAs when incorporating uncertainty-guided recovery data.

This work contributes to bridging the gap between large-scale VLA capabilities and their efficient, adaptive deployment by proposing and evaluating methods for active, continual learning, addressing the limitations of passive fine-tuning and the challenge of retaining prior knowledge while adapting to new data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inefficiencies and drawbacks of passive imitation learning for fine-tuning large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors identify several problems:
- **Reactive Data Collection:** Passive learning only reveals the need for more data after a policy has failed, which can be time-consuming and potentially unsafe in real-world robotic deployments.
- **Lack of Guidance:** The approach offers little insight into the optimal amount of additional data required, potentially leading to inefficient under- or over-collection.
- **Suboptimal Data Utility:** Passive recollection does not target specific areas of policy weakness. Demonstrators may spend effort on task parts the policy already masters, while critical failures might originate from a small number of difficult sub-steps or recovery from distribution-shifted intermediate states.

Based on these motivations, the central hypothesis guiding this work is that collecting demonstrations from states where the policy exhibits high uncertainty will yield more informative and thus more efficient data collection compared to conventional passive methods.

The key objectives of this research are structured around four research questions (RQs) and one follow-up experiment:
- **RQ-1: Evaluate the efficiency of active versus passive data collection.** The objective is to determine whether demonstrations collected from high-uncertainty states lead to superior policy performance compared to unguided, passively collected demonstrations, when controlling for data volume and task distribution.
- **RQ-2: Compare online and offline active data collection strategies.** This objective seeks to ascertain if online data collection—where a demonstration is collected in real-time from the first encountered uncertain state—is as effective as, or more efficient than, dense offline collection from all uncertain states identified in a rollout.
- **RQ-3: Investigate the viability of fine-tuning VLAs solely on new recovery data.** The goal is to determine whether such an approach leads to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned behaviors and to underscore the necessity of continual learning mechanisms.
- **RQ-4: Assess the efficacy of standard continual learning techniques.** The objective is to evaluate whether replay-based data mixing and regularization-based methods, specifically Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), can mitigate catastrophic forgetting while simultaneously enabling effective adaptation to the new, uncertainty-guided recovery data.
- **Follow-up Experiment (Replay Scope):** To understand the trade-offs of replay strategies, examining how much prior data is necessary for replay to be effective (e.g., full dataset replay vs. task-specific replay).

Through these objectives, the research aims to establish an active, continual learning paradigm that enables VLAs to adapt efficiently to new tasks or environments while retaining their broad capabilities. The core contribution is an empirical study demonstrating the benefits of uncertainty-guided recovery demonstrations and identifying effective strategies for their integration into large robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes an active, continual learning pipeline for fine-tuning autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically using π0-FAST. This pipeline comprises two main components: an active learning mechanism for identifying uncertain states for data collection, and a continual learning component for integrating new data without incurring catastrophic forgetting.

### 4. Active Learning Pipeline

1. **Initial Policy Training:** The process begins with a π0-FAST policy pre-trained on the LIBERO dataset, following the original authors' recipe for 30,000 steps. This initial policy (πθ0) serves as the baseline and the rollout policy for active data collection. π0-FAST tokenizes continuous robot actions and uses a next-token prediction objective, taking a language instruction, visual observation, and robot state as input.

2. **Uncertainty Quantification (UQ):** During policy rollouts (τi), an inference-time introspection method called INSIGHT is applied at each step to identify states where the policy is likely to require assistance. INSIGHT leverages token-level probability features (entropy, log-probability, Dirichlet-based uncertainty estimates) to predict help signals. Two variants of INSIGHT are evaluated:
 - **Strong INSIGHT:** Trained with step-wise labels indicating when real robot executions required human help.
 - **Weak INSIGHT:** Trained from episode-level success/failure labels on the LIBERO dataset. The study primarily uses Strong INSIGHT due to its demonstrated generalization.

3. **Active Recovery Data Collection Strategies:** Based on INSIGHT's help predictions, two methods for collecting new recovery demonstrations (Dnew) are explored:
 - **Online Dataset (Donline):** Simulates real-time intervention by collecting one recovery demonstration from the *first* high-uncertainty state encountered in each rollout (t_online = min{t : ht = 1}).
 - **Offline Dataset (Doffline):** Simulates post-hoc data collection from completed rollouts by collecting demonstrations from *every* high-uncertainty state identified (T_offline = Ti).
 - **Passive Start-State Baselines (Dpassive):** For comparison, passive datasets are collected by initiating demonstrations from task initial states, matching the task distribution and number of demonstrations of the active datasets. This isolates the benefit of uncertainty-guided state selection.

4. **Demonstration Recording:** For each selected state (either active or passive), the simulator is reset to that state, and an expert recovery trajectory to task completion is recorded.

5. **Fine-tuning Objective:** The collected data is used to fine-tune the VLA model using the same autoregressive cross-entropy objective (LCE) as the initial policy training.

### 5. Continual Learning Strategies

To address catastrophic forgetting when integrating new recovery data, several continual learning strategies are evaluated:

1. **Replay-based Data Mixtures:** These methods involve mixing the newly collected recovery dataset (Dnew) with existing data:
 - **New-only fine-tuning (Dnew):** Fine-tuning exclusively on the new recovery data.
 - **Full replay (Dold ∪ Dnew):** Mixing Dnew with the entire original training dataset (Dold).
 - **LIBERO-10 replay (D_LIBERO10 ∪ Dnew):** Mixing Dnew with data from all LIBERO-10 tasks.
 - **Targeted replay (D_collected_tasks ∪ Dnew):** Mixing Dnew only with data relevant to the specific tasks for which recovery data was collected.

2. **Regularization-based Method (Elastic Weight Consolidation - EWC):** EWC is applied as a method to penalize changes to parameters deemed important for previously learned behaviors. It adds a regularization term to the cross-entropy loss: L_EWC(θ) = L_CE(θ) + λ Σj F_j (θ_j - θ_0,j)^2. The diagonal Fisher information matrix (F) is computed on a reference dataset, and the coefficient (λ) controls the stability-plasticity trade-off.

3. **Learning-Rate Ablations:** To investigate if simpler mechanisms reduce forgetting, fine-tuning is conducted with:
 - **Standard Learning Rate:** The original VLA training schedule (linear warmup to 2.5 × 10^-5, cosine decay to 2.5 × 10^-6).
 - **Low, Constant Learning Rate:** A significantly smaller constant rate (2.5 × 10^-8).

### 6. Experiment Overview and General Setup

- **Platform:** All experiments are conducted within the LIBERO-10 simulation environment.
- **Evaluation:** Policies are evaluated using 50 rollouts per task across the 10 LIBERO tasks, totaling 500 rollouts per checkpoint.
- **Metrics:** Performance is assessed using three metrics:
 - **Overall success:** Average success rate across all 10 LIBERO tasks.
 - **Collected-task success:** Success rate on the 5 specific tasks (2, 3, 5, 8, 9) for which recovery data was collected.
 - **Retained-task success:** Success rate on the remaining 5 tasks (0, 1, 4, 6, 7) not targeted for recovery data.
- **Statistical Analysis:** Two-sided two-proportion z-tests are used for statistical comparisons of rollout success counts, with significance levels indicated.
- **Normalization Statistics:** For replay-based experiments, new normalization statistics are typically recomputed on the aggregated dataset. For new-only experiments, old normalization statistics from the original dataset are intentionally reused to act as a regularizer, providing a more stringent test for catastrophic forgetting.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded five principal findings concerning active and continual learning for autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

**Finding 1: High-uncertainty recovery demonstrations are more informative than passively collected start-state demonstrations.**
- **Result:** Online recovery data collection, guided by either Strong INSIGHT or Weak INSIGHT, led to statistically significant improvements in policy performance compared to passive start-state collection, even when data volume and task distribution were matched. For instance, Strong INSIGHT online recovery achieved 72.4% overall success, an improvement over both passive collection (60.2%) and the original baseline (59.8%). This improvement was statistically significant (p = 0.0001).
- **Discussion:** This indicates that actively identifying and collecting data from states where the policy struggles provides more valuable supervision than simply adding more task-relevant data from initial states. Ablations using old normalization statistics showed more muted improvements, suggesting that normalization can act as an implicit regularizer, affecting the model's plasticity.

**Finding 2: Online recovery collection is as effective and more demonstration-efficient than offline collection.**
- **Result:** Online data collection, which gathers one demonstration from the first high-uncertainty state, achieved comparable overall success (72.4%) to offline collection (68.4%), which collected from every high-uncertainty state. The difference in overall success was not statistically significant (p = 0.1659). Both methods showed nearly identical performance on collected tasks (49.2% for online, 48.4% for offline).
- **Discussion:** This suggests that intervening at the first point of predicted uncertainty is sufficient to provide effective corrective supervision, making online collection more efficient as it requires fewer recovery demonstrations. The initial uncertain state likely marks the divergence from successful behavior, and a correction there can influence the remainder of the task.

**Finding 3: Fine-tuning solely on newly collected recovery data leads to catastrophic forgetting.**
- **Result:** When VLAs were fine-tuned exclusively on new recovery data ("new-only" fine-tuning), performance on retained tasks (tasks not targeted for recovery) severely collapsed, and overall success fell significantly below the original baseline. For example, a Strong INSIGHT online new-only checkpoint achieved only 28.4% overall success, with a mere 0.4% on collected tasks and 56.4% on retained tasks. In contrast, replay-based training successfully preserved retained-task performance while improving collected tasks.
- **Discussion:** This highlights that while recovery data is informative for specific failure modes, its distribution is narrow. Relying solely on it does not provide sufficient coverage to maintain previously acquired behaviors, underscoring the necessity of continual learning strategies.

**Finding 4: Low learning rates and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) reduce forgetting but limit adaptation to new data, revealing a stability-plasticity tradeoff.**
- **Result:** Both low-learning-rate new-only training (62.8% overall success, 91.2% retained-task success) and EWC regularization (e.g., λ=10^12 achieving 61.4% overall, 90.8% retained-task success) were more effective at preserving prior behaviors than standard new-only fine-tuning. However, their gains on collected tasks were smaller (e.g., 34.4% for low LR, 32.0% for EWC) compared to full replay (72.4% overall, 49.2% collected-task success).
- **Discussion:** These regularization and reduced-update methods address part of the forgetting problem but inherently involve a stability-plasticity tradeoff. Stronger constraints preserve old behavior but suppress effective learning from recovery data, indicating that they do not fully match the balance provided by comprehensive replay.

**Finding 5: Replay-based data mixing is the most reliable strategy, but replay coverage is critical.**
- **Result:** Full replay and LIBERO-10 replay (mixing new data with only LIBERO-10 dataset) maintained strong retained-task performance while improving collected tasks. LIBERO-10 replay achieved 68.6% overall success, close to full replay's 72.4%, with no significant difference (p = 0.1877). Conversely, targeted replay (mixing new data with only data from collected tasks) resulted in a significant reduction in retained-task performance (83.6% vs. 90.0% for LIBERO-10 replay, p = 0.0345). EWC combined with targeted replay did not consistently prevent performance degradation.
- **Discussion:** This indicates that replay is highly effective, but its utility depends on sufficient coverage of the behaviors intended for retention. Restricting replay too narrowly biases adaptation toward new tasks at the expense of previously learned skills, and parameter regularization alone cannot reliably compensate for this lack of diverse prior data.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides an empirical foundation for an active, continual learning paradigm within Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, marking a step toward more adaptive and efficient robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved Fine-tuning Efficiency:** The central finding that uncertainty-guided recovery demonstrations are more informative than passively collected data indicates a pathway to more efficient VLA fine-tuning. By focusing data collection on states where the policy struggles, this approach can reduce the overall data required and the expert effort, accelerating the adaptation process for new tasks or environments. This has direct implications for reducing the cost and time associated with deploying large, pre-trained robot policies.

- **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting in VLAs:** The explicit demonstration of catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning VLAs solely on new data underscores a critical challenge for lifelong robot learning. This work highlights that active data collection must be integrated with robust continual learning strategies. The finding that replay-based methods, particularly with sufficient coverage of prior data (e.g., LIBERO-10 replay or full replay), effectively mitigate forgetting while allowing adaptation offers a practical and effective solution for practitioners.

- **Guidance for Future VLA Development:** The empirical study provides design lessons for the development of adaptive VLA models. It establishes the necessity of both intelligent data collection mechanisms (like INSIGHT-based uncertainty estimation) and effective data integration strategies (like replay). This informs future research directions in VLA architectures, uncertainty quantification, and continual learning algorithms tailored for robotic tasks.

- **Shift Towards Proactive Robot Learning:** The proposed paradigm encourages a shift from reactive learning (collecting data after failures) to more proactive approaches where robots identify their own needs for assistance. This aligns with the long-term goal of robots that can autonomously learn and improve over extended periods in dynamic, open-world environments.

- **Identification of Open Challenges:** By detailing limitations such as reliance on simulation, the quality of the help predictor, the use of a specific VLA family and benchmark, and the simplicity of evaluated continual learning methods, the paper clearly outlines avenues for future research. This includes exploring more sophisticated continual learning algorithms (e.g., LoRA, adapters), developing better-calibrated uncertainty estimates, and evaluating the approach on diverse VLA architectures and real-world robotic platforms. The call for broader evaluation metrics beyond task success (e.g., intervention cost, recovery length, safety) further guides comprehensive assessment in this field.

In summary, this research contributes to the fundamental understanding of how large robot policies can actively and continually learn from targeted experience, offering both practical strategies for improving current VLA deployment and a roadmap for overcoming persistent challenges in lifelong robot learning.
