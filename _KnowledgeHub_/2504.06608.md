# A Cross-Domain Few-Shot Learning Method Based on Domain Knowledge Mapping

> [!summary] Summary
> A cross-domain few-shot learning method based on domain knowledge mapping is introduced, which dynamically adjusts knowledge transfer according to domain similarity. This approach outperforms established meta-learning and cross-domain few-shot learning methods on six diverse datasets, demonstrating strong performance, especially on tasks with high domain transfer difficulty like CUB and Plantae, and showing effectiveness in industrial defect detection.

## Problem

- Traditional few-shot learning methods assume training and testing tasks are from the same domain (i.i.d.), which is unrealistic for cross-domain applications.
- Pre-trained features from a source domain often do not align well with target domain features, hindering generalization.
- Models can overfit to source domain characteristics during meta-knowledge transfer, limiting adaptability to new domains.

## Method

- A three-phase framework (pre-training, training, testing) is employed for structured adaptation.
- A ==maximum mutual information-based hybrid-supervised learning== approach is used in pre-training to improve feature extraction and prevent mode collapse.
- A ==domain knowledge mapping layer== and a ==domain classifier== are introduced to dynamically adjust knowledge transfer based on domain similarity.

## Results

- The method consistently surpasses traditional meta-learning and existing cross-domain few-shot learning methods across various few-shot settings (**5-way/2-way 1-shot** and **5-shot**).
- It shows stronger performance on datasets with high domain transfer difficulty, such as CUB and Plantae.
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each component: hybrid-supervised pre-training enhances fine-grained feature extraction, domain knowledge mapping separates task-relevant features, and dynamic weighting optimizes knowledge transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maximizing mutual information during pre-training helps generate robust features, preventing category mode collapse and improving fine-grained feature extraction for cross-domain generalization.
> - Dynamically adjusting knowledge transfer based on domain similarity allows the model to efficiently leverage source domain information while adapting to new target domains.
> - Generating a pseudo-unseen domain and training a domain classifier enables the model to estimate domain similarity and guide the knowledge mapping process effectively.
