# Breaking Barriers: Do Reinforcement Post Training Gains Transfer To Unseen Domains?

> [!summary] Summary
> The study investigates the transferability of reasoning ability enhancements from Reinforcement Post Training (RPT) in Large Language Models (LLMs) to domains not encountered during post-training. Findings indicate that RPT benefits are primarily confined to the trained domains, showing inconsistent or negative transfer to out-of-domain tasks, though some structured reasoning skills exhibited cross-domain transfer.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Post Training (RPT) evaluations for LLMs primarily assess performance within the post-training domains.
- There is a lack of empirical understanding regarding the generalizability of RPT-induced reasoning improvements to genuinely novel, unseen domains.
- Many reasoning tasks are either underrepresented or completely absent from current RPT training corpora, raising questions about RPT's real-world applicability.

## Method

- A two-stage investigation pipeline was designed, combining an ==observational study== of 18 existing open-source RPT models and an interventional study with controlled RPT fine-tuning on single, disjoint domains.
- RPT was applied to LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) using a consistent algorithm (GRPO) and hyperparameters across math, code, and knowledge-intensive reasoning domains.
- Models were evaluated across 16 diverse benchmarks, categorized into math, code, and knowledge-intensive reasoning, using aggregated accuracy improvement, common odds ratio, and statistical significance testing.

## Results

- RPT models achieved substantially higher average accuracy improvements on in-domain tasks (e.g., **2.87%** increase) compared to out-of-domain tasks (e.g., -**3.19%** decrease), with some showing statistically significant performance drops on OOD tasks.
- Models RPT-trained on math and code data demonstrated mutual generalizability, for instance, math-trained models showed **2.18%** gain in math and **4.77%** in code, while code-trained models achieved **9.49%** in code and **15.44%** in math (observational study).
- RPT generalizability remained weak across variations in algorithms, base models, and increased training steps, with larger models exhibiting increased in-domain overfitting (**16.5%** more ID gain than OOD gain with size increase).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RPT-induced reasoning gains are largely domain-specific, demonstrating negligible or even detrimental transfer to arbitrary out-of-domain tasks.
> - Generalization occurs between domains with similar underlying reasoning structures, such as from math to code and vice versa.
> - Unstructured-to-structured generalization shows promise, suggesting that comprehensive knowledge-intensive RPT might implicitly teach foundational reasoning components transferable to structured problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed substantial advancements in diverse reasoning domains, encompassing mathematics, quantitative reasoning, code generation, program synthesis, and knowledge-intensive tasks across legal, financial, and biomedical fields. Recent models have demonstrated near-expert performance on established benchmarks such as GSM8K, MATH, AIME, SWE-bench Verified, and GPQA Diamond.

A notable development in enhancing LLM capabilities is Reinforcement Post Training (RPT), particularly Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RPT-enhanced models have achieved significant performance gains, in some instances matching or exceeding top human performance in competitive mathematical and coding benchmarks. This success has led to a question regarding the scope of improvements provided by RPT: whether these gains represent truly generalizable enhancements to an LLM's reasoning abilities, similar to the broad improvements observed from pretraining, or if they are largely confined to the domains on which the models were post-trained.

The existing research landscape, characterized by the evaluation of RPT models primarily within their post-training domains, has left this question largely unaddressed. Prior studies typically assess RPT models on tasks that are intrinsically similar to their fine-tuning data, thus providing limited evidence on their transferability to new, unseen domains or tasks with different reasoning structures. This paper directly addresses this gap by systematically investigating the generalizability of RPT, moving beyond in-domain evaluations to explore cross-domain and intra-domain transfer. It aims to clarify the boundaries of RPT's applicability and its capacity to impart broadly generalizable reasoning skills to LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to comprehensively understand the generalizability of reasoning ability improvements gained through Reinforcement Post Training (RPT) in Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly focusing on their transferability to domains not encountered during the post-training phase.

The motivation for this study stems from a critical limitation in current RPT evaluation methodologies. While RPT has demonstrably enhanced LLM performance across various reasoning tasks, existing evaluations predominantly assess these models on data from the same domains used for post-training. This practice has left an empirical gap regarding how effectively RPT-induced gains generalize to genuinely novel domains. The authors identify that many reasoning tasks remain either underrepresented or entirely absent from existing RPT training corpora, necessitating an investigation into the boundaries of RPT's real-world applicability.

To systematically address this, the authors formulated four specific research questions (RQs):

- **RQ1: Cross-domain generalization:** This question explores the extent to which reasoning capabilities acquired through RPT transfer to tasks belonging to domains explicitly excluded from the training data. This aims to determine if RPT fosters a broader, domain-agnostic improvement in reasoning.
- **RQ2: Role of reasoning structure:** This investigates how the inherent structure of reasoning required by a task influences generalization. It examines whether skills learned from highly structured domains (e.g., mathematics, code generation) can transfer to less-structured domains (e.g., medical or legal reasoning), and vice versa, probing the nature of reasoning patterns amenable to transfer.
- **RQ3: Intra-domain generalization:** This question assesses the effectiveness of RPT gains in generalizing across different subdomains within the same high-level domain. It seeks to understand if improvements within a broad domain (like "knowledge-intensive reasoning") extend uniformly across its varied sub-areas.
- **RQ4: Stability of generalization:** This aims to ascertain whether the generalizability of RPT is consistent across variations in key hyperparameters and configurations, such as model size, the specific RPT training algorithm used, and the number of RPT steps. This addresses concerns about the robustness of observed generalization patterns.

To answer these questions, the authors designed a two-stage investigation pipeline. The first, an observational study, was motivated by the need for an initial, broad view of RPT generalizability across a diverse set of existing open-weight models, acknowledging the confounding factors of varied training configurations and data mixes. The second, an interventional study, was motivated by the need to isolate the effects of RPT by controlling these confounding variables. By fine-tuning LLMs with RPT on single, disjoint domains under consistent configurations, the interventional study aimed to provide a clearer, more granular understanding of RPT's generalizability mechanisms. The specific focus on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) was chosen because it represents the dominant instantiation of RPT for tasks requiring verifiable reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a two-stage investigation pipeline comprising an observational study and an interventional study, designed to comprehensively assess the generalizability of Reinforcement Post Training (RPT). The methodologies for evaluation settings and experimental setups for both studies were standardized where possible.

#### 3.1. Evaluation Settings

- **Benchmarks:** Sixteen popular benchmarks were selected, categorized into three primary reasoning domains:
 - **Math:** GSM8K, MATH-500 (easier); AIME 2024, AMC 2023 (challenging).
 - **Code:** MBPP, HumanEval (easier); BigCodeBench, LiveCodeBench, USACO, Codeforces (challenging); Polyglot (multi-language generalization).
 - **Knowledge-intensive reasoning:** PubMedQA, MedQA (medical); TabFact (fact verification); LegalBench (legal); FinBench (financial).
- **Generation Configurations:** Consistent hyperparameters were applied across all models. The maximum response length was set to min{16192, C}, where C represents the model’s context window. Smaller benchmarks (AMC 2023 and AIME 2024) were executed 16 times per model, while all other benchmarks were run once. Each model utilized its default chat template and system prompt.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Aggregated accuracy improvement (Δ(D)i,j):** This metric quantified the average accuracy difference between an RPT model `i` and its base model `j` for a given domain `D`.
 - **Common odds ratio estimate (ˆθ(D)i,j):** This estimated the correlation between the RPT process and accuracy improvement on domain `D`. An odds ratio greater than 1 indicated RPT-induced improvement, while a value less than 1 indicated a decrease.
 - **Statistical Significance:** The Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) test (Agresti, 2013) was used to determine statistical significance (p < 0.05) under the null hypothesis of no correlation (odds ratio = 1).
- **Reasoning Template Analysis (Appendix B):** To understand underlying reasoning patterns, approximately 100 tasks from each benchmark were sampled. Claude Sonnet 4.5 generated reasoning traces, which were then tagged by GPT-4o into categories such as READ_RESTATE, SETUP, PLAN, EXECUTE_STEP, CONTROL_FLOW, VERIFY, FINAL_ANSWER, and OTHER. Jeffreys divergence was used to quantify the symmetric divergence between reasoning-step type distributions within and across domains.

#### 3.2. Experimental Settings

- **Observational Study:**
 - **Model Selection:** An initial pool of 466 Hugging Face models was filtered based on criteria including Text Generation task support, keywords related to reasoning (e.g., "reasoning," "chain-of-thought"), and community popularity (>10 likes). Automatic prefiltering using o4-mini, followed by manual verification, identified 31 RPT models. From these, 18 models were chosen based on public disclosure of RPT datasets, model sizes ranging from 1.5B to 8B (including larger variants), and base models capable of coherent responses and basic instruction following (i.e., not purely pretrained). Table 1 provided details on these models, including their base models and RPT domains.
 - **Evaluation:** Each of the 18 selected RPT models was compared against its corresponding base model across the 16 benchmarks. Performance was categorized as "in-domain" (ID) if the benchmark domain matched the model's disclosed RPT training domain, and "out-of-domain" (OOD) otherwise.
- **Interventional Study:**
 - **Goal:** To isolate the impact of RPT by controlling for dataset variations, RL algorithms, and hyperparameters.
 - **Base Model:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025) was used as the consistent base model.
 - **Training Data Curation:** Three disjoint single-domain datasets, each with 40,000 problems, were curated to ensure no overlap with evaluation sets:
 - **Math:** Sampled from the math split of Eurus-2-RL (Cui et al., 2025) and NuminaMath-CoT (LI et al., 2024).
 - **Code:** Sampled from KodCode (Xu et al., 2025), DeepCoder-Preview (Luo et al., 2025a), Apps (Hendrycks et al., 2021), TACO (Li et al., 2023), and the code split of Eurus-2-RL.
 - **Knowledge-intensive Reasoning:** High-quality, non-math, non-code data from the multi-subject RPT dataset (Su et al., 2025b), filtered using o3-mini (OpenAI, 2025) to exclude math, code, or fact-recall questions.
 - **RPT Algorithm and Hyperparameters:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) was consistently applied, using the same settings as DeepCoder (Luo et al., 2025a). Reward definitions were binary and domain-specific (Appendix D.1): mathematical equivalence for math, unit test passing for code, and string match for knowledge. Reward quality was manually verified (94-99%). Models were trained for one epoch with a batch size of 64, context length of 8,192, learning rate of 10^-6, and an entropy coefficient of 0, on 8 H100 GPUs. Training rewards showed noticeable increases across domains (Appendix D.2).
 - **Additional Validation Experiments:**
 1. Training with DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), an alternative state-of-the-art RL algorithm.
 2. Extended training to 2 epochs, with evaluation of intermediate checkpoints.
 3. Using Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (Meta AI, 2024a) as an alternative base model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's findings, derived from both observational and interventional studies, consistently highlight limitations in the generalizability of Reinforcement Post Training (RPT) across diverse domains.

#### 4.1. RPT Gains Do Not Generalize to Arbitrary Unseen Domains (RQ1)

- **Existing RPT Models (Observational Study):** A comparison of 18 open-source RPT models against their base models revealed that RPT yielded substantially higher average accuracy improvements on in-domain (ID) tasks (2.87% increase in pass@1) compared to out-of-domain (OOD) tasks (-3.19% decrease in pass@1). This represented a 6.07% difference. Some RPT models demonstrated statistically significant performance drops on OOD tasks. For example, DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, trained on math data, showed a 5.1% gain in math tasks but only 1.7% in other domains. Absolute_Zero_Reasoner-Coder-7B, despite minimal training data, experienced a 30.12% ID improvement but a 23.31% OOD decrease. The results indicated that RPT gains are largely domain-specific.
- **Single-Domain Finetuning (Interventional Study):** Models post-trained exclusively on a single domain (math, code, or knowledge-intensive) showed no statistically significant improvement on OOD tasks. The Math-RPT model and Code-RPT model even exhibited statistically significant performance drops on OOD tasks, while achieving statistically significant in-domain gains. The Knowledge-RPT model also did not show statistically significant gains on its OOD tasks.

#### 4.2. RPT Gains Generalize Across Domains with Similar Reasoning Patterns (RQ2)

- **Structured-to-Structured Generalization:** Models RPT-trained on math and code data demonstrated mutual generalizability. In the observational study, math-trained models achieved an average 2.18% improvement on math tasks and 4.77% on code tasks. Similarly, code-trained models showed 9.49% improvement on code and 15.44% on math. The interventional study further supported this, showing stronger and more consistent generalization from math to code than vice versa, aligning with the idea that mathematical reasoning provides a more foundational structured thinking pattern. Reasoning template analysis (Appendix B.1) confirmed math and code share more similar templates (Jeffreys divergence of 0.18).
- **Structured-to-Unstructured Generalization:** RPT models trained on structured domains (math, code) exhibited reduced or negative improvements on knowledge-intensive domain tasks. Observational data showed an average -0.27% pass@1 improvement on knowledge-intensive tasks for structured-domain RPT models, contrasting with significant gains in their native structured domains. Interventional results corroborated this, with Math-RPT and Code-RPT models showing performance drops or statistically significant drops on knowledge-intensive tasks.
- **Unstructured-to-Structured Generalization:** RPT models trained on unstructured knowledge-intensive data showed promising gains in structured tasks. In the observational study, knowledge-intensive RPT models achieved higher pass@1 improvements on math (21.40%) and code (12.16%) than within their own domain. The interventional Knowledge-RPT model achieved statistically significant gains on math tasks and no noticeable degradation on code tasks, while underperforming in its own domain. This suggested that unstructured reasoning might encompass broader representational complexity that implicitly includes components of structured reasoning.

#### 4.3. Intra-Domain RPT Gains Depend on Subdomain Structural Similarity (RQ3)

- **Structured Reasoning Intra-Domain Generalization:** Consistent with prior work, RPT models trained on structured domains generalized effectively across tasks within the same domain. Math-trained models showed 2.18% improvement on math tasks, and code-trained models showed 9.49% on code tasks. The interventional study confirmed these largest gains in their respective training domains, suggesting consistent structured reasoning templates facilitate reliable generalization.
- **Unstructured Reasoning Intra-Domain Consistency:** Models trained on knowledge-intensive (unstructured) data showed limited or negative transfer to other unstructured tasks from different subdomains. Fino1-8B, trained on financial data, exhibited performance drops on medical, legal, and general tabular knowledge tasks. The interventional Knowledge-RPT model underperformed the base model on knowledge-intensive tasks, with statistically significant degradation. This indicates that unstructured reasoning tasks are highly diverse and domain-specific, lacking a shared logical template for broad intra-domain generalization. Reasoning template analysis (Appendix B.2) showed higher Jeffreys divergence within the knowledge-intensive domain (0.19) compared to math (0.15) and code (0.14).

#### 4.4. RPT Generalizability Remains Weak Across Configuration Variants (RQ4)

- **Consistency Across Algorithms:** Comparing GRPO and DAPO for math RPT showed similar gain gaps between in-domain and out-of-domain performance, suggesting that the core optimization behavior of RPT, leading to domain-specific learning, predominates over algorithmic differences.
- **Consistency Across Base Models:** Using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct as base models yielded similar gain gaps, indicating that the lack of RPT generalizability is intrinsic to the RPT process itself, rather than specific to the base model architecture or pretraining.
- **Decrease with Increased Training Steps:** The gap between in-domain and out-of-domain gains widened and then stabilized as the number of RPT training steps increased. This suggested that prolonged exposure to domain-specific data leads to increased overfitting, enhancing in-domain performance while offering diminishing OOD gains (Figure 5, Appendix H).
- **No Improvement with Larger Model Sizes:** Analysis of model-size variants revealed that, on average, in-domain gains increased by 16.5% more than out-of-domain gains as model size grew. This pattern indicated that larger models were more prone to overfitting their RPT training domains, amplifying in-domain benefits without proportional generalization (Appendix C).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a more nuanced understanding of the capabilities and limitations of Reinforcement Post Training (RPT) in enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs). By systematically investigating the generalizability of RPT gains across various domains, the study offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Refined Understanding of RPT Effectiveness:** The findings challenge the implicit assumption that RPT universally improves an LLM's reasoning abilities in a broadly generalizable manner. Instead, the research demonstrates that RPT benefits are largely domain-specific, with inconsistent and often vanishing gains on domains unseen during post-training. This refines the current understanding of RPT as a powerful, yet specialized, optimization technique.
- **Guidance for RPT Application:** The study's conclusion that RPT models are most effective when target tasks share similar reasoning patterns with the RPT data provides practical guidance for LLM developers and practitioners. It suggests that careful consideration of domain similarity between RPT data and the intended application domain is crucial for maximizing performance benefits and avoiding negative transfer effects.
- **Informing Future RPT Development:** By pinpointing the specific conditions under which RPT generalizes (e.g., structured-to-structured domains) and fails to generalize (e.g., structured-to-unstructured domains), the research highlights critical areas for future RPT algorithm development. It underscores the need for new RPT methodologies capable of fostering broader cross-domain knowledge transfer, particularly from structured reasoning skills to more flexible, context-sensitive unstructured reasoning tasks.
- **Implications for LLM Reasoning Research:** The work contributes to a deeper understanding of how different types of reasoning (structured vs. unstructured) are learned and transferred in LLMs. The observation that unstructured-to-structured generalization can be promising suggests that comprehensive, knowledge-intensive RPT might implicitly teach foundational reasoning components that are transferable to structured problems, opening new avenues for research into the hierarchy and interdependencies of reasoning skills.
- **Benchmarking and Evaluation Paradigms:** The study implicitly advocates for the development of more diverse and challenging evaluation benchmarks that explicitly test cross-domain generalization. This could push the field beyond in-domain performance metrics to truly assess the robustness and transferability of LLM improvements.
- **Resource Allocation and Efficiency:** Recognizing the limitations of RPT generalizability could lead to more strategic allocation of computational resources in RPT. Instead of aiming for universal improvements, resources might be better focused on domain-specific RPT, or on developing more sophisticated data curation and algorithmic approaches for achieving targeted cross-domain transfer.

In summary, this paper moves beyond simply demonstrating RPT's efficacy to delineate its boundaries, thereby providing a more grounded and practical perspective on its role in advancing LLM reasoning. It suggests that achieving genuinely generalizable reasoning in LLMs through RPT will require a more nuanced approach to data selection, algorithmic design, and architectural considerations.
