# PhysGame: Uncovering Physical Commonsense Violations in Gameplay Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces PhysGame, the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate Video LLMs' ability to identify physical commonsense violations in gameplay videos. The paper also develops PhysInstruct and PhysDPO, specialized datasets for training, and presents PhysVLM, a Video LLM that achieves 80.3% accuracy on PhysGame, outperforming other open-source models and approaching proprietary model performance while maintaining general video understanding.

## Problem

- Video Large Language Models (Video LLMs) lacked a comprehensive benchmark and dedicated training to evaluate and improve their ability to recognize violations of fundamental physical laws in videos.
- Existing video understanding benchmarks primarily focus on general comprehension rather than specific physical commonsense reasoning.
- A significant performance gap existed between proprietary and open-source Video LLMs in detecting physical anomalies.

## Method

- Introduced **PhysGame**, the first benchmark of 880 gameplay videos systematically categorized by physical anomaly (mechanics, kinematics, optics, material properties), each with a multiple-choice question.
- Created two specialized datasets for training: **PhysInstruct** (140,057 Q&A pairs for instruction tuning) and **PhysDPO** (34,358 pairs for Direct Preference Optimization), both focused on physical commonsense.
- Developed **PhysVLM**, a Video LLM based on PPLLaVA and Qwen2-7B, trained in a two-stage process combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (==SFT==) on hybrid general and physical datasets, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on physical and general preference data.

## Results

- PhysVLM achieved 80.3% accuracy on the PhysGame benchmark, outperforming other open-source models (e.g., VideoLLaMA 52.4%, LLaMA-VID 57.9%) and narrowing the gap with proprietary models (GPT-4o 88.2%, Claude 3 Opus 84.4%).
- PhysVLM maintained strong performance on general video understanding benchmarks, scoring 67.8% on Video-MME and 61.5% on VCG, slightly outperforming previous best open-source models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both PhysInstruct and PhysDPO datasets are critical, contributing 16.7% and 8.4% performance gains on PhysGame, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specialized training using instruction-tuning (PhysInstruct) and preference optimization (PhysDPO) on physical commonsense data is crucial for enhancing Video LLMs' ability to detect physics violations.
> - The ability to identify physical anomalies in gameplay videos provides a robust testbed for evaluating AI systems' understanding of fundamental physics principles.
> - Improving physical commonsense reasoning in Video LLMs does not diminish their general video understanding capabilities, suggesting a complementary relationship.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video Large Language Models (Video LLMs) have made remarkable progress in understanding and reasoning about video content, performing impressively on tasks like visual question answering and temporal event recognition. However, a critical capability has remained largely untested: the ability to recognize when something in a video violates the fundamental laws of physics.

This is where the new research paper "PhysGame: Uncovering Physical Commonsense Violations in Gameplay Videos" makes its contribution. The researchers from Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence, Peking University, Alibaba Group, University of Waterloo, and other institutions have created a novel benchmark to evaluate how well Video LLMs can identify physical anomalies in gameplay videos.

Gameplay videos provide an ideal testing ground for physical commonsense reasoning. Video games simulate physical environments but often contain glitches that defy real-world physics—characters walking through walls, objects floating in mid-air, or bodies contorting impossibly. These moments of "broken physics" create a perfect opportunity to assess whether AI systems truly understand the physical principles that govern our world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper introduces three major contributions: (1) PhysGame, a benchmark of 880 gameplay videos with physical anomalies; (2) PhysInstruct and PhysDPO, datasets for training models in physical reasoning; and (3) PhysVLM, a new Video LLM specifically enhanced with physical commonsense capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The PhysGame Benchmark

PhysGame represents the first comprehensive benchmark focused specifically on evaluating physical commonsense reasoning in Video LLMs. The development process included:

1. **Video Collection**: The researchers collected 880 gameplay videos containing glitches from various games, each clearly exhibiting violations of physics principles.

2. **Categorization**: The glitches were systematically categorized across four physical domains:
 - **Mechanics**: Involving forces, collisions, and physical interactions
 - **Kinematics**: Related to motion, position, and trajectory
 - **Optics**: Concerning light, shadows, and visual rendering
 - **Material Properties**: Dealing with substance behavior, like water or fire

3. **Question Generation**: Each video was paired with a carefully crafted multiple-choice question about the specific physical violation, requiring the model to identify what aspects of physics were being broken.

4. **Quality Control**: The authors employed both human inspection and LLM-assisted verification to ensure the benchmark's quality and accuracy.

What distinguishes PhysGame from existing video understanding benchmarks is its explicit focus on physics violations rather than general comprehension. While benchmarks like Video-MME and VCG test broad understanding capabilities, PhysGame specifically probes whether models can recognize when simulated physics diverges from reality—a crucial capability for AI systems that need to operate in and reason about the physical world.

#### 3.2. Datasets for Physical Commonsense Training

The research team observed a significant performance gap between proprietary models (like GPT-4o and Claude 3) and open-source alternatives when tested on PhysGame. To address this gap, they created two specialized datasets:

#### 3.3. PhysInstruct

PhysInstruct contains 140,057 question-answering pairs generated by prompting GPT-4o with gameplay videos and their titles. The dataset serves as instruction-tuning material to help Video LLMs better understand physical phenomena. Each entry includes:

- A gameplay video segment
- A question about physical properties or interactions
- A detailed answer explaining the relevant physics

The dataset covers diverse physical concepts across mechanics, kinematics, optics, and material properties, creating a broad foundation for physical reasoning.

#### 3.4. PhysDPO

To further refine models' physical understanding, the researchers created PhysDPO, a dataset of 34,358 training pairs for Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). This dataset includes:

- **Preferred responses**: Accurate answers from PhysInstruct
- **Dis-preferred responses**: Generated using three "hacking" techniques:
 1. **Meta-information hacking**: Using misleading video titles
 2. **Temporal hacking**: Providing fewer frames from the video
 3. **Spatial hacking**: Using lower resolution frames

By training models to prefer accurate physical reasoning despite incomplete or misleading information, PhysDPO helps develop more robust physical understanding capabilities.

The equation for the DPO loss function used in training is:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\theta; \theta_{\text{ref}}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l) \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}(y_l|x)} \right) \right]$$

Where $\theta$ represents the model parameters, $\theta_{\text{ref}}$ refers to the reference model parameters, $\pi_\theta$ is the policy (probability distribution), $y_w$ and $y_l$ are the preferred and dis-preferred responses respectively, and $\beta$ is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of the optimization.

#### 3.5. PhysVLM Architecture and Training

Building on these specialized datasets, the researchers developed PhysVLM, a physical knowledge-enhanced Video LLM designed to excel at physical commonsense reasoning while maintaining strong general video understanding capabilities.

#### 3.6. Architecture

PhysVLM builds upon the PPLLaVA architecture with several key modifications:

1. Base LLM: Qwen2-7B replaces Vicuna-7B for enhanced language modeling capabilities
2. Visual Encoder: Utilizes CLIP ViT-L/14 to process video frames
3. Projection Layer: Maps visual features to the language embedding space
4. Temporal Integration: Handles sequential information across video frames

The architecture can be expressed as:

```python
# Simplified pseudocode for PhysVLM architecture
def PhysVLM(video_frames, text_prompt):
    # Process each frame with visual encoder
    visual_features = [CLIP_ViT(frame) for frame in video_frames]

    # Project visual features to language space
    projected_features = projection_layer(visual_features)

    # Integrate temporal information
    temporal_features = temporal_integration(projected_features)

    # Process with LLM
    text_embeddings = Qwen2_tokenize(text_prompt)
    output = Qwen2_LLM(text_embeddings, temporal_features)

    return output
```

#### 3.7. Training Process

The training of PhysVLM followed a two-stage process:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: The model was trained on a hybrid dataset combining:
 - PhysInstruct (physical commonsense)
 - LLaVA-1.5 (general image understanding)
 - VideoInstruct1 (general video understanding)

2. **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**: Further refinement using:
 - PhysDPO (physical commonsense preferences)
 - ShareGPT4V (general image preferences)
 - VideoInstruct2 (general video preferences)

This combined approach allowed PhysVLM to develop strong physical reasoning capabilities while maintaining high performance on general video understanding tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments revealed several key findings:

#### 4.1. Performance on PhysGame

PhysVLM significantly outperformed existing open-source Video LLMs on the PhysGame benchmark:

- **Proprietary Models**: GPT-4o (88.2%), Claude 3 Opus (84.4%)
- **PhysVLM**: 80.3% accuracy
- **Other Open-Source Models**: VideoLLaMA (52.4%), LLaMA-VID (57.9%)

This demonstrates that the specialized training approach effectively narrowed the gap between open-source and proprietary models for physical commonsense reasoning.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Other Benchmarks

Importantly, PhysVLM maintained strong performance on general video understanding benchmarks:

- **Video-MME**: 67.8% (compared to 66.5% for the previous best open-source model)
- **VCG**: 61.5% (compared to 60.2% for the previous best open-source model)

This indicates that enhancing physical commonsense reasoning does not come at the expense of general video understanding capabilities but rather complements them.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The researchers conducted ablation studies to understand the contribution of each component:

1. **Without PhysInstruct**: Performance dropped by 16.7% on PhysGame
2. **Without PhysDPO**: Performance dropped by 8.4% on PhysGame
3. **Without both**: Performance was comparable to baseline models

These results confirm that both the instruction tuning (PhysInstruct) and preference optimization (PhysDPO) components significantly contributed to PhysVLM's capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper has several important implications and potential applications:

#### 5.1. Game Development and Testing

Game developers can leverage physical commonsense-aware Video LLMs to automatically detect physics glitches during testing, potentially saving thousands of hours in quality assurance and improving player experience.

#### 5.2. Robotics and Physical Interaction

For robots to safely and effectively interact with the physical world, they must understand how objects behave and identify when something is behaving unexpectedly. The physical reasoning capabilities developed in this research could enhance robots' ability to navigate real-world environments.

#### 5.3. AR/VR Experience Enhancement

Augmented and virtual reality experiences rely on convincing physics simulations. Models that can detect physics violations could help developers create more immersive and believable virtual environments.

#### 5.4. Educational Tools

PhysVLM-like models could serve as educational tools, helping students understand physical principles by identifying and explaining violations in examples—turning "physics bugs" into learning opportunities.

#### 5.5. Broader AI Safety

The ability to recognize when something violates physical laws is a crucial component of common sense that helps humans navigate the world safely. Instilling this capability in AI systems represents an important step toward more reliable and trustworthy artificial intelligence.

#### 5.6. Conclusion

"PhysGame: Uncovering Physical Commonsense Violations in Gameplay Videos" makes several significant contributions to the field of video understanding and AI reasoning. By creating the first benchmark specifically focused on physical commonsense violations, developing specialized training datasets, and demonstrating a successful approach to enhancing Video LLMs with physical reasoning capabilities, the authors have addressed an important gap in existing AI systems.

The impressive performance of PhysVLM—approaching the capabilities of proprietary models while maintaining strong general video understanding—demonstrates that targeted training on physical commonsense can substantially improve AI's understanding of the world. As Video LLMs continue to evolve, the ability to recognize and reason about physical principles will be increasingly important for applications ranging from gaming to robotics to education.

This research represents a meaningful step toward AI systems that not only process what they see but truly understand the physical world that governs it—a capability that will be essential as AI becomes more deeply integrated into our physical environment.
