# View-Invariant Policy Learning via Zero-Shot Novel View Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> VISTA is a data-augmentation scheme for view-invariant visuomotor policies: each demonstration frame is replaced by an image rendered from a randomly sampled camera pose using a zero-shot single-image diffusion novel-view-synthesis model (ZeroNVS), with action labels unchanged and LPIPS-based rejection of bad renders. Policies trained this way outperform depth-reprojection and PixelNeRF baselines on out-of-distribution viewpoints in simulation and on a real Franka.

## Problem

- Policies trained on fixed-viewpoint RGB demonstrations consistently fail under camera pose shifts, and most large robot datasets supply neither multi-view images nor camera extrinsics.
- Simulator-rendered view augmentation and physically repositioning cameras during real data collection buy robustness only at the cost of sim-to-real transfer or heavy manual effort.
- Explicit 3D pipelines (point clouds, voxels, canonical reprojection) and view-invariant latent objectives demand calibrated extrinsics, static scenes, or costly inference-time computation.

## Method

- **VISTA** (==View Synthesis Augmentation==): replace each observation-action pair's image with a ==ZeroNVS== render at an independently sampled target extrinsic, holding the action label fixed; only relative pose is needed.
- ==LPIPS rejection filter==: renders whose perceptual distance from the input image exceeds a threshold are rejected and resampled, suppressing ZeroNVS's extreme close-up failure mode.
- ==In-domain finetuning== of the NVS model: ZeroNVS finetuned on multi-view data from eight MimicGen tasks, and separately on 3000 DROID trajectories, then applied zero-shot to held-out tasks.

## Results

- Under perturbed viewpoints, ZeroNVS augmentation lifts success from **9.3%** to **23.3%** on Threading and **16.7%** to **36.0%** on Nut Assembly; PixelNeRF hurts, dropping Threading to **4.7%**.
- On quarter-circle-arc views, MimicGen-finetuned ZeroNVS roughly doubles the next best method: Hammer **52.0%** vs **27.3%**, Threading **32.0%** vs **17.3%**, cross-embodiment Nut Assembly **26.0%** vs **18.7%**.
- Real Franka cup-on-saucer: DROID-finetuned augmentation reaches the cup **44/50** and completes **20/50** across five viewpoints, versus **14/40** and **4/40** for the single-view policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative 3D priors learned from robot-free image data transfer to robot policies: viewpoint invariance can be bought by augmentation instead of calibration or reconstruction.
> - Render fidelity, not augmentation itself, decides the outcome; low-quality synthetic views such as PixelNeRF actively degrade policies below no augmentation at all.
> - Wrist cameras and third-person view augmentation give complementary rather than redundant gains, and a large oracle gap persists for far-off-distribution camera poses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with a strong push towards developing generalizable policies that can operate across diverse embodiments, environments, and tasks. A significant challenge in this pursuit lies in the **generalization of visuomotor policies to varying observational modalities, particularly changes in camera viewpoint**. Policies trained on images collected from fixed viewpoints consistently struggle when presented with visual inputs from different camera poses. This limitation severely hinders the scalability and versatility of learned robotic skills.

Existing approaches to tackle viewpoint invariance can be broadly categorized:
- **Data Augmentation in Simulation:** Training with augmented data collected in simulation can improve robustness but introduces the notorious "sim-to-real gap," requiring additional efforts for real-world deployment.
- **Large-scale Real-World Data Collection:** Physically varying camera poses during large-scale real robot data collection is effective but entails significant manual effort and high costs.
- **Explicit 3D Representations:** Methods that form explicit 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, voxels, NeRFs) can leverage inherent equivariance properties. While robust, these often demand well-calibrated camera extrinsics and multi-view observations, which are not universally available in existing datasets and can be computationally intensive at inference.
- **Latent Robust Representations:** Learning latent representations that are inherently robust to camera pose variations, often through view synthesis or contrastive learning as auxiliary objectives. These, too, often rely on accurate extrinsics or can incur high computational costs at inference.
- **Wrist-Mounted Cameras:** Using eye-in-hand cameras offers localized information, which is helpful but not always sufficient for tasks requiring a broader scene understanding, and does not obviate the need for robust third-person observations.

This paper positions itself as a novel and practical solution to the viewpoint invariance problem by **leveraging advancements in large-scale generative models for single-image novel view synthesis (NVS)**. While generative image models have been applied in robotics for semantic data augmentation, cross-embodiment transfer, or high-level planning, their direct application to creating viewpoint-invariant policies from *single-view offline robotic trajectory data* has remained relatively unexplored. The innovation lies in using zero-shot NVS models as a data augmentation strategy (VISTA) to inject 3D priors learned from vast visual datasets into policy training, without requiring multi-view robotic data, depth sensors, or camera calibration for target views. This approach aims to provide the benefits of 3D awareness without the associated complexities and costs, making it highly relevant for scaling robotic learning with widely available datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from a critical bottleneck in deploying visuomotor policies: their **lack of generalization to variations in observational viewpoint**. Policies trained on carefully controlled, fixed camera setups fail catastrophically when the camera pose changes, even incrementally. This fragility is a significant impediment to developing truly general-purpose manipulation systems that can adapt to diverse sensor configurations and deployment scenarios.

Current solutions, such as collecting multi-view data in simulation or real-world, or using complex 3D representations, come with substantial drawbacks:
- **Cost and Effort:** Simulators require expertise for realistic rendering and bridging the sim-to-real gap. Collecting multi-view real-world data is time-consuming and labor-intensive.
- **Data Requirements:** Explicit 3D methods often necessitate calibrated multi-view images or depth information, which are often absent in large existing datasets (e.g., Open X-Embodiment).
- **Inference Time Impact:** Some methods that rely on complex 3D processing or latent space manipulation can increase inference latency, which is undesirable for real-time robotic control.

Given these limitations, the **key objective of this research is to develop a method that can learn policies robust to observational viewpoint changes directly from widely available, offline single-view RGB robotic trajectory data**. The authors propose to achieve this by **leveraging 3D scene priors embedded within large-scale generative models for single-image novel view synthesis (NVS)**.

Specifically, the paper aims to:
1. **Investigate the efficacy of Zero-Shot Novel View Synthesis (NVS) models** within a simple data augmentation scheme (VISTA) to enhance viewpoint robustness.
2. **Empirically analyze the performance** of policies trained with NVS-augmented data across various out-of-distribution camera viewpoints in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.
3. **Evaluate whether finetuning NVS models on in-domain robotic data** can further improve downstream policy robustness for unseen tasks and larger viewpoint shifts.
4. **Understand the complementary nature** of VISTA with other domain-gap reduction strategies, such as the use of wrist-mounted cameras.
5. **Demonstrate the applicability and benefits of VISTA** for learning policies directly on real robots using real-world data.

The proposed VISTA approach offers several compelling advantages that underscore its motivation: it can leverage diverse large-scale 2D image datasets, requires no depth information or camera calibration, places no limitations on the form of the policy, and critically, does not impact policy inference time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, named **View Synthesis Augmentation (VISTA)**, revolves around a simple yet effective data augmentation scheme. The authors frame the problem as visual imitation learning, where a policy $\pi(a|o)$ needs to be learned from a dataset of expert demonstrations $D$ consisting of trajectories $(o_t, a_t)$, where $o_t$ includes RGB image observations $o_I$ captured from a fixed third-person camera with extrinsics $E_{orig}$. The goal is for the policy to generalize to test images captured from camera extrinsics $E_{test}$ sampled from a diverse distribution.

The VISTA approach operates as follows (summarized in Figure 3 of the paper):

1. **Novel View Synthesis (NVS) Model:** The method utilizes a single-image NVS model, $M(I_{context}, f, E_{context}, E_{target})$, which takes an input image $I_{context}$ (from $E_{context}$) and a target camera pose $E_{target}$ (along with field of view $f$) to render a new image of the same scene from the target viewpoint.
2. **Data Augmentation:** During policy training, for each observation-action pair $(o_t, a_t)$ in the demonstration dataset, VISTA replaces the original image $o_I$ with a synthesized image. This is done by randomly sampling a target camera extrinsics $E_{target}$ from a training distribution $E_{train}$ and then generating the new image $o'_t = M(o_t, f, E_{context}, E_{target})$. The action label $a_t$ is kept constant, effectively telling the policy "this is what the scene looks like from this new perspective, and the robot still performs the same action."
3. **Zero-Shot Application:** A key aspect is the *zero-shot* application of the NVS model. The primary NVS model used is **ZeroNVS**, a latent diffusion model initialized from Stable Diffusion and fine-tuned on diverse 3D scenes. This model is chosen for its strong zero-shot performance across various scene types and its ability to produce crisp, realistic novel views. ZeroNVS can be used directly (pretrained on general image data) or finetuned on a separate dataset of robotic scenes before being applied zero-shot to *new tasks*.
4. **Failure Mode Mitigation:** ZeroNVS, despite its capabilities, can sometimes produce images with visual artifacts (e.g., extreme close-ups due to poor extrinsics in its training data). To address this, the authors implement a filtering mechanism: synthesized images with a perceptual similarity (LPIPS) distance larger than a threshold $\eta$ from the input image are rejected and resampled. If repeated attempts fail, the original image is used.
5. **Policy Learning:**
 - **Simulated Experiments:** Behavior cloning with a Gaussian mixture model output, leveraging the `robomimic` framework.
 - **Real-World Experiments:** Diffusion policies, recognized for their success in real-robot learning, are employed.
 - VISTA itself does not modify the policy architecture or its inference behavior, meaning the augmented policy runs at the same speed as a non-augmented one.

**Experimental Setup:**
The authors conduct extensive empirical analyses in both simulated and real-world environments:
- **Simulated Experiments:** Use `robomimic` on the MuJoCo simulator and MimicGen tasks (Lift, Nut Assembly, Threading, Hammer, Coffee, Stack, PickPlace). Datasets are based on expert demonstrations.
 - **Test Viewpoint Distributions:** Two types: "Perturbations" (small, incremental changes) and "Quarter Circle Arc" (larger, more challenging viewpoint shifts, akin to constructing a different view).
- **Real-World Experiments:** Utilize a Franka Emika Panda robot for a "place cup on saucer" task, collecting 150 expert demonstrations. External observations are captured from an original viewpoint and four novel views.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Single view:** No augmentation.
 - **Simulator (oracle):** Renders ground-truth novel views directly from the simulator (an upper bound).
 - **Depth estimation + Reprojection:** A geometry-based pipeline involving off-the-shelf depth estimation (ZoeDepth), 3D point cloud reconstruction, and re-rendering, followed by inpainting with Stable Diffusion.
 - **PixelNeRF:** Another NVS model, trained on similar diverse datasets as ZeroNVS, for direct comparison between NVS model types.
 - **Multi-View Masked Autoencoder (MV-MAE):** (In appendix) A self-supervised pretraining method assuming multi-view data.

The methodology systematically evaluates VISTA's effectiveness and its interaction with other factors like NVS model finetuning and the inclusion of wrist cameras.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis yielded several key findings, demonstrating the effectiveness of VISTA in improving viewpoint robustness for visuomotor policies:

**Q1: Performance of Pre-trained Novel View Synthesis Models**
- **ZeroNVS Outperforms Baselines for Perturbed Viewpoints:** Policies trained with ZeroNVS augmentation (even when ZeroNVS is pre-trained on out-of-domain data and has not seen robotic arms) significantly improve robustness to incremental viewpoint changes ("perturbed" viewpoints) compared to a single-view baseline. For example, on the Lift task, ZeroNVS augmentation achieved 95.3% success vs. 72.7% for single-view and 93.3% for Depth estimation + Reprojection (Table 1).
- **Depth Estimation + Reprojection Offers Modest Gains:** This geometry-based baseline provided some improvements but was generally less effective than ZeroNVS.
- **PixelNeRF Performed Poorly:** PixelNeRF failed to synthesize views with sufficient fidelity, leading to a drop in performance, demonstrating the importance of the quality of the NVS model.

**Q2: Effect of Finetuning View Synthesis Models on In-Domain Data**
- **Significant Gains from Finetuning:** Finetuning ZeroNVS on simulated robotic data (MimicGen tasks) drastically improved performance on more challenging "quarter circle arc" viewpoints for *held-out* tasks (Unseen Object, Shared Object, X-Embodiment categories). These improvements often doubled the success rate compared to using the pre-trained ZeroNVS model (e.g., Threading: 32.0% for finetuned ZeroNVS vs. 17.3% for pre-trained ZeroNVS; Coffee: 32.7% vs. 15.3%) (Table 2).
- **Qualitative Improvement in Image Fidelity:** Finetuning ZeroNVS on robotic data led to higher fidelity synthesized images, especially for robot arm appearances, reducing the domain gap between generated and real images (Figure 4).

**Q3: Use of Wrist-Mounted Cameras to Reduce Domain Gap**
- **Complementary Benefits:** Wrist-mounted camera observations, while beneficial on their own, also proved complementary to third-person view augmentation. Adding wrist camera observations slightly improved performance across all augmentation techniques (Figure 5), suggesting that VISTA enhances global scene understanding from third-person views, which is distinct from the local information provided by wrist cameras.

**Q4: Performance on Real Robots**
- **Effectiveness in Real-World:** VISTA successfully improved policy viewpoint robustness in real-world settings. On a "place cup on saucer" task, ZeroNVS augmentation (ZeroNVS aug + wrist) significantly increased aggregated success rates (39/50 completions) compared to policies trained with original data only (14/40 completions) (Table 3).
- **Further Gains from Finetuning on Real Robotic Data:** Finetuning ZeroNVS on a large, diverse real multi-view robotic dataset (DROID) yielded additional performance gains (44/50 completions for ZeroNVS (DROID) + wrist), demonstrating the benefit of targeted NVS model adaptation.
- **Single-View Policies Fail:** Policies trained solely on single-view data struggled significantly with viewpoint changes in real-world deployment.

**Additional Findings (Appendices):**
- **Increasing Augmented Transitions:** Modest improvements in policy performance were observed when increasing the number of augmented transitions per original dataset transition, suggesting that a single augmentation is a strong baseline, but further augmentation can help (Figure 10).
- **Comparison to MV-MAE:** VISTA significantly outperformed a multi-view masked autoencoder (MV-MAE) baseline, demonstrating its superior ability to generalize to novel viewpoints (Table 5).
- **Saliency Analysis:** GradCAM++ visualizations indicated that wrist camera observations primarily focused on objects near the gripper, while third-person camera views with VISTA concentrated more on the robotic arm itself (Figure 12).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "View-Invariant Policy Learning via Zero-Shot Novel View Synthesis" paper makes significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, with profound implications for the development of more generalizable and robust robotic systems.

**1. Enabling Generalization to Diverse Viewpoints:**
The most immediate and impactful contribution is providing a practical and effective method for training visuomotor policies that are robust to changes in camera viewpoint. This addresses a long-standing challenge that limits the scalability and real-world applicability of learned robotic skills. By overcoming the fragility of policies to observational shifts, VISTA moves robotics closer to deployment in unstructured and dynamic environments with varied sensor configurations.

**2. Leveraging Large-Scale Generative Models for Robotics:**
The work effectively demonstrates how powerful 3D priors learned by large-scale generative models (specifically, diffusion models for novel view synthesis like ZeroNVS) can be harnessed for robotic learning. This is a crucial step in bridging the gap between advancements in foundation models in computer vision and their application in embodied AI. It shows that these models can provide meaningful "3D common sense" even in a zero-shot manner for robotic tasks, paving the way for more sophisticated integrations.

**3. Enhancing Data Efficiency and Accessibility:**
A critical advantage of VISTA is its ability to learn viewpoint-invariant policies from readily available *single-view offline RGB robotic trajectory data*. This significantly increases the utility of existing datasets, many of which lack multi-view observations, depth information, or camera calibration details. By sidestepping the need for expensive multi-view data collection or complex 3D reconstruction pipelines, VISTA makes robust policy learning more accessible and data-efficient, accelerating research and development.

**4. Policy-Agnostic and Inference-Time Neutral:**
VISTA is designed as a data augmentation scheme, meaning it does not impose limitations on the policy architecture or impact the policy's inference time. This allows it to be broadly applicable to various robotic learning paradigms (e.g., different imitation learning algorithms, reinforcement learning) without introducing additional computational overhead during real-time robot operation, which is vital for practical deployment.

**5. Stepping Stone Towards Foundational Models for Robotics:**
By tackling one of the critical axes of variation (observational viewpoint generalization), this research contributes to the broader goal of developing "foundation models" for robotics – single, highly capable models that can perform a multitude of tasks across diverse conditions. VISTA's ability to imbue policies with 3D understanding from diverse visual data moves towards more generalizable and adaptable robotic intelligence.

**Potential Impact:**
The potential impact of this work is substantial. Robots equipped with VISTA-trained policies could be more easily deployed in new environments with different camera setups, reducing calibration and retraining efforts. This could accelerate the adoption of robots in logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, and home environments. Furthermore, the approach encourages further exploration into how other forms of generative AI and large visual datasets can be effectively integrated into robotic learning pipelines to overcome generalization challenges, opening new avenues for research in embodied AI.
