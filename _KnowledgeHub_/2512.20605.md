# Emergent temporal abstractions in autoregressive models enable hierarchical reinforcement learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel hierarchical reinforcement learning paradigm, termed 'internal RL,' leverages emergent temporal abstractions within pretrained autoregressive models to efficiently solve complex, sparse-reward tasks. This approach enables a metacontroller to discover and operate on abstract actions, drastically outperforming traditional reinforcement learning and prior HRL methods on challenging grid world and continuous control environments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning finetuning of autoregressive models is inefficient for long-horizon tasks due to token-level action generation and sparse rewards.
- Discovering effective temporally-abstract actions for Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) remains a longstanding challenge, with existing methods often converging to degenerate options.
- Standard RL struggles with exploration and credit assignment in complex environments requiring extended sequences of low-level actions.

## Method

- Pretrain a base autoregressive model on behavioral data to implicitly learn rich internal representations of observation-action sequences.
- Introduce a ==self-supervised metacontroller== that operates on the *frozen* base model's internal activations to unsupervisedly discover and sequence temporally-abstract actions.
- Implement =='internal RL'== where a causal abstract action policy learns to output ==controller codes==, leveraging a ==binarized switching mechanism== for robust temporal abstraction.

## Results

- The 'internal RL' approach dramatically outperforms standard token-level RL finetuning and other HRL baselines, solving sparse-reward tasks where baselines fail within **a million episodes**.
- The self-supervised metacontroller successfully discovers meaningful temporally-abstract actions, with its switching unit learning to operate in a quasi-binary fashion aligned with ground-truth subgoal changes.
- Simple linear controllers, when inserted at mid-depth layers of the pretrained base model, enable compositional generalization to novel and longer sequences of subgoals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autoregressive models, pretrained on next-token prediction, implicitly learn linearly controllable representations of temporally-abstract subgoals within their internal activations.
> - Freezing the pretrained base model during metacontroller training is crucial for the unsupervised discovery of meaningful, subgoal-aligned temporal switching patterns.
> - Hierarchical control can be achieved by directly steering the internal representations of pretrained foundation models, allowing them to operate on a contracted timescale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas of artificial intelligence:

- **Autoregressive Models and Reinforcement Learning:** The paper begins by acknowledging the "revolution in artificial intelligence (AI), driven primarily by autoregressive sequence models," specifically Transformers. These models, pretrained on massive datasets using next-token prediction, have achieved "unprecedented success" across diverse domains when finetuned with reinforcement learning (RL). The authors highlight their ability to infer latent variables (in-context learning) and serve as rich action priors for exploration.
- **Challenges in RL Efficiency and Sparse Rewards:** A key motivation for this research is the inefficiency of standard RL finetuning for autoregressive models. Since these models generate actions "one token at a time," exploration relies solely on token-level variations. This approach becomes "highly inefficient" for long-horizon tasks, especially when rewards are sparse—a pervasive and difficult problem in real-world RL.
- **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL):** The paper directly addresses the aforementioned inefficiency by drawing upon the principles of HRL. HRL aims to tackle complex tasks by breaking them down into temporally-abstract actions or "subroutines" that operate over extended timescales. This approach, inspired by human problem-solving, can drastically reduce the search space and simplify credit assignment. However, the paper notes that discovering effective subroutines via deep RL remains a "longstanding challenge," with existing methods like option-critic often suffering from theoretical issues and practical failures (e.g., converging to degenerate options).
- **Interpretability and Control of Internal Representations:** This research also connects to the growing interest in understanding and manipulating the internal workings of large neural networks. Recent studies have explored "mechanistic interpretability," linear probing, and causal interventions to decode and steer the behavior of LLMs. The paper references work on LoRA finetuning and "representation engineering," positioning its approach as a form of latent action generation and reinforcement within these powerful models.
- **Theoretical Foundations of Intelligence:** The paper explicitly links its findings to long-standing theoretical ideas in AI. It references Jürgen Schmidhuber's "wake-sleep training loop" theory, which proposes an iterative process between self-supervised learning for history compression and RL for generating new experiences and exploiting temporal abstractions. It also draws parallels to Yann LeCun's Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), particularly the "configurator module" responsible for modulating a world model for specific goals. By connecting to these foundational theories, the work places itself in a lineage of research striving for more generally intelligent autonomous agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research is to overcome a fundamental limitation in applying reinforcement learning to autoregressive models: the inefficiency arising from token-by-token action generation, particularly in sparse-reward, long-horizon tasks. The authors identify that current RL finetuning, while successful, struggles with exploration and credit assignment when rewards are infrequent and require extended sequences of correct actions.

From this overarching motivation, the paper articulates several key objectives:

- **Demonstrate Emergent Temporal Abstractions:** A primary objective is to investigate whether autoregressive models, despite being trained solely on next-token prediction, implicitly learn representations of "temporally-abstract actions" within their internal activations. The hypothesis is that their "in-context latent variable inference capabilities" imbue them with this potential.
- **Enable Unsupervised Discovery of Abstract Actions:** To make HRL practical, the goal is to develop a method that can discover these temporally-abstract actions without requiring explicit, step-by-step supervision or pre-defined abstract action labels. This necessitates a self-supervised learning approach.
- **Develop a Novel Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning Paradigm:** The ultimate objective is to introduce a new HRL method, termed "internal RL," that can effectively leverage these emergent abstractions. This method should enable learning from sparse rewards in tasks where standard token-level RL finetuning fails by operating on a "contracted timescale" within the model's internal representations.
- **Facilitate Efficient Exploration and Credit Assignment:** By learning and acting with abstract actions, the new paradigm aims to drastically reduce the effective search space and simplify the credit assignment problem, leading to "many orders of magnitude faster" learning than traditional approaches.
- **Provide a General Framework for Foundation Model Control:** Beyond specific tasks, the research seeks to offer a promising avenue for realizing hierarchical RL within large foundation models, opening up new possibilities for their control and application in complex domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a multi-stage approach centered around a **metacontroller** that operates on the internal activations of a **pretrained autoregressive model**.

#### 3.1. Pretraining the Base Autoregressive Model

The initial step involves pretraining a base autoregressive sequence model ($f_\theta$) from scratch on a behavioral dataset ($D$) containing observation-action sequences. Critically, this dataset *does not* include rewards or explicit goal/task descriptors.
- **Model Architectures:** For discrete grid world environments, a standard causal Transformer is used. For continuous control tasks (MuJoCo Ant), an efficient State-Space Model (SSM), specifically Hawk, is employed.
- **Training Objective:** Models are trained to minimize the negative log-likelihood of next-action prediction, with an auxiliary loss for next-observation prediction, effectively learning a behavioral and world model.
- **Environments:** Experiments are conducted on two hierarchically-structured environments:
 - **Discrete Grid World:** Agents navigate to colored locations in a specific order.
 - **Continuous MuJoCo Ant:** A quadrupedal robot must be controlled at a joint level to navigate to colored locations in sequence, requiring both low-level motor control and high-level planning. Abstract actions in both environments correspond to moving towards specific subgoals.

#### 3.2. Probing and Controlling Internal Representations

Before introducing the full metacontroller, the authors conduct mechanistic interpretability experiments to validate their core hypothesis:
- **Linear Probing:** Linear classifiers are trained to decode ground-truth abstract subgoals from the residual stream activations ($e_{l,t}$) at different layers ($l$) and timesteps ($t$). This demonstrates that the pretrained models *learn to represent* temporally-abstract subgoals despite only being trained on next-token prediction.
- **Causal Model Intervention (Linear Controllers):** A low-rank linear residual stream controller ($U_t$) is introduced to additively modify activations ($e_{t,l} \leftarrow e_{t,l} + U_t e_{t,l}$) at a given depth. Controllers are specific to subgoals and manually activated with ground-truth labels. These are trained to maximize action log-likelihood.
- **Key Finding:** These simple controllers, particularly when inserted at *mid-depth* layers of the base model, enable the model to achieve compositional generalization on novel, longer subgoal sequences not seen during training, without further base model retraining.

#### 3.3. Unsupervised Metacontroller for Abstract Action Discovery

To move beyond ground-truth labels, a **metacontroller** ($c_\phi$) is introduced and trained in a self-supervised manner, with the *pretrained base model ($f_\theta$) kept frozen*.
- **Architecture:** The metacontroller is a recurrent hypernetwork with an encoder-decoder structure:
 - **Controller Encoder:** A recurrent neural network (GRU-based) that generates parameters ($\mu_t, \Sigma_t$) for a Gaussian distribution over candidate controller codes ($\tilde{z}_t$). Crucially, this encoder is **non-causal**, receiving an embedding ($s(e_{1:T})$) of the *entire sequence* of latent activities (from an internal sequence embedder $f_{emb}$) to approximate the true posterior over latent actions.
 - **Recurrent Switching Unit:** A causal component ($f_{switch}$) that outputs a continuous switching gate $\beta_t \in$. This gate interpolates between the previous controller code ($z_{t-1}$) and the new candidate ($\tilde{z}_t$) to form the current controller code ($z_t = \beta_t \odot \tilde{z}_t + (1 - \beta_t) \odot z_{t-1}$). This mechanism is central to learning temporal abstraction.
 - **Controller Decoder:** A feedforward network ($f_{hyp}$) that maps the temporally-integrated controller code ($z_t$) to the parameters of the linear internal controller ($U_t$) that modifies the base model's residual stream.
- **Training Objective:** The metacontroller parameters ($\phi$) are trained by minimizing a self-supervised variational inference objective (an ELBO), comprising the next-action prediction loss (using the controlled base model) and a KL divergence regularizer ($D_{KL}(N(\mu_t, \Sigma_t) || N(0,I))$). This encourages sampling meaningful abstract actions from a standard normal prior at test time.
- **Dataset:** Trained on a "cleaner" version of the behavioral dataset ($D^*$) to facilitate abstract action discovery.

#### 3.4. Internal Reinforcement Learning (Internal RL) Paradigm

After the metacontroller has learned to discover and sequence abstract actions, the system transitions to an RL phase:
- **Conceptual Shift:** The autoregressive model, along with the *causal parts* of the metacontroller, are "subsumed into the environment."
- **Abstract Action Policy:** A causal abstract action policy $\pi(z_t | e_{1:t})$ is learned via RL. Its actions are the controller codes ($z_t$), and its observations are the base model's residual stream activations ($e_t$).
- **Binarized Switching:** For RL, the continuous switching gate $\beta_t$ is binarized using a Heaviside step function ($H(\beta_t - \beta_{threshold})$). This ensures that once an abstract action (controller) is selected, it persists for multiple low-level timesteps until a new switch signal is emitted, allowing the policy to operate on a truly abstract timescale.
- **RL Algorithm:** A modified version of GRPO (Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO) tailored for sparse, single-final-reward settings, using empirical advantage estimation (critic-free).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several compelling findings that validate its proposed approach:

- **Emergence of Linearly Controllable Abstract Action Representations:** Pretrained autoregressive models (Transformers, SSMs) implicitly learn representations of temporally-abstract subgoals within their residual streams. These representations are linearly decodable, with accuracy increasing with layer depth, and can be causally manipulated by simple linear controllers. This confirms the initial hypothesis that foundation models contain exploitable latent structures for planning.
- **Compositional Generalization with Internal Controllers:** By inserting linear controllers at mid-depth layers of the pretrained base model and activating them in sequence (even with ground-truth labels), the system successfully solves novel, complex tasks requiring compositional generalization (unseen combinations and longer sequences of subgoals). This demonstrates the practical utility of these emergent representations.
- **Unsupervised Discovery of Temporally-Abstract Actions:** The self-supervised metacontroller, when trained on a *frozen* pretrained base model, successfully discovers meaningful temporally-abstract actions. Its switching unit ($\beta_t$) learns to operate in a "quasi-binary, sparsely-switching fashion," with switches perfectly coinciding with ground-truth changes in abstract actions/subgoals. This is a significant achievement as it requires no explicit supervision for abstraction.
- **Criticality of Freezing the Base Model:** A rate-distortion analysis reveals that keeping the pretrained base model *frozen* during metacontroller training is crucial for the discovery of subgoal-aligned switching patterns. Co-training the metacontroller and base model leads to degenerate solutions where this structure is lost. This highlights the importance of the two-stage training process: pretraining provides robust, abstract representations that are then "steered" by the metacontroller.
- **"Internal RL" Dramatically Outperforms Baselines:** The core result is the superior performance of "internal RL" on sparse-reward, hierarchically-structured tasks (grid world and MuJoCo Ant). It achieves high initial success rates and efficient credit assignment, solving tasks where all baselines fail to learn within a million episodes.
 - **Baselines that Failed:**
 - Standard token-level RL finetuning (GRPO adapted for pretrained LLMs).
 - An ablation of the metacontroller without the temporal integration unit (forced switching at every timestep).
 - A metacontroller that was co-trained with the base model (not frozen).
 - CompILE, a prior hierarchical RL method relying on variational inference from unlabeled demonstrations.
- **Efficiency of Internal RL:** The success of internal RL is attributed to its ability to act on an abstract timescale, drastically reducing the effective time horizon for policies and simplifying credit assignment compared to raw action space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in reinforcement learning, particularly at the intersection with large-scale foundation models. Its potential impact spans several domains:

- **Breakthrough in Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning:** The paper offers a compelling solution to the "longstanding challenge" of discovering and leveraging temporally-abstract actions in HRL. By showing that these abstractions can emerge naturally in pretrained autoregressive models and then be efficiently exploited, it provides a powerful new paradigm for tackling complex, sparse-reward tasks that have historically been intractable for standard RL.
- **Unlocking Foundation Models for Advanced Control:** This work demonstrates a concrete pathway to leverage the rich, latent representations learned during the self-supervised pretraining of foundation models (like LLMs) for general-purpose, efficient RL. It moves beyond simply finetuning for token generation to actually *controlling* the internal "thought processes" or "plans" embedded within these models. This could enable LLMs to engage in more sophisticated reasoning, planning, and goal-directed behavior in interactive environments.
- **Novel Paradigm for Model Interpretability and Control:** "Internal RL" introduces a new approach to model steering and interpretability. Instead of merely probing for existing representations, the metacontroller is "predictive and interventive," directly influencing the model's behavior via latent actions. The unsupervised discovery of discrete, meaningful abstract actions also offers a path towards more human-interpretable control mechanisms for complex AI systems, distinct from existing methods like sparse autoencoders (SAEs).
- **Empirical Validation of Theoretical AI Frameworks:** The findings provide strong empirical support for foundational theories in AI, such as Schmidhuber's wake-sleep hypothesis for intelligence acquisition and LeCun's JEPA concept of a "configurator module." This demonstrates how abstract thinking and planning can arise from the interaction of self-supervised learning and reinforcement.
- **Addressing Limitations of Autoregressive Models:** By enabling exploration at a higher level of temporal abstraction, the work counters the "stochastic parrot" critique of autoregressive models, demonstrating their capacity to form consistent temporal abstractions and robust plans, even in continuous control environments with hidden discrete structures.
- **Future Avenues for Research:** The success of internal RL on controlled environments opens up exciting avenues for future work, including scaling the approach to larger-scale models and more complex real-world tasks, particularly in LLM reasoning. Investigating how additional recurrence within the metacontroller could further enhance its capabilities for dynamic time compression in reasoning problems is also a promising direction.

In summary, "internal RL" is a pivotal contribution that bridges the gap between the representational power of large pretrained models and the goal-directed learning capabilities of hierarchical reinforcement learning. It proposes a robust, efficient, and potentially scalable method for developing more intelligent and autonomous AI agents capable of navigating complex, open-ended search spaces.
