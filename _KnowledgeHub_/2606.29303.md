# Occlusion-Robust Multi-Object Decoupling for Physics-Based Robotic Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> MF-MOD reconstructs multiple entangled real-world objects into complete, simulation-ready 3D Gaussians without manual masks. It treats occlusion-induced object coupling as sparse-view reconstruction: SAM2-trained segmentation fields give coarse instance splits, then joint 2D and 3D-aware Score Distillation Sampling plus intra/inter-object similarity priors fill missing geometry and texture, enabling Material Point Method simulation of robot-object collisions.

## Problem

- Physics-Gaussian simulation methods handle only a single isolated object in simple scenes, assuming clean separation from background that rarely holds in real captures.
- Amodal completion and object-scene decoupling work recovers occluded objects but cannot simultaneously separate multiple entangled instances and reintegrate them for simulation.
- Mask-based inpainting pipelines leave holes, jagged edges, and geometric inconsistencies after separation, producing artifacts that break physically plausible multi-object interaction.

## Method

- **MF-MOD**'s scene stage reconstructs with ==planar-based Gaussian splatting==, augmenting each Gaussian with a 32-dimensional ==segmentation affinity feature== decoded by an MLP into per-point instance labels.
- Joint ==SDS== objective adds a ==2D diffusion prior== for texture fidelity and a reference-view-conditioned ==3D-aware SDS== for cross-view consistency, weighted at 1e-4 alongside the RGB loss.
- ==Similarity-based geometry reasoning== fills missing regions by cosine-similarity-weighted aggregation of Gaussian particles transferred across intra-object regions and inter-object instances of similar category.

## Results

- Achieves **17.83** PSNR on rendered dynamic scenes versus **14.72** for Physics3D, **13.89** PhysDreamer, and **13.86** PhysGaussian, with comparable **0.59** SSIM.
- Ablations show removing similarity-based geometry reasoning collapses PSNR to **10.21**; dropping 2D-SDS gives **14.28** and dropping the 3D-aware loss gives **16.57**.
- Segmentation backbone barely matters: SAM yields **17.63** PSNR and **0.52** SSIM versus SAM2's **17.83** and **0.59**, and Franka arm collisions render plausibly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing occlusion-driven object entanglement as sparse-view reconstruction lets generative 3D priors replace explicit masks for separating and completing contacting objects.
> - Geometric regularization from repeated structures dominates diffusion priors here, so exploiting object regularity beats scaling generative supervision for simulation-ready assets.
> - Sequential per-object decoupling scales poorly with object count, and relighting after separation is unhandled, limiting large cluttered scenes and lighting-varying captures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of interactive 3D reconstruction and physical simulation, an area critical for applications such as industrial digital twins, robotic manipulation, virtual reality, and world modeling. Existing research has made strides in integrating physics-based simulation with 3D scene representations, notably using 3D Gaussian Splatting as simulation particles (e.g., PhysGaussian). Other methods leverage multimodal large language models and video generation models to infer material properties (e.g., PhysDreamer, DreamPhysics, PhysFlow) or enhance scene understanding for language-guided interactions (e.g., Feature Splatting).

However, a significant limitation across many of these approaches is their primary focus on simulating single objects within simplified scenes. These methods often assume that the target object is already completely isolated from its background, which is frequently not the case in complex, real-world environments. The challenge intensifies when dealing with multi-object scenes characterized by occlusions and limited viewpoint coverage, which impede the disentanglement and complete reconstruction of individual objects.

In parallel, amodal completion techniques (e.g., O2Recon, Amodal3R) aim to reconstruct occluded objects. DecoupledGaussian attempts to separate foreground objects from the background for physical simulation. Despite these efforts, a comprehensive solution capable of simultaneously achieving multi-object separation and amodal completion, while ensuring the lossless recovery of both geometry and texture required for accurate physics-based multi-object interaction, has remained elusive. Prior methods often result in fragmented objects with holes or geometric inconsistencies, which are unsuitable for realistic physical simulations.

This research addresses this gap by proposing a method for lossless multi-object 3D reconstruction from sparse and occluded real-world views. It specifically aims to provide complete, simulation-ready 3D objects that can interact accurately and coherently without artifacts, thus bridging the gap between scene reconstruction, multi-object decoupling, and physically plausible robotic interaction. The key contribution is its mask-free approach to recovering complete individual object geometries and textures, which is essential for accurate physics simulation in multi-object environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a mask-free method for lossless multi-object 3D reconstruction from sparse and occluded real-world views. This reconstruction is specifically designed to enable physically plausible robotic interaction through Material Point Method (MPM) simulation.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges and limitations in current interactive 3D reconstruction and physical simulation:

- **Multi-Object Coupling and Occlusion:** In real-world scenarios, objects frequently interact, occlude each other, and are observed from limited viewpoints. This coupling hinders the non-destructive disentanglement and complete reconstruction of individual objects, leading to fragmented geometries and missing textures.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Methods for Multi-Object Physics:** While methods like PhysGaussian enable physics-based simulation using 3D Gaussian Splatting, they typically operate on single, pre-isolated objects. Amodal completion techniques exist to recover occluded objects, but they generally do not achieve the "lossless separation" required for accurate multi-object physics simulation—meaning individual objects are not fully recovered in both geometry and texture. This can lead to simulation artifacts such as holes, missing parts, or geometric inconsistencies when multiple objects are intended to interact.
- **Need for Simulation-Ready Objects:** For realistic dynamic interactions, especially in robotic manipulation, each separated object must be geometrically complete and texturally accurate. Incomplete or inconsistent reconstructions prevent accurate collision detection, deformation, and material response during simulation.
- **Manual Masking Overhead:** Many reconstruction and segmentation approaches rely on explicit 2D or 3D masks, which can be labor-intensive and impractical for complex, dynamic, or numerous multi-object scenes. A mask-free approach significantly improves practicality and scalability.
- **Enabling Advanced Robotic Interaction:** The ultimate goal is to facilitate more realistic and robust robotic interaction. By providing complete and physically consistent 3D representations of individual objects, the proposed method aims to enhance the capabilities of robots in understanding and manipulating complex environments.

To address these motivations, the paper reformulates multi-object decoupling as a sparse-view reconstruction problem, aiming to recover fragmented geometries by integrating advanced diffusion priors and geometry-aware regularization, thereby ensuring both texture fidelity and 3D consistency crucial for physically accurate simulations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Mask-Free Multi-Object Decoupling (MF-MOD) method operates through a three-stage pipeline: Scene Reconstruction and Segmentation, Lossless Multi-Object Decoupling, and Multi-Object Physical Interaction.

**A. Overview of Pipeline**
1. **Scene Reconstruction and Segmentation:**
 - Input: Multi-view images and their corresponding segmentation maps generated by SAM 2.
 - Process: PGSR (Planar-based Gaussian Splatting for Reconstruction) is used to reconstruct the 3D scene, represented by 3D Gaussian Splatting.
 - Segmentation: Each Gaussian point is augmented with a 32-dimensional segmentation affinity feature. A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) is trained to predict the segmentation label for each Gaussian point based on this feature, facilitating subsequent object separation.
2. **Lossless Multi-Object Decoupling:**
 - Initial State: The reconstructed scene is decomposed into multiple foreground objects and the background based on the learned segmentation affinity field. These separated components may contain holes or missing regions due to occlusion and sparse views.
 - Recovery: A joint Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) process is applied, combined with reasoning-based geometry regularization, to recover the missing geometry and texture of each object. This ensures geometric completeness and consistent internal infilling.
3. **Multi-Object Physical Interaction:**
 - Integration: The restored and completed objects are reintegrated into the scene.
 - Simulation: Dynamic robotic interactions are simulated using a modified Material Point Method (MPM). To handle diverse material properties, the standard MPM is extended, allowing each object to be treated as a distinct part within a unified simulation entity. Simulation particles associated with each part can be assigned unique material parameters, enabling realistic multi-material interactions.

**B. Joint 2D-3D Priors for Multi-Object Decoupling**
The core of lossless decoupling lies in recovering occluded or unobserved regions. This is framed as a sparse-view reconstruction problem and addressed using a unified Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework, integrating two complementary diffusion priors:

1. **2D Diffusion Prior (L_2D-SDS):**
 - Mechanism: Standard Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), inspired by DreamFusion, distills knowledge from a pre-trained 2D diffusion model into 3D scene generation.
 - Function: It optimizes 3D Gaussian Splatting parameters by leveraging the gradient of a pseudo-loss computed from the 2D diffusion model's noise prediction on rendered images. This primarily ensures high-fidelity texture reconstruction and infilling based on semantic cues from the diffusion model.
 - Limitation: Without explicit 3D supervision, 2D SDS alone does not guarantee 3D consistency across different viewpoints.

2. **3D-aware Diffusion Prior (L_3D-SDS):**
 - Mechanism: A modification of the SDS loss, inspired by, which aligns the gradient of a rendered view not just with textual guidance, but with the rendering from a designated *reference view*.
 - Function: This approach implicitly models 3D structure and ensures visual consistency across multiple viewpoints. By utilizing a complete reference view as a geometric and textural prior, it guides the recovery of missing regions in other views, leading to more coherent and complete 3D geometry and texture.
 - Implementation: Pre-trained model weights from (Zero-1-to-3) are adopted, which were trained on a large dataset of 3D objects from single images.

3. **Total Reconstruction Loss:** The overall reconstruction loss combines a standard RGB color loss (L_color) from the 3D Gaussian Splatting training process with both 2D-SDS and 3D-SDS losses, weighted by λ1 and λ2 (set to 1e-4).
 L_all = L_color + λ_1 L_2D-SDS + λ_2 L_3D-SDS

**C. Similarity-Based Geometry Reasoning**
To address geometric and appearance coupling further, particularly between neighboring or contacting objects, a similarity-based geometry reasoning strategy is introduced for regularization. This leverages two structural priors:

1. **Intra-object Self-similarity:** Captures the repeatability and consistency of local geometric structures within the same object across different viewpoints (e.g., a chair's legs).
2. **Inter-object Mutual Similarity:** Identifies commonalities in shape, material, and semantic context among multiple instances of similar object categories (e.g., two similar cups).

- **Mechanism:** Given incomplete observations of N objects {X^obs_i}, a completion function F_recover aims to recover the full geometry and texture X^full_i. This function utilizes semantic and structural priors (Φ_sim) from a multimodal foundation model and textual descriptions (L) to guide similarity matching.
- **Feature Alignment:** Inter-object mutual similarity (S_ij) is quantified via cross-object feature alignment using a learnable geometric encoder E(·) and a similarity metric (e.g., cosine similarity).
- **Reconstruction:** Missing regions (X^missing_i) are reconstructed through weighted Gaussian particle aggregation from reference regions (X^obs_j). The weights (w_ij) are determined by the similarity (S_ij), and T_j→i represents a geometric alignment transformation (pose, scale, semantic context) from object j to object i. This concept is generalized to intra-object self-similarity by considering distinct regions within the same object.

This strategy allows the model to infer missing geometry in a physically plausible manner without explicit annotations, effectively disentangling appearance-geometry coupling and preserving individual object integrity for downstream physics simulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of MF-MOD across various aspects, including visual quality, physical plausibility of dynamics, multi-object decoupling, and robotic interaction.

**A. Experimental Settings:**
- **Datasets:** Four real-world static scenes from PhysDreamer (carnation, alocasia plant, coiled telephone cord, beanie hat), a soccer ball from Feature Splatting, and a self-captured tissue box scene were used.
- **Metrics:** For real-world scenes without ground-truth material properties, visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, MS-SSIM) of rendered videos were used as proxies for performance evaluation.

**B. Comparison with the State of the Art:**
- **Dynamic Motion Effects:** Comparisons against PhysDreamer, PhysGaussian, and DG4D (Fig. 2 and 3) showed that MF-MOD produces dynamic interactions consistent with real-world physics. Competing approaches exhibited limitations, such as DG4D being restricted to periodic motions, and PhysGaussian generating unnatural or physically implausible deformations. Time-slice visualizations confirmed the temporal consistency and physical plausibility of the generated dynamics by MF-MOD.
- **Quantitative Visual Quality:** In dynamic scene synthesis, MF-MOD achieved competitive visual fidelity (PSNR 17.83, SSIM 0.59, MS-SSIM 0.45) compared to PhysDreamer (PSNR 13.89), PhysGaussian (PSNR 13.86), and Physics3D (PSNR 14.72) (Table I). This indicates that the method maintains high visual quality while preserving accurate physical dynamics.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
- **Module Contribution:** Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of each module (Table II).
 - **w/o SBGR (Similarity-Based Geometry Reasoning):** Removing this module led to the most significant performance degradation across all metrics (PSNR 10.21, SSIM 0.32, MS-SSIM 0.30), indicating that geometry was distorted, underscoring its crucial role in geometric accuracy and infilling.
 - **w/o 3D-aware Loss:** Performance dropped (PSNR 16.57, SSIM 0.47, MS-SSIM 0.41), confirming the importance of 3D consistency.
 - **w/o 2D-SDS:** Also showed a notable degradation (PSNR 14.28, SSIM 0.39, MS-SSIM 0.31), highlighting the necessity of the 2D diffusion prior for texture fidelity.
- **Segmentation Model Choice:** Comparing SAM and SAM2 for initial segmentation, SAM2 offered slight improvements (PSNR 17.83 vs 17.63 for SAM), but the difference was minor. This suggests the method's robustness and insensitivity to the specific version of the segmentation model.

**D. Evaluation of Decoupling and Robotic Interaction:**
- **Individual Object Recovery:**
 - **Soccer Ball (Fig. 4):** MF-MOD successfully inferred the geometry and texture of occluded regions (e.g., filling holes and smoothing jagged artifacts on the back side of the ball).
 - **Tissue Box (Fig. 5a):** Compared to DecoupledGaussian, MF-MOD demonstrated higher geometric accuracy and substantially improved texture quality in completing a more complex everyday object.
- **Full Scene Recovery:** When a foreground object (alocasia plant) was removed, MF-MOD successfully reconstructed the background (Fig. 5b), demonstrating its ability to maintain scene consistency even in the absence of foreground elements.
- **Robotic Interaction:** The method's efficacy was demonstrated in an industrial robotic interaction scenario (Fig. 6). A Franka robotic arm interacted with 3D objects (extracted and completed from a toy dozer scene). The resulting precise collision interactions confirmed that the reconstructed 3D objects are practically valuable for industrial robotic manipulation tasks, enabling realistic and accurate physical responses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Mask-Free Multi-Object Decoupling (MF-MOD) method represents a notable advancement in interactive 3D reconstruction and physical simulation, with significant implications across several domains.

- **Addressing a Critical Research Gap:** The work effectively tackles the challenge of lossless multi-object separation and amodal completion in complex, occluded scenes, a limitation that has constrained the utility of prior physics-based simulation methods to single objects or simplified environments. By reformulating multi-object decoupling as a sparse-view reconstruction problem and providing a mask-free solution, it removes a substantial practical hurdle.
- **Enabling Realistic Multi-Object Robotic Interaction:** The most direct impact is on robotic manipulation. By producing complete, geometrically accurate, and texturally rich 3D objects, MF-MOD facilitates physically plausible robotic interactions via MPM simulation. This allows robots to engage with multiple objects in complex scenes, perform precise collision detections, and simulate deformations or material properties with greater fidelity. Such capabilities are crucial for developing more robust and autonomous robotic systems in industrial settings and beyond.
- **Creation of "Simulation-Ready" 3D Assets:** The method generates 3D objects that are ready for simulation without requiring extensive manual cleanup or mask-based reconstruction. This streamlined process reduces the overhead associated with creating accurate physical models, thereby accelerating the development and testing of robotic applications and virtual environments.
- **Advancements in 3D Reconstruction with Diffusion Models:** The integration of joint 2D and 3D diffusion priors within a Score Distillation Sampling framework for geometry recovery and texture inpainting demonstrates a sophisticated approach to leveraging generative models for challenging 3D reconstruction tasks. The use of similarity-based geometry reasoning further enhances the plausibility of inferred missing regions, pushing the boundaries of what can be reconstructed from sparse and occluded views.
- **Broadening Applications:** Beyond robotics, the ability to reconstruct and simulate multi-object scenes with high fidelity has potential implications for industrial digital twins, allowing for virtual testing and optimization of manufacturing processes. It can also enhance virtual reality experiences by populating virtual worlds with interactable, physically consistent objects, and contribute to the development of more comprehensive and dynamic world models in AI research.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as sequential processing overhead for many objects and issues with lighting variations post-separation, the core contributions lay a foundation for more complex and realistic interactive simulations, offering substantial practical value for various scientific and engineering applications.
