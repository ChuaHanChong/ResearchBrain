# PromptCap: Prompt-Guided Task-Aware Image Captioning

> [!summary] Summary
> PROMPTCAP introduces an image captioning model that generates question-aware captions, tailoring visual descriptions for black-box Large Language Models. This approach achieved 60.4% accuracy on OK-VQA and 73.1% on A-OKVQA, outperforming prior methods by providing task-relevant visual context.

## Problem

- Black-box Large Language Models (LLMs) require visual information to be converted into text, but generic image captions often lack task-specific details.
- Generic image captions, used as context for LLMs in Visual Question Answering (VQA), often omit crucial visual information needed to answer specific questions.
- Developing datasets of task-aware captions typically requires costly and time-consuming human annotation.

## Method

- Introduces PROMPTCAP, an image captioning model based on a pre-trained ==OFA encoder-decoder Transformer==, which generates captions conditioned on both an image and a ==natural language prompt (question)==.
- Develops an innovative ==data synthesis pipeline== leveraging GPT-3 with few-shot in-context learning to create question-aware caption training data from existing VQA datasets.
- Implements a ==two-stage filtering process== for synthesized captions, using VQA accuracy and CIDEr scores to ensure quality.

## Results

- PROMPTCAP + GPT-3 achieved **60.4%** accuracy on OK-VQA and **73.1%** on A-OKVQA (multiple-choice), outperforming prior methods on knowledge-based VQA benchmarks.
- Ablation studies showed PROMPTCAP provided an absolute accuracy gain of **3.8%** on OK-VQA, **5.3%** on A-OKVQA, and **9.2%** on VQAv2 compared to generic captioning.
- The system demonstrated strong generalization, outperforming supervised baselines on the WebQA benchmark without specific training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing Large Language Models with prompt-guided, task-aware image captions substantially improves their performance on knowledge-based Visual Question Answering tasks.
> - High-quality training data for specific visual-linguistic tasks can be synthesized cost-effectively using powerful black-box LLMs and few-shot in-context learning.
> - The selection of in-context examples using similarity metrics is crucial for maximizing the performance of black-box LLMs in multimodal inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the critical intersection of computer vision and natural language processing, specifically contributing to the field of **multimodal AI**, with a strong focus on **Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering (VQA)**.

Traditionally, VQA tasks involve answering questions about an image based solely on its visual content. However, **knowledge-based VQA** (e.g., OK-VQA, A-OKVQA) extends this by requiring external, real-world knowledge and commonsense reasoning beyond what is directly visible in the image. Early approaches to knowledge-based VQA involved complex systems that retrieved external knowledge from structured sources like knowledge graphs (ConceptNet, Wikidata) or unstructured texts (Wikipedia, web search) and then integrated this knowledge with visual features to answer questions.

The advent of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** like GPT-3 has significantly reshaped the landscape of knowledge-based VQA. These models, with their vast pre-training on diverse text corpora, exhibit remarkable capabilities in knowledge retrieval and reasoning. Recent state-of-the-art methods (e.g., PICa, KAT, REVIVE) leverage LLMs as implicit knowledge bases, achieving strong performance by treating VQA as a text-to-text task.

A key challenge in using black-box LLMs (those accessible only via APIs without internal parameter access for fine-tuning) for visual tasks is bridging the modality gap: LMs understand text, not pixels. The common solution has been to convert images into generic text descriptions (e.g., image captions or tags) using a separate image captioning model. The LLM then uses this generic caption as context to answer questions.

PROMPTCAP directly addresses a critical limitation of this established pipeline: **generic image captions often miss crucial visual details relevant to a specific question.** For instance, a generic caption might describe "a car," but if the question is "What brand of car is this?", the caption might not include "Subaru." This leads to a bottleneck where the LLM, despite its reasoning capabilities, is deprived of the necessary visual information.

PROMPTCAP's contribution is a novel solution to this bottleneck. Instead of a generic caption, it proposes a **prompt-guided, task-aware captioning model**. This means the caption generated is not merely a summary of the image but is *tailored* to the specific question asked, ensuring that task-relevant visual details are explicitly included. This work moves beyond simply integrating LMs with vision models by focusing on a more intelligent and flexible way of *representing* visual information for textual processing by LMs. It pioneers a method of synthesizing training data for such a model, avoiding costly human annotation for a novel type of visual-linguistic task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of PROMPTCAP is to develop an **image captioning model that can generate "question-aware" or "prompt-guided" captions**, acting as a superior interface between visual inputs and black-box Large Language Models (LLMs) for tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA).

The core motivations driving this objective are:

- **Bridging the Modality Gap for Black-Box LLMs:** Large, powerful LLMs like GPT-3 are often only accessible via APIs, preventing direct fine-tuning on multimodal datasets or access to their internal representations. To leverage their strong knowledge retrieval and reasoning capabilities for VQA, images must be converted into text.
- **Limitations of Generic Image Captions:** Prior work has used generic image captioning models to summarize images into text for LLMs. However, generic captions are inherently limited. They often fail to capture specific visual details that might be critical for answering a particular question, especially in knowledge-based VQA where nuanced visual cues can unlock external knowledge. For example, a generic caption might say "a street with traffic lights," but miss "McDonald's" on a sign, preventing the LLM from answering "What kind of food does the restaurant on the sign serve?"
- **Enhancing LLM Performance on VQA:** By providing captions that are explicitly conditioned on the user's question, PROMPTCAP aims to equip the LLM with the most relevant visual information, thereby improving its accuracy and reasoning ability on VQA tasks, particularly those requiring external knowledge.
- **Avoiding Costly Human Annotation for New Caption Types:** Creating a dataset of "question-aware" captions from scratch would be immensely expensive and time-consuming. A key motivation was to devise an efficient, scalable method to synthesize such training data without requiring new manual annotations.

In essence, PROMPTCAP seeks to intelligently "focus" the visual description provided to an LLM, ensuring that the critical information needed for a specific task (like answering a question) is highlighted, thereby making the black-box LLM a more effective visual reasoner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PROMPTCAP's methodology involves a novel approach to prompt-guided image captioning, coupled with an innovative training data synthesis pipeline to circumvent the need for new annotations.

#### 3.1. PROMPTCAP Model Architecture and Functionality

PROMPTCAP is an image captioning model designed to take two inputs: an image (I) and a natural language prompt (P). The model's objective is to generate a caption (C) that is not only descriptive of the image but also *customized* to the instructions or question embedded within the prompt. This prompt-guided caption should:
1. Cover visual details relevant to the prompt's instruction (e.g., question).
2. Describe main objects, similar to generic captions.
3. Optionally, use auxiliary information from the prompt if necessary (e.g., to infer details).

The model adopts an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture, specifically starting from the pre-trained **OFA (One-For-All)** model. OFA is a unified multimodal pre-trained model that handles various vision-language tasks within a sequence-to-sequence framework. For PROMPTCAP, the image is transformed into image patches, and the prompt text is encoded using byte-pair encoding (BPE). These textual and visual tokens are concatenated and fed into the encoder, and the decoder is trained to generate the prompt-guided caption. The training uses a negative log-likelihood loss.

#### 3.2. Training Data Synthesis (Key Innovation)

Since no existing datasets provide question-aware captions, the authors propose a pipeline to *synthesize* such training data using a powerful black-box LLM, **GPT-3**, via few-shot in-context learning. This is a crucial aspect of the methodology.

- **Training Example Generation with GPT-3:**
 - The process leverages existing VQA datasets (e.g., VQAv2) which provide image-question-answer triples.
 - For each VQA sample, the corresponding generic human-written COCO captions (typically 5 per image) are used as "Original Context."
 - GPT-3 is prompted with a task instruction ("Summarize the context to help answer the question") and **20 human-written in-context examples**. These examples demonstrate how a generic caption, a question, and an answer can be "rewritten" into a concise, question-aware caption that facilitates answering.
 - Given the "Original Context" (COCO captions), the question, and the ground-truth answer, GPT-3 generates a "Synthesized question-aware caption." The images themselves are *not* directly fed to GPT-3 during this synthesis; only their textual descriptions (COCO captions) are used.
 - This leverages GPT-3's strong few-shot learning and commonsense reasoning capabilities to generalize from the provided examples and create new question-aware captions.

- **Training Example Filtering:**
 - To ensure the quality of the synthesized captions, 5 candidate captions are generated by GPT-3 for each VQA sample.
 - A two-stage filtering process selects the best candidate:
 - **Soft VQA Accuracy:** For each candidate caption, the authors use a text-only VQA system (another GPT-3 instance in an in-context learning setup, similar to the inference pipeline) to predict an answer to the original question. This predicted answer is then compared to the ground-truth answers using a "soft VQA accuracy" metric. This metric uses Character Error Rate (CER) to allow for slight surface form variations (e.g., "coins" vs. "coin"), scoring based on the three lowest-CER ground-truth answers. Candidates with higher soft VQA accuracy are preferred.
 - **CIDEr Score (Tie-breaker):** If multiple candidates achieve the same highest soft VQA accuracy, the CIDEr score between the candidate caption and the original COCO ground-truth captions is used as a tie-breaker. The candidate with the highest CIDEr (indicating closer resemblance to generic captions while still being task-aware) is selected.

#### 3.3. VQA Inference Pipeline

The ultimate goal is to use PROMPTCAP with a black-box LLM (GPT-3) for VQA. This involves two steps:

1. **Image-to-Text Conversion (using PROMPTCAP):** Given a test image and its corresponding question, PROMPTCAP generates a question-aware caption. This converts the multimodal VQA sample into a purely textual question-answering sample (context: PROMPTCAP caption; question: original question).
2. **GPT-3 In-Context Learning for VQA:** The converted textual VQA sample is then fed into GPT-3. This is done in an in-context learning setting, where GPT-3 is prompted with several "demonstration" examples (context-question-answer triples) generated similarly, followed by the test instance. The demonstrations are either randomly selected or, for better performance, retrieved based on CLIP embeddings (summing cosine similarities of question and image embeddings) to find the most visually and textually similar examples from the training set. GPT-3 then generates the answer based on these demonstrations and the test context.

This entire methodology intelligently leverages the strengths of different models: a visual encoder-decoder (OFA-based PROMPTCAP) for image understanding and caption generation, and a powerful LLM (GPT-3) for knowledge retrieval, reasoning, and text generation, all while minimizing manual annotation efforts for the novel prompt-guided captioning task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate PROMPTCAP's effectiveness in enhancing the performance of black-box LLMs on knowledge-based VQA tasks and its generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on Knowledge-Based VQA

- **OK-VQA:** PROMPTCAP + GPT-3 achieved an accuracy of **60.4%** on the OK-VQA validation set. This significantly outperforms all existing methods, including those that use additional knowledge sources, ensemble multiple components, or require end-to-end fine-tuning. For instance, it surpasses REVIVE (58.0%), KAT (54.4%), and Flamingo (57.8% with 32-shot learning). This is a substantial gain, especially considering PROMPTCAP uses no additional knowledge base beyond GPT-3 and no ensemble.
- **A-OKVQA:** Similar impressive results were observed on A-OKVQA, with PROMPTCAP + GPT-3 achieving **73.1%** accuracy on multiple-choice evaluation and **59.6%** on direct-answer evaluation. These scores are significantly higher than previous state-of-the-art methods like GPV-2 (60.3% multi-choice, 48.6% direct-answer).

These results confirm PROMPTCAP's effectiveness as a "visual front-end" for LMs, enabling them to leverage their inherent knowledge and reasoning abilities more effectively by providing task-relevant visual context.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Extensive ablation studies were conducted to quantify the contribution of PROMPTCAP and the underlying language model (GPT-3).

- **Performance Benefit from PROMPTCAP:**
 - Compared to a generic captioning model (OFA-Cap, sharing the same architecture and trained on similar data), PROMPTCAP consistently improved VQA performance across datasets.
 - It provided a **3.8%** absolute accuracy gain on OK-VQA, **5.3%** on A-OKVQA, and a notable **9.2%** on VQAv2 (a traditional VQA dataset focusing on visual information). This confirms that conditioning captions on the question significantly enhances downstream VQA performance, even for non-knowledge-based tasks.

- **Performance Benefit from Language Model (GPT-3 vs. Flan-T5-XXL):**
 - GPT-3 yielded substantial gains on knowledge-based VQA tasks compared to Flan-T5-XXL (11B parameters).
 - GPT-3 improved OK-VQA by **18.4%** and A-OKVQA by **14.8%** over Flan-T5-XXL. This highlights GPT-3's superior knowledge retrieval and reasoning capabilities, which are crucial for these tasks.
 - Interestingly, on VQAv2, GPT-3 only offered a **3.2%** accuracy gain over Flan-T5-XXL. This suggests that for tasks primarily relying on visual content *within* the image, the larger LM's external knowledge is less critical, and the quality of the image-to-text conversion becomes paramount.

- **Ablation on GPT-3 Prompting (In-Context Examples):**
 - The study confirmed that using more in-context examples (demonstrations) generally leads to better performance for GPT-3.
 - Retrieving the *most similar* in-context examples using CLIP embeddings significantly outperforms using random examples (e.g., **5.2%** absolute accuracy gain for PROMPTCAP on OK-VQA with n=32 examples). This validates the importance of example selection in few-shot prompting.

#### 4.3. Domain Transfer on WebQA

- PROMPTCAP + GPT-3 demonstrated strong generalization abilities on WebQA, a multimodal multi-hop reasoning benchmark with images and questions from different domains than the training data.
- Without any specific training on WebQA's compositional questions, the system outperformed all supervised baselines, and also outperformed the generic OFA-Cap approach, showing its ability to generalize to unseen question types and image domains.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis and Caption Comparison

- Qualitative examples (Figure 5) clearly illustrate how PROMPTCAP generates captions with question-relevant details (e.g., car brand, complementary color, specific clothing item), enabling GPT-3 to answer correctly where generic captions fail.
- Quantitative analysis of caption quality (Table 7) showed that GPT-3 synthesized question-aware captions are highly similar to generic COCO captions. PROMPTCAP generates captions that are closer to these synthesized question-aware captions (higher CIDEr and SPICE scores compared to OFA-Cap), indicating it successfully learns the task.
- Remarkably, PROMPTCAP can also generate high-quality generic captions (by prompting "what does the image describe?"), even surpassing OFA-Cap on standard COCO metrics, suggesting its broad applicability.

In summary, PROMPTCAP is highly effective in improving VQA performance by generating question-aware captions, particularly for knowledge-based tasks that benefit from LLMs' reasoning. The innovative data synthesis method is a key enabler, and the model demonstrates strong generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PROMPTCAP research presents significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact across several domains of AI:

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

- **Novel Bridging of Modalities for Black-Box LLMs:** PROMPTCAP offers a highly effective and flexible way to connect powerful, proprietary LLMs (like GPT-3) to visual data. By generating **task-aware, prompt-guided captions**, it overcomes the fundamental limitation of generic image descriptions, which often fail to convey question-specific information. This makes LLMs far more potent and practical tools for multimodal understanding.
- **Innovative Data Synthesis Pipeline:** The method of synthesizing high-quality, task-specific training data using GPT-3 via few-shot in-context learning is a groundbreaking contribution. This approach bypasses the costly and time-consuming process of manual annotation for a new type of visual-linguistic data, opening doors for creating diverse and large-scale datasets for various novel multimodal tasks. This "self-supervised" or "LLM-supervised" data generation paradigm is a significant methodological advancement.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging knowledge-based VQA benchmarks (OK-VQA and A-OKVQA) without resorting to complex ensembles or additional external knowledge bases (beyond GPT-3 itself) underscores the fundamental effectiveness of the prompt-guided captioning approach. The consistent gains over generic captions, even on traditional VQA, further validate its utility.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen domains (WebQA) suggests that PROMPTCAP learns robust representations and reasoning patterns, making it a versatile component in multimodal systems.
- **Interpretable Reasoning Pathway:** As hinted in the NLVR2 demo, the explicit textual caption generated by PROMPTCAP can offer a more interpretable pathway for the LLM's reasoning, allowing researchers and users to better understand *why* an LLM arrived at a particular answer by examining the provided visual context.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancing Multimodal AI Applications:** PROMPTCAP provides a generalizable module for feeding structured visual information into text-only LLMs. This could catalyze advancements in a wide range of multimodal applications beyond VQA, such as visual storytelling, detailed image retrieval, visually-grounded dialogue systems, and even assistive technologies for visually impaired individuals.
- **Enabling New Research Paradigms:** The data synthesis methodology could inspire similar approaches for generating training data for other complex, task-specific AI problems across various modalities where direct human annotation is prohibitive. This could accelerate research and development in areas where data scarcity is a bottleneck.
- **Democratizing Access to Powerful LLMs:** By making black-box LLMs more effective at understanding visual content, PROMPTCAP implicitly democratizes their use for multimodal tasks. Researchers and developers without the resources to fine-tune massive multimodal models can still leverage the power of off-the-shelf LLMs for visually-aware applications.
- **Foundation for Future Hybrid AI Systems:** The work demonstrates the power of combining specialized vision models (PROMPTCAP) with generalized language models (GPT-3). This "modular AI" approach, where components specialize in their respective domains and communicate effectively, represents a promising direction for building more robust, scalable, and interpretable AI systems.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors)

- **Focus on VQA:** The current PROMPTCAP implementation is primarily focused on knowledge-based VQA. Future work could extend its application to a broader array of vision-language tasks and instructions (e.g., image paragraph captioning, visual reasoning tasks like NLVR2).
- **Textual Abstraction Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that images contain rich information that cannot always be fully abstracted into text. While PROMPTCAP improves the relevance of text descriptions, it is not a complete solution for deep visual understanding. Complementary methods (e.g., direct visual feature integration, object embeddings) might still be necessary for comprehensive visual data comprehension.

Despite these limitations, PROMPTCAP represents a significant step forward in integrating powerful language models with visual understanding, offering a flexible, efficient, and highly effective framework for multimodal AI.
