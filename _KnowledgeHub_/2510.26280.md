# Thor: Towards Human-Level Whole-Body Reactions for Intense Contact-Rich Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Thor, a reinforcement learning framework, enables humanoid robots to exhibit human-level whole-body reactions in intense contact-rich environments. The framework uses a decoupled policy and a Force-Adaptive Torso-Tilt (FAT2) reward function, allowing the Unitree G1 robot to pull forces up to 48% of its body weight and successfully execute tasks like opening fire doors and pulling loaded carts.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to maintain stability and generate adaptive forces during intense, contact-rich interactions due to high dimensionality and inherent instability.
- Existing model-based and learning-based control methods are limited by accurate modeling requirements, conservative balance assumptions (CoM within support polygon), or suboptimal performance in rich force interactions.
- Many reinforcement learning approaches for humanoids fail to leverage the waist's potential for efficient force distribution and effective high-intensity force interaction.

## Method

![[2510.26280-method.png]]
*Complete system architecture showing the decoupled policy structure with three Actor-Critic networks, training process with FAT2 rewards, and deployment pipeline from simulation to real-world implementation.*

- Thor introduces a novel decoupled policy architecture that partitions control into lower body, waist, and upper body modules, inspired by human biomechanics and multi-agent RL.
- A Force-Adaptive Torso-Tilt (FAT2) reward function is implemented, encouraging human-like torso tilting beyond the conventional CoM-within-support-polygon constraint to enhance force generation.
- The framework employs two-stage curriculum learning and domain randomization, specifically for force disturbances, to enhance robustness and facilitate sim-to-real transfer on the Unitree G1 robot.

## Results

- The Unitree G1 robot achieved peak pulling forces of 167.7 N (approximately 48% of its body weight) in backward locomotion, demonstrating a 68.9% improvement over state-of-the-art baselines.
- Thor successfully performed complex real-world tasks, including opening a fire door with one hand (requiring ~60 N of force) and pulling a cart loaded with 70 kg.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the FAT2 reward function was the primary contributor to enhanced force-interaction capabilities, while the decoupled architecture significantly mitigated high-dimensionality issues and anomalous waist behaviors under high loads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling humanoid control into distinct lower body, waist, and upper body modules effectively mitigates high-dimensionality challenges and improves force transmission efficiency.
> - Human-inspired adaptive torso tilting, as encouraged by the FAT2 reward, is critical for enabling humanoids to generate and withstand greater interaction forces by dynamically adjusting their Center of Mass.
> - The robot can implicitly perceive interactive forces via proprioceptive signals, allowing for adaptive posture adjustments during deployment without needing direct force measurements.
