# OccLLaMA: An Occupancy-Language-Action Generative World Model for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> OccLLaMA unifies occupancy, language, and action into one autoregressive LLaMA-based world model for autonomous driving. A sparsity-aware VQVAE-like scene tokenizer discretizes semantic occupancy into a shared vocabulary with text and action tokens, enabling next-token/scene prediction across 4D occupancy forecasting, motion planning, and VQA with competitive results.

## Problem

- MLLM-based driving methods learn a direct perception-to-action mapping, ignoring world dynamics and the relation between actions and future scene evolution.
- Existing driving world models predict single sensor modalities like video, point clouds, or occupancy but cannot jointly forecast scenes, reason in language, and act.
- Multi-modal representations for occupancy generation struggle to align features produced across different modalities within one coherent framework.

## Method

- **Scene Tokenizer**: a sparse ==VQVAE==-like encoder that discards air voxels, uses ==pillar embeddings== plus a ==Swin-transformer==, and decouples voxel/class heads to handle occupancy sparsity and class imbalance.
- **Unified Vocabulary**: scene, language, and action tokens (via ==waypoint binning==) merged into one vocabulary with special boundary tokens, letting one ==LLaMA== model consume and emit any modality.
- **Next Token/Scene Prediction**: enhanced ==LLaMA== with ==spatial attention== and ==learnable scene queries== predicting an entire occupancy scene in one step alongside standard ==causal next-token prediction==.

## Results

- On 4D occupancy forecasting with ground-truth occupancy input, OccLLaMA reaches **19.93** mIoU and **29.17** IoU averaged over 3s, beating OccWorld's **17.14** mIoU and **26.63** IoU, especially at longer horizons.
- On motion planning with occupancy-only input, OccLLaMA achieves **1.14m** average L2 and **0.49%** average collision rate, outperforming OccWorld (**1.17m**, **0.60%**) and OccNet (**2.25m**, **0.69%**).
- On NuScenes-QA under LLaMA-2-7b, OccLLaMA attains **53.4%** overall accuracy versus LiDAR-LLM's **48.6%** and LLaVA's **47.4%**, with the largest gains on status and existence questions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic occupancy is an effective shared substrate for unifying 3D perception, language reasoning, and action within a single autoregressive vocabulary.
> - Action-specific tokenization outperforms reusing language vocabulary for trajectories, since it preserves physical priors instead of inheriting language inductive bias.
> - Pretraining for cross-modal occupancy-language-action alignment before instruction tuning substantially boosts downstream QA performance, showing world understanding transfers to reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Autonomous driving systems require a deep understanding of the world to navigate safely and efficiently. While recent advances in Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown promise in connecting perception with action, they often lack a crucial component found in human cognition: a world model that can predict how the environment evolves over time and how actions influence this evolution.

OccLLaMA addresses this gap by introducing a unified occupancy-language-action generative model for autonomous driving. Unlike previous approaches that focus solely on perception-to-action mapping, OccLLaMA integrates vision, language, and action modalities to create a comprehensive world model that can forecast scene evolution, reason about the environment using natural language, and interact with the world through planned actions.

### 2. Background and Motivation

Current approaches to autonomous driving world modeling typically fall into one of several categories:

1. **Video prediction models** that forecast future frames but struggle with 3D spatial reasoning
2. **Point cloud prediction models** that capture 3D geometry but lack semantic understanding
3. **Occupancy prediction models** that represent both 3D structure and semantics but are often limited to visual forecasting

Each of these approaches addresses only part of the problem. Video prediction lacks 3D spatial understanding, point cloud models struggle with semantics, and occupancy models typically focus solely on visual forecasting without integrating language or action.

Human drivers, in contrast, rely on a cognitive world model that allows them to predict how the environment will change, reason about these changes verbally, and plan actions accordingly. OccLLaMA aims to emulate this cognitive process by creating a unified model that integrates all three modalities—vision, language, and action—within a single framework.

### 3. System Architecture

OccLLaMA has three key components:

1. **Scene Tokenizer**: Converts 3D semantic occupancy data into discrete tokens
2. **Unified Multi-Modal Vocabulary**: Combines scene, language, and action tokens into a single vocabulary
3. **Generative World Model**: An enhanced LLM that predicts the next token across all modalities

This architecture enables OccLLaMA to handle diverse tasks such as 4D occupancy forecasting, motion planning, and visual question answering within a unified framework. By learning to predict across modalities, the model develops a comprehensive understanding of how the environment evolves and how actions impact this evolution.

### 4. The Scene Tokenizer

The scene tokenizer converts continuous 3D semantic occupancy data into discrete tokens that can be processed by the language model. This component addresses several challenges specific to occupancy data:

1. **Sparsity**: Most voxels in a scene are empty, creating an imbalance problem
2. **Class imbalance**: Some semantic classes (like pedestrians) appear less frequently than others
3. **Efficiency**: Processing full occupancy grids is computationally expensive

To address these challenges, OccLLaMA employs a VQVAE-like tokenizer with several key innovations:

- **Sparse encoding**: Inspired by point cloud processing, the encoder only processes occupied voxels
- **Decoupled occupancy and semantics**: The model separates the task of determining if a voxel is occupied from assigning its semantic class
- **Hierarchical structure**: The tokenizer processes the scene at multiple resolutions to capture both fine details and broader context

This approach results in an efficient scene representation that preserves both geometric and semantic information while being compatible with the autoregressive prediction framework of language models.

### 5. Unified Multi-Modal Vocabulary

To enable seamless integration of vision, language, and action, OccLLaMA creates a unified vocabulary containing:

1. **Scene tokens**: Discrete representations of semantic occupancy derived from the scene tokenizer
2. **Language tokens**: Standard text tokens from the LLaMA vocabulary
3. **Action tokens**: Discretized waypoints representing vehicle trajectories

The action vocabulary is created by dividing the continuous space of possible waypoints into discrete bins, allowing the model to output trajectories as sequences of tokens. This unified vocabulary enables the model to switch between modalities while maintaining the autoregressive prediction framework.

The mathematical formulation for this unified vocabulary can be represented as:

$$V = V_{scene} \cup V_{language} \cup V_{action}$$

Where $V_{scene}$ contains scene tokens, $V_{language}$ contains language tokens, and $V_{action}$ contains action tokens.

### 6. Generative World Model

OccLLaMA builds upon the LLaMA architecture but extends it to handle the multi-modal vocabulary. Key enhancements include:

1. **Spatial attention**: To capture the spatial relationships within the scene
2. **Learnable scene queries**: To predict the entire scene in one forward step, reducing inference time
3. **Mixed autoregressive generation**: To handle the different modalities in a unified framework

The model is formulated as an autoregressive prediction task:

$$p(x_1, x_2..., x_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p(x_i | x_1, x_2..., x_{i-1})$$

Where each token $x_i$ can be a scene, language, or action token. This allows the model to generate sequences that smoothly transition between different modalities.

### 7. Training Methodology

OccLLaMA employs a three-stage training approach:

1. **Scene Tokenizer Training**: Training the VQVAE-like tokenizer to effectively represent semantic occupancy as discrete tokens
2. **Occupancy-Language-Action Pre-training**: Training the LLM to predict across all three modalities, learning the dynamics of the world and how actions influence the environment
3. **Instruction Tuning**: Fine-tuning using prompt-based instructions for specific autonomous driving tasks like forecasting, planning, and question answering

This progressive training approach ensures that the model first learns effective representations of each modality before learning to integrate them for complex tasks. The instruction tuning phase enables the model to respond to natural language queries and commands, making it more accessible for human interaction.

### 8. Experimental Results

OccLLaMA was evaluated on three key tasks:

1. **4D Occupancy Forecasting**: Predicting how the semantic occupancy of a scene will evolve over time
2. **Motion Planning**: Generating safe and efficient vehicle trajectories
3. **Visual Question Answering**: Answering natural language questions about the scene

The results demonstrate that OccLLaMA achieves competitive performance across all three tasks:

- In **4D occupancy forecasting**, OccLLaMA shows enhanced long-term prediction capabilities compared to existing methods, particularly for dynamic objects like vehicles
- In **motion planning**, the model generates trajectories that are both safe and efficient, relying solely on semantic occupancy without additional inputs like HD maps
- In **visual question answering**, OccLLaMA can accurately answer questions about the scene, leveraging its understanding of both spatial relationships and semantics

The experimental results also highlight several interesting findings:

1. **Action-specific tokens** lead to performance gains in forecasting and planning, indicating the importance of preserving physical priors
2. **Pre-training for modality alignment** improves overall performance in visual question answering
3. **Spatial attention** significantly enhances the model's ability to capture relationships within the scene

### 9. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, OccLLaMA has several limitations:

1. **Inference Delay**: The large number of parameters leads to slower inference times
2. **Limited Context Window**: The model can only process a finite temporal context, limiting its ability to reason about very long-term dependencies
3. **Discretization Errors**: Converting continuous actions to discrete tokens introduces some approximation errors

Future work will focus on:

1. **Improving efficiency** through model compression and optimization
2. **Expanding the context window** to enable reasoning over longer time horizons
3. **Incorporating additional modalities** such as radar and lidar data
4. **Exploring zero-shot generalization** to new environments and scenarios

### 10. Conclusion

OccLLaMA represents a significant step toward creating more comprehensive world models for autonomous driving. By unifying vision, language, and action within a single generative framework, the model demonstrates the potential to overcome the limitations of existing approaches that focus solely on perception-to-action mapping.

The ability to simultaneously forecast scene evolution, reason about the environment in natural language, and plan actions based on this understanding brings autonomous driving systems closer to human-like cognition. As autonomous vehicles continue to advance, world models like OccLLaMA will play an increasingly important role in enabling safe, efficient, and interpretable driving systems.

The unified approach shown in OccLLaMA has implications beyond autonomous driving, potentially benefiting other embodied AI applications where understanding the relationship between perception, language, and action is crucial. By demonstrating that large language models can effectively integrate these modalities, this work opens new avenues for research in embodied intelligence.
