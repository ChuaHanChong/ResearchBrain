# Sample-Efficient Distributionally Robust Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning via Online Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> The `f`-MORNAVI algorithm provides the first provably effective online learning framework for Distributionally Robust Markov Games, enabling multi-agent systems to learn robust policies directly from environmental interactions without relying on simulators or prior data. This method achieves high-probability regret bounds and demonstrates convergence to robust equilibria, showing enhanced resilience to model uncertainty in numerical experiments.

## Problem

- Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) policies trained in simulators often fail in real-world deployment due to the "Sim-to-Real gap" caused by environmental uncertainties and model mismatches.
- Existing Distributionally Robust Markov Games (DRMGs) approaches rely on impractical assumptions, either requiring perfect simulators or large, comprehensive offline datasets.
- Developing robust multi-agent systems necessitates online learning algorithms that can learn from direct environmental interaction while managing exploration-exploitation and worst-case environmental uncertainty.

## Method

- Introduced `f`-MORNAVI (Multiplayer Optimistic ==Robust Nash Value Iteration==), a model-based meta-algorithm for online learning in episodic, finite-horizon DRMGs.
- The algorithm integrates pessimistic robust optimization with optimistic online exploration by using UCB-based bonus terms in Q-function estimates to quantify estimation error.
- Developed divergence-specific bonus term constructions for Total Variation and Kullback-Leibler uncertainty sets, ensuring efficient exploration tailored to the uncertainty's geometry.

## Results

- `f`-MORNAVI provided the first high-probability regret bounds for online DRMGs, achieving 	ilde{O}(√(\min\{\rho_{min)^{-1}, H\} H^2 S K \prod_{i \in M} A_i}) for TV-DRMGs (under a fail-state assumption).
- For KL-DRMGs, `f`-MORNAVI achieved a regret bound of 	ilde{O}(√(H^4 \exp(2H^2) S K \prod_{i \in M) A_i / (\rho_{min}^2 P^\star_{min})}), demonstrating sample-efficient learning of robust policies.
- Numerical experiments on 2-agent, 2-step DRMGs validated `f`-MORNAVI's convergence to robust equilibria and its enhanced robustness against increasing model uncertainty compared to non-robust baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online learning in DRMGs is inherently challenging, demonstrated by lower bounds showing linear regret with "support shifting" in TV-DRMGs and the "curse of multi-agency" related to the joint action space for KL-DRMGs.
> - Combining pessimistic robust optimization with optimistic exploration is crucial for efficiently learning robust policies from online interactions in uncertain multi-agent environments.
> - Robust equilibria in general-sum DRMGs can be learned with provable sample efficiency and theoretical guarantees through online interaction, which had previously been considered intractable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has become a fundamental framework for developing intelligent multi-agent systems, demonstrating capabilities in areas such as strategic gaming, autonomous transportation, and distributed robotics. MARL commonly relies on training policies within high-fidelity simulators before deployment in real-world environments. However, a significant challenge, known as the "Sim-to-Real gap," arises from inevitable model mismatches between training simulations and complex real-world dynamics. These mismatches can stem from environmental uncertainties such as noise, unmodeled physical effects, or adversarial perturbations, leading to brittle policies that perform poorly or fail in practice. This vulnerability is amplified in multi-agent settings due to cascading interactions among agents, which can destabilize collective strategies.

Distributionally Robust Markov Games (DRMGs) offer a principled approach to address this challenge by optimizing for worst-case performance over a defined set of plausible environmental models. This robust optimization strategy provides formal performance guarantees and promotes the discovery of more generalizable policies. Prior research in DRMGs has primarily focused on two settings: the generative model setting, where algorithms assume access to a perfect simulator that can be queried at will, and the offline setting, which presupposes the availability of a large, comprehensive, pre-collected dataset. While these approaches have advanced the theoretical understanding and algorithmic development of DRMGs, their assumptions often disconnect them from real-world applications where high-fidelity simulators are unavailable, and extensive offline data collection is infeasible or costly. In domains such as autonomous systems or personalized healthcare, agents must learn policies directly through online, sequential interaction with unknown and dynamic environments. This necessitates new algorithms capable of navigating the exploration-exploitation dilemma under the additional burden of worst-case environmental uncertainty.

This paper addresses this gap by pioneering the study of online learning in DRMGs. It aims to develop provably effective algorithms that enable multi-agent systems to learn robust policies purely from direct environmental interactions, without relying on prior data or simulators. This work distinguishes itself from prior efforts by tackling the inherent challenges of online exploration and learning within a distributionally robust framework for multi-agent systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to design and analyze provably effective online learning algorithms for Distributionally Robust Markov Games (DRMGs). The motivation for this work stems from a critical disconnect between existing DRMG research and the practical requirements of many high-stakes real-world applications.

**Motivation:**
1. **Addressing the Sim-to-Real Gap in MARL:** Multi-agent systems trained in simulators frequently fail upon deployment in real-world environments due to discrepancies (model mismatches) between simulated and actual conditions. These discrepancies can be caused by various environmental uncertainties, including noise, unmodeled dynamics, or even adversarial attacks. In MARL, such uncertainties are exacerbated by the complex, interdependent interactions among agents, which can lead to rapid destabilization of learned collective behaviors.
2. **Limitations of Current DRMG Approaches:** Existing DRMG algorithms largely depend on two assumptions:
 - **Generative Models/Simulators:** Many methods assume access to a "perfect oracle" or simulator that can generate data on demand for any state-action pair. This is often impractical or impossible in real-world scenarios like autonomous systems or personalized healthcare, where high-fidelity simulators are either too complex to build or inherently limited in capturing full real-world variability.
 - **Large Offline Datasets:** Other approaches rely on the availability of massive, static, and comprehensive datasets collected prior to learning. Collecting such exhaustive datasets that cover all critical scenarios and potential uncertainties is often infeasible and costly in practice.
3. **Necessity for Online Learning:** In many crucial applications, agents must learn policies directly through interaction with the environment (online learning). In this paradigm, data acquisition is not free; every action carries a real cost, and naive exploration can lead to severe consequences. Therefore, algorithms are needed that can effectively manage the exploration-exploitation trade-off while simultaneously optimizing for worst-case performance under environmental uncertainty.
4. **Enabling Truly Robust Multi-Agent Systems:** The ultimate goal is to enable MARL systems to cope with model misspecification while learning purely from experience, thus bridging the gap between theoretical robustness and practical deployment in unpredictable real-world settings.

### 3. Key Objectives

1. **Characterize the Hardness of Online DRMGs:** The authors first aim to rigorously demonstrate the inherent difficulties of online learning in DRMGs, particularly regarding issues like "support shifting" (where the optimal worst-case transition might lie outside the nominal model's support) and the "curse of multi-agency."
2. **Develop a Novel Online Learning Framework:** The central objective is to introduce a model-based meta-algorithm, `f`-MORNAVI (Multiplayer Optimistic Robust Nash Value Iteration for `f`-Divergence Uncertainty Set), specifically designed for episodic, finite-horizon DRMGs with interactive data collection. This framework is intended to synergize the pessimism required for robust optimization with the optimism essential for efficient online exploration.
3. **Provide Provable Guarantees:** A core objective is to establish the first theoretical guarantees for online learning in general-sum DRMGs. This includes providing rigorous, high-probability regret bounds for `f`-MORNAVI, thereby formally characterizing the sample complexity required to learn an optimal robust policy for different `f`-divergence uncertainty sets (Total Variation and Kullback-Leibler).
4. **Demonstrate Sample Efficiency and Convergence:** The research aims to show that the proposed algorithms can converge to an $\epsilon$-optimal robust policy with high sample efficiency, challenging the perception that finding robust equilibria in general-sum DRMGs is intractable through online interaction.
5. **Address the "Curse of Multi-Agency":** Through theoretical analysis and comparison with prior works, the paper seeks to highlight whether the proposed approach can mitigate or is inherently limited by the exponential dependence on the joint action space, a common challenge in MARL.

By achieving these objectives, the authors seek to establish a new, practical pathway for developing multi-agent systems that are not only capable of complex coordinated behavior but are also resilient to the uncertainties of real-world deployment.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce the **Multiplayer Optimistic Robust Nash Value Iteration (MORNAVI)** algorithm, a model-based meta-algorithm for online learning in episodic, finite-horizon Distributionally Robust Markov Games (DRMGs). This approach integrates principles of pessimism for robustness with optimism for efficient online exploration. The algorithm operates in three main stages: Nominal Transition Estimation, Optimistic Robust Planning, and Policy Execution with Data Collection.

#### 4.1. Problem Formulation: Distributionally Robust Markov Games (DRMGs)

The game is defined by $MG_{rob} = \{M, S, A, H, \{P_i\}_{i \in M}, r\}$, where $M$ is the set of $m$ agents, $S$ is the finite state space, $A = A_1 \times \cdots \times A_m$ is the joint action space, $H$ is the horizon length, $\{P_i\}_{i \in M}$ are agent-specific uncertainty sets of transition kernels, and $r$ is the reward function. The work considers non-stationary DRMGs where rewards $r_{i,h}(s,a)$ can vary with step $h$.

A key assumption is the **agent-wise (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set**, where each agent $i$'s uncertainty set $P_i$ is independently defined over all horizons, states, and joint actions. This allows the transition kernel at step $h$ from state $s$ with joint action $a_h$ to be chosen arbitrarily from $P^{\rho_i}_{i,h,f}(s_h, a_h)$. The uncertainty sets are specifically defined using **f-divergences** (e.g., Total Variation (TV) and Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence) centered around a nominal kernel $P^\star$. Each agent aims to maximize its own worst-case performance (robust value function $V^{\pi, \rho_i}_{i,h}$) over its uncertainty set. The solution concept focuses on computing robust notions of equilibrium, such as Robust Nash Equilibrium ($\epsilon$-NE), Robust Coarse Correlated Equilibrium ($\epsilon$-CCE), and Robust Correlated Equilibrium ($\epsilon$-CE). The performance metric is **robust regret**, defined as the cumulative gap between the algorithm's performance and an optimal robust policy.

#### 4.2. f-MORNAVI Algorithm Design

The `f`-MORNAVI algorithm (Algorithm 1 in the paper) proceeds in K episodes, iteratively updating its understanding of the environment and policy.

1. **Stage 1: Nominal Transition Estimation (Line 4)**
 - At the beginning of each episode $k$, the algorithm maintains an empirical estimate of the nominal transition kernel, $\hat{P}^k_h(s'|s,a)$, based on all historical data $D$ collected from previous episodes.
 - The estimate is a standard maximum likelihood estimate: $\hat{P}^k_h(s'|s,a) = N^k_h(s,a,s') / N^k_h(s,a)$, where $N^k_h(s,a,s')$ is the count of transitions from $(s,a)$ to $s'$ at step $h$, and $N^k_h(s,a)$ is the total count of visiting $(s,a)$ at step $h$. If a state-action pair has not been visited, a uniform distribution is used as a prior.
 - This model-based approach is chosen to handle the non-linearity of worst-case expectations in DRMGs, which can make model-free estimators biased or sample-inefficient.

2. **Stage 2: Optimistic Robust Planning (Lines 5-9)**
 - This stage is crucial for balancing robust optimization and online exploration. `f`-MORNAVI constructs the episode policy $\pi^k$ using optimistic robust planning.
 - It maintains **optimistic** ($Q^k_h(s,a)$) and **pessimistic** ($Q^k_h(s,a)$) estimates of the robust Q-function. These estimates incorporate a **bonus term** $\beta^k_{i,h,f}(s,a)$ based on the Upper-Confidence-Bound (UCB) principle.
 - The optimistic Q-value is given by: $Q^k_{i,h}(s,a) = \min\{r_{i,h}(s,a) + \sigma_{\hat{P}^{\rho_i}_{i,h,f}(s,a)}[V^k_{i,h+1}] + \beta^k_{i,h,f}(s,a), H\}$.
 - The pessimistic Q-value is given by: $Q^k_{i,h}(s,a) = \max\{r_{i,h}(s,a) + \sigma_{\hat{P}^{\rho_i}_{i,h,f}(s,a)}[V^k_{i,h+1}] - \beta^k_{i,h,f}(s,a), 0\}$.
 - Here, $\sigma_P[V]$ is the support function representing the worst-case expected value over the uncertainty set, calculated using its dual representation (Lemma 7). $\hat{P}^{\rho_i}_{i,h,f}$ is the uncertainty set centered at the empirical kernel $\hat{P}^k_h$.
 - The bonus term $\beta^k_{i,h,f}(s,a)$ is carefully constructed to be data-driven and tailored to the geometry of the chosen uncertainty set (TV or KL divergence). Its purpose is to quantify the estimation error between the empirical and true robust Bellman operators, ensuring that the estimated robust Q-values form a high-probability confidence interval around the true robust Q-values, thus encouraging exploration of less-visited state-action pairs.
 - **EQUILIBRIUM Subroutine:** Given these estimated robust Q-functions $\{Q^k_{i,h}(s,\cdot)\}_{i \in M}$, a chosen equilibrium concept (Nash, CCE, or CE) is computed for the matrix-form game with these payoffs to determine the joint policy $\pi^k_h(\cdot|s)$ for the current episode. The corresponding V-functions are then updated. The paper notes that computing NE can be computationally hard (PPAD-hard) in general-sum games, while CCE and CE are tractable.

3. **Stage 3: Execution of Policy and Data Collection (Lines 10-16)**
 - The learned policy $\{\pi^k_h\}_{h=1}^H$ is executed in the environment for episode $k$.
 - Observed rewards and next states are collected and appended to the dataset $D$, which is then used to refine the empirical transition kernel for the next episode.

**Bonus Term Construction (Detailed):**
- **TV-Divergence:** The bonus term for TV-divergence accounts for variance and the difference between optimistic and pessimistic value function estimates. It involves terms related to the empirical variance of the value function, the difference between the upper and lower bounds of the value function (which serves as a measure of current uncertainty), and a base term related to the counts of state-action visits. A "fail-state assumption" (Assumption 3) is adopted for the TV case to prevent support shifting issues, ensuring the worst-case kernel support is covered by the nominal one.
- **KL-Divergence:** For KL-divergence, the bonus term is proportional to $H/\rho_i$ and inversely proportional to the square root of $(N^k_h(s,a) \cdot \hat{P}^k_{min,h}(s,a))$. $\hat{P}^k_{min,h}(s,a)$ is the minimum positive empirical transition probability from $(s,a)$, which addresses the sensitivity of KL divergence to small probabilities. This term directly relates to controlling the deviation between empirical and true log-moment generating functions, which arise from the dual formulation of KL-DRMGs.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings, including theoretical lower bounds on regret illustrating the inherent hardness of online learning in DRMGs, and upper bounds demonstrating the sample efficiency of their proposed MORNAVI algorithm for both Total Variation (TV) and Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence uncertainty sets. Numerical experiments further support these theoretical findings.

#### 5.1. Hardness of Online Learning in DRMGs (Section 4)

The authors demonstrate that online learning in DRMGs is inherently challenging, providing two distinct lower bounds on cumulative regret:

1. **Hardness with Support Shift (Theorem 1):** In the presence of "support shifting"—where the optimal worst-case transition kernel may assign positive probability to states not supported by the nominal kernel—any online algorithm for TV-DRMGs suffers a linear regret. Specifically, there exists a TV-DRMG where the regret lower bound is $\Omega(\rho K \cdot \min\{H, \prod_{i \in M} A_i\})$. Here, $\rho$ is the uncertainty radius, $K$ is the number of episodes, $H$ is the horizon, and $\prod_{i \in M} A_i$ is the size of the joint action space. This implies that without additional assumptions, efficient learning is intractable, as the regret grows linearly with $K$ and exponentially with the number of agents through the joint action space. This scenario is particularly relevant for TV divergence, where an adversary can "shift" probability mass to unobserved states.

2. **Hardness without Support Shift (Theorem 2):** Even when support shifting is not an issue (e.g., in KL-divergence settings where the worst-case support is covered by the nominal one), online learning in DRMGs still faces significant challenges. The paper shows that for a constructed KL-DRMG, any learning algorithm suffers a cumulative regret lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt{K \prod_{i \in M} A_i})$. This result highlights the "curse of multi-agency," indicating an inevitable dependence on the joint action space, which differentiates online DRMGs from their non-robust counterparts where this dependence can sometimes be broken. This arises because agents must explore the entire model to estimate worst-case transitions.

#### 5.2. Theoretical Guarantees of MORNAVI (Section 5)

The paper provides the first provable guarantees for online learning in general-sum DRMGs.

1. **Regret Bound for Total Variation (TV) Divergence (Theorem 4):**
 - For TV-DRMGs, under a standard "fail-state assumption" (Assumption 3), which mitigates the support shifting issue by ensuring zero reward and absorbing transitions for "failure states," the TV-MORNAVI algorithm achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\min\{\rho_{min}^{-1}, H\} H^2 S K \prod_{i \in M} A_i})$. Here, $\rho_{min} = \min_{i \in M} \rho_i$ is the minimum uncertainty radius among agents, $S$ is the number of states, and $\tilde{O}$ hides logarithmic factors. This bound holds for finding robust Nash, Coarse Correlated, or Correlated Equilibria.

2. **Regret Bound for Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence (Theorem 5):**
 - For KL-DRMGs, no fail-state assumption is needed as KL divergence naturally prevents support shifts. The KL-MORNAVI algorithm achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^4 \exp(2H^2) S K \prod_{i \in M} A_i / (\rho_{min}^2 P^\star_{min})})$. Here, $P^\star_{min}$ is the smallest positive entry of the nominal kernel. The $\exp(2H^2)$ term is acknowledged as inherent to KL-based robust RL, stemming from the duality formulation, but is manageable for moderate horizons and non-vanishing $P^\star_{min}$.

3. **Sample Complexity (Corollary 6):**
 - Based on the regret bounds, the paper derives the sample complexity (total number of samples $KH$) required to find an $\epsilon$-approximate equilibrium:
 - **TV-DRMG:** $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2} \min\{\rho_{min}^{-1}, H\} H^3 S \prod_{i \in M} A_i)$.
 - **KL-DRMG:** $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2} \rho_{min}^{-2} (P^\star_{min})^{-1} H^5 \exp(2H^2) S \prod_{i \in M} A_i)$.
 - These results demonstrate that, despite the inherent hardness, MORNAVI can learn an equilibrium with efficient sample complexity.

#### 5.3. Numerical Experiments (Section D)

The authors conduct small-scale numerical experiments on 2-agent, 2-step DRMGs (both fully cooperative and general-sum) for both KL and TV uncertainty sets.
- **Convergence:** The experiments show that the MORNAVI algorithm converges to robust equilibria in practice.
- **Robustness:** The robust equilibria learned by MORNAVI demonstrate enhanced robustness against model uncertainty, maintaining more stable performance as the uncertainty radius ($\rho$) increases, compared to non-robust Nash Value Iteration baselines. While non-robust algorithms may outperform MORNAVI when $\rho \approx 0$ (as MORNAVI is conservative), their performance degrades significantly with increasing model mismatch, whereas MORNAVI's performance remains more stable.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step in the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) by pioneering the study of online learning in Distributionally Robust Markov Games (DRMGs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Theory and Practice for Robust MARL:** Current DRMG research has largely relied on ideal assumptions such as access to generative models or large offline datasets, limiting applicability in real-world scenarios where data collection is costly or impossible. This work addresses this critical disconnect by providing the first provable guarantees for online learning in DRMGs, where agents learn directly from environmental interactions. This establishes a more practical pathway for developing truly robust multi-agent systems that can cope with environmental uncertainties in dynamic, unknown environments.

2. **Novel Algorithmic Framework:** The introduction of `f`-MORNAVI, a Multiplayer Optimistic Robust Nash Value Iteration algorithm, is a key contribution. Its design synergizes the pessimism essential for robust optimization with the optimism required for efficient online exploration, a crucial balance in interactive learning settings. The algorithm's adaptability to different `f`-divergence measures (Total Variation and Kullback-Leibler) further enhances its utility.

3. **First Theoretical Guarantees for Online DRMGs:** The paper provides the first rigorous, high-probability regret bounds for online learning in general-sum DRMGs. These theoretical results formally characterize the sample complexity needed to achieve $\epsilon$-optimal robust policies, demonstrating that robust equilibria can be found efficiently through online interaction. This advances the fundamental understanding of learning in uncertain multi-agent environments.

4. **Characterization of Inherent Hardness:** By deriving minimax lower bounds, the research provides crucial insights into the intrinsic difficulties of online DRMGs. The identification of "support shifting" as a major challenge for TV-DRMGs (leading to linear regret without specific assumptions) and the "curse of multi-agency" (evidenced by the dependence on the joint action space even without support shifts) clearly delineate the boundaries of what is efficiently achievable. These hardness results are vital for guiding future research directions.

5. **Comparison and Contextualization with Prior Work:** The work meticulously compares its results with existing literature in generative model and offline settings. While `f`-MORNAVI achieves sample complexities comparable to or surpassing previous offline and generative model approaches in many parameters, it explicitly highlights its dependence on the product of individual action spaces ($\prod_{i \in M} A_i$). This prompts a critical open question regarding the "curse of multi-agency" and whether it can be fundamentally overcome in general online DRMG settings, contrasting with prior works that bypassed this issue through stronger assumptions or specialized problem structures (e.g., specific uncertainty sets, powerful oracles, or coverage coefficients in offline learning). This open question is a significant future research direction identified by the authors.

6. **Practical Implications for Real-World Systems:** The ability to learn robust policies online, without reliance on perfect simulators or extensive pre-collected data, has direct implications for high-stakes applications like autonomous navigation, air traffic control, distributed robotics, and personalized healthcare, where robustness to uncertainty is paramount and direct interaction is the primary learning modality. The validated robustness of MORNAVI in numerical experiments suggests a path towards more reliable deployment of MARL systems.

In summary, this paper lays a foundational stone for online distributionally robust multi-agent reinforcement learning, providing both a novel algorithmic solution with theoretical guarantees and a comprehensive analysis of the field's inherent challenges and future directions.
