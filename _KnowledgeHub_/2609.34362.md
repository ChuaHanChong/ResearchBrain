# FutureDuet: Decoupling Observation Access from Future Supervision in World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FutureDuet is a world action model that keeps both main and wrist cameras for control but gives each view its own training-only future objective. The main view predicts future RGB plus interaction masks and robot skeletons, forming a Task State. The wrist views predict future V-JEPA latents, forming an Interaction State. ActionDiT reads both states. It reaches 94.2% clean and 94.1% randomized success on RoboTwin50 and 99.2% on LIBERO.

## Problem

- Multi-view world action models fuse main and wrist observations into one visual stream, so every camera the policy sees must predict the same future-video target.
- Wrist cameras move with the end effector, mixing local contact changes with viewpoint shifts and self-occlusion, which makes pixel-level future reconstruction a poorly matched training target.
- Future RGB prediction on the main view captures scene appearance but does not explicitly emphasize the task objects and robot motion that actually guide manipulation.

## Method

- **Decoupled dual-stream design**: a ==VideoDiT== encodes the main view and a lightweight online encoder handles the wrist views; ==learned-query adapters== compress each into a Task State and an Interaction State.
- Structured main-view supervision: a shared ==DPT-style decoder== reads noisy VideoDiT features and predicts future SAM 3 interaction masks and projected robot skeleton maps, alongside ==flow-matching== future RGB prediction.
- **Wrist future-latent prediction**: a predictor conditioned on the stop-gradient Task State regresses frozen V-JEPA 2.1 future wrist latents with an ==L1 loss==; all auxiliary heads are pruned at inference.

## Results

- RoboTwin50: **94.2%** clean and **94.1%** randomized success, beating Fast-WAM by **2.3 points** in both settings; LIBERO average reaches **99.2%**, best on Spatial, Object and Goal.
- On six hard precise-interaction RoboTwin50 tasks, gains over Fast-WAM average **9.2 points** clean and **12.8** randomized, accounting for **48%** and **66%** of total net gain.
- Wrist study: separate pathway **60.9%** vs unified RGB **55.8%**; future latents raise it to **69.7%** vs **64.1%** for RGB, beating deployed V-JEPA (**63.2%**) at **~200 ms** vs **245 ms** latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Which observations a policy reads and what future each stream is trained to predict are separate design choices; tying them together forces mismatched targets onto views with different dynamics.
> - For moving, self-occluding cameras, predicting pretrained latent features beats pixel reconstruction, and training-only teacher targets bring a heavy encoder's benefit without its inference cost.
> - Auxiliary targets shape where the action head looks: masks shift attention toward objects and skeletons toward the robot, so target choice is a lever for controlling readout.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

FutureDuet starts from a practical gap in robot manipulation. Reaching an object does not guarantee completing the contact, opening, hooking, or alignment needed to finish a task. The authors' motivating examples are opening a microwave far enough, engaging a mug handle on a hook, and aligning with and actuating a switch. They interpret these terminal interactions as situations where close-range wrist evidence can help. The examples motivate the method but do not establish that wrist views alone cause the measured gains.

FutureDuet keeps both a fixed main camera and moving wrist cameras available for choosing actions. It trains each view to represent a different kind of future: scene-level progress for the main view and local interaction for the wrist views. Its gains are larger on persistently difficult tasks than benchmark-wide.

(b) Success-rate gains over Fast-WAM in percentage points for six tasks, including Open Microwave (+10 clean, +21 randomized), Hanging Mug (+12/+14), and Turn Switch (+12/+18).

### 2. Where the gains concentrate

On the 50-task bimanual RoboTwin50 benchmark, FutureDuet reports 94.2% clean and 94.1% randomized success. That is 2.3 points above Fast-WAM in each setting. The paper says this comparison matches data, backbone, preprocessing, training budget, checkpoint selection, and evaluation.

The clearest payoff is on the six tasks that remain below 90% success for FutureDuet in both evaluation settings. On these tasks, its average gain in task success rate over Fast-WAM is 9.2 percentage points in clean evaluation and 12.8 points under randomization. The benchmark-wide gain is 2.3 points.

### 3. Different cameras, different futures

A World Action Model (WAM) is a robot policy trained with future visual prediction in addition to action learning. The two camera types pose different prediction problems. A main camera gives a stable view of the scene. Wrist cameras reveal close-up interaction, but they also move with the robot, which changes what appears in the image. Rather than training both views on one uniform future-video objective, FutureDuet decouples which observations a policy can use from what each view is trained to predict.

Decoupling future supervision across views. (a) Main and wrist views are concatenated into a multi-view image and trained with one future objective. (b) In the proposed design, main-view task-evolution modeling yields a Task State and wrist-view interaction-evolution modeling yields an Interaction State. The Duet Interface brings these states together for action generation.

For the main view, future RGB models scene evolution, and object masks and robot skeletons add structured supervision. Wrist features are trained to predict future wrist latents rather than reconstruct future pixels. ActionDiT combines the Task State and Interaction State to generate actions. The auxiliary future-prediction modules are removed for deployment. The authors' "no inference overhead" claim therefore concerns those auxiliary modules.

### 4. What the wrist ablation tests

A controlled study covers Open Microwave, Hanging Mug, and Turn Switch. It reports three-seed means over 100 randomized evaluation episodes per task per seed. Main-view supervision stays RGB-only. The study compares a "unified" design, in which wrist observations are incorporated into the shared pathway, with a lightweight "separate" wrist pathway.

Adding wrist access raises mean success from 34.3% for main-only to 55.8% for unified RGB. A separate pathway without wrist future prediction reaches 60.9%. This comparison supports an advantage for pathway separation, although it is not a perfectly isolated single-factor intervention. With the separate pathway fixed, future RGB supervision reaches 64.1% and future-latent supervision reaches 69.7%, a 5.6-point advantage for latents. This supports the claim that the target representation matters under that setup. It does not show that latent targets win on every task.

### 5. Contribution and boundary

FutureDuet keeps both camera streams for control while assigning scene-level and local-interaction supervision to different views. It improves task success most on persistently difficult tasks. The evidence comes from simulation, and real-robot performance is not established.
