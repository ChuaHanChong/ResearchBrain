# COCOI: Contact-aware Online Context Inference for Generalizable Non-planar Pushing

> [!summary] Summary
> A deep reinforcement learning framework, COCOI, enables robots to infer contact dynamics context online from multi-modal sensors for generalizable non-planar pushing tasks. The system demonstrated a 90% success rate in real-world deployments by adapting to diverse objects and dynamics, including unseen ones.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning policies often lack adaptability to varying object dynamics, hindering real-world deployment for contact-rich manipulation.
- Robots struggle to infer complex contact physics and comprehend intricate 3D object states from high-dimensional monocular camera perception.
- Non-planar pushing, which requires maintaining an object's upright orientation while pushing, presents significant challenges beyond simpler 2D planar pushing tasks.

## Method

- Developed **COCOI**, a ==deep Q-learning== framework that extends a basic pushing controller with a ==Contact-aware Online COntext Inference== module.
- Implemented a ==contact-aware sampling== strategy that collects history sensor observations only when contact force exceeds a threshold, ensuring dynamics-relevant context inference.
- Employed ==RetinaGAN==, a generative adversarial network, for ==visual domain adaptation== to bridge the sim-to-real gap, allowing policies trained on synthetic images to deploy on real robots.

## Results

- COCOI achieved higher success rates in simulation (comparable to a ground truth Oracle model) and showed an average **50%** relative improvement over a baseline in varied initial placement and dynamics conditions.
- The method demonstrated robust performance against object mass and friction coefficients outside the training range, and successfully manipulated unseen objects and dynamically changing structures like a cup stack.
- A COCOI policy, trained exclusively on synthetic images with RetinaGAN, successfully transferred to a real robot, achieving a **90%** success rate for the non-planar "push-in-bin" task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online inference of a latent dynamics context representation through contact-rich interaction with multi-modal sensors significantly improves policy generalization across diverse object properties.
> - Actively sampling interaction history when contact forces are present is more effective for learning relevant dynamics context than uniform sampling.
> - The learned context representations effectively encode structured dynamics information, allowing the policy to adapt to unobservable object properties like mass distribution and friction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

General contact-rich manipulation represents a significant challenge in robotics, primarily due to the complexities of contact physics and the diversity of object dynamics. Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising avenue, leveraging the representational capabilities of deep neural networks and policy optimization through extensive interaction. However, existing RL policies often exhibit limited adaptability to environments with varied dynamics properties, tending to specialize in their training conditions and thus failing to generalize to new scenarios. This lack of robustness poses a barrier to the practical deployment of robots in tasks involving diverse objects or changing environmental conditions.

The broader research landscape for adapting learning-based policies to different task settings and dynamics variations includes several approaches. One common strategy involves training policies in randomized simulated environments to enhance robustness, with some methods extending to leverage real-world experience for simulation adaptation. Other research focuses on deriving task context representations for domain-specific planning, using meta-learning to identify task patterns or employing online system identification modules to learn dynamics parameters. Beyond purely learning-based methods, planning-control frameworks have also been utilized to bridge the domain gap by employing model predictive control or robust controllers to mitigate dynamics variations and disturbances.

Within the specific domain of pushing tasks, earlier work primarily focused on analytical approaches for quasi-static planar pushing. More recent efforts have introduced data-driven methods to model pushing physics, with many concentrating on planar pushing problems and using deep learning to infer object properties. These approaches typically consider objects that are inherently stable when pushed on a flat surface, effectively reducing the problem to a two-dimensional domain.

Non-planar pushing, where objects can move in full six degrees of freedom, is a less explored area. Current work in non-planar pushing often relies on specific object libraries, precise object detection, and localization, or uses simulators as predictive models for limited scenarios. These methods frequently struggle with generalization to diverse, unseen objects or environments.

This work, COCOI, positions itself at the intersection of these challenges. It addresses the need for generalizable contact-rich manipulation by developing a deep RL method that explicitly infers contact dynamics context online. This approach distinguishes itself from prior work by focusing on the online, contact-aware inference of dynamics properties, particularly for non-planar pushing tasks involving diverse 3D objects, using multi-modal sensor inputs from a monocular camera and a wrist force-torque sensor. By tackling the complexities of non-planar pushing and dynamics generalization, COCOI aims to expand the capabilities of robots in more challenging, real-world manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a deep reinforcement learning (RL) method that enables robots to perform generalizable contact-rich manipulation tasks despite variations in object dynamics properties. This overarching goal is motivated by several key challenges inherent in current robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Limited Adaptability of Existing RL Policies:** Traditional RL policies are often trained in specific dynamics settings, leading to specialization that makes them vulnerable to variations in the environment or object properties. For contact-rich tasks, where interaction physics plays a critical role, this lack of adaptability significantly hinders real-world deployment where objects and environments are inherently diverse. The motivation is to create a policy that is robust to these dynamics variations.

2. **Difficulty of Understanding Complicated Contact Physics:** Robots struggle to infer high-dimensional perception and comprehend the intricate contact physics involved in manipulating objects. Unlike humans, who effortlessly adapt to various object shapes and dynamics using visual and tactile perception, robots lack this intrinsic capability. The objective is to equip robots with a mechanism to effectively understand and leverage dynamic properties through interaction.

3. **Introduction of a Novel and Challenging Task: Non-planar Pushing:** To thoroughly evaluate the proposed method, the authors introduce a novel "non-planar pushing task." This task requires a robot to push an object to a target location while simultaneously maintaining its upright orientation, moving beyond the simpler, predominantly 2D "planar pushing" problems typically studied in prior research. This task is representative of common everyday scenarios (e.g., pushing a glass of water without spilling) but presents specific challenges:
 - **Visual Perception Complexity:** Unlike planar pushing, where top-down views can provide clear features, non-planar pushing requires interpreting complex 3D object states from a monocular camera, making critical information extraction challenging.
 - **Vital Dynamics Reasoning:** The dynamics properties are not directly observable from raw sensor data, and reasoning about these properties (e.g., mass distribution, friction) is crucial to prevent the object from tipping over.
 - **Requirement for Generalizability:** The policy must be effective across a wide range of objects with different appearances, shapes, masses, and friction coefficients, requiring a method that can adapt to unknown properties.

4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** A practical motivation is to enable policies trained in simulation to be directly deployed on real robots. This requires addressing the visual discrepancy between synthetic simulation images and real-world camera feeds, a common challenge in transferring RL policies.

In summary, the key objectives are to design and validate **Contact-aware Online COntext Inference (COCOI)**, a deep RL method that:
- Encodes a latent context representation of dynamics properties online using contact-rich interactions.
- Utilizes multi-modal perception (monocular camera image and wrist force-torque sensor readings).
- Enables dynamics-aware planning, thereby improving robustness to domain variations.
- Demonstrates its effectiveness on a novel and challenging non-planar pushing task.
- Facilitates successful sim-to-real transfer for real-world robotic deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Contact-aware Online COntext Inference (COCOI), a deep reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to address generalizable non-planar pushing. The methodology involves formulating the problem as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), developing a Q-learning-based controller, integrating a novel context inference module, and employing a visual domain adaptation technique for sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The non-planar pushing task is formalized as a POMDP. The full task state ($s$) is unobservable. Observations consist of high-dimensional monocular camera images (where the target location is rendered as a red dot) and low-dimensional proprioceptive data (gripper height, open/close status). Actions ($a$) comprise designated gripper position and orientation, gripper open/close commands, and a termination boolean. A sparse binary reward function is used, granting a reward of 1 if the object is within a threshold distance (5 cm) of the target and its tilting angle remains below 0.1 radians, with a small penalty per timestep to encourage efficiency.

#### 3.2. Learning Basic Pushing Controller

The control policy is trained using Q-learning, specifically a distributional variant known as QT-Opt, suitable for continuous state-action spaces.
- **Q-function Representation:** A two-stream deep neural network is used for the Q-function.
 - One stream processes high-dimensional inputs: stacked current and initial camera images are fed into a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder.
 - The second stream handles low-dimensional inputs: stacked gripper height, open/close status (state), and designated gripper pose, open/close command, and termination boolean (action) are fed into a Fully Connected Network (FCN) encoder.
 - The outputs of these two encoders are combined and then passed through another CNN-followed-by-FCN structure to predict the Q-value.
- **Training Process:** Distributed workers collect data tuples ($s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}$), which are stored in a replay buffer. Each optimization iteration samples a batch of these tuples. The optimal policy action is estimated using an online sampling-based Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) based on the current Q-function. The Q-function network weights are updated via gradient descent to minimize the Bellman error.

#### 3.3. Contact-aware Online Context Inference (COCOI)

To infer object dynamics properties, COCOI extends the basic Q-function architecture with an additional module.
- **COI Module (COntext Inference):** This module consists of parallel streams that encode history sensor observations into a dynamics context representation. Each stream takes a history sample $H_\tau = (I_\tau, I_{\tau+1}, f_\tau)$, which includes a pair of consecutive camera images ($I_\tau, I_{\tau+1}$) and a force reading ($f_\tau$), separated by 0.5 seconds. These inputs are processed by CNN and FCN encoders. The outputs from multiple history samples (three samples were found to be effective) are averaged to obtain the final dynamics context representation. This context representation is then concatenated with the state-action representation before being fed into the final Q-value prediction network, allowing the policy to be dynamics-aware.
- **Contact-aware Sampling Strategy:** To ensure that history samples contain relevant dynamics information, COCOI employs a contact-aware sampling strategy. Samples are only collected when the magnitude of the contact force measured by the wrist force-torque sensor exceeds a specified threshold (1 Newton). This active sampling ensures that the robot is interacting with the object when context is inferred, making the samples representative of contact dynamics. This is contrasted with a "Vanilla COI" (VCOI) strategy, which uses a uniform sampling interval for history observations.

#### 3.4. GAN for Visual Gap Bridging (RetinaGAN)

To enable sim-to-real transfer, the visual discrepancy between simulated and real-world images is addressed. RetinaGAN, a generative adversarial network (GAN), is utilized to generate synthetic images from the simulator that appear realistic while maintaining object-detection consistency. The RL policy is trained exclusively with these synthetic simulation images and then directly deployed on a real robot.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Environment:** All policies are trained in PyBullet.
- **Task Environment:** A flat surface (desk or trash bin) is randomly placed, and a 3D object is initialized upright with a random target position.
- **Objects:** A dataset of 75 diverse 3D objects (e.g., cups, bottles) is used, divided into 64 for training and 11 unseen objects for testing, including a challenging stack of cups.
- **Dynamics Randomization:** During training, object mass (0.05-0.5 kg) and friction coefficient (0.5-1.0) are randomized.
- **Success Criteria:** An episode is successful if the object is pushed to within 5 cm of the target and its tilt angle remains below 0.1 radians.
- **Policy Models for Comparison:**
 1. **Baseline:** The basic Q-function network without context inference.
 2. **VCOI:** Vanilla COI with uniformly sampled history observations.
 3. **Oracle:** Identical to the baseline but with ground truth object mass and friction coefficient directly provided as additional low-dimensional state input.
 4. **COCOI:** The proposed method with contact-aware sampling.
- **Training Details:** Policies are trained for 80k steps with a batch size of 2048, using a learning rate of 0.0001 and momentum of 0.9. An initial rule-based scripted policy assists in generating successful episodes for exploration in the early stages of training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the capabilities of COCOI across various settings, dynamics properties, and sim-to-real transfer scenarios.

#### 4.1. Policy Learning and Comparison

- **Training Performance (Figure 7):** COCOI and the Oracle model achieved significantly higher success rates during training compared to VCOI and the Baseline model. COCOI reached a success rate that was comparable to or slightly surpassed that of the Oracle, which had access to ground truth dynamics parameters (mass and friction coefficient). This result suggests that COCOI can infer dynamics properties, potentially capturing more comprehensive information beyond just mass and friction, such as object shape and specific contact points. The initial rule-based policy's low success rate (<5%) highlighted the inherent difficulty of the non-planar pushing task.

#### 4.2. Performance under Domain Variations

- **Varying Initial Placement (Table II):** COCOI consistently outperformed both VCOI and the Baseline model across different initial ranges for object and target placement. On average, COCOI showed a relative improvement of 50% in success rate compared to the Baseline and 20% compared to VCOI, achieving performance levels similar to the Oracle.
- **Varying Dynamics Properties (Table III):** COCOI exhibited robust performance when dynamics properties were varied beyond the training range.
 - It consistently outperformed other methods when tested with friction coefficients (0.0-0.5 and 1.0-1.5) and object masses (0.5-1.0 kg) outside the training distributions.
 - COCOI also demonstrated superior performance on unseen objects and the challenging "cup stack" scenario, where object inertia can dynamically change.
 - Notably, the Oracle model's performance degraded significantly in cases where the friction parameter was outside the training range. This indicated a potential for the Oracle policy to overfit to the explicit input of dynamics parameters, whereas COCOI's inferred representation showed greater generalization.

#### 4.3. Interpretation of Context Representations

- **t-SNE Visualization (Figure 8):** A t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) visualization of the inferred context representations revealed clear separation between clusters corresponding to different dynamics parameter settings (e.g., combinations of mass and friction). Representations within a single episode were also observed to be grouped more closely together, suggesting consistency in the learned context for a given interaction. Furthermore, the spatial arrangement of these clusters in the t-SNE plot was consistent with the magnitudes of the underlying dynamics properties (e.g., largest mass/friction settings were furthest from smallest). These observations indicate that COCOI successfully learns to infer structured and meaningful dynamics information from contact interactions.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Figure 9):** For real-world evaluation, a "push-in-bin" task was designed. Using RetinaGAN for visual domain adaptation, a COCOI policy trained solely on synthetic images was deployed on a real robot. The policy achieved a 90% success rate over 10 real-world pushing episodes, demonstrating successful transfer across both the visual and dynamics domain gaps.
- **Learned Behaviors:** The trained RL policy exhibited intelligent behaviors, such as breaking contact with the object when it began to lean and utilizing the gripper fingers for subtle impacts to maintain stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces COCOI, a method that enhances the adaptability and generalizability of deep reinforcement learning policies for complex contact-rich manipulation tasks. The work addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: enabling robots to interact effectively with diverse objects under varying and unobservable dynamics properties.

The **primary significance** of COCOI lies in its ability to infer contact dynamics context online using multi-modal sensor inputs. By actively sampling during contact-rich interactions, the method provides the robot with a dynamics-aware representation, which has been shown to be crucial for tasks involving unstable objects or variable physical properties. This online inference capability allows the policy to adapt to unknown or changing object characteristics during manipulation, reducing the need for extensive prior knowledge or object-specific training.

The successful application of COCOI to a novel and challenging **non-planar pushing task** is a notable contribution. Unlike prior work predominantly focused on planar pushing, this task requires maintaining an object's upright orientation while pushing, which is more representative of real-world scenarios and introduces complexities related to 3D object dynamics and stability. The demonstrated robustness of COCOI across variations in object mass, friction, shape, and even to unseen objects and dynamically changing structures (like a cup stack) indicates a significant step towards more generalizable manipulation capabilities.

Furthermore, the work successfully demonstrates **sim-to-real transfer** for a complex contact-rich task by combining COCOI with visual domain adaptation (RetinaGAN). This finding is critical for the practical deployment of learned robotic skills, as it suggests a viable pipeline for developing robust manipulation policies in simulation and transferring them to physical robots without requiring extensive real-world data collection.

The **potential impact** of this research is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** Robots equipped with COCOI-like capabilities could perform a wider range of manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, such as tidying diverse objects, handling delicate items, or performing assembly tasks with components that have manufacturing variations.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration:** A robot that can infer object properties online and adapt its behavior could interact more safely and effectively in shared workspaces, understanding how to manipulate objects in a manner consistent with human expectations.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The approach lays groundwork for extending manipulation capabilities to even more complex scenarios, such as pushing multiple objects, navigating cluttered environments, or interacting with non-rigid objects (e.g., cloth), which are identified as promising future research directions.

In conclusion, COCOI represents an advancement in robotic manipulation by providing a robust and generalizable method for handling contact-rich tasks with diverse dynamics. Its proven effectiveness in simulation and successful deployment in a real-world scenario highlight its potential to expand the capabilities of autonomous systems.
