# Repurposing Geometric Foundation Models for Multi-view Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST AI introduce Geometric Latent Diffusion (GLD), a framework that repurposes the multi-level feature space of geometric foundation models as the latent space for multi-view diffusion. This approach yields enhanced geometric consistency and photorealistic quality in Novel View Synthesis (NVS) while demonstrating 4.4x faster training convergence and robust zero-shot 3D geometry decoding.

## Problem

- Existing latent diffusion models for Novel View Synthesis often use 2D latent spaces lacking explicit geometric awareness, leading to inconsistent 3D structures across generated views.
- Many diffusion-based NVS methods rely on external geometric conditioning (e.g., depth maps), which introduces complexity, dependencies, and potential artifacts from imperfect priors.
- The powerful internal geometric representations of 'geometric foundation models' have primarily been confined to discriminative tasks, with their potential for generative processes remaining largely unexplored.

## Method

- **Geometric Latent Diffusion (GLD)** redefines the latent space for ==multi-view diffusion== by directly utilizing the multi-level, geometry-aware feature space extracted from a frozen ==geometric foundation model== (e.g., Depth Anything 3).
- A multi-view diffusion model is trained to synthesize these geometric features up to an optimized ==boundary layer== (level 1), with deeper features propagated deterministically, balancing efficiency and detail.
- A ==cascaded feature generation== approach ensures coherence between synthesized features at different levels, improving overall consistency and generation quality for both RGB images and dense geometry.

## Results

- GLD achieved **4.4x** faster training convergence compared to VAE-based latent diffusion models, significantly reducing computational overhead.
- The framework consistently outperformed VAE/DINO baselines and most state-of-the-art NVS methods in both 2D (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and 3D (AbsRel, RMSE, ATE, RPE) metrics across multiple datasets, demonstrating superior geometric consistency.
- GLD enables zero-shot dense geometry prediction and reconstruction from synthesized latents, producing significantly cleaner and more coherent 3D point clouds than other generative NVS methods without additional training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Features from geometric foundation models are highly expressive, capable of reconstructing high-fidelity RGB images and dense geometry, making them suitable latent spaces for NVS.
> - Diffusing features up to an optimal 'boundary layer' (specifically level 1) provides the best trade-off between geometric correspondence and photometric detail for efficient and high-quality NVS.
> - Cascaded generation of shallower features, conditioned on deeper synthesized latents, is crucial for maintaining cross-level alignment and overall coherence in multi-level geometric representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in Novel View Synthesis (NVS), a task that generates unseen viewpoints of a 3D scene. Recent advancements in image synthesis have largely been driven by diffusion models operating in latent spaces, such as those provided by Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). These latent spaces, however, have primarily been designed for 2D image generation and often lack explicit geometric awareness, which is critical for NVS.

Previous diffusion-based NVS methods often rely on 2D VAE latent spaces and, to ensure geometric consistency, frequently integrate external geometry conditioning like depth-based warping. This approach places the burden on the generative model to implicitly learn or explicitly incorporate geometric structures. Simultaneously, the field of 3D vision has seen the emergence of "geometric foundation models," which are designed to understand and represent 3D scenes from multiple views, producing features that encode strong geometric correspondences. However, the utility of these internal geometric representations has predominantly been confined to discriminative tasks (e.g., dense 3D reconstruction, camera pose estimation), without being leveraged for generative processes.

This research aims to bridge this gap by exploring the application of these geometrically consistent feature spaces as latent spaces for multi-view diffusion models. It extends the concept of Representation Autoencoders (RAE), which use frozen semantic encoders for 2D diffusion, to the domain of geometry-aware generation. By directly operating within a latent space inherently rich in 3D structure, the work seeks to reduce the reliance on external geometric conditioning or the generative model's implicit discovery of geometry, thereby potentially improving geometric consistency, synthesis quality, and training efficiency for NVS.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and validate Geometric Latent Diffusion (GLD), a framework that repurposes the feature space of geometric foundation models as the latent space for multi-view diffusion, specifically for Novel View Synthesis (NVS).

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing approaches to NVS:
1. **Inadequate Latent Spaces for Geometry-aware Generation:** While latent diffusion models have significantly advanced single-image generation by utilizing VAE or semantically structured latent spaces, these spaces are generally designed for 2D appearance modeling. NVS, however, fundamentally requires geometric consistency across viewpoints, which these view-independent latent spaces do not inherently provide. This often leads to geometrically inconsistent outputs, despite photorealistic image quality in some prior methods.
2. **Reliance on External Geometric Conditioning:** Many current diffusion-based NVS methods attempt to impose geometric consistency by adding external conditioning, such as depth maps or depth-based warping. This approach introduces additional complexity and dependency on the accuracy of these external priors, which can lead to artifacts if the conditioning is imperfect.
3. **Unexplored Potential of Geometric Foundation Models for Generation:** Geometric foundation models (e.g., Depth Anything 3, VGGT) have demonstrated strong capabilities in understanding and encoding 3D scene geometry from multi-view inputs. Their internal features are known to capture robust cross-view geometric correspondences and can support high-fidelity 3D reconstructions. However, these models have traditionally been used for discriminative tasks, and their feature spaces have not been systematically explored as latent spaces for generative tasks like NVS.
4. **Improving Training Efficiency and Generation Quality:** Experience from 2D image generation indicates that the choice of latent space significantly impacts both generation quality and training efficiency (e.g., RAEs converging faster than VAEs). The researchers hypothesize that a latent space inherently encoding geometric structure could similarly benefit NVS, leading to faster convergence and improved consistency.

By addressing these points, the research aims to demonstrate that a geometrically consistent latent space can lead to a more principled, efficient, and robust approach to NVS, enabling the generation of both high-fidelity RGB images and coherent 3D geometry from a single latent representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Geometric Latent Diffusion (GLD) framework operates by leveraging the multi-level feature space of a geometric foundation model as its latent space for multi-view diffusion. The primary backbone used is Depth Anything 3 (DA3), with VGGT also explored as an alternative. Given source images and target camera poses, GLD aims to synthesize corresponding target views.

The methodology proceeds through three main stages:

#### 3.1. Overview and Feature Representation

The DA3 backbone extracts multi-view, multi-level features {F_l} (L=4 levels) for both source and target views. The core idea is that these synthesized features {˜F_tgt_l} can be decoded by a dedicated RGB decoder D_rgb(·) into target images ˜I_tgt, and by the original geometric decoder D_geo(·) into target geometry ˜G_tgt (e.g., depth maps). The multi-view diffusion model processes a joint feature ˜F_l, concatenating source and target features along the view dimension.

#### 3.2. Validating the Reconstruction Capability of Geometric Features

Prior to diffusion, the suitability of the DA3 feature space for generative modeling is established. A ViT-based RGB decoder, D_rgb(·), is trained to reconstruct RGB images from the multi-level features {F_l} extracted by the frozen DA3 encoder E_geo(·). To enhance robustness and ensure the decoder effectively utilizes features from all levels, a level-wise dropout strategy is employed during training, where individual feature levels are randomly masked. This stage confirms that the DA3 feature space contains sufficient information for high-fidelity RGB reconstruction.

#### 3.3. Multi-view Diffusion and Determining the Boundary Layer (k)

Explicitly diffusing all L feature levels is computationally intensive. The approach proposes to diffuse only up to an optimal "boundary layer" k, from which deeper features (l > k) can be deterministically propagated through the frozen layers of E_geo(·).
- **Multi-view Diffusion Architecture and Training:** The diffusion models for each level (M_l) are based on the DiT_DH architecture and trained with a flow-matching objective. They are designed to synthesize a joint feature map ˜F_l for N source and M target views. To enforce geometric consistency, 3D self-attention with PRoPE (Plücker Ray Positional Encoding) is incorporated, and the models are conditioned on source-only features F_src_l and camera poses.
- **Boundary Layer Evaluation:** To identify the optimal boundary k ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}, four independent diffusion models {M_l} are trained, each responsible for synthesizing features at a specific level l. During evaluation, features up to k ({˜F_l}^k_l=0) are synthesized using their respective models, and deeper features (l > k) are derived by propagating ˜F_k through E_geo(·). The performance (RGB quality and geometric accuracy) is assessed for different k values to determine the shallowest boundary sufficient for high-quality NVS. Level 1 was identified as the optimal boundary.

#### 3.4. Cascaded Feature Generation

Based on the boundary layer evaluation, level 1 is fixed as the synthesis boundary. While deeper levels are propagated, shallower features (e.g., level 0) still need to be generated. To ensure cross-level alignment and coherence between ˜F_0 and ˜F_1, a cascaded model M_1→0 is employed. This model synthesizes level 0 features conditioned on the previously generated level 1 latent (˜F_1). It shares the same architecture and training configuration as M_0 but is trained by conditioning on a noisy version of ground-truth F_1 to improve robustness to imperfect latents during inference.

**Training Details:**
- GLD is trained from scratch on a combination of RealEstate10K, DL3DV, HyperSim, and TartanAir datasets.
- The diffusion model uses AdamW optimizer, a fixed learning rate, and EMA decay.
- Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) with camera embedding dropout is applied.
- Training occurs at multiple resolutions to match DA3 training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several key findings regarding the effectiveness of Geometric Latent Diffusion (GLD) for Novel View Synthesis (NVS).

#### 4.1. Reconstruction Capability of Geometric Features

The ViT-based RGB decoder D_rgb(·) demonstrated high-fidelity image reconstruction from the multi-level features of the frozen DA3 encoder. Quantitatively, it achieved competitive PSNR (35.41), SSIM (0.960), and LPIPS (0.019) scores compared to standard VAEs (e.g., Stable Diffusion, SDXL), indicating that DA3 features are sufficiently expressive for high-quality image synthesis. Analysis of individual feature levels revealed that level 1 successfully balances high geometric correspondence (PCK of 35.98, comparable to level 2's 40.70) with strong photometric information, making it an optimal latent space for NVS. Deeper levels (e.g., level 2, 3) provided strong geometric cues but lost photometric detail, while shallower levels (level 0) had rich appearance but poor geometric correspondence.

#### 4.2. Boundary Layer Selection and Cascaded Design

- **Boundary Layer:** Experiments varying the synthesis boundary `k` showed that explicitly synthesizing features up to level 1 (`k=1`) yielded the best NVS performance across both 2D (PSNR: 13.61, SSIM: 0.366, LPIPS: 0.555) and 3D metrics (AbsRel: 0.191, RMSE: 0.311, δ < 1.25: 0.744). Deeper boundaries (k=2, 3) resulted in performance degradation, likely due to increased abstraction and loss of fine-grained spatial details.
- **Cascaded Generation:** The cascaded approach for generating shallower features (level 0 conditioned on level 1) consistently improved both 2D and 3D metrics compared to independent generation, confirming that cross-level consistency is beneficial for coherent multi-level representations.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Performance (2D and 3D Metrics)

GLD was evaluated on in-domain (Re10K, DL3DV) and out-of-domain (Mip-NeRF 360) datasets, comparing against general-purpose visual encoders (VAE, DINO) and state-of-the-art NVS methods.
- **Against VAE/DINO Baselines:** GLD consistently outperformed VAE and DINO across all 2D (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and 3D (ATE, RPE, Reproj, MEt3R) metrics on all benchmarks. For instance, on Re10K, GLD achieved 16.362 PSNR (vs. VAE 15.656), 0.630 SSIM (vs. VAE 0.606), and 0.431 LPIPS (vs. VAE 0.456). Significant gains in 3D metrics were observed, with up to 2.8x lower ATE and 2.6x lower RPE compared to baselines. This confirms the DA3 feature space's superiority as a latent representation for NVS.
- **Against State-of-the-Art NVS Methods:** Despite being trained from scratch on smaller datasets without large-scale text-to-image (T2I) pretraining, GLD surpassed most state-of-the-art NVS methods (e.g., MVGenMaster, Matrix3D, CAMEO, NVComposer) in 2D metrics on both in-domain benchmarks. On the out-of-domain Mip-NeRF 360, GLD achieved state-of-the-art PSNR and competitive results for other 2D metrics, demonstrating generalization capabilities even without object-centric training data. In 3D consistency, GLD showed significantly superior performance than most fine-tuned methods on in-domain benchmarks, highlighting the advantages of its geometry-aware latent space. While MVGenMaster showed comparable or slightly better pose accuracy in some cases (likely due to external depth warping), GLD achieved the lowest reprojection error on Mip-NeRF 360.
- **Training Efficiency:** GLD achieved a 4.4x faster training convergence compared to using the VAE latent space (Fig. 1c), as indicated by PSNR convergence over training steps.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4, 10, 11, 12) showed that GLD produces structurally coherent views that closely align with ground truth, outperforming VAE and DINOv2 baselines, particularly under large viewpoint changes. It remained competitive with fine-tuned methods. The method avoids artifacts sometimes seen in approaches reliant on warped source views from external geometry estimators when estimation fails.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Number of Source Images:** GLD's performance advantages in 3D metrics (e.g., ATE, RPE) increased with fewer source views (e.g., 3x lower ATE on DL3DV with N=1 vs. 2x with N=4), indicating that the geometric priors in DA3 features are particularly beneficial in sparse input scenarios.
- **VGGT Backbone:** An evaluation with VGGT as an alternative geometric foundation model backbone confirmed that the core hypothesis generalizes. GLD with VGGT consistently outperformed VAE and DINO baselines, especially in 3D metrics, albeit with slightly lower overall performance than DA3.

#### 4.6. Zero-shot Geometry Evaluation

As a byproduct of the generation process, GLD's synthesized multi-level DA3 features could be decoded by the original pretrained DPT-based geometric decoder to predict depth and ray maps without additional training.
- **Depth Evaluation:** On the ETH3D dataset, GLD produced more accurate depth estimates (AbsRel: 0.160, SqRel: 0.410, δ1: 0.800) compared to Matrix3D, suggesting effective geometry grounding.
- **3D Reconstruction Visualization:** Unprojecting synthesized pixels into 3D space yielded point clouds that exhibited consistent 3D geometry across diverse camera trajectories (Fig. 6). Comparisons with Matrix3D (Fig. 7, 13) showed GLD producing significantly cleaner and more coherent reconstructions, especially in extrapolative settings and complex occlusions.

#### 4.7. Analysis of Geometric Correspondences

Examination of cross-view correspondence in 3D attention maps revealed that the diffusion model trained in the DA3 latent space exhibited the strongest correspondence across layers, particularly in the decoder blocks. This suggests that the DA3 latent space not only enables better decoding but also facilitates the diffusion model's learning of robust cross-view correspondences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Geometric Latent Diffusion (GLD), a novel framework that significantly advances Novel View Synthesis (NVS) by repurposing the feature space of geometric foundation models as the latent space for multi-view diffusion. The work holds several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Improved Geometric Consistency and Generation Quality:** By operating in a latent space inherently encoding strong geometric priors, GLD consistently produces more geometrically consistent and photorealistic novel views compared to methods relying on general-purpose 2D latent spaces. This addresses a fundamental limitation of prior NVS approaches that often struggled with maintaining 3D coherence across views.
2. **Enhanced Training Efficiency:** The framework achieves a 4.4x faster training convergence rate compared to VAE-based latent diffusion models. This efficiency gain is crucial for developing and deploying high-performance NVS systems, potentially reducing computational resources and development cycles.
3. **Reduced Reliance on External Conditioning:** GLD's ability to operate directly on geometry-aware features means it does not require explicit external geometry conditions (e.g., warped depth maps) during inference. This simplifies the NVS pipeline and makes it more robust, as it avoids potential artifacts arising from imperfect external geometry estimations.
4. **Zero-Shot Geometry and 3D Reconstruction:** A notable byproduct of GLD's design is the ability to decode dense geometric predictions (e.g., depth maps, 3D point clouds) from the synthesized latents without any additional training or fine-tuning. This "zero-shot" capability for geometry provides a unified generative model for both appearance and structure, which could be impactful for various 3D vision applications.
5. **Competitive Performance with Large-Scale Pretraining:** Despite being trained from scratch on comparatively smaller datasets and without leveraging large-scale text-to-image (T2I) pretraining, GLD achieves performance competitive with, and in many cases superior to, state-of-the-art NVS methods that benefit from such extensive pretraining. This suggests that a well-designed task-specific latent space can offer substantial advantages, potentially reducing the need for massive generative priors for specific tasks.
6. **Paradigm Shift for Latent Space Design:** The work advocates for a shift towards task-specific latent space design for geometry-aware generative tasks. By systematically analyzing and repurposing existing geometric foundation models, it demonstrates that specialized latent representations can yield significant improvements over generic ones. This paradigm could inspire future research into designing or adapting latent spaces for other complex, multi-modal, or structured generation problems beyond NVS.
7. **Broader Applications:** The ability to generate geometrically consistent novel views and 3D reconstructions could have implications across various fields, including virtual reality, augmented reality, robotics (for scene understanding and simulation), content creation (for generating 3D assets), and medical imaging.
