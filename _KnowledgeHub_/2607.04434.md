# RoboDojo: A Unified Sim-and-Real Benchmark for Comprehensive Evaluation of Generalist Robot Manipulation Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboDojo introduces a unified simulation and real-world benchmark for the comprehensive evaluation of generalist robot manipulation policies. It features 42 diverse simulation tasks across five capability dimensions and 18 challenging real-world tasks on three robot embodiments, enabling efficient policy diagnosis and reproducible physical testing.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation benchmarks offer narrow task scopes and limited coverage of diverse manipulation capabilities.
- The separation of simulation (scalable but lacks physical fidelity) and real-world (high fidelity but costly/hard to reproduce) evaluation hinders comprehensive assessment.
- A lack of standardized real-world evaluation protocols and unified development infrastructures impedes systematic policy comparison and rapid iteration.

## Method

- Developed **RoboDojo**, a unified benchmark featuring 42 simulation tasks across five capability dimensions (Generalization, Memory, Precision, Long-Horizon, Open) and 18 real-world tasks across three robot embodiments.
- Introduced **RoboDojo-RealEval**, a standardized real-world evaluation system with consistent hardware, scene resets, and remote cloud access to ensure reproducibility and efficiency.
- Created **XPolicyLab**, a unified infrastructure for standardized policy development, training, and deployment across both simulation and real-world environments.

## Results

- The top-performing policy achieved an **8.80%** average success rate and a **13.07** score in simulation, with significant performance degradation under scene-level randomization.
- Real-world evaluation showed the best policy attaining only a **12.8%** overall success rate, demonstrating the challenges in reliable physical deployment and revealing execution instabilities.
- RoboDojo's heterogeneous parallel simulation achieved up to a **1.94x** speedup, and RoboDojo-RealEval completed **180** real-world trials in approximately **3.4 hours**, demonstrating improved evaluation efficiency and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A significant performance gap exists between current generalist robot manipulation policies and human experts, with the best policies achieving low success rates in both simulation and real-world settings.
> - Policies demonstrate unbalanced strengths, struggling particularly with tasks demanding precision, memory-conditioned execution, and open-semantic grounding, especially under scene randomization.
> - Real-world evaluation reveals deployment-specific failure modes like action jitter and unsafe contacts, highlighting the critical need for a combined sim-and-real approach to capture physical complexities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence and robot learning has witnessed advancements in generalist robot manipulation policies and embodied foundation models. These systems aim to enable robots to perform diverse tasks in the physical world. However, the systematic and comprehensive evaluation of these policies presents ongoing challenges.

Existing benchmarks often address specific aspects of robot manipulation. Some focus on language-conditioned long-horizon tasks (e.g., CALVIN), while others examine generalization (e.g., RoboTwin), memory-dependent manipulation (e.g., RMBench), or precise manipulation (e.g., AutoBio). Many benchmarks primarily operate in either simulation or the real world. Simulation environments, such as those built on SAPIEN, MuJoCo, PyBullet, or Isaac Sim, offer efficient and scalable evaluation, facilitating rapid feedback and model iteration. However, they may not fully capture the complexities of real-world physics, including contact-rich dynamics, actuation errors, and perception noise. Conversely, real-world benchmarks (e.g., ManipulationNet, RoboChallenge, RoboArena) provide direct assessment of policy performance under physical conditions but are typically resource-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to reproduce due to variations in hardware, scene setup, and evaluation protocols.

The current work, RoboDojo, positions itself as an integrated solution that bridges these limitations. It seeks to provide a unified sim-and-real benchmark that combines the diagnostic capabilities and scalability of simulation with the physical fidelity and reproducibility of real-world evaluation. By addressing the narrow task scope, limited capability coverage, and evaluation environment separation prevalent in prior work, RoboDojo aims to offer a more holistic assessment of generalist robot manipulation policies. It expands on previous benchmarks by introducing a greater diversity of challenging tasks, covering multiple manipulation capabilities simultaneously, and establishing standardized protocols for real-world testing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RoboDojo is to systematically and comprehensively evaluate the capabilities and limitations of generalist robot manipulation policies. The researchers identify several motivations and specific goals:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:**
 - **Narrow Task Scope:** Many current benchmarks rely on tasks that are simple, short-horizon, or skill-narrow, often sharing similar manipulation patterns. This limits their ability to provide diagnostic coverage across distinct and complementary challenges. RoboDojo aims to introduce more diverse tasks to evaluate a broader spectrum of manipulation skills.
 - **Limited Capability Dimensions:** Existing benchmarks frequently focus on specific aspects like distributional robustness (e.g., object or layout variations) but lack systematic evaluation across critical capability dimensions such as generalization to unseen scenarios, memory-dependent manipulation, precise control, long-horizon sequential execution, open-vocabulary instruction following, bimanual coordination, and tool use. RoboDojo is designed to provide specific tasks targeting these dimensions.
 - **Separation of Simulation and Real-World Evaluation:** The dichotomy between efficient, scalable simulation and costly, hard-to-reproduce real-world evaluation is a significant limitation. Simulation offers rapid feedback but cannot fully capture physical deployment challenges, while real-world evaluation provides direct evidence but lacks efficiency and standardization. RoboDojo intends to unify these two environments.

- **Enabling Comprehensive and Reproducible Evaluation:**
 - **Broad Capability Coverage:** Develop a simulation benchmark with 42 tasks organized into five distinct capability dimensions: Generalization, Memory, Precision, Long-Horizon, and Open. These dimensions are designed to evaluate aspects beyond simple object or layout variations.
 - **Challenging Real-World Validation:** Complement simulation with 18 real-world tasks across three robot embodiments. These tasks are designed to expose policies to challenging physical deployment conditions like contact-rich interactions, perception noise, and actuation errors, which are difficult to model in simulation.
 - **Efficient and Scalable Feedback:** Implement heterogeneous parallel simulation in Isaac Sim to improve evaluation throughput, allowing for faster policy iteration and capability-oriented diagnosis.
 - **Standardized Real-World Testing:** Introduce RoboDojo-RealEval, a reproducible real-world evaluation system with standardized hardware setup, scene reset procedures, evaluation protocols, and deployment interfaces. This aims to ensure consistent physical testing conditions and enable remote cloud access for evaluation.
 - **Unified Development and Deployment Infrastructure:** Provide XPolicyLab, an infrastructure that standardizes policy development, training, and deployment. This allows policies to be integrated once and evaluated across both simulation and real-world settings with minimal adaptation.

- **Fostering Progress in Generalist Robotics:**
 - Establish a public leaderboard with systematic analysis of policy performance to track advancements and identify current limitations.
 - Facilitate a unified evaluation loop that supports fast iteration in simulation and validates improvements in reproducible real-world settings.
 - Encourage community contributions and open-source development through governance by a non-profit foundation.

By addressing these motivations, RoboDojo aims to provide a robust platform for advancing research toward more capable and generalist robot manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboDojo employs a multi-faceted methodology encompassing benchmark construction, technical implementation, and strict evaluation integrity protocols to achieve its objectives.

#### 3.1. RoboDojo Benchmark Construction

The benchmark is structured into distinct simulation and real-world components.

#### 3.1.1. Simulation Benchmark

- **Task Design:** Comprises 42 tasks for the ARX X5 bimanual platform, categorized into five capability dimensions:
 - **Generalization (12 tasks):** Evaluates robustness to unseen variations in backgrounds, lighting, clutter (up to 25 objects), and target objects. Includes 100 supplementary DLC (Domain Level Control) trajectories for data-level augmentation.
 - **Memory (6 tasks):** Assesses long-context observation modeling and non-Markovian decision making, requiring policies to remember past events (e.g., object categories, demonstration sequences).
 - **Long-Horizon (8 tasks):** Challenges policies to execute sequences of dependent steps, infer task structure, and maintain progress without accumulating errors (e.g., sorting multiple objects, requiring bimanual handovers).
 - **Precision (8 tasks):** Focuses on fine-grained manipulation, requiring accurate visual grounding, smooth motion prediction, and stable local control under strict spatial tolerances (e.g., inserting tubes into narrow holes).
 - **Open (8 tasks):** Evaluates open-semantic grounding, skill recombination, and language-conditioned transfer to unseen task specifications, where required skills exist in training data but not the exact task.
- **Training Data Setting:** Provides 3,500 trajectories (20.66 hours) across 35 task directories (Generalization, Memory, Long-Horizon, Precision, plus auxiliary DLC). Data is collected via automated trajectory synthesis or VR-based teleoperation at 25 Hz, including robot states, language annotations, and multi-view RGB-D observations. The Open dimension is excluded from training.
- **Evaluation Setting:** All 42 simulation tasks are evaluated for 50 episodes each (2,100 total episodes). For Generalization tasks, 25 episodes are standard, and 25 are randomized. Performance is measured by success rate and average score, aggregated as the mean across the five capability dimensions.

#### 3.1.2. Real-World Benchmark

- **Task Design:** Features 18 real-world tasks (6 per embodiment) across three bimanual robot platforms: ARX X5, Piper, and Piper X. These tasks are selected to expose policies to contact-rich interactions, perception noise, actuation errors, and embodiment-specific constraints. There is no one-to-one mapping with simulation tasks; rather, real-world tasks serve as a complementary test of physical deployability.
- **Training Data Setting:** Collects 1,800 trajectories (17.91 hours) using a homogeneous leader-follower teleoperation setup. Each task has 100 demonstrations from four operators. Data includes robot states, language annotations, and synchronized RGB observations from head and wrist cameras. All trajectories are manually inspected for quality and consistency.
- **Evaluation Setting:** Policies are evaluated for 10 trials per task using the RoboDojo-RealEval platform. Pre-collected evaluation layouts are replayed to ensure consistent initial conditions. End-effector control commands are converted to robot motions using the Pink motion planner. Trials have a maximum execution horizon and can be manually stopped for safety. Evaluation videos are independently scored by three double-blind evaluators, considering both final success and intermediate progress.

#### 3.2. Technical Implementation

**4.2.1. Simulation Platform:** Built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab (inheriting from MagicSim).
- **Configuration-Driven Setup:** Tasks are defined via modular YAML specifications, decoupling task logic from simulator execution. This enables diverse yet reproducible scene instances (objects, poses, clutter, lighting, backgrounds) under deterministic seed control.
- **Physically Grounded Digital Asset Library:** Includes rigid, articulated, and deformable objects collected from online repositories, reconstructed real objects, and existing datasets (e.g., ClothesNet). Assets are annotated with semantic and task-level metadata (e.g., graspable regions) and validated for physical reliability.
- **Heterogeneous Parallelism:** Allows multiple environments within the same simulator process to run different object categories, asset geometries, clutter layouts, and task configurations concurrently. This improves evaluation speed while preserving scene diversity. Multi-GPU sharding further extends scalability.
- **Simulation Data Collection:** Supports automated trajectory synthesis (composing low-level skills using cuRobo v2 motion planner) and VR-based teleoperation (mapping VR controller inputs to end-effector motions).

**4.2.2. Real-World Platform: RoboDojo-RealEval:** A standardized hardware and software system for reproducible physical evaluation.
- **Standardized Hardware:** Fixed-height white table workspace (1.2m x 1.2m), external frame (1.5m x 1.5m x 2.1m) with black curtains, three linear LED lights for stable illumination, custom structural components for fixed robot and camera poses. Supports ARX X5, Piper, and Piper X robots.
- **Controlled Workflow:** Touchscreen-based web interface for scene reset, policy execution, emergency stop, and video collection. Layout replay uses transparent overlays to guide evaluators in restoring consistent initial conditions.
- **Remote Evaluation:** Provides cloud-based remote access, allowing users to submit policies and evaluate them under shared physical test configurations.

**4.2.3. XPolicyLab:** A unified infrastructure for policy development and deployment.
- **Standardized Interfaces:** Defines common data formats (unified observation/action format, pose conventions), a Model abstraction with `update_obs`, `get_action`, and `reset` methods (including batched versions), and an offline debug mode.
- **Deployment Protocol:** Uses a lightweight client-server communication protocol (WebSocket and MessagePack) for low-latency action queries between policy servers and environment clients (simulator or real robot).
- **Package Organization:** Standardizes policy package structure (installation, data processing, training, model serving, evaluation scripts) to enhance reproducibility and comparison.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Integrity and Anti-Gaming Protocols

- **Leaderboard Governance:** Maintained by AI MMLab Club, a non-profit foundation, with joint operation by global academic partners. No commercial funding or sponsorship.
- **Official Remote Evaluation:** Policies must be evaluated via the RoboDojo online system (submitting packages or connecting remote servers). Scores are computed by the official system.
- **Reproducibility Requirements:** Simulation results require evaluation over three random seeds; real-world results cover all three robot embodiments.
- **Hidden Verification:** Submitted models are also evaluated on hidden, randomized layouts to detect overfitting to public layouts.
- **Open-Source Artifact:** For official verified leaderboard publication, participants must release training/deployment code, checkpoints, configuration files, and reproducibility instructions via XPolicyLab, along with evaluation videos for community inspection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboDojo provides extensive experimental results from evaluating 30 policies on its simulation benchmark and 10 policies on its real-world benchmark, including human expert teleoperation as a reference.

#### 4.1. Analysis of RoboDojo Simulation Performance

- **Substantial Gap to Generalist Manipulation:** The best-performing policy achieved an 8.80% average success rate and a 13.07 average score, significantly below human experts (76.03% success rate, 80.42 score). This indicates that current policies are far from reliable in diverse manipulation scenarios.
- **Unbalanced Capability Development:** Policies show fragmented strengths across dimensions. Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA, Spatial Forcing, and π0.5 are among the leaders, but no single policy excels consistently across all dimensions. Precision, Memory, and Open tasks remain particularly challenging.
- **Performance Collapse under Scene-Level Randomization:** Scene randomization in the Generalization dimension caused significant performance drops across nearly all policies. For example, Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA experienced a 92.9% relative score drop from standard to random settings. While explicit 3D spatial grounding (e.g., Spatial Forcing) showed some improvement in robustness, absolute performance remained low, indicating sensitivity to visual and spatial distribution shifts. Failures often involved low-level control and recovery, not just high-level recognition.
- **Partial Progress on Long-Horizon Tasks, Difficult Skill Composition:** Long-Horizon tasks showed relatively better performance compared to Precision, Memory, and Open dimensions (best success rate <15%). Policies could often complete partial stages, but frequently failed to reach the final goal due to accumulated errors, indicating a lack of reliable skill composition, stage-level decision-making, and error recovery.
- **Precision as a Major Bottleneck:** Precision tasks proved highly challenging. The top policy, X-VLA, achieved only a 12.00% success rate. Failures often stemmed from weak state-conditioned correction and action jitter, suggesting issues with robust 3D localization, smooth action prediction, and closed-loop contact control. Policies often followed open-loop action sequences without verifying preconditions.
- **Memory Mechanisms Help, but Memory-Conditioned Execution Remains Critical:** While policies with explicit memory (Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA, EventVLA) or world models (X-WAM) showed some advantages in Memory tasks, their success rates remained low. The challenge lies not just in remembering past observations but in effectively guiding state-conditioned decisions and robust action execution based on that memory.
- **Open-Semantic Manipulation Largely Unsolved:** The Open dimension was the most challenging, with policies achieving near-zero performance (best success rate 1.67%). This highlights a critical limitation in robust semantic-to-action grounding, where policies struggle to interpret novel language instructions, identify functional relations, and recombine skills in unfamiliar ways, even if individual skills exist in training data.

#### 4.2. Analysis of RoboDojo Real-World Performance

- **Unreliable Real-World Deployment:** The best policy, π0.5, achieved only a 12.8% overall success rate, far below human experts' 100%. This indicates that even policies successful in simulation are not reliably deployable in the physical world.
- **Partial Progress Rarely Translates to Completion:** High scores relative to success rates suggest policies often make partial progress but fail to complete full tasks. Real-world failures are attributed to execution bottlenecks such as precise alignment, contact handling, sub-step transitions, and recovery from errors under physical uncertainty.
- **Partial Alignment Between Sim and Real Performance:** While some policies like π0.5 performed well in both environments, others showed discrepancies (e.g., InternVLA-A1 and GalaxeaVLA performed better in real-world relative to their simulation ranking). This underscores that real-world factors (perception noise, calibration errors, actuation latency, contact instability) are not fully captured by simulation, necessitating a unified sim-and-real benchmark.
- **Exposure of Execution Instability and Safety-Critical Behaviors:** Real-world evaluation revealed deployment-specific failure modes beyond aggregate metrics, such as action jitter, oscillatory motions, repeated ineffective actions, and unsafe contacts. These issues, while merely reducing scores in simulation, pose hardware risks in reality, indicating a need for policies to optimize for smoothness, contact-aware feedback, and bounded control.

#### 4.3. Evaluation Efficiency

- **Simulation Efficiency:** Heterogeneous parallel simulation significantly improved throughput. Under zero-action rollouts, RoboDojo achieved a 1.94x speedup over non-heterogeneous parallel simulation (77.4 interactions/s vs. 40.0 interactions/s). With π0.5 policy inference, it still showed a 1.63x speedup (64.0 interactions/s vs. 39.2 interactions/s), enabling large-scale evaluation and rapid feedback.
- **Real-World Efficiency:** RoboDojo-RealEval completed the 18-task evaluation (180 physical trials) in approximately 3.4 hours (202.0 minutes). Each 10-trial task averaged 11.2 minutes, indicating that the standardized setup and local policy deployment reduce operational overhead for physical benchmarking.

#### 4.4. Evaluation Stability

- **Simulation Stability:** RoboDojo demonstrated consistent simulation evaluation results across different RTX 4090 GPUs. The largest cross-GPU standard deviation for success rate was 1.1 percentage points, and for score, it was 1.07, supporting its suitability for fair leaderboard comparison despite minor non-determinism.
- **Real-World Stability:** RoboDojo-RealEval produced stable aggregate real-world results across three independent evaluation rounds. The standard deviation for overall success rate was at most 1.3 percentage points, and for score, 1.2 points. Task-level variance was observed, as expected in real-world scenarios due to subtle physical differences, but these variations were averaged out at the aggregate level, supporting the repeatability of the platform.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboDojo represents a notable contribution to the field of robot manipulation by introducing a unified sim-and-real benchmark designed for comprehensive, efficient, and reproducible evaluation of generalist robot policies. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key areas:

- **Comprehensive Capability Diagnosis:** By designing 42 simulation tasks across five distinct capability dimensions (Generalization, Memory, Precision, Long-Horizon, and Open), RoboDojo provides a granular diagnostic tool. This moves beyond simple aggregate metrics to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of policies, thereby guiding targeted research and development efforts. The detailed analysis of current policy limitations, such as the struggles with open-semantic grounding and contact-rich precision, directly informs future research priorities.
- **Bridging the Simulation-to-Real Gap:** The integration of a substantial real-world benchmark with standardized protocols (RoboDojo-RealEval) addresses a critical challenge in robot learning. It provides a means to assess whether advancements in simulation translate effectively to the physical world, revealing deployment-specific bottlenecks (e.g., perception noise, actuation errors, contact instability) that are often obscured in simulation-only evaluations. This unified approach facilitates a more complete and realistic understanding of policy performance.
- **Enhanced Reproducibility and Fairness:** RoboDojo-RealEval's standardized hardware setup, scene reset procedures, and remote cloud-based access significantly improve the reproducibility of real-world robot experiments. This standardization promotes fair comparisons between different policies and reduces the influence of incidental factors, a long-standing challenge in real-robot research. The open-source artifacts and strict governance protocols further bolster trust and verifiability within the research community.
- **Accelerated Development and Iteration:** The implementation of heterogeneous parallel simulation substantially boosts evaluation efficiency, allowing for faster policy iteration cycles. Coupled with XPolicyLab's unified infrastructure for policy development and deployment, RoboDojo lowers the engineering barrier for researchers, enabling them to integrate, train, and evaluate policies across both simulated and physical environments with reduced overhead.
- **Community Building and Progress Tracking:** The establishment of a public leaderboard, maintained by a non-profit foundation and supported by academic partners, fosters healthy competition and transparent tracking of progress in generalist robot manipulation. It provides a common reference point for the field, highlighting leading approaches and identifying areas requiring further innovation.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed findings from RoboDojo—such as the severe performance gap with human experts, the fragility of generalization under scene randomization, the difficulties in skill composition for long-horizon tasks, and the nascent state of open-semantic manipulation—provide concrete directions for future research. It emphasizes the need for robust visual-spatial grounding, contact-aware control, memory-conditioned execution, and stronger multimodal alignment to achieve truly generalist robotic capabilities.
- **Extensibility:** RoboDojo is designed as an extensible platform, with plans for future expansion to dexterous, humanoid, tactile, and mobile manipulation. This forward-looking design positions it as a foundational platform for evaluating a broader spectrum of embodied AI challenges as the field evolves.

In summary, RoboDojo provides a robust and comprehensive framework that is poised to become a central benchmark for evaluating and advancing the capabilities of generalist robot manipulation policies, addressing current limitations and charting a course for future innovation in embodied intelligence.
