# UniDex: A Robot Foundation Suite for Universal Dexterous Hand Control from Egocentric Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> UniDex introduces a robot foundation suite that enables universal dexterous hand control by transforming egocentric human videos into a large-scale robot-centric dataset and leveraging a unified action space for diverse robot hands. The suite achieves 81% average task progress on real-world tasks, enables zero-shot skill transfer across different hand embodiments, and offers an efficient human-robot co-training setup that reduces data collection costs.

## Problem

- Collecting large, broadly usable pretraining datasets for dexterous hands is labor-intensive and costly due to the unique challenges of teleoperation.
- The wide variation in degrees of freedom (DoFs), morphology, and kinematics across different dexterous hands prevents effective data and policy transfer.
- Existing robot foundation policies and VLA models predominantly cater to parallel-jaw grippers, leaving dexterous manipulation underserved by generalist approaches.

## Method

- Develop UniDex-Dataset, a large-scale robot-centric dataset derived from egocentric human videos using a novel ==kinematic and visual retargeting pipeline==.
- Introduce UniDex-VLA, a 3D vision-language-action policy trained with a ==Function Actuator Aligned Space (FAAS)== that unifies control across diverse dexterous hand embodiments.
- Provide UniDex-Cap, a portable hardware-software setup for efficient ==human-robot data co-training==, transforming real-time human demonstrations into robot-executable trajectories.

## Results

- UniDex-VLA achieved **81%** average task progress on five challenging real-world tool-use tasks, showing a significant performance improvement over baselines, with pretraining being critical.
- The system demonstrated zero-shot skill transfer to unseen robot hands, achieving **60%** success on the Oymotion Hand and **40%** on the Wuji Hand for tasks trained on a different embodiment.
- UniDex-Cap enabled efficient human-robot co-training, with an approximate 2:1 human-to-robot data exchange rate for fine-tuning, which reduced the overall data collection cost by **5.2x**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Appropriately retargeted egocentric human videos can generate a large-scale, diverse, and robot-centric dataset, effectively mitigating the data scarcity in dexterous manipulation.
> - A unified Function
Actuator
Aligned Space (FAAS) allows for effective zero-shot skill transfer and control policies that generalize across highly diverse dexterous robot hands with varying DoFs and morphologies.
> - Combining large-scale pretraining with 3D vision-language-action policies provides strong motion priors and spatial awareness, enabling efficient adaptation to complex, real-world dexterous tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly dexterous manipulation, faces several challenges. Learning from demonstrations has become a common paradigm for visuomotor control, allowing robots to acquire complex skills. However, achieving general, human-level manipulation remains difficult due to the labor-intensive and costly nature of collecting real-robot teleoperation data, which leads to a data bottleneck. Most existing robot foundation policies and vision-language-action (VLA) models primarily focus on parallel-jaw grippers. Dexterous hands, despite being essential for everyday tool-use and tasks infeasible with grippers, have seen limited development of foundation models due to unique challenges.

These challenges include: (i) the difficulty and scarcity of collecting large, broadly usable pretraining datasets for dexterous hands; (ii) the significant variation in degrees of freedom (DoFs), morphology, kinematics, and appearance among dexterous hands, which hinders data and policy transfer; and (iii) the inherent high dimensionality of dexterous hand control, requiring specialized action spaces and learning algorithms.

Previous efforts to address data scarcity have involved learning from human videos. However, these methods often introduce significant visual and kinematic domain gaps between human and robot hands. Approaches include using human hand trajectories for planning, retargeting with sim-to-real pipelines, human-in-the-loop corrections, or co-training with robot data. While some methods pretrain on egocentric human videos to predict human hand motion, they often require complex, specialized alignment stages to adapt to robot actions during fine-tuning.

This research aims to address these issues by developing a robot foundation suite that explicitly tackles the data scarcity, embodiment heterogeneity, and high-dimensionality of dexterous manipulation. It leverages abundant egocentric human video data, transforms it into a robot-centric format, introduces a unified action space for cross-hand transfer, and provides a practical human-robot co-training setup. The work positions itself as a step toward more general, scalable, and transferable dexterous manipulation by providing a comprehensive solution that builds upon and extends prior work on VLA models and human-to-robot transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop "UniDex," a comprehensive robot foundation suite designed for universal dexterous hand control across heterogeneous robot embodiments. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in current robotic manipulation research.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Dexterous Hand Data Scarcity:**
 - **Motivation:** Collecting real-robot teleoperation data for dexterous hands is time-consuming, expensive, and scales poorly, leading to a significant data bottleneck. This limits the ability to train robust and generalizable dexterous manipulation policies.
 - **Objective:** To overcome this, the authors aim to leverage readily available egocentric human videos. The rationale is that dexterous robot hands are often designed to mimic human hands, and humans naturally generate extensive manipulation data in daily life. This human data is cheaper and more diverse than robot teleoperation data.
 - **Approach:** Constructing UniDex-Dataset by transforming human egocentric videos into robot-executable trajectories, thereby creating a large-scale, robot-centric pretraining dataset.

2. **Enabling Control Across Heterogeneous Hand Embodiments:**
 - **Motivation:** Dexterous hands vary widely in their physical characteristics, including DoFs (ranging from 6 to 24), morphology, kinematics, and appearance. This heterogeneity makes it challenging to transfer data and learned policies from one hand to another, limiting the universality of manipulation systems. Simply porting gripper-based VLA designs is insufficient for this complexity.
 - **Objective:** To enable skill transfer and unified control across a broad spectrum of dexterous hand platforms.
 - **Approach:** Designing the Function–Actuator–Aligned Space (FAAS), a unified action space that maps functionally similar actuators across different hands to shared coordinates. This function-centric control interface aims to abstract away embodiment-specific details while preserving task-relevant control semantics, facilitating cross-hand generalization.

3. **Managing High-Dimensional Dexterous Control:**
 - **Motivation:** Dexterous hand control involves a high number of DoFs, which demands expressive action spaces and effective learning algorithms. This high dimensionality exacerbates the data scarcity problem and makes policy learning more complex.
 - **Objective:** To develop a robust and effective policy that can handle the high-dimensional control requirements of dexterous hands while being pretrained on diverse data.
 - **Approach:** Training UniDex-VLA, a 3D vision-language-action policy pretrained on the large-scale UniDex-Dataset and then finetuned with specific task demonstrations. This policy is designed to reason about fine 3D geometry and contact affordances from egocentric single-view observations, aligning geometric perception with the unified FAAS action space.

4. **Facilitating Efficient Human-Robot Data Co-training:**
 - **Motivation:** Even with a large pretraining dataset derived from human videos, fine-tuning for specific tasks still benefits from, or may require, some amount of real-robot data. Reducing the reliance on costly robot demonstrations during this fine-tuning stage is beneficial.
 - **Objective:** To provide a practical and efficient means for human-robot data co-training, lowering the overall data collection cost.
 - **Approach:** Developing UniDex-Cap, a portable capture setup that records synchronized RGB-D streams and human hand poses. This setup converts the captured human data into robot-executable trajectories using the same transformation pipeline as UniDex-Dataset, enabling it to be effectively combined with smaller amounts of robot data.

In summary, the key objectives are to establish a scalable foundation for dexterous manipulation through a diverse, robot-centric dataset derived from human videos, a unified VLA policy capable of cross-hand generalization, and a practical system for efficient human-robot data co-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of UniDex comprises three main components: UniDex-Dataset, UniDex-VLA, and UniDex-Cap, designed to collectively address the challenges of dexterous manipulation.

#### 3.1. UniDex-Dataset: Robot-Centric Data Generation

**Overview:** UniDex-Dataset is a large-scale, diverse, and robot-centric dataset derived from existing egocentric human manipulation videos. It aims to provide sufficient data for pretraining universal dexterous hand policies.

**Data Sources:** The dataset is constructed from four open-source RGB-D egocentric human manipulation datasets: H2O, HOI4D, HOT3D, and TACO. Language instructions are annotated where needed, and videos are segmented into task-aligned trajectory clips, with invalid segments filtered out.

**Human-Robot Transformation Pipeline:** A core innovation is the pipeline to transform human video data into robot-executable trajectories, addressing kinematic and visual domain gaps.

1. **Kinematic Retargeting:**
 - **Fingertip Alignment:** The primary goal is to align human fingertip trajectories with those of the robot hand in 3D, while allowing for a global hand-base adjustment to ensure physically plausible hand-object contacts.
 - **Inverse Kinematics (IK) with Alignment Offset:** Given a human hand pose, `m` fingertip targets are extracted. A 6-DoF alignment offset (dummy base) is introduced before the real robot base. The IK problem minimizes the difference between the robot's fingertip positions (calculated via forward kinematics, incorporating the dummy base and an offset) and the human fingertip targets.
 - **Mimic Joint Handling:** For robot hands with mimic joint structures (e.g., Inspire, Oymotion, Agility), an iterative correction process updates dependent joints based on their master joints according to kinematic model specifications, repeated until convergence of fingertip error.
 - **Human-in-the-Loop Procedure:** This is a two-stage process:
 - *Automatic Stage:* An initial IK solution is obtained using PyBullet's multi-end-effector IK solver, minimizing fingertip error while respecting joint limits.
 - *Interactive Stage:* A user-friendly GUI allows manual adjustment of the 6-DoF `T_offset` (three translations and three rotations) and other IK parameters. The user visually inspects alignment, particularly for contact-rich segments, and iteratively refines `T_offset` until plausible hand-object contacts are achieved. This calibration is performed once per human dataset and robot hand to account for systematic differences.

2. **Visual Alignment:**
 - **Pointcloud Processing:** Pointclouds are computed from RGB-D frames.
 - **Human Hand Masking:** Human hands are masked using WiLoR and SAM2, and corresponding points are removed from the pointcloud.
 - **Robot Hand Insertion:** The retargeted robot hand mesh is placed into the scene, and its geometry is rendered into the pointcloud.
 - **Reprojection:** The fused pointcloud is reprojected back to the RGB-D frame via a pinhole camera model to prevent occlusions, matching the single-view setting used during real-world fine-tuning.

**Dataset Scale and Diversity:** The resulting UniDex-Dataset comprises 9 million paired image–pointcloud–action frames (at 30 fps) and over 50,000 trajectories across eight dexterous hand platforms (Inspire, Leap, Shadow, Allegro, Ability, Oymotion, Xhand, Wuji), covering active DoFs from 6 to 24.

#### 3.2. UniDex-VLA: Unified Vision-Language-Action Policy

**Unified Action Space: Function–Actuator–Aligned Space (FAAS):**
- **Concept:** FAAS is a function-centric, unified action representation designed to enable skill transfer across diverse dexterous hand embodiments. It maps functionally similar actuators (controllable DoFs) to shared coordinates, abstracting away hand-specific kinematic details.
- **Structure:** FAAS is an 82-dimensional vector:
 - The first 18 dimensions encode wrist poses (9 dimensions per hand, each consisting of a 6D continuous rotation representation and 3D translation).
 - The remaining 64 dimensions encode joint commands, with 32 slots reserved for each hand. Of these, 21 base actuator slots are shared across all hands, and the rest are for hand-specific DoFs or future hands. For example, joints responsible for thumb-index pinch or finger curling are mapped to consistent FAAS indices across different hands.

**VLA Policy Architecture:**
- **Input Observation:** At time `t`, the policy receives `o_t = [P_t, l_t, q_t]`, where `P_t` is a single-view colored pointcloud, `l_t` is a natural-language instruction, and `q_t` is a vector of robot proprioceptive states (both `q_t` and action outputs `a_t` are in FAAS).
- **Action Output:** The policy predicts an `H`-step action chunk `A_t = [a_t..., a_{t+H-1}]`, representing relative wrist pose and abstracted joint commands.
- **Model Structure:** The architecture builds on π0 but replaces its 2D vision encoder with Uni3D, a 3D pointcloud encoder. Uni3D, a vanilla ViT design, is initialized from a 2D pretrained ViT to align pointcloud features with image-text features.
- **Training Objective:** The policy is trained using a conditional flow-matching objective, which learns a vector field to generate denoised action chunks at inference time via forward-Euler integration.
- **Training Phases:** UniDex-VLA undergoes large-scale pretraining on UniDex-Dataset, followed by finetuning with task-specific demonstrations.

#### 3.3. UniDex-Cap: Human-Robot Data Co-training Setup

**Purpose:** UniDex-Cap is a practical, portable data-capture setup designed to efficiently collect human demonstrations and convert them into robot-executable trajectories for co-training with real-robot data. This aims to reduce the overall data collection cost.

**Hardware Configuration:** It combines an Apple Vision Pro for human hand and head pose estimation with an Intel RealSense L515 for high-quality RGB-D streams. These two sensors are physically coupled using a custom 3D-printed mount, ensuring a rigid and stable relative transform.

**Calibration:** A lightweight GUI is provided for estimating the constant extrinsic transform between the Vision Pro and RealSense. Users record a short clip and adjust hand/wrist poses (visualized as a skeleton) to align with the 3D hand point cloud from RealSense. This ensures time-synchronized and geometrically consistent annotations.

**Data Conversion Pipeline:** Captured human data is fed through the same human-to-robot transformation pipeline (Section 3.2) used for UniDex-Dataset. Additionally, a viewpoint transformation aligns human and robot perspectives, and human motion is downsampled to match typical teleoperation speeds. This enables seamless integration of transformed human data with real-robot data during policy fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluated UniDex-VLA's performance on real-world tasks, its generalization capabilities, and the effectiveness of UniDex-Cap for human-robot data co-training.

#### 4.1. Performance

- **Benchmark Tasks:** UniDex-VLA was evaluated on five challenging real-world tool-use tasks: Make Coffee, Sweep Objects, Water Flowers, Cut Bags, and Use Mouse. These tasks involved an Inspire Hand (6 active DoFs) and a Wuji Hand (20 active DoFs) mounted on a 7-DoF Franka robotic arm.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** UniDex-VLA was compared against Diffusion Policy (DP), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), and π0 (a strong VLA baseline). A version of UniDex-VLA without pretraining was also included as an ablation. All methods were finetuned with 50 demonstrations per task.
- **Results:** UniDex-VLA achieved an 81% average task progress across all five tasks, significantly outperforming the baselines. For instance, π0 achieved 38% average task progress. UniDex-VLA demonstrated its largest performance gain on the "Use Scissors to Cut Bags" task, showing an 84.6% increase in average task progress compared to the best competing method. The performance difference between UniDex-VLA (No Pretrain) and UniDex-VLA highlighted the substantial benefit derived from pretraining on UniDex-Dataset. These results indicate that pretraining provides strong motion priors for dexterous control, enabling efficient adaptation to complex behaviors.

#### 4.2. Generalization

UniDex-VLA exhibited generalization across spatial configurations, objects, and hands.

- **Spatial Generalization:** In the "Make Coffee" task, UniDex-VLA demonstrated robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) placements of the kettle and dripper. By augmenting training data with geometrically edited pointclouds (translating objects along x/y axes), UniDex-VLA maintained high success rates across a wider workspace, attributed to its 3D perception and the spatial awareness imparted by pretraining.
- **Object Generalization:** When the original black kettle was replaced with an unseen, smaller purple kettle with different colors, sizes, and functional parts (handle, spout), UniDex-VLA maintained strong performance. This suggests the policy's capacity for generalizable tool understanding, which is important for robust tool-use.
- **Hand Generalization (Zero-shot Skill Transfer):** A policy trained for the "Make Coffee" task on the Inspire Hand (6 active DoF) was deployed zero-shot (without fine-tuning) on the Wuji Hand (20 active DoFs) and Oymotion Hand (6 active DoFs, different kinematics). UniDex-VLA achieved 60% success on Oymotion and 40% on Wuji. In contrast, baseline methods achieved near-zero success rates. This finding supports the effectiveness of combining pretraining across diverse dexterous hands with the Function–Actuator–Aligned Space (FAAS) for enabling zero-shot cross-hand skill transfer.

#### 4.3. UniDex-Cap for Human-Robot Data Co-training

- **Co-training Study:** A quantitative study was conducted on the "Make Coffee" task using UniDex-Cap to collect human demonstrations, transform them into robot trajectories, and co-train them with real-robot data.
- **Impact of Retargeted Human Data:** The study found that increasing the number of co-trained transformed human demonstrations (h) consistently improved average task progress for a fixed number of robot demonstrations (r). However, performance remained near zero without any real-robot data, indicating that robot data is indispensable.
- **Human–Robot Exchange Rate:** The results suggested an approximate 2:1 exchange rate, meaning roughly two transformed human demonstrations could substitute for one real-robot demonstration to achieve comparable performance. For instance, obtaining a success rate of ~87% could be achieved with 50 robot demos, or with 10 robot demos + 70 human demos.
- **Cost Efficiency:** Given that human demonstrations collected via UniDex-Cap were approximately 5.2 times faster to acquire than real-robot demonstrations, co-training with transformed human data significantly reduced the overall data collection cost while preserving performance. This demonstrates that egocentric human videos can both scale pretraining and reduce the need for expensive real-robot data during fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniDex research presents a comprehensive robot foundation suite that addresses several critical challenges in dexterous robotic manipulation, offering a pathway toward more capable and adaptable robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Scalability of Data Collection:** UniDex-Dataset significantly mitigates the data scarcity bottleneck for dexterous manipulation. By converting abundant egocentric human videos into robot-executable trajectories, the approach provides a large-scale, diverse, and robot-centric dataset (9M frames, >50K trajectories, 8 hands). This method is inherently more scalable and cost-effective than collecting real-robot teleoperation data.
- **Universal Dexterous Control:** The Function–Actuator–Aligned Space (FAAS) is a key innovation for enabling general-purpose dexterous manipulation. By unifying the action space across diverse hand embodiments (6-24 DoFs), it allows for skill transfer and control policies that are not tied to specific hardware. This reduces the fragmentation often seen in dexterous robot research, where models are highly specialized.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** UniDex-VLA, through its pretraining on UniDex-Dataset and use of 3D vision, demonstrates high performance on complex, real-world tool-use tasks. Its ability to generalize across spatial configurations, unseen objects, and even zero-shot to entirely new hand embodiments is a notable step towards robust and versatile robot manipulation. This includes tasks requiring fine 3D geometry reasoning and contact affordances.
- **Efficient Human-Robot Collaboration:** UniDex-Cap provides a practical and efficient pipeline for human-robot data co-training. By enabling the rapid collection and transformation of human demonstrations, it can substantially reduce the need for costly real-robot teleoperation during the fine-tuning phase of policy learning. This offers a tangible pathway to lower development costs and accelerate the deployment of dexterous robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The release of UniDex-Dataset, the FAAS action space, UniDex-VLA, and UniDex-Cap forms a foundation suite. The authors also provide protocols for researchers to contribute new hands or human datasets, fostering community-driven growth and accelerating progress in dexterous manipulation research.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Accelerated Development of Dexterous Robots:** By providing a scalable data source and a unified control framework, UniDex can significantly speed up the development and deployment of dexterous robots in various applications, from industrial assembly to assistive robotics.
- **Broader Accessibility of Dexterous Manipulation:** The reduction in data collection costs and the ability to transfer skills across different hardware could make dexterous manipulation more accessible to a wider range of researchers and industries that currently lack extensive robot fleets or teleoperation infrastructure.
- **Human-Robot Co-training Paradigms:** The quantitative study on human-robot data co-training highlights a practical paradigm for leveraging human expertise more efficiently. This could lead to new methodologies for teaching robots complex skills by combining readily available human demonstrations with targeted robot experience.
- **Advancements in Generalist Robot Models:** UniDex contributes to the broader goal of developing generalist robot models. By creating a unified policy that works across heterogeneous dexterous hands and generalizes to new tasks and objects, it pushes the boundaries of what robot foundation models can achieve in complex physical interactions.

#### 5.3. Limitations

The authors acknowledge a limitation in their current work: UniDex does not yet fully leverage large action-free (or weakly labeled) egocentric activity datasets. Integrating such data represents a promising future direction for further scaling dexterous pretraining and potentially enhancing the model's understanding of human intent and interaction patterns even without explicit action supervision.
