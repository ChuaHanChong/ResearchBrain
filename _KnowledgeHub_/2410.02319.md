# QDGset: A Large Scale Grasping Dataset Generated with Quality-Diversity

> [!summary] Summary
> QDGset extends QDG-6DoF, a Quality-Diversity grasp sampler, into a data-augmentation pipeline: object meshes are deformed by per-axis scaling, and grasp archives found on the reference object bootstrap the evolutionary search on each deformed variant. Stopping right after bootstrapping cuts evaluations per robust grasp by up to 20%, yielding 62M 6DoF parallel-jaw grasps over 40k objects.

## Problem

- Prior synthetic grasp datasets sample poses with simple priors like antipodal plus random, making grasp discovery sample-inefficient and costly to scale to many objects.
- Real-world collected or hand-annotated grasp datasets give high quality labels but cannot scale to the volume data-driven grasp policies need for generalization.
- Existing QD grasp sampling restarts optimization from scratch for every new object, discarding prior archives and capping how fast object coverage can grow.

## Method

- Mesh augmentation applies a diagonal per-axis scaling matrix with factors drawn from [0.5, 1.5] to each object point cloud, producing new-but-similar graspable objects.
- QD transfer learning seeds the new object's evolutionary run with the reference object's success archive as initial population, then halts right after the bootstrap generation.
- Dataset assembly merges six object sources (KIT, 3DNet, YCB, GraspNet-1Billion, EGAD, ShapeNet), rescales oversized ShapeNet meshes to YCB-like sizes, and downsamples meshes above 300kB.

## Results

- Across 431 runs, 76% produced more robust grasps when stopped after bootstrapping; on beneficial objects the ratio improved from 20.7 to 16.4 evaluations per robust grasp.
- Transfer succeeded for 78% of 1304 reference objects (10/10 augmentations yielding at least 500 grasps), with augmented-object grasp quality distributions matching the core sets.
- The released dataset holds 62M grasps on 40353 objects, roughly 3.5x the grasps and 4.5x the objects of ACRONYM, mostly 1000-5000 grasps per object.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Warm-starting evolutionary search helps only early: after 100k evaluations the bootstrapped runs fall behind, since inherited populations constrain exploration into local minima.
> - Cheap geometric mesh perturbation plus archive reuse is a general recipe for scaling any simulation-validated dataset, not just grasping.
> - Augmentation range must match the deployment domain; too-small perturbations collapse grasps into center-of-mass alignments and degrade downstream policy quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping is increasingly moving towards data-driven strategies, leveraging advances in artificial intelligence and machine learning. Historically, analytical methods were prevalent, but their limitations in exploration and generalization have led to a surge in data-intensive approaches. These modern methods, such as reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and models based on diffusion, variational autoencoders, or neural representations, require extensive, high-quality datasets to achieve robust generalization capabilities.

Existing grasping datasets are obtained through various means, including real-world collection and annotation, or automatic generation in simulation. While real-world data offers authenticity, it faces scalability challenges. Consequently, synthetic grasping datasets, generated in simulation to validate grasps, have gained prominence. Prominent examples include ACRONYM, which has been utilized to train numerous advanced AI methods. However, these datasets are often generated using relatively simple grasp sampling methods, which may not be maximally efficient in exploring the vast space of possible grasps.

Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms represent a family of optimization techniques designed to produce diverse and high-performing solutions. Recent work, such as the QDG-6DoF framework, has demonstrated that QD can significantly enhance the sample efficiency of grasp generation compared to methods used in prior datasets like ACRONYM. This current work builds upon the QDG-6DoF framework, addressing the need for even larger and more diverse synthetic grasping datasets to further advance data-driven robotic learning. It positions itself by proposing a method to scale up the generation of such datasets, specifically through data augmentation and transfer learning, thereby contributing to the development of more generalizable and adaptable robotic grasping policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to extend the existing QDG-6DoF framework to facilitate the large-scale generation of synthetic grasping datasets. This overarching goal is motivated by several factors:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity for Advanced AI:** Modern data-driven AI techniques for robotic learning, particularly in skill acquisition, necessitate vast amounts of high-quality data to generalize effectively across new objects and scenarios. Skills like grasping remain partially solved due to the challenging exploration aspect, where randomly initialized policies rarely yield successful grasps, making learning difficult without sufficient data.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Current Data Generation:** While synthetic grasping datasets exist and have been impactful (e.g., ACRONYM), their generation methods often rely on simple sampling techniques. These methods can be less sample-efficient, leading to increased computational costs and potential limitations in the diversity of generated grasps.
- **Enhancing Sample Efficiency:** The QDG-6DoF framework has already shown improved sample efficiency compared to previous methods. This work aims to further reduce the number of evaluations required to discover robust grasps, making the data generation process more economical and scalable.
- **Increasing Dataset Size and Diversity:** To enable high-representational-power architectures and efficient foundation models, datasets need to be significantly larger and more diverse than current state-of-the-art offerings. This includes expanding the number of objects and the variety of grasp poses associated with them.
- **Facilitating Collaborative Data Generation:** The authors aim to provide a method that simplifies data generation for the research community, with the ambition of fostering a large-scale, collaborative dataset of synthetic grasps that can be continuously expanded.

Specifically, the work aims to achieve the following:
- Extend QDG-6DoF to incorporate data augmentation by combining transformations of object meshes with a Quality-Diversity transfer learning approach.
- Demonstrate that this extended approach reduces the number of evaluations required to generate a robust grasp compared to existing best methods.
- Introduce QDGset, a large-scale dataset of 6DoF parallel jaw gripper grasps on a substantial number of simulated objects, significantly exceeding the size of previous state-of-the-art datasets.
- Provide a methodology and tools for researchers to easily generate new data and contribute to a growing, collaborative synthetic grasp dataset, supporting sim2real transfer applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on extending the QDG-6DoF framework for generating object-centric 6DoF grasp poses by integrating data augmentation and transfer learning techniques.

**Quality-Diversity (QD):** The core of the data generation process relies on Quality-Diversity algorithms. QD optimization aims to generate a diverse set of high-performing solutions within a defined behavior space. It maintains an archive of solutions, each associated with a behavior descriptor and a fitness value. The goal is to densely cover the reachable behavior space while maximizing fitness within local regions.

**Object-Centric Grasps:** Grasps are represented as 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6DoF) poses relative to the object's frame. For the parallel-jaw gripper considered (a Franka Emika Panda 2-finger gripper, consistent with the ACRONYM dataset), the gripper is assumed to be initially wide open and then closed as much as possible to execute the grasp.

**Data Generation with QDG-6DoF:** The QDG-6DoF framework takes 3D models of a gripper and an object as input. It explores their interactions in simulation to identify grasp poses. It leverages robotic priors (e.g., gripper close to the object surface) and QD mutation-selection mechanisms to efficiently explore the search space and discover successful grasps across the object's surface. The parameters structuring the QD optimization (parameter space Θ, behavior space B, behavior function ϕ_B, fitness function f, distance function d_B, and archive A) are consistent with the vanilla QDG-6DoF.

**Data Augmentation:** To scale up the dataset, a data augmentation method is proposed.
- A set of initial "core" objects P = (P_i) is defined, where each object P_i is represented by a point cloud.
- An augmented object P^a_i is generated by applying a linear deformation φ_D_a to the points of P_i. This deformation is characterized by a diagonal matrix D_a = DIAG(α_1, α_2, α_3), where α_1, α_2, α_3 are scaling factors along the principal directions (x, y, z) of the object, centered at zero.
- The coefficients α_j are randomly chosen from an empirically defined range [0.5, 1.5], ensuring that the augmented objects remain graspable by the target gripper. This process generates an augmented object dataset P' = (P^a_i), where `a_max` is the number of augmentations per core object.

**Transfer Learning (Grasp Transfer):** The augmented objects are then processed using QDG-6DoF, but with a transfer learning approach to reduce computational cost.
- For a given core object, an initial "bootstrapping archive" A_bs of successful grasps is generated using standard QDG-6DoF.
- When generating grasps for a new, augmented version of that core object, A_bs is used as the initial population for the QDG-6DoF optimization process.
- All grasps from A_bs are evaluated once on the augmented object at the initialization generation. The optimization process is then stopped immediately after this bootstrapping step, rather than running for an extended number of evolutionary steps. This approach aims to leverage pre-existing knowledge from similar objects to rapidly obtain new grasp repertoires.

**Object Selection and Preprocessing:**
- The QDGset incorporates object models from six publicly available datasets: KIT, 3DNet, YCB, GraspNet-1Billion, EGAD, and ShapeNet. This selection aims to ensure diversity, including primitive, daily, complex, and adversarial objects.
- To ensure graspability by the Franka Emika Panda gripper, ShapeNet objects, which are often large, were resized. A scaling factor μ_i was applied to each ShapeNet object's point cloud to align its size distribution with that of the YCB dataset (objects graspable by a human hand).
- For efficient simulation, object models larger than 300kB were downsampled to reduce their computational load.
- The QDGset is designed to be scalable; currently, only 27% of the core objects have been augmented, with only 10 augmentations conducted on a small subset of objects as a proof-of-concept.

**Dataset Characteristics (QDGset):** The generated QDGset includes 6DoF parallel jaw gripper grasps. Each grasp is labeled with a probability to transfer to the real world, based on previous research. The dataset aims to provide diverse grasps on diverse objects, including successful and unsuccessful grasp poses, which is beneficial for learning models that utilize negative samples.

**Experimental Evaluation:**
- **Bootstrapping QD:** Experiments were conducted on 450 augmented objects derived from selected KIT and 3DNet reference objects. The performance of QDG-6DoF with bootstrapping was compared to starting from scratch, measuring the number of robust grasps generated per evaluation (a grasp is robust if it survives three trials under domain randomization). The QD optimization in these experiments ran for 100k trials, but the impact of stopping after just the bootstrapping step was also analyzed.
- **Grasp Transfer Efficiency:** The transferability rate was assessed by analyzing how grasps generated on core 3DNet and KIT objects transferred to their augmented counterparts (3DNet-aug and KIT-aug). The quality distributions of transferred grasps were compared to those of the original grasps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Bootstrapping Quality-Diversity:**
- The evaluation of the bootstrapping approach demonstrated a significant improvement in sample efficiency for grasp generation. Across 431 runs on augmented objects, 76% resulted in more robust grasps when the optimization was stopped immediately after the bootstrapping step, compared to starting from scratch.
- For reference objects where bootstrapping was beneficial (e.g., 3DNet n°410, KIT n°5, 22, 111), an average of 309 robust grasps were generated for approximately 5059 evaluations, yielding a ratio of 16.4 evaluations per robust grasp. In contrast, starting from scratch produced an average of 244 robust grasps for the same number of evaluations, resulting in a ratio of 20.7 evaluations per grasp. This represents up to a 20% reduction in the required number of evaluations per discovered robust grasp.
- However, the study also indicated that continuing the QD optimization process for an extended duration (e.g., 100k evaluations) after bootstrapping tended to diminish or even negate the initial gains. The difference in generated grasps between bootstrapped and from-scratch runs after 100k evaluations was close to zero or often negative, suggesting that prolonged transfer from potentially dissimilar objects could constrain exploration and lead to local minima. Consequently, for QDGset generation, the optimization for augmented objects was stopped directly after the bootstrapping evaluations.

**Grasp Transferability:**
- The proposed grasp transfer method proved effective. For 78% of the 1304 considered reference objects, all 10 augmentations successfully transferred at least 500 grasps from their respective core object's archive.
- The quality distributions of grasps generated on augmented datasets (KIT-aug, 3DNet-aug) were found to be similar to those of the core datasets (KIT, 3DNet). This indicates that the transfer process maintains the diversity of grasp qualities (both good and bad), which is a desirable feature for training learning algorithms that benefit from negative samples.

**QDGset Characteristics:**
- **Scale and Diversity:** QDGset contains approximately 62 million 6DoF parallel jaw gripper grasps on about 40,353 simulated objects. This represents a significant expansion compared to the previous state-of-the-art dataset, ACRONYM, which contains 17.7 million grasps on 8,872 objects. QDGset has about 3.5 times more grasps and 4.5 times more objects than ACRONYM.
- **Object Sources:** The dataset integrates objects from KIT, 3DNet, YCB, GraspNet-1Billion, EGAD, and ShapeNet, providing a wide range of object types (primitive, daily, complex, adversarial).
- **Object Rescaling:** ShapeNet objects were resized to be graspable by the chosen gripper and resemble daily objects, with their size distribution shifted to align with YCB objects.
- **Grasps per Object:** Most objects in QDGset yield between 1,000 and 5,000 grasp poses. The average number of grasps per object varies with complexity, e.g., 3391 for KIT and 1286 for ShapeNet, reflecting the challenges posed by different 3D models and the gripper's limitations (some objects were ungraspable).
- **Quality Annotation:** Each grasp is labeled with a quality metric, specifically a probability to transfer in the real world, based on prior research using domain randomization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of robotic grasping by introducing QDGset and, more fundamentally, by providing an efficient, scalable methodology for generating large-scale synthetic grasping datasets.

**Enhanced Data Generation:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating a data augmentation method that combines object mesh transformations with Quality-Diversity (QD) transfer learning. This approach markedly improves the sample efficiency of grasp generation, reducing the number of evaluations needed to find robust grasps by up to 20%. This efficiency gain is crucial for producing the vast quantities of data required by modern AI models.

**Creation of QDGset:** The release of QDGset represents a substantial expansion in the availability of 6DoF grasp poses on a diverse collection of simulated objects. With approximately 62 million grasps on over 40,000 objects, QDGset significantly surpasses existing datasets in scale. This larger and more varied dataset provides a richer resource for training sophisticated learning architectures, potentially leading to more generalized and adaptable robotic grasping policies.

**Facilitating Advanced AI Research:** The availability of such a large and diverse dataset can support the development of high-representational-power architectures and efficient foundation models in robotics. These models rely on extensive data for generalization across different platforms and scenarios. The diverse nature of objects (from daily items to adversarial shapes) and the range of grasp qualities (including both successful and unsuccessful attempts) in QDGset can aid in training more robust and intelligent grasping systems.

**Promoting Collaborative Research:** A key aspect of this work is the proposal of a method that allows researchers to easily generate new data, with the goal of fostering a large-scale, collaborative dataset of synthetic grasps. This modular and extensible approach could accelerate research by enabling community contributions and tailoring datasets to specific application domains.

**Potential for Sim2Real Transfer:** The generated grasps are annotated with a probability of real-world transfer, leveraging previous work on domain randomization. This feature is intended to support the development of grasping policies that are robust enough to transfer from simulation to real-world robotic systems.

**Future Research Directions and Impact:**
- **Multi-finger Grippers:** The methodology can be extended to grippers beyond parallel-jaw types, opening avenues for research into more dexterous manipulation.
- **Semantic Grasping:** Associating grasps with semantic labels (e.g., "grasping a handle for pouring") could integrate low-level grasping skills with high-level robot decision-making, potentially leveraging large language models for more intelligent task execution.
- **Adaptive Augmentation:** Tailoring augmentation strategies (e.g., range of deformations, local perturbations, or different physical properties) to specific application domains could optimize dataset relevance for targeted industrial or domestic scenarios.
- **Advanced Transfer Learning:** Exploring more sophisticated transfer learning methods for QD, such as training models to propose optimal bootstrapping candidates, could further enhance efficiency.

In conclusion, this work provides both a large-scale data resource and an efficient method for its generation, addressing a critical need in data-driven robotic learning. Its contributions aim to advance the development of more capable and generalizable robotic grasping systems.
