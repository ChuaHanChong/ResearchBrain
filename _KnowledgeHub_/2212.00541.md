# Predictive Sampling: Real-time Behaviour Synthesis with MuJoCo

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepMind's MJPC framework, built on MuJoCo, democratizes real-time model predictive control for robotics, providing an open-source, interactive tool that synthesizes complex behaviors efficiently and demonstrates the unexpected effectiveness of simple sampling-based optimization.

## Problem

- Model-based predictive control, while powerful, requires specialized expertise and complex implementations, making it inaccessible to many robotics researchers.
- The slow iteration and debugging cycles in robotics research, due to non-interactive tools and long simulation times, hinder cognitive performance and slow down development.
- A lack of accessible, performant, and transparent model-based optimization tools limits the development of hybrid AI approaches that combine learning and control.

## Method

- ==Introduces MuJoCo MPC (MJPC)==, an open-source, interactive application and software framework built on the high-fidelity and fast MuJoCo physics engine.
- Develops an asynchronous agent-planner architecture and a user-friendly graphical interface that provides real-time feedback for dynamic adjustment of parameters and instant visualization of results.
- Implements various planners, including derivative-based (iLQG, Gradient Descent) and a simple zero-order '**Predictive Sampling**' method, with efficient derivative computation and spline-based control parameterization.

## Results

- MJPC successfully synthesizes complex, robust, and adaptable locomotion and manipulation behaviors for high-dimensional systems (e.g., 27-DOF humanoid, Unitree A1 quadruped, Shadow Hand) in real-time, often with planning times between 1 and 20 milliseconds.
- The interactive GUI significantly enhances policy design and debugging, allowing researchers to observe immediate visual feedback when modifying task or planner parameters.
- The 'Predictive Sampling' algorithm demonstrates unexpected effectiveness, often matching or exceeding the performance of more elaborate derivative-based planners in the tested scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instantaneous feedback through interactive tools is crucial for accelerating robotics research, enabling rapid iteration and deeper understanding of control policies.
> - Simple, zero-order 'Predictive Sampling' can be surprisingly competitive with more complex derivative-based methods in the dynamic, receding-horizon context of real-time predictive control.
> - Prioritizing performant algorithms, simple code, and user accessibility is more impactful for broader research adoption than solely focusing on algorithmic novelty.
