# Convolutional Occupancy Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Convolutional Occupancy Networks combine convolutional feature encoders (projected to canonical planes or a 3D volume, processed by U-Nets) with implicit occupancy decoders, adding translation equivariance and local reasoning to implicit 3D reconstruction. This scales fine-grained shape recovery from single objects to full multi-room indoor scenes and generalizes from synthetic to real data.

## Problem

- Prior implicit reconstruction methods use simple fully-connected architectures with a single global latent code, unable to integrate local observation information.
- Fully-connected implicit models lack inductive biases like translation equivariance, forcing purely global reasoning and overly smooth surfaces.
- Existing implicit approaches are validated only on simple single-object geometry and fail to scale to complex or large-scale multi-room scenes.

## Method

- **Plane/Volume Encoder** projects ==PointNet== or ==3D-CNN== point/voxel features onto canonical 2D planes or a 3D voxel grid via ==average pooling== to build spatial feature maps.
- **Convolutional Decoder** applies 2D or 3D ==U-Net== hourglass networks with skip connections to the pooled features, aggregating local and global context equivariantly.
- **Convolutional Occupancy Prediction** queries ==bilinearly/trilinearly interpolated features== at arbitrary 3D points, fed into a ==ResNet-based fully-connected occupancy network==, enabling ==sliding-window inference== for arbitrarily large scenes.

## Results

- On ShapeNet point-cloud reconstruction, the multi-plane variant reaches **0.884 IoU** versus **0.761** for ONet and **0.689** for PointConv baselines.
- On synthetic indoor scenes, the 64-cubed volumetric variant achieves **0.849 IoU** and **0.964 F-Score** versus **0.475 IoU** and **0.541 F-Score** for ONet.
- On real ScanNet scenes trained only on synthetic data, the 64-cubed volumetric model attains **0.886 F-Score**, beating trimmed Screened Poisson Reconstruction's **0.847**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting convolutional, spatially-local inductive biases into implicit representations lets accuracy and scene scale grow together instead of trading off.
> - Volumetric 3D features generalize better under real-world domain shift and noise than higher-resolution 2D plane features, despite costing more memory.
> - Complementary feature types can be combined for a further accuracy boost, showing planar and volumetric encodings capture partially non-overlapping geometric information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the rapidly evolving field of 3D reconstruction, specifically focusing on the intersection of deep learning and geometric representations. Historically, 3D reconstruction has relied on various explicit representations:

- **Volumetric representations** (e.g., voxels) are intuitive but suffer from cubic memory requirements, limiting their resolution and scalability to large scenes.
- **Point clouds** are lightweight and capture fine details but lack topological information, making it challenging to represent surfaces consistently.
- **Mesh-based representations** explicitly define surfaces with vertices and faces but are often difficult to predict directly with neural networks, especially for arbitrary topologies.

In recent years, a paradigm shift has occurred with the advent of **deep implicit neural representations**. Instead of explicitly discretizing 3D space, these methods learn a continuous function (typically an occupancy probability or signed distance function) that maps any 3D point to a value indicating whether it is inside or outside a shape. Pioneering works like Occupancy Networks (ONet) and DeepSDF demonstrated the power of this approach, offering continuous representations that naturally handle complex topologies and arbitrary resolutions.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation of most existing implicit methods: their reliance on **simple fully-connected network architectures**. This architectural choice restricts them in several ways:
1. **Limited Scalability:** They are primarily successful for reconstructing single objects and struggle with the complexity and scale of entire 3D scenes.
2. **Lack of Local Information Integration:** Fully-connected networks process information globally, failing to effectively incorporate local geometric details and relationships present in the input.
3. **Absence of Inductive Biases:** They do not inherently possess inductive biases like translational equivariance, which are crucial for structured reasoning and generalization in spatial tasks. This leads to overly smooth reconstructions and difficulty handling variations in object pose or scene layout.

"Convolutional Occupancy Networks" (ConvONet) directly addresses these limitations. The paper positions itself as a natural evolution of implicit representations by integrating the well-established strengths of **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**. CNNs have revolutionized 2D computer vision due to their ability to capture hierarchical features, integrate local and global context, and inherently enforce translational equivariance. By porting these advantages to the 3D domain for implicit representations, ConvONet aims to create a more powerful and versatile 3D reconstruction framework.

The authors also meticulously differentiate their work from concurrent or related efforts:
- **PIFu and DISN:** While these also use convolutions with implicit representations, they operate primarily in the 2D image domain, limiting them to image-based inputs and single-object reconstruction. ConvONet, in contrast, is "world-centric," operating directly in 3D space and supporting diverse 3D inputs.
- **Concurrent work like Chibane et al. and Jiang et al.:** The authors acknowledge these, noting that Chibane et al. focuses on single objects and uses lossy discretization, while Jiang et al. relies on 3D point normals and requires optimization at inference. ConvONet, in contrast, demonstrates scene-level reconstruction without such restrictions or additional inference-time optimization.

Thus, ConvONet represents a significant advancement by combining the continuity and topological flexibility of implicit functions with the robustness, scalability, and inductive biases of convolutional architectures, moving implicit 3D reconstruction beyond single objects to complex, large-scale scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Convolutional Occupancy Networks is to overcome the inherent limitations of existing implicit neural representations for 3D reconstruction, particularly their restricted expressiveness, inability to scale to complex scenes, and lack of mechanisms for incorporating local context and inductive biases.

The authors identify four key desirable properties for an ideal 3D geometric representation:
a) **Encode complex geometries and arbitrary topologies:** Implicit representations already excel here, providing continuous surfaces without discretization artifacts or topological constraints.
b) **Scale to large scenes:** This is a major challenge for traditional implicit models due to their global encoding and fully-connected architectures.
c) **Encapsulate local and global information:** Many existing implicit models struggle with integrating local details, leading to overly smooth reconstructions.
d) **Be tractable in terms of memory and computation:** Efficiency is crucial for practical applications, especially with high-resolution or large-scale data.

The motivation for ConvONet stems from the observation that while implicit methods offer continuous and topology-free representations, their reliance on simple fully-connected networks as decoders, often coupled with global feature encoders, leads to several shortcomings:
- **Loss of Detail:** Global encodings struggle to preserve fine-grained geometric details.
- **Poor Generalization to Scenes:** The inability to integrate local information and structured reasoning prevents them from scaling effectively to large, complex scenes with multiple objects and intricate layouts.
- **Lack of Translational Equivariance:** The absence of this crucial inductive bias, naturally present in CNNs, hinders the model's ability to learn robust features that apply across different spatial locations. This means they cannot effectively perform "structured reasoning" in 3D space.

Therefore, the core motivation is to "combine the complementary strengths of convolutional neural networks with those of implicit representations." By doing so, the authors aim to develop a novel, more flexible, and robust implicit representation that can accurately reconstruct detailed objects and entire indoor scenes from noisy, sparse inputs like point clouds or low-resolution voxels, while also generalizing well across different datasets and scenarios.

Specifically, their contributions are summarized as:
- Identifying major limitations of current implicit 3D reconstruction methods.
- Proposing a flexible translation equivariant architecture enabling accurate 3D reconstruction from object to scene level.
- Demonstrating the model's generalization capabilities from synthetic to real scenes and to novel object categories and scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Convolutional Occupancy Networks propose a novel architecture that integrates convolutional operations into the framework of implicit neural representations. The core idea is to replace the simple fully-connected network architecture, which limits previous implicit models, with convolutional encoders and U-Net-based decoders that process spatial feature grids. This allows for incorporating inductive biases like translational equivariance and integrating both local and global information effectively.

The overall model (illustrated in Fig. 2) consists of three main components: an Encoder, a Convolutional Decoder, and an Occupancy Prediction Network.

#### 3.1. Encoder

The encoder's role is to convert the raw 3D input (e.g., a noisy point cloud or a coarse occupancy grid) into a structured feature representation that can be processed by convolutional layers. The method is input-agnostic, but the paper focuses on 3D inputs.
- **Initial Feature Extraction:** A task-specific neural network first processes the input. For voxelized inputs, a one-layer 3D CNN is used. For 3D point clouds, a shallow PointNet with local pooling is employed.
- **Feature Aggregation into Grids:** The extracted point/voxel features are then aggregated into either planar or volumetric feature grids:
 - **Plane Encoder (Fig. 2a):** Input features are orthographically projected onto canonical 2D planes (e.g., the ground plane, or all three axis-aligned planes: XY, XZ, YZ). Features falling into the same 2D pixel cell are aggregated using average pooling. This results in feature planes of resolution H × W × d (where d is feature dimension). The authors explore a single ground plane variant and a multi-plane variant using three canonical planes for richer 3D information.
 - **Volume Encoder (Fig. 2b):** Input features are aggregated into a 3D feature volume by average pooling features within the same voxel cell. This yields a volume of H × W × D × d. While more expensive, it directly captures 3D spatial relationships.

#### 3.2. Decoder

The decoder component is where the convolutional power of the model is unleashed. It processes the feature grids generated by the encoder using U-Net architectures, which are well-suited for capturing both local and global context through downsampling, upsampling, and skip connections. This design inherently endows the model with translation equivariance.
- **Single-Plane Decoder (Fig. 2c):** A 2D U-Net processes the features from a single ground plane.
- **Multi-Plane Decoder (Fig. 2d):** Each of the three canonical feature planes is processed separately by 2D U-Nets with shared weights.
- **Volume Decoder (Fig. 2e):** A 3D U-Net processes the volumetric features.

The choice of U-Net depth ensures that the receptive field covers the entire feature plane or volume, allowing for comprehensive context integration. The convolutional operations enable "inpainting" of features, which is crucial for reconstructing complete shapes from sparse inputs.

#### 3.3. Occupancy Prediction

Given the processed feature maps from the decoder, the model needs to predict the occupancy probability for any arbitrary 3D query point `p` in space.
- **Feature Interpolation:** For a given query point `p`, a point-wise feature vector `ψ(p, x)` is obtained by interpolating the features from the processed grids. Bilinear interpolation is used for 2D planes (single and multi-plane decoders), and trilinear interpolation for 3D volumes (volume decoder). This allows the continuous querying of the implicit function.
- **Fully-Connected Occupancy Network:** The interpolated feature vector `ψ(p, x)` is then concatenated with the query point's coordinates `p` and fed into a small, fully-connected neural network, `fθ(p, ψ(p, x))`. This network, comprising multiple ResNet blocks, outputs the occupancy probability (a value between 0 and 1) for the given point `p`. This part is conceptually similar to the decoder in ONet, but crucially, it now operates on spatially informed convolutional features.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference

- **Training:** During training, query points `p` are uniformly sampled within the volume of interest. The model predicts their occupancy values, and the parameters are optimized using a binary cross-entropy loss against ground truth occupancy. Adam optimizer is used.
- **Inference:** To extract the 3D mesh from the learned implicit function, the Multiresolution IsoSurface Extraction (MISE) algorithm is applied. A key advantage of the fully-convolutional design is its scalability for large scenes: the model can be applied in a "sliding-window" fashion over overlapping crops of the input point cloud, allowing reconstruction of entire apartments or multi-floor buildings.

The methodology strategically combines established strengths: the continuous nature of implicit representations, the local-to-global reasoning and translational equivariance of CNNs, and efficient U-Net architectures for feature processing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted a comprehensive set of experiments to evaluate ConvONet's performance across different scales, input types, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Object-Level Reconstruction (ShapeNet)

- **Inputs:** Noisy point clouds (3000 points with Gaussian noise) and low-resolution voxelizations (32^3).
- **Metrics:** Volumetric IoU, Chamfer Distance (Chamfer-L1), Normal Consistency, and F-Score.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Noisy Point Clouds (Table 1, Fig. 3):** All ConvONet variants significantly outperformed the baselines (ONet and PointConv). The `Ours-2D (3 × 64^2)` (multi-plane) model achieved the best performance across all metrics (e.g., IoU of 0.884 vs. ONet's 0.761) while using less GPU memory than ONet. The `Ours-3D (32^3)` also showed strong performance comparable to `Ours-2D (3 × 64^2)` but used more memory. The results demonstrated that convolutional processing of projected features is more effective than direct point feature aggregation.
 - **Voxel Super-Resolution (Table 2, Fig. 4):** For recovering high-resolution details from coarse 32^3 voxels, the `Ours-2D (3 × 64^2)` and `Ours-3D (32^3)` variants achieved comparable top performance (IoU around 0.75), both significantly outperforming ONet. Notably, the `Ours-2D (3 × 64^2)` variant used only 37% of the GPU memory of the `Ours-3D (32^3)` variant. The single-plane variant (`Ours-2D (64^2)`) struggled here, suggesting multiple views are crucial for resolving ambiguities in coarse voxel inputs.
- **Convergence:** ConvONet demonstrated faster convergence during training compared to baselines (Table 1, right plot).

#### 4.2. Scene-Level Reconstruction (Synthetic Indoor Scene Dataset)

- **Inputs:** Noisy point clouds (10000 points) of synthetic rooms with multiple objects.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** ConvONet variants (Table 3, Fig. 5) consistently and dramatically outperformed learning-based baselines (ONet, PointConv) and even the traditional SPSR (Screened Poisson Surface Reconstruction, even when trimmed).
 - The `Ours-3D (64^3)` variant achieved the best performance (IoU 0.849, Chamfer-L1 0.042, F-Score 0.964), showing the strength of higher-resolution volumetric features for complex scenes.
 - The multi-plane variants (`Ours-2D (3 × 128^2)`) also performed exceptionally well, demonstrating their scalability.
 - The combination of multi-plane and volumetric features (`Ours-2D-3D`) also showed very strong results, suggesting the complementarity of these representations.
- **Qualitative Results:** ConvONet produced smooth, geometrically detailed reconstructions of entire scenes, whereas baselines often resulted in low accuracy or noisy surfaces.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Synthetic Rooms)

- **Performance at Similar GPU Memory (Table 4a):** The multi-plane approach (`Ours-2D (3 × 128^2)`) slightly outperformed the single plane (`Ours-2D (192^2)`) and volumetric (`Ours-3D (32^3)`) approaches when memory usage was similar, indicating an efficient balance of detail and context.
- **Feature Interpolation Strategy (Table 4b):** Bilinear interpolation significantly outperformed nearest neighbor interpolation (IoU 0.805 vs 0.766), confirming its importance for generating smooth and accurate implicit surfaces from the feature grids.

#### 4.4. Reconstruction from Point Clouds on Real-World Datasets (ScanNet v2, Matterport3D)

- **Generalization Test:** Models trained solely on the synthetic indoor scene dataset were evaluated on real-world datasets.
- **ScanNet v2 (Table 5, Fig. 6):** ConvONet variants consistently outperformed all learning-based baselines by a significant margin. Volumetric-based models (`Ours-3D (64^3)` and `Ours-3D (32^3)`) performed best on this real-world data (e.g., F-Score 0.886 for Ours-3D (64^3)), indicating their higher robustness to noise and domain shift, likely due to the better feature aggregation properties of 3D CNNs. The model was able to reconstruct smooth scenes with geometric details at various scales.
- **Matterport3D (Fig. 1c):** The authors demonstrated the method's scalability to very large scenes (multi-room, multi-floor buildings) by implementing a fully convolutional version that runs in a sliding-window fashion. This confirmed the ability to reconstruct detailed interior layouts despite significant domain differences from the training data.

In summary, the results unequivocally show that ConvONet significantly advances the state-of-the-art in implicit 3D reconstruction. It achieves superior accuracy, scales effectively from single objects to large scenes, and demonstrates impressive generalization capabilities from synthetic to real data, all while often maintaining or improving memory efficiency compared to previous implicit methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Convolutional Occupancy Networks represent a crucial step forward in the field of 3D reconstruction using deep implicit representations. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to effectively address the major limitations of prior works and establish a more robust and scalable framework.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Bridging the Gap between CNNs and Implicit Representations:** The most significant contribution is the successful integration of convolutional neural networks' strengths (translational equivariance, hierarchical feature learning, local-global context integration) into implicit function-based 3D reconstruction. This enables implicit models to perform structured reasoning and handle complex spatial variations more effectively.
2. **Scalability to Complex Scenes:** Unlike previous implicit methods predominantly limited to single objects, ConvONet demonstrates remarkable scalability to large-scale indoor scenes (e.g., entire apartments from Matterport3D). This significantly broadens the applicability of implicit representations for practical scenarios like architectural modeling, virtual reality, and robotics.
3. **Improved Reconstruction Quality and Detail:** The empirical results consistently show superior performance across various metrics (IoU, Chamfer Distance, F-Score, Normal Consistency) for both object-level and scene-level reconstruction. This translates to more accurate and finely detailed 3D models from noisy or sparse inputs.
4. **Robustness and Generalization:** The work showcases strong generalization capabilities, with models trained on synthetic data performing exceptionally well on real-world datasets like ScanNet and Matterport3D. This indicates the learned representations are more robust to domain shifts and variations in input characteristics, a critical factor for real-world deployment.
5. **Memory Efficiency and Faster Convergence:** The multi-plane architecture, in particular, demonstrates a favorable balance of performance and memory efficiency, enabling higher resolution feature processing with fewer resources compared to purely volumetric approaches or previous global encoding methods. Faster convergence also implies more efficient training cycles.
6. **Flexible Input Handling:** The method is compatible with various 3D input representations (point clouds, coarse voxels) and can potentially be extended to other modalities, showcasing its versatility.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancing 3D Computer Vision:** ConvONet sets a new benchmark for implicit 3D reconstruction, paving the way for more expressive and accurate neural representations of 3D geometry. It encourages further research into spatially aware deep learning architectures for 3D tasks.
- **Applications in Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Accurate and scalable 3D reconstruction is fundamental for robot navigation, scene understanding, and interaction. ConvONet's ability to reconstruct detailed scenes from noisy sensor data could significantly benefit these domains.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality:** High-fidelity 3D models are essential for immersive AR/VR experiences. ConvONet could facilitate the creation of realistic digital twins of environments, enabling more compelling virtual worlds and augmented overlays.
- **Content Creation and Digital Twins:** The technology could streamline the process of generating 3D content for various industries, from gaming to engineering, by reconstructing complex environments from scanned data.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concepts introduced, particularly the use of convolutional feature grids for implicit functions, can serve as a foundation for numerous follow-up works. The authors themselves suggest future applications in implicit appearance modeling and 4D reconstruction (dynamic scenes), highlighting the broader impact.

**Acknowledged Limitations:**
The authors are transparent about the current limitations: the method is not rotation equivariant (only translation equivariant with respect to voxel size multiples), and a performance gap still exists between synthetic and real data. These limitations offer clear directions for future research.

In conclusion, Convolutional Occupancy Networks represent a significant leap in deep learning for 3D reconstruction, demonstrating that implicit neural representations, when augmented with convolutional inductive biases, can achieve unprecedented levels of detail, scalability, and generalization. This work is poised to influence a wide range of applications requiring robust and accurate 3D spatial understanding.
