# VIDEOP2R: Video Understanding from Perception to Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces VIDEOP2R, a process-aware reinforcement fine-tuning framework for Large Video Language Models (LVLMs) that explicitly disentangles video perception from reasoning. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on six out of seven video understanding benchmarks, demonstrating an average accuracy improvement of 1.3% over prior top methods and 5.8% over its base model.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) frameworks for Large Video Language Models (LVLMs) are process-agnostic, treating video understanding as a single, undifferentiated procedure.
- This monolithic approach leads to blurred credit assignment in reinforcement learning, making it difficult to pinpoint whether errors stem from visual misinterpretation (perception) or faulty inference (reasoning).
- Current Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data and coarse reward signals conflate perception and reasoning, hindering targeted error correction and efficient learning in LVLMs.

## Method

- VIDEOP2R employs a ==two-stage reinforcement fine-tuning framework== based on a Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct base model, explicitly modeling perception and reasoning as distinct processes.
- The first stage involves Supervised Fine-Tuning with ==VIDEOP2R-CoT-162K==, a new, automatically generated dataset featuring ==process-aware CoT annotations== that clearly separate visual observations from subsequent reasoning.
- The second stage utilizes ==Process-Aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (PA-GRPO)==, an extension of GRPO that provides distinct reward signals for perception tokens and reasoning tokens, enabling fine-grained credit assignment.

## Results

- VIDEOP2R achieved state-of-the-art results on **six** out of **seven** diverse video understanding benchmarks (VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, VCR-Bench, MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME), with an average accuracy gain of **1.3%** over previous state-of-the-art methods and **5.8%** over its base model.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effect of both the process-aware Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning stages, yielding an average improvement of **5.8%** compared to **2.7%** (SFT-only) and **3.1%** (RL-only).
- Process-aware SFT outperformed process-agnostic SFT by **2.1%**, and PA-GRPO achieved a **2.3%** average accuracy gain over process-agnostic GRPO, with all components of the process-aware reward function proving essential for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and training perception and reasoning separately significantly enhances video understanding and reasoning capabilities in LVLMs, outperforming process-agnostic alternatives.
> - A process-aware reward mechanism (PA-GRPO) improves RL training efficiency by mitigating 'advantage collapse' and reduces the 'Think-Answer Mismatch' rate by promoting faithful reasoning traces.
> - The textual perception outputs generated by VIDEOP2R are information-sufficient, demonstrating that the model effectively extracts and verbalizes semantically rich visual evidence, often leading to better downstream reasoning than raw video input for a base model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video understanding constitutes a fundamental challenge within computer vision, demanding the extraction of information from dynamic visual inputs. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated improved reasoning capabilities through Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), a two-stage framework comprising Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). This success has motivated the adaptation of RFT to Large Video Language Models (LVLMs) for video reasoning tasks. Prior efforts in this domain have designed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation pipelines and developed RL variants, notably Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), exhibiting advantages over conventional SFT in various video benchmarks.

However, the existing landscape of video RFT frameworks largely treats video as an alternative modality to text, often adopting a process-agnostic approach. This perspective tends to conflate distinct components of visual reasoning, specifically perception (extracting salient visual information) and reasoning (organizing evidence and drawing inferences). This monolithic treatment can lead to several limitations:
1. **Blurred Credit Assignment:** Assigning a single, final reward to the entire reasoning trajectory, which encompasses both perception and reasoning, can obscure the specific sources of error. For instance, a faulty perception might lead to an incorrect conclusion, but the single reward provides no clear signal to correct the perceptual step independently.
2. **Inability to Address Specific Error Types:** Errors originating from perception (e.g., missing visual cues) differ from those in reasoning (e.g., logical inference flaws). Process-agnostic systems struggle to provide targeted feedback for these distinct error types.

VIDEOP2R aims to address these identified limitations by introducing a process-aware RFT framework. This work contributes to the evolving field of multi-modal AI by proposing a structured approach that explicitly disentangles perception and reasoning within video understanding systems. By doing so, it attempts to enable more precise credit assignment, targeted error correction, and, consequently, more robust and reliable video reasoning capabilities in LVLMs. This positions VIDEOP2R as an advancement toward more interpretable and functionally decomposed multi-modal reasoning models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the video reasoning ability of Large Video Language Models (LVLMs) by integrating a process-aware methodology into the Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) framework. The authors aim to model perception and reasoning as distinct, sequentially processed stages within video understanding tasks.

This objective is motivated by several considerations:

1. **Success of RFT in LLMs:** Reinforcement Fine-Tuning has demonstrated effectiveness in improving the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). This success provides a strong impetus to extend and adapt RFT to the video domain for LVLMs, leveraging the benefits of both supervised pre-training and reward-driven refinement.

2. **Decomposability of Visual Reasoning:** The authors highlight that visual reasoning is inherently decomposable into two core processes:
 - **Perception:** The initial stage of extracting relevant, salient information from visual inputs.
 - **Reasoning:** The subsequent stage of organizing this extracted visual evidence and making inferences or drawing conclusions.
 Existing video RFT frameworks often overlook this natural decomposition, treating the entire process as a single, undifferentiated procedure.

3. **Limitations of Process-Agnostic Approaches:** Current video RFT frameworks suffer from two main challenges due to their process-agnostic nature:
 - **Ineffective Error Correction:** When perception and reasoning are conflated, errors (e.g., a missed visual detail during perception leading to a faulty inference during reasoning) are attributed to the entire output. This "blurred credit assignment" prevents the model from receiving specific feedback for each process, hindering its ability to learn how to correct errors at their origin.
 - **Coarse Reward Signals:** Traditional RFT typically assigns a single final reward to the entire generated trajectory (e.g., the complete Chain-of-Thought and final answer). This coarse feedback mechanism provides insufficient granularity for optimizing distinct perception and reasoning abilities, making it difficult for the model to refine specific sub-skills.

4. **Lack of Structured Data and Rewards:** To address the above, two specific challenges need to be overcome:
 - **Absence of Process-Aware CoT Data:** Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets for video typically merge perception and reasoning annotations, lacking explicit disentanglement. This limits the ability to supervise models to perform process-separated inference.
 - **Absence of Fine-Grained Rewards:** The reliance on single-scalar, final rewards hinders the application of targeted reinforcement signals to perception and reasoning sub-processes.

By explicitly separating perception and reasoning, VIDEOP2R aims to provide clearer training signals, enable more precise error attribution, and ultimately foster more robust and explainable video understanding models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VIDEOP2R introduces a novel process-aware RFT framework for LVLMs, structured into two main stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). The foundational model used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

#### 3.1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Stage

The SFT stage focuses on constructing a high-quality, process-aware Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset and training the base model to encourage explicit separation of perception and reasoning during inference.

##### 3.1.1. Process-Aware CoT Template

A standardized template is designed to explicitly disentangle perception from reasoning:
```
<observation>...</observation>
<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>
```
- The `<observation>` segment is intended for the perception process, where the model extracts relevant visual evidence.
- The `<think>` and `<answer>` segments capture the reasoning process, where the model processes the extracted evidence and states the final answer.

##### 3.1.2. Three-Step CoT Generation Pipeline

A three-step pipeline is developed to automatically generate process-aware CoT data from existing Visual Question Answering (VQA) samples:
1. **Process-aware CoT Generation:** For each VQA sample, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct is used to generate an initial CoT trace, encompassing both perception and reasoning in the specified segments.
2. **CoT Verification:** The generated responses undergo a consistency and correctness check. The final answer of each response is evaluated using task-specific metrics (e.g., exact word match, ROUGE-based similarity, Word Error Rate for OCR, or relative error for numerical/regression tasks). Samples with low-quality answers or those deviating from the CoT template are filtered out.
3. **Observation Sufficiency Verification:** To ensure the quality of the perception segment, a cross-modal validation strategy is employed. Claude 3.7 Sonnet, acting as an LLM-as-Judge, assesses whether the content within the `<observation>` segment alone, when provided with the question and correct answer (without the raw visual input), contains sufficient visual evidence to support the final reasoning process. This step filters out observations that are insufficient or irrelevant.

##### 3.1.3. Data Construction

This pipeline is applied to a public dataset of 260K image- and video-based VQA pairs, resulting in **VIDEOP2R-CoT-162K**, a dataset containing 162,062 high-quality process-aware CoT samples. This dataset is then used to fine-tune the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model in the SFT stage.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Stage: Process-Aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (PA-GRPO)

Following SFT, the model is further refined using a novel process-aware variant of GRPO, termed PA-GRPO. This algorithm aims to provide more granular reward signals aligned with the perception-reasoning separation.

##### 3.2.1. Standard GRPO Recap

GRPO extends Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) by removing the need for a learned critic. It samples a group of candidate responses, assigns rule-based rewards to each, and normalizes these rewards within the group to compute a relative advantage for policy updates. A KL regularization term is used to prevent large policy deviations from a fixed reference model (SFT model).

##### 3.2.2. PA-GRPO Extension

PA-GRPO adapts GRPO by assigning separate reward signals to the perception and reasoning token segments.
- **Response Representation:** For each question, a sampled response `o` is explicitly segmented into perception tokens (`o_i,P` within `<observation>`) and reasoning tokens (`o_i,R` within `<think>` and `<answer>`).
- **Process-Specific Rewards:**
 - **Perception Accuracy Reward (R_acc,P):** An LLM-as-Judge (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) evaluates if the `o_i,P` segment is sufficient to support the correct answer, given the question and the correct answer. This yields a binary reward (0 or 1).
 - **Reasoning Accuracy Reward (R_acc,R):** Task-specific evaluation metrics (as used in CoT verification) are applied to the `o_i,R` segment (specifically the final answer) to assess its correctness, yielding a reward in.
 - **Format Reward (R_form,k):** A binary reward (0 or 1) is assigned if the `o_i,P` and `o_i,R` segments adhere to the predefined process-aware template using regular expression matching.
 - **Length Reward (R_len,k):** A fixed reward (0.2) is given if both accuracy and format rewards are non-zero, and the segment length falls within specified ranges ( for perception, for reasoning). This encourages concise yet informative responses.
- **Process-Aware Reward Assignment:** The overall reward for each segment is a sum: `R_i,k = R_acc,k + R_form,k + R_len,k` for k ∈ {P, R}.
- **Process-Aware Advantage Computation:** Crucially, rewards are normalized *separately* within their respective process groups (perception group vs. reasoning group) to obtain `A_i,k`. This prevents disparate scales and distributions of perception and reasoning rewards from being conflated.
- **Optimization Objective:** The PA-GRPO objective function extends GRPO's by summing the clipped PPO-like terms over both the perception (k=P) and reasoning (k=R) processes, each weighted by its respective process-aware advantage `A_i,k`, while maintaining the KL regularization.

#### 3.3. Training Protocol and Evaluation

The training pipeline involves one epoch of SFT on VIDEOP2R-CoT-162K, followed by 1,000 RL updates over the original 260K VQA data using PA-GRPO. The model after SFT is termed VIDEOP2R-SFT, and the final model after RL is VIDEOP2R. The approach is evaluated on seven video understanding and reasoning benchmarks: VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU, VCR-Bench (reasoning) and MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME (understanding), using their respective official evaluation protocols.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate VIDEOP2R yielded several key findings regarding its performance, architectural components, and underlying mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Main Performance Evaluation

VIDEOP2R achieved competitive performance across the seven video understanding and reasoning benchmarks. It established state-of-the-art (SotA) results on six of the seven benchmarks and ranked second on the remaining one. The average accuracy improvement over the previous SotA was 1.3%. Compared to the base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VIDEOP2R demonstrated consistent accuracy gains ranging from 1.9% to 9.1% across all benchmarks. This suggests that explicit modeling of perception and reasoning contributes to consistent performance improvements and enhanced generalization. The observed performance gap between RFT-trained models (including VIDEOP2R) and models trained solely with SFT or instruction tuning indicates the effectiveness of the RFT paradigm in expanding model capabilities. A slight performance drop on MMVU was attributed to the potential lack of domain-specific knowledge in the training data for certain scientific questions.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

A comprehensive ablation study assessed the contribution of VIDEOP2R's components:
- **Two-stage Training:** Both SFT-only (VIDEOP2R-SFT) and RL-only training showed improvements over the baseline (+2.7% and +3.1% respectively). However, the combined two-stage VIDEOP2R framework yielded a more substantial average improvement of +5.8%, indicating that both stages are complementary and necessary for maximizing model capability.
- **Process-aware Modeling:**
 - **SFT Stage:** The process-aware SFT (VIDEOP2R-SFT) resulted in a 2.1% average accuracy gain over a process-agnostic SFT approach (which used reasoning-only segments).
 - **RL Stage:** The process-aware RL (VIDEOP2R with PA-GRPO) outperformed a process-agnostic RL (standard GRPO, which used a single reasoning reward across all output tokens) by an average of 2.3% across six benchmarks. These results validate that incorporating perception annotations in SFT and utilizing process-aware credit assignment in RL provides clearer training signals and improves video understanding and reasoning.
- **Reward Design in PA-GRPO:** Ablating individual reward components in PA-GRPO revealed that all components contribute to performance:
 - Removing any of `R_R` (reasoning reward), `R_P` (perception reward), or `R_L` (length reward) led to notable performance drops.
 - Specifically, removing `R_P` sometimes resulted in performance worse than the SFT baseline, underscoring the importance of perceptual feedback.
 - When the separation of rewards was removed (assigning both perception and reasoning rewards to all output tokens), performance remained competitive but still trailed PA-GRPO, confirming the benefit of fine-grained, separate reward assignment.
 - An exception was observed on VSI-Bench, where removing `R_L` improved performance. This was attributed to VSI-Bench questions often requiring extensive, fine-grained descriptions that might exceed the set length targets, suggesting a potential area for dynamic length reward adaptation.
- **Robustness of Judge Model:** Experiments with different LLMs as judges for perception accuracy reward (Llama3.1 variants vs. Claude 3.7) demonstrated that VIDEOP2R's performance remains robust. Stronger judge models, correlating with higher human agreement on perception correctness, led to better overall performance.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Perception Representations

An analysis of VIDEOP2R's generated perception outputs indicated their significant value. When Qwen2.5-VL-7B was provided with *text-only questions augmented by VIDEOP2R's perceptions*, its performance (55.5% average accuracy) surpassed its performance when given *raw video input* (52.9%). This suggests that the textual perceptions generated by VIDEOP2R encapsulate semantically rich and sufficient visual information to support reasoning, sometimes more effectively than the model's direct processing of video frames. Furthermore, in both text and video input modalities, augmenting prompts with VIDEOP2R's perceptions consistently yielded better performance than using Qwen's own perceptions.

#### 4.4. Advantages of PA-GRPO over GRPO

PA-GRPO demonstrated advantages over standard GRPO in several aspects:
- **Training Efficiency:** PA-GRPO consistently exhibited fewer instances of "advantage collapse" during RL training (where sampled responses receive nearly identical rewards, leading to zero gradients). By decomposing rewards into perception and reasoning components, PA-GRPO maintains non-zero gradients even if one component saturates, leading to better utilization of training samples and improved efficiency.
- **Mitigating Think-Answer Mismatch:** Models trained with reasoning augmentation can suffer from "Think-Answer Mismatch," where the generated reasoning trace is unfaithful to the final (potentially correct) answer. Single-reward GRPO can inadvertently reinforce such unfaithful traces. PA-GRPO, with its separate rewards for faithful perception and correct answers, showed notably lower mismatch rates compared to single-reward trained models (Video-R1, VideoRFT), promoting more reliable and consistent reasoning.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

- **Success Cases:** VIDEOP2R successfully performs process-aware inference, accurately describing visual cues (e.g., monkey's actions, zigzag pattern) and reasoning effectively to reach correct answers.
- **Failure Cases:** Identified failure modes include:
 - **Key-frame Missing:** Uniform frame sampling can miss critical visual evidence, leading to incomplete observations and subsequent reasoning errors.
 - **Overly Detailed Visual Configuration:** Questions requiring an excessive number of fine-grained visual details (e.g., in VSI-Bench) can cause the model to compress or omit critical perceptual information, leading to errors, especially when descriptions exceed typical length targets. This suggests a need for more dynamic length rewards or adaptive sampling strategies.
- **Stepwise Capability Evolution:** Qualitative examples demonstrated a progressive improvement from the base model to VIDEOP2R-SFT and then to the final VIDEOP2R, showcasing how each stage contributes to enhanced process-aware perception and reasoning.
- **Impact of Task Difficulty:** VIDEOP2R showed larger performance gains on more challenging reasoning (e.g., "Interpret," "Adapt" in VideoMMMU) and perception (e.g., "Medium" duration videos in Video-MME) tasks, indicating its particular benefit for complex scenarios.
- **Model Size and Dataset Composition:** VIDEOP2R demonstrated effective scaling to smaller models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B), significantly improving its performance. The VIDEOP2R-CoT-162K dataset was also found to provide a strong foundation for SFT and generalize well to other RFT pipelines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents VIDEOP2R, a process-aware RFT framework that models video understanding as distinct perception and reasoning stages. This approach holds several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancement in Video Reasoning Models:** By explicitly disentangling perception and reasoning, VIDEOP2R addresses a fundamental limitation of prior process-agnostic video RFT frameworks. This structured approach facilitates a more accurate understanding of how LVLMs process visual information and arrive at conclusions, leading to enhanced performance across diverse video understanding and reasoning benchmarks.

2. **Improved Interpretability and Debugging:** The separation of perception and reasoning offers a more granular view into model operations. This can improve the interpretability of multi-modal models, allowing researchers and developers to diagnose whether errors originate from misperceptions of visual cues or flaws in logical inference. This capability is crucial for building more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

3. **High-Quality, Scalable Data Generation:** The proposed three-step CoT generation pipeline provides an automatic and scalable method for creating high-quality, process-aware supervision data. This pipeline, yielding the VIDEOP2R-CoT-162K dataset, can serve as a valuable resource for future research in multi-modal CoT learning and structured reasoning.

4. **Enhanced Reinforcement Learning for Multi-modal Tasks:** The Process-Aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (PA-GRPO) algorithm advances reinforcement learning techniques for multi-modal models. By providing separate reward signals for perception and reasoning, PA-GRPO enables more precise credit assignment, mitigates issues like advantage collapse, and reduces undesirable behaviors such as "Think-Answer Mismatch," leading to more faithful and robust reasoning.

5. **Generalizability and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains of VIDEOP2R across a variety of benchmarks, question types, and even different base model sizes (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B vs. 7B) highlight the generalizability and robustness of the process-aware modeling paradigm. Its particular effectiveness on more challenging reasoning and perception tasks suggests its utility for complex real-world applications.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The findings of this work open several avenues for future research, including:
 - Developing dynamic length reward mechanisms to better accommodate varying descriptive needs in perception and reasoning.
 - Implementing adaptive frame sampling strategies to ensure critical visual information is captured.
 - Integrating factual and domain-specific knowledge into LVLMs to overcome limitations observed in highly specialized tasks.
 - Exploring how the explicit perception representations generated by such models could be leveraged in downstream applications where raw video access is constrained or textual summaries are beneficial.

In summary, VIDEOP2R represents a significant step towards developing more sophisticated, interpretable, and capable video language models by explicitly acknowledging and optimizing the distinct processes of perception and reasoning.
