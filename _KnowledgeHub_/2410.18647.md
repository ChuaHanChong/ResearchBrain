# Data Scaling Laws in Imitation Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and associated AI labs uncovered approximate power-law data scaling relationships for robotic manipulation, showing that diversifying training environments and objects is more critical than increasing demonstration count for achieving zero-shot generalization of single-task policies. This work provides an efficient data collection strategy, enabling policies for new tasks to achieve around 90% success rates in novel, unseen conditions with moderate data.

## Problem

- Robotics lacked established scaling laws, unlike fields like NLP and CV, hindering the development of truly generalizable policies.
- Achieving zero-shot generalization for robotic policies in novel environments and with unseen objects remained a significant challenge.
- Existing large-scale robotic dataset collection efforts often lacked principled guidance on how to optimize for generalization, especially in diverse, real-world settings.

## Method

- Conducted a comprehensive empirical study on over 40,000 human demonstrations, collected across diverse real-world environments and objects using the ==Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI)==.
- Trained single-task robot policies using an improved Behavior Cloning approach (Diffusion Policy with fine-tuned DINOv2 visual encoder and temporal ensemble) and evaluated them rigorously on entirely unseen environments and objects.
- Systematically varied the number of training environments, objects, and demonstrations to analyze their individual and combined impact on policy generalization performance.

## Results

- Policy generalization, measured as the optimality gap, exhibited an approximate power-law relationship with the number of training objects, environments, or combined environment-object pairs.
- An efficient data collection strategy was derived: prioritize collecting data in as many diverse environments as possible, with one unique object per environment, and approximately 50 demonstrations per environment-object pair.
- This strategy was validated on two new tasks, achieving approximately **90%** success rates in novel environments with unseen objects, demonstrating its practical utility and efficiency in real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalization performance in robotic manipulation follows approximate power-law relationships with the diversity of training environments and objects.
> - Increasing the diversity of training environments and objects is significantly more effective for achieving zero-shot generalization than merely increasing the number of demonstrations per instance.
> - Zero-shot generalization for single-task policies to novel environments and unseen objects is achievable with a moderate data investment by prioritizing diversity in data collection.
