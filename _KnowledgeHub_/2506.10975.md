# GenWorld: Towards Detecting AI-generated Real-world Simulation Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces GenWorld, a comprehensive dataset of over 100,000 real-world simulated videos, and SpannDetector, a new method for identifying AI-generated content by detecting violations of physical consistency. SpannDetector achieved 72.44% accuracy on challenging world model outputs like Cosmos videos, significantly outperforming existing detection methods.

## Problem

- Existing AI video detection methods primarily rely on pixel-level artifacts, which are becoming increasingly rare and difficult to detect in high-quality generative models.
- Current detection datasets contain too much non-realistic or low-quality content, making them ineffective for training detectors to identify sophisticated, real-world simulating AI-generated videos.
- State-of-the-art AI video detectors struggle significantly with content from advanced world models that produce visually indistinguishable but physically inconsistent real-world simulations.

## Method

- Introducing **GenWorld**, a large-scale dataset of over 100,000 videos focused on real-world simulation scenarios (e.g., autonomous driving, robot operations) using diverse state-of-the-art generative models.
- Developing **SpannDetector**, a novel detection framework that identifies AI-generated videos by analyzing violations of physical consistency and 3D geometric integrity across video frames.
- **SpannDetector** integrates a frozen **Dust3R** model for 3D feature extraction and a temporal memory module to track and analyze multi-view consistency over time, rather than relying on pixel-level artifacts.

## Results

- SpannDetector achieved 72.44% accuracy in detecting videos generated by Cosmos, an advanced world model, outperforming the best competing method (VideoMAE) which reached only 30.38% accuracy on the same content.
- The GenWorld dataset challenges human perception, with participants achieving only 89.4% accuracy in distinguishing real from AI-generated videos, compared to 100% on some existing datasets.
- Traditional detection methods like DeMamba showed severe overfitting, with accuracy dropping from 98.41% on seen generation types to as low as 0.61% on unseen types, while SpannDetector demonstrated better generalization with less severe performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - AI-generated videos, even those visually convincing, often contain subtle but detectable violations of physical consistency and 3D geometry across viewpoints or over time.
> - Detection methods focusing on physical consistency are more robust and generalizable against advanced generative models like world models, which are becoming increasingly challenging for pixel-level artifact detectors.
> - High-quality, real-world focused datasets are crucial for benchmarking and advancing AI-generated video detection, as existing datasets often contain non-realistic or easily detectable content.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The rapid advancement of AI video generation technology has created a pressing need for reliable detection methods, particularly for videos that simulate real-world scenarios. While much attention has been focused on detecting deepfakes of human faces, a new generation of AI models can now create highly realistic videos of driving scenes, robot operations, and other real-world activities that could have significant societal impact if used maliciously.

This paper introduces **GenWorld**, a comprehensive dataset and detection framework specifically designed to address the challenge of identifying AI-generated videos that simulate real-world scenarios. The research team from Tsinghua University presents both a large-scale benchmark dataset and a new detection method called **SpannDetector** that moves beyond traditional pixel-level artifact detection to focus on physical consistency violations.

### 2. The Problem with Current Approaches

Existing approaches to AI-generated video detection face several critical limitations when dealing with modern, high-quality generative models. Traditional detection methods primarily focus on identifying pixel-level artifacts - subtle inconsistencies in image generation that can reveal artificial origins. However, as AI models become more sophisticated, these visual artifacts are becoming increasingly rare and difficult to detect.

The research identifies three main problems with current detection datasets and methods:

**Limited Real-World Focus**: Many existing datasets include significant amounts of non-realistic content such as cartoons, anime, or gaming footage. Some datasets even mislabel animated content as "real," which means detectors trained on this data learn to distinguish between animation styles rather than genuine forgery characteristics.

**Quality Inconsistencies**: Current datasets often contain low-quality generated videos that are easily distinguishable by humans, making them poor representatives of the most challenging and potentially harmful AI-generated content.

**Inadequate Detection Strategies**: Most detection methods rely on identifying generation artifacts at the pixel level. However, advanced models like world models can produce videos that are visually indistinguishable from real footage while still containing subtle violations of physical laws or 3D consistency.

The paper demonstrates that even state-of-the-art detectors struggle significantly when confronted with videos generated by advanced world models like Cosmos, which can produce driving scenes and other real-world simulations that are nearly indistinguishable from authentic footage.

### 3. The GenWorld Dataset

To address these limitations, the researchers developed GenWorld, a carefully curated dataset comprising over 100,000 videos specifically focused on real-world simulation scenarios. The dataset represents a significant advancement in both scale and quality compared to existing resources.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

The GenWorld dataset was constructed through a systematic process that prioritizes real-world relevance and generation quality:

**Real Video Collection**: The team gathered 10,850 authentic videos from four established datasets covering key real-world domains:
- Kinetics-400: Human activities and interactions
- NuScenes: Autonomous driving scenarios
- RT-1: Robot manipulation tasks
- DL3DV-10K: 3D vision scenes

**AI-Generated Video Creation**: Using advanced language models to annotate the real videos, the team created detailed prompts that were then used to generate 89,446 AI-generated videos across 10 different state-of-the-art generative models. This approach ensures that the generated content maintains semantic consistency with real-world scenarios.

The generative models span three categories of increasing sophistication:
- **Text-to-Video (T2V)**: Models like OpenSora-T, Latte, and ModelScope that generate videos from text descriptions
- **Image-to-Video (I2V)**: Models such as OpenSora-I and SEINE that create videos from static images
- **Video-to-Video (V2V)**: Advanced world models like Cosmos that can transform existing videos while maintaining realistic physics

#### 3.2. Key Characteristics

GenWorld distinguishes itself through several important features:

**Real-World Simulation Focus**: Every video in the dataset represents scenarios with potential real-world impact, from autonomous driving to industrial robotics.

**High Quality and Realism**: By employing cutting-edge generative models, the dataset contains videos that are genuinely challenging to distinguish from authentic footage.

**Cross-Prompt Diversity**: The inclusion of T2V, I2V, and V2V generations allows researchers to study different types and levels of video forgery systematically.

### 4. SpannDetector: A New Detection Paradigm

Recognizing that traditional pixel-level detection methods are becoming inadequate against high-quality generative models, the researchers developed SpannDetector, which focuses on detecting violations of physical consistency rather than visual artifacts.

#### 4.1. Core Insight

The key insight behind SpannDetector is that while AI-generated videos may appear visually perfect, they often struggle to maintain consistent 3D geometry and physical relationships across different viewpoints or over time. Real-world videos naturally preserve these physical relationships because they capture actual 3D scenes, while generated videos may contain subtle but detectable inconsistencies in how objects relate to each other spatially.

#### 4.2. Architecture and Mechanism

SpannDetector integrates several sophisticated components:

**Dust3R Integration**: The system uses a frozen Dust3R model, originally designed for 3D reconstruction, to extract features related to scene geometry and spatial relationships. This provides a foundation for analyzing the physical consistency of video content.

**Temporal Memory Module**: Inspired by Spann3R, the detector incorporates a memory mechanism that tracks consistency across multiple frames rather than just analyzing pairs of adjacent frames. This temporal component is crucial for identifying inconsistencies that may only become apparent over longer sequences.

**Multi-View Consistency Analysis**: For each pair of consecutive frames, the system analyzes how well the 3D structure and spatial relationships are maintained. Real videos show regular patterns in this analysis, while AI-generated videos exhibit irregular and noisy patterns.

The mathematical framework processes frame pairs through several stages:

1. **Feature Extraction**: Frame features are extracted using the frozen Dust3R encoder: $f^e_t = F_{enc}(I_t)$

2. **Memory Integration**: Current frame features query the temporal memory to integrate historical information: $f^c_t = \text{Attention}(f^e_t, f^{m,k}_{t-1}, f^{m,v}_{t-1})$

3. **Consistency Scoring**: The combined features are processed to generate authenticity scores: $s_t = \text{Scorer}(F_{dec}(f^e_t, f^c_t))$

4. **Final Prediction**: Video-level decisions are made by averaging frame-level scores across the entire sequence.

### 5. Key Findings

The evaluation of SpannDetector on the GenWorld dataset reveals several important insights about the current state of AI-generated video detection.

#### 5.1. Performance Against Advanced Models

SpannDetector demonstrated superior performance compared to existing detection methods, particularly when dealing with the most challenging AI-generated content. Most significantly, it achieved 72.44% accuracy in detecting videos generated by Cosmos, the advanced world model, while the best competing method (VideoMAE) only achieved 30.38% accuracy on the same content.

This dramatic improvement highlights the effectiveness of the physical consistency approach, especially against generators that produce visually convincing but physically inconsistent content.

#### 5.2. Cross-Generative Model Analysis

The evaluation revealed significant variation in detection difficulty across different generative models:

- **Easier to Detect**: Videos from models like Lavie and VideoCrafter showed more detectable artifacts
- **Moderate Difficulty**: Text-to-video models generally fell in the middle range
- **Most Challenging**: Advanced world models like Cosmos produced the most difficult-to-detect content

#### 5.3. Generalization Capabilities

A crucial finding concerns the generalization of detection methods across different types of generation:

**Traditional Methods**: Existing detectors like DeMamba showed severe overfitting, achieving high accuracy (98.41%) when trained and tested on the same generation type but failing dramatically when tested on different types (as low as 0.61% accuracy).

**SpannDetector Advantage**: While SpannDetector's performance also decreased when tested on unseen generation types, the degradation was much less severe, indicating that physical consistency provides more generalizable forensic features.

#### 5.4. User Study Results

Human evaluation studies confirmed the challenging nature of the GenWorld dataset. Participants achieved only 89.4% accuracy in distinguishing real from AI-generated videos in GenWorld, compared to 100% accuracy on existing datasets like GenVideo. This demonstrates that GenWorld successfully captures the most deceptive and challenging AI-generated content.

### 6. Implications and Future Directions

The research presents several important implications for the field of AI-generated content detection and digital forensics.

#### 6.1. Paradigm Shift in Detection

The success of SpannDetector suggests a fundamental shift is needed in how we approach AI-generated video detection. Rather than focusing solely on pixel-level artifacts that become increasingly rare as models improve, the field should embrace physical consistency and 3D understanding as more robust and future-proof detection strategies.

This shift is particularly important as world models and other advanced generative systems become more prevalent. These models can produce content that is visually indistinguishable from real footage while still containing subtle violations of physical laws.

#### 6.2. Benchmark for Future Research

GenWorld establishes a new standard for evaluating AI-generated video detection methods. Its focus on high-quality, real-world simulation content provides a more realistic and challenging benchmark than previous datasets. The dataset's comprehensive coverage of different generation methods and real-world scenarios makes it an invaluable resource for advancing the field.

#### 6.3. Explainable Detection

The physical consistency approach offers potential for more explainable AI detection systems. Rather than providing binary "real" or "fake" classifications, future systems based on this approach could potentially identify specific types of physical inconsistencies, providing forensic analysts with more detailed information about why content was flagged as potentially artificial.

#### 6.4. Applications Beyond Detection

The insights from this research extend beyond simple detection to broader applications in content verification, digital forensics, and AI safety. Understanding how AI-generated content fails to maintain physical consistency could inform the development of more robust AI systems and better methods for ensuring the integrity of digital evidence.

The work also highlights the ongoing arms race between content generation and detection technologies, emphasizing the need for continued research and development in both areas to maintain the ability to distinguish authentic from artificial content in an increasingly sophisticated digital landscape.

As AI-generated content becomes more prevalent and convincing, frameworks like GenWorld and detection methods like SpannDetector will play crucial roles in maintaining trust and authenticity in digital media, with implications spanning from journalism and social media to legal proceedings and autonomous systems.
