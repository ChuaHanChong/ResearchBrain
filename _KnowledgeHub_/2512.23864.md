# Learning to Feel the Future: DreamTacVLA for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamTacVLA enhances Vision-Language-Action models by integrating high-resolution, vision-based tactile sensing, explicit spatial alignment, and a predictive tactile world model. This approach enables robots to anticipate future tactile states and refine actions, achieving success rates up to 95.0% in contact-rich manipulation tasks like Peg-in-Hole.

## Problem

- Existing vision-centric VLA models are "contact-blind," struggling with tasks requiring fine-grained interaction and understanding of physical contact (force, texture, slip).
- Prior attempts to integrate tactile sensing often relied on sparse, low-dimensional signals, which are insufficient for capturing the high-resolution spatial and temporal dynamics needed for dexterous manipulation.
- Robots currently lack a robust mechanism to anticipate future tactile consequences of their actions, limiting their ability to perform precise adjustments in contact-rich environments.

## Method

- Introduced **DreamTacVLA**, a hierarchical perception and action refinement framework that grounds VLA models in contact physics through multi-scale sensory input and predictive modeling.
- Developed a hybrid dataset, combining physics-based simulated tactile images with real-world demonstrations, to provide scalable, high-fidelity data for training.
- Implemented a "Think–Dream–Act" loop where a unified policy proposes a draft action, a pre-trained tactile ==world model== predicts future tactile states, and the policy refines the action based on this anticipated feedback.

## Results

- DreamTacVLA achieved state-of-the-art success rates in real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks: **95.0%** for Peg-in-Hole, **85.7%** for USB Insertion, **81.1%** for Gear Assembly, and **74.6%** for Tool Stabilization.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the full DreamTacVLA model, combining both Hierarchical Spatial Alignment (HSA) and the "Dream" mechanism, outperformed individual ablations by an average of **22.3%**.
- Vision-only baselines and policies with basic tactile integration showed substantially lower success rates (e.g., ACT **35.2%**-**62.6%** SR), highlighting DreamTacVLA's effectiveness in tasks demanding physical contact understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical Spatial Alignment (HSA) is crucial for reconciling multi-scale sensory streams (macro-vision, local-vision, micro-tactile), explicitly grounding tactile information within broader visual contexts.
> - Integrating an action-conditioned tactile world model allows robots to "dream" of future tactile outcomes, enabling anticipatory contact reasoning that is vital for robust and fine-grained manipulation.
> - The synergy between explicit spatial alignment and anticipatory tactile reasoning is critical, leading to superior performance in contact-rich tasks compared to using either component in isolation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a significant paradigm for general-purpose robotic control, leveraging vast amounts of web-scale knowledge to enable robots to perform various manipulation tasks. Models such as RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and Octo have demonstrated generalization across tasks and embodiments, primarily relying on visual and linguistic inputs. However, a limitation of these vision-centric VLA models is their performance in contact-rich manipulation scenarios, where successful execution often necessitates an understanding of physical contact, friction, and slip. These fine-grained physical interactions are often ambiguous or unobservable through visual data alone.

Previous attempts to integrate tactile information into VLA pipelines have frequently utilized low-dimensional force or torque signals. While these signals can provide basic contact detection, they are generally sparse and lack the spatial resolution required to inform policies about the precise nature or location of contact events. This scarcity of detailed contact information hinders a robot's ability to perform dexterous tasks that demand nuanced physical interaction, such as precise insertion or manipulation of deformable objects.

Furthermore, the deployment of high-resolution vision-based tactile sensors presents practical challenges, including their cost, fragility, and the difficulty of acquiring large-scale real-world datasets for training. This data scarcity issue limits the scalability of tactile-aware models. In parallel, existing predictive world models in robotics focus primarily on forecasting full visual observations in high-dimensional latent spaces, which can be computationally intensive and may not be optimally suited for capturing the dynamics of localized contact physics. While some VLA architectures have begun integrating predictive modeling, their application to fine-grained, action-conditioned tactile prediction remains an area for further development.

This research aims to address these identified gaps by developing a framework that explicitly grounds VLA models in high-resolution contact interactions. It seeks to combine multi-scale sensory perception, including vision and detailed tactile feedback, with an anticipatory mechanism for predicting the future consequences of actions in terms of tactile interaction. The work positions itself at the intersection of VLA models, multimodal grounding, high-resolution tactile sensing, and predictive world models, with a specific focus on enabling robust contact-rich manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to augment Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with a sophisticated understanding and predictive capability of physical contact, thereby overcoming their "contact-blindness" in complex manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and challenges in robotic manipulation:

1. **Addressing Deficiencies in Contact-Rich Manipulation**: Current VLA models, while effective in visually-guided tasks, frequently fail in scenarios requiring precise physical contact, such as insertion, assembly, or dexterous manipulation. This failure stems from their inability to perceive and reason about how physical contact evolves during interaction. The research aims to equip VLA agents with this crucial capability.

2. **Enhancing Tactile Awareness Beyond Low-Dimensional Signals**: Existing methods for integrating tactile inputs often rely on sparse and ambiguous low-dimensional force or torque signals. These provide insufficient information about the precise location, geometry, or dynamics of contact. A key motivation is to incorporate high-resolution vision-based tactile sensing, which provides richer information about texture, geometry, and slip, essential for human-level dexterity.

3. **Mitigating Tactile Data Scarcity and Sensor Vulnerability**: High-resolution tactile sensors are often expensive and fragile, making large-scale real-world data collection prohibitive. To circumvent this, the authors aim to develop a scalable data generation strategy involving high-fidelity digital twins and simulated environments, complemented by real-world data.

4. **Enabling Multi-Scale Sensory Integration**: Contact-rich manipulation requires integrating information from various spatial scales: macro-level task context (third-person view), local end-effector guidance (wrist-camera view), and micro-level contact details (fingertip tactile cues). A significant challenge is to effectively bridge the modality gap between fundamentally different visual and tactile signals. The objective is to establish spatial correspondence and create a unified latent representation.

5. **Compelling Models to Actively Utilize Tactile Information**: VLA backbones are typically pretrained without tactile inputs, leading to a tendency to ignore tactile information when naively appended. The research is motivated to design a framework that actively compels the model to use tactile signals, especially for capturing fine-grained contact physics that vision cannot provide.

6. **Developing a Computationally Efficient Predictive Mechanism**: Traditional world models, which predict full high-dimensional RGB observations, are computationally expensive and can be unstable, making them challenging for real-time robotic deployment. The authors seek a more lightweight, tactile-centric predictive model that forecasts future tactile signals in a latent space, thereby learning the evolution of local contact physics without explicit planning.

In summary, the key objectives are:
- To introduce a novel contrastive loss (Hierarchical Spatial Alignment, HSA) for spatially aligning multi-scale sensor data (tactile, wrist camera, third-person view).
- To develop a tactile world model capable of predicting future high-resolution tactile signals in a latent space, thereby learning an implicit understanding of contact physics.
- To propose a two-stage "Think-Dream-Act" policy that leverages this tactile prediction capability for action refinement, allowing the robot to anticipate and react to contact consequences.
- To construct a large-scale hybrid dataset (simulated and real-world) to support the training of tactile-aware policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, DreamTacVLA, addresses the limitations of vision-centric VLA models in contact-rich manipulation by integrating high-resolution vision-based tactile images with standard visual and language inputs. It operates through a hierarchical perception scheme and a two-stage learning procedure incorporating a "Think-Dream-Act" inference mechanism.

#### 3.1. Architecture Components

The framework is built upon a shared LLM backbone (CLIP-based) and comprises three main components:

1. **Multimodal Encoders (Eψ)**: These encoders process various sensory streams. A CLIP Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder is used for third-person and wrist images, as well as for language prompts. An MLP processes the robot's proprioceptive state. All feature tokens are concatenated into a unified sequence. In the initial training stage, these encoders are optimized with a Hierarchical Spatial Alignment (HSA) loss to produce a spatially-aligned multimodal representation, H(t)align.

2. **Tactile World Model (Wϕ)**: This component functions as an implicit physics engine. It is a pre-trained, frozen V-JEPA2 (ViT-L/ViT-G) model, trained on a large, unlabeled dataset of tactile image sequences. Its role is to encode a current tactile image Iτ into a rich, latent embedding zτ that captures the underlying physical state. Wϕ remains frozen throughout subsequent policy training, serving as a stable feature extractor.

3. **Unified Policy (πθ)**: This policy comprises a CLIP-based multimodal encoder and an Action Expert transformer. It implements the "Think-Dream-Act" loop:
 - **Think**: The policy initially generates a draft action a(t)draft based on the current aligned state H(t)align and a null tactile prediction.
 - **Dream**: A lightweight Forecasting MLP (Fη) predicts the future latent tactile embedding, H(t+N)dream, by taking the current tactile embedding z(t)τ (from Wϕ) and the draft action a(t)draft as inputs. This anticipated future tactile state represents the predicted contact consequences of the draft action.
 - **Act**: Conditioned on both the current observation H(t)align and the dreamed future state H(t+N)dream, the policy generates the refined, final action a(t)final for execution.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Procedure

1. **Stage 1: Pre-training Spatial Alignment & World Model (Initial Policy)**:
 - **Objective**: To train the multimodal encoders (Eψ) and policy (πθ) to understand the spatial relationship between the tactile sensor and the visual world, and to establish a baseline action policy.
 - **Hierarchical Spatial Alignment (HSA) Loss (L_HSA)**: This novel contrastive loss enforces spatial understanding across three visual scales (third-person, wrist, tactile).
 - The 3D pose of the tactile sensor, derived from robot forward kinematics and camera calibrations, is projected onto 2D bounding boxes (B(t)w, B(t)tp) in the wrist and third-person camera views.
 - Mean-pooled feature vectors are extracted: hτ from tactile tokens, hw from wrist-view tokens within B(t)w, and htp from third-person view tokens within B(t)tp.
 - An InfoNCE contrastive loss pulls these corresponding representations closer, explicitly aligning the micro-vision tactile image with specific, localized regions in the macro and local camera feeds.
 - **Action Loss (L_action)**: The Action Expert is trained using a behavior cloning objective with an ℓ1 loss to predict an H-step action sequence based on the fused multimodal inputs.
 - **Total Loss**: L_Stage1 = L_action + λ_HSA * L_HSA. During this stage, a zero-tensor (Hnull) is used in place of the future tactile prediction as the world model is not yet active for dreaming.

2. **Pretrained Tactile World Model (Wϕ)**: (Pre-training occurs prior to Stage 2)
 - Wϕ (V-JEPA2) is self-supervisedly pre-trained on a large, unlabeled dataset of tactile image sequences. It uses a latent masked forecasting approach via a teacher-student architecture. The student predicts latent representations of masked tactile patches, conditioned on available context, by minimizing the L2 distance to EMA-updated teacher embeddings. This training compels Wϕ to learn the underlying physics of tactile interaction. Once trained, Wϕ is frozen.

3. **Stage 2: Finetuning with a Latent Dream (Refined Policy)**:
 - **Objective**: To finetune the entire pre-trained system (Eψ and πθ) by integrating the learned tactile prediction capability to refine actions based on anticipated contact outcomes. The Forecasting MLP (Fη) is trained from scratch here.
 - **Think-Dream-Act Loop**: The policy follows the three steps detailed above.
 - **Latent Forecasting Loss (L_W)**: An L2 loss is applied between the predicted future latent tactile embedding (˜z(t+N)τ from Fη) and the ground-truth future tactile embedding (z(t+N)τ from the frozen Wϕ), compelling Fη to accurately forecast tactile consequences.
 - **Total Loss**: L = λ_action * L_action + λ_HSA * L_HSA + λ_W * L_W. Gradients are backpropagated through Eψ, πθ, and Fη, while Wϕ remains frozen.

#### 3.3. Data Generation

To overcome tactile data scarcity, a hybrid large-scale dataset was constructed:
- **Simulation Data**: Approximately 80% of the dataset was generated in NVIDIA IsaacSim, utilizing a high-fidelity physics-based tactile sensor model (TacEx and Taxim-style) to synthesize realistic GelSight-like tactile observations. Automated expert demonstrations (cuRobo) were used to collect 1000 demonstrations per task with randomized object poses.
- **Real-world Data**: Approximately 20% of the dataset consisted of 100 expert demonstrations per task collected on a Dobot Xtrainer robot equipped with two GelSight sensors and Realsense D405 cameras.
The dataset totals 2 million tactile frames across four tasks and nine objects, with success-based filtering and strict step-level synchronization of modalities.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The policy and encoders are initialized from a pretrained CLIP model (clip-vit-large-patch14). The tactile image encoder (V-JEPA2 ViT-L/G) is pretrained and frozen, with a lightweight residual adapter (a 3-layer MLP with attention pooling, adding 1.8% overhead in trainable parameters) for task-specific adaptation. The action expert is an action transformer predicting a 7-DOF action (6D end-effector pose + 1D gripper state) over a 45-step horizon. Inference involves bypassing the CVAE encoder and using temporal aggregation for action smoothing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the hypothesis that robust, contact-rich manipulation in robotic agents benefits from both a high-resolution, spatially-grounded understanding of the current contact state and a predictive world model that forecasts future tactile interactions.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware**: Experiments were conducted on a real-world Dobot Xtrainer platform equipped with a parallel gripper, two GelSight tactile sensors, and two Realsense D405 cameras (wrist and third-person views).
- **Tasks**: Four challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks were used for evaluation:
 1. **Peg-in-Hole**: A precision task where the port is partially occluded.
 2. **USB Insertion**: Requires sub-millimeter tolerances and is visually ambiguous.
 3. **Gear Assembly**: Involves aligning a small gear onto a shaft.
 4. **Tool Stabilization**: Maintaining a stable upright pose of a vertical cylinder using a gripped cube vertex under disturbances.
Each task demands precise contact handling, slip detection, and iterative pose refinement.
- **Evaluation Metric**: Task success rate (SR) was measured over 100 trials for each task in real-world settings.
- **Baselines**: The proposed DreamTacVLA was compared against:
 - State-of-the-art VLA and imitation learning baselines: ACT, Diffusion Policy, and π0.
 - A controlled baseline: ACT (w/ Tactile), to isolate the contribution of tactile data itself.
 - Ablation variants of DreamTacVLA: "Ours (HSA-Only, No Dream)" and "Ours (No HSA, Dream-Only)."

#### 4.2. Main Results

The full DreamTacVLA model ("Ours (HSA & Dream)") consistently achieved the highest task success rates across all four contact-rich manipulation tasks, as detailed in Table 1:
- **Peg-in-Hole**: 95.0% success rate.
- **USB Insertion**: 85.7% success rate.
- **Gear Assembly**: 81.1% success rate.
- **Tool Stabilization**: 74.6% success rate.

Observations regarding baseline performance:
- Vision-only baselines (ACT, Diffusion Policy, π0) exhibited lower success rates, particularly in tasks with visual ambiguity or high precision requirements like USB Insertion and Gear Assembly. For example, ACT achieved 35.2% on Peg-in-Hole and Diffusion Policy 56.3% on USB Insert.
- The "ACT (w/ Tactile)" baseline demonstrated an improvement over vision-only methods (e.g., 45.6% for Peg-in-Hole vs. 35.2% for ACT), indicating that the inclusion of tactile sensing is beneficial. However, its performance remained substantially lower than DreamTacVLA, suggesting that DreamTacVLA's architecture utilizes tactile feedback more effectively for decision-making.

DreamTacVLA's performance, particularly in Peg-in-Hole and USB Insertion, suggests its capacity for precise micro-slip perception, iterative pose refinement, and stable contact maintenance. The "Think-Dream-Act" mechanism enabled controlled residual adjustments near targets, indicating that the tactile world model provided high-frequency predictive feedback for fine-grained corrections. The model also demonstrated reliable handling of variations with fewer demonstrations (50 for Peg-in-Hole), implying strong physical grounding and generalization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of HSA and World Model**:
 - "Ours (HSA-Only, No Dream)": This variant, using HSA-aligned encoders but lacking the world model's temporal foresight, showed an average performance of 54.7% across tasks (e.g., 60.8% for Peg-in-Hole). While it maintained coarse alignment, it often failed to perform the fine residual adjustments necessary for task completion, behaving inconsistently.
 - "Ours (No HSA, Dream-Only)": This variant, trained without the L_HSA loss but with the dreaming mechanism, achieved an average of 70.0% (e.g., 75.4% for Peg-in-Hole). This performance exceeded the "HSA-Only" variant, indicating the significant impact of the dreaming component. However, policies in this configuration frequently experienced misalignment, demonstrating that implicit spatial grounding is insufficient for consistent success.
 - The full DreamTacVLA model ("HSA & Dream") achieved an average 22.3% improvement over both ablations, highlighting the complementary and synergistic roles of explicit spatial grounding (HSA) and temporal imagination (dreaming) for reliable insertion and contact-rich behaviors.

- **World Model Sizes (L_W)**: Ablations on the world model's components (Figure 8) indicated that tactile forecasting was the most critical element. Predicting only future visual images provided a minor performance boost. However, predicting all future modalities (tactile and vision) yielded the best results, suggesting a more consistent cross-modal model.

- **Tactile Dataset Size**: Investigating the influence of dataset size (Figure 8) revealed a consistent improvement in performance with more training data. The model's performance began to converge around 60% of the total dataset, indicating that the current data collection was sufficient for the evaluated tasks, though further scaling could offer marginal gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamTacVLA represents a development in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring precise physical interaction. By introducing a framework that grounds Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in contact interactions and enables them to "feel the future," the work addresses a limitation of previous vision-centric robotic policies.

The primary significance of this work lies in several key contributions:
- **Enhanced Contact Awareness**: The framework equips robots with a higher level of tactile awareness, integrating high-resolution vision-based tactile signals with visual and language cues. This allows robots to perceive fine-grained contact geometry, slip, and deformation, which are critical for dexterity but often invisible to standard vision systems.
- **Multi-Scale Sensory Integration**: The Hierarchical Spatial Alignment (HSA) loss provides a mechanism for effectively fusing information across different spatial scales—from global task context to local end-effector views and micro-level fingertip contact. This explicit grounding helps the model understand the physical relationship between different sensory inputs.
- **Anticipatory Contact Reasoning**: The "Think–Dream–Act" strategy, combined with a tactile world model, enables the robot to anticipate the future tactile consequences of its actions. This predictive capability allows for proactive action refinement and iterative adjustments, moving beyond reactive control to more intelligent, physically-grounded decision-making.
- **Scalable Data Generation**: The hybrid approach to data collection, combining a high-fidelity digital twin in simulation with real-world experiments, offers a scalable solution to the data scarcity and sensor fragility challenges associated with high-resolution tactile sensing.

The consistent outperformance of DreamTacVLA against state-of-the-art baselines and its own ablations across challenging contact-rich tasks in real-world settings demonstrates the efficacy of its design principles. The results suggest that robust manipulation behavior emerges when spatial grounding of tactile information is combined with the ability to temporally imagine future tactile interactions.

Potential impacts of this research include:
- **Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation**: Enabling robots to perform tasks requiring delicate touch, fine assembly, and manipulation of deformable objects with greater reliability and precision, thereby broadening the scope of robotic applications in manufacturing, logistics, and service industries.
- **Improved Generalization**: The framework's ability to learn from a hybrid dataset and integrate diverse sensory information could lead to more generalist robotic agents capable of adapting to novel contact conditions.
- **Future Robotic Policy Development**: The work highlights the importance of action-conditioned tactile prediction, influencing future research directions for building more physically intelligent and intuitive robotic systems.

While the "Think–Dream–Act" mechanism may introduce inference overhead, the authors suggest future work on policy distillation and adaptive dreaming could lead to faster, single-pass reasoning. Furthermore, scaling tactile world models with larger multimodal corpora is identified as a path toward developing more general agents that can reason about physical interactions with a level of intuition approaching that of humans.
