# What You See Is What Matters: A Novel Visual and Physics-Based Metric for Evaluating Video Generation Quality

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed VAMP (Visual Appearance and Motion Plausibility), a novel metric designed to evaluate the quality of generated videos by assessing both visual appearance consistency and the physical plausibility of motion. VAMP offers interpretable insights into video quality without requiring reference videos, demonstrating superior sensitivity to perceptual degradations compared to existing metrics like ClipSim and Inception Score.

## Problem

- Existing video quality metrics (e.g., FVD, IS, ClipSim) primarily operate in latent feature spaces, often failing to capture nuanced aspects of video quality critical for human perception.
- Current metrics lack interpretability, struggle to assess temporal consistency and physical plausibility, and overlook subtle visual details like texture or minor artifacts.
- Evaluating generated videos requires comprehensive assessment beyond static image metrics, accounting for smooth transitions, physically realistic object movements, and semantic coherence.

## Method

- The **VAMP** (Visual Appearance and Motion Plausibility) metric was developed, explicitly focusing on human visual perception by measuring both visual appearance consistency and motion plausibility.
- **VAMP** constructs an Appearance Score from color, shape, and texture similarity using methods like Earth Mover’s Distance, symmetric Hausdorff distance, and GLCM for feature extraction.
- A Motion Score is calculated based on velocity and acceleration consistency to quantify the physical plausibility and temporal coherence of object movements.

## Results

- VAMP demonstrated effective sensitivity to five types of video corruptions (brightness, Gaussian noise, impulse noise, defocus blur, black shapes) across five severity levels, showing incremental changes aligned with human perceptual degradation.
- In evaluating state-of-the-art generative models, VAMP scores indicated that Pika achieved the highest overall quality (VAMP: **0.778**), followed by VideoCrafter2 (**0.696**) and ModelScope (**0.650**).
- Compared to baseline metrics, VAMP showed comparable performance to FVD in capturing degradation but offered superior stability and consistency, while ClipSim and Inception Score showed limited sensitivity to various perceptual degradations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video quality evaluation benefits from directly measuring explicit visual and physical attributes, offering a more intuitive and interpretable assessment aligned with human perception.
> - Decomposing video quality into appearance and motion components allows for granular insights into specific areas of degradation, facilitating targeted model improvements.
> - VAMP's reference-free nature expands its applicability, allowing evaluation of novel generated content without the need for ground-truth videos.
