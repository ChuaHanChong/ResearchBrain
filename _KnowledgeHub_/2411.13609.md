# What You See Is What Matters: A Novel Visual and Physics-Based Metric for Evaluating Video Generation Quality

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed VAMP (Visual Appearance and Motion Plausibility), a novel metric designed to evaluate the quality of generated videos by assessing both visual appearance consistency and the physical plausibility of motion. VAMP offers interpretable insights into video quality without requiring reference videos, demonstrating superior sensitivity to perceptual degradations compared to existing metrics like ClipSim and Inception Score.

## Problem

- Existing video quality metrics (e.g., FVD, IS, ClipSim) primarily operate in latent feature spaces, often failing to capture nuanced aspects of video quality critical for human perception.
- Current metrics lack interpretability, struggle to assess temporal consistency and physical plausibility, and overlook subtle visual details like texture or minor artifacts.
- Evaluating generated videos requires comprehensive assessment beyond static image metrics, accounting for smooth transitions, physically realistic object movements, and semantic coherence.

## Method

- The **VAMP** (Visual Appearance and Motion Plausibility) metric was developed, explicitly focusing on human visual perception by measuring both visual appearance consistency and motion plausibility.
- **VAMP** constructs an Appearance Score from color, shape, and texture similarity using methods like Earth Mover’s Distance, symmetric Hausdorff distance, and GLCM for feature extraction.
- A Motion Score is calculated based on velocity and acceleration consistency to quantify the physical plausibility and temporal coherence of object movements.

## Results

- VAMP demonstrated effective sensitivity to five types of video corruptions (brightness, Gaussian noise, impulse noise, defocus blur, black shapes) across five severity levels, showing incremental changes aligned with human perceptual degradation.
- In evaluating state-of-the-art generative models, VAMP scores indicated that Pika achieved the highest overall quality (VAMP: **0.778**), followed by VideoCrafter2 (**0.696**) and ModelScope (**0.650**).
- Compared to baseline metrics, VAMP showed comparable performance to FVD in capturing degradation but offered superior stability and consistency, while ClipSim and Inception Score showed limited sensitivity to various perceptual degradations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video quality evaluation benefits from directly measuring explicit visual and physical attributes, offering a more intuitive and interpretable assessment aligned with human perception.
> - Decomposing video quality into appearance and motion components allows for granular insights into specific areas of degradation, facilitating targeted model improvements.
> - VAMP's reference-free nature expands its applicability, allowing evaluation of novel generated content without the need for ground-truth videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced rapid advancements, particularly with the emergence of diffusion models, leading to the potential for creating highly realistic and dynamic video content. These developments support applications in areas such as filmmaking, virtual reality, autonomous systems, and robotics. However, the evaluation of the quality of these generated videos remains a significant challenge.

Existing metrics, such as Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), Inception Score (IS), and ClipSim, primarily assess video quality by comparing features in a latent space. This approach often falls short in providing an interpretable understanding of video quality from a human visual perspective. For instance, FVD measures statistical similarity but lacks intuitive interpretability, while ClipSim captures high-level semantic alignment but may overlook finer visual details like texture quality or minor artifacts that impact human perception. These limitations become more pronounced as video generation models increase in complexity and realism.

A comprehensive video quality metric, aligned with human visual perception, needs to consider both visual appearance (e.g., color, texture, shape consistency) and motion coherence (e.g., realistic movement and adherence to physical laws). Current metrics often struggle to capture temporal consistency, physical plausibility, and long-term coherence, which are crucial for realistic video content.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a novel metric, VAMP (Visual Appearance and Motion Plausibility), designed to evaluate video quality based on both visual appearance consistency and the physical plausibility of motion. By focusing on attributes relevant to human perception rather than solely latent space representations, VAMP aims to offer a more intuitive and comprehensive measure of video quality, thereby bridging the divide between computational evaluation and human assessment of video realism. This work positions itself as a tool to facilitate the development of more realistic and coherent video generation models by providing a more aligned evaluation framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and comprehensive metric for evaluating the quality of generated videos that closely aligns with human visual perception. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Metrics:** Current metrics for video quality evaluation, such as FVD, IS, and ClipSim, primarily operate in latent feature spaces. While useful for statistical comparisons, they often fail to capture the nuanced aspects of video quality that are critical for human perception. Specifically:
 - **Lack of Interpretability:** FVD, for example, provides a numerical score but does not directly convey interpretable insights into *why* a video might be considered high or low quality from a human perspective.
 - **Incomplete Assessment:** ClipSim, while capturing semantic alignment, can overlook subtle visual details such as texture consistency, minor artifacts, or precise color fidelity, which are important for human observers.
 - **Temporal and Physical Disregard:** Many metrics do not adequately assess temporal consistency (smoothness and coherence across frames) or physical plausibility (adherence to natural laws of motion and geometry), which are fundamental to realistic video content.

2. **Aligning with Human Visual Perception:** Humans evaluate video realism based on a combination of visual fidelity and dynamic coherence. This includes:
 - **Visual Appearance Consistency:** Ensuring that elements like color, shape, and texture remain consistent and realistic across frames.
 - **Motion Coherence:** Verifying that object movements are smooth, continuous, and adhere to physical laws (e.g., realistic velocity and acceleration). The paper argues that a robust metric should explicitly incorporate these two dimensions.

3. **Tackling Unique Challenges of Video Evaluation:** Video generation presents complexities beyond static image generation, requiring metrics to account for:
 - **Temporal Consistency:** The need for coherent visual appearance and smooth transitions throughout the video sequence.
 - **Physical Plausibility:** Objects and movements must behave in a physically realistic manner, maintaining geometric consistency.
 - **Semantic Consistency:** The actions and content depicted must be logically meaningful and align with real-world expectations.
 - **Multimodal Coherence:** When applicable, the interplay between visual and auditory elements should also be considered, though this is not a direct focus of VAMP's current design.

To achieve its objective, the research outlines three specific contributions:
- Introducing VAMP, a new metric that directly targets visual appearance consistency and motion consistency, moving beyond the limitations of latent-space metrics.
- Developing a corrupted video dataset with five types of common corruptions, each at five severity levels, to rigorously test the sensitivity and robustness of VAMP and other metrics.
- Conducting extensive experiments on both real and synthetically generated videos from state-of-the-art models to provide a detailed comparison of VAMP's performance against existing approaches.

The motivation is to establish a valuable tool for measuring video generation quality that supports researchers in their pursuit of more realistic and coherent generated video content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed metric, VAMP (Visual Appearance and Motion Plausibility), is designed to evaluate generated video quality by focusing on both visual fidelity and motion coherence, aligning with human perception. The methodology consists of three main steps: point sampling, mask selection, and VAMP score construction.

#### 3.1. Point Sampling and Mask Selection

This initial stage aims to identify and track regions of interest (objects) within a video sequence. The paper explores two primary methods for this:

- **SAM2-based Sampling:** This method utilizes the auto-segmentation capabilities of SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2) to identify key features and objects in the initial frames of a video. The centroids of these identified key regions serve as input points to initiate the tracking process. SAM2 is then employed to track the movement of these regions across subsequent frames, generating segmentation masks that delineate the objects of interest. These masks are crucial for isolating objects for downstream calculations.
- **SIFT-based Sampling (Alternate):** As an alternative, a more computationally efficient approach involves using Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) features combined with DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) clustering. SIFT identifies distinctive key points within a frame, and DBSCAN then groups these points into clusters, treating outliers as noise. This method automatically forms a variable number of regions, and its CPU-only nature helps reduce computational burden on GPUs. The supplementary material provides further details on this approach and its trade-offs.

#### 3.2. VAMP Score Construction

The VAMP score is composed of two primary components: an appearance score and a motion score, which are combined to provide a comprehensive evaluation.

- **Appearance Score (S_appearance):** This score assesses the visual consistency of objects across frames, drawing from three fundamental attributes of human visual perception: color, shape, and texture.
 - **Color Similarity (S_color):** Evaluated by comparing the color distributions of corresponding objects between consecutive frames. The Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD), also known as Wasserstein distance, is used to quantify dissimilarity between normalized color histograms (for red, green, and blue channels). The color similarity score is calculated as 1 minus the average EMD across the three color channels, where lower EMD indicates higher similarity.
 - **Shape Similarity (S_shape):** Assesses the structural integrity and geometric consistency of object contours between consecutive frames. The symmetric Hausdorff distance is employed to measure the maximum distance between points on the contours of corresponding objects in two frames. The shape similarity score is computed as 1 divided by (1 plus the symmetric Hausdorff distance), ensuring that smaller distances yield higher similarity.
 - **Texture Similarity (S_texture):** Measures the consistency of fine-grained surface details of objects across frames. Object images are resized, converted to grayscale, and their texture features are extracted using the Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM). GLCM properties (contrast, dissimilarity, homogeneity, energy, correlation) are computed for multiple orientations and flattened into feature vectors. Cosine similarity between these feature vectors from consecutive frames quantifies texture similarity.
 - **Overall Appearance Score:** A weighted average of the color, shape, and texture similarity scores: S_appearance = w_c * S_color + w_s * S_shape + w_t * S_texture, where the weights (w_c, w_s, w_t) sum to 1 and can be tuned based on application requirements.

- **Motion Score (S_motion):** This score evaluates the physical plausibility and temporal coherence of object movements within the generated video.
 - **Velocity Consistency (S_vel):** Assesses the stability of object motion by analyzing the trajectories of object centroids. Velocities are calculated as the Euclidean distance between centroids of consecutive frames. The consistency is quantified by computing the standard deviation of these velocities across all frames, normalized by the mean velocity. The score is defined as exp(-(std(v) / mean(v))), where lower variance indicates smoother motion.
 - **Acceleration Consistency (S_acc):** Evaluates the smoothness of changes in velocity. Accelerations are calculated as the differences between velocities in consecutive frames. The variance of these accelerations is then computed. The score is defined as exp(-Var(a)), where lower variance implies smoother motion transitions.
 - **Overall Motion Score:** A weighted combination of the velocity and acceleration consistency scores: S_motion = w_vel * S_vel + w_acc * S_acc, where weights (w_vel, w_acc) sum to 1.

- **Final VAMP Score (S_VAMP):** The ultimate VAMP score for a video integrates both the appearance and motion scores. It is computed as a weighted sum of the average appearance and motion scores across all detected objects and frames: S_VAMP = (1/mn) * Σ (α * S_appearance + β * S_motion), where 'm' is the number of objects, 'n' is the number of frames, and α and β are the weights for appearance and motion scores, respectively, with α + β = 1. A higher S_VAMP score reflects better alignment with human perception of video quality, offering an intuitive and practical metric for evaluating generated video content.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated VAMP through two experimental setups: corrupted video evaluation and generated video evaluation, comparing its performance against established metrics like ClipSim, Inception Score (IS), and Fréchet Video Distance (FVD).

#### 4.1. Corrupted Video Evaluation

- **Dataset and Corruption:** The UCF101 dataset, containing videos of human actions, was used. Five types of corruptions were applied: brightness, Gaussian noise, impulse noise, defocus blur, and black shapes, each at five distinct severity levels (1-5).
- **VAMP's Sensitivity:**
 - VAMP demonstrated effective capture of both visual appearance and motion consistency. The VAMP-A (appearance-only) and VAMP-M (motion-only) components quantified these aspects, showing sensitivity to degradation levels (Table 1). VAMP-M maintained robust scores even under challenging corruptions like impulse noise and defocus blur.
 - Figure 4 illustrated that VAMP balances responses to visual and motion degradations, exhibiting incremental changes across corruption levels that align with human perceptual degradation.
- **Comparison with FVD:** VAMP performed comparably to FVD in reliably capturing increasing degradation in video quality as corruption levels intensified (Table 1, Figure 4). While FVD sometimes showed sharper increases under Gaussian and Impulse Noise, a key advantage of VAMP is its reference-free nature, unlike FVD which requires ground-truth videos. This makes VAMP more broadly applicable in scenarios lacking reference data. VAMP also exhibited greater stability and consistency in its response to corruption types like Black Shapes compared to FVD, which showed negative correlations to corruption levels in some instances.
- **Limitations of ClipSim and IS:**
 - IS showed only minor changes across different corruption levels for brightness and Gaussian noise, indicating limited sensitivity to perceptual degradations (Table 1).
 - ClipSim remained largely unchanged across all tested corruption types, even for severe distortions like impulse noise and defocus blur (Figure 4). It failed to effectively capture changes caused by Black Shapes. These results suggest that IS and ClipSim may not adequately reflect the impact of degradations on visual quality or motion consistency, particularly when detailed visual appearance and temporal coherence are critical.

#### 4.2. Generated Video Evaluation

- **Setup:** 3,000 prompts from the VidProM dataset were used to generate videos with four state-of-the-art models: Pika, VideoCraft2, Text2Video-Zero, and ModelScope.
- **Model Performance (Table 2):**
 - **Pika** consistently achieved the highest scores across all VAMP components (Color: 0.993, Shape: 0.477, Texture: 0.997, Motion: 0.788, VAMP: 0.778), indicating superior visual fidelity and motion coherence.
 - **VideoCrafter2** followed Pika with strong performance (Color: 0.957, Shape: 0.448, Texture: 0.975, Motion: 0.683, VAMP: 0.696).
 - **ModelScope** showed competitive appearance scores but comparatively lower motion scores (Color: 0.909, Shape: 0.414, Texture: 0.956, Motion: 0.631, VAMP: 0.650).
 - **Text2Video-Zero** exhibited the lowest motion and overall VAMP scores (Motion: 0.448, VAMP: 0.314), with appearance sub-scores not reported in the table.
- **VAMP Weight Sensitivity (Supplementary):** Analysis revealed that VAMP scores are sensitive to the chosen weights (α and β) for appearance and motion, and this sensitivity varies with corruption type and generative model. For instance, Gaussian Noise and Impulse Noise benefited from higher motion weighting (lower α), while Brightness and Black Shapes were more affected by appearance weighting (higher α). Pika demonstrated robustness across a wide range of α values, suggesting a well-balanced and high-quality output.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Trade-off (Supplementary)

- The supplementary material also discusses the trade-off between SAM2-based and SIFT-based sampling. SIFT sampling was significantly faster (0.095 s/frame) than SAM2 sampling (2.69 s/frame). However, SAM2-based sampling yielded more representative masks and, consequently, more reliable VAMP scores with stronger correlations to corruption levels, indicating superior performance despite higher computational cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VAMP presents several significant implications for the field of video generation and its evaluation:

1. **Enhanced Evaluation Standard:** VAMP introduces a new standard for evaluating video generation quality that moves beyond latent space comparisons. By directly measuring visual appearance and physical motion plausibility, it offers an assessment that aligns more closely with human perception, which is crucial for applications where human realism is paramount.

2. **Improved Interpretability:** Unlike many existing metrics that provide abstract scores, VAMP’s components (color, shape, texture, velocity, acceleration consistency) offer granular and interpretable insights into specific aspects of video quality. This interpretability can help researchers understand precisely *where* generated videos fall short, facilitating targeted improvements in model architectures and training.

3. **Reference-Free Assessment:** A key advantage of VAMP is its ability to operate without requiring ground-truth or reference videos. This significantly expands its applicability to real-world scenarios where reference videos may be unavailable, costly to obtain, or impractical to use. This makes VAMP a versatile tool for evaluating novel video content.

4. **Guidance for Model Development:** By providing a detailed breakdown of quality, VAMP can serve as a robust foundation for future advancements in video generation. Researchers can use VAMP scores to benchmark models more effectively, identify strengths and weaknesses, and guide the development of algorithms that produce more realistic, temporally consistent, and physically plausible video content. For example, if a model consistently scores low on motion consistency, efforts can be directed towards improving its temporal coherence modules.

5. **Broader Applications:** The ability to accurately and comprehensively evaluate video quality has direct implications for various applications:
 - **Movie Making and Virtual Reality:** More realistic generated content can enhance immersion and visual storytelling.
 - **Autonomous Systems and Robotics:** Highly plausible simulated environments and object interactions are critical for training and validating AI in these safety-critical domains.
 - **Content Creation:** Improved metrics can help creators and developers ensure the high quality of synthetic media.

In summary, VAMP addresses a critical gap in video generation evaluation by offering a comprehensive, interpretable, and reference-free metric. Its potential impact lies in accelerating research and development in video synthesis by providing a more human-centric and actionable feedback mechanism for model performance.
