# World Models Can Leverage Human Videos for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta and NYU developed DexWM, a world model that leverages large-scale human video data and a novel action representation to enable dexterous manipulation. The model achieved an 83% zero-shot success rate in real-world object grasping tasks on a robotic arm with an Allegro gripper, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art behavior cloning methods without any real-world training data.

## Problem

- Developing control policies for dexterous manipulation is challenging due to complex contact physics and high degrees of freedom.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale robotic datasets for training deep learning models capable of dexterous gripper control.
- Existing direct policy learning methods struggle with generalization to novel tasks and robust planning in physical environments, particularly for sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- Introduced ==DexWM==, a latent space world model that learns environment dynamics using a frozen DINOv2 encoder and a ==Conditional Diffusion Transformer-based predictor==.
- Employed a novel, fine-grained action representation using ==3D hand keypoint differences== and camera motion, alongside an auxiliary ==Hand Consistency Loss== for accurate hand configuration learning.
- Trained DexWM by pre-training on large-scale human egocentric videos (EgoDex) and non-dexterous robot videos, then fine-tuned on minimal exploratory robot simulation data, and used it for planning via ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)==.

## Results

- Achieved an **83%** zero-shot success rate in real-world dexterous grasping tasks on a Franka Panda arm with an Allegro gripper, without any fine-tuning on real-world robot data.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art behavior cloning (Diffusion Policy) in simulation, achieving **72%** for Reach, **28%** for Place, and **58%** for Grasp, compared to **16%**, **8%**, and **0%** respectively.
- The 'Hand Consistency Loss' led to a substantial **34%** increase in PCK@20 (Percentage of Correct Keypoints) for 4-second predictions on EgoDex, validating its importance for fine-grained hand pose accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-training on large-scale human egocentric videos effectively transfers manipulation priors, substantially improving robot task performance and bridging the data scarcity gap.
> - A specialized fine-grained action representation and an auxiliary 'Hand Consistency Loss' are crucial for world models to capture and preserve dexterous hand configurations, which are critical for manipulation tasks.
> - World models, when integrated with Model Predictive Control, enable robust planning and superior zero-shot generalization from simulation to real-world robots, significantly reducing the need for expensive real-world training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant challenge in robotics: enabling dexterous manipulation, which involves complex, fine-grained control of multi-fingered hands to interact with objects. Traditional parallel-jaw grippers offer limited dexterity, whereas human-like dexterous grippers allow for sophisticated tool use, precise movements, and in-hand manipulation. Achieving this level of control in robots is difficult due to the high degrees of freedom and complex contact dynamics involved.

Existing approaches to vision-based manipulation policies often struggle with generalization and robust planning in physical environments. World models, which learn environmental dynamics from observations and actions, present a promising avenue. However, previous world models, while effective for simpler tasks or coarse action spaces (e.g., text, navigation, whole-body motion), typically lack the fine-grained action representations necessary to capture dexterous dynamics. Learning such models directly from robot data is also hampered by the scarcity of large-scale datasets for dexterous grippers.

This paper builds upon recent trends that leverage human training data and cross-embodiment learning to bridge these gaps. It integrates concepts from diffusion and flow matching objectives for video generation, action-conditioned predictive models (world models), and imitation learning from egocentric human videos. By focusing on learning a state-transition model from human data and then applying it within a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for robotic tasks, this research aims to provide a robust solution for dexterous manipulation that generalizes across different embodiments and tasks, without requiring extensive task-specific robot training. It positions itself as a method that specifically tackles the challenge of fine-grained dexterous actions within the world model paradigm, a limitation of prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a world model capable of accurately predicting the future states of an environment under dexterous hand-object interactions. This objective is motivated by several key challenges in robotic manipulation:

- **Complexity of Dexterous Manipulation:** Everyday tasks and high-stakes applications require a level of dexterity that current robotic systems, particularly those relying on parallel-jaw grippers, cannot achieve. Dexterous grippers, designed to mimic human hands, introduce high degrees of freedom and complex contact dynamics, making their control and modeling challenging.
- **Limitations of Current Manipulation Policies:** While deep learning has advanced vision-based manipulation policies, these methods often struggle with generalization to novel tasks and robust planning in dynamic physical environments. An effective system requires a model that understands the causal relationship between actions and environmental changes.
- **Inadequacy of Existing World Models for Dexterity:** Current world models can learn environmental dynamics but typically operate with coarse action spaces (e.g., text commands, navigation movements, or whole-body poses). These representations are insufficient to capture the subtle finger motions and fine-grained contact interactions essential for dexterous manipulation.
- **Data Scarcity for Dexterous Robots:** Training robust models for dexterous robots is difficult due to the limited availability of large-scale, finely annotated datasets specifically for dexterous robot manipulation. Human videos, on the other hand, contain rich examples of dexterous interactions but need effective mechanisms for transfer to robotic embodiments.

To address these motivations, the authors propose DexWM, a latent space world model with the following specific objectives:

- **Predict Future Latent States Conditioned on Dexterous Actions:** Develop a model that can predict subsequent environmental states, including hand and object configurations, given current states and fine-grained dexterous actions.
- **Leverage Large-Scale Human and Non-Dexterous Robot Data:** Overcome data scarcity for dexterous robots by pre-training the world model on large-scale egocentric human interaction videos (EgoDex) and non-dexterous robot manipulation sequences (DROID), enabling cross-embodiment dynamics learning.
- **Design a Fine-Grained Action Representation:** Define an action representation that accurately captures subtle changes in hand configurations and camera movements using 3D finger keypoints and camera poses, in contrast to coarser action spaces.
- **Enhance Dexterity Modeling with Auxiliary Loss:** Improve the model's ability to capture fine-grained dexterous dynamics by incorporating an auxiliary hand consistency loss that explicitly enforces accurate hand configuration predictions, recognizing that visual feature prediction alone may be insufficient.
- **Enable Zero-Shot Transfer to Robotic Manipulation:** Demonstrate that a world model trained on human videos can facilitate robust planning for novel robot manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping, placing, reaching) in both simulation and real-world scenarios through a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, with minimal robot-specific fine-tuning.

In essence, the research aims to establish a framework where a learned understanding of dexterous human interactions can be effectively transferred to control complex robotic manipulators, leading to more generalized and robust robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces DexWM, a latent space world model designed to predict the unfolding of dexterous hand-object interactions. The approach encompasses problem formulation, state and action representations, a predictor model, a specialized loss function, and a planning framework for robot tasks.

**Problem Formulation:**
The system models the environment's latent state ($s_{k_i}$) at timestep $k_i$, which encodes the dexterous hand's configuration and the scene's geometry. The agent executes a dexterous action ($a_{k_1 \to k_2}$) between timesteps $k_1$ and $k_2$. An egocentric RGB image ($I_{k_i}$) is captured and mapped to a latent state $s_{k_i}$ using an encoder $E_\phi$. The core objective is to learn a predictor $f_\theta$ that maps a current latent state and an action to a predicted future state: $\hat{s}_{k_2} = f_\theta(s_{k_1}, a_{k_1 \to k_2})$. The predictor can optionally take multiple past states as input.

**State Representation (Latent States):**
To focus on underlying system dynamics rather than pixel-level details, DexWM uses patch-level features from a pre-trained DINOv2 image encoder ($E_\phi$) as its latent state representation. Specifically, DINOv2-L with a patch size of 14 pixels and an embedding dimension of 1024 is employed. Images are resized to 224x392 pixels, yielding $P=448$ patch embeddings ($s_{k_i} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times d}$). The DINOv2 encoder is kept frozen during training, leveraging its semantically rich features and generalization capabilities.

**Action Representation:**
The action representation is critical for capturing fine-grained dexterity. Instead of coarse actions, DexWM defines actions as the difference in 3D hand keypoint positions between timesteps. Following the MANO parameterization, each hand has 21 keypoints. The action vector $H_{k_i} = \{H^L_{k_i}, H^R_{k_i}\}$ encodes hand configurations. To account for agent movement, two modifications are made:
1. Keypoints $H_{k_2}$ are expressed in the coordinate frame of $k_1$ using known rigid transformation $T^{k_1}_{k_2}$.
2. Changes in camera translation ($\delta t_{k_1 \to k_2} \in \mathbb{R}^3$) and orientation ($\delta q_{k_1 \to k_2} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, as Euler angles) are appended to the action vector.
The final action vector $a_{k_1 \to k_2} \in \mathbb{R}^{44 \times 3}$ combines the transformed keypoint differences and camera pose changes.
For datasets without native dexterous hand data (e.g., DROID with parallel-jaw grippers), dummy keypoints are approximated at the end-effector using concentric circles whose radii vary with gripper open/close state. For robot data, keypoints are computed via forward kinematics from known joint angles. The Allegro gripper's 4 fingers are mapped to the 5-finger action space by duplicating the last Allegro finger's keypoints for the pinky finger.

**Predictor Model:**
The predictor $f_\theta$ is based on the Conditional Diffusion Transformer (CDiT) architecture. It takes a history of observed latent states ($s_{k_0}, \dots, s_{k_n}$) and the current action ($a_{k_n \to k_{n+1}}$) to predict the next state ($\hat{s}_{k_{n+1}}$). The model is designed to be deterministic, which facilitates faster inference. CDiT incorporates strong action conditioning through AdaLN layers, where the flattened action vector (132 dimensions) is projected as conditioning input to each transformer block. Unlike diffusion-based CDiT methods, DexWM directly regresses future latent states, avoiding iterative denoising. For inference and planning, multistep prediction is performed autoregressively. Training involves randomly skipping states to improve generalization and predicting $s_{k_{i+1}}$ using context up to $s_{k_i}$ for $i \in \{1, \dots, 9\}$.

**Hand Consistency Training Loss:**
While the primary loss is a Mean Squared Error on predicted DINOv2 embeddings ($L_{state}$), this alone was found insufficient for capturing fine-grained dexterous details because hands occupy a small image region. An auxiliary hand consistency (HC) loss ($L_{HC}$) is introduced. A separate transformer-based network $g_\theta$ predicts heatmaps of fingertip and wrist locations ($\hat{V}_{k_{n+1}}$) from the predicted state $\hat{s}_{k_{n+1}}$. This loss ensures that the predicted state is rich enough to recover keypoint positions. The total loss is $L = L_{state} + \lambda L_{HC}$, with $\lambda=100$.

**Robot Task Planning:**
DexWM facilitates zero-shot robot manipulation through a goal-conditioned Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework. Given initial ($s_0$) and goal ($s_g$) states, the objective is to optimize a sequence of robot joint angles ($\Theta_0, \dots, \Theta_{T-1}$) over a planning horizon $T$. These joint angles are mapped to actions ($a_k$) via forward kinematics $G(\Theta_k)$. The world model $f_\theta$ is used to predict future states $\hat{s}_{k+1}$. The Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is employed to optimize the joint angles by sampling candidate sequences, rolling them out through DexWM, and selecting top-K elite sequences to update a Gaussian sampling distribution.
The planning cost $C = C_{state} + \mu C_{kp}$ includes:
- $C_{state}$: L2 distance between the final predicted latent state $s_T$ and the goal state $s_g$.
- $C_{kp}$: Euclidean distance between keypoint pixel locations from heatmaps $\hat{V}_T$ and $\hat{V}_g$ predicted from $s_T$ and $s_g$. This combination improves planning. For grasping, an additional cost on end-effector orientation maintains neutral poses.
During optimization, camera motion components of the action vector are excluded as the camera is stationary on the robot. The optimized first action is executed via a low-level controller, and the process is repeated in a receding horizon fashion for MPC.

**Datasets and Training Regimen:**
- **Pre-training:** DexWM is pre-trained on EgoDex (829 hours of egocentric human videos with hand/pose annotations) and DROID (100 hours of non-dexterous robot manipulation with approximated keypoints).
- **Fine-tuning:** For robotic tasks, the model is fine-tuned on approximately 4 hours of exploratory, non-task-specific dexterous data collected in the RoboCasa simulation environment. This data uses randomized environments and object placements, employing noisy execution of ground-truth trajectories (from a "Lift" dataset) or random 3D targets to ensure broad, unbiased coverage and bridge the embodiment gap without task-specific learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several key findings across ablation studies, open-loop trajectory evaluation, and human-to-robot transfer:

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Human Video Data:** Training DexWM on EgoDex (human video) in addition to DROID (non-dexterous robot data) significantly improves open-loop rollout performance on RoboCasa simulation data. This is evidenced by a lower embedding L2 error (0.79 vs 1.03 at 4s) and higher PCK@20 (percentage of correct keypoints within 20 pixels) (17 vs 13 on average), while maintaining performance on EgoDex. This indicates the benefit of human data for cross-embodiment dynamics learning.
- **Model Size:** Larger predictor models (DexWM-S to DexWM-XL, 30M to 450M parameters) consistently yield better performance. DexWM-XL (456M parameters) shows the best embedding prediction error and keypoint overlap, suggesting higher model capacity aids dynamics learning.
- **Backbone Encoder:** DexWM demonstrates modularity and robust performance across various state-of-the-art vision encoders (DINOv2, DINOv3, Web-SSL, V-JEPA2, SigLIP2). While DINOv2 generally performs strongest, the architecture is not restricted to a single backbone, showcasing adaptability.
- **Hand Consistency Loss:** The auxiliary hand consistency (HC) loss significantly improves fine-grained dexterity. It results in a 34% increase in PCK@20 at 4 seconds prediction time (60% vs 26%) on EgoDex, indicating better capture of local hand information, which is beneficial for robot planning.

**Open-Loop Trajectory Evaluation:**
- **Performance Comparison:** DexWM outperforms prior world models with coarser action spaces.
 - Compared to NWM* (navigation actions only), DexWM shows significantly lower embedding L2 error (0.67 vs 0.74 at 4s) and higher PCK@20 (60% vs 34%).
 - Compared to PEVA* (upper-body poses without finger articulation), DexWM achieves a slightly higher L2 error (0.67 vs 0.62 at 4s) but significantly higher PCK@20 (60% vs 56%), indicating superior accuracy in hand position, crucial for dexterity. Lower perceptual similarity (L2 error) does not always correlate with accurate hand location.
- **Action Transfer:** Qualitatively, DexWM demonstrates better action transfer, accurately predicting fine-grained hand states in new environments that match reference trajectories, unlike PEVA* which produces inaccurate hand configurations.
- **Controllability:** DexWM exhibits reliable controllability, accurately following unseen atomic actions (e.g., moving hand up, down, forward) and capturing physical interactions (e.g., a hand colliding with a cup causing the cup to move).
- **Comparison with Text-Conditioned Models:** DexWM provides physically grounded predictions for hand-object interactions, contrasting with text-conditioned models like Cosmos-Predict2, which may introduce objects based on text prompts without physical consistency.

**Human Video to Robot Transfer:**
- **Simulation Results:** DexWM consistently outperforms baselines on unseen robotic manipulation tasks (reach, place, grasp) in RoboCasa simulation after fine-tuning on a small amount of exploratory robot data.
 - Reach: 72% success rate (vs 16% for Diffusion Policy, 18% for DexWM without pre-training).
 - Place: 28% success rate (vs 8% for Diffusion Policy, 8% for DexWM without pre-training).
 - Grasp: 58% success rate (vs 0% for Diffusion Policy, 14% for DexWM without pre-training).
 - Pre-training Diffusion Policy on human data and fine-tuning on robot data still yields low average simulation success (4%), indicating the advantage stems from modeling dexterous dynamics via the world model, not just pre-training.
- **Zero-Shot Real Robot Transfer:** DexWM demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer to real-world robot grasping using a Franka Panda arm with an Allegro gripper. Without any real-world fine-tuning, it achieves an 83% success rate (10 out of 12 trials). In contrast, Diffusion Policy trained on exploratory simulation data completely fails on the real task. This highlights the robustness of the world model approach in bridging the sim-to-real gap and handling dataset quality issues.

**Failure Analysis (Simulation):**
A breakdown of failure cases in simulation showed that "place" tasks were significantly affected by "drop" failures (70%), where the robot loses the object. Grasping tasks faced "last cm" errors (20%) and "state drift" (18%), while reaching tasks were primarily affected by "last cm" errors (24%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation and world modeling, with several important implications:

- **Bridging the Human-Robot Dexterity Gap:** DexWM successfully leverages large-scale egocentric human video data to learn complex, fine-grained dexterous interaction dynamics. This approach effectively addresses the data scarcity problem inherent in training for high-DOF robot manipulators, enabling robots to learn skills from human demonstrations without direct robot-specific task training.
- **Advancement in World Models for Fine-Grained Control:** The introduction of a keypoint-based action representation and an auxiliary hand-consistency loss significantly improves the ability of world models to capture and predict subtle dexterous actions. This capability extends the applicability of world models beyond coarse action spaces, opening new avenues for their use in tasks requiring precise manipulation.
- **Robust Zero-Shot Transfer to Real-World Robotics:** The demonstration of strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to real-world robot tasks (e.g., 83% grasping success) with minimal robot-specific fine-tuning is a notable achievement. This suggests that a sufficiently rich world model, even if primarily trained on human and simulated data, can generalize effectively to physical robot systems. This robustness is particularly valuable for deploying robots in varied and unconstrained environments.
- **Enhanced Robot Planning and Control:** By serving as a state-transition model within an MPC framework, DexWM enables robust planning of waypoint trajectories for downstream control. This approach offers greater robustness compared to direct action prediction methods (like behavior cloning), as it allows for online optimization and adaptation to novel scenarios.
- **Modularity and Scalability:** The modular design of DexWM, particularly its compatibility with various pre-trained vision encoders and scalability with model size, suggests a flexible framework that can benefit from ongoing advancements in self-supervised learning and larger model architectures.
- **Potential for Generalizable Embodied AI:** This work contributes to the broader goal of building generalizable embodied AI agents capable of human-like interaction with the physical world. By demonstrating effective learning from diverse data sources and transfer across embodiments, DexWM lays groundwork for future robots that can perform complex tasks in unstructured environments.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also highlight future research directions:
- The current framework primarily plans with image-based goals; extending this to text-specified goals would enhance usability.
- Reliance on approximately four hours of exploratory simulation data to bridge the embodiment gap. Reducing or removing this dependency would further improve generality.
- The current setup assumes static scenes without external agents. Incorporating stochasticity and handling dynamic, interactive environments remains a challenge for future work.

Despite these limitations, DexWM represents a substantial step towards more capable and autonomous robots, demonstrating how intelligent world modeling from diverse data sources can unlock dexterous manipulation skills.
