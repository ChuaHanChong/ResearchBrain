# Unleashing Perception-Time Scaling to Multimodal Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced the Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) paradigm to enhance the fine-grained visual perception abilities of Large Vision-Language Models. This approach significantly improved high-precision visual estimation, with the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model achieving an 88.3% average relative accuracy on a new diagnostic benchmark.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) exhibit a gap between strong reasoning capabilities and limited precision in visual perception.
- Existing "Fast Perception" approaches in LVLMs yield one-shot visual judgments, which impedes fine-grained visual estimation and can lead to visual hallucinations.
- Inference-time scaling methods primarily enhance linguistic reasoning but offer only marginal improvements for visual perception and lack mechanisms for decomposing complex perceptual tasks.

## Method

- Developed **DisTANCE**, a novel ==perception-centric benchmark== with synthetic geometric images, to rigorously evaluate and diagnose limitations in LVLM visual estimation.
- Introduced Perception-Time Scaling (PTS), a new paradigm that reforms the perception process by encouraging token-rich visual descriptions and decomposing complex tasks into intermediate sub-problems.
- Designed a two-stage training pipeline involving Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to establish structured perception patterns, followed by GRPO (Reinforcement Learning) with a customized exponential reward function for high-precision optimization.

## Results

- Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) boosted the Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** model's average relative accuracy (RA_avg) on the DisTANCE benchmark from **21.5%** to **88.3%**, and high-precision RA_0.1 from **8.0%** to **64.7%**.
- The approach demonstrated robust generalization, improving performance on out-of-domain visual estimation tasks such as DisTANCE_ood, Geoperception (approx. **20%** gain), and LEGO-Puzzles.
- PTS led to consistent improvements in general multimodal reasoning (e.g., MathVision) and perception-centric tasks (CV-Bench, BLINK), accompanied by increased perception-related token usage and enhanced image attention in early and final transformer layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current LVLMs, even those optimized for reasoning, exhibit limited precision in visual estimation due to a "Fast Perception" paradigm that lacks token-rich perceptual elaboration.
> - LVLMs fail to decompose complex visual estimation tasks, leading to a consistent increase in relative error with target value complexity.
> - Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) effectively transforms visual perception into a structured, step-by-step process, allowing it to leverage reinforcement learning for substantial gains in fine-grained accuracy, akin to inference-time scaling for reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and evolving challenge in the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs): the gap between their impressive *reasoning* capabilities and their often-limited *visual perception* precision.

**Current Landscape:**
Recent breakthroughs in LVLMs have largely been driven by "inference-time scaling" techniques, particularly those leveraging reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) or Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. These methods encourage models to produce longer, more explicit reasoning chains, leading to significant performance gains in complex multimodal reasoning tasks, such as mathematical problem-solving and multidisciplinary understanding. Leading examples include models inspired by DeepSeek-R1, which focuses on incentivizing reasoning capabilities.

**Identified Gap:**
However, the paper highlights a crucial limitation: these reasoning-centric scaling strategies primarily benefit the *reasoning stage*, with their impact on *visual perception* remaining "unclear" and "marginal." Existing LVLMs typically employ a "Fast Perception" paradigm, treating visual understanding as a one-shot judgment. This quick, token-minimal approach hinders precise visual estimation and can even exacerbate issues like visual hallucinations when models prioritize linguistic fluency over accurate visual grounding. While previous work has investigated hallucination (e.g., in object recognition or relationships) and spatial reasoning, a dedicated focus on improving *fine-grained visual estimation* through structured perceptual processes, particularly when integrated with inference-time scaling, has been lacking.

**Contribution and Alignment:**
This paper steps into this gap by:
1. **Diagnosing the Problem:** Introducing **DisTANCE**, a novel, perception-centric benchmark for visual estimation. Unlike existing real-world spatial reasoning benchmarks, DisTANCE uses synthetic geometric images to isolate and rigorously test the model's ability for precise numerical and comparative estimation of fundamental geometric attributes (length, perimeter, area). This allows for a clean evaluation of *perception* without confounding factors like common-sense knowledge from real-world images.
2. **Proposing a Solution:** Presenting **Perception-Time Scaling (PTS)**, a new paradigm that reforms the perception process itself. By making perception structured and explicit through token-rich descriptions and decomposition, PTS aims to align visual understanding with the step-by-step nature of inference-time reasoning. This is a significant conceptual shift from "fast perception" to "deliberate perception."
3. **Integrating with Advanced Training:** Demonstrating how PTS can be effectively combined with advanced reinforcement learning techniques (specifically GRPO, a form of RLVR) to optimize not just reasoning but also the underlying perceptual steps.

In essence, this work seeks to extend the success of inference-time scaling from the linguistic reasoning domain to the visual perception domain, fostering more "perception-aware" LVLMs. It critically examines why current methods fall short for perception and offers a concrete architectural and training methodology to overcome these limitations, thus advancing the frontier of robust and precise multimodal AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations driving this research stem from a critical observation about the current state of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs):

**Primary Motivation:**
While inference-time scaling methods, such as those employing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) or Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, have substantially boosted the *reasoning capabilities* of LVLMs, their impact on *visual perception* remains marginal and largely unexplored. The authors note that existing reasoning-enhanced LVLMs may even be more prone to visual hallucinations, suggesting a disconnect between advanced reasoning and accurate visual grounding. This leads to the fundamental question: *Can perception also benefit from inference-time scaling, and if not, why?*

**Specific Limitations Identified (motivating proposed solutions):**
1. **"Fast Perception" Paradigm:** Current LVLMs tend to treat visual understanding as a "one-shot output." They summarize perceptual information using short, fixed patterns, lacking the token-rich elaboration or intermediate steps seen in their reasoning processes. This minimal representation limits the depth of visual understanding and prevents the benefits of inference-time scaling from transferring to perception.
2. **Failure to Decompose Complex Perception:** LVLMs struggle with complex perception tasks, particularly when target values increase. Unlike their step-by-step approach to reasoning, they often attempt to directly output an answer for complex visual estimations without decomposing the problem into simpler, tractable sub-problems. This indicates a lack of "perceptual decomposition" ability.

### 3. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations and identified limitations, the paper sets out to achieve the following:

1. **Systematically Evaluate Perception:** To rigorously assess the visual estimation capabilities of LVLMs and diagnose the limitations of current inference-time scaling methods on perception, the authors aim to introduce a novel, perception-centric benchmark called **DisTANCE**. This benchmark is designed to focus purely on visual estimation of geometric attributes, minimizing confounding factors.
2. **Propose a Novel Perception Paradigm:** To overcome the "fast perception" paradigm, the research aims to introduce **Perception-Time Scaling (PTS)**. This new paradigm is designed to encourage token-rich visual descriptions and decompose complex perception tasks into intermediate, tractable sub-problems, thereby aligning perception with the structured, step-by-step nature of inference-time reasoning.
3. **Enhance Perception Accuracy and Generalization:** The ultimate goal is to significantly improve the precision and accuracy of LVLMs in visual estimation tasks, both within the training domain and in out-of-domain scenarios, by applying PTS in conjunction with advanced reinforcement learning techniques.
4. **Investigate Underlying Mechanisms:** To understand *how* PTS improves perception, the research aims to analyze its influence on model behavior, specifically focusing on the proportion of perception-related tokens and the model's attention distribution over image tokens.
5. **Demonstrate Broader Impact:** To show that improving fine-grained perception can also yield consistent gains in general multimodal reasoning and real-world perception benchmarks, even when synthetic data is used.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The research follows a two-pronged approach: first, diagnosing the limitations of existing LVLMs on perception with a new benchmark, and second, proposing and validating a novel paradigm, Perception-Time Scaling (PTS), to address these limitations.

#### 4.1. DisTANCE: Probing Perception of LVLMs with Visual Estimation

To systematically examine LVLMs' perception capabilities, the authors introduce **DisTANCE** (Distance reasoning Task with Analytical Numeric and Comparative Estimation).

- **Task Formulation:** DisTANCE focuses on visual estimation tasks, requiring models to estimate geometric attributes (length, perimeter, area) in synthetic images. Questions are designed for precise quantitative relations (e.g., "What is the height of the image if the radius of the red circle is 1 unit?"). All tasks involve *relative estimations* to minimize confounding effects from absolute image resolution.
- **Benchmark Details:** It comprises 300 unique image-question pairs, split evenly into 100 examples for length, perimeter, and area estimation. Images are automatically synthesized using Python, featuring 3 to 7 colored geometric shapes (circles, triangles, rectangles) with varied properties.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** The primary metric is **Relative Accuracy (RA)**, defined as `|ŷ - y|/y < θ`, where `ŷ` is the prediction, `y` is the ground truth, and `θ` is a threshold. `RA_avg` averages performance across thresholds `{0.5, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1}`, while `RA_0.1` specifically measures high-precision performance.
- **Evaluated Models:** A broad range of models were tested, including proprietary LVLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Step-R1-V-mini) and various open-source LVLMs (Qwen2.5-VL series, InternVL3 series), as well as several reasoning-oriented LVLMs (R1-OneVision, Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, VLAA-Thinker, NoisyRollout).
- **Key Findings from DisTANCE Evaluation (Diagnosed Limitations):**
 1. **Limited Precision:** Most LVLMs perform poorly, especially on area estimation, and struggle with high-precision tasks.
 2. **Marginal Gains from Reasoning Methods:** Inference-time scaling paradigms yield only marginal improvements on perception-centric tasks over base models.
 3. **Fast Perception Paradigm:** LVLMs typically produce short, low-token perceptual summaries, without elaboration, indicating a preference for "fast perception." Reasoning-oriented models generate longer answers but dedicate a small fraction to perception.
 4. **Failure to Decompose:** The relative error consistently increases with the complexity (target length) of the estimation, showing that models do not decompose complex perception tasks step-by-step.

#### 4.2. Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) Paradigm

To address the diagnosed "Fast Perception" limitations, PTS reformulates perception as a structured, step-by-step reasoning process.

- **Perception Elaboration:**
 - Encourages token-rich descriptions of visual outcomes.
 - Abstract visual attributes (e.g., distance) are represented using **symbolic tokens**. Specifically, `k` full symbolic token groups `(<==========>)`, each representing 1.0 unit, and a residual `ϕ_res(r)` are used. Each `=` within the `>` delimiters represents a fixed length `δ = 0.1` unit. This forces the model to construct perception outputs incrementally.
- **Perception Decomposition:**
 - Trains models to break down complex perception tasks into simpler sub-problems.
 - For distance estimation, a reference segment `d_r` (defined as 1.0 unit) is chosen. The model then performs a "tiling" process, incrementally accumulating `d_r` along the target distance `d_t` until `d_t` is fully covered. This mimics human ruler-based measurement and transforms perception into a step-by-step process compatible with inference-time scaling.

#### 4.3. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

PTS is implemented through a two-stage training process:

1. **Cold Start for Perception Pattern (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT):**
 - **Structured Reasoning Process:** Each reasoning chain under PTS follows a five-stage structure: (1) Review (task description), (2) Hint (symbolic encoding & decomposition strategy definition), (3) Reference (selection of a 1.0-unit reference segment), (4) Estimation (visual comparison of other segments to the reference using symbolic tokens), and (5) Calculation (applying formulas for the final result).
 - **Dataset Creation:** A synthetic training dataset (6,000 PTS-style examples across length, perimeter, area) is generated using GPT-4o, guided by manually collected few-shot demonstrations and the defined 5-stage structure. Training images are distinct from evaluation benchmarks.
 - **Training:** LVLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) are supervised fine-tuned on this dataset to acquire the initial structured perception reasoning pattern.

2. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (GRPO):**
 - **Optimization:** After SFT, the model is further optimized using GRPO (Group-based Reinforcement Learning from Policy Optimization), a method designed to improve reasoning abilities with verifiable rewards.
 - **Customized Reward for Regression:** Instead of binary accuracy, a continuous exponential reward function `r(o) = e^(-α * |o-d_t|/d_t)` is used. This function heavily penalizes larger relative errors and grants high rewards for small errors, specifically incentivizing high-precision estimation. The hyperparameter `α` controls sensitivity.
 - **Label Normalization:** To stabilize training, initial GRPO training is performed on normalized samples (target values < 1) before introducing data with random distributions. This addresses the issue of varying absolute error tolerances for the same relative error threshold.
 - **Perception-Specific Optimization:** GRPO's policy gradient mechanism allows reward signals to be applied to intermediate perception steps within the explicit reasoning chain, enabling step-by-step refinement of perceptual accuracy.

#### 4.4. Experimental Settings

- **Baselines:** Compared against (1) different data patterns (Direct answer, conventional Chain-of-Thought with SFT and GRPO), (2) Spatial-aware LVLMs (SpaceThinker, Spatial-R1), and (3) Tool-augmented LVLMs (Visual Sketchpad, DetToolChain).
- **Backbone Models:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B were chosen as state-of-the-art LVLMs for implementing PTS.
- **Evaluated Benchmarks:**
 - **In-domain:** DisTANCE.
 - **Out-of-domain:** DisTANCE_ood (unseen geometric shapes), Geoperception (LHC subset for line comparison), LEGO-Puzzles (Height subset for 3D object height comparison).
 - **General Multimodal:** MathVision (math reasoning), MMBench & MMVet (general VQA), HalluBench (hallucination), CV-Bench & BLINK (perception-centric tasks).

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experiments reveal significant improvements enabled by Perception-Time Scaling (PTS), particularly in fine-grained visual estimation and its generalization capabilities.

1. **Existing LVLMs Underperform on DisTANCE:**
 - Evaluation on the DisTANCE benchmark (Table 1) shows that both proprietary (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash) and open-source LVLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) exhibit limited precision in visual estimation.
 - Area estimation is particularly challenging, with most models scoring below 20% `RA_0.1`.
 - Crucially, even reasoning-enhanced LVLMs (e.g., Vision-R1, R1-OneVision) show only marginal gains over their base counterparts, supporting the "Fast Perception" hypothesis. Scaling model size alone has limited effect.

2. **PTS Significantly Enhances High-Precision Visual Estimation on DisTANCE:**
 - When combined with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) using GRPO, PTS dramatically improves performance (Table 3).
 - For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, `RA_avg` soars from a baseline of 21.5% to **88.3%**.
 - High-precision `RA_0.1` jumps from 8.0% to **64.7%** on average, with impressive scores of 70.0% (length), 74.0% (perimeter), and 50.0% (area).
 - PTS consistently outperforms all baselines, including those trained with direct answers, conventional Chain-of-Thought (CoT), spatial-aware LVLMs, and tool-augmented LVLMs. This highlights the effectiveness of internalizing the perception process rather than relying on external modules.

3. **Strong Generalization to Out-of-Domain Estimation Tasks:**
 - PTS demonstrates robust generalization across diverse visual estimation tasks beyond its training domain (Table 4).
 - It improves performance on **DisTANCE_ood** (geometric shapes not seen during training).
 - For **Geoperception (LHC subset)**, PTS boosts accuracy by approximately 20% (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 59.8% to 78.7%).
 - In **LEGO-Puzzles (Height subset)**, PTS improves accuracy in judging the relative heights of 3D objects (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 30.0% to 33.0%). These results underscore the paradigm's transferability to unseen scenarios and different visual modalities (2D to 3D inference).

4. **PTS Improves General and Perception-Centric Multimodal Tasks:**
 - When integrated with math reasoning data (Geometry3K) and applied to general multimodal benchmarks (Table 5), PTS consistently improves performance.
 - Notably, it strengthens reasoning (e.g., MathVision) and brings consistent gains across diverse domains, especially on **CV-Bench** and **BLINK**, which are perception-centric tasks where fine-grained visual understanding is critical.
 - This indicates that, despite being trained solely on synthetic images, PTS effectively transfers its enhanced perceptual abilities to real-world scenarios.

5. **Influence on Model's Perception Mechanism:**
 - **Perception Ratios:** PTS significantly increases the proportion of perception-related tokens in the model's output (Figure 4a). This token-rich elaboration provides more "surface area" for the reinforcement learning process to explore and refine, leading to substantial gains in accuracy that CoT methods (with minimal perceptual content) cannot achieve.
 - **Image Attention:** PTS training leads to models consistently assigning higher attention to image regions, particularly in the early and final transformer layers (Figure 4b). This suggests that PTS improves both low-level visual grounding at the input stage and reinforces image-conditioned reasoning throughout the decoding process.

6. **Ablation Studies and Data Efficiency:**
 - Ablation studies confirm the necessity of key PTS designs: the exponential reward function, label normalization, and the chosen symbolic granularity (Table 7). Removing any component degrades performance.
 - Data efficiency analysis (Figure 6) shows that training with only length estimation data still yields substantial gains across all sub-tasks (length, perimeter, area). This implies that a strong ability in primitive distance estimation is a foundational skill that generalizes effectively to more complex compositional tasks.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, "UNLEASHING PERCEPTION-TIME SCALING TO MULTIMODAL REASONING MODELS," carries substantial significance and potential impact on the development of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs):

1. **Paradigm Shift in Perception for LVLMs:** The most significant contribution is the introduction of **Perception-Time Scaling (PTS)**, which challenges the prevailing "Fast Perception" paradigm. By explicitly structuring the perceptual process with token-rich elaboration and decomposition, PTS moves LVLMs beyond one-shot visual judgments towards a more deliberate, step-by-step visual understanding. This conceptual shift opens new avenues for integrating visual processing more deeply into the reasoning pipeline.

2. **Enhanced Precision and Accuracy:** PTS dramatically improves the high-precision visual estimation capabilities of LVLMs. Achieving `RA_0.1` scores of over 60% on complex geometric estimation tasks, compared to a baseline of 8%, represents a leap forward. This enhanced precision is crucial for real-world applications requiring fine-grained visual understanding, such as scientific analysis, engineering, medical imaging, and robotics, where small errors can have significant consequences.

3. **Unlocking Inference-Time Scaling for Perception:** The work successfully demonstrates that by making perception explicit and structured, it can effectively benefit from powerful inference-time scaling techniques like Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (GRPO). Previously, these techniques were largely restricted to enhancing linguistic reasoning. PTS bridges this gap, enabling the robust optimization of visual understanding alongside reasoning.

4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The observed generalization of PTS to out-of-domain synthetic images (DisTANCE_ood), fine-grained geometric comparisons (Geoperception), and even 3D spatial tasks (LEGO-Puzzles) highlights its robustness. Furthermore, its positive transfer to real-world, perception-centric multimodal benchmarks (CV-Bench, BLINK)—despite being trained purely on synthetic data—underscores its foundational strength and broad applicability. This suggests that teaching models robust, generalizable perceptual primitives can have far-reaching benefits.

5. **Greater Interpretability:** By breaking down perception into explicit, symbolic steps, PTS inherently offers a more interpretable mechanism for how LVLMs arrive at their visual estimations. This transparency can aid in debugging, understanding failure modes, and building greater trust in AI systems, as their visual "thought process" becomes traceable.

6. **Diagnostic Benchmark for Future Research:** The introduction of **DisTANCE** provides a lightweight yet powerful diagnostic tool for the research community. Its focus on synthetic 2D geometric shapes allows for a clear, isolated evaluation of fundamental visual perception skills, free from confounding real-world knowledge. This benchmark can serve as a standard for evaluating future advancements in LVLM perception.

7. **Foundation for More Perception-Aware LVLMs:** This research paves the way for a new generation of "perception-aware" LVLMs that can perform sophisticated reasoning *grounded in highly accurate and detailed visual understanding*. It encourages a holistic view of multimodal AI development, where both reasoning and perception are explicitly scaled and optimized.

In conclusion, this paper not only identifies a critical shortcoming in current LVLMs but also offers an effective and well-validated solution. By emphasizing the structured nature of perception and aligning it with established reasoning enhancement techniques, it sets a promising direction for developing more intelligent, precise, and trustworthy multimodal AI systems. The commitment to publicly releasing code and data will further catalyze research in this important area.
