# Unleashing Perception-Time Scaling to Multimodal Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced the Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) paradigm to enhance the fine-grained visual perception abilities of Large Vision-Language Models. This approach significantly improved high-precision visual estimation, with the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model achieving an 88.3% average relative accuracy on a new diagnostic benchmark.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) exhibit a gap between strong reasoning capabilities and limited precision in visual perception.
- Existing "Fast Perception" approaches in LVLMs yield one-shot visual judgments, which impedes fine-grained visual estimation and can lead to visual hallucinations.
- Inference-time scaling methods primarily enhance linguistic reasoning but offer only marginal improvements for visual perception and lack mechanisms for decomposing complex perceptual tasks.

## Method

- Developed **DisTANCE**, a novel ==perception-centric benchmark== with synthetic geometric images, to rigorously evaluate and diagnose limitations in LVLM visual estimation.
- Introduced Perception-Time Scaling (PTS), a new paradigm that reforms the perception process by encouraging token-rich visual descriptions and decomposing complex tasks into intermediate sub-problems.
- Designed a two-stage training pipeline involving Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to establish structured perception patterns, followed by GRPO (Reinforcement Learning) with a customized exponential reward function for high-precision optimization.

## Results

- Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) boosted the Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** model's average relative accuracy (RA_avg) on the DisTANCE benchmark from **21.5%** to **88.3%**, and high-precision RA_0.1 from **8.0%** to **64.7%**.
- The approach demonstrated robust generalization, improving performance on out-of-domain visual estimation tasks such as DisTANCE_ood, Geoperception (approx. **20%** gain), and LEGO-Puzzles.
- PTS led to consistent improvements in general multimodal reasoning (e.g., MathVision) and perception-centric tasks (CV-Bench, BLINK), accompanied by increased perception-related token usage and enhanced image attention in early and final transformer layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current LVLMs, even those optimized for reasoning, exhibit limited precision in visual estimation due to a "Fast Perception" paradigm that lacks token-rich perceptual elaboration.
> - LVLMs fail to decompose complex visual estimation tasks, leading to a consistent increase in relative error with target value complexity.
> - Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) effectively transforms visual perception into a structured, step-by-step process, allowing it to leverage reinforcement learning for substantial gains in fine-grained accuracy, akin to inference-time scaling for reasoning.
