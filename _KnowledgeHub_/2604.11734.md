# Multi-ORFT: Stable Online Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Multi-Agent Diffusion Planning in Cooperative Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> MULTI-ORFT proposes a framework for multi-agent cooperative driving, integrating a scene-conditioned diffusion model with stable online reinforcement learning. It achieved a 10.0% reduction in collision rate and an 11.1% reduction in off-road rate, alongside a 1.66% increase in average speed on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset closed-loop benchmark compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent diffusion planners struggle to generate trajectories consistent with scene constraints like road geometry and traffic rules.
- Imitation learning-based planners suffer from distribution shift in reactive environments and do not explicitly optimize for safety or efficiency objectives, leading to performance degradation in closed-loop settings.
- Applying online reinforcement learning to fine-tune generative models like diffusion policies for multi-agent driving is unstable due to interaction uncertainty, error accumulation, and challenges in credit assignment for multi-step denoising.

## Method

- MULTI-ORFT employs a two-stage approach: a scene condition-enhanced ==diffusion== pre-training phase and a stable online reinforcement learning post-training phase.
- Pre-training uses a symmetric scene encoder and a multi-agent denoising decoder with an AdaLN-Zero adaptive modulation mechanism to robustly learn scene-conditioned joint distributions of multi-agent trajectories.
- The online RL phase formulates a two-level MDP and introduces Variance-Gated Group Relative Policy Optimization (VG-==GRPO==) to stabilize policy updates, specifically addressing non-stationarity in multi-agent environments.

## Results

- MULTI-ORFT achieved a **10.0%** lower collision rate (**1.89%** from **2.10%**) and an **11.1%** lower off-road rate (**1.36%** from **1.53%**), while increasing average speed by **1.66%** (8.61 m/s from 8.47 m/s) on the WOMD closed-loop benchmark compared to SMART-tiny-CLSFT.
- Online post-training reduced the collision rate by **7.4%**, the off-road rate by **19.0%**, and increased average speed by **3.0%** compared to MULTI-ORFT's own pre-trained policy.
- Ablation studies confirmed that AdaLN-Zero reduced off-road rates by **17.0%** (from **2.05%** to **1.68%**), and the Variance-Gated mechanism was crucial for preventing training collapse, demonstrating its necessity for stable online multi-agent learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - AdaLN-Zero's adaptive modulation mechanism, combined with cross-attention, effectively integrates scene context into diffusion models, significantly improving road adherence and scene consistency in generated trajectories.
> - Formulating a two-level Markov Decision Process allows for stable step-wise policy optimization within the diffusion denoising chain while leveraging sparse environment feedback for overall objective alignment.
> - The Variance-Gated Group Relative Policy Optimization (VG-GRPO) mechanism critically enhances online RL stability in multi-agent systems by adaptively handling low-variance reward scenarios, preventing gradient vanishing and ensuring robust policy refinement.
