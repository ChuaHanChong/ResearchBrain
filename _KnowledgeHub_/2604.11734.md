# SCORP: Scene-Consistent Multi-agent Diffusion Planning with Stable Online Reinforcement Post-Training for Cooperative Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces SCORP, a framework that integrates multi-agent diffusion planning with stable online reinforcement post-training to generate scene-consistent plans for cooperative driving. It addresses the challenge of creating realistic, safe, and efficient multi-agent behaviors in complex interactive traffic scenarios.

## Problem

- Modeling complex and coupled behaviors of multiple agents in dense, interactive driving scenarios for Connected Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs).
- Generating cooperative plans that are simultaneously realistic, safe, and efficient.
- Capturing the stochastic and multimodal nature of real traffic behaviors effectively.

## Method

- Proposing **SCORP**, a multi-agent planning framework leveraging ==diffusion models== for generative planning.
- Integrating a ==Scene-Consistent== approach to ensure generated plans align with the environmental context and interactions.
- Utilizing ==Stable Online Reinforcement Post-Training== to refine and optimize the plans for desired metrics.

## Results

- Achieved scene-consistent planning for cooperative multi-agent driving.
- Generated more realistic and safer multi-agent behaviors compared to methods relying solely on imitation learning.
- Demonstrated improvements in overall traffic efficiency within dense, interactive driving environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models provide a probabilistic framework capable of modeling complex, multimodal distributions of driving behaviors and trajectories.
> - Combining generative planning with reinforcement learning post-training can enhance the realism, safety, and efficiency of cooperative driving behaviors.
> - Reinforcement learning allows for fine-tuning plans derived from imitation learning, addressing limitations in achieving optimal safety and efficiency.

The development of Advanced Connected Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) has initiated cooperative multi-agent driving paradigms, which hold potential for enhancing safety and traffic efficiency. A central challenge in this domain is multi-agent behavior modeling, particularly in dense, interactive scenarios where agent behaviors are coupled. Real traffic exhibits stochastic and multimodal behaviors, making it challenging to generate cooperative plans that are simultaneously realistic, safe, and efficient.
