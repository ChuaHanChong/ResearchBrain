# Diff-DAgger: Uncertainty Estimation with Diffusion Policy for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Virginia developed Diff-DAgger, an interactive learning framework that leverages a diffusion policy's intrinsic loss to accurately estimate uncertainty in robotic manipulation tasks. It achieves a 39.0% higher F1 score in task failure prediction and reduces DAgger iteration wall-clock time by 7.8 times compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional interactive imitation learning methods like robot-gated DAgger struggle with highly expressive, multi-modal policies (e.g., diffusion models) due to inefficient uncertainty estimation.
- Ensemble-DAgger, a common approach, misinterprets multi-modal decision points (where multiple valid actions exist) as high uncertainty, leading to unnecessary expert queries and inefficient data collection.
- Existing DAgger methods are computationally expensive, especially when used with complex policies, hindering scalability and slowing down the learning process.

## Method

- **Diff-DAgger** utilizes the ==diffusion policy's own training objective== (diffusion loss) as a direct and reliable indicator of uncertainty during robot rollouts.
- It employs a ==dynamic α-quantile threshold== for the diffusion loss, along with a ==patience mechanism==, to distinguish between true out-of-distribution states requiring expert help and in-distribution multi-modal states.
- The framework integrates this uncertainty estimation into the standard ==DAgger iterative loop==, collecting expert demonstrations for genuinely uncertain states and efficiently updating the single diffusion policy.

## Results

- **Diff-DAgger** achieved an average of **39.0%** higher F1 score and accuracy in predicting task failures across simulated visuomotor tasks compared to baseline methods like Ensemble-DAgger and Thrifty-DAgger.
- It consistently outperformed baselines in task completion rates, showing a **13.7%** improvement in state-based tasks and a **25.2%** improvement in visuomotor tasks with the same amount of expert data.
- The method reduced the average policy update time by a factor of **7.8x** (to **0.54** hours per iteration) in visuomotor pushing, with substantial reductions in training (**5.1x**), threshold setting (**48x**), and inference (**3.8x**) times.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The intrinsic loss of a diffusion policy can serve as a robust and computationally efficient proxy for uncertainty, effectively identifying out-of-distribution states in complex, multi-modal robotic tasks.
> - Existing ensemble-based uncertainty estimation methods are fundamentally ill-suited for multi-modal policies as they conflate multi-modal distributions with high uncertainty, leading to inefficient expert queries.
> - An intelligent, model-inherent uncertainty estimation mechanism enables more efficient use of expert demonstrations and significantly reduces the wall-clock time required for interactive learning iterations.
