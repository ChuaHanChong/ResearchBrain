# Learning to Ball: Composing Policies for Long-Horizon Basketball Moves

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed a novel framework for composing diverse motor skills in physics-based characters, specifically addressing complex, long-horizon tasks like basketball maneuvers with ill-defined intermediate phases. The system learned intricate actions such as shoot-off-the-dribble with a 98.3% ball catching rate and 91.8% shot accuracy, leveraging heterogeneous and unstructured motion data.

## Problem

- Composing drastically different primitive motor skills into coherent, long-horizon behaviors for physics-based characters remains challenging for existing methods like Mixture of Experts or skill chaining.
- Intermediate subtasks often lack clearly defined initial or terminal states (e.g., 'gathering' a basketball), making them difficult for traditional policy training and chaining approaches.
- Acquiring high-fidelity, structured motion capture data with synchronized full-body, hand, and ball trajectories is expensive and limits the scalability of learning complex skills.

## Method

- A multi-stage framework that trains primitive policies from unstructured and heterogeneous motion data (2D video, 3D mocap, hand-only data) using adversarial imitation learning.
- A policy integration framework categorizes and handles three types of skill transitions: direct execution (Type A), mutual adaptation (Type B), and, crucially, intermediate policies (Type C) which leverage the preceding policy for initial states and the succeeding policy's value function for reward shaping.
- A high-level 'soft-routing' policy uses learned weights to orchestrate transitions between primitive policies, achieving smooth action blending while maintaining dominance of a single skill, guided by user commands.

## Results

- The system achieved a **98.3%** ball catching rate and **91.8%** overall shot accuracy for the 'shoot-off-the-dribble' task, significantly outperforming baselines which failed to integrate skills effectively (e.g., DirectExecution had **0.7%** catching rate).
- Generated motions exhibited dynamic dribbling, intelligent ball gathering that adapts posture to align with the hoop, and seamless transitions between various basketball actions, including cooperative and competitive team play.
- Ablation studies confirmed that value-function-based reward shaping for intermediate policies and the soft-routing approach were critical for robust performance and smooth transitions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning intermediate skills with ill-defined goals can be effectively achieved by shaping their reward function using the state value function of the subsequent, well-defined skill.
> - Simultaneous mutual adaptation, where both the intermediate policy and the succeeding pre-trained policy are fine-tuned, is crucial for robust skill integration and handling varied states.
> - A soft-routing mechanism, which linearly combines actions from primitive policies with learned weights, allows for seamless and responsive transitions between diverse motor skills, outperforming hard-switching heuristics.
