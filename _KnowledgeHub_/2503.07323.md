# Navigating Motion Agents in Dynamic and Cluttered Environments through LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Dartmouth College developed a training-free framework that enables pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform autonomous navigation in dynamic and cluttered environments. The system, which encodes spatial information as text tokens, achieved single-agent success rates up to 0.882 and demonstrated multi-agent coordination and dynamic obstacle avoidance in real-world floor plans.

## Problem

- Traditional path navigation methods (RL, A*) often struggle with zero-shot generalization to novel environments, dynamic multi-agent interactions, and require extensive data or are limited to static settings.
- Previous LLM applications for spatial reasoning were typically confined to simplified, static environments with restricted movements or focused on symbolic reasoning, not grounded, embodied tasks.
- There was a need to leverage LLMs' inherent generalization capabilities for complex spatial reasoning in dynamic, real-time scenarios without task-specific training.

## Method

- A training-free framework that uniformly encodes environment maps, agent states, and trajectories as discrete text tokens, enabling LLMs to act as spatial reasoners.
- Implemented a closed-loop replanning mechanism with 'additive' and 'compositional' multi-turn strategies for iterative path refinement and collision resolution in dynamic environments.
- Utilized an 'anchor-based' trajectory representation, where paths are sequences of sparse key points, to facilitate LLM planning and align with human-like navigation.

## Results

- Achieved a single-agent success rate (SR) of **0.781** for o3-mini in one turn, which improved to **0.882** (additive strategy) and **0.865** (compositional strategy) with four turns of interaction.
- Demonstrated successful multi-agent coordination, with o3-mini achieving an SR of **0.720** for two agents and **0.643** for three agents, showcasing feasibility in complex dynamic scenarios.
- The methodology was extended to downstream applications including guiding environment-aware humanoid motion generation and planning paths in 3D/2.5D environments, such as navigating stairs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained LLMs possess intrinsic spatial reasoning abilities that can be effectively leveraged for complex, embodied navigation tasks without explicit task-specific training or fine-tuning.
> - Text-based input representations (grid or code) proved more effective for LLM spatial reasoning than image-based inputs, with performance depending on the LLM's architecture and interpretation capabilities.
> - Multi-turn interactions and iterative path refinement significantly improve navigation performance, robustness, and enable LLMs to handle dynamic obstacles and multi-agent coordination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned within the emerging field of leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for embodied spatial reasoning and autonomous navigation. Traditional approaches to path navigation, particularly in multi-agent scenarios, have largely relied on reinforcement learning (RL) or classical planning algorithms such as A*. While RL methods can achieve high performance in controlled settings, they typically require extensive data collection and training, often struggling with zero-shot generalization to novel environments or tasks. Classical planning methods like A* are effective for single-agent navigation in static environments but face challenges in dynamic or multi-agent scenarios due to complex interactions and collision avoidance requirements.

Previous studies exploring LLMs for spatial reasoning have often focused on verbal reasoning tasks (e.g., question answering), pattern recognition, or simplified navigation in static, synthetic environments with restricted movement (e.g., 4 or 8 discrete directions on small grids). These prior works generally do not address grounded, embodied spatial tasks that require real-time interaction with dynamic and cluttered environments, nor do they typically support multi-agent coordination or continuous movement. Some works have used language models for perception-conditioned action selection but without broader path planning capabilities.

This paper differentiates itself by investigating the inherent spatial reasoning ability of pre-trained LLMs in dynamic environments for global path planning. It proposes a training-free framework that enables LLMs to function as spatial reasoners, overcoming the limitations of simplified environments, restricted movement sets, and single-agent focus seen in previous LLM studies. By encoding environments, agents, and paths uniformly as discrete text tokens, the work bridges the gap between language reasoning and complex spatial tasks. It aims to demonstrate LLMs' zero-shot adaptability to novel scenarios, multi-agent coordination, closed-loop replanning, and dynamic obstacle avoidance, offering an alternative to data-intensive learning-based approaches while also suggesting a complementary relationship with RL. The research also extends to downstream applications such as environment-aware humanoid motion generation and 3D path planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the capabilities of motion agents empowered by Large Language Models (LLMs) towards autonomous navigation in dynamic and cluttered environments. The authors aim to demonstrate that pre-trained LLMs can serve as effective spatial reasoners for complex path planning tasks without requiring task-specific training or fine-tuning.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in prior research on LLMs' spatial reasoning and conventional navigation methods:

1. **Limited Scope of Previous LLM Spatial Reasoning:** Existing studies on LLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities often confine movements to simple, static environments with restricted directions (e.g., four directions on small grids). They typically focus on single-agent scenarios or symbolic reasoning rather than grounded, embodied tasks in complex, dynamic settings. The authors aim to expand the applicability of LLMs to more realistic and challenging navigation problems.
2. **Challenges with Conventional Navigation Methods:** Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for path navigation, while effective in controlled environments, often necessitate extensive data collection and training. They also frequently struggle with generalization to novel or unseen scenarios, which limits their practical deployment flexibility. Classical pathfinding algorithms, such as A*, are generally designed for static environments and single agents, proving less adaptable to dynamic, multi-agent interactions and collision avoidance.
3. **Exploiting LLM Generalization and Zero-Shot Adaptability:** LLMs have demonstrated powerful generalization capabilities and comprehension of world knowledge across various domains. The authors are motivated to investigate whether these inherent abilities can be extended to spatial reasoning tasks, particularly in a zero-shot setting, to provide robust and flexible solutions that circumvent the data and training requirements of RL.
4. **Addressing Multi-Agent Coordination and Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance:** Navigation in environments with multiple interacting agents and dynamic obstacles presents significant challenges. The research seeks to explore LLMs' capacity for multi-agent coordination, collision resolution, and real-time, closed-loop replanning, which are crucial for practical autonomous systems.
5. **Alignment with Human Intuition:** The paper proposes an "anchor-based" trajectory representation that more closely mimics human navigation, where paths are conceptualized as sequences of key points rather than dense, discrete movements. This approach is intended to be more intuitive for LLM planning and align with human-like maneuvering and obstacle avoidance.

By addressing these points, the research aims to pave the way for semantically grounded, interactive navigation systems that are flexible and scalable for applications in autonomous robotics, virtual reality, and human-robot interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology involves transforming spatial navigation problems into a text-based format that LLMs can process, utilizing their reasoning capabilities without explicit training for navigation. The system operates in a training-free, zero-shot manner.

#### 3.1. Spatial Encoding and Representation

The system uniformly encodes the environment, agents, and their trajectories as discrete text tokens.

- **Agent and Trajectory Representation:** Paths are represented as sequences of sparse anchor points $\{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_k\}$, where each $x_i$ is a position. These points are connected by straight lines. The total travel distance $D(T) = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} \|x_i - x_{i+1}\|_2$. A sub-path between $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$ is valid if no point $\gamma(t) = (1-t)x_i + tx_{i+1}$ for $t \in$ lies within an obstacle set $O$. This anchor-based approach is intended to be more flexible than fixed discrete movements and align with how humans navigate.
- **Spatial Environment Representation:** The environment (e.g., floor plans) is represented in two text-based formats:
 - **Grid-based (G):** A 2D grid where $g_{i,j}=1$ denotes an obstacle and $g_{i,j}=0$ denotes free space.
 - **Code-based (C):** A list of obstacle coordinates, e.g., `obstacles.append((i1, j1)..., (in, jn))`.
 The authors argue that text-based representations bridge the gap between spatial reasoning and natural language processing, leveraging LLMs' text processing strengths and avoiding "redundant information" found in image inputs.

#### 3.2. System Architecture (Figure 2)

The LLM-centered system operates through the following pipeline:

1. **Environment Encoding:** A floor map is initially encoded into either a grid-based (G) or code-based (C) textual format, forming the environment $E$.
2. **Initial Trajectory Generation:** Given $N$ agents, each with a starting point $s_i$ and a target point $t_i$, the LLM $L$ generates initial trajectories $T_i$ for all agents based on $E$ and the set of start/target points $S = \{s_i\}$ and $T = \{t_i\}$.
3. **Simulation and Collision Detection:** Agents are simulated in environment $E$ following their generated trajectories.
4. **Closed-Loop Replanning (Path Refining):** If a collision occurs (either with static obstacles or other dynamic agents), the involved agent(s) query the LLM. The query includes the agent's current position $p_i(t)$ and details of the detected collisions $C_t$, requesting a refined path $T'_i$. This multi-turn interaction capability allows for iterative refinement.
5. **Output:** The system outputs collision-free trajectories for all agents, ensuring they reach their target points.

#### 3.3. Path Refining Strategies

Two strategies are proposed for iterative trajectory refinement based on LLMs' multi-turn capability:

- **Additive Strategy:** When an agent gets stuck during the $i$-th trial for reason $r$ (including its current position), the LLM recalculates the entire motion plan holistically, effectively restarting from the original starting point $s$ to the destination $t$. $T_{i+1} = L(s, t, r)$.
- **Compositional Strategy:** This approach refines the trajectory incrementally. If an agent is stuck at position $p_i$ during the $i$-th trial for reason $r$, the LLM plans the remainder of the path from $p_i$ to the destination $t$. $T_{i+1} = L(p_i, t, r)$.
In multi-agent scenarios, the LLM coordinates across agents using either strategy.

#### 3.4. Dataset and Evaluation

- **Dataset:** The R2V dataset (Liu et al., 2017), comprising 815 realistic rectilinear floor plans from actual buildings, is used. These floor plans are converted into textual formats. For each floor plan, three pairs of start and target points are randomly sampled. The A* algorithm is used to generate obstacle-free optimal paths, serving as ground truth.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** The performance of LLMs is assessed using four standard metrics:
 - **Success Rate (SR):** Percentage of episodes where the agent reaches the goal.
 - **Success weighted by Path Length (SPL):** Rewards shorter, more optimal paths for successful runs.
 - **Completion Rate (CR):** Measures the fraction of the planned route the agent covers.
 - **Weighted Success Rate (WSR):** Assigns higher weight to successes on longer (and thus typically harder) optimal paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated several modern LLMs, including GPT-4o, Gemini, DeepSeek, Llama, OpenAI o3-mini, and Claude-Sonnet, in a zero-shot setting on real floorplans from the R2V dataset.

#### 4.1. Single-Agent Path Navigation (Single Trial)

- **Performance of LLMs:** Models with advanced reasoning capabilities, particularly o3-mini (SR: 0.781) and DeepSeek-R1 (SR: 0.673), demonstrated stronger performance in spatial navigation compared to general-purpose models.
- **Input Representation Comparison:** Textual input representations generally outperformed image-based input (GPT-4o image SR: 0.370 vs. GPT-4o code SR: 0.420). Among textual formats, code-based input often yielded better performance for most models due to explicit coordinate encoding. However, for o3-mini, the grid-based format was more effective (SR: 0.781 for grid vs. 0.507 for code), which the authors attribute to its ability to interpret spatial patterns intuitively.
- **Baseline Comparison:** A simple baseline of moving directly from start to destination without navigation achieved an SR of 0.370, significantly lower than the best-performing LLMs with textual inputs.

#### 4.2. Multi-Turn Interactions for Path Refining

- **Performance Improvement:** Both additive and compositional path refining strategies consistently improved overall performance metrics (SR, SPL, CR, WSR) as the number of turns increased (up to 4 turns).
- **Strategy Comparison:**
 - The **additive strategy** generally achieved higher success rates (SR and WSR), as it recalculates the complete path from the origin at each step, integrating all prior adjustments. For o3-mini, SR increased from 0.781 (1 turn) to 0.882 (4 turns).
 - The **compositional strategy**, which refines the trajectory incrementally from the current stuck position, exhibited higher SPL and CR. For o3-mini, SPL increased from 0.710 (1 turn) to 0.786 (4 turns). The compositional approach was observed to be more susceptible to suboptimal adjustments.

#### 4.3. Multi-Agent Scenarios

- **Performance with Increasing Agents:** As the number of agents increased from one to three, performance scores (SR and CR) decreased but remained within a practical range. For example, o3-mini with additive strategy showed SR of 0.871 (1 agent), 0.720 (2 agents), and 0.643 (3 agents) after multiple turns. This indicated the feasibility of multi-agent coordination by LLMs in complex, dynamic environments.
- **Strategy Impact:** Different update strategies (additive vs. compositional) showed minimal impact on overall performance in multi-agent settings. The compositional approach tended to yield slightly lower scores, suggesting the additive approach might be more effective in globally resolving coordination conflicts.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results and Applications

- **Visualized Navigation:** Visualizations demonstrated that the LLM-based system could generate effective obstacle-free trajectories for both single and multiple agents. The system autonomously adjusted paths multiple times to resolve issues in scenarios involving multiple agents navigating and avoiding obstacles and one another.
- **Humanoid Motion Generation:** The system was shown to integrate with motion generation agents (e.g., OmniControl, TLControl, Motion-Agent) as a downstream task. By providing an obstacle-free trajectory, the LLM-based navigation system could guide inherently non-environment-aware motion generation models to produce realistic, environment-aware humanoid movements.
- **3D/2.5D Extension:** The methodology was extended to 3D environments by representing floor maps as height maps and generating 3D anchor points. This allowed the system to plan paths that involve vertical movement, such as navigating stairs, and avoid obstacles in three dimensions.

Overall, the results indicate that modern LLMs, especially those with strong reasoning capabilities, can achieve success rates around 80% in single-agent, single-turn navigation and approach 90% with multi-turn refinement. The system demonstrated capabilities for multi-agent scenarios with a manageable decrease in performance, all in a zero-shot setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of Large Language Models' (LLMs) inherent spatial reasoning capabilities and their application in autonomous navigation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in LLM Spatial Reasoning:** This work is positioned as an early study that significantly expands the scope of LLMs in spatial reasoning beyond static or symbolic tasks. By demonstrating LLMs' ability to perform complex pathfinding, dynamic obstacle avoidance, and multi-agent coordination in cluttered, real-world-inspired environments, it extends the practical utility of these models into embodied AI domains.
2. **Training-Free and Zero-Shot Generalization:** A key contribution is the development of a training-free framework that leverages the zero-shot generalization capabilities of pre-trained LLMs. This approach offers an alternative to data-intensive, task-specific training methods common in reinforcement learning, potentially reducing development costs and increasing adaptability to novel scenarios without prior exposure.
3. **Realistic and Dynamic Navigation:** The use of real indoor floorplans (R2V dataset) and the focus on continuous movement, dynamic obstacles, multi-agent interaction, and closed-loop replanning represent a move towards more realistic navigation challenges compared to previous LLM studies limited to simplified grids or static environments. This enhances the applicability of LLMs to practical robotics and autonomous systems.
4. **Novel Methodology for Spatial-Language Bridging:** The uniform encoding of environments, agents, and paths as discrete text tokens provides a methodology for bridging the gap between spatial reasoning and natural language processing. This text-based input strategy, which proved effective over image-based input, capitalizes on LLMs' strengths in processing structured language.
5. **Multi-Agent Coordination and Collision Resolution:** The demonstration of LLMs coordinating multiple agents and autonomously resolving dynamic collisions highlights their potential for managing complex interactions in shared environments. This capability is critical for applications involving teams of robots or agents.
6. **Benchmarking and Dataset Contribution:** By constructing a comprehensive dataset based on real floorplans and proposing detailed evaluation protocols (SR, SPL, CR, WSR), the research provides a framework for benchmarking future LLM performance in spatial navigation tasks.
7. **Downstream Applications:** The proof-of-concept extensions to environment-aware humanoid motion generation and 3D path planning illustrate the versatility of the system. This integration with motion synthesis models suggests a pathway for creating more intelligent and contextually appropriate behaviors for virtual agents and robots.
8. **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work is intended to inspire further research in areas such as autonomous robotics, virtual reality, and interactive human-robot interaction. It opens new possibilities for semantically grounded, interactive navigation and encourages exploration of hybrid approaches combining LLMs with other techniques like deep reinforcement learning. Future work could address real-world deployment on physical robots and local environment embedding for scalability.
