# Navigating Motion Agents in Dynamic and Cluttered Environments through LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Dartmouth College developed a training-free framework that enables pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform autonomous navigation in dynamic and cluttered environments. The system, which encodes spatial information as text tokens, achieved single-agent success rates up to 0.882 and demonstrated multi-agent coordination and dynamic obstacle avoidance in real-world floor plans.

## Problem

- Traditional path navigation methods (RL, A*) often struggle with zero-shot generalization to novel environments, dynamic multi-agent interactions, and require extensive data or are limited to static settings.
- Previous LLM applications for spatial reasoning were typically confined to simplified, static environments with restricted movements or focused on symbolic reasoning, not grounded, embodied tasks.
- There was a need to leverage LLMs' inherent generalization capabilities for complex spatial reasoning in dynamic, real-time scenarios without task-specific training.

## Method

- A training-free framework that uniformly encodes environment maps, agent states, and trajectories as discrete text tokens, enabling LLMs to act as spatial reasoners.
- Implemented a closed-loop replanning mechanism with 'additive' and 'compositional' multi-turn strategies for iterative path refinement and collision resolution in dynamic environments.
- Utilized an 'anchor-based' trajectory representation, where paths are sequences of sparse key points, to facilitate LLM planning and align with human-like navigation.

## Results

- Achieved a single-agent success rate (SR) of **0.781** for o3-mini in one turn, which improved to **0.882** (additive strategy) and **0.865** (compositional strategy) with four turns of interaction.
- Demonstrated successful multi-agent coordination, with o3-mini achieving an SR of **0.720** for two agents and **0.643** for three agents, showcasing feasibility in complex dynamic scenarios.
- The methodology was extended to downstream applications including guiding environment-aware humanoid motion generation and planning paths in 3D/2.5D environments, such as navigating stairs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained LLMs possess intrinsic spatial reasoning abilities that can be effectively leveraged for complex, embodied navigation tasks without explicit task-specific training or fine-tuning.
> - Text-based input representations (grid or code) proved more effective for LLM spatial reasoning than image-based inputs, with performance depending on the LLM's architecture and interpretation capabilities.
> - Multi-turn interactions and iterative path refinement significantly improve navigation performance, robustness, and enable LLMs to handle dynamic obstacles and multi-agent coordination.
