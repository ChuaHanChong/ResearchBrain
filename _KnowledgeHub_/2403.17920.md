# TC4D: Trajectory-Conditioned Text-to-4D Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> TC4D introduces a framework that generates 4D (animated 3D) scenes by decomposing object motion into distinct global and local components, conditioned by user-defined trajectories. This approach enables synthesizing complex animations where objects move across environments while undergoing local deformations, bridging the realism gap between 2D video generation and existing 4D methods.

## Problem

- Existing 4D generation techniques are primarily restricted to local movements within a confined scene bounding box, limiting the magnitude and scope of motion.
- There is a realism disparity between state-of-the-art 2D video generation models (which handle global motion) and current 4D generation methods (which offer limited global motion).
- Current 4D architectures struggle with efficient synthesis of large-scale global motion, requiring computationally expensive representations for entire trajectories.

## Method

- **TC4D** explicitly factors motion into global rigid transformations, which are guided by user-specified 3D trajectories, and local deformation fields.
- It optimizes local deformations using a trajectory-aware adaptation of Video ==Score Distillation Sampling== (VSDS), leveraging a pre-trained text-to-==video diffusion model==.
- The method employs regularization techniques, including a smoothness penalty and diffusion timestep annealing, to ensure temporally consistent and high-quality local motion.

## Results

- TC4D was preferred in **85%** of user comparisons for overall quality, and **89%** for motion quality and **92%** for motion amount, significantly outperforming baseline methods.
- The system enables novel applications such as animating objects along arbitrary user-defined trajectories and creating compositional scenes with multiple dynamic elements.
- Qualitative results demonstrate improved realism and significantly greater motion in generated 4D scenes compared to previous methods, maintaining comparable geometry quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing motion into distinct global and local components allows for efficient synthesis of large-scale movements without requiring the volumetric representation to encompass the entire trajectory.
> - Trajectory-aware VSDS effectively decouples the desired long duration of generated 4D scenes from the limited frame output of underlying video diffusion models.
> - Strategic use of regularization, such as a smoothness penalty and annealing diffusion timesteps, is critical for producing smooth and temporally coherent local deformations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has witnessed substantial advancements, particularly in synthesizing dynamic visual content. This work builds upon progress in text-to-video generation, which has enabled the creation of dynamic visual media with increasing fidelity. These 2D video generation capabilities have, in turn, spurred the development of techniques for 3D video (or 4D) generation, where animated 3D scenes are produced conditioned on various inputs such as videos, images, or text prompts. Applications for realistic and controllable 4D content generation span diverse fields, including visual effects, virtual reality, and industrial design.

Existing methods for 4D generation typically animate a volumetric scene representation through learned deformations or appearance changes predicted by neural representations or generative models. A fundamental limitation of these techniques is that the synthesized motion is often confined to a specific region, such as a 3D bounding box containing the volumetric representation. Consequently, the generated motion is predominantly local, with magnitudes generally smaller than the scale of the scene itself. For example, current approaches can produce characters that perform actions like walking in place, gesticulating, or swaying. However, they generally lack the capacity to synthesize global motions, such as an object moving across a larger scene. This constraint results in a noticeable gap in realism and scope between the motion effects achievable with state-of-the-art 2D video generation models and those produced by existing 4D generation methods.

This paper positions itself to address this identified gap by introducing a method designed to generate 4D content that incorporates both local deformations and large-scale global movements. It seeks to bring the realism and complexity of motion in 4D generation closer to that of advanced 2D video generation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance text-to-4D generation by enabling the synthesis of scenes with both local and global motion components. This addresses a critical limitation in existing 4D generation techniques, which are primarily restricted to generating localized movements within a confined scene bounding box.

The motivation for this work arises from several factors:
- **Limitations of Existing 4D Representations**: Current 4D representations, such as deformation models or time-dependent neural representations, are inherently limited in the scale of motion they can generate. They struggle to synthesize movements that extend significantly beyond the localized bounding box used for rendering. This means objects can animate in place (e.g., walking, waving) but cannot traverse a scene.
- **Realism Gap with 2D Video Generation**: There is a disparity in the realism of motion between state-of-the-art 2D video generation models, which can depict objects moving freely across a scene with both global and local animations, and existing 4D generation methods, which offer limited global motion. Bridging this gap is crucial for creating more compelling and dynamic 4D content.
- **Architectural Incompatibility for Global Motion**: Existing 4D architectures are not well-suited for synthesizing global motion efficiently. Attempting to model large-scale movements solely through deformation would require an expansive scene bounding box (leading to computational inefficiency and high memory usage) and the challenging optimization of long-range deformations.
- **Temporal Resolution Constraints of Supervision Models**: Current open-source text-to-video models, often used for supervision in 4D generation, typically produce a limited number of frames (e.g., a few tens of frames). This poses a challenge for generating 4D scenes that exhibit coherent global motion over extended durations.
- **Enhancing Controllability and Expressiveness**: By enabling explicit control over global motion through trajectory conditioning, the research aims to provide a more flexible and expressive framework for 4D content creation, facilitating novel applications like animating objects along arbitrary paths and compositing multiple dynamic elements.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the TC4D approach include:
1. Proposing a novel motion representation that decomposes motion into distinct global and local components.
2. Developing a mechanism to guide this motion decomposition using trajectory information.
3. Decoupling the temporal resolution and total duration of generated 4D scenes from the inherent output constraints of the underlying video generation model used for supervision.
4. Demonstrating improved realism and an increased amount of motion in generated 4D scenes compared to prior methods.
5. Showcasing novel applications such as generating 4D scenes along user-defined trajectories and enabling compositional scene synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TC4D (Trajectory-Conditioned Text-to-4D Generation) is an approach that factors motion into global and local components to synthesize dynamic 3D scenes. The method initiates with a static 3D scene, then integrates global and local motion effects using trajectory conditioning and a trajectory-aware version of video score distillation sampling (VSDS).

#### 3.1. 4D Scene Representation

The core representation is a deformable 3D Neural Radiance Field (NeRF), denoted $N_{3D}$, which models appearance and geometry within a unit bounding box. This $N_{3D}$ captures the scene's canonical pose. The 4D scene at any given time $t$ is represented by warping sampling rays with a time-varying deformation field $F(x, t)$ to the canonical pose, such that $N_{4D}(x, t) = N_{3D}(F(x, t))$. In TC4D, this deformation $F$ is explicitly decomposed into a global rigid transformation $R$ and a local deformation field $D$:
$N_{4D}(x, t) = N_{3D}(D \circ R(x, t))$.
Here, $R(x, t)$ applies rotation, translation, and scaling to the bounding box, while $D(x_d, t)$ maps a coordinate $x_d$ from the globally transformed space to the canonical non-deformed space $x_c$.

#### 3.2. Modeling Global Motion with Trajectory Conditioning

Global motion, denoted $R$, is implemented as a rigid transformation of the scene's bounding box along a user-specified 3D trajectory $T$. This trajectory is parameterized as a cubic spline $T(t)$ where $t \in$, with control points provided by the user. The rigid transformation involves both translation and rotation. The translation directly follows $T(t)$, and the rotation matrix aligns the gradient of the trajectory $T' = dT/dt$ with the "front" face normal $\hat{n}$ of the bounding box, while maintaining the vertical component perpendicular to the ground plane. This factorization isolates large-scale motion, preventing the need for the volumetric representation to encompass the entire trajectory, which would be computationally intensive and memory-inefficient.

#### 3.3. Modeling Local Motion with Trajectory-Aware VSDS

Local motion is captured by optimizing the deformation model $D$ using guidance from a trajectory-aware adaptation of Video Score Distillation Sampling (VSDS), which leverages a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model.

- **Video Score Distillation Sampling (VSDS)**: This procedure involves rendering a video sequence $v$ from the 4D representation. Noise $\epsilon$ is then added to $v$ according to a diffusion process, governed by a diffusion timestep $t_d$. A pre-trained video diffusion model, conditioned on a text embedding $y$, predicts the added noise $\hat{\epsilon}$. This prediction is used to compute the score distillation sampling gradient $\nabla_\theta L_{4D}$, which optimizes the 4D representation parameters $\theta$. In TC4D, the parameters of $N_{3D}$ are frozen, and only the parameters of the deformation model $D$ are optimized to learn local motion.

- **Incorporating the Trajectory (Trajectory-Aware VSDS)**: A challenge with applying VSDS for dynamic objects along long trajectories is that pre-trained video diffusion models are designed for a finite number of frames ($N$) and have a limited intrinsic motion scale. Naively sampling $N$ frames across a long trajectory would force the diffusion model to represent motion ranges inconsistent with its training data.
 To address this, the trajectory is divided into segments of length $\Delta t$, chosen to roughly match the intrinsic motion scale $M_v$ of the video diffusion model. During optimization, random start times $t_0$ are sampled along the trajectory, and $N$ evenly spaced frame times are selected within the corresponding segment. Videos are rendered for these segments, and the deformation model $D$ is optimized using the VSDS gradient.

- **Regularization and Annealing**:
 - **Smoothness Penalty**: To regularize the generated deformation field, a smoothness penalty $L_{smooth}$ is applied. This penalty, which calculates finite-difference approximations to spatial and temporal derivatives in the 3D displacement video sequence, helps to dampen high-frequency displacements and ensures smoother local motion.
 - **Diffusion Timestep Annealing**: The VSDS gradients from late diffusion timesteps ($t_d \approx 1$) can introduce jittery, high-frequency motion. TC4D mitigates this by annealing the sampling interval of diffusion timesteps during optimization. Initially, $t_d$ is uniformly sampled from $[0.02, 0.98]$. This interval is linearly decayed during training to $[0.02, 0.5]$, promoting temporally consistent motion.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Scene Initialization**: The static $N_{3D}$ is initialized using hybrid SDS, combining supervision from 3D-aware text-to-image diffusion models (MVDream, ProlificDreamer) and text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) to achieve high-quality appearance and 3D structure.
- **Neural Representations**: Both $N_{3D}$ and the local deformation field $D$ are implemented using hash-encoded feature grids, followed by a small multi-layer perceptron (MLP) for decoding $D$ into a 3D deformation vector.
- **Optimization**: The model is trained using the Adam optimizer for 10,000 iterations for each of the three stages (static 3D initialization and dynamic deformation training). The deformation field $D$ is initialized to a small offset.
- **Rendering**: Images are rendered at resolutions native to the specific diffusion models used for supervision (e.g., 256x256 for 3D-aware SDS, 512x512 for VSD, and 512x320 for video SDS).
- **Computational Resources**: Training takes approximately 10 hours on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, using about 80GB of VRAM.

For comparative evaluation, a modified version of the 4D-fy baseline is used, where its static output is post-hoc rigidly transformed along the same trajectories provided to TC4D.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TC4D focused on both qualitative assessment of generated 4D scenes and quantitative analysis through user studies and CLIP scores.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Improved Motion Realism and Amount**: Visualizations demonstrate that TC4D synthesizes scenes with a significantly greater amount and realism of motion compared to the 4D-fy baseline. For instance, examples like "batman riding a camel" or "a deer walking" show TC4D producing coherent walking animations consistent with the trajectory, whereas 4D-fy typically generates mostly static objects that are merely rigidly transformed along the path, or exhibit only small, incoherent local movements. The supplementary videos are highlighted as the most definitive demonstration of these improvements.
- **Compositional Scenes and Arbitrary Trajectories**: The method supports novel applications, including animating objects along user-defined trajectories and combining multiple dynamic objects within a compositional scene, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
- **Automated Generation Potential**: The paper explores the integration of large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT for automated scene layout and trajectory generation. While some imperfections (e.g., object scaling inconsistencies) were observed, this indicates potential for future fully automated 4D scene creation.
- **Geometry Quality**: Visualizations of depth maps and normals suggest that TC4D maintains a geometry quality comparable to 4D-fy, indicating that the enhanced motion does not come at the expense of structural integrity.
- **Effect of Scale**: TC4D can incorporate scaling as part of the rigid transformation along a trajectory, allowing for dynamic changes in object size (e.g., "a flame getting larger"), a capability not straightforward with purely translational global motion.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **User Study**: A user study involving 20 evaluators compared TC4D against a modified 4D-fy baseline and ablation variants across several metrics: Appearance Quality (AQ), 3D Structure Quality (SQ), Motion Quality (MQ), Motion Amount (MA), Text Alignment (TA), and Overall preference.
 - **Overall Preference**: TC4D was preferred in 85% of comparisons, a statistically significant margin ($p < 0.05$).
 - **Motion Quality and Amount**: Users showed strong preferences for TC4D in Motion Quality (89%) and Motion Amount (92%), confirming the method's effectiveness in generating more and higher-quality motion.
 - **Other Metrics**: Preferences for Appearance Quality (55%) and Text Alignment (81%) also favored TC4D, while 3D Structure Quality (52%) showed a preference that was not statistically significant. This suggests TC4D maintains or slightly improves appearance and alignment while significantly advancing motion capabilities.
- **CLIP Score**: CLIP scores, which measure the cosine similarity between text and image embeddings, were used for evaluation. TC4D achieved a score of 32.01, comparable to 4D-fy (31.90), but higher than DreamGaussian4D (28.70). The paper notes that CLIP score is a limited metric for motion quality as it assesses single frames, thus not fully capturing the benefits of TC4D's dynamic capabilities.
- **Comparison with DreamGaussian4D**: A separate user study comparing TC4D to a modified DreamGaussian4D (an image-to-4D method adapted post-hoc with trajectories) showed overwhelming preference for TC4D across all metrics, with significant advantages in motion quality and amount. DreamGaussian4D's 3D structure was noted to be sensitive to input image alignment.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An extensive ablation study assessed the contribution of different components of TC4D, with results indicating statistically significant preferences for the full TC4D model in most cases:
- **Without local deformation**: This variant (rigid transformation only) produced unrealistic, rigid motion, with users overwhelmingly preferring TC4D's inclusion of deformation.
- **Without trajectory (training and rendering)**: Removing the trajectory entirely, reverting to conventional deformation-based 4D generation, resulted in less complex animations.
- **Without trajectory training**: Animating the scene via rigid transformation *after* training (without trajectory guidance during optimization) yielded animations less coherent with the trajectory.
- **Without trajectory-aware VSDS**: This led to coarser trajectory sampling and failure to synthesize smooth motion.
- **Without global transformations**: Local deformations were significantly reduced, highlighting the importance of factorizing global motion.
- **Without smoothness penalty**: Removing this regularization term led to a modest negative effect on perceived motion quality, indicating its role in dampening high-frequency jitter.
- **Without diffusion timestep annealing**: This procedure, designed to regularize motion, was found to improve temporal coherence. Its removal resulted in jittery, high-frequency deformation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TC4D framework represents a notable advancement in text-to-4D generation by addressing the critical challenge of synthesizing large-scale, global motion in conjunction with local deformations. By explicitly factoring motion into global and local components and conditioning it on user-defined trajectories, the work overcomes limitations of previous methods that were confined to animating objects within fixed locations or small bounding boxes.

### 6. Significance

- **Enhanced Realism and Expressiveness**: The method significantly improves the realism and amount of generated motion in 4D scenes, producing animations where objects can traverse environments, which was a notable gap compared to 2D video generation.
- **Novel Motion Model**: The decomposition of motion into global rigid transformations (along a spline trajectory) and local deformations (guided by trajectory-aware VSDS) provides a more robust and scalable approach to motion synthesis.
- **Expanded Applications**: TC4D enables new capabilities such as synthesizing animations along arbitrary trajectories, which can be user-specified, and facilitates compositional scene generation where multiple dynamic objects interact or move within a larger scene.
- **Addressing Temporal Constraints**: The trajectory-aware VSDS mechanism effectively decouples the desired long duration of 4D scenes from the typically limited frame output of current video diffusion models, making the approach practical for extended animations.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Visual Effects and Content Creation**: The ability to generate realistic 4D content with controllable global motion can revolutionize visual effects pipelines, virtual reality experiences, and other forms of digital content creation by reducing manual animation efforts.
- **Robotics and Simulation**: Generating dynamic 3D assets moving along specified paths could benefit simulation environments for robotics, autonomous driving, and virtual training.
- **Foundation for Future 4D Models**: By open-sourcing its code, TC4D provides a foundation that can be built upon, particularly as 2D video generation models continue to improve in quality and temporal coherence.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations:
- **Motion Imperfections**: Current results exhibit some imperfections, such as imperfect leg motion, foot sliding, or asymmetric and not fully cyclic animations. These are attributed to the quality of publicly available pre-trained text-to-video models (e.g., VideoCrafter2's 16-frame output may be insufficient for capturing complete cyclic motions).
Future work is expected to benefit from rapid improvements in 2D video generation models. Specific avenues for future research include:
- Extensions to generate scenes with multiple interacting objects.
- Adding motion to existing in-the-wild 3D assets.
- Synthesis of motion across unbounded, large-scale scenes.
- Developing automated text-to-4D metrics to provide more comprehensive quantitative evaluations beyond single-frame-centric metrics like CLIP score.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The authors include an ethics statement, acknowledging the potential for misuse of realistic, animated 3D scenes to generate misinformation. They condemn such misuse and emphasize the importance of ongoing research into countermeasures.
