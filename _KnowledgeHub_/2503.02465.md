# UAV-VLRR: Vision-Language Informed NMPC for Rapid Response in UAV Search and Rescue

> [!summary] Summary
> The UAV-VLRR framework integrates vision-language models with an onboard non-linear model predictive control system to enhance search and rescue operations for drones. It achieved an average mission completion time 33.75% faster than an off-the-shelf autopilot and 54.6% faster than a human pilot in simulated disaster scenarios while maintaining robust obstacle avoidance.

## Problem

- Current drone-based search and rescue (SAR) operations suffer from inefficient manual control and tedious waypoint setting, leading to delays and human error under pressure.
- Traditional UAV mission planning methods struggle to adapt to dynamic, unpredictable, and obstacle-rich disaster environments, causing a lack of adaptability.
- Human operators in chaotic SAR scenarios often experience cognitive overload, impairing judgment and slowing critical response times.

## Method

- A multimodal system combines ChatGPT-4o (LLM) for natural language command processing and Molmo-7B-D BnB (VLM) for aerial image interpretation, identifying target points and obstacles.
- Identified pixel coordinates are converted to real-world Cartesian coordinates, which are then fed into an onboard Non-linear ==Model Predictive Control (NMPC)== system.
- A point-to-point NMPC directly computes optimal, safe, and rapid quadrotor trajectories and control inputs, eliminating the need for an external trajectory generator and facilitating onboard deployment.

## Results

- The UAV-VLRR framework completed missions an average of **33.75%** faster than an off-the-shelf autopilot and **54.6%** faster than a human pilot across two complex scenarios.
- The multimodal system accurately identified target points and obstacles, with a maximum coordinate error of 23 cm, falling within the 25 cm safety threshold.
- UAV-VLRR demonstrated superior safety and control efficiency, producing more direct and optimized flight paths with effective obstacle avoidance compared to baseline methods, which included a higher propensity for human pilot collisions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating high-level semantic understanding from vision-language models with low-level agile control (NMPC) allows for rapid, context-aware autonomous drone navigation.
> - The point-to-point NMPC design, which directly generates trajectories and control, enables real-time onboard computation on resource-constrained hardware like OrangePi.
> - Natural language mission planning significantly simplifies the operator interface, reducing cognitive load and enhancing efficiency in emergency response compared to manual input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses challenges within the field of Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) applications for search and rescue (SAR) operations, specifically focusing on improving response times and operational efficiency in complex, disaster-stricken environments.

**Current Landscape and Limitations:**
Traditional UAV-based SAR missions often rely on manual flight control or predefined waypoint setting, which can be inefficient and lead to delays, especially under high-stakes conditions where human operators may experience cognitive overload. Existing UAV mission planning techniques frequently utilize handcrafted obstacle maps, LiDAR-based navigation, or heuristic path-planning algorithms. While these methods can be effective in structured environments, they often struggle to adapt to the dynamic and unpredictable nature of disaster zones, which may contain constantly changing obstacles such as collapsed buildings, debris, and vegetation. This highlights a need for more intelligent and autonomous systems capable of real-time environmental interpretation and adaptive flight path generation.

**Advances in Multimodal Vision-Language Approaches for Robotics:**
The integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) represents a significant advancement in robotic systems. The advent of Vision Transformers (ViTs) laid the groundwork for models capable of integrating diverse modalities like text, images, and video. More recent developments, such as OpenAI's ChatGPT-4o and the Molmo model, demonstrate real-time reasoning across multiple modalities, allowing robots to interpret user requests involving visual and textual information. In the UAV domain, prior work like UAV-VLA utilized multimodal systems to generate actionable mission paths from text-image pairs. Further research, including UAV-CodeAgents, UAV-VLPA*, RaceVLA, and CognitiveDrone, has expanded these capabilities to include reasoning for navigation, global route optimization, and real-time control in dynamic environments like drone racing.

**Developments in Safe Agile Control for Drones:**
Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) is recognized for its effectiveness in agile drone control, as exemplified by its use in high-speed drone racing by teams like ETH Zurich. Research has explored NMPC's advantages in tracking dynamically infeasible trajectories and achieving time-optimal flight, though often at a high computational cost, sometimes requiring off-board processing. Challenges include ensuring computational efficiency for real-time onboard deployment and integrating robust obstacle avoidance. Previous work also includes considerations for safe drone landings in crowded areas.

**Positioning of UAV-VLRR:**
The UAV-VLRR framework builds upon these advancements by integrating the semantic understanding capabilities of state-of-the-art vision-language models with an agile NMPC system. Unlike many NMPC applications that rely on external trajectory generation, UAV-VLRR employs a point-to-point NMPC, which simplifies computational requirements and facilitates onboard deployment. This approach aims to bridge the gap between high-level human-language mission planning and low-level, rapid, and safe autonomous drone execution in complex, obstacle-rich environments, thereby addressing the limitations of manual control and traditional path-planning in SAR operations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged inefficiencies and limitations of current drone-based search and rescue (SAR) operations, particularly in emergency scenarios. These limitations include:
- **Inefficient Manual Control:** Traditional manual drone control or the manual setting of waypoints is often slow, requires significant operator skill, and is prone to human error, especially under the intense pressure of emergency situations.
- **Cognitive Overload:** In chaotic disaster zones, human responders can experience cognitive overload, leading to impaired judgment, overlooked critical details, and delays in mission planning.
- **Lack of Adaptability:** Conventional UAV mission planning techniques, which rely on pre-defined maps or heuristic algorithms, struggle to adapt to the unpredictable and rapidly changing environments characteristic of disaster areas (e.g., collapsed structures, debris).

To address these challenges, the key objectives of the UAV-VLRR research are:
- **Improve Response Times:** Develop a system that can significantly reduce the time required to complete SAR missions, recognizing that faster response can be critical for saving lives.
- **Enhance Operator Interface:** Provide a more intuitive and natural method for SAR operators to plan missions. This involves enabling mission planning through natural language commands rather than tedious manual waypoint input.
- **Autonomous and Safe Navigation:** Create an intelligent system that can autonomously interpret aerial imagery, identify relevant targets and obstacles, and generate actionable flight paths in real time. This system must also ensure the drone executes these missions rapidly and safely, incorporating built-in obstacle avoidance.
- **Onboard Rapid Control:** Implement a control mechanism capable of agile and precise drone movement, facilitating quick coverage of designated points while operating safely in cluttered environments. The control system is designed to run onboard the UAV to ensure real-time responsiveness.

In essence, the research aims to establish a UAV system that integrates human-like understanding of complex aerial scenes with rapid, autonomous, and safe flight capabilities, thereby creating a more efficient and reliable tool for emergency SAR operations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UAV-VLRR system is designed as a rapid SAR framework comprising two main integrated components: a multimodal system for scene interpretation and a non-linear model predictive control (NMPC) for rapid drone response.

**A. Vision-Language Integration for Accurate Object Identification**
This component is responsible for interpreting aerial imagery and natural language commands to identify mission-critical elements.
- **System Architecture:** It employs a multimodal system that combines a Large Language Model (LLM) and a Vision-Language Model (VLM).
 - **LLM Agent:** ChatGPT-4o is utilized to process natural language input, extracting semantic information to identify "target points" (goal objects) and "obstacles."
 - **VLM Agent:** The quantized Molmo-7B-D BnB 4-bit model handles image processing. It works in conjunction with the LLM to identify the specified goal objects within the input image.
- **Input and Output:** The system receives an image-text pair as input. The output consists of the pixel coordinates of the identified target points and obstacles.
- **Coordinate Conversion:** Once pixel coordinates are obtained, they are converted into real-world Cartesian coordinates. This conversion is based on the camera's height ($h_{camera}$) and its field-of-view (FoV) parameters.
 - The real-world width and height of the image frame are calculated using the formulas:
 - Real width (m) = $2 \cdot h_{camera} \cdot tan(\frac{\theta_{horizontal}}{2})$
 - Real height (m) = $2 \cdot h_{camera} \cdot tan(\frac{\theta_{vertical}}{2})$
 - Where $\theta_{horizontal}$ and $\theta_{vertical}$ are derived from the camera's diagonal FoV and aspect ratio. These real-world coordinates define the target points and obstacle locations, which are then transmitted to the NMPC component.

**B. NMPC for Rapid Response**
This component is responsible for generating optimal, safe, and rapid flight trajectories for the quadrotor based on the target and obstacle information from the multimodal system.
- **Control Architecture:** A point-to-point non-linear model predictive control (NMPC) architecture is used. This design avoids the need for an external trajectory generation module, allowing the NMPC to directly compute both the optimal trajectory and the necessary control inputs for rapid execution.
- **Quadrotor Dynamics:** The system models the quadrotor's dynamics using 13 states: position ($p_W$), linear velocity ($v_W$) in the world frame, quaternion orientation ($q_B$), and body angular rates ($\omega_B$). The control inputs are the four motor forces ($u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4$). The dynamics include thrust, gravity, and body torques derived from motor forces and drone geometry.
- **Discretization:** The continuous-time dynamics are discretized using the 4th order Runge-Kutta method to facilitate numerical optimization: $x(k+1) = f_{RK4}(x(k), u(k), \delta t)$.
- **Optimization Problem Formulation:** The NMPC problem is formulated as a multiple shooting scheme, minimizing a cost function $J(x, u)$ over a prediction horizon $N$:
 - $J(x, u) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} l(x_u(k), u(k))$
 - The stage cost $l(x, u)$ includes terms for tracking error, control effort, and obstacle avoidance: $l(x, u) = \|x_u - x_r\|^2_Q + \|u\|^2_R + Penalty_{Obs}$.
 - $\|x_u - x_r\|^2_Q$: Penalizes deviations from the desired state (target point).
 - $\|u\|^2_R$: Penalizes large control inputs.
 - $Penalty_{Obs}$: A penalty term is incorporated to enforce obstacle avoidance, based on the coordinates received from the multimodal system.
 - **Constraints:** The optimization is subject to:
 - Quadrotor dynamics.
 - Initial state ($x_u(0) = x_0$).
 - Bounds on control inputs ($u_{min} \leq u(k) \leq u_{max}$).
 - State constraints ($x(k) \in X$), including ensuring the drone avoids obstacles.
- **Implementation:** The optimization problem is solved iteratively in real-time onboard the drone using the CasADi framework.

**C. Experimental Setup**
- **Location:** Experiments were conducted within the drone arena of the Intelligent Space Robotics Lab at Skoltech.
- **Mission Task:** The command provided was: "Fly around each of the center of the X on yellow objects. Avoid three legs of red tripod stands."
- **Experiment Types:** Three distinct experiments were performed for comparison:
 1. UAV-VLRR framework.
 2. Drone utilizing an off-the-shelf autopilot.
 3. A human drone pilot, who was provided the same aerial image and prompt, and controlled the drone with access to a belly-mounted camera.
- **Scenarios:** Two different scenarios were used:
 - Scene 1: Involved three target points and two obstacles.
 - Scene 2: Involved four target points and three obstacles.
- **Hardware:** The multimodal system ran on a remote server equipped with an RTX 4090 GPU and an Intel Core i9-13900K processor. The drone's NMPC was executed on an onboard computer, specifically an OrangePi.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Mission completion time was the primary metric. For UAV-VLRR, timing began upon code launch. For the human pilot, timing began when the image was presented.
- **Multimodal System Accuracy Check:** The accuracy of the multimodal system's object identification was assessed by comparing its detected coordinates with Vicon ground truth data. A detection was considered accurate if it fell within a 25 cm radius of the actual object, accounting for a safety radius used in the NMPC.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance of the UAV-VLRR framework compared to an off-the-shelf autopilot and a human pilot.

**A. Multimodal System Results**
The multimodal system successfully identified target points and obstacles in both Scene 1 and Scene 2 based on image-text pairs.
- **Scene 1 Accuracy:** For Scene 1 (3 targets, 2 obstacles), the maximum coordinate error for target points was 16 cm (for Target 3, X-coordinate) and for obstacles was 19 cm (for Obstacle 2, X-coordinate). Most errors were considerably lower.
- **Scene 2 Accuracy:** For Scene 2 (4 targets, 3 obstacles), the maximum coordinate error for target points was 23 cm (for Target 3, X-coordinate) and for obstacles was 19 cm (for Obstacle 2, Y-coordinate). One target point (Target 1, X-coordinate) showed 0 cm error.
- **Overall Accuracy:** The identified points generally fell within the specified 25 cm accuracy threshold, which was defined to account for the NMPC's safety radius and obstacle gain. The authors noted that non-ideal imaging conditions, such as image distortion or skewing due to a low altitude capture, contributed to some of the observed errors.

**B. Mission Results**
The mission completion times, measured in seconds, were recorded for each experiment type across both scenes.

- **Scene 1 (3 targets, 2 obstacles):**
 - UAV-VLRR (Experiment 1): 28 seconds
 - Off-the-shelf Autopilot (Experiment 2): 40 seconds
 - Human Pilot (Experiment 3): 57 seconds
 - In Scene 1, UAV-VLRR completed the mission 30% faster than the off-the-shelf autopilot and 50.9% faster than the human pilot.

- **Scene 2 (4 targets, 3 obstacles):**
 - UAV-VLRR (Experiment 1): 30 seconds
 - Off-the-shelf Autopilot (Experiment 2): 48 seconds
 - Human Pilot (Experiment 3): 72 seconds
 - In Scene 2, UAV-VLRR completed the mission 37.5% faster than the off-the-shelf autopilot and 58.3% faster than the human pilot.

- **Comparative Performance:**
 - **Speed:** UAV-VLRR consistently achieved the fastest mission completion times in both scenarios. On average, UAV-VLRR was 33.75% faster than the off-the-shelf autopilot and 54.6% faster than the human pilot.
 - **Safety:** The multimodal setup (Experiments 1 and 2) demonstrated superior performance over human pilots. During manual flights, the human pilot exhibited a higher propensity for collisions with obstacles.
 - **Control Efficiency:** The custom NMPC used in UAV-VLRR was more efficient than the off-the-shelf autopilot, consistently completing missions faster.
 - **Flight Paths:** Visual representations of the flight trajectories for both scenes (Figures 6 and 7) illustrate that UAV-VLRR generally produced more direct and optimized paths while effectively avoiding obstacles, in contrast to the more varied and sometimes less efficient paths of the other two methods.

These results indicate that the integrated vision-language informed NMPC framework significantly improves the speed and safety of UAV operations in simulated search and rescue missions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UAV-VLRR framework presents advancements in the application of autonomous UAVs for search and rescue (SAR) operations, with several significant implications:

- **Enhanced Response Times in Emergency Scenarios:** The demonstrated average mission completion times, which are 33.75% faster than an off-the-shelf autopilot and 54.6% faster than a human pilot, highlight the potential for significantly reducing response times in real-world emergencies. This reduction could be critical in situations where rapid intervention affects outcomes.

- **Intuitive and Natural Operator Interaction:** By leveraging a Vision-Language Model (VLM) and Large Language Model (LLM) (ChatGPT-4o), the system allows operators to define complex SAR missions using natural language commands. This approach simplifies mission planning, reduces cognitive load on human operators, and eliminates the need for manual waypoint input, which can be inefficient and error-prone in high-stress environments.

- **Autonomous Navigation with Obstacle Avoidance:** The integration of real-time environmental interpretation with Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) enables the UAV to autonomously generate and execute rapid, safe flight paths while actively avoiding obstacles. This capability is particularly valuable in unpredictable disaster zones where the environment is dynamic and cluttered.

- **Robustness in Complex Environments:** Traditional UAV mission planning methods often struggle in unstructured and changing environments. UAV-VLRR's ability to interpret aerial imagery and adapt its trajectory in real-time offers a more robust solution for navigating areas with collapsed buildings, debris, or dense vegetation.

- **Onboard Computational Feasibility:** The use of a point-to-point NMPC, which does not rely on an external trajectory generation module, contributes to reduced computational demands. This design facilitates deployment on resource-constrained onboard computers (e.g., OrangePi), which is essential for fully autonomous, real-time operation in the field without constant reliance on remote high-performance computing.

- **Foundation for Future Autonomous Systems:** This work lays a foundation for future advancements in autonomous aerial robotics for SAR. The identified areas for future work—including adaptive learning, multi-UAV coordination, and integration of dynamic environmental factors like moving obstacles—suggest pathways toward even more sophisticated and resilient SAR systems.

In summary, UAV-VLRR represents a step towards more efficient, safer, and user-friendly autonomous drone operations in critical search and rescue contexts, offering a technological contribution that could ultimately enhance disaster response capabilities.
