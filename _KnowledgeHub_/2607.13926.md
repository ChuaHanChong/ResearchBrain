# S-squared-VLA: Decoupling Semantic and Spatial Streams in Vision-Language-Action Models for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Wuhan University of Technology introduce S2^22-VLA, an autonomous driving framework that decouples semantic reasoning and spatial perception to overcome the "semantic-physical gap" and "spatial representation collapse". This architecture achieved a 87.1 PDMS on the NAVSIM benchmark, including a 98.4% No Collision rate, by generating precise, boundary-aware trajectories from a single front-view camera.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) applied to autonomous driving suffer from a "semantic-physical gap" where discrete, high-level reasoning outputs lose fine-grained spatial details necessary for continuous vehicle control.
- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often experience "spatial representation collapse" due to single-stream designs that entangle semantic reasoning with low-level control, leading to a bias towards semantics over precise geometry.
- This entanglement results in a lack of explicit spatial and geometric priors, causing VLA models to generate trajectories that may be logically sound but physically unsafe or inconsistent with road boundaries.

## Method

- Introduces **S2^22-VLA**, an architecture that explicitly decouples semantic reasoning and spatial perception into distinct processing streams within Vision-Language-Action models.
- A **Multi-Scale Semantic Stream**, powered by ==InternVL3-2B==, extracts high-level intent and contextual understanding, while a **Task-Driven Spatial Stream**, using a ==ViT== and ==dynamic resolution==, preserves fine-grained visual details and integrates geometric priors via auxiliary perception tasks (==BEV map== and agent prediction).
- A **Dual-Stream Planning Adapter** then fuses these decoupled semantic and spatial representations through cascaded ==cross-attention== to generate safe and physically feasible continuous trajectories.

## Results

- S2^22-VLA achieved a new state-of-the-art Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of **87.1** on the NAVSIM closed-loop benchmark under a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) setting, using only a single front-view camera.
- The framework demonstrated superior safety and efficiency, with the highest No Collision (NC) rate of **98.4%** and Ego Progress (EP) of **81.6%** among all evaluated VLM/VLA methods.
- S2^22-VLA outperformed a range of vision-only and LiDAR-fused end-to-end models, as well as existing VLM and VLA baselines, by generating smooth, boundary-aware trajectories that closely aligned with expert demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling semantic reasoning from fine-grained spatial perception in autonomous driving models prevents the degradation of geometric information and enhances trajectory precision.
> - Injecting geometric priors into a dedicated spatial stream through auxiliary tasks like Bird's Eye View (BEV) map generation and dynamic agent prediction significantly improves adherence to road boundaries and obstacle avoidance.
> - Fusing multi-scale semantic features with uncompressed, task-driven spatial features allows for robust high-level intent understanding and precise low-level control, leading to safer and more coherent driving actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving has seen a progression from traditional modular pipelines to end-to-end (E2E) learning paradigms. E2E models aim to directly map raw sensor inputs to vehicle control actions or waypoints, integrating perception, prediction, and planning within a single framework. While E2E models like UniAD and VAD have demonstrated advancements, they often operate as data-driven "black boxes," lacking semantic interpretability and high-level reasoning capabilities crucial for complex, long-tail scenarios or multi-agent interactions.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged to address these limitations by incorporating semantically grounded reasoning. Trained on extensive image-text datasets, VLMs align visual inputs with natural language, enabling understanding and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. Models such as DriveLM and DriveGPT4 exemplify this by generating driving decisions along with interpretable rationales, enhancing generalization. However, applying VLMs to low-level vehicle control introduces a "semantic-physical gap." VLM outputs are typically discrete (e.g., "turn left"), whereas vehicle control requires continuous, high-frequency trajectory planning. This tokenization process often degrades fine-grained spatial and geometric information essential for precise motion generation. Consequently, VLMs frequently function as high-level "advisors" that rely on separate downstream controllers.

To bridge this coherence gap between language and action, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have been developed. Originating in robotics (e.g., RT-2), VLAs unify perception, reasoning, and control into a single policy, grounding free-form instructions into trajectories and generating continuous control signals. Despite these efforts, existing VLA architectures, particularly those relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), often suffer from a "spatial representation collapse." This occurs because they typically employ a single-stream design that maps highly abstract semantic representations to low-level control. This entanglement leads to cross-modal alignment on heavily compressed embeddings, neglecting the rich spatial cues available in earlier visual features and introducing a semantic-over-geometry bias. This limitation results in a lack of explicit spatial and geometric priors, potentially leading to unsafe or geometrically inconsistent trajectories.

The presented work, S$^2$-VLA, positions itself within this landscape by directly addressing the spatial representation collapse in VLA models for autonomous driving. It aims to overcome the semantic-physical gap and the entanglement of reasoning and control by explicitly decoupling semantic and spatial processing, thereby enhancing the generation of precise, boundary-aware trajectories.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for the development of S$^2$-VLA are rooted in addressing fundamental limitations within current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving.

Firstly, a core motivation is the **semantic-physical gap** inherent in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when applied to autonomous driving. While VLMs excel at high-level reasoning and understanding natural language instructions (e.g., "turn left"), their outputs are typically discrete and symbolic. Autonomous driving, however, requires continuous, high-frequency, and physically compliant control actions, such as precise trajectory planning. The process of tokenizing visual information into discrete language tokens for VLM processing inevitably leads to a loss of fine-grained spatial and geometric details. This degradation makes it challenging for VLMs to generate the accurate, low-level control signals necessary for safe navigation, forcing them to act as open-loop advisors rather than direct controllers.

Secondly, the paper identifies a critical structural limitation in existing VLA architectures: **spatial representation collapse**. Traditional VLA models often employ a single-stream design that unifies perception, reasoning, and control. In an attempt to align high-level semantic features with low-level control actions, these models typically rely on heavily compressed representations from deep network layers. This entanglement of reasoning with execution results in a progressive loss of fine-grained spatial and geometric information. The model becomes biased towards semantic understanding at the expense of precise geometric awareness, leading to trajectories that might be logically consistent but physically infeasible or unsafe due to a lack of explicit spatial priors. Such trajectories may exhibit issues like curb encroachment, lane boundary violations, or imprecise obstacle avoidance.

To counteract these limitations, the key objectives of S$^2$-VLA are:
1. **To explicitly decouple semantic and spatial processing streams** within VLA models. This aims to allow the model to leverage robust high-level reasoning from VLMs while simultaneously preserving and utilizing fine-grained spatial and geometric priors from visual inputs.
2. **To develop a dedicated semantic stream** that can effectively extract multi-scale VLM features for robust intent reasoning without compromising fine-grained visual details crucial for control.
3. **To design an independent spatial stream** that bypasses the autoregressive language bottleneck, directly preserving uncompressed spatial features from the visual encoder. This stream is intended to be explicitly equipped with rich spatial and geometric priors through auxiliary perception supervision (e.g., map and agent prediction).
4. **To create a dual-stream planning adapter** that can effectively fuse the high-level semantic intent with precise spatial constraints. This fusion mechanism aims to ensure that generated trajectories are both semantically aligned with instructions and physically feasible, respecting environmental boundaries and dynamic obstacles.
5. **To achieve state-of-the-art performance** in closed-loop autonomous driving benchmarks, specifically demonstrating improved safety metrics like collision avoidance and drivable area compliance, by mitigating the identified spatial representation collapse.

By addressing these issues, the researchers aim to develop a VLA framework that can generate safe, boundary-aware, and physically compliant trajectories, thereby advancing the capabilities of end-to-end autonomous driving systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

S$^2$-VLA proposes a novel architecture designed to decouple semantic and spatial processing streams in Vision-Language-Action models for autonomous driving. The overall framework integrates multimodal inputs and processes them through three main components: a Multi-Scale Semantic Stream, a Task-Driven Spatial Stream, and a Dual-Stream Planning Adapter.

**Multimodal Input and Initial Processing:**
The model takes various inputs: a high-level navigation command (C$_{nav}$), ego-vehicle motion history (T$_{hist}$), a front-view image (I), and learnable visual and action queries. These inputs are initially tokenized, and visual features are encoded.

**A. Multi-Scale Semantic Stream:**
This stream is responsible for extracting high-level semantic features and driving intent.
- **Backbone:** InternVL3-2B, which combines an InternViT visual encoder with a Qwen2.5 language model, serves as the backbone. It jointly processes text and visual tokens.
- **Query Injection:** N$_{act}$ = 64 learnable action queries (Q$_{act}$) are injected at the input to guide the network in aggregating execution-relevant features.
- **Sparse Multi-Scale Sampling:** Instead of using only the final layer or all layers, the semantic stream extracts hidden states from a carefully selected subset of Transformer layers (L = {3, 8, 13, 18, 23, 24}). This strategy balances computational efficiency with the need to capture both fine-grained details (shallow layers) and high-level semantics (deeper layers). The output comprises dense semantic features (V$_{sem}$) and action-centric features (V$_{act}$).
- **Ego-State Integration:** The ego-vehicle's historical states are encoded via a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) into an ego-state embedding (E$_{ego}$). This embedding is concatenated with the action-centric features at each sampled layer to form a unified state memory (E$_{mem}$), providing kinematic priors.

**B. Task-Driven Spatial Stream:**
This stream is designed to preserve fine-grained spatial details and inject explicit geometric priors, circumventing the compression inherent in language models.
- **Visual Input and Feature Extraction:** A dynamic resolution strategy is employed, partitioning each high-resolution front-view image into N$_{patch}$ = 9 parallel-processed patches (eight local crops and one global thumbnail). N$_{vis}$ = 64 learnable visual queries (Q$_{vis}$) are introduced per patch to actively capture spatial information. These queries, along with standard class and patch tokens, form the input sequence to a Vision Transformer (ViT). After encoding, the visual queries are decoupled and reorganized into a spatially dense visual feature (V$_{vis}$). Tile-level positional embeddings (P$_{tile}$) are added, and a Transformer encoder processes the sequence to establish global spatial coherence.
- **Auxiliary Perception Heads:** Two heads are used to explicitly ground the spatial stream in geometric priors:
 - **Map Head:** The 1D visual token sequence is reshaped into a 2D spatial grid (V$_{2D}$) and then decoded by upsampling and convolutional layers into a high-resolution Bird's Eye View (BEV) multi-class semantic map ($\hat{M}_{sem}$). This head focuses on a local, visible BEV space ahead of the ego-vehicle. It is supervised by a weighted cross-entropy loss (L$_{map}$), which emphasizes safety-critical features like drivable areas and lane boundaries.
 - **Agent Head:** Following the DETR paradigm, a Transformer decoder with N$_{agent}$ = 30 learnable queries predicts dynamic objects. An MLP head decodes these queries into continuous agent predictions, including oriented 2D bounding boxes and classification confidences. This head is supervised by a combination of L1 spatial regression loss and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) classification loss (L$_{agent}$), after optimal bipartite matching using the Hungarian algorithm.
- **Spatial Feature Output:** Through this multi-task supervision, the spatial stream is regularized to encode metric layouts and spatial boundaries. The intermediate representation V is linearly projected to form V$_{spatial}$, a dense, geometrically grounded feature.

**C. Dual-Stream Planning Adapter:**
This module fuses the decoupled semantic and spatial representations to generate dynamically feasible trajectories. It is a cascaded multi-stage cross-attention decoder.
- **Initialization:** M = 8 learnable planning tokens (P$^{(0)}$) representing future trajectory waypoints are initialized.
- **Cascaded Blocks (N stacked):** Each block refines the planning tokens through two sequential stages:
 1. **Semantic and State Alignment:** Planning tokens (P$^{(l-1)}$) are aligned with multi-scale semantic features (V$_{sem}$) and ego-state memory (E$_{mem}$) via parallel multi-head cross-attention (MHCA) and self-attention (MHSA). A learnable gating parameter modulates the contribution of semantic features. This stage establishes coarse-grained navigation intent.
 2. **Visual Spatial Refinement:** The semantically fused planning tokens (P$_{fuse}$) are then refined using the dense geometric features (V$_{spatial}$) from the spatial stream via another MHCA. This injects explicit spatial and geometric priors, forcing the planning tokens to adhere to real-world motion constraints and obstacle boundaries.
- **Trajectory Decoding and Multi-Task Objective:** After N blocks, the final planning tokens (P$^{(N)}$) are processed by a lightweight MLP decoder to predict the future ego-vehicle trajectory ($\hat{Y}$). The prediction is supervised by an L1 regression loss, augmented with kinematic smoothness constraints (L$_{smooth}$) that penalize excessive acceleration and jerk. The entire S$^2$-VLA framework is trained end-to-end using a joint multi-task objective function (L$_{total}$) that balances planning (L$_{plan}$), dynamic agent perception (L$_{agent}$), and static map grounding (L$_{map}$).

**Training Procedure:**
A three-stage training procedure is employed:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The pre-trained VLM backbone is fine-tuned on the ReCogDrive VQA dataset for 3 epochs to enhance instruction-following and spatial reasoning.
2. **LoRA Training:** With the VLM backbone frozen, LoRA is applied, and the intent extraction and auxiliary perception modules are trained from scratch for 4 epochs, temporarily decoupling visual spatial features from the planning adapter.
3. **Visual Refinement Optimization:** Perception and intent components are frozen, and the visual refinement module is optimized for 4 epochs to align generated trajectories with precise spatial constraints.
The entire framework is then optimized for end-to-end trajectory planning on the NAVSIM benchmark.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of S$^2$-VLA was conducted on the NAVSIM closed-loop benchmark, emphasizing real-time interaction with dynamic environments. The performance was measured using the Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS), a composite metric reflecting No Collision (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Time to Collision (TTC), Comfort (Comf), and Ego Progress (EP). All comparisons were made under a purely supervised fine-tuning (SFT) setting, excluding post-training reinforcement learning.

**Quantitative Results:**

1. **Overall Performance:** S$^2$-VLA achieved a PDMS of 87.1, setting a new benchmark for VLA models under SFT. This score was achieved using only a single front-view camera.

2. **Comparison with E2E Models:**
 - S$^2$-VLA surpassed several vision-only E2E models, including UniAD (83.4 PDMS) and PARA-Drive (84.0 PDMS).
 - It also outperformed established LiDAR-fused E2E methods like ARTEMIS (87.0 PDMS) and DRAMA (86.9 PDMS). This indicates that the dedicated spatial stream effectively infers 3D structural constraints from 2D imagery, mitigating depth ambiguity.
 - While DiffusionDrive achieved a slightly higher PDMS of 88.1, it relies on an iterative diffusion decoder and explicit LiDAR point clouds, which provide inherent 3D physical boundaries. S$^2$-VLA's competitive performance without these additional resources highlights its representational efficiency.

3. **Comparison with VLM/VLA Models:**
 - S$^2$-VLA significantly improved upon a naive VLM baseline (InternVL3-2B), which used the same backbone but relied on autoregressive text-head decoding for trajectories, by 3.0 PDMS points (84.1 to 87.1). This gain directly demonstrates the resolution of the semantic-geometric misalignment issue caused by discrete language tokens.
 - It outperformed recent VLA methods like ReCogDrive (86.5 PDMS) and ImagiDrive (86.4 PDMS), even with a comparatively smaller backbone.
 - Specifically, S$^2$-VLA achieved the highest No Collision (NC) rate of 98.4 among all evaluated VLM/VLA methods and the highest Ego Progress (EP) score of 81.6. These safety-critical gains are attributed to explicit spatial grounding, enabling accurate perception of obstacle proximity and road curvature.

**Qualitative Analysis:**

- **E2E Transfuser:** Showed unsafe behaviors such as curb encroachment and boundary violations in complex road topologies, suggesting limitations in synthesizing geometric precision with contextual reasoning from raw sensor fusion alone.
- **InternVL3-2B (VLM baseline):** Exhibited systematic geometric deviations in high-curvature turns and intersections, frequently failing to maintain lane centering. This validated the hypothesis that representing continuous spatial geometry via discrete language tokens introduces accumulating rounding errors, leading to imprecise paths.
- **ReCogDrive (VLA baseline):** Despite incorporating VLM-derived cognitive tokens, its long-range predictions still diverged from ground truth, especially in challenging turns and areas with unclear lane markings. This supported the core motivation that deep feature compression in standard VLM backbones degrades fine-grained spatial details crucial for boundary-aware planning.
- **S$^2$-VLA:** Consistently generated trajectories that closely aligned with expert demonstrations across diverse and complex scenarios. The planned paths remained smooth, stable, and strictly adhered to physical road structures, even with occlusions or degraded lane markings. This demonstrated the successful fusion of contextual understanding and precise spatial grounding achieved by decoupling semantic and geometric perception.

**Ablation Study:**

The ablation study confirmed the contribution of each key component:
- **BaseVLM:** A VLM baseline (InternVL3-2B) achieved a PDMS of 84.1.
- **Multi-level Semantic Features:** Introducing the planning adapter with multi-level semantic features improved PDMS by 1.5 points (84.1 to 85.6). Gains were notable in EP (+1.1) and TTC (+1.2), indicating improved long-horizon planning. However, DAC only showed a modest gain (+0.9), confirming semantic reasoning alone is insufficient for precise geometric alignment.
- **ViT-based Spatial Stream:** Adding the spatial stream further increased PDMS to 86.2 (+0.6), with the most significant improvement in DAC (94.0 to 94.5). This highlighted the spatial stream's role in preserving geometric details and enforcing structural alignment, thus mitigating geometric limitations.
- **Auxiliary Perception Tasks:** Incorporating BEV map generation and agent prediction tasks yielded an additional 0.9 PDMS gain, reaching the final score of 87.1. This significantly improved safety-critical metrics, including NC (+0.3) and EP (+1.0), by providing holistic physical priors for both static boundaries and dynamic obstacles. This dual objective ensured latent representations capture essential environmental information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The S$^2$-VLA framework introduces a novel architectural approach to Vision-Language-Action models for autonomous driving, addressing critical limitations that have hindered their practical deployment. The primary significance of this work lies in its explicit decoupling of semantic reasoning from spatial perception, offering a method to mitigate the "semantic-physical gap" and "spatial representation collapse" observed in prior VLM and VLA architectures.

By demonstrating that continuous spatial features can be effectively preserved and utilized, bypassing the inherent quantization bottleneck of discrete language heads, S$^2$-VLA contributes to the development of more robust and reliable autonomous driving systems. The achieved state-of-the-art performance on the NAVSIM closed-loop benchmark, particularly the highest No Collision rate among evaluated VLM/VLA methods, indicates that this architectural design directly translates into improved safety and performance. The ability to generate trajectories that are not only semantically aligned with high-level instructions but also physically feasible and boundary-aware represents a step towards safer human-robot interaction in complex environments.

The methodology of integrating auxiliary perception tasks (BEV map generation and agent prediction) within the spatial stream is a significant contribution. This explicit injection of geometric priors enables the model to understand and react to both static road structures and dynamic obstacles with greater precision, which is crucial for real-world driving scenarios. This approach suggests a path forward for enhancing the spatial awareness of monocular vision-based autonomous systems, potentially reducing the reliance on expensive 3D sensors like LiDAR for certain tasks while still achieving competitive performance.

The impact of this work extends to improving the interpretability and generalizability of autonomous driving models. By allowing the semantic stream to handle high-level reasoning and contextual understanding, while the spatial stream ensures low-level geometric fidelity, S$^2$-VLA moves beyond the "black box" nature of some E2E systems. This decoupling could facilitate better diagnostics and understanding of model failures, contributing to the trustworthiness of autonomous vehicles.

However, the paper also acknowledges two limitations that suggest future research directions. The computational overhead associated with dense extraction and dual-stream fusion of spatial features indicates a need for more efficient feature representation mechanisms, potentially through sparser sampling or alternative fusion techniques. Additionally, the orthogonal nature of the dual-stream architecture to training paradigms opens avenues for further performance enhancement through post-training strategies like closed-loop reinforcement learning, which could build upon the current SFT-based achievements.

In summary, S$^2$-VLA's successful mitigation of spatial perception deficiencies in VLMs represents a valuable advancement, offering a blueprint for designing integrated autonomous driving policies that effectively combine the strengths of high-level semantic reasoning with the imperative of precise, physically constrained control.
