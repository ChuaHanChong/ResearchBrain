# Matrix-Game: Interactive World Foundation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Skywork AI developed Matrix-Game, a 17-billion-parameter interactive world foundation model that generates game worlds from a reference image and user actions. The model achieves superior action controllability and physical consistency, outperforming previous models on a new comprehensive benchmark for Minecraft environments.

## Problem

- Existing interactive video datasets are scarce and lack fine-grained action annotations for training robust world models.
- Prior world models struggle to accurately simulate physical dynamics and achieve fine-grained, frame-by-frame controllability in generated environments.
- A lack of standardized, objective evaluation benchmarks hinders consistent comparison and progress in interactive world modeling research.

## Method

- Developed **Matrix-Game**, a **17-billion-parameter** multi-modal ==Diffusion Transformer==, for interactive image-to-world generation, conditioned purely on a reference image and discrete/continuous actions.
- Curated **Matrix-Game-MC**, a large-scale dataset of **2,700 hours** of unlabeled Minecraft videos and **1,200 hours** of action-labeled gameplay, with a ==two-stage progressive training strategy==.
- Introduced **GameWorld Score**, a unified benchmark assessing models across visual quality, temporal quality, action controllability, and physical rule understanding.

## Results

- **Matrix-Game** surpassed prior open-source Minecraft world models across all eight dimensions of the GameWorld Score benchmark, showing **0.95** keyboard accuracy and **0.76** object consistency.
- Fine-grained action control was demonstrated with over **88%** accuracy for individual keyboard actions and over **89%** accuracy for mouse-based camera movements across eight directions.
- Double-blind human evaluations confirmed Matrix-Game's superiority, achieving a **96.3%** win rate in overall quality and maintaining strong visual and temporal coherence in long-duration autoregressive generations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning a diffusion model purely on a reference image and user actions allows for intuitive, fine-grained control and fosters spatial intelligence in world generation.
> - Scaling both the model size (17 billion parameters) and the curated interactive video dataset significantly improves generalization and understanding of physical rules across diverse virtual environments.
> - A two-stage progressive training approach, leveraging unlabeled data for broad world understanding before fine-tuning with action-labeled data, improves model stability and interactive performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in artificial intelligence: world models, video diffusion models, controllable video generation, and game video generation.

**World Models:** The paper begins by establishing the fundamental importance of world models, which enable intelligent agents to perceive, simulate, and reason about their environments. They are crucial for downstream tasks like autonomous driving, embodied intelligence, and generative game engines. Matrix-Game aims to contribute to this core area by providing a highly controllable and interactive world model.

**Video Diffusion Models:** The authors highlight video diffusion models as a leading paradigm for world modeling, due to their ability to learn fine-grained spatiotemporal dynamics and generate visually coherent videos. When conditioned on structured inputs, these models offer a flexible and scalable approach to modeling agent-environment interactions. Matrix-Game builds upon this foundation, utilizing a multi-modal diffusion transformer architecture.

**Controllable Video Generation:** The paper acknowledges the limitations of text-to-video generation in terms of precision and determinism. It discusses the evolution of controllable video generation, from reference image guidance to fine-grained camera control. Matrix-Game distinguishes itself by focusing on "interactive image-to-world generation," where control is provided through low-level, continuous keyboard and mouse actions, enabling precise, intuitive control in open-ended environments, a significant step beyond higher-level textual or coarse camera controls.

**Game Video Generation:** This is the most direct application area for Matrix-Game. The paper places its work in context with other notable game world generation models such as Genie, DIAMOND, GameNGen, OASIS, PlayGen, GameGenX, WorldMem, Matrix, Genie 2, and MineWorld. It critiques many existing approaches for overfitting to specific game datasets, exhibiting limited generalization, and being constrained by relatively limited model capacity and dataset scale. Matrix-Game directly addresses these limitations by scaling up both model size (17 billion parameters) and training data significantly, with the explicit goal of achieving stronger generalization and a more robust understanding of physical rules across diverse virtual game worlds. By focusing on Minecraft, an open-ended environment with rich interactions, the work is directly applicable to creating more realistic and controllable virtual worlds. The model's unique emphasis on learning *purely from raw images* (conditioned on a reference image and actions) rather than relying on text prompts, aligns with the concept of "Spatial Intelligence" and differentiates it from many text-to-video or text-to-world models.

In summary, Matrix-Game pushes the boundaries of interactive world modeling by developing a large-scale, image-conditioned diffusion model capable of fine-grained control, trained on an extensive and carefully curated dataset, and evaluated by a novel, comprehensive benchmark. This positions it as a significant advancement in generating highly controllable, physically consistent, and visually realistic interactive game worlds.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of Matrix-Game is driven by a clear set of motivations stemming from the challenges faced by current video-based world models, and aims to achieve ambitious objectives in the realm of interactive game world generation.

**Motivation:**
1. **Data Scarcity:** Acquiring high-quality, richly annotated interactive video datasets (e.g., with precise actions and camera movements) is challenging and expensive, especially at scale. This limits the ability of models to learn complex agent-environment interactions.
2. **Modeling Physical Dynamics and Controllability:** Existing world models struggle to accurately model the physical dynamics of an environment and achieve fine-grained, frame-by-frame controllability over time. Models need to generate not only visually coherent videos but also respond precisely to structured control signals.
3. **Lack of Standardized Evaluation:** The absence of a unified, objective benchmark makes it difficult to consistently compare different world models and quantify improvements, thereby hindering progress in the research area.

### 3. Key Objectives

To address these challenges, the authors set forth three core objectives, embodied in the Matrix-Game project:

1. **Introduce Matrix-Game as an Interactive World Foundation Model:** Develop a large-scale, diffusion-based model specifically designed for controllable game world generation. This model aims to enable precise control over character actions and camera movements while maintaining high visual quality and temporal coherence. A key design principle is to learn world understanding *purely from visual input* (a reference image), rather than relying on text prompts, aligning with the concept of Spatial Intelligence.
2. **Curate Matrix-Game-MC, a Comprehensive Minecraft Dataset:** Construct a novel, large-scale dataset tailored for Minecraft world modeling. This dataset comprises both vast amounts of unlabeled gameplay video clips for environment understanding and a substantial collection of high-quality, action-labeled clips with fine-grained keyboard and mouse annotations for interactive video generation. This addresses the data scarcity challenge.
3. **Develop GameWorld Score, a Unified Evaluation Benchmark:** Create a comprehensive benchmark that can systematically evaluate world models across multiple dimensions, including visual quality, temporal quality, action controllability, and physical rule understanding. This addresses the lack of standardized evaluation and facilitates objective comparison.

In essence, the overarching goal is to advance the frontier of interactive image-to-world generation by developing a robust, scalable foundation model, supported by a rich dataset and a rigorous evaluation framework, ultimately empowering users to interactively explore, guide, and construct coherent virtual worlds.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The methodology behind Matrix-Game is multi-faceted, encompassing large-scale dataset curation, a novel model architecture, a two-stage training strategy, and a comprehensive evaluation benchmark.

#### 4.1. Matrix-Game-MC: A Large-scale Dataset for Game World Generation

To overcome data scarcity and support complex world modeling, the authors curated **Matrix-Game-MC**, a comprehensive dataset based on Minecraft.

- **Unlabeled Data Collection (2,700 hours):**
 - Sourced from MineDojo Dataset, comprising 6,000 hours of raw gameplay.
 - A three-stage hierarchical filtering pipeline was applied:
 1. **Video Quality & Aesthetic Filtering:** Using DOVER and LAION predictor for sufficient resolution, clarity, and visual appeal.
 2. **Content Filtering:** Menu-State filtering (Inverse Dynamics Model to remove non-gameplay), Subtitle filtering (CRAFT text detector for intrusive text), and Human Face filtering (DeepFace for streamer face cams).
 3. **Motion & Camera Filtering:** GM-Flow for balanced motion and IDM for stable camera movements, discarding clips with too little/excessive motion or aggressive viewpoint changes. This resulted in 2,700 hours of 720p video clips.
- **Labeled Data Creation (over 1,026 hours):**
 - **Hybrid Strategy:** Combines Exploration Agent trajectories from the MineRL platform (curriculum-guided VPT agents for diverse behaviors) and Unreal Procedural Simulation (custom, high-fidelity environments for precise ground-truth kinematic and interaction annotations).
 - **Curation Strategies for Quality:**
 - **Camera Motion Restriction:** Yaw and pitch angles restricted to 15 degrees per frame to ensure viewpoint stability and temporal consistency.
 - **MineRL Engine Modification:** Disabled frustum-based chunk loading to prevent scene pop-ins; real-time monitoring to terminate recording during non-meaningful gameplay (e.g., agent near death, stuck, menus).
 - **Scenario Diversification:** Curated 14 distinct Minecraft biomes (e.g., forest, desert, ocean) and balanced discrete actions (move, jump, attack) within each.
 - **Final Labeled Dataset:** Over 1,026 hours for 33-frame training, and an expanded 1,200 hours for 65-frame training, ensuring balanced coverage across 14 Minecraft biomes and Unreal environments.

#### 4.2. Matrix-Game: An Interactive World Foundation Model

The core of the methodology is the **Matrix-Game** model, a 17-billion-parameter interactive world foundation model.

- **Image-to-World Generation Paradigm:** Inspired by Cosmos and Genie-2, Matrix-Game uses a latent diffusion model, specifically a multi-modal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT). Crucially, it learns *purely from raw images*, using a single reference image as the primary prior for world understanding, unlike many text-conditioned models.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Latent Space:** Video sequences are compressed into a spatiotemporally reduced latent space (8x spatial, 4x temporal) by a 3D Causal VAE.
 - **Diffusion Transformer:** A MMDiT processes reference image tokens (from a visual encoder) and noisy latent video representations.
 - **Autoregressive Generation:** To enable long-duration video generation, an autoregressive strategy is employed. The last `k=5` frames of a generated clip are used as motion conditions for the subsequent clip. Latent representations of these motion frames are concatenated with noisy latents along the channel dimension, along with a binary mask. Gaussian noise and Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) are applied to motion frames and reference images during training to improve robustness. The token replacement trick from HunyuanVideo I2V is also adopted for stable image-to-video generation.
 - **Action Injection (Controllability):** A dedicated control module (based on GameFactory design) integrates frame-level user actions. Discrete keyboard actions (up, down, left, right, jump, attack) are integrated via cross-attention, while continuous mouse movements (pitch angle changes) are concatenated with the input latent, processed through an MLP, and then temporal self-attention. Group operation trick aligns action signals with compressed latent tokens. CFG is also applied to action signals.
- **Model Training:** A two-stage progressive training strategy using rectified flow loss for improved stability and efficiency.
 - **Stage 1: Unlabeled Training for Game World Understanding:**
 - Initialization: Pretrained weights from HunyuanVideo I2V. Text branch replaced with an image branch. Action control module excluded.
 - Data: 2,700 hours of unlabeled 720p Minecraft videos. Trained with mixed frame counts (17, 33, 65) and aspect ratios.
 - Refinement: Further refined on an 870-hour subset of high-quality clips for coherent spatial structures and higher perceptual quality.
 - **Stage 2: Action-labeled Training for Interactive World Generation:**
 - Action control module integrated.
 - Data: 1,200 hours of action-labeled 720p 33-frame video clips from Minecraft and Unreal Engine. Fixed resolution and frame count initially.
 - Refinement: Further training on a balanced 1,200-hour dataset of 720p 65-frame clips, spanning 8 distinct Minecraft biomes and Unreal data, to capture long-range temporal dependencies and mitigate class imbalance.

#### 4.3. GameWorld Score: A Unified Benchmark for Minecraft World Models

To address the lack of standardized evaluation, a novel benchmark, GameWorld Score, was developed. It assesses models across eight fine-grained dimensions grouped into four pillars:

- **Visual Quality:**
 - **Aesthetic Quality:** LAION aesthetic predictor for perceived visual appeal.
 - **Image Quality:** MUSIQ predictor for low-level visual artifacts (e.g., noise, blurriness).
- **Temporal Quality:**
 - **Temporal Consistency:** Pairwise CLIP feature similarity between frames to assess background stability and flickering.
 - **Motion Smoothness:** Reconstruction error using a pretrained video frame interpolation network to detect unnatural dynamics.
- **Action Controllability:** Measures how well the video follows input actions, using an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) trained on 1,962 hours of Minecraft gameplay.
 - **Keyboard Control Accuracy:** Precision across four grouped action categories (e.g., forward/back/empty).
 - **Mouse Control Accuracy:** Precision for 9 camera movement categories (8 directions + empty), based on rotational angle changes.
- **Physical Rule Understanding:**
 - **Object Consistency:** Reprojection error between co-visible pixels across frames, using DROID-SLAM for depth and camera pose, isolating geometric consistency.
 - **Scenario Consistency:** MSE between paired frames from symmetric camera motions (e.g., up-then-down) to assess overall scene preservation.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

Matrix-Game demonstrates significant advancements in controllable game world generation, outperforming existing baselines across multiple quantitative metrics and qualitative human evaluations.

1. **GameWorld Score Benchmark Performance:**
 - Matrix-Game consistently **outperforms prior open-source Minecraft world models**, including Oasis and MineWorld, across all eight dimensions of the GameWorld Score benchmark (Table 2, Figure 2).
 - It shows particularly **strong gains in action controllability** (Keyboard Accuracy and Mouse Accuracy) and **physical consistency** (Object Consistency and Scenario Consistency). For instance, its Keyboard Accuracy reaches 0.95 and Mouse Accuracy 0.95, significantly higher than MineWorld (0.86 and 0.64) and Oasis (0.77 and 0.56). Object Consistency is 0.76 (vs. 0.51 for MineWorld and 0.56 for Oasis).
 - It also maintains competitive to superior performance in **visual quality** (Image Quality 0.72 vs. 0.69/0.65; Aesthetic 0.49 vs. 0.47/0.48) and **temporal quality** (Temporal Consistency 0.97 vs. 0.95/0.94; Motion Smoothness 0.98, matching baselines).

2. **Action Controllability:**
 - Detailed evaluation (Table 3) confirms Matrix-Game's **fine-grained control capabilities**. For keyboard actions, it achieves accuracies over 88% for all individual actions, with "forward" at 99% and "jump" at 95%.
 - For mouse-based camera movements across eight directions, which are typically more challenging, Matrix-Game maintains high accuracy, **exceeding 89% for all directions**, significantly surpassing baselines. This highlights its ability to precisely translate user inputs into desired in-game behaviors (Figures 12-14).

3. **Scenario Generalization:**
 - Matrix-Game consistently **outperforms baselines across eight diverse Minecraft scenarios** (beach, desert, forest, hills, icy, mushroom, plains, river) (Figure 9). This demonstrates its robustness and ability to generalize across varied terrain types, object distributions, and interaction dynamics, a key improvement over prior models which often overfit to specific scenarios.

4. **Autoregressive Generation for Long Videos:**
 - The model successfully employs an autoregressive multi-segment setting, maintaining **strong local temporal consistency between segments**. This allows for the generation of smooth, visually coherent, and controllable long-range video outputs without geometric misalignment or abrupt motion shifts, crucial for extended interactive sessions (Figure 10).

5. **Human Evaluation:**
 - Double-blind human studies overwhelmingly favored Matrix-Game, confirming its superiority across all assessed dimensions (Figure 8). It achieved a **96.3% win rate in Overall Quality**, 93.76% in Controllability, 98.23% in Visual Quality, and 89.56% in Temporal Consistency. These subjective preferences align strongly with the quantitative GameWorld Score, reinforcing the benchmark's reliability.

**Discussions on Failure Cases:**
The paper also candidly discusses limitations:
- **Edge Case Generalization:** Occasional struggles with precise controllability or spatial consistency in visually complex or underrepresented scenarios due to insufficient data coverage (Figure 11a).
- **Physics Understanding:** Room for improvement in modeling physical dynamics (e.g., agent walking through leaves) due to the scarcity of high-fidelity, physics-supervised data (Figure 11b).

Despite these limitations, the consistent and significant outperformance across a comprehensive benchmark and human evaluations demonstrates Matrix-Game's robust capabilities.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

Matrix-Game represents a substantial contribution to the fields of generative AI, embodied AI, and interactive content creation, with far-reaching significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancing Interactive World Modeling:** Matrix-Game introduces a novel paradigm of "interactive image-to-world generation" that shifts from text-driven generation to purely visual (reference image and actions) conditioning. This allows for a more intuitive and direct method of guiding world creation, moving towards true spatial intelligence where models build understanding directly from visual and physical cues.

2. **Bridging the Gap to Realistic Interactive Environments:** By achieving unprecedented levels of fine-grained control over character actions and camera movements, coupled with high visual fidelity and temporal coherence, Matrix-Game brings generative models closer to creating truly interactive and believable virtual worlds. This is critical for developing more sophisticated embodied agents and AI systems that can reason and operate within dynamic environments.

3. **Establishing a Standardized Evaluation Benchmark:** The introduction of **GameWorld Score** is a crucial contribution. By offering a unified, comprehensive benchmark that evaluates visual quality, temporal quality, action controllability, and physical rule understanding, it addresses a critical gap in the field. This benchmark will enable researchers to objectively compare and quantify improvements in interactive world models, accelerating progress and fostering rigorous scientific inquiry.

4. **Providing a Large-scale, High-Quality Dataset:** The curated **Matrix-Game-MC** dataset, comprising thousands of hours of both unlabeled and richly action-labeled Minecraft gameplay, is an invaluable resource. Its scale and meticulous curation protocol will fuel future research by providing the necessary data for training robust, controllable, and physically grounded world models.

5. **Technical Achievements and Scalability:** The development of a 17-billion-parameter diffusion transformer trained with a two-stage rectified flow pipeline demonstrates significant technical prowess in scaling generative models. Its autoregressive generation capability for long-duration videos further enhances its practical utility, allowing for sustained interactive experiences.

6. **Empowering Content Creation and Research:** By open-sourcing both the Matrix-Game model weights and the GameWorld Score benchmark, Skywork AI is making a substantial contribution to the broader AI community. This will lower the barrier to entry for researchers and developers, enabling faster iteration, comparative studies, and the development of new applications in generative game engines, interactive storytelling, and virtual reality.

7. **Future Trajectories for AI:** The work directly points to exciting future directions, including improving long-term temporal consistency, enriching action spaces beyond basic keyboard/mouse controls, and expanding the framework to more complex game environments (e.g., Black Myth: Wukong, CS:GO). This vision outlines a path toward more general-purpose and physically grounded world generation that could revolutionize various domains, from gaming and entertainment to simulation for robotics and autonomous systems.

In conclusion, Matrix-Game not only presents a state-of-the-art model for interactive world generation but also lays foundational groundwork—a dataset and a benchmark—that will significantly shape and accelerate future research in the critical area of physically grounded and controllable AI models capable of simulating and interacting with dynamic virtual environments.
