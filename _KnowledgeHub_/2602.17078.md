# Safe Continuous-time Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning via Epigraph Form

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed Epigraph-based PINN Actor-Critic Iteration (EPI), a framework for safe continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning. EPI effectively manages state constraints in dynamic environments by transforming discontinuous value functions into a continuous form via an epigraph reformulation, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art safe MARL baselines in both cost reduction and constraint satisfaction.

## Problem

- Existing Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) algorithms primarily operate in discrete-time, limiting their applicability to real-world, high-frequency, or irregular-interval continuous dynamics.
- Current continuous-time MARL (CT-MARL) methods based on Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) struggle with safety constraints that introduce discontinuities into value functions.
- Safe MARL methods primarily exist for discrete-time settings and do not naturally extend to continuous dynamics without relying on fully known system dynamics.

## Method

- Formulates safe continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning as a ==Continuous-Time Constrained== Markov Decision Process (CT-CMDP), explicitly including state-dependent safety constraints.
- Introduces an epigraph reformulation that transforms the CT-CMDP problem, converting potentially discontinuous value functions (due to safety constraints) into a continuous and differentiable form suitable for PINN-based learning.
- Develops Epigraph-based PINN Actor-Critic Iteration (EPI), an actor-critic framework that utilizes an inner-outer optimization scheme and z-independent critic networks for stable learning and real-time execution without expensive root-finding.

## Results

- EPI consistently achieved higher cumulative rewards (lower costs) and significantly lower constraint violation rates across various Safe Multi-Particle Environments (MPE) and Safe Multi-Agent MuJoCo benchmarks, outperforming baselines like MACPO, MAPPO-Lag, SAC-Lag, EPPO, and CBF.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that both the target loss and the value gradient iteration (VGI) loss are crucial for performance in infinite-horizon settings, preventing value function drift and ensuring gradient consistency.
- The epigraph reformulation was empirically shown to be critical for handling discontinuous value functions, with its absence leading to severely mis-scaled value predictions and unstable actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The epigraph reformulation effectively converts discontinuous value functions caused by state constraints into a continuous form, making them amenable to approximation by neural networks and HJB PDE solvers.
> - Designing critic networks to be z-independent and employing a revised outer optimization for optimal auxiliary state (z*) computation significantly reduces nonstationary noise during training and eliminates costly root-finding at execution.
> - Combining residual, target, and value gradient iteration (VGI) losses in the critic learning provides robust value function approximation and gradient consistency in infinite-horizon settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has demonstrated capabilities in various domains, including strategic games, multi-robot coordination, and wireless communication. However, the majority of existing MARL algorithms operate within a discrete-time Markov Decision Process (MDP framework), typically assuming fixed decision intervals. This discrete-time formulation presents limitations in complex, real-world scenarios characterized by high-frequency dynamics or irregular decision intervals, such as autonomous driving and financial trading. In these contexts, fixed-step discretization can lead to inaccuracies in policy learning, performance degradation, and unstable learning. This limitation highlights a need for continuous-time MARL (CT-MARL) frameworks.

Current CT-MARL research largely focuses on Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equations, often approximated using Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs). These approaches aim to eliminate the need for fixed time discretization, thereby enabling MARL in continuous-time domains. However, existing HJB-based CT-MARL methods typically do not account for safety constraints, such as collision penalties. The introduction of such constraints often results in discontinuities in the value functions, which complicates accurate approximation by HJB-based PINNs.

Separately, research in safe MARL has explored various methods, including primal–dual techniques, trust-region approaches (e.g., MACPO), and Lagrangian relaxation methods (e.g., MAPPO-Lagrange, SAC-Lag). Epigraph forms have also been employed in discrete-time safe optimal control. While these methods provide mechanisms for safety assurance, they are primarily developed for discrete-time settings and do not inherently extend to continuous dynamics. Some efforts have integrated safety into continuous-time multi-agent systems, but these often rely on fully known system dynamics and optimal control algorithms, which limits their applicability in scenarios where dynamics are partially known or complex.

This work addresses the intersection of CT-MARL and safety. It aims to develop a general-purpose CT-MARL algorithm that can effectively incorporate and manage state constraints, particularly when these constraints introduce discontinuities in the value function, a challenge that prior CT-MARL approaches have largely not resolved.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current MARL frameworks in addressing real-world continuous-time decision-making problems that involve safety constraints.

1. **Addressing Discrete-Time Limitations**: Existing MARL algorithms predominantly rely on discrete-time MDPs with fixed decision intervals. This formulation is often inadequate for dynamic, high-frequency environments like autonomous driving or financial trading, where decisions need to be made continuously or at irregular intervals. Discrete-time approaches in such settings can lead to degraded performance and unstable learning due to their inability to accurately represent non-uniform temporal dynamics.
2. **Overcoming Discontinuity Challenges in CT-MARL**: While continuous-time MARL (CT-MARL) approaches built on Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equations and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have emerged, they typically struggle with safety constraints. Safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance penalties) often introduce discontinuities into the value function. These discontinuities make it difficult for HJB-based PINNs to accurately approximate the value functions, thereby hindering the application of CT-MARL in safety-critical domains.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Formulate Safe CT-MARL as a CT-CMDP**: To formally define the problem of safe multi-agent reinforcement learning in continuous time, including explicit state constraints.
2. **Develop an Epigraph-Based Reformulation**: To introduce a novel epigraph-based method that transforms the CT-CMDP, converting the discontinuous value functions caused by state constraints into a continuous form. This aims to make the problem amenable to PDE-based learning techniques.
3. **Propose a PINN-Based Actor-Critic Method**: To design a learning framework that can solve the epigraph-reformulated CT-CMDP. This involves developing an actor-critic architecture optimized for continuous-time learning.
4. **Enhance Epigraph Training Stability and Efficiency**: To refine the epigraph-based training by integrating inner and outer optimization processes. This objective aims to provide more stable learning signals during training and eliminate the need for computationally expensive root-finding algorithms during execution.
5. **Provide Theoretical Guarantees**: To establish the theoretical foundation of the proposed method by proving the existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions for the epigraph-based HJB PDEs.
6. **Validate Performance on Benchmarks**: To empirically demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method against state-of-the-art safe MARL baselines in adapted continuous-time multi-agent environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Epigraph-based PINN Actor-Critic Iteration (EPI), addresses safe continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning (CT-MARL) by formulating it as a continuous-time constrained Markov Decision Process (CT-CMDP) and employing an epigraph-based reformulation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formalized as a CT-CMDP defined by a tuple M = (X, {U_i}, N, f, {l_i}, c, {t_k}, γ).
- **State and Action Spaces**: X represents the global state space, and U = U_1 × ... × U_N is the joint control space for N agents.
- **Dynamics**: The system evolves according to time-invariant nonlinear dynamics ẋ(t) = f(x(t), u(t)).
- **Policies**: Each agent i employs a decentralized policy π_i, with π denoting the joint policy.
- **Cost and Constraints**: Agents share a non-negative cost function l = Σ l_i. State-dependent safety constraints are defined by c(x) ≤ 0, forming the feasible set F.
- **Objective**: The goal is to minimize the discounted cumulative cost ∫ γ^(τ-t) l(x(τ), u(τ)) dτ, subject to all states x(τ) remaining within the feasible set for all τ ≥ t.

#### 3.2. Epigraph Reformulation

To address the discontinuity of the value function when state constraints are violated, an epigraph reformulation is introduced.
- **Auxiliary State**: An auxiliary state variable z(t) ∈ R is augmented to the system, with dynamics ẋ(t) = -l(x(t), u(t)) - ln γ ⋅ z(t).
- **Auxiliary Value Function**: The auxiliary value function V(x, z) is defined as min_u max(max_τ c(x(τ)), ∫ γ^(τ-t) l(x(τ), u(τ))dτ - z).
- **Value Equivalence (Lemma 3.1)**: This reformulation establishes an equivalence where the original constrained value v(x) can be found as min{z ∈ R | V(x, z) ≤ 0}. This implies that the sub-zero level set of V corresponds to the epigraph of v.
- **Optimality Condition (Lemma 3.2)**: For a small time interval h, V(x, z) satisfies a Bellman-like optimality condition: V(x, z) = min_u max(max_τ∈[t,t+h] c(x(τ)), γ^h V(x(t+h), z(t+h))).
- **Epigraph-based HJB PDE (Theorem 3.3)**: V(x, z) is proven to be the unique viscosity solution of an HJB PDE: max(max_τ c(x) - V(x, z), min_u H(x, z, ∇_x V, ∂_z V)) = 0, where H is the Hamiltonian.

#### 3.3. Epigraph Learning Framework

The framework integrates the epigraph-based inner-outer optimization into an actor-critic paradigm.

#### 3.3.1. Revised Outer Optimization

The goal is to find the optimal auxiliary state z* that provides the minimal z satisfying both cost and safety objectives.
- **Z-Independent Networks**: Crucially, the return value network V_ret_ψ(x) and constraint value network V_cons_ϕ(x) are designed to be functions of the state x *only*, making them z-independent.
- **Z* Computation**: For each episode, z* is computed by solving a scalar search problem: z* = min{z ∈ R | max{V_cons_ϕ(x), V_ret_ψ(x) - z} ≤ 0}. This search uses the currently learned value networks along a predicted rollout.
- **Benefits**: This revised approach avoids the nonstationary noise introduced by random z sampling (present in prior epigraph formulations) and eliminates the need for costly root-finding during execution, facilitating real-time deployment. z* is clipped to a predefined range [z_min, z_max].

#### 3.3.2. Inner Optimization with Critic Learning

The critic consists of V_ret_ψ(x) and V_cons_ϕ(x). Together with the computed z*, they form the composite epigraph-based value function ˜V(x, z*) = max{V_cons_ϕ(x), V_ret_ψ(x) - z*}. Critic training uses three types of losses:
- **(i) Residual Loss (L_Residual)**: Penalizes violations of the epigraph-based HJB PDE (Eq. 10), ensuring the learned value function satisfies the underlying continuous-time dynamics.
- **(ii) Target Loss (L_Target)**: For infinite-horizon problems lacking boundary conditions, this loss measures the discrepancy between ˜V and a numerical target V_tgt, derived from rolling out the current policy. It serves to stabilize value approximations and prevent drift.
- **(iii) Value Gradient Iterations (L_VGI)**: This loss enforces consistency of the learned value gradients ∇_x ˜V based on a recursive relation (Eq. 12). Accurate value gradients are considered essential for both precise value approximations and policy improvement.
- **Overall Critic Objective**: L_Critic = λ_res L_Residual + λ_tgt L_Target + λ_vgi L_VGI, where λ weights balance the contributions.

#### 3.3.3. Actor Learning

Actor training utilizes an epigraph-based advantage function.
- **Epigraph-based Q-function**: Defined as Q(x_t, z*_t, u_t) = max{c(x_t), γ^h V(x_{t+h}, z*_{t+h})} for a short time interval h.
- **Epigraph-based Advantage Function (Lemma 3.4)**: A(x_t, z*_t, u_t) = Q - V. As h→0, this is equivalent to max{c(x_t) - V(x_t, z*_t), ∇_x V ⋅ f - ∂_z V ⋅ l + ln γ ⋅ V}.
- **Dynamics and Cost Networks**: Since true dynamics f(x, u) and cost function l(x, u) are often unknown, two neural networks, f_ξ and l_ϕ, are trained via supervised regression on observed transitions to approximate them (Eq. 17).
- **Actor Update**: The actor π_θ(u|x) is updated by minimizing the expected surrogate advantage A_θ, which substitutes ˜V, f_ξ, and l_ϕ into the advantage expression (Eq. 18, 19).
- **CTDE Structure**: A centralized training, decentralized execution (CTDE) setup is used, where each agent's actor π_i takes local observations, but training signals are derived from the global state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the EPI method against state-of-the-art baselines across adapted continuous-time Safe Multi-Particle Environments (MPE) and Safe Multi-Agent MuJoCo benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance Against Baselines (Q1)

- **Superior Performance**: EPI consistently demonstrated higher cumulative rewards (lower costs) and lower constraint violation rates compared to all tested baselines (MACPO, MAPPO-Lag, SAC-Lag, EPPO, CBF) across various MPE and MuJoCo environments (Figures 2, 3, 4, 6).
- **Baseline Limitations**:
 - EPPO, a traditional epigraph method, often achieved suboptimal solutions and showed less stable convergence, attributed to the noise introduced by randomly sampling the auxiliary state *z* during training.
 - MACPO, a trust-region-based method, was observed to be overly conservative, leading to strong violation rejection but sometimes at the expense of task performance.
 - Lagrangian-based methods (SAC-Lag, MAPPO-Lag) exhibited instability in balancing objectives under stringent safety requirements.
 - CBF achieved reasonable constraint satisfaction but was also noted as conservative, with performance susceptible to approximation errors in learned barrier functions.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study of Critic Loss Components (Q2)

- **Importance of Target and VGI Losses**: An ablation study on MPE tasks (Figures 5, 7) indicated that removing the target loss (L_Target) or the value gradient iteration (VGI) loss (L_VGI) led to significant performance degradation. This is attributed to the infinite-horizon setting where L_Target provides crucial anchoring for value approximations and L_VGI ensures the consistency of learned value gradients, both vital for accurate value functions and policy improvement.
- **Role of Residual Loss**: The removal of the residual loss (L_Residual) had a comparatively minor effect, suggesting that its role in regularizing the PDE structure is less critical when value gradients are robustly optimized by VGI.
- **Optimal Weighting**: Balanced weighting of the losses (as in EPI) consistently yielded the best performance, with over-emphasizing any single component degrading results.

#### 4.3. Impact of Epigraph Reformulation (Q3)

- **Improved Value Approximation and Stability**: A comparison on a didactic coupled oscillator environment (Figure 8) showed that EPI, utilizing the epigraph reformulation, closely tracked the ground truth for both value functions and agent actions.
- **Discontinuity Challenges**: An ablation without the epigraph reformulation, where constraints were treated as direct penalties, resulted in severely mis-scaled value predictions and unstable actions. This highlighted the difficulty of approximating discontinuous value functions with neural networks and validated the epigraph's role in converting discontinuous penalties into a smooth, continuous optimization problem.

#### 4.4. Sensitivity to Auxiliary Variable z (Q4)

- **Optimal Balance**: Experiments varying the auxiliary state *z* from its optimal value *z* (Figure 9) demonstrated that suboptimal *z* choices shifted the trade-off between cost and constraint satisfaction.
- **Trade-off Behavior**: Smaller *z* values (e.g., *z* - 0.5*z_max*) led to significantly higher constraint violation rates with only slight cost reductions, indicating a prioritization of reward over safety. Conversely, larger *z* values (e.g., *z* + 0.5*z_max*) reduced violations but increased overall cost, signifying a greater emphasis on constraint satisfaction. This confirmed that *z* is crucial for achieving an optimal balance.

#### 4.5. Robustness under Stochastic Settings (Q5)

- **Sensitivity to Noise Magnitude**: Performance under stochastic dynamics (Appendix D.2, Figure 11) showed that EPI maintained performance under low noise levels (σ^2 = 0.1), similar to noise-free conditions. However, performance degraded significantly under mid (σ^2 = 0.5) and high (σ^2 = 1.0) noise. This suggests that while PINN-based value approximations can handle small perturbations, the method's lack of explicit uncertainty modeling renders it sensitive to larger, accumulated disturbances.

#### 4.6. Effect of Discretization Interval ∆t (Q6)

- **Inverse Relationship with Performance**: The average distance to the target increased as the discretization interval ∆t became larger (Appendix D.3, Figure 12). This outcome is consistent with continuous-time control principles, where smaller ∆t (higher temporal resolution) allows for more precise control and accurate value gradient propagation, leading to better task accuracy. Larger ∆t amplifies approximation errors from the mismatch between continuous-time formulation and discrete rollouts.

#### 4.7. Trajectory of z\* During Training (Appendix D.4)

- The evolution of *z* during training (Figure 13) aligned with expected epigraph semantics. In early training (infeasible states), *z* remained at its clipped upper bound. As the policy improved and trajectories became feasible, *z* decreased smoothly, eventually dominated by the return branch.

#### 4.8. Comparison with Traditional Epigraph and Discrete-Time Baselines (Appendix D.5, D.6)

- EPI outperformed traditional z-dependent epigraph methods (Figure 14), achieving lower cumulative reward with less variance. This validated the benefits of the z-independent critic design in reducing nonstationary noise.
- EPI also outperformed discrete-time baselines adapted to continuous-time environments (Figure 15), underscoring the advantages of continuous-time-specific design components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for safe continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning (CT-MARL) that addresses a notable gap in the existing literature: the incorporation of explicit state constraints, particularly when they introduce discontinuities, into continuous-time MARL algorithms.

The significance of this work can be highlighted as follows:

1. **Enabling Safe CT-MARL**: By proposing a CT-CMDP formulation and an epigraph-based reformulation, this work enables safe reinforcement learning in continuous-time multi-agent systems, a domain previously challenging due to the difficulties in handling value function discontinuities caused by safety constraints. This expands the applicability of MARL to safety-critical, high-frequency, or irregular-interval environments such as autonomous driving, air traffic control, multi-robot systems, and financial trading.
2. **Enhanced Learning Stability and Efficiency**: The introduction of an improved epigraph training scheme, which integrates inner and outer optimization while maintaining z-independent critics, contributes to more stable learning signals and eliminates the need for computationally expensive root-finding during execution. This represents a practical advancement for real-time deployment of safe CT-MARL policies.
3. **Robust Value Function Approximation**: The combination of residual, target, and value gradient iteration (VGI) losses in the critic learning process provides a robust mechanism for approximating value functions and their gradients in infinite-horizon settings, where traditional boundary conditions are unavailable. This ensures the accuracy and reliability of the learned policies.
4. **Theoretical Foundation**: The proof of existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions for the epigraph-based HJB PDEs provides a solid theoretical underpinning for the proposed methodology, reinforcing its mathematical rigor.
5. **Improved Performance Over Baselines**: The consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art safe MARL baselines in both adapted MPE and MuJoCo environments demonstrates the practical effectiveness and robustness of the EPI method in achieving both cost reduction and constraint satisfaction.

The potential impact of this research is substantial. It provides a foundational framework for developing and deploying intelligent agents in complex, safety-critical continuous-time environments. This could lead to:
- More reliable and safer autonomous systems that can operate with continuous control actions while adhering to strict safety protocols.
- Advancements in multi-robot coordination where agents must operate continuously and avoid collisions.
- Development of more sophisticated algorithms for financial trading that can react to market dynamics in real time while managing risk constraints.
- Further research into incorporating explicit uncertainty modeling into continuous-time safe MARL frameworks, building upon the foundations laid by this work.
