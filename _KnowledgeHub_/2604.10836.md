# HO-Flow: Generalizable Hand-Object Interaction Generation with Latent Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> HO-Flow generates realistic 3D hand-object interaction sequences by employing an interaction-aware variational autoencoder and an auto-regressive latent flow matching model. This framework achieves 98.25% physical plausibility and nearly three times higher semantic diversity on the GRAB benchmark, while consistently reducing hand-object interpenetration across diverse interaction scenarios.

## Problem

- Generating dynamic 4D hand-object interaction sequences that are physically plausible and temporally coherent remains challenging.
- Existing methods struggle with high computational costs due to numerous degrees of freedom or lose fine-grained interaction details through motion quantization or frame-independent latent learning.
- Generalizing hand-object interaction generation to diverse, unseen objects and tasks while minimizing interpenetration artifacts is difficult.

## Method

- An **Interaction-aware Variational Autoencoder** (==Inter-VAE==) compresses short temporal sequences of hand-object motions into a latent space, using hand-centric object point clouds and ==kinematic awareness==.
- A **masked auto-regressive** (==MAR==) transformer, combined with a ==flow matching== objective, generates long-range temporally coherent motions in the continuous latent space.
- Object motions are predicted relative to the initial frame, enabling pre-training on large synthetic datasets like ==GraspXL== for improved generalization.

## Results

- The model achieved **98.25%** physical plausibility and nearly **3x** higher semantic diversity on the GRAB benchmark, demonstrating reduced interpenetration compared to prior state-of-the-art.
- On the challenging OakInk dataset for out-of-distribution generalization, the method reached **89.76%** physical plausibility and maintained the highest sample diversity, outperforming existing approaches.
- User studies confirmed a consistent preference for HO-Flow's generated interactions, with preference ratios between **0.62** and **0.72** over LatentHOI across multiple benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming object point clouds into hand-joint local coordinate systems, combined with separate hand and object latents, creates an expressive, interaction-aware latent motion representation.
> - An auto-regressive transformer with a continuous latent flow matching objective effectively models long-range temporal dependencies and generates smooth, physically consistent motions.
> - Predicting object motions relative to the initial frame and pre-training on synthetic datasets significantly enhances generalization to unseen objects and tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generating realistic 3D hand-object interactions (HOI) is a fundamental challenge in computer vision and robotics, with broad applications in fields such as character animation, virtual reality, and robotic manipulation. Historically, much research has focused on synthesizing static grasping poses. While these methods have achieved notable results in generating plausible static grasps, extending generative frameworks to the dynamic, temporal 4D domain of HOI introduces significant complexities. Key challenges include maintaining physical plausibility (e.g., preventing hand-object interpenetration), preserving temporal coherence over extended sequences, and ensuring generalization across diverse actions and objects.

Previous approaches to dynamic HOI generation have explored several paradigms. Early methods leveraging diffusion models, such as DiffH2O, directly generate sequences of raw hand and object poses. However, this approach faces high computational costs due to the numerous degrees of freedom of articulated hand models. Additionally, treating poses as independent variables can fail to model underlying interaction distributions effectively, leading to artifacts like floating hands or physically implausible contacts.

More recent advancements, exemplified by LatentHOI, mitigate the high dimensionality by embedding hand-object poses into a more compact latent space. However, existing latent representations often learn frame-independently, which can compromise temporal coherence and neglect fine-grained, contact-aware cues crucial for high-fidelity synthesis.

In terms of generative architectures, auto-regressive (AR) Transformers have demonstrated efficacy in modeling long-range temporal dependencies. Nevertheless, these models typically necessitate the quantization of continuous motions into discrete tokens, a process that can suppress contact-rich features essential for realistic manipulation. Conversely, diffusion and flow-based models operate directly in continuous space, thereby avoiding quantization artifacts. However, these models frequently perform holistic denoising over entire sequences, leading to substantial computational expense and a tendency to produce globally inconsistent motions, often relying on less expressive U-Net architectures rather than Transformer-based models.

HO-Flow positions itself within this landscape by addressing these limitations. It seeks to integrate an expressive, interaction-aware motion representation with an efficient temporal generative architecture. By operating in a continuous latent space and employing auto-regressive temporal reasoning, HO-Flow aims to enhance physical plausibility, temporal coherence, and generalization, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in text-conditioned 3D hand-object interaction generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize realistic and generalizable 3D hand-object interaction motion sequences. These sequences are to be generated conditioned on text descriptions and canonical 3D object models, emphasizing physical plausibility and temporal coherence.

The motivation for HO-Flow stems from several limitations observed in existing methodologies:

1. **Improving Expressive Motion Representation**: Current latent representations for HOI often suffer from being learned frame-independently. This approach limits their capacity to capture rich interaction dynamics and fine-grained, contact-aware cues, which are critical for high-fidelity and temporally coherent synthesis. The motivation is to develop an **interaction-aware variational autoencoder (Inter-VAE)** that encodes *short-horizon temporal sequences* of hand and object motions into a unified latent manifold. This VAE is designed to incorporate hand and object kinematics and process object point clouds in *hand-centric coordinates*, thereby capturing both global motion trajectories and intricate contact-rich coordination.

2. **Enhancing Temporal Coherence and Smoothness**: Generative models, especially those based on diffusion, frequently operate by holistic denoising of entire sequences, which is computationally intensive and can lead to globally inconsistent or jittery motions. Auto-regressive models, while good for long-range dependencies, often discretize continuous motions, losing fine-grained detail. The motivation is to leverage a novel generative architecture that combines the strengths of both. This involves a **masked flow matching model** that integrates *auto-regressive temporal reasoning* (via a Transformer backbone) with *continuous latent generation* (using a flow matching objective). This design aims to produce smooth, long-range temporally coherent motions while reducing jitter and outlier frames.

3. **Improving Generalization to Diverse Objects and Tasks**: A significant challenge in HOI generation is the ability to generalize to unseen objects and novel interaction scenarios. Existing methods can be sensitive to dataset-specific coordinate conventions and often lack sufficient training data to cover a wide range of interactions. The motivation is to predict object motions *relative to the initial frame*, which helps to normalize motion representations and detach them from specific dataset conventions. This design choice also enables effective **pre-training on large-scale synthetic data** (e.g., GraspXL), providing a robust prior that facilitates superior transferability to novel objects and out-of-distribution interactions.

4. **Ensuring High Physical Plausibility**: A fundamental requirement for realistic HOI is the absence of physical inconsistencies, most notably interpenetration between the hand and the object. The motivation behind HO-Flow's design, particularly its interaction-aware representation and continuous latent generation, is to model the coupled dynamics between the hand and the object with sufficient fidelity to inherently produce physically grounded interactions that minimize such artifacts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HO-Flow framework operates in two main stages: first, learning an expressive motion representation, and second, generating motion sequences in this latent space.

#### 3.1. Interaction-aware Variational Autoencoder (Inter-VAE)

The Inter-VAE is designed to compress sequences of hand-object interactions into compact, structured latent representations. It takes as input an object point cloud, MANO hand poses (parameterized by joint rotations), and object poses (translation and rotation). To mitigate data scarcity, especially for bi-manual manipulation, the method mirrors left-hand data to supplement right-hand data.

- **Kinematic-aware Object Transformation**: To capture fine-grained interaction features, the object point clouds are transformed into the local coordinate systems of various hand joints. This process involves:
 1. Transforming object point clouds into the world coordinate system using the object's pose sequence.
 2. Deriving the global transformation for each hand joint using its pose parameters and the MANO kinematic tree.
 3. Transforming the world-frame object point clouds into each joint's local frame using the inverse of the joint transformations.
 4. Concatenating these hand-relative point clouds, alongside canonical and world-frame object point clouds, to form a rich representation (`p_h_o`) encoding hand-centric geometric context.

- **Spatial-Temporal Encoding**: The combined point cloud features are first processed by a PointNet++-based spatial encoder to extract per-frame interaction features. These features are then fused with explicit hand pose and object pose parameters via MLP layers, yielding distinct spatial features for the hand and object. Subsequently, temporal encoders, consisting of 1D convolutional layers, aggregate these spatial features across short temporal sequences to produce compact latent codes (`z_h`, `z_o`) for the hand and object, respectively, with a temporal stride of 4.

- **Motion Reconstruction and Loss**: To optimize the learned latent space, dedicated hand and object decoders upsample the latent features to the original sequence length. These decoders use convolutional layers to reconstruct the original hand and object motion trajectories. The VAE is supervised by a composite loss function (`L_VAE`), which includes:
 - Reconstruction losses for hand pose (6D rotation), hand translation (relative to object), and MANO mesh vertices.
 - Reconstruction losses for object rotation (6D rotation) and object translation.
 - KL regularization terms on the latent posteriors for both hand and object latents, encouraging the latents to conform to a standard normal distribution.
 A notable design choice is detaching the object latent (`z_o`) when conditioning the hand decoder, which aims to prevent conflicts between optimizing `z_h` and `z_o` gradients.

#### 3.2. Auto-regressive Flow Matching Model

Following the training and freezing of the Inter-VAE, an auto-regressive flow matching model is trained to generate interaction motion latents in the learned structured latent space (`Z`).

- **Unified Motion Token**: For each temporal step, the object and hand latents for both the right hand and its mirrored left counterpart are concatenated to form a unified motion token (`z_t`). These tokens are then arranged into a sequence `Z`.

- **Condition Encoding**: The model conditions generation on the object geometry and a natural-language task description.
 - The task description is encoded using a frozen CLIP text encoder to obtain a semantic embedding.
 - The object geometry is represented by a Basis Point Set (BPS) encoding, which yields a fixed-length descriptor.
 - These features are projected into a shared conditioning space and summed to form a final condition vector (`c`).

- **Context-aware Transformer**: To capture long-range temporal dependencies and facilitate progressive generation, a masked auto-regressive transformer (MAR) architecture is employed. The sequence of motion tokens is embedded, augmented with sinusoidal positional encodings, and processed by a stack of transformer blocks. Each block utilizes Adaptive Layer-Norm (AdaLN) modulation, conditioned on the encoded task and object features (`c`), to produce contextual features. During training, a masked modeling strategy is used: a subset of token positions is corrupted (e.g., replaced by a learnable [MASK] token or Gaussian noise) to enable the model to learn to inpaint masked regions.

- **Flow Matching in Latent Space**: For each masked latent position, a conditional flow matching model is trained. This model defines a probability path as a straight-line linear interpolation between an initial noise distribution and the ground-truth latent target. The model's objective is to predict the velocity field (the time-derivative of this path) that transports the noise to the target latent. This velocity field is parameterized by a lightweight MLP, conditioned on the contextual features from the MAR transformer. This formulation allows for efficient inference via ODE solvers to generate latent tokens. Classifier-free guidance and an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of model parameters are used during training for stability.

- **Generalization Enhancements**: Object motions are predicted relative to the initial frame. This strategy helps in avoiding issues arising from dataset-specific coordinate conventions and enables effective pre-training on large-scale synthetic data like GraspXL, which is critical for enhancing generalization to unseen objects and tasks. The entire framework (VAE and flow matching model) is first pre-trained on GraspXL and then fine-tuned on real datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of HO-Flow was conducted on three benchmarks: GRAB, OakInk, and DexYCB, utilizing metrics for latent representation quality (hand joint/vertex error, object translation error, Chamfer distance) and motion generation quality (Contact Ratio, Interpenetration Volume/Depth, Physics Score, Sample Diversity).

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies

- **Interaction-aware Variational Autoencoder (Inter-VAE)**:
 - Incorporating 6D object poses and world-coordinate object point clouds significantly reduced object reconstruction errors.
 - Transforming object point clouds into hand-joint local coordinate systems further enhanced hand-object interaction feature extraction, improving hand and object metrics.
 - Using separate latent representations for the hand and object facilitated more discriminative motion feature extraction, boosting accuracy.
 - Pre-training the Inter-VAE on synthetic GraspXL data provided a substantial improvement in generalization to unseen objects, leading to the best performance across all reconstruction metrics.

- **Auto-regressive Latent Flow Matching Model**:
 - Direct generation in the original pose space (baseline) yielded limited physical fidelity and high interpenetration.
 - Adopting a frame-independent latent representation (similar to LatentHOI) struggled with temporal coherence and physical realism.
 - Employing HO-Flow's Inter-VAE for joint spatial-temporal encoding of hand-object features significantly improved physical fidelity, reduced penetration errors, and increased motion diversity.
 - Integrating the context-aware auto-regressive transformer further refined generation, leading to a sharp reduction in penetration metrics and a boost in physical plausibility.
 - Pre-training the generative model on GraspXL resulted in the best overall performance, demonstrating improvements in physical plausibility, reduced interpenetration, and increased sample diversity.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art

- **GRAB Benchmark**: HO-Flow (especially with pre-training) demonstrated superior performance over LatentHOI and other methods. It achieved the lowest interpenetration volume and depth for both hands, improved physical plausibility from 96.16% (LatentHOI) to 98.25%, and significantly increased semantic diversity, nearly tripling LatentHOI's score. This indicates the generation of physically grounded and diverse interactions while suppressing unnatural interpenetrations.

- **OakInk Benchmark (Out-of-Distribution Generalization)**: HO-Flow consistently showed strong performance on this challenging dataset featuring novel objects. It reduced hand-object interpenetrations and intersection depths compared to LatentHOI, improved physical plausibility to 89.76% (outperforming Text2HOI and diffusion-based approaches), and maintained the highest sample diversity. Pre-training on GraspXL provided a more substantial performance gain in this setting.

- **DexYCB Benchmark (Single-Hand Manipulation)**: For single-hand tasks, HO-Flow also exhibited strong capabilities. It reduced the Intersection Depth (ID) from 2.01mm (LatentHOI) to 1.20mm, increased physical plausibility (Phy) to 95.41% (vs. 88.52% for LatentHOI), and improved semantic diversity.

#### 4.3. Generative Training Paradigm

- **Diffusion vs. Flow Matching**: An ablation study comparing DDPM-based diffusion with flow matching showed that flow matching consistently outperformed diffusion across all evaluation metrics, achieving lower interpenetration, better contact ratio, and higher physical plausibility and diversity. This led to its adoption as the default training strategy.

#### 4.4. Impact of Prediction Steps

- Increasing the number of prediction steps during inference generally improved motion quality and physical plausibility. 18 steps were identified as an optimal trade-off, offering substantial quality gains (e.g., reducing IVr from 8.51 cm³ at 6 steps to 5.31 cm³ at 18 steps and increasing Phy from 90.63% to 98.25%) without incurring excessive inference latency.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Performance and User Study

- Qualitative comparisons with LatentHOI demonstrated that HO-Flow synthesizes more realistic interaction motions with fewer hand-object penetrations.
- A user study across three benchmarks indicated a consistent preference for HO-Flow's results, with preference ratios ranging from 0.62 to 0.72 compared to LatentHOI's 0.28 to 0.38.
- The model produced natural and realistic hand-object interactions in both bi-manual and single-hand settings across a wide range of objects and tasks.
- Identified failure cases included inaccurate object orientation leading to penetration or failure to grasp semantically specified object parts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HO-Flow represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D hand-object interaction generation. By combining an expressive, interaction-aware latent motion representation with an efficient auto-regressive flow matching generative model, it addresses key limitations of prior work, particularly concerning physical plausibility, temporal coherence, and generalization.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: HO-Flow establishes new benchmarks for physical plausibility and motion diversity in text-conditioned HOI generation across diverse datasets, including out-of-distribution scenarios. This indicates a more robust and capable generative framework.
- **Enhanced Realism and Fidelity**: The reduction in hand-object interpenetration and the increase in physical plausibility metrics suggest that the generated interactions are more physically accurate and visually believable. The hand-centric object transformation and the holistic encoding of temporal interaction sequences contribute to capturing fine-grained contact dynamics.
- **Improved Generalization**: The method's ability to generalize effectively to unseen objects and tasks, further bolstered by large-scale pre-training on synthetic data, is critical for real-world applicability. This reduces the need for extensive, task-specific real-world data collection.
- **Computational Efficiency**: The use of flow matching in a compact latent space provides a balance between high generation quality and computational efficiency compared to some previous diffusion models, which is beneficial for practical deployment.

The potential impact of HO-Flow is substantial across various domains:
- **Character Animation and Virtual Reality**: Animators and content creators could leverage HO-Flow to synthesize complex and realistic hand-object interactions from high-level text descriptions, significantly reducing manual labor and enhancing the realism of virtual characters in games, films, and VR/AR applications.
- **Robotics**: The ability to generate diverse and physically plausible robot manipulation trajectories could provide valuable synthetic data for training dexterous robotic systems. It could also inform the design of more robust and adaptive manipulation policies, particularly for tasks involving contact-rich interactions.
- **Human-Computer Interaction**: Understanding and generating natural hand-object interactions is foundational for developing more intuitive and responsive human-computer interfaces, especially in immersive environments.

Future research directions, as suggested by the identified failure cases, could involve scaling training with more diverse language instructions and explicitly incorporating object affordance cues into the generation process to improve semantic understanding and grasping accuracy for even more complex scenarios.
