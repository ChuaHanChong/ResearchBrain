# Enigmata: Scaling Logical Reasoning in Large Language Models with Synthetic Verifiable Puzzles

> [!summary] Summary
> The Enigmata suite enhances Large Language Models' (LLMs) logical reasoning abilities for puzzles by providing scalable, controllable, and verifiable synthetic puzzle data alongside an optimized Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training framework. This approach leads to models like Qwen2.5-32B-Enigmata achieving 62.6% on the Enigmata-Eval benchmark and demonstrating improved general reasoning in larger models.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate limitations in 'purely logical reasoning' puzzles that require deductive inference and abstract pattern recognition without relying on extensive domain knowledge.
- Existing puzzle research primarily focuses on evaluation benchmarks, lacking the training methods and scalable resources necessary for modern LLMs to acquire and improve these skills.
- Current puzzle datasets often lack diversity, scalability, and granular control over difficulty, hindering effective curriculum learning and generalization for LLM training.

## Method

- The **Enigmata** suite introduces a comprehensive data and training framework, featuring 36 diverse ==puzzle tasks== each equipped with an auto-generator for scalable data creation and a rule-based verifier for objective reward signals.
- Difficulty control for puzzles is implemented by identifying key variables and establishing three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard) based on GPT-4o performance analysis, enabling curriculum learning.
- An optimized two-stage training recipe combines Rejection Fine-tuning (RFT) for foundational patterns with VC-PPO (Value-Consistency Proximal Policy Optimization) using multi-task training paradigms (Mix-training and Multi-stage RL) tailored for long Chain-of-Thought generation.

## Results

- The trained model, Qwen2.5-**32B**-Enigmata, achieved **62.6%** on the Enigmata-Eval benchmark, outperforming strong baselines like OpenAI's o1 (**29.0%**) and achieving **32.8%** on ARC-AGI 1.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization to out-of-domain puzzle benchmarks such as KOR-Bench while maintaining comparable performance on mathematical reasoning tasks.
- When applied to a larger Mixture-of-Experts model (Seed1.5-Thinking), additional Enigmata training improved performance on advanced math (AIME 2024 by +**0.8%**, BeyondAIME by +**0.4%**) and STEM (GPQA Diamond by +**0.8%**) benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A generator-verifier design enables scalable creation of high-quality, verifiable puzzle prompts, which is crucial for effective Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in logical reasoning tasks.
> - Multi-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) combined with comprehensive Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is critical for training LLMs to acquire and transfer complex logical reasoning abilities, especially for multi-step puzzles.
> - Training larger, more powerful LLMs with synthetic, knowledge-orthogonal puzzle data can yield a 'free lunch' effect, surprisingly boosting performance on general advanced reasoning capabilities in domains like advanced math and STEM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable success in complex reasoning tasks such as mathematics and coding, primarily through Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). However, a persistent challenge remains in equipping these models with robust logical reasoning skills for puzzles that require pure deduction and pattern recognition, rather than extensive domain knowledge. The paper introduces *Enigmata*, a comprehensive suite designed to address this gap by providing scalable, controllable, and verifiable puzzle data and an optimized training framework.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 2.1. Current State of LLM Reasoning

Large Language Models (LLMs) have made significant strides in various complex reasoning domains. Approaches like Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) have proven particularly effective in tasks where the correctness of a generated solution can be objectively verified, such as mathematical problem-solving, STEM challenges, and code generation. Models like OpenAI's o1, DeepSeek's R1, and Google's Gemini 2.5 have demonstrated impressive capabilities in these areas, leveraging the RLVR paradigm to refine their reasoning processes based on concrete feedback signals.

#### 2.2. Gaps in Puzzle Reasoning Research

Despite these advancements, LLMs continue to exhibit limitations in "purely logical reasoning" puzzles—those that require deductive inference and abstract pattern recognition without relying on extensive domain-specific knowledge. Humans often find these puzzles intuitive, yet LLMs struggle. The paper identifies several key deficiencies in the existing research landscape concerning these types of puzzles:

- **Lack of Training Resources:** Most prior work on puzzles has focused on creating benchmarks for evaluation (e.g., KOR-Bench, NPR, SearchBench) rather than providing the necessary training methods and scalable resources for modern LLMs to acquire and improve these skills.
- **Limited Data Diversity and Scalability:** Existing puzzle datasets often lack variety in puzzle types, making it difficult for models to generalize across different logical structures. Furthermore, they frequently offer little control over the generation of new examples or the ability to adjust puzzle difficulty, which is crucial for curriculum learning in RL.
- **Suboptimal Training Approaches:** Previous attempts to enhance LLMs' puzzle-solving abilities typically fall into two categories: tool-based methods (e.g., relying on code interpreters) that don't directly improve the model's intrinsic reasoning, or RLVR approaches that focus on only one or a few puzzle types, thereby limiting generalization.

#### 2.3. Enigmata's Position and Contribution

*Enigmata* directly addresses these identified gaps. It is positioned as the "first comprehensive suite tailored for improving LLMs with puzzle reasoning skills." By combining a scalable, controllable puzzle dataset with an optimized RLVR training recipe, *Enigmata* aims to provide the necessary infrastructure to fundamentally enhance LLMs' logical reasoning abilities. The suite's generator-verifier design is a key innovation, enabling the scalable creation of high-quality, verifiable puzzle prompts essential for effective RL training, particularly for long chain-of-thought reasoning. This multi-task, controllable framework distinguishes *Enigmata* from prior benchmarks and single-task RL efforts, seeking to foster generalizable logical reasoning rather than narrow task-specific expertise.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to significantly advance the logical reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models, particularly for "puzzle tasks" that demand abstract deduction and problem-solving without relying on vast external knowledge. The motivation stems from the observed shortcomings of even advanced LLMs in these human-solvable puzzles, a stark contrast to their proficiency in domain-specific tasks like math or coding.

The key objectives that drive the *Enigmata* project are multifaceted:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive Puzzle Suite:** The primary goal is to introduce *Enigmata*, a holistic suite encompassing data, evaluation, and a training methodology. This suite is designed to be a unified, controllable framework for cultivating and assessing logical reasoning.
2. **Create Scalable and Controllable Puzzle Data (Enigmata-Data):** A core objective is to overcome the limitations of existing puzzle datasets (lack of diversity, scalability, and difficulty control). *Enigmata-Data* aims to provide 36 diverse tasks across seven distinct reasoning categories, each equipped with an "auto-generator" for unlimited, controllable data generation and a "rule-based verifier" for automatic, objective evaluation. This generator-verifier pair is crucial for seamless integration into RLVR training pipelines.
3. **Establish a Rigorous Evaluation Benchmark (Enigmata-Eval):** To accurately measure progress and challenge state-of-the-art models, the project aims to create *Enigmata-Eval*, a comprehensive and rigorously sampled benchmark derived from *Enigmata-Data*, ensuring no data leakage between training and evaluation sets.
4. **Propose Optimized Training Strategies (Enigmata-Model):** The research seeks to develop and validate effective multi-task RLVR strategies, specifically tailored to train LLMs on the diverse puzzle types within *Enigmata*. This includes exploring rejection fine-tuning and different multi-task RL paradigms (Mix-training and Multi-stage RL) to achieve robust and generalizable reasoning skills.
5. **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Generalization:** A crucial objective is to train models using *Enigmata* and show their superior performance on both in-domain puzzle benchmarks (like *Enigmata-Eval* and ARC-AGI) and out-of-domain reasoning tasks (like KOR-Bench and advanced mathematics), thereby proving the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed framework.
6. **Investigate Cross-Domain Benefits:** An ambitious motivation is to explore whether logical reasoning training on synthetic puzzles can yield "free lunch" benefits, surprisingly boosting performance on knowledge-intensive domains like advanced math and STEM, especially when applied to larger, more capable base models.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The *Enigmata* project employs a systematic and multi-component approach to enhance LLMs' logical reasoning. It comprises *Enigmata-Data* for data generation and evaluation, and *Enigmata-Model* for the training recipe.

#### 4.1. Enigmata-Data: The Puzzle Dataset

*Enigmata-Data* is the foundation of the suite, designed for comprehensive enhancement and evaluation of LLMs' reasoning.

- **Puzzle Categories:** The dataset includes 36 puzzle tasks categorized into seven broad reasoning types:
 - **Crypto Puzzle:** Tests understanding of cryptography and pattern recognition (e.g., Crypto KPA, Crypto KKA).
 - **Arithmetic Puzzle:** Challenges numerical reasoning and basic arithmetic under constraints (e.g., Game24, Countdown).
 - **Logic Puzzle:** Assesses deductive reasoning and inference from premises (e.g., Knights and Knaves, Zebra Logic).
 - **Grid Puzzle:** Requires reasoning about numbers, patterns, and placements in structured grids (e.g., Sudoku, Star Battle).
 - **Graph Puzzle:** Involves reasoning about nodes, edges, and paths in graph structures (e.g., Hamiltonian Path, NL Navigation).
 - **Search Puzzle:** Demands efficient exploration of state space to find solutions under rules and constraints (e.g., Minesweeper, Tic Tac Toe).
 - **Sequential Puzzle:** Focuses on understanding and predicting sequences of steps (e.g., Eight Puzzle, Fifteen Puzzle).

- **Data Construction Pipeline:** The data is built in three phases:
 1. **Tasks Collection and Design:** 36 logic puzzle tasks are curated; 30 are designed with custom generators for scalability, while 6 draw from existing datasets. Many tasks involve multi-step reasoning.
 2. **Auto-Generator and Verifier Development:** Crucially, 30 tasks have auto-generators for scalable data creation, and all 36 tasks have corresponding rule-based "auto-verifiers." These verifiers automatically check solution correctness, providing objective rewards for RL training.
 3. **Sliding Difficulty Control:** For each task, key variables (e.g., grid size, blank cell count) are identified to control difficulty. Three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard) are established by analyzing GPT-4o's performance (pass@k metric) across different parameter settings, allowing for curriculum learning.

- **Enigmata-Eval Benchmark:** A rigorous evaluation benchmark is created by systematically sampling from the broader *Enigmata-Data*. It consists of 4,758 puzzle instances, aiming for 50 instances per task per difficulty level. Strict separation protocols ensure no data leakage between training and evaluation sets.

#### 4.2. Enigmata-Model: The Training Recipe

The training methodology is a two-stage process designed to systematically build generalizable reasoning abilities.

- **Stage 1: Rejection Fine-tuning (RFT):**
 - **Purpose:** To establish foundational reasoning patterns and stabilize initial training, addressing potential instability of direct RL.
 - **Data:** Combines puzzles from *Enigmata* (uniformly sampled across tasks and difficulty, 12,041 samples) and high-quality mathematical problems from a DeepSeek-R1-distilled dataset (12,533 samples). ARC-AGI puzzle training data is also included to facilitate a "cold-start" for difficult tasks.
 - **Method:** For puzzles, 8 candidate solutions are generated using DeepSeek-R1, and one correct solution is selected for RFT. A balanced 1:1 ratio of puzzles to mathematics is maintained.
 - **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct serves as the base model, fine-tuned for 2 epochs.

- **Stage 2: RL with Verifiable Puzzles:**
 - **Algorithm:** Uses VC-PPO (Value-Consistency Proximal Policy Optimization), a variant of PPO tailored for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation.
 - **Reward Mechanism:** The automated verifiers (v_i) for each of the 36 *Enigmata* tasks instantly score responses as correct/incorrect, providing objective rewards directly to VC-PPO.
 - **Data Generation:** The auto-generators (g_i) create unlimited examples at controlled difficulty levels. The number of examples per task and difficulty level (N_i,d) can be adjusted, allowing control over data mix and overall dataset size. This forms a fully automatic RL pipeline.
 - **VC-PPO Modifications:** Key adaptations for long-CoT:
 - **Removing KL Divergence Constraint:** Unlike traditional RLHF, the KL penalty is removed (kl_loss_weight=0.0) to allow the policy greater freedom to explore and learn.
 - **Value Pretraining:** The value model is pretrained on Monte Carlo returns from a fixed SFT policy to ensure stable critic initialization.
 - **Decoupled GAE (Generalized Advantage Estimation):** Different lambda values are used for policy (λ_policy=0.95) and value model (λ_critic=1.0) to improve sample efficiency and optimize over long sequences.

- **Multi-task Training Paradigms:** To foster general logical reasoning and prevent overspecialization, two main multi-task strategies are explored:
 1. **Mix-training RL:** All selected puzzle types (from *Enigmata*, ARC-AGI 1&2 public training sets) are integrated simultaneously with AIME mathematical problems, maintaining a 1:1 puzzle-to-mathematics ratio. This provides broad exposure to diverse tasks.
 2. **Multi-stage RL:** A curriculum-based approach for difficult tasks. Phase 1 involves intensive training on ARC-AGI 1&2 and AIME until performance stabilizes. Phase 2 gradually introduces *Enigmata-Data* while retaining earlier datasets to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of *Enigmata* demonstrates significant advancements in LLMs' logical reasoning capabilities, particularly for puzzles.

#### 5.1. Benchmarking Performance

- **Superior Puzzle Reasoning:** The trained model, **Qwen2.5-32B-Enigmata**, consistently surpasses strong baseline models, including OpenAI's o3-mini-high (25.8%) and o1 (29.0%), on the *Enigmata-Eval* benchmark (62.6%). It also achieves impressive scores on the notoriously challenging ARC-AGI 1 (32.8%) and ARC-AGI 2 (0.6%) benchmarks, outperforming models like Gemini 2.5 Pro and DeepSeek-R1.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Domain Puzzles:** The model demonstrates robust generalization to out-of-domain (OOD) puzzle benchmarks like KOR-Bench, indicating that the training recipe effectively enhances general logic reasoning skills beyond the specific tasks it was trained on.
- **Maintenance of Math Reasoning:** Despite being trained on puzzles, Qwen2.5-32B-Enigmata maintains comparable performance on mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., AIME 2024), indicating that the training strategy preserves existing general reasoning capabilities while specializing in logic-specific skills.

#### 5.2. Category-Specific Performance

Detailed analysis across *Enigmata-Eval* categories reveals that Qwen2.5-32B-Enigmata excels in structured reasoning categories, particularly:
- **Crypto (96.0%):** Demonstrates strong understanding of rule-based pattern recognition.
- **Arithmetic (93.7%):** Shows high proficiency in numerical reasoning under constraints.
- **Logic (90.2%):** Indicates effective deductive reasoning.
The model also performs competitively in Search tasks (70.4%), suggesting developed planning and state-space exploration capabilities. Spatial and sequential tasks, however, remain more challenging for the model, indicating areas for future improvement.

#### 5.3. Generalization with Model Scaling

A crucial and surprising finding is the "free lunch" effect when *Enigmata* data is used to train larger models. While Qwen2.5-32B-Enigmata showed limited generalization benefits to general reasoning outside of puzzles, scaling the experiment to a much larger Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model, **Seed1.5-Thinking** (20B activated, 200B total parameters), yielded significant benefits:
- **Boost in Advanced Math and STEM:** Additional RL training with *Enigmata* data (**Seed1.5-Thinking-Enigmata**) generally improved performance on AIME 2024 (+0.8%), AIME 2025 (+1.9%), BeyondAIME (+0.4%), and GPQA Diamond (+0.8%).
- **Knowledge-Orthogonal Benefits:** This demonstrates that even synthetic, knowledge-orthogonal puzzle data can enhance general advanced reasoning capabilities in larger, more powerful models, providing a valuable and unexpected improvement to state-of-the-art models.

#### 5.4. Ablation Studies

- **Training Data Size:** Increasing the amount of *Enigmata-Train* data progressively enhances in-domain *Enigmata-Eval* performance. A small amount of puzzle data provides significant benefits, but excessive data can lead to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned knowledge and a slight degradation in OOD performance.
- **Data Difficulty Control:** A balanced difficulty ratio (Easy:Medium:Hard = 1:1:1) in training data leads to more robust complex reasoning performance. The *Enigmata* difficulty tagging method performs comparably to more complex historical reward variation (HRV) strategies for in-domain tasks, while providing superior results on OOD benchmarks.
- **Multi-task Training Paradigms:**
 - **Multi-Stage RL** excels at building deeper task-specific reasoning and transferring to complex unseen tasks like ARC-AGI and *Enigmata*, benefiting from a curriculum-based learning approach.
 - **Mix-Training RL** shows better generalization to overall OOD tasks, suggesting that diverse simultaneous training helps models learn broader reasoning strategies.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Impact:** Comprehensive SFT (including all tasks) is crucial for both multi-stage and mix-training, especially for complex tasks (e.g., Light Up), enabling models to achieve optimal results by providing strong foundational knowledge.

#### 5.5. Analysis of Training Dynamics and Strategy

- **SFT's Role in RL:** For simple tasks (e.g., Countdown), SFT is not essential, as RL can achieve similar improvements from a cold start. For medium-complexity tasks (e.g., Minesweeper), Mix-Training struggles without SFT, but Multi-stage RL can still learn. For high-complexity tasks (e.g., Light Up), comprehensive SFT combined with Multi-stage RL dramatically outperforms other approaches, suggesting that complex reasoning requires both strong initial knowledge and structured learning.
- **Response Length and Accuracy:** On ARC-AGI 1, tasks where the model generated more concise reasoning generally showed higher accuracy. This suggests potential inefficiencies in longer reasoning chains and highlights opportunities for optimization.
- **Code Utilization:** Surprisingly, enabling code utilization in Qwen2.5-32B-Enigmata **hindered** performance on puzzle tasks. This implies that current models struggle to effectively leverage generated code for complex reasoning without actual execution.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

*Enigmata* represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model reasoning, offering a unified and highly controllable framework for developing and evaluating logical problem-solving abilities. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Scalable RLVR for Logical Reasoning:** By providing auto-generators and rule-based verifiers for 36 diverse puzzle tasks, *Enigmata* overcomes a major bottleneck in RLVR—the need for high-quality, verifiable training data. This scalable, automated pipeline opens new avenues for large-scale, continuous training of LLMs on logical reasoning.
2. **Boosting State-of-the-Art Reasoning Capabilities:** The consistent outperformance of Qwen2.5-32B-Enigmata on challenging benchmarks like ARC-AGI and *Enigmata-Eval* validates the effectiveness of the proposed dataset and training recipes. This demonstrates that LLMs can achieve superior logical reasoning skills through targeted RLVR.
3. **Revealing Cross-Domain Generalization:** The most surprising finding is the "free lunch" effect observed with larger models (Seed1.5-Thinking). The fact that synthetic, knowledge-orthogonal puzzle data from *Enigmata* can enhance performance on advanced math and STEM reasoning tasks (AIME, BeyondAIME, GPQA Diamond) suggests a profound underlying connection. This implies that improving foundational logical and abstract reasoning, even with synthetic data, can yield broad benefits across diverse, complex problem-solving domains. This could lead to more efficient training strategies for general-purpose reasoning models, reducing reliance on extremely large, diverse, and potentially knowledge-dense datasets.
4. **Providing a Controllable Research Platform:** *Enigmata*'s ability to control puzzle difficulty and data mixing ratios offers researchers a powerful tool for fine-grained experimentation. This enables deeper insights into curriculum learning, catastrophic forgetting, and the optimal strategies for developing robust and generalizable reasoning in LLMs.
5. **Guiding Future Research:** The identified areas of struggle (spatial/sequential puzzles, ineffective code utilization without execution) provide clear directions for future work. The framework can serve as a benchmark for exploring novel architectural designs, prompting strategies, and RL algorithms to tackle these persistent challenges.

In essence, *Enigmata* provides both the "what" (a diverse and controllable dataset) and the "how" (an optimized RLVR training recipe) for advancing LLMs' logical reasoning. Its demonstrated ability to push the boundaries of reasoning performance and, more profoundly, to generalize across seemingly disparate domains, positions it as a foundational contribution for the development of more intelligent and versatile AI systems.

### 7. Conclusion

The *Enigmata* suite, comprising *Enigmata-Data*, *Enigmata-Eval*, and *Enigmata-Model*, presents a novel and comprehensive approach to enhancing the logical reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models. By leveraging a uniquely designed dataset featuring 36 diverse, synthetically generated, and verifiable puzzle tasks with controllable difficulty, alongside an optimized multi-stage Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training paradigm, the authors have successfully trained models that consistently outperform existing state-of-the-art LLMs on challenging puzzle benchmarks like ARC-AGI. Crucially, the research demonstrates a compelling "free lunch" generalization effect: training on *Enigmata*'s knowledge-orthogonal synthetic puzzles can surprisingly boost performance on advanced math and STEM reasoning tasks in larger models. This work not only provides a powerful, unified framework for advancing logical reasoning research but also offers significant insights into how foundational reasoning skills, even when acquired from synthetic data, can broadly enhance the overall intelligence of LLMs.
