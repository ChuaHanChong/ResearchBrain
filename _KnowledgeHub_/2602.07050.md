# Interpreting Physics in Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Analyzing video world models like V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAE-v2, researchers identified a "Physics Emergence Zone" where representations of physical concepts are constructed, observing an early emergence of speed and acceleration magnitude before motion direction. The study reveals that core physical variables are encoded in high-dimensional, orthogonal subspaces using a circular population code rather than a compact, shared "physics engine" representation.

## Problem

- Despite strong performance on intuitive physics benchmarks, the internal mechanisms of how video world models understand and represent physical information remain opaque.
- A lack of deep interpretability for video models hinders understanding where, how, and in what form physical variables are encoded.
- The cognitive science debate on whether intuitive physics in humans is based on a 'physics engine' or 'heuristic-based' reasoning lacks empirical evidence from advanced AI systems.

## Method

- The research employed a multi-faceted interpretability methodology, including layerwise probing, subspace geometry analysis, targeted attention ablations, and activation steering on V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAE-v2.
- Synthetic datasets with ground-truth physical quantities (e.g., motion direction, speed) and intuitive physics benchmarks were used to precisely quantify representations.
- Generalized Linear Models and orthogonal probing sequences characterized individual neuron tuning and the effective dimensionality of physical encodings.

## Results

- Physical representations peak in the middle layers (post-PEZ) and degrade towards the output, with the PEZ being specific to tasks requiring global spatiotemporal coherence.
- Motion direction is encoded in a high-dimensional (40-80 independent features) circular population code, where individual neurons exhibit sinusoidal tuning curves.
- Causal interventions revealed that steering motion direction requires coordinated manipulations across dozens of orthogonal dimensions, indicating complex, distributed control over these physical features.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "Physics Emergence Zone" exists at approximately one-third depth of the encoder, marking a sharp transition where global physical concepts like motion direction and intuitive physics judgments become accessible.
> - Scalar physical quantities (speed, acceleration magnitude) emerge in earlier layers, while motion direction emerges later at the PEZ, paralleling biological vision hierarchies.
> - Representations for physics judgments and motion direction occupy nearly orthogonal high-dimensional subspaces, suggesting models do not use a compact, shared 'physics engine' but rather distributed, task-specific features built on local spatiotemporal attention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a fundamental question at the intersection of artificial intelligence, cognitive science, and computational neuroscience: how do modern video world models acquire and represent physical understanding? It positions itself within several burgeoning research landscapes:

**a. Interpretability and Explainable AI (XAI) for Video Models:** While interpretability for text and image models has seen significant advancements, understanding the internal workings of video models remains less explored due to their higher dimensionality and temporal complexity. Previous video interpretability studies often relied on proxy tasks, diagnostic benchmarks, or visualization-based approaches that quantified or visualized temporal concepts without characterizing their *representational form*. This paper directly tackles this gap by applying targeted interpretability analyses (probing, subspace analysis, ablations) to reveal *where* and *how* physical information is organized within video encoders, moving beyond mere behavioral performance or superficial visualization. It specifically contrasts with black-box evaluations of models on physics benchmarks, aiming to open the black box itself.

**b. Video World Models and Physical Reasoning:** The paper builds upon rapid progress in unsupervised video models, particularly encoder-based architectures like V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAE-v2, which are designed to capture reusable environmental structure for prediction, imagination, or planning. While these models achieve strong performance on intuitive physics benchmarks, they also exhibit systematic failures, raising questions about the nature of their internal physical representations. This work directly investigates whether these models infer stable physical structures akin to a "physics engine" or rely on more distributed, task-specific approaches. It challenges the assumption that strong predictive performance on physics tasks necessarily implies a human-like understanding of underlying physical principles.

**c. Cognitive Science Debate on Intuitive Physics:** A central theme of this paper is its direct engagement with a long-standing debate in cognitive science concerning the representational form of intuitive physics in humans. This debate often dichotomizes between "physics engine" views (positing compact, reusable latent state variables for quantities like momentum) and "heuristic-based" views (emphasizing domain-specific rules or perceptual shortcuts). By analyzing the internal representations of video world models, the authors use these advanced AI systems as a novel substrate to explore and gather evidence for these competing cognitive theories, suggesting that AI models can offer insights into human cognition.

**d. Connections to Neuroscience:** The research explicitly draws parallels between the observed organizational principles of motion processing in video models and those in biological vision systems. Findings such as the early emergence of speed followed by later emergence of direction, and the representation of direction as a circular population code, directly echo known hierarchies and neural coding schemes in primate visual cortex. This positions the work at the fertile intersection of AI and neuroscience, suggesting that video world models could serve as valuable computational models for understanding biological intelligence.

**e. Contrast with Language Model Interpretability and Physics Simulators:** The paper also contextualizes its findings by contrasting them with interpretability studies in large language models, where semantic concepts often align with low-dimensional steering directions. The discovery that physical variables in video models require high-dimensional, coordinated interventions highlights fundamental differences in representational geometry across modalities and model types. Furthermore, it contrasts with transformers trained directly on PDE simulations that *can* exhibit low-dimensional, interpretable physical phenomena, suggesting that the *training domain and objective* critically influence whether models develop compact, interpretable "state variables."

In essence, this paper significantly advances our understanding of how complex AI systems perceive and reason about the physical world, contributing simultaneously to the fields of AI interpretability, cognitive modeling, and computational neuroscience.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to move beyond black-box evaluations of video world models' performance on physics benchmarks and instead **directly examine the internal representations of physical information** within these models. The authors aim to characterize *where*, *how*, and *in what form* physical variables are encoded.

This overarching goal is broken down into several specific questions:
- **Localization:** Where in the network (i.e., at which layers) does physical information become accessible and constructed?
- **Organization:** How is this physical information organized across different layers and spatial/temporal patches within the network?
- **Representational Form:** What is the geometric nature of these physical representations? Do they resemble factorized, compact "physics engine" variables, or are they more distributed and implicit, akin to "heuristic-based" reasoning?
- **Mechanisms:** What computational mechanisms (e.g., specific attention patterns) underpin the emergence and processing of physical information?
- **Causality and Control:** Can these internal physical representations be causally manipulated (steered) to alter model predictions, and if so, how?

The motivation for this deep interpretability study stems from several critical areas:
- **Bridging the Performance-Understanding Gap:** Despite impressive performance on physics tasks, the *how* and *why* behind model predictions remain opaque. Understanding the internal workings is crucial for building trust, improving robustness, and diagnosing systematic failures.
- **Advancing Scientific Modeling:** If AI models can truly infer stable physical structures, they could offer powerful tools for scientific modeling in complex domains where analytic simulators are incomplete (e.g., climate systems, fluid dynamics, materials science). Mechanistic understanding is key to leveraging AI for scientific discovery.
- **Informing AI Design:** Discovering whether self-supervised video models spontaneously develop explicit "physics engine" representations versus distributed ones has implications for future AI architecture and training objective design. If compact, interpretable state variables are desired, explicit inductive biases might be necessary.
- **Contributing to Cognitive Science:** The research directly engages with the long-standing cognitive science debate about intuitive physics. By testing hypotheses about representational forms (factorized vs. distributed) within AI models, the paper provides a novel empirical avenue to inform theories of human cognition.
- **Drawing Parallels with Neuroscience:** Identifying similarities between AI models' internal representations and those found in biological brains (e.g., circular population codes for direction) opens avenues for AI to serve as a computational model for understanding biological intelligence.

In summary, the authors are driven by a fundamental curiosity about the nature of physical intelligence in artificial systems, with the goal of generating insights that benefit AI development, scientific research, and our understanding of intelligence itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a multi-faceted interpretability methodology to probe the internal representations of physics in state-of-the-art video world models. Their approach combines layerwise analysis with advanced probing techniques, subspace geometry, attention ablations, and activation steering.

**a. Models Under Study:**
The research focuses on two prominent self-supervised video transformer architectures:
- **V-JEPA 2 (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture):** This model trains an encoder to map spatiotemporal patches to latent representations, while a predictor forecasts representations of masked or future patches. The authors analyze V-JEPA 2 at three scales: Large, Huge, and Giant, leveraging its temporally structured features encouraged by the predictive objective.
- **VideoMAE-v2 G (Masked Autoencoder):** This model reconstructs masked pixels from video frames. The authors analyze its frozen pretrained encoder, which is designed to preserve visual detail through its pixel-level reconstruction loss.

Both models are studied in their frozen, pretrained encoder states to understand their learned representations without further task-specific fine-tuning influencing internal structure.

**b. Probing Methodology:**
To localize and characterize information, the authors use layerwise probing on the residual stream of every encoder layer:
- **Linear Probes:** Applied to mean-pooled space-time patches, these probes provide a direct readout of what information is linearly accessible within the representation. This is the primary method for identifying where physical concepts emerge.
- **Attentive-MLP Probes:** These patch-preserving probes complement linear probes by capturing more complex spatial and temporal structures, helping to confirm findings where pooling might obscure detail.

**c. Diagnostic Tasks and Datasets:**
Two main types of tasks are used, supported by carefully constructed datasets:
- **Intuitive Physics (IntPhys Dataset):** To assess coarse-grained physical understanding, models are probed on their ability to distinguish between physically *possible* and *impossible* video sequences (e.g., violating object permanence, shape constancy, spatiotemporal continuity). The IntPhys dataset (Riochet et al., 2021) is critical because possible and impossible variants differ only at a "breakpoint" frame, forcing models to rely on high-level motion dynamics rather than static visual cues.
- **Fine-Grained Motion Variables (Synthetic Toy-Ball Dataset):** For precise analysis of explicit physical quantities, a synthetic dataset is generated using the Kubric simulator (Greff et al., 2022). This dataset features a single ball with precisely controlled constant velocity or acceleration, allowing for ground-truth measurements of Cartesian components (v_x, v_y, a_x, a_y) and polar decompositions (speed, motion direction, acceleration magnitude).
- **Control Tasks:** To ensure the observed phenomena are specific to physical reasoning, several control tasks are used: CLEVRER object counting, ImageNet classification, Something-Something-v2 video classification, and shuffled vs. non-shuffled video discrimination. These help differentiate generic processing from physics-specific computations.

**d. Interpretability Techniques:**
Beyond basic probing, the paper employs a suite of advanced techniques:
- **Subspace Geometry Analysis:** To quantify representational overlap, the authors compute principal angles, projection overlap, and Grassmann distance between the decoding subspaces for motion direction, speed, and IntPhys judgments. This helps determine if tasks share underlying latent features.
- **Patch-Level Decoding and Spatial Generalization:** This technique analyzes how physical information (specifically direction) is distributed across different spatial patches and whether probes trained on one region generalize to others, revealing the transition from local to global representations.
- **Targeted Attention Ablations:** The authors analyze the attention head distance across layers and identify specific attention heads in the "Physics Emergence Zone" that exhibit unusually local spatiotemporal processing. They then causally test the importance of these heads by suppressing local attention only in the PEZ and observing performance degradation on various tasks.
- **Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) for Tuning Analysis:** To characterize individual neuron selectivity, GLMs are fitted to predict neuron activations from stimulus parameters (e.g., motion direction, speed). This reveals tuning curves, preferred directions, and population-level coding schemes (e.g., circular geometry).
- **Orthogonal Probe Sequence (Amnesic Probing):** To estimate the effective dimensionality of physical representations, a sequence of linear probes is iteratively trained, with each subsequent probe trained on the residual space orthogonal to previously learned directions. This reveals how many independent features are needed to encode a variable.
- **Activation Steering Experiments:** This gold standard interpretability technique involves directly manipulating activations in the identified high-dimensional direction subspace to causally change the decoded motion direction. This establishes the causal impact of the feature and the complexity required for control.

By combining these diverse methodologies, the research provides a comprehensive and rigorous analysis of physical representations within complex video models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several crucial findings about how video world models internally represent physical information, challenging some prevailing assumptions about their mechanistic understanding:

**a. The Physics Emergence Zone (PEZ):** Across all V-JEPA 2 models (Large, Huge, Giant) and VideoMAE-v2 G, a **sharp and consistent transition** was identified at approximately **one-third of the encoder's depth**. This "Physics Emergence Zone" (PEZ) marks the point where representations for distinguishing physically possible from impossible videos (IntPhys task) and encoding motion direction become reliably accessible to linear probes.
 - **Peak and Degradation:** Interestingly, physical representations are strongest in the middle layers, shortly after the PEZ, and **degrade towards the output layers**. This suggests that final layers may optimize for other objectives, and intermediate representations hold more informative physical structure for downstream tasks.
 - **Task Specificity:** The PEZ is not a generic depth-dependent effect but is **specific to tasks requiring global spatiotemporal coherence**, such as IntPhys discrimination and shuffled-video detection. Static tasks (ImageNet) or tasks requiring only local temporal cues (object counting, SSv2) do not exhibit this emergence pattern.

**b. Asymmetry in Motion Variable Emergence:**
 - **Scalar Quantities Emerge Early:** Scalar physical quantities like **speed and acceleration magnitude** are accessible from the *earliest layers* of the network.
 - **Direction Emerges at PEZ:** In stark contrast, **motion direction** only becomes reliably decodable at the PEZ. This pattern mirrors hierarchies in biological vision, where speed-sensitive motion energy appears early, and direction selectivity arises later.
 - **Acceleration Independence:** Notably, acceleration does not explicitly rely on an intermediate velocity representation; acceleration-like signals can be extracted directly from early layers.
 - **Local-to-Global Shift:** At the PEZ, direction information transitions from being fragmented and spatially localized across patches to becoming **broadly distributed and spatially redundant**, enabling position-invariant decoding and improved spatial generalization.

**c. Representational Orthogonality and Shared Substrate:**
 - **Distinct Subspaces:** Despite their co-emergence at the PEZ, the representations for possible-vs-impossible physics judgments and motion direction occupy **nearly orthogonal representational subspaces**. Subspace overlap analyses showed overlap levels statistically indistinguishable from random subspaces of equivalent dimensionality. This finding strongly argues against the idea of these models using shared, compact latent variables, akin to a unified "physics engine."
 - **Shared Circuit-Level Mechanism (Local Attention):** Instead of shared latent features, the tasks rely on a **shared circuit-level substrate:** attention heads within the PEZ that exhibit unusually **local spatiotemporal processing**. Suppressing this local attention specifically within the PEZ severely degrades performance on both direction decoding and IntPhys discrimination, while leaving static tasks largely unaffected. This identifies a critical role for localized spatiotemporal attention in constructing coherent physical representations.

**d. High-Dimensional, Circular Population Code for Direction:**
 - **Circular Population Code:** At the PEZ, MLP units selectively encode motion direction, forming a **ring-shaped population code with circular geometry**, reminiscent of neural population codes in motion neuroscience. Individual neurons show smooth, sinusoidal tuning curves tiling the 360-degree angular space.
 - **High Dimensionality and Structured Redundancy:** Direction is encoded in a surprisingly **high-dimensional subspace**, requiring roughly 40-50 (and up to 80) independent features for decoding. Probe performance across successive orthogonalizations exhibits a characteristic **sawtooth pattern**, consistent with direction being encoded through approximately sinusoidal feature *pairs* (e.g., sine and cosine components), suggesting structured redundancy rather than a simple low-dimensional vector.
 - **Difficult Steering:** Unlike many semantic concepts in language models that can be steered via low-dimensional interventions, effective steering of motion direction in these video models requires **coordinated interventions across dozens of orthogonal dimensions**. Single-feature manipulations yield minimal change.

These findings suggest that modern video world models, despite their impressive behavioral performance on physical reasoning tasks, do not internally implement a compact, factorized "physics engine." Instead, they rely on distributed, task-specific representations built upon shared low-level spatiotemporal computations, where even core physical variables like direction are encoded in complex, high-dimensional population codes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers profound implications across several scientific and engineering domains, significantly advancing our understanding of intelligence in artificial systems.

**a. Reinterpreting Intuitive Physics in AI and Cognitive Science:**
The most significant impact lies in challenging the "physics engine" hypothesis in both AI and cognitive science. The findings strongly suggest that current self-supervised video world models, despite their strong predictive performance on intuitive physics benchmarks, do *not* rely on compact, reusable latent state variables for physical reasoning. Instead, their internal representations of motion direction and physics judgments are distributed, high-dimensional, and largely orthogonal in latent space. This provides empirical support for "heuristic-based" accounts of intuitive physics from cognitive science, suggesting that robust physical behavior can emerge from complex, task-specific computations rather than a neatly factorized, explicit physics simulator. This insight has the potential to reshape how researchers conceptualize and design AI systems aiming for human-like physical understanding.

**b. Advancing AI Interpretability and Transparency:**
This work represents a significant step forward for AI interpretability, particularly for the challenging domain of video models. It moves beyond superficial visualizations or indirect performance metrics to provide a deep, mechanistic characterization of internal representations. By identifying the "Physics Emergence Zone," the specific computational role of local spatiotemporal attention, and the complex population coding for direction, the authors offer concrete insights into how these black-box models construct and process dynamic information. Such detailed interpretability is crucial for building more trustworthy, reliable, and controllable AI systems, and for diagnosing subtle failures in their understanding. The finding that intermediate layers might hold more informative physical structure also has practical implications for selecting features for downstream tasks or for improving generative models.

**c. Inspiring Neuroscience Research through AI Models:**
The striking parallels observed between the AI models' internal representations and those in biological vision systems are highly significant. The sequential emergence of speed before direction, and the representation of direction as a circular, high-dimensional population code, directly mirror well-established findings in primate visual cortex. This suggests that advanced video world models can serve as valuable *computational models* for neuroscience, offering a controlled, inspectable substrate to generate and test hypotheses about biological intelligence. This cross-pollination can accelerate discoveries in both AI and neuroscience.

**d. Informing Future AI Architectures and Training Objectives:**
The revelation that current self-supervised models do not spontaneously develop compact, interpretable "physics engine" variables has direct implications for future AI design. If the goal is to build AI systems with truly interpretable, factorized physical understanding (e.g., for robotic control, scientific discovery, or explainable decision-making), then simply scaling up self-supervised learning on natural video might not be sufficient. Explicit inductive biases, different training objectives, or novel architectural designs might be necessary to encourage the emergence of more structured, factorized physical representations. The contrast with PDE-trained transformers, which *do* exhibit low-dimensional steering, further highlights that the choice of training domain and objective is critical in shaping the interpretability of learned representations.

**e. Bridging Disciplines:**
The paper exemplifies successful interdisciplinary research, effectively bridging machine learning, cognitive science, and neuroscience. By framing AI interpretability questions in the context of cognitive theories and drawing parallels to neuroscientific observations, the work enriches all participating fields and opens new avenues for collaborative inquiry into the nature of intelligence.

In conclusion, "Interpreting Physics in Video World Models" is a landmark study that not only deepens our understanding of how advanced AI systems perceive the physical world but also offers novel insights into fundamental questions about intelligence and representation, with wide-ranging implications for the future development and application of AI.
