# Image Editing As Programs with Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Image Editing As Programs (IEAP) is a Diffusion Transformer editing framework that uses a vision-language model to turn a free-form instruction into a sequence of five atomic operations. Each operation runs through a lightweight LoRA adapter on one shared backbone. It beats prior open-source editors on MagicBrush and AnyEdit, especially for layout-changing, multi-step edits.

## Problem

- Diffusion editors handle structure-preserving edits such as color or style well, but degrade sharply on structurally inconsistent edits like add, remove, move, resize and action change that require layout changes.
- Existing diffusion-transformer editing methods cover only a narrow set of routine operations and lack comprehensive evaluation, so their real capability on diverse and layout-altering edits is unclear.
- End-to-end models treat each instruction as one monolithic mapping, which fails on complex multi-step instructions: they omit requested edits or alter unrelated regions, even with limited training data for layout patterns.

## Method

- ==Chain-of-thought== reasoning by a vision-language agent parses each instruction into a program over five ==atomic primitives==: RoI localization, RoI inpainting, RoI editing, RoI compositing and global transformation, run by a ==neural program interpreter==.
- Localization chains an LLM and a segmentation model. For move and resize, the LLM edits an ==in-context layout representation==, and the layout difference is converted into ==affine transforms== applied to the RoI mask.
- Four specialized ==LoRA adapters== (rank 128) on **FLUX.1-dev** handle inpainting, attribute editing, compositing and global change. Compositing uses an ==annular mask== from dilation minus erosion to blend boundaries.

## Results

- On MagicBrush, IEAP scores CLIP-image **0.922**, CLIP-output **0.247** and DINO **0.897**, best among baselines; its L1 of **0.060** is marginally above ICEdit's **0.058**.
- On AnyEdit, IEAP reaches a GPT-4o score of **4.41** versus **4.13** for ICEdit, with DINO **0.825** versus **0.765**. On local semantic edits it scores **4.42** versus **4.08**.
- Ablations on local semantic editing show that removing chain-of-thought and reduction drops the GPT score from **4.42** to **4.10**, and removing specialized RoI inpainting drops it to **3.65**, the largest loss.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When a generative model fails on a class of edits, diagnose which structural property drives the failure. Here layout change is the cause, so decompose it into simpler operations.
> - A small set of reusable atomic primitives with per-primitive adapters can beat one end-to-end model, and it composes to long multi-step instructions without retraining.
> - Sequential program execution has costs: repeated edits progressively degrade image quality and compositing can leave shadows inconsistent, so error accumulation is the main risk of decomposition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image generation and editing has been revolutionized by the advent of diffusion models, which have achieved remarkable success in synthesizing high-fidelity images from text prompts. More recently, Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have emerged as a powerful architecture, setting new benchmarks in generative fidelity due to their scalability and ability to capture long-range dependencies through full-attention mechanisms.

However, despite these advancements, instruction-driven image editing, where users modify existing images using natural language commands, remains a significant challenge. Current diffusion models, including those based on DiTs, primarily excel at "structurally consistent" edits—modifications that preserve the original image layout or make subtle attribute changes (e.g., changing an object's color or a background's tone). They exhibit a notable performance degradation when faced with "structurally inconsistent" edits, which demand substantial layout modifications, such as adding or removing objects, or significantly changing their position or size within the scene. This limitation stems from the combinatorial complexity of spatial-layout modifications, which is difficult for end-to-end models to learn from limited training data. While some existing methods have attempted to extend DiTs to instructional editing, they are often restricted to a narrow set of common operations and lack comprehensive evaluation across diverse, challenging scenarios.

"Image Editing As Programs" (IEAP) directly addresses this critical gap. Instead of treating instruction-driven editing as a monolithic, end-to-end task, IEAP proposes a **reductionist and programmatic approach**. It posits that complex, structurally inconsistent edits can be systematically decomposed into a sequence of simpler, "atomic operations." By modularizing these operations and leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for instruction parsing, IEAP aims to unlock the full potential of DiT architectures for robustly handling a wide spectrum of editing tasks, from simple adjustments to profound structural transformations. This work positions itself as a unified framework that not only pushes the boundaries of DiT-based editing but also offers a more interpretable and controllable paradigm for complex image manipulation, moving beyond the limitations of existing end-to-end approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a crucial observation regarding the current limitations of diffusion models, particularly Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), in instruction-driven image editing. While these models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in text-to-image generation and subtle image modifications, they **struggle significantly with structurally inconsistent edits**—those that necessitate substantial changes to the image's spatial layout, such as adding new objects, removing existing ones, or altering their positions and sizes. The authors' preliminary empirical analysis, which involved a taxonomy study of image editing instructions, concretely highlighted this "performance dichotomy": existing methods excel at layout-preserving edits but falter dramatically when layout modifications are required. This difficulty is attributed to the inherent combinatorial complexity of scene reconfiguration and the empirical limitations of DiT architectures when trained on such complex patterns with constrained data.

To overcome this fundamental challenge, the **key objective** of this paper is to introduce **ImageEditingAsPrograms (IEAP)**. IEAP is conceived as a unified image editing framework built upon the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, designed to efficiently and robustly handle diverse types of editing operations, especially those involving layout changes.

The core objectives and motivations can be summarized as:

1. **Identify and Systematically Analyze the Gap:** To conduct a comprehensive taxonomy study of instruction-driven image editing and empirically demonstrate where current DiT-based methods fall short, specifically highlighting their poor performance on structurally inconsistent edits.
2. **Propose a Reductionist Paradigm:** To introduce a novel approach that decomposes complex editing instructions into a sequence of "atomic operations," thereby simplifying the learning problem for the underlying generative model.
3. **Develop a Programmatic Framework:** To build IEAP, which leverages Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning via a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based agent to parse free-form instructions into these atomic operations, and then executes them sequentially using a neural program interpreter.
4. **Enhance Robustness and Generalization:** To enable the framework to generalize robustly across a wide range of editing tasks, from simple adjustments to substantial structural changes, by modularizing and sequencing edits.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To demonstrate empirically that IEAP significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and semantic fidelity, particularly for complex, multi-step instructions, even rivaling leading proprietary models.

In essence, the motivation is to transform the challenging, holistic task of instruction-driven image editing into a structured, programmable problem, making it more tractable and controllable for advanced generative models like DiTs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IEAP's methodology is grounded in a **programmatic reduction framework** that systematically decomposes complex, free-form image editing instructions into a sequence of modular, "atomic operations." This approach aims to decouple semantic and spatial reasoning, addressing the challenge of structurally inconsistent edits.

The overall pipeline of IEAP can be broken down into three main stages:

1. **Instruction Parsing via Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:**
 - A Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based agent, specifically GPT-4o, takes the original complex editing instruction and the input image (encoded as a data URI).
 - It performs CoT reasoning to break down the instruction into a series of simpler, atomic operations. This process is guided by a carefully designed prompt that defines 13 categories of atomic instructions (e.g., Add, Remove, Color Change, Move, Style Change). The VLM outputs a numbered list of these categorized instructions.

2. **Neural Program Interpretation:**
 - The sequence of atomic operations derived from the CoT reasoning forms an executable "program."
 - A neural program interpreter then dynamically executes this plan by sequentially invoking relevant specialized modules, each implemented as a lightweight adapter sharing the same Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone.

3. **Atomic Operations (5 Primitives):** IEAP abstracts all editing instructions into five fundamental atomic primitives:

 - **RoI Localization:** This is the crucial first step for any local edit.
 - For most edits, an LLM extracts a "text Region of Interest (RoI)" from the instruction.
 - A segmentation model (e.g., SAM2) then uses this text RoI to accurately segment and obtain the "image RoI" (mask `R`).
 - For "add" operations, where no specific object may be initially present, the LLM determines an appropriate image RoI based on the overall scene layout and the instruction.
 - For "move" and "resize" operations, an LLM updates the spatial layout of the image by taking the current layout and instruction as input, producing a modified layout. Geometric differences are converted into affine transformations applied to the RoI mask (`R'`).

 - **RoI Inpainting:** Used for additive or substitutive operations (e.g., "add," "remove," "replace").
 - An LLM extracts the semantic entity (`E`) from the instruction.
 - A prompt (`P`) is constructed (e.g., "add E on the black region" or "fill in the hole of the image" for removal).
 - A specialized inpainting model (`M_inpaint`), trained by the authors, generates new content within the localized RoI, seamlessly filling or completing the region.

 - **RoI Editing:** Applies to property change operations (e.g., color, texture, appearance, expression).
 - A trained attribute editing model (`M_attr`) directly modifies the visual attributes within the RoI based on the instruction.

 - **RoI Compositing:** Ensures seamless reintegration of the edited RoI back into the full image.
 - An "annular mask" is constructed around the transformed RoI (`R'`) using morphological dilation and erosion.
 - A fusion network (`M_fusion`), trained on ring-masked object boundaries, refines the pre-composited image, guiding smooth boundary blending. The prompt used is "inpaint the black-bordered region so that the object’s edges blend smoothly with the background."

 - **Global Transformation:** Handles modifications affecting the entire image consistently (e.g., illumination, weather, style).
 - A trained global transformation model (`M_global`) performs these full-image edits.

**Training Details:**
The authors fine-tune four specialized models (RoI inpainting, RoI editing, RoI compositing, and global transformation) on FLUX.1-dev using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for efficiency. They leverage the AnyEdit dataset and augment it with CelebHQ-FM for facial expression edits. Specific training setups are defined for inpainting (blacking out RoI pixels) and compositing (blacking out annular mask regions). All models share a DiT backbone, indicating a unified architectural foundation.

This modular, programmatic approach allows IEAP to break down highly complex and interconnected editing challenges into manageable, sequential steps, significantly enhancing its ability to handle intricate instruction-driven transformations that conventional end-to-end methods struggle with.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of IEAP demonstrates its superior performance across a wide range of image editing tasks, particularly for complex and structurally inconsistent edits, when compared to both open-source and leading proprietary models.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Experiments and Observations

The authors' initial taxonomic study and empirical analysis using GPT-4o ratings revealed a critical "performance dichotomy." Diffusion models, including DiT-based ones, showed high proficiency in "structurally consistent" edits (e.g., local attribute editing, overall content editing) that preserve image layout. However, they exhibited **significant degradation** when handling "local semantic editing" operations (e.g., add, remove, replace, action change, move, resize) that require explicit spatial-layout modifications. This finding directly motivated the programmatic decomposition approach of IEAP.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art Open-Source Methods

- **Qualitative Comparisons:** Visual results (Figure 5) clearly show IEAP's comprehensive superiority. It consistently produces outputs that accurately follow instructions, maintain structural consistency, and achieve high instance-level fidelity across various editing scenarios, including those involving significant structural changes (e.g., adding a bird on a stool, replacing a woman with a mirror, changing action to running). Baseline methods (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, UltraEdit, ICEdit) frequently fail to execute instructions accurately or introduce undesirable artifacts.
- **Quantitative Comparisons (Table 1):** IEAP achieves state-of-the-art performance on both the MagicBrush and AnyEdit test sets.
 - On MagicBrush, it shows the best CLIP_im (caption alignment), CLIP_out (semantic consistency), and DINO (structural detail preservation) scores, with a negligible increase in L1 (pixel deviation) compared to the best.
 - On AnyEdit, IEAP demonstrates significant and comprehensive improvements across all evaluation metrics, including average GPT-4o scores, highlighting its overall superiority.
- **Fine-Grained Analysis (Table 2):** When evaluating performance across different types of editing operations, IEAP consistently outperforms competitors in:
 - **Local Semantic Editing:** This category, identified as the most challenging in preliminary experiments, sees IEAP achieve the highest scores across CLIP_im, L1, DINO, and GPT (4.42 vs. 4.08 for ICEdit, 3.90 for MagicBrush), validating its effectiveness in handling layout changes.
 - **Local Attribute Editing:** IEAP maintains leading performance.
 - **Overall Content Editing:** IEAP also excels, showing its broad applicability.

#### 4.3. Comparisons with Cutting-Edge Proprietary Multimodal Models

- **Qualitative Comparisons (Figure 6):** IEAP was compared against powerful closed-source models like SeedEdit (Doubao), Gemini 2.0 Flash, and GPT-4o on complex, multi-step instructions. IEAP frequently rivals and often surpasses these leading models. While proprietary models sometimes omit specified instructions or introduce irrelevant alterations, IEAP faithfully executes each instruction, maintaining superior image consistency and instance preservation across multiple sequential edits. This highlights the effectiveness of its decomposition strategy for intricate tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Module-wise Ablation (Table 3, Figure 7):** Comprehensive ablation studies on the AnyEdit local semantic editing test set confirm the necessity and significant contribution of each component in the IEAP framework:
 - **"w/o CoT & Reduction" (end-to-end pipeline):** This resulted in a marked performance deterioration across all metrics, conclusively proving the vital role of the programmatic decomposition and CoT reasoning.
 - **"w/o RoI Inpainting" / "w/o RoI Editing":** Replacing specialized models with a generic inpainting model led to noticeable performance declines, underscoring the importance of tailored modules for specific atomic operations.
 - **"w/o Layout Reconfiguration":** Removing the LLM-guided layout adjustment caused a significant drop, demonstrating the necessity of precise spatial reasoning for movement and resizing.
 - **"w/o Annular Mask Integration":** A modest but consistent drop indicated the importance of the compositing step for seamless boundary blending.
- Qualitative ablations (Figure 7) visually reinforced these findings, showing issues like strange fillings, inconsistent instances, or unnatural edges when components were removed.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that IEAP's novel programmatic decomposition, combined with specialized atomic operations and robust CoT reasoning, enables it to overcome critical limitations in instruction-driven image editing, achieving state-of-the-art results across a wide range of complexities and outperforming strong baselines and even some leading commercial models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Image Editing As Programs" (IEAP) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of instruction-driven image editing, particularly for complex and structurally inconsistent manipulations that have historically posed substantial challenges for generative models.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing a Critical Gap:** IEAP directly tackles the fundamental limitation of existing diffusion models and DiTs in handling layout-altering edits. By moving beyond end-to-end approaches, it makes these complex transformations tractable and controllable.
- **Novel Programmatic Paradigm:** The core contribution is the innovative concept of viewing image editing as an executable program, decomposed into atomic operations orchestrated by CoT reasoning. This modularity offers enhanced interpretability and control over the editing process, a crucial aspect often lacking in black-box generative models.
- **Robust Generalization:** By defining a small set of atomic operations and sequentially executing them, IEAP demonstrates robust generalization across a wide spectrum of editing tasks, from minor attribute changes to significant scene reconfigurations, outperforming state-of-the-art open-source methods and competing effectively with leading proprietary models on intricate, multi-step instructions.
- **Leveraging Foundational Models:** The framework effectively integrates the strengths of Diffusion Transformers (for high-fidelity image generation), Large Language Models (for instruction parsing and semantic reasoning), and specialized segmentation models (for precise RoI localization), showcasing a powerful multimodal synergy.
- **Paving the Way for DiT Applications:** IEAP expands the practical utility of Diffusion Transformers beyond text-to-image generation, demonstrating their capability for fine-grained and controllable image manipulation, thus establishing them as a versatile backbone for complex conditional generation tasks.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Digital Content Creation and Design:** IEAP can significantly streamline workflows for artists, graphic designers, and advertisers by offering intuitive, language-driven control over complex visual changes, reducing the need for manual, time-consuming manipulations.
- **Visual Storytelling and Media Production:** Its ability to perform structurally inconsistent edits with high fidelity enables more dynamic and precise visual narratives, potentially revolutionizing content creation in film, animation, and interactive media.
- **Scientific Visualization:** Researchers can leverage IEAP to generate and modify complex visualizations with greater accuracy and flexibility, aiding in data interpretation and communication.
- **Accessibility Technologies:** The framework holds promise for generating more descriptive and contextually relevant visual representations for individuals with visual impairments, enhancing their understanding of digital content.
- **Future Research Direction:** The programmatic and modular approach could inspire similar decomposition strategies in other complex generative AI tasks, fostering new architectures and methodologies that prioritize interpretability, control, and robust generalization.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Dynamic Scenes and Physical Interactions:** IEAP struggles with highly dynamic or non-rigid content (e.g., motion blur, fluids) and simulating physically consistent lighting and shadows (e.g., reflections, accurate shadow directions after action changes), leading to unnatural results.
- **Inference Latency:** The sequential execution of atomic operations, while powerful, introduces significant latency (5-9 seconds per operation on an H100 GPU), precluding real-time interactivity for applications like AR/VR.
- **Resource Requirements:** The need for high-memory GPUs (e.g., NVIDIA H100 80GB) limits reproducibility and accessibility for researchers with fewer resources.
- **Quality Degradation over Iterations:** Multiple sequential edits can progressively degrade overall image quality.

Future work aims to address these limitations by:
- Integrating **physics-aware compositing** and **motion-compensated inpainting** to better handle dynamic content and physical consistency.
- Exploring **differentiable lighting models** or **neural rendering modules** for global illumination coherence.
- Implementing **performance optimizations** through model distillation, operation fusion, and sparse attention strategies to reduce latency.
- Focusing on **memory optimization** and support for smaller-footprint architectures to enhance accessibility.
- Developing **iterative refinement and error-correction mechanisms** to mitigate quality degradation.
- Extending the framework to **video-based complex instruction editing**, which presents additional challenges related to temporal coherence.

#### 5.4. Societal Impacts and Ethical Safeguards

The authors responsibly discuss both the positive and negative societal impacts. Positively, IEAP enhances precision and control for creative domains and potentially aids in scientific and accessibility applications. However, its high-fidelity editing capabilities raise concerns about **misinformation and privacy**, particularly the misuse for creating deepfakes, manipulated images for disinformation, identity falsification, or tampering with sensitive private imagery.

To mitigate these risks, the authors state that IEAP adheres to strict ethical standards. Safeguards include:
- **Data Filtering:** Utilizing GPT-4o-filtered subsets of AnyEdit and the compliance-oriented CelebHQ-FM dataset to reduce harmful biases.
- **Transparency and Traceability:** The modular nature of IEAP inherently facilitates transparency, as the editing process is broken down into identifiable steps. This supports the development of future content provenance systems capable of detecting and flagging manipulated media, contributing to broader AI safety and accountability efforts.

In conclusion, IEAP represents a significant leap forward in instruction-driven image editing, particularly for challenging structural modifications. Its programmatic approach offers a robust, interpretable, and high-performing solution, with clear implications for diverse applications while also emphasizing responsible development and deployment in the face of ethical concerns.
