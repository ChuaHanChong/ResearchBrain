# From Skills to Talent: Organising Heterogeneous Agents as a Real-World Company

> [!summary] Summary
> OneManCompany (OMC) is an open-source framework that models multi-agent systems as self-organizing and self-evolving AI organizations, enabling them to tackle open-ended, real-world projects. The framework achieved an 84.67% success rate on the PRDBench software development benchmark, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines by 15.48 percentage points.

## Problem

- Current multi-agent systems rely on fixed team structures and tightly coupled coordination logic, proving inflexible and brittle for novel, open-ended projects.
- Existing frameworks face challenges with incompatible runtimes and session-bound learning, hindering persistent knowledge accumulation and interoperation among diverse agents.
- A lack of a unifying organizational abstraction prevents the systematic management, coordination, and evolution of AI agent workforces for complex, real-world tasks.

## Method

- OneManCompany (OMC) employs a "Talent–Container" architecture that unifies heterogeneous agents as "Employees," featuring portable identities and abstracting diverse runtime environments.
- Project execution is formalized as an "Explore-Execute-Review" (E2R) tree search, providing structured planning, execution, and evaluation with formal guarantees for task termination and deadlock freedom.
- OMC implements systematic self-evolution mechanisms at both individual agent and organizational levels, including self-reflection, updated Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), and HR performance management for continuous learning.

## Results

- OMC achieved a success rate of **84.67%** on the PRDBench software development benchmark, demonstrating a 15.48 percentage point improvement over state-of-the-art baselines.
- The framework successfully managed and coordinated multi-agent teams across diverse domains, including content generation, game development, audiobook production, and automated research surveys.
- Case studies highlighted dynamic recruitment of specialized agents, autonomous skill creation (e.g., programmatic sprite slicing), and efficient cross-modal coordination, often at low costs (e.g., ~$4.48 for content generation).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling agent capabilities from organizational management through a Talent–Container architecture enables dynamic workforce assembly, reusability, and seamless interoperability of heterogeneous AI agents.
> - Formalizing project execution as an E2R tree search with explicit review gates significantly enhances quality control and task completion reliability, particularly for complex, long-horizon projects.
> - Implementing continuous self-evolution at both individual and organizational levels allows AI workforces to adapt, learn from experience, and maintain performance over time without constant human intervention.
