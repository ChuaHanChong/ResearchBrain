# From Skills to Talent: Organising Heterogeneous Agents as a Real-World Company

> [!summary] Summary
> OneManCompany (OMC) is an open-source framework that models multi-agent systems as self-organizing and self-evolving AI organizations, enabling them to tackle open-ended, real-world projects. The framework achieved an 84.67% success rate on the PRDBench software development benchmark, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines by 15.48 percentage points.

## Problem

- Current multi-agent systems rely on fixed team structures and tightly coupled coordination logic, proving inflexible and brittle for novel, open-ended projects.
- Existing frameworks face challenges with incompatible runtimes and session-bound learning, hindering persistent knowledge accumulation and interoperation among diverse agents.
- A lack of a unifying organizational abstraction prevents the systematic management, coordination, and evolution of AI agent workforces for complex, real-world tasks.

## Method

- OneManCompany (OMC) employs a "Talent–Container" architecture that unifies heterogeneous agents as "Employees," featuring portable identities and abstracting diverse runtime environments.
- Project execution is formalized as an "Explore-Execute-Review" (E2R) tree search, providing structured planning, execution, and evaluation with formal guarantees for task termination and deadlock freedom.
- OMC implements systematic self-evolution mechanisms at both individual agent and organizational levels, including self-reflection, updated Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), and HR performance management for continuous learning.

## Results

- OMC achieved a success rate of **84.67%** on the PRDBench software development benchmark, demonstrating a 15.48 percentage point improvement over state-of-the-art baselines.
- The framework successfully managed and coordinated multi-agent teams across diverse domains, including content generation, game development, audiobook production, and automated research surveys.
- Case studies highlighted dynamic recruitment of specialized agents, autonomous skill creation (e.g., programmatic sprite slicing), and efficient cross-modal coordination, often at low costs (e.g., ~$4.48 for content generation).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling agent capabilities from organizational management through a Talent–Container architecture enables dynamic workforce assembly, reusability, and seamless interoperability of heterogeneous AI agents.
> - Formalizing project execution as an E2R tree search with explicit review gates significantly enhances quality control and task completion reliability, particularly for complex, long-horizon projects.
> - Implementing continuous self-evolution at both individual and organizational levels allows AI workforces to adapt, learn from experience, and maintain performance over time without constant human intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have significantly enhanced the capabilities of individual AI agents, enabling them to perform complex tasks such as code generation, tool use, web interaction, and long-horizon reasoning. This progress is largely attributed to modular skill ecosystems and tool integrations, which allow agents to expand functionality without altering their underlying models. However, this paper identifies a limitation in current multi-agent systems: they often rely on fixed team structures, tightly coupled coordination logic, session-bound learning, and incompatible runtimes. Existing orchestrator methods, such as CrewAI, AutoGen, and Paperclip, are described as either rigid to novel projects or lacking convergence guarantees. While dynamic agentic workflows have demonstrated adaptability in task decomposition, they generally operate within pre-configured environments with fixed teams and shared runtimes.

The authors argue that this landscape lacks a unifying organizational abstraction that decouples the management and evolution of an agent workforce from the capabilities of individual agents. They propose a shift in perspective from individual agents and their skills to the "AI organization" that governs them. This concept introduces three properties largely absent in current paradigms: structured coordination, lifecycle management, and experience-driven evolution. The work positions itself as addressing a fundamental research gap concerning how AI agent workforces can be automatically organized, coordinated, and evolved to address open-ended tasks across diverse domains, drawing an analogy to organization design in human enterprises.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing multi-agent systems, particularly their inability to scale to open-ended, real-world projects due to a lack of a principled organizational layer. Current systems are characterized by:
- **Fixed team structures:** Inflexible and brittle when confronted with novel project requirements.
- **Tightly coupled coordination logic:** Often hardcoded or relying on unconstrained negotiation, leading to a lack of convergence guarantees.
- **Incompatible runtimes:** Preventing interoperation between agents from different families.
- **Session-bound learning:** Restricting self-improvement to individual sessions or specific frameworks, without persistent organizational knowledge accumulation.

To address these issues, the key objective is to introduce "OneManCompany" (OMC), a framework designed to elevate multi-agent systems to an organizational level. This framework aims to achieve the following specific objectives:

1. **Establish a principled organizational layer:** To govern how an AI agent workforce is assembled, governed, and improved over time, decoupled from individual agent capabilities.
2. **Introduce portable agent identities:** Encapsulate skills, tools, and runtime configurations into "Talents," allowing for flexible deployment and reuse.
3. **Abstract heterogeneous backends:** Orchestrate Talents through typed organizational interfaces to enable seamless interoperation between agents running on different underlying technologies (e.g., LangGraph, Claude Code, script processes).
4. **Enable dynamic workforce management:** Implement a community-driven "Talent Market" for on-demand recruitment, allowing the organization to dynamically adjust its capabilities by hiring and managing agents as needed.
5. **Develop a structured decision-making process:** Operationalize organizational decision-making through an "Explore-Execute-Review" (E2R) tree search, unifying planning, execution, and evaluation in a hierarchical loop. This aims to decompose tasks into accountable units and aggregate execution outcomes for systematic review and refinement.
6. **Provide formal guarantees for task execution:** Ensure termination and deadlock freedom within the E2R loop, mirroring feedback mechanisms in human organizations.
7. **Facilitate systematic self-improvement:** Implement mechanisms for both individual agent evolution (e.g., refining working principles through feedback) and organization-level evolution (e.g., distilling lessons into updated Standard Operating Procedures or SOPs, performance management).

Ultimately, the motivation is to transform multi-agent systems from static, pre-configured pipelines into self-organizing and self-improving AI organizations capable of adapting to open-ended tasks across diverse domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OMC is presented as an open-source framework structured around three core pillars, designed to mirror the operational mechanisms of human companies: workforce management, project execution, and self-evolution.

#### 3.1. On-Demand Organisation of Heterogeneous Agents by Talent–Container Architecture

This pillar addresses the challenge of unifying heterogeneous agents under a single execution model.
- **Employee Composition:** The fundamental unit, an Employee, consists of a **Talent** and a **Container**.
 - **Talent:** A portable package defining an agent’s cognitive identity (prompts, role, working principles, skills, tools, resources). It is designed to be deployable across OMC instances without execution-specific dependencies.
 - **Container:** The runtime environment that hosts a Talent (e.g., LangGraph-based, Claude Code-based, script-based). It provides an organizational layer through six typed interfaces that mediate all agent-platform interactions:
 - **Execution:** Dispatches tasks and returns results/costs.
 - **Task:** Manages a per-employee task queue with mutual exclusion.
 - **Event:** Provides an organizational event bus for communication.
 - **Storage:** Handles persistent memory for state management.
 - **Context:** Assembles the execution prompt.
 - **Lifecycle:** Applies pre- and post-execution hooks for validation, guardrails, and self-improvement.
- **Properties:** This architecture yields identity-substrate separation (same Talent on different Containers), multi-tenancy with isolation (all interactions via typed interfaces), and extensibility (new runtimes require only a Container implementation).
- **Digital Talent Market:** OMC integrates a community-driven marketplace for verified, benchmark-validated agent implementations (Talents).
 - **Sourcing:** Talents can be community-contributed, AI-recommended (assembling skills from the web), or internally promoted from high-performing employees.
 - **Hiring Pipeline:** An HR agent queries the market, compiles a shortlist, and upon CEO approval, an automated pipeline provisions the Talent with a Container and registers the new employee. This enables dynamic team assembly based on project requirements.

#### 3.2. Strategy Search in Vast Organisational Space by E2R Tree

This pillar formalizes project execution as a search process over organizational strategies, inspired by Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS).
- **Tree Structure (T):** Consists of nodes (V), decomposition edges (E_tree), and dependency edges (E_dep).
 - **Nodes:** Represent organizational states with task descriptions, assigned employees, status, results, costs, and shared workforce/resource states.
 - **Decomposition Edges:** Define how a parent task is broken into child sub-tasks.
 - **Dependency Edges:** Specify execution ordering constraints, forming a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG).
- **Actions (A):** The system can perform decompose, assign, recruit, review, and iterate actions.
- **Policy (π):** A mapping from the current tree to a strategy (a decomposition-and-assignment plan), executed by a supervising agent (e.g., COO).
- **Three Stages of E2R:**
 - **Explore:** Executive agents apply the policy to select a strategy, including task decomposition and employee assignment, which may involve recruiting new Talents.
 - **Execute:** Assigned employees carry out tasks via their Containers. The outcomes include results and execution costs.
 - **Review:** A reviewer evaluates task results, producing a quality signal (accept/reject). Rejection leads to re-entering the Explore stage for a new strategy.
- **Iterated Search with External Oracle:** The CEO acts as a meta-level controller, providing policy overrides, injecting new requirements, and triggering new search episodes. Policy updates improve the context for supervising agents.
- **Bounded Rationality and Circuit Breakers:** To ensure practical termination, OMC implements limits on review rounds, task execution time, and total cost budget.
- **DAG-based Task Decomposition and Execution:**
 - **AND-Semantics:** A node is considered resolved only when all its child sub-tasks are resolved.
 - **Task Lifecycle State Machine (FSM):** Each task node progresses through states (e.g., pending, processing, completed, accepted, failed). A critical `completed -> accepted` transition requires explicit supervisor review. A bounded retry path for `failed` tasks prevents indefinite cycling.
 - **Scheduling and Dependency Resolution:** Nodes become executable when their dependencies are met. A scheduler assigns tasks to employees in FIFO order, ensuring mutual exclusion.
 - **Formal Guarantees:** The design provides invariants such as DAG acyclicity, mutual exclusion, schedule idempotency, review termination, cascade completeness, dependency completeness, and recovery correctness.

#### 3.3. Self-Evolution: How Agents and the Organisation Improve

This pillar focuses on continuous learning and adaptation.
- **Individual-Level Evolution:** Agents maintain persistent profiles including progress logs and LLM-summarized working principles. Self-reflection is triggered by CEO one-on-ones and post-task reviews, leading to updates in the agent’s Talent artifacts without modifying the underlying foundation model.
- **Organisation-Level Evolution:**
 - **Project Retrospectives:** Upon project completion, the COO convenes retrospectives to aggregate self-assessments and objective signals (e.g., retry counts, rejection reasons). Findings are distilled into individual feedback and updated organizational SOPs, which are injected into future agent contexts.
 - **Performance Review and HR Lifecycle:** A formal HR agent initiates periodic performance reviews. Underperforming employees (failing multiple reviews) are placed on Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs). Persistent failure under PIP triggers automated offboarding, and the capability gap is flagged for re-recruitment from the Talent Market.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OMC involved quantitative assessment on a benchmark and qualitative analysis through cross-domain case studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation on PRDBench

- **Benchmark:** OMC was evaluated on PRDBench, a benchmark comprising 50 project-level software development tasks defined by Product Requirement Documents (PRDs). The tasks require long-horizon reasoning, hierarchical task decomposition, and multi-agent coordination, representing "wild dynamic agentic workflows."
- **Experimental Setup:** The system was configured with a founding team (CEO, HR, EA, COO, CSO – LangGraph-based agent using Gemini 2.1 Flash Lite Preview) and three specialists recruited from the Talent Market for the first PRD project: a Software Engineer (Claude Code-based), a Software Architect (Claude Code-based), and a Code Reviewer (from the agency-agents project). The evaluation followed a single-attempt zero-shot setting, with automated script-based evaluation.
- **Success Rate:** OMC achieved a success rate of 84.67%, which represents a 15.48 percentage point improvement over the state of the art. For comparison, baselines like Minimal Claude-4.5 achieved 69.19% and Commercial CodeX 62.09%.
- **Cost:** The total cost for 50 tasks was $345.59, averaging approximately $6.91 per task. Cost data for baselines was not reported, preventing a direct comparison of cost efficiency.
- **Contributing Factors:** The authors attributed the performance to three design aspects: dynamic task tree adaptation, the enforced `completed→accepted` review gate for quality control, and the Talent–Container separation enabling heterogeneous agent recruitment.

#### 4.2. Cross-Domain Case Studies

Four case studies demonstrated OMC's generality and capabilities beyond software development:
- **Content Generation:**
 - **Task:** Assemble a search-and-writing team to produce a weekly trend summary of AI Agent repositories on GitHub and email it.
 - **Outcome:** The system autonomously recruited a Researcher (GPT-4o) and a Writer (Claude Sonnet 4). It produced a verified report and emailed it in under 10 minutes at a cost of approximately $4.48. Repository links and star counts were manually verified for accuracy.
- **Game Development:**
 - **Task:** Create a street-fighting web game with high-quality visuals and animations.
 - **Outcome:** OMC recruited a Game Developer (Claude Sonnet 4) and an Art Designer (Gemini 2.5 with NanoBanana tool). After an external evaluator rejected the initial build due to improperly segmented sprites, the system autonomously extended the Art Designer's capabilities by creating a new skill for programmatic sprite slicing, reprocessed assets, and delivered the corrected product. This case highlighted human-in-the-loop iteration and dynamic skill creation.
- **Audio Book Development:**
 - **Task:** Produce an illustrated audiobook-style short drama retelling episodes of Peaky Blinders using animal characters, including scene illustrations, English voice-over, background music, and video composition.
 - **Outcome:** A Novel Writer and an AV Producer (Gemini 3.1 Pro with custom tools) were recruited. The system orchestrated scriptwriting, image generation, voice synthesis, and video assembly for two episodes, costing $1.57. This demonstrated cross-modal coordination and tool-augmented generation.
- **Automated Research Survey:**
 - **Task:** Survey "world models for embodied AI and robotics" (2021–2026), produce a detailed mind map, and propose three research ideas.
 - **Outcome:** The HR agent recruited three specialists: a Research Scientist (Claude Sonnet 4.6), a Research Paper Scientist (Claude Sonnet 4.6), and an AI Engineer (self-hosted). The project completed in under an hour at a cost of $16.26. It generated 17 structured documents, a mind map with approximately 70 nodes referencing 35+ papers, and three novel research proposals with technical formulations and timelines. Manual verification confirmed the quality and novelty of the outputs.

Across all case studies, the system demonstrated an ability to recruit appropriate specialists, decompose projects, execute tasks across heterogeneous backends, and deliver results based on a single CEO prompt, without requiring domain-specific configuration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces OMC as a framework designed to bridge a gap in multi-agent system capabilities by implementing a principled organizational layer analogous to human enterprises. This approach offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Enabling Complex, Open-Ended Tasks:** By providing mechanisms for dynamic team assembly, structured coordination, and systematic self-improvement, OMC is designed to enable AI agent workforces to tackle project-level tasks that typically exceed the capabilities of single agents or static multi-agent pipelines. This could expand the domains where AI agents can be effectively deployed.
- **Addressing Agent Heterogeneity and Interoperability:** The Talent–Container architecture provides a uniform interface over heterogeneous agent backends, addressing a limitation where different agent families cannot easily interoperate. This could foster broader integration and collaboration among diverse AI capabilities.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Predictability:** The E2R tree search, coupled with DAG-based execution and a finite state machine, provides formal guarantees for task termination and deadlock freedom. This structured execution and review process can lead to more reliable and auditable AI systems, reducing the incidence of silent stalls or unchecked errors.
- **Long-Term Adaptability and Evolution:** The explicit mechanisms for individual agent self-reflection and organizational learning (SOPs, HR performance management, automated offboarding) introduce persistent improvement loops. This capability allows the AI organization to adapt, learn from experience, and maintain performance over time without requiring continuous human intervention or retraining of foundation models, which is a step towards more autonomous AI systems.
- **Development of an AI Talent Ecosystem:** The concept of a community-driven Talent Market has the potential to foster an ecosystem where verified, reusable agent packages can be shared and recruited on demand. This could standardize agent capabilities and accelerate the development and deployment of AI solutions.
- **Broader Applicability:** The human-enterprise analogy suggests that the principles behind OMC are generalizable beyond software development. The case studies in content generation, game development, audiobook production, and research illustrate its potential applicability across various industries and complex workflows, impacting areas requiring coordinated intelligence.
- **Cost-Performance Management:** The inclusion of an adaptive dispatch mode, allowing simple tasks to bypass multi-agent coordination, indicates consideration for cost-performance trade-offs, which is relevant for practical deployment in real-world scenarios.
