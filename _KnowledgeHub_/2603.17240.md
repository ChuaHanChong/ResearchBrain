# GigaWorld-Policy: An Efficient Action-Centered World--Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> GigaWorld-Policy, from GigaAI, introduces an efficient action-centered World–Action model that significantly reduces inference latency for robot control while improving task success rates in both simulation and real-world manipulation. The model achieves a 9x speedup over prior World-Action Models and a 7% higher average success rate in real-world tasks compared to Motus by leveraging future visual dynamics as dense training supervision without explicit video generation during inference.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are often limited by supervision sparsity, leading to low-diversity actions and reliance on contextual shortcuts rather than robust physical consistency.
- Existing World-Action Models (WAMs) suffer from high inference latency due to iterative future video rollouts and are prone to error propagation from pixel-level video prediction, hindering real-time deployment.
- Integrating dense supervision from generative world models into robotic policies without incurring significant computational overhead at inference time remains a challenge.

## Method

- A unified ==diffusion Transformer== model is developed to jointly learn action chunks and sparsely sampled future visual observations, utilizing ==flow matching== for both action and visual dynamics prediction.
- **GigaWorld-Policy** employs an ==action-centered== design that leverages future visual dynamics primarily as dense supervision during training, enabling action-only decoding at inference time for low-latency operation.
- A ==curriculum training pipeline== is implemented, starting with initialization from a large-scale pre-trained web-video model, followed by pre-training on diverse embodied data, and fine-tuning on target-robot task trajectory data.

## Results

- GigaWorld-Policy achieved a per-step inference latency of **360 ms** on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, representing an approximate **9x speedup** compared to state-of-the-art WAMs like Motus (3231 ms).
- The model demonstrated an average success rate of **0.83** in real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming Motus by **7%** and VLM-based VLA models by **14%**.
- It exhibited superior data efficiency, achieving comparable performance to VLA baselines using only **10%** of the training data in real-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling explicit future video generation from action inference is effective; it allows retaining the benefits of world-model-based supervision during training while achieving significantly lower latency during execution.
> - The causal self-attention mask within the unified Transformer efficiently integrates action generation and feedforward visual dynamics modeling, which improves the learning of action-conditioned dynamics and ensures optional video prediction at inference.
> - Progressive curriculum pre-training, leveraging both generic web videos and diverse embodied data, effectively transfers broad physics priors and leads to more data-efficient policy learning for robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control, which aim to enable robots to understand language instructions and execute tasks based on visual observations. A significant challenge in VLA models, particularly those based on Vision-Language Models (VLMs), is supervision sparsity. While observations and task conditioning provide rich information, action supervision is often sparse and of low diversity. This can lead models to infer actions from shallow contextual signals rather than physically grounded behaviors. Attempts to address this involve injecting future-state supervision by predicting future visual observations; however, VLM-based VLAs are primarily optimized for discriminative reasoning, making it difficult to enforce physical consistency in generated actions.

A related paradigm incorporates World Models (WMs), typically used in video generation, into robot policy learning. These World–Action Models (WAMs) leverage large-scale video data to provide dense temporal supervision in the observation space, beyond sparse action labels. They achieve this by optimizing joint objectives for future visual dynamics and action prediction. While beneficial for learning, existing WAMs often require iterative sampling to roll out future videos at inference time, leading to high latency. Furthermore, errors in pixel-level video prediction can propagate and compound, potentially degrading long-horizon control.

GigaWorld-Policy aims to address the limitations of existing WAMs by introducing an action-centered and efficient approach. It seeks to harness the benefits of future visual dynamics for dense supervision during training while mitigating the high inference latency and error propagation issues associated with explicit video generation at inference time. This positions GigaWorld-Policy as an advancement in WAM design, focusing on practical real-time deployment without sacrificing the richness of world-model-based supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for GigaWorld-Policy stems from the observed deficiencies in current robotic control models, particularly concerning supervision quality and operational efficiency. The paper identifies two core problems:

1. **Supervision Sparsity in VLA Models:** Traditional VLA policies rely heavily on action supervision from demonstrations. This supervision is often sparse and lacks the diversity needed for robust, physically consistent action generation. While auxiliary future-state prediction (forecasting future visual observations) can densify training signals, existing VLM-based VLAs are primarily designed for discriminative tasks, making them less suitable for high-fidelity generative modeling required to enforce continuity and physical plausibility in predicted actions.
2. **Inference Latency and Error Propagation in World-Action Models (WAMs):** World Models offer a promising avenue by providing dense temporal supervision through future visual dynamics. However, current WAMs typically require explicit generation of future videos during inference. This process, often involving iterative sampling (e.g., diffusion models), introduces substantial computational overhead and high latency, thereby hindering real-time closed-loop control. Additionally, small errors in pixel-level video prediction can accumulate over time, leading to degraded performance in long-horizon tasks.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of GigaWorld-Policy are detailed as follows:

- **Develop an action-centered and efficient World–Action model:** The central objective is to leverage the dense supervision benefits of future visual dynamics during training while decoupling action prediction from mandatory future video generation at inference. This aims to create a model that learns robust policies without incurring the typical WAM latency costs.
- **Enable low-latency action generation for real-time control:** By designing the model such that explicit future-video prediction becomes optional during inference, the goal is to substantially reduce computational overhead, avoid compounding visual prediction errors, and facilitate low-latency, real-time closed-loop control for robotic systems.
- **Enhance learning efficiency and action plausibility:** By optimizing future action sequences and action-conditioned visual dynamics within the same model, the objective is to allow future visual dynamics to act as a regularization signal. This provides denser supervision, which is intended to improve learning efficiency and ensure the plausibility and physical consistency of learned actions.
- **Establish a robust pre-training paradigm:** The work aims to develop a curriculum training pipeline that effectively integrates diverse data sources. This involves initializing from generic video generation models, followed by pre-training on embodied, robot-centric data (real robot and egocentric human videos), and finally post-training on target-robot task trajectories. The motivation is to inject strong physics priors and adapt representations to embodiment-specific dynamics, leading to more generalizable and robust policies.

These objectives collectively aim to bridge the gap between the rich supervision offered by world models and the real-time efficiency demanded by practical robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GigaWorld-Policy is designed as an action-centered World–Action model that integrates action prediction with future visual dynamics modeling within a unified causal sequence model.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is formulated as sequential decision-making where, at each time step \(t\), the robot receives multi-view RGB observations (\(o_t\)), a natural-language instruction (\(l\)), and proprioceptive state (\(s_t\)). The policy is tasked with predicting an action chunk of length \(p\), denoted as \(a_{t:t+p-1}\). Unlike VLA policies that solely model an action distribution, GigaWorld-Policy adopts a world-modeling perspective, learning how visual observations evolve under an executed action chunk. It parameterizes two complementary conditional distributions:
1. Action modeling: \((a_{t:t+p-1}, c_t) \sim g_\Theta(\cdot | o_t, s_t, l)\), where \(c_t\) is an action latent conditioning signal.
2. Visual feedforward dynamics modeling: \((o_{t+\Delta}, o_{t+2\Delta}, \dots, o_{t+K\Delta}) \sim g_\Theta(\cdot | o_t, s_t, l, c_t)\), predicting sparse future observations within the \(p\)-step horizon, with \(\Delta\) as the temporal stride.

**Architecture:**
The core of GigaWorld-Policy is a 5B-parameter diffusion Transformer (Wan et al., 2025), initially pre-trained for video generation. Key architectural components include:
- **Input Tokenization:**
 - **Multi-view observations:** Three camera views (left, front, right) are composited into a single image (\(o_{comp_t}\)) to facilitate joint cross-view reasoning and consistency.
 - **Sparse Future Frames:** Instead of dense frame-by-frame prediction, the model forecasts a sparse set of future composite observations \(\{ o_{comp_{t+k\Delta}} \}_{K}^{k=1}\) with a fixed temporal stride \(\Delta\) (e.g., 12 steps for an action chunk length of 48).
 - **Encoders:** A pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encodes current and future composite observations into spatiotemporal visual tokens (\(T_o\), \(T_f\)). Proprioceptive states and actions are projected into state tokens (\(T_s\)) and action tokens (\(T_a\)) respectively. Language instructions are encoded by a pre-trained language encoder into instruction tokens (\(T_l\)).
- **Shared Transformer Blocks:** All token types are processed by a single stack of Transformer blocks, sharing query, key, and value projection matrices. This design aims to tightly couple action tokens with visual evidence while maintaining the computational profile of the pre-trained backbone. Positional encodings are differentiated for visual (2D) and state/action (1D temporal) tokens.
- **Causal Self-Attention Mask:** This is a critical component for unifying action generation and feedforward visual-dynamics modeling. Modality-specific tokens are concatenated into a unified sequence \(T_t = [T_o; T_s; T_a; T_f]\). A blockwise causal attention mask is applied to enforce specific dependencies:
 - State (\(T_s\)) and current observation (\(T_o\)) tokens can attend to each other.
 - Action tokens (\(T_a\)) can attend to \(T_s\) and \(T_o\).
 - Future-video tokens (\(T_f\)) can attend to \(T_s\), \(T_o\), and \(T_a\).
 - This masking prevents information leakage from future predicted frames into action generation (actions do not "see" the future video they are supposed to cause), while enabling future-frame prediction to be conditioned on the predicted actions. The language instruction (\(T_l\)) is provided via cross-attention, outside this causal ordering.

**Training Process and Data:**
GigaWorld-Policy employs a curriculum training pipeline:
1. **Initialization from Web-Video Foundation Model:** The model is first initialized from a large-scale pre-trained web-video model (Wan et al., 2025) to acquire generalizable visual-dynamics knowledge.
2. **Embodied Data Pre-training:** It is then further pre-trained on a diverse dataset of approximately 10,000 hours of robot-centric video data, comprising real-world robot manipulation videos (e.g., Agibot, RDT, RoboMind, ATARA) and large-scale egocentric human videos (e.g., EgoDex, Ego4D, Something-Something V2). This stage adapts the model to embodiment-specific viewpoints and interaction dynamics. During this stage, only the video flow-matching objective is optimized.
3. **Post-training on Target-Robot Trajectories:** Finally, the model is specialized for instruction-conditioned action prediction by post-training on target-robot task trajectory data that aligns images, language, and actions.

**Training Objective:**
The model is trained using flow matching to optimize both action prediction and visual feedforward dynamics modeling. For a modality \(x\) (action or future video tokens), a noised variable \(x^{(s)}\) is constructed, and the model predicts its target velocity \(\dot{x}^{(s)}\).
- **Video Loss (\(\mathcal{L}_{video}\)):** The model learns to predict the velocity field of future VAE latents (\(z_f\)) conditioned on history and the executed action chunk.
- **Action Loss (\(\mathcal{L}_{action}\)):** The model optimizes an action flow-matching objective conditioned on history (current observation, state, and language).
- **Combined Loss (\(\mathcal{L}_{all}\)):** During post-training, a weighted combination of these losses is used: \(\mathcal{L}_{all} = \lambda_{video} \mathcal{L}_{video} + \lambda_{action} \mathcal{L}_{action}\). For example, \(\lambda_{action}=5\) and \(\lambda_{video}=1\) are used to prioritize action prediction while using video dynamics as a regularizer.

**Inference:**
The key feature of GigaWorld-Policy at inference time is its ability to operate in an action-only mode for low-latency control.
- **Action-Only Decoding:** At each control step, the model forms a context from the instruction, current proprioceptive state, and multi-view observation. It then samples only the action chunk tokens (\(T_a\)) from the learned action flow model, without instantiating any future video tokens. This is achieved by integrating the learned velocity field for actions from \(s=0\) to \(s=1\). This directly outputs the continuous action chunk.
- **Optional Future-Video Prediction:** If future visual prediction is required (e.g., for planning or debugging), the video branch can be explicitly enabled. This can be done by including future video tokens in the input and denoising them jointly or by reusing the KV cache from action denoising.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GigaWorld-Policy was conducted in both simulation and real-world environments, focusing on task success rate and inference latency, in comparison to state-of-the-art VLA and WAM baselines.

#### 4.1. Inference Speed Comparison

- **Latency:** GigaWorld-Policy achieved an inference latency of 360 ms per step on an NVIDIA A100 GPU. This represents an approximate 9× speedup compared to the state-of-the-art WAM baseline, Motus (3231 ms), and was faster than Cosmos-Policy (1413 ms).
- **Trade-off:** Compared to VLM-based VLA models like \(\pi_{0.5}\) (225 ms) and GigaBrain-0 (452 ms), GigaWorld-Policy introduced a modest increase in latency over the fastest VLA but delivered improved performance. The results suggest a favorable balance between world-model-based capabilities and practical inference efficiency.

#### 4.2. Simulation Benchmark Experiments

- **Setup:** Evaluated on over 50 manipulation tasks in RoboTwin 2.0 simulation, under both clean and heavily randomized scenes. All models were trained on mixed datasets of demonstrations from clean and randomized scenes.
- **Performance:** GigaWorld-Policy demonstrated comparable performance to Motus while providing a significant inference speedup. For example, the average success rate on randomized scenes was 0.85 for GigaWorld-Policy compared to 0.87 for Motus. In contrast, GigaWorld-Policy significantly outperformed VLA baselines, achieving an average success rate of 0.86 (clean) and 0.85 (randomized), which was over 44 percentage points higher than \(\pi_{0.5}\) (0.43 clean, 0.44 randomized). This indicates that the action-centered WAM paradigm strengthens policy learning through joint supervision of actions and visual dynamics.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiment

- **Setup:** Conducted on an AgileX PiPER 6-DoF robotic arm across four manipulation tasks: "Clean the Desk," "Scan a QR Code," "Sweep up Trash," and "Stack Bowls."
- **Performance:** GigaWorld-Policy achieved an average real-world success rate of 0.83, outperforming both WAM and VLA baselines. It attained a 7% higher success rate than Motus (0.76) and a 14% higher success rate than \(\pi_{0.5}\) (0.69).
- **Efficiency Impact:** The authors attribute the real-world advantage over slower WAMs (like Motus) to the efficiency gains. Slower inference introduces control latency, reducing the effective action update rate and weakening closed-loop error correction, thus highlighting the practical benefit of GigaWorld-Policy's low-latency design.

#### 4.4. Data Efficiency

- **Finding:** GigaWorld-Policy exhibited improved sample efficiency. It matched the maximum success rate achieved by the \(\pi_{0.5}\) VLA baseline using only 10% of the training data. This suggests that the model's pre-trained representations and dense visual dynamics supervision lead to more efficient learning from limited robot demonstrations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Pre-training:**
 - Training from scratch yielded a 0.45 success rate (SR).
 - Initializing from a pre-trained video model alone improved SR to 0.57.
 - Using only embodied data pre-training (without video model initialization) resulted in an SR of 0.73.
 - Combining both pre-trained video initialization and embodied data pre-training achieved the highest SR of 0.83, indicating complementary benefits from both stages.
 - Scaling the amount of embodied data pre-training consistently improved real-world success, from 57% without embodied pre-training to 83% with the full dataset.
- **Impact of Number of Predicted Future Frames (\(K\)):**
 - Enabling feed-forward dynamics modeling consistently improved the success rate. The model with no future video prediction (\(K=0\)) achieved an SR of 0.60, while predicting future frames with a stride of \(\Delta=12\) (\(K=4\)) yielded an SR of 0.83, an absolute gain of 0.23.
 - Predicting denser future videos (smaller \(\Delta\), larger \(K\)) did not lead to additional performance gains, and in some cases, slightly degraded performance, suggesting a moderate amount of future modeling is sufficient.
- **Role of Causal Self-Attention for Video–Action Modeling:**
 - Compared to a self-attention variant where all tokens could attend to each other, the causal self-attention mask (where actions do not attend to future video) yielded similar task success rates (0.83 vs 0.81).
 - However, the causal mask is stated to be more deployable due to ensuring optional video prediction at inference.
 - The causal mask also resulted in higher future video generation quality (PSNR 28.41 vs 27.87, SSIM 0.901 vs 0.892) and more accurate predictions of fine-grained object state changes. This suggests that enforcing a physically meaningful factorization between action prediction and forward visual dynamics, by preventing information leakage, benefits the learned dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GigaWorld-Policy presents a contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically in robotic control, by addressing a critical trade-off between policy learning efficacy and real-time operational efficiency.

The work's primary significance lies in demonstrating that the rich, dense supervision provided by world models can be effectively leveraged during training without incurring substantial inference-time computational costs. By making future-video prediction optional at inference, GigaWorld-Policy achieves a substantial (9×) speedup over prior World–Action Models, moving such models closer to real-time closed-loop control requirements. This efficiency gain is crucial for practical robot deployments, where control latency directly impacts task success and robustness.

The observed improvements in task success rates, both in simulation and real-world experiments, indicate that the action-centered design and the multi-stage pre-training paradigm result in more accurate and reliable policies. The enhanced data efficiency is also significant, as collecting real-world robot data is resource-intensive. GigaWorld-Policy's ability to achieve comparable performance with significantly less data suggests it can facilitate the development of capable robotic systems in settings where data acquisition is limited.

Furthermore, the architectural innovation of the causal self-attention mask is notable. This design choice formally separates action generation from direct reliance on predicted future visual states during training, while still allowing the visual dynamics model to inform action learning. This approach not only enables efficient inference but also leads to improved quality in the learned forward dynamics, potentially contributing to more robust and physically consistent actions.

The potential impact of GigaWorld-Policy extends to enabling more sophisticated and autonomous robotic applications. By offering a model that combines the representational power of large-scale video models with the low-latency demands of real-time control, it could accelerate the development of generalist robots capable of performing complex manipulation tasks in dynamic and unstructured environments. This research provides a foundation for future investigations into scalable, efficient, and robust World-Action Models, bridging the gap between advanced generative models and the practical needs of embodied AI.
