# Beyond Human Data: Scaling Self-Training for Problem-Solving with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind developed ReSTEM_{EM}EM, an iterative self-training method based on Expectation-Maximization, to enable Large Language Models to improve problem-solving abilities using synthetic data. Applied to PaLM 2 models, this approach significantly outperforms models fine-tuned solely on human data on complex math and coding benchmarks while also demonstrating robust transfer capabilities.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) heavily rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using human-generated data, which is finite, costly, and time-consuming to acquire.
- Acquiring high-quality, diverse human data for complex problem-solving tasks like advanced math and coding is particularly resource-intensive and limits LLM capabilities.
- Previous self-training methods for LLMs often showed limited scalability to very large models (e.g., >7B parameters) or struggled with complex, verifiable tasks.

## Method

- The research introduces **ReST$_{EM}$**, a ==self-training== method theoretically grounded in the ==Expectation-Maximization (EM)== framework for reinforcement learning, which iteratively refines an LLM.
- The method alternates between a ==Generate (E-step)== phase, where the current policy samples multiple solutions filtered for correctness, and an ==Improve (M-step)== phase, where the base pre-trained LLM is fine-tuned on only the correct, ==reward-weighted samples==.
- Key design choices include not augmenting with human data and consistently ==fine-tuning the base pre-trained model== in each iteration to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and improve transfer performance.

## Results

- Google DeepMind's ReST$_{EM}$ significantly outperforms models fine-tuned on human-generated data (SFT) on both MATH and APPS benchmarks, with larger gains observed for larger PaLM 2 models (e.g., PaLM 2-L gains **6.34%** on MATH and **6.4%** on APPS).
- ReSTEM_{EM}EM-tuned models demonstrate strong positive transfer to related held-out benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval), with no major degradation on general capabilities (Big-Bench Hard), and the PaLM 2-L model performs exceptionally well on the 2023 Hungarian high school math finals, surpassing most models.
- Iterative refinement over multiple ReSTEM_{EM}EM iterations proves more effective than collecting a single larger dataset, and distillation from a larger ReSTEM_{EM}EM-tuned model to a smaller one via synthetic data also shows strong performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ReSTEM_{EM}EM effectively scales self-training to large language models (PaLM 2-L) for complex reasoning, challenging previous observations of limited scalability for such methods.
> - The iterative self-training approach, by fine-tuning the base pre-trained model in each step, achieves better transfer performance to held-out tasks while maintaining strong task-specific gains.
> - Model-generated solutions, when filtered for correctness, can be more effective for fine-tuning than human-written solutions, even with fewer examples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has been significantly propelled by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on vast amounts of human-generated data. However, this approach faces inherent limitations: the sheer quantity of high-quality human data is finite, its acquisition is costly and time-consuming, and its diversity can be constrained. This paper directly addresses this bottleneck by exploring methods to "go beyond human data," a critical direction in the evolution of LLMs.

The work positions itself within the burgeoning field of self-improvement for language models, particularly those leveraging synthetic data and reinforcement learning (RL) from feedback. Historically, RL for language generation often involves complex online policy gradient methods that are computationally intensive, especially for models with billions of parameters. This paper, building on the concept of Reinforced Self-Training (ReST) by Gulcehre et al. (2023), proposes ReST$_{EM}$, a self-training method theoretically grounded in the Expectation-Maximization (EM) framework for reinforcement learning. EM provides a principled approach to decouple data collection (E-step) from policy optimization (M-step), making it significantly more tractable for large LLMs.

ReST$_{EM}$ distinguishes itself from existing literature in several key ways. While similar EM-based approaches like Expert Iteration (ExiT) (Anthony et al., 2017), Self-Taught Reasoner (STaR) (Zelikman et al., 2022), Rejection Sampling Fine-tuning (RFT) (Yuan et al., 2023), and Iterative Maximum Likelihood (IML) (Agarwal et al., 2019) have been explored, this paper pushes the boundaries by:
- **Scaling to Larger Models:** Unlike most prior works that applied self-training to relatively smaller models (up to 7B parameters), ReST$_{EM}$ demonstrates significant gains on PaLM 2 models of varying scales, including PaLM 2-L, which has hundreds of billions of parameters. This directly challenges previous observations of limited scalability for self-training with larger LLMs (Yuan et al., 2023).
- **Challenging Domains:** The evaluation focuses on competition-level mathematical problem-solving (MATH dataset) and code generation (APPS dataset), tasks where correctness is verifiable via scalar feedback (binary reward). These domains are considerably more complex than elementary reasoning or simpler NLP tasks typically studied.
- **Refined Methodology:** ReST$_{EM}$ makes specific modifications, such as always fine-tuning the *base* pre-trained model in each iteration (rather than the model from the previous iteration) and not augmenting with human-generated outputs, which is shown to improve transfer performance.
- **Empirical Depth:** The paper provides comprehensive ablation studies on the number of iterations, samples, and training data size, as well as an analysis of performance across difficulty levels and transferability to held-out benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, Hungarian HS finals, Big-Bench Hard).

In essence, this work marks a significant step towards enabling LLMs to robustly self-improve on complex reasoning tasks, paving the way for a future where high-quality human data is less of a limiting factor in AI development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively "go beyond human data" for problem-solving tasks that allow for automatic verification of correctness via scalar feedback. The authors aim to demonstrate that self-training methods can significantly improve LLM performance, surpassing models trained solely on human-generated data, thereby reducing the reliance on such expensive and scarce resources.

The core motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing the Human Data Bottleneck:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on human-collected data is a prevalent practice for boosting LLM performance. However, acquiring high-quality, diverse, and sufficient human data, especially for complex problem-solving tasks (e.g., advanced math, coding), is resource-intensive and often limits model capabilities.
- **Exploring Synthetic Data Scalability:** Model-generated synthetic data offers a promising alternative due to its scalability and cost-effectiveness. The paper seeks to rigorously test if the quality of such data can be ensured and if it can effectively drive performance gains, particularly for larger LLMs where previous attempts showed limited scalability.
- **Leveraging Verifiable Tasks:** The authors focus on tasks like math and coding, where a clear, external, scalar (binary) feedback signal (e.g., correct/incorrect answer, passing/failing test cases) is available. This setting simplifies the quality assessment of generated samples, making it ideal for investigating self-training.
- **Proposing a Principled Self-Training Approach:** The motivation includes developing a robust and theoretically grounded self-training approach (ReST$_{EM}$) that decouples data generation from policy optimization, making it suitable for large-scale LLM training.

Ultimately, the paper seeks to validate that self-training with external feedback is a viable and powerful paradigm for advancing LLM capabilities in complex domains, lessening the dependency on human data, and facilitating further self-improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research revolves around **ReST$_{EM}$ (Reinforced Self-Training based on Expectation-Maximization)**, a simple yet effective self-training method. ReST$_{EM}$ is explicitly framed as an application of the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm to reinforcement learning (RL) for language models. The method iteratively refines the language model by alternating between two main steps: Generate (E-step) and Improve (M-step).

**Theoretical Grounding (Expectation-Maximization for RL):**
The paper formally describes ReST$_{EM}$'s connection to EM. The goal is to maximize the likelihood of obtaining a high reward (an "optimal" outcome, $O=1$). Since summing over all possible output sequences $\boldsymbol{y}$ is intractable, EM maximizes a lower bound (ELBO) on this log-likelihood.
- **E-step (Expectation):** In this step, the algorithm infers a variational distribution $q(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{x})$ that represents the likelihood of an output sequence $\boldsymbol{y}$ being optimal given an input $\boldsymbol{x}$, based on the current model parameters $\theta_t$. For non-negative rewards and a specific function mapping reward to optimality, this effectively means weighting the output samples from the current policy $p_{\theta_t}(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{x})$ by their reward $r(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{y})$.
- **M-step (Maximization):** Given the inferred distribution $q_{t+1}(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{x})$ from the E-step, this step updates the model parameters $\theta_{t+1}$ by minimizing the KL divergence between $q_{t+1}$ and the model's distribution $p_\theta$. This is equivalent to maximizing a reward-weighted negative log-likelihood loss on the collected samples.

**ReST$_{EM}$ Algorithm (Algorithm 1 in the paper):**
ReST$_{EM}$ applies this EM framework iteratively (for $I$ iterations) as follows:

1. **Generate (E-step):**
 - For each input context $\boldsymbol{x}$ from the training dataset $\mathcal{D}$, the *current policy* $p_\theta$ generates multiple output sequences (solutions). Specifically, for the experiments, 32 samples were generated for MATH problems and 64 for APPS problems using top-K sampling (K=40) and a temperature of 0.7 to encourage diversity.
 - These generated samples are then filtered using a binary reward function $r(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{y})$. For MATH, correctness is verified against a ground-truth answer; for APPS, code correctness is verified by passing unit tests. Only correct solutions (reward = 1) are kept.
 - To prevent overfitting on easier problems that yield many correct solutions, a "cut-off threshold" is applied, limiting the maximum number of correct solutions per problem included in the training dataset (10 for both MATH and APPS). This creates the training dataset $\mathcal{D}_i$ for the current iteration.

2. **Improve (M-step):**
 - The *base pre-trained language model* (not the model from the previous ReST iteration) is supervised fine-tuned on the dataset $\mathcal{D}_i$ collected in the Generate step. This is a crucial distinction from some prior ReST variants.
 - The fine-tuning objective is to minimize the reward-weighted negative log-likelihood loss: $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{y}) \sim \mathcal{D}_i} [r(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{y}) \log p_\theta(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{x})]$. Since rewards are binary (0 or 1), this effectively means minimizing negative log-likelihood only for the *correct* samples.
 - The fine-tuning continues as long as the reward on a validation dataset ($\mathcal{D}_{val}$) improves. The fine-tuned model (now $p_\theta$) is then used for the next Generate step.

**Key Differences from Prior Work (as highlighted by the authors):**
- **No Human Data Augmentation:** Unlike original ReST (Gulcehre et al., 2023), ReST$_{EM}$ does not augment the generated dataset with human-generated outputs, assuming such data might not always be optimal or readily available.
- **Fine-tuning Base Model:** ReST$_{EM}$ always fine-tunes the base pre-trained LLM in each M-step, rather than the model from the previous iteration. This design choice is shown to improve transfer performance on held-out tasks.
- **Binary Rewards Focus:** The method is optimized for settings with binary rewards, simplifying the reward weighting.

The models used are PaLM 2-S, PaLM 2-S*, and PaLM 2-L, indicating the method's application to large-scale, state-of-the-art LLMs. The training datasets are Hendrycks' MATH and APPS (Introductory), chosen for their automatic evaluability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical results presented in the paper demonstrate the significant effectiveness and scalability of ReST$_{EM}$ for problem-solving tasks.

- **Superiority over Human Data Fine-tuning:**
 - ReST$_{EM}$ substantially outperforms models fine-tuned solely on human-generated data (SFT) on both the MATH and APPS benchmarks. For example, on MATH, PaLM 2-L with ReST$_{EM}$ shows notably larger performance gains than PaLM 2-L-SFT.
 - Even in an "apples-to-apples" comparison where ReST$_{EM}$ uses only one model-generated correct solution per problem (ReST$^*$(5K)) selected from the MATH dataset, it still outperforms SFT on human-written solutions (SFT(5K)), suggesting that model-generated solutions are often "more in-distribution" or better suited for fine-tuning.

- **Favorable Scaling with Model Size:**
 - Contrary to observations in some prior work (e.g., Yuan et al., 2023 on GSM8K), ReST$_{EM}$ demonstrates *larger* performance gains as model capacity increases. On MATH, PaLM 2-L shows a 6.34% improvement with ReST$_{EM}$ compared to 5.94% for PaLM 2-S. Similarly, on APPS, PaLM 2-L gains 6.4% while PaLM 2-S* gains 5.6%. This indicates that the method effectively leverages larger model capacities.

- **Impact of Multiple Iterations:**
 - For MATH, multiple iterations of ReST$_{EM}$ yield incremental performance gains, both on the MATH test set and for transfer to GSM8K. The second and third iterations still provide small but meaningful improvements.
 - However, for APPS, most gains are achieved in the first iteration, with subsequent iterations sometimes leading to a slight regression in performance. This suggests potential overfitting on the smaller APPS training dataset.
 - A direct ablation shows that performing multiple iterations of ReST$_{EM}$ (e.g., 3 iterations) is more effective than collecting a 3x larger dataset and performing a single iteration, highlighting the benefit of iterative refinement.

- **Improved Pass@K and Majority Voting:**
 - ReST$_{EM}$-fine-tuned models show enhanced pass@K performance across all values of K (number of samples generated), with the most significant improvements often seen at K=1. This indicates a general improvement in the model's ability to produce correct solutions.
 - For MATH, majority voting with 64 samples on a ReST$_{EM}$-tuned PaLM 2-L model achieves 48.82% test accuracy, significantly higher than the base model's 44.02%, further reinforcing the quality of generated solutions.

- **Robust Transfer Capabilities:**
 - Models fine-tuned with ReST$_{EM}$ demonstrate strong positive transfer to related held-out benchmarks like GSM8K (for math) and HumanEval (for coding), confirming that the learned capabilities generalize beyond the training distribution.
 - Crucially, evaluation on the challenging Big-Bench Hard (BBH) suite of tasks shows *no major degradation* in general capabilities. In fact, the MATH-tuned model even outperforms the base model on BBH tasks when using chain-of-thought prompting, suggesting an overall improvement in reasoning.
 - The ReST$_{EM}$-tuned PaLM 2-L model performs exceptionally well on the 2023 Hungarian high school finals exam in mathematics, surpassing all existing models except GPT-4, demonstrating strong real-world problem-solving ability.

- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **ReST vs. ReST$_{EM}$:** Fine-tuning the base model in each ReST$_{EM}$ iteration (instead of the previous iteration's model as in original ReST) leads to comparable task-specific performance but substantially better transfer performance, highlighting the importance of mitigating "catastrophic forgetting."
 - **Dataset Size Efficiency:** ReST$_{EM}$ is surprisingly sample-efficient in terms of the number of *input problems*. Using as few as 1000 MATH questions still yields significant gains, although performance generally improves with more training problems.
 - **Difficulty Level Improvement:** ReST$_{EM}$ consistently improves performance across all question difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard, very hard), with the highest gains observed for medium and hard problems, indicating its ability to tackle moderately challenging problems effectively.
 - **Distillation Effectiveness:** ReST$_{EM}$-generated data from a larger model (PaLM 2-L) can effectively distill knowledge into a smaller model (PaLM 2-S), outperforming fine-tuning on human-written solutions for the smaller model. This suggests the utility of ReST$_{EM}$ for creating specialized, smaller models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a compelling case for the efficacy of self-training methods, particularly ReST$_{EM}$, in advancing the capabilities of Large Language Models for complex problem-solving. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Reduced Dependence on Human Data:** The most immediate and significant impact is the demonstrated ability to "go beyond human data." This reduces the reliance on expensive, time-consuming, and often limited human-generated datasets for fine-tuning. For specialized domains like advanced mathematics or code generation, where expert human data is scarce, this paradigm shift is transformative. It allows for continuous model improvement even after human data sources are exhausted.

- **Enhanced Scalability of LLM Development:** By decoupling data collection and policy optimization via the EM framework, ReST$_{EM}$ makes self-training computationally feasible for massive LLMs, which was a challenge for earlier online RL methods. The finding that ReST$_{EM}$ scales favorably with increasing model capacity suggests a powerful path for leveraging larger and more powerful foundational models.

- **Automated Self-Improvement Loop:** This work contributes to the vision of LLMs capable of self-improvement. While ReST$_{EM}$ still requires an initial dataset of problems and an automatic reward function, it significantly automates the "improvement" cycle by generating and leveraging high-quality synthetic data. This could lead to more autonomous and efficient development cycles for AI systems.

- **Creation of Specialized and Robust LLMs:** The substantial performance gains on MATH and APPS, coupled with robust transfer to related benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, Hungarian HS finals), indicate that ReST$_{EM}$ can create highly specialized and capable LLMs without sacrificing general abilities (as shown by BBH evaluations). This is crucial for deploying LLMs in domain-specific applications.

- **Implications for Distillation:** The success of distilling knowledge from a larger ReST$_{EM}$-tuned model to a smaller one using model-generated data suggests a powerful new paradigm for creating efficient, task-specific smaller models. This can make advanced capabilities more accessible by reducing inference costs.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The paper openly acknowledges several limitations. ReST$_{EM}$ still requires an initial training set of problems (collected from humans) and an automatically computable reward function, limiting its immediate applicability to domains without such mechanisms. Furthermore, while it improves pass@1 performance significantly, it doesn't fully close the gap to pass@K (where K is large), suggesting further room for improvement in generating diverse and consistently correct solutions. Future research will likely focus on automating the remaining manual parts of the pipeline (e.g., reward function design, initial problem set creation) and exploring algorithmic improvements to fully leverage the potential of pass@K.

In summary, "Beyond Human Data" provides a robust, empirically validated framework for self-improving LLMs, marking a significant step towards more autonomous and scalable AI development, particularly in complex reasoning and problem-solving domains.
