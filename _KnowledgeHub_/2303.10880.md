# Rotating without Seeing: Towards In-hand Dexterity through Touch

> [!summary] Summary
> Touch Dexterity performs in-hand object rotation without vision, using 16 cheap binary force sensors spread over an Allegro hand's palm, links, and fingertips. A PPO policy trained in simulation on diverse objects with domain randomization transfers zero-shot to hardware, rotating unseen everyday objects roughly five turns in 30 seconds while sensorless and open-loop baselines stall below one.

## Problem

- High-resolution tactile sensors are costly, so prior systems mount them only on fingertips, leaving palm and finger links blind and restricting feasible manipulation tasks.
- Rich tactile signals are hard to simulate faithfully, so the resulting Sim2Real gap blocks the large-scale simulated sample collection that high-DOF hands need.
- Vision-based in-hand rotation suffers occlusion, while proprioception-only policies handle fingertip rotation of few objects and were never shown to generalize to unseen objects.

## Method

- Dense binary touch skin: 16 ==Force-Sensing Resistors== cover one side of the Allegro hand, thresholded into on/off contact bits that are trivially simulated, shrinking the ==Sim2Real== gap.
- ==PPO== policy over joint positions, binary contacts, previous position targets, and a commanded rotation axis, using asymmetric actor-critic observations and stacked history in an MLP.
- Reward mixes a finite-difference rotation angle in the axis normal plane with velocity, falling, work, torque, and fingertip-distance terms; ==EMA== action smoothing plus physics ==domain randomization==.

## Results

- On the real hand, the touch policy turned the seen cuboid **4.91** rounds in 30 seconds versus **2.50** for continuous-force sensing, **0.58** open-loop, **0.25** sensorless.
- In simulation, disabling sensors dropped unseen-object rotation from **594.4** to **186.5** and unseen-physics rotation from **919.3** to **369.0**, confirming touch drives robustness and generalization.
- Ablating fingertip or palm sensors collapsed cuboid rotation from **4.91** to **0.17** and **0.42**; a tactile shape-prediction model halved reconstruction error, **0.22** versus **0.45**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Many cheap binary sensors can beat few precise ones: 16 contact bits span 2^16 states, enough to feel object state without vision.
> - Deliberately coarsening a sensor modality is a Sim2Real strategy: low-fidelity observations transfer more reliably than rich ones simulators cannot reproduce.
> - Coverage matters more than resolution: removing either palm or fingertip sensors collapsed performance, so fingertip-only instrumentation caps achievable whole-hand dexterity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic dexterous manipulation aims to enable robots to perform complex tasks with multi-fingered hands, mimicking human-level dexterity. This paper addresses a critical challenge within this field: achieving robust in-hand manipulation without relying on visual feedback. Traditionally, dexterous manipulation research has predominantly leveraged visual sensors (e.g., cameras, depth sensors) to perceive object states and guide manipulation. However, vision-based approaches suffer from inherent limitations in real-world scenarios, such as object occlusion by the robot's own hand or in environments with poor lighting conditions (as aptly illustrated by the "washing pan in the dark" analogy in the introduction).

In response to these vision limitations, several alternative approaches have emerged:
- **Proprioception-only methods:** These infer object state and interaction from robot joint positions and torques. While promising for certain tasks, they often struggle with complex, high-degree-of-freedom in-hand manipulation of diverse objects, as the implicit information is insufficient for precise control or generalization.
- **Tactile sensing methods:** Drawing inspiration from human biology, where touch plays a vital role in dexterity, researchers have explored various tactile sensors. Early tactile sensors often focused on high-resolution force or geometry measurement in small areas, typically fingertips. While providing rich, precise contact information, these "high-quality" sensors face significant challenges:
 - **Limited Coverage:** Their expense and complexity often restrict their deployment to small areas, like fingertips, limiting their utility for whole-hand manipulation tasks where objects interact with palms and finger links.
 - **High Sim2Real Gap:** The intricate physical modeling required to accurately simulate high-resolution tactile sensors makes transferring policies trained in simulation to the real world exceptionally difficult. This "Sim2Real gap" necessitates vast amounts of real-world training data, which is time-consuming and costly.

"Rotating without Seeing" positions itself precisely at the intersection of these challenges. It directly confronts the limitations of vision-based systems by pursuing a purely touch-based approach and addresses the difficulties of existing tactile sensing by proposing a novel system design. Instead of aiming for high-fidelity, small-area tactile sensing, the authors propose using a large array of low-cost, dense *binary* Force-Sensing Resistors (FSRs) that cover a significant portion of the robot hand (fingertips, links, and palm). This design paradigm shifts the focus from precise local contact *measurements* to a broader, coarser map of *contact presence*.

This work contributes to the broader landscape by demonstrating that a distributed, binary tactile sensing system, combined with reinforcement learning and robust domain randomization, can:
1. **Overcome the Sim2Real gap:** The binary nature of the sensor readings significantly simplifies simulation, making the transfer to real robots much more feasible and robust.
2. **Enable whole-hand manipulation:** The wide coverage of the sensors provides a richer understanding of object-hand interaction across the entire grasping surface, crucial for complex in-hand reorientation.
3. **Achieve generalization:** The policy trained with this system can generalize to diverse, novel objects not seen during training, a significant step towards truly adaptive manipulation.
4. **Offer a practical, low-cost solution:** The use of inexpensive FSRs makes this approach more accessible and scalable compared to systems relying on bespoke, expensive tactile skins.

Thus, the paper represents a significant step towards making touch-driven dexterous manipulation a practical reality, particularly for tasks where vision is occluded or unavailable, bridging a critical gap in current robotic capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to empower multi-finger robot hands with the ability to perform complex in-hand dexterous manipulation using only touch, mirroring human capabilities in scenarios where vision is absent or obscured. The authors title their work "Rotating without Seeing," directly encapsulating this core ambition.

The key motivations driving this research stem from several inherent limitations of current robotic manipulation systems and the under-explored potential of tactile sensing:

1. **Addressing Vision Limitations:** The primary motivation is to overcome the inherent vulnerabilities of vision-based manipulation. Real-world scenarios often involve occlusions (e.g., the robot's own hand blocking the object, clutter in the environment) or low-light conditions, rendering vision unreliable or impossible. Humans are adept at manipulating objects by feel alone, and the authors aim to bestow similar robustness upon robots.
2. **Enabling True Dexterity:** While vision can provide global object information, tactile sensing offers invaluable *contact information* that is difficult to infer from vision alone. This includes details about local geometry, force distribution, and micro-slips, which are crucial for stable grasping and precise manipulation. The paper argues that true dexterity requires integrating this rich contact feedback.
3. **Overcoming Challenges of Existing Tactile Sensors:**
 - **Cost and Coverage:** Many high-resolution tactile sensors are expensive and typically limited to small areas (e.g., fingertips). This restricts the range of tasks a robot hand can perform, especially those requiring interaction with the palm or multiple finger links. The authors aim for a system with *large coverage* over the entire hand.
 - **Sim2Real Gap:** The precise and delicate nature of many advanced tactile sensors makes their accurate simulation extremely difficult, leading to a substantial "Sim2Real gap." This hinders the use of powerful simulation-based reinforcement learning techniques, often forcing reliance on costly real-world data collection. The authors seek a sensor design that *minimizes this gap*.
4. **Achieving Generalization to Novel Objects:** A significant challenge in dexterous manipulation is enabling robots to manipulate objects they have never encountered before. The authors hypothesize that a well-designed tactile system, combined with appropriate learning, can implicitly infer object properties and facilitate generalization.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Design a novel tactile sensing system:** Propose and implement a low-cost, dense array of binary force sensors covering the Allegro hand to provide comprehensive contact information while minimizing Sim2Real challenges.
- **Develop a reinforcement learning framework for touch-only in-hand rotation:** Create an end-to-end RL pipeline capable of learning complex manipulation policies solely from tactile feedback and robot proprioception, without any visual input.
- **Demonstrate successful in-hand object rotation:** Validate the system's ability to rotate objects around given axes (x, y, z) in both simulation and the real world.
- **Prove generalization to unseen objects:** Show that the learned policy can effectively manipulate novel objects that were not part of the training dataset.
- **Analyze the contribution of tactile information:** Conduct extensive ablation studies to understand how different aspects of the tactile sensing contribute to robustness, generalization, and even implicit shape understanding.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide a practical and scalable solution for enabling highly dexterous, vision-agnostic robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Touch Dexterity" system proposed in this paper integrates a novel hardware design for tactile sensing with a sophisticated Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipeline, carefully engineered to minimize the Sim2Real gap and enable robust, touch-only in-hand manipulation.

#### 3.1. Real-world System Setup

The hardware setup comprises an XArm robot arm fitted with a 16-Degree-of-Freedom (DOF) Allegro Hand. The core innovation lies in the tactile sensing array: 16 low-cost Force-Sensing Resistors (FSRs), each costing around $12, are strategically attached across one side of the Allegro Hand. This includes the palm, finger links, and fingertips, ensuring broad coverage of potential contact points (as shown in Figure 1). An STM32F microcontroller collects the analog voltage signals from these FSRs, which are then binarized using a fixed threshold ($ \theta_{th} $) and transmitted as digital signals to the host controller. The crucial choice of binarizing the continuous force measurements is justified by its ability to reduce noise and, more importantly, drastically minimize the Sim2Real gap compared to trying to precisely model non-linear and noisy continuous force signals.

#### 3.2. Simulation Setup

Training is conducted in the IsaacGym simulator, a high-performance GPU-based physics engine known for its ability to run thousands of parallel environments. In simulation, each contact sensor is modeled as a fixed link on the Allegro Hand. The simulator provides the net contact force ($ \Vert F \Vert $) over each sensor link. This force is then binarized using a calibrated threshold ($ \tilde{\theta}_{th} = 0.01N $) to match the real-world sensor behavior. The simplicity of binary contact detection in simulation makes it highly congruent with the real-world binarized FSR output, which is a key factor in successful Sim2Real transfer.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Problem: In-hand Rotation

The chosen benchmark task is in-hand object rotation, where an object initially placed in the palm needs to be rotated around a specified axis (x, y, or z). This task is challenging due to the complex object motion (sliding, rolling) within the hand and the need for coordinated, large-motion finger movements to maintain grasp stability while achieving rotation. The goal is to perform this rotation on "unseen" objects, meaning both without vision and for objects not encountered during training.

#### 3.4. Learning Touch Dexterity (Reinforcement Learning)

The in-hand rotation problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and solved using Reinforcement Learning.

- **State Space (Observations):** The policy receives the Allegro hand's joint positions ($ q_t \in \mathbb{R}^{16} $), the binary tactile sensor observations ($ o_t \in \{0, 1\}^{16} $), the previous joint position target ($ \tilde{q}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{16} $), and the target rotation axis ($ k \in S^2 $). To provide temporal context, the current state is stacked with three historical states as input to the policy network (an MLP).
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs a relative control command ($ a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{16} $) for the joint positions. To ensure smooth motion, an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is applied to the actions ($ \tilde{a}_t = \eta a_t + (1-\eta) \tilde{a}_{t-1} $), which then updates the target joint position ($ \tilde{q}_{t+1} = \tilde{q}_t + \tilde{a}_t $). A PD controller then drives the hand to this new target at a control frequency of 10Hz.
- **Reward Function:** A carefully designed, composite reward function guides learning:
 - $ r_{rot} $: Rotation reward, calculated as the clipped rotated angle ($ \Delta\theta $) of a randomly sampled unit vector projected onto the normal plane of the rotation axis. This is more robust than using noisy angular velocity from the simulator.
 - $ r_{vel} $: Penalty for object velocity, encouraging stable rotation.
 - $ r_{fall} $: Large negative penalty if the object falls out of the hand.
 - $ r_{work} $: Penalty for controller work ($ -\langle \vert\tau\vert, \vert\dot{q}_t\vert \rangle $), promoting smooth finger motion.
 - $ r_{torque} $: Penalty for large torques ($ -\Vert\tau\Vert $).
 - $ r_{dist} $: Distance reward, encouraging fingertips to stay close and interact with the object.
- **Reset Strategy:** Episodes are reset if the object significantly deviates from its initial palm position or if its major axis misalignment with the rotation axis is too large, pruning unpromising explorations.
- **Training Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to train both the policy and value networks, which are multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Asymmetric Observation:** To aid training, the value network is provided with "privileged information" (e.g., ground-truth object pose, contact forces, physics parameters) that is unavailable to the policy network, improving the value estimation.

#### 3.5. Domain Randomization

Extensive domain randomization is applied during simulation training to improve Sim2Real transfer and policy robustness:
- **Physics Randomization:** Object properties (initial position, mass, shape, friction) and PD controller gains are randomized. Additionally, each tactile sensor's output is randomized (e.g., flipping active outputs with probability $p$), and signal delays are modeled.
- **Non-physics Randomization:** White noise is injected into policy observations and actions to enhance robustness to real-world perturbations.
This comprehensive randomization aims to expose the policy to a wide variety of conditions, forcing it to learn generalized behaviors rather than overfitting to specific simulation parameters.

#### 3.6. System Identification

Before real-world deployment, the PD controller coefficients are tuned to ensure that the controller's response in the real robot closely matches its behavior in simulation, further bridging the Sim2Real gap.

In essence, the methodology combines a pragmatic, low-fidelity yet high-coverage sensor design with a robust, simulation-driven reinforcement learning paradigm, focusing on generalization and effective Sim2Real transfer through careful reward shaping and extensive domain randomization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in this paper comprehensively evaluate the "Touch Dexterity" system in both simulation and real-world scenarios, addressing its effectiveness, robustness, generalization capabilities, and the critical role of tactile information.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results: Policy Learning with Different Sensing Capabilities

- **Impact of Tactile Information:** In single-object training (e.g., a cuboid), policies trained with tactile sensors (Our method) achieved higher Cumulative Rotation Reward (CRR) compared to policies without sensors (No-Sensor) or with low-sensitivity sensors (LS-Sensor). The LS-Sensor did show improved Time-to-Fall (TTF), suggesting even low sensitivity helps stability.
- **Multi-Object Training:** For multi-object training (using Object Set A), the tactile policy significantly outperformed all baselines. Both No-Sensor and LS-Sensor policies largely failed, indicating that fine-grained, high-sensitivity tactile information over broad coverage is crucial for generalizing across diverse object geometries.

#### 4.2. Simulation Results: Robustness and Generalization

- **Robustness to Physics Shifts:** When tested on unseen physics parameters (different friction and mass ranges), the full tactile policy showed minimal performance drop, demonstrating high robustness. In contrast, the No-Sensor and DS-Sensor (tactile policy with sensors disabled at evaluation) showed clear performance degradation. LS-Sensor also performed well, suggesting low sensitivity is sufficient for robustness in single-object cases.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** The tactile policy successfully generalized to unseen object sets (Object Set B, after training on Object Set A). Disabling tactile input during evaluation (DS-Sensor) led to a significant drop in performance for both seen and unseen objects, quantitatively confirming the vital role of continuous tactile feedback for generalization.

#### 4.3. Real-World Results: Dexterity without Vision

This is the most critical validation of the system. Policies trained in simulation were directly deployed on a real Allegro Hand.
- **Superior Performance:** "Our method" (binary FSRs) significantly outperformed all baselines across both seen (Object Set C1-C5) and unseen real-world objects (e.g., tomato, apple, orange, soup can, rubber duck). It achieved substantially higher Cumulative Rotation Angle (CRA) and Time-to-Fall (TTF), often rotating objects for the full 30-second trial duration without dropping them.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Openloop Policy (OL):** Achieved limited rotation (max 180 degrees) before failing, highlighting the difficulty of the task without feedback.
 - **No-Sensor:** Performed poorly, confirming that proprioception alone is insufficient for adaptive in-hand manipulation.
 - **CT-Sensor (Continuous Tactile Signals):** While better than No-Sensor, it showed poorer generalization and higher variance across objects compared to the binary system. This finding strongly supports the authors' hypothesis that binarized signals are more suitable for Sim2Real transfer due to the reduced gap in force measurement.
- **Adaptive Behavior:** Qualitative observations indicated that the tactile policy could immediately adjust finger motions when objects moved to unstable positions, a crucial adaptive behavior absent in sensor-less baselines.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis: Sensor Response and Sim2Real Alignment

- Visualization of contact signals (binary activation patterns) in both simulation and real-world experiments for cuboid rotation demonstrated a general alignment in overall patterns. While simulation patterns were slightly denser and richer, the fundamental similarities explained the successful Sim2Real transfer. The diversity of activation patterns in simulation was hypothesized to aid transfer.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study I: Importance Analysis of Sensors

- Dividing sensors into "Fingertip" and "Palm" groups, the authors trained policies disabling one group at a time. Both "No-Fingertip" and "No-Palm" policies performed similarly to the DS-Sensor (no tactile input during evaluation), achieving significantly worse results than the full tactile policy. This indicates that both fingertip and palm contact information are essential for successful in-hand rotation.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study II: A Shape Understanding Perspective

- The authors investigated whether tactile information contributes to implicit shape understanding. A temporal-CNN model trained to predict object shapes from tactile rollout trajectories achieved significantly lower Mean Squared Error (MSE) (0.22) when using touch information compared to an ablated model without touch (0.45). Qualitative reconstructions further showed superior shape inference with tactile data, suggesting that the binary tactile information indeed helps the robot to perceive and understand object geometry.

#### 4.7. Rotation Around Other Axes

- The system successfully learned rotation around x and y axes, in addition to z-axis rotation. While generally successful, performance was slightly lower for some objects, which the authors attributed to the current sensor array layout potentially lacking sufficient coverage for critical contacts on the side of finger links required for these rotations. The learned primitives were demonstrated to enable human-robot shared control for high-level object reorientation commands.

In summary, the key findings unequivocally demonstrate that a low-cost, dense array of binary tactile sensors, coupled with an RL framework and extensive domain randomization, enables unprecedented touch-only dexterity in robot hands. This approach offers superior robustness, generalization, and practical Sim2Real transfer, addressing longstanding challenges in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Touch Dexterity" research by Yin et al. represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, with profound implications and potential impacts on future robot capabilities.

#### 5.1. Pioneering Touch-Only Dexterity for Diverse Objects

This work stands out as one of the first to successfully demonstrate truly human-like, touch-only in-hand dexterous manipulation of diverse, *unseen* objects on a real robot. While previous works have explored tactile sensing or proprioception for limited tasks or object sets, "Touch Dexterity" showcases a level of adaptability and generalization that was previously unattainable without vision. This achievement fundamentally shifts the paradigm of what is possible for robots operating in vision-constrained or vision-denied environments.

#### 5.2. Novel and Practical Tactile Sensing Paradigm

The paper introduces a highly practical and impactful approach to tactile sensing. Instead of pursuing ever-more-complex and expensive high-resolution sensors that often suffer from a large Sim2Real gap, the authors advocate for a dense array of low-cost, binary force sensors. This design choice is critical:
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** Using inexpensive FSRs ($12 each) makes the system much more accessible and scalable, reducing the barrier to entry for widespread adoption in robotics research and applications.
- **Sim2Real Bridge:** The binarization of sensor signals dramatically simplifies their simulation, making accurate real-world transfer a reality. This elegant solution sidesteps one of the most persistent challenges in tactile robotics, unlocking the full potential of simulation-based reinforcement learning for tactile tasks.
- **Whole-Hand Coverage:** The strategic placement of sensors across the palm, finger links, and fingertips provides a comprehensive "touch map" of the object, which is essential for complex multi-finger manipulation where objects interact with various parts of the hand.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Robustness and Generalization

The successful generalization to unseen objects and robustness to variations in physics parameters are hallmarks of an intelligent and adaptable manipulation system. This is crucial for robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments where they encounter novel items. The findings suggest that the tactile information, even in its binary form, provides sufficient cues for the learned policy to infer object properties and adapt its manipulation strategy, essentially allowing the robot to "feel" the object's shape and interaction dynamics. The implicit shape understanding demonstrated in the ablation study further supports this.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Future Robotic Dexterity

The learned rotation primitives around different axes offer a versatile toolkit for more complex manipulation tasks. This work lays the groundwork for:
- **Advanced Reorientation:** By combining these primitives, robots can achieve more sophisticated object reorientation, enabling tasks like assembly, grasping unknown tools, or fine-tuning object placement.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** The ability to accept high-level commands for rotation axes, as demonstrated with shared control, opens possibilities for more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration.
- **Exploration of Denser Arrays:** While effective, the authors acknowledge that denser sensor arrays could further enhance performance, particularly for rotations around x and y axes involving more intricate finger-link contacts. This points to clear avenues for future research building upon this foundation.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact on Robotics Applications

The ability to perform dexterous manipulation without relying on vision has immense practical implications across various sectors:
- **Industrial Automation:** Handling delicate or irregularly shaped objects, even in low-light conditions or with occluded workspaces.
- **Service Robotics:** Assisting with household chores, personal care, or warehouse operations where objects are diverse and lighting conditions vary.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Operating in environments where visual information is limited (e.g., underwater, smoke-filled areas, or extraterrestrial exploration), enhancing robot autonomy and capability in hazardous conditions.
- **Medical Robotics:** Manipulating surgical instruments or tissue with tactile feedback, improving precision and safety.

In conclusion, "Rotating without Seeing: Towards In-hand Dexterity through Touch" provides a compelling demonstration of the power of a cleverly designed tactile sensing system combined with modern reinforcement learning. It offers a practical, scalable, and robust solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics, paving the way for a new generation of truly "dexterous" and perceptually rich robot hands.
