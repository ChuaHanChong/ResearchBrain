# Spatial RoboGrasp: Generalized Robotic Grasping Control Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ZhiCheng AI, Stanford University, and Peking University developed Spatial RoboGrasp, a framework that integrates multimodal perception, domain-randomized augmentation, and 6-DoF grasp affordances with a diffusion-based policy for robotic grasping. This system achieved up to 40% improvement in grasp success and 45% higher task success rates under environmental variation compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Current imitation learning models, particularly diffusion-based policies, rely heavily on raw RGB inputs, leading to poor spatial reasoning, overfitting, and fragility to real-world environmental variations like lighting, occlusion, and novel object geometries.
- Existing robotic datasets often lack perceptual diversity, causing models to perform poorly when deployed outside controlled lab settings.
- Achieving precise and generalizable robotic grasping is challenged by the high cost and complexity of traditional 3D sensing hardware, while simpler point-based affordance methods lack sufficient detail.

## Method

- The **Spatial RoboGrasp** framework was developed, integrating four core modules: AugFusion for domain-randomized RGB augmentation, a ==Monocular Depth Estimation== module (Depth Anything V2) for geometric context, a Grasp Prediction module (YOLO-based) generating 6-DoF grasp poses, and a diffusion-based Robotic Action Head.
- AugFusion enhances visual robustness by exposing the policy to diverse in-distribution and out-of-distribution corruptions during training, encouraging invariant feature learning.
- A custom YOLO-based module predicts 2D grasp boxes, which are then converted to full 6-DoF grasp poses using monocular depth, providing explicit spatial guidance to the diffusion policy without relying on expensive 3D sensors.

## Results

- Spatial RoboGrasp consistently outperformed all baselines, achieving an average of 15–**30%** improvement in both Task Success Rate (TSR) and Grasp Success Rate (GSR) across various tasks under different environmental conditions.
- The model demonstrated exceptional resilience to extreme lighting variations, maintaining high success rates (e.g., **82%** TSR for PickCup) while baseline Diffusion Policy models experienced drastic performance declines (e.g., **27%** TSR for PickCup).
- It exhibited enhanced generalization to diverse and novel objects with few demonstrations, and effectively followed explicit task prompts to disambiguate targets in cluttered scenes, demonstrating learning of various grasping strategies (e.g., handle, wall, diameter grasps for cups).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating multimodal inputs (RGB and monocular depth) with domain-randomized augmentations provides invariant spatial representations, significantly enhancing robustness to extreme environmental variations (e.g., lighting changes).
> - Explicit 6-DoF grasp affordances, derived from a lightweight grasp prediction module, provide crucial spatial grounding that improves few-shot generalization to novel objects and enables goal-directed grasping in cluttered scenes.
> - A diffusion-based policy, conditioned on these rich, spatially grounded observations, can effectively learn and generate diverse, precise manipulation actions, outperforming purely vision-based or less-informed methods.
