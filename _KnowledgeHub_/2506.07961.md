# BridgeVLA: Input-Output Alignment for Efficient 3D Manipulation Learning with Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> BridgeVLA introduces a 3D Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that enhances robot manipulation efficiency by aligning 3D inputs and actions within a 2D image space, achieving a 95.4% success rate with only 3 trajectories per task in real-robot experiments. The approach leverages orthographic projections for input and 2D heatmap predictions for actions, pre-training a VLM backbone for spatial grounding.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while effective at generalizable understanding, often require vast datasets and struggle with intrinsic 3D spatial reasoning for precise robot manipulation.
- Dedicated 3D manipulation policies are sample-efficient but typically lack broad language conditioning and semantic understanding derived from large pre-trained VLMs.
- Previous attempts to integrate 3D information into 2D VLMs for action prediction faced input distributional shift and lost spatial structural priors by converting actions to token sequences, leading to poor data efficiency.

## Method

- Projects raw 3D point cloud inputs into multiple 2D ==orthographic projection== images, allowing the pre-trained 2D VLM backbone (PaliGemma) to process inputs in its native 2D format.
- Introduces a 2D-heatmap pre-training phase for the VLM backbone, teaching it to ground target objects and output spatially aware 2D heatmaps conditioned on text.
- Predicts 2D heatmaps for translational actions, which are then back-projected to estimate 3D target points, while other actions (rotation, gripper, collision) are predicted using an MLP from global and local features.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **88.2%** on 18 diverse RLBench tasks, outperforming the state-of-the-art RVT-2 (**81.4%**), with notable improvements in high-precision tasks like "Insert Peg" (**88.0%** vs. **40.0%**).
- Demonstrated exceptional real-robot data efficiency, achieving a **95.4%** success rate across 10+ tasks with only 3 trajectories per task, significantly surpassing other VLA methods.
- Exhibited superior generalization to visual disturbances (**64.0%** average success on COLOSSEUM vs. RVT-2's **56.7%**) and novel object-skill combinations on GemBench, establishing new state-of-the-art results for L2 and L3 generalization levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing distributional shift by aligning 3D inputs to the pre-trained 2D VLM's native format is crucial for effectively leveraging its broad visual-language knowledge.
> - Employing 2D heatmaps as an intermediate output for action prediction provides denser spatial supervision and incorporates structural priors, leading to more efficient and stable learning than direct 3D regression or token-based methods.
> - A dedicated pre-training stage for 2D heatmap prediction empowers the VLM backbone with the necessary spatial grounding capability to bridge language semantics with precise 3D manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen a rapid convergence with large pre-trained models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), aiming to imbue robots with generalizable skills and robust understanding from diverse data. BridgeVLA positions itself at the forefront of this trend, specifically addressing critical limitations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for 3D robot manipulation.

**Current Landscape & Challenges:**

1. **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Most VLA models, exemplified by works like RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0, primarily leverage 2D image inputs and general-purpose VLMs. While highly effective at learning language-conditioned policies and transferring web knowledge, they typically require vast amounts of data for training and often struggle with the inherent 3D spatial reasoning crucial for precise robot manipulation. Their outputs are often token sequences representing actions, lacking the explicit spatial structure beneficial for 3D tasks.
2. **3D Robot Manipulation Policies:** A parallel line of research focuses on 3D policies, which inherently leverage the spatial structure of 3D data (e.g., point clouds or voxels) for efficient learning. Methods like PerAct, Act3D, RVT, and RVT-2 demonstrate superior sample efficiency in complex 3D tasks by operating directly within or projecting to a 3D observation space. However, many of these are "non-VLA" models, often lacking direct language conditioning or the broad semantic understanding derived from large-scale VLM pre-training. Integrating language often requires ad-hoc mechanisms, rather than leveraging a unified VLM backbone.
3. **Emerging 3D VLA Models:** Recent efforts have begun to explore combining VLMs with 3D information (e.g., 3D-VLA, Lift3D, FP3, PointVLA, SpatialVLA). However, these approaches often face two key challenges:
 - **Inefficient Action Representation:** Many convert actions into token sequences for next-token prediction, which fails to exploit the spatial structural priors that make 3D policies sample-efficient. This leads to poor data efficiency compared to dedicated 3D policies.
 - **Input Misalignment:** Pre-trained VLMs are typically trained on 2D images. Integrating 3D inputs directly (e.g., injecting 3D positional encodings) can cause a significant distributional shift from the original VLM pre-training, hindering the VLM's ability to effectively leverage its broad knowledge.

**BridgeVLA's Contribution to the Landscape:**

BridgeVLA directly tackles these challenges by proposing a novel paradigm that seeks to unify the "effectiveness of VLA models with the efficiency of 3D policies." It achieves this by focusing on **input-output alignment within a unified 2D image space**:

- **Solving Input Misalignment:** Instead of injecting 3D information into a 2D VLM backbone directly, BridgeVLA projects raw 3D point cloud inputs into multiple 2D orthographic projection images. This allows the pre-trained 2D VLM backbone (PaliGemma) to process inputs in its native 2D format, minimizing distributional shift and fully leveraging its rich visual-language understanding.
- **Leveraging Spatial Priors in Output:** Unlike other 3D VLA methods that predict token sequences, BridgeVLA predicts 2D heatmaps for translational actions. These heatmaps, aligned with the 2D input images, explicitly capture spatial structure. This approach is reminiscent of successful 3D policies like RVT/RVT-2 and PerAct, which unify observation and action spaces.
- **Equipping VLM with Heatmap Prediction:** Recognizing that standard VLMs don't predict heatmaps, BridgeVLA introduces a novel and scalable 2D heatmap pre-training phase. This pre-training explicitly teaches the VLM backbone to ground objects and output spatially aware 2D heatmaps conditioned on text, bridging the gap between its original token-prediction capability and the downstream heatmap-based action prediction.

By meticulously aligning both the input to the VLM backbone and the action output space with a 2D image-like representation, BridgeVLA carves out a unique and highly effective niche, demonstrating superior sample efficiency and generalization in 3D manipulation tasks while fully harnessing the power of pre-trained VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of BridgeVLA is to develop a **unified 3D Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of learning robot manipulation both efficiently and effectively.** This overarching goal is motivated by several key challenges in the current state of robotic manipulation learning:

1. **Bridging 2D VLA Effectiveness and 3D Policy Efficiency:** Existing VLA models, while powerful for generalizable understanding from language and diverse 2D visual data, typically require vast datasets and often lack the intrinsic 3D spatial reasoning for precise manipulation. Conversely, dedicated 3D manipulation policies are highly sample-efficient due to their structural priors but often lack the broad language understanding and generalization capabilities of VLAs. The key motivation is to combine the best aspects of both worlds.
2. **Addressing Data Inefficiency in Current 3D VLAs:** Previous attempts to integrate 3D signals into VLMs for action prediction often convert actions into token sequences, which loses the spatial structure inherent in 3D data. This token-based prediction strategy for actions leads to poor sample efficiency, failing to capitalize on the strengths of efficient 3D policies that align observation and action in a unified spatial space. BridgeVLA aims to overcome this by reintroducing spatial structure to the action output.
3. **Overcoming Input Misalignment between 2D VLMs and 3D Tasks:** Pre-trained VLMs are typically trained on 2D images. When fine-tuning these models for 3D manipulation, directly feeding 3D inputs (e.g., point clouds or depth information integrated into 2D features) can cause a significant "distributional shift" from the VLM's original pre-training. This misalignment can degrade the VLM's performance and its ability to transfer broad knowledge effectively. BridgeVLA seeks a method that keeps the VLM backbone operating in its native 2D input space during fine-tuning.
4. **Equipping VLMs with Spatially Aware Output Capabilities:** Original VLMs are often designed for tasks like image captioning or visual question answering, where the output is typically a sequence of text tokens. For robotic manipulation, especially 3D tasks, a spatially aware output (e.g., a 2D heatmap indicating target locations) is far more beneficial for precise control. A key motivation is to pre-train the VLM backbone to produce such spatially structured outputs, making it suitable for downstream policy learning.
5. **Achieving Robust Generalization in Real-World Scenarios:** Ultimately, the motivation is to build a robot manipulation system that can generalize robustly to various unseen conditions, including visual disturbances (distractors, lighting, background changes), novel object configurations, and new combinations of objects and skills, with minimal real-world data collection.

In essence, BridgeVLA is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of large, pre-trained VLMs for 3D robot manipulation by intelligently redesigning the input and output interfaces to leverage both the VLM's semantic power and 3D data's spatial efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BridgeVLA employs a novel, two-phase training methodology designed to align 3D manipulation tasks with the 2D input-output structure of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), ensuring both efficiency and effectiveness.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

BridgeVLA learns a multi-task 3D robot manipulation policy $\pi: (o, l) \rightarrow a$, mapping observation ($o$) and language instruction ($l$) to an action ($a$). The action consists of a 6-DoF end-effector pose, a gripper state (open/close), and a collision flag. It operates iteratively, predicting the next keyframe action and then executing it using a motion planner. The model uses the PaliGemma VLM as its backbone, which comprises a SigLIP vision encoder and a Gemma transformer backbone.

#### 3.2. 2D-Heatmap Pre-training

The core VLM backbone (PaliGemma) is originally pre-trained to produce token sequences, not spatially structured heatmaps. To equip it with the necessary heatmap prediction capability for downstream policy learning, BridgeVLA introduces a dedicated pre-training stage:

- **Objective:** Train the VLM backbone to ground target objects by predicting 2D heatmaps conditioned on text inputs.
- **Dataset:** The 120K object detection split of RoboPoint.
- **Ground-Truth Heatmap Construction:** For each image and a text prompt describing target objects, ground-truth heatmaps ($H_{gt}$) are created. For each object's bounding box, a Gaussian probability map is generated, centered at the bounding box's centroid (Eq. 2). These individual maps are then averaged and normalized to form a single $H_{gt}$ for all objects of interest (Eq. 3). This provides dense, spatially-aware supervision.
- **Model Architecture & Training:**
 - An image and language instruction (e.g., "Find all instances of frock") are fed into the VLM backbone.
 - The SigLIP vision encoder and language token embeddings of the VLM are frozen during this pre-training.
 - Output image tokens from the VLM are rearranged according to their patch positions to reconstruct a spatial feature grid.
 - A **convex upsampling block** converts this feature grid into a 2D heatmap with the same resolution as the input image. This module learns pixel-wise interpolation weights, allowing for fine spatial detail recovery, unlike fixed upsampling methods.
 - The model is trained with a cross-entropy loss to predict heatmaps that localize objects of interest.
- **Key Advantage:** This pre-training outputs a spatially aware 2D heatmap, a critical departure from conventional next-token prediction, and is highly scalable as it can leverage various vision-language datasets convertible to heatmap prediction tasks (e.g., keypoint detection, semantic segmentation).

#### 3.3. 3D Action Fine-tuning

After the 2D-heatmap pre-training, the model is fine-tuned for 3D robot manipulation:

- **Input Alignment for 3D Data:**
 - From the robot's RGB-D cameras, a 3D point cloud of the scene is reconstructed.
 - To align with the VLM backbone's 2D image input requirement, three orthographic projection images (from top, front, and right viewpoints) are rendered from the point cloud, similar to RVT and RVT-2.
 - These three 2D projection images, along with the task instruction, are fed into the pre-trained VLM backbone.
 - Crucially, no additional information like robot states or per-pixel 3D position inputs are incorporated during the VLM forward pass to minimize distributional shift from pre-training.
- **Output Alignment and Action Prediction:**
 - The VLM backbone, now capable of heatmap prediction, generates a 2D heatmap for each of the three views.
 - **Translational Actions:** These 2D heatmaps are back-projected to estimate scores for 3D points in a uniformly distributed grid across the robot workspace. The 3D point with the highest score determines the end-effector's translational target in the next keyframe.
 - **Rotational, Gripper, and Collision Actions:** For these actions, features are integrated from global and local contexts. Global features are obtained by max-pooling output tokens from each orthographic projection image. Local features are extracted from the heatmap peak of each view. All these tokens are concatenated and passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict rotational actions (discretized Euler angles via cross-entropy loss), binary gripper actions, and a collision avoidance flag (binary cross-entropy losses).
- **Coarse-to-Fine Refinement:** Following prior work, BridgeVLA uses a coarse-to-fine strategy. An initial action prediction is made on the full point cloud. Then, the point cloud is cropped and zoomed in around the predicted translation, and a second forward pass refines the action prediction.
- **Training Loss:** The total loss is a sum of cross-entropy losses for translation ($L_{trans}$) and rotation ($L_{rot}$), and binary cross-entropy losses for gripper ($L_{gripper}$) and collision ($L_{collision}$) prediction (Eq. 4).
- **Geometric Robustness:** Random rigid-body transformations are applied to the point cloud and ground-truth action during training.

This meticulous design, particularly the input projection and heatmap-based output, enables BridgeVLA to leverage the semantic reasoning of VLMs while maintaining the spatial efficiency critical for 3D robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BridgeVLA demonstrates strong performance across extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **RLBench Benchmark (Q1):**
 - **Effectiveness:** BridgeVLA achieves an average success rate of 88.2% across 18 diverse tasks, significantly surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (RVT-2 at 81.4%). Its average rank is 2.03, the best among all compared methods.
 - **Precision:** Notably, BridgeVLA excels in tasks requiring high precision, such as "Insert Peg" (88.0% vs. RVT-2's 40.0%) and "Sort Shape" (60.8% vs. RVT-2's 35.0%), indicating its strong capability for accurate manipulation.
 - **Limitations:** Performance in "Place Cups" was the lowest for BridgeVLA, hypothesized to be due to frequent occlusion of target keypoints in orthographic projection views.
- **COLOSSEUM Benchmark (Q3):**
 - **Robustness to Visual Disturbances:** BridgeVLA shows superior generalization to unseen perturbations, improving the average success rate from 56.7% (RVT-2) to 64.0% across 14 challenging generalization settings (object texture/color/size changes, backgrounds, lighting, distractors, camera poses).
 - **Consistent Outperformance:** BridgeVLA ranked best in 13 out of 14 evaluated perturbation types, demonstrating strong robustness against visual variations.
- **GemBench Benchmark (Q4):**
 - **Hierarchical Generalization:** BridgeVLA establishes a new state of the art with an average success rate of 50.0% across four hierarchical generalization levels (L1: Novel Placements, L2: Novel Rigid Objects, L3: Novel Articulated Objects, L4: Novel Long-Horizon Tasks).
 - **Object & Skill Generalization:** It achieves state-of-the-art results specifically in L2 (novel rigid objects) and L3 (novel articulated objects), confirming its strong ability to generalize to novel object-skill combinations and objects from unseen categories.
 - **Long-Horizon Limitations:** Similar to most baselines, BridgeVLA shows limited performance in the L4 setting (long-horizon tasks), suggesting a need for more advanced task decomposition strategies (e.g., using LLMs).

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

- **Overall Performance & Data Efficiency (Q2):**
 - BridgeVLA outperforms a state-of-the-art baseline (RVT-2) by an average of 32% across seven diverse real-world settings on 13 tasks.
 - **Remarkable Sample Efficiency:** BridgeVLA achieves a 95.4% success rate across 10+ tasks with **only 3 trajectories per task** for training. This significantly surpasses other VLA methods like π0, which fail completely in this low-data regime, and even SpatialVLA, which performs poorly even with 50 trajectories.
- **Robustness and Generalization (Q3, Q4):**
 - **Visual Disturbances:** BridgeVLA demonstrates strong robustness against visual disturbances (Distractor, Lighting, Background, Height settings), outperforming RVT-2 significantly in these challenging environments.
 - **Unseen Instructions (Combination, Category):** It generalizes robustly to novel object-skill combinations (Combination setting) and objects from previously unseen categories (Category setting). BridgeVLA's performance in "Combination" is particularly strong, highlighting its ability to understand language semantics and apply learned skills creatively.
 - **Category Performance Nuance:** While outperforming baselines in the "Category" setting, the absolute success rate for novel objects was lower. This is attributed to: 1) distributional shift between third-person pre-training images and robot projection images, and 2) the difference between object localization (pre-training) and keypoint prediction for manipulation (fine-tuning). Ablation studies confirm the importance of 2D heatmap pre-training for language-related generalization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Q5)

- **Necessity of Heatmap Prediction:** Directly regressing 3D positions (without intermediate heatmaps) drastically reduced performance on RLBench (from 88.2% to 31.4%). This validates that heatmaps provide denser supervision, incorporate helpful spatial priors, and maintain alignment with input images, making learning more effective and stable.
- **Impact of 3D Position Input to VLM:** Incorporating per-pixel 3D position inputs into the VLM backbone (as done in some 3D VLAs) lowered RLBench performance from 88.2% to 56.2%. This confirms that maintaining input alignment with the VLM's original 2D pre-training distribution is crucial for leveraging its broad knowledge.
- **Value of 2D Heatmap Pre-training:** Ablating the 2D heatmap pre-training significantly impaired BridgeVLA's generalization to language-related settings in real-robot experiments, with performance falling below RVT-2. This highlights that this specific pre-training step is essential for connecting language semantics with image observations in a spatially aware manner suitable for manipulation.

In summary, BridgeVLA sets a new benchmark for efficient and effective 3D robot manipulation by intelligently aligning inputs and outputs within a 2D image space, demonstrating exceptional data efficiency, robust generalization, and precision, particularly in low-data real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BridgeVLA represents a significant advancement in the field of robot manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between the rich semantic understanding of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the demanding spatial precision and sample efficiency required for 3D robotic tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **High Sample Efficiency for 3D Manipulation:** The most striking impact of BridgeVLA is its demonstrated ability to achieve high success rates with extremely limited real-world data (e.g., 95.4% success with only 3 trajectories per task). This is a critical breakthrough for real-world robotics, where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and often dangerous. It significantly lowers the barrier to entry for deploying VLA models in practical applications.
2. **Robust Generalization to Unseen Conditions:** BridgeVLA's superior performance on benchmarks like COLOSSEUM and GemBench, and its robust generalization in real-robot experiments against visual disturbances and novel instructions, indicate a high degree of adaptability. This means robots powered by BridgeVLA can operate reliably in varied, unstructured environments, handling changes in lighting, background, object appearances, and even unexpected distractors, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
3. **Principled Integration of 2D VLMs with 3D Robotics:** The paper offers a novel and effective paradigm for leveraging off-the-shelf, large-scale 2D pre-trained VLMs for 3D tasks. By projecting 3D inputs to 2D multi-view images and having the VLM predict 2D heatmaps (after specialized pre-training), BridgeVLA minimizes the "distributional shift" that often plagues attempts to adapt 2D models to 3D data. This principled alignment strategy could become a standard for integrating powerful 2D foundation models into 3D embodied AI.
4. **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning through Heatmap Prediction:** Moving beyond token-based action prediction, BridgeVLA's heatmap-based output strategy for translational actions explicitly reintroduces spatial structural priors. This is a powerful mechanism for achieving fine-grained precision, as evidenced by its strong performance in tasks requiring precise alignment. The proposed 2D heatmap pre-training for VLMs is a novel component that empowers them with spatial grounding capabilities previously missing.
5. **Accelerating Robot Learning Research and Deployment:** By offering an efficient and effective framework, BridgeVLA can accelerate research into more complex manipulation tasks and real-world robot deployment. Researchers can focus more on higher-level reasoning or long-horizon tasks, knowing that the underlying low-level control can be learned efficiently. For industry, the reduced data requirements translate directly into faster development cycles and lower costs for automation solutions.

**Future Work and Directions:**
The paper outlines promising future directions that could further enhance BridgeVLA's capabilities:
- **Diverse Pre-training Tasks:** Expanding pre-training to include tasks like semantic segmentation and keypoint detection could imbue the VLM with even richer spatial understanding relevant to manipulation.
- **More Expressive Action Decoding:** Integrating advanced action decoding methods, such as diffusion policies, could lead to more nuanced and robust action generation, potentially addressing limitations in complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Dynamic View Selection:** Addressing issues like target occlusion by dynamically selecting projection views could further improve reliability in cluttered scenes.
- **LLMs for Long-Horizon Tasks:** Leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for hierarchical task decomposition remains a crucial area for improving performance in multi-stage, complex tasks (as seen in GemBench L4).

In conclusion, BridgeVLA represents a significant step towards general-purpose robot manipulation. Its focus on input-output alignment, combined with a specialized VLM pre-training strategy, offers a blueprint for building intelligent robotic agents that are not only effective but also remarkably data-efficient and robust in complex, real-world 3D environments.
