# Dream-MPC: Gradient-Based Model Predictive Control with Latent Imagination

> [!summary] Summary
> Dream-MPC is a gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach that leverages learned policy networks and differentiable world models to improve performance and computational efficiency in continuous control tasks. The method, integrating policy-guided warm-starting, uncertainty regularization, and action reuse, demonstrates competitive or superior performance to gradient-free MPC and other state-of-the-art baselines across 24 diverse control problems.

## Problem

- Gradient-free, sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods are computationally expensive for high-dimensional control and real-time applications.
- Prior gradient-based MPC methods often performed worse than gradient-free counterparts, attributed to gradient stability issues that were not thoroughly analyzed.
- Fully amortized reactive policies can exhibit limited generalization capabilities when deployed online without explicit planning.
- Existing approaches did not fully exploit the differentiability of learned world models for efficient trajectory optimization.

## Method

- Generates a small number of candidate action trajectories by rolling out a ==stochastic policy network== in the latent space for warm-starting optimization.
- Refines candidate trajectories using gradient ascent on an MPC objective function that includes a novel ==uncertainty regularization== term.
- Amortizes optimization iterations by reusing previously optimized actions from prior time steps to enhance computational efficiency.
- Integrates with existing model-based RL frameworks (**TD-MPC2**, **BMPC**, **Dreamer**) by replacing their planners or enhancing their training/inference procedures.

## Results

- Dream-MPC (BMPC) improved the interquartile median (IQM) by **26.7%** and the mean normalized score by **20.5%** compared to BMPC's MPPI planner.
- Achieved significantly reduced world model evaluations per time step (75 evaluations) when integrated into Dreamer, a notable efficiency gain over methods requiring thousands of evaluations.
- Demonstrated competitive or superior performance across 24 continuous control tasks, including those with visual observations, and exhibited more stable planner gradients than previous gradient-based MPC methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of policy prior, uncertainty regularization, and action reuse for performance and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A high-quality policy prior for warm-starting is crucial for the efficient and effective convergence of gradient-based MPC in learned latent spaces.
> - Uncertainty regularization within the MPC objective mitigates extrapolation errors and value overestimation, enhancing the robustness of planning.
> - Amortizing optimization through action reuse significantly improves computational efficiency and overall performance, especially during training.
> - Gradient-based planning, when properly formulated with stability mechanisms, can outperform or match gradient-free methods and lead to more stable planner gradients.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Reinforcement Learning (RL) has seen significant advancements, particularly in robotics and autonomous systems. Within RL, two main paradigms exist: model-free and model-based methods. Model-free RL, while successful in various domains, often encounters challenges related to sample efficiency and generalization, especially in complex, high-dimensional environments. In contrast, model-based RL (MBRL) offers potential advantages in sample efficiency and generalization, provided an accurate model of the environment can be learned.

Recent advancements in MBRL have focused on learning "world models" from data, which are then utilized for decision-making through planning. A prevalent approach involves integrating learned world models with Model Predictive Control (MPC) to perform planning via imaginary rollouts. These state-of-the-art hybrid methods, such as those in the Dreamer family or TD-MPC, typically rely on gradient-free, sampling-based optimization techniques, including the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) or Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), for trajectory optimization. While these gradient-free methods can yield strong empirical results and be parallelized on GPUs, they are often computationally intensive. Their efficiency significantly degrades in high-dimensional action spaces, where the search space for sampling-based methods grows exponentially, posing challenges for real-time applications and embedded systems with limited computational resources.

An alternative direction involves gradient-based planning, which backpropagates gradients through a differentiable world model to iteratively optimize action sequences. Gradient-based methods generally promise greater computational efficiency, as their computational load increases minimally with action dimension, unlike sampling-based approaches. However, prior research has empirically indicated that gradient-based planning methods often perform worse than their gradient-free counterparts, with the underlying reasons for this performance discrepancy not being thoroughly investigated. Some works have attempted to combine policy networks with gradient-based MPC, but these have often been limited to simpler tasks or shown suboptimal performance.

This work, Dream-MPC, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the observed limitations of previous gradient-based MPC methods. It aims to develop a gradient-based MPC framework that not only matches but surpasses the performance of gradient-free equivalents and state-of-the-art baselines, while simultaneously improving computational efficiency. By introducing novel components like uncertainty regularization and action reuse, Dream-MPC seeks to overcome the empirical shortcomings that have historically hindered gradient-based planning in MBRL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate Dream-MPC, a novel gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach that leverages learned world models and policy networks to achieve high-performance and computationally efficient continuous control. The underlying motivation stems from several critical observations and limitations within existing model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms:

1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency of Gradient-Free MPC:** Current state-of-the-art model-based RL methods often employ gradient-free, sampling-based MPC for trajectory optimization (e.g., CEM, MPPI). These methods, while effective, are computationally expensive. They require sampling and evaluating hundreds or thousands of action sequences at each time step, leading to high computational loads that hinder their applicability in real-time robotics and resource-constrained embedded systems. The authors are motivated by the inherent efficiency benefits of gradient-based optimization, where gradients can directly guide the search for optimal actions, offering a more directed and potentially faster optimization process, especially as action space dimensionality increases.

2. **Overcoming Empirical Performance Gaps of Prior Gradient-Based Methods:** Despite the theoretical advantages of gradient-based optimization, previous attempts to integrate it with learned world models have empirically demonstrated inferior performance compared to gradient-free methods. The reasons for this consistent underperformance have not been systematically analyzed in detail in prior work. A key motivation for Dream-MPC is to identify and address these specific limitations, such as issues with gradient quality or susceptibility to local optima, thereby enabling gradient-based MPC to realize its full potential.

3. **Leveraging Policy Networks for Improved Optimization Warm-Starts:** While fully amortized methods like Dreamer learn reactive policies for faster inference, they can sometimes lack generalization capabilities. Hybrid approaches combine policy networks with online planning. Dream-MPC is motivated to utilize a learned policy network not as a fixed controller, but as a "warm-start" mechanism to generate initial, high-quality candidate trajectories. This guides the subsequent gradient-based optimization, mitigating the susceptibility of gradient ascent to getting trapped in poor local optima.

4. **Enhancing Robustness through Uncertainty Awareness:** Learned world models, especially when queried on unobserved state-action pairs during planning, can lead to extrapolation errors and value overestimation. This motivates the inclusion of uncertainty awareness directly into the MPC objective. By penalizing trajectories associated with high model uncertainty, the method aims to guide planning towards more reliable regions of the learned model, improving the robustness and safety of the decision-making process.

5. **Improving Computational Efficiency via Optimization Amortization:** To further enhance computational efficiency beyond just using gradients, the authors are motivated to amortize optimization efforts over time. By reusing previously optimized action sequences to initialize planning for the next time step, the method aims to reduce the number of required optimization iterations, making the planning process more agile and suitable for high-frequency control.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Propose Dream-MPC, a novel gradient-based MPC method that achieves state-of-the-art performance on continuous control tasks.
- Demonstrate that Dream-MPC can outperform gradient-free MPC methods while improving computational efficiency.
- Overcome previous limitations of gradient-based MPC by introducing uncertainty regularization and action reuse.
- Evaluate the method extensively across various environments, observation types, and base RL algorithms to establish its broad applicability and effectiveness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dream-MPC proposes a gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach that iteratively optimizes action sequences using a learned world model and a policy network. The core idea is to generate a small number of candidate trajectories from a policy prior and refine them via gradient ascent, rather than relying on extensive sampling.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture and Flow (Figure 2, Algorithm 1)

The method operates in a receding horizon fashion. At each time step $t$, the current observation $s_t$ is first encoded into a latent representation $z_t$ using an encoder $h(s_t)$. This latent state then serves as the starting point for MPC planning.

#### 3.2. Candidate Trajectory Generation

Instead of sampling a large population of random action sequences, Dream-MPC generates a small number ($N$) of initial candidate action sequences. This is achieved by performing imaginary rollouts of a stochastic policy network $\pi_\theta$ in the latent space $z$, using a learned latent dynamics model $d$.
- For each candidate $n$, actions $\hat{a}_{\tau}^{(n)}$ are sampled from $\pi_\theta$ given the latent state $z_{\tau}^{(n)}$, and the next latent state $z_{\tau+1}^{(n)}$ is predicted by $d(z_{\tau}^{(n)}, \hat{a}_{\tau}^{(n)})$ for $\tau = t..., t+H$, where $H$ is the planning horizon.
- If the policy is deterministic, small perturbations are added to the initial action sequence to create diverse candidates.
The policy network serves as a "warm-start," providing a reasonable initial solution for the optimization, which is empirically shown to be critical for the performance of gradient-based methods.

#### 3.3. Action Reuse for Amortized Optimization

To enhance computational efficiency and provide a better initialization for subsequent time steps, Dream-MPC incorporates action reuse. The action sequence for the current planning step $a_{t:t+H}^{(n)}$ is initialized as a weighted mixture of the newly rolled-out policy actions $\hat{a}_{\tau}^{(n)}$ and the corresponding actions $\tilde{a}_{\tau-1}^{(n)}$ that were optimized in the previous time step:
- $a_{\tau}^{(n)} = \rho \cdot \tilde{a}_{\tau-1}^{(n)} + (1 - \rho) \cdot \hat{a}_{\tau}^{(n)}$, where $\rho$ is the reuse coefficient.
- For the last action at $t+H$, where no previously planned action exists for $t+H-1$, it is initialized with the planned action from the previous time step's last planning step. This amortizes the optimization iterations over time.

#### 3.4. Gradient Ascent Optimization with Uncertainty Regularization

Each of the $N$ candidate trajectories undergoes $I$ iterations of gradient ascent to maximize an MPC objective $J$.
- **World Model Predictions:** During the imaginary rollout for each candidate, the learned world model predicts future latent states, rewards $\hat{r}$, and terminal values $\hat{q}$ for each state-action pair.
- **Uncertainty Estimation:** To account for potential extrapolation errors and value overestimation from the world model, Dream-MPC estimates epistemic uncertainty $u_t$. This is calculated as the product of the average and standard deviation of predictions from an ensemble of $M$ Q-functions: $u_t = \text{avg}([\hat{q}_1..., \hat{q}_M]) \cdot \text{std}([\hat{q}_1..., \hat{q}_M])$. The regularization strength is scaled by the magnitude of mean value predictions.
- **MPC Objective ($J$):** The objective function for optimization combines the discounted predicted rewards, the terminal value at the horizon, and an uncertainty regularization term:
 $J = \sum_{h=t}^{H-1} (\gamma^h \cdot R(z_h, a_h) - \lambda_{unc} \cdot u_h) + \gamma^H \cdot Q(z_{t+H}, a_{t+H}) - \lambda_{unc} \cdot u_{t+H}$.
 Here, $\lambda_{unc}$ is a task-agnostic coefficient balancing return maximization and uncertainty minimization.
- **Action Optimization:** Gradients of the objective $J$ with respect to the action sequence $a_{t:t+H}^{(n)}$ are backpropagated through the differentiable world model components. The action sequence is then updated via gradient ascent: $a_{t:t+H}^{(n)} \leftarrow a_{t:t+H}^{(n)} + \alpha \nabla_a J^{(n)}$, where $\alpha$ is the optimization step size.

#### 3.5. Action Selection

After $I$ optimization iterations for all $N$ candidates, the first action $a_t^{(k)}$ from the candidate trajectory $k$ that achieved the highest objective value $J^{(k)}$ is selected and applied to the environment. The entire planning process is then repeated at the next time step.

#### 3.6. Integration into Existing Frameworks

Dream-MPC is integrated and evaluated primarily within TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), a model-based RL algorithm known for using MPPI. Dream-MPC replaces TD-MPC2's MPPI planner. Furthermore, it is integrated into BMPC (Wang et al., 2025), an extension of TD-MPC2 that learns a higher-quality policy by imitating an MPC expert, providing a stronger policy prior. The method is also integrated into Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020), a generative world model, to demonstrate broader applicability.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

The method is evaluated on 24 continuous control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite, HumanoidBench, and Meta-World, encompassing diverse challenges including high-dimensional state and action spaces, sparse rewards, and visual observations. Performance metrics include optimality gap, median, interquartile median (IQM), and mean normalized scores, along with performance profiles. Ablation studies are conducted to analyze the contribution of the policy prior, gradient ascent, uncertainty regularization, and action reuse. Inference times are benchmarked on a GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Dream-MPC across a diverse set of continuous control tasks and various base RL algorithms yielded several key findings regarding its performance, efficiency, and underlying mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Aggregate Performance Compared to Baselines

- **Outperformance:** Dream-MPC, particularly when integrated with BMPC (Dream-MPC (BMPC)), achieved the highest aggregate performance across all 24 tasks. It improved the Interquartile Median (IQM) by 26.7% and the mean normalized score by 20.5% compared to BMPC, and outperformed other state-of-the-art baselines including TD-MPC2, Dreamer-v3, and SAC (Figure 1, Figure 3, Tables 11-13).
- **Impact of Policy Quality:** When integrated with TD-MPC2 (Dream-MPC (TD-MPC2)), it significantly improved the performance of the underlying policy (144.7% IQM, 43.4% mean improvement over policy-only TD-MPC2). However, it did not consistently match TD-MPC2's MPPI planner's performance. This finding emphasizes that a high-quality policy prior is crucial for gradient-based MPC, as a weak policy prior (like that of TD-MPC2's policy-only variant) can limit the effectiveness of subsequent gradient optimization (Table 1, Tables 14-16).

#### 4.2. Performance with Visual Observations

- Dream-MPC (BMPC) was also effective with image-based observations. It improved the performance of the underlying BMPC policy and, for several tasks, outperformed the MPPI planner used by BMPC, demonstrating its applicability in visual control settings (Table 2).

#### 4.3. Training with Gradient-Based MPC

- When Dream-MPC was used throughout the training process (replacing MPPI in TD-MPC2), it demonstrated performance comparable to TD-MPC2's MPPI planner for simpler control problems. For more complex, high-dimensional tasks (e.g., Dog Run), its asymptotic performance was slightly lower. This is attributed to potentially less exploration compared to sampling-based MPPI, leading to premature convergence (Figure 4).

#### 4.4. Integration into Dreamer

- Integrating Dream-MPC into Dreamer, a generative world model, resulted in improvements in both sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. Dream-MPC (Dreamer) outperformed Dreamer, PlaNet, SAC+AE, and even gradient-free (CEM+policy) and previous gradient-based (Grad-MPC, Policy+Grad-MPC) variants, even on tasks with challenging dynamics (e.g., Acrobot Swingup) and using only visual observations (Table 3, Figure 13).

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Policy Prior:** The policy prior proved to be a critical component. Removing it (initializing from a Gaussian distribution instead) severely degraded performance, especially for high-dimensional problems (Table 4).
- **Gradient Ascent:** The optimization through gradient ascent consistently improved performance over using the policy only, affirming its value (Table 4).
- **Uncertainty Regularization:** This component was found to be particularly important when Dream-MPC was used during training, contributing to stability. While its benefit was less pronounced for the already strong BMPC base, it generally improved performance or maintained it, indicating its role in mitigating value approximation errors (Figure 5a, Tables 4, 14-16).
- **Action Reuse:** Reusing previously optimized actions was crucial for computational efficiency and performance, especially during training (Figure 5a).
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Dream-MPC showed robustness to variations in the uncertainty regularization coefficient ($\lambda_{unc}$) and the action reuse coefficient ($\rho$), suggesting that the method is not overly sensitive to these choices (Figure 5b).

#### 4.6. Inference Time

- Dream-MPC exhibited competitive inference times. For lower-dimensional problems, it was comparable to or slightly faster than MPPI. For high-dimensional tasks, while inference time increased, it remained significantly faster than prior gradient-based methods like Grad-MPC, which typically involve a larger number of candidate evaluations (Table 5, Table 17).

#### 4.7. Gradient Analysis (for Dreamer Integration)

- An analysis of planner gradients on the Pendulum-v1 environment showed that Dream-MPC's gradients maintained more stable mean and bounded variance across increasing planning horizons when using a learned dynamics model, compared to Grad-MPC, which experienced significant variance increases.
- The Expected Signal-to-Noise Ratio (ESNR) for Dream-MPC also remained more stable for longer horizons with the learned model, indicating greater robustness of its gradients compared to Grad-MPC (Figure 14, Figure 15).

#### 4.8. Value Estimation and Policy Mismatch Analysis

- For high-dimensional tasks like Dog Run, TD-MPC2 demonstrated tendencies for value overestimation or underestimation, particularly due to a "policy mismatch" where the MPC-generated actions deviate from the policy prior distribution.
- BMPC's bootstrapping mechanism helped mitigate this mismatch, resulting in lower value estimation errors.
- Empirical correlations indicated that lower episode returns were associated with higher absolute value approximation errors and greater variance in predicted Q-values (critic ensemble disagreement). This highlights the importance of accurate value estimates and uncertainty awareness for effective planning (Figure 17, Figure 18, Figure 19).
- Dream-MPC generally showed slightly weaker correlations between these metrics than MPPI, suggesting that by staying closer to the nominal policy actions, it might be less susceptible to out-of-distribution queries of the world model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Dream-MPC represents a notable advancement in the field of model-based reinforcement learning, particularly in the domain of continuous control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Validation of Gradient-Based Planning:** The work successfully addresses a long-standing challenge in the literature: the empirical underperformance of gradient-based MPC compared to gradient-free methods when utilizing learned world models. By demonstrating that Dream-MPC can not only achieve but often exceed the performance of state-of-the-art gradient-free baselines, this research validates the potential of gradient-based trajectory optimization as a viable and superior approach. This challenges prior assumptions and opens new avenues for research in this area.

2. **Enhanced Computational Efficiency for Real-World Applications:** Dream-MPC significantly improves computational efficiency by requiring substantially fewer model evaluations per time step compared to sampling-based MPC methods. The competitive inference times observed, even for high-dimensional tasks, suggest that Dream-MPC is more suitable for real-time robotic control and deployment on embedded systems where computational resources and stringent latency requirements are common constraints. This practical benefit can accelerate the adoption of advanced planning techniques in autonomous systems.

3. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The introduction of uncertainty regularization directly into the MPC objective helps mitigate issues like extrapolation errors and value overestimation, which are common when learned world models are queried on unseen states. Coupled with the amortization of optimization iterations through action reuse, Dream-MPC demonstrates improved robustness against model inaccuracies and local optima. Its successful application across diverse tasks, including those with visual observations and high-dimensional spaces, and with different world model architectures (generative and control-centric), indicates a higher degree of generalization and reliability.

4. **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed ablation studies, gradient analyses, and investigations into value estimation errors and policy mismatch provide valuable insights into the factors critical for successful gradient-based planning. These findings highlight the importance of high-quality policy priors, calibrated value estimates, and robust gradient signals. This can guide future research efforts in designing more effective and stable model-based RL algorithms, particularly in areas like model accuracy, exploration strategies, and value function learning. The observation that gradient-based MPC might be less affected by certain model errors due to staying closer to the nominal policy could also have implications for offline RL scenarios.

5. **Ethical and Safety Considerations:** By advancing planning capabilities in autonomous and robotic systems, Dream-MPC contributes to technologies that require careful consideration of ethical and safety implications. The authors acknowledge this, noting that deployment to real systems necessitates addressing aspects such as out-of-distribution generalization and transparency in decision-making. The improved robustness and reduced computational burden offered by Dream-MPC could indirectly support the development of safer and more predictable intelligent agents.
