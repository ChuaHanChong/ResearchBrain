# LLM-grounded Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley introduced LLM-grounded Video Diffusion (LVD), a training-free framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate dynamic scene layouts (DSLs) which then guide text-to-video diffusion models. LVD achieved a 49.4% video-text alignment accuracy on a new benchmark, significantly improving over a 23.0% baseline, and was preferred by humans for both alignment and visual quality.

## Problem

- Current text-to-video models frequently struggle to generate realistic spatial layouts and consistent temporal dynamics, especially when prompted with complex instructions.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, diverse, paired text-video training data that comprehensively covers varied motion patterns and object attributes, leading to high training costs and limited model capabilities.
- Existing controllable diffusion methods often require training specific adapters or fine-tuning models, which can be computationally expensive and risk 'catastrophic forgetting' in pre-trained models.

## Method

- **LVD** introduces a two-stage, training-free pipeline: an LLM acts as a 'spatiotemporal planner' and a DSL-grounded video generator synthesizes the final video.
- A Large Language Model (GPT-4) interprets text prompts and generates Dynamic Scene Layouts (DSLs), which are sequences of bounding boxes linked across frames, using minimal in-context examples to generalize physical properties.
- An existing text-to-==video diffusion model== (ModelScope) is guided by these DSLs through adjustments to its ==cross-attention== maps and an energy function, steering the video generation towards satisfying explicit spatiotemporal constraints without parameter ==fine-tuning==.

## Results

- LLM-generated DSLs (using GPT-4) achieved a high average accuracy of **98%** in aligning with complex prompt requirements on a new 500-prompt benchmark, significantly outperforming a retrieval-based baseline (**14%**).
- LVD (with GPT-4) achieved an average video-text alignment accuracy of **49.4%** on the benchmark, representing a substantial improvement over the base ModelScope diffusion model (**23.0%**) and the retrieval-based baseline (**9.7%**).
- LVD improved video generation quality, achieving lower FVD scores (828 on UCF-101, 565 on MSR-VTT) compared to ModelScope (984 on UCF-101, 590 on MSR-VTT), and was significantly preferred by human evaluators for both text-prompt alignment (**85.8%** vs. **3.2%**) and visual quality (**66.0%** vs. **14.5%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) possess an emergent capability to generate coherent dynamic scene layouts from text alone, demonstrating an inherent understanding of physical properties (e.g., gravity, elasticity) and spatiotemporal dynamics with minimal few-shot examples.
> - Explicitly guiding video diffusion models with intermediate spatiotemporal layouts (DSLs) provides precise control over object-level spatial relations and temporal dynamics, significantly enhancing the fidelity of video generation from complex text prompts.
> - A modular, training-free approach effectively improves text-to-video generation by leveraging the reasoning power of pre-trained LLMs and diffusion models, circumventing the need for extensive paired training data, high computational costs, or risks of catastrophic forgetting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has witnessed significant advancements, particularly in text-to-image synthesis using diffusion models. These models have demonstrated the capacity to produce high-quality visual content based on textual prompts. Building upon this progress, research has extended to text-to-video generation, which presents a substantially more complex challenge due to the inherent intricacies of capturing and synthesizing coherent spatiotemporal dynamics.

Current text-to-video models, while capable, frequently encounter difficulties in generating realistic spatial layouts and consistent temporal dynamics, especially when prompted with complex instructions. Issues such as restricted motion, incorrect object interactions, or misalignment between the prompt and the generated video are common. This limitation arises partly from the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, paired text-video training data that comprehensively covers varied motion patterns and object attributes, as well as the substantial computational costs associated with training such models.

A related area of research involves controllable diffusion models, which aim to incorporate explicit conditioning signals into the generation process. Works like ControlNet, GLIGEN, and Shape-guided Diffusion for images, and Control-A-Video for videos, introduce architectural designs or adapter mechanisms to guide diffusion models with signals like edge maps, depth maps, or bounding boxes. These methods demonstrate the value of explicit controls in steering content generation.

More recently, large language models (LLMs) have been explored as intelligent planners for visual content creation, particularly in text-to-image generation. These studies have shown that LLMs possess a surprising ability to generate detailed spatial bounding box arrangements from text prompts, which can then be used to condition image diffusion models. This has led to improvements in generating images with coherent spatial relationships, bypassing some of the challenges of direct text-to-image generation.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of text-to-video generation, controllable diffusion models, and LLM-based planning. It investigates whether the planning capabilities of LLMs, previously demonstrated for static spatial layouts in images, can be extended to dynamic spatiotemporal layouts for videos. The proposed LLM-grounded Video Diffusion (LVD) aims to leverage LLMs as an intermediate "spatiotemporal planner" to guide video diffusion models, thereby addressing the persistent challenges of generating accurate and dynamic videos from complex text prompts without requiring extensive new training data or model retraining. This represents a training-free approach to enhance the interpretability and control over the video generation process, differentiating it from methods that rely on training specific adapters or fine-tuning models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of text-conditioned video diffusion models in generating videos that accurately reflect intricate spatiotemporal dynamics described in textual prompts. Current models often struggle with generating realistic motion, correct object attributes, and coherent spatial relationships, leading to videos that are either restricted in motion or misaligned with the input prompt.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and observations:

1. **Complexity of Text-to-Video Generation**: Generating videos from text is inherently more difficult than generating images. It requires not only understanding static object appearances and spatial relationships but also synthesizing temporal coherence, object motion patterns, and interactions over time. This involves reasoning about physical properties (e.g., gravity, elasticity), object attributes, and viewpoint changes.

2. **Limitations of Existing Text-to-Video Models**: While significant progress has been made, existing text-to-video diffusion models frequently produce videos that lack realistic spatial layouts or accurate temporal dynamics, failing to faithfully capture the full complexity of a given text prompt. This is often due to the difficulty in encoding and learning diverse motion patterns directly from text through available datasets.

3. **Emergent Capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs)**: Recent work in text-to-image generation demonstrated that LLMs can act as effective "spatial planners," translating abstract text prompts into concrete spatial bounding box layouts. This capability suggests that LLMs might possess an inherent understanding of spatial relationships that could be extended to the temporal domain. The authors were motivated to investigate if LLMs could similarly generate *dynamic* scene layouts (DSLs) that encompass both spatial arrangements and temporal movements.

4. **Desire for a Training-Free Solution**: Training large-scale text-to-video models or fine-tuning existing ones for specific controls demands extensive paired text-video data and significant computational resources. Such training also risks "catastrophic forgetting," where new training might degrade existing capabilities. The researchers sought a training-free approach that could leverage the power of pre-trained LLMs and diffusion models without modifying their parameters, thus making the solution more accessible and efficient.

5. **Intermediate Representation for Enhanced Control**: By introducing Dynamic Scene Layouts (DSLs) as an intermediate representation, the authors aimed to create a bridge between the high-level semantic understanding of text prompts and the low-level visual generation process of diffusion models. This intermediate step was hypothesized to provide more granular and precise control over object-level spatial relations and temporal dynamics, which are often implicitly and imprecisely captured when generating directly from text.

In essence, the research aimed to harness the reasoning abilities of LLMs to overcome the limitations of current video diffusion models in handling complex spatiotemporal prompts, providing a novel and efficient pipeline for more faithful and controllable video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LLM-grounded Video Diffusion (LVD) method proposes a two-stage, training-free pipeline to enhance text-to-video generation by explicitly guiding the process with spatiotemporal layouts.

**Overall LVD Pipeline:**
The LVD pipeline transforms the text-to-video generation task into two distinct stages:
1. **Stage 1: LLM as Spatiotemporal Planner**: A Large Language Model (LLM) is used to interpret a user's text prompt and generate a corresponding Dynamic Scene Layout (DSL).
2. **Stage 2: DSL-grounded Video Generator**: An existing text-to-video diffusion model is then guided by both the original text prompt and the generated DSL to synthesize the final video.

Both stages of the LVD pipeline operate in a training-free manner, meaning no parameters of the LLM or the base diffusion model are updated during this process.

**Stage 1: LLM-based Spatiotemporal Planner**
The core of the first stage is to determine whether LLMs can generate plausible spatiotemporal dynamics from text alone.
- **Dynamic Scene Layout (DSL) Representation**: The LLM is instructed to output DSLs, which are sequences of bounding boxes linked across frames. Each bounding box includes:
 - A unique numerical identifier (`id`)
 - The name of the enclosed object (`name`)
 - Numerical coordinates representing the box's top-left (x, y) position, width, and height (`box`).
 - Boxes corresponding to the same object across different frames maintain the same `id`.
- **Prompting Strategy for LLM**: To elicit the desired dynamic scene layouts, the LLM is provided with a carefully constructed prompt comprising:
 - **Task Instructions**: The LLM is instructed to act as a "video bounding box generator," with specifications for the coordinate system ( to), canvas size, number of frames (six frames at two frames per second), and the requirement for realistic bounding boxes that do not overlap or exceed frame boundaries. It is also prompted to assume real-world physics (gravity, elasticity) and perspective geometry.
 - **In-context Examples**: Critically, the LLM is given a few-shot learning setup. Only one example is provided for each key physical property or camera motion:
 - **Gravity**: Demonstrated with "A woman walking from the left to the right and a man jumping on the right in a room."
 - **Elasticity**: Illustrated with "A red ball is thrown from the left to the right in a garden."
 - **Perspective Projection**: Shown with "The camera is moving away from a painting."
 The research emphasizes that a minimal number of examples (just one per property) is surprisingly sufficient for the LLM to comprehend and generalize these concepts.
 - **Reasoning Before Generation**: For interpretability, the LLM is asked to output a brief reasoning statement before generating the bounding box sequences for each frame, explaining its dynamic choices.
- **LLM Selection**: GPT-4 (and GPT-3.5 for comparison) was primarily used for this stage, accessed via API calls or the ChatGPT web interface.
- **Investigation Findings (Section 3)**: Through various query scenarios, the authors found that LLMs (specifically GPT-4) could:
 - Generate realistic DSLs aligned with complex prompts, even for previously unseen spatiotemporal sequences.
 - Selectively apply physical properties (e.g., elasticity for a ball but not a rock, even without explicit object attribute mentions in examples).
 - Generalize to properties not explicitly taught (e.g., inferring air friction for a paper airplane).
 - Understand the impact of different camera viewpoints on object dynamics without explicit instructions. These capabilities suggest that the LLM's understanding of world dynamics is embedded in its pre-trained weights rather than solely derived from the provided in-context examples.

**Stage 2: DSL-grounded Video Generator**
This stage leverages the generated DSLs to guide a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model, ModelScope (Wang et al., 2023), without any fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism of Guidance**: The guidance is applied by adjusting the cross-attention maps within the diffusion U-Net architecture. Cross-attention layers are responsible for integrating textual information into the latent features, and the attention maps typically highlight regions where specific objects are generated.
- **Energy Function (E) for Guidance**: An energy function is defined to encourage the diffusion model to generate objects within the specified bounding boxes and follow their temporal dynamics:
 - **E_topk**: This term encourages high cross-attention values for an object *inside* its corresponding bounding box mask (M) and low values *outside* the mask. It is formulated as `-Topk(A · M) + Topk(A · (1 - M))`, where `A` is the attention map, `M` is the binary mask derived from the DSL bounding box, and `Topk` averages the top-k values.
 - **E_CoM (Center-of-Mass)**: This term ensures temporal consistency by aligning the spatial position and velocity of the attention map's center of mass (`p_A_t`) with that of the bounding box mask (`p_M_t`) across consecutive frames. It is formulated as `||p_A_t - p_M_t||^2_2 + ||(p_A_{t+1} - p_A_t) - (p_M_{t+1} - p_M_t)||^2_2`.
 - **Overall Energy E**: A weighted sum of `E_topk` and `E_CoM` is used.
- **DSL Guidance Step**: Before each denoising step of the diffusion process, a DSL guidance step is introduced. In this step, the gradient of the total energy `E` with respect to the partially denoised video latent is calculated. This gradient is then used to update the video latent via gradient descent, effectively steering the video generation towards satisfying the DSL constraints. Classifier guidance is also employed in this process.
- **Hyperparameters**: The energy function is scaled by a factor (e.g., 5), with DSL guidance performed multiple times (e.g., 5 times) within the initial denoising steps (e.g., first 10 steps). Different weights are applied to foreground and background, and for E_CoM. The "learning rate" for gradient descent is adjusted per denoising step.

This modular and training-free approach allows LVD to leverage the high-level reasoning of LLMs and the generative power of diffusion models, while providing explicit control over complex spatiotemporal dynamics in video outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LLM-grounded Video Diffusion (LVD) focused on two main aspects: the quality of LLM-generated Dynamic Scene Layouts (DSLs) and the alignment and quality of the final generated videos.

**Evaluation of Generated DSLs**
A new benchmark comprising five distinct tasks was introduced to assess the alignment between LLM-generated DSLs and the input text prompts: generative numeracy, attribute binding, visibility, spatial dynamics, and sequential actions. Each task consisted of 100 programmatically generated prompts, totaling 500 prompts.
- **Superior LLM Performance**: LVD, particularly when utilizing GPT-4 as the spatiotemporal planner, achieved a high average accuracy of 98% in generating DSLs that correctly aligned with the prompt requirements. This significantly outperformed a retrieval-based baseline, which only achieved 14% accuracy, indicating that direct retrieval from video datasets often fails to provide matching spatiotemporal layouts.
- **Few-shot Generalization**: The high performance of GPT-4 was achieved using only three in-context examples, one for each key physical property (gravity, elasticity, perspective projection). Further experiments revealed that adding more retrieval-based examples did not substantially improve performance, underscoring the LLM's inherent few-shot generalization capabilities.
- **GPT-3.5 Performance**: GPT-3.5 also showed strong performance with 77% average accuracy but exhibited struggles with sequential tasks, sometimes failing to execute multiple actions correctly or simultaneously.
- **Robustness**: Ablation studies confirmed that three in-context examples were sufficient for stable performance, and the choice of these examples had minimal impact on overall accuracy.

**Evaluation of Generated Videos (Video-Text Alignment)**
The generated videos were evaluated using the same five-task benchmark, assessing how well the videos adhered to the spatial layouts and temporal dynamics specified in the text prompts. Bounding boxes for evaluation were extracted using the OWL-ViT detector.
- **Significant Improvement over Baselines**: LVD (with GPT-4) achieved an average video-text alignment accuracy of 49.4%, representing a substantial improvement over the base ModelScope diffusion model (23.0%) and the retrieval-based baseline (9.7%). This demonstrates LVD's effectiveness in generating videos that are more faithful to complex text prompts.
- **Resolution Impact**: When videos were generated at a higher resolution (512x512), the performance gap between LVD and the baselines became even more pronounced, suggesting better control over motion with higher-resolution latents.
- **Ablations on Energy Function**:
 - The `E_topk` term alone proved effective for text-video alignment.
 - Adding the `E_CoM` (Center-of-Mass) term further boosted alignment, particularly for tasks involving sequential actions, by ensuring temporal consistency.
 - Alternative energy functions (`E_ratio` from Chen et al., 2023a, and `E_corner` from Xie et al., 2023) did not improve or even degraded performance, indicating the specific design of LVD's energy function was crucial.
- **Guidance Step Ablations**: The study found that 5 DSL guidance steps between denoising steps were optimal, as performance plateaued beyond this number, balancing effectiveness and computational efficiency.

**Evaluation of Video Quality (FVD Scores)**
While not the primary objective, LVD's impact on overall video quality was assessed using the Frechet Video Distance (FVD) score on UCF-101 and MSR-VTT datasets. Lower FVD scores indicate higher quality.
- **Improved Quality**: LVD improved video generation quality compared to the ModelScope baseline on both UCF-101 (FVD of 828 for LVD vs. 984 for ModelScope) and MSR-VTT (FVD of 565 for LVD vs. 590 for ModelScope). This suggests that guiding the model with explicit DSLs results in actions and motions that are visually more similar to real-world videos, even without explicit quality optimization.

**Qualitative Visualizations and Evaluator-Based Assessment**
- **Visual Fidelity and Prompt Alignment**: Qualitative examples demonstrated LVD's ability to consistently generate videos that align with complex prompts, including correct object counts, managing object appearance/disappearance mid-video, following sequential directions, and accurately binding attributes to multiple objects (e.g., distinct colors for different objects). LVD also handled ambiguous prompts by making reasonable assumptions that were often detailed in the LLM's reasoning statements.
- **Human Evaluation**: A human evaluation involving 10 participants confirmed these findings. LVD was significantly preferred for both text-prompt alignment (85.8% for LVD vs. 3.2% for baseline) and visual quality (66.0% for LVD vs. 14.5% for baseline), indicating a clear and consistent enhancement.

**Failure Cases**
The paper acknowledged two types of failure cases:
1. **Inherited Limitations**: LVD, being training-free, inherits limitations from its base diffusion model. For instance, if the base model struggles with specific artistic styles or synthesizing certain objects, LVD's output will reflect these deficiencies, even if the motion is correctly guided.
2. **Unintended Energy Minimization**: In some instances, the DSL guidance process could lead to unexpected energy minimization strategies. For example, instead of realistically moving an object, the model might attempt to "hide" it or make it disappear/reappear to satisfy the attention map constraints, which is not aligned with the intended motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LLM-grounded Video Diffusion (LVD) model introduces several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of generative AI and beyond.

**1. Pioneering LLM for Spatiotemporal Planning**: This work provides empirical evidence that large language models possess an unexpected capability to generate coherent dynamic scene layouts (DSLs) from text prompts alone. This extends the previously observed LLM ability for static spatial planning in images to the more complex domain of spatiotemporal dynamics. The demonstration that LLMs can generalize to unseen motion sequences, selectively apply physical properties (e.g., gravity, elasticity, air friction), and understand the role of viewpoint in object dynamics, largely based on their inherent knowledge rather than extensive examples, opens new avenues for leveraging LLMs as intelligent world simulators or planners.

**2. Enhanced Text-to-Video Generation with Complex Prompts**: LVD addresses a critical limitation of current text-to-video diffusion models, which often struggle with intricate spatiotemporal instructions. By introducing DSLs as an intermediate planning stage, LVD significantly improves the alignment between complex text prompts and the generated video content, leading to more faithful representation of desired attributes and motion patterns. This directly contributes to making text-to-video generation more controllable and reliable for nuanced descriptions.

**3. Training-Free and Modular Pipeline**: The training-free nature of LVD is a significant advantage. It bypasses the formidable challenges associated with acquiring large-scale paired text-video training data, the substantial computational cost of training, and the risk of catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning pre-trained models. This makes the method resource-efficient and immediately applicable to existing text-to-video diffusion models. Furthermore, its modular two-stage architecture allows for independent improvements. As LLMs become more capable or as new, more precise DSL-conditioned video generators emerge, LVD's overall performance can improve without needing to redesign the entire pipeline.

**4. Improved Video Quality and Human Perception**: Beyond just aligning with prompts, LVD was shown to improve the overall perceptual quality of generated videos, as indicated by better FVD scores on standard benchmarks and strong preferences from human evaluators. This suggests that explicit spatiotemporal guidance not only makes videos more semantically correct but also visually more realistic and coherent.

**5. Foundation for Future Controllable Generation**: LVD lays a foundational groundwork for future research in highly controllable and interpretable video generation. The ability to leverage LLMs for generating precise temporal bounding box sequences opens possibilities for guiding other fine-grained properties, such as human poses, facial expressions, or complex object interactions. This could lead to systems that allow users unprecedented control over the narrative and physical realism of generated video content.

The work's impact extends to applications requiring high-fidelity video synthesis, such as content creation, animation, virtual reality, and simulation, where precise control over dynamic elements is paramount. By demonstrating the power of LLM-grounded planning, LVD offers a promising paradigm shift for overcoming some of the most persistent challenges in generative video modeling.
