# LLM-grounded Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley introduced LLM-grounded Video Diffusion (LVD), a training-free framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate dynamic scene layouts (DSLs) which then guide text-to-video diffusion models. LVD achieved a 49.4% video-text alignment accuracy on a new benchmark, significantly improving over a 23.0% baseline, and was preferred by humans for both alignment and visual quality.

## Problem

- Current text-to-video models frequently struggle to generate realistic spatial layouts and consistent temporal dynamics, especially when prompted with complex instructions.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, diverse, paired text-video training data that comprehensively covers varied motion patterns and object attributes, leading to high training costs and limited model capabilities.
- Existing controllable diffusion methods often require training specific adapters or fine-tuning models, which can be computationally expensive and risk 'catastrophic forgetting' in pre-trained models.

## Method

- **LVD** introduces a two-stage, training-free pipeline: an LLM acts as a 'spatiotemporal planner' and a DSL-grounded video generator synthesizes the final video.
- A Large Language Model (GPT-4) interprets text prompts and generates Dynamic Scene Layouts (DSLs), which are sequences of bounding boxes linked across frames, using minimal in-context examples to generalize physical properties.
- An existing text-to-==video diffusion model== (ModelScope) is guided by these DSLs through adjustments to its ==cross-attention== maps and an energy function, steering the video generation towards satisfying explicit spatiotemporal constraints without parameter ==fine-tuning==.

## Results

- LLM-generated DSLs (using GPT-4) achieved a high average accuracy of **98%** in aligning with complex prompt requirements on a new 500-prompt benchmark, significantly outperforming a retrieval-based baseline (**14%**).
- LVD (with GPT-4) achieved an average video-text alignment accuracy of **49.4%** on the benchmark, representing a substantial improvement over the base ModelScope diffusion model (**23.0%**) and the retrieval-based baseline (**9.7%**).
- LVD improved video generation quality, achieving lower FVD scores (828 on UCF-101, 565 on MSR-VTT) compared to ModelScope (984 on UCF-101, 590 on MSR-VTT), and was significantly preferred by human evaluators for both text-prompt alignment (**85.8%** vs. **3.2%**) and visual quality (**66.0%** vs. **14.5%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) possess an emergent capability to generate coherent dynamic scene layouts from text alone, demonstrating an inherent understanding of physical properties (e.g., gravity, elasticity) and spatiotemporal dynamics with minimal few-shot examples.
> - Explicitly guiding video diffusion models with intermediate spatiotemporal layouts (DSLs) provides precise control over object-level spatial relations and temporal dynamics, significantly enhancing the fidelity of video generation from complex text prompts.
> - A modular, training-free approach effectively improves text-to-video generation by leveraging the reasoning power of pre-trained LLMs and diffusion models, circumventing the need for extensive paired training data, high computational costs, or risks of catastrophic forgetting.
