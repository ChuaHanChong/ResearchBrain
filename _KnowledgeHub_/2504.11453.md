# A Clean Slate for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework for offline reinforcement learning from Oxford researchers introduces standardized evaluation protocols and a single-file implementation architecture, while demonstrating how combining components from existing approaches leads to two new algorithms (TD3-AWR and MoBRAC) that achieve stronger performance across multiple benchmarks through careful ablation and systematic integration.

## Problem

- Inconsistent evaluation protocols and unclear problem definitions make it difficult to fairly compare offline RL algorithms
- Complex, entangled implementations obscure the impact of individual algorithmic choices
- Existing methods show poor generalization across different types of tasks

## Method

- Developed rigorous taxonomy of offline RL evaluation variants with explicit quantification of ==tuning budgets==
- Created Unifloral framework that integrates core components from diverse methods into unified hyperparameter space
- Provided clean, minimal JAX implementations to improve code clarity and training speed

## Results

- TD3-AWR outperformed ReBRAC baseline on 6 of 9 datasets via IQL component integration, while MoBRAC achieved state-of-the-art performance among model-based methods on most benchmarks
- JAX implementations demonstrated significant speedups compared to existing offline RL libraries
- Established clear protocols for reproducible evaluation and fair algorithm comparison

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based methods tend to overfit to locomotion tasks and perform poorly on other domains
> - "Distractor policies" with high peak but inconsistent performance can mislead hyperparameter tuning
> - Combining aspects of multiple existing approaches in a systematic way enables better generalization

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses several critical shortcomings in the offline reinforcement learning (RL) field. The authors identify two primary problems that have hindered progress:

- **Ambiguous Problem Setting:** Inconsistent evaluation protocols and ill-defined problem statements lead to unfair comparisons between algorithms, preventing clear progress.
- **Opaque Algorithmic Design:** Intricate and entangled algorithm implementations make it difficult to isolate the impact of individual methodological choices.

The paper acknowledges previous efforts that have highlighted similar issues:

- **Evaluation Rigor:** Cites work from Wang et al., Paine et al., Konyushova et al., Smith et al., Matsushima et al., and Kurenkov and Kolesnikov, who have discussed the practical risks of deploying policies of unknown quality in the real world.
- **Open-Source Implementations:** Acknowledges and builds upon prior work like Clean Offline RL (CORL), JAX-CORL, OfflineRLKit, and d3rlpy, which provide open-source implementations of offline RL algorithms.
- **Method Unification:** Inspired by work from Lu et al., Sikchi et al., Prudencio et al., Hessel et al., and Muesli, which seeks to ablate and unify a range of methods.

By offering a rigorous taxonomy of offline RL evaluation variants and proposing a transparent evaluation protocol, this work directly aims to improve the quality and reproducibility of future research. Their work contributes directly to addressing concerns about reproducibility and fair comparison. They go further by providing a unified algorithm, Unifloral, that combines diverse approaches into a single framework, enabling algorithm development within a shared hyperparameter space. This unifying approach promises to streamline research and facilitate more targeted investigations of specific algorithmic components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The main objectives of the research are:

- **Clarify and formalize the offline RL problem setting** by introducing a rigorous taxonomy of offline RL evaluation variants.
- **Propose a transparent and reproducible evaluation protocol** for offline RL algorithms, including explicit quantification of online tuning budgets.
- **Provide clean, minimalistic, single-file implementations** of various model-free and model-based offline RL methods to enhance clarity and improve training speed.
- **Develop a unified offline RL algorithm (Unifloral)** that integrates core components from diverse prior methods into a coherent framework with a single, unified hyperparameter space.
- **Demonstrate the utility of Unifloral** by developing two novel algorithms (TD3-AWR and MoBRAC) that outperform established baselines.

The motivation behind this work stems from the observation that the offline RL field has been hampered by ambiguity, inconsistency, and lack of clarity. The authors argue that these issues have undermined both practical application and theoretical progress in the area.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a multi-pronged approach:

- **Taxonomy Development:** They establish a clear taxonomy of offline RL evaluation variants, categorizing methods based on the degree of permitted online interaction during pre-deployment and post-deployment phases.
- **Evaluation Protocol Design:** They propose a rigorous evaluation protocol that involves:
 - Training multiple policies with different hyperparameter settings.
 - Evaluating these policies online and recording their episodic scores.
 - Simulating hyperparameter tuning using a multi-armed bandit framework to select the best policy based on limited online interactions.
- **Algorithm Reimplementation:** They provide single-file reimplementations of several existing offline RL algorithms using JAX, aiming for code clarity and minimal differences between algorithms.
- **Unified Algorithm Development (Unifloral):** They design a unified algorithm that encapsulates diverse prior approaches within a single hyperparameter space. Unifloral includes components from model design, critic objective, actor objective, and dynamics modeling.
- **Experimental Validation:** They empirically validate their framework by developing two novel algorithms (TD3-AWR and MoBRAC) using Unifloral and evaluating their performance against established baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The key findings and results of the research are:

- **Inconsistent Algorithm Performance:** Existing offline RL algorithms exhibit inconsistent performance across different datasets, suggesting a lack of generalization.
- **Overfit Model-Based Methods:** Model-based algorithms often perform poorly on non-locomotion tasks, indicating overfitting to specific environments.
- **Distractor Policy Phenomenon:** The presence of "distractor policies" (suboptimal policies with high peak performance) can negatively impact evaluation by misleading hyperparameter tuning algorithms.
- **Speedups with JAX Implementation:** The authors' JAX-based reimplementations achieve significant training speedups compared to existing libraries like OfflineRL-Kit and CORL.
- **Improved Performance with Novel Algorithms:** The novel algorithms developed using Unifloral, TD3-AWR and MoBRAC, demonstrate substantial performance improvements over established baselines. Specifically, TD3-AWR combines aspects from ReBRAC and IQL and strictly dominates ReBRAC on 6 out of 9 datasets. MoBRAC uses ReBRAC as the policy optimizer, with synthetic rollouts generated from a MOPO world model, and outperforms other model-methods for all datasets, except for MOPO in maze2d-large-v1.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work has several important implications:

- **Improved Evaluation Practices:** The proposed taxonomy and evaluation protocol can promote more rigorous, transparent, and reproducible research in offline RL.
- **Enhanced Algorithm Design:** The clean reimplementations and unified algorithm framework can facilitate a deeper understanding of existing methods and accelerate the development of new algorithms.
- **Practical Applications:** By addressing the challenges of offline RL evaluation and algorithm design, this work can contribute to the development of more reliable and effective RL agents for real-world applications.
- **Community Impact:** Providing open-source tools and a unified framework can foster greater collaboration and accelerate progress within the offline RL community.

The Unifloral framework and the demonstration of TD3-AWR and MoBRAC highlight the potential for more systematic and targeted research in offline RL. The speed improvements are also critical, as they can lower the barrier to entry to researchers who don't have access to huge computational resources. The identification of the distractor policy phenomenon and its impact on evaluation is also noteworthy, and highlights how much the offline RL field still has to learn. The release of the implementation on GitHub also ensures that the rest of the RL community can benefit from this work.
