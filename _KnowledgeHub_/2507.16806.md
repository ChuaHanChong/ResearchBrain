# Beyond Binary Rewards: Training LMs to Reason About Their Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT developed Reinforcement Learning with Calibration Rewards (RLCR), an approach that trains large language models to concurrently improve answer accuracy and calibrate their confidence. RLCR integrates a Brier score into the RL reward function, enabling models to generate accurate answers alongside well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, reducing Expected Calibration Error on HotpotQA from 0.37 to 0.03 while maintaining task accuracy.

## Problem

- Standard reinforcement learning with binary rewards for large language models (LMs) often leads to overconfidence and increased hallucination, degrading calibration.
- Existing RL-based calibration methods frequently optimize solely for calibration, potentially sacrificing task accuracy.
- LMs struggle to reliably communicate uncertainty, undermining their trustworthiness in critical applications.

## Method

- Introduces ==RLCR (Reinforcement Learning with Calibration Rewards)==, which trains LMs to generate reasoning chains, answers, and explicit numerical confidence scores.
- Employs an augmented reward function combining a binary correctness reward with a ==negative Brier score== to incentivize both accuracy and calibration.
- Theoretically proves that this ==bounded proper scoring rule== encourages both accurate predictions and calibrated confidence matching the true probability of correctness.

## Results

- RLCR matches the task accuracy of standard binary-reward RL (RLVR) on in-domain datasets (e.g., **62.1%** vs. **63.0%** on HotpotQA), while dramatically improving calibration.
- On HotpotQA, RLCR reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from **0.37** (RLVR) to **0.03**, and on math datasets from **0.26** to **0.10**.
- RLCR consistently and substantially improves calibration on out-of-distribution tasks, outperforming all baselines without sacrificing accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is possible to jointly optimize large language models for both task correctness and calibrated confidence without compromising accuracy.
> - Integrating explicit, textual uncertainty analysis within Chain-of-Thought reasoning can contribute to better calibration.
> - Training LMs to reason about and express uncertainty improves their generalization and robustness, particularly on out-of-distribution tasks.
