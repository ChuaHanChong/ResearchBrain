# PTLD: Sim-to-real Privileged Tactile Latent Distillation for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The PTLD framework enables learning dexterous manipulation policies with tactile feedback by distilling real-world privileged sensor information, bypassing the need for accurate tactile sensor simulation. This approach resulted in an average 182% improvement in total rotation for in-hand rotation tasks and a 57% increase in goals reached for continuous reorientation compared to proprioception-only baselines.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered robot hands is inherently complex, making tasks requiring contact-rich interactions difficult to automate.
- Accurately simulating tactile sensors is challenging, leading to a significant sim-to-real gap that hinders the deployment of simulation-trained tactile policies.
- Existing privileged distillation methods often distill to modalities still facing sim-to-real gaps or are limited by the performance ceiling of initial proprioceptive teacher policies, preventing robust real-world performance.

## Method

- **PTLD** introduces a novel approach that uses an ==instrumented real-world setup== to provide ==privileged sensor== data (e.g., object pose from external cameras) to a simulation-trained policy.
- An ==Asymmetric Actor Critic (AAC)== framework with an ==online latent distillation loss== is employed to efficiently train a privileged sensor policy in simulation, streamlining the process from two stages to one.
- A ==tactile encoder== is then trained via supervised learning using collected real-world data, matching real-world tactile observations and proprioception to the latent representations generated by the privileged sensor policy, using ==DAgger== to mitigate distribution shift.

## Results

- PTLD policies demonstrated superior robustness and performance, achieving an average **182%** improvement in total rotation over proprioception-only baselines on the in-hand rotation task.
- For the continuous in-hand reorientation task, PTLD-trained policies with tactile feedback achieved a **57%** increase in goals reached compared to proprioception-only policies.
- Tactile information incorporated into latent representations reduced 6D object rotation prediction errors by approximately **50%** (from **0.43** to **0.21** Radians for absolute pose), confirming its utility for enhanced state estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging real-world, high-fidelity privileged sensor information for latent distillation effectively bypasses the challenges of simulating complex tactile sensors, providing a robust path for sim-to-real transfer.
> - The Asymmetric Actor Critic (AAC) framework combined with an online latent distillation loss allows for efficient single-stage training of powerful privileged teacher policies in simulation.
> - Tactile sensing provides crucial information for object state estimation, significantly reducing errors in 6D rotation prediction and enabling sophisticated recovery behaviors during manipulation.
