# ARC-AGI Without Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> CompressARC, developed by researchers at Carnegie Mellon University, addresses the ARC-AGI benchmark by achieving 20% accuracy on evaluation puzzles without any pretraining, learning entirely at inference time from the target puzzle. It leverages a custom equivariant neural network and the Minimum Description Length principle to discover abstract reasoning patterns with extreme data efficiency.

## Problem

- The ARC-AGI benchmark poses a challenge for abstract visual reasoning, requiring rapid skill acquisition and generalization from minimal input-output examples.
- Prevailing state-of-the-art methods, particularly those based on Large Language Models, heavily rely on massive pretraining data and extensive computational resources.
- Directly applying information-theoretic principles like Minimum Description Length (MDL) for program discovery is computationally intractable.

## Method

- CompressARC reframes ARC-AGI as a "code-golfing" problem, aiming to find the shortest self-contained program capable of generating a given puzzle, guided by the ==Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle==.
- It employs an ==inference-time learning== approach for each individual puzzle, approximating MDL optimization using a differentiable loss function composed of KL divergence (for seed length) and cross-entropy (for reconstruction error).
- A custom, parameter-efficient (**76K** parameters) neural network architecture is utilized, featuring strong ==inductive biases== such as equivariance to permutations, rotations, and flips, and processing information across "multitensors" of varying ranks.

## Results

- The model correctly solved **20%** of evaluation set puzzles and **34.75%** of training set puzzles (with 2 allowed guesses) without any pretraining or use of the ARC-AGI training set.
- CompressARC demonstrates extreme data efficiency, performing all its "learning" purely at inference time, using only the single target puzzle as input data.
- Qualitative analysis shows the system generalizes to a diverse range of ARC-AGI puzzles, performing tasks like color assignment, infilling, cropping, connecting dots, and identifying pixel adjacencies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstract reasoning and generalization can be achieved with extreme data efficiency, challenging the conventional necessity of massive pretraining for complex AI capabilities.
> - The Minimum Description Length principle can be effectively operationalized in deep learning through differentiable approximations, providing a practical link between information compression and intelligent behavior.
> - Carefully engineered architectural inductive biases, such as equivariance and multitensor processing, are crucial for enabling rapid, few-shot generalization from extremely limited data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research directly addresses the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC-AGI) benchmark, a widely recognized challenge for artificial general intelligence (AGI) designed by François Chollet. ARC-AGI puzzles test a system's ability to rapidly acquire new skills and generalize from minimal input-output examples, focusing on abstract visual reasoning. The broader research landscape for ARC-AGI is dominated by two main approaches:

- **Large Language Model (LLM) based methods:** These approaches convert visual puzzles into textual descriptions and leverage the vast pretraining knowledge of LLMs (often fine-tuned or prompted extensively) to generate solutions or programs that solve the puzzles. These methods, exemplified by OpenAI's o3, have achieved the highest scores (e.g., 87.5% on semi-private evaluation) but require massive internet-scale pretraining data and significant inference-time compute. They represent the "conventional wisdom" that intelligence in such tasks is derived from immense data exposure.
- **Traditional Deep Learning and Program Synthesis:** Other methods involve neural networks trained specifically on ARC-AGI data (e.g., Hierarchical Reasoning Model, HRM, achieving 40.3%) or rule-based, symbolic AI systems that search through a space of hand-written domain-specific languages to find rules. These often involve some form of "inference-time training" or test-time adaptation, where models are adjusted to individual puzzles.

CompressARC deliberately positions itself in stark contrast to the prevailing LLM paradigm and even other deep learning methods by making an extreme claim: it achieves non-trivial performance on ARC-AGI *without any pretraining whatsoever* and *without using the ARC-AGI training set*. Its "learning" occurs entirely at inference time, based solely on the target puzzle itself. This radical data efficiency challenges the fundamental assumption that "intelligence must arise from massive pretraining and data" for complex reasoning tasks. It suggests an alternative, information-theoretic path to intelligence that emphasizes compression and generalization from minimal observations, reminiscent of algorithmic information theory and Kolmogorov complexity. By eschewing pretraining, CompressARC offers a novel perspective on how generalizable capabilities might be instilled in AI systems under extreme data constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **counter the conventional wisdom** that solving IQ-test-like visual puzzles from the ARC-AGI benchmark requires capabilities derived from massive pretraining. The authors aim to demonstrate that abstract reasoning and generalization can be achieved with extreme data efficiency.

The core motivations driving this objective are:

- **Challenging the Pretraining Paradigm:** The overwhelming success of LLMs has led to a belief that large datasets and extensive pretraining are prerequisites for advanced AI capabilities. CompressARC seeks to provide a compelling counter-example, suggesting that other principles, like information compression, can also lead to robust intelligence.
- **Exploring Data-Limited Intelligence:** Many real-world problems, such as drug discovery or protein design, inherently lack massive datasets for pretraining. If AI systems can exhibit intelligence and strong generalization with minimal data, it opens up new avenues for applying deep learning in these critical, data-scarce domains.
- **Investigating Minimum Description Length (MDL) as a Path to AGI:** The paper is motivated by the theoretical links between compression and intelligence, drawing inspiration from the Hutter Prize and Solomonoff Induction. The authors hypothesize that minimizing the description length of a problem inherently leads to finding the underlying "rules" or "intelligence" required to solve it, embodying Occam's razor.
- **Developing an Alternative Framework for Generalization:** By operating purely during inference time and learning solely from the target puzzle, CompressARC explores a fundamentally different mechanism for generalization than those typically found in deep learning, which often relies on transferring knowledge learned from vast prior data.

In essence, the authors are probing the minimal requirements for intelligent behavior, proposing that compression principles can unlock impressive generalization abilities even under extreme data poverty.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CompressARC's methodology is built upon the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, reframing the ARC-AGI challenge as a "code-golfing" problem. The goal is to find the shortest possible self-contained program that can output the entire ARC-AGI dataset, with unsolved grids filled in arbitrarily. The core idea is that the shortest program will implicitly encode the "correct" rules, aligning with Occam's razor.

The approach is structured in three conceptual steps, approximated for computational feasibility:

1. **Program Space Restriction (Algorithm 1: Template Program):** The authors define a restricted space of programs using a template. This template consists of an `equivariant_NN` architecture and several "empty spaces" for hard-coded values, referred to as "seeds" (e.g., `seed_z`, `θ` for network weights, `seed_error`). For each puzzle, this template program randomly samples a tensor `z` from a standard normal distribution (using `seed_z`), processes it through `equivariant_NN` (parameterized by `θ`), and then samples puzzle colors from the resulting logits (using `seed_error`). The objective is to make this complete program, when filled with seeds, as short as possible.

2. **Seed Optimization (Algorithm 2: MDL via Code-Golf):** Conceptually, Algorithm 2 aims to find the optimal seeds and network weights (`θ`) that minimize the total length of the program generated by Algorithm 1. The length is primarily determined by the bits required to encode the seeds and weights. This involves manipulating `seed_z` to imitate sampling from a learned normal distribution and `seed_error` to guarantee that the sampled puzzle `P_filled` matches the known parts of the true puzzle `P`. The algorithm would then perform gradient descent on the parameters of the learned input distribution (`μ`, `Σ`) and the network weights (`θ`) to minimize the total seed length. However, a full algorithmic search for the absolute shortest program is computationally intractable.

3. **Differentiable Approximation (Algorithm 3: CompressARC):** To make the seed optimization tractable, CompressARC approximates the seed length minimization using differentiable terms, analogous to a Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
 - `seed_z` length is approximated by the KL divergence between the learned input distribution `N(μ, Σ)` and a standard normal distribution `N(0, I)`. This term encourages `z` to be simple and easy to describe.
 - `seed_error` length is approximated by the cross-entropy between the `grid_logits` produced by the neural network and the known parts of the target puzzle `P`. This term acts as a reconstruction loss, ensuring the network can accurately reproduce the given puzzle examples.
 - The total loss `L` is thus `KL(N(μ, Σ)||N(0, I)) + cross-entropy(grid_logits, P)`. During inference, an Adam optimizer minimizes this loss with respect to `μ`, `Σ`, and `θ` for a fixed number of steps (e.g., 2000 iterations per puzzle). After optimization, the network uses the learned `grid_logits` to sample the `P_filled` output, representing the solution to the test puzzle.

**Novel Architecture:** A crucial part of CompressARC is its custom neural network architecture, designed to have strong inductive biases for ARC-AGI puzzles and enable effective compression.
- **Equivariance:** The network is explicitly designed to be equivariant to permutations of example order, color permutations, rotations, and flips, reflecting the inherent symmetries in ARC-AGI puzzles. This reduces the need for `z` to encode such invariant properties, thus shortening description length.
- **Multitensors:** Instead of a single tensor, the network operates on "multitensors"—a collection of tensors of varying ranks and dimensions (e.g., [example, color, height, width, channel] down to [color, channel]). This allows for more granular information storage and processing, providing useful inductive biases.
- **Specialized Operations:** The core of the network features a residual backbone, similar to a transformer, but uses specialized operations tailored for grid-based reasoning:
 - **Multitensor Communication Layer:** Facilitates interaction and information exchange between different tensors within the multitensor.
 - **Softmax Layer:** For working with internal one-hot representations.
 - **Directional Cummax/Shift Layer:** Allows non-equivariant operations (cumulative max, pixel shifts) to be applied equivariantly across cardinal and diagonal directions, storing results in a dedicated "direction" dimension.
 - **Directional Communication Layer:** Enables communication between different directional slices, preserving rotation and reflection symmetries.
 - **Standard Layers:** Element-wise nonlinearity (SiLU) and channel-wise normalization.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** Despite its complexity, the entire model has only 76K parameters, with most weights in linear projections mapping to and from the residual stream. This small parameter count also contributes to a shorter "program length."

The overall approach uniquely integrates deep learning's differentiable optimization with the information-theoretic principle of MDL, all within a highly data-constrained, inference-time learning paradigm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CompressARC's main findings demonstrate a surprising level of capability for a model operating under extreme data constraints:

- **Significant Performance without Pretraining:** CompressARC correctly solves **20% of evaluation set puzzles** and **34.75% of training set puzzles** (with 2 allowed guesses) after 2000 inference-time training steps per puzzle. This is achieved without any pretraining on external datasets, or even using the provided ARC-AGI training set for general model training. This directly challenges the notion that massive pretraining is indispensable for abstract reasoning.
- **Extreme Data Efficiency:** The model performs all its "learning" purely at inference time, using only the single target puzzle as input data. This contrasts sharply with state-of-the-art LLM-based methods that rely on internet-scale pretraining data and other deep learning models that train on the entire ARC-AGI dataset.
- **Qualitative Generalization:** Despite the data limitations, CompressARC solves a "diverse distribution of creative ARC-AGI puzzles." The paper highlights specific abilities it can perform, such as assigning colors to procedures, infilling, cropping, connecting dots, and identifying pixel adjacencies. It also details limitations, like difficulties with repeating operations many times, counting, complex geometric transformations (rotation, reflection, rescaling), topological properties, or long-range pattern extensions.
- **Case Studies:**
 - **"Color the Boxes" (272f95fa):** CompressARC successfully learns to identify box structures, assign colors based on direction (red for up, blue for down, green for right, yellow for left), and handle corners and the center. Analysis of the KL loss breakdown (Figure 6b) shows specific multitensors (e.g., [example, height, channel], [example, width, channel], [direction, color, channel]) actively encoding crucial information like row/column dividers and color-direction correspondences, while others are compressed to near-zero information content.
 - **"Bounding Box" (6d75e8bb):** The model learns to identify and draw a bounding box around a red shape. Information flow analysis shows specific tensors capturing row/column extents and color roles.
 - **"Center Cross" (41e4d17e):** The system accurately draws magenta rays from the center of blue "bubble" shapes, maintaining correct color layering without mistakenly drawing rays on top of bubbles.

- **Learning Dynamics:** The loss function, composed of KL divergence and cross-entropy, shows the KL term dominating for most of the training, while reconstruction error remains low. The breakdown of KL contributions by individual multitensors provides insight into where CompressARC stores task-relevant information.

In summary, CompressARC demonstrates a proof-of-concept that a specialized, equivariant deep learning architecture, guided by the MDL principle and optimized purely at inference time with minimal data, can achieve non-trivial abstract reasoning capabilities on a challenging benchmark like ARC-AGI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of "ARC-AGI WITHOUT PRETRAINING" lies in its direct challenge to a foundational assumption of modern AI and its demonstration of an alternative pathway to intelligent behavior.

- **Challenging LLM Hegemony and Pretraining Dogma:** The paper provides compelling evidence that massive pretraining, while powerful, might not be the *only* or *necessary* path to solve complex reasoning tasks. By achieving 20% accuracy on ARC-AGI evaluation puzzles with literally zero pretraining data and minimal parameters (76K), CompressARC directly questions the efficiency and fundamental necessity of internet-scale datasets for certain types of AGI capabilities. This could inspire research into more data-efficient learning paradigms.
- **Re-emphasizing Information Theory and Compression:** The work re-validates the long-standing theoretical connection between intelligence and compression (MDL, Kolmogorov Complexity, Solomonoff Induction). It shows a practical, differentiable way to operationalize MDL for deep learning, converting a combinatorial search problem into a gradient-descent optimization. This could open new avenues for integrating information-theoretic principles into neural network design.
- **Potential for Data-Limited Domains:** The extreme data efficiency demonstrated by CompressARC has significant implications for scientific and industrial applications where large, high-quality datasets are scarce. Fields like drug discovery, material science, and personalized medicine often grapple with limited experimental data. A framework that can generalize robustly from few examples could revolutionize how deep learning is applied in these areas.
- **Rethinking Generalization:** CompressARC's "inference-time learning" from a single sample offers a novel perspective on generalization. Instead of transferring knowledge from a vast, diverse training set, it leverages strong inductive biases encoded in its architecture and the MDL principle to "discover" the underlying rules of a new problem on the fly. This highlights the power of carefully engineered architectures and learning algorithms, even more so than the sheer volume of data.
- **A "Complimentary Route to AGI":** The authors propose CompressARC as a "proof of concept" for a possible alternative or complementary route to AGI. While its absolute performance on ARC-AGI is currently lower than LLM-based solutions, its unique methodology points towards a different paradigm—one where efficiency, inductive biases, and direct problem compression are paramount. This could lead to hybrid AI systems that combine the strengths of massive pretraining with the data-efficiency and rapid adaptation capabilities of MDL-based approaches.

The paper is a thought-provoking contribution that pushes the boundaries of deep learning's capabilities under stringent data constraints, offering a fresh perspective on the nature of intelligence and how it might be achieved in artificial systems.
