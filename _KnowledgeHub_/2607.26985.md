# SymmGrid: Super-Scaling On-Robot Learning with Parallelized Symmetries and Egocentric-Exocentric Visual Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> SymmGrid introduces a data augmentation framework that leverages parallelized, branched symmetries to super-scale on-robot deep reinforcement learning. This method accelerates wall-clock training times by up to 2.17x and improves policy success rates by up to 1.27x on real robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current deep reinforcement learning (DRL) policy training directly on physical robots faces impractically long wall-clock training times, often ranging from 20 to 180 minutes.
- Existing symmetry-based DRL methods typically utilize only a limited number of symmetries for data augmentation, not fully exploiting the potential for invariant transformations.
- The inherent slowness of on-robot learning limits its practical deployment and wide adoption, despite its advantage in capturing real-world physics over simulation-based training.

## Method

- **SymmGrid** models a Markov Decision Process (MDP) as a ==symmetry tree==, applying global, reward-invariant, and dynamics-invariant ==affine transformations== (specifically translations) to entire trajectories.
- It generates a K×KK × KK×K grid of symmetric states and actions in parallel from each observed trajectory, 'super-scaling' the data augmentation for the DRL replay buffer.
- The framework incorporates specific handling for visual perception, using a pointer-based system for egocentric views and ==homographies== for exocentric camera views to ensure visual consistency in transformed states.

## Results

- Achieved wall-clock training convergence speedups of **1.37x-2.17x** and evaluation success rate improvements of **1.09x-1.27x** across peg-insertion, cable routing, and object relocation tasks.
- Reduced training times to as low as **10.9 minutes** for cable routing, demonstrating progress toward sub-10 minute on-robot learning, and **16.6 minutes** for peg-insertion.
- Improved trajectory-wide normalized Area Under the Curve (nAUC) ratios by up to **2.59x** compared to the SERL baseline, indicating enhanced overall efficiency and robustness of learned policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parallelizing global affine transformations, particularly translations, can significantly multiply the effective training data from limited real-robot interactions, thus accelerating on-robot learning.
> - Careful integration of visual perception, differentiating between invariant egocentric camera views and homography-warped exocentric views, is essential for robust symmetry-based data augmentation.
> - Even with conceptually simple symmetries, creating a 'geometric grid structure' of equivalent states and actions enables DRL policies to learn faster and more robustly from diverse, consistent experiences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of accelerating deep reinforcement learning (DRL) policy training directly on physical robots, a process termed "on-robot learning." This domain is recognized for its potential to allow robots to learn real-world physics and mitigate "sim2real" transfer issues common in simulation-based training. However, on-robot learning is often bottlenecked by extensive wall-clock training times, typically ranging from 20 to 180 minutes for existing state-of-the-art methods. Such durations limit the practical deployment and widespread adoption of learning robots.

Previous research has leveraged symmetry principles to enhance DRL efficiency and performance. Approaches include using invariant transformations for experience replay (e.g., Invariant Transform Experience Replay (ITER)), embedding cyclic or dihedral groups directly into neural network architectures (e.g., Equivariant Q-Learning, SO(2)-Equivariant RL), and applying data augmentation techniques. These methods have demonstrated improvements in sample efficiency and performance but often utilize a limited number of symmetries in their data generation processes.

SymmGrid positions itself within this landscape by introducing a method that aims to "super-scale" data augmentation through parallelized, branched symmetries. It seeks to overcome the limitation of current methods that extract a restricted number of symmetries from interactions. By generating a significantly larger and more diverse set of symmetric equivalences, SymmGrid aims to further reduce wall-clock training times and enhance policy performance, pushing towards sub-10 minute on-robot learning. The work extends preliminary concepts of accelerating on-robot learning with fractal symmetries, specifically focusing on a constant branching strategy and providing extensive real-robot evaluations, including complex visual scene handling for both egocentric and exocentric setups.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly accelerate wall-clock training times for deep reinforcement learning policies directly on physical robots while simultaneously improving policy performance and robustness. The motivation stems from the recognition that current on-robot learning approaches, despite their advantages in capturing real-world physics, are impractically slow for rapid deployment and wide adoption of robotic systems. Training times of 20-180 minutes are identified as a major bottleneck.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Accelerate On-Robot Learning Speed:** To reduce the wall-clock training convergence times for DRL policies in physical robots, aiming for a substantial reduction towards sub-10 minute learning in manipulation tasks. This addresses the practical barrier for deploying learning robots in dynamic environments.
- **Improve Policy Performance and Robustness:** To not only speed up learning but also enhance the final success rates and overall efficiency of the learned policies, as measured by metrics such as normalized Area Under the Curve (nAUC). This ensures that accelerated learning does not compromise the quality of the learned behavior.
- **Super-Scale Data Augmentation:** To develop a method that can generate a large quantity of diverse and consistent training experiences by leveraging parallelized symmetries. This addresses the limitation of existing symmetry-based DRL methods that typically exploit only a few symmetries, thereby maximizing the utility of each real-world interaction.
- **Handle Diverse Visual Perception Setups:** To effectively integrate this data augmentation framework with both egocentric (robot-mounted cameras where the scene appears invariant under certain transformations) and exocentric (fixed-world cameras where the scene changes with robot transformations) visual perception. This requires specific solutions, such as homographies for exocentric views, to ensure consistency in transformed states.
- **Conceptual Simplicity and Ease of Implementation:** To design a framework that, despite its potential for super-scaling, remains conceptually straightforward and relatively easy to integrate into existing DRL pipelines, facilitating broader adoption.

The research is motivated by the fundamental role of symmetry in physics and its potential to yield invariant or equivariant representations that can accelerate learning. By modeling a Markov Decision Process (MDP) under a "symmetry tree" and applying affine transformations that create a "geometric grid structure" of symmetric equivalences, the authors aim to populate replay buffers with a rich set of experiences. This is hypothesized to overcome the data scarcity issue in real-world robot interactions and enable faster, more robust learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SymmGrid introduces a trajectory-level augmentation framework built upon parallelized symmetries. The core methodology involves transforming original robot trajectories into a multitude of symmetric equivalents, which are then used to populate the DRL replay buffer.

**A. Branched Markov Decision Processes (MDPs):**
The work models an MDP as a tree where episodic trajectories, comprising state-action pairs, are encoded as vertices and edges. A global affine transformation, $\sigma(\cdot)$, is applied to entire contact-rich feasible trajectories. This transformation is designed to be reward-invariant and dynamics-invariant, decomposing into state transformations $\sigma_S(\cdot)$ and action transformations $\sigma_A(\cdot)$.
Specifically, the symmetry operator is defined as a translation: $\sigma_t(x) = I(x) + t_k$, where $I$ is the identity transformation and $t_k$ is a translation vector. This applies to both states $s'_i = \sigma_S(s_i)$ and actions $a'_i = \sigma_A(a_i)$. For the experiments, state transformations were limited to pure translations of positional components (e.g., end-effector position $x_{eff}$), while orientation, velocities, wrenches, and gripper positions remained unchanged. Action transformations were applied trivially, meaning the same control inputs are executed but from the translated states.

**B. Branching Geometric Structure and Affine Global Transformations:**
The transformations are designed to produce a $K \times K$ grid geometric structure of symmetric states and actions, including the original trajectory. These transformations are "global," meaning the agent and objects are transformed identically. A "constant branching strategy" $b_{const}(0)$ is adopted, generating parallelized symmetries.
To optimize wall-clock efficiency, all transformations across the $K \times K$ grid are computed in parallel. The translations $t_{(k,k)}$ are along the robot's base xy-plane and constrained within a "Symmetry Workspace" (SymWS), which is centered at the robot's end-effector position. The branch positioning is controlled by indices $k_x, k_y$ into the grid, the branch index $b_{i,j}$, and the total branch count $b_c_h$ for that depth. The total generated dataset, $\sigma(\Gamma)$, is inserted into the replay buffer $B$.

**C. Visual Perception Handling:**
The state representation includes RGB images and proprioception information (pose, twist, wrench, finger information).
- **Egocentric Visual Setups:** For wrist-mounted cameras, the visual scenes remain invariant under the applied translational symmetries, as the camera moves with the robot. To avoid storing redundant images, a pointer-based system is used in the replay buffer, referencing the original image for all its symmetric transformations.
- **Exocentric Visual Setups:** For fixed global cameras, the scene changes perspective when the robot's state is transformed. Homographies are employed to warp the original global images to match their expected views after a symmetric transformation.
 - **Homography Implementation:** A homography $H(\Delta)$ is a projective mapping using a calibration matrix $M$ and a planar transformation matrix $T(\Delta)$. For SymmGrid, custom homographies $H_b(\Delta)$ are pre-computed using the known translation information. To improve computational efficiency, a "backward mapping" is used (determining which input pixel maps to an output pixel), and the `remap()` function is applied at sampling time. Instead of storing $K^2$ modified images, only the original image is stored, and transformations are computed in place on demand. An index value is appended to the replay buffer to identify the correct transformation. Edge pixel replication is used for filling pixels outside the original image boundary after warping.

**D. DRL Algorithm and Baseline:**
SymmGrid is integrated with Sample Efficient Reinforcement Learning (SERL), which uses Reinforcement Learning with Prior Data (RLPD). RLPD leverages both demonstration data (20-30 demonstrations) and online data, managed in separate replay buffers, and operates with asynchronous actor and learner processes. A low-level impedance controller runs at 1K Hz. Sparse rewards are used, either position-based (peg-insertion) or image-based binary classifiers (cable-routing, object-relocation).

**E. Experimental Setup:**
- **Robot Platform:** Franka FR3 research robot.
- **Tasks:** Peg-insertion, cable routing, and object relocation, all involving contact-rich manipulation.
- **Vision Backbone:** Pre-trained ResNet-10, connected to proprioception and processed via a 2-layer MLP for policy and Q-network.
- **Action Space:** 6D or 7D end-effector delta pose, with angular updates capped to primarily allow z-axis rotations.
- **Hyperparameters:** Same as SERL, except for SymmGrid-specific parameters (e.g., $K \times K$ grid size, Symmetry Workspace width, replay buffer size). A 27x27 grid size, 0.3m SymWS width, and 3.6 million entry replay buffer were used for peg-insertion and cable-routing, with variations for object relocation.
- **Computational Hardware:** AMD Ryzen Threadripper 1950x, 128 GB RAM, NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU.

**F. Performance Measurement:**
Three metrics are used:
- **Efficiency:** Wall-clock training time to convergence.
- **Performance:** Evaluation success rate.
- **Trajectory-wide comparison:** Normalized Area Under the Curve (nAUC) and nAUC ratios across methods, providing an overall measure of efficiency and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive training and evaluation results conducted directly on real robot manipulation tasks, comparing SymmGrid against the SERL baseline.

**A. Overall Performance Summary (Abstract):**
- **Wall-clock training convergence speedups:** 1.37x-2.17x.
- **Evaluation success rate improvements:** 1.09x-1.27x.
- **Fastest training convergence times:** 16.6, 10.9, and 79.3 minutes for peg-insertion, cable routing, and object relocation, respectively.
- **Trajectory-wide nAUC ratios:** Up to 2.59x improvement.

**B. Peg-Insertion Experiments (Egocentric Visual Setup):**
- **Training Convergence (Fig. 3a):** SymmGrid converged to 100% accuracy in 14.77 minutes (fastest) and averaged 18.5 minutes, compared to SERL's average of 26 minutes. This represents a 1.41x average speedup (28.8% reduction in training time).
- **Evaluation Success Rate (Fig. 3b):** SymmGrid achieved 90% success rate in 15.9 minutes (average), while SERL took 20.13 minutes, a 26.6% speedup. SymmGrid reached 100% success at 27 minutes.
- **nAUC (Trajectory-wide):** SymmGrid's nAUC was 0.607, versus SERL's 0.499, a 1.22x increase up to 3500 steps.

**C. Cable-Routing Experiments (Egocentric Visual Setup):**
- **Training Convergence (Fig. 4a):** SymmGrid reached 100% accuracy in 11.9 minutes (fastest) and averaged 98% in 22.8 minutes. SERL did not reach 98% within the 25-minute evaluation window, peaking at an average of 74.8%. SymmGrid achieved SERL's 78.9% success rate in 11.0 minutes, a 117.3% speedup.
- **Evaluation Success Rate (Fig. 4b):** SymmGrid achieved 98% success as fast as 10.9 minutes and 90% in 15.9 minutes. SERL took 20.13 minutes for 90%, a 1.27x speedup. SymmGrid reached 99.2% success at 18.5 minutes, which SERL did not achieve within the allotted time.
- **nAUC (Trajectory-wide):** SymmGrid's nAUC was 0.63, versus SERL's 0.58, a 9.0% increase over 6000 steps.

**D. Object-Relocation Experiments (Egocentric and Exocentric Visual Setup with Homographies):**
- **Homography Calibration:** Using 20 non-collinear points for a 640x480 image, 16 RANSAC inliers yielded an RMSE of 1.387 px and maximum inlier error of 2.240 px, indicating consistent shifts. Visualizations (Fig. 5) show successful warping but also some artifact formation.
- **Training Convergence (Forward Model, Fig. 6a):** SymmGrid's training converged to 92% accuracy in 79.3 minutes (fastest) and averaged 94.6 minutes, while SERL reached the same value on average in 130.0 minutes. This is a 35.4 minute difference or 37.4% speedup. At 100 minutes, SymmGrid achieved an average success rate of 93.7% versus SERL's 60.0%. In terms of sample steps, SymmGrid reached 60% success at 5851 steps, a 70% improvement over SERL.
- **Evaluation Success Rate (Single checkpoint at 10,000 steps):** For the forward model, SymmGrid achieved 99.3% success compared to SERL's 74.7% (a 33.0% gain). For the backward model, SymmGrid achieved 95.3% versus SERL's 79.3% (a 20.2% gain).
- **nAUC (Trajectory-wide):** SERL achieved an nAUC of 0.1911, while SymmGrid achieved 0.494, a 2.59x ratio.
- **Ablation Studies:** Object relocation showed that optimal performance was achieved with fewer constant branches (3 or 9) and a narrower symmetric workspace (0.175 m), in contrast to the 27 branches used for egocentric tasks. This is attributed to challenges with homographies, parallax, and artifacts in exocentric scenes with multiple depths.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 7):** The robot exhibited emergent behaviors beyond simple pinching for a soft, compressible ball, including sliding, pressing and dragging, and combinations thereof. The system also demonstrated resilience to camera blockages and object displacement.

**E. Hardware Note:** SymmGrid achieved these results using an NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU, which has significantly less computational capacity (FP32 TFLOPs and bandwidth) than the RTX 4090 used by SERL, highlighting the efficiency gains of SymmGrid.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SymmGrid introduces a novel paradigm for accelerating reinforcement learning by leveraging branched symmetries for data augmentation. The work demonstrates that applying a conceptually simple framework of parallelized global transformations to generate symmetric trajectory equivalences can yield substantial improvements in on-robot learning.

The key significance and potential impacts are:

- **Dramatic Acceleration of On-Robot Learning:** SymmGrid significantly reduces the wall-clock training times for robotic manipulation tasks, achieving speedups of 1.37x to 2.17x over state-of-the-art methods. The recorded convergence times (as low as 10.9 minutes for cable routing) bring on-robot learning closer to the practical "sub-10 minute" threshold for robot and humanoid manipulation tasks. This acceleration directly addresses a major bottleneck preventing wider adoption of DRL in real-world robotics.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance and Robustness:** Beyond just speed, SymmGrid consistently improved evaluation success rates (1.09x-1.27x) and overall trajectory-wide performance (up to 2.59x nAUC ratio), indicating that the augmented data is not merely increasing quantity but also quality and diversity of training experiences. This leads to more robust and higher-performing policies.
- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** By enabling faster direct learning on physical robots, SymmGrid reduces the reliance on simulation and helps robots learn directly from real-world contact physics. The observation of emergent, nuanced manipulation behaviors (e.g., sliding, pressing, pinching for a soft ball) that would be challenging to model accurately in simulation underscores the value of real-robot training.
- **Efficient Data Augmentation:** The "super-scaling" approach of generating a large grid of symmetric equivalences from each physical interaction maximizes the utility of costly real-robot data. The method's ability to handle both egocentric and exocentric visual inputs, including the efficient use of homographies for the latter, makes it versatile for different robotic setups.
- **Lowering Barriers to Entry:** The combination of significant speedups and improved performance can lower the technical and resource barriers for researchers and practitioners to deploy DRL on physical robots, potentially fostering innovation in various domains requiring complex robot manipulation.

While the framework exhibits high efficiency, the research also highlights a limitation regarding exocentric cameras in complex scenes. Parallax, interpolation, and border artifacts introduced by homographies can reduce the validity and diversity of augmented data, especially with very large symmetry grids. This suggests areas for future research, such as more sophisticated warp mechanisms or integrating depth information for enhanced 3D consistency. Despite this, SymmGrid represents a notable advancement in making on-robot DRL a more feasible and effective approach for real-world robotic applications.
