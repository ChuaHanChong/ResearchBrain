# When Silence Is Golden: Can LLMs Learn to Abstain in Temporal QA and Beyond?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST, Tongji University, and University of Tübingen introduce a two-stage training pipeline combining Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning with Reinforcement Learning to teach Large Language Models to abstain in temporal question answering. This approach enabled a Qwen2.5-1.5B model to achieve superior Exact Match scores, surpassing GPT-4o by up to 5.80% on the TimeQA-Hard benchmark, by fostering both accurate temporal reasoning and honest uncertainty recognition.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate confident but incorrect responses (hallucinations) rather than acknowledging uncertainty, which compromises their reliability in sensitive applications.
- Temporal Question Answering (QA) presents unique challenges due to evolving events, dynamic timelines, and ambiguous temporal expressions, making it difficult for LLMs to determine answerability and correctly abstain.
- Existing abstention methods, such as calibration or prompting, often prove insufficient for complex reasoning tasks like temporal QA and may require extensive additional supervision, limiting their generalization.

## Method

- A two-stage training paradigm is proposed, starting with ==Chain-of-Thought== (CoT) Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to teach explicit reasoning steps, serving as a 'cold start'.
- This SFT-initialized model is then further optimized using Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), leveraging a rule-based reward system.
- The reward system is designed to encourage correct answers, explicit format adherence, and honest abstention ('No Answer') when questions are unanswerable, while penalizing hallucinations and incorrect abstentions.

## Results

- The RL-trained Qwen2.5-**1.5B**-Instruct model achieved the highest Exact Match scores, surpassing GPT-4o by **3.46%** on TimeQA-Easy and **5.80%** on TimeQA-Hard.
- Time-relevant sub-context extraction often outperformed using the full original context, and negative examples within prompts encouraged abstention, increasing both true and false positives.
- Abstention capabilities showed limited generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, with RL-trained models exhibiting overconfidence, indicating that generalizing abstention across domains remains a challenge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining CoT-SFT with subsequent RL training is crucial for effectively teaching LLMs both temporal reasoning and the ability to abstain, significantly outperforming SFT alone.
> - Smaller LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) can achieve state-of-the-art performance in complex reasoning and abstention tasks when initialized with explicit CoT guidance and fine-tuned with RL, challenging the sole reliance on model scale.
> - While SFT can improve overall QA performance, it tends to induce overconfidence and harm abstention reliability; RL, when properly configured, is more effective at cultivating genuine uncertainty recognition and honest abstention, though it remains sensitive to task complexity and hyperparameters.
