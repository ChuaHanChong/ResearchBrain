# When Silence Is Golden: Can LLMs Learn to Abstain in Temporal QA and Beyond?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKUST, Tongji University, and University of Tübingen introduce a two-stage training pipeline combining Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning with Reinforcement Learning to teach Large Language Models to abstain in temporal question answering. This approach enabled a Qwen2.5-1.5B model to achieve superior Exact Match scores, surpassing GPT-4o by up to 5.80% on the TimeQA-Hard benchmark, by fostering both accurate temporal reasoning and honest uncertainty recognition.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate confident but incorrect responses (hallucinations) rather than acknowledging uncertainty, which compromises their reliability in sensitive applications.
- Temporal Question Answering (QA) presents unique challenges due to evolving events, dynamic timelines, and ambiguous temporal expressions, making it difficult for LLMs to determine answerability and correctly abstain.
- Existing abstention methods, such as calibration or prompting, often prove insufficient for complex reasoning tasks like temporal QA and may require extensive additional supervision, limiting their generalization.

## Method

- A two-stage training paradigm is proposed, starting with ==Chain-of-Thought== (CoT) Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to teach explicit reasoning steps, serving as a 'cold start'.
- This SFT-initialized model is then further optimized using Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), leveraging a rule-based reward system.
- The reward system is designed to encourage correct answers, explicit format adherence, and honest abstention ('No Answer') when questions are unanswerable, while penalizing hallucinations and incorrect abstentions.

## Results

- The RL-trained Qwen2.5-**1.5B**-Instruct model achieved the highest Exact Match scores, surpassing GPT-4o by **3.46%** on TimeQA-Easy and **5.80%** on TimeQA-Hard.
- Time-relevant sub-context extraction often outperformed using the full original context, and negative examples within prompts encouraged abstention, increasing both true and false positives.
- Abstention capabilities showed limited generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, with RL-trained models exhibiting overconfidence, indicating that generalizing abstention across domains remains a challenge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining CoT-SFT with subsequent RL training is crucial for effectively teaching LLMs both temporal reasoning and the ability to abstain, significantly outperforming SFT alone.
> - Smaller LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) can achieve state-of-the-art performance in complex reasoning and abstention tasks when initialized with explicit CoT guidance and fine-tuned with RL, challenging the sole reliance on model scale.
> - While SFT can improve overall QA performance, it tends to induce overconfidence and harm abstention reliability; RL, when properly configured, is more effective at cultivating genuine uncertainty recognition and honest abstention, though it remains sensitive to task complexity and hyperparameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities across numerous Natural Language Processing tasks. However, a documented limitation is their tendency to generate confident but incorrect responses, often referred to as hallucinations, rather than acknowledging uncertainty or abstaining from answering. This behavior compromises reliability, particularly in high-stakes applications such as medicine and law. Efforts to mitigate this include calibration techniques, training adaptations, prompting strategies, and consistency-based methods. While these approaches have shown some success in general Question Answering (QA) tasks, they often struggle with complex reasoning or necessitate extensive additional supervision, which can limit their generalization.

The challenges are further amplified in temporal QA, a domain that requires models to reason over evolving events, dynamic timelines, and frequently ambiguous temporal expressions. Unlike static factual queries, temporal questions often involve information that changes over time, making the determination of answerability complex and necessitating a robust abstention capability. Previous research has largely not systematically explored LLM abstention specifically within the context of temporally sensitive QA, leaving a gap in understanding how LLMs handle uncertainty and contradictions in dynamic temporal environments.

This work addresses this gap by presenting the first empirical study focused on training LLMs to abstain in temporal QA. It frames abstention as a trainable skill, moving beyond purely calibration-based approaches which may not capture uncertainty in complex reasoning effectively. By investigating various information types and training methodologies, including the integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision with Reinforcement Learning (RL), the research aims to provide a foundational understanding of how abstention and temporal reasoning can be jointly optimized in LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically analyze the influence of different information types, model scales, and training strategies on LLMs' temporal reasoning capabilities, specifically when coupled with an abstention mechanism. The authors explicitly state their goal is not merely to develop a superior model, but to achieve a deeper understanding of these underlying factors.

The motivation for this study stems from several observations regarding current LLM limitations:
- **Prevalence of Hallucinations**: LLMs frequently produce fluent but erroneous answers, even when a question is unanswerable or supporting information is insufficient. This lack of uncertainty awareness undermines their reliability and presents risks in sensitive applications.
- **Challenges in Temporal Reasoning**: Temporal QA inherently involves dynamic facts and evolving contexts. Questions often become unanswerable due to outdated information, contradictions, or ambiguous temporal scopes. Current LLMs, exemplified by GPT-4o's performance on the unanswerable subset of TimeQA, demonstrate significant difficulty in identifying and correctly abstaining from such queries. For instance, models might incorrectly answer a temporal question if they fail to account for a time-sensitive event, such as a divorce, that renders the original relationship invalid for the specified period.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Abstention Methods for Complex Reasoning**: While methods like calibration, training adaptations, and prompting have been applied to improve LLM refusal capabilities, their effectiveness in complex reasoning domains like temporal QA is largely unexplored. Calibration may not reliably capture uncertainty in intricate reasoning processes, and many training-based approaches require substantial additional supervision, limiting their generalization. The ambiguity and potential contradictions inherent in temporal expressions make abstention decisions particularly challenging in this setting.
- **Framing Abstention as a Teachable Skill**: The authors motivate an approach that conceptualizes abstention not just as a post-hoc calibration problem, but as a skill that can be directly taught to LLMs through specialized training pipelines. This includes leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision to guide reasoning and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with tailored reward functions to encourage honesty and discourage hallucinations.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to contribute towards building more reliable LLMs that can effectively reason about time-sensitive information while possessing the crucial ability to acknowledge their limitations and abstain when appropriate.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This study defines the task as temporal Question Answering with abstention, where an LLM must either generate the correct answer or explicitly abstain ("No Answer") if the question is unanswerable given a question, context, and auxiliary information. The methodology encompasses various information extraction techniques and a two-stage training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Implicit Reasoning Information Extraction

This category involves providing contextual signals that indirectly assist the LLM in reasoning without explicit step-by-step guidance.
- **Time-related Information Extraction**: For each question `q` with a timestamp `t_q` and a background context `c`, a time-related sub-context `tc` is extracted. This process utilizes GPT-4o-mini with a specific time-filtering prompt designed to identify and retain only sentences or facts relevant to the temporal scope of `q`. The aim is to reduce noise from temporally irrelevant information.
- **Knowledge Graph (KG) Extraction**: Temporal KG facts, represented as quadruples `(h, r, t, τ)` (head entity, relation, tail entity, timestamp), are retrieved from the context `c` using the GPT-4o API. Two strategies are employed for retrieving top-k relevant KG facts:
 - **Semantic Similarity Retrieval (Faiss)**: KG quadruples are converted into natural language sentences, and these, along with the question, are encoded into a shared vector space using Sentence-BERT. Faiss is then used for efficient retrieval of semantically similar KG facts.
 - **Keyword Lexical Matching (KeyBERT)**: KeyBERT extracts salient keywords from the question, which are then matched against components of each KG quadruple. Quadruples are scored based on keyword overlap, and the top-matching ones are selected.
 Retrieved KGs are subsequently rephrased into natural language for LLM input.

#### 3.2. Explicit Reasoning Information Extraction (Chain-of-Thought, CoT)

For explicit reasoning guidance, the study leverages Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting.
- The GPT-o1 API is used to generate paired reasoning outputs `{r, â}` for each question `q` and context `c`, where `r` represents intermediate reasoning steps and `â` is the final answer.
- A "trusted reasoning set" is curated by retaining only samples where the generated answer `â` precisely matches the ground-truth answer `a`. This filtered dataset (2,119 samples for TimeQA-Easy, 1,045 for TimeQA-Hard) serves as high-quality supervision for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- During the **CoT-SFT phase**, the model is trained to generate both the reasoning steps `r` and the final answer `a` from the expert model, thereby learning a step-by-step reasoning process. This SFT model then serves as a "cold start" for subsequent Reinforcement Learning.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training Details

The SFT-initialized model is further optimized using Reinforcement Learning.
- **Algorithm**: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is utilized. This approach samples a group of outputs for a given question and optimizes the policy by maximizing an objective function. This function considers rewards, advantage, and a KL divergence penalty to a reference model, preventing the new policy from deviating too far.
- **Reward Modeling (Rule-Based)**: A two-component, rule-based reward system is designed to encourage honesty and format adherence:
 - **Format Reward (`R_format`)**: Awards 0.5 for correctly enclosing the thinking process within `<think>...</think>` tags and the final answer within `<answer>...</answer>` tags; otherwise, 0.
 - **Answer Reward (`R_ans`)**:
 - 1.0 if the model generates "No Answer" (`o = No Answer`) when the ground truth is also "No Answer" (`a = No Answer`), incentivizing honest abstention.
 - `Rouge-L(o, a) + EM(o, a)` if both `o` and `a` are actual answers, rewarding accuracy.
 - 0 otherwise, penalizing hallucinations (producing an answer when `a = No Answer`) and over-abstaining (producing "No Answer" when `a` exists).
 - The final reward `R` is the sum of `R_format` and `R_ans`.
- **Training Procedure**: The model undergoes CoT-SFT for one epoch, followed by GRPO training for three epochs.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines**: A range of open-source (Qwen2.5 variants, Llama3.2-3B) and closed-source (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, o4-mini, Qwen3-4B-Thinking) LLMs are included. A classifier baseline is also used to predict answerability.
- **Datasets**:
 - **Temporal QA**: TimeQA (Easy and Hard subsets), designed for time-sensitive questions with both answerable and unanswerable examples.
 - **Non-Temporal (Out-Of-Distribution, OOD) QA**: MMLU, HellaSwag, and SQuAD v2. MMLU and HellaSwag are modified to include artificial unanswerable questions to evaluate generalization of abstention.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: ROUGE (R-1, R-2, R-L) for lexical overlap, BERTScore (BS) for semantic similarity, and Exact-Match (EM) for precision. For abstention, True Positive (TP), False Positive (FP), and False Negative (FN) rates for unanswerable questions are reported.
- **Training Settings**: Full-parameter SFT (2 epochs, learning rate 1e-5), and RL with GRPO (CoT-SFT pre-training for 1 epoch, then GRPO for 3 epochs with specific hyperparameters).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. LLM Variants and Implicit Information (Direct Inference)

- Initial direct inference (INF) without context demonstrates low performance across both open- and closed-source models, even for GPT-4o, indicating the inherent difficulty of temporal QA without explicit guidance. Model performance generally correlates with size.
- Providing the original context (`c`) significantly improves ROUGE and EM on TimeQA-Easy, but improvements are less pronounced on TimeQA-Hard. Reasoning-capable models (e.g., o4-mini, Qwen3-4B-Thinking) exhibit stronger performance.
- Using time-relevant sub-context (`tc`) often outperforms using the full original context (`c`), particularly on TimeQA-Easy, suggesting that filtering irrelevant temporal information is beneficial.
- Knowledge Graphs (KGs) are less effective than direct contextual input. Extraction from whole contexts performs slightly better than chunked contexts, possibly due to continuity preservation. Semantic (Faiss) and lexical (KeyBERT) KG retrieval yield comparable results.

#### 4.2. RL vs. SFT Training

- **RL with CoT-SFT Cold Start**: A Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model, initialized with CoT-SFT and then fine-tuned with RL, achieves the highest Exact-Match scores. It surpasses GPT-4o by 3.46% on TimeQA-Easy and 5.80% on TimeQA-Hard. This indicates that RL, when combined with explicit CoT guidance, can significantly enhance reasoning and abstention capabilities even in smaller models.
- **Importance of CoT-SFT**: RL training on a base model without CoT-SFT initialization fails, underscoring that CoT-SFT is crucial for eliciting core reasoning abilities and providing a suitable starting point for RL.
- **SFT Limitations**: Supervised Fine-Tuning alone, even with full-parameter tuning on larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) using contexts or KGs, generally performs worse than the RL-trained 1.5B model. SFT tends to improve ROUGE scores but may decrease EM, implying a tendency towards producing similar rather than exact answers.

#### 4.3. Abstention Performance and Reliability

- **SFT and Overconfidence**: SFT models, while trained for abstention, exhibit overconfident behavior. They reduce False Positives (incorrect abstentions) but also decrease True Positives (correct abstentions) and increase False Negatives (hallucinations), indicating that SFT improves answer coverage more than genuine uncertainty recognition.
- **RL and Contrasting Abstention Patterns**: On TimeQA-Easy, RL significantly enhances honesty by increasing True Positives and reducing hallucinations. However, on TimeQA-Hard, this trend reverses; the model avoids abstention and shows a sharp increase in False Negatives, suggesting that correctly abstaining in more complex temporal reasoning scenarios remains challenging for the model.

#### 4.4. Effects of Various Prompt Designs

- Negative examples within prompts (NEGATIVE PROMPT) encourage the model to abstain more frequently, leading to increases in both True Positives and False Positives, while reducing False Negatives. This implies a hint towards abstention but also an increased risk of over-prudence.
- Positive and Contrastive prompts exhibit similar performance patterns.

#### 4.5. Effects of the Number of KGs

- An increase in the number of KGs provided to the model during inference results in less frequent abstention (a dramatic drop in False Positives). This suggests that more KGs make the model more "confident," but presents a trade-off as it may degrade abstention capability even if it enhances overall QA performance.

#### 4.6. Effects of Hyperparameters

- **KL Coefficient (β)**: The study confirms that the model's performance is sensitive to the `β` hyperparameter in GRPO, with lower values potentially yielding better results, consistent with findings in related works.
- **Reward Design**: The specific composition of the answer reward (`R_ans`) impacts performance. Using ROUGE alone hurts performance, especially on harder cases. Replacing EM with BERTScore can lower accuracy on easier tasks. Combining ROUGE, BERTScore, and EM can assist on challenging tasks by providing complementary signals, but may degrade performance on simpler ones where EM is sufficient.
- **Data Distribution**: Imbalanced datasets, where unanswerable questions are rare (e.g., 12.4% in TimeQA-Easy), lead models to largely ignore abstention. Conversely, artificially balancing the dataset (e.g., 50% unanswerable) can cause the model to collapse into always abstaining, demonstrating the critical need for careful data distribution and reward tuning.

#### 4.7. Generalization Analysis

- **TimeQA to OOD QA**: Models trained on TimeQA-Easy with SFT show a partial transfer of abstention ability to non-temporal OOD tasks (MMLU, HellaSwag, SQuAD v2), indicated by consistent improvements in true positives. This effect is weaker when trained on TimeQA-Hard. However, RL training on TimeQA datasets induces *overconfidence* in OOD tasks, with the models rarely abstaining correctly. This indicates that generalizing abstention ability across domains is challenging.
- **Between TimeQA-Easy and Hard**: Under RL, training on TimeQA-Easy proves more effective for performance on TimeQA-Hard than training directly on Hard. This suggests that the 1.5B model may struggle to extract useful reward signals from the more complex Hard set. In contrast, SFT shows that generalizing from Hard to Easy can sometimes outperform in-distribution training, indicating robust supervised signals from harder examples for cross-difficulty generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of natural language processing by providing the first systematic investigation into teaching LLMs to abstain in temporal question answering. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enhancing LLM Reliability and Trustworthiness**: By demonstrating that abstention can be a teachable skill, particularly through a CoT-SFT and RL pipeline, this work offers a pathway to mitigate the critical issue of LLM hallucination. Equipping models with the ability to accurately recognize uncertainty and refuse to answer when appropriate is fundamental for their deployment in high-stakes environments (e.g., medical diagnostics, legal advice) where incorrect yet confident answers can lead to adverse consequences.
- **Empowering Smaller Models**: The finding that a relatively small Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model, when properly initialized with CoT-SFT and trained with RL, can surpass larger, state-of-the-art closed-source models like GPT-4o in temporal QA with abstention, has significant implications for resource-constrained research and deployment. It suggests that architectural scale is not the sole determinant of performance in complex reasoning and reliability tasks, highlighting the importance of effective training methodologies.
- **Nuanced Understanding of Training Paradigms**: The study's detailed analysis reveals critical insights into the effects of different training methods. It establishes that while SFT can enhance general QA performance, it may inadvertently induce overconfidence and harm abstention reliability. Conversely, RL, when carefully designed and initiated with CoT, can be highly effective in cultivating true abstention capabilities, although its performance remains sensitive to task complexity and hyperparameter tuning. These findings inform future LLM training strategies.
- **Guiding Feature Engineering and Prompt Design**: The comparative analysis of implicit (temporal sub-context, KGs) versus explicit (CoT) reasoning cues demonstrates that explicit, step-by-step CoT supervision provides more substantial benefits for joint reasoning and abstention than implicit information alone. This guides developers and researchers in prioritizing their efforts when preparing data or designing prompts for complex tasks.
- **Highlighting Generalization Challenges**: The observed difficulty in generalizing abstention capabilities to out-of-distribution domains, and the contrasting generalization patterns between easy and hard temporal QA subsets, underscore a persistent challenge in LLM development. This points to the necessity for more principled and robust uncertainty-aware training and evaluation strategies that go beyond in-distribution performance metrics to ensure true adaptability and reliability across diverse tasks and domains.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: This work lays a foundation for several avenues of future research, including the development of larger-scale, domain-specific abstention datasets, investigations into multilingual abstention, and the exploration of abstention within more complex interactive and multi-turn reasoning scenarios.

In summary, this research advances the understanding of LLM reliability, offering practical insights and a methodological framework for building more discerning and trustworthy AI systems capable of recognizing their own limits.
