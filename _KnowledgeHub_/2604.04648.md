# From Curiosity to Caution: Mitigating Reward Hacking for Best-of-N with Pessimism

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, "caution," introduces an inference-time pessimistic reward estimate for Best-of-N sampling, effectively preventing reward hacking by penalizing out-of-distribution responses. This approach consistently improves large language model performance on mathematical reasoning tasks, yielding up to a 15.5% increase in true reward accuracy while maintaining monotonic gains with more samples.

## Problem

- Large language models often suffer from "reward hacking" during Best-of-N sampling, where performance initially improves but then degrades for larger numbers of generated samples (N) due to exploitation of reward model imperfections.
- Existing solutions either require constant improvement of inherently fragile reward models or employ overly conservative regularization techniques that penalize genuinely high-quality but atypical responses.
- Reward models, trained on limited human preference data, can learn spurious correlations that are exploited by increasing N, leading to selections that are out-of-distribution (OOD) and of lower true quality.

## Method

- The authors introduce "caution," an inference-time scheme that modifies ==Best-of-N== selection by using a pessimistic reward estimate: the original reward minus a penalty proportional to the uncertainty of that reward estimate.
- Uncertainty is quantified by the prediction error of a lightweight predictor network, trained offline to reconstruct hidden features from a specific, frozen layer of the reward model using in-distribution data.
- At inference, the response with the highest pessimistic reward is selected from the N generated candidates, effectively penalizing responses for which the reward model's internal features are unfamiliar or OOD.

## Results

- Caution successfully mitigated reward hacking on the GSM8K mathematical reasoning dataset, demonstrating monotonically improving performance with increasing N and achieving a **15.5%** improvement over standard Best-of-N's final accuracy at N=512.
- The method proved effective even on out-of-distribution (MATH-500) and out-of-domain (BigBench-Hard) prompts, where a "pessimism-only" variant (prioritizing distributional familiarity) sometimes outperformed the combined reward model and pessimism.
- Ablation studies confirmed the approach's robustness to hyperparameter choices for the pessimism weight and highlighted that using semantically grounded features from the reward model significantly outperforms traditional random network distillation for OOD detection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The principle of curiosity-driven exploration, typically used for intrinsic motivation in online reinforcement learning, can be effectively repurposed for offline pessimistic learning and out-of-distribution detection in language models.
> - Semantic grounding of the uncertainty signal is crucial; distilling features from the reward model itself, rather than random network features, enables the uncertainty to capture task-relevant out-of-distribution patterns.
> - A practical balance can be achieved between mitigating reward hacking and fully leveraging inference-time compute, avoiding the over-conservatism of broad distributional regularization methods.
