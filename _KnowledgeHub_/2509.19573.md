# Chasing Stability: Humanoid Running via Control Lyapunov Function Guided Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework, CLF-RL, enables robust and dynamic running with a full flight phase on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot by integrating Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) and optimized trajectories into reinforcement learning rewards. This approach achieves accurate global reference tracking (lateral error < 0.25m, yaw error < 0.2 radians) and demonstrates strong resilience to external disturbances at speeds up to 2.2 m/s.

## Problem

- Humanoid running involves complex, nonlinear, and hybrid dynamics, making real-time, robust controller design challenging, especially for transient motions and high speeds.
- Classical control methods struggle with unforeseen disturbances and generating diverse, robust behaviors beyond steady-state gaits for full humanoids.
- Reinforcement learning's effectiveness for dynamic locomotion is often hindered by the manual, heuristic design of reward functions, which can lead to unstable training or suboptimal policies.

## Method

- Offline generation of a multi-domain gait library (1.1 m/s to 3.0 m/s) through trajectory optimization, defining dynamically feasible reference trajectories and virtual constraints.
- Integration of ==Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs)== into the reinforcement learning reward function, providing terms for trajectory tracking and enforcing a CLF decay condition to systematically promote stable behavior during training.
- Deployment on the Unitree G1 humanoid using a layered control architecture, combining the learned RL policy with PD controllers for lateral/yaw stabilization and Lidar-based odometry for accurate global reference tracking.

## Results

- The learned policy achieves robust, high-speed running with a full flight phase on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, consistently operating at speeds up to **2.2** m/s both indoors and outdoors.
- A direct comparison showed that integrating the CLF decay condition into the reward function significantly improved the policy's velocity tracking performance, particularly at higher running speeds.
- The robot maintains accurate global reference tracking using onboard sensors, keeping lateral position error below **0.25** meters and yaw error below **0.2** radians, and recovers successfully from forceful external disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) and dynamically feasible trajectories into RL reward functions provides a systematic and principled alternative to heuristic reward engineering, leading to improved learning stability and performance.
> - Explicitly penalizing violations of the CLF decay condition in the reward promotes certifiably stable behavior during training, enhancing the policy's ability to track desired velocities at high speeds.
> - Reinforcement learning, when guided by control-theoretic principles, can generate robust, dynamic motions with complex hybrid phases (e.g., flight) and strong disturbance rejection capabilities for full humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of highly dynamic and robust locomotion for humanoid robots stands as one of the most formidable challenges in robotics. This paper tackles this challenge head-on by situating itself at the critical intersection of classical control theory and modern reinforcement learning, aiming to leverage the strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their individual limitations.

Historically, bipedal locomotion research has been dominated by classical control methods, with early pioneers like Raibert demonstrating impressive hopping and running. More recently, the Hybrid Zero Dynamics (HZD) framework, notably advanced by researchers like Ames and Grizzle, has provided a rigorous theoretical foundation for generating certifiably stable, periodic gaits for bipeds. HZD relies on offline trajectory optimization and virtual constraints, using tools like feedback linearization and Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) for online tracking. While HZD offers strong stability guarantees, its main limitations lie in the difficulty of synthesizing real-time controllers for highly nonlinear and hybrid dynamics, especially for transient motions and robust disturbance rejection, as it primarily optimizes for steady-state periodic orbits. Furthermore, extending HZD to full humanoids with complex contact dynamics and diverse behaviors remains computationally intensive and analytically challenging.

In parallel, Model Predictive Control (MPC) schemes have emerged to address the need for planning and control over a predictive horizon, particularly for contact-rich behaviors. Approaches like Contact Implicit MPC (CI-MPC) and sample-based methods attempt to handle complex contact sequences implicitly or through rollouts. However, these methods are often computationally demanding, making real-time execution on resource-constrained robot hardware difficult, and have yet to consistently achieve robust humanoid running with flight phases.

More recently, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has rapidly gained traction as a dominant paradigm for legged robot control. RL's ability to learn complex, contact-rich behaviors directly from experience, generate diverse motions, operate robustly, and execute lightweight policies on-board has led to significant breakthroughs in quadrupedal and bipedal locomotion, including running. However, RL is not without its drawbacks. A critical challenge is the extensive hand-tuning of reward functions, which can be heuristic, lead to unstable learning, prohibitively long training times, or policies that fail to achieve desired behaviors. Furthermore, policies trained solely in simulation often struggle with sim-to-real transfer due to model mismatch and unmodeled dynamics, necessitating techniques like domain randomization. Some works have attempted to combine pre-computed trajectories (from reduced-order models or full-order HZD) with RL, but often rely on ad-hoc reward shaping to encourage proximity to these trajectories. Another avenue, imitation learning from human motion data, can reproduce demonstrated motions but often falls short in producing dynamically stable steady-state motions with robust tracking capabilities.

This paper positions itself directly against these limitations. It seeks to develop a principled "model-guided" approach that eliminates the need for heuristic RL reward design by embedding the theoretical rigor of CLFs and dynamically feasible trajectories (derived from multi-domain trajectory optimization, which shares conceptual links with HZD) directly into the RL reward signal. By doing so, it aims to achieve:
1. **Systematic Reward Shaping:** Overcoming the "black art" of RL reward design with theoretically grounded stability conditions.
2. **Certifiable Stability:** Injecting stability guarantees from classical control into the RL training process.
3. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** Leveraging RL's capacity to handle disturbances and generate transient behaviors, which classical methods struggle with.
4. **Full Humanoid Locomotion:** Extending beyond bipeds to full humanoids and achieving high-speed running with flight phases.
5. **Practical Deployment:** Focusing on accurate global reference tracking using only on-board sensors, making the system viable for integration into real-world autonomy stacks.

In essence, this work represents a significant step forward in integrating control-theoretic insights into data-driven learning for complex robotic systems, aiming to achieve both the performance and guarantees that have historically been difficult to combine.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust, highly dynamic, and certifiably stable running behaviors on humanoid robots through a novel control framework. This framework, termed CLF-RL, aims to blend the theoretical strengths of nonlinear control with the practical advantages of reinforcement learning.

The motivations driving this work stem from several critical challenges in dynamic humanoid locomotion:

- **Difficulty of Dynamic Control:** Humanoid running involves complex, nonlinear, and hybrid dynamics (alternating between single-support and flight phases), requiring controllers that are both robust to disturbances and precise in execution, operating near the robot's dynamic limits. Designing such controllers using classical methods for real-time operation has proven exceedingly difficult, especially for transient motions.
- **Limitations of Classical Control for Transients and Robustness:** While classical control methods like Hybrid Zero Dynamics (HZD) offer valuable insights into stability and can generate periodic, certifiably stable gaits, they typically optimize for steady-state motions. Their ability to generate robust transient behaviors and reject unforeseen disturbances is inherently limited, as they often rely on fast convergence to pre-defined virtual constraints within continuous domains, which may not always compensate for destabilizing impacts or unexpected perturbations.
- **Challenges in Reinforcement Learning Reward Design:** While RL excels at handling complex dynamics and learning robust, contact-rich behaviors, its effectiveness is highly dependent on well-shaped reward functions. Crafting and tuning these heuristic reward terms is often a time-consuming, trial-and-error process that can lead to unstable learning, suboptimal policies, or prohibitively long training times. There is a strong need for more principled and systematic approaches to reward design.
- **Gap in Current Hybrid Approaches:** Existing hybrid approaches that combine pre-computed trajectories with RL often use ad-hoc methods for integrating trajectory information into the reward. This can still lead to the aforementioned issues with reward shaping and may not fully leverage the stability guarantees offered by the underlying classical control theory.
- **Lack of Full Humanoid Capabilities:** Many prior works on bipedal running focus on simpler bipeds rather than full humanoids, and often struggle with low positional drift for confined environments (like treadmills) or fail to achieve a true flight phase. Furthermore, achieving accurate global reference tracking using only onboard sensors, a critical component for integrating dynamic motions into a full autonomy stack, remains a significant challenge.

To address these motivations, the authors set out with the following key objectives:
1. **Develop CLF-RL:** Introduce a novel approach that embeds Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) and optimized dynamic reference trajectories directly into the reinforcement learning reward function.
2. **Systematize Reward Design:** Eliminate the need for handcrafting and tuning heuristic reward terms by using a principled, CLF-based reward structure.
3. **Promote Certifiable Stability:** Encourage stable behavior during learning by leveraging the mathematical guarantees of CLFs.
4. **Expand Dynamic Capabilities:** Enable humanoid running that explicitly includes both flight and single-support phases, pushing the robot's dynamic limits.
5. **Achieve Robustness and Performance:** Train policies that operate reliably on treadmills and in outdoor environments, demonstrating resilience to external disturbances.
6. **Enable Autonomy Integration:** Develop policies that achieve accurate global reference tracking using only onboard sensors, making a crucial step toward deploying these dynamic capabilities within broader robotic autonomy systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "CLF-RL" framework presented in this paper is a multi-stage, model-guided approach designed to generate robust and high-performance running policies for humanoids. It meticulously combines offline trajectory optimization, control Lyapunov functions (CLFs), and reinforcement learning (RL).

**A. Multi-Domain Trajectory Optimization (Offline Gait Library Generation)**

The process begins with the offline generation of a gait library, spanning forward speeds from 1.1 m/s to 3.0 m/s, along with a standing pose. This is achieved through a multi-domain trajectory optimization routine.
- **Hybrid System Model:** The humanoid robot's locomotion is modeled as a nonlinear hybrid dynamical system with three distinct domains: Single Support Phase (SSP), Double Support Phase (DSP), and Flight Phase (FLT). Each domain is governed by its specific dynamics and constraints.
- **Optimization Formulation:** The problem is cast as a multiple-shooting trajectory optimization. The domain sequence (e.g., SSP followed by FLT for running) is fixed, but the timing spent within each domain is optimized.
- **Periodicity and Impact Invariance:** To generate steady-state gaits, periodicity constraints are enforced. For running, only half of a gait cycle (SSP then FLT) is optimized, with symmetric re-mapping of states and the application of an impact map at the end to ensure smooth transitions between steps.
- **Virtual Constraints:** The optimization incorporates virtual constraints, which are desired end-effector or joint trajectories parameterized by Bezier curves. These constraints are enforced to fit the optimized nodes of the desired outputs. Crucially, position-based virtual constraints are defined relative to the stance foot, providing flexibility.
- **Computational Tools:** The optimization problem is solved using Casadi for symbolic differentiation, Pinocchio for rigid body dynamics, and IPOPT for numerical optimization.
- **Efficient Library Generation:** A "warm-starting" strategy is employed where solutions for faster gaits are used to initialize the optimization for incrementally slower gaits. This significantly speeds up the generation of a comprehensive gait library, preventing convergence to drastically different or suboptimal solutions.

**B. Reinforcement Learning Design with CLF-based Reward (Policy Training)**

The heart of the CLF-RL approach lies in integrating the stability principles from classical control into the RL reward function.
- **CLF-based Tracking Reward:** To promote accurate trajectory tracking and convergence to the optimized steady-state trajectories, Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) are incorporated into the reward. For each joint or end effector, a reduced-order double-integrator system is assumed, allowing for the use of a quadratic CLF, $V(\eta) = \eta^T P \eta$, where $\eta$ is the error between the actual outputs and their virtual constraints.
 - **Tracking Term ($r_v$):** A reward term $r_v = w_v \exp(-\frac{V_t}{\sigma_v})$ exponentially rewards being close to the desired trajectory (low CLF value).
 - **Decay Condition Term ($r_{\dot{v}}$):** A critical term, $r_{\dot{v}} = -w_{\dot{v}} \text{clip}(\frac{\dot{V}_t + \lambda V_t}{\sigma_{\dot{v}}}, 0, 1)$, penalizes the policy if the CLF's derivative does not satisfy the desired decay condition ($\dot{V}_t < -\lambda V_t$). This term directly incentivizes certifiably stable behavior, ensuring convergence to the desired manifold. The derivative $\dot{V}_t$ is approximated using finite differences.
- **Domain-Specific Rewards ($r_{dom}$):** Rewards are designed to encourage the appropriate contact sequence for each hybrid domain.
 - In the Single Support Phase (SSP), $r_{hol}$ rewards the stance foot for remaining stationary (low position and velocity relative to its initial contact point).
 - In the Flight Phase (FLT), $r_{con}$ penalizes contact forces, encouraging the feet to be off the ground.
- **Regularization Rewards ($r_{reg}$):** Standard regularization terms are included to shape the policy:
 - Torque minimization.
 - Action rate minimization (smooth actions).
 - Joint limit penalties (to prevent over-extensions).
 - Torque limit penalties (to prevent motor saturation).
- **Total Reward:** The overall reward function is a weighted sum: $R = r_{reg} + r_v + r_{\dot{v}} + r_{dom}$.
- **Policy Architecture and Training:**
 - Actor and critic networks use fully connected feedforward architectures with ELU activation.
 - **Actor Observations:** Include base angular velocity, projected gravity, commanded velocity, joint angles/velocities, previous action, and sinusoidal phase observations (to track gait cycle progress).
 - **Critic Privileged Information:** Provides additional state information (e.g., foot velocities, reference trajectories, contact states) to stabilize value estimation and improve training efficiency without being available to the actor during deployment.
 - **Simulation Environment:** Training is performed using IsaacLab and IsaacSim, leveraging GPU-accelerated physics simulation.
 - **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for policy training.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Critical for sim-to-real transfer, various physical parameters are randomized during training (e.g., COM position, additional torso mass, PD gains, restitution, friction, joint armatures) along with periodic velocity impulses. This exposes the policy to a wide range of conditions, making it more robust.
 - **Gait Library Integration:** The commanded forward body velocity ($v_{d_x}$) is used to select the closest speed gait from the offline-generated library, which then provides the virtual constraints for the CLF-based reward.
 - **Velocity Commands:** The policy learns to respond to lateral ($v_{d_y}$) and yaw ($\omega_{d_z}$) velocity commands by dynamically adjusting stance and swing foot locations/orientations and the center of mass position.

**C. Straight Running (Deployment Architecture)**

For real-world deployment on the Unitree G1 humanoid, particularly for tasks like treadmill running that demand precise lateral control, a layered control architecture is implemented:
- **PD Control Layer:** Independent Proportional-Derivative (PD) controllers are used to regulate lateral motion and heading (yaw angle), driving them to zero.
- **Odometry:** Lidar-based odometry (Fast-LIO) provides real-time position and yaw estimates. Velocity estimates are smoothed with a moving average filter.
- **Command Integration:** The outputs of these PD controllers generate lateral and yaw velocity commands, which are then fed as observations to the RL policy. Controller outputs are saturated to stay within the trained limits of the RL policy.
- **Hardware Platform:** The learned policy is deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, with all sensing and computation performed entirely onboard.

This comprehensive methodology, by carefully structuring the problem and integrating control-theoretic principles into the learning process, aims to create a robust and adaptable controller for highly dynamic humanoid running.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings from both simulation and extensive hardware experiments, demonstrating the efficacy and robustness of the CLF-RL framework for humanoid running.

- **Trajectory Tracking Performance in Simulation:**
 - The learned policy effectively tracks the desired virtual constraints derived from the multi-domain trajectory optimization. Even without explicit transient motion data in the gait library, the policy is capable of generating the necessary transient responses to converge smoothly to the steady-state running orbits.
 - While showing generally good tracking, the paper notes that the policy makes small sacrifices in perfect trajectory tracking (e.g., for COM z-axis) in favor of achieving overall robustness, an inherent trade-off in RL for real-world deployment.

- **Benefits of CLF-based Reward:**
 - A direct comparison in Mujoco simulation revealed that the inclusion of the CLF decreasing condition in the reward function (incentivizing $\dot{V}_t < -\lambda V_t$) significantly enhances the policy's velocity tracking performance, particularly at higher running speeds. This empirically validates the theoretical advantage of CLFs, demonstrating that they not only provide a systematic way to design rewards but also lead to superior performance compared to purely heuristic tracking rewards.

- **Robust and Dynamic Running on Hardware:**
 - The policy successfully produces natural and highly dynamic running behaviors on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, consistently achieving a full flight phase where both feet are off the ground. This multi-domain behavior (alternating single-support and flight phases) is a key characteristic of true running and a significant achievement for full humanoids.
 - The robot demonstrates reliable operation in diverse environments, running both indoors on a treadmill and outdoors, highlighting the effectiveness of domain randomization and the learned policy's adaptability.

- **Accurate Global Reference Tracking:**
 - Utilizing only onboard sensors and compute, the robot achieves accurate global position and velocity tracking in outdoor environments. Specifically, the policy can track commanded forward velocities while keeping lateral position error below 0.25 meters and yaw error below 0.2 radians. This precise tracking capability, driven by the layered PD control architecture and Fast-LIO odometry, is a crucial step towards integrating such dynamic locomotion into a full autonomy stack.

- **Disturbance Rejection Capabilities:**
 - The policy exhibits remarkable robustness to external disturbances while running at approximately 2.2 m/s on a treadmill. The robot successfully recovers from forceful impacts from dodge balls (to the torso and leg) and collisions with wooden planks (striking the stance toe, heel, or being kicked by the swing toe). Importantly, this recovery is achieved without any explicit object detection or avoidance mechanisms, showcasing the inherent robustness embedded in the learned control policy.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that the CLF-RL framework enables humanoids to perform high-speed, dynamic running with flight phases, while offering exceptional robustness to disturbances and accurate tracking capabilities—all critical for real-world applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Chasing Stability" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics and autonomous systems, with a broad potential impact across several dimensions:

- **Bridging Classical Control and Reinforcement Learning:** This work represents a powerful synthesis of two distinct, yet complementary, paradigms in robotics. By deeply embedding Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) and dynamically feasible trajectories into the Reinforcement Learning (RL) reward, the authors have effectively bridged the gap between the rigorous stability guarantees of classical nonlinear control and the robustness, adaptability, and complex behavior generation capabilities of modern RL. This integrated approach addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: how to combine the certifiable performance of model-based methods with the learning power of model-free approaches.
- **Systematic and Principled Reward Design:** One of the most significant practical impacts is the elimination of heuristic reward engineering in RL. The CLF-RL framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded method for shaping rewards, which can drastically reduce training time, improve learning stability, and lead to more optimal policies. This could generalize to many other complex dynamic tasks for robots, making RL more accessible and reliable for practical applications.
- **Breakthroughs in Humanoid Dynamics:** Achieving robust, high-speed running with a full flight phase on a full-scale humanoid robot like the Unitree G1 is a major technical accomplishment. Humanoids are inherently more complex than bipeds due to their full-body dynamics, higher degrees of freedom, and the challenges of managing torso and arm movements during highly dynamic gaits. This work pushes the boundaries of what is dynamically possible for these robots, moving them closer to agile, human-like locomotion.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The demonstrated ability to withstand significant external disturbances (e.g., impacts from dodge balls and planks) while maintaining high-speed running highlights an unprecedented level of robustness for humanoid locomotion. This resilience is critical for real-world deployment, where unforeseen interactions and environmental uncertainties are commonplace. The use of domain randomization further underscores the practicality of the approach for achieving sim-to-real transfer.
- **Integration into Autonomy Stacks:** The accurate global position and velocity tracking capabilities, achieved using only onboard sensors (Lidar-based odometry) and compute, are crucial for integrating dynamic locomotion into a full autonomy stack. This means humanoids can now not only move dynamically but also precisely navigate and follow trajectories in unstructured environments, paving the way for more complex and useful autonomous tasks beyond basic locomotion.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The CLF-RL framework is described as "readily extendable to other motions and allows them to be highly optimized for any given robot." This suggests a powerful, generalizable methodology that could be applied to develop controllers for a wide range of complex, dynamic behaviors (e.g., jumping, climbing, manipulation while moving) across different robotic platforms, including other humanoids or even novel legged designs.
- **Advancing the Field of Robotics:** This research contributes significantly to the long-term vision of creating highly capable, autonomous humanoid robots that can operate effectively and robustly in human-centric environments. By demonstrating a robust solution to dynamic running, it sets a new benchmark and inspires further exploration into the combination of theoretical control guarantees with data-driven learning for the next generation of robotic systems.
