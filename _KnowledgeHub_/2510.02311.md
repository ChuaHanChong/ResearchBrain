# Inferring Dynamic Physical Properties from Video Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces PhysVid, a new dataset with quantitative ground-truth annotations for elasticity, viscosity, and dynamic friction across synthetic and real-world videos. The study assesses whether pre-trained video foundation models inherently understand these dynamic physical properties, revealing that while models show promising capabilities on synthetic data, their performance often lags behind physics-informed oracle methods, especially for absolute value prediction and generalization to real-world scenarios without domain adaptation.

## Problem

- Inferring dynamic physical properties like elasticity, viscosity, and friction from visual observations has been a long-standing challenge in computer vision.
- Existing physics prediction methods often rely on simulation supervision or produce implicit representations that lack interpretability in terms of concrete physical quantities.
- There is a scarcity of suitable datasets providing quantitative ground-truth annotations for dynamic physical properties to rigorously evaluate AI models.

## Method

- The authors developed **PhysVid**, a new dataset with quantitative ground-truth annotations for elasticity, viscosity, and friction, across both synthetic and real-world videos.
- They propose oracle methods that leverage domain-specific visual cues and classical computer vision to establish an upper bound for inferable information.
- Pre-trained video foundation models (generative, self-supervised) are probed using a lightweight visual prompting mechanism, and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are evaluated with various text prompting strategies for property inference.

## Results

- Oracle methods achieved near-perfect performance on synthetic data (ROC AUC **0.99**-**1.00**, Pearson Correlation **0.98**-**1.00**) and strong performance on real-world data (ROC AUC **0.87**-**1.00**, Pearson Correlation **0.80**-**0.87**), setting a high benchmark.
- DynamiCrafter and V-JEPA-2 showed strong performance on synthetic datasets (e.g., ROC AUC **1.00**, Pearson Correlation **0.89**-**0.99** for elasticity/viscosity on test-1), but performance for friction and real-world generalization was more challenging, with domain adaptation improving friction performance on real-world test-3 significantly.
- MLLMs generally showed lower performance on synthetic data (Pearson Correlations often near zero) but occasionally performed better on real-world test-3, with GPT-4o and Gemini-**2.5**-pro achieving high ROC AUCs (e.g., **0.99** for viscosity) for relative comparisons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generative (DynamiCrafter) and self-supervised (V-JEPA-2) models can learn useful representations for inferring dynamic physical properties, achieving strong performance on synthetic data.
> - Current video foundation models exhibit a noticeable performance gap compared to physics-informed oracle methods, especially for absolute value prediction and generalizing to real-world scenarios without specific domain adaptation.
> - MLLMs generally perform worse than dedicated video models on quantitative physical tasks but can benefit from tailored prompting strategies, sometimes relying more on semantic cues than direct kinematic motion, particularly in real-world contexts.
