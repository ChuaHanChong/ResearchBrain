# Beyond Binary Success: Sample-Efficient and Statistically Rigorous Robot Policy Comparison

> [!summary] Summary
> N-SCORE introduces a nonparametric sequential framework for rigorously comparing robot policies, enabling efficient decision-making with various performance metrics. It achieves up to 70% reduction in simulation evaluation trials and 45% on hardware compared to batch methods, while robustly controlling Type-1 error rates.

## Problem

- Hardware evaluation for robot policies is resource-intensive, slow, and expensive, limiting the number of trials for robust assessment.
- Traditional binary success/failure metrics are coarse, obscuring detailed robot behavior, and existing sample-efficient evaluation methods are often restricted to narrow parametric metrics, making them unsuitable for generalized or nonparametric performance measures.
- Inherent stochasticity in robot task performance necessitates statistically rigorous comparison methods to avoid false discoveries (Type-1 errors).

## Method

- **N-SCORE** is a ==nonparametric sequential comparison== framework that utilizes a bounded, real-valued ==generalized progress metric== for diverse performance evaluation.
- It employs a ==multiplicative evidence integrator==, based on ==supermartingale theory==, to accumulate evidence for policy superiority with guaranteed ==Type-1 error control==.
- The framework adaptively optimizes a ==time-varying scaling factor (ξ_n)== for the evidence integrator online, maximizing the expected growth rate and improving sample efficiency.

## Results

- Achieved up to **70%** reduction in simulation evaluation trials and **45%** on hardware for policy comparison when using discrete partial credit metrics, compared to batch methods.
- Outperformed existing nonparametric sequential methods (e.g., WSR) by approximately **15%** in average time-to-decision and **5 percentage points** in empirical power on simulated nonparametric data.
- Demonstrated a **20%** total sample complexity improvement over WSR in continuous reinforcement learning benchmarks and saved over **450 trials** in a real-world multi-policy comparison on RoboArena.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalized progress metrics, beyond binary success, significantly enhance the informativeness and sample efficiency of robot policy evaluation.
> - Online optimization of the evidence aggregation rate (ξ_n) based on data distribution is crucial for maximizing sample efficiency in sequential policy comparisons.
> - A supermartingale-based stopping rule provides rigorous, anytime-valid Type-1 error control, ensuring statistical reliability in resource-constrained robotics experiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot policy synthesis has advanced, resulting in increasingly capable generalist manipulation policies. These policies often incorporate complex architectures and training procedures, making analytical assessment of design choices challenging. Empirical performance evaluation is therefore crucial. However, this evaluation faces significant constraints, particularly in real-world robotics.

Hardware evaluation is considered the gold standard but is resource-intensive, slow, and expensive, often limiting trials to a small number (e.g., 10-60). Simulation offers reduced time burden but suffers from sim-to-real gaps, making it an imperfect proxy for real-world performance. Furthermore, conventional evaluation metrics, such as binary task success or failure, can be coarse, obscuring detailed information about robot behavior. For instance, a policy completing 90% of a task might still be labeled a "failure." The inherent stochasticity in environment configurations and policy actions also introduces uncertainty, meaning observed performance is a noisy estimate of true expected performance, necessitating statistically rigorous analysis.

Policy comparison is a particularly important form of evaluation, addressing counterfactual design questions (e.g., comparing policy architectures, data choices, or action tokenizations). Rigorous, statistically assured decision-making is essential for reliable progress in the field, as it helps distinguish genuine improvements from statistical noise.

Existing evaluation methods exhibit various limitations:
- **Active learning and asymptotic approaches** may lack statistical rigor in small-data regimes typical of robotics.
- **Batch and fully nonparametric methods** are not maximally sample-efficient due to their fixed evaluation size or lack of adaptation to the comparison setting.
- **Near-optimal approaches** like STEP and θ-SAVI are effective but often constrained to narrow parametric metrics (e.g., binary success or discrete partial credit) and do not readily generalize to continuous or complex nonparametric measures. Extending these to broader parametric families often becomes computationally intractable.
- **Nonparametric methods** like kernel density estimation (KDE) can represent complex data distributions but may not be valid in finite samples or easily generalizable to sequential settings.
- **Safe, Anytime-Valid Inference (SAVI)** frameworks address sequential evaluation and robustness against "p-hacking." The Waudby-Smith-Ramdas (WSR) method is a related SAVI approach for estimating the mean of general bounded random variables but is not specifically optimized for policy comparison.

This work, N-SCORE (Nonparametric Sequential COmparison for Rigorous Evaluation), aims to address these limitations by introducing a unified framework for robot policy comparison that is sample-efficient, statistically rigorous, and applicable to a broad range of evaluation metrics, including discrete partial credit, continuous rewards, and trajectory smoothness, encompassing both parametric and nonparametric comparison problems. It builds upon SAVI principles to offer a sequential, early-stopping procedure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for comparing robot policies, addressing the increasing complexity of robot policy design and the practical constraints of real-world evaluation.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are:

1. **Addressing Evaluation Resource Constraints:** Modern robot policies are complex, requiring significant resources for training and validation. Hardware evaluation, the most reliable measure, is expensive and slow. This necessitates methods that can extract maximal information from limited trials, minimizing the evaluation burden.
2. **Enhancing Informativeness of Performance Measures:** Traditional binary success/failure metrics often obscure valuable information about robot behavior. A policy that completes a substantial portion of a task but ultimately fails is distinct from one that performs minimally, yet both register as "failure" under a binary scheme. The work aims to support a broader range of informative metrics, such as discrete partial credit scores, continuous episodic rewards, or measures of trajectory quality (e.g., smoothness).
3. **Ensuring Statistical Rigor:** Robot policy performance is inherently stochastic due to variations in environment configurations and policy execution. Observed empirical performance is therefore a noisy estimate of true performance. Any comparison must account for this uncertainty to provide statistically assured decisions, guarding against false discoveries (Type-1 errors). This is crucial for establishing reliable progress in the field.
4. **Maximizing Sample Efficiency through Sequential Evaluation:** Standard batch evaluation methods require a pre-specified number of trials, which may be excessive if a clear difference between policies emerges early. A key objective is to implement sequential testing, allowing evaluators to stop early once sufficient statistical evidence has accumulated to reach a decision at a pre-specified level of confidence (Type-1 error rate). This adaptive approach minimizes unnecessary evaluation trials, optimizing resource allocation.
5. **Achieving Generality across Metric Types:** Existing sample-efficient and rigorous methods are typically confined to narrow, parametric performance metrics (e.g., binary outcomes). A central motivation is to develop a unified approach that is valid for a wide spectrum of generalized progress metrics, from discrete to continuous, and applicable in both parametric and nonparametric settings, without sacrificing statistical rigor or sample efficiency.
6. **Formalizing the Comparison Problem:** The work formally defines the policy comparison problem as testing whether the expected performance of one policy is strictly better than another (H1) against the null hypothesis that it is not (H0). The goal is to design an evaluation procedure that minimizes the expected number of trials (E[N]) and the Type-2 error rate (β, false negatives), subject to a hard constraint on the Type-1 error rate (α*, false positives). This multi-objective optimization problem encapsulates the desiderata of speed and correctness.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of current robot policy evaluation by providing a statistically sound, versatile, and resource-efficient comparison framework that enables reliable decision-making amidst the inherent complexities and resource constraints of robotics research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

N-SCORE (Nonparametric Sequential COmparison for Rigorous Evaluation) is designed as a sequential, statistically rigorous, and sample-efficient framework for comparing robot policies using generalized progress metrics. The methodology is rooted in the principles of Safe, Anytime-Valid Inference (SAVI) and hypothesis testing.

**A. Generalized Progress Metric Definition:**
The framework models robot task completion performance with a real-valued scalar measure, `R`, which is assumed to be a "generalized progress metric." This is defined as a random variable `M(ω)` that is bounded, i.e., `P[M ∈] = 1`. This definition allows for normalization of any bounded real-valued performance measure to the interval. The framework makes no other structural assumptions about the distribution of `R`.

**B. Problem Formulation as Hypothesis Testing:**
The comparison problem is formulated in a frequentist statistical testing context. For two policies, `π0` and `π1`, whose performance outcomes are drawn from distributions `DR` and `DR` respectively, the objective is to test:
`E[R | DR] < E[R | DR]` (i.e., `π1` is better than `π0`).

This translates into formal hypotheses:
- **Null Hypothesis (H0):** `(DR, DR) ∈ S-`, where `S-` includes all pairs of distributions where `E[R | DR] ≥ E[R | DR]`. This represents the skeptical view that `π1` is not better.
- **Alternative Hypothesis (H1):** `(DR, DR) ∈ S+`, where `S+` includes all pairs of distributions where `E[R | DR] < E[R | DR]`. This represents the desired outcome that `π1` is better.

The core requirement is to control the Type-1 Error rate (`α*`), ensuring that the probability of falsely rejecting H0 (concluding `π1` is better when it is not) is at most `α*`. Within this constraint, the goal is to minimize the expected number of samples (`E[N]`) and the Type-2 Error rate (`β`, the probability of failing to reject H0 when H1 is true).

**C. Evidence Integrator (Martingale Construction):**
N-SCORE utilizes a scalar-valued "evidence integrator" `X_n`, which is a martingale process designed to reflect the accumulating evidence for H1. Starting with `X_0 = 1`, the process evolves multiplicatively after each pair of evaluation trials (`r_0,n` for `π0` and `r_1,n` for `π1`):
`X_n+1 = (1 + ξ_n * (r_1,n - r_0,n)) * X_n`

Here, `ξ_n > 0` is a positive scaling factor that modulates the rate of evidence aggregation.
- If `r_1,n > r_0,n` (evidence for `π1` being better), `X_n` grows.
- If `r_1,n < r_0,n` (evidence for `π0` being better), `X_n` shrinks.

The key is that `ξ_n` can be optimized online based on the previously accumulated evidence (the "natural filtration" `F_n-1`), as long as its choice is independent of the current trial's outcomes (`r_0,n`, `r_1,n`).

**D. Stopping Rule:**
The procedure stops early when `X_n` exceeds a pre-defined threshold. The threshold is set to `1/α*`, where `α*` is the desired Type-1 error rate.
- If `X_n ≥ 1/α*`, the procedure stops and rejects H0 (concluding `π1` is better).
- If `X_n < 1/α*` after `N_max` trials, the procedure fails to reject H0.

**E. Theoretical Properties:**

1. **Null Stability Property (Lemma 1):** The paper proves that for any scenario where H0 is true, and for any valid `ξ_n ∈`, the expected value of the evidence integrator `X_n` is contracting or stable with respect to its current value. This `E[X_n+1 / X_n | F_n] ≤ 1` property makes `X_n` a nonnegative supermartingale under H0.
2. **Type-1 Error Control (Theorem 1):** Building on Lemma 1 and Ville's Inequality, the paper demonstrates that the probability of `X_n` exceeding `1/α*` when H0 is true is bounded by `α*`. This formally guarantees that N-SCORE rigorously controls the Type-1 error rate at the specified `α*`. The use of time-varying `ξ_n` (measurable with respect to `F_n-1`) does not violate this control.
3. **Efficient Optimization of `ξ_n` (Remark 1):** This is a critical component for sample efficiency. N-SCORE optimizes `ξ_n` online by leveraging insights from kernel density estimation (KDE). It constructs nonparametric representations of the outcome distributions `D[i]R` by discretizing continuous progress scores into `k` bins. This allows the algorithm to "pretend" the data is from a parametric discrete partial credit setting when choosing `ξ_n`, enabling efficient optimization.
 - **Signal Effect:** `ξ_n` is increased when there is more direct evidence for H1 (i.e., `r_1,n > r_0,n`). This effect is approximately linear in `ξ_n`.
 - **Hysteresis Effect:** `ξ_n` is decreased due to "self-negating" outcomes (e.g., `r_1,n - r_0,n` followed by `r_0,n - r_1,n`), which reduce `X_n` without changing the mean performance difference. This effect is approximately quadratic in `ξ_n`.
 N-SCORE's optimization of `ξ_n` balances these two effects to maximize the expected growth rate of `X_n` when H1 is true, thereby accelerating decision-making.

In essence, N-SCORE continuously monitors the accumulated evidence using a multiplicative martingale, ensures statistical rigor by bounding the Type-1 error through the supermartingale property, and optimizes sample efficiency by adaptively adjusting the evidence aggregation rate based on the observed data distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates N-SCORE through extensive validation on simulated and real-world datasets, addressing three core research questions (RQs).

**A. RQ1 & RQ2: Sequential Evaluation with Informative Metrics**

The experiments demonstrate that sequential testing procedures and the use of more informative evaluation metrics can significantly improve sample efficiency.

1. **Artificial Bernoulli Sequences:**
 - On 35 artificially generated Bernoulli distributions, N-SCORE, STEP, and θ-SAVI all exhibited significantly lower average time-to-decision (TTD) compared to a batch evaluation size of 1000 samples. This supports the benefit of early stopping.
 - N-SCORE, STEP, and θ-SAVI showed comparable statistical power, whereas WSR demonstrated substantially lower power in the Bernoulli regime due to higher variance.

2. **LBM 1.0 Binary Evaluation (Real-World Data):**
 - Using real-world binary success/failure data from the LBM 1.0 study, sequential procedures (N-SCORE, STEP, θ-SAVI, WSR) achieved 25-35% savings in simulation evaluations (reducing ~2000 nominal trials by 500-700) and 16-25% savings in hardware evaluations (reducing ~500 nominal trials by up to 125), compared to batch methods.

3. **Simulated Nonparametric Data:**
 - When comparing policies on data generated from complex nonparametric densities, where only N-SCORE and WSR are applicable, N-SCORE showed improved performance.
 - N-SCORE outperformed WSR by approximately 15% in average TTD and by approximately 5 percentage points in empirical power. This demonstrates N-SCORE's effectiveness in complex, distribution-agnostic settings while maintaining efficiency.

4. **LBM 1.0 Partial Credit Evaluation (Real-World Data):**
 - Re-analyzing the LBM 1.0 data using discrete partial credit metrics (which are more informative than binary success), N-SCORE yielded substantial savings.
 - In high-fidelity simulation, partial credit metrics with N-SCORE led to approximately 70% TTD savings over batch methods, translating to a nearly 1400-sample reduction. This also represented over 50% improvement compared to sequential binary methods like STEP.
 - On hardware trials, N-SCORE achieved approximately 45% TTD reduction over batch and 24-30% reduction compared to state-of-the-art binary procedures (e.g., STEP).
 - These findings empirically support the benefits of using sequential evaluation and fine-grained, informative metrics for faster policy comparison.

**B. RQ3: Improving Sample Complexity Over Baselines**

This section details the performance differences between N-SCORE and other sequential baselines.

1. **Simulated Nonparametric Data:**
 - The moderate TTD gap (around 15% average savings) between N-SCORE and WSR is attributed to N-SCORE's efficient online optimization of `ξ_n`, a mechanism not present in WSR. N-SCORE was able to statistically confirm its own lower TTD compared to WSR in approximately 525 evaluations.

2. **Simulated Bernoulli Data:**
 - In this parametric setting, STEP was identified as optimal. N-SCORE and θ-SAVI performed similarly, indicating N-SCORE's ability to approximate parametric structure effectively. WSR, however, showed substantially higher TTD compared to these methods.

3. **LBM 1.0 Success and Partial Credit:**
 - On real-world LBM 1.0 data, STEP remained optimal for binary success metrics. For both binary and discrete partial credit evaluations on this dataset, N-SCORE, θ-SAVI, and WSR demonstrated comparable effectiveness.

4. **RoboArena (Real-World Multi-Policy Comparison):**
 - In a multi-policy comparison on the RoboArena benchmark using continuous progress scores, N-SCORE distinguished the performance of all four policies. It required over 200 fewer trials for one policy and resulted in total savings of at least 450 trials compared to WSR. WSR failed to separate two policies even after exhausting all available 641 trials. This efficacy is attributed to N-SCORE's optimized `ξ_n`.

5. **Real-Valued Continuous Metrics (Reinforcement Learning):**
 - Comparing various RL algorithms (PPO, TD3, DDPG, SAC) on Mujoco benchmarks using continuous episodic rewards, N-SCORE demonstrated significant improvements over WSR, especially in cases where policies performed similarly but had high variance (e.g., PPO vs. DDPG on InvertedPendulum-v4, saving over 400 trials).
 - Overall, N-SCORE showed an additional 20% improvement in total sample complexity compared to WSR across these RL evaluations.

In summary, N-SCORE effectively matches the sample efficiency of specialized parametric methods (like θ-SAVI) in parametric contexts, while simultaneously outperforming more general nonparametric methods (like WSR) in terms of sample efficiency for complex, nonparametric metrics. It consistently achieves reduced time-to-decision and, in several cases, higher empirical power (ability to separate policies) across diverse evaluation settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for robot policy comparison that holds implications for the development and validation of advanced robotics.

1. **Accelerated Robot Policy Development:** The high cost and time required for hardware evaluation are significant bottlenecks in robotics research. By demonstrating up to 70% reduction in evaluation burden compared to standard batch methods and up to 50% compared to existing sequential binary methods, N-SCORE can significantly accelerate the iteration cycle for robot policy design. This enables researchers to test more design interventions (e.g., new architectures, training procedures, data choices) in less time and with fewer resources.
2. **Enhanced Reliability of Research Findings:** The framework provides statistically rigorous comparisons by controlling the Type-1 error rate at a pre-specified level. This means that reported policy improvements are less likely to be false discoveries resulting from statistical noise, thereby contributing to more reliable and reproducible research outcomes in the field.
3. **Broader Applicability of Evaluation Metrics:** Moving beyond binary success/failure, N-SCORE supports a wide range of informative metrics, including discrete partial credit task progress, continuous episodic rewards, and measures of trajectory quality. This allows researchers to gain a more nuanced understanding of robot behavior and performance, capturing progress even when a task is not fully completed. The empirical evidence suggests that these fine-grained metrics can further improve sample efficiency.
4. **Unified and Versatile Evaluation Tool:** N-SCORE offers a unified approach applicable to both parametric and nonparametric comparison problems. This versatility reduces the need for specialized statistical methods tailored to specific metric types, simplifying the evaluation process for roboticists.
5. **Codification of Best Practices:** The work contributes to the establishment of more rigorous evaluation protocols in robotics. By highlighting the practical benefits of sequential methods and informative metrics, it encourages the adoption of statistically sound procedures, which is critical for systematic progress in a field often characterized by resource-limited empirical studies.

The findings suggest that N-SCORE can become a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners, enabling more efficient and reliable assessment of robot capabilities and progress benchmarking. Future work could further enhance its efficiency by utilizing the full risk budget more tightly and integrating advanced kernel density estimation insights, potentially extending its applicability to unbounded performance measures.
