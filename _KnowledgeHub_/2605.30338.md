# REST3D: Reconstructing Physically Stable 3D Scenes from a Single Image

> [!summary] Summary
> REST3D reconstructs physically stable, simulation-ready 3D scenes from one RGB image. VLM agents infer a gravity-aware scene tree of support relations, image-to-3D meshes are canonicalized against that tree, then Cross-Entropy-Method optimization scores candidate layouts by simulator rollouts under stability, velocity, penetration and layout energies. Stability rises from 8% to 96% on Replica with near-zero collisions.

## Problem

- Single-image reconstruction methods optimize visual fidelity only, producing floating objects and interpenetration that collapse the moment the scene enters a physics simulator.
- Retrieval-based assembly improves plausibility but is bounded by asset-database coverage, returning mismatched objects and failing outright on many real-world and casual images.
- Image-conditioned scene generation leans on strong learned layout priors, yielding template-like arrangements that are physically valid yet inconsistent with the observed input image.

## Method

- Agentic physical scene understanding: a ==VLM== scene analyzer lists open-vocabulary instances, a segmentation agent prompts ==SAM3== with a verifier agent looping until masks pass, then spatial reasoning builds a support scene tree.
- Scene-tree-guided canonicalization: ==SAM3D== meshes initialize the world-frame layout, the dominant vertical direction is aligned to gravity, and tree traversal drops each child onto its parent support surface.
- Physics-constrained ==CEM== optimization: ==Isaac Gym== rollouts score sampled 6-DoF pose adjustments by stability, velocity, penetration and layout energies, applied divide-and-conquer to local scene-tree groups first, then the global layout.

## Results

- On Replica, stable rate reaches **95.8%** versus **8.3%** for SAM3D, collision rate drops to **0.0%** from **66.7%**, and position drift falls from **1.116 m** to **0.094 m**.
- ScanNet++ and custom casual images give **93.6%** and **95.5%** stability at **0%** failure, while retrieval-based DigitalCousins fails on **33.3%** and **48.0%** of scenes; geometry stays near SAM3D.
- Ablations: canonicalization alone lifts stability from **12.0%** to **45.8%**, full optimization to **94.9%**; global-only optimization fails on **10%** of scenes and dropping stability energy fails on **5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chamfer Distance and F-Score reward interpenetrating reconstructions; simulator-derived metrics like collision and stability rate are needed to certify a scene as simulation-ready.
> - A support-relation tree turns an unstructured scene into small solvable subproblems: local-then-global optimization converges where joint optimization over all objects diverges.
> - Running a physics engine inside the objective, rather than as post-hoc validation, lets sampling-based search trade tiny geometric error for large physical-validity gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "REST3D" addresses a significant challenge within the field of 3D scene reconstruction, specifically focusing on generating physically stable and visually consistent 3D representations from a single RGB image. This area of research is critical for converting casual 2D captures into functional digital assets for applications such as immersive interaction, content creation, and robotics.

Prior research in 3D scene reconstruction has explored various input modalities, including multi-view RGB images, RGB-D scans, and videos, which often yield accurate reconstructions but require specialized sensing setups or dense visual observations. The single-image setting is inherently more challenging due to the limited visual information available.

Existing single-image 3D reconstruction methods, such as those leveraging ResNet, 3D CNNs, or more recent foundation models like image-to-3D models (e.g., Gen3DSR, SceneGen, SAM3D), primarily concentrate on visual consistency and geometric plausibility. However, these methods frequently overlook the physical structure of a scene, leading to common issues like objects floating in the air or penetrating each other. Such physically inconsistent outputs result in unstable behavior when imported into physics simulators, making them unsuitable for interactive or simulation-driven applications. SAM3D, for instance, excels at individual object reconstruction but does not inherently enforce scene-level physical plausibility.

A parallel line of research involves 3D scene generation, where methods synthesize scenes from text or images, often employing agentic pipelines and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). While these generative approaches can produce physically plausible layouts, they frequently rely on strong learned scene priors. This reliance can lead to generated scenes that are plausible but inaccurate, failing to faithfully reproduce the specific object arrangements observed in a given input image. Retrieval-based methods like DigitalCousins attempt to improve physical plausibility by assembling scenes from retrieved 3D assets, but their effectiveness is constrained by the coverage and fidelity of the underlying 3D asset databases, often resulting in mismatched objects.

REST3D positions itself by aiming to bridge the gap between these two categories of approaches. It seeks to develop a framework that can reconstruct 3D scenes from a single image with both high visual fidelity to the input and robust physical stability, making the output directly usable in physics simulations. This contrasts with existing methods that tend to optimize for either visual appearance or generic physical plausibility, but rarely both in a manner consistent with a specific input observation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reconstruct physically stable 3D scenes from a single RGB image. The framework, termed REST3D, aims to convert casual image captures into "simulation-ready" digital assets. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives:

1. **Achieve Physical Plausibility:** The reconstructed 3D scenes must adhere to fundamental physical laws, specifically avoiding common violations such as object floating (where objects are suspended without support) and object penetration (where objects geometrically overlap).
2. **Ensure Visual Consistency:** While addressing physical constraints, the reconstructed scene must faithfully represent the object presence, geometry, appearance, and spatial arrangement observed in the input image. This means the output should not be merely physically plausible in a generic sense, but a physically correct rendition of the *specific* input scene.
3. **Produce Simulation-Ready Assets:** The reconstructed scenes should be stable when imported into a physics simulator. This implies that objects should settle naturally and remain in their intended positions without exhibiting uncontrolled dynamics (e.g., falling, sliding, or exploding) due to initial physical inconsistencies.
4. **Enable Immersive Applications:** The stable, high-fidelity 3D scenes are intended to serve as foundational assets for advanced applications such as immersive human-object interaction in virtual reality (VR) environments, content creation, and gaming.

The motivation for REST3D stems from the limitations of current single-image 3D reconstruction and scene generation methods:

- **Deficiency in Physical Structure:** Despite advancements in visual reconstruction, existing single-image 3D reconstruction techniques largely neglect the physical structure of scenes. They prioritize geometric and visual fidelity from the 2D input, often producing 3D models where objects that should be resting on surfaces are floating, or objects are interpenetrating. These physically invalid states lead to immediate and disruptive instabilities when scenes are subjected to physics simulations.
- **Inaccuracy in Scene Generation:** While image-conditioned scene generation methods have improved in generating physically plausible layouts, they typically rely on strong learned priors or heuristics. This often results in "plausible but inaccurate" object arrangements that do not precisely match the specific observed scene in the input image. Such methods aim for generic plausibility rather than faithful reconstruction of an observed reality.
- **Demand for Functional Digital Assets:** There is a growing need for 3D assets that are not only visually appealing but also functionally correct for interactive and dynamic virtual environments. The current gap means that manual post-processing is often required to correct physical inconsistencies, which is time-consuming and labor-intensive. REST3D seeks to automate the generation of such functional, simulation-ready assets directly from common image captures.

By integrating physical scene understanding with physics-constrained refinement, REST3D aims to resolve these limitations, providing a framework that can produce 3D scenes that are both visually consistent with a single input image and robustly stable for physics-based interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The REST3D framework operates in three sequential stages: Scene-Tree Construction, Scene Initialization and Canonicalization, and Physics-Constrained Optimization.

#### 3.1. Scene-Tree Construction

This initial stage focuses on inferring a structural representation of the scene, capturing physical states and inter-object relationships.

1. **Open-vocabulary Object List Analysis:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Gemini 3 Flash, acts as a scene analyzer. It processes the input RGB image to generate a structured, open-vocabulary list of objects. To ensure distinct identification, the VLM is prompted to include disambiguating attributes (e.g., "plant on dining table" rather than a generic "plant"). The ground or floor is manually added as a canonical reference.
2. **Agentic Instance Segmentation:** For each identified object, an agentic system is employed to generate a precise segmentation mask. This system comprises a segmentation agent ($A_{seg}$) and a verifier agent ($A_{ver}$). $A_{seg}$ refines object descriptions into detailed prompts for SAM3, an instance segmentation model, to generate candidate masks. $A_{ver}$ evaluates the quality of these masks by overlaying them on the input image and performing visual-language reasoning to determine if they match the target object. This iterative feedback loop continues until a mask is confirmed or the object is determined to be absent.
3. **Scene Tree Construction via Spatial Reasoning:** Utilizing the object identifiers and their corresponding segmentation masks, a support-relation scene tree ($T$) is constructed. This tree captures the hierarchical physical layout of the scene. The root of the tree comprises four canonical support nodes: ground, wall, ceiling, and ground-wall. A VLM analyzes mask-overlaid object pairs to infer support relationships (e.g., which object supports another) and the type of relation (e.g., "on," "hanging," "attached to"). This process also determines whether an object is movable or fixed. For objects supported by multiple surfaces (e.g., a radiator touching both ground and a wall), a composite parent ("ground-wall") is assigned. This scene tree serves as a structural prior for subsequent reconstruction steps.

#### 3.2. Scene Initialization and Canonicalization

This stage takes the inferred scene structure and initial 3D object models to create a coarsely aligned and physically meaningful scene.

1. **Object Reconstruction and Scene Initialization:** For each object identified and segmented, an image-to-3D model (SAM3D) is used to reconstruct its individual 3D mesh. SAM3D also estimates the object's initial rotation, translation, and scale, which are then used to initialize a raw 3D scene ($S_{raw}$) in the world frame. However, $S_{raw}$ often contains physical inconsistencies such as inaccurate global pose, objects floating, or inter-object collisions.
2. **Scene Canonicalization:** To address these inconsistencies, the scene is canonicalized using the constructed scene tree $T$. First, the global orientation is corrected: the dominant vertical direction from ground-supported objects and large furniture is estimated and aligned with the world's vertical axis (opposite to gravity). Second, coarse spatial consistency is enforced by traversing the scene tree. Objects are vertically translated to lie on their supporting surfaces, resolving initial vertical penetrations and producing a more physically plausible initial scene ($S_{cano}$). Despite these improvements, residual inconsistencies like horizontal inter-object intersections may remain.

#### 3.3. Physics-Constrained Optimization

The final stage refines object poses to achieve physical stability under simulation while maintaining visual consistency with the initial canonicalized layout. This is achieved using a physics simulator (Isaac Gym) and a divide-and-conquer strategy guided by the scene tree, employing the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) for optimization.

1. **Local Group Optimization:**
 - The scene tree $T$ is traversed in post-order to define local groups, each consisting of a non-root node and its direct children. This ensures that child groups are optimized before their parent's group.
 - For each local group, CEM is used to find optimal pose adjustments from the $S_{cano}$ layout. CEM is an iterative, population-based stochastic optimization technique. At each iteration, it samples K candidate pose adjustments from a Gaussian distribution, applies them to the initial poses, and evaluates the resulting scene's physical feasibility using a physics simulator. An "elite set" of the lowest-energy samples is selected, and the Gaussian distribution is updated by moment matching over these elite samples, steering the search toward stable solutions.
 - **Energy Function (E):** The energy function (Equation 2) combines several terms to evaluate each candidate layout:
 - $E_{stab}$: Measures the deviation in position and rotation of objects after forward simulation, penalizing significant drift.
 - $E_{vel}$: Measures the linear velocity of objects at an intermediate simulation step, serving as an early indicator of instability.
 - $E_{pen}$: Quantifies geometric overlap (interpenetration) between objects, both at their initial placement and after settlement in simulation.
 - $E_{layout}$: Ensures consistency with the canonicalized scene $S_{cano}$, preventing objects from excessively dispersing to achieve stability at the expense of fidelity to the original layout.
2. **Global Layout Optimization:** After all local groups are optimized, they are treated as rigid units. A second CEM pass is performed globally over these optimized groups and any objects directly supported by the ground or ground-wall nodes. This joint optimization further refines the overall scene layout, with all group children being included and evaluated in the simulator.
3. **Wall and Ceiling Object Placement:** Objects identified as wall- or ceiling-attached in the scene tree are handled heuristically. They are initially placed at their $S_{cano}$ positions. If intersections with the already optimized scene are detected, they are translated along the normal direction of their associated (fitted) room surfaces until collision-free placement is achieved. These objects are then fixed during simulation.

The final output is an optimized scene ($S$) that is both visually consistent with the input image and physically stable in a simulator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that REST3D significantly improves physical stability and reconstruction quality compared to state-of-the-art methods. Evaluation was conducted on synthetic (Replica), real-world (ScanNet++), and a custom dataset of challenging casual images, using physical metrics (collision rate, stability rate, position drift, linear/angular velocity) and geometric metrics (Chamfer Distance, F-score, B-IoU).

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Physical Plausibility:** REST3D consistently exhibited superior physical stability. On the Replica dataset, it achieved a 0.0% collision rate, a 95.8% stable rate, a position drift of 0.094 m, a linear velocity of 0.152 m/s, and an angular velocity of 0.557 rad/s. This represents an 83%-point improvement in stability over SAM3D† (which achieved 8.3%). Similar significant improvements were observed on ScanNet++ and Custom datasets, with collision rates of 5.9% and 1.2%, and stable rates of 93.6% and 95.5%, respectively. In contrast, baselines like SceneGen showed drifts exceeding 10 m and high velocities, indicating severe instability.
- **Geometric Metrics:** For Replica and ScanNet++ datasets, REST3D demonstrated strong geometric reconstruction quality, with a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.007 and F-score of 0.919 on Replica, and CD of 0.019 and F-score of 0.807 on ScanNet++. While SAM3D† sometimes achieved slightly better geometric scores (e.g., lower CD), this was attributed to its lack of physical constraints, allowing interpenetrating objects to align more closely to initial raw geometry, whereas REST3D's physical separation introduces minor geometric deviations for stability.
- **Failure Rate:** REST3D successfully reconstructed all scenes across all datasets (0.0% failure rate), comparable to SAM3D†. Retrieval-based methods like DigitalCousins, however, showed high failure rates (e.g., 48.0% on the Custom dataset) due to limitations in their asset databases.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visualizations in a physics simulator (Isaac Gym) showed that scenes reconstructed by baseline methods frequently suffered from floating objects, interpenetration, and subsequent collapse or instability during simulation. REST3D's reconstructions consistently remained stable, maintaining their intended layouts throughout the simulation. DigitalCousins sometimes showed incorrect spatial relations, while SceneGen and Gen3DSR often produced inaccurate layouts or object misclassifications. SAM3D† struggled with global orientation errors and failed to infer wall-attachment relations, leading to objects like posters falling. REST3D also demonstrated robustness across diverse input styles, including Gaussian-rendered and cartoon-style scenes.
- **Inference Time and API Cost:** REST3D's total inference time was reported as 25.8 minutes per scene, comparable to or faster than existing baselines like Gen3DSR (39.3 min/scene). Its API cost ($0.47/scene using Gemini 3 Flash) was lower than DigitalCousins ($1.25/scene using GPT-4o).

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Scene Canonicalization:** This step significantly improved physical stability from the raw SAM3D output ($S_{raw}$) to $S_{cano}$, reducing large pose and orientation errors. However, canonicalization alone was insufficient, with $S_{cano}$ still exhibiting instability due to unresolved object intersections.
- **Global-only Optimization:** Removing the local group optimization and performing only a single global optimization increased the failure rate by approximately 10% and reduced overall stability. This highlights the effectiveness of the scene-tree-guided divide-and-conquer strategy for handling complex scenes.
- **Energy Terms:** Each component of the energy function contributed to the final performance. Removing the stability term ($E_{stab}$) led to a 5% failure rate. Removing the penetration term ($E_{pen}$) resulted in slightly better geometric metrics but reduced physical stability, confirming the trade-off. Omitting the layout consistency term ($E_{layout}$) caused objects to deviate more from the original layout as the optimizer prioritized stability. The full energy function achieved the best overall results.
- **Effect of Scene Tree on Baselines:** Simply introducing the scene tree to SAM3D† ($S_{raw}$) or $S_{cano}$ led to some improvement in stability, particularly for unsupported objects. However, significant instability persisted due to geometric interpenetrations that the scene tree alone could not resolve, underscoring the necessity of physics-constrained optimization.
- **CEM Elite Fraction:** Varying the CEM elite fraction (ρ) showed that an optimal value (ρ=0.025) balanced exploration and exploitation, yielding the most stable solutions.

#### 4.3. Real-world VR Interaction

To demonstrate the practical utility and physical plausibility of the reconstructed scenes, the authors developed a VR interaction system. This system allowed users to interact with the virtual objects in real-time (30 FPS) using a Meta Quest Pro headset. Hand motions were tracked and mapped to a simulated dexterous robotic hand within Isaac Gym, facilitating physically grounded interactions. This demonstration underscored REST3D's potential for immersive applications where stable and interactive virtual environments are crucial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in REST3D represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D scene reconstruction from single images, primarily due to its emphasis on generating physically stable and simulation-ready digital assets. This capability has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Bridging the Reality-Simulation Gap:** REST3D directly addresses a critical limitation of previous single-image 3D reconstruction methods, which often generated visually plausible but physically invalid scenes. By integrating sophisticated physical scene understanding with physics-constrained optimization, the framework can convert casual 2D observations into 3D environments that behave realistically within physics simulators. This bridge is essential for creating more faithful digital twins and interactive virtual worlds.
- **Enabling Immersive and Interactive Applications:** The ability to reconstruct stable 3D scenes from a single image is transformative for immersive technologies. The demonstrated VR interaction system highlights REST3D's potential for:
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Users can interact naturally with reconstructed virtual objects without encountering physical anomalies like floating or collapsing elements, enhancing the realism and immersion of VR/AR experiences. This could facilitate the rapid creation of interactive virtual spaces for training, gaming, or telepresence.
 - **Content Creation:** Artists and designers can more efficiently generate simulation-ready 3D assets and environments from real-world photos, significantly streamlining workflows for game development, film production, and architectural visualization by reducing the need for extensive manual physical property adjustments.
- **Advancing Robotics and Embodied AI:** Physically plausible 3D scenes are critical for training embodied AI agents and robots in simulation. REST3D provides a method to generate realistic and stable training environments directly from observational data, potentially improving the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of learned policies. Robots can practice manipulation and navigation in virtual worlds that accurately mimic the physical constraints of real-world environments.
- **Novelty in Methodology:** The framework's modular design, incorporating agentic VLM-based scene understanding for hierarchical support relationships and a CEM-based physics-constrained optimization, represents a robust and flexible approach. This methodology allows for leveraging advancements in foundation models for scene interpretation while explicitly enforcing physical laws through simulation-driven refinement. The divide-and-conquer strategy for optimization proves particularly effective for complex scenes.
- **Robustness Across Diverse Inputs:** The successful application of REST3D across synthetic, real-world, and even stylized datasets (including Gaussian-rendered and cartoon-style images) demonstrates its robustness and generalizability. This broad applicability suggests its utility in various domains where casual image captures need to be converted into functional 3D representations.

Despite its significant contributions, the paper acknowledges certain limitations, such as reliance on the robustness of VLMs and the current focus on rigid objects, which could be addressed in future work to further expand its impact. The explicit modeling of architectural elements (e.g., walls) as support structures during optimization is also identified as an area for improvement. Nevertheless, REST3D's core contribution of generating physically stable and visually consistent 3D scenes from single images marks a substantial step toward more functional and realistic digital representations of the world.
