# Real2Edit2Real: Generating Robotic Demonstrations via a 3D Control Interface

> [!summary] Summary
> Real2Edit2Real generates robotic manipulation demonstrations from raw multi-view RGB data using depth as a 3D control interface. It fine-tunes a feed-forward reconstruction model for metric-scale geometry, edits point clouds with kinematic robot pose correction to synthesize novel trajectories, then renders demonstrations via a depth-conditioned multi-view video diffusion model. Policies trained on data from 1-5 source demos match or beat 50 real demos, a 10-50x data-efficiency gain.

## Problem

- MimicGen-style and graphics-engine pipelines render demonstrations in simulators, incurring visual and physics gaps and requiring per-object assets, so existing real demonstrations cannot be augmented directly.
- Point-cloud editing methods enhance 3D policies but cannot produce RGB images, leaving them incompatible with the dominant multi-view RGB visuomotor and VLA training paradigm.
- Gaussian-splatting and video-generation augmentation either need dense scans that limit scalability or vary only texture and viewpoint, never object spatial layout or robot trajectories.

## Method

- **Metric-VGGT**: a hybrid training recipe fine-tuning a feed-forward reconstruction transformer, supervising camera pose and point maps from simulation and masked metric depth from real sensors.
- Depth-reliable spatial editing: motion/skill segment decomposition, motion planning, background inpainting plus depth filtering, and ==URDF-based robot pose correction== that re-renders arm depth for kinematic consistency.
- ==3D-controlled video generation==: a dual-attention (intra-view plus cross-view) diffusion backbone conditioned on depth with Canny edge, action and ray maps, plus smooth object relocation and condition dropout.

## Results

- Across four real tasks, 200 demos generated from 5 source demos gave **78.8%** (Go-1) and **81.3%** success, versus 61.3% for both policies trained on 50 real demos.
- Scaling generated data from one source demo raised Go-1 from **50.0% at 50 generated to 77.5% at 400**, surpassing the 50-real-demo baseline past 300 generated.
- Video quality beat the GE-Sim baseline (**FVD 352.9 vs 663.4**, SSIM 0.8647 vs 0.7491); height editing lifted an out-of-distribution platform task from **0/5 to 4/5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Depth is a practical bridge between 3D editability and 2D pixels: it encodes motion and contact, so editing it steers photorealistic video without any simulator or asset library.
> - Editing a robot as a rigid body corrupts control signals; re-solving inverse kinematics and re-rendering link depth is what makes generated interactions physically plausible.
> - Source-demo diversity and generated-demo volume both help, but generation quality degrades on articulated and deformable objects absent from the video model's training distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has made advancements through the use of large-scale datasets and sophisticated visuomotor policy architectures, such as Diffusion Policy and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, a persistent challenge is the substantial cost and labor required to collect diverse, high-quality real-world demonstrations, which limits the robustness and generalization capabilities of policies. A particular bottleneck is spatial generalization in manipulation tasks, where objects are arranged in various configurations, demanding extensive data coverage.

Existing approaches to alleviate this data collection burden include synthesizing demonstrations from limited existing data. Some methods, such as MimicGen-style works, segment and transform object-centric trajectories to adapt to new arrangements. Others, like Real2Render2Real and RoboSplatt, utilize 3D Gaussian Splatting and trajectory generation to synthesize demonstrations from human or robot videos. However, these rendering-based techniques often introduce visual and physics domain gaps due to their reliance on graphics engines, and they typically require digital assets for manipulated objects, which restricts their direct application to augmenting existing real-world demonstrations. Furthermore, some 3D point-cloud editing methods like DemoGen, while enhancing spatial generalization for 3D policies, are not compatible with the prevalent paradigm of multi-view RGB images and 2D policies in current robot learning and deployment.

Separately, advances in 3D geometry reconstruction, particularly feed-forward models, enable sparse-view reconstruction, which is relevant for robotic camera views. However, these models face challenges such as domain gaps between pre-trained data and real-world robot scenarios, including issues with camera pose accuracy and metric alignment. In parallel, progress in video generation models has improved conditional and temporal consistency, leading to applications in robotics such as predictive video generation, action-conditioned simulation environments, and multi-modal conditioned video generation. However, many of these video generation methods have not primarily focused on spatial generalization.

Real2Edit2Real positions itself to address the gap in rapidly scaling up real-world 2D multi-view manipulation videos with novel object placements and corresponding trajectories, while simultaneously preserving visual realism and interaction fidelity. It aims to provide a unified framework that avoids reliance on simulation engines or digital assets, directly augmenting raw RGB demonstrations for VLA training by bridging 3D editability with 2D visual data through a 3D control interface, specifically leveraging depth information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named Real2Edit2Real, that efficiently generates diverse robotic demonstrations from a limited number of source demonstrations. This is intended to improve the robustness and spatial generalization of visuomotor policies, particularly in manipulation tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in current robot learning:
- **High Cost of Data Collection:** Training robust visuomotor policies, especially for tasks requiring spatial generalization across varied object configurations, necessitates large, diverse datasets. Collecting these real-world demonstrations is a time-consuming, expensive, and labor-intensive process, leading to a significant bottleneck in policy development and deployment.
- **Limitations of Existing Data Augmentation Methods:**
 - **Simulation-to-Real Gap:** Approaches that generate data within simulation environments often encounter a "sim-to-real" gap, where policies trained in simulation do not transfer effectively to the real world due to discrepancies in physics and visuals.
 - **Reliance on Digital Assets:** Many methods require 3D models or assets of objects and scenes, making it difficult to augment arbitrary real-world demonstrations directly without prior asset creation.
 - **Incompatibility with Dominant Paradigms:** While some methods augment 3D point-cloud data, a significant portion of current robot learning and deployment relies on multi-view RGB images and 2D policies. Existing solutions often fail to augment 2D multi-view videos with novel spatial configurations while maintaining visual realism and interaction fidelity.
 - **Lack of Spatial Generalization Focus:** Many video generation methods in robotics primarily focus on visual enhancement or temporal consistency but do not adequately address the generation of novel object placements and corresponding manipulation trajectories, which are crucial for spatial generalization.

To mitigate these issues, Real2Edit2Real aims to:
- Significantly reduce the burden of repetitive data collection.
- Bridge the gap between 3D editability (for spatial manipulation) and 2D visual data (for policy input) using a 3D control interface.
- Generate spatially augmented multi-view robotic demonstrations directly from raw RGB observations, without requiring simulation engines or digital assets.
- Produce demonstrations featuring novel object placements and corresponding new trajectories.
- Ensure that generated demonstrations are compatible with VLA training.

The core insight motivating the methodology is that depth inherently encodes both robot motion and object interactions, thereby serving as a natural and reliable interface for translating between 3D scene manipulations and 2D visual observations for video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Real2Edit2Real is a three-module framework designed to generate spatially augmented multi-view robotic demonstrations. It processes a source multi-view RGB demonstration, along with robot kinematic and camera parameters, to synthesize a large set of augmented demonstrations.

#### 3.1. Metric-scale Geometry Reconstruction

The framework initiates by reconstructing the detailed 3D geometry of the scene from the multi-view RGB observations of the source demonstration. This is achieved using a feed-forward 3D reconstruction model, specifically a fine-tuned VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer). To enhance accuracy and scale-awareness in humanoid robot scenarios with sparse camera views (head, left wrist, right wrist), a hybrid training paradigm is proposed:
- **Camera Pose Training:** Simulated data is used to supervise camera pose prediction (L1 loss). This leverages the perfect accuracy and metric consistency of camera poses derived from virtual sensors and URDF models in simulation, addressing real-world calibration inaccuracies.
- **Depth Map Training:** A mixed approach is used for depth map supervision. Simulated data provides noise-free and geometrically precise depth, while real-world data (from depth sensors) offers metric-scale geometry. The Lconf loss with an uncertainty mask is applied to both real and simulated depth maps to leverage their complementary strengths, filtering noise in real data and mitigating scale deviations in simulated data.
- **Point Map Training:** Simulated data also supervises the point map loss.
The total training loss combines weighted camera loss, depth loss, and point map loss, with a higher weight applied to the camera loss for optimization stability.

#### 3.2. Depth-reliable Spatial Editing

Upon obtaining the reconstructed scene geometry, this module synthesizes novel object placements and corresponding manipulation trajectories in 3D space. It ensures that the generated depth maps are physically consistent with robot kinematics.
- **Trajectory Synthesis:** A point-cloud demonstration is segmented into "motion segments" (robot moves freely) and "skill segments" (robot interacts with objects). When an object's position is transformed, the same transformation is applied to the robot's end-effector point cloud within the corresponding skill segment. This maintains the original robot-object interaction relation. New motion segments are generated using motion planning algorithms.
- **Depth Projection:** The edited point cloud is projected from updated camera poses (transformed rigidly with the robot's end-effector) to generate new depth maps. To address holes and noise in these projected depth maps, the process incorporates background inpainting (using an image-edit model to remove foreground objects and robot from the first frame, then reconstructing background geometry) and depth filtering. An additional point cloud alignment procedure corrects metric inconsistencies introduced by image editing by aligning estimated table planes.
- **Robot Pose Correction (RPC):** A critical step involves correcting robot poses. Instead of treating the entire robot as a rigid body, only the end-effector is transformed. The remaining arm links are then realigned to maintain kinematic validity. This is achieved by segmenting original robot links using URDF and source joint states, and then re-rendering the arm depth with synthesized actions and inverse kinematics (IK). This ensures physically plausible robot configurations and accurate depth observations free from rigid-body artifacts.

#### 3.3. 3D-Controlled Video Generation

After generating edited depth, action, and camera pose sequences, this module converts them into 2D visual observations suitable for policy training. A Transformer-based video generation model, fine-tuned from GE-Sim, synthesizes multi-view manipulation videos from the first frame.
- **Dual-attention Mechanism:** The model incorporates both intra-view attention (for spatial context within each view) and cross-view attention (for multi-view correspondence). This design promotes multi-view consistency and reduces computational cost.
- **Depth Control Interface:** Depth maps serve as the primary 3D control signal. They are concatenated with image latent representations and fed into the transformer backbone, conditioning video generation on 3D structural cues that encode robot motion and object interactions. Auxiliary conditioning signals, including Canny edges (derived from depth), action maps, and ray maps, are also incorporated to further sharpen object boundaries, improve motion grounding, and enhance multi-view consistency.
- **Smooth Object Relocation (SOR):** To seamlessly integrate object relocation into video generation, the framework interpolates object translation and rotation during spatial editing. This synthesizes smooth object movement trajectories before the manipulation task begins, converting static image editing into a continuous video generation process.
- **Training Details:** The model is trained on episodes from the Agibot-World dataset. Depth maps are globally normalized across all views within a training chunk. A random dropout mechanism is applied to depth and Canny edge conditions during training (independently 0.5 probability, jointly 0.1 probability) to improve robustness against imperfect or noisy control signals from spatial editing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on four real-world robotic manipulation tasks demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the Real2Edit2Real framework.

#### 4.1. Gen2Real Policy Learning

- **Data Efficiency:** Policies trained on data generated from a limited number of source demonstrations (1 to 5) achieved comparable or superior success rates compared to policies trained on a significantly larger number of real-world demonstrations (50). This represents a 10-50x improvement in data efficiency.
 - For instance, policies (Go-1 and π0.5) trained on 200 demonstrations generated from just 5 source demonstrations achieved average success rates of 78.8% and 81.3%, respectively. This performance surpassed policies trained on 50 real-world demonstrations, which achieved an average success rate of 61.3%.
- **Spatial Generalization:** The increase in source demonstrations, by introducing more diverse robot-object interaction patterns and expanding the spatial coverage of generated data, contributed to the improved spatial generalization of downstream policies. Policies trained on generated data exhibited robustness to novel object placements across the workspace during evaluation.

#### 4.2. More Applications (Flexibility and Extensibility)

- **Height Editing:** The framework demonstrated the ability to generate demonstrations with varied object heights. A policy trained on 40 generated demonstrations (20 on tabletop, 20 on platform) achieved an 80% success rate on an unseen platform height, whereas a policy trained on 20 real tabletop demonstrations failed completely (0% success) when faced with the platform height.
- **Texture Editing:** Real2Edit2Real supports modifying background textures through first-frame editing. Policies trained on generated data including different desktop textures achieved a 68% success rate across various textures, outperforming policies trained on 50 real demonstrations with a single texture (50% success rate).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Geometry Reconstruction:** The proposed hybrid training paradigm for Metric-VGGT yielded cleaner point clouds and more accurate camera poses in robot scenarios compared to using standard VGGT or raw real-world depth data, which often suffer from noise and inaccurate poses.
- **Robot Pose Correction (RPC):** The RPC module was shown to be crucial. Without it, erroneous depth maps resulted in blurry and inconsistent video generation. With RPC, depth maps were kinematically consistent, leading to realistic robot motions in synthesized videos.
- **Smooth Object Relocation (SOR):** This mechanism was essential for precise object placement. Without SOR, generated object placements often exhibited noticeable errors, rendering demonstrations unusable.

#### 4.4. Visualization and Additional Quantitative Results

- **Generated Videos:** Visualizations confirmed that the generated videos successfully relocate objects, synthesize correct manipulation trajectories, and maintain multi-view consistency and realistic visual appearance across all four tasks.
- **Video Generation Quality:** Quantitative evaluation indicated that the 3D-controlled video generation module produced demonstrations with enhanced visual realism compared to GE-Sim, showing improvements in metrics like FVD, LPIPS, SSIM, and PSNR.
- **Diffusion Policy Performance:** Experiments with Diffusion Policy on the "Mug to Basket" task showed that policies trained on data generated from 1-5 source demonstrations could surpass the performance of those trained on 50 real demonstrations.
- **Generation Time Analysis:** The framework exhibits parallel scalability; leveraging multi-GPU acceleration significantly reduces data generation time, enabling Real2Edit2Real to achieve higher success rates more quickly than manual teleoperation.
- **Generated Data Scaling Analysis:** Increasing the number of generated demonstrations from a single source consistently improved success rates for downstream policies. Generating more than 300 demonstrations from a single source demonstration could lead to average success rates surpassing those achieved with 50 real demonstrations.
- **Ablation of Control Conditions:** Removing either the depth control or the Canny edge constraint from the video generation model resulted in significant degradation of video quality, including object blurring and incorrect interactions, underscoring the importance of these control signals.

#### 4.5. Limitations

The paper identifies limitations including the computational bottleneck of the video generation module in resource-constrained scenarios and challenges in generalizing to articulated or deformable objects, attributed to their scarcity in the training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Real2Edit2Real presents a framework that offers a method for generating diverse and high-quality robotic demonstrations, addressing a significant challenge in the field of robot learning. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** By enabling 10-50x data efficiency, the framework directly tackles the high cost and labor-intensiveness associated with collecting large-scale real-world robotic demonstration datasets. This can accelerate the development and deployment of robotic systems by reducing the resource barrier for training robust visuomotor policies.
- **Enhanced Policy Robustness and Generalization:** The ability to synthesize demonstrations with novel object placements and manipulation trajectories, while maintaining visual realism and kinematic consistency, directly contributes to improving the spatial generalization capabilities of learned policies. This can lead to robots that are more adaptable and reliable in varied real-world environments.
- **Bridging 3D and 2D Modalities:** The framework's core insight of using depth as a 3D control interface for 2D video generation provides a principled way to integrate 3D geometric editing with visual synthesis, avoiding the domain gaps typically associated with simulation-based or rendering-based approaches.
- **Elimination of Simulation and Assets:** The method's independence from simulation engines and digital assets for objects represents a practical advantage. It allows for the direct augmentation of raw RGB demonstrations, making it applicable to a broader range of real-world scenarios without the prerequisite of creating detailed virtual models.
- **Flexibility and Extensibility for Unified Data Generation:** The demonstrated capabilities for height and texture editing suggest that Real2Edit2Real has the potential to serve as a unified framework for generating a wide variety of augmented robotic data. This could support broader forms of generalization (e.g., to different environments, lighting, or object properties) beyond just spatial configuration.
- **Compatibility with Modern VLA Policies:** By generating multi-view RGB videos, the framework is directly compatible with current mainstream Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, facilitating their training and potentially accelerating progress in foundation models for robotics.

In summary, Real2Edit2Real contributes to making robot learning more data-efficient, robust, and scalable, potentially democratizing access to high-quality training data for a wider range of robotic applications. The framework’s ability to generate realistic and kinematically consistent demonstrations from minimal real-world input positions it as a valuable tool for future research and development in embodied AI.
