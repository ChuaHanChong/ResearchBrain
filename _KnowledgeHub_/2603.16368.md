# Encoding Predictability and Legibility for Style-Conditioned Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> The Style-Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) framework enables robots to dynamically adjust their motion between legible and efficient styles based on environmental spatial ambiguity by constraining a pre-trained diffusion model. This method demonstrates improved adaptive transparency scores, reaching up to 0.76 in non-ambiguous navigation tasks, and successfully transfers to a physical robot while maintaining high task completion rates.

## Problem

- Balancing robot motion legibility, crucial for human understanding and trust, with operational efficiency (e.g., speed, energy consumption) in human-robot collaboration (HRC).
- Existing robot motion generation approaches often prioritize a fixed style (either always legible or always efficient) or require manual tuning for style modulation, lacking adaptive context-awareness.
- Efficient paths can be ambiguous to human observers, especially when multiple goals are plausible, while overly legible motions can be sub-optimal in terms of time and energy.

## Method

- Development of **SCDP**, a modular framework that dynamically modulates a pre-trained ==Diffusion Policy== towards either ==legibility== or ==predictability== based on environmental spatial ambiguity.
- A ==post-training pipeline== where a base Diffusion Policy's weights are frozen, and lightweight external modules (scene encoder, legibility/predictability predictors) are trained to constrain the diffusion process via ==FiLM conditioning==.
- Integration of an ==ambiguity detection module== that uses an ==ellipse of ambiguity== to assess spatial configuration, activating legibility conditioning only when necessary, otherwise prioritizing efficiency.

## Results

- SCDP achieved superior adaptive transparency scores in non-ambiguous scenarios (e.g., **0.74** for Block Reach, **0.76** for Navigation), significantly outperforming baselines by up to **0.44** points by effectively prioritizing efficiency.
- In ambiguous scenarios, SCDP maintained competitive legibility (detachment scores of **0.70** for Block Reach, **0.64** for Navigation) while substantially improving efficiency (**0.43** and **0.47** respectively) compared to Legibility Diffuser.
- The framework successfully transferred to a physical Franka Emika Panda robot for the Block Reach task with a mean inference time of approximately **5 seconds** and maintained a high task success rate exceeding **0.98** across all experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contextual information about spatial ambiguity can be effectively encoded and utilized to adaptively condition generative models for robot behavior without requiring full retraining of the base policy.
> - Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) offers a lightweight and modular mechanism to dynamically bias the output of a diffusion policy towards specific motion styles like legibility or predictability.
> - Training external style-predictor MLPs on targeted subsets of expert demonstrations allows for data-driven learning of context-dependent motion styles, enabling a nuanced balance between legibility and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses a fundamental challenge within human-robot collaboration (HRC): the balance between operational efficiency and the transparent communication of a robot's intentions through its motion. In HRC, the ability of a robot to convey its intent, known as motion legibility, is crucial for fostering human understanding, safety, and trust. Legible motion allows human observers to infer a robot's intended goal solely from its movement, which is particularly vital in environments where other communication channels (e.g., speech or gaze) are unavailable or limited.

However, generating highly expressive, legible movements often necessitates deviations from optimal, direct paths, leading to trajectories that are sub-optimal, exaggerated, or consume unnecessary time and energy. Conversely, purely efficient movements, while optimal from a robotic perspective, can be ambiguous to human observers, especially when multiple goals are plausible. Previous research, such as that by Dragan et al., established the distinction between legible and predictable motion, often modeling human inference using Bayesian frameworks. Learning-based approaches, including reinforcement learning and supervised observer models, have also been employed to optimize legibility metrics.

More recently, imitation learning, particularly leveraging generative models like diffusion models, has emerged as a promising avenue for modeling complex and diverse robot trajectory distributions. `Diffusion Policy` is a notable example that utilizes diffusion models for visuomotor policy learning. `Legibility Diffuser` specifically extended this by using diffusion with classifier-free guidance to generate legible motion. While effective, `Legibility Diffuser` operates independently of the environment's spatial ambiguity and requires manual tuning of guidance weights during inference, presenting a limitation in adaptable behavior.

The present work builds upon these advancements by seeking to bridge the gap between fixed-style legible motion generation and purely efficient motion. It positions itself by proposing an adaptive mechanism that allows a robot to dynamically shift its motion style between legibility and predictability based on the contextual spatial ambiguity of the environment. This addresses the need for robots to intelligently decide *when* to be legible and *when* to prioritize efficiency, thereby providing a more nuanced and practical solution for HRC compared to methods that either always prioritize legibility or require manual intervention for style adjustment. The work draws inspiration from advancements in conditional generative modeling, where context vectors are used to constrain diffusion processes without altering the base model, applying this concept to robot motion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to address the inherent trade-off between generating legible robot motion and maintaining operational efficiency in human-robot collaboration. While legibility is essential for human understanding, safety, and trust, it often comes at the cost of generating sub-optimal or exaggerated trajectories that consume more time and energy. Conversely, purely efficient paths might be ambiguous to human observers, particularly in complex or cluttered environments where multiple goals appear plausible. The core problem is the lack of robotic systems that can adaptively determine when to prioritize motion legibility and when to favor efficiency based on the current environmental context. Existing approaches often either generate legible motion regardless of necessity or require manual parameter tuning for style modulation, limiting their practical applicability.

To address this challenge, the authors propose the Style-Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) framework with the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a modular framework for adaptive trajectory generation:** The central objective is to create a system that can dynamically modulate the robot's motion style—either towards legibility or efficiency—by constraining a pre-trained diffusion model. This modulation should be contingent on the spatial ambiguity detected within the environment.

2. **Implement a post-training pipeline for style conditioning:** The framework aims to integrate style conditioning without requiring a complete retraining or modification of the base diffusion policy. This involves freezing the weights of an already trained base policy and introducing lightweight external modules, specifically a scene encoder and conditioning predictors, trained in a post-training phase.

3. **Enable environment-aware style modulation:** A crucial objective is to incorporate an ambiguity detection mechanism that can assess the spatial configuration of the environment. This module should then activate the appropriate conditioning (legibility or predictability) only when necessary, preventing the generation of unnecessarily expressive (and thus sub-optimal) motions in clear scenarios.

4. **Enhance interpretability and efficiency:** The research aims to demonstrate that SCDP can effectively improve motion interpretability (legibility) in ambiguous settings where goal confusion is high, while simultaneously preserving or improving optimal efficiency in scenarios where the robot's goal is already obvious, thereby striking a practical balance.

5. **Maintain base policy performance:** A fundamental goal is to ensure that the style-conditioning modules modulate the trajectory path without compromising the underlying task performance (e.g., successful goal reaching) of the base diffusion model.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aim to provide a more adaptable, efficient, and user-friendly robot behavior in human-robot interactive settings, where the robot intelligently adjusts its communication style through motion based on real-time environmental needs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Style-Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) is presented as a composite framework designed to modulate trajectory generation by dynamically constraining a pre-trained diffusion model towards either legibility or predictability, depending on the environment's spatial ambiguity. The methodology integrates a base diffusion policy with external, lightweight modules for scene encoding, style prediction, and ambiguity detection.

#### 3.1. Task Formalization

The problem is modeled as a discrete-time, infinite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) where a robot agent moves towards a specific goal `g*` from a set of possible goals `G`. The policy `π` selects actions `a_t` based on observations `s_t`. The objective for legible motion is to maximize `p(g* | s_t, a_t)` such that `g*` is distinct from `g-`, while for efficient motion, it is to minimize the cost of reaching `g*`, effectively maximizing `p(ξ | g*)`. Demonstrations `D` include trajectories `ξ_i` and the positive goal `g*`.

#### 3.2. Base Diffusion Policy

The core of SCDP is a `Diffusion Policy` model, specifically its U-Net architecture, which adapts Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) to learn conditioned action sequences from demonstrations. This base model estimates the noise component `ϵ_θ` added to an action sequence, conditioned on the observation, which in this work is limited to the goal state `g*` and the current robot state `s_t`. The base Diffusion Policy, with approximately 70 million parameters, is initially trained on a foundational dataset of 200 expert demonstrations for 100 epochs, and its weights are subsequently frozen during the SCDP training phase.

#### 3.3. Scene Encoding

To provide contextual awareness, a scene encoder is introduced. This module learns a latent representation of the environment's spatial configurations, particularly the relationships between the target goal `g*` and any distractor goals `g-`.
- **Input:** The coordinates of `g*` and all `g-` goals.
- **Processing:** For each `g-` goal, a relative vector `r_i = g-i - g*` and Euclidean distance `j_i = ||g-i - g*||_2` are computed. These are combined with `g-i` to form an enriched vector `~g_i`. The `g*` is also enriched as `~g* = [g* 0 0]^T`. All enriched vectors are concatenated into a single input `x`.
- **Architecture:** A 3-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) acts as the scene encoder `S`.
- **Output:** A latent contextual vector `c ∈ R^s`.
- **Training:** The scene encoder is trained independently using a reconstruction-based autoencoder architecture on 5,000 randomized goal configurations for 50 epochs, ensuring a robust representation of spatial relations. This MLP has approximately 26,000 parameters.

#### 3.4. Constraining the Diffusion

This component is responsible for encoding the trajectory style. It operates as a post-training pipeline where the base Diffusion Policy's U-Net weights are frozen.
- **Architecture:** Two separate 4-layer MLP backbones, one for the legibility predictor and one for the predictability predictor, each with 1 million parameters.
- **Process:** The context vector `c` from the scene encoder is fed into the active predictor MLP, which generates two vectors, `γ` and `β`.
- **Modulation:** These `γ` and `β` parameters are then used to modulate the U-Net's bottleneck layer vector `h` through a FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) conditioning mechanism: `FiLM(h) = γ ⊙ h + β`. This mechanism allows the external module to constrain the diffusion process towards a desired distribution without altering the internal structure of the U-Net.
- **Training:** The predictor MLPs are fine-tuned for 300 epochs on specialized subsets of the original dataset. Specifically, the legibility predictor is trained on the top 20% most legible demonstrations, and the predictability predictor on the top 20% most efficient demonstrations. This enables the MLPs to learn to compensate for the residual differences between style-specific trajectories and the general paths the base model was originally trained to reproduce.

#### 3.5. Ambiguity Detection

An ambiguity detection module acts as an arbitrator at inference time, determining whether legibility or efficiency conditioning is required.
- **Mechanism:** It approximates spatial ambiguity using an "ellipse of ambiguity" `E`.
- **Construction:** The ellipse is defined such that the robot's current state `s_t` is one focal point, and it's centered at `e = s_t + κ(g* - s_t)`, where `κ ∈ (0.5, 1)` is a scaling factor.
- **Condition:** A scene is classified as spatially ambiguous if any negative goal `g-` falls within this elliptical boundary, using a quadratic form `(g- - e)^T M (g- - e) ≤ 1`, where `M` is a symmetric positive-definite matrix.
- **Selection:** If the scene is flagged as ambiguous, the legibility predictor is activated. Otherwise, the predictability predictor is activated, constraining the generation towards the most efficient path.

#### 3.6. Real-World Deployment (Sim-to-Real Transfer)

The model's portability was evaluated on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot. Object detection for `g*` and `g-` was handled by a fine-tuned YOLO model using an Intel RealSense RGB-D camera, with 2D bounding boxes projected into 3D space. The state-based Diffusion Policy facilitated a reality gap-free transfer, mirroring simulation performance without further calibration. The lightweight additional modules ensured that the mean total inference time remained around 5 seconds, similar to the base model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SCDP focused on its ability to balance legibility and efficiency across varying environmental contexts, specifically addressing two research questions: whether SCDP increases legibility when needed and reduces it to prioritize efficiency when unnecessary. Experiments were conducted on two tasks, Block Reach and Navigation, each with ambiguous and non-ambiguous environmental scenarios, using a Gazebo simulator for data collection.

#### 4.1. Datasets and Baselines

The base `Diffusion Policy` and `Legibility Diffuser` (a reproduced version with specified hyperparameters) were used as baselines, alongside performance metrics for the training dataset. Each task's dataset comprised 200 procedurally generated demonstrations covering a range of trajectory styles.

#### 4.2. Metrics

Performance was assessed using three metrics:
- **Detachment Score (D):** A proxy for legibility, quantifying divergence from a negative goal `g-` along the trajectory. Normalized.
- **Trajectory Efficiency (E):** The reciprocal of the total Euclidean distance traveled, indicating path optimality. Normalized.
- **Adaptive Transparency Score (T):** A combined trade-off metric defined as `T = (1 - w_amb) * D_hat + w_amb * E_hat`. The weight `w_amb` is a continuous sigmoid function of the normalized Euclidean distance between goals, allowing for fluid transition between prioritizing detachment (`D_hat`) in ambiguous scenarios and efficiency (`E_hat`) in unambiguous ones.

#### 4.3. Performance Analysis

The results, averaged over 100 inferences in simulation, demonstrated SCDP's adaptive balancing capability:

- **Adaptive Transparency Score (Table 1):**
 - **Ambiguous Scenarios:** `Legibility Diffuser` achieved the highest adaptive transparency scores (0.61 for Block Reach, 0.62 for Navigation), indicating its strong prioritization of detachment. SCDP followed closely with scores of 0.58 and 0.59, significantly outperforming the standard `Diffusion Policy` (0.52 and 0.50). This suggests that in ambiguous contexts, SCDP successfully biases motion towards legibility, albeit not as aggressively as `Legibility Diffuser`.
 - **Non-Ambiguous Scenarios:** SCDP consistently outperformed all other methods, achieving the highest scores (0.74 for Block Reach, 0.76 for Navigation). This indicates that when legibility is not critical, SCDP effectively switches to prioritize efficiency, leading to a better overall trade-off. `Legibility Diffuser` performed poorly in these scenarios (0.43 and 0.32), highlighting its lack of adaptability.

- **Separated Detachment and Efficiency Scores (Table 2):**
 - **Ambiguous Scenarios (High Legibility Demand):** `Legibility Diffuser` exhibited the highest detachment scores (0.89 for Block Reach, 0.85 for Navigation), confirming its strong emphasis on expressive motion. However, this came at the cost of very low efficiency (0.13 and 0.02). SCDP achieved competitive detachment scores (0.70 and 0.64) while maintaining considerably higher efficiency (0.43 and 0.47) compared to `Legibility Diffuser`, showcasing a more balanced approach.
 - **Non-Ambiguous Scenarios (High Efficiency Demand):** SCDP demonstrated the highest efficiency scores (0.80 for Block Reach, 0.85 for Navigation) and appropriately lower detachment scores. In contrast, `Legibility Diffuser` showed high detachment but very low or even negative efficiency in these contexts, indicating highly sub-optimal trajectories when legibility was unnecessary. `Diffusion Policy` maintained moderate efficiency but lacked the adaptive high efficiency of SCDP.

- **Success Rate:** Across all experiments and methods, the success rate for reaching the target goal remained above 0.98. This confirms that the style-conditioning modules effectively modulate trajectory style without compromising the fundamental task of reaching the goal.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Observations (Figure 4)

Qualitative analysis visually supported these findings, illustrating how SCDP generates intent-expressive motions in ambiguous scenes to resolve goal confusion, while reverting to efficient, direct paths when ambiguity is low. `Diffusion Policy` generally captured the data distribution, and `Legibility Diffuser` often collapsed onto the most legible mode regardless of context.

#### 4.5. Discussion

The authors noted that `Legibility Diffuser` can be tuned for maximal intent expression, potentially exceeding SCDP's legibility in specific highly ambiguous situations due to SCDP's reliance on learned data. However, SCDP offers a data-driven, environment-aware alternative that maintains high efficiency in clear scenarios without requiring explicit guidance tuning, thus providing a superior overall trade-off across diverse environmental contexts.

#### 4.6. Real-World Deployment

SCDP was successfully deployed on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot for the Block Reach task. Utilizing a YOLO model for object detection and depth information for 3D projection, the state-based `Diffusion Policy` allowed for effective sim-to-real transfer without extensive fine-tuning. The lightweight nature of the additional modules ensured negligible latency, maintaining a mean inference time of 5 seconds, comparable to the base model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Style-Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) framework represents a notable contribution to the field of human-robot collaboration and robot motion planning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Adaptive and Context-Aware Robot Behavior:** The most significant contribution is the ability of robots to dynamically adapt their motion style between legibility and efficiency based on the spatial ambiguity of the environment. This moves beyond static or manually-tuned approaches, allowing robots to be transparent only when needed and efficient otherwise. This adaptive behavior can lead to more intuitive, safer, and ultimately more effective human-robot interactions.

2. **Modular and Efficient Framework:** SCDP introduces a modular post-training pipeline that allows for style conditioning without requiring retraining or modifying a pre-trained base diffusion policy. This is a practical advantage, as it avoids the computational expense and data requirements of training large models from scratch. The lightweight nature of the scene encoder and conditioning predictor MLPs ensures that negligible latency is added during inference, making it suitable for real-time applications.

3. **Data-Driven Style Learning:** By training the conditioning predictors on specialized subsets of demonstrations (e.g., top 20% most legible or most efficient), the framework learns to generate context-appropriate trajectories directly from data. This data-driven approach contrasts with methods that rely on explicit cost functions or manually tuned guidance weights, offering a potentially more generalized and scalable solution.

4. **Enhanced Human-Robot Trust and Safety:** In ambiguous scenarios, the robot's ability to generate clearly legible motion can reduce human uncertainty about its intent, thereby increasing trust and enhancing safety in shared workspaces. In unambiguous scenarios, prioritizing efficiency ensures that the robot does not waste time or energy, leading to smoother and more productive collaboration.

5. **Generalizability of the Conditioning Approach:** The authors suggest that the proposed learning method for style conditioning could be extended to other trajectory-level concepts beyond legibility and predictability, such as safety or comfort. This implies that the modular architecture could serve as a flexible foundation for incorporating various behavioral "styles" into robot motion, making diffusion policies more versatile.

6. **Improved Practical Deployment:** The successful deployment of SCDP on a physical robot with efficient sim-to-real transfer capabilities underscores its practical applicability. The reliance on state-based observations helps avoid the reality gap often encountered in vision-based systems, facilitating easier integration into real-world robotic platforms.

While the current ambiguity detection module is a geometrical proxy, the framework lays the groundwork for incorporating more sophisticated detection mechanisms. Future user studies could empirically validate human perception of these adaptive trajectories, further solidifying the impact of legible motion on human trust and interaction quality. The ability to handle a higher number of potential goals will also be an important area for future exploration to enhance the framework's scalability and robustness in complex environments.
