# Encoding Predictability and Legibility for Style-Conditioned Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> The Style-Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) framework enables robots to dynamically adjust their motion between legible and efficient styles based on environmental spatial ambiguity by constraining a pre-trained diffusion model. This method demonstrates improved adaptive transparency scores, reaching up to 0.76 in non-ambiguous navigation tasks, and successfully transfers to a physical robot while maintaining high task completion rates.

## Problem

- Balancing robot motion legibility, crucial for human understanding and trust, with operational efficiency (e.g., speed, energy consumption) in human-robot collaboration (HRC).
- Existing robot motion generation approaches often prioritize a fixed style (either always legible or always efficient) or require manual tuning for style modulation, lacking adaptive context-awareness.
- Efficient paths can be ambiguous to human observers, especially when multiple goals are plausible, while overly legible motions can be sub-optimal in terms of time and energy.

## Method

- Development of **SCDP**, a modular framework that dynamically modulates a pre-trained ==Diffusion Policy== towards either ==legibility== or ==predictability== based on environmental spatial ambiguity.
- A ==post-training pipeline== where a base Diffusion Policy's weights are frozen, and lightweight external modules (scene encoder, legibility/predictability predictors) are trained to constrain the diffusion process via ==FiLM conditioning==.
- Integration of an ==ambiguity detection module== that uses an ==ellipse of ambiguity== to assess spatial configuration, activating legibility conditioning only when necessary, otherwise prioritizing efficiency.

## Results

- SCDP achieved superior adaptive transparency scores in non-ambiguous scenarios (e.g., **0.74** for Block Reach, **0.76** for Navigation), significantly outperforming baselines by up to **0.44** points by effectively prioritizing efficiency.
- In ambiguous scenarios, SCDP maintained competitive legibility (detachment scores of **0.70** for Block Reach, **0.64** for Navigation) while substantially improving efficiency (**0.43** and **0.47** respectively) compared to Legibility Diffuser.
- The framework successfully transferred to a physical Franka Emika Panda robot for the Block Reach task with a mean inference time of approximately **5 seconds** and maintained a high task success rate exceeding **0.98** across all experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contextual information about spatial ambiguity can be effectively encoded and utilized to adaptively condition generative models for robot behavior without requiring full retraining of the base policy.
> - Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) offers a lightweight and modular mechanism to dynamically bias the output of a diffusion policy towards specific motion styles like legibility or predictability.
> - Training external style-predictor MLPs on targeted subsets of expert demonstrations allows for data-driven learning of context-dependent motion styles, enabling a nuanced balance between legibility and efficiency.
