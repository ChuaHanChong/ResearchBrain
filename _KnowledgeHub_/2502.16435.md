# Human Cognitive Benchmarks Reveal Foundational Visual Gaps in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The VISFACTOR benchmark, derived from human cognitive psychology tests, reveals that frontier Multimodal Large Language Models achieve only 30.17% accuracy on foundational visual cognition tasks, a substantial gap compared to human performance of 78.8%. This work identifies specific deficiencies in MLLMs' basic visual reasoning, including mental rotation, spatial relations, and figure-ground discrimination.

## Problem

- MLLMs demonstrate high performance on comprehensive benchmarks but paradoxically fail on seemingly simple visual reasoning tasks that humans find effortless.
- Existing evaluation methodologies primarily focus on overall task performance, obscuring MLLMs' deficiencies in foundational visual cognitive abilities.
- Current benchmarks lack psychometric rigor and are susceptible to overfitting, necessitating a new assessment approach grounded in human cognitive psychology.

## Method

- Introduces **VISFACTOR**, a benchmark adapting 20 vision-centric subtests from the ==Factor-Referenced Cognitive Test (FRCT)== battery, covering ==visualization==, ==perception==, ==memory==, and ==reasoning==.
- Modifies test formats (e.g., ==decomposed multiple choice==, ==grouped-consistency items==) and employs ==synthetic augmentation== to reduce chance-level accuracy and generate infinite, difficulty-controlled test cases.
- Evaluates 23 frontier MLLMs and a human baseline using ==zero-shot prompting==, assessing accuracy across 10 FRCT factors.

## Results

- The best-performing MLLM, GPT-5.1, achieved an overall accuracy of **30.17%**, while human participants scored **78.8%**, demonstrating a substantial performance gap.
- MLLMs consistently struggled with core visual tasks such as mental rotation, spatial relation inference, and figure–ground discrimination, regardless of model size or recency.
- The parametric generator successfully modulated task difficulty, with MLLM performance decreasing progressively across 'Easy,' 'Normal,' and 'Hard' generated subsets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs tend to rely on high-level concept recognition for visual tasks, struggling with robust low-level visual pattern memorization and comparison, especially with abstract stimuli.
> - Models exhibit fundamental limitations in visual attention and fine-grained detail perception, failing to discern subtle features or accurately process geometric properties like length, proportion, and continuous angular orientation.
> - Chain-of-Thought prompting provides only marginal overall gains for MLLMs on VISFACTOR, sometimes degrading performance on perceptual and spatial visualization tasks.
