# Beyond Imitation: Reinforcement Learning-Based Sim-Real Co-Training for VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, termed RL-Co, integrates interactive simulation-based reinforcement learning with real-world demonstration regularization. This approach increases real-world task success rates to 64.0% for OpenVLA and 66.2% for π_0.5, while also enhancing generalization and data efficiency for robot manipulation.

## Problem

- VLA models, typically trained with limited real-world demonstrations, exhibit fragility and poor generalization to novel scenarios and real-world execution.
- High costs and difficulty associated with collecting sufficient real-world robot manipulation data pose a significant scaling bottleneck.
- Policies trained in simulation often experience a performance degradation ("sim-to-real gap") when deployed on physical robots.

## Method

- An **RL-Co** (==RL-based sim-real co-training==) framework is introduced, comprising a ==two-stage training process== for VLA models.
- The first stage initializes the VLA policy through ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== using both real-world and simulated demonstrations.
- The second stage refines the policy using ==reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation==, regularized by ==ongoing SFT on real-world data== to prevent domain drift.

## Results

- Real-world average task success rates increased to **64.0%** for OpenVLA models and **66.2%** for π_0.5 models, outperforming real-only SFT and SFT co-training baselines.
- Policies trained with RL-Co demonstrated enhanced generalization capabilities and stronger robustness to distribution shifts, such as unseen objects and perturbed initial states.
- The framework significantly improved data efficiency, requiring fewer real-world demonstrations to achieve high performance compared to purely supervised methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating interactive reinforcement learning in simulation, continuously anchored by real-world supervision, is crucial for expanding VLA policy capabilities and improving real-world performance.
> - Effective policy initialization through supervised fine-tuning using both real and simulated demonstrations significantly bootstraps simulation competence for efficient subsequent RL.
> - Real-world data serves as a vital regularizer during simulation-based RL, mitigating domain drift and catastrophic forgetting of real-world behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robots is a significant objective in robotics research, with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models emerging as a promising approach. These models integrate visual observations, language instructions, and action generation to facilitate complex robotic tasks. A common method for adapting VLA models to specific tasks involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using large-scale robot demonstrations and visual-language priors. However, policies trained with limited real-world demonstrations often exhibit fragility when encountering novel scenes, object variations, or closed-loop execution discrepancies. The collection of extensive real-world robot data is a time-consuming, expensive, and difficult process to scale.

Simulation environments offer a potential solution to this data bottleneck by providing a scalable and low-cost platform for generating trajectories and facilitating closed-loop interaction. Nevertheless, policies trained exclusively in simulation typically face the "sim-to-real gap," where performance degrades upon deployment in physical environments. Existing strategies to address this gap include domain randomization, which introduces variations in visual and physical parameters, and real-to-sim-to-real pipelines, which aim to improve simulator fidelity through scene reconstruction and system identification. While these methods can reduce the sim-to-real discrepancy, they often necessitate careful design, precise reconstruction, or task-specific simulator tuning.

Sim-real co-training presents an alternative approach by incorporating real-world data directly into the training process, rather than relying solely on zero-shot transfer from simulation. Prior work in co-training focuses on aligning sim-real representations or joint observation-action distributions, and some studies indicate that even low-fidelity or weakly aligned simulations can enhance real-world policies by expanding the task-relevant training distribution. However, a limitation of most existing co-training methods is their treatment of simulation as a static source of demonstrations for SFT, largely neglecting the benefits of active closed-loop interaction.

The integration of reinforcement learning (RL) into policy improvement directly addresses this limitation. RL enables policies to learn through trial-and-error interaction, which can mitigate the closed-loop distribution shifts inherent in pure imitation learning. However, direct RL application to robotic manipulation often encounters challenges such as sparse rewards, long task horizons, and high exploration costs. Consequently, combining imitation learning with RL, through mechanisms like demonstration initialization or behavior-prior regularization, is a common practice. Recent advancements in VLA and large robot policies have similarly utilized SFT followed by RL to improve task success and generalization. However, these methods typically operate within a single interaction domain or depend on zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. This paper situates itself within this broader context by proposing a sim-real VLA post-training framework that specifically exploits cost-effective simulation interaction while concurrently anchoring policy updates with real demonstrations to prevent drift from real-world visual observations, action distributions, and execution priors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the real-world deployment capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by effectively integrating interactive simulation training with real-world data. The authors highlight a critical limitation in existing sim-real co-training methodologies: most approaches primarily leverage simulation as a static repository of demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This static utilization fails to exploit the potential for large-scale, closed-loop interaction that simulation environments offer, leading to constrained gains in real-world performance and generalization capabilities.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that while simulation provides a scalable and cost-effective means to generate diverse training data, simply mixing simulated demonstrations with real-world data through SFT does not fully unlock the benefits of active learning. Policies trained via imitation are susceptible to covariate shift and compounding errors during closed-loop execution in the real world. Reinforcement learning, with its emphasis on reward-guided interaction, can address these issues by enabling policies to explore a wider range of behaviors and adapt to dynamic environments. However, applying RL directly to real robots is often impractical due to safety concerns, high reset costs, and poor sample efficiency.

Therefore, the key objectives of this paper are:

1. **Develop an RL-based Sim-Real Co-Training Framework**: To design a novel framework, termed RL-Co, that allows VLA models to learn through interactive reinforcement learning within simulation environments.
2. **Preserve Real-World Capabilities and Mitigate Forgetting**: To incorporate mechanisms that explicitly prevent domain drift and catastrophic forgetting of real-world behaviors during simulation-driven RL fine-tuning. This involves using real-world demonstrations as cross-domain anchors.
3. **Improve Real-World Success and Generalization**: To empirically demonstrate that the proposed RL-Co framework leads to higher real-world task success rates and improved generalization to unseen task variations (e.g., novel objects, perturbed initial states) compared to existing SFT-based methods.
4. **Enhance Data Efficiency**: To show that RL-Co can significantly reduce the amount of real-world demonstration data required for effective robot deployment, thereby offering a more practical and scalable training pathway.
5. **Validate Across Architectures**: To evaluate the framework's effectiveness across different VLA model architectures to confirm its generic applicability.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of passive data utilization from simulation and pave the way for more robust, generalized, and data-efficient VLA models for real-world robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed RL-based sim-real Co-training (RL-Co), employs a two-stage design to leverage interactive simulation while preserving real-world capabilities for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and VLA Post-Training

The framework operates in a two-domain manipulation setting: a real-world task domain (T_real) and a corresponding simulation domain (T_sim). Both domains share the robot embodiment, action interface, language instructions, and task-level initial-state distribution, but allow for visual and dynamics discrepancies. VLA policies (π_θ) map recent observations and language instructions to robot actions. The paper utilizes two standard VLA post-training primitives: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on demonstrations and reinforcement learning (RL) via reward-guided interaction.

#### 3.2. Framework Design: RL-Co

**Stage I: SFT Co-Training for Policy Initialization**
The first stage focuses on warm-starting the VLA policy (π_θ) from a pre-trained state (not yet adapted to the target tasks) using a combination of real-world (D_real) and simulated (D_sim) demonstrations. This is achieved through a supervised fine-tuning objective:
L_SFT(θ) = α L_SFT(θ; D_sim) + (1 − α) L_SFT(θ; D_real)
Here, α ∈ controls the relative contribution of simulated data. This stage serves two key purposes:
1. **Real-World Knowledge Injection**: Enables the policy to quickly acquire task-specific knowledge crucial for real-world deployment.
2. **Simulation Capability Bootstrapping**: Provides the policy with sufficient competence in simulation, forming a non-trivial starting point for the subsequent RL phase.

**Stage II: Sim-Real Co-Training with Real-Regularized RL**
Following initialization, Stage II aims to expand the policy's capabilities through online interaction in simulation while preventing degradation of its real-world performance. This is achieved by combining a reinforcement learning loss with an auxiliary supervised loss on real-world data:
L_total = L_RL + β L_SFT(θ; D_real)
Here:
1. **L_RL**: The reinforcement learning term drives policy updates by encouraging exploration and maximizing task rewards in the simulation environment. This allows for closed-loop interaction and skill expansion.
2. **β L_SFT(θ; D_real)**: The auxiliary SFT loss on the real-world dataset (D_real) acts as a regularizer. Its purpose is to anchor the policy to real demonstrations, mitigate catastrophic forgetting of previously learned real-world behaviors, and prevent domain drift that could arise from purely simulation-driven updates. β is a hyperparameter balancing the contribution of RL and real-world regularization.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setting

- **Tasks**: Four real-world tabletop manipulation tasks were used: Pick and Place, Push Cube via Instruction, Open Drawer, and Close Drawer. These tasks require a combination of perception, language grounding, and control.
- **Robot**: A Franka Emika Panda robot with 7-DoF end-effector delta control was used in both real and simulated environments. Visual input was from a fixed RGB camera.
- **Simulation Environment**: ManiSkill was used to construct simulation environments, aligned with the real setup at the task level but allowing for differences in object identity, materials, textures, and lighting. Lightweight system identification was performed for the Franka controller to better align low-level robot response.
- **Data Generation**:
 - **D_real**: 20-50 successful expert demonstrations per task collected via human teleoperation.
 - **D_sim**: 1,000 successful simulated trajectories per task generated using MimicGen, seeded by replayed real-world trajectories to ground the simulation data in real-world behaviors.
- **VLA Models**: The framework was instantiated on two representative VLA architectures:
 - **OpenVLA**: A next-token prediction-based model. RL fine-tuning followed Liu et al..
 - **π_0.5**: A flow-matching-based model. RL fine-tuning utilized ReinFlow implemented with RLinf.
- **Evaluation**: Methods were evaluated on independently sampled initial states, with task success rate as the primary metric. Generalization was tested under unseen object categories and perturbed robot initial states. Comparisons were made against real-only SFT and SFT-based sim-real co-training baselines. Additional baselines included heavy visual domain randomization, video-based data augmentation (Cosmos-Transfer), one-stage RL co-training, and RialTo-style distillation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate the RL-Co framework yielded several findings across various tasks and VLA architectures.

#### 4.1. Real-World Effectiveness Across Tasks and VLA Models

RL-Co consistently improved real-world success rates. On OpenVLA, average success rate improved to 64.0% from 16.5% (real-only SFT) and 40.0% (SFT co-training). Specifically, on tasks like Pick and Place, OpenVLA's success increased from 6.3% (real-only SFT) to 58.8% (RL-Co). For π_0.5, average success rate increased to 66.2% from 26.7% (real-only SFT) and 45.9% (SFT co-training). On Open Drawer, π_0.5's success rose from 0.0% (real-only SFT) to 65.0% (RL-Co). These results indicate that leveraging closed-loop simulation RL, while anchoring updates with real-world SFT, effectively expands policy capabilities.

#### 4.2. Generalization Under Distribution Shifts

RL-Co demonstrated stronger robustness to distribution shifts. On the Pick and Place task with π_0.5, real-only SFT experienced sharp performance degradation under unseen objects and perturbed initial states. SFT-based sim-real co-training improved robustness by incorporating simulated variation, but RL-Co maintained the strongest performance and the smallest drops. This suggests that interactive simulation aids the policy in acquiring closed-loop behaviors that transfer more reliably to novel real-world conditions.

#### 4.3. Targeted Comparison with Stronger Visual-Diversity Baselines

The performance gains of RL-Co were not solely attributable to increased visual diversity. When compared to baselines employing heavy visual domain randomization in simulation and video-based data augmentation (Cosmos-Transfer) on real demonstrations, RL-Co still achieved superior results. For example, on Pick and Place with π_0.5, Cosmos augmentation yielded 67.2% success, while RL-Co achieved 81.3%. This indicates that RL-Co's benefit stems from expanding the policy's action-level behavioral repertoire through reward-driven interaction, rather than just visual variations.

#### 4.4. Mechanistic Analysis and Design Ablations

- **Effect of Simulation SFT Initialization (Stage I)**: Ablations showed that without simulated SFT initialization, the policy exhibited extremely poor sample efficiency and remained near-trivial after extensive interaction steps (e.g., over three million steps). The sim-real SFT co-training in Stage I was crucial for providing a strong initialization, enabling efficient RL optimization in Stage II.
- **Role of Real-World Supervision in Two Stages**: Removing the Stage II real-world SFT regularization substantially reduced real-world success, indicating domain drift during simulation RL without explicit real-data anchoring. Further removing Stage I real-world SFT led to additional performance degradation, suggesting that supervised learning extracts useful priors from limited real demonstrations more efficiently than RL alone. Removing real-world supervision from both stages resulted in a collapse of performance.
- **Sensitivity to Co-Training Ratio (α) and Regularization Weight (β)**: RL-Co consistently improved over corresponding SFT-co-trained policies and exceeded SFT-only models across a range of α (Stage I data mixture) and β (Stage II regularization weight) values. This indicates the framework's robustness to hyperparameter variations once a reasonable SFT initialization is established.

#### 4.5. Real-Data Efficiency

RL-Co demonstrated enhanced data efficiency. On the Open Drawer task, RL-Co achieved performance levels comparable to or superior to supervised baselines (real-only SFT and SFT co-training) with significantly fewer real-world demonstrations. This suggests that RL-Co can effectively amplify limited real data through interactive simulation, offering a more data-efficient path to deployment than collecting more physical demonstrations.

#### 4.6. Additional Dexterous Evaluation

On the more precision-demanding Peg Insertion task, RL-Co improved real-world success from 32.5% (SFT-based sim-real co-training) to 42.5% (RL-Co) with the π_0.5 backbone, indicating its applicability to tasks requiring fine manipulation.

#### 4.7. Sim-Real Correlation During RL Co-Training

Analysis of simulation success rate versus real-world success rate during RL co-training showed a positive correlation, suggesting that improvements observed in simulation generally translated to real-world gains. This supports the use of simulation convergence as a practical signal for checkpoint selection.

#### 4.8. Additional Baselines

- **One-Stage RL Co-Training**: A variant attempting to bypass Stage I SFT initialization by directly applying Stage II RL co-training (with mixed real/sim SFT regularization) failed to acquire the task, achieving 0.0% real-world success. This reinforced the necessity of the SFT warm-up stage.
- **RialTo-style Distillation**: This baseline, which involved training a simulation expert and then using its generated trajectories for SFT co-training, achieved 75.0% success on Pick and Place with π_0.5. While outperforming standard SFT co-training, it remained below RL-Co's 81.3%, indicating that direct closed-loop policy improvement within the VLA model itself is more effective than distilling a simulation expert via static trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RL-Co framework presents a method for enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by systematically integrating interactive simulation with real-world data. This approach addresses a notable gap in existing sim-real co-training methods, which predominantly rely on static supervised fine-tuning and thus do not fully exploit the benefits of closed-loop interaction available in simulation.

The demonstrated improvements in real-world task success rates across diverse manipulation tasks and VLA architectures signify a practical advancement for robot learning. The framework's ability to yield stronger generalization to unseen task variations suggests that policies trained with RL-Co acquire more robust and adaptable behaviors, which is critical for deployment in dynamic and unstructured real-world environments. Furthermore, the observed reduction in the requirement for real-world demonstration data indicates a more scalable and cost-effective pathway for developing and deploying VLA models. This capability is particularly relevant given the inherent expense and logistical challenges associated with collecting large volumes of high-quality real-robot data.

By enabling VLA models to learn through reward-driven interaction in simulation while simultaneously preventing domain drift and catastrophic forgetting via real-world supervision, RL-Co contributes to the development of more capable and resilient robot policies. This work suggests that interactive simulation, when appropriately grounded in real-world experience, can unlock performance benefits that static imitation objectives alone cannot fully realize. The findings encourage further exploration of hybrid training paradigms that judiciously combine the scalability of simulation with the specificity of real-world data.

The potential impact of this research extends to broader applications in robotics, including general-purpose manipulation, service robotics, and manufacturing, where robust and adaptive control is paramount. While the current study focuses on tabletop manipulation with a single robot embodiment, the generic two-stage design of RL-Co suggests potential for extension to more complex tasks, longer task horizons, heterogeneous sim-real settings, and a wider array of robot embodiments. Future research directions could involve refining sim-real alignment techniques and developing more efficient sim-real RL algorithms to further amplify the observed gains.
