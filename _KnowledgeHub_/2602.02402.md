# SoMA: A Real-to-Sim Neural Simulator for Robotic Soft-body Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SoMA introduces a real-to-sim neural simulator for robotic soft-body manipulation, leveraging hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splats to model force-driven dynamics. It achieves 20% improvement in resimulation accuracy and generalization over baselines on real-world datasets, enabling stable and action-conditioned simulation of complex deformable objects for tasks like T-shirt folding.

## Problem

- Existing physics-based simulators struggle with accurately inferring physical parameters from real-world visual data, leading to a significant "reality gap" for deformable objects.
- Neural dynamics models, while good at reconstructing motion, lack explicit support for robot-conditioned interaction, making them unsuitable for predicting novel robot actions or ensuring long-horizon stability.
- Collecting large-scale, real-world robot manipulation data for deformable objects is prohibitively costly and time-consuming, necessitating robust real-to-sim transfer.

## Method

- SoMA represents deformable objects using hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splats, where dynamics are propagated through a graph structure for multi-scale coherence.
- It models object dynamics as forces (environmental and robot-induced) driving Gaussian splat motion, predicted by neural interaction modules, enabling action-conditioned control and generalization.
- Multi-resolution training and blended supervision (occlusion-aware image loss plus momentum consistency regularization) are used to ensure long-horizon stability and robustness to partial observability.

## Results

- SoMA demonstrates a 20% improvement in resimulation accuracy and generalization compared to state-of-the-art baselines on real-world robotic soft-body manipulation tasks.
- The simulator achieves superior quantitative performance across image-based (e.g., PSNR 33.51 in resimulation vs. PhysTwin's 28.77) and depth-based metrics in both resimulation and generalization scenarios.
- It qualitatively produces highly accurate and stable dynamic evolutions for complex tasks like T-shirt folding, maintaining coherent geometry and realistic deformations over long interaction horizons where baselines exhibit artifacts or collapse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling robot-object interactions as forces acting on neural representations (Gaussian splats) is crucial for achieving generalization to unseen manipulation actions.
> - Combining multi-resolution temporal training with multi-resolution image reconstruction significantly improves long-horizon stability and prevents error accumulation in neural dynamics models.
> - A blended supervision approach, including occlusion-aware image loss and physics-inspired momentum consistency regularization, effectively handles partial observability and unobserved dynamics in real-world scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotics heavily relies on efficient and accurate simulation for data generation, policy learning, and system testing. Simulating deformable objects, such as cloth or ropes, under rich robot interactions, presents a fundamental challenge, particularly in achieving "real-to-sim" (R2S) transfer – that is, accurately replicating real-world behaviors in a virtual environment. SoMA directly addresses this challenge, positioning itself at the intersection of computer vision, physically-based simulation, and data-driven neural dynamics.

Existing approaches to simulating deformable objects generally fall into two categories, each with significant limitations for robotic manipulation:

1. **Physics-based simulators:** Methods like Finite Element Method (FEM), Material Point Method (MPM), or Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) are excellent at ensuring consistent interaction and physical plausibility over long horizons. However, they critically depend on predefined physical models and parameters (e.g., stiffness, friction, density) that are exceedingly difficult to accurately infer from real-world visual data. Differentiable simulators attempt to optimize these parameters but are still constrained by simplified physical assumptions and struggle with complex, real-world interactions and occlusions. Their reliance on explicit physical models often leads to a significant "reality gap" when trying to match real-world observations.

2. **Neural dynamics modeling and 4D reconstruction:** These data-driven approaches learn object motion directly from visual data, often leveraging neural representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) or, more recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for dynamic scenes (4D reconstruction). While they excel at reconstructing observed motions and capturing dynamic geometry, their primary focus is reproduction rather than prediction or generalization. Crucially, they typically offer limited support for robot-conditioned interaction, meaning they cannot easily simulate how an object *would* deform under a novel robot action or beyond the trajectories seen during training. State-based neural simulators often struggle with long-horizon stability and generalization, especially when faced with out-of-distribution robot actions.

SoMA's contribution is to **bridge these two worlds**. It leverages the strengths of neural representations (3D Gaussian Splatting for high-fidelity rendering and efficient dynamics encoding) while addressing the critical needs of robotic manipulation: explicit robot-conditioned control, interaction-consistency, long-horizon stability, and generalization beyond observed data. By modeling forces and interactions directly on Gaussian splats within a unified neural framework, SoMA moves beyond mere reconstruction to enable truly *action-driven simulation* that is both data-fidelity (from real observations) and interaction-aware (from robot kinematics and environmental forces). This approach aims to create a physically meaningful, yet data-learned, simulator that can significantly reduce the "real-to-sim" gap for complex soft-body manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind SoMA is to overcome the inherent challenges and limitations of collecting large-scale, real-world robot manipulation data, especially for tasks involving deformable objects like cloth folding or handling flexible materials. Such data collection is prohibitively costly, time-consuming, and risky. Real-to-sim (R2S) simulation offers a scalable alternative for data synthesis, augmentation, and policy learning, but existing simulators fall short in critical aspects.

The key objectives that SoMA aims to achieve are:

1. **High Physical Fidelity and Interaction Consistency:** To faithfully capture the geometry and complex dynamics of deformable objects under rich robot-object interactions without introducing R2S bias. This includes ensuring stable and coherent object behavior over sustained interactions, which is crucial for long-horizon tasks.
2. **Action-Conditioned Control:** To enable robot actions (specifically, joint-space actions) to directly drive object dynamics within the simulation. This moves beyond simply tracking observed motion to allowing controllable, causal interaction, which is essential for policy learning and planning in robotics.
3. **Generalization to Unseen Manipulations:** To create a simulator that can accurately predict object behavior even when confronted with novel robot actions or contact configurations not present in the training data. This is a significant challenge for purely data-driven methods that tend to memorize trajectories.
4. **Stability and Long-Horizon Simulation:** To provide a robust simulator that does not suffer from error accumulation, drift, or structural collapse over extended periods of interaction, a common issue in many data-driven dynamic systems.
5. **Unified Representation and End-to-End Learning:** To integrate deformable object representation, robot actions, and environmental effects within a single, unified latent neural space, allowing for end-to-end learning directly from multi-view RGB observations without relying on predefined physical models or parameters.
6. **Addressing Partial Observability:** To handle common challenges in real-world scenarios, such as partial occlusion of the deformable object by the robot end-effector or self-contact, which makes direct 3D tracking or pure image-based supervision unreliable.

By pursuing these objectives, SoMA aims to provide a practical and robust simulation tool that can significantly accelerate research and development in robot manipulation of soft bodies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoMA is designed as a unified neural simulator that directly operates on learned Gaussian splat (GS) representations, coupling deformable dynamics, environmental forces, and robot joint actions within a unified latent neural space. The methodology comprises several innovative components:

#### 3.1. Object Representation: Hierarchical Gaussian Splats

Deformable objects are represented as a collection of Gaussian splats (g_i = {x_i, Σ_i, m_i, a_i} for position, covariance, mass, and attributes). To improve efficiency and capture multi-scale dynamics, these splats are organized into a hierarchical graph structure. Fine-level splats are clustered recursively to form higher-level nodes, aggregating physical states. Dynamics are propagated top-down through this hierarchy using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), allowing coherent global motion while preserving local deformations.

#### 3.2. Problem Formulation and Overall Framework

Given multi-view RGB images (I_{t,i}) and robot joint states (R_t), SoMA reconstructs an initial object state (G_0) as GS. The core task is to learn an action-conditioned simulator ϕ_θ that predicts the next object state: G_t = ϕ_θ(G_{t-1}, G_{t-2}, R_t). Training is end-to-end, minimizing a reconstruction loss between rendered images (ˆI_{t,i}) from G_t and observed images (I_{t,i}).

#### 3.3. Scene Initialization via R2S Mapping

To ensure consistency between real-world observations and the simulation space:
- **Reconstruction:** Multi-view RGB images and estimated camera poses are used to reconstruct the deformable object as an initial set of Gaussian splats (G_0) in the camera coordinate system, leveraging existing GS reconstruction techniques.
- **Robot Conditioning:** A crucial step to align the virtual and physical robots. A global scale factor and rigid transformation are computed by matching reconstructed geometry with known robot dimensions and the pose of a robot-mounted camera. This allows mapping robot joint configurations (q_t) via forward kinematics (FK) into the simulation space (T_ee^sim(t)), defining robot actions (end-effector pose and gripper state c_t).
- **Physical Reference Frames:** The supporting table plane is fitted from the reconstructed point cloud to determine the gravity direction, aligning the simulation's physical orientation with the real scene.

#### 3.4. Force-Driven GS Dynamics Modeling

Unlike state-based neural models, SoMA explicitly models interactions as forces to enable generalization across diverse interaction patterns.
- **Force-Driven Dynamics:** For each GS node/cluster, linear velocity and rotation are predicted from historical states and the aggregated interaction force (f_i) using a hierarchical neural dynamics model (ψ_θ).
- **Environment Force:** Gravity and supporting forces (for splats close to the surface) are explicitly calculated and aggregated hierarchically.
- **Robot Force:** Robot-induced forces are predicted using a graph-based neural interaction module (Φ_θ), which considers the interaction between robot control points (derived from end-effector pose) and neighboring GS nodes, along with the gripper state. The total interaction force (f_i = f_env_i + f_rob_i) then drives the dynamics.

#### 3.5. Multi-Resolution Training (MRT) for Long-Horizon Stability

To combat error accumulation and instability over long rollouts, especially with GS-based representations that lack easy reinitialization:
- **Multi-resolution Temporal:** A coarse-to-fine scheme is employed. Initial training uses a larger temporal stride (k⋅dt) to capture long-range dynamics, followed by fine-tuning at the original resolution (dt) on sampled subsequences for detailed dynamics.
- **Multi-resolution Image:** GS geometry is reconstructed from super-resolution images for detail, while dynamics training is performed at the original image resolution for computational efficiency.

#### 3.6. Blended Supervision for Partial Observability

Real-world manipulation often involves occlusions. To address this:
- **Occlusion-aware Image Supervision:** The image reconstruction loss (L_img) is applied only to visible object regions, identified by object masks. This prevents spurious gradients from occluded areas.
- **Momentum Consistency Regularization (L_mom):** To regularize unobserved (occluded) dynamics and mitigate drift, a physics-inspired momentum conservation constraint is enforced between adjacent hierarchy levels of the GS representation. This provides a self-supervised physical regularization without requiring ground-truth forces.

Together, these components create an interaction-aware, physically-consistent, and stable neural simulation pipeline for complex soft-body manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SoMA demonstrates significant advancements in real-to-sim simulation for robotic soft-body manipulation, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods across various metrics and challenging scenarios.

**Quantitative Performance:**
- SoMA achieves a **20% improvement** in resimulation accuracy and generalization compared to baselines on real-world robot manipulation datasets.
- Across image-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and depth-based metrics (Abs Rel, RMSE), SoMA consistently yields superior results in both resimulation (on training trajectories) and generalization (on unseen manipulation sequences). For instance, in resimulation, SoMA achieves PSNR of 33.51 compared to PhysTwin's 28.77 and GausSim's 31.69. Similarly, for generalization, SoMA significantly outperforms baselines across all metrics.

**Qualitative Performance:**
- **Resimulation:** SoMA produces visually accurate resimulated trajectories that closely match ground-truth observations over long horizons. The simulated object dynamics remain stable, accurately preserving both global motion and local deformations, unlike PhysTwin (which shows deviations under occlusion) and GausSim (which often becomes static or unstable).
- **Generalization:** A critical strength of SoMA is its ability to generalize. It maintains accurate and stable dynamic evolution even when presented with unseen robot actions and novel contact configurations, closely matching ground-truth observations. This highlights the effectiveness of its action-conditioned and force-driven dynamics modeling.
- **Complex Interactions (T-shirt Folding):** SoMA successfully simulates highly challenging tasks like T-shirt folding, which involve long-horizon dynamics, large deformations, and frequent self-contacts. It produces coherent geometry and realistic dynamics throughout the entire folding process, while baselines exhibit pronounced artifacts, inconsistent deformations, or structural collapse. Quantitatively, SoMA again significantly outperforms baselines on this complex task (e.g., PSNR 27.57 vs. PhysTwin's 22.85).

**Ablation Studies:**
The ablation studies confirm the importance of SoMA's key methodological components:
- **Multi-Resolution Training (MRT):** Disabling MRT leads to a consistent drop in performance (e.g., PSNR drop from 32.73 to 31.97 in resimulation), demonstrating its crucial role in stabilizing long-horizon simulation and preventing error accumulation.
- **Blended Supervision (Occlusion-aware image + Momentum consistency):** The "Img-only" variant (removing blended supervision) suffers the largest degradation, particularly in generalization (PSNR drops from 31.49 to 29.17). This underscores the necessity of combining masked image supervision for visible regions with physics-inspired momentum consistency regularization for unobserved dynamics, especially for learning transferable interaction dynamics in occluded scenes.
- **Joint Training:** Training on all object domains slightly reduces resimulation accuracy but improves generalization, indicating potential for learning more robust dynamics from diverse data and scalability.

**Evaluation on External Datasets:**
SoMA also performs robustly when applied to PhysTwin's datasets (which use 3D-tracked hand trajectories instead of robot actions). It consistently outperforms baselines, demonstrating its adaptability beyond purely robot-action-driven settings and its ability to effectively model interaction dynamics driven by external motion trajectories.

In summary, SoMA provides a robust, accurate, and generalizable neural simulator for soft-body manipulation, making significant strides in handling complex real-world robot interactions and long-horizon tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SoMA represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI, robotics, and physically-based simulation, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Reduced Reality Gap for Real-to-Sim (R2S) Transfer:** SoMA's core contribution is its ability to learn deformable object dynamics directly from real-world RGB observations and anchor them to real robot kinematics. This fundamentally reduces the "reality gap" that plagues traditional physics-based simulators (due to parameter estimation challenges) and purely data-driven neural dynamics models (due to lack of interaction-awareness and generalization). A smaller reality gap means simulations are more faithful to reality, enabling more effective transfer of policies learned in simulation to physical robots.

2. **Enabling Scalable Data Generation for Robot Learning:** The high cost and risk associated with collecting real-world interaction data, especially for delicate deformable objects, is a major bottleneck in robot learning. SoMA offers a scalable alternative by providing a practical virtual environment capable of synthesizing vast amounts of interaction data. This can significantly accelerate the development and training of robust manipulation policies for soft-bodied objects, leading to more capable and versatile robots.

3. **Advanced Robot Policy Learning and Development:** By providing a stable, action-conditioned, and long-horizon simulator, SoMA enables:
 - **Prediction and Planning:** Robots can use SoMA to predict the outcomes of different manipulation strategies, allowing for better planning and decision-making for complex tasks.
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** The simulator can serve as a robust environment for training reinforcement learning agents, where policies can be learned and refined in a safe and efficient virtual space.
 - **Task-Level Analysis:** Complex tasks like T-shirt folding, which involve sequential actions, large deformations, and self-contacts, can be thoroughly analyzed and optimized within SoMA, fostering the development of intelligent manipulation strategies.

4. **Novel Research Direction at the Intersection of Fields:** SoMA's approach of leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting for dynamics modeling, coupled with explicit force-driven robot-object interactions and robust training strategies, opens up new avenues for research. It encourages further exploration into how neural representations can encode physical properties and interaction dynamics more effectively, bridging the gap between computer graphics, vision, and physics.

5. **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated on specific objects like cloth, rope, and dolls, the principles behind SoMA—hierarchical neural dynamics, force-driven interactions, and robust supervision for partial observability—are broadly applicable. This suggests potential for future expansion to a wider range of deformable materials, complex manipulation scenarios, and even other domains requiring interaction-aware dynamic scene modeling.

**Limitations and Responsible AI Considerations:**
The authors acknowledge that SoMA's performance is dependent on the quality of visual reconstruction and may degrade under severe occlusion or highly complex contact patterns that are drastically outside the training distribution. This highlights the inherent data-driven nature of the system. While offering immense potential, the authors wisely emphasize that simulation-based models must be applied with caution, and real-world deployment requires rigorous verification to ensure safety and reliability. This responsible AI stance is crucial, reminding that even the most advanced simulators are tools that must be used judiciously in conjunction with real-world testing. Future research will naturally focus on expanding the diversity of training data and enhancing robustness to even more extreme conditions.
