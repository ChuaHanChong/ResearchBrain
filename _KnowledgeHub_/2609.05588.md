# GE-Act 2.0: Pretraining and Scaling a World-Action Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GE-Act 2.0 is a world-action model for robotic manipulation trained from scratch on manipulation data instead of inheriting a pretrained video generator. A control-oriented autoencoder compresses frames aggressively, a single-step visual planner denoises multi-view future latents in one differentiable pass, and an inverse dynamics model decodes actions; selective co-training filters action-compatible futures. Scaling to 30,000 hours lifts zero-shot out-of-distribution success to 44.1% across 100 real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing world-action models inherit video autoencoders and generators tuned for reconstruction fidelity, leaving dense latents and leaving from-scratch pretraining and scaling of the whole system unstudied.
- Multi-step diffusion makes differentiating action loss through generation costly, so prior systems decode actions from intermediate generator states and cannot pretrain inverse dynamics separately on instruction-free or failed rollouts.
- Most evaluations fine-tune on target tasks under the same scenes and lighting, entangling capability acquired in pretraining with capability added by downstream adaptation.

## Method

- **Control-oriented autoencoder (CoAE)**: framewise encoder with 64x spatial downsampling to 24 tokens per frame, trained with pixel, ==LPIPS== and ==adversarial== reconstruction plus ==cosine alignment== to three frozen visual teachers.
- **Single-step visual planner (SVP)**: conditional ==MeanFlow== objective over a multi-view ==DiT==, conditioned on gated fusion of frozen vision-language-model text-span states, fully denoising dense and sparse future latents in one pass.
- **KASO** co-training: sample N candidate futures, score each by the active ==inverse dynamics model=='s velocity disagreement at a high-noise action probe, replay the top-k generation noise with gradients, retaining both pretraining losses.

## Results

- Scaling co-training data from **300** to **30,000** hours raised zero-shot OOD success from **17.1%** to **44.1%** on G1-OP and **13.4%** to **31.1%** on the data-scarce G2-90D embodiment.
- Controlled ablation: KASO reached **40%** single-object pick success versus **12%** for both end-to-end variants, and **37.5%** four-object macro pick success versus **22.5%** at equal **95%** follow score.
- Skill coverage correlated with success (Pearson r = **0.80**, **1.94** logit per decade); on RoboTwin Hard it scored **60.52%** against **47.90%** for the strongest baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Judge a control latent space by action-recovery probing, not reconstruction fidelity; extreme compression costs little accuracy when latents are aligned to complementary frozen visual teachers.
> - Pairing an independently generated future with a recorded action injects mismatched supervision that collapses action modes; compatibility should be judged in action space, not pixel space.
> - Capability tracks skill-specific and phrase-specific data coverage, so broadening long-tail skills and rare referring expressions matters more than raw hours alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements through large-scale pretraining, particularly in areas such as language models, multimodal understanding, and visual generation. In robotics, this trend has led to the development of vision-language-action (VLA) policies, which directly map observations and instructions to motor commands. While effective at scale, these VLA policies typically focus on a direct action objective, which does not inherently require explicit modeling of the underlying physical dynamics of interaction.

An emerging paradigm in robot manipulation is the development of World-Action Models (WAMs). WAMs predict future scene states, leveraging these predictions to support action generation. This approach offers two primary advantages for scaling robot learning:
1. **Visual-generation pretraining:** WAMs can learn physical and semantic scene structures from large volumes of action-free video data, a scale often unattainable with robot demonstrations alone.
2. **Inverse dynamics learning:** An inverse dynamics model (IDM) within a WAM can potentially learn from robot trajectories that lack explicit instruction or success annotations, such as failed attempts or deployment rollouts.

However, existing WAM research commonly builds upon video generators that have been pretrained on generic video data (e.g., natural video), rather than specifically on manipulation data from scratch. Substantial effort in these systems is often directed towards coupling these pre-existing video generators with action models. This leaves two system-level questions largely unaddressed: how to pretrain visual generation and inverse dynamics models on their respective, often complementary, data sources before their integration, and how the capabilities of a WAM scale with manipulation data when all its trainable visual-generation and action components are initialized without prior general video training.

GE-Act 2.0 positions itself to address these gaps by focusing on the pretraining and scaling of a WAM from scratch, specifically on manipulation data. It distinguishes itself from generalist robot policies and other WAMs by:
- **Representation:** Instead of inheriting latent spaces optimized for general video reconstruction, GE-Act 2.0 introduces a control-oriented autoencoder (CoAE) designed for aggressive compression while preserving control-relevant information.
- **Generation:** It develops a single-step visual planner (SVP), contrasting with conventional multi-step video generators, to enable independent pretraining of the visual generator and the inverse dynamics model. This facilitates the use of diverse data types (action-free video for SVP, instruction-free trajectories for IDM).
- **Action-Generation Alignment:** It introduces a mechanism called Knowledge-Aligned Selective Optimization (KASO) to manage the "validity gap" – the mismatch that can occur when a generated future, representing one plausible outcome mode, is paired with a recorded action from a different outcome mode in co-training. This contrasts with simpler teacher-forcing or detached-sampling approaches.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** The work emphasizes zero-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation without task-specific fine-tuning. This differs from many existing WAM and VLA evaluations that include downstream supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on target tasks or embodiments, thereby entangling pretrained and adaptation capabilities.

This research contributes to the development of more generalizable and scalable robotic manipulation systems by systematically investigating how to build and scale WAMs from fundamental components using diverse manipulation-specific data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the pretraining and scaling of a world-action model (WAM) designed from scratch specifically for robotic manipulation data. This addresses two central, unresolved system-level questions within existing WAM research:
1. **Decoupled Component Pretraining:** How can visual generation models and inverse dynamics models be separately pretrained on their respective, often heterogeneous, data sources before being effectively integrated? Existing WAMs typically rely on video generators pretrained on general natural video, which may not be optimized for robotic control.
2. **Scalability from Scratch:** How do the capabilities of a WAM scale with the volume of manipulation data when all its trainable visual-generation and action components are initialized without any prior training on broad, non-manipulation video data?

To achieve these overarching objectives, GE-Act 2.0 focuses on three specific design problems for scalable WAM pretraining:

1. **Representation Learning for Control (CoAE):** The motivation here is to create a latent space that is both efficient for future prediction and informative for action recovery. Current systems often adopt latent spaces from standard video autoencoders, which are optimized for reconstruction fidelity and result in dense latent grids, imposing a high computational burden for future prediction within a control loop. The objective is to develop a *control-oriented autoencoder (CoAE)* that learns an aggressively compressed latent space while explicitly preserving information crucial for both visual prediction and the subsequent inference of robot actions.

2. **Enabling Decoupled and One-Step Visual Generation (SVP):** The goal is to allow the visual generation and inverse dynamics components to leverage different types of data more effectively. Visual generation can benefit from action-free video, while inverse dynamics can learn from instruction-free robot trajectories (e.g., failed attempts, play data). However, multi-step video generators, common in prior WAMs, make standalone IDM pretraining difficult and costly to integrate due to the computational overhead of backpropagating action gradients through many sequential denoising steps. The objective is to introduce a *single-step visual planner (SVP)* that produces a complete future visual state in one differentiable pass, thereby enabling separate pretraining of the SVP and IDM on complementary data, followed by efficient end-to-end co-training.

3. **Aligning Generated Futures with Actions (KASO):** A core challenge in co-training WAMs is the "validity gap." Even if a generated future visually depicts a plausible outcome for a given observation and instruction, it may represent a different valid behavioral mode than the specific recorded action used as a supervision signal. This mismatch can lead to ambiguous supervision for the inverse dynamics model, potentially blurring distinct action modes and reducing the diversity of the action space. The objective is to develop *Knowledge-Aligned Selective Optimization (KASO)*, a mechanism that selects generated future states that are judged behaviorally compatible with the recorded action, thereby reducing mismatched supervision and preserving a broader action-space coverage.

A significant motivation for the evaluation protocol is to measure the *intrinsic capability* acquired during pretraining. Unlike many prior works that use task-specific fine-tuning, this research evaluates pretrained checkpoints directly under zero-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions. This approach isolates the capabilities developed during pretraining and measures how they evolve with increasing training data, rather than combining them with skills learned during downstream adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GE-Act 2.0 is a world-action model with a decoupled two-stage architecture designed for pretraining from scratch on manipulation data. It integrates three core components: a Control-Oriented Autoencoder (CoAE), a Single-Step Visual Planner (SVP), and an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM). All trainable generative and action components, except for the CoAE (which is initialized from DC-AE), are initialized from scratch on manipulation data.

### 4. System Overview

The system operates as follows:
1. **Observation Encoding:** CoAE encodes current multi-view observations into compact visual latents.
2. **One-Step Future Prediction:** Conditioned on these latents and an instruction, the SVP generates dense and sparse future visual latents in a single pass.
3. **Action Decoding:** The IDM combines current and predicted visual latents with proprioception to predict dense (executed) and sparse (auxiliary target) action sequences.

### 5. Control-Oriented Autoencoder (CoAE)

- **Purpose:** To learn an aggressively compressed, control-oriented latent space from manipulation video, making future prediction efficient and preserving action-relevant information.
- **Architecture:** A framewise 2D autoencoder with a 64x spatial downsampling factor (e.g., 256x384 frame to 4x6 grid of 512-channel latents, 24 tokens per frame). It is initialized from a 128-channel DC-AE by transferring compatible weights and expanding latent width.
- **Training Objective (L_CoAE):** Combines pixel reconstruction with multi-teacher feature alignment.
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L_rec):** L2 pixel loss, LPIPS perceptual loss, and an adversarial loss (Eq. 1).
 - **Alignment Loss (L_align):** Cosine similarity matching of the CoAE latent (processed by separate heads) to final-layer representations of three frozen visual teachers: SigLIP 2 (language-aligned semantics), V-JEPA 2.1 (spatiotemporal structure), and DINOv3 (dense visual features) (Eq. 2).
- **Evaluation:** Probed for action recovery (MAE) and instruction grounding (caption-matching error) using a fixed two-layer IDM probe and an eight-layer VLM-based network, respectively.

### 6. Single-Step Visual Planner (SVP)

- **Purpose:** To provide an explicit future-visual interface to the IDM by producing a complete future in one differentiable pass, enabling standalone IDM pretraining on instruction-free data.
- **Multi-Scale Visual Prediction:** Predicts `N_d` dense frames over an action-execution horizon `H` and `N_s` sparse frames up to a clip end `T` (Eq. 3). This captures both fine motion and longer-term task intent.
- **Unified Understanding and Generation:**
 - Uses a frozen Qwen3.5-2B Vision-Language Model (VLM) as a multimodal understanding component. The VLM processes the current head-view observation and instruction, and its text-span hidden states (contextualized by the image) are fused via an input-dependent gate (Eq. 4, 5).
 - This scene-grounded `c_vlm` conditions the flow generator. The IDM does not directly receive language, meaning language influences action through generated future visual states.
- **Conditional MeanFlow for One-Step Planning:**
 - Formulates conditional multi-view visual prediction as a one-step MeanFlow problem. This maps Gaussian noise to a complete future visual-latent sequence in a single differentiable forward pass.
 - The flow generator (a 36-block multi-view Diffusion Transformer, DiT) learns an average-velocity field (u_θ) and an auxiliary instantaneous-velocity head (v_θ).
 - Conditional path: Observed frames remain fixed (zero velocity), while future frames evolve from noise to data (Eq. 11).
 - Training Objective: L_u and L_v losses are applied only to prediction coordinates (Eq. 13).
 - Inference: Conditioning slots contain clean latents, prediction slots contain pure noise. A single forward pass fully denoises predicted frames.

### 7. Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)

- **Purpose:** To translate visual transitions (current observation, future visual latents) and proprioception into robot actions.
- **Architecture:** A transformer model (28 blocks, 1152 width) with self-attention over action tokens and cross-attention to visual latents.
- **Output:** Predicts dense control-rate action chunk and sparse longer-horizon actions using a flow-matching objective (ℓ_FM) (Eq. 15).
- **Pretraining:** IDM is pretrained separately on recorded robot trajectories where actions and future visual latents are inherently compatible.

### 8. World-Action Alignment and KASO

- **Decoupled Pretraining:** CoAE trains on video, SVP trains on instruction-video, and IDM trains on action-labeled robot trajectories.
- **Co-training for Joint Optimization:** The SVP and pretrained IDM are connected. The generated future `ˆz` from SVP (Eq. 14) is fed to the IDM, and action loss (L_E2E) propagates to update both.
- **Retaining Pretraining Losses (E2E+PT):** To prevent degradation of capabilities, the SVP’s video objective (L_SVP) and the IDM’s recorded-video objective (L_IDM) are retained during co-training (Eq. 17).
- **The Validity Gap:** Even with E2E+PT, a mismatch can occur: `ˆz` may depict a different outcome mode from the recorded action `a` with which it's paired for supervision, as manipulation can be multimodal.
- **Knowledge-Aligned Selective Optimization (KASO):**
 - **Mechanism (Algorithm 1, Figure 7):** For each training step, N candidate future videos (`ˆz_n`) are sampled from the SVP.
 - **Compatibility Probe:** The active IDM is probed at a high-noise point (`t_p`) on its action-flow path. It compares its action response to each `ˆz_n` with its response to the *recorded* future `z` for the same action input `a_tilde` (Eq. 19). This measures behavioral distinguishability.
 - **Selection:** The top-k candidates with the smallest discrepancy (lowest energy E_n) are selected (typically k=1).
 - **Differentiable Replay:** The SVP replays the selected candidates' generation noises with gradients enabled.
 - **Joint Update:** Only the selected candidates contribute to the generated-video action loss (L_sel_E2E) (Eq. 20), which is then combined with L_SVP and L_IDM for the full KASO objective (Eq. 21). Selection is online, dynamically adapting to model changes.
- **Controlled Real-Robot Ablation:** A study comparing E2E, E2E+PT, and KASO on a 300-hour co-training mixture (atop full-scale pretrained components) on pick and place tasks to isolate the effect of KASO.

### 9. Training Data and Mixture Composition

GE-Act 2.0 uses three stage-specific data mixtures:
- **SVP Pretraining:** 39,000 hours of instruction-video data, including action-free egocentric/human video.
- **IDM Pretraining:** 32,000 hours of action-labeled robot trajectories, including demonstrations, failure data, and deployment rollouts.
- **Co-training:** 30,000 hours of instruction-video-action data, integrating components with KASO.
Data sources include G1-OP, G2-OP, G2-90D, simulation, open-source datasets, and rollout/failure data, with varying proportions across stages (e.g., G1-OP > 50% of co-training, G2-90D < 2%). Nested co-training pools of 300, 1,200, 5,000, and 30,000 hours are used for scaling experiments.

### 10. Main Findings and Results

#### 10.1. Control-Oriented Autoencoder (CoAE) Performance

- **Compactness-Informativeness:** CoAE achieved a favorable operating point, balancing high compression (1/16th tokens per frame compared to DINOv3/V-JEPA 2.1) with strong performance in control-relevant tasks.
- **Action Recovery:** CoAE achieved an Action MAE of 0.01673, trailing DINOv3 and V-JEPA 2.1 by 13-31% but significantly outperforming DC-AE (0.02573, 54% higher error) and SigLIP (0.03138, 88% higher error).
- **Instruction Grounding:** CoAE demonstrated high accuracy in instruction grounding with a 2.05% caption-match error rate, which was 24% lower than the next-best DC-AE and approximately half the error of V-JEPA 2.1 (4.15%). This indicated its ability to preserve semantic and spatiotemporal information crucial for language-conditioned tasks.

#### 10.2. World-Action Alignment with KASO

- **Toy Two-Stage System:** On a controlled toy system designed to expose the "validity gap," KASO successfully recovered both the multimodal video distribution and the bimodal action distribution. In contrast, E2E and E2E+PT methods, which do not filter for action compatibility, tended to collapse the action density to the mean, demonstrating that the validity gap can lead to loss of action diversity.
- **Controlled Real-Robot Ablation:**
 - KASO achieved a 95% follow score on a four-object scene, matching E2E+PT.
 - However, KASO significantly improved macro-average pick success on the four-object scene to 37.5%, compared to 22.5% for E2E+PT (+15 percentage points).
 - On a single-object pick task, KASO raised pick success from 12% (E2E+PT) to 40% (+28 percentage points). These results indicated that KASO effectively converts correct target contacts into successful pickups by providing better aligned action supervision.

#### 10.3. Scaling Manipulation Skills (Zero-Shot OOD)

- **Suite-Level Success Rate (SR):**
 - For the dominant embodiment (G1-OP), suite-level SR increased from 17.1% (300 hours) to 44.1% (30,000 hours), representing a total gain of 27.0 percentage points.
 - For the data-scarce embodiment (G2-90D), suite-level SR increased from 13.4% (300 hours) to 31.1% (30,000 hours), a total gain of 17.7 percentage points.
 - Both curves showed substantial improvement between 5,000 and 30,000 hours, suggesting no saturation within the measured data range.
- **Broad Repertoire Improvement:** The gains were broadly distributed, with 19 of 20 G1-OP skill groups and 18 of 20 G2-90D groups showing improvement. The number of tasks with non-zero SR increased from 39 to 76 for G1-OP and from 24 to 72 for G2-90D.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The 17.7-point gain on G2-90D, despite comprising less than 2% of the co-training data, suggested useful cross-embodiment transfer from the broader corpus. Several G2-90D skill groups with minimal specific data demonstrated improvement from zero to non-zero SR.
- **Correlation with Skill-Specific Data Volume:** A strong correlation was found between skill-specific training coverage (hours) and zero-shot OOD success (Pearson r = 0.80; Spearman ρ = 0.85) across 19 non-trivial skill groups. The fitted relationship indicated a gain of 1.94 logit units in SR for every tenfold increase in skill-specific training hours. This accounted for the emergence of demanding skills (e.g., Stack, Pass, Straighten) with sufficient data and explained divergent performance for seemingly similar motions (e.g., Wipe vs. Sweep).

#### 10.4. Instruction Following in Complex Scenes

- **Fine-Grained Grounding:**
 - GE-Act 2.0 reliably grounded object identity, color, direct position, and shape, achieving a Follow Score of at least 90.0% for both pick and place primitives in controlled scenes.
 - Performance was less consistent for size (82.5%/65.7% Follow Score) and weakest for order (13.3%/26.7% Follow Score).
 - The capability profile broadly mirrored the long-tailed distribution of referring expressions observed in both human-written RefCOCOg and the GE-Act 2.0 training corpus, suggesting that data frequency influenced grounding performance.
- **Instruction Following under Conflicts (Qualitative):**
 - **Behavioral Conflict:** The model demonstrated an ability to redirect its trajectory or switch arms in response to a revised instruction, even when already committed to a previous behavior (e.g., target or arm switch).
 - **Semantic Conflict:** The policy successfully followed explicit instructions that contradicted conventional scene associations (e.g., "put the cup into the shoebox"), indicating control by explicit commands over memorized scene priors.

#### 10.5. OOD Performance on Simulation Benchmarks (after SFT)

- **RoboTwin Clean-to-Random:** After SFT on Easy/Clean, GE-Act 2.0 achieved the strongest overall performance on OOD environment variations, leading in five out of six conditions, including a 60.52% success rate on the combined Hard randomization (12.62 points above the previous best baseline).
- **GenieSim-Instruction:** After SFT on training demonstrations, GE-Act 2.0 obtained the highest average normalized score (0.770) on OOD instruction-following tasks, notably outperforming baselines on logical composition and common-sense reference.
- **LIBERO-Plus:** After SFT on standard LIBERO, GE-Act 2.0 achieved 80.4% overall success on robustness tests. It performed best under camera (94.1%) and sensor-noise (95.5%) perturbations but showed weaknesses in robot-state and background perturbations.

### 11. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the advancement of robot manipulation capabilities by systematically addressing fundamental challenges in pretraining and scaling world-action models. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Pioneering Scalable WAM Pretraining:** GE-Act 2.0 demonstrates a viable methodology for pretraining a comprehensive world-action model entirely from scratch on manipulation data. This approach offers an alternative to reliance on general video generators and facilitates the exploitation of diverse, manipulation-specific data sources.
- **Modular Architectural Innovations:** The introduction of components like the Control-Oriented Autoencoder (CoAE), Single-Step Visual Planner (SVP), and Knowledge-Aligned Selective Optimization (KASO) provides novel solutions to critical problems in WAM development. CoAE offers an efficient and control-relevant latent space, SVP enables decoupled pretraining and efficient gradient propagation, and KASO addresses the supervision mismatch inherent in multimodal action spaces.
- **Empirical Evidence for Data Scaling:** The comprehensive zero-shot OOD evaluations across 100 real-robot tasks and two embodiments provide robust empirical evidence that increasing the volume and diversity of manipulation data leads to substantial and broad improvements in pretrained robot capabilities. This informs future data collection and curriculum learning strategies for generalist robots.
- **Demonstrated Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The observed gains on the sparsely represented G2-90D embodiment, despite its minimal presence in the training corpus, highlight the potential for useful knowledge transfer across different robot hardware, contributing to the goal of building more generalist and adaptable policies.
- **Enhanced Instruction Following and Robustness:** The study’s findings on fine-grained referential grounding and instruction following under behavioral and semantic conflicts suggest progress towards policies that can interpret and execute explicit commands more robustly in complex and dynamic environments, even when these commands contradict learned priors or ongoing actions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By establishing a scalable world-action pretraining framework, GE-Act 2.0 opens avenues for further exploration, such as leveraging massive egocentric manipulation video datasets and integrating WAMs with "System-2" capabilities (e.g., deliberate reasoning, long-horizon planning, and self-correction) to move towards more comprehensive embodied intelligence. The diagnostic simulation benchmarks also provide a basis for identifying and addressing specific robustness limitations.
