# A Gait Driven Reinforcement Learning Framework for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a framework that integrates a real-time, model-based gait planner with reinforcement learning, enabling humanoid robots to achieve stable, periodic bipedal locomotion. The method uses dynamic decoupling and structured reward composition to accelerate learning and enhance trajectory tracking, outperforming baseline approaches in both simulation and physical robot experiments.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based gait generation methods suffer from high computational complexity and limited adaptability to precise robot dynamics or varying environmental conditions.
- Purely reinforcement learning approaches for locomotion often require prolonged training times, lack interpretability, and produce gaits with irregular periodicity, hindering deployment on physical robots.
- Existing hybrid approaches struggle to concurrently achieve real-time whole-body joint trajectory planning and satisfy the exploratory requirements for efficient learning in high-dimensional humanoid systems.

## Method

- A real-time gait planner is developed by decoupling the 3D robot into two 2D planar models (X and Y) and approximating them as ==Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulums (H-LIPs)==, using Bézier polynomials for stable trajectory generation.
- An infinite-horizon reinforcement learning framework (PPO-based actor-critic) is employed, utilizing proprioceptive measurements.
- A structured multi-objective reward composition is designed, including penalties for foot velocity/force during incorrect phases, rewards for correct leg phase enforcement, and incentives for precise joint trajectory tracking to guide policy learning.

## Results

- The proposed gait planning method achieves real-time capability, generating X-model trajectories in approximately **5.82** ms and Y-model trajectories in **0.027** ms, meeting real-time requirements.
- In simulation, the full reward composition (ILEM-1) led to significantly faster convergence to stable locomotion and superior tracking of desired forward and omni-directional velocities compared to baselines.
- On a physical humanoid robot, the framework enabled consistent movement at specified velocities and maintained gait cycles closely adhering to the designed 0.8s, outperforming baseline methods which exhibited highly variable and incorrect gait cycles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the 3D humanoid robot into 2D H-LIP models allows for computationally efficient, real-time generation of high-dimensional joint trajectories while preserving critical dynamical properties.
> - A carefully designed structured reward composition, incorporating foot dynamics, phase enforcement, and trajectory tracking, significantly accelerates the reinforcement learning process and leads to the acquisition of periodic and stable bipedal gaits.
> - Integrating model-based analytical planning with data-driven reinforcement learning can effectively bridge the gap between robustness, real-time performance, and adaptability in complex humanoid locomotion tasks.
