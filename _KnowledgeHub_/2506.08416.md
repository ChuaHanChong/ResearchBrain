# A Gait Driven Reinforcement Learning Framework for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a framework that integrates a real-time, model-based gait planner with reinforcement learning, enabling humanoid robots to achieve stable, periodic bipedal locomotion. The method uses dynamic decoupling and structured reward composition to accelerate learning and enhance trajectory tracking, outperforming baseline approaches in both simulation and physical robot experiments.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based gait generation methods suffer from high computational complexity and limited adaptability to precise robot dynamics or varying environmental conditions.
- Purely reinforcement learning approaches for locomotion often require prolonged training times, lack interpretability, and produce gaits with irregular periodicity, hindering deployment on physical robots.
- Existing hybrid approaches struggle to concurrently achieve real-time whole-body joint trajectory planning and satisfy the exploratory requirements for efficient learning in high-dimensional humanoid systems.

## Method

- A real-time gait planner is developed by decoupling the 3D robot into two 2D planar models (X and Y) and approximating them as ==Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulums (H-LIPs)==, using Bézier polynomials for stable trajectory generation.
- An infinite-horizon reinforcement learning framework (PPO-based actor-critic) is employed, utilizing proprioceptive measurements.
- A structured multi-objective reward composition is designed, including penalties for foot velocity/force during incorrect phases, rewards for correct leg phase enforcement, and incentives for precise joint trajectory tracking to guide policy learning.

## Results

- The proposed gait planning method achieves real-time capability, generating X-model trajectories in approximately **5.82** ms and Y-model trajectories in **0.027** ms, meeting real-time requirements.
- In simulation, the full reward composition (ILEM-1) led to significantly faster convergence to stable locomotion and superior tracking of desired forward and omni-directional velocities compared to baselines.
- On a physical humanoid robot, the framework enabled consistent movement at specified velocities and maintained gait cycles closely adhering to the designed 0.8s, outperforming baseline methods which exhibited highly variable and incorrect gait cycles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the 3D humanoid robot into 2D H-LIP models allows for computationally efficient, real-time generation of high-dimensional joint trajectories while preserving critical dynamical properties.
> - A carefully designed structured reward composition, incorporating foot dynamics, phase enforcement, and trajectory tracking, significantly accelerates the reinforcement learning process and leads to the acquisition of periodic and stable bipedal gaits.
> - Integrating model-based analytical planning with data-driven reinforcement learning can effectively bridge the gap between robustness, real-time performance, and adaptability in complex humanoid locomotion tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of stable and efficient bipedal locomotion for humanoid robots in dynamic and unstructured environments constitutes a fundamental and ongoing challenge in robotics. Historically, gait generation methods have relied on model-based optimization or heuristic rules. These traditional approaches, while offering analytical guarantees, often encounter limitations such as high computational complexity and reduced adaptability to precise system dynamics, which restrict their practical application in less structured settings.

Recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) have presented alternative methodologies, enabling robots to acquire locomotion policies through environmental interaction. RL-based techniques have shown capability in generating motions without extensive manual objective design and have facilitated sim-to-real transfer of learned gaits. However, purely learning-based approaches are characterized by several limitations, including issues with interpretability and prolonged training durations. Furthermore, the gaits produced by RL methods frequently exhibit irregular periodicity, which can impede their deployment on physical platforms where stability and rhythmic motion are critical for reliable operation.

A recognized research gap exists in effectively integrating the robustness offered by model-based approaches with the adaptability of data-driven learning methods. Probabilistic model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) has been explored to enhance sample efficiency and generalizability, typically involving the learning of a data-driven dynamics model for subsequent skill execution via probabilistic model-predictive control. Nevertheless, MBRL often incurs significant computational costs, leading previous bipedal locomotion studies on physical robots to primarily utilize offline planning or reduced-order dynamics models.

While hybrid approaches that combine intrinsic models with RL have emerged, existing solutions have often struggled to concurrently achieve real-time planning of whole-body joint trajectories and satisfy the exploratory requirements of the learning process. Model-based controllers, despite their fidelity to dynamics, face challenges in real-time trajectory synthesis for high-dimensional humanoid systems. Concurrently, RL policies typically demand substantial computational resources to converge to periodically stable gaits. This paper positions itself within this context by proposing a framework that seeks to bridge these challenges by integrating real-time gait planning with structured reward composition within an RL framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time gait-driven training framework that enables humanoid robots to learn stable and periodic bipedal locomotion efficiently. This framework aims to address specific limitations identified in both traditional model-based and contemporary reinforcement learning approaches for humanoid robot locomotion.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges:
1. **Computational Complexity and Adaptability of Model-Based Methods:** Traditional model-based approaches for gait generation, while providing analytical guarantees, often suffer from high computational costs and limited adaptability to precise robot dynamics or varying environmental conditions. This restricts their real-time application in unstructured or dynamic environments.
2. **Poor Interpretability and Long Training Times of Purely RL Methods:** Purely reinforcement learning strategies can generate locomotion policies through interaction. However, these methods typically require extensive training times, and the learned policies can lack interpretability. Additionally, RL-generated motions frequently exhibit irregular periodicity, which can compromise stability and rhythmicity—qualities crucial for physical robot deployment.
3. **Balancing Real-Time Planning and Learning Requirements in Hybrid Approaches:** Existing hybrid methods, which attempt to combine model-based and learning-based techniques, often struggle to strike a balance between real-time efficiency in planning whole-body joint trajectories for high-dimensional systems and the exploratory demands of the learning process. Model-based components may be too slow for real-time trajectory synthesis, while RL components may still require excessive resources to achieve periodic gaits.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:
- **Develop a dynamic-aware gait planner:** This planner is designed to generate high-dimensional joint trajectories in real-time for 3D humanoid robots. This is achieved by decoupling the 3D robot model into two 2D subsystems and approximating them as Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulums (H-LIP), thereby preserving crucial dynamical properties while enabling computational tractability.
- **Design an effective structured reward composition strategy:** This strategy aims to guide the reinforcement learning policy to acquire periodic bipedal gaits more efficiently. The reward functions are specifically designed to enforce periodicity, encourage trajectory tracking, and optimize for time efficiency.
- **Reduce learning time and enhance locomotion performance:** By integrating the real-time gait planner with the structured reward composition, the framework seeks to accelerate the training convergence of the RL policy and improve the robustness and periodicity of the robot's locomotion.
- **Demonstrate effectiveness through empirical validation:** The proposed method is intended to be validated through gait design examples, simulation comparisons against baseline methods, and experimental deployment on a physical humanoid robot, demonstrating superior learning efficiency and locomotion quality.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to create a scalable solution for bipedal locomotion that combines the advantages of analytical model simplifications with data-driven adaptation, leading to more reliable and training-efficient humanoid robots capable of operating in real-world dynamic settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a real-time gait planner with a reinforcement learning (RL) framework using a structured reward composition. The approach is bifurcated into gait design and network training, operating in parallel.

**A. Robot Modeling and Decoupling:**
The humanoid robot model is simplified to facilitate real-time planning. The 3D robot is decoupled into two independent 2D planar models:
- **X-model:** A five-link planar robot representing motion along the x-axis.
- **Y-model:** A three-link planar robot representing motion along the y-axis.
This decoupling assumes minimal influence between orthogonal directions. The upper body is treated as a rigid body, and ankle joints are considered parallel.
Both decoupled 2D models are then approximated as a Hybrid Linear Inverted Pendulum (H-LIP) model. The H-LIP model is a point-mass model with a constant Center of Mass (CoM) height and two telescopic legs with point-feet. It consists of continuous single support phase (SSP) dynamics (¨p = λ²p) and instantaneous double support phase (DSP) state transitions, which account for changes in CoM position and velocity upon foot contact.

**B. Gait Planner Design:**
The gait planner is designed to generate desired joint trajectories (ϕ(t)) for both the X-model and Y-model, ensuring adherence to H-LIP dynamics and kinematic constraints. Bézier polynomials are used to parameterize these trajectories due to their numerical stability.

- **X-model Gait Design (Algorithm 1):**
 - The goal is to generate 5-dimensional joint trajectories (ϕx(t)) that satisfy specific properties derived from the H-LIP dynamics and contact conditions. These properties include:
 - Symmetric leg switching at contact (ϕx(0) = Rϕx(T_SSP) and ˙ϕx(0) = R˙ϕx(T_SSP)).
 - Swing leg contact at t=0 and t=T_SSP (Γx(ϕx(0)) = 0 and Γx(ϕx(T_SSP)) = 0).
 - Swing leg clearance during SSP (Γx(ϕx(t)) > 0, ∀t ∈ (0, T_SSP)).
 - Satisfaction of H-LIP CoM velocity conditions at step end.
 - Constant CoM height (zx_com(ϕx(t)) = z0).
 - A particular choice for Bézier polynomial parameters is adopted (e.g., ϕx1(T_SSP) = ϕx4(T_SSP)) to simplify the problem.
 - Algorithm 1 iteratively determines Bézier polynomial parameters based on desired step length (Lxϕ) and walking speed (Vx), ensuring the properties are met, and calculates the trajectory ϕx5(t) to maintain constant CoM height. This process involves specific algebraic relationships between initial and final Bézier control points and the H-LIP parameters.
- **Y-model Gait Design (Algorithm 2):**
 - Similar to the X-model, 3-dimensional joint trajectories (ϕy(t)) are designed for the Y-model over two swing periods (2T_SSP) to account for non-colliding legs.
 - The properties for ϕy(t) are analogous, ensuring symmetric leg behavior, contact conditions, swing leg clearance, H-LIP CoM dynamics, and constant CoM height.
 - Algorithm 2 determines Bézier polynomial parameters (βij) for the Y-model trajectory based on walking velocity (Vy) and the derived relationships.
- **Full Robot Trajectory:** The joint trajectories from the X-model and Y-model are then combined and transformed using kinematic relationships (Table I) to obtain the desired 12-dimensional joint angles for the full humanoid robot (q_des(t)). This gait planner operates in real-time.

**C. Reinforcement Learning Framework:**
The learning framework is based on an infinite-horizon partially observable Markov decision process, utilizing proprioceptive measurements from an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) and joint encoders.
- **Network Architecture:** The framework comprises an actor, a critic, and an estimator network.
- **Optimization:** The actor-critic is optimized using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, while the estimator is optimized via regression techniques.
- **Reward Composition:** Three reward functions are designed to accelerate learning and achieve periodic bipedal gait:
 1. **Foot Velocity and Force Penalty (r_vf):** Penalizes horizontal foot velocity during the stance phase (when the foot should be stationary) and foot forces during the swing phase (when the foot should be airborne). A sigmoid function (C_i) is used to smoothly transition between stance and swing phase penalties based on the leg phase (τ'_i).
 2. **Correct Phase Enforcement (r_cs):** Rewards the robot for maintaining the correct leg phase (stance or swing) as defined by C_i. It compares the expected phase (C_i) with the actual contact status (S_i), where S_i=1 indicates ground contact and S_i=0 indicates no contact.
 3. **Joint Trajectory Tracking (r_track):** Rewards the robot for keeping its actual joint angles (q_actual) close to the desired joint trajectories (q_des) generated by the gait planner. This reward is formulated as an exponential decay function, e^-2||q_des - q_actual||, to encourage precise tracking.

The overall approach combines the analytical rigor of the H-LIP model and Bézier curve parameterization for real-time trajectory generation with the adaptive capabilities of reinforcement learning, guided by a structured reward system that prioritizes periodicity and adherence to planned movements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings based on gait design examples, simulation comparisons, and experimental evaluations.

**A. Gait Design Example:**
- **X-model:** For a specified step size of 0.25 m and walking speed of 0.625 m/s, with a CoM height (z0) of 0.856 m and Bézier polynomial order N=4, Algorithm 1 successfully generated the desired joint trajectories. The stick animation demonstrated a 0.25 m step length, with the torso angle exhibiting periodic changes over a 0.4 s swing period (T_SSP).
- **Y-model:** For a step size of 0.32 m and walking speed of 0.8 m/s, with z0 = 0.856 m, Algorithm 2 generated corresponding trajectories. The stick animation showed a 0.32 m step length, with periodic torso angle changes over two swing periods (2T_SSP).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The gait planning method demonstrated real-time capability. For 1000 sampled velocity points, the total computation time for the X-model was approximately 5.82 ms and for the Y-model was 0.027 ms (Table III). This indicates that the proposed gait planning can be completed within 1 ms, meeting real-time requirements.
- **Combined Trajectories:** The combined X and Y model trajectories produced continuous and periodic joint movements for the 12-joint humanoid robot across one gait cycle (2T_SSP), as shown in Figure 10.

**B. Simulation Comparison:**
The effectiveness of the proposed reward composition was evaluated in the Isaac Sim reinforcement learning environment. Three methods were compared:
- **ILEM (Isaac Lab Example Method):** A baseline method.
- **ILEM-2:** ILEM combined with the first two reward compositions (r_vf and r_cs).
- **ILEM-1:** ILEM combined with all three reward compositions (r_vf, r_cs, and r_track).

- **Learning Efficiency:** Figure 11, which tracks the average robot survival duration during training, indicated that ILEM-1 achieved balance in a shorter time compared to ILEM-2 and the pure ILEM. ILEM-1 showed a faster convergence to stable locomotion.
- **Locomotion Performance (Forward Walking):** When given a velocity command of 0.625 m/s, the robot trained with pure ILEM remained stationary despite maintaining balance (Figure 13). In contrast, the robot trained with ILEM-1 successfully tracked the given velocity command with a specified periodic gait (Figure 12). This outcome supported the effectiveness of the gait planner and the proposed reward composition in achieving desired locomotion.
- **Locomotion Performance (Omni-directional Walking):** For simultaneous x-axis velocity (0.625 m/s) and y-axis velocity (0.8 m/s) commands, Figure 14 showed that ILEM-1 again achieved balance in a shorter time and exhibited superior performance compared to the original ILEM. Simulation results in MuJoCo after 12,000 training steps with ILEM-1 demonstrated effective tracking of the expected position along the y-axis and good velocity tracking along the x-axis (Figure 15 and Figure 16), confirming the framework's ability to utilize the decoupled model for multi-directional velocity planning.

**C. Experiment Comparison:**
Experiments were conducted on a physical humanoid robot using networks trained with ILEM-1 and the Isaac Lab example methods.
- **ILEM Performance:** The robot trained using the Isaac Lab example method was unable to walk at the specified speed (Figure 17). Its gait cycles were highly variable and did not achieve the specified 0.8s gait cycle, ranging from 0.119s to 0.372s (Table IV).
- **ILEM-1 Performance:** In contrast, the robot trained with ILEM-1 successfully achieved movement at the specified velocity (Figure 18). Its gait cycles were consistently close to the designed 0.8s, with measured cycles ranging from 0.732s to 0.82s (Table V). This demonstrated that the proposed reward composition enabled the robot to reliably move according to the specified speed and adhere to the desired periodic gait cycle.

In summary, the findings indicate that the proposed framework, particularly with the complete reward composition (ILEM-1), significantly reduces training time, enhances balancing capability, and improves the ability to track desired velocities and periodic gait cycles in both simulation and real-world scenarios, outperforming baseline methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of humanoid robot locomotion by effectively integrating real-time model-based planning with data-driven reinforcement learning. The significance and potential impact of this research can be considered in several areas:

**A. Bridging Model-Based Robustness and Data-Driven Adaptability:** The framework successfully addresses a key research gap by combining the analytical guarantees of model-based control with the adaptive capabilities of reinforcement learning. The dynamic decoupling strategy and H-LIP approximation enable real-time generation of whole-body joint trajectories, which traditionally has been a computational bottleneck for high-dimensional humanoid systems. This provides a method that is both dynamically consistent and computationally efficient.

**B. Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Robustness:** The introduction of a structured multi-objective reward composition within the reinforcement learning framework significantly reduces the required training time. By explicitly incentivizing periodicity, trajectory tracking, and correct phase behavior, the learning process is guided more effectively, leading to faster convergence to stable and rhythmic gaits. This efficiency is critical for practical applications, as it reduces the computational resources and time needed to deploy new locomotion skills.

**C. Achieving Periodic and Controllable Gaits:** A central challenge in purely learning-based approaches is the generation of consistently periodic motions. The proposed reward composition directly addresses this by enforcing periodicity, resulting in gaits that are not only stable but also rhythmic and predictable. This predictability is crucial for the reliable operation of humanoid robots, particularly when interacting with dynamic environments or performing tasks that require precise timing and coordination. The demonstrated ability to track specified velocities in both x and y directions also highlights improved controllability.

**D. Scalability and Real-World Applicability:** The real-time nature of the gait planner, combined with the efficient learning framework, suggests a scalable solution for bipedal locomotion. The successful transfer of learned policies from simulation to a physical humanoid robot, demonstrating robust and specified gait performance, indicates strong potential for deployment in real-world dynamic settings. This could expand the range of tasks and environments in which humanoid robots can operate autonomously.

**E. Future Research Directions:** This work lays a foundation for future research in hybrid control architectures for complex robotic systems. It suggests avenues for developing more sophisticated gait planners that can adapt to larger variations in terrain or disturbances while maintaining real-time performance. Furthermore, the principles of structured reward design could be extended to other challenging robotic manipulation and interaction tasks, where specific dynamic properties or behavioral patterns need to be learned efficiently.

In summary, this research offers a practical and effective solution for enabling humanoid robots to achieve stable, periodic, and adaptable bipedal locomotion, marking a step forward in the development of more capable and reliable autonomous robotic systems.
