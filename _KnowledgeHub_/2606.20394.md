# Agentic AutoResearch forSpace Autonomy: An Auditable, LLM-Driven Research Agent for Aerospace Control Problems

> [!summary] Summary
> The AutoResearch framework from MIT leverages an LLM-driven research agent and a statistical credibility layer to autonomously develop and validate learned control policies for aerospace problems, demonstrating robust performance with certified statistical honesty on spacecraft rendezvous and collision avoidance tasks. This system achieved an improvement of 15.0𝜎 margin in terminal distance for rendezvous and a 5.2𝜎 margin in a safety-aware docking score, ensuring all-seeds safety and strict feasibility where random search failed.

## Problem

- Developing robust and reliable learned policies for autonomous spacecraft GNC functions is an iterative, labor-intensive process, prone to manual errors.
- Machine learning results are susceptible to random seed variations, leading to reproducibility issues and potential over-claiming of performance improvements.
- There is a need to automate the machine learning research loop for safety-critical aerospace control while ensuring transparency, reproducibility, and statistical honesty.

## Method

- The **AutoResearch** framework integrates an LLM as an offline research agent, autonomously proposing, executing, and analyzing machine learning experiments within a 'family' abstraction.
- A statistical credibility layer is embedded into the autonomous research loop to filter LLM conclusions, distinguishing genuine improvements from stochastic noise through measured seed noise, reseeded verification, and leave-one-out pruning.
- The framework was applied to two aerospace control problems: Clohessy–Wiltshire relative rendezvous and safety-constrained collision-avoidance docking, both calibrated against optimal-control benchmarks.

## Results

- For the rendezvous problem, the agent achieved an improvement of 15.0𝜎 margin in mean terminal distance over the baseline, with the audited best configuration yielding **0.101** ± **0.007** meters and a **100%** success rate on reseeded verification.
- For the collision-avoidance docking problem, the agent improved the safety-aware docking score by a 5.2𝜎 margin, with all 10 reseeds achieving strict feasibility, a feat random search could not replicate.
- Leave-one-out pruning successfully identified the specific load-bearing hyperparameter edits (e.g., number of demonstrations, training epochs, hidden width) that were critical for observed performance gains in both problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM-driven agents can systematically explore and improve learned control policies for complex aerospace problems by autonomously proposing and executing experiments.
> - Integrating a rigorous statistical credibility audit is essential for autonomous ML experimentation to ensure the trustworthiness and reproducibility of results, particularly in safety-critical domains.
> - The framework can identify effective and safety-compliant policies, with targeted edits confirmed as genuinely load-bearing, demonstrating a capability beyond undirected search methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) as autonomous agents, automated machine learning (AutoML), and spacecraft guidance, navigation, and control (GNC). It addresses challenges prevalent in the development of learned policies for spacecraft autonomy, specifically the iterative, labor-intensive nature of hyperparameter tuning and model selection, coupled with the susceptibility of machine learning results to random seed variations and subsequent over-claiming.

Historically, spacecraft GNC functions were explicitly hand-coded. More recently, learned policies, often distilled from expert solvers or trained from data, have become common for tasks such as rendezvous and proximity operations. The development of these learned components necessitates an iterative research process, involving architecture and hyperparameter selection, experimentation, and performance evaluation. This process is time-consuming and, if conducted informally, can lead to spurious conclusions regarding genuine improvements versus stochastic noise.

The emergence of LLMs capable of code comprehension, reasoning, and action has opened avenues for automating aspects of scientific research. Previous work has demonstrated LLMs driving end-to-end research loops in machine learning (e.g., AI Scientist, FunSearch) and acting as optimizers (e.g., OPRO, OptFormer). Other LLM-driven agents have been developed to autonomously conduct machine learning experiments, covering tasks from code generation and execution to multi-agent pipelines for data-to-deployment (e.g., MLAgentBench, AIDE, AutoML-Agent, AgentHPO). While these systems enhance experimental speed and scope, the broader machine learning community has extensively documented the risks of rapid experimentation without statistical rigor, emphasizing issues like seed sensitivity and under-powered evaluation that lead to reproducibility failures (e.g., Henderson et al., Agarwal et al., NeurIPS reproducibility program).

Concurrent research also explores the application of LLMs directly within the spacecraft control loop, using them as autonomous operators or for synthesizing control policies. The present work distinguishes itself by framing the LLM as an *offline research agent* that develops and validates control policies, rather than operating the spacecraft directly.

The unique contribution of this paper lies in integrating statistical discipline directly into an LLM-driven research loop. Prior systems have not combined an in-loop statistical credibility audit—encompassing measured seed noise, reseeded verification, and leave-one-out ablation of agent edits—with application to safety-critical aerospace control problems calibrated against optimal-control benchmarks. This work aims to bridge the gap between autonomous experimentation speed and statistical trustworthiness in the context of learned aerospace GNC.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges associated with developing robust and reliable learned policies for increasingly autonomous spacecraft. The iterative process of designing, training, and validating machine learning models for guidance, navigation, and control (GNC) functions is characterized by its slowness and reliance on manual intervention. Furthermore, the inherent stochasticity in machine learning, particularly due to random initialization seeds, makes it difficult to ascertain whether an observed improvement in performance is a true gain or merely a favorable random outcome, leading to potential over-claiming and reproducibility issues within the research community.

In response to these challenges, the authors posed a central question: can the machine learning research loop for aerospace control be automated effectively, while simultaneously ensuring transparency, reproducibility, and the statistical honesty of the findings?

This central question led to three specific objectives, which constitute the main contributions of the paper:

1. **Development of a reusable framework for agentic experimentation:** The objective was to create a generalized framework, named AutoResearch, that allows a large language model (LLM) to autonomously propose, execute, and analyze machine learning experiments. This framework was designed to be problem-agnostic within a specified "family contract," enabling its application across diverse aerospace control problems without modification to the core agent loop. The family contract defines a standardized problem description, an editable training script with delimited hyperparameter regions, a single structured metric for evaluation, and an append-only run log.
2. **Integration of a credibility layer for trustworthy autonomous results:** A key objective was to embed a statistical credibility layer directly into the autonomous research loop. This layer aims to filter the LLM agent's conclusions by systematically distinguishing genuine improvements from mere stochastic noise. The layer incorporates three mechanisms:
 - **Measured per-problem seed noise:** Quantifying the inherent variability of the metric due to random seeds for a baseline configuration.
 - **Reseeded verification:** Re-evaluating the agent's best configurations using fresh, independent random seeds to confirm that reported gains are robust and reproducible.
 - **Leave-one-out pruning:** Systematically ablating individual edits made by the agent to explicitly determine which changes are genuinely load-bearing for the observed performance improvements.
3. **Demonstration and auditing of results on aerospace control problems:** The final objective was to apply the AutoResearch framework and its credibility layer to two distinct, yet calibrated, aerospace control problems: a Clohessy–Wiltshire relative rendezvous and a safety-constrained collision-avoidance docking past a keep-out zone. These problems were selected because they have known optimal-control benchmarks and fixed baseline configurations, allowing for a clear assessment of policy performance and measured seed noise. The goal was to demonstrate that the framework could not only produce effective learned policies but also certify their reported gains as genuine and statistically significant against measured noise, and to compare the LLM agent's performance against undirected search methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AutoResearch framework integrates an LLM as an offline research agent to automate the machine learning experimentation loop for aerospace control problems, coupled with an intrinsic credibility layer to validate results. The approach is structured around a "family" abstraction, an agent loop, and a statistical audit.

**A. Problem Formalization: The Family Abstraction**
A "family" defines a self-contained research problem. It consists of:
- `program.md`: A plain-language description detailing the problem goal, metric, baseline, and permissible changes.
- `train.py`: A single Python script implementing the dynamics, data handling, model, and evaluation. It features a delimited "editable region" containing hyperparameters the agent can modify.
- `family.json`: A registry for the target metric, reference baseline, and current best configuration.
- `runs.jsonl`: An append-only log of successful experiments.
A configuration (𝜃) comprises a finite set of editable hyperparameters, each with a declared domain (integer, real, log-scaled real, categorical). Evaluating a configuration `𝜃` at a random seed `s` yields a scalar metric `f(𝜃; s)`. The true objective is the seed-averaged mean `F(𝜃)`. A `𝜎_dir` (orientation) indicates whether larger or smaller metric values are better.

**B. The Agent Loop**
The agent loop consists of three modules: a proposer, a runner, and an orchestrator. The loop operates in a "blind mode" to prevent the LLM from recalling known good configurations; problem descriptions are redacted, and the run history is limited to `(configuration, primary-metric)` pairs. The LLM used in the reported campaigns is Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 4.5.

- **Proposer**: This module wraps the LLM, presenting it with the current context (warnings, `program.md`, `family.json`, random-search summary, `runs.jsonl`, current hyperparameters). The LLM is constrained to emit one of two structured tool calls:
 - `propose_experiment`: Requires a hypothesis, hyperparameter overrides, axis labels, expected mechanism, comparison to the prior best, and a numerical failure prediction.
 - `stop_research`: Requires a status, conclusion, and tested axes.
 This structured output ensures machine-readability of the LLM's decisions.
- **Runner**: The runner reads the `train.py` script, identifies the editable region, and applies the proposed hyperparameter overrides by rewriting matching value expressions. It executes `train.py` as an isolated subprocess with a wall-clock timeout and parses a single `FINAL_METRIC` line from its standard output. This in-place editing ensures reproducibility of each recorded experiment.
- **Orchestrator**: This module manages the state machine, validating proposals, invoking the runner, and updating the best configuration in `family.json`. It logs all iterations, including invalid proposals, to `proposals.jsonl`. Key orchestrator functions include:
 - **Distinct points enforcement**: Rejects proposals for configurations or override maps previously seen to ensure exploration of unique points.
 - **Termination triggers**: Halts the campaign based on criteria such as elapsed wall-clock time, number of successful runs, achievement of the family target, iteration cap, consecutive failed/invalid proposals (capped at 3), cumulative LLM spend, or a plateau predicate.
 - **Justification gate for `stop_research`**: An agent's stop request is accepted only if the target is met, or if at least six experiments are completed and four distinct axes have been explored, preventing premature or unsubstantiated conclusions.

**C. The Credibility Layer**
This layer operates offline and post-hoc on the recorded data from the agent loop, making the results statistically trustworthy by addressing seed noise and confirming reproducibility.

- **Measured Seed Noise**: A fixed baseline configuration (𝜃₀) is run across `K` seeds (K=10 in demonstrations). The sample mean `𝜇₀` and Bessel-corrected sample standard deviation `𝜎` of the metric are calculated. This `𝜎` serves as the fundamental unit of noise against which all subsequent claims are denominated. The orchestrator uses a single-seed verdict (confirmed, regressed, within noise) based on `𝜎` for in-loop decisions and a plateau predicate for early stopping.
- **Reseeded Verification**: After the agent loop completes, the top configurations are re-run on a set of *fresh, held-out seeds* (S_R). A `train-loss parity guard` excludes reseeds with significantly divergent training losses. The reseeded mean `𝜇_R`, not the single-seed best, becomes the headline number. A configuration `𝜃̂` is accepted only if:
 1. `𝜎_dir(𝜇_R - 𝜇₀) ≥ 2𝜎`: Its reseeded mean improves on the baseline by at least two standard deviations of the measured seed noise (effect-size gate).
 2. `Feasible(𝜃̂; s)` for *every* `s ∈ S_R`: It meets all hard safety constraints on every reseed (all-seeds safety).
 The improvement margin `m` is reported as the number of `𝜎` units separating `𝜇_R` from `𝜇₀`.
- **Leave-One-Out Pruning**: For an accepted configuration `𝜃̂`, each modified parameter `i` is individually reverted to its default, and the metric is remeasured. `Δ_i` quantifies the oriented change from this reversion. A negative `Δ_i` indicates the parameter is load-bearing; a positive `Δ_i` suggests the agent moved it incorrectly. Decisions are made in noise units, and for inconclusive single-seed probes, a two-seed mean is used. The credibility layer also checks for group effects: if dropping individually inert parameters together negatively impacts the metric or feasibility, the full configuration is retained, otherwise a `𝜃_pruned` configuration is published.
- **External Sanity Check**: The agent's performance is compared against a random search over the same editable parameter space, starting from a shared first iteration and under a matched per-experiment budget. The comparison, `V`, determines if the LLM agent "wins" (improvement > 2𝜎), "random wins" (degradation > 2𝜎), or if it's a "tie." This comparison is a secondary check, with the primary claim resting on the reseeded verification gate.

**D. Demonstration Problems**
The framework was exercised on two spacecraft guidance problems:
1. **Clohessy–Wiltshire relative rendezvous**: Unconstrained rendezvous over 80 steps (800s) with thrust limits. Expert demonstrations from minimum-energy optimal control. Policy is a time-conditioned multilayer perceptron trained by behavioral cloning. Metric is mean terminal distance (lower is better), with success requiring terminal distance < 25m and speed < 0.05 m/s.
2. **Safety-constrained collision-avoidance docking**: Longer horizon (240 steps, 2400s), larger thrust authority. Involves a hard keep-out sphere. Expert demonstrations from minimum-energy optimal control with radial half-space constraints. Policy is a time-conditioned multilayer perceptron trained by behavioral cloning. Metric is a safety-aware docking score (`score = 𝑑_T + 10 𝜈`), where `𝑑_T` is mean terminal distance and `𝜈` is mean fraction of rollout steps inside KOZ (lower is better). Feasibility requires success rate ≥ 0.80, zero KOZ violations, and non-negative minimum keep-out clearance. This problem also includes a discrete-time predictive control-barrier-function (CBF) safety filter as an editable Boolean parameter for run-time assurance.

Both problems provide optimal-control benchmarks (near-zero performance) and fixed baseline configurations to calibrate seed noise, allowing the credibility layer to function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AutoResearch framework was applied to two distinct aerospace control problems in blind mode, and its identical credibility audit was applied to the best configuration identified. The findings consistently demonstrated the LLM agent's ability to develop effective control policies and for the credibility layer to certify their robustness.

**A. Rendezvous Problem**
- **Agent Performance**: Over a single campaign of 57 experiments, the LLM agent systematically reduced the mean terminal distance of the behaviorally cloned policy. The initial policy yielded approximately 16 meters, while the agent's single-seed best achieved 0.094 meters. This final performance approaches that of the optimal-control expert. The agent's search exhibited directed progress, gradually increasing network width and training duration while annealing the learning rate.
- **Policy Characteristics**: The audited best policy featured a hidden width of 512 units (the single-seed best was 640), 4 layers, GELU activations, utilized 256 expert demonstrations, a small learning rate, and a large training budget. Visualizations confirmed that the learned policy successfully steered the spacecraft along trajectories closely matching the optimal-control solutions.
- **Credibility Audit**:
 - **Measured Seed Noise**: The baseline configuration, run over 10 seeds, exhibited a mean terminal distance of 4.04 ± 0.26 meters, with a success rate of approximately 75%.
 - **Reseeded Verification**: The agent's audited best configuration, re-evaluated over 10 fresh, held-out seeds, yielded a mean terminal distance of 0.101 ± 0.007 meters, achieving a 100% success rate on every seed. This represented an improvement of 3.94 meters (95% CI [3.75, 4.12]m) over the baseline, corresponding to a **15.0𝜎 margin**, significantly exceeding the 2𝜎 acceptance threshold. The audit promoted a slightly narrower network (512 units) as more reproducible than the single-seed best.
 - **Comparison to Random Search**: A random search over the same parameters, conducted over 60 trials, plateaued near 0.635 meters, trailing the LLM agent's reseeded result by more than six-fold.
 - **Leave-one-out Pruning**: Five parameters were identified as load-bearing for the improvement: number of demonstrations (`N_DEMOS`), training epochs (`EPOCHS`), learning rate (`LEARNING_RATE`), hidden width (`HIDDEN_DIM`), and depth (`N_LAYERS`). While four other parameters (batch size, weight decay, gradient clip, activation) showed no individual signal, their combined removal regressed the metric, leading to the retention of the full configuration as the family best.

**B. Collision-Avoidance Docking Problem**
- **Agent Performance**: In a single campaign of 23 experiments, the LLM agent successfully reduced the safety-aware docking score from a poor default to a single-seed best of 0.33, well within the target of 0.7. The agent consistently maintained the run-time Control Barrier Function (CBF) filter in the 'on' state. The search progression was methodical, deepening the network, increasing demonstrations, and extending training duration. Visualizations confirmed that the learned policy executed detours around the keep-out zone, precisely matching the optimal-control expert's trajectories while maintaining non-negative minimum clearance.
- **Policy Characteristics**: The audited best policy featured a hidden width of 256 units (the single-seed best was 128), 4 layers, the full set of 256 expert demonstrations, longer training, and an annealed learning rate. The crucial `RUNTIME_CBF_FILTER` was consistently engaged.
- **Credibility Audit**:
 - **Measured Seed Noise**: The baseline configuration, over 10 seeds, yielded a score of 8.45 ± 1.55, but was not strictly feasible (did not meet docking criteria).
 - **Reseeded Verification**: The agent's audited best configuration, re-evaluated over 10 fresh seeds, achieved a score of 0.38 ± 0.07. Crucially, **all 10 reseeds were strict-feasible**, meeting the criteria for success rate, zero KOZ violations, and non-negative clearance. This constituted an improvement of 8.07 (95% CI [6.97, 9.18]) over the baseline, equating to a **5.2𝜎 margin**, passing both the effect-size and all-seeds safety gates. Similar to the rendezvous, the audit promoted a wider, more reproducible network (256 units) over the single-seed best.
 - **Comparison to Random Search**: This problem demonstrated a categorical advantage for the LLM agent. Over 24 trials, random search **failed to produce a single strict-feasible policy**. Its best score was 6.46, an order of magnitude higher than the agent's reseeded mean. Random search frequently disabled the safety filter, leading to KOZ penetration, and even when the filter was on, it failed to learn effective docking maneuvers.
 - **Leave-one-out Pruning**: Only two edits were identified as load-bearing: the number of demonstrations (`N_DEMOS`) and the training epochs (`EPOCHS`). A distilled recipe incorporating only these two edits, even reverting other parameters, still cleared the 5𝜎 gate and maintained strict feasibility. While other agent choices like wider networks improved reproducibility, they were not strictly necessary to clear the baseline performance and safety gates.

In both cases, the credibility layer identified specific configurations that were demonstrably robust and reproducible, distinguishing them from potentially lucky single runs and providing detailed insights into the effective hyperparameter choices.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AutoResearch, a framework for LLM-driven autonomous machine learning experimentation integrated with a rigorous statistical credibility layer. The primary significance of this work lies in providing a reusable, auditable process for developing and certifying learned control policies, particularly within the safety-critical domain of aerospace applications.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Enhanced Reproducibility and Trustworthiness:** The framework directly addresses critical issues in machine learning research, such as the reproducibility crisis, over-claiming, and seed sensitivity. By integrating measured seed noise, reseeded verification, and leave-one-out pruning directly into the research loop, AutoResearch establishes a higher standard of statistical rigor for autonomous experimentation. This ensures that reported performance gains are genuine and robust across different random seeds and experimental conditions, moving beyond anecdotal evidence from single "lucky" runs.
- **Automation of the Research Loop for Safety-Critical Systems:** The work demonstrates the feasibility of autonomously driving the complex and labor-intensive process of developing learned control policies. For aerospace applications, where safety and reliability are paramount, automating this process while ensuring rigorous validation can accelerate development cycles and reduce human-induced errors or biases in hyperparameter tuning and model selection.
- **Practical Application in Aerospace Control:** The successful demonstration on two distinct spacecraft guidance problems (unconstrained rendezvous and safety-constrained docking) highlights the framework's applicability to real-world aerospace challenges. The ability to autonomously identify policies that not only achieve high accuracy but also strictly adhere to safety constraints (as shown in the docking problem where undirected search failed categorically) is critical for future space autonomy missions.
- **Transparency and Explainability of Agent Decisions:** The structured nature of the LLM's proposals and the leave-one-out pruning process contribute to the transparency of the autonomous research. Researchers can trace the agent's hypotheses, parameter choices, and verify which edits genuinely contribute to performance improvements, distinguishing load-bearing changes from inert "hitchhikers." This level of insight is valuable for understanding the learned policies and refining future experimentation.
- **Generalizability of the Framework:** The "family contract" abstraction allows the identical agent loop to be applied across diverse problems with varying physics, suggesting that the AutoResearch framework could be adapted to other domains beyond aerospace where rigorous, automated ML experimentation is beneficial.

**Potential Impact:**
The potential impact of AutoResearch extends to various aspects of space mission design and operations:
- **Accelerated Development of Autonomous Systems:** By automating the iterative tuning and validation of learned GNC policies, the framework can significantly shorten the development timeline for new autonomous spacecraft capabilities, from orbital maneuvers to planetary landing.
- **Increased Reliability and Safety:** The built-in credibility layer fosters greater confidence in the performance and safety assurances of learned components. This is crucial for deploying AI-driven systems in high-stakes space environments where failures can have catastrophic consequences.
- **Resource Optimization:** Efficiently discovering optimal or near-optimal policies can lead to more fuel-efficient maneuvers, reduced operational costs, and extended mission lifetimes.
- **Reduced Human Workload:** Automating the experimentation process frees human experts to focus on higher-level problem definition, innovative architecture design, and comprehensive analysis, rather than repetitive tuning tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Autonomous Research:** AutoResearch lays groundwork for more sophisticated LLM-driven scientific discovery in engineering and machine learning, particularly where statistical rigor is a non-negotiable requirement.

**Limitations and Future Work (as acknowledged by authors):**
- The current campaigns are limited to tens of iterations, contrasting with thousands typical in large-scale automated search, indicating a trade-off between throughput and per-experiment credibility.
- The influence of the specific LLM backend (Claude Sonnet 4.5) on the search behavior is not yet fully characterized.
- The random-search comparison, while indicative, was not perfectly matched in terms of parameter sampling range, making it a secondary check rather than a definitive statement on search strategy superiority.
- The reported results are based on single agent runs; the run-to-run variance of the agent's own search process is not yet characterized.

Future work aims to address these limitations by characterizing LLM backend sensitivity, refining the random-search comparison, and further validating the agent's consistency. The overarching goal remains to establish LLM-driven experimentation as a dependable and honest tool for developing and validating the learned autonomy required for future space missions.
