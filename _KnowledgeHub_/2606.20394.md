# Agentic AutoResearch forSpace Autonomy: An Auditable, LLM-Driven Research Agent for Aerospace Control Problems

> [!summary] Summary
> The AutoResearch framework from MIT leverages an LLM-driven research agent and a statistical credibility layer to autonomously develop and validate learned control policies for aerospace problems, demonstrating robust performance with certified statistical honesty on spacecraft rendezvous and collision avoidance tasks. This system achieved an improvement of 15.0𝜎 margin in terminal distance for rendezvous and a 5.2𝜎 margin in a safety-aware docking score, ensuring all-seeds safety and strict feasibility where random search failed.

## Problem

- Developing robust and reliable learned policies for autonomous spacecraft GNC functions is an iterative, labor-intensive process, prone to manual errors.
- Machine learning results are susceptible to random seed variations, leading to reproducibility issues and potential over-claiming of performance improvements.
- There is a need to automate the machine learning research loop for safety-critical aerospace control while ensuring transparency, reproducibility, and statistical honesty.

## Method

- The **AutoResearch** framework integrates an LLM as an offline research agent, autonomously proposing, executing, and analyzing machine learning experiments within a 'family' abstraction.
- A statistical credibility layer is embedded into the autonomous research loop to filter LLM conclusions, distinguishing genuine improvements from stochastic noise through measured seed noise, reseeded verification, and leave-one-out pruning.
- The framework was applied to two aerospace control problems: Clohessy–Wiltshire relative rendezvous and safety-constrained collision-avoidance docking, both calibrated against optimal-control benchmarks.

## Results

- For the rendezvous problem, the agent achieved an improvement of 15.0𝜎 margin in mean terminal distance over the baseline, with the audited best configuration yielding **0.101** ± **0.007** meters and a **100%** success rate on reseeded verification.
- For the collision-avoidance docking problem, the agent improved the safety-aware docking score by a 5.2𝜎 margin, with all 10 reseeds achieving strict feasibility, a feat random search could not replicate.
- Leave-one-out pruning successfully identified the specific load-bearing hyperparameter edits (e.g., number of demonstrations, training epochs, hidden width) that were critical for observed performance gains in both problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM-driven agents can systematically explore and improve learned control policies for complex aerospace problems by autonomously proposing and executing experiments.
> - Integrating a rigorous statistical credibility audit is essential for autonomous ML experimentation to ensure the trustworthiness and reproducibility of results, particularly in safety-critical domains.
> - The framework can identify effective and safety-compliant policies, with targeted edits confirmed as genuinely load-bearing, demonstrating a capability beyond undirected search methods.
