# DexVIP: Learning Dexterous Grasping with Human Hand Pose Priors from Video

> [!summary] Summary
> DexVIP learns dexterous 30-DoF robotic grasping from in-the-wild YouTube how-to videos. It curates human grasp frames, estimates 3D hand poses with FrankMocap, clusters them into a per-object consensus pose retargeted to the robot, and adds a pose-matching auxiliary reward alongside success and affordance-contact rewards in PPO. Across 27 objects it reaches 68% grasp success, beating affordance and teleoperation-demo baselines while training 20% faster.

## Problem

- Dexterous hands have 24-30 DoF action spaces, so pure reinforcement learning explores poorly and most exploratory hand trajectories return no useful reward signal.
- Existing demonstration pipelines need motion gloves, VR rigs, or kinesthetic guidance, confining data collection to labs and adding overhead for every new object.
- Such proxies insert indirection: the person manipulates hardware rather than the real object, and prior observation-based imitation needs paired robot-human data or shared environments.

## Method

- Curates 715 HowTo100M grasp frames across 27 objects, runs ==FrankMocap== for 3D hand joint angles, then ==k-medoid clustering== picks the largest cluster's medoid as the consensus target pose.
- Retargets each consensus human pose to the Adroit hand by ==inverse kinematics==, giving an object-specific target robot pose used as supervision without any state-action trajectory.
- Trains **PPO** actor-critic over RGB, depth, affordance map, proprioception and contact distances with reward alpha*R_succ + beta*R_aff + gamma*R_pose + eta*R_entropy, gating R_pose on 30% touch-sensor activation.

## Results

- On the 16 ContactDB objects **DexVIP** reaches **68%** grasp success versus **60%** for the affordance-only GRAFF policy and **59%** for DAPG trained on 25 VR mocap demos per object.
- Reward ablation raises success from **60%** with affordance reward alone to **63%** adding touch and **68%** adding the video hand-pose prior, and training converges **20%** faster than GRAFF.
- On 11 objects lacking mocap demos DexVIP drops only **4%** (**68%** to **65%**) while DAPG falls **15%** and GRAFF **12%**; under heavy sensing and actuation noise it still holds **64%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Morphological similarity between human and multi-fingered robot hands turns pose estimation into a free reward signal, no paired demonstrations or shared visual environment required.
> - Constraining how to grasp, not just where, improves stability and post-grasp functionality, and shrinks the effective exploration space enough to speed up training.
> - Image-level supervision scales far cheaper than teleoperation (seconds versus about 5 minutes per demo), but consensus poses fail when object orientation contradicts the canonical grasp.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of dexterous multi-fingered robotic hands represents an area of significant interest within robotics, aiming to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in human-centric environments. These hands, often possessing 24 to 30 degrees of freedom (DoF), offer substantial flexibility compared to simpler end effectors like parallel jaw grippers. However, this high degree of articulation introduces a formidable action space, making the learning of manipulation strategies challenging, particularly when demanding high generalization to novel objects.

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a prominent approach to address these challenges, allowing robots to dynamically adapt manipulation strategies through closed-loop feedback. To mitigate the high sample complexity associated with exploring vast action spaces, current RL methods often incorporate imitation learning (LfD) from expert demonstrations. These demonstrations typically involve humans operating robots through specialized interfaces such as virtual reality (VR) teleoperation, motion capture (mocap) systems, or kinesthetic manipulation.

Despite their utility, traditional LfD paradigms have several limitations. They necessitate specialized, expensive equipment and confined lab environments, restricting data collection to specific settings and requiring significant overhead for each new task or object. Furthermore, the human demonstrator often operates a proxy robot rather than directly interacting with real-world objects, which can introduce an "indirection" that leads to artificial or non-functional grasps. This can result in policies that merely lift objects rather than preparing them for subsequent functional use.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing an alternative approach: learning dexterous grasping policies from observations of human-object interactions in "in-the-wild" internet videos. This paradigm shift aims to eliminate the need for specialized lab setups, leverage the naturalness of real human activities to learn functional grasps, and provide a scalable, less expensive method for obtaining expert knowledge, thereby advancing the field of dexterous robotic manipulation. The work builds upon existing research in dexterous RL, imitation learning from visual observations, and visual affordance prediction, while introducing a novel method for integrating human pose priors from unconstrained video data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an approach, named DexVIP, that enables dexterous multi-fingered robotic hands to learn effective grasping policies by observing human-object interactions captured in "in-the-wild" video data. This is achieved by extracting human hand pose priors from these videos and integrating them into a deep reinforcement learning framework.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key challenges and opportunities in dexterous robotic manipulation:

1. **Complexity of Dexterous Hand Control**: Multi-fingered robotic hands, such as the 30-DoF Adroit hand, possess a large number of degrees of freedom. This expansive action space makes learning effective manipulation policies difficult, as random exploration is highly inefficient. Reinforcement learning methods struggle with the associated sample complexity.
2. **Limitations of Traditional Imitation Learning**: Current methods for leveraging human expertise, such as teleoperation in virtual reality or kinesthetic teaching, suffer from significant practical drawbacks:
 - **Specialized Equipment and Environment**: They require costly and complex setups (e.g., motion gloves, VR platforms, high-precision trackers), which are typically confined to lab environments. This limits the accessibility of data collection and the diversity of scenarios that can be explored.
 - **Indirect Demonstrations**: Humans demonstrate tasks indirectly, often through a proxy interface (e.g., controlling a virtual hand). This artificiality can lead to unnatural motions or non-functional grasps that prioritize merely lifting an object rather than preparing it for subsequent use (e.g., grasping a mug by its body instead of its handle for drinking).
 - **Scalability Challenges**: Adding new objects or manipulation tasks requires repeated and often time-consuming data collection using these specialized setups, hindering the scalability of robot learning to a wider range of real-world scenarios.
3. **Untapped Potential of "In-the-Wild" Data**: The morphological similarity between human and dexterous robot hands suggests that observing human interactions with objects could provide valuable insights for robot control. Vast amounts of "in-the-wild" video data, such as YouTube how-to videos, naturally contain demonstrations of humans performing everyday activities with objects. This data source is readily available and implicitly contains functional grasps, offering a rich, scalable, and less constrained alternative to lab-based demonstrations.
4. **Accelerating Learning and Generalization**: By providing a hand pose prior derived from natural human behavior, the robot agent can be guided toward meaningful grasp configurations, thereby accelerating the learning process and improving sample efficiency. Furthermore, extracting a visual prior based on 3D hand pose is intended to tolerate viewpoint variations and complex backgrounds present in "in-the-wild" data, promoting generalization across diverse visual environments and objects.
5. **Reduced Supervision Requirements**: The proposed approach aims to utilize only visual input (hand pose) from human data, without requiring full state-action trajectories, simplifying the data collection and processing pipeline compared to conventional LfD.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to develop more scalable, efficient, and natural methods for teaching dexterous robots complex manipulation skills, particularly grasping, by directly leveraging the abundant and authentic human expertise available in internet video.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexVIP, the proposed approach, integrates human hand pose priors derived from "in-the-wild" YouTube videos into a deep reinforcement learning framework for dexterous grasping. The methodology encompasses several key stages: reinforcement learning setup, curation of human-object interaction video frames, extraction and clustering of human hand poses, retargeting these poses to the robot, and the design of a video-informed reward function.

**Reinforcement Learning Framework for Dexterous Grasping:**
The task is framed as an RL problem within the MuJoCo physics simulator, utilizing a 30-DoF Adroit robotic hand (24-DoF hand + 6-DoF arm). An actor-critic model is employed for learning the grasping policy, trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

- **Observations ($o_t^r$)**: At each timestep $t$, the robot receives multimodal observations:
 - **Visual Inputs**: An egocentric hand-mounted camera provides an RGB image ($I_t^r$) and a corresponding depth map ($D_t^r$). Additionally, a binary affordance map ($A_t^r$), indicating functional grasp regions on the object, is inferred from the initial RGB image using a pre-trained affordance prediction network.
 - **Motor Inputs**: These include the robot's proprioception ($P_t^r$, comprising joint angles $p_t^r$ and angular velocities $v_t^r$) and hand-object contact distances ($d_t^r$), which are pairwise distances between object affordance regions and contact points on the hand.
 - **Sensors**: The hand is equipped with 21 touch sensors ($T^r$) uniformly distributed across the palm and fingers.
- **Action Space ($a_t^r$)**: The policy outputs 30 continuous joint angle values for the Adroit hand, which are applied to the robot's actuators.
- **Feature and Policy Learning**: Visual inputs are processed by a three-layer Convolutional Neural Network ($f_V$) to generate a visual embedding ($V_t$). Motor inputs are processed by a two-layer fully connected network ($f_M$) to generate a motor embedding ($M_t$). These embeddings are concatenated and fed into the actor and critic networks to estimate the policy distribution and state values.

**Object-Specific Human Hand Pose Priors from Video:**
This component details how human demonstrations are leveraged.

- **Object Dataset**: The study uses 27 household objects, including 16 from the ContactDB dataset (for comparison with prior work) and 11 additional objects, for which 3D models are available from various public repositories.
- **Video Frame Dataset Curation**: Video frames containing human-object interactions are curated from the HowTo100M dataset, a large collection of instructional YouTube videos. The focus is on videos featuring target household objects in active, functional use. This process yielded 715 frames, averaging 26 grasp images per object.
- **Target Hand Pose Acquisition**:
 - **3D Human Hand Pose Inference**: For each curated image, the state-of-the-art FrankMocap method is used to estimate the 3D human hand pose ($p_h$), providing 3D joint angles. Only right-hand detections are considered.
 - **Consensus Pose Identification**: For each object class, a set of inferred human hand poses $P(c)$ is collected. Due to potential variability, k-medoid clustering is applied to $P(c)$ to identify a "consensus" target human hand pose ($p_h_c^*$), represented by the medoid of the largest cluster. This process helps filter outliers and capture representative functional poses.
 - **Hand Pose Retargeting**: The consensus human pose $p_h_c^*$ is then transformed into a corresponding target robot pose $p_r_c^*$ suitable for the Adroit hand. This retargeting mechanism (detailed in supplementary material) accounts for the differing kinematic chains and degrees of freedom between the FrankMocap human hand model and the Adroit robot hand. It involves converting joint positions from world coordinates to root-relative, then to parent-relative, and finally mapping local joint angles to the Adroit's revolute joints. This process provides a prior for meaningful robot hand configurations.

**Video-Informed Reward Function:**
The total reward function ($R$) for the RL agent is a weighted sum of four components:
$R = \alpha R_{succ} + \beta R_{aff} + \gamma R_{pose} + \eta R_{entropy}$

- **$R_{succ}$ (Success Reward)**: A positive reward (+1) is given if the object is successfully lifted off the table and remains airborne for at least 50 timesteps, indicating a stable grasp.
- **$R_{aff}$ (Affordance Reward)**: A negative reward, calculated as the Chamfer distance between sampled points on the robot's hand and points on the inferred object affordance region. This incentivizes the robot to grasp the object at functional regions.
- **$R_{pose}$ (Pose Prior Reward)**: A negative reward, representing the mean per-joint angle error between the robot's current pose ($p_t^r$) and the target human-like pose ($p_r_c^*$) for that object. This reward is only applied when at least 30% of the robot's touch sensors are activated, ensuring the pose matching occurs during object contact. It hierarchically weights joint errors, prioritizing alignment of parent joints. Arm azimuth and elevation are ignored to allow flexibility in arm orientation.
- **$R_{entropy}$ (Entropy Reward)**: Encourages exploration of the action space by maximizing the entropy of the policy's output distribution.
- **Coefficients**: The weights are set as $\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 1$, $\gamma = 1$, $\eta = 0.001$.

The entire network is trained using Adam with a learning rate of 5e-5, for 150 million agent steps, with an episode length of 200 time steps. A single grasping policy is trained on all 27 curated objects across four random seed initializations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate the impact of learning pose priors from video and compare DexVIP's performance against existing methods and baselines.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Performance was assessed using four normalized metrics (0-100%, higher is better):
1. **Grasp Success**: Object lifted for at least 50 time steps.
2. **Grasp Stability**: Object held after applying 1 Newton perturbation forces in six orthogonal directions.
3. **Functionality**: Hand lies close to the ground truth affordance region after a successful grasp.
4. **Posture**: Distance between the target human hand pose and the agent's hand pose after a successful grasp.

**Comparative Performance:**
DexVIP was compared against four methods:
- **COM (Center of Mass)**: Uses object's center of mass as a grasp prior.
- **TOUCH**: Rewards only for hand-object contact via touch sensors.
- **GRAFF**: A state-of-the-art RL model using object-centric visual affordances without hand pose priors.
- **DAPG**: A hybrid imitation+RL model trained with motion-glove VR demonstrations (25 demos per ContactDB object, or similar object demos for others).

Evaluated on 16 ContactDB objects, DexVIP consistently outperformed all compared methods across all metrics. It showed a significant boost in grasp success and stability compared to GRAFF, which also utilizes object affordances but lacks a hand pose prior. DexVIP achieved comparable functionality scores with GRAFF, while notably excelling in the Posture metric, indicating that the learned policies produced human-like grasping behaviors. Qualitative examples further illustrated DexVIP's ability to grasp objects with natural human-like postures, contrasting with the often unusual poses generated by methods like COM or GRAFF.

**Training Efficiency:**
DexVIP demonstrated faster learning, achieving successful policies 20% faster than GRAFF. This efficiency is attributed to the hand pose priors, which guide the agent towards more effective grasp configurations from an earlier stage of training, thereby improving sample efficiency.

**Expert Data Scalability and Efficiency:**
A key advantage of DexVIP is its efficiency in data collection compared to traditional demonstration methods. Collecting a single teleoperation demonstration for DAPG typically takes approximately 5 minutes due to complex setup, whereas curating a video frame for DexVIP takes only a few seconds. This efficiency translates to better scalability; when evaluated on 11 non-ContactDB objects for which specific mocap demonstrations were unavailable, DAPG's success rate dropped by 15% (from 59% to 50%). In contrast, DexVIP experienced only a marginal 4% drop (from 68% to 65%), maintaining comparable performance and demonstrating its ability to leverage readily available internet data for new objects.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies confirmed the incremental benefit of each reward component. A policy using only $R_{aff}$ achieved 60% success. Adding touch sensor rewards (R_aff + Touch) increased success to 63%. The full DexVIP model (R_aff + Touch + Pose prior) achieved 68% success, validating the combined effectiveness of the proposed reward structure, particularly the hand pose prior.

**Robustness to Noise and Physical Parameters:**
To simulate real-world conditions, experiments were conducted with various noise sources during training and testing, including Gaussian noise on proprioception, actuation, pixel perturbations in images, and object tracking failures. Under these challenging conditions, DexVIP maintained a grasp success rate of 64%, remarkably outperforming even the noise-free models of other methods. This indicates a strong robustness of the learned policies, supporting their potential for sim2real transfer. Furthermore, DexVIP exhibited robustness to variations in object mass (0.5kg to 1.5kg) and scale (0.8x to 1.2x of training size).

**Pose Prediction and Object Shape Variation Impact:**
The study investigated the impact of using inferred human poses versus ground truth poses for training. Policies trained with inferred poses performed comparably to those trained with ground truth poses (68% vs. 70% success, respectively), suggesting robustness to potential errors in pose prediction and that the clustering mechanism effectively filters out outlier poses. DexVIP also demonstrated robustness to variations in object shape between the video data and the simulator, successfully grasping generic object meshes from the same category even without exact matches.

**TSNE Analysis:**
TSNE analysis of human and robot hand poses revealed a meaningful distribution across different object morphologies, indicating that DexVIP's learned poses accurately reflect human-like grasping behaviors for various objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexVIP research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a method for learning dexterous robotic grasping that addresses several long-standing challenges associated with traditional learning from demonstration (LfD) paradigms.

**Novelty and Practicality:** The core novelty lies in leveraging "in-the-wild" human-object interaction videos to derive human hand pose priors for dexterous robotic grasping. This approach moves beyond the limitations of lab-confined, resource-intensive demonstration collection methods, offering a scalable and cost-effective alternative. By observing real human interactions, DexVIP can learn functional grasps that are inherently more natural and task-oriented compared to arbitrary lifting strategies derived from indirect teleoperation.

**Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that DexVIP outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, including those relying on specialized human demonstrations, in terms of grasp success, stability, and the naturalness of the learned postures. The integration of human pose priors also accelerates the reinforcement learning process, making it 20% more sample-efficient. This efficiency is critical for complex tasks with large action spaces.

**Scalability and Generalization:** A significant impact of DexVIP is its improved scalability. The method allows for the rapid incorporation of new objects by simply curating additional video frames, a process that is orders of magnitude faster and simpler than setting up new teleoperation experiments. This enhances the generalization capabilities of robotic policies to novel objects and diverse environments, as the visual model focusing on 3D hand pose tolerates viewpoint variations and complex visual backgrounds.

**Robustness for Real-World Deployment:** The demonstrated robustness to various sources of noise (sensory, actuation, tracking) and variations in object physical properties (mass, scale) is a crucial step towards bridging the simulation-to-reality gap. While conducted in simulation, these experiments provide an encouraging indication of the potential for DexVIP's policies to be transferred and deployed effectively on real dexterous robots.

**Reduced Supervision Requirements:** By requiring only visual input for hand pose priors, rather than full state-action trajectories, DexVIP simplifies the data collection and annotation burden, democratizing access to expert knowledge for robot learning.

**Future Trajectories:** This work represents an encouraging advancement towards training robotic manipulation agents from weakly supervised and easily accessible Internet data. The success in grasping suggests promising avenues for expanding this methodology to learn more complex, fine-grained manipulation skills beyond simple grasping, further enabling robots to perform intricate tasks in human-centric environments. The shift from highly controlled, expensive demonstrations to widely available, natural video data holds the potential to significantly accelerate the development of more adaptable and skilled robotic systems.
