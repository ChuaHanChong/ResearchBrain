# ReactiveBFM: Reactive Closed-Loop Motion Planning Towards Universal Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed ReactiveBFM, a closed-loop motion planning framework for humanoid whole-body control that integrates a reactive motion planner with a Behavior Foundation Model. The system demonstrated robust real-time adaptation and error recovery on a Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving a 93.1% task success rate under severe perturbations and enabling zero-shot dynamic target reaching.

## Problem

- Existing generative motion planners suffer from "cumulative exposure bias," leading to failure when real-world execution deviates from ideal training trajectories.
- Humanoids require dynamic adaptation to environmental shifts and real-time command updates, which open-loop planners cannot provide.
- A significant latency mismatch exists between slower auto-regressive planning and the high-frequency demands of motion tracking and control.

## Method

- **ReactiveBFM**'s closed-loop framework integrates an ==auto-regressive motion diffusion model== as a reactive planner with a pre-trained ==Behavior Foundation Model (BFM)==.
- A ==scheduled auto-regressive prefix sampling== strategy trains the planner to actively learn error-recovery from imperfect physical states, mitigating ==cumulative exposure bias==.
- An ==asynchronous replanning== mechanism with ==TensorRT acceleration== and ==temporal ensembling== addresses latency mismatches and ensures smooth, non-blocking control execution.

## Results

- Achieved a **93.1%** task success rate and **2.0%** fall rate under severe physical perturbations (**100N** external force) in sim-to-sim evaluations, outperforming open-loop baselines by **28.6%** in success rate.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot dynamic target reaching on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid, maintaining continuous execution for over **40 seconds** with a **90%** success rate across 10 real-world trials.
- Enabled seamless streaming interactive control, allowing real-time command updates and robust recovery from strong physical disturbances (e.g., repeated kicks, forced rotation reversals) on the physical robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning error-recovery behaviors from imperfect states via scheduled self-rollout and prefix noise is crucial for robust generative planning in dynamic environments.
> - A compact kinematic representation and rigorous data curation improve physical plausibility and reduce the burden on generative planners, enhancing hardware executability.
> - Asynchronous planning combined with an ego-centric reset strategy enables continuous adaptation to moving targets and seamless interactive control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose humanoid robots capable of operating in unstructured, unpredictable environments is a significant challenge in robotics. Current research efforts in humanoid control can be broadly categorized into low-level execution engines and high-level motion generators.

Low-level control has seen advancements through universal trackers and Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs). These models, often trained using large-scale Reinforcement Learning across diverse datasets, provide robust policies for tracking unseen kinematic references with high precision. Examples include Unitracker, Twist2, and SONIC. However, these systems typically require precise trajectory inputs and operate as execution engines rather than planning systems.

High-level motion generation, frequently employing generative models, has made progress in synthesizing kinematic motions. These models often train on human motion capture (MoCap) data or retargeted robot corpora. However, many existing motion generators perform offline synthesis (e.g., Mimic2DM, DreamControl, Imagine2Real) or sparse keyframe interpolation (e.g., SONIC). These approaches operate in an open-loop paradigm, meaning they generate trajectories without real-time feedback from the environment or the robot's actual state. Consequently, they are susceptible to environmental shifts, physical disturbances, and tracking discrepancies, leading to cumulative physical drift and potential failures when deployed in dynamic real-world scenarios.

A key architectural limitation in cascading existing generative planners with BFMs is the "cumulative exposure bias." Traditional planners, trained with teacher forcing on perfect historical inputs, are not equipped to handle the inevitable tracking errors that arise in real-world execution. Once the robot's state deviates from the training distribution, these planners often fail to recover, causing errors to compound. Furthermore, there is a latency mismatch between the typically lower-frequency auto-regressive planning and the high-frequency requirements of motion tracking and control.

ReactiveBFM aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a closed-loop planning-control framework that directly addresses cumulative exposure bias and latency challenges, enabling real-time, adaptive, and robust whole-body control for humanoids in dynamic environments. It integrates a reactive motion planner with a BFM, allowing for continuous generation and adaptation of context-aware trajectories.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReactiveBFM is to enable universal humanoid whole-body control that is truly reactive and robust in real-world, unpredictable environments. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and motivations:

1. **Overcome Cumulative Exposure Bias in Reactive Planning:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing generative motion planners, when deployed in a closed-loop manner, suffer from cumulative exposure bias. They are trained using "teacher forcing," where they condition on perfect ground-truth prefix trajectories. In real-world deployment, even minor discrepancies between planned and executed motions can push the planner out of its training distribution, leading to compounding errors and system failure.
 - **Objective:** Develop a training strategy that allows the generative planner to actively learn error-recovery behaviors from imperfect physical states, rather than ideal trajectories, ensuring robust execution despite real-time tracking errors and disturbances.

2. **Achieve Seamless Reactivity to Environmental Shifts and Command Updates:**
 - **Motivation:** Humanoids in unstructured environments must dynamically adapt to unforeseen physical interactions and evolving task constraints. Open-loop planners cannot respond to these changes, leading to rigidity and accumulated physical drift.
 - **Objective:** Design a system that can continuously generate and adapt context-aware whole-body trajectories on the fly, responding to real-time proprioceptive feedback, text instructions, and target positions, even during mid-execution command changes. This includes ensuring spatio-temporal fluidity during instruction updates.

3. **Resolve Latency Mismatch between Planning and Control:**
 - **Motivation:** Auto-regressive motion generation typically operates at a lower frequency than the high-frequency demands of motion tracking and low-level control (e.g., 50 Hz). A naive cascade would lead to blocking issues or insufficient responsiveness.
 - **Objective:** Implement an architectural solution that reconciles this severe latency mismatch, allowing the high-frequency control loop to operate uninterrupted while the planner generates trajectories asynchronously.

4. **Enhance Robustness to Physical Disturbances and Enable Zero-Shot Generalization:**
 - **Motivation:** Humanoid robots must maintain stability and perform tasks even when subjected to external perturbations or when encountering novel task variations (e.g., moving targets not seen during training).
 - **Objective:** Create a system that demonstrates high physical agility and resilience under severe perturbations, and can perform complex tasks like zero-shot moving target reaching, generalizing from limited static training data to dynamic real-world scenarios.

5. **Ensure Physically Plausible and Smooth Trajectory Generation:**
 - **Motivation:** Raw motion datasets often contain physically implausible artifacts (e.g., foot sliding). Over-parameterized kinematic representations can also accelerate physical drift and introduce kinematic misalignment.
 - **Objective:** Utilize a compact, kinematically complete representation and rigorous data curation to ensure the planner generates dynamically valid, hardware-executable trajectories. Additionally, enforce temporal smoothness during replanning to prevent jerky movements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReactiveBFM integrates a reactive whole-body motion planner with a Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) within a closed-loop framework.

#### 3.1. Reactive Whole-Body Motion Planner

The core of ReactiveBFM is an auto-regressive (AR) motion diffusion model acting as the reactive, closed-loop planner. This planner is designed to mitigate cumulative exposure bias and ensure robust execution.

- **Auto-Regressive Formulation and Compact Representation:**
 - Online reactive whole-body planning is formulated as an AR motion generation problem. The model synthesizes feasible reference trajectories in real-time, conditioned on language instructions, target positions, and historical proprioception.
 - A compact 36-dimensional representation `x_i = [p_i, q_i, θ_i]` is used, consisting of the root's global translation (`p_i`), quaternion rotation (`q_i`), and joint positions (`θ_i`) for 29 degrees of freedom (DoFs) of the Unitree G1. This minimal representation reduces over-parameterization and kinematic misalignment burdening the generative planner.
 - The planner is an AR Motion Diffusion Model (AR-MDM) that predicts a clean motion chunk `X_0` from a noisy signal `X_τ` and conditions `c` (text, target positions, prefix trajectory), optimizing a standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss.

- **Scheduled Auto-Regressive Prefix Sampling:**
 - To address cumulative exposure bias, a curriculum learning paradigm is introduced during training.
 - **Warm-up:** Initially, teacher forcing (conditioning on perfect ground-truth prefix trajectories) is used to rapidly warm up the diffusion model.
 - **Scheduled Sampling:** As training progresses, the probability of using ground-truth prefixes linearly decays. The model transitions into a "self-rollout" mode, where it conditions on its own previously generated predictions over an extended horizon.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Gaussian noise is injected into the prefix representations. This forces the planner to learn error-recovery behaviors, enabling stable trajectory generation even when the robot's current state deviates from ideal conditions.

- **Replanning Smoothness and Condition Adherence:**
 - **Temporal Regularization:** The training objective is augmented with a temporal consistency loss, including first-order (velocity) and second-order (acceleration) terms. This penalizes non-smooth transitions, ensuring holistic trajectory smoothness and zero-order continuity at the prefix-generation boundary, thus preventing jerky motions.
 - **Condition Dropout:** Independent dropout is applied to text instructions and target positions during training. This forces the planner to capture an unconditioned motion prior, preventing collapse into a single conditioning modality.
 - **Cross-Motion Transitions:** The system simulates cross-motion transitions to handle streaming interactive control where new commands are issued mid-execution. The generalized prior enables the model to synthesize stable transitions when reacting to unpredictable conditional shifts, preventing tracking errors from escalating into cumulative exposure bias.

- **High-Quality Data Curation:**
 - To prevent the planner from proposing unreachable states or suffering from physically infeasible artifacts (e.g., foot sliding) present in raw datasets, a rigorous physical curation pipeline is applied.
 - Diverse text-motion corpora (100STYLE, AMASS-HumanML3D, Kungfu) are processed.
 - **Physical Corrections:** Kinematic canonicalization and explicit physical corrections via simulation (PyBullet) are enforced to eliminate dynamically invalid motions.
 - **Goal-Conditioned Reaching Data:** 10,000 goal-conditioned reaching trajectories are synthesized using a pre-trained PhysHSI policy to enhance target-reaching capabilities.
 - The combined corpus yields 37.14 hours of dynamically verified motion data, equipping the planner to translate high-level intents and dynamic spatial targets into safe, hardware-executable trajectories.

#### 3.2. Closed-Loop System & Real-World Deployment

The system is designed for robust real-world deployment by addressing inference latency, replanning consistency, and dynamic generalization.

- **Asynchronous Replanning and Acceleration:**
 - An event-driven asynchronous execution mechanism prevents the planner from blocking the 50 Hz control loop. A non-blocking planner thread is triggered when unexecuted reference frames drop below a safe margin (N_buf = 10 frames), extracting the latest historical proprioception.
 - TensorRT compilation optimizes both planner (19.3 ms) and controller (5.9 ms) inference latency.
 - The controller process is strictly isolated with real-time CPU scheduling priority to ensure the 50 Hz control loop is never preempted, maintaining responsiveness.

- **Temporal Ensembling and Spatial Alignment:**
 - Asynchronous replanning generates overlapping reference trajectories, which could induce high-frequency oscillations. Temporal ensembling via trajectory chunking (blending overlapping positional and rotational references) mitigates this, enforcing spatio-temporal consistency and eliminating inference-induced jitter.
 - A unified spatial reference is ensured through data canonicalization during planner training and hardware calibration at controller initialization, aligning both modules within a global world coordinate system.

- **Zero-Shot Dynamic Target Reaching via Ego-Centric Reset:**
 - To enable dynamic target reaching, the system employs a continuous ego-centric alignment strategy. At each replanning step, the robot's instantaneous root pose is reset as the new global origin, and the target's position is transformed into this egocentric frame. This decomposes continuous dynamic tracking into a sequence of static-reaching sub-tasks, aligning with the static training prior.
 - Physical momentum is preserved by conditioning the AR planner on historical proprioception. This allows robust, zero-shot generalization to long-horizon dynamic target reaching.

#### 3.3. Behavior Foundation Model (BFM)

ReactiveBFM utilizes a pre-trained BFM, which shares an architecture similar to SONIC. This universal tracking controller is trained via PPO on over 102 million frames of retargeted motion data at 50 FPS. It employs a comprehensive global tracking reward and extensive domain randomization, providing robust adaptability to out-of-distribution state deviations. This inherent resilience complements the closed-loop planning paradigm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReactiveBFM's performance was evaluated through rigorous sim-to-sim benchmarking and real-world deployments on the Unitree G1 humanoid.

- **Real-World Zero-Shot Dynamic Target Reaching:**
 - The system successfully performs long-horizon zero-shot dynamic target reaching, generalizing without any dynamic-target training data.
 - It maintained continuous execution for over 40 seconds in trials.
 - A robust 90% success rate was achieved across 10 real-world trials, demonstrating intricate whole-body coordination and on-the-fly replanning to reach moving targets.

- **Real-World Streaming Interactive Control and Task Switching:**
 - ReactiveBFM enables seamless transitions across diverse, long-horizon maneuvers (locomotion, upper-body motions, dynamic whole-body actions) under continuous text commands.
 - The planner's ability to condition on the latest proprioceptive prefix and current textual instruction allows new commands to overwrite previous intents without robot resets, validating the training strategy for condition switching and proprioceptive grounding.

- **Real-World Robustness Under Physical Disturbances:**
 - The system demonstrates superior recovery from external physical perturbations (e.g., repeated heavy kicks, forced rotation reversals, strikes with a 3kg ball, being dragged off-balance).
 - Unlike open-loop baselines that fail by rigidly tracking obsolete references, ReactiveBFM dynamically conditions future planning on the altered physical state, showcasing active recovery rather than passive resilience from the controller alone.

- **Sim-to-Sim Evaluation (Quantitative Benchmarking):**
 - **Overall Performance:** ReactiveBFM achieved a 93.1% task success rate, a fall rate of 2.0%, and a root-relative mean per-joint position error (E_rMPJPE) of 34.6mm under severe perturbations (100N external force). This significantly outperformed cascaded open-loop baselines (e.g., TextOp+SONIC), which had a 64.5% success rate and 23.5% fall rate, demonstrating a 28.6% improvement in success rate over cascaded open-loop baselines.
 - **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing scheduled self-rollout significantly degraded performance (70.5% success, 9.3% fall rate), highlighting the importance of learning error-recovery.
 - Without temporal consistency loss, smoothness decreased, and success rate dropped (83.0% success, 7.5% fall rate).
 - Using a dense representation (instead of compact) resulted in slightly lower success (89.1%) and higher error (40.3mm), confirming the benefit of the compact representation.
 - **Replanning Smoothness:** ReactiveBFM achieved a replanning smoothness of 96.9mm, which was superior to other closed-loop baselines.
 - **Survival Time:** The system demonstrated a survival time of 29.8 seconds, outperforming baselines.

- **Timeline & Latency Analysis:**
 - TensorRT acceleration reduced planner inference to 19.3 ms and controller inference to 5.9 ms.
 - The asynchronous replanning mechanism allows the controller to maintain a 50 Hz execution loop while the planner runs at ~1.6 Hz (triggered every ~0.62s).
 - Temporal ensembling successfully resolved overlapping prediction chunks, preventing replanning-induced jitter and ensuring fluid hardware execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReactiveBFM represents a step towards universal humanoid whole-body control by addressing fundamental challenges in reactive planning and real-world deployment.

The introduction of a closed-loop planning-control framework that fundamentally resolves cumulative exposure bias through a scheduled prefix sampling curriculum is a key contribution. This enables the generative planner to actively learn error-recovery behaviors, a critical capability for robust operation in dynamic and unpredictable environments. The demonstration of this robustness on a physical humanoid, with a 93.1% success rate under severe perturbations and significantly reduced fall rates compared to open-loop baselines, highlights the practical effectiveness of this approach.

The ability to achieve zero-shot moving target reaching, generalizing from static training data to dynamic tasks without specific training, showcases the system's adaptability and efficient reuse of existing priors. This capability is enhanced by the ego-centric reset strategy, which enables continuous dynamic tracking. Furthermore, the support for streaming interactive control with seamless transitions between complex, long-horizon text-conditioned behaviors is a step towards more intuitive human-robot interaction.

The methodological innovations, including a compact kinematic representation, temporal regularization, condition dropout, asynchronous replanning with TensorRT acceleration, and trajectory chunking for temporal ensembling, collectively address the latency mismatch and ensure spatio-temporally fluid execution on hardware. These solutions are crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap and enabling real-time performance.

The potential impact of ReactiveBFM lies in advancing the autonomy of humanoid robots. By providing a framework for robust, adaptive, and reactive whole-body control, it can expand the range of tasks humanoids can perform in unstructured environments, moving beyond pre-defined trajectories to intelligent, real-time adaptation. This could have implications for applications in various domains, including logistics, assistance, and exploration, where humanoids need to respond to dynamic changes and complex commands.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges two main limitations:
1. **Lack of Explicit Object Interaction Modeling:** ReactiveBFM does not explicitly model precise whole-body humanoid-object interaction, which may limit its performance in contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks.
2. **Limited Sensory Modalities:** The online planner is currently conditioned only on compact target positions, text commands, and kinematic states. This may be insufficient for tasks requiring high-dimensional visual or tactile feedback.

Future work is suggested to extend the framework to handle humanoid-object interaction (HOI) and incorporate richer sensory modalities.
