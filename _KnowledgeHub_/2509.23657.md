# Beyond English-Centric Training: How Reinforcement Learning Improves Cross-Lingual Reasoning in LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforcement Learning (RL) consistently demonstrates superior cross-lingual reasoning generalization in large language models compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). Counter-intuitively, RL training on non-English data yields better performance and generalization than English training, a finding not observed with SFT.

## Problem

- Despite advancements, significant performance gaps persist in complex reasoning tasks for non-English large language models (LLMs).
- The specific impact of reinforcement learning on cross-lingual generalization in LLMs, as opposed to general reasoning improvement, remained systematically underexplored.
- The prevailing English-centric training paradigm for LLMs had not been challenged to determine if non-English data could offer distinct benefits for RL in a multilingual context.

## Method

- Researchers conducted a systematic comparative study between Reinforcement Learning (RL) using ==GRPO== and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to evaluate their influence on cross-lingual reasoning generalization.
- Models like Qwen2.5-3B-Base were fine-tuned on mathematical and reasoning datasets translated into English, Chinese, and German to assess the impact of training language.
- Evaluations were performed across ten diverse languages and multiple reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MGSM, MMLU-ProX-Lite) to comprehensively measure generalization capabilities.

## Results

- RL consistently and significantly outperformed SFT across all ten evaluation languages and reasoning benchmarks, showing an average accuracy improvement of +17.5 to +25.2 points over SFT on MGSM.
- RL models trained on non-English data (e.g., German) achieved systematically higher average performance (e.g., **71.5%** on MGSM) and stronger cross-lingual generalization compared to those trained on English data (**62.7%**), a benefit specific to the RL paradigm.
- Mechanistic analyses confirmed that linguistic flexibility and robust sampling strategies in RL contribute to its superior generalization, while forcing linguistic consistency degrades performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Linguistic inconsistency, where RL models use mixed or non-training languages in internal reasoning steps, paradoxically contributes to stronger cross-lingual generalization by leveraging pre-trained multilingual knowledge.
> - Full RL, with its active exploration and iterative policy improvement, is crucial for learning generalizable reasoning strategies, outperforming off-policy rejection sampling.
> - Models with stronger cross-lingual generalization exhibit minimal semantic drift from the base model's representations, suggesting that preserving pre-trained multilingual structures is key for effective transfer.
