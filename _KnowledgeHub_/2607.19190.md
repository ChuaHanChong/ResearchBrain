# Agentic Real2Sim: Physics-based World Modeling with Vision-Language Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Agentic Real2Sim automatically converts real-world robotic interaction recordings into physically accurate, simulatable digital twins, supporting rigid, deformable, and humanoid domains. It achieved 48% replay success on 100 DROID rigid manipulation episodes using an open-weight VLM backend, demonstrating cost-effectiveness compared to proprietary models.

## Problem

- Converting real-world robotic interaction recordings into high-fidelity, physically accurate simulations (Real2Sim) is a labor-intensive and manual process.
- Existing methods for visual reconstruction, geometry recovery, object state inference, and physical parameter estimation are often brittle, ad-hoc, and do not scale well for complex, dynamic episodes.
- Current approaches struggle to integrate diverse interaction elements—such as robot movements, manipulated objects, camera setups, and physical parameters—into a unified, runnable simulation.

## Method

- Introduces an **"Agentic Real2Sim"** framework that orchestrates deterministic visual processing and simulation tools with ==VLM-driven agents== for ambiguous decisions and refinement.
- The architecture consists of four linked agents: ==Visual Processing==, ==Physical-Prior Inference==, ==Scene Preparation==, and ==Simulator-in-the-Loop Grasp Optimization==, which collectively transform raw observations into a simulatable episode twin.
- Utilizes a common ==episode contract== and domain-specific adapters to generalize **Agentic Real2Sim** conversion across rigid-object manipulation, deformable-object interaction, and humanoid motion.

## Results

- Achieved **48%** replay success for 100 DROID rigid manipulation episodes using the open-weight Gemma 4 31B VLM backend, with an additional **8%** partial successes.
- Demonstrated that the open-weight Gemma 4 31B VLM delivered the highest success rate (**48%**) at the lowest cost (**$2.62** per 100 episodes), outperforming proprietary models like GPT-5.4 (**43%** success, **$82.30** cost).
- Qualitatively extended the framework's applicability to deformable object interactions (PhysTwin-style) and humanoid motion (BFM-Zero-style), showing consistent simulation of recovered geometry, deformation, and motion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM-driven agentic pipelines can effectively coordinate complex multi-tool workflows for real-to-sim conversion, automating tasks historically requiring significant manual effort.
> - Open-weight Visual-Language Models (VLMs) can achieve comparable or superior performance to expensive proprietary models in this orchestrational role, significantly reducing the computational cost of large-scale conversions.
> - The framework's modular design, separating deterministic tools from VLM decisions and employing domain-specific adapters, enables versatile application across diverse physical interaction types, from rigid to deformable and humanoid.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of converting real-world robotic interaction recordings into physically accurate and runnable simulations, a process termed "Real2Sim." Historically, this conversion has been labor-intensive, requiring extensive manual effort for tasks such as visual reconstruction, scene geometry recovery, object state inference, physical parameter estimation, and the assembly of actors, objects, cameras, poses, and trajectories within a simulator. Existing manual workflows involve tuning visual foundation models, cleaning meshes, aligning coordinate frames, and integrating disparate visual perception tools and simulators, leading to brittle and non-scalable solutions.

This research integrates and extends several lines of existing work:
- **Automated Real2Sim Alignment:** Previous efforts have focused on reconstructing simulation-ready object assets and estimating physical parameters from robotic interactions (e.g., Pfaff et al., 2025) or aligning visual and dynamic properties for robotic manipulation policy development (e.g., Fan et al., 2025). These works highlight the insufficiency of purely photorealistic scene reconstruction for robotics, emphasizing the need to recover dynamics, contacts, and task-relevant behaviors. Agentic Real2Sim aligns with this emphasis on physical fidelity but distinguishes itself by targeting the *conversion of entire recorded interaction episodes* into simulatable twin episodes, preserving observed actors, manipulated objects, and trajectories, rather than focusing on isolated object scanning or scene construction for new policy training setups.
- **VLM-driven Asset Generation:** Recent advancements leverage Visual-Language Models (VLMs) for generating visually realistic and physically plausible assets. Frameworks like SceneSmith (Pfaff et al., 2026) and LychSim (Ma et al., 2026) employ agentic VLM pipelines using a "generate-critic-improve" paradigm to build simulation-ready scenes or articulated assets. Agentic Real2Sim adopts this agentic VLM approach for synthetic scene generation and real-to-sim construction. However, it places additional emphasis on reconstructing comprehensive manipulation behaviors directly from real-world recordings, including the generation of physical parameters and appropriate asset placement to recreate physically realistic scenes.
- **Robotic Interaction Datasets:** The field benefits from large-scale in-the-wild datasets such as DROID (Khazatsky et al., 2024), which provides synchronized RGB, depth, calibration, language instructions, and robot trajectories for rigid-object manipulation. Beyond rigid interactions, PhysTwin (Jiang et al., 2025) focuses on deformable manipulation, and BFM-Zero (Li et al., 2025) explores humanoid motion control. Agentic Real2Sim aims to provide a unified conversion solution that spans these diverse interaction types—rigid robot-object interaction, deformable-object interaction, and whole-body motion—addressing a broader range of simulation needs compared to frameworks focused on a single dataset format.
- **Visual Processing and Simulation Tools:** The framework relies on established visual and simulation components, including SAM 3 (Carion et al., 2025) for open-vocabulary segmentation, SAM 3D (Chen et al., 2025) for geometry extraction, FoundationStereo (Wen et al., 2025) for stereo depth estimation, FoundationPose (Wen et al., 2024) for 6D object pose estimation, and deformable simulation tools like PhysTwin and EMPM (Chen et al., 2026). The paper clarifies that while these are essential building blocks, the core contribution of Agentic Real2Sim lies in the "episode contract and alignment loop" that orchestrates these tools, critic feedback, and domain-specific repairs into executable episode twins.

The broader landscape indicates a growing need for scalable, automated solutions that bridge the gap between real-world observations and high-fidelity physical simulations, particularly for applications in robotic learning and evaluation. Agentic Real2Sim positions itself as a significant step towards fulfilling this need by providing a generalized framework for physical world modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the inherent complexity and manual effort involved in current Real2Sim conversion pipelines. Despite advances in large real-world robot datasets and simulation capabilities, converting real physical interaction episodes into simulatable digital twins remains challenging. A simple "plumbing problem" approach, where observations are collected, assets reconstructed, and dropped into a physics engine, is insufficient. Real-world interactions involve dynamic elements like robots, manipulated objects, camera setups, object states, contacts, and physical parameters, all of which must be accurately captured and integrated. The existing reliance on manual tuning and ad-hoc solutions limits the scalability and efficiency of generating simulation-ready data.

The paper articulates three key objectives (contributions) for Agentic Real2Sim:

1. **Develop an Agentic Real2Sim Pipeline for DROID Episodes:** The first objective is to introduce a comprehensive pipeline capable of converting real-world DROID robot manipulation episodes into MuJoCo-simulated episodic twins. This pipeline aims to transform recorded robot-object interactions into simulatable artifacts through a structured sequence of steps: visual processing, physical-prior inference, scene preparation, and simulator-in-the-loop refinement. This is presented as a foundational step towards achieving scalable interaction conversion across various robotic tasks.

2. **Demonstrate Interchangeable VLM Backend Support and Cost Efficiency:** The second objective is to show that the Agentic Real2Sim framework is not dependent on a specific VLM provider. It aims to demonstrate that various VLM backends can be interchangeably used to drive agentic decisions. A specific goal is to illustrate that an open-weight VLM can achieve comparable conversion success rates to more expensive, proprietary frontier models, thereby significantly reducing the computational cost associated with large-scale conversion efforts. This objective addresses the practical barriers to adopting VLM-driven pipelines.

3. **Generalize the Framework Beyond Rigid-Object Manipulation:** The third objective is to extend the applicability of the Agentic Real2Sim framework beyond its initial focus on DROID-style rigid-object manipulation. This involves demonstrating that the same core conversion process and "episode contract" can be adapted to handle more complex scenarios, specifically deformable-object interaction (in a PhysTwin-style setting) and humanoid motion (with BFM-Zero-style motion context). This objective aims to establish the framework's versatility and its potential as a generalized solution for physical world modeling across different interaction types.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to produce high-fidelity, real-world-aligned digital twins that can serve as valuable assets for downstream robotics tasks, particularly for policy learning and evaluation. By automating and streamlining the real-to-sim conversion process, the work seeks to accelerate robot development and research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Agentic Real2Sim is designed to convert a real physical interaction episode into a "simulatable episode twin," which is defined as a simulator artifact that preserves the observations, actors, manipulated entities, geometry, temporal state, physical parameters, and evaluation evidence necessary to replay or query the original episode. The episode twin is formally represented as $\mathcal{T} = (\mathcal{O}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{G}, \mathcal{S}_{1:T}, \Theta, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{M})$, where $\mathcal{O}$ denotes real observations, $\mathcal{A}$ actors or end effectors, $\mathcal{G}$ geometry and appearance assets, $\mathcal{S}_{1:T}$ simulator states over time, $\Theta$ physical and alignment parameters, $\mathcal{B}$ the simulator backend, and $\mathcal{M}$ the metrics and traces for conversion success. The system is engineered to share pipeline stages, artifact contracts, replay loops, and critic interfaces across different domains, while allowing domain-specific skill and tool invocations.

The architecture comprises four linked agents, with a clear distinction between deterministic tools and VLM-driven agentic decisions:

1. **Visual Processing Agent:**
 - **Function:** This agent processes raw video recordings to extract fundamental visual information. It identifies relevant objects, selects optimal keyframes for analysis, segments actors and objects, reconstructs or imports 3D geometry, estimates object scales, and tracks 6D poses over time.
 - **Tools:** It invokes deterministic tools such as SAM 3 for open-vocabulary segmentation, SAM 3D for 3D geometry recovery from image evidence, FoundationStereo for stereo depth estimation, and FoundationPose for 6D object pose estimation and tracking.
 - **Agentic Decisions:** The agent leverages VLMs for decisions that involve ambiguity, such as determining which objects are relevant to the task, selecting keyframes with clear masks, evaluating the acceptability of segmentations or pose tracks (via mask and tracking critics with retry budgets), and identifying the ground plane.
 - **Output:** The extracted visual state is compiled into a canonical episode folder containing object meshes, scales, pose tracks, robot trajectories, camera metadata, first-frame observations, masks, and task semantics.

2. **Physical-Prior Inference Agent:**
 - **Function:** This agent bridges the gap between visual evidence and simulator requirements by inferring physical properties.
 - **Agentic Decisions:** It uses VLMs to convert visual observations and task context into simulator-facing priors, including object identity, material class, mass hints, and other contact-relevant attributes necessary for physical simulation.

3. **Scene Preparation Agent:**
 - **Function:** This agent constructs the initial simulator scene using the artifacts generated by the preceding agents.
 - **Tasks:** It performs a deterministic calibration stage, optimizes the robot base pose against the robot mask, and estimates the ground plane's orientation and offset based on the previously identified ground reference. It then loads the calibrated scene into the MuJoCo physics engine.
 - **Agentic Decisions:** The agent decides whether to proceed with a deterministic sweep over object shift parameters for grasp optimization or to engage an LLM-assisted loop for refinement, particularly if initial attempts are unsatisfactory.

4. **Simulator-in-the-Loop Grasp Optimization Stage:**
 - **Function:** This final stage refines the placement of the manipulated object to ensure successful grasping within the simulation, which is critical for replicating interaction dynamics.
 - **Approach:** It can either execute a deterministic sweep, evaluating a batch of candidate object shifts against contact and grasp outcomes, or employ an LLM-assisted loop. In the latter, the agent proposes refinements based on rendered keyframes and structured replay summaries, iteratively optimizing object placements.

**Conversion for DROID Episodes:** For DROID-style rigid-object interactions, the pipeline specifically takes synchronized camera streams, calibration data, robot trajectory, depth/stereo-derived geometry, and language/task context as input. It rectifies video, uses VLM for object discovery, and employs critics to ensure segmentation and pose tracking quality before writing the resolved visual state. The scene preparation agent then calibrates the scene, optimizes robot base pose, and loads it into MuJoCo for grasp optimization.

**Generalization to Deformable and Humanoid Episodes:** The framework's flexibility is demonstrated through "adapters" for deformable and humanoid domains:
- **Deformable Adapter:** This adapter, inspired by PhysTwin/EMPM, replaces rigid object pose tracking with tracking of geometry, points, particles, springs, or material states. It utilizes simulator rollouts to verify whether recovered physical parameters accurately reproduce observed deformation behaviors.
- **Humanoid Adapter:** Based on BFM-Zero, this adapter replaces object-centric scene preparation with motion-context retrieval, embodiment-specific initialization, and closed-loop replay against retargeted reference motions. It incorporates motion priors from the humanoid/video domain to achieve realistic whole-body motion simulation.

Both adapters reuse the shared episode folder contract, visual evidence, structured replay metrics, and repair interfaces, adapting them to expose domain-specific states and refinement variables. This modularity allows the system to extend its capabilities beyond rigid-body physics without a complete re-architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Agentic Real2Sim primarily focused on the rigid DROID manipulation setting, with qualitative assessments for deformable and humanoid interactions.

**Evaluation Metric for DROID Conversions:**
The primary metric for rigid DROID conversions was "replay success," an episode-level measure of consistency between the simulated episode and the real episode. This metric was determined through a structured, VLM-based process:
- A fixed set of real and simulated keyframes (start, middle_1, middle_2, end) were prepared.
- Reconstruction candidates from the latest grasp sweep were deemed eligible if they were grasped, had a replay video, and exhibited finite and reasonable lift/displacement statistics.
- Up to five eligible candidates, ranked by peak object displacement, were sent to three independent VLM judges.
- Each judge independently compared the real and simulated keyframes using a structured schema, scoring candidates out of 10 points. Deductions were made for: wrong target object (-4 points), wrong final object location (-3 points), wrong action (-2 points), and wrong final gripper location (-1 point). Starting-pose drift was recorded but not directly subtracted.
- A score of 8 or higher indicated a "pass" (replay success), 7 a "partial," and 6 or lower a "fail."
- An episode was considered a replay success ($r_e=1$) if at least one of the three judges assigned a best-candidate score of 8 or higher. The reported replay score was the maximum of the three judge-best scores.

**DROID Episode Conversion at Scale (DROID-100):**
The framework was evaluated on DROID-100, a randomly selected set of 100 rigid manipulation episodes spanning various objects, camera viewpoints, occlusion patterns, and manipulation verbs.

- **Quantitative Results (using Gemma 4 31B VLM backend):**
 - **Replay Successes:** 48 out of 100 episodes.
 - **Partial Successes:** 8 out of 100 episodes.
 - **Failures:** 44 out of 100 episodes.
 - **Total Model Bill:** $2.62 for the 100 episodes.

**Multi-VLM Support and Cost Efficiency:**
The study investigated the performance and cost efficiency of four different VLM backends: Gemma 4 31B (open-weight), Qwen 3.6 35B (open-weight), GPT-5.4 (proprietary), and Claude Haiku 4.5 (proprietary). The pipeline configuration remained constant, with only the VLM backend varying.

- **Observed Replay Success Counts:**
 - Gemma 4 31B: 48/100
 - Qwen 3.6 35B: 45/100
 - GPT-5.4: 43/100
 - Claude Haiku 4.5: 37/100
- **Model Bills (for 100 episodes):**
 - Gemma 4 31B: $2.62
 - Claude Haiku 4.5: $9.17 (approx. 3.5x cost of Gemma)
 - Qwen 3.6 35B: $13.10 (approx. 5.0x cost of Gemma)
 - GPT-5.4: $82.30 (approx. 31.4x cost of Gemma)

These results indicate that all four VLM backends exhibited broadly similar outcome compositions in terms of replay success. Notably, the open-weight Gemma 4 31B model achieved the highest observed success count while incurring the lowest model cost. This is attributed to the VLM's role being narrowly scoped to orchestrate deterministic specialist components and make schema-constrained decisions, rather than performing complex geometry or physics computations directly. The consistent performance across different VLMs, coupled with the significant cost differences, suggests that VLM backend choice largely reduces to cost for this application, making open-weight models a practical option for large conversion batches. The fact that absolute replay success remained below 50% for all backends suggests that the primary headroom for improvement lies more in the upstream visual and simulation components than in the choice of VLM alone.

**Deformable and Humanoid Episode Conversion:**
These domains served as qualitative stress tests for the framework's generalization capabilities.

- **Deformable (PhysTwin-style):** The framework demonstrated the ability to convert episodes involving objects such as rope, cloth, plush items, and elastoplastic materials. Representative real-sim matches showed the recovered geometry and simulated deformation, with the material response qualitatively aligning with visible bending, stretching, and contact sequences. Failure cases were also presented.
- **Humanoid (BFM-Zero-style):** The framework successfully converted Unitree G1 clips of locomotion and short whole-body motions (retargeted from LAFAN1). Qualitative side-by-side motion comparisons indicated agreement in gross pose, balance, and motion-phase between the simulated humanoid and the reference motion after tuning the controller's joint-level gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Agentic Real2Sim presents a significant step towards addressing the current limitations in real-to-sim conversion for robotic applications, offering several areas of potential impact:

- **Automation and Scalability:** The framework automates a process that has traditionally been labor-intensive and manual. By converting real-world physical interaction recordings into simulatable digital twins, it enables a more scalable approach to generating high-fidelity simulation data. This automation can significantly reduce the human effort and time required to prepare complex robotic interaction scenarios for simulation, accelerating research and development cycles.

- **Generalized Physical World Modeling:** A key impact is the framework's ability to generalize across diverse interaction domains. By successfully converting rigid-object manipulation, deformable-object interaction, and humanoid motion episodes using a common "episode contract," Agentic Real2Sim demonstrates a unified approach to physical world modeling. This reduces the need for distinct and specialized Real2Sim pipelines for different types of robotic tasks, fostering a more integrated and versatile simulation ecosystem.

- **Cost-Effective Simulation Data Generation:** The finding that open-weight VLM backends can achieve comparable conversion success rates to proprietary models at a fraction of the cost has substantial implications. This cost efficiency democratizes access to advanced real-to-sim capabilities, making it economically feasible for researchers and developers to generate large volumes of simulation data. It promotes the adoption of VLM-driven pipelines by mitigating the financial barriers often associated with cutting-edge models.

- **Enabling Downstream Robotics Tasks:** The ultimate goal is to provide high-fidelity, real-world-aligned episode twins that can serve as simulator assets for critical downstream robotics tasks. Specifically, the framework aims to support policy learning and evaluation. By generating physically plausible and visually consistent simulated episodes, Agentic Real2Sim can provide a rich, controlled environment for training and testing robotic policies, potentially leading to more robust and capable robots. This direct link to policy development is a primary driver of the framework's significance.

- **Modular and Extensible Architecture:** The deliberate separation of deterministic tools from VLM-driven agentic decisions enhances the framework's modularity. This design allows for easier integration of new perception or simulation components and facilitates experimentation with different VLM backends or agentic decision-making strategies. Such a structure promotes future research and development, allowing for targeted improvements without requiring a complete overhaul of the system.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the current focus on rigid-object DROID episodes for quantitative evaluation and sensitivity to upstream perception or simulator feedback errors, highlight clear avenues for future research. The framework serves as a foundation upon which subsequent work can build, focusing on extending automated conversion to other domains, systematically evaluating agentic components for reliability, improving replay stability, and further enabling policy learning for the robotics community.
