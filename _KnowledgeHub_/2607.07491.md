# Smooth Operator: A Real-Time Sampling-Based Algorithm for Kinematic Hand Retargeting

> [!summary] Summary
> A real-time sampling-based retargeting (SBR) algorithm is introduced for kinematic hand teleoperation, designed to overcome the jitter issues of gradient-based methods. This gradient-free approach generates smoother, more consistent robot hand movements, resulting in higher task success rates and reduced cognitive workload for human operators in dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Gradient-based retargeting algorithms often converge to local minima, leading to high-frequency command spikes (jitter) that degrade teleoperation quality and grasp stability.
- The complex, non-linear kinematic mapping between human and robot hands makes smooth and consistent control challenging.
- Previous evaluations of retargeting algorithms often lacked statistical rigor, used small sample sizes, or introduced confounding factors like compensatory arm movements.

## Method

- Developed a novel gradient-free, ==sampling-based retargeter (SBR)== utilizing the ==Model Predictive Optimized Path Integral (MPOPI)== and ==Improved Cross-Entropy Method (iCEM)== to evaluate a broad distribution of candidate joint configurations.
- Implemented the **SBR** framework using JAX and MJX for efficient parallelization, ensuring real-time performance with an average latency of 3.664 ms.
- Established a rigorous evaluation methodology, including a user study with a Balanced Latin Square design, Mixed Effects Models, and a static-fixture hand mount to isolate the retargeter's performance.

## Results

- SBR achieved the highest real-world task success rate (**54.1%**) and the lowest cognitive workload (**36.4**), outperforming DexPilot (**44.0%** success, **44.0** workload) and GeoRT (**26.6%** success, **56.4** workload) in manipulation tasks.
- In simulation, SBR demonstrated superior kinematic consistency (Motion Preservation **0.776**, Flatness **7.17e-07**) and accuracy (Cosine Similarity **0.9969**), effectively eliminating high-frequency control jitter.
- An ablation study showed the sampling-based approach reduced joint jitter by **36.4%** while maintaining comparable tracking losses to gradient-based methods, which achieved jitter reduction only by increasing tracking loss.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sampling-based optimization inherently acts as a kinematic low-pass filter, effectively reducing high-frequency jitter by probabilistically bounding trajectory variance, which gradient-based methods struggle with in loss-flat regions.
> - Providing smoother, more predictable robot movements through SBR significantly reduces human operator cognitive workload (NASA-TLX score of 36.4) and enhances task reliability.
> - Simulation metrics like Cosine Similarity and Flatness show some predictive power for real-world success rates and cognitive workload, but further research is needed to refine simulation-to-real correlation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is experiencing advancements through learning-based models such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and Video Action Models (VAMs). These models require high-quality teleoperation data for effective training and operation. Retargeting algorithms are central to this process, bridging the kinematic gap between human and robot embodiments to translate human intent into robot commands. This translation can occur in real-time (online retargeting) or through offline processing.

A common challenge in existing retargeting approaches, particularly those relying on gradient-based optimization, is the propensity to converge to various local minima. This can result in high-frequency command spikes, referred to as "jitter." Jitter degrades the quality of teleoperation data, compromises grasp stability, and necessitates the use of aggressive low-pass filtering, which in turn reduces the system's teleoperation bandwidth. The complexity of the non-linear mapping between human and robot hand kinematics exacerbates these issues.

Furthermore, the rigorous evaluation of retargeting algorithms has been a recognized challenge. Previous studies often employed small sample sizes of operators, which can limit statistical rigor. Some evaluations did not adequately account for operator adaptation over time or when switching between retargeters, potentially violating assumptions of statistical independence. Additionally, the concurrent control of both robotic hand and arm in some experimental setups could confound results, as operators might compensate for poor hand retargeting by adjusting arm movements, making it difficult to isolate the true performance of the hand retargeting algorithm.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel gradient-free, sampling-based retargeting method. While sampling-based control has found applications in robot navigation and locomotion, its application to real-time human hand motion retargeting has been largely unexplored. Prior sampling optimization efforts in humanoid retargeting, such as DynaRetarget, were designed for offline processing where computational latency is not a primary concern. The present work positions itself as the first to achieve real-time sampling-based retargeting for human hand control, aiming to provide smoother, more consistent control and a robust evaluation framework for the community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the quality and consistency of real-time kinematic hand teleoperation, thereby enhancing the utility of human demonstrations for advanced robotic learning models. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

- **Addressing Jitter in Teleoperation:** Current gradient-based retargeting algorithms frequently produce "jitter," characterized by aggressive, high-frequency command spikes. This phenomenon arises from their tendency to converge to different local minima in the complex, non-linear mapping between human and robot hand kinematics. Jitter directly degrades the quality of collected teleoperation data, impairs the operator's experience, reduces grasp stability, and necessitates post-hoc filtering that limits system responsiveness. The research aims to mitigate this by developing a method that inherently generates smoother commands.

- **Developing a Gradient-Free Retargeting Method:** Motivated by the limitations of gradient-based solvers, a key objective is to introduce a novel gradient-free, sampling-based retargeter (SBR). This method is designed to overcome the issues of local minima and jitter by evaluating a broad distribution of candidate joint configurations rather than relying on local derivative information. The explicit design goal is to achieve low-jitter, real-time kinematic retargeting for dexterous robotic hands.

- **Ensuring Real-Time Performance:** While sampling-based control methods are known for robustness, their computational demands can be high. A significant objective is to implement the SBR framework using efficient parallelization techniques (e.g., JAX and MJX) to ensure it can operate effectively in real-time for teleoperation applications.

- **Enhancing Operator Experience and Reducing Workload:** Jitter and unpredictable robot responses increase the cognitive load on human operators. By providing smoother and more consistent robot movements, the research aims to make teleoperation more intuitive and reduce operator fatigue, thereby improving the overall user experience during dexterous manipulation tasks.

- **Establishing a Rigorous Evaluation Methodology:** A critical motivation stems from the perceived lack of rigorous evaluation methodologies in existing retargeting research. Previous studies often suffered from small sample sizes, failed to account for operator adaptation and learning effects, or introduced confounding factors by allowing robotic arm movements to compensate for hand retargeting deficiencies. This work aims to establish a more robust benchmarking protocol, employing statistical methods like Balanced Latin Squares and Mixed Effects Models, and isolating hand retargeter performance via a static-fixture hand mount. This objective seeks to provide the research community with a standardized and statistically sound approach to guide future retargeting research.

- **Facilitating High-Quality Data Collection:** Ultimately, the improved fidelity, smoothness, and intuitiveness of the retargeting system are intended to enable the collection of higher-quality human demonstration data. Such data is foundational for training advanced learning-based models for robotic manipulation, directly impacting their capabilities and performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved the development of a novel sampling-based retargeter (SBR), followed by its comprehensive evaluation through both offline simulation analysis and rigorous real-world user studies.

#### 3.1. Sampling-Based Retargeter (SBR) Algorithm

The SBR is framed as a gradient-free optimization problem. It leverages the Model Predictive Optimized Path Integral (MPOPI) framework for control stability and the Improved Cross-Entropy Method (iCEM) for Adaptive Importance Sampling (AIS).
- **Mechanism:** Instead of using local gradients, SBR evaluates a broad distribution of candidate joint configurations for the robot hand. In each iteration, it identifies a subset of "elite" samples (those with the lowest costs). A cost-weighted softmax function is then applied to these elite samples to update the control distribution's mean ($\mu$) and covariance ($\Sigma$). This process probabilistically bounds trajectory variance, enabling the optimizer to act as an inherent kinematic low-pass filter, which generates smooth, low-jitter motion.
- **Implementation:** To ensure real-time performance, the framework is implemented in JAX for efficient parallelization of computations and utilizes MJX for forward kinematics and cost evaluations.
- **Cost Function:** The objective function (J) is a modified version of DexPilot’s loss function, incorporating:
 - Keyvector matching: Minimizes the squared Euclidean distance between corresponding human and robot keyvectors (fingertips, knuckles, etc.).
 - Pose regularization: Penalizes deviation from a neutral robot hand pose.
 - Velocity regularization: Penalizes large changes in joint angles between successive time steps, promoting smoothness.
- **Algorithm 1 Steps:** The algorithm iteratively samples `N` control candidates from a Gaussian distribution, computes their costs, selects `M` elite samples, and uses a softmax function to update the mean and covariance of the control distribution over `L` cycles. Finally, joint limits are applied.
- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** An ablation study was conducted to optimize core SBR sampling hyperparameters (number of samples `N` and update cycles `L`) to balance accuracy and latency. This analysis, visualized through iso-compute contours, indicated that a balanced distribution of computational effort across `N` and `L` yields better optimization than scaling a single parameter. The final configuration utilized 8192 samples and 20 update cycles, achieving an average latency of 3.664 ms.

#### 3.2. Offline Analysis (Simulation)

A set of retargeting heuristics, drawn from existing literature, was used to evaluate SBR and benchmark it against state-of-the-art baselines in a MuJoCo simulation environment.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Accuracy:** Quantified by Cosine Similarity (directional alignment of keyvectors, optimal at 1) and Scale Ratio (spatial magnitude preservation, optimal at 1).
 - **Consistency:** Assessed via Motion Preservation (directional tracking of human and robot landmark velocities, optimal at 1) and Flatness (penalizes high-frequency control jitter, optimal at 0).
 - **Efficiency:** Measured by Workspace Utilization (volumetric overlap between reachable workspaces, higher is better) and Latency (processing time, lower is better).
- **Baselines:** SBR was benchmarked against three open-source retargeters operating on the mimic P0.50 hand: DexPilot (heuristic-based HKVM), Hybrid (HKVM with joint angle warmstarting), and Geometric Retargeter (GeoRT, unsupervised learning-based).
- **Input Data:** Prerecorded human hand data from MANUS Metagloves Pro was used as driving input.
- **Hardware:** Benchmarking was performed on a workstation equipped with a 13th Gen Intel Core i9, 64 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 4090.

#### 3.3. Real-World Experiments (User Study)

A user study was conducted to evaluate SBR's real-world performance.
- **Hardware:** The mimic P0.50 robotic hand, teleoperated via MANUS Metagloves Pro.
- **Experimental Setup:** The robotic hand was attached to a static fixture to isolate the retargeter's mapping fidelity, preventing operators from using compensatory robotic arm movements. The control rate for all retargeters was fixed at 30Hz.
- **Participants:** A cohort of 18 participants with varying levels of teleoperation experience (including novices) participated.
- **Tasks:** Three dexterous finger-gaiting tasks, adapted from Naughton et al., were selected: Card Pickup (Task A, foundational grasp), Vertical Cube Rotation (Task B, complex finger-gaiting), and Screwdriver Pivot (Task C, precision control and contact stability).
- **Experimental Design:** To mitigate order and learning effects, a Balanced Latin Square design was employed for assigning algorithm sequences to participants. A strict two-minute familiarization period was given per retargeter before recorded trials, without task-specific practice. Algorithms were not manually tuned for individual operators.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Task Performance:** Measured by completion time and a granular success score (1.0 for perfect, 0.5 for marginal success, 0.0 for failure). A single manual object reset during a trial capped the score at 0.5.
 - **Cognitive Burden:** Quantified using the NASA Task Load Index (TLX) (scored 0-100), administered after initial, midpoint, and final trials for each algorithm block.
- **Statistical Analysis:** Mixed Effects Models (MEMs) were used for Completion Time and NASA-TLX to account for intra-subject variance, operator learning curves, and baseline dexterity differences. Logistic Generalised Estimating Equations (GEE) were employed for Success Rate to estimate population-averaged performance probabilities. Significance testing involved Dunnett correction for comparisons against the DexPilot baseline and Holm-Bonferroni correction for all pairwise comparisons.

#### 3.4. Gradient vs. Sampling-Based Optimization Ablation

An ablation study was performed to theoretically compare the performance differences between gradient-based and sampling-based approaches.
- **Setup:** The DexPilot loss function was used, and different optimization approaches (RMSProp, SLSQP, and sampling-based) were compared while keeping other components identical. A batch optimization framework was implemented for gradient methods to provide a fair comparison with the large sample count of the sampling method.
- **Analysis:** Comparison included tracking loss, joint jitter, and Hessian eigen-direction analysis to understand the behavior of optimizers in "loss-flat bands." The effect of explicitly adding a velocity regularization term to gradient-based methods was also investigated.

#### 3.5. Sim-to-Real Metric Correlation Analysis

The Spearman rank correlation coefficient (ρ) was computed between the simulation-derived heuristics (Accuracy, Consistency, Efficiency) and the observed real-world performance markers (Success Rate, Completion Time, Overall Workload) to assess their predictive power. Correlation magnitudes were interpreted using Cohen's conventions for effect sizes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings from both simulation and real-world experiments, as well as specific ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Offline Simulation Analysis (Table 1)

- **Consistency:** SBR demonstrated superior kinematic consistency, achieving the highest Motion Preservation score (0.776) and an order-of-magnitude reduction in Flatness (7.17e-07) compared to baselines. This indicates effective elimination of high-frequency control jitter.
- **Accuracy:** SBR exhibited the highest Cosine Similarity (0.9969), suggesting superior preservation of geometric intent.
- **Efficiency:** SBR maintained competitive latency (3.664 ms). Its Workspace Utilization was 21.0%, lower than GeoRT (35.2%) but comparable to DexPilot (26.1%) and Hybrid (22.5%).

#### 4.2. Real-World User Study Results

The real-world user study involved 18 participants performing three dexterous manipulation tasks with the mimic P0.50 hand. Mixed Effects Models and Logistic Generalised Estimating Equations were used for statistical analysis to account for operator variability and learning effects.

- **Task Reliability (Success Rate):**
 - SBR achieved the highest overall estimated marginal mean success rate of 54.1%. This was significantly higher than DexPilot (44.0%, p = 0.042) and GeoRT (26.6%, p < 0.001), and statistically comparable to Hybrid (52.1%, p = 0.79).
 - The advantage was most pronounced in Task A (Card Pickup), where SBR yielded a 70.9% success rate, compared to DexPilot's 55.0% (p = 0.014) and GeoRT's 33.3% (p < 0.001).
 - SBR also significantly outperformed GeoRT in Task B (Vertical Cube Rotation) with 58.6% vs. 37.2% (p = 0.013).

- **Cognitive Usability (NASA-TLX Workload):**
 - SBR recorded the lowest overall cognitive workload score of 36.4 (p < 0.001). This represented a reduction compared to DexPilot (44.0), GeoRT (56.4, p < 0.001), and Hybrid (38.4, p < 0.001).
 - SBR consistently showed lower workload scores across all tasks, with its lowest on Task A (29.2 points).

- **Completion Time:**
 - SBR had a slightly higher overall completion time of 29.7 seconds compared to DexPilot (23.3 s, p = 0.014) and Hybrid (21.6 s, p = 0.001). However, it was faster than GeoRT (33.3 s, p < 0.001).
 - This increased completion time for SBR was primarily localized to Task B (Vertical Cube Rotation), where it averaged 61.9 seconds against DexPilot’s 39.1 seconds (p = 0.001).

- **Qualitative Findings:** Operators consistently described SBR as smooth, responsive, and predictable, contrasting with the erratic jitter observed in gradient-based baselines like Hybrid. This perceived stability was identified as a key factor in operator preference.

#### 4.3. Gradient vs. Sampling-Based Optimization Ablation

- **Jitter Reduction:** Using the same core loss function, the sampling-based approach achieved a 36.4% reduction in joint jitter while maintaining comparable tracking losses to gradient-based baselines (RMSProp, SLSQP).
- **Hessian Eigen-Direction Analysis:** This analysis indicated that gradient-based methods exhibit erratic behavior and fail to converge consistently within the "loss-flat bands" (lower-ranked eigen-directions) of the loss landscape. Sampling-based methods, in contrast, navigated these regions stably.
- **Velocity Regularization Trade-offs:** While explicit velocity regularization in gradient-based methods could reduce jitter by a factor of 7.18, this came at the cost of increasing the mean tracking loss by 0.0232. SBR achieved low-jitter tracking natively without such penalties.

#### 4.4. Sim-to-Real Metric Correlation Analysis (Figure 17)

- **Cosine Similarity:** Showed a statistically significant positive correlation with real-world success rates (r = 0.28) and a moderate negative correlation with overall cognitive workload (r = -0.31).
- **Scale Ratio:** Exhibited a near-moderate inverse correlation with success rates (r = -0.28) and a direct correlation with overall workload (r = 0.28), suggesting that a slightly lower scale ratio might be beneficial.
- **Flatness:** Presented a modest positive association with overall workload (r = 0.25).
- **Motion Preservation:** No statistically significant correlation was found with real-world success rates, completion times, or cognitive workload.
- **Workspace Utilization:** Displayed an unexpected medium-sized inverse relationship with task success (r = -0.30) and a near large-sized direct effect with overall workload (r = 0.46), suggesting that higher simulation workspace utilization did not correlate with better real-world performance for the evaluated methods.
- None of the simulation metrics showed a statistically significant correlation with real-world completion times.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a novel approach to real-time kinematic hand retargeting and establishing a more robust evaluation framework.

- **Advancement in Retargeting Algorithms:** The Sampling-Based Retargeter (SBR) represents a significant development as a gradient-free method for real-time human hand teleoperation. By inherently generating smooth, low-jitter commands, SBR addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional gradient-based retargeting algorithms, which often suffer from erratic movements due to local minima. This improved control fidelity has direct implications for the stability of dexterous grasps and manipulation tasks.

- **Enhanced Teleoperation Experience and Data Quality:** The reduction in jitter and operator cognitive load (as evidenced by NASA-TLX scores) suggests that SBR can provide a more intuitive and user-friendly teleoperation experience. This enhancement is crucial for applications requiring extended human-in-the-loop operation. Furthermore, the generation of smoother, more consistent robot trajectories is expected to lead to the collection of higher-quality demonstration data, which is essential for training advanced learning-based robotic manipulation models such as VLA and VAM.

- **Robust Benchmarking Methodology:** The paper proposes and utilizes a rigorous evaluation methodology for retargeting algorithms, incorporating a Balanced Latin Square experimental design and Mixed Effects Models. This approach systematically accounts for confounding factors like operator learning, fatigue, and inter-subject variability, and isolates retargeter fidelity by fixing the robot arm. This methodology provides a valuable framework for the robotics community to conduct more statistically sound evaluations of future retargeting research.

- **Practical Applicability:** Demonstrated superior performance in specific real-world tasks (e.g., card pickup) and consistently high user satisfaction across a diverse participant cohort indicates SBR's practical utility. This suggests that SBR can serve as a scalable foundation for collecting high-quality dexterous manipulation data for various robotic applications.

- **Theoretical Insights into Optimization:** The ablation study comparing gradient-based and sampling-based optimization methods, coupled with the Hessian eigen-direction analysis, provides theoretical insights into why sampling-based approaches offer superior consistency. It highlights that sampling methods can navigate complex, loss-flat regions of the optimization landscape more effectively than gradient-based methods, which tend to exhibit erratic behavior in such areas. This understanding can inform the design of future control and optimization algorithms in robotics.

- **Future Research Directions:** While SBR demonstrated improved performance in many aspects, the identified trade-off in completion time for complex tasks (like vertical cube rotation) indicates an area for future optimization. Additionally, the sim-to-real correlation analysis, while providing initial benchmarks, suggests that further research is needed to develop more predictive simulation metrics that accurately translate to real-world teleoperation performance.
