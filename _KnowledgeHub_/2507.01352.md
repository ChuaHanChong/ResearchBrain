# Skywork-Reward-V2: Scaling Preference Data Curation via Human-AI Synergy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at 2050 Research, Skywork AI, developed Skywork-Reward-V2, a series of open reward models trained on SynPref-40M, a 40 million-pair preference dataset curated via a two-stage human-AI synergistic pipeline. These models achieve state-of-the-art performance across seven major reward model benchmarks, with the 8B model surpassing a 70B model by a 14.8 average score margin and demonstrating enhanced instruction following and factuality capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing open reward models often perform inadequately on evaluation benchmarks and struggle to capture the full spectrum of nuanced human preferences.
- Current preference datasets are limited by narrow scope, synthetic labeling, and insufficient quality control, hindering the development of robust and generalizable reward models.
- Traditional single-benchmark evaluations for reward models frequently fail to correlate with real-world performance or downstream task effectiveness, leading to unreliable progress assessment.

## Method

- A two-stage ==human-AI synergistic== pipeline was introduced for curating SynPref-40M, a 40 million-pair preference dataset, combining human annotation quality with AI scalability.
- Stage 1 involved small-scale, iterative human-in-the-loop curation with adaptive retrieval and preference-aware labeling to create high-quality 'gold' and 'silver' datasets.
- Stage 2 scaled data curation automatically to millions of 'in-the-wild' preference pairs using consistency checks with trained reward models and LLM judges, including a 'preference correction' mechanism.

## Results

- Skywork-Reward-V2 models achieved state-of-the-art performance across seven major RM benchmarks, with the **8B** model (Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-**8B**-**40M**) obtaining an 88.6 average score, outperforming a **70B** model (INF-ORM-Llama3.1-**70B**) by 14.8 points.
- The models demonstrated superior performance in correctness (JudgeBench, RMB best-of-N accuracy), strong resistance to stylistic biases (RM-Bench), and advanced capabilities in instruction following and factuality (RewardBench v2).
- Training on just **1.8%** (**290K** samples) of the curated data surpassed the performance of previous state-of-the-art **70B** open RMs, and including 'corrected' preference pairs yielded additional performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Data quality and rigorous curation are more critical for robust reward model performance than sheer data quantity or larger model architectures.
> - A synergistic human-AI approach effectively scales preference data curation, combining human judgment for quality control with AI for broad coverage and efficiency.
> - Identifying and 'correcting' suboptimal preferences by flipping labels of originally chosen/rejected pairs significantly enhances reward model performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reward Models (RMs) are integral components in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) pipelines, which are a standard approach for post-training Large Language Models (LLMs). While advancements in LLM reasoning capabilities and verifiable rewards have driven interest in rule-based reward functions for specific tasks like math or coding, these approaches often fall short in capturing the complexity, nuance, and occasional conflicting nature of general human preferences. Consequently, effective modeling of sophisticated human preferences remains a considerable challenge.

Previous efforts to model human preferences have involved curating diverse datasets, utilizing either automatic methods or human annotators to generate preference pairs for pairwise contrastive learning. Beyond data construction, some research has focused on improving reward modeling through enhanced loss functions or modified model architectures. Evaluation of RMs has traditionally relied on benchmarks such as RewardBench. However, multiple studies have indicated that scores on RewardBench may not consistently correlate with downstream task performance (e.g., best-of-N selection or policy training), and in some instances, may even show inverse relationships. This suggests that improvements on a single benchmark do not reliably indicate generalizable progress in reward modeling. The current work addresses these limitations by focusing on the underlying quality and scale of preference datasets, proposing that deficiencies in data—rather than solely model architecture or training techniques—are a primary impediment to developing robust open reward models capable of capturing a broad spectrum of human preferences. The paper positions itself as advancing open reward models by addressing fundamental data limitations through a novel human-AI synergistic curation pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance both the quality and quantity of preference data to improve the development of open reward models. The authors identify several motivations for this focus:

1. **Poor Performance of Current Open Reward Models:** Existing state-of-the-art open RMs are noted to perform inadequately on many evaluation benchmarks. They frequently fail to capture the spectrum of nuanced and sophisticated human preferences, indicating a gap between current RM capabilities and the complexity of human judgment.
2. **Limitations of Existing Preference Datasets:** The authors hypothesize that the performance brittleness of RMs stems predominantly from limitations in current preference datasets. These datasets are often characterized as narrowly scoped, synthetically labeled, or lacking rigorous quality control. Such issues hinder the RMs' ability to learn robust and generalizable human preferences.
3. **Ineffectiveness of Advanced Training Techniques:** The paper observes that even approaches incorporating advanced training techniques have not consistently yielded significant performance improvements in RMs. This suggests that improvements beyond current performance plateaus may necessitate addressing fundamental data-level issues rather than solely focusing on algorithmic or architectural modifications.
4. **Inconsistent Evaluation Metrics:** The researchers highlight the limitations of single-benchmark evaluations, such as RewardBench, which may not accurately reflect real-world performance or correlate with downstream task effectiveness. This necessitates a more comprehensive evaluation suite and, consequently, a more robust and versatile reward model.
5. **Scaling Data Curation:** The need for large-scale preference data is acknowledged, but manual human annotation is costly and inefficient. The motivation is to devise a scalable data curation pipeline that can leverage the strengths of both human judgment (for quality) and AI (for scalability) to overcome this bottleneck.

To address these challenges, the work aims to create a large-scale, high-quality preference dataset and subsequently train a suite of reward models that are versatile across various capabilities, including alignment with human preferences, objective correctness, safety, resistance to stylistic biases, and best-of-N scaling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces SynPref-40M, a large-scale preference dataset comprising 40 million preference pairs, and details a two-stage human-AI synergistic pipeline for its curation. The pipeline is designed to leverage human annotation quality and AI scalability.

#### 3.1. Pipeline Overview

The pipeline operates in two stages. Stage 1 focuses on small-scale, iterative human-in-the-loop curation, where humans provide verified annotations and LLMs assist in guided automatic curation. Stage 2 scales data curation automatically, guided by the reward models trained in Stage 1, with consistency checks eliminating the need for further human supervision.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Small-Scale Human-in-the-Loop Curation

This iterative procedure, comprising eight iterations, aims to collect and refine a high-quality "gold" dataset (D_gold) and a larger "silver" dataset (D_silver).

- **Seed Preference Data Initialization:** The process begins by collecting publicly available preference data to form an unverified pool (D_un) from over 40 diverse sources. For each preference pair (conversation `x`, winning response `y_w`, losing response `y_l`), LLM-generated preference attributes are collected. These attributes consist of a 5-tuple: task category, preference objectivity, controversiality, desired attributes, and annotation guideline, serving as metadata for diversity and instance-specific criteria.
- **Human Verification and Annotation Protocol:** A small, high-quality, and diverse set of preference pairs (e.g., Skywork-Reward-Preference-80K-v0.2) initializes the seed data. Human annotators perform strict verification following a predefined protocol. This protocol allows the use of external tools (search engines, frontier LLM assistants, domain-specialized LLMs) for fact-checking or code verification, but explicitly prohibits full reliance on LLMs for final judgment. Annotators are responsible for the final judgment based on attributes and guidelines. Preference pairs produced and verified by humans contribute to D_gold, while LLM-verified portions contribute to D_silver. Preference attributes in D_gold are also human-edited and verified.
- **Reward Model Training and Evaluation:** A pointwise Bradley-Terry reward model is initialized and trained on D_silver. The best checkpoint is selected based on validation accuracy on D_gold, serving as the "best reward model" for subsequent steps.
- **Error-Driven Adaptive Preference Retrieval:** To identify and address weaknesses in the current RM, an adaptive retrieval mechanism is employed. The RM is evaluated on D_gold to pinpoint misclassified pairs. New samples are then retrieved from D_un that are similar to these misclassified or low-confidence pairs (based on embeddings of `x` and attributes `a`). The retrieval prioritizes instances where the RM performs poorly or shows low confidence.
- **Preference-Aware Labeling:** For the pairs selected via adaptive retrieval, similar human-labeled examples from D_gold are retrieved and used as few-shot examples to guide LLM annotators. An ensemble of strong LLMs aggregates final judgments, first via intra-model self-consistency and then across models to mitigate bias. LLM-labeled samples are added to D_silver, and human-labeled ones to D_gold. This iterative process refines both the data and the reward model.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Large-Scale Automatic Curation of Preference Data in the Wild

This stage scales data curation to tens of millions of "in-the-wild" preference data pairs (D_wild) without further human annotation.

- **Preference Consistency with the Best Reward Model:** Preference pairs from D_wild that the current best reward model (from Stage 1) identifies with high confidence (prediction `p > 0.5`) are directly retained without re-annotation. For the remaining pairs where a mismatch is detected (i.e., the RM's prediction does not agree with the original label), LLM annotation is used to make the final judgment. This LLM annotation follows the same preference-aware labeling procedure as in Stage 1, utilizing few-shot examples from D_gold.
- **Preference Consistency with the Gold Reward Model:** A separate "gold reward model" is trained using all cumulative human-verified samples from Stage 1 to approximate the true human preference distribution. From the unverified pool, only those pairs whose original chosen-rejected labels are consistent with *both* the gold reward model and *either* the LLM judges *or* the Stage 1 best reward model are retained. Approximately 5 million preference pairs pass this consistency mechanism. The discarded data, where preferences contradict the gold RM, can optionally be "recycled" by flipping their chosen-rejected labels, leveraging these identified "incorrect" preferences for training.

#### 3.4. Reward Model Training

The Skywork-Reward-V2 series comprises eight reward models ranging from 0.6B to 8B parameters, using Llama 3.1, Llama 3.2, and Qwen3 series as backbones. Models are trained exclusively on a 26 million curated subset from SynPref-40M using the pointwise Bradley-Terry objective. Training uses a maximum context length of 16K tokens, with specific learning rates and batch sizes (linear decay for early stages, constant learning rate and large batch size for final runs). An experimental release, Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B-40M, is trained on 26 million curated pairs plus additional flipped (human-agreeing) pairs from the discarded 14 million pairs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents a comprehensive evaluation of the Skywork-Reward-V2 series across seven major reward model benchmarks, along with ablation studies on data quantity, quality, and annotation methods.

#### 4.1. Comprehensive Evaluation of Skywork-Reward-V2 Series

- **General Preferences:** The Skywork-Reward-V2 models demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across all seven benchmarks (RewardBench, RewardBench v2, PPE Preference, PPE Correctness, RMB, RM-Bench, JudgeBench). They outperform larger models (e.g., 70B parameters) and emerging generative reward models. The 8B reward model, Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B-40M, achieved the highest average score (88.6), significantly surpassing the INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (73.8). Even the 1.7B variant of Skywork-Reward-V2 was observed to outperform a 70B model on most benchmarks, suggesting the importance of data quality over sheer model size.
- **Correctness Preferences:** On JudgeBench, Skywork-Reward-V2 models, particularly Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B, show strong performance on knowledge tasks and competitive performance on math, outperforming several state-of-the-art LLM-as-a-Judge and reasoning models in specific categories. For best-of-N (BoN) accuracy, across helpfulness and harmlessness splits in RMB, all eight RMs outperformed GPT-4o, with the top variant achieving a 20-point margin. BoN scaling curves on PPE Correctness further show superior and consistently improving performance for Skywork-Reward-V2 models as N increases.
- **Resistance Against Style Biases:** Evaluation on RM-Bench, which assesses robustness to stylistic differences, indicates that Skywork-Reward-V2 models maintain higher and more consistent performance across varying difficulty levels (Easy, Normal, Hard) compared to baseline models. This suggests that training on SynPref-40M leads to more debiased representations of preferences.
- **Superiority in Advanced Capabilities:** On RewardBench v2, Skywork-Reward-V2 models demonstrated advanced capabilities, particularly in precise instruction following (where most existing RMs scored below 50). Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B and its 40M variant outperformed strong proprietary models and generative reward models in this category, as well as showing a significant increase in Factuality scores.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on Data Quantity and Quality

- **Impact of Uncurated vs. Curated Data:** Increasing the amount of uncurated data resulted in minimal performance gains. In contrast, training with curated data (from both Stage 1 and Stage 2) showed consistent and significant performance improvements.
- **Quality Over Quantity:** Training on a small fraction (1.8%, approximately 290K samples) of the 16 million-pair curated mixture was sufficient to surpass the performance of previous state-of-the-art 70B open RMs, highlighting the importance of data quality.
- **Preference "Correction":** The curation process not only selected high-quality data but also identified "incorrect" preferences (where original chosen/rejected labels were suboptimal). By flipping the labels of this discarded data and including it in training, further consistent performance gains were achieved.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Annotation Method

Controlled experiments on Stage 1 components revealed:

- **Limited Impact of Simple LLM Curation:** Pure LLM curation provided only marginal improvements (0.1 points) over baseline models, aligning with observations that scaling uncurated synthetic data yields negligible gains.
- **Crucial Role of Human Curation:** Human curation delivered the largest performance improvement (2.3 points over the seed RM baseline), underscoring the necessity of human involvement for data quality.
- **Adaptive Retrieval's Contribution:** Incorporating error-driven adaptive retrieval to guide LLM annotation with human-curated examples yielded a 0.9-point gain, indicating its effectiveness in enhancing LLM curation quality with minimal overhead.
- **Value of Annotation Protocols:** Human annotation quality increased progressively when provided with LLM-generated preference attributes (1.1-point gain) and further with the full annotation protocol (including external tools, 3.2-point gain), validating the rigor of the human curation process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a substantial contribution to the field of large language model alignment and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).

1. **Advancement of Open Reward Models:** The Skywork-Reward-V2 series establishes a new state of the art for open reward models across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating that smaller models (e.g., 8B parameters) can outperform significantly larger ones (e.g., 70B parameters) when trained on high-quality, comprehensively curated data. This can potentially lower computational resource requirements for achieving advanced RLHF capabilities.
2. **Redefining Preference Data Curation:** The introduction of SynPref-40M and its two-stage human-AI synergistic pipeline offers a methodological framework for scaling preference data curation while maintaining stringent quality control. This pipeline effectively combines the quality assurance of human annotation with the scalability of AI, addressing a key bottleneck in RLHF research.
3. **Emphasis on Data Quality:** The extensive ablation studies empirically validate the hypothesis that data quality is as, if not more, critical than sheer data quantity or sophisticated model architectures for robust reward model performance. The finding that a small percentage of highly curated data can surpass previous state-of-the-art models highlights the "untapped potential" within existing preference datasets.
4. **Robust and Versatile AI Alignment:** The demonstrated versatility of Skywork-Reward-V2 across diverse capabilities—including general preferences, objective correctness, resistance to stylistic biases, safety, and best-of-N scaling—suggests a pathway toward more robust and aligned AI systems. These models are less susceptible to superficial cues and better capture the underlying substance of human preferences.
5. **Practical Guidance for Resource Allocation:** The practical guidance for cost-performance budgeting, derived from the scaling results, provides a framework for practitioners to systematically plan data curation efforts, balancing human verification costs with automated scaling benefits.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including the exploration of personalized reward models to capture diverse human preferences, a deeper investigation into the nature and utility of "discarded" or "flipped" preference data, and the potential for fully automatic, high-quality data labeling using future generations of frontier LLMs with advanced reasoning and agentic capabilities.
