# Skywork-Reward-V2: Scaling Preference Data Curation via Human-AI Synergy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at 2050 Research, Skywork AI, developed Skywork-Reward-V2, a series of open reward models trained on SynPref-40M, a 40 million-pair preference dataset curated via a two-stage human-AI synergistic pipeline. These models achieve state-of-the-art performance across seven major reward model benchmarks, with the 8B model surpassing a 70B model by a 14.8 average score margin and demonstrating enhanced instruction following and factuality capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing open reward models often perform inadequately on evaluation benchmarks and struggle to capture the full spectrum of nuanced human preferences.
- Current preference datasets are limited by narrow scope, synthetic labeling, and insufficient quality control, hindering the development of robust and generalizable reward models.
- Traditional single-benchmark evaluations for reward models frequently fail to correlate with real-world performance or downstream task effectiveness, leading to unreliable progress assessment.

## Method

- A two-stage ==human-AI synergistic== pipeline was introduced for curating SynPref-40M, a 40 million-pair preference dataset, combining human annotation quality with AI scalability.
- Stage 1 involved small-scale, iterative human-in-the-loop curation with adaptive retrieval and preference-aware labeling to create high-quality 'gold' and 'silver' datasets.
- Stage 2 scaled data curation automatically to millions of 'in-the-wild' preference pairs using consistency checks with trained reward models and LLM judges, including a 'preference correction' mechanism.

## Results

- Skywork-Reward-V2 models achieved state-of-the-art performance across seven major RM benchmarks, with the **8B** model (Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-**8B**-**40M**) obtaining an 88.6 average score, outperforming a **70B** model (INF-ORM-Llama3.1-**70B**) by 14.8 points.
- The models demonstrated superior performance in correctness (JudgeBench, RMB best-of-N accuracy), strong resistance to stylistic biases (RM-Bench), and advanced capabilities in instruction following and factuality (RewardBench v2).
- Training on just **1.8%** (**290K** samples) of the curated data surpassed the performance of previous state-of-the-art **70B** open RMs, and including 'corrected' preference pairs yielded additional performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Data quality and rigorous curation are more critical for robust reward model performance than sheer data quantity or larger model architectures.
> - A synergistic human-AI approach effectively scales preference data curation, combining human judgment for quality control with AI for broad coverage and efficiency.
> - Identifying and 'correcting' suboptimal preferences by flipping labels of originally chosen/rejected pairs significantly enhances reward model performance.
