# CMP: Robust Whole-Body Tracking for Loco-Manipulation via Competence Manifold Projection

> [!summary] Summary
> Competence Manifold Projection (CMP) enhances robust whole-body tracking for legged mobile manipulators by projecting arbitrary task-space inputs onto a learned safety manifold. This method improved survival rates in out-of-distribution scenarios by up to 10-fold in simulation and significantly in real-world tests, enabling 'best-effort' task execution instead of failures while maintaining in-distribution tracking performance.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body control policies for legged mobile manipulators are fragile to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) inputs, leading to unpredictable or physically unsafe behaviors.
- Real-time enforcement of infinite-horizon safety is computationally intractable due to the temporal-spatial complexity and fragmented nature of the safe command subspace.
- Traditional safety solutions often disrupt task continuity (e.g., emergency stops) or fail to accurately distinguish unmastered/unsafe behaviors from inputs that are simply outside the training distribution.

## Method

![[2604.07457-method.png]]
*CMP pipeline: target trajectories in the TCP frame are encoded by an Intent Encoder into a raw latent intention for the Low-level Policy, while a TD-trained Safety Estimator and Isomorphic Latent Space enable O(1) safety enforcement by truncating latents beyond the safety radius.*

- The **CMP** (Competence Manifold Projection) framework projects arbitrary task-space inputs onto a ==learned safety manifold== using a ==hierarchical latent space== approach for whole-body control.
- A ==Lower-Bounded Safety Estimator==, trained via ==Temporal Difference learning==, approximates the ==Maximum Probability of Perpetual Safety== to distinguish truly unmastered intentions from benign OOD inputs.
- An ==Isomorphic Latent Space (ILS)== aligns safety levels with the geometry of the latent space, enabling real-time, ==O(1) projection== of potentially unsafe commands onto the closest safe hypersphere.

## Results

- In simulation, CMP achieved up to a **10-fold** increase in survival rate for OOD-Geometry tasks (**46.9%** vs. **4.7%**) and nearly a **6-fold** increase for OOD-Sensor noise (**40.3%** vs. **6.9%**), while maintaining similar in-distribution tracking accuracy.
- Real-world experiments showed CMP achieved a **100%** survival rate for in-distribution tasks and significantly higher rates for moderate (**93.3%**) and extreme (**86.7%**) out-of-distribution scenarios compared to baselines.
- The framework operates with low latency (**2.99 ms**), comparable to an unshielded baseline, enabling "best-effort" task continuation by smoothly projecting unsafe commands to the closest feasible intention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Infinite-horizon safety for dynamic robotic systems can be analytically reduced to a frame-wise, single-step latent inclusion problem using a hierarchical latent framework and a 'Maximum Probability of Perpetual Safety' metric.
> - Training a safety estimator with a lower-bounded target via Temporal Difference learning is crucial for accurately distinguishing between truly unsafe behaviors and inputs merely outside the training distribution, thereby improving robustness.
> - An isomorphic mapping between safety probability and latent space geometry enables efficient, O(1) online projection of unsafe commands to a 'best-effort' safe alternative, preserving task continuity.
