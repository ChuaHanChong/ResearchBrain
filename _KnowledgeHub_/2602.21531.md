# LiLo-VLA: Compositional Long-Horizon Manipulation via Linked Object-Centric Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNC, Georgia Tech, and CMU developed LiLo-VLA, a modular framework that combines classical motion planning with object-centric Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for long-horizon robotic manipulation. The system achieved an average success rate of 69% on a new 21-task simulation benchmark and 85% in real-world experiments, demonstrating robust compositional generalization and effective failure recovery.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with compositional generalization, requiring extensive task-specific demonstrations for novel long-horizon task sequences.
- End-to-end VLA policies are brittle and prone to cascading failures due to sensitivity to minor environmental variations and overfitting to specific visual features.
- Extending current VLA paradigms to complex, multi-stage tasks in unstructured environments faces limitations in scalability and robustness.

## Method

- LiLo-VLA employs a modular framework that decouples long-horizon tasks into a Reaching Module for global transport and an Interaction Module for local object-centric manipulation.
- The Reaching Module utilizes classical motion planning to navigate the end-effector to an approach pose relative to the target object, incorporating state perturbation during training for robustness.
- The Interaction Module uses an object-centric VLA policy with egocentric wrist-mounted camera views, enhanced by visual clutter augmentation (masking and mask-aware data augmentation), and integrated with a closed-loop recovery mechanism for failures.

## Results

- LiLo-VLA achieved an average success rate of 69% and average progress of 86% across 21 simulation tasks, vastly outperforming monolithic VLA policies like Pi0.5 (28% SR) and OpenVLA-OFT (2% SR).
- The framework demonstrated strong compositional generalization, maintaining an 85% success rate on novel, permuted task sequences in simulation, where baselines collapsed to 0% SR.
- In real-world experiments on a physical robot, LiLo-VLA achieved an 85% average success rate across 8 tasks, showing robust generalization to diverse layouts and permuted skill orders, including 8-step ultra-long sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling global transport from local interaction through a modular architecture is crucial for achieving compositional generalization and scalability in long-horizon manipulation tasks.
> - Object-centric visual policies, relying on egocentric camera views and augmented with masking strategies, significantly enhance robustness to environmental clutter and observation space shifts.
> - A robust closed-loop recovery mechanism, particularly one that distinguishes between skills and intelligently backtracks upon object loss, is fundamental for preventing cascading failures in multi-step robotic operations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "LiLo-VLA" positions itself at the intersection of general-purpose robotics, long-horizon manipulation, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The overarching goal in robotics is to enable systems to perform complex, multi-stage tasks in unstructured environments, moving beyond single, atomic skills.

Historically, solving long-horizon tasks has been approached through **Task and Motion Planning (TAMP)**. Classical TAMP methods offer strong guarantees for symbolic planning and geometric feasibility but often require accurate state estimation and known object models, limiting their applicability in dynamic, real-world settings. More recent advancements in hierarchical learning have integrated learned models for low-level motions, and **Large Language Models (LLMs)** have been leveraged for abstract task decomposition, improving high-level semantic planning. However, these LLM-based approaches often assume the existence of robust low-level execution skills, which is a non-trivial challenge.

In parallel, **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** like RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, Pi0, and Pi0.5 have emerged as a dominant paradigm in robot learning. These models, often leveraging large-scale pre-training and Transformer architectures, demonstrate impressive capabilities in diverse atomic skill execution and semantic reasoning by unifying perception and control. They represent a significant step towards generalist policies for short-horizon manipulation tasks.

However, the authors highlight two fundamental limitations when extending current VLA paradigms to *long-horizon* tasks:
1. **Lack of Compositional Generalization:** Existing VLAs struggle to adapt to novel task sequences not explicitly seen during training. They tend to overfit to specific demonstrated trajectories rather than flexibly recombining learned atomic skills, requiring extensive task-specific demonstrations for new sequences.
2. **Prone to Cascading Failures:** VLAs are often brittle to minor environmental variations because they can overfit to specific visual features or spatial configurations. A single failure at any stage of a long-horizon task can lead to subsequent errors, cascading throughout the sequence and jeopardizing the entire mission. While VLM-based recovery methods exist, they typically assume robust underlying skills and may fail if the skill policy itself is sensitive to environmental changes.

LiLo-VLA directly addresses these limitations by proposing a modular framework that synergizes the strengths of classical motion planning and modern VLA policies. It builds upon ideas from modular skill learning and integrated motion planning with learned policies but aims to overcome their shortcomings, such as ad-hoc integration or data inefficiency. Specifically, by decoupling global transport from local interaction and focusing on object-centric VLA policies, LiLo-VLA seeks to provide a more robust and scalable solution for long-horizon manipulation than prior end-to-end VLA approaches like Long-VLA, which can be data-inefficient due to coupling. The paper also contributes to the ongoing effort to create more robust robot learning systems that can handle real-world complexities, including visual clutter and dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of LiLo-VLA is to enable general-purpose robots to master complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in unstructured environments. This ambitious goal is motivated by the recognized limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models when applied to multi-stage activities.

The authors identify two critical challenges that current VLA paradigms face in long-horizon scenarios:
1. **Combinatorial Complexity and Lack of Compositional Generalization:** Standard VLA models, while proficient at diverse atomic skills, struggle with the sheer number of possible sequences of these skills. They often require training on vast amounts of task-specific demonstrations for each novel sequence, making zero-shot generalization to unseen compositions extremely difficult. This combinatorial explosion limits their scalability to truly open-ended, long-horizon tasks.
2. **Cascading Failures and Environmental Sensitivity:** End-to-end VLA policies are prone to overfitting to specific visual features or spatial configurations in their training data. This sensitivity makes them brittle, meaning minor variations in the environment, irrelevant visual clutter, or slight deviations in execution can lead to failures that then cascade, undermining the entire long-horizon task. Existing recovery mechanisms often assume the underlying skills are robust, failing to address the root cause of policy brittleness.

To overcome these significant hurdles, LiLo-VLA is designed with the following key objectives:
- **Achieve Zero-Shot Compositional Generalization:** The system should be able to perform novel long-horizon tasks by flexibly recombining learned atomic skills without requiring explicit training or demonstrations for those specific task sequences.
- **Ensure Robustness to Environmental Variations:** The manipulation policy should be invariant to global workspace layouts, background clutter, and task-irrelevant visual features. This requires the policy to focus strictly on the objects of interest.
- **Enable Robust Failure Recovery:** The system must be able to detect and recover from execution failures effectively, preventing cascading errors. This recovery mechanism should go beyond simple retries and address situations where the policy's execution context has shifted.
- **Improve Data Efficiency:** By modularizing the problem, the system aims to reduce the data requirements compared to end-to-end approaches that attempt to learn global transport and local interaction simultaneously.

In essence, the motivation behind LiLo-VLA is to bridge the gap between the impressive atomic skill capabilities of modern VLAs and the demands of real-world, multi-stage robotic operations, ultimately pushing towards more capable and autonomous general-purpose robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LiLo-VLA proposes a modular framework designed to synergize classical motion planning with modern VLA policies, decoupling the execution of long-horizon manipulation tasks into distinct phases.

**A. Overview of LiLo-VLA Architecture**
The framework processes a sequence of symbolic actions, $T = \{a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_N\}$, where each action $a_i$ is a parameterized predicate involving a primitive skill category (e.g., Pick, Place) and a reference object. The core idea is a two-phase process for each atomic skill:
1. **Reaching Module:** Handles global transport of the end-effector to an *approach pose* relative to the target object.
2. **Interaction Module:** Executes the contact-rich manipulation using an object-centric VLA policy.

This modular design decouples collision-free global transport from fine-grained local interaction, allowing each module to focus on its specialized task.

**B. The Reaching Module: Global Transport**
This module's role is to safely navigate the robot's end-effector from its current position to the vicinity of the target object, preparing for the interaction phase.
- **Relative Goal Generation with Perturbation:** The module determines a robust *Approach Pose* ($T_{approach}$) relative to the target object's frame. This pose incorporates a skill-specific offset to ensure a consistent starting point for the VLA. To make the downstream VLA policy robust to real-world noise (e.g., perception errors, imperfect motion planning), a perturbation strategy is applied during demonstration generation. Training trajectories are initialized from *perturbed start poses* ($T_{init}$) sampled around the canonical $T_{approach}$, allowing the VLA to learn to compensate for deviations.
- **Collision-Free Motion Planning:** During deployment and data generation, MPLib, a classical motion planner, is used to generate collision-free trajectories from the robot's current configuration to the determined target pose ($T_{approach}$ or $T_{init}$). This ensures safe global movement without requiring learned motion planning.

**C. The Interaction Module: Object-Centric VLA**
Once the Reaching Module brings the robot close to the object, the Interaction Module takes over for fine-grained manipulation. Its design emphasizes object-centricity for robustness.
- **Object-Centric Observation Space:** The policy relies *exclusively* on egocentric visual inputs from a wrist-mounted camera. This deliberate choice mitigates the "observation space shift" (OSS) problem, where task-irrelevant visual changes from static third-person views degrade performance. The wrist view provides a consistent perspective relative to the workspace during interaction, helping the robot focus on relevant objects.
- **Visual Clutter Augmentation:** To counter policy overfitting to environmental distractors, a two-pronged approach is used:
 - **Deployment Masking:** A heuristic masking strategy covers non-target objects with black rectangles derived from their bounding boxes.
 - **Mask-Aware Data Augmentation:** During training, a random erasing augmentation is applied *only* to background regions (pixels not belonging to the gripper, reference object, or grasped object). This simulates the visual artifacts of deployment masking, ensuring the policy is robust to these changes and stays within its learned distribution.

**D. Compositional Execution and Failure Recovery**
The modules are integrated into a closed-loop execution pipeline:
- **Sequential Execution Pipeline:** For each skill $a_i$ in the plan:
 1. The 6D pose of the target object is estimated.
 2. The Reaching Module plans and executes a collision-free trajectory to the approach pose ($T_{approach}$).
 3. The Interaction Module performs the manipulation using the VLA policy.
 4. A geometric verification function $V(a_i)$ determines if the skill's effect has been achieved.
- **Closed-Loop Recovery Mechanism:** This is a crucial aspect for robustness in long-horizon tasks.
 - If a failure is detected for a skill *not involving holding an object* (e.g., a Pick attempt), the system triggers a *local retry*. It re-estimates the object pose and resets the end-effector to $T_{approach}$, allowing the VLA to retry with an updated state.
 - If a failure occurs during a skill that *requires object retention* (e.g., a Place operation), the system conservatively assumes the object has been lost. It *backtracks* the skill index to the most recent Pick skill, ensuring the robot re-acquires the object before attempting the subsequent transport and placement.

**E. Experimental Setup**
- **Benchmarks:** A novel 21-task simulation benchmark is introduced, consisting of:
 - **LIBERO-Long++:** 6 tasks from LIBERO-Long augmented with randomized visual distractors and evaluated with permuted skill orders to test visual robustness and compositional generalization.
 - **Ultra-Long:** 3 custom tasks (Kitchen Organization, Cooking Preparation, Living Room Organization) chaining up to 16 atomic skills, pushing limits of temporal scalability and workspace saturation.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Multiple skill permutations (2-3 per scenario) are tested to rigorously assess zero-shot compositional generalization.
- **Baselines:** Pi0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT, state-of-the-art generalist policies. Baselines are trained on either continuous long-horizon demos (fixed order) or aggregate atomic skill datasets, and prompted sequentially for novel orders.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR) for completing the entire sequence in order, and Average Progress (AP) for the continuous sequence of correct actions.
- **Implementation Details:** Ground-truth poses and segmentation masks are used in simulation to isolate policy failures from perception errors. OpenVLA-OFT is the backbone for simulation, while Pi0.5 is used for real-world validation to demonstrate model-agnosticism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates LiLo-VLA's superior performance in compositional long-horizon manipulation across both simulation and real-world environments, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines.

**A. Simulation Experiments (Table I)**
- **Overall Performance:** LiLo-VLA achieves an average success rate (SR) of 69% and average progress (AP) of 86% across all 21 simulation tasks. This drastically outperforms Pi0.5 (28% SR, 31% AP) and OpenVLA-OFT (2% SR, 4% AP), highlighting the limitations of monolithic VLA policies for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **LIBERO-Long++ (Visual Clutter & Reordering):**
 - On "Original" sequences (seen during training in a fixed order), Pi0.5 performs well (83% SR), comparable to LiLo-VLA (78% SR).
 - However, on "Variant" sequences (novel, permuted skill orders), Pi0.5 collapses to 0% SR, indicating it overfits to training trajectories and fails to ground altered language instructions compositionally.
 - In contrast, LiLo-VLA maintains robust performance on "Variant" sequences (85% SR), demonstrating effective compositional generalization by isolating atomic skills.
- **Ultra-Long (Scalability):** For tasks with up to 16 steps, both Pi0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT achieve 0% SR. LiLo-VLA, however, maintains a 44% SR. This success is attributed to its modular design, which decouples global transport from local interaction, making the interaction policy invariant to evolving geometric configurations and spatial shifts that challenge end-to-end models.

**B. Ablation Studies (Table I & Figures 4, 5)**
- **Necessity and Robustness of the Reaching Module:**
 - Removing the Reaching Module entirely ("w/o Reaching") results in a 0% SR across all tasks, even with privileged ground-truth object poses. This confirms that an explicit motion planner is a structural prerequisite for bridging spatially distributed skills, as standard VLAs cannot implicitly learn complex long-horizon transport dynamics.
 - The "State Perturbation" strategy (Fig. 4) for training the interaction policy is crucial for robustness. A policy trained without perturbation degrades significantly under noisy initial poses, while the full LiLo-VLA maintains stable performance (no statistically significant drop), effectively bridging the domain gap between planner precision and policy generalization.
- **Visual Robustness via Object-Centric Design:**
 - Evaluation on the BOSS-C1 benchmark (Fig. 5) demonstrates that the "Wrist Only" camera configuration, which LiLo-VLA uses, achieves the highest success rate and the lowest performance degradation under Observation Space Shift (OSS). This confirms the inherent robustness of wrist-mounted, egocentric views to background clutter.
 - Removing the "Masking" strategy ("w/o Masking") leads to a significant decline in overall SR from 69% to 48%. This highlights the importance of mask-aware data augmentation and visual masking during deployment for enforcing object-centricity and preventing overfitting to spurious visual features.
- **Efficacy of Closed-Loop Recovery:**
 - Removing the closed-loop recovery mechanism ("w/o Recovery") causes a catastrophic performance drop, plummeting the overall SR from 69% to 8%. The failure is absolute (0% SR) in the Ultra-Long tasks. This underscores that recovery is not an optional optimization but a fundamental component for scaling to extended horizons, where cumulative errors make open-loop execution unfeasible.

**C. Real-World Experiments (Figure 6)**
- **System Implementation:** LiLo-VLA was deployed on a Franka Emika Panda robot with a Robotiq gripper, using a dual-camera setup (wrist-mounted ZED Mini for interaction, fixed ZED 2 for reaching). A modular perception stack (YOLOE for detection/segmentation, FoundationPose for 6D pose) was used, transitioning from oracle perception in simulation. Notably, a Pi0.5 backbone was used for real-world VLA, validating the framework's model-agnostic nature.
- **Generalization Performance:** Across 8 real-world tasks (4-8 steps), LiLo-VLA achieved an 85% average success rate.
 - **Standard Conditions:** 100% SR across all three tasks (Task 1, 2, 3), demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Diverse Layout (Variant 1):** Strong resilience to spatial shifts and visual distractors, maintaining 100% SR on Task 4 and 80% on Task 5.
 - **Change Sequence (Variant 2):** Robust performance even with permuted skill orders (Task 6, 7, 8), including the 8-step ultra-long Task 8, which achieved 60% SR with 85% average progress.
- **Qualitative Results (Figures S1, S2, S3):** Visualizations showcase the effectiveness of random masking for focusing the policy on target objects and the autonomous recovery process where the system re-estimates object pose and resets the arm after grasp failures.

In summary, the results rigorously demonstrate that LiLo-VLA effectively addresses the core challenges of compositional generalization, robustness to environmental variations, and failure recovery in long-horizon manipulation, providing a significant leap forward compared to current end-to-end VLA approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LiLo-VLA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Robust General-Purpose Robotics:** LiLo-VLA directly tackles the critical limitations of contemporary Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models—namely, their struggle with compositional generalization and susceptibility to cascading failures in multi-stage tasks. By overcoming these hurdles, the work brings robots closer to achieving general-purpose manipulation capabilities in unstructured, dynamic environments, a grand goal of the robotics community.

- **A Principled Modular Paradigm:** The framework offers a principled and elegant modular design that effectively synergizes the strengths of classical robotics (robust motion planning) with modern data-driven learning (VLA policies). This "best of both worlds" approach allows each component to excel at its specialized function—global transport for motion planners, and local, contact-rich interaction for VLA policies—without the common pitfalls of ad-hoc integration or data-inefficient end-to-end learning. This modularity is a critical conceptual contribution, providing a blueprint for future hybrid robot control systems.

- **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Variability:** LiLo-VLA significantly improves robustness in several key areas:
 - **Visual Clutter and Distractors:** Through its object-centric VLA design and mask-aware data augmentation, the system ensures the policy focuses strictly on task-relevant objects, making it invariant to irrelevant visual features and background noise.
 - **Spatial Variations:** The use of a Reaching Module and relative goal generation ensures the system can adapt to different workspace layouts and object placements without retraining the interaction policy.
 - **Execution Failures:** The closed-loop recovery mechanism, especially its intelligent backtracking for object-holding skills, provides an essential layer of resilience, preventing minor errors from escalating into complete task failures. This is crucial for long-horizon tasks where cumulative errors are inevitable.

- **Scalability to Extreme Horizons:** The introduction of the "Ultra-Long" benchmark, featuring tasks up to 16 atomic skills, and successful real-world execution of 8-step tasks, demonstrates LiLo-VLA's unprecedented ability to scale to much longer horizons than previously feasible with end-to-end VLA models. This opens doors for robots to perform more intricate and prolonged tasks.

- **Improved Data Efficiency:** By decoupling the learning problem into mastering independent, object-centric atomic skills, LiLo-VLA dramatically reduces the data requirements for compositional generalization. Instead of needing to observe combinatorially complex long-horizon trajectories, the system only needs sufficient data for each unique atomic skill, making the approach far more practical for real-world deployment where data collection is expensive.

- **New Benchmarks for Future Research:** The creation of the LIBERO-Long++ and Ultra-Long benchmarks provides a rigorous, challenging testbed for evaluating future long-horizon manipulation systems. These benchmarks, with their emphasis on visual robustness, temporal scalability, and zero-shot compositional generalization, will undoubtedly drive further innovation in the field.

- **Real-World Validation and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful deployment and robust performance of LiLo-VLA on a physical robot system, using diverse real-world layouts and permuted skill sequences, validate its practical applicability and effective sim-to-real transfer. This empirical evidence strengthens its credibility and impact.

**Potential Future Impact:** LiLo-VLA's contribution lays a strong foundation for developing truly autonomous robots capable of performing complex, multi-step tasks in dynamic, unpredictable environments. This could impact various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics and household automation, where robots need to intelligently interact with multiple objects over extended periods.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** While significant, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as reliance on external perception models (e.g., for transparent or occluded objects) and performance being bounded by the underlying VLA backbone. These point to future research avenues, including active perception strategies that allow robots to autonomously seek optimal viewpoints, further enhancing the system's robustness and autonomy.

In conclusion, LiLo-VLA represents a critical step towards overcoming key bottlenecks in robotic manipulation, offering a robust, scalable, and data-efficient framework that promises to accelerate the development of more capable and intelligent general-purpose robotic systems.
