# Imitation Learning Based on Disentangled Representation Learning of Behavioral Characteristics

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a robot motion generation model that enables online, fine-grained adjustment of continuous robot actions in response to qualitative human modifier directives. It achieves this by combining disentangled representation learning, which maps directives to specific latent variables, with an action chunking mechanism for stable real-time execution, demonstrating successful adaptation for temporal and physical modifiers in a wiping task.

## Problem

- Translating qualitative human instructions (e.g., "wipe strongly") into continuous robot control parameters for online, fine-grained motion modulation.
- The inability of traditional imitation learning and disentangled representation learning methods to adapt robot behaviors dynamically during task execution.
- Achieving smooth and stable transitions in robot motion when modifier directives are changed in real-time.

## Method

- A ==Conditional Variational AutoEncoder (CVAE)== is trained offline to learn the distribution of action sequences, with its latent space structured using ==weak supervision== from human-assigned modifier labels.
- The latent variable is partitioned into constrained variables, each aligned to a specific modifier directive via a ==classification loss==, and unconstrained variables.
- An online inference mechanism uses commanded latent variables and ==action chunking== with ==weighted averaging== to generate smooth, continuously adjustable robot motions based on real-time modifier directives.

## Results

- The proposed CVAE-LSTM (Proposed) and ACT (Proposed) models achieved **100%** and **88.9%** Task Success Rates, respectively, in the wiping task when responding to temporal and physical directives.
- Proposed methods significantly reduced Modifier Directive Errors (MDEs) for constrained latent variables (e.g., temporal MDE of **0.22** for the intended temporal variable), demonstrating successful disentanglement of temporal and physical characteristics.
- Action chunking dramatically improved stability, with **0%** Task Success Rate when not applied, but **100%** TSR for both proposed models with appropriate weighting, confirming its necessity for online motion generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangled representation learning with weak supervision can effectively create an interpretable latent space where specific dimensions directly correspond to qualitative behavioral characteristics like speed and force.
> - Action chunking with weighted averaging is critical for ensuring motion stability and smoothness during online adaptation, mitigating oscillations when modifier directives are changed dynamically.
> - While effective for continuous temporal and physical modifiers, the proposed framework struggles with disentangling discrete, symbolic spatial directives, suggesting a need for specialized architectural components for such features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is rapidly evolving, with Imitation Learning (IL) standing out as a major paradigm for teaching robots complex behaviors from human demonstrations. However, as the paper points out, traditional IL methods often struggle with generating motions that can adapt to varying environmental conditions or respond to qualitative human instructions.

This research addresses a critical gap in the current IL landscape, specifically at the intersection of:

- **Fine-Grained Motion Modulation vs. High-Level Task Planning**: Recent advancements in combining large language models (LLMs) with robot control (e.g.,) have enabled robots to understand high-level task instructions and switch between skills. However, these foundation models often "face significant challenges in performing online, fine-grained modulation of continuous motion parameters, such as speed, force, and smoothness," a limitation also highlighted in recent audits of robotic foundation models. This paper directly tackles this limitation by focusing on *qualitative modifier directives* (e.g., "wipe strongly," "move fast") that require continuous adjustment of motion characteristics.
- **Disentangled Representation Learning (DRL) for Robot Control**: DRL aims to separate the underlying factors of variation in data into independent latent variables. While DRL has been applied to robot learning to learn interpretable or controllable latent spaces from demonstrations using weak supervision, existing methods primarily support *feedforward motion generation*. This means they generate motion in a one-shot manner based on pre-selected latent variables and lack the ability for *online modification or continuous editing* during execution.
- **Online Adaptation of Robot Behaviors**: The ability for a robot to dynamically adjust its behavior in real-time based on human input is crucial for real-world deployment. Conventional batch-based IL or DRL approaches are ill-suited for such dynamic environments. This paper explicitly seeks to develop a motion generation model that can respond to modifier directives *during task execution*, defining "online motion generation" as a key objective.
- **Action Chunking in Imitation Learning**: The paper relates to recent advancements in IL, such as Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), which generates sequences (chunks) of actions for smoother motion. The proposed method integrates this concept to facilitate online adjustments without abrupt transitions.

In summary, this work positions itself as a crucial step towards creating more adaptable and user-friendly robots by enabling them to interpret and respond to the nuanced, qualitative instructions that humans naturally use to guide behavior, going beyond simple task execution to fine-grained online control. It extends the utility of disentangled representations by making them applicable in dynamic, interactive settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a motion generation model that allows robots to dynamically adjust their actions in response to human-issued *modifier directives* (qualitative instructions imposing behavioral conditions) *during online task execution*.

The motivation stems from two main challenges in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Qualitative Human Instructions and Continuous Robot Actions**: Human instructions, such as "wipe strongly" or "move slowly," are often qualitative and imprecise. Translating these high-level, subjective directives into concrete, continuous robot control parameters (like force or speed) is difficult. Existing methods, especially those relying on large language models, excel at high-level task selection but struggle with this fine-grained, online modulation. There is a need for a mechanism that can learn a direct and intuitive mapping from such directives to specific behavioral characteristics.
2. **Achieving Online Adaptability and Smoothness in Motion Generation**: Many imitation learning and disentangled representation learning methods are designed for batch processing, generating an entire motion sequence in one go. This limits their applicability in dynamic scenarios where humans might provide feedback or new directives mid-task. The goal is to enable robots to adjust their current motion trajectory smoothly and immediately based on new directives, avoiding jerky or unstable transitions.

To address these, the study outlines two specific challenges it aims to overcome:

1. **Acquiring Latent Representations that Link Modifier Directives and Motions**: High-dimensional robot motions are hard to control directly. Using latent variables from autoencoders offers a solution, but these variables often lack interpretability. The objective is to use Disentangled Representation Learning (DRL) with weak supervision to create a latent space where specific latent variables directly correspond to and can be manipulated by modifier directives, making motion editing intuitive.
2. **Adapting to Modifier Directives During Online Motion Generation**: To achieve real-time adaptation, the system needs to map modifier directives to robot actions and generate subsequent actions online. This involves not only generating the *next* action but also ensuring *smooth transitions* when modifier directives are changed during execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces an online motion generation model designed to respond to modifier directives by leveraging disentangled representation learning and action chunking.

#### 3.1. Data Collection Method

The research employs a **bilateral control teleoperation method** to collect human demonstration data. This setup involves a human operating a leader robot, which in turn controls a follower robot that interacts with the environment. Specifically, **four-channel bilateral control** is utilized, enabling the simultaneous capture of both position and force information. This is crucial for tasks involving contact with the environment, allowing the robot to learn from the dynamics of human interaction (e.g., force applied during wiping). The robot system used for data collection and reproduction is the **CRANE-X7 robot**, which has eight degrees of freedom. For each time step `t`, the robot's joint angle `q_t`, angular velocity `dq_t`, and torque `tau_t` are recorded, forming the state vector `s_t = [q_t, dq_t, tau_t]`. Demonstrations are sequences of these states.

#### 3.2. Policy Learning (Offline Architecture)

The learning model, depicted in Figure 2(A), consists of two main architectures trained offline:

1. **CVAE for Action Sequence Generation**: The collected motion data is segmented into short action sequences `A_t = [s_t, s_{t+1}, ..., s_{t+W-1}]` using a sliding window of width `W` (set to 50). A **Conditional Variational AutoEncoder (CVAE)** learns the distribution of these action sequences.
 - **Encoder**: Maps an input action sequence `A_t` and its initial state `s_t` to the parameters (mean and variance) of a latent distribution `q_phi(z|A_t, s_t)`.
 - **Decoder**: Reconstructs the action sequence `A_t` from a latent variable `z` sampled from this distribution, conditioned on the initial state `s_t`.
 - **Loss Functions**:
 - **Reconstruction Loss (`L_rec`)**: Mean squared error between the predicted (`hat{A}_t`) and actual (`A_t`) action sequences.
 - **KL Regularization Loss (`L_kl`)**: KL divergence between the approximate posterior `q_phi(z|A_t, s_t)` and a standard Gaussian prior `p(z) = N(0, I)`, encouraging the latent space to be well-structured.

2. **Disentangled Representation Learning (DRL) with Weakly Supervised Labels**: To associate the CVAE's latent variables with modifier directives, weak supervision is introduced.
 - **Modifier Directives and Weak Labels**: The model assumes `S` types of modifier directives (e.g., speed, force). For each type, weakly supervised labels `y_s ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 1.0}` are assigned to demonstration segments by the human demonstrator, representing low, moderate, and high intensity of the directive (e.g., Slow, Moderate, Fast).
 - **Latent Variable Partitioning**: The latent variable `z` from the CVAE is divided into two parts:
 - `z_c_s`: `S` constrained latent variables, each intended to correspond to a specific modifier directive.
 - `z_u_n`: `N` unconstrained latent variables.
 - **Modifier Classification Loss (`L_modi`)**: Each constrained latent variable `z_c_s` is fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict its corresponding weak label `y_s`. The training objective for this is a **binary cross-entropy loss (`L_bce`)**. This loss forces `z_c_s` to encode the demonstrator's intended modifier directives.
 - **Overall Training Loss (`L`)**: The final loss is a weighted sum: `L = alpha * L_rec + beta * L_kl + gamma * L_modi`. The coefficients (`alpha`, `beta`, `gamma`) control the balance between reconstruction accuracy, latent space regularization, and disentanglement.

#### 3.3. Online Inference

During online execution (Figure 2(B)), the robot receives its current state `s_t` and a command value `z_cmd` representing the desired modifier directives.
- **Commanded Latent Variables**: The unconstrained latent variables `z_u_n` are fixed at 0. The constrained latent variables `z_c_s` are manually adjusted by the human operator to reflect the desired modifier (e.g., setting `z_c_1` to a value corresponding to "strong" wiping).
- **Action Sequence Generation**: The decoder generates a predicted action sequence `hat{A}_t` of length `W` based on `s_t` and `z_cmd`.
- **Action Chunking with Weighted Averaging**: To ensure smooth motion and mitigate abrupt transitions when `z_cmd` is modified online, the next robot state `hat{s}_{t+1}` is computed as a weighted average of `hat{A}_t` and previously generated action sequences. The weights `w_i` are typically designed to give more importance to recent predictions and are chosen as `w_i = 1 / log(i+1)` in the proposed method (where `i` is the relative step in the predicted sequence). This mechanism is similar to Action Chunking (e.g., ACT).
- **Robot Control**: The computed `hat{s}_{t+1}` is used as the command signal for the robot's position and force control system.

This methodology combines a robust data collection strategy, a CVAE for learning motion distributions, a novel weak supervision approach for disentangling behavioral characteristics in the latent space, and an action chunking mechanism for stable online adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the proposed method in two tasks: Wiping and Pick-and-Place (the latter detailed in the Appendix). The evaluation focused on two key aspects: (1) whether generated motions accurately reflected modifier directives, and (2) the effect of Action Chunking on motion stability.

#### 4.1. Motion Generation Using Modifier Directives (Wiping Task)

- **Metrics**: Task Success Rate (TSR) and Modifier Directive Errors (MDEs). MDE quantifies the alignment between intended directives and generated motion by comparing the slopes and intercepts of feature-value-vs-directive-label lines between reference and generated motions. Lower MDE indicates better alignment.
- **Comparison Methods**:
 - **CVAE-LSTM**: CVAE with LSTM for encoder/decoder.
 - **ACT**: CVAE with Transformer for encoder/decoder (Action Chunking with Transformers).
 - **CVAE-LSTM (Proposed)**: CVAE-LSTM extended with the disentangled representation learning constraint.
 - **ACT (Proposed)**: ACT extended with the disentangled representation learning constraint.
- **Experimental Setup (Wiping Task)**: The robot wiped a whiteboard. Modifier directives included:
 - **Temporal**: Slow, Moderate, Fast (measured by wiping cycle completion time).
 - **Physical**: Weak, Moderate, Strong (measured by average minimum joint torque).
 - The latent space had 3 dimensions: `z_c_1` (physical), `z_c_2` (temporal), and `z_u_1` (unconstrained).
- **Results (Table 2)**:
 - **Task Success Rate (TSR)**:
 - CVAE-LSTM (Proposed) achieved 100% TSR (9/9 trials).
 - ACT (Proposed) achieved 88.9% TSR (8/9 trials).
 - Baseline CVAE-LSTM achieved 100% TSR (13/13 trials), but without disentanglement.
 - Baseline ACT had 0% TSR, indicating instability without the proposed modifications, especially for online adaptation.
 - **Modifier Directive Errors (MDEs)**:
 - **Baseline CVAE-LSTM** showed high MDEs (all > 0.90), with little differentiation across latent variables (e.g., `z_1` phys MDE = 1.01, `z_2` temp MDE = 1.06). This confirms its entangled representation, where latent variables do not clearly correspond to specific directives.
 - **CVAE-LSTM (Proposed)** significantly improved disentanglement for the constrained variables. For `z_1` (intended for physical), its physical MDE was 0.63, while for `z_2` (intended for temporal), its temporal MDE was very low at 0.22. This demonstrates successful learning of the correspondence between latent variables and specific modifier directives. However, `z_1` still had a temporal MDE of 0.69, suggesting some residual entanglement.
 - **ACT (Proposed)** also showed improved disentanglement compared to original ACT, with a temporal MDE of 0.43 for `z_1`. However, it exhibited an unintended association: the physical MDE for `z_2` (intended for temporal) remained high at 1.95. This indicates that while the proposed method generally improves directive alignment, the orthogonality and independence of latent variable representations can still be a challenge across different model architectures.

#### 4.2. Effect of Action Chunking on Motion Stability

- **Experimental Setup**: Wiping task, comparing the proposed methods with and without action chunking, and with different weighting functions.
- **Results (Table 3)**:
 - When action chunking was **not applied** ("No weight"), both CVAE-LSTM (Proposed) and ACT (Proposed) resulted in 0% TSR, indicating severe oscillations and instability during online execution.
 - Applying action chunking dramatically improved stability and performance. The weighting function `w_i = 1 / log(i+1)` achieved a 100% TSR for both CVAE-LSTM (Proposed) and ACT (Proposed). Other weighting functions (`1/(i+1)`, `exp(-m*i)`) also showed improvements, though with varying effectiveness depending on the model (e.g., `1/(i+1)` worked for CVAE-LSTM but not ACT).
 - This clearly demonstrates the critical role of temporal smoothing via action chunking in mitigating abrupt transitions caused by online changes to the latent command `z_cmd`.

#### 4.3. Limitations (Pick-and-Place Task - Appendix A)

- **Experimental Setup (Pick-and-Place Task)**: Robot picks a block and places it. Modifier directives: temporal (Slow, Moderate, Fast) and spatial (Left, Center, Right).
- **Results (Table 4)**: While all models achieved 100% TSR, the MDEs for the proposed methods were generally high (exceeding 1.00 for most combinations), indicating a failure to effectively disentangle spatial directives.
- **Conclusion**: The framework struggled with spatial (symbolic/discrete) directives. This suggests that the continuous latent variables and weak supervision setup might not be ideal for capturing discrete, symbolic features. This highlights a need for architectural elements (e.g., gating mechanisms) that explicitly handle such directive types.
- **Other Limitations**:
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning**: The loss function weighting parameters (`alpha`, `beta`, `gamma`) were empirically tuned per task, indicating a need for more adaptive tuning strategies.
 - **Input Modalities**: Experiments were limited to proprioceptive inputs (joint angles, velocities, torques). Extending to multimodal sensory inputs (e.g., vision, audio) is necessary for directives like "carefully" or "thoroughly."

Overall, the findings confirm that the proposed disentangled representation learning and action chunking effectively enable online, fine-grained motion adaptation to qualitative temporal and physical modifier directives in tasks involving continuous parameter adjustments. However, challenges remain for discrete/symbolic directives and generalizability across all modifier types and architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning and human-robot interaction, offering several important contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Online, Fine-Grained Motion Adaptation**: The most direct and impactful contribution is the ability for robots to dynamically adjust their continuous motion parameters (like speed and force) *during execution* based on real-time, qualitative human instructions. This bridges a critical gap where current large language models excel at high-level task selection but falter in fine-grained, online modulation. This capability makes robots much more responsive and adaptable to human commands in dynamic environments, moving beyond pre-programmed or one-shot motion generation.
2. **Interpretable and Controllable Latent Space**: By using weakly supervised disentangled representation learning, the method creates an interpretable latent space where specific dimensions directly correspond to meaningful behavioral characteristics (e.g., "fast" or "strong"). This makes it intuitive for human operators to control and modify robot behaviors, improving the transparency and usability of robot systems. This is a crucial step towards human-in-the-loop robot control that leverages human intuition.
3. **Enhanced Motion Stability with Action Chunking**: The integration of action chunking with weighted averaging is vital for the practical deployment of online adaptation. It effectively mitigates abrupt transitions and oscillations that would otherwise occur when modifier directives are changed dynamically. This ensures that the robot's movements remain smooth and stable, which is critical for safety and task success in real-world scenarios.
4. **Advancement in Imitation Learning for Qualitative Instructions**: The paper demonstrates a robust method for teaching robots to understand and execute qualitative human instructions, a common form of human communication that robots have historically struggled with. This expands the scope and applicability of imitation learning, making it a more powerful tool for complex human-robot collaboration.
5. **Robust Data Collection Methodology**: The reliance on four-channel bilateral control for data collection is a strong foundation, as it captures rich force and position information essential for learning behaviors that involve physical interaction. This robust data underpins the model's ability to learn nuanced physical modifiers.

**Potential Impact**:

- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration**: Robots capable of understanding and responding to nuanced human directives in real-time will be invaluable in collaborative settings, allowing humans to guide and refine robot actions more naturally. This could lead to more intuitive teleoperation, co-manipulation, and assistance robots.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy in Dynamic Environments**: The ability to adapt motions online means robots can better handle unexpected changes or variable conditions, making them more robust and autonomous in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The identified limitations, particularly concerning the handling of discrete/symbolic spatial directives and the need for adaptive hyperparameter tuning, open promising avenues for future research. This includes exploring hybrid latent spaces, incorporating gating mechanisms, and developing multimodal learning approaches to further enhance directive-based control.
- **Democratization of Robot Programming**: Simplifying the way humans can instruct and modify robot behaviors, moving beyond complex coding to intuitive directives, could make robotics more accessible to a wider range of users, from industrial workers to home users.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes to making robots more intelligent, adaptable, and user-friendly by providing a practical framework for online, fine-grained control based on human modifier directives. It moves the field closer to truly intuitive human-robot interaction.
