# STIV: Scalable Text and Image Conditioned Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> STIV integrates image conditioning into a Diffusion Transformer via frame replacement plus joint image-text classifier-free guidance, unifying text-to-video and text-image-to-video in one model. Systematic ablations on stability, efficiency, and data curation yield an 8.7B model scoring 83.1 on VBench T2V and 90.1 on VBench-I2V, beating CogVideoX, Pika, Kling, Gen-3.

## Problem

- No systematic recipe exists unifying architecture, training, and data choices for scalable DiT-based video generation, leaving development ad hoc.
- Prior image-conditioning methods for video generation target U-Net architectures; integrating image conditions into DiT remains unsolved.
- Stability tricks like QK-norm alone prove insufficient at larger model scales, and no prior approach unifies T2V and TI2V in a single model.

## Method

- ==Frame Replacement== swaps the noised first latent frame with the un-noised image-condition latent inside ==DiT== blocks, masking its loss, natively propagating conditioning through ==spatial-temporal attention==.
- ==Joint Image-Text Classifier-Free Guidance (JIT-CFG)== combines random image-condition dropout during training with two-term guidance at inference, fixing static-motion staleness and enabling one model for T2V and TI2V.
- Progressive training recipe plus stability/efficiency stack (==QK-norm==, ==sandwich-norm==, ==factorized spatial-temporal attention==, ==MaskDiT==, ==AdaFactor==, ==RoPE==) initializes T2I to T2V to **STIV** across scales 600M-8.7B.

## Results

- **8.7B** STIV-M-512 model reaches VBench T2V Total **83.1** and Semantic **78.5-79.5**, surpassing CogVideoX-5B (**81.6**), Kling (**81.9**), Gen-3 (**82.3**).
- Same model achieves state-of-the-art VBench-I2V Total **90.1**, beating SVD (**89.9**) and Animate-Anything (**89.8**), with JIT-CFG raising dynamic degree from **10.2** to **24.0**.
- Data engine ablation shows quality over volume: Panda-10M curated subset beats larger Panda-30M on VBench Total (**66.2** vs **65.6**); direct video captioning (VCap) cuts hallucination DSG-Video scores versus frame-based captioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single scalar-simple mechanism, frame replacement, can outperform more complex cross-attention plus loss-constrained variants, complexity added ad hoc often hurts video quality.
> - Random condition dropout paired with joint guidance is a general recipe for merging conditional and unconditional generation tasks into one model without task-specific heads.
> - Curated, high-quality, motion-aware data consistently beats raw data volume, caption richness and hallucination control matter more than dataset scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Placement within the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced rapid advancements, notably catalyzed by models like Sora, which leverage the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture. Sora's emergence set a new benchmark for video quality and coherence, prompting researchers to actively explore systematic approaches to developing robust and scalable models. Before DiT architectures, many pioneering video generation models, such as VideoLDM, VideoCrafter, and DynamiCrafter, relied on U-Net architectures, often facing limitations in scaling and handling complex temporal dynamics. The shift towards DiT architectures, characterized by their ability to model long-range dependencies efficiently, has opened new avenues for improved video quality and consistency.

Despite this progress, several challenges persist. Pure text-to-video (T2V) models often struggle with generating coherent and realistic videos that are well-grounded in external references, leading to inconsistencies or "hallucinations". To mitigate this, text-image-to-video (TI2V) models were introduced, using an initial image frame as a concrete grounding for the generated video. However, integrating image conditions seamlessly into DiT architectures, developing stable large-scale training strategies, and curating high-quality training datasets remain significant hurdles. Existing studies often focus on isolated aspects, overlooking their collective impact, and few approaches have successfully unified T2V and TI2V capabilities within a single framework.

STIV directly addresses these gaps by presenting a "comprehensive study that systematically explores the interplay of model architectures, training recipes, and data curation strategies." It aims to provide a clear, systematic "recipe" that unifies T2V and TI2V tasks, scales effectively, and is adaptable to various downstream applications. By building upon the DiT architecture and introducing novel integration techniques for image conditioning, STIV positions itself as a crucial contribution towards democratizing and standardizing the development of cutting-edge video generation models, moving beyond proprietary "black-box" solutions like Sora towards transparent and extensible frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **STIV (Scalable Text and Image Conditioned Video Generation)**, a comprehensive, systematic, and extensible method for generating high-quality videos conditioned on both text and images. The authors explicitly state a "pressing need for a clear, systematic recipe that can guide the development of robust and scalable models," indicating a desire to move beyond ad-hoc solutions to a more principled framework.

The core motivations driving this work are:
1. **Unification of T2V and TI2V Tasks:** Existing models often specialize in either text-to-video or text-image-to-video generation. STIV aims to create a single model capable of excelling at both tasks simultaneously, thereby enhancing versatility and reducing complexity.
2. **Seamless Integration of Image Conditions:** While image conditioning is vital for grounding video content, its effective integration into DiT architectures remains a challenge. The authors sought innovative techniques to blend visual inputs smoothly with textual cues within the DiT framework.
3. **Scalability and Training Stability:** Developing models that scale to large sizes and higher resolutions often introduces training instabilities and memory inefficiencies. A key motivation was to identify and implement stable, efficient large-scale training strategies.
4. **Improving Data Quality and Curation:** Recognizing that model performance heavily relies on the quality of training data, the researchers aimed to develop advanced data curation strategies to enhance datasets for video generation.
5. **Enabling Versatile Applications:** Beyond foundational T2V/TI2V capabilities, the motivation extends to demonstrating how a robust framework can be easily adapted for diverse applications, such as video prediction, frame interpolation, multi-view generation, and long video generation.

By addressing these motivations, STIV aims to empower future research and accelerate progress towards more reliable and versatile video generation solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

STIV's methodology is a systematic exploration of model architectures, training recipes, and data curation, centered around a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) framework.

#### 3.1. Core Architecture

The STIV model builds upon the **PixArt-α** architecture, which converts input frames into spatial and temporal latent embeddings using a frozen Variational Autoencoder (VAE). These embeddings are then processed by a stack of learnable DiT-like blocks.
- **Text Encoding:** A T5 tokenizer and an internally trained CLIP text encoder are used to process text prompts, providing rich text embeddings.
- **Factorized Spatial-Temporal Attention:** The model employs factorized spatial and temporal attention to efficiently handle video frames. Spatial self-attention is performed by folding the temporal dimension into the batch, followed by temporal self-attention by folding the spatial dimension. This design allows for preloading weights from text-to-image (T2I) models.
- **Singleton Conditions:** Micro conditions (image resolution, crop coordinates, sampling stride, number of frames) are encoded using sinusoidal embeddings and an MLP, then combined with the diffusion timestep embedding and the last CLIP text token embedding. These "singleton conditions" provide shared scale-shift-gate parameters for spatial attention and feed-forward layers.
- **Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE):** 2D RoPE is applied for spatial attention and 1D RoPE for temporal attention, providing inductive bias for relative relationships and adaptability to varying resolutions.
- **Flow Matching:** Instead of traditional diffusion loss, STIV uses a Flow Matching training objective, which defines a conditional optimal transport between source (Gaussian) and target distributions, using linear interpolates.

#### 3.2. Model Scaling and Stabilization

To address training instability and memory challenges with larger models, STIV incorporates:
- **Stable Training Recipes:**
 - **QK-Norm:** RMSNorm is applied to query and key vectors before attention logits calculation, significantly stabilizing training.
 - **Sandwich-Norm:** Stateless layer normalization is applied as both pre-norm and post-norm to Multi-Head Attention (MHA) and Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layers.
- **Efficient DiT Training:**
 - **MaskDiT:** Randomly masks 50% of spatial tokens before passing them into major DiT blocks, followed by unmasked fine-tuning.
 - **AdaFactor Optimizer:** Replaces AdamW, improving efficiency and reducing memory consumption.
 - **Gradient Checkpointing:** Only stores self-attention outputs to further reduce memory.

#### 3.3. Image Conditioning for TI2V

STIV integrates image conditions through a simple yet effective strategy:
- **Frame Replacement:** During training, the noised first frame latent is replaced with the un-noised latent of the image condition, and the loss for this frame is masked out. During inference, the un-noised image condition latent is used for the first frame at each diffusion step. This flexible approach allows the model to default to T2V if no image condition is provided, or support video prediction and frame interpolation with multiple image conditions.
- **Image Condition Dropout:** During training, both text and image conditions are randomly dropped out. This prevents the model from overfitting to the image condition, promoting motion generation, and naturally enables multi-task training for both T2V and TI2V.
- **Joint Image-Text Classifier-Free Guidance (JIT-CFG):** This extends standard CFG by leveraging both text and image conditions during inference. The velocity estimates are modified based on unconditional, text-only, and joint text-image conditions. The paper also explores a Separate Image and Text Classifier-Free Guidance (SIT-CFG) but finds JIT-CFG to be simpler and equally effective with fewer inference passes.

#### 3.4. Progressive Training Recipe

STIV adopts a progressive training pipeline (Figure 4) to facilitate adaptation to higher resolutions and longer durations:
1. A T2I model is trained first.
2. This T2I model initializes a T2V model.
3. The T2V model then initializes the STIV model.
4. For higher resolutions and longer durations, interpolated RoPE embeddings are used, and model weights are initialized from lower-resolution, shorter-duration models.

#### 3.5. Systematic Ablation Studies

The methodology includes extensive ablation studies across T2I, T2V, and TI2V tasks (Figure 5), evaluating the impact of each design choice on various metrics. Key insights inform the final STIV architecture.

#### 3.6. Video Data Engine

Recognizing the criticality of data quality, the authors developed a comprehensive **Video Data Engine** (Figure 8):
- **Video Pre-processing:** PySceneDetect is used to analyze, detect, and segment scenes, removing inconsistent parts to produce coherent clips.
- **Feature Extraction:** Features like motion score, aesthetic score, and temporal consistency are extracted to guide filtering.
- **Data Filtering:** An automated infrastructure filters videos based on quality metrics. For example, Panda-70M is curated into Panda-30M and a high-quality Panda-10M subset.
- **Video Captioning:** LLaVA-Hound-7B, a video LLM, is employed for dense, motion-aware captioning, addressing limitations of frame-based captioning and LLM summarization.
- **Caption Evaluation (DSG-Video):** A novel metric, DSG-Video, is introduced to detect hallucinated objects in captions by using an LLM to generate questions and a multimodal LLM to verify object presence across video frames.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

STIV demonstrates strong performance across T2I, T2V, and TI2V tasks, establishing new state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks, largely attributed to its systematic recipe and architectural innovations.

#### 4.1. Text-to-Image (T2I) Ablations

The T2I ablation studies (Table 2) showed substantial improvements from:
- **Stabilization Techniques:** QK-Norm, sandwich-norm, and singleton condition norm allowed for stable training with higher learning rates (2e-4) and improved quality.
- **Noising/Denoising Formulation:** Flow Matching, combined with CFG-Renormalization, significantly improved all evaluated metrics (e.g., FID reduced from 26.17 to 21.16).
- **Training Cost Optimization:** AdaFactor, MaskDiT, and Shared AdaLN reduced memory usage without significant performance degradation, enabling larger models.
- **Enhanced Components:** Micro conditions, RoPE, internal VAE, and an internally trained CLIP model consistently improved metrics.
- **Data Quality:** Incorporating synthetic recaptions achieved the best overall results, highlighting the importance of high-quality, rich captions. Human evaluations confirmed these automatic metric improvements.

#### 4.2. Text-to-Video (T2V) Performance

The T2V models, serving as the foundation for STIV, showed distinct scaling behaviors (Table 9, Table 11):
- **Parameter Scaling:** Increasing model size from XL (600M) to M (8.7B) led to a significant increase in VBench-Semantic scores (from 72.5 to 74.8), indicating improved semantic alignment, while VBench-Quality showed modest gains.
- **Resolution Scaling:** Increasing spatial resolution from 256x256 to 512x512 for the M model further boosted VBench-Semantic scores (from 74.8 to 77.0), demonstrating improved visual fidelity and detail.
- **Model Initialization:** Jointly initializing from both a low-resolution T2V model and a high-resolution T2I model achieved better VBench metrics and FVD values compared to training from scratch (Table 6b). Similarly, for higher frame counts, initializing from shorter T2V models with interpolated RoPE proved more effective (Table 6c).
- **SFT and Temporal Upsampler (TUP):** Fine-tuning with high-quality Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) data significantly enhanced VBench-Quality (82.2 to 83.9). Further integration of a temporal upsampler (+ TUP) boosted the VBench-Quality score to 84.4, achieving **state-of-the-art (SOTA) quality** among both open and closed-source models.
- **Overall T2V SOTA:** The best T2V model (M-512 UnmaskSFT + TUP) achieved a VBench-Total score of 83.0 (and 84.4 Quality), surpassing leading open-source models like CogVideoX-5B (81.6), LaVie-2 (81.8), Allegro (81.1), and closed-source models like Gen-2 (80.6), PIKA (80.7), EMU3 (81.0), KLING (81.9), and Gen-3 (82.3).

#### 4.3. Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) Performance

STIV's design for TI2V proved highly effective (Table 10, Table 12):
- **Frame Replacement Effectiveness:** Ablations demonstrated that Frame Replacement (FR) alone was a robust and effective approach, yielding strong I2V consistency scores without negatively impacting other video quality dimensions (Table 3). Other strategies like Cross Attention (CA) or Large Projection (LP) could lead to over-constraining the model or reduced dynamic degree.
- **Image Condition Dropout and JIT-CFG:** This combination was critical for multi-task training and resolving the "staleness" issue (static motion) observed in high-resolution TI2V models. It enabled the model to perform both T2V and TI2V tasks comparably to specialized single-task models (Table 4). JIT-CFG also increased dynamic degree (from 10.2 to 24.0, Table 5).
- **Overall TI2V SOTA:** The STIV-M-512 model achieved a **state-of-the-art VBench-I2V Total score of 90.1**, outperforming other competitive models like DynamicCrafter-512 (89.1), Animate-Anything (89.8), and SVD (89.9). Even the STIV-M-512-JIT model, prioritizing motion, maintained a strong 89.8.

#### 4.4. Data Engine Impact

The Video Data Engine played a crucial role in enhancing model performance:
- **Filtering:** Training on the higher-quality Panda-10M dataset yielded better FVD and VBench scores compared to the larger but lower-quality Panda-30M (Table 7), demonstrating that data quality outweighs sheer volume.
- **Captioning:** Using the LLaVA-Hound-7B based Video Captioning (VCap) significantly reduced hallucination rates (DSG-Video_i from 6.4 to 5.3, DSG-Video_s from 24.0 to 15.0) and increased caption richness compared to frame-based methods (FCapLLM), leading to improved T2V model performance (Table 8).

#### 4.5. Flexible Applications

STIV's architecture proved highly versatile for various downstream applications (Figure 10, Figure 11, Figure 12):
- **Video Prediction:** A STIV-V2V model (conditioned on the first four frames) showed significantly lower FVD scores on MSRVTT and MovieGenBench compared to a T2V model (183.7 vs. 536.2 FVD on MSRVTT), indicating high fidelity for future frame prediction.
- **Frame Interpolation:** STIV-TUP (a temporal upsampler) achieved low FID (2.0) and FVD (5.9) on MSRVTT for interpolation, demonstrating its ability to generate smooth intermediate frames.
- **Multi-View Generation:** Despite using only temporal attention for cross-view consistency, STIV-TI2V-XL achieved comparable PSNR/SSIM and LPIPS scores to Zero123++ on the GSO dataset, validating its effectiveness in capturing 3D information and maintaining consistency across novel views.
- **Long Video Generation:** A hierarchical framework leveraging STIV for keyframe prediction and interpolated frame generation successfully demonstrated the creation of long videos (e.g., 380 frames), addressing the challenge of extended duration synthesis.

#### 4.6. Class-to-Video (UCF-101)

On the UCF-101 dataset for label-to-video generation, STIV-XL-128 achieved SOTA Inception Score (IS) of 93.4 and FVD of 124 (Table 17), significantly outperforming previous models like MAGVIT (IS 83.6, FVD 159), further confirming its robust generative capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on STIV holds significant implications for the future of video generation research and its practical applications.

**1. A Transparent and Extensible Recipe:** Perhaps the most significant contribution is the provision of a "clear, systematic recipe" for building scalable text-image-conditioned video generation models. This systematic study of architectures, training strategies, and data curation offers invaluable insights that can guide future research, enabling the community to iterate and build upon a well-understood foundation, rather than relying on proprietary, opaque models.

**2. Unification of T2V and TI2V Capabilities:** By successfully integrating text and image conditioning into a single DiT-based framework, STIV addresses a long-standing challenge in the field. This unification simplifies model development, reduces computational overhead by allowing one model to handle multiple tasks, and offers enhanced control and versatility for users, as the output can be grounded by both semantic prompts and visual references.

**3. State-of-the-Art Performance:** STIV's achievement of new state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like VBench (for both T2V and TI2V) demonstrates its capability to generate high-quality, coherent, and semantically aligned videos. This performance places it at the forefront of generative video models, competing with both leading open-source and closed-source solutions.

**4. Versatility and Practical Applicability:** The demonstrated flexibility of the STIV framework across various downstream applications—including video prediction, frame interpolation, multi-view generation, and long video generation—underscores its potential for real-world impact. These capabilities are crucial for diverse domains such as autonomous driving (prediction), media production (interpolation, long video), and embodied AI (multi-view consistency), accelerating the development of more intelligent and capable AI systems.

**5. Advancements in Training Stability and Efficiency:** The integration of stability techniques (QK-Norm, sandwich-norm) and efficiency measures (MaskDiT, AdaFactor) is crucial for scaling video generation models to billions of parameters and higher resolutions. These contributions provide practical guidelines for researchers and practitioners working with large-scale generative models, ensuring training feasibility and robustness.

**6. Importance of Data Quality and Curation:** The emphasis on a sophisticated Video Data Engine and the quantitative proof of its benefits highlight the critical role of data in achieving high-performance generative models. The development of metrics like DSG-Video for hallucination detection also advances the tools available for rigorous dataset evaluation.

In conclusion, STIV provides a powerful, versatile, and transparent framework that significantly advances the state of scalable video generation. By systematically addressing key challenges and offering a "recipe" that combines architectural innovation, robust training, and meticulous data curation, it is poised to accelerate progress towards more versatile, reliable, and controllable video generation solutions, benefiting both the research community and various industrial applications.
