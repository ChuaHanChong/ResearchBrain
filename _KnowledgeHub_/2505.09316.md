# Scent of Knowledge: Optimizing Search-Enhanced Reasoning with Information Foraging

> [!summary] Summary
> InForage, developed at Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence, is a reinforcement learning framework that optimizes large language models for search-enhanced, multi-step reasoning by emulating human information foraging strategies. The framework dynamically interacts with external knowledge, achieving an average Exact Match (EM) score of 41.0 across diverse question-answering benchmarks by explicitly rewarding intermediate retrieval quality and reasoning efficiency.

## Problem

- Static Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is insufficient for complex, ambiguous, or multi-step information needs due to its pre-inference retrieval approach.
- Current dynamic search-enhanced reasoning methods struggle with iteratively accumulating partial information patches and evaluating the context-dependent utility of retrieved data.
- LLM optimization typically focuses only on final answer correctness, overlooking the effectiveness and efficiency of intermediate information retrieval steps.

## Method

- Develops **InForage**, an RL framework inspired by ==Information Foraging Theory==, which models search-enhanced reasoning as an iterative process of gathering "information patches" guided by "information scent."
- Implements a multi-component reward system that explicitly balances final answer accuracy (R_outcome), cumulative information gain (R_gain), and reasoning efficiency (R_efficiency).
- Employs a two-stage training strategy: Supervised Fine-Tuning on a custom human-guided dataset for initial behavior learning, followed by Reinforcement Learning using PPO with the designed rewards.

## Results

- InForage consistently surpassed all baselines, achieving an average Exact Match (EM) score of **41.0** across various QA benchmarks, demonstrating strong robustness and generalizability.
- It showed superior performance on multi-hop tasks and a novel real-time web QA dataset, securing an EM of **44.1** on the "Self*" dataset, indicating its efficacy in complex, open-ended information-seeking.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the custom dataset, supervised fine-tuning, and the specific information gain and efficiency penalty rewards are all crucial for the framework's superior performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling search-enhanced reasoning as "information foraging" provides a robust, principled framework for LLMs to dynamically gather knowledge and adapt to complex information needs.
> - A multi-component reward system, valuing both final answer correctness and intermediate retrieval quality and efficiency, is essential for training LLMs in strategic information-seeking behavior.
> - Training on a fine-grained, human-guided dataset of iterative search trajectories is critical for equipping LLMs with the ability to perform complex, multi-hop reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and advanced reasoning/agentic AI. It addresses a critical limitation of traditional LLMs and RAG systems: their inadequacy in handling complex, ambiguous, or multi-step information-seeking tasks that require dynamic interaction with external knowledge.

**The Evolution from Static RAG to Dynamic Reasoning:**
Initially, RAG (Lewis et al., 2020a) emerged as a solution to LLMs' inherent knowledge cutoff and hallucination issues by concatenating pre-retrieved information with the input prompt. While effective for simple factoid questions, this "static, pre-inference retrieval" approach falls short when information needs are not immediately obvious or require iterative exploration. The paper explicitly states that "this approach often lacks adaptiveness, especially for complex tasks in which users’ information needs are ambiguous, rationale-based, or not directly searchable."

**Emergence of Test-Time Scaling and Agentic LLMs:**
Recent advancements in "test-time scaling techniques" have equipped LLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities, enabling complex behaviors like long chain-of-thought reasoning and dynamic tool use (Snell et al., 2024; Muennighoff et al., 2025). Models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek R1 have demonstrated iterative self-reflection and refinement, particularly in areas like mathematical problem-solving and coding. This shift has motivated researchers to move retrieval from a static step to a "dynamic, inference-time process," allowing LLMs to interact adaptively with external knowledge throughout their reasoning.

**Addressing Challenges in Dynamic Retrieval:**
However, enabling dynamic search-enhanced reasoning presents two main challenges:
1. **Partial Information Patches:** Complex tasks rarely yield all necessary information from a single retrieval. Instead, each search typically uncovers only a "local information patch," requiring iterative accumulation and integration through reasoning.
2. **Context-Dependent Value of Information:** The utility of a retrieved piece of information is not intrinsic but depends on its contribution to the overall reasoning process and final answer accuracy. Each retrieval decision influences both immediate reasoning and ultimate correctness.

**Leveraging Cognitive Science:**
To overcome these challenges, the authors draw inspiration from **Information Foraging Theory (IFT)** (Pirolli and Card, 1999). IFT explains how humans efficiently seek information by balancing the expected value of information against the costs of exploration, guided by "information scent" – the perceived relevance of cues. This cognitive theory provides a strong conceptual framework for designing LLMs that can emulate human-like adaptive search strategies.

InForage contributes to the growing body of work on "search-augmented reasoning" (e.g., Search-o1, Search-R1, DeepResearcher), but distinctively formalizes this process through IFT and introduces a novel reward mechanism that explicitly values intermediate retrieval quality and efficiency, rather than solely focusing on the final answer. This positions InForage as a more holistic and cognitively aligned approach to building robust, adaptive, and efficient reasoning agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptive framework that enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform complex, multi-step reasoning by dynamically interacting with external knowledge sources. This objective is driven by the following motivations:

- **Overcoming Static RAG Limitations:** Traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems use static, pre-inference retrieval, which is insufficient for complex tasks requiring ambiguous, multi-step, or evolving information needs. The paper aims to move beyond this by enabling LLMs to dynamically retrieve and integrate information during the inference process.
- **Emulating Human Information-Seeking Behavior:** Humans efficiently resolve complex information needs through iterative online searches. The authors are motivated to imbue LLMs with similar adaptive strategies, drawing formal inspiration from Information Foraging Theory (IFT) to model this dynamic process.
- **Optimizing Intermediate Retrieval Quality:** Existing reasoning methods often optimize LLMs based solely on the correctness of final answers, neglecting the crucial role of intermediate retrieval steps. A key motivation is to explicitly reward effective information-seeking behavior at each stage of the reasoning process, recognizing that meaningful retrieval actions incrementally shape the reasoning trajectory and final accuracy.
- **Developing a Comprehensive Training Framework:** To achieve dynamic, reward-guided reasoning, there is a need for specialized training data and an effective optimization paradigm. The paper aims to construct a human-guided dataset capturing iterative search and reasoning trajectories for complex web tasks, and to design a reinforcement learning (RL) framework that leverages this data.
- **Enhancing LLM Robustness and Adaptability:** The ultimate goal is to build LLM agents that are not only accurate but also robust and adaptive, capable of navigating vast and evolving information spaces more efficiently and effectively for real-world knowledge-intensive applications.

In summary, the paper aims to formalize search-enhanced reasoning as a dynamic information-seeking process through IFT, propose an RL framework (InForage) that rewards intermediate retrieval quality and efficiency, and construct a unique dataset to facilitate the training of such advanced reasoning agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InForage proposes a reinforcement learning (RL) framework for search-enhanced reasoning, fundamentally shifting retrieval from a static, pre-inference step to a dynamic, inference-time process. This methodology is inspired by Information Foraging Theory (IFT) and comprises a structured reasoning trajectory, a novel reward design, and a two-stage optimization process, supported by a custom-built dataset.

#### 3.1. Information Foraging Perspective

The core idea is to model search-enhanced reasoning as "information foraging," where the LLM's intermediate reasoning steps and generated subqueries represent the "information scent." This scent guides the model to iteratively use retrieval tools to gather "information patches" (local documents) from a broader knowledge space. The optimal objective is to collect all necessary information patches efficiently to generate an accurate answer with minimal search effort. The framework rewards not just the final answer but also effective knowledge gathering throughout the process.

#### 3.2. InForage Architecture and Reasoning Trajectory

InForage structures the LLM's generation process into a sequential composition of specialized blocks:
- `<think> reasoning content </think>`: Captures intermediate reasoning and planning. This reflects the evolving "information scent."
- `<search> subquery </search>`: Emits a subquery based on the current reasoning, initiating a retrieval action.
- `<info> retrieved information </info>`: Contains the results of the search, representing the "information patch" gathered.
- `...`: This process iterates, allowing for multi-step information gathering.
- `<answer> final answer </answer>`: Produces the ultimate response when sufficient evidence is accumulated.

#### 3.3. Reward Design for Reinforcement Learning

To encourage strategic information-seeking, InForage employs a multi-component reward system based on the optimization goal in Eq. 3, which balances information completeness and trajectory efficiency. This addresses the sparse supervision problem of relying solely on outcome-based rewards.
1. **Outcome Reward ($R_{outcome}$):** Directly reflects final task success, typically an Exact Match (EM) or F1 score of the generated answer.
2. **Information Gain Reward ($R_{gain}$):** Rewards the cumulative coverage of retrieved patches relative to the *expected knowledge set* ($D^*$). It's defined as the maximum coverage achieved at any point during the reasoning trajectory, incentivizing the retrieval of valuable, non-redundant evidence.
3. **Efficiency Penalty ($R_{efficiency}$):** Discourages excessively long reasoning paths by applying an exponential decay (β) based on the total number of reasoning-retrieval steps ($T$). This promotes concise and cost-effective information foraging.

The final reward is a weighted sum: $R = R_{efficiency} \cdot (R_{outcome} + \alpha \cdot R_{gain})$, where $\alpha$ balances final correctness and intermediate information gathering.

#### 3.4. Optimization Strategy

InForage is trained in two stages:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The foundation LLM (Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct) is first fine-tuned on a dataset of human-guided web browsing trajectories and corresponding QA pairs. This teaches the model to perform iterative reasoning and retrieval by mimicking expert behavior.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with PPO:** After SFT, the model is further optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017). The rule-based rewards ($R$) guide the model to self-explore and refine its reasoning trajectories. During PPO, gradient updates are restricted to model-generated tokens, excluding retrieved information patches to focus learning on the agent's decision-making.

#### 3.5. Training Dataset Construction

A crucial part of the methodology is the creation of a novel, large-scale, human-guided dataset designed to capture intricate information-seeking trajectories, as existing QA datasets lack this fine-grained supervision.
- **Open-Ended Information Browsing:** Annotators start with a seed factoid claim, iteratively query Google Search, select relevant web pages, extract "bridge" claims, and formulate subsequent queries. Each example requires at least three non-redundant claims or conditions to ensure multi-hop complexity.
- **QA Pair Generation:** Once sufficient evidence is gathered, GPT-4o synthesizes a coherent, multi-hop question-answer pair from the collected claims. Human annotators verify these for quality.
- **Automated Scaling:** After 500 manual samples, GPT-4o is leveraged as an autonomous agent to scale the annotation process, generating 20,000 coherent reasoning trajectories. The final dataset includes 19,500 training examples and 500 evaluation examples (referred to as the "Self" dataset), specifically designed for real-time, open-ended web tasks.
- **Diverse Web Corpus and Golden Evidence:** To ensure knowledge freshness and prevent memorization, web pages published between January 1, 2025, and March 31, 2025, are crawled, filtered, and used. Critical to supervision, each annotated example includes explicit "golden web URLs" as the verified factual basis, allowing for precise evaluation and rewarding of intermediate retrieval performance.

This comprehensive approach—from theoretical grounding to bespoke data creation and advanced RL training—enables InForage to learn and execute adaptive, multi-step search-enhanced reasoning effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of InForage demonstrates its superior performance across a wide array of question-answering and multi-hop reasoning tasks, highlighting the effectiveness of its information foraging approach.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Across Benchmarks

- **Consistent Outperformance:** InForage consistently outperforms all non-retrieval and retrieval-augmented baselines across both in-domain (e.g., NQ*, HQA*) and out-of-domain datasets (e.g., TriviaQA, PopQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle).
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The framework demonstrates strong robustness and generalizability, indicated by its high average Exact Match (EM) score of 41.0, significantly surpassing the second-best baseline (Search-R1-GRPO at 35.6).
- **Real-time Web QA (Self Dataset):** InForage shows exceptional strength on the self-constructed real-time web QA dataset ("Self*"), achieving an EM of 44.1, which is notably higher than the next best (Search-o1 at 36.7). This validates its effectiveness on complex, open-ended tasks requiring real-time, multi-step reasoning over evolving knowledge sources.

#### 4.2. Strength in Multi-hop and Complex Tasks

- **Multi-hop Tasks:** Search-enhanced reasoning methods, particularly Search-R1 and InForage, exhibit significant gains on multi-hop tasks like 2WikiMultihopQA and MuSiQue. InForage achieves 42.8 EM on 2Wiki (vs. Search-R1-GRPO's 36.9) and 17.2 EM on MuSiQue (vs. Search-R1-GRPO's 15.4). This underscores its ability to iteratively decompose queries, formulate subgoals, and retrieve contextually relevant information, mimicking human problem-solving.
- **Adaptive Reasoning:** The results confirm the hypothesis that adaptive, reasoning-aware retrieval is crucial for complex tasks. While prior RAG methods (e.g., Self-RAG, RQRAG) perform well on narrow tasks, their performance degrades under ambiguity. InForage's principled integration of retrieval into the reasoning loop, guided by its multi-faceted reward signals, enables it to navigate complex knowledge spaces more effectively.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study Insights

The ablation studies validate the contributions of InForage's key design components:
- **Training Settings:**
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Removing the SFT stage leads to a consistent performance drop, confirming its role in providing a strong initialization and aligning the model with reasoning-aligned behaviors.
 - **Self-constructed Dataset:** Excluding the self-constructed dataset during training degrades performance, emphasizing its importance for learning complex, multi-step information foraging strategies.
- **Reward Design:**
 - **Information Gain Reward (IGR):** Ablating IGR consistently decreases performance across datasets, proving its effectiveness in promoting meaningful intermediate retrievals.
 - **Efficiency Penalty (EP):** Removing EP generally degrades performance, except on the self-constructed dataset, suggesting that complex real-world tasks might inherently require longer reasoning chains. This highlights a nuanced balance between step efficiency and answer completeness.
- **RL Algorithms:** PPO generally yields better results than GRPO, indicating its suitability for optimizing nuanced reasoning quality in complex tasks with learned reward signals.
- **Model Scaling:** Replacing the Qwen2.5-3B foundation model with Qwen2.5-7B leads to further performance gains, demonstrating InForage's ability to scale effectively with larger model capacities.

#### 4.4. Case Study

A case study on a multi-conditional tennis player query illustrates InForage's reasoning process. While a vanilla RAG fails, InForage accurately decomposes the query, iteratively retrieves relevant evidence (e.g., players with 10+ major titles, major Florida tournaments, specific player performance, ranking milestones), and correctly identifies "Novak Djokovic." Notably, InForage achieves this with a Qwen2.5-3B model, matching the performance of a much larger, state-of-the-art commercial LLM like Gemini 2.5 Pro (which has built-in search tools), thereby showcasing its efficiency and effectiveness.

In conclusion, the results unequivocally demonstrate InForage's strong capabilities in search-enhanced reasoning, particularly for complex, multi-hop information-seeking tasks, validating its unique theoretical foundation and methodical design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper presents InForage as a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), with considerable potential impact across various knowledge-intensive applications.

#### 5.1. Bridging Cognitive Science and AI

The most significant contribution is the formalization of search-enhanced reasoning through **Information Foraging Theory (IFT)**. By modeling LLM behavior on human-like information-seeking strategies (e.g., following "information scent" to gather "information patches"), InForage offers a cognitively aligned paradigm. This theoretical grounding provides a principled way to design more intelligent and intuitive AI agents, moving beyond purely data-driven or heuristic approaches.

#### 5.2. Advancing Dynamic and Adaptive Reasoning

InForage represents a crucial shift from static, pre-inference retrieval to dynamic, inference-time information gathering. By integrating retrieval actions directly into the reasoning process and explicitly rewarding intermediate information gain alongside final answer correctness and efficiency, the framework enables LLMs to adaptively navigate complex, evolving information spaces. This capability is vital for real-world scenarios where user information needs are often ambiguous, multi-faceted, or require iterative exploration.

#### 5.3. Novel Reward System and Training Data

The proposed multi-component reward mechanism (Outcome Reward, Information Gain Reward, Efficiency Penalty) provides richer, less sparse supervision signals for reinforcement learning. This allows for more effective optimization of LLMs to perform strategic subquery generation and evidence integration. Complementing this is the creation of a unique, human-guided, multi-hop search reasoning dataset. This dataset, with its fine-grained trajectories, rationale-dependent queries, and explicit "golden evidence," addresses a critical gap in existing resources and is instrumental for training advanced reasoning agents.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Performance on Complex Tasks

Empirical results consistently demonstrate InForage's superior performance, especially on multi-hop and real-time web-based question-answering tasks. Its ability to decompose complex queries, iteratively retrieve relevant evidence, and synthesize information leads to more accurate and robust answers compared to traditional RAG and even other dynamic retrieval methods. The case study further highlights its efficiency, matching larger commercial models with a significantly smaller foundation LLM.

#### 5.5. Broad Applicability and Future Directions

The framework's core principles are highly generalizable.
- **Knowledge-Intensive Domains:** The dynamic, adaptive reasoning capabilities of InForage are directly applicable to domains like scientific writing, investigative journalism, legal analysis, knowledge curation, and complex problem-solving, where iterative information gathering and synthesis are paramount.
- **Tool-Agnostic Design:** While the paper focuses on web search as the external tool, the underlying framework and dataset pipeline are explicitly designed to be tool-agnostic. This opens avenues for extending InForage to optimize interactions with other tools such as code compilers, structured databases, APIs, or even specialized reasoning engines, paving the way for more general-purpose, tool-augmented AI agents.
- **Scalability:** The demonstrated scalability with larger foundation models (3B to 7B) suggests that InForage can leverage future advancements in LLM capacity for even greater performance.

In conclusion, InForage offers a unified, principled, and empirically validated solution for overcoming the limitations of static retrieval in LLMs. By fostering adaptive, human-like information foraging, it significantly advances the state of the art in search-enhanced reasoning, pushing LLMs closer to becoming truly intelligent and versatile assistants for complex knowledge-intensive tasks.
