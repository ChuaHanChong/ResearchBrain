# FM-VLA: Force-based Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models in Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FM-VLA enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with a novel force-based memory for long-horizon temporal reasoning in contact-rich manipulation tasks. The model achieved an average success rate of 83.3% across challenging memory-dependent tasks, outperforming existing baselines, while adding minimal inference latency.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models primarily operate under a Markovian assumption, limiting their ability to handle non-Markovian tasks requiring long-horizon temporal context like sequential action counting or tracking interaction progress.
- Vision-based memory solutions are computationally expensive and struggle with tasks where visual changes are subtle, ambiguous, or occluded, which is common in contact-rich manipulation.
- Previous approaches integrating force signals into VLAs focused on instantaneous action correction or local interaction states, lacking mechanisms for accumulating long-horizon temporal information about interaction history.

## Method

- **FM-VLA** augments a VLA policy with two proprioceptive streams: a long-horizon wrench history and a short-window joint-state history to capture comprehensive temporal context.
- A task-agnostic, pretrained ==Variational Autoencoder (VAE)== compresses raw, high-frequency wrench histories into a compact, informative latent space, encoding macroscopic temporal patterns.
- A short-window joint-state projector provides immediate motion awareness, complementing the long-horizon force memory, and these memory tokens are injected into the VLA's ==action expert==.

## Results

- FM-VLA achieved an **83.3%** average success rate across three contact-rich, memory-dependent manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming memoryless VLA baselines (**27.8%**) and visual-memory baselines (**33.3%** on 'Press Buttons').
- Demonstrated substantial performance gains in tasks critically dependent on temporal reasoning over force signals, such as 'Press Buttons (N times)' (**72.2%** success) and 'Wipe Dishes (N rounds)' (**77.8%** success).
- The approach introduced a negligible inference overhead of **3.3 ms** compared to the base VLA model, making it computationally efficient and suitable for real-time robotic applications, in contrast to much slower vision-based memory methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Force signals provide a robust and unambiguous source for tracking cumulative contact events and interaction progress over long horizons, particularly in tasks with subtle or ambiguous visual cues.
> - Pretraining a VAE on wrench time-series reconstruction effectively compresses noisy force histories into a few latent tokens, which encode crucial macroscopic temporal patterns like force magnitudes, onset timings, and contact counts.
> - Combining long-term force-based memory with short-term joint-state history is essential for stable and effective contact-rich manipulation, balancing episodic event tracking with immediate motion awareness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models represent a notable area of research in robotic manipulation, leveraging large-scale vision-language pretraining to achieve generalization across various tasks. However, a prevalent characteristic of many existing VLA architectures is their reliance on a Markovian assumption, where decision-making is treated as a memoryless mapping from the current observation to the next action. This assumption limits their applicability in real-world scenarios that demand long-horizon temporal context, such as tasks requiring sequential action counting, tracking interaction progress over multiple steps, or reasoning with partially observed states.

Prior efforts to address these limitations have primarily focused on vision-based memory mechanisms. Approaches like MemoryVLA and MEM incorporate explicit memory banks of past visual observations or multi-scale memory spanning language and short-term visual histories. Despite these advancements, visual memory methods encounter challenges when visual changes are subtle or observations are occluded, leading to ambiguous temporal event tracking. For instance, repeated presses of a button might produce minimal visual displacement, making it difficult for vision-based systems to discern individual contact events. Additionally, visual memory can incur substantial computational overhead due due to the storage and processing of numerous past frames, increasing input token length and inference latency.

Concurrently, there has been increasing interest in integrating force or tactile signals into VLA models, as demonstrated by works such as ForceVLA and TA-VLA. These approaches utilize force signals to enhance action prediction based on current observations or short-term context, recognizing that force measurements provide direct and unambiguous information about interaction dynamics. However, their primary focus has been on instantaneous corrective signals or local interaction states (e.g., detecting contact or measuring applied force), rather than accumulating long-horizon temporal information necessary for non-Markovian decision-making, such as counting repeated actions over an extended period.

FM-VLA positions itself at the intersection of these two research directions by proposing a novel memory mechanism that leverages force signals for *long-horizon* temporal context understanding. It aims to overcome the limitations of vision-based memory in visually ambiguous, contact-rich tasks, while extending the utility of force sensing beyond immediate action correction to encompass episodic interaction tracking. This work, therefore, addresses a gap in the current VLA landscape by offering a lightweight and robust solution for tasks that are inherently non-Markovian and heavily rely on physical contact history.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to augment Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with a mechanism for temporal context reasoning, specifically for non-Markovian, contact-rich manipulation tasks where current observations alone are insufficient for accurate decision-making.

The key motivations and more specific objectives include:

- **Addressing the Markovian Assumption Limitation:** Existing VLA models largely operate under a memoryless paradigm, which is inadequate for tasks that require understanding accumulated temporal context, such as counting repeated actions, tracking interaction progress, or reasoning about partially observed states over an episode. The motivation is to enable VLAs to handle these inherently non-Markovian challenges.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Vision-Based Memory:** While memory-augmented VLAs exist, they often rely on visual memory. This approach faces two major drawbacks:
 - **Computational Expense:** Storing and processing multiple past image frames increases computational load and inference latency.
 - **Visual Ambiguity and Occlusion:** Many critical temporal events, particularly in contact-rich manipulation, result in subtle or no discernible visual changes (e.g., a button press with minimal travel). Visual memory is also susceptible to occlusion. The motivation is to find a more direct and robust signal for these scenarios.
- **Extending the Role of Force Sensing:** Previous VLA models incorporating force or torque sensing primarily use these signals for immediate action correction or to improve prediction based on current observations. They do not typically accumulate long-horizon temporal information about interaction progress. The motivation is to leverage force signals as a lightweight, long-horizon memory mechanism for tracking accumulated contact events and interaction history.
- **Developing a Compact and Informative Force Representation:** Raw force signals are high-frequency, noisy, and unstructured, making direct injection into VLA models challenging for long-horizon tasks. A key objective is to transform this raw history into a meaningful, compact, and informative representation that effectively guides action generation without requiring task-specific labels.
- **Mitigating Repetitive Pre-Contact Behaviors:** Relying solely on long-term force history might lead to a lack of short-term motion awareness, potentially inducing undesirable repetitive behaviors before physical contact is established. An objective is to incorporate complementary information to address this.
- **Demonstrating Effectiveness in Challenging Tasks:** The research aims to evaluate the proposed force-based memory on real-world robotic manipulation tasks that are memory-dependent, contact-rich, and involve visually ambiguous temporal events, thereby demonstrating its practical utility and superiority over existing approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FM-VLA is designed to augment a VLA policy with two complementary proprioceptive streams: a long-horizon wrench history and a short-window joint-state history. The policy is formulated as π(a_t | o_t, l, h_t), where `h_t` is the temporal history representation.

The methodology proceeds in two main stages: a task-agnostic pretraining of a force memory encoder and then fine-tuning of the VLA model.

#### 3.1. FM-VLA Architecture

The FM-VLA architecture builds upon π_0.5_, a VLA model that utilizes a VLM (PaliGemma with a SigLIP vision encoder) to process image and language instructions, which then conditions a flow-matching action expert to generate actions. FM-VLA introduces specialized modules to integrate proprioceptive memory:

- **Wrench History Processing:**
 - **First-order EMA Smoothing:** Raw 6-axis wrench readings (3-axis force, 3-axis torque) are sampled at 30 Hz (down-sampled from 100 Hz sensor) and smoothed using a causal first-order exponential moving average (EMA) with α = 0.3. This process aims to reduce high-frequency noise while preserving relevant event onsets and peaks.
 - **Randomized Noise Pre-padding:** During training, each wrench history is prepended with a random-length prefix of low-amplitude Gaussian noise. This augmentation serves to prevent the model from shortcutting on the sequence length as a cue for episode progress, forcing it to extract temporal structure from the signal content itself. This padding is disabled during inference.

- **Proprioceptive Memory Design:**
 - **Force Memory Encoder (Pretrained VAE):** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is employed to compress long-horizon wrench histories into a compact, informative latent space. This VAE is pretrained in a task-agnostic manner on wrench time-series reconstruction.
 - **Architecture:** Both the encoder and decoder are implemented as Perceiver-IO stacks. The encoder takes a wrench history `F` and processes individual quantile-normalized and Fourier-positionally-encoded wrench tokens through an input MLP. It uses cross-attention to extract features into a small set of `K` (typically 8) learnable latent query tokens, interleaved with self-attention blocks over these latents.
 - **Output:** The encoder outputs posterior parameters (μ_k, log σ_k^2) for each latent query, from which latent representation `Z` (K tokens) is sampled using a reparameterization trick. The decoder reverses this process, reconstructing the wrench sequence.
 - **Short State History Projector:** Recognizing that immediate motion awareness is important, a lightweight mechanism is used for joint-state history. A short window (10 frames, spanning approximately the last 0.9 seconds at 30 Hz, from offsets {−27, −24. . . , −3, 0}) of the most recent joint positions and gripper states is extracted. This `W` × `d_s` tensor is flattened and projected into a single `d_h`-dimensional token (`z_s`) using a zero-initialized linear layer. This module is learned end-to-end with the VLA finetuning without pretraining.

- **Memory Token Injection:**
 - During VLA training and inference, the frozen force encoder provides only the posterior mean μ_f (from `Z` ∈ R^(K×d_z)). This is then projected from the VAE latent dimension `d_z` to the action-expert's hidden dimension `d_h` using another zero-initialized linear layer, yielding `K` wrench memory tokens (Z_f ∈ R^(K×d_h)).
 - These `K` wrench tokens and the single short-state token (`z_s`) are appended to the action-expert suffix, specifically after the noisy-action tokens. This placement ensures that the noisy-action tokens retain their original RoPE positions from the base policy pretraining, while providing memory as additional conditioning for the flow-matching action expert.

#### 3.2. Training

The training of FM-VLA is a two-stage process:

- **Stage 1: Force-VAE Pretraining:**
 - The force memory VAE is trained on the wrench histories collected from all tasks, using an inverse-frequency task sampling strategy to balance contributions from datasets of varying sizes.
 - The objective is a masked-ELBO loss (L_VAE) which includes a masked reconstruction term over valid frames and a free-bits-regularized KL divergence term on each latent dimension. This regularization prevents posterior collapse and encourages the VAE to learn a structured latent space capturing macroscopic temporal patterns like force magnitudes, onset timings, and contact counts.

- **Stage 2: VLA Finetuning:**
 - The pretrained force encoder is frozen and set to evaluation mode, supplying only the posterior mean μ_f.
 - The VLA model, including the VLM encoder, the flow-matching action expert, the short-window state projector (Proj_ψ), and the wrench latent projector (W_f), are fine-tuned end-to-end.
 - The fine-tuning uses a rectified-flow objective, where the model predicts the constant velocity (ϵ − a_0) of a straight-line path between a clean action chunk (a_0) and Gaussian noise (ϵ). The velocity prediction (v_θ) is conditioned on the noisy action chunk (a_k), noise level (k), current observation context (c_t), and the newly introduced force memory (Z_f) and state history (z_s) tokens. Per-view image dropout is also applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FM-VLA was conducted on an AgiBot G1 bimanual humanoid robot across three contact-rich tasks requiring temporal history integration: "Find a Block Under Two Cups", "Press Buttons (N times)", and "Wipe Dishes (N rounds)". Success rates were the primary metric, with 18 trials per task.

#### 4.1. Force Memory Effectiveness

- **Overall Performance:** FM-VLA achieved an average success rate of 83.3%, significantly outperforming all baseline methods.
- **Task-Specific Performance:**
 - **Buttons (N times) and Wipe Dishes (N rounds):** FM-VLA demonstrated a substantial advantage, achieving 72.2% and 77.8% success rates, respectively. These tasks critically depend on precise temporal reasoning over force signals to count discrete contact events that cause minimal visual change.
 - **Memoryless Baselines:** The base VLA, π_0.5_, without temporal conditioning, struggled considerably, with an average success rate of 27.8% and a 0.0% success rate on the wiping task, confirming the inadequacy of memoryless policies for these non-Markovian problems.
 - **Short-Term Force Conditioning:** TA-VLA, which uses a short moving window of recent wrench readings, performed similarly poorly (22.2% average). This indicated that while short-term force signals provide instantaneous contact information, they cannot retain the long-term event counts necessary for tasks requiring episodic memory.
 - **Visual Memory:** The visual-memory baseline, π-MEM, showed moderate improvements on "Find a Block Under Two Cups" and "Wipe Dishes" but performed poorly on "Press Buttons" (33.3%). This outcome supports the claim that visual memory is often insufficient for tasks where state changes are subtle or lack clear visual cues, highlighting the value of force-based memory for such scenarios.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Modality: What to Remember?**
 - **Force History Only:** When only force memory tokens from the VAE were used (without short-term state history), the average success rate dropped to 25.9%. The policy exhibited erratic pre-contact motions due to a lack of short-term spatial awareness.
 - **State History Only:** Relying solely on proprioceptive joint states resulted in an average success rate of 40.7%. While it achieved 100% success on the spatially-driven "Find a Block Under Two Cups" task, it failed on contact-driven counting tasks.
 - **Conclusion:** These ablations demonstrated that combining long-term force memory (for contact events) with short-term state history (for pre-contact motion awareness) is crucial for stable and effective contact-rich manipulation.

- **Architecture: Why VAE?**
 - **GRU Long-History Encoder:** Replacing the VAE with a GRU recurrent encoder for force history resulted in an average success rate of 33.3%. The GRU struggled with vanishing gradients over long, high-frequency sequences, leading to the loss of early contact events (e.g., 5.6% on Wipe).
 - **Q-Former Long-History Encoder:** Using a Q-Former cross-attention module, trained end-to-end, yielded an average success rate of 57.4%. This approach tended to overfit to instantaneous peaks rather than capturing the holistic temporal structure of force signals.
 - **Conclusion:** The VAE, pretrained on a continuous wrench reconstruction objective, proved more effective. It compelled the latent space to encode macroscopic structures like force magnitudes, onset timings, and contact counts into a few tokens, making task-relevant signals more accessible for the VLA action expert.

- **Capacity: How Many Tokens?**
 - Ablation on the number of VAE latent tokens (K) for the "Wipe Dishes" task revealed that 8 tokens delivered the peak success rate (77.8%).
 - Using fewer tokens (4) created an informational bottleneck, degrading performance.
 - Using more tokens (16 or 32) also unexpectedly degraded performance (55.6% and 33.3% respectively). This was attributed to a potential distribution shift, as the pretrained π_0.5_ action expert is optimized for a limited total number of input tokens, and exceeding this limit with many additional force tokens might disrupt coherent action generation.

#### 4.3. Inference Efficiency

- FM-VLA exhibited a latency of 64.0 ms, introducing a negligible overhead of 3.3 ms compared to the base π_0.5_ model (60.7 ms).
- In contrast, π-MEM (vision-based memory) demonstrated significantly higher latencies, ranging from 99.8 ms (with K=5 memory frames, 39.1 ms overhead) to 190.0 ms (with K=16 memory frames, 129.3 ms overhead).
- This comparison highlights the efficiency advantage of FM-VLA, indicating that force-based memory can provide robust temporal context with minimal impact on real-time operation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FM-VLA model contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a novel approach to temporal context understanding in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly for contact-rich and non-Markovian tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in VLA Capabilities:** Current VLA models often struggle with tasks requiring long-horizon temporal reasoning, especially when visual cues are ambiguous or insufficient. FM-VLA directly addresses this by providing a mechanism for VLAs to accumulate and leverage interaction history, expanding their applicability to a broader range of complex real-world manipulation scenarios.
- **Novelty of Force-Based Temporal Memory:** This work is positioned as the first to treat the proprioceptive-wrench stream as a lightweight, long-horizon memory for VLAs, distinct from its traditional use for instantaneous conditioning. This paradigm shift offers a robust alternative to vision-based memory, particularly in scenarios where physical contact dynamics are more informative than visual changes.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** By encoding force histories into compact memory tokens, FM-VLA offers a more direct and unambiguous representation of contact events and interaction progress compared to visual signals, which can be prone to ambiguity, occlusion, or subtle changes. This can lead to more reliable task execution in contact-rich environments.
- **Computational Efficiency:** FM-VLA introduces minimal inference overhead (3.3 ms) compared to the base VLA model. This efficiency advantage over vision-based memory approaches (which can add tens to hundreds of milliseconds in latency) is crucial for real-time robotic applications where prompt decision-making is essential.
- **Effective Two-Stage Learning Paradigm:** The methodology of pretraining a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) on wrench time-series reconstruction in a task-agnostic manner, followed by fine-tuning the VLA, demonstrates an effective way to extract meaningful representations from noisy, high-frequency sensor data. This approach could be generalizable to other sensor modalities and tasks, fostering structured latent spaces that capture macroscopic interaction patterns.
- **Improved Performance in Challenging Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate significant performance gains (e.g., from 0% to 77.8% success rate on wiping tasks) in memory-dependent, contact-rich manipulation tasks that are challenging for memoryless and vision-based memory baselines. This showcases the practical utility of force-based memory.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, such as exploring hierarchical or adaptive compression techniques for handling extremely long-horizon tasks that might exceed the capacity of a fixed 8-token bottleneck. Additionally, pretraining the force VAE on larger, more diverse robot datasets could further enhance the generalizability and performance of the force memory encoder.

In summary, FM-VLA represents a step towards more capable and generalist robotic systems by enabling them to understand and act upon the rich temporal context inherent in physical interactions, leading to more robust and efficient manipulation in complex real-world settings.
