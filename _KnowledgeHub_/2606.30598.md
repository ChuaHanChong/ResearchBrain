# Towards in-the-wild Egocentric 3D Hand-Object Pose Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Bristol and Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems introduced EPIC-Contact, a new large-scale dataset featuring 3D hand-object pose and contact annotations for in-the-wild egocentric videos. They also developed HOPformer, an end-to-end learning framework that leverages hand priors and cross-attention to jointly estimate bi-manual hand and object poses, achieving state-of-the-art performance with a 6.2 percentage point increase in success rate (SR@0.05) on ARCTIC and improved generalization to EPIC-Contact.

## Problem

- Existing 3D hand-object pose estimation methods relied heavily on 'in-lab' datasets with limited diversity, backgrounds, and scripted interactions, leading to poor generalization in real-world egocentric videos.
- Lack of large-scale, diverse 3D ground-truth supervision for unscripted, in-the-wild egocentric interactions posed a significant bottleneck for training robust learning-based models.
- Joint hand-object pose estimation approaches often struggled to effectively model the intricate interactions and mutual occlusions between hands and objects, limiting accuracy in challenging scenes.

## Method

- Created **EPIC-Contact**, a new dataset with 62.3K frames from 2,272 egocentric video clips, featuring dense 3D hand-object pose and ==bijective contact annotations== derived via an ==optimization pipeline (EC-fit)== from manual contact labels.
- Developed **HOPformer**, an end-to-end ==Transformer-based== model for joint 3D bi-manual hand-object pose estimation from single RGB images, incorporating a ==cross-attention decoder== to explicitly condition object features on ==specialized hand priors==.
- Utilized a ==two-stage training strategy==, pre-training HOPformer on exocentric ARCTIC data before fine-tuning on both ARCTIC and the new egocentric EPIC-Contact dataset to enhance robustness and generalization.

## Results

- HOPformer achieved a **82.4%** Success Rate at 0.05 object diameter (SR@0.05) on the ARCTIC egocentric dataset, improving by **6.2 percentage points** over the previous state-of-the-art, and reduced Contact Deviation (CDev) to **31.9 mm**.
- On the challenging in-the-wild EPIC-Contact dataset, HOPformer improved SR@0.05 from **17.6%** (baseline) to **29.8%** and reduced CDev from **30.1 mm** to **20.7 mm**, demonstrating better generalization.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the cross-attention decoder, specialized hand priors, and the interaction loss were crucial for HOPformer's performance, with a **99.5%** object classification accuracy on ARCTIC and an inference time of approximately **9.12 frames per second**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Manual annotation of fine-grained contact regions on hand meshes, combined with a contact-axis representation for objects and an optimization framework (EC-fit), enables scalable generation of 3D hand-object pose and contact ground truth for in-the-wild egocentric videos.
> - Explicitly modeling the interaction between hand and object features via a cross-attention mechanism, where object features are conditioned on strong hand priors, significantly improves joint pose estimation accuracy and physical consistency, especially under occlusion and ambiguity.
> - A specialized hand pose encoder (WiLoR) provides more effective hand priors for the cross-attention decoder compared to generic visual features, highlighting the importance of domain-specific feature representations for robust interaction modeling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Estimating the 3D pose of hands and objects during interaction is a research area with implications for various human-centric applications, including augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), robotics, human-computer interaction, and assistive technologies. Prior work in this domain has largely relied on data collected in controlled, "in-lab" environments, often utilizing expensive motion capture (MoCap) systems. These controlled settings, while providing accurate ground truth 3D poses, typically feature limited backgrounds, object instances, and highly scripted interactions, which do not reflect the complexity and diversity of real-world scenarios.

A significant challenge in extending these methods to "in-the-wild" egocentric videos, captured from a first-person perspective, arises from several factors. These include the wide variety of objects and hand poses, severe mutual occlusions between hands and objects, ambiguous contact regions, and the difficulty in obtaining large-scale 3D ground-truth supervision for unscripted data. Existing learning-based methods, while showing progress in controlled settings, often demonstrate limited generalization capabilities when applied to unconstrained environments. Approaches for joint hand-object pose estimation have historically struggled to effectively model the intricate interactions between hand and object poses, impacting their performance in diverse and challenging scenes. Some works explore CAD-free object reconstruction, but their practical performance in complex scenes is reported to be insufficient. This research addresses the noted limitations by introducing a new dataset designed for in-the-wild egocentric scenarios and proposing a learning-based model intended to overcome the generalization challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of 3D hand-object pose estimation by enabling accurate recovery of both hand and object poses from unscripted, "in-the-wild" egocentric RGB videos in a single forward pass. This overarching goal is broken down into two main specific objectives, each motivated by identified limitations in the existing research landscape.

#### 2.1. Motivation for Addressing Data Scarcity (EPIC-Contact)

Current methods for 3D hand-object pose estimation are significantly constrained by the lack of diverse, large-scale 3D ground-truth supervision for in-the-wild interactions. The prevailing approach for acquiring such data involves expensive motion capture (MoCap) systems. However, MoCap systems necessitate careful calibration, are difficult to scale for wide adoption, and typically yield data with minimalist backgrounds that do not represent the cluttered and complex environments of real-world egocentric scenes. This data scarcity hinders the development and evaluation of learning-based models that can generalize effectively to unconstrained settings.

- **Objective 1: Introduce a scalable, in-the-wild egocentric dataset with dense 3D hand-object pose and contact annotations.**
 To overcome the supervision bottleneck, the authors aimed to create a novel dataset, **EPIC-Contact**, by introducing a pipeline for obtaining 3D annotations from existing egocentric video clips. This dataset is designed to feature diverse backgrounds, cluttered environments, and challenging object characteristics (e.g., small, transparent, heavily occluded objects) within natural interactions. A core motivation was to develop an efficient manual annotation process for bijective 3D contact correspondences on both hands and objects, which could then be leveraged by an optimization framework to derive posed 3D hand-object meshes. This approach sought to provide the necessary ground truth for training and evaluating models on complex real-world data.

#### 2.2. Motivation for Robust Joint Pose Estimation (HOPformer)

While progress has been made in individual 3D hand pose estimation, joint hand-object pose estimation methods have often failed to adequately model the intricate interactions between hands and objects. This inadequacy is particularly evident in challenging scenarios involving severe occlusions and ambiguous contact, where mutual context between the hand and object is critical for accurate pose prediction. Existing learning-based approaches often do not leverage strong hand priors effectively, limiting their ability to generalize to diverse in-the-wild scenes.

- **Objective 2: Propose an end-to-end learning framework for joint 3D bi-manual hand-object pose estimation that leverages strong hand priors.**
 Motivated by the robustness of data-driven 3D hand reconstruction methods, the authors aimed to design a network, **HOPformer**, that could guide joint 3D hand and object pose estimation by integrating structural hand priors into object feature representations. The goal was to develop a single-pass model capable of predicting the poses of two hands and the interacting object, ensuring robustness across both controlled (in-lab) and unconstrained (in-the-wild) images. The emphasis was on a cross-attention framework to explicitly model the interaction between hand and object features, leading to more accurate and robust pose predictions, especially under occlusion.

In summary, the research is driven by the need for more realistic training data and more robust models to perform 3D hand-object pose estimation in complex, unscripted egocentric videos, thereby bridging the gap between controlled lab settings and practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the stated objectives through two main contributions: the introduction of the EPIC-Contact dataset and the development of the HOPformer model.

#### 3.1. EPIC-Contact Dataset Creation

The EPIC-Contact dataset is developed to provide 3D posed hand-object meshes for in-the-wild egocentric images. The pipeline leverages existing video clips from the EPIC-Grasps dataset, which features diverse hand-object interactions and 3D meshes for 9 object categories, crucially identifying segments of "stable grasp."

- **Annotating Hand Contact Regions:**
 The process begins by annotating hand regions in contact with an object. Annotators use a "vertex painting" tool on a uniformly subdivided MANO hand mesh (N_V = 3106 vertices) to capture fine-grained contact. Videos provide richer context for annotators to infer and label contact, including occluded regions. Inter-annotator agreement is validated using Fleiss’ Kappa score (κ_h = 0.61).

- **Contact Regions on Objects (Bijective Contact Transfer):**
 To establish a bijective mapping between hand and object contact vertices, a "contact axis" representation (from ContactEdit) is used. The hand is divided into three regions (thumb, four fingers, palm). For each region, a 2-DoF contact axis is defined and transferred to the object surface via two clicks, facilitating corresponding contact points on the object. The distance between fingers for the "four fingers" region is dynamically adjusted by selecting from 41 common flat hand pose configurations based on geodesic distance to an estimated hand pose from WiLoR. Inter-annotator agreement for object contact (κ_o = 0.62) is reported.

- **Scaling the Objects:**
 To match the template object mesh scale to the actual object instance in the video, a Vision-Language Model (VLM, Gemini 2.5) is employed. The VLM estimates multiple degrees-of-scale (e.g., diameter and handle length for a pan) using class-specific prompts, enabling non-uniform scaling of object meshes. This scaling is verified against real-world measurements for a subset of objects, achieving a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.94 cm (5.9% relative error).

- **EC-fit Pipeline: From Contact to Posed Hand-Object Meshes:**
 This optimization-based pipeline derives 3D hand-object interaction meshes from the contact annotations.
 - **Pose Initialization:** 3D hand pose is initialized using WiLoR on the clip's central frame. Object pose is initialized using multiple strategies, including random poses and category-aware priors.
 - **Contact-based Alignment:** The pipeline optimizes object rotation (r_o) and translation (t_o) by minimizing a contact loss (L_con) that measures the squared distance between bijective hand and object contact vertices.
 - **Image-guided Refinement:** Hand and object poses are refined using the central frame (I) with an occlusion-aware mask loss (L_om), which excludes occluded object regions. A hand mask loss (L_hm) is applied to the hand. A penetration loss (L_p) is included to prevent interpenetration. A regularization loss (L_reg) maintains consistency with initial hand pose predictions. Manual verification and correction are integrated.
 - **Clip-level Annotations:** For clips with stable grasps, the object-to-hand pose estimated for the central frame is broadcast across the entire clip, leveraging hand poses from WiLoR. Per-frame confidence scores are generated based on temporal smoothness.

#### 3.2. HOPformer: Hand-Object Pose Transformer

HOPformer is an end-to-end learning framework designed for joint 3D bi-manual hand-object pose estimation from an RGB image.

- **Overview and Notations:**
 The model takes an RGB image as input and predicts the pose (Θ = {θ, β} for MANO hands; ω = {R_o, T_o, articulation} for objects) for visible hands and the interacting object.

- **Learning-based Pose Estimation:**
 - **Feature Extraction:** A DINOv2 (ViT-G backbone) extracts generic object tokens (z_o, Φ_o). WiLoR extracts specialized hand pose tokens (z_h, Φ_h).
 - **Cross-Attention Decoder:** Object tokens are linearly projected into a query sequence (X_o), and hand tokens into a memory sequence (X_h). These are fed into an L-layer Transformer Decoder (D_θ), where L is set to 12. In each layer, object representations undergo self-attention, then cross-attention to attend to hand context, and finally a position-wise feed-forward network. This process refines object features by conditioning them on hand priors.
 - **Aggregation Module:** The refined object sequence (X_o^(L)) is passed through a residual connection, a learnable linear aggregation module (A), and layer normalization to produce aggregated interaction features (z_i).
 - **Prediction Heads:** The feature sequence z_i is routed to dedicated MLPs that predict different components of hand poses (right/left hand global orientation, joint angles, shape, root) and articulated object pose.

- **Object Mesh Retrieval:**
 To provide a 3D reconstruction, a classification head is added to the object features (z_o) to predict the object's category (c). The predicted class is used to retrieve the corresponding 3D mesh (O_c) from a predefined model pool (M). The regressed pose (ω) is then applied to the retrieved mesh.

- **Training Signal (Losses):**
 HOPformer is trained using a combination of frame-level losses:
 - **Hand Losses (L_h):** L_h_2D (2D joint projection), L_h_3D (3D joint), L_h_θ (MANO pose), L_h_β (MANO shape), L_h_T (weak-perspective camera parameters).
 - **Object Losses (L_o):** L_o_3D (3D keypoint), L_o_2D (2D keypoint projection), L_o_T (weak-perspective camera), L_o_c (classification), L_o_ω (object pose, using geodesic loss for rotation).
 - **Interaction Loss (L_int):** A CDev-based loss (L_int_ro, L_int_lo) is added for frames where the hand is in contact with an object.
 The overall loss is L = L_r + L_l + L_o + L_int. The model is trained in two stages: pre-training on exocentric views (ARCTIC) followed by fine-tuning on egocentric views (ARCTIC and EPIC-Contact). AdamW optimizer is used with specific learning rate schedules. A weak perspective camera model and 6D rotation representation are employed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results for both the EPIC-Contact dataset and the HOPformer model, evaluated on both in-lab and in-the-wild datasets.

#### 4.1. EPIC-Contact Dataset Characteristics and Quality

- **Scale and Diversity:** EPIC-Contact comprises 2,272 video clips (62.3K frames) featuring 9 object categories, providing diverse in-the-wild egocentric interactions.
- **Annotation Quality:** The hand contact region annotations achieved a Fleiss’ Kappa score (κ_h) of 0.61, indicating consistent annotator agreement. The object contact annotations achieved κ_o of 0.62.
- **EC-fit Accuracy:** Evaluation of the EC-fit pipeline on a subset of the ARCTIC dataset showed that the pipeline yields posed meshes with a Pose L2 Error of 1.9 mm and a Mean Relative-Root Position Error (MRRPE) of 8.0 mm compared to 3D ground truth. The hand-object penetration depth (0.79 cm) and volume (20.9 cm³) for EPIC-Contact meshes are comparable to those found in MoCap-based datasets like ARCTIC.
- **Robustness to Annotation Noise:** HOPformer demonstrated robustness to the small errors present in the EC-fit generated poses, with performance metrics remaining largely stable when trained with EC-fit poses versus ground truth MoCap data on ARCTIC.

#### 4.2. HOPformer Performance on ARCTIC (In-lab, Egocentric)

HOPformer was evaluated against state-of-the-art learning-based baselines (ArcticNet-SF and JointTransformer) on the ARCTIC dataset's egocentric split.
- **Overall Performance:** HOPformer achieved a 82.4% Success Rate at 0.05 object diameter (SR@0.05), representing a 6.2 percentage point improvement over JointTransformer, which was the previous state-of-the-art.
- **Contact and Relative Pose:** Contact Deviation (CDev) was reduced from 35.0 mm to 31.9 mm, and Mean Relative-Root Position Error (MRRPE_ro) was 29.4 mm (compared to 29.9 mm for JointTransformer).
- **Motion Consistency:** Motion Deviation (MDev) was significantly reduced from 10.4 mm to 7.3 mm, and Acceleration Error (ACC_h/o) showed improvements (4.8 / 6.2 m/s² vs. 6.9 / 10.1 m/s²).
- **Hand Accuracy:** Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) for hands improved from 20.0 mm to 16.1 mm.
- **Object Properties:** Average Articulation Error (AAE) was comparable (5.0° vs. 4.9°). Object classification accuracy was 99.5%.
- **Exocentric Results:** HOPformer also outperformed baselines on the ARCTIC exocentric split, showing similar or larger margins of improvement across most metrics.

#### 4.3. HOPformer Performance on EPIC-Contact (In-the-wild, Egocentric)

- **Generalization:** HOPformer demonstrated improved pose estimation and interaction consistency on the challenging EPIC-Contact dataset compared to baselines.
- **Contact and Relative Pose:** CDev was reduced from 30.1 mm (JointTransformer) to 20.7 mm. MRRPE_ro improved from 78.6 mm to 65.8 mm.
- **Motion Consistency:** MDev was reduced from 20.0 mm to 11.4 mm, and ACC_h/o improved (2.5 / 4.1 m/s² vs. 3.1 / 9.5 m/s²).
- **Hand Accuracy:** MPJPE for hands improved from 22.9 mm to 19.9 mm.
- **Object Properties:** SR@0.05 increased from 17.6% to 29.8%, and SR@0.1 from 56.9% to 69.7%. Object classification accuracy was 52.9%.
- **Challenge of EPIC-Contact:** The substantial drop in baseline performance from ARCTIC to EPIC-Contact (e.g., JointTransformer's SR@0.05 from 76.2% to 17.6%) highlights the increased difficulty posed by novel object instances, transparency, occlusion, and clutter in the in-the-wild dataset.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (on ARCTIC)

- **Loss Contributions:** All training loss terms contributed to performance. Object supervision losses (L_o) and the interaction loss (L_int) were identified as particularly critical for object pose accuracy and physical consistency, respectively. Keypoint supervision losses (L_3D_o,h) were most important for hand accuracy.
- **Architectural Design:**
 - Replacing the cross-attention decoder with naive feature concatenation and an MLP resulted in a sharp performance collapse, confirming the importance of hand-conditioned object feature modulation.
 - Using generic DINOv2 features instead of pose-specialized WiLoR features for the hand prior degraded both hand and object quality, underscoring the value of specialized hand priors.
 - Disabling self-attention within the decoder and removing the learnable aggregation module consistently hurt performance, indicating their roles in token communication and compact representation learning.
 - Increasing decoder depth (L) up to 12 layers progressively improved performance, supporting the iterative refinement mechanism.
- **Scalability:** HOPformer demonstrated improved performance on most metrics as the number of object categories in the training set increased, suggesting its generalizability.
- **Cross-dataset Transfer:** Initializing HOPformer with weights pre-trained on ARCTIC consistently improved performance on EPIC-Contact compared to training from scratch, indicating the benefits of additional data during pre-training.
- **Runtime:** HOPformer achieved a median inference time of 106.63 ms per frame (approximately 9.12 frames per second) on an NVIDIA GH200 GPU.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes contributions to the field of 3D hand-object pose estimation, particularly for in-the-wild egocentric scenarios.

**Dataset Contribution:** The introduction of **EPIC-Contact** addresses a critical bottleneck in research by providing a large-scale (2.3K clips, 62.3K frames), diverse, and challenging dataset with unique dense, bijective 3D hand-object contact correspondences and posed meshes. Unlike prior MoCap-based datasets, EPIC-Contact captures complex, cluttered, and unscripted real-world interactions. This dataset is designed to serve as a benchmark for training and evaluating future hand-object pose estimation methods, potentially driving advancements in model robustness and generalization capabilities beyond controlled laboratory settings. Its detailed annotation pipeline, including VLM-based object scaling and quality checks, offers a scalable approach for generating such supervision.

**Model Contribution:** The proposed **HOPformer** model represents an end-to-end, learning-based approach that jointly predicts bi-manual hand and object poses from a single RGB image. A key aspect of HOPformer is its cross-attention decoder, which explicitly conditions object features on strong hand priors derived from a specialized hand pose encoder. This architectural design enables the model to effectively leverage contextual information between hands and objects, particularly under severe occlusions and ambiguous contact, which are common in egocentric videos. By achieving state-of-the-art results on both the established in-lab ARCTIC benchmark and the newly introduced in-the-wild EPIC-Contact dataset, HOPformer demonstrates improved accuracy and generalization. The model's ability to maintain physical consistency (reduced CDev and MDev) and improve object pose estimation in challenging scenes is significant.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancement in Perception Systems:** The work contributes to developing perception systems that can robustly understand human-object interactions in unconstrained environments. This is a foundational step for applications requiring a detailed understanding of how humans manipulate objects.
- **Robotics:** Improved hand-object pose estimation could enhance robotic manipulation capabilities, enabling robots to better understand human intentions during collaboration or to perform complex tasks in dynamic, human-centric environments.
- **Augmented/Virtual Reality:** More accurate 3D hand-object reconstruction in real-time could lead to more immersive and interactive AR/VR experiences, where virtual objects can be manipulated realistically.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** The ability to precisely track hand and object poses can enable more natural and intuitive interfaces for human-computer interaction, including gesture recognition and assistive technologies.
- **Future Research Catalyst:** The release of the EPIC-Contact dataset, HOPformer code, and checkpoints provides open resources for the research community, encouraging further exploration into joint hand-object pose estimation, contact reasoning, and generalization to real-world scenarios.

The limitations, such as the time-consuming annotation process and current focus on a limited number of object categories, are acknowledged, suggesting avenues for future research to further automate annotation and scale to a broader range of objects and articulated interactions.
