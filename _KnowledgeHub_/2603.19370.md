# VAMPO: Policy Optimization for Improving Visual Dynamics in Video Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VAMPO introduces a post-training framework that optimizes the visual dynamics of diffusion-based video action models using reinforcement learning. This approach refines the precision of predicted visual dynamics, leading to improved action generation and robust performance in complex robotic manipulation tasks across simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based video action models, trained with likelihood-surrogate objectives, do not explicitly optimize for the high-precision visual dynamics crucial for robotic control.
- This objective mismatch leads to subtle errors in predicted object pose, spatial relationships, and contact timing, which are then amplified when consumed by downstream robotic policies.
- Amplified errors result in compounding failures, reduced task success rates, and limit the robustness of VLA systems in fine-grained manipulation.

## Method

- The multi-step ==denoising== process of the Video Prediction Model (VPM) is re-framed as a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)== to enable direct ==policy optimization==.
- An ==Euler Hybrid sampler== is introduced, applying ==SDE-style stochasticity== only at the first denoising step, enabling tractable ==policy gradient== estimation while preserving temporal consistency.
- The VPM is fine-tuned using ==GRPO== with a verifiable relative ==latent-consistency reward== that directly measures alignment with expert future visual dynamics.

## Results

- VAMPO achieved the highest task completion rates across various task lengths (1-10 steps) and the best average trajectory length on CALVIN ABC→D and L-CALVIN benchmarks, outperforming existing state-of-the-art VLM- and VPM-based methods.
- The method demonstrated consistent improvements in video generation quality and reduced L1 loss in latent space across multiple real-world robot manipulation scenarios, including grasping and precise placement.
- Ablation studies confirmed that GRPO was more effective than DDPO, and applying SDE-style denoising only at the first step yielded superior performance compared to multi-step stochasticity or pixel-level rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly optimizing for control-relevant visual dynamics in diffusion models via RL significantly enhances their utility for robotic control, surpassing the limitations of purely likelihood-based training.
> - The selective application of stochasticity (Euler Hybrid sampler) at the most critical early denoising step provides stable and efficient policy gradient updates, mitigating long-horizon credit assignment.
> - Improvements in visual dynamics lead to a richer vision-action coupling, allowing downstream action models to leverage a larger number of independent visual directions for more sophisticated and nuanced control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems for robotic control, specifically focusing on Video Action Models (VAMs). VAMs represent a paradigm shift from traditional Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by explicitly learning and predicting visual dynamics from large-scale video data. This approach offers two primary advantages: enabling scalable pretraining from video corpora without requiring action annotations, and providing a strong prior for downstream action learning in robotic manipulation by capturing state evolution.

Historically, VAMs have evolved from predicting pixel-level future images, which are intuitive but computationally intensive and often contain extraneous visual details. The field has increasingly transitioned to latent-level representations, which encode dynamics more compactly and efficiently. These latent representations, often extracted from intermediate denoising features of diffusion models, aim to suppress nuisance visual variability while retaining task-relevant dynamics and affordances, thus facilitating more robust control.

Despite this progress, a fundamental limitation persists in current diffusion-based video predictors: they are typically trained using likelihood-surrogate objectives, such as ELBO-style formulations. While these objectives encourage globally plausible predictions aligned with the data distribution, they do not explicitly optimize for the precision-critical visual dynamics necessary for fine-grained manipulation tasks. This objective mismatch can lead to subtle but significant errors in areas like object pose, spatial relations, and contact timing, which are then amplified when consumed by downstream robotic policies.

VAMPO directly addresses this limitation by integrating principles from reinforcement learning (RL) within the generative process of diffusion models. This approach builds upon recent advancements in training diffusion models with RL, such as Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO) and its extensions like GRPO. By framing multi-step denoising as a sequential decision process, VAMPO aims to directly optimize the generative model for control-relevant dynamics, thereby bridging the gap between generative plausibility and operational precision in VLA systems. The work offers a post-training framework that enhances the utility of existing video action models without requiring architectural changes, positioning it as a significant refinement in the development of more reliable and precise robot control systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to directly enhance the precision of visual dynamics within video action models through policy optimization, thereby improving their utility for robotic manipulation.

The motivation for this objective stems from a critical identified limitation in current video action models:
1. **Objective Mismatch in Training:** Existing diffusion-based video prediction models (VPMs) are predominantly trained using likelihood-surrogate objectives, such as the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). While these objectives are effective at encouraging the model to generate globally plausible video frames or latent representations that align with the training data distribution, they do not explicitly optimize for the specific, high-precision visual dynamics crucial for robotic control.
2. **Precision-Critical Errors:** This objective mismatch frequently leads to subtle yet significant errors in the predicted visual dynamics. These errors manifest as inaccuracies in object pose, fine-grained spatial relationships between objects and the robot, and the precise timing of contact events. For example, a prediction might appear visually coherent but show a gripper slightly misaligned with an object, a small gap where contact should occur, or a contact event happening a frame too early or late.
3. **Amplification in Downstream Policies:** When these subtly erroneous visual representations are fed into a downstream action generation model (AGM) or control policy, their inaccuracies are not merely carried over but can be substantially amplified. Near decision boundaries, even minor representational errors can cause the policy to select an incorrect action (e.g., closing a gripper prematurely, attempting to pass an obstacle on the wrong side). This leads to an undesirable feedback loop where an incorrect action alters the environment state, further deviating from the intended trajectory, and ultimately resulting in compounding errors and reduced task success rates.
4. **Need for Direct Optimization:** To overcome these compounding failures and achieve robust, precise manipulation, the research is motivated by the need for a training paradigm that directly optimizes the VPM for the accuracy of its predicted visual dynamics, rather than relying solely on indirect likelihood-based surrogates. By formulating the multi-step denoising process as a sequential decision process, the aim is to introduce a reward function that explicitly measures consistency with expert visual dynamics in latent space. This direct optimization strategy is hypothesized to penalize control-critical errors more effectively, thereby narrowing the gap between the model's generative capabilities and its requirements for action-centric deployment.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond the limitations of purely generative objectives and implement a post-training framework that explicitly aligns the visual predictions of VAMs with the exacting precision demanded by robotic manipulation tasks, leading to more reliable action generation and enhanced generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VAMPO (Video Action Model Policy Optimization) proposes a post-training framework that applies policy optimization to enhance the visual dynamics of diffusion-based video action models. The methodology builds upon the standard latent-level video action modeling paradigm, which consists of a Video Prediction Model (VPM) and an Action Generation Model (AGM).

**Base Video Action Model Paradigm:**
- **Video Prediction Model (VPM):** This module anticipates a latent representation of the future, conditioned on the current observation and a language instruction. The VPM is instantiated using Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), a latent video diffusion model. It is initially trained to reconstruct a clean latent representation (x₀), encoded from an expert future video clip (N), by minimizing an L2 loss between the predicted clean latent and the target (L_Video). During inference, a predictive visual representation (`h-hat`) is extracted from the multi-layer hidden states of the denoiser at the first denoising step, designed to capture high-level spatiotemporal structure.
- **Action Generation Model (AGM):** This module generates an action sequence (`a₀`) conditioned on the VPM's predictive visual representation (`h-hat`) and the language instruction (`l`). A diffusion policy is adopted for the AGM, trained to reconstruct a ground-truth action sequence by minimizing an L2 loss (L_Action).

**VAMPO's Post-Training Framework:**
VAMPO's approach modifies the VPM's denoising process to enable direct optimization of visual dynamics via reinforcement learning.

1. **Problem Formulation as an MDP:**
 - The multi-step denoising process of the VPM is re-framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
 - **State (sᵢ):** Defined by the noisy latent (x_σᵢ), the current noise level (σᵢ), and the conditioning input (cᵥ = (o, l)).
 - **Action (aᵢ):** The predicted next denoised latent (x_σᵢ₋₁).
 - **Initial State (ρ₀):** A distribution over initial conditions (p(cᵥ)) at the highest noise level.
 - **Transition Kernel (P):** Describes how the state changes after an action is taken.
 - **Reward Function (R):** A terminal reward R(sᵢ, aᵢ) is provided only at the final denoising step (i=0), denoted as r(x_σ₀), which evaluates the quality of the generated output.
 - **Objective:** To learn a denoising policy π that maximizes the expected cumulative reward J(π).

2. **Denoising with SDE-Sampling (Euler-Ancestral):**
 - The standard VPM uses a deterministic Euler Discrete solver (ODE), which lacks the stochasticity and tractable transition density required for policy gradient methods.
 - To enable policy optimization, VAMPO introduces the Euler-Ancestral sampler. This transforms the ODE update into an SDE transition by injecting step-wise Gaussian noise. This yields an explicit Gaussian transition density, p_θ(x_σᵢ₋₁ | x_σᵢ, cᵥ), which is necessary for policy gradient estimation.

3. **Reinforcement Learning with Euler Hybrid Sampler:**
 - **Euler Hybrid Sampler (Core Design):** To mitigate long-horizon credit assignment problems and preserve temporal consistency, VAMPO introduces a hybrid sampling strategy. SDE-style stochasticity (Euler-Ancestral) is applied *only at the very first denoising step* (from σ_I to σ_I₋₁). For all subsequent denoising steps (i < I), the deterministic Euler Discrete update function is used.
 - **Rationale:** The first denoising step is critical for forming the action-relevant visual representation (`h-hat`). Limiting stochasticity to this step allows for tractable, low-variance policy-gradient estimation via reparameterization while preventing "reward hacking" from perturbations in later steps that are less relevant to the early-stage visual dynamics. It also improves training stability and efficiency by reducing the number of steps requiring backpropagation through stochastic operations.
 - **Optimization with GRPO:** VAMPO employs Group-normalized Proximal Policy Optimization (GRPO) to fine-tune the VPM.
 - **Rollout:** For each conditional input, the VPM performs G rollouts using the Euler Hybrid sampler to generate G diverse candidate future latent representations ({x_g_σ₀}).
 - **Verifiable Relative Latent-Consistency Reward:** For each generated latent (x_g_σ₀), a reward `r` is computed by comparing it against the ground-truth expert future latent (x₀). This reward is a combination of negative L1 distance and cosine similarity: `r = -λ_L1 ||x_σ₀ - x₀||₁ + λ_cos (x_σ₀ · x₀ / (||x_σ₀|| ||x₀||))`. This non-adversarial reward directly measures alignment in the latent space.
 - **Advantage Calculation:** Group-normalized advantages (A_g) are calculated for each sample (r_g - mean(rewards) / std(rewards)). Since the reward is terminal, this advantage is applied to the single stochastic denoising step.
 - **Policy Update:** The VPM's parameters (θ) are updated by maximizing a GRPO objective, which is a clipped surrogate objective similar to PPO, using the calculated advantages. This process iteratively guides the VPM to generate high-reward denoising trajectories that are more consistent with expert dynamics.

**Summary of Training Procedure:**
The training involves sampling conditions and expert future representations, generating multiple rollouts with the Euler Hybrid sampler, computing verifiable latent-consistency rewards, calculating group-normalized advantages, and finally updating the VPM's policy parameters using the GRPO objective. The AGM can be frozen or fine-tuned concurrently or sequentially, with the paper exploring both options.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of VAMPO across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, addressing five key questions.

**Q1. Visual Dynamics Modeling Quality:**
- **Quantitative:** Analysis of L1 loss between predicted and ground-truth latents during training shows an overall improving trend. While minor fluctuations are observed, the visual dynamics encoded in the latent space progressively become more coherent and structured as post-training proceeds.
- **Qualitative:** Visual comparisons indicate that the VAMPO-optimized VPM produces latent representations that decode into more accurate object poses, spatial relationships, and contact progression than the baseline. This leads to outcomes such as "hallucination suppression," "planning correction," and "action rectification."
- **Real-World Validation:** VAMPO demonstrates consistent improvements in video generation quality across three real-world scenes with varied robot embodiments and background conditions (Agibot Genie 01, Flexiv, VidowX), evidenced by reduced L1 loss values after post-training.

**Q2. Policy Performance Gains:**
- **Simulated Environment (CALVIN ABC→D):**
 - Optimizing only the VPM (with the downstream AGM frozen) consistently improves overall task completion metrics, including average trajectory length, albeit modestly. This indicates that improved visual dynamics alone enhance the foundation for action generation.
 - When *both* the VPM is post-trained with VAMPO and the AGM is subsequently fine-tuned, there are substantially larger improvements in task success rates and average trajectory length. This suggests that optimized visual dynamics enable more accurate action dynamics and facilitate better downstream policy learning.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks (L-CALVIN):** VAMPO yields larger performance gains on longer-horizon manipulation tasks (up to 10 steps), highlighting the importance of precise visual dynamics modeling for complex, extended task sequences.
- **Real-World Scenarios:** VAMPO consistently outperforms the base policy across multiple real-world manipulation benchmarks, including grasping in clutter, single-arm precise placement, and dual-arm coordinated grasp-and-place on the Agibot Genie 01 platform.

**Q3. Mechanisms of Improved Policy Performance:**
- **Vision-Action Coupling:** To quantify the relationship between visual dynamics and actions, the effective rank (ER) of the Jacobian (∂a/∂v) is measured. VAMPO achieves substantial improvements in both Average ER and normalized Average ER Ratio (ERR). This indicates that when the AGM is conditioned on VAMPO's improved visual dynamics, it relies on a larger number of mutually independent visual directions for generating actions, suggesting a richer and more informative vision-action coupling.

**Q4. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods:**
- **CALVIN ABC→D:** VAMPO achieves the highest task completion rates across various task lengths (1 to 5) and the best average trajectory length among compared VLM-based and VPM-based VLAs. It surpasses its base policy (VPP) and other VPP-based extensions, demonstrating performance gains without requiring architectural modifications or additional data.
- **L-CALVIN:** For challenging long-horizon tasks, VAMPO maintains its superior performance, showing the best task completion rates across 1 to 10 steps and the highest average trajectory length among all evaluated methods.

**Q5. Components-wise Analysis (Ablation Studies):**
- **Post-Training Steps:** Performance generally improves with more GRPO post-training steps, reaching its peak around 1400 steps, while remaining consistently above the baseline despite minor fluctuations.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** GRPO consistently outperforms DDPO, achieving higher success rates. This is attributed to GRPO's more stable and informative gradient updates enabled by its within-group optimization and clipping mechanism.
- **Hybrid Denoising Type:** Applying SDE-style stochasticity only at the first denoising step (1-step SDE) yields better performance than applying it for more steps (e.g., 5-step SDE). This confirms the hypothesis that limiting stochasticity to the early, action-relevant stage effectively mitigates reward hacking and improves efficiency.
- **Reward Type:** Latent-space rewards result in better performance compared to pixel-level rewards. This finding supports the premise that latent representations are more directly aligned with underlying dynamics and task-relevant semantics, whereas pixel-level consistency does not always translate to accurate control-relevant visual dynamics.

In conclusion, the findings indicate that VAMPO effectively improves the precision of visual dynamics in video action models through a targeted policy optimization approach. This improvement translates into enhanced action generation, robust generalization, and superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VAMPO's contributions carry significant implications for the field of robotic learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems.

1. **Addressing a Fundamental Limitation:** The work identifies and offers a concrete solution to a critical objective mismatch prevalent in current diffusion-based video action models. By demonstrating that likelihood-based generative objectives are insufficient for ensuring precision-critical visual dynamics, VAMPO highlights a fundamental gap between generative model training and the demands of real-world robotic control. This clarity in problem definition and its resolution can guide future research efforts in generative model design for control.

2. **Enhanced Robot Control and Generalization:** VAMPO provides a method for directly optimizing control-relevant predictive signals. The empirical results, showing substantial improvements in task success rates, average trajectory length, and generalization across diverse simulated and real-world manipulation scenarios, demonstrate the practical value of this approach. More precise visual dynamics directly translate to more reliable and accurate action generation, which is crucial for deploying robots in complex, unstructured environments.

3. **Richer Vision-Action Coupling:** The analysis demonstrating an increased effective rank of the Jacobian between visual dynamics and actions provides mechanistic insight into *why* VAMPO improves performance. This finding suggests that by refining visual dynamics, the downstream action model can leverage a broader and more independent set of visual cues, leading to more sophisticated and nuanced control policies. This understanding can inform the design of future control architectures.

4. **Methodological Innovation:** The introduction of the Euler Hybrid sampler is a notable methodological contribution. It offers a practical strategy for integrating reinforcement learning with multi-step diffusion processes by selectively injecting stochasticity at the most critical stages. This design balances the need for tractable policy gradient estimation with the desire to preserve the temporal coherence and efficiency of the generative model, mitigating common challenges like long-horizon credit assignment.

5. **A New Direction for VLA Systems:** VAMPO proposes that future video-based robot learning and VLA systems should move beyond solely maximizing data likelihood. Instead, they should actively pursue direct optimization of predictive signals that are specifically relevant to control. This shift in perspective could lead to the development of more robust, precise, and intrinsically goal-oriented generative models that are better suited for the demands of autonomous agents. The framework's ability to act as a post-training step also means it can enhance existing models without requiring extensive architectural overhauls or new data collection, making it a readily implementable improvement.

Overall, VAMPO's significance lies in its capacity to unlock higher levels of precision and reliability in robot manipulation by systematically refining the visual dynamics that underpin control decisions, thereby pushing the capabilities of intelligent robotic systems forward.
