# Blending Supervised and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning with Prefix Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Prefix Reinforcement Fine-tuning (Prefix-RFT), a hybrid post-training approach for large language models that unifies supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT). The method consistently outperforms existing SFT, RFT, and two-staged baselines on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and general domain tasks, while demonstrating data efficiency and robustness across different model scales.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often leads to problematic generalization, suboptimal solution paths, and reduced robustness in complex reasoning tasks due to its behavioral cloning nature.
- Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) struggles with sparse reward signals, high sensitivity to initial policies, and might primarily refine existing capabilities rather than instilling fundamentally new knowledge.
- The traditional sequential 'SFT-then-RFT' pipeline is a heuristic approach, lacking a principled framework to formally integrate the complementary strengths of SFT's dense supervision and RFT's goal-oriented optimization.

## Method

- ==Prefix-RFT== unifies SFT and RFT training objectives by introducing a hybrid loop where a prefix from an expert demonstration guides an on-policy continuation by the model, creating a blended trajectory.
- An ==entropy-based clipping mechanism== is applied during training, incorporating only a top-k% of high-entropy demonstration tokens in the gradient calculation to ensure stable updates and target critical learning junctures.
- A ==cosine decay scheduler== dynamically adjusts the length of the sampled prefix, starting with longer, more guiding prefixes and gradually decreasing their length to encourage exploration and mitigate positional bias.

## Results

- Prefix-RFT consistently achieved superior performance over standalone SFT, RFT, and sequential SFT-then-RFT baselines across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME 2024/2025) and three general domain reasoning tasks.
- The method demonstrated significant data efficiency, maintaining strong performance even with a **99%** reduction in training data (from **45k** to **0.45k** samples), and showed robustness to the quality of demonstration generators.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the entropy-based clipping (top **20%** high-entropy tokens) and the cosine decay scheduler were critical components, outperforming alternative strategies and contributing to the method's stable and superior outcomes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT and RFT, despite their distinct origins, share a consistent underlying optimization structure, enabling their principled integration within a single training objective.
> - Providing high-quality prefixes effectively guides exploration, offering constrained autonomy to the model to begin on a promising path while still allowing it to discover superior continuations.
> - Targeting high-entropy demonstration tokens for reinforcement updates is crucial for stable learning, preventing overfitting and focusing learning on ambiguous steps where the model is most uncertain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The post-training of Large Language Models (LLMs) typically employs two primary paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT). SFT involves training models on curated, labeled datasets to mimic expert demonstrations. It is valued for its simplicity and effectiveness in adapting models to specific tasks. However, SFT fundamentally operates as a form of behavioral cloning, which can result in limitations in generalization and robustness. The model may merely memorize patterns rather than developing robust problem-solving capabilities.

RFT, conversely, aims to overcome these SFT limitations by optimizing models based on feedback signals, often in the form of rewards, which can significantly enhance performance. Recent models, such as OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1, have demonstrated RFT's potential in complex tasks like mathematical reasoning and coding. Despite these advancements, RFT presents its own challenges. Learning signals from rewards can be sparse, making credit assignment difficult in multi-step tasks and potentially leading to unexpected behaviors. The effectiveness of RFT is also highly dependent on the quality of the initial policy, and some research suggests that RFT primarily refines existing capabilities rather than instilling new knowledge or significantly raising the model's intrinsic capability ceiling. The gains observed from RFT might stem from perfecting what the model has already learned during pre-training and SFT.

The current research landscape often treats SFT and RFT as distinct, consecutive stages (e.g., SFT-then-RFT). However, their complementary strengths—SFT for dense supervision and knowledge injection, and RFT for goal-oriented optimization and behavioral shaping—motivate an integrated approach. Emerging work, such as LUFFY, UFT, and ReLIFT, also explores blending offline datasets with RFT training to leverage both paradigms. This paper contributes to this growing area by proposing a novel, unified framework that aims to harmonize SFT's stable knowledge acquisition with RFT's exploratory power in a single training process, thereby addressing the limitations of each method when applied in isolation or in a strictly sequential manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the recognized complementarity between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), along with the limitations observed when these methods are applied independently or sequentially.

The specific problems and limitations with existing post-training techniques are detailed as follows:
- **SFT Limitations:** While SFT is effective at adapting models to tasks by mimicking demonstrations, its nature as behavioral cloning can lead to problematic generalization and robustness issues. It primarily focuses on replicating observed behaviors rather than developing underlying competence, which can limit a model's ability to solve problems outside its training distribution or generalize to new scenarios. SFT provides dense supervision, but this supervision can be overly restrictive and may not always align directly with downstream task performance.
- **RFT Limitations:** RFT aims to improve models by optimizing directly for task performance via rewards. However, it often faces challenges such as sparse reward signals, making it difficult to attribute credit in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks. This can lead to the model learning "unexpected behaviors." Furthermore, RFT's efficacy is highly dependent on the initial policy's strength; it tends to refine existing capabilities rather than instilling new knowledge, potentially limiting how much it can truly elevate the model's inherent problem-solving capacity. Some studies question whether RFT can genuinely raise the upper bound of a base policy.

Recognizing these limitations and the distinct strengths of each paradigm, the authors articulate their core motivation:
- **Complementarity:** SFT is adept at injecting knowledge that the model might not discover through exploration, effectively expanding the model's "knowledge boundary" and potentially elevating the RFT's capability ceiling. Conversely, RFT provides the necessary goal-oriented objective to steer the model away from mere behavioral cloning towards robust problem-solving. This suggests that an ideal system would leverage both.

This leads to the central research question: **How can a framework be developed that formally integrates the process supervision of SFT with the goal-oriented optimization of RFT?**

The key objectives of this work are to:
1. **Propose a unified view of SFT and RFT:** Demonstrate that, despite their different theoretical origins, SFT and RFT share a consistent underlying optimization structure.
2. **Introduce Prefix Reinforcement Fine-tuning (Prefix-RFT):** Develop a hybrid post-training approach that leverages offline demonstration datasets within RFT training to combine learning from both demonstration and exploration.
3. **Empirically validate Prefix-RFT:** Demonstrate that this method is effective across various model scales, families, and qualities/quantities of demonstration data, particularly in mathematical reasoning problems, which serve as a robust testbed.
4. **Analyze the method's mechanisms:** Investigate how Prefix-RFT effectively synthesizes SFT and RFT, dynamically adjusts its learning strategy based on problem difficulty, and pushes the model to solve problems intractable for pure RFT.

The ultimate goal is to create a simple, practical, and effective method that blends the advantages of SFT and RFT, expanding the model's reasoning capabilities beyond what standalone methods or simple sequential approaches can achieve.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Prefix Reinforcement Fine-tuning (Prefix-RFT)**, a hybrid approach designed to integrate Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) within a unified framework. This methodology is built upon a "unified view" of SFT and RFT, which posits that both share a consistent optimization structure.

#### 3.1. Unified View of SFT and RFT

The paper begins by framing SFT and RFT as having inherently consistent parameter update dynamics.
- **SFT Objective:** The SFT loss aims to minimize the negative log-likelihood of an expert demonstration ($y^*$) given a prompt ($x$), effectively pushing the model ($\pi_\theta$) towards the expert policy ($\pi_{off}$). The gradient primarily focuses on updating the log probability of expert sequences.
- **Policy Gradient (RFT):** RFT methods utilize the current policy ($\pi_\theta$) to generate trajectories ($y$) and collect rewards. These samples are then used to compute a policy gradient to update the policy. For LLMs, each token generation is treated as an action, and the policy gradient is weighted by an estimated advantage ($\hat{A}_t$) for each token.
- **PPO Training Objective:** To improve sample efficiency, RFT for LLMs typically uses the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) objective. PPO introduces an importance sampling ratio ($r_t$) and a clipping mechanism to allow multiple gradient updates from collected samples. The PPO gradient similarly updates the log probability of tokens, but with a dynamic, per-token weight incorporating the advantage and clipping.

This analysis concludes that all three methods (SFT, policy gradient, PPO) apply a gradient to the log probability of a sequence. SFT implicitly assumes an advantage of 1 for expert tokens, while RFT explicitly uses calculated advantages.

#### 3.2. Prefix-RFT Framework

Prefix-RFT is designed to blend these approaches by incorporating offline demonstration datasets into RFT training.
- **Hybrid Trajectory Generation:** For a given prompt $x$ and an expert demonstration $y^*$, the method generates $N-1$ online rollouts using the current policy ($\pi_{\theta_{old}}$), as in standard RFT. For the $N$-th sequence, a *prefix* ($y^*_{<L}$) is sampled from the expert demonstration. The remaining part of the sequence ($y_{\ge L}$) is then generated by the current policy. These two parts are stitched together to form a "hybrid trajectory" ($y^{(N)}$). This process replaces one standard on-policy rollout with a prefix-guided hybrid rollout, maintaining the same rollout budget as standard RFT.
- **Advantage Estimation and Gradient Update:** All $N$ sequences (standard online rollouts and the hybrid trajectory) are used to estimate advantages ($\hat{A}_t$). The gradient update for Prefix-RFT follows the unified view, where token-specific weights ($\alpha_{i,t}$, $\beta_{i,t}$) are set to the clipped PPO weight ($W_{PPO_{i,t}}$).
 - For tokens from the on-policy continuation ($t \ge L$ in the hybrid trajectory) and standard rollouts, the update functions as learning from exploration.
 - For prefix tokens ($t < L$ in the hybrid trajectory), the update functions as learning from imitation. Crucially, these prefix tokens are reinforced using the advantage estimated from the *full hybrid trajectory*. This allows the model to learn from partial demonstrations, with the advantage indicating the utility of the given prefix. If a hybrid trajectory leads to a higher reward, the guiding prefix is reinforced.

#### 3.3. Key Components for Constrained and Targeted Learning

The method incorporates two specific mechanisms to manage the integration of demonstration data:
- **Entropy-based Clipping for Constrained Update on Demonstrations:** Offline expert demonstrations ($\pi_{off}$) can be distributionally distant from the current policy ($\pi_\theta$). Directly updating based on these tokens can lead to excessively large gradients, potentially dominating the RFT objective and causing instability or overfitting to superficial features. To address this, an entropy-based clipping approach is proposed. Only a top-k% subset of demonstration tokens with the highest entropy are used for updating. Low-entropy tokens are often either already matched by the current policy (little learning signal) or represent confident mismatches that would cause sharp, potentially detrimental, overwrite updates. By focusing on high-entropy tokens, the method targets positions where the model is uncertain, allowing the trajectory-level advantage to decide the reinforcement strength. The advantages of all other prefix tokens are set to zero.
- **Cosine Decay Scheduler for Controlling Prefix Length:** The prefix length ($L$) is determined by a variable $l \in$ (i.e., $L = \lfloor l \cdot |y^*| \rfloor$). To mitigate positional bias (early tokens are naturally sampled more if $l$ is uniform) and introduce a curriculum, a cosine-decay scheduler is used. The variable $l$ is randomly sampled from $U(\text{low}, \text{high})$, where $\text{high}$ is a constant (e.g., 0.95) and $\text{low}$ gradually decreases from $\text{high}$ to near zero over the training duration. This allows the model to initially learn from longer expert prefixes and progressively rely more on its own generation as training advances, mirroring a curriculum learning strategy.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

Mathematical reasoning problems serve as the primary testbed due to the availability of reliable verifiers. The training data comprises a length-filtered subset of the OpenR1-Math-220K dataset, with each problem accompanied by a DeepSeek-R1-generated demonstration. Qwen2.5-Math-7B is the main base model, with additional experiments on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, and Qwen3-base-1.7B. The RFT algorithm used is Dr.GRPO. Baselines include standard SFT, RFT, SFT+RFT, RFT with SFT Loss, and recent hybrid methods like UFT, ReLIFT, and LUFFY. Evaluation metrics include Pass@k for math reasoning benchmarks (AIME, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, Olympiad) and general domain reasoning tasks (ARC-c, GPQA, MMLU-Pro).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of Prefix-RFT across various benchmarks and conditions.

#### 4.1. Performance on Math and General Reasoning Benchmarks

- **Superiority over Baselines:** Prefix-RFT consistently outperforms standalone SFT and RFT, as well as two-staged SFT-then-RFT baselines and parallel mixed-policy RFT methods (UFT, ReLIFT, LUFFY) on six math reasoning tasks and three general domain reasoning tasks. For example, on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Prefix-RFT achieves an average math reasoning performance of 51.8%, surpassing LUFFY (50.1%) and SFT+RFT (48.2%). Similar trends are observed for general domain reasoning.
- **Ineffectiveness of Direct Mixing:** Directly applying SFT loss on off-policy data during RFT training ("RL w/ SFT Loss") proves counterproductive, confirming concerns about gradient dominance.
- **Model Scale and Family Robustness:** Prefix-RFT demonstrates consistent superior performance across different model scales (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen3-1.7B-Base) and model families, indicating its general applicability.

#### 4.2. Expansion of Reasoning Capabilities (Pass@k Results)

- Evaluating Pass@k with a large sampling budget (n=2048) on AIME datasets reveals that Prefix-RFT is the only method that significantly raises the model's performance upper bound. While RFT's Pass@2048 remains close to the base model, and SFT can even degrade performance at larger budgets (due to constrained solution space), Prefix-RFT consistently outperforms all baselines across the sampling spectrum. It achieves a 6.67 percentage point improvement in Pass@2048 over the base model on both AIME24 and AIME25. This suggests Prefix-RFT enables the model to discover novel solutions beyond simply improving the likelihood of known ones.

#### 4.3. Analysis of SFT and RFT Integration

- **Complementary Learning Paradigms:** Analysis of training trajectories (Avg@16, Best@16, and SFT loss on demonstrations) shows SFT initially improves problem-solving potential (high Best@16) but can struggle with robust performance (lower Avg@16). RFT, conversely, achieves better overall performance (higher Avg@16) but is limited by the initial policy's ability (lower Best@16 on hard problems) and deviates from the demonstration distribution. This confirms the complementary nature of SFT and RFT.
- **Prefix-RFT's Blending:** Prefix-RFT achieves higher Avg@16 and Best@16 scores and lower SFT loss on demonstrations compared to RFT, indicating it effectively learns from demonstrations while optimizing for task performance. It particularly shows notable gains on problems that were previously intractable for pure RFT.

#### 4.4. Dynamic Transition between SFT and RFT

- **Overall Training Dynamics:** The "Reward with Prefix" (reward of rollouts starting with a prefix) quickly surges, especially for larger models, demonstrating rapid leverage of prefixes. The gap between this and the "Training Reward" (overall reward) stays positive but gradually narrows, indicating a diminishing advantage assigned to the prefix over training. This "decreasing shaded area" signifies a smooth, desirable transition from SFT-like guidance to RFT-like self-exploration.
- **Individual Example Level:** Analysis of SFT loss change on demonstrations across problems of varying difficulty shows a positive correlation: the model experiences greater loss reduction (learns more intensively from demonstrations) for more challenging problems (lower accuracy). For easier problems, SFT loss reduction is marginal, indicating less reliance on demonstrations. This highlights a dynamic, example-level transition between learning from demonstrations and self-exploration.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Entropy-based Clipping:** The "top 20% high-entropy" clipping strategy outperforms variants that relax the clipping ratio (top 50%, top 80%) or use random/low-entropy selections. This validates that constraining updates on demonstrations is crucial to prevent overfitting and that high-entropy tokens provide a richer, more targeted learning signal.
- **Cosine Decay Scheduler:** The proposed cosine decay scheduler significantly outperforms a uniform prefix length scheduler, suggesting it better modulates training dynamics and incentivizes learning from demonstrations, especially early in training.
- **Prefix Gradient Strategies:** Freezing prefix gradients yields performance similar to standard RFT, confirming the necessity of explicit learning from demonstrations. Updating all prefix tokens without clipping leads to marginal gains and unstable training dynamics (e.g., response length explosion), reinforcing the need for entropy-based constraint.
- **Dynamic vs. Static Weighting:** Dynamic advantage-based weighting significantly outperforms a static weighting approach (e.g., constant 0.001 weight), demonstrating the value of selectively learning from high-value prefixes.
- **Learning from Prefix vs. Guidance Only:** Merely using prefixes as guidance (similar to a DR-PO variant) without including them in loss calculation results in poor performance, emphasizing that the performance gain comes from actively reinforcing high-quality prefixes.

#### 4.6. Data Efficiency and Quality Robustness

- **Limited Data Quantity:** Prefix-RFT shows strong data efficiency. Reducing the training set size by 99% (from 45k to 0.45k samples) results in only a moderate performance drop, while still outperforming SFT and RFT baselines.
- **Suboptimal Data Quality:** The method exhibits robustness to the quality of demonstration generators. Performance is nearly identical whether demonstrations are generated by a 1.5B or a 32B model. However, the most challenging benchmarks (e.g., AIME) remain more sensitive to the quality and scale of demonstration data.

#### 4.7. Code Generation Transferability

- Preliminary experiments on code generation tasks (MBPP+, HumanEval+, Coder1-test) using Qwen3-1.7B-Base show that Prefix-RFT consistently improves performance over RFT, suggesting that the core design is not specific to mathematical reasoning but generalizes to other verifier-based domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Prefix-RFT, a method that integrates Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) in a unified framework. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several dimensions:

1. **Advancement in LLM Post-training:** The work challenges the traditional view of SFT and RFT as distinct, sequential stages. By proposing a unified framework and demonstrating its empirical effectiveness, it suggests a more integrated approach to LLM post-training. This could lead to more robust and capable LLMs by systematically leveraging the strengths of both paradigms.

2. **Improved Generalization and Reasoning Capabilities:** The results, particularly the Pass@k analysis, indicate that Prefix-RFT not only improves the likelihood of known solutions but also expands the model's reasoning boundaries. This capability to solve problems beyond what pure RFT or SFT can achieve implies a potential for models to develop more intrinsic problem-solving skills rather than merely memorizing or refining existing patterns. Such an advancement is critical for tasks requiring deep reasoning, like complex mathematics or scientific problem-solving.

3. **Efficiency and Robustness:** The method demonstrates robustness across various model scales, model families, and under different demonstration quantities and qualities. This indicates its practical viability for real-world applications where obtaining large quantities of perfectly curated expert demonstrations can be challenging or expensive. Its data efficiency, showing strong performance even with significantly reduced demonstration data, is particularly impactful for resource-constrained scenarios.

4. **Principled Integration of Off-Policy Data:** The design choices, such as entropy-based clipping and cosine decay scheduling, provide principled mechanisms for managing the integration of off-policy demonstration data into an on-policy reinforcement learning framework. These components address critical challenges like gradient dominance and positional bias, contributing to the stability and effectiveness of the hybrid training. The analysis regarding the dynamic transition between SFT and RFT, both at an overall training level and individual example level, provides insights into how models can adapt their learning strategy based on problem difficulty and current capabilities.

5. **Broader Applicability:** While primarily validated on mathematical reasoning problems, the preliminary results on code generation suggest the method's transferability to other verifier-based domains. This broadens its potential impact beyond specific niches, making it relevant for a wider range of tasks where ground-truth verification is possible.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The work identifies limitations and avenues for future exploration, such as extending Prefix-RFT to open-ended generation and noisy-reward settings, and developing systematic demonstration selection strategies when multiple candidates are available. These open questions can guide subsequent research efforts in the field of LLM post-training and hybrid learning.

In summary, Prefix-RFT offers a practical and theoretically grounded approach to enhancing LLM capabilities by effectively blending two dominant fine-tuning paradigms. Its demonstrated performance gains, robustness, and analytical insights contribute to a deeper understanding of how LLMs learn and how their post-training can be optimized.
