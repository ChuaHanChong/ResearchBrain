# SAHOO: Safeguarded Alignment for High-Order Optimization Objectives in Recursive Self-Improvement

> [!summary] Summary
> SAHOO is a monitoring framework that keeps recursive self-improvement loops aligned through three safeguards: a Goal Drift Index combining semantic, lexical, structural and distributional signals with learned weights; constraint-preservation checks enforcing safety invariants; and regression-risk quantification that halts cycles undoing prior gains. Across 189 code, math and truthfulness tasks it raised quality up to 18.3% while holding drift below threshold.

## Problem

- Recursive self-improvement moved from theory to deployed critique-revise loops, yet no systematic way existed to verify that iterative self-modification does not erode alignment.
- Prior self-improvement work such as self-taught code generation optimized capability alone, layering no alignment safeguard onto the improvement loop.
- Existing drift detection used single signals and fixed thresholds, missing that misalignment surfaces jointly as semantic, lexical, structural and distributional shift across cycles.

## Method

- **Goal Drift Index**: composite detector fusing ==embedding cosine distance==, ==token-frequency Jensen-Shannon divergence==, ==structural feature deltas== and ==1-Wasserstein distributional distance==, with weights and per-task thresholds fit by ==logistic regression== on calibration data.
- **Constraint Preservation Score**: logical predicates over format, content and ethics; violations are fed back into the next improvement prompt with severity-tiered penalties, and zero preservation triggers a hard stop.
- Regression-risk estimator plus **Capability Alignment Ratio**: ==Gaussian probability== that quality drops below best-so-far minus delta, using volatility and fitted trend; CAR divides quality gain by measured drift.

## Results

- Over **189 tasks** on Qwen3-8B, quality rose **0.672 to 0.795** on HumanEval (**+18.3%**) and **0.689 to 0.805** on GSM8K (**+16.8%**), but only **+3.8%** on TruthfulQA.
- Mean GDI stayed under the **0.44** threshold (code **0.320**, math **0.330**, truthfulness **0.354**), stabilizing by cycles 3-5; bootstrapped **95%** intervals averaged **0.084** wide.
- Constraints held perfectly for code and math; truthfulness averaged **0.987** with **170** violations, mostly fabrication (**53.5%**). Regressions hit **4.5%** of **3,780** cycles, **0.7%** excluding one outlier.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Alignment cost is not constant: early improvement cycles are nearly free, while later gains buy quality at steadily worse drift exchange rates.
> - Semantic and distributional shift dominate drift; lexical surface change is a weak proxy, so word-level monitors will miss most misalignment.
> - Domains with verifiable success criteria preserve constraints trivially; open-ended factual tasks trade fluency against truth, so they need tighter thresholds and human oversight.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of recursive self-improvement (RSI) in AI has deep roots in theoretical computer science and AI safety research, with foundational work exploring the potential for systems to autonomously enhance their capabilities (e.g., Schmidhuber’s Gödel machines). Historically, this was largely a theoretical construct. However, the advent of powerful large language models (LLMs) and multimodal systems has shifted RSI from theoretical speculation to an empirically tractable reality. Modern AI systems can now critique, revise, and evaluate their own outputs, demonstrating a rudimentary form of self-modification.

This shift, while promising for accelerating AI capabilities, introduces a critical and urgent challenge: **alignment drift**. As systems iteratively improve, how can we ensure they do not subtly, yet fundamentally, drift away from their intended alignment goals or safety constraints? This is precisely the "control problem" articulated by researchers like Stuart Russell, and the broader concern of value alignment discussed by Soares and Fallenstein. The paper directly addresses this by operationalizing alignment preservation through concrete measurements and safeguards, moving beyond abstract principles to a deployable framework.

The work builds upon several existing research threads:
- **AI Alignment and Safety:** It explicitly draws on the growing body of work in AI alignment (e.g., Ji et al., 2025; Yin et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025) which recognizes that preserving alignment during self-modification is non-trivial. SAHOO provides a practical mechanism to address this, focusing on preventing unintended deviations.
- **Drift Detection in Machine Learning:** The methodology incorporates techniques from concept drift detection, distribution shift analysis, and embedding space analysis, which are established fields in machine learning for identifying changes in data or model behavior over time. The novelty here lies in their specific application to *recursive self-improvement and alignment preservation* in an integrated multi-signal framework. Zelikman et al. (2024) explore self-improving code generation, suggesting a capability focus, while SAHOO explicitly layers alignment safeguards onto such processes.
- **Formal Methods and Constraint Satisfaction:** The concept of constraint preservation aligns with principles from formal methods and specification-based verification, where adherence to predefined rules is paramount. SAHOO's unique contribution is integrating these constraints directly into the iterative improvement loop with explicit violation penalties and a hard stopping criterion.
- **Stopping Criteria in Iterative Learning:** The regression risk framework relates to work on convergence and stopping rules in iterative learning processes (e.g., Leike, 2023). SAHOO extends this by simultaneously monitoring both quality *and* alignment, recognizing that improvement in one dimension should not compromise the other.

In essence, SAHOO serves as a critical bridge between the theoretical concerns of AI safety and the practical deployment of self-improving AI systems. It provides a structured, data-driven approach to ensure that the pursuit of enhanced capabilities does not inadvertently lead to undesirable or unsafe behaviors, a problem of increasing urgency as AI systems become more autonomous and powerful.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SAHOO stems from the urgent need to address alignment drift in AI systems capable of recursive self-improvement. While such systems promise "unbounded capability gains," the core challenge is ensuring that these gains do not come at the cost of "subtle misalignments that compound across cycles." The paper argues that a system is not genuinely improved if its increased capability (e.g., code generation) is offset by a decrease in alignment (e.g., truthfulness).

The specific objectives of SAHOO are to:

1. **Develop a Principled and Verifiable Alignment Preservation Mechanism:** The current landscape lacks robust, systematic ways to verify that self-improvement does not lead to misalignment. SAHOO aims to fill this gap.
2. **Detect and Quantify Alignment Drift:** Recognize that alignment drift is multi-faceted, manifesting across semantic, lexical, structural, and distributional dimensions. A key objective is to create a comprehensive metric, the Goal Drift Index (GDI), that captures these diverse forms of deviation.
3. **Ensure Safety-Critical Invariants are Maintained:** Implement mechanisms to enforce explicit safety-critical constraints (e.g., syntactic correctness, non-hallucination) throughout the improvement process.
4. **Prevent Regression to Lower Quality or Hazardous States:** Introduce safeguards to identify and prevent situations where iterative improvements accidentally undo prior gains or revert to undesirable behaviors.
5. **Characterize the Capability-Alignment Trade-off:** Provide a framework (Capability Alignment Ratio, CAR) for understanding and quantifying the inherent tension between maximizing capabilities and preserving alignment, allowing for informed decision-making.
6. **Empirically Validate the Framework:** Demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of SAHOO across diverse real-world tasks that probe different alignment failure modes.
7. **Provide an Open, Calibratable Methodology:** Offer a data-driven framework where parameters and thresholds are learned from calibration data rather than being arbitrarily set, making it adaptable and deployable by practitioners in various settings.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to make the safe and aligned deployment of recursive self-improving AI systems a measurable, controllable, and systematically validated reality, thereby mitigating potential existential risks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAHOO (Safeguarded Alignment for High-Order Optimization Objectives) is a practical framework designed to monitor and control alignment drift during recursive self-improvement. It integrates three complementary safeguards and a sophisticated evaluation approach.

#### 3.1. Core Safeguards

1. **Goal Drift Index (GDI): A Multi-Signal Detector**
 The GDI is a composite metric that quantifies alignment drift across four modalities:
 - **Semantic Drift:** Measured using normalized cosine distance between mean-pooled hidden state embeddings of baseline and current responses. It captures changes in meaning.
 - **Lexical Drift:** Measured by Jensen Shannon Divergence (JSD) between token frequency distributions of baseline and current responses. It detects shifts in vocabulary usage.
 - **Structural Drift:** Quantified by normalized differences in structural features like response length, line count, and presence of code blocks. It identifies changes in output organization.
 - **Distributional Drift:** Assessed using the 1-Wasserstein distance (Earth Mover’s Distance) between the distributions of historical response embeddings and current response embeddings. This captures cumulative shifts in statistical properties.

 These four components are combined into a single GDI using **learned weights** (w_s, w_ℓ, w_st, w_d) that are calibrated on validation data. The calibration process involves logistic regression on human-evaluated drift labels to maximize detection accuracy, ensuring data-driven weighting rather than arbitrary choices. Task-specific GDI thresholds are also learned to maximize the F1 score for drift detection.

2. **Constraint Preservation Checks (CPS)**
 This safeguard enforces explicit safety-critical invariants defined as a set of logical predicates (C1, C2... CK) that all model outputs must satisfy. Constraints can cover format, content, logical properties, and ethical guidelines.
 - **Measurement:** The Constraint Preservation Score (CPS) is the fraction of satisfied constraints, acting as a "hard metric" (either satisfied or not).
 - **Violation Penalties:** Unsatisfied constraints are explicitly highlighted in the improvement prompts for subsequent cycles, with penalties tiered by severity.
 - **Hard Stopping Rule:** The system immediately halts if any cycle yields zero constraint preservation, preventing continued operation under critical safety breaches.

3. **Regression-Risk Quantification**
 This mechanism identifies and flags when improvement cycles inadvertently undo prior gains or lead to a decline in quality.
 - **Regression Risk (Rc):** Defined as the probability that the quality at cycle 'c' (Qc) falls below a meaningful threshold (Qmax - δ) relative to the best quality achieved prior to that cycle (Qmax).
 - **Estimation:** Regression probability is estimated using historical volatility (standard deviation), fitted linear trend of quality, and the gap between current and maximum quality (normalized by volatility), approximated by a Gaussian distribution. A trend adjustment factor increases risk for significant negative trends.
 - **Thresholding and Stopping:** A calibrated decision threshold (τR) is used; exceeding it triggers a warning and potential halting of improvement cycles.

#### 3.2. Experimental Design

- **Base Model:** Qwen3-8B was selected for its capability and tractability.
- **Benchmarks:** Three benchmarks were chosen to probe distinct alignment failure modes:
 - **HumanEval (code generation):** Assesses syntactic correctness and semantic fidelity, susceptible to drift yielding incorrect or unsafe code.
 - **TruthfulQA (truthfulness):** Measures factual accuracy, prone to drift producing fluent falsehoods or eroding grounding.
 - **GSM8K (mathematical reasoning):** Tests multi-step problem-solving, vulnerable to drift amplifying confidence in incorrect solutions.
- **Calibration Phase:** A crucial initial step involving a small subset of tasks (18 tasks: 6 per domain) run for three improvement cycles. This data is used to:
 - Learn GDI component weights.
 - Estimate baseline drift statistics.
 - Calibrate per-task drift thresholds.
 - Establish baseline quality and constraint preservation patterns.
 This ensures all parameters are data-driven.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Beyond the core safeguards, performance was assessed using:
 - **Quality:** Task-specific metrics (pass@1 for code, accuracy for truthfulness, exact match for math).
 - **Capability Alignment Ratio (CAR):** (Qc - Q0) / GDIc, which quantifies the efficiency of capability improvement per unit of alignment degradation.
 - **Stability Score:** An overall measure combining various stability aspects.
 - **Cycle Count:** Number of cycles until termination.
- **Stopping Rules:** A composite set of rules ensures termination upon the first trigger: quality plateau, regression risk threshold, zero constraint preservation, maximum cycle count, or GDI threshold exceedance.
- **Statistical Precision:** Bootstrapping with 2000 samples was used to estimate 95% confidence intervals for GDI measurements, ensuring reliability.

The methodology emphasizes a rigorous, data-driven, and multi-faceted approach to quantify, monitor, and control alignment in recursively self-improving AI systems, moving beyond qualitative concerns to concrete, measurable safeguards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of SAHOO across 189 tasks (63 per domain) with up to 20 improvement cycles yielded several significant findings:

1. **Overall Effectiveness and Capability Gains:** SAHOO successfully enabled substantial capability improvements while maintaining alignment.
 - **Code Generation (HumanEval):** Quality improved from 0.672 to 0.795 (+18.3%).
 - **Mathematical Reasoning (GSM8K):** Quality increased from 0.689 to 0.805 (+16.8%).
 - **Truthfulness (TruthfulQA):** Quality rose modestly from 0.678 to 0.704 (+3.8%).
 This demonstrates the framework's ability to drive meaningful quality gains across diverse domains.

2. **Effective Drift Control (GDI):** The Goal Drift Index remained well below the critical threshold of 0.44 across all domains.
 - Mean GDI: Code (0.320), Math (0.330), Truthfulness (0.354).
 - GDI trajectories showed an early increase, stabilization by cycles 3-5, and sustained low drift thereafter, indicating effective control.
 - **Drift Component Analysis:** Semantic drift was the dominant contributor to GDI (weight 0.38), followed by distributional (0.29), structural (0.21), and lexical (0.12), suggesting that meaning and output distribution changes are primary drivers of misalignment. These weights generalized across task types.

3. **Constraint Preservation (CPS):** SAHOO achieved very high constraint preservation.
 - **Perfect Adherence:** Code generation and mathematical reasoning tasks maintained perfect CPS (1.000) across all cycles, indicating that formal and structured constraints are effectively preserved.
 - **Truthfulness Challenges:** Truthfulness tasks showed occasional constraint violations (mean CPS 0.987, 170 total violations). Fabrication (53.5%) and overconfidence (28.2%) were the dominant failure types, highlighting an inherent tension between fluency and factuality in this domain. Crucially, the framework prevented *catastrophic alignment failures* where CPS dropped below acceptable thresholds.

4. **Regression Risk and Stability:** The framework demonstrated high stability and effective regression prevention.
 - **High Stability Scores:** Mean stability score of 0.825 across all tasks.
 - **Low Regression Rate:** Only 170 regression events across 3,780 cycles (4.5%). Excluding a single extreme outlier task, the rate dropped to 0.7%, with a median of only two events among affected tasks, confirming effective suppression of regressions.
 - **Early Detection:** For tasks exhibiting instability, it was detected early (mean 4.5 cycles to threshold breach), enabling timely intervention.

5. **Convergence Analysis:**
 - Most tasks (91.5%) converged within the cycle budget (mean 8.2 cycles), indicating that improvement plateaus relatively quickly.
 - Convergence speed varied: code (7.1 cycles), math (8.9 cycles), truthfulness (10.4 cycles), reflecting the clarity of success criteria.

6. **Capability Alignment Ratio (CAR) Frontier:**
 - CAR analysis revealed a clear Pareto structure, with optimal performance clustered at high quality and low drift.
 - CAR dynamics showed initially high efficiency (approaching 1.0) in early cycles (1-2), then declined to 0.6-0.7 and stabilized. This implies that early improvements are low-cost, while later gains require accepting greater alignment costs.
 - Truthfulness gains consistently imposed larger alignment costs (CAR 0.599) compared to code (0.671) and math (0.675).

7. **Long Horizon Stability:** The framework consistently prevented catastrophic failures over extended cycles. Mean stability score was 0.825, with only three tasks falling below 0.70 due to atypical conditions. Linear trend modeling of GDI showed mild average upward drift but heterogeneous dynamics, with many tasks showing neutral or negative drift.

8. **Statistical Precision:** Bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals for GDI had a mean width of 0.084, providing precision of approximately ±0.042 around observed drift values, ensuring dependable detection of threshold crossings.

In summary, SAHOO successfully demonstrated that significant capability improvements in RSI systems are achievable while maintaining robust alignment control. It provided quantitative insights into the nature of drift, the effectiveness of constraint enforcement, and the inherent trade-offs between capability and alignment across different task domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SAHOO framework represents a significant step forward in the field of AI safety and the practical deployment of advanced AI systems, particularly those capable of recursive self-improvement. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Safe Recursive Self-Improvement:** The most direct and profound impact is demonstrating that "principled alignment preservation during recursive self-improvement is both feasible and effective." This moves the discussion from purely theoretical concerns about uncontrolled AI growth to practical, measurable safeguards, providing a pathway for safely realizing the benefits of self-improving systems.
2. **Operationalizing AI Alignment:** SAHOO provides concrete, quantifiable metrics (GDI, CPS, CAR) and processes (calibration, stopping rules) for addressing the complex and often abstract problem of AI alignment. This "measurable, deployable, and systematically validated" framework transforms alignment from a philosophical challenge into an engineering problem with tangible solutions.
3. **Mitigating Existential Risk (X-risk):** By making alignment drift measurable and controllable, SAHOO directly contributes to mitigating existential risks associated with advanced AI. It offers a crucial mechanism to "bound and detect accumulating misalignment before it compounds into catastrophic behavior," providing an early warning system against potentially dangerous system evolutions.
4. **Informed Decision-Making for Practitioners:** The Capability Alignment Ratio (CAR) framework is particularly impactful as it allows practitioners to quantitatively understand the trade-offs between capability gains and alignment costs. This enables more rational and risk-aware decisions regarding when to continue or halt improvement cycles, and how to allocate resources for monitoring.
5. **Early Warning and Intervention:** The framework's ability to detect instability and regression risk early (mean 4.5 cycles) is critical. This provides human operators with sufficient lead time to intervene before drift becomes severe or irreparable, enhancing human oversight capabilities in autonomous systems.
6. **Domain-Specific Alignment Strategies:** The finding that alignment costs and vulnerabilities vary significantly by task type (e.g., truthfulness being more challenging than code generation) is highly impactful. This guides practitioners in tailoring their alignment strategies, allocating more conservative thresholds or intensive human oversight for high-risk domains, and focusing constraint refinement on specific failure types (like fabrication in truthfulness tasks).
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** SAHOO opens numerous avenues for further research, including developing more sophisticated constraint specification methods, investigating adversarial robustness of drift detectors, integrating with mechanistic interpretability for deeper understanding of model internals, and extending to multi-agent self-improvement scenarios.
8. **Promoting Transparency and Auditability:** The framework encourages comprehensive logging of improvement cycles, drift measurements, and decision-making, which is essential for auditing and understanding the evolutionary trajectory of self-improving AI systems.

While acknowledging its limitations (e.g., reliance on baseline alignment, explicit constraint specification, and vulnerability to adversarial manipulation for highly deceptive systems), SAHOO is presented as a "necessary but insufficient mitigation." It provides a robust, practical layer of safety for controlling alignment during moderate capability gains, emphasizing the need for complementary approaches like mechanistic interpretability and richer value learning to address the full spectrum of existential risks. Overall, SAHOO makes a vital contribution by transforming the abstract problem of AI alignment into a concrete, actionable, and empirically validated engineering challenge.
