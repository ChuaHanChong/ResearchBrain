# Exploring Temporal Representation in Neural Processes for Multimodal Action Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> The Deep Modality Blending Network (DMBN) was enhanced with Positional Time Encoding (DMBN-PTE) to improve its representation of temporal information for multimodal action prediction. This modification led to a dramatically improved internal temporal representation, allowing the model to better generalize to action sequences with temporal arrests and achieving orders of magnitude lower losses in reconstructing context time.

## Problem

- Many existing MNS-inspired robotic models struggle with processing raw multimodal signals for action prediction, limiting the depth of learned correlations.
- Prior predictive models often focus on others' actions or lack robust mechanisms for self-action prediction over extended time scales.
- The Deep Modality Blending Network (DMBN), while promising, failed to effectively represent temporal information, leading to poor generalization when action sequences deviated from typical temporal order.

## Method

- The **DMBN** (Deep Modality Blending Network) was re-implemented and evaluated for its temporal representation capabilities using synthetic sequences (permuted, frozen) and quantitative regression tests.
- A modification called **DMBN-PTE** (==Positional Time Encoding==) was proposed, which projects temporal information into the hidden space and adds it to encoded modal features.
- ==Training data augmentation==, including varying action speeds and repeated frames, was used to enhance the DMBN-PTE's robustness to diverse temporal dynamics.

## Results

- The original DMBN failed to generalize to sequences with permuted or frozen temporal structures, consistently reconstructing an "ordered" sequence instead.
- Quantitative tests confirmed the original DMBN's encoders actively silenced temporal input, exhibiting **orders of magnitude** higher loss in reconstructing time compared to baselines (e.g., **29.63e-3** for image encoder vs. **0.69e-3** for DMBN-PTE).
- DMBN-PTE significantly improved the internal temporal representation, allowing it to capture freezing behaviors more accurately in frozen sequences, demonstrating better adaptation to temporal arrests.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simply feeding temporal information as an input channel in CNP-based multimodal networks can cause the network to effectively ignore or "silence" that information.
> - Explicitly projecting and integrating temporal information into the hidden representation, rather than treating it as raw input, is critical for learning robust and generalizable action dynamics.
> - Non-autoregressive, probabilistic models like those based on Conditional Neural Processes are valuable for action prediction as they can represent uncertainty and predict multiple future points in parallel.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of cognitive robotics, specifically focusing on developing robotic systems capable of understanding and predicting the actions of others, analogous to human social cognitive abilities. The work draws significant inspiration from neuroscientific theories, particularly the Mirror Neuron System (MNS) and Predictive Coding (PC).

The MNS, initially discovered in monkeys and later identified functionally in humans, is implicated in action recognition, imitation, and empathy. The Simulation Theory of mindreading posits that mentalization arises from an internal simulation of observed actions. Recent neuroscientific findings extend the role of the MNS to self-actions, suggesting its involvement in coordinating forward and inverse models for action planning and learning. This property is considered fundamental to the development of mirroring abilities in infants, where self-motion outcomes are learned before attention shifts to other agents. Concurrently, the human brain's predictive nature, described by Predictive Coding theory, emphasizes constant forecasting and error minimization.

In robotics, there is a recognized interest in endowing robots with human-like cognitive capabilities to foster better integration and transparency in human society. MNS-inspired robotic models aim to achieve this by enabling robots to understand and predict the external world. However, existing robotic solutions face several limitations:
1. **Multimodality:** Many models operate on highly pre-processed data, which can limit the ability to learn deep correlations between different sensory modalities (e.g., vision and proprioception). Effective MNS-inspired models should process raw multimodal signals to connect observations with motor plans.
2. **Prediction of Self-Actions:** While crucial for acquiring a model of the self and the environment, many predictive models focus on others' actions or lack explicit mechanisms for self-action prediction.
3. **Temporal Prediction:** A significant number of existing predictive models are autoregressive, generating predictions iteratively, which can lead to error accumulation over longer prediction horizons. Non-autoregressive models capable of parallel, long-range predictions are desired.
4. **Perspective Shifting:** The challenge of aligning first-person and third-person perspectives remains largely unaddressed, with models typically operating from a fixed viewpoint.

The Deep Modality Blending Network (DMBN) has been identified as a promising architecture that addresses some of these limitations by leveraging Conditional Neural Processes (CNP) for parallel, long-term prediction in a multimodal context. This paper specifically focuses on evaluating and enhancing the DMBN's temporal representation capabilities, particularly for self-action prediction. By refining how temporal information is processed, the work aims to expand the DMBN's applicability and contribute to the development of more robust, MNS-inspired cognitive robotic systems capable of autonomous action forecasting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the development of robotic systems that can autonomously learn to forecast actions over extended time scales and progressively refine these predictions based on incoming observations. This goal is directly motivated by insights into human cognition, particularly the functional properties of the Mirror Neuron System (MNS) and the brain's predictive nature.

The specific objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Investigate the applicability of Conditional Neural Processes (CNP) for self-supervised multimodal action prediction in robotics:**
 - **Motivation:** Inspired by the human MNS, which facilitates understanding and predicting others' actions, the authors seek to explore computational models that replicate this ability in robots. The MNS's ontogenetic development, where self-action prediction precedes the understanding of others, provides a preliminary focus. CNPs are considered a suitable candidate due to their ability to model distributions of data, make parallel predictions, and quantify uncertainty, features that align with the brain's predictive capabilities. The self-supervised paradigm is chosen to enable autonomous learning without extensive human annotation.

2. **Evaluate the performance and limitations of the existing Deep Modality Blending Network (DMBN) as an MNS-inspired model:**
 - **Motivation:** The DMBN was identified as a promising architecture because it uses CNPs to predict future actions in parallel, addresses multimodal input (visual and proprioceptive), and potentially allows for perspective shifting. However, a thorough evaluation of its inherent capabilities and specific limitations is necessary before further development. The motivation here is to understand whether the current DMBN formulation effectively captures the dynamics required for robust action prediction, particularly regarding its temporal representation.

3. **Identify specific shortcomings of the DMBN, with a particular focus on its temporal representation and generalization capabilities:**
 - **Motivation:** Initial qualitative observations suggested that despite reconstructing observed actions, the DMBN might struggle with sequences where the temporal order is altered or interrupted. Understanding the root cause of these difficulties is crucial for improving the model. The hypothesis was that the way time is encoded and processed within the DMBN might be inadequate for learning a generalizable representation of action dynamics. This objective aimed to confirm this hypothesis quantitatively.

4. **Propose and evaluate modifications to the DMBN to facilitate the learning of a more robust representation of temporal information:**
 - **Motivation:** Once the temporal representation was identified as a critical weakness, the motivation was to introduce enhancements that would allow the network to explicitly leverage and learn from temporal cues. Drawing inspiration from successful temporal encoding mechanisms in other neural network architectures (e.g., Transformers), the goal was to develop a method that better integrates time into the network's hidden representations, thereby improving its ability to generalize across different temporal dynamics.

5. **Introduce DMBN-Positional Time Encoding (DMBN-PTE) as a step towards expanding the architecture's applicability for longer-term and more nuanced action prediction:**
 - **Motivation:** The ultimate aim is to create robotic systems that can reliably forecast actions over significant time horizons, adapting to variations in speed, pauses, and reorderings. DMBN-PTE is envisioned as a foundational improvement that lays the groundwork for such capabilities by providing the network with a more sophisticated understanding of time, which is essential for predicting the complex, dynamic nature of real-world actions.

In summary, the research is motivated by the aspiration to imbue robots with MNS-inspired cognitive abilities for understanding and predicting action, starting with self-actions. It critically evaluates an existing promising architecture (DMBN), diagnoses its limitations in temporal understanding, and proposes a targeted modification (DMBN-PTE) to overcome these issues, thereby moving closer to the goal of robust, autonomous action forecasting in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research investigates the temporal representation in Neural Processes for multimodal action prediction by evaluating and modifying an existing architecture, the Deep Modality Blending Network (DMBN), which is based on Conditional Neural Processes (CNPs).

#### 3.1. Core Architectural Foundation: Conditional Neural Processes (CNP)

The study leverages Conditional Neural Processes (CNPs) as the underlying computational framework. CNPs belong to the Neural Process family, combining properties of traditional Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) with those of Gaussian Processes (GP). They are designed to operate on sets of data, learning to model the distribution of a target set conditioned on a context set. Key characteristics of CNPs include:
- **Set Operator:** Individual context points are encoded, and their representations are combined using a set operator (e.g., averaging) to form a single, representative element for the context set. This makes CNPs permutation-invariant to the order of context points.
- **Positional Information:** Positional information is incorporated into the representative element before decoding, enabling the reconstruction of different target elements.
- **Probabilistic Output:** CNPs output both mean and variance for the target set, representing the reconstruction uncertainty.

#### 3.2. Baseline Architecture: Deep Modality Blending Network (DMBN)

The DMBN, as presented in, is chosen due to its multimodal nature, non-autoregressive prediction capabilities, and use of CNPs. Its original setup involves:
- **Input:** Data from a 7-degree-of-freedom robot arm in a simulation environment. This includes sequences of grasping and pushing motions, comprising both visual (images from a fixed viewpoint) and proprioceptive (joint values) modalities.
- **Encoding:** Two separate encoders are used: a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for visual input and a Fully Connected Network (FCN) for proprioceptive input.
- **Feature Combination:** The encoded features from both modalities are combined via averaging to produce a shared multimodal hidden representation.
- **Temporal Input (Original DMBN):** In the original DMBN, context time is introduced as a new channel in both the visual and proprioceptive input streams. Target times are appended to the combined representative element of the context set before decoding.
- **Decoding:** This combined representation, augmented with target times, is passed through separate decoders for each modality. These decoders generate both mean and variance signals, effectively reconstructing a distribution over the target set.

#### 3.3. Evaluation of the Original DMBN

To evaluate the DMBN, the authors reimplemented the architecture in PyTorch, making adjustments to replicate original results (e.g., splitting decoder heads for mean and variance outputs).
- **Qualitative Assessment:** The DMBN's output was qualitatively assessed on a standard test sequence (t-sequence) to observe its reconstruction quality.
- **Generalization Test with Synthetic Sequences:** To probe the DMBN's understanding of temporal dynamics, synthetic sequences were generated:
 - **Permuted Sequence (p-sequence):** The time instants associated with each observation were permuted, but the semantic content of the observations remained the same.
 - **Frozen Sequence (f-sequence):** Sections of the test sequence were frozen, meaning frames were repeated, simulating pauses in motion.
 - The model's output on these sequences was visually inspected to determine if it recognized and reproduced the manipulated temporal structure or defaulted to an "ordered" reconstruction.
- **Quantitative Assessment of Temporal Encoding ("Time as Channel" Problem):** To objectively verify whether the DMBN was effectively using the temporal information provided as a channel, FCN heads were trained on the frozen encoders of the DMBN. The objective of these FCNs was to regress back to the context time sequence, essentially inverting the temporal encoding process. The performance of these regression heads was compared against two baselines:
 - **Null Baseline:** Encoders receiving a null time signal.
 - **Random Baseline:** Untrained encoders.
 This comparison aimed to quantify if the DMBN's encoders actively silenced or ignored the temporal information.

#### 3.4. Proposed Modification: DMBN-Positional Time Encoding (DMBN-PTE)

Based on the identified limitations in temporal representation, a revised architecture, DMBN-PTE, was proposed, drawing inspiration from positional encodings used in Transformer networks.
- **Revised Temporal Encoding ("Time as Context"):**
 - Temporal information (context time) is projected into the hidden space using a single-layer FCN.
 - This projected temporal information is then *added* to the encoded features from the modalities.
 - The combined result undergoes a non-linear projection (e.g., through an activation function) back into the same hidden space.
 - For target times, the same transformation is applied, but the resulting temporal encoding is *subtracted* from the set-representing elements before being passed to the decoders. This subtraction is designed to "revert" the process of injecting time, allowing the decoder to isolate the signal from its temporal context.
- **Training Data Augmentation:** To enhance the DMBN-PTE's robustness to temporal variations, the training dataset was augmented by:
 - Randomly modifying the speed of sections within the action sequences.
 - Adding repeated frames within sequences.

#### 3.5. Evaluation of DMBN-PTE

The modified DMBN-PTE was evaluated both quantitatively and qualitatively:
- **Quantitative Assessment:** The FCN head regression test (as described in 4.3) was re-applied to the DMBN-PTE encoders to measure the effectiveness of the new temporal encoding method in making temporal information accessible within the hidden layers.
- **Qualitative Assessment:** The DMBN-PTE's generated outputs for the t-, p-, and f-sequences were visually examined to determine if the architectural changes resulted in improved generalization and a more accurate representation of the manipulated temporal dynamics.

This systematic approach, moving from initial evaluation to problem identification, architectural modification, and re-evaluation, enabled the authors to assess and improve the temporal representation capabilities of the DMBN for multimodal action prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presented several findings regarding the DMBN's performance in multimodal action prediction and the effectiveness of the proposed DMBN-PTE modification.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of the Original Deep Modality Blending Network (DMBN)

- **Qualitative Reconstruction on Standard Sequences:** The original DMBN demonstrated a qualitatively good reconstruction of visuo-motor sensory signals during partially observed standard test sequences (t-sequences). However, it exhibited a general overestimation of variance in its probabilistic output, indicating a degree of uncertainty in its predictions.

- **Difficulty in Generalization to Manipulated Time Sequences:**
 - When presented with a **permuted sequence (p-sequence)**, where the time instants associated with observations were randomized, the DMBN's output unexpectedly reconstructed an "ordered" underlying sequence. This observation suggested that the network largely ignored the specific time associated with each observation's content, prioritizing the semantic content and its typical progression.
 - Similarly, for **frozen sequences (f-sequences)**, the DMBN struggled to accurately represent the temporal arrest, often continuing to predict motion or recovering from the "freeze" prematurely, rather than capturing the extended pause.
 This indicated a fundamental limitation in the DMBN's ability to generalize action dynamics when the temporal structure deviated from the learned, canonical order.

- **Quantitative Evidence of Silenced Temporal Information ("Time as Channel"):** To further investigate the observed temporal insensitivity, FCN heads were trained to regress back to the context time from the frozen DMBN encoders.
 - The results (Table 1) showed that the DMBN encoders performed worse than the "Random" baseline and only marginally better than the "Null" baseline in reconstructing the input time. Specifically, DMBN's image encoder loss was 29.63 (1e-3) and joint encoder loss was 86.56 (1e-3), while the Random baseline achieved 2.18 and 4.48 respectively.
 - This quantitative analysis confirmed that the original method of inserting time as a channel in the input (as in Fig. 1a.i) effectively resulted in the temporal information being actively "silenced" or largely ignored by the network during the encoding process. Consequently, the network did not form a robust internal representation of time that could be used for flexible prediction.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of the Proposed DMBN-Positional Time Encoding (DMBN-PTE)

- **Improved Temporal Encoding ("Time as Context"):** The DMBN-PTE architecture, which integrates temporal information more explicitly by projecting it into the hidden space and combining it with feature encodings, demonstrated a significant improvement in representing time.
 - The quantitative regression test showed dramatically lower losses for DMBN-PTE when attempting to reconstruct context time from its encoders (Image Encoder Loss: 0.69 (1e-3); Joint Encoder Loss: 0.19 (1e-3)). These values are orders of magnitude smaller than those achieved by the original DMBN and the baselines.
 - This result indicates that the revised method of incorporating time (inspired by positional encodings and using a distinct projection/subtraction mechanism) successfully allowed the network to learn and maintain a robust representation of temporal information within its hidden layers.

- **Partial Improvements in Generalization:**
 - When applied to the **frozen sequences (f-sequence)**, the DMBN-PTE showed an improved ability to capture the freezing behavior for almost the entire duration of the freeze, demonstrating better adaptation to temporal arrests compared to the original DMBN.
 - However, similar to the original DMBN, the DMBN-PTE still reconstructed an unexpectedly "ordered" signal for the **permuted sequences (p-sequence)**. This suggests that while the network now has access to temporal information, it may still be prone to underfitting the context set's specific temporal order or exhibiting excessive memorization of typical sequence dynamics, preventing it from fully disentangling content from arbitrary temporal arrangement.

In summary, the study identified that the original DMBN struggled to represent temporal information effectively, leading to poor generalization. The DMBN-PTE successfully addressed this by incorporating a positional time encoding mechanism, significantly improving the network's internal representation of time and its ability to capture temporal arrests. However, challenges remain in fully generalizing to arbitrarily permuted temporal sequences, suggesting further research is needed for complete disentanglement of content and temporal order.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of cognitive robotics and machine learning by addressing a fundamental challenge in multimodal action prediction: the robust representation and utilization of temporal information. The findings and the proposed architecture, DMBN-PTE, carry several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Advancement in MNS-Inspired Robotics:** The work contributes to the development of Mirror Neuron System (MNS)-inspired robotic architectures. By focusing on self-action prediction and refining the temporal understanding, DMBN-PTE represents a step towards creating robots that can learn internal models of their own actions, a prerequisite for understanding and predicting the actions of others, aligning with theories of MNS ontogeny. This moves beyond merely reactive or imitative behaviors towards more cognitive, predictive capabilities in robots.

2. **Improved Generalization in Multimodal Action Prediction:** The identification of the original DMBN's limitations in handling variable temporal dynamics and the subsequent improvement offered by DMBN-PTE are significant. The ability to properly encode and utilize temporal context is crucial for robots operating in dynamic, real-world environments where actions can vary in speed, pauses, and ordering. A network that can accurately predict actions despite such temporal variability would lead to more adaptable and robust robotic systems.

3. **Enhanced Generative Models for Time Series Data:** The study highlights the advantages of non-autoregressive and probabilistic generative models, such as those based on Conditional Neural Processes (CNPs), for action prediction. Unlike autoregressive models that accumulate errors over time, CNP-based architectures can predict multiple future points in parallel and naturally represent uncertainty through mean and variance outputs. This capability is valuable for robotic decision-making and planning, where anticipating potential outcomes and their likelihoods is essential.

4. **Addressing a Core Limitation in Neural Processes:** The paper explicitly identifies and provides a solution to a limitation in how temporal information was initially handled within a CNP-based multimodal network. The "Time as Channel" versus "Time as Context" discussion, and the successful implementation of a positional time encoding, offer insights for future applications of Neural Processes to high-dimensional, multimodal time series data, not just in robotics but potentially in other domains requiring temporal forecasting.

5. **Foundation for Online and Curiosity-Driven Learning:** The improved temporal representation in DMBN-PTE lays groundwork for more sophisticated learning paradigms. Future work outlined by the authors, such as exploring online learning and curiosity-driven exploration, could leverage the model's ability to represent uncertainty. This could enable robots to actively explore their environment to reduce prediction errors or acquire new knowledge, fostering autonomous skill acquisition and adaptation in dynamic settings.

6. **Towards Human-Robot Interaction and Transparency:** The broader motivation for MNS-inspired robotics is to enable robots to achieve human-like cognition, facilitating their integration and transparency in human society. A robot that can accurately predict human actions, or even its own actions in a human-understandable way, can engage in more intuitive and safer interactions. DMBN-PTE contributes to this long-term vision by refining a critical component for action understanding and prediction.

In conclusion, this research refines a key aspect of multimodal action prediction in cognitive robotics, making MNS-inspired architectures more capable of handling the temporal complexities of real-world actions. The DMBN-PTE serves as a foundational step towards more robust, adaptable, and autonomously learning robotic systems, with implications for a wide range of applications requiring sophisticated temporal reasoning and predictive capabilities.
