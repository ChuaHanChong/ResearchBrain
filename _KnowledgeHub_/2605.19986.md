# Beyond Binary Success: A Diagnostic Meta-Evaluation Framework for Fine-Grained Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MetaFine introduces a diagnostic meta-evaluation framework that disentangles robotic manipulation competency into understanding, perception, and controlled behavior for fine-grained tasks. The framework revealed that conventional binary success metrics inflate VLA model capabilities by up to 70% and identified specific architectural bottlenecks in semantic grounding, local spatial structure preservation, and action generation.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI benchmarks use binary success rates (pass/fail), which overstate reported capabilities (up to 70%) and obscure underlying failure reasons.
- This scalar evaluation prevents diagnosis of architectural bottlenecks in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across instruction understanding, perception, and motor execution.
- Heterogeneous task specifications across current manipulation benchmarks (e.g., RLBench, ManiSkill) hinder unified comparison and diagnosis of policy performance.

## Method

- **MetaFine** introduces a compositional task graph with atomic skills and an extensible asset library, allowing unified task reconstruction from diverse benchmarks via adapters.
- It implements a three-dimensional diagnostic protocol to evaluate understanding (semantic interventions), perception (geometric/photometric perturbations), and controlled behavior (stage-wise success, trajectory stability).
- A hybrid real–sim evaluation combines limited real-world rollouts with scalable simulation, using 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== for environment registration and Prediction-Powered Inference for robust performance estimates.

## Results

- Binary success metrics inflated VLA capabilities by up to **70%** (top policies: **80%** on "Grasp Part" vs **12%** on "Rotate Along" under fine-grained constraints), while hybrid real-sim PPI evaluation reduced grasping-task performance-estimate std from up to **11.5%** to **2.6-2.9%**.
- VLA policies universally scored **0%** on semantically modified instructions, indicating reliance on instruction-scene correlations; policies with decoupled pathways showed frequent behavioral arrest in multi-stage tasks.
- OpenVLA-OFT's dual encoder (DINOv2+SigLIP) demonstrated better spatial fidelity for tasks like peg-in-hole compared to π0.5's single SigLIP encoder, and a multi-scale visual encoder causally improved grasp success (**39%** to **67%**) and alignment (**0%** to **32%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained manipulation requires a multi-dimensional evaluation beyond binary success to accurately assess understanding, perception, and controlled behavior.
> - VLA semantic grounding primarily relies on instruction-scene correlations rather than genuine compositional understanding (causing instruction-following deficits), and the visual encoder's ability to preserve local spatial structure is a separate bottleneck for precision.
> - Action generation models face a dilemma between stability and expressiveness, where deterministic methods can be rigid and stochastic methods can suffer from spatial drift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) and robotic manipulation has progressed significantly, with capabilities extending from coarse object movement to more intricate tasks. However, fine-grained manipulation, which includes actions such as threading a needle or precisely aligning components, presents a distinct level of complexity. Success in this domain relies on a tight integration of local attribute grounding, high-fidelity spatial perception, and accurate motor execution that respects tight physical constraints. This contrasts with coarser manipulation tasks, which may often succeed based on global scene context and approximate object-level transport.

Existing embodied AI benchmarks typically evaluate manipulation competency using binary success rates (pass or fail). This approach can overstate reported capabilities, potentially by a substantial margin (e.g., up to 70%), and obscures the underlying reasons for failure. Such scalar evaluation cannot differentiate whether a task failed due to a misunderstanding of the instruction, inaccurate perception of the environment, or issues during physical execution. This limitation hinders the identification of specific architectural bottlenecks in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby impeding their development and real-world deployment.

Current research on fine-grained manipulation often addresses isolated aspects. For instance, some work focuses on part-level grasping [24–26], while others investigate dexterous manipulation requiring contact-rich control. Advancements in 3D understanding, such as PartNet for hierarchical part annotations and PartInstruct for part-level instruction following, also contribute to this domain. However, these efforts generally rely on binary success metrics for evaluation and do not provide a unified framework to diagnose failures across the multifaceted demands of fine-grained tasks.

Similarly, visual motor policies and VLA models, which map raw observations to robot actions, have diversified in terms of action generation paradigms (deterministic regression, diffusion, flow matching) and visual representations (2D image encoders, 3D point clouds, dual-encoder fusions). Models like RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, and π0.5 integrate language conditioning for more general capabilities. Despite these architectural advancements, the continued reliance on single-scalar evaluation metrics prevents a clear understanding of how specific architectural choices influence different aspects of manipulation competency.

Existing manipulation benchmarks like RLBench, ManiSkill, CALVIN, LIBERO, and RoboTwin offer diverse tasks but also share the limitations of binary success metrics and heterogeneous task specifications. This heterogeneity makes direct comparison and diagnosis of policy performance across different benchmarks challenging.

Finally, advancements in real-to-sim evaluation, including methods like SIMPLER, RialTo, and 3D Gaussian Splatting [37–40], aim to bridge the gap between simulation and real-world performance. While these approaches enhance simulator fidelity, they often assume that simulation can sufficiently replace real-world rollouts, an assumption that may not always hold.

This work introduces MetaFine to address these limitations by providing a diagnostic meta-evaluation framework. It aims to decouple manipulation competency, absorb heterogeneous benchmarks into a unified diagnostic space, and offer a statistically grounded hybrid real-to-sim evaluation, thereby shifting the focus from superficial ranking to systematic diagnosis of capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to address the shortcomings of conventional evaluation metrics in embodied AI, specifically for fine-grained manipulation tasks. The paper argues that prevailing benchmarks, by collapsing complex manipulation capacities into binary success rates, create an "illusion of competency." This illusion masks the true limitations and architectural bottlenecks of VLA models, hindering their progress and real-world applicability.

The key objectives of introducing the MetaFine framework are:
- **To Disentangle Manipulation Competency:** A core objective is to move beyond pass-or-fail outcomes by decomposing manipulation competency into three distinct axes: **understanding** (of symbolic goals and instructions), **perception** (high-fidelity spatial awareness), and **controlled behavior** (constraint-respecting motor execution). This allows for a more granular diagnosis of where failures occur.
- **To Expose Architectural Bottlenecks:** By disentangling competencies, the framework aims to identify specific weaknesses in VLA model architectures that are otherwise obscured by aggregate success rates. This includes pinpointing whether failures stem from language grounding, visual representation, or action generation.
- **To Provide a Unified Diagnostic Protocol:** MetaFine seeks to absorb diverse external benchmarks (e.g., RoboTwin, CALVIN, LIBERO) and reconstruct their tasks into diagnostic scenarios under a unified protocol. This enables consistent comparison of policies across different task suites, addressing the fragmentation in existing evaluation methodologies.
- **To Facilitate Actionable Design Insights:** The ultimate goal is to transform benchmarking from a mechanism for ranking models into an "actionable compass." By identifying the specific layer or capacity responsible for failure, MetaFine intends to guide researchers in making targeted improvements to VLA models.
- **To Support Hybrid Real–Sim Validation:** Recognizing the cost and statistical instability of real-world robot experiments, MetaFine aims to integrate a hybrid evaluation approach. This involves using limited real-world rollouts to calibrate scalable simulation-based estimates, thereby achieving more stable and reproducible physical benchmarking.
- **To Build Extensible Community Infrastructure:** MetaFine is designed as an open and extensible framework, providing a compositional task graph, an asset library, and supporting resources to encourage community contributions and continuous expansion of evaluation scenarios.

In essence, the motivation is to replace a coarse, outcome-based assessment with a fine-grained, diagnostic evaluation that reveals *why* a robot fails, not just *if* it fails. This shift is crucial for systematically advancing the layered capacities required for genuine robotic dexterity in complex, fine-grained tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MetaFine is introduced as a diagnostic meta-evaluation framework designed to provide a comprehensive analysis of fine-grained manipulation capabilities, moving beyond binary success metrics. It comprises several interconnected components and protocols:

#### 3.1. Compositional Task Graph and Asset Library

- **Atomic Skills:** MetaFine defines a vocabulary of fundamental fine-grained manipulation "atomic skills" (e.g., Grasp Part, Press Part, Toggle Part, Rotate Along, Slide Along, Open Hinge, Align, Insert, Move To, Flip). Each skill is formally specified by preconditions (p), postconditions (q), and physical constraints (C) that must be maintained during execution.
- **Task Composition:** Tasks are generated by composing these atomic skills into a directed graph. Sequential, conditional, and parallel edges define how skills can combine, based on the compatibility of their preconditions and postconditions (`q_i ⇒ p_j`). This allows for the creation of tasks with varying complexity and allows for the systematic decomposition of long-horizon tasks into stages.
- **Extensible Asset Library:** The framework includes a library of 431 objects, 1,078 labeled parts, and 4,312 candidate grasp poses. Objects are annotated with part-level segmentation, grasp pose candidates, and manipulation constraints (e.g., rotation axes, hinge axes). A lightweight annotation toolkit facilitates rapid expansion of this library.
- **Benchmark Absorption:** MetaFine uses an adapter mechanism to integrate tasks from heterogeneous external benchmarks (e.g., RoboTwin, CALVIN, LIBERO). These adapters map the external benchmarks' objects, robots, actions, and attributes onto MetaFine's standardized representations, thereby enabling evaluation under a unified diagnostic protocol. Missing fine-grained annotations in absorbed assets are backfilled using the automated annotation toolkit.

#### 3.2. Three-Dimensional Diagnostic Protocol

MetaFine disentangles manipulation competency along three axes, each with specific intervention and measurement strategies:
- **Understanding Evaluation:**
 - **Semantic Interventions:** The protocol applies controlled semantic modifications to natural-language instructions while keeping the visual scene fixed. Examples include part substitution (e.g., "grasp cap" to "grasp body"), directional reversal ("move left" to "move right"), or property alteration (e.g., color).
 - **Metrics:** Measures include the drop in success rate (`∆drop`) under the original acceptance criteria and the success rate (`SRmod`) when evaluated against the *modified* instruction's criteria.
 - **Compound Commands:** For multi-stage instructions, stage-wise success is evaluated to assess if policies can parse and transition between sub-instructions or suffer behavioral arrest.
- **Perception Evaluation:**
 - **Perturbations:** Geometric perturbations (camera viewpoint shifts in position and orientation) and photometric perturbations (lighting intensity, direction, and color temperature variations) are applied at three severity levels (L1, L2, L3).
 - **Metrics:** Robustness is quantified using the Area Under the Success Curve (AUSC), which aggregates success rates across perturbation levels. Geometric and photometric AUSC are reported separately.
- **Controlled Behavior Evaluation:**
 - **Stage-wise Success:** Long-horizon tasks are decomposed into their constituent atomic skills, and acceptance criteria are evaluated independently at each stage. This reveals the specific point in a sequence where a policy fails.
 - **Trajectory Stability:** The smoothness of generated action sequences is measured using a `Stability` metric (exponential of negative mean squared difference between successive actions). This differentiates stable but misdirected motion from jerky, uncoordinated execution.

#### 3.3. Hybrid Real–Sim Evaluation

- **Objective:** To achieve statistically robust real-world performance estimates despite limited hardware budgets.
- **Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI):** MetaFine adopts a two-stage PPI estimator. This combines a small set of real-world rollouts (n) with a larger set of simulation-only rollouts (N). The real rollouts serve as a "rectifier" to correct for any systematic bias between simulation and real outcomes, while the large simulation set provides a low-variance estimate.
- **Real2Sim Registration:** Physical workspaces are reconstructed into simulation environments using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from mobile device scans. This creates a digital twin that aligns camera intrinsics, robot base pose, and object placements, allowing for a shared evaluation distribution (`D_eval`) between real and simulated trials.
- **Evaluation Pipeline:** The protocol involves defining `D_eval`, sampling `n + N` configurations, 3DGS-based real2sim registration, paired execution (all `n + N` in sim, `n` in real), and calibrated reporting of PPI estimates with confidence intervals.

#### 3.4. Evaluated Policies

The framework was used to benchmark seven representative VLA policies, covering diverse architectural families:
- ACT (ResNet, deterministic regression, no language)
- DP3 (Point cloud encoder, diffusion, no language)
- Octo (Lightweight encoder, diffusion, language conditioned)
- OpenVLA (DINOv2+SigLIP dual encoder, autoregressive discrete actions, Llama 2 7B backbone)
- OpenVLA-OFT (OpenVLA architecture, MLP head for continuous ℓ1 regression)
- π0 (SigLIP encoder, PaliGemma VLM, flow-matching action expert)
- π0.5 (SigLIP encoder, PaliGemma VLM, joint language and flow-matching optimization)

All policies were fine-tuned on MetaFine's demonstration data under matched conditions (100 demonstrations per task variant), ensuring that performance differences reflect architectural properties rather than varying training efforts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art VLA models using the MetaFine framework revealed several findings regarding fine-grained manipulation capabilities:

- **Finding 1: Conventional binary metrics inflate apparent competency.**
 - When fine-grained constraints (e.g., specific part contact, precise direction) are enforced, the success rates of VLA models significantly drop compared to object-level success metrics. For instance, the best policy achieved 80% on "Grasp Part" (requiring specific part grasping) but only 12% on "Rotate Along."
 - Performance further degraded under perceptual perturbations. Under severe lighting perturbation, the top policy for "Grasp Part" dropped from 80% to 15%. This demonstrates that binary metrics mask substantial discrepancies between apparent and genuine physical mastery, with capabilities potentially overestimated by up to 70%.
 - Trajectory smoothness analysis also revealed dissociations, where fluid motion might not correspond to task success, indicating well-controlled but misdirected actions.

- **Finding 2: Current VLAs exhibit deficits in semantic grounding and instruction following.**
 - **Attribute-level interventions:** When instructions were semantically modified (e.g., changing "grasp the cap" to "grasp the body" while keeping the scene fixed), all five evaluated VLA policies universally scored 0% on the modified instruction. While policies showed sensitivity to the changed language (some `SRdrop` values up to 34%), this did not translate into correct spatial re-grounding, indicating reliance on instruction-scene correlations rather than genuine compositional understanding.
 - **Compound commands:** In multi-stage tasks (e.g., sorting colored cubes), policies with unified language–action co-optimization (π0.5, π0) achieved non-zero success across all stages. In contrast, policies with decoupled pathways (OpenVLA-OFT, OpenVLA, Octo) frequently exhibited behavioral arrest, collapsing to 0% success in later sub-tasks, suggesting they failed to preserve or update linguistic structure beyond the initial sub-task.

- **Finding 3: The visual encoder's preservation of local spatial structure is a key precision bottleneck.**
 - **Spatial fidelity:** In the peg-in-hole task, all five VLAs achieved near-zero overall success (0–3%). However, stage-wise evaluation revealed differences. OpenVLA-OFT grasped the peg in 47% of trials and aligned it in 19%, while π0.5 grasped in 39% but never aligned (0%). Visualizations (CAM) indicated that OpenVLA-OFT's dual encoder (DINOv2+SigLIP) showed more concentrated activation on target parts than π0.5's single SigLIP encoder, suggesting the precision bottleneck originates at the visual frontend.
 - **Encoder-level adaptation:** Freezing downstream components of π0.5 and applying one-shot adaptation (LoRA fine-tuning) solely to the visual encoder output for viewpoint robustness achieved 71.00% AUSC on "Grasp Part," matching or exceeding full-backbone LoRA (69.25%). This suggests that geometric robustness degradation primarily stems from the visual encoder.
 - **Causal intervention:** Replacing π0.5's single-scale SigLIP encoder with a multi-scale cross-attention encoder, while keeping the VLM backbone and action head fixed, significantly improved grasp success (39% to 67%) and unlocked alignment capabilities (0% to 32%). This demonstrated a causal link between the visual frontend and fine-grained precision, confirming the encoder as a manipulable bottleneck.

- **Finding 4: Action generation faces a stability–expressiveness dilemma under perceptual uncertainty.**
 - **Deterministic regression (OpenVLA-OFT):** Produced coherent approach trajectories despite perceptual bias. However, this stability became rigid in constrained phases, leading to repetitive, ineffective approaches without corrective diversity.
 - **Stochastic generation (π0.5, π0):** Offered richer corrective diversity but could accumulate spatial drift under ambiguous visual inputs, leading to inconsistent actions over long horizons.
 - The results suggest that fine-grained behavior likely requires stage-adaptive mechanisms that balance stable convergence when the spatial target is reliable with controlled exploration when small corrective motions are needed.

- **Real-world validation via hybrid evaluation:**
 - MetaFine's hybrid real–sim validation, using 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-to-sim transfer and Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI), demonstrated improved stability for real-world performance estimates. For a fine-grained grasping task, PPI calibration reduced the standard deviation of estimates from n=20 hardware-only trials from 11.5% to 2.6% for π0.5 and from 5.8% to 2.9% for π0. This method enables more stable and comparable physical benchmarking with limited real-world data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MetaFine framework represents a shift in the evaluation paradigm for embodied AI, particularly for fine-grained manipulation. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Beyond Binary Success:** By challenging the reliance on single-scalar success rates, MetaFine moves evaluation from merely ranking models to providing a detailed diagnosis of their capabilities. This is crucial for fine-grained tasks where local attributes, spatial relations, and constrained execution can each independently lead to failure.

- **Actionable Design Insights:** The framework’s ability to disentangle competency into understanding, perception, and controlled behavior offers actionable insights for VLA model design. Researchers can identify whether a failure stems from a language grounding deficiency, a lack of spatial fidelity in visual representations, or limitations in action generation. This diagnostic power can guide targeted architectural repairs and co-design efforts across the sensorimotor pipeline, rather than relying on independent scaling of individual components. Specifically, it suggests:
 - Language should function as a compositional spatial constraint rather than a mere task-level cue.
 - Visual frontends need optimization for local spatial structure preservation under variations, not just semantic recognition.
 - Action generation should be stage-adaptive, balancing stability with expressive corrective abilities.

- **Unified Benchmarking Infrastructure:** MetaFine provides an open and extensible infrastructure, including a compositional task graph and an asset library. This allows external benchmarks to be absorbed and re-evaluated under a shared diagnostic protocol, translating heterogeneous task suites into comparable evidence. This addresses the fragmentation in the field, making cross-benchmark comparisons meaningful by explicitly linking performance differences to specific competency dimensions.

- **Reproducible and Scalable Real-World Evaluation:** The hybrid real–sim evaluation pathway, leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-to-sim transfer and Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI), offers a practical solution for conducting robust and reproducible real-world evaluations. By statistically anchoring scalable simulation data with limited real-world rollouts, MetaFine makes physical benchmarking more accessible and reliable, especially under typical hardware constraints. This prevents simulation from merely replacing reality, instead using it to enhance the statistical power of real-world measurements.

- **Community Expansion and Evolution:** Designed as an open ecosystem, MetaFine encourages continuous expansion of its atomic skills, assets, perturbations, and real-world registrations by the research community. This ensures the framework can evolve with advancements in robot capabilities and new failure modes, maintaining its relevance as fine-grained manipulation continues to be an open-ended research domain.

In summary, MetaFine provides a foundational shift in how fine-grained manipulation capabilities are assessed, offering a diagnostic compass for researchers to systematically identify and address the complex, layered challenges inherent in achieving genuine robotic dexterity.
