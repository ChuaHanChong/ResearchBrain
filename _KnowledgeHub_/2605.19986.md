# Beyond Binary Success: A Diagnostic Meta-Evaluation Framework for Fine-Grained Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MetaFine introduces a diagnostic meta-evaluation framework that disentangles robotic manipulation competency into understanding, perception, and controlled behavior for fine-grained tasks. The framework revealed that conventional binary success metrics inflate VLA model capabilities by up to 70% and identified specific architectural bottlenecks in semantic grounding, local spatial structure preservation, and action generation.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI benchmarks use binary success rates (pass/fail), which overstate reported capabilities (up to 70%) and obscure underlying failure reasons.
- This scalar evaluation prevents diagnosis of architectural bottlenecks in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across instruction understanding, perception, and motor execution.
- Heterogeneous task specifications across current manipulation benchmarks (e.g., RLBench, ManiSkill) hinder unified comparison and diagnosis of policy performance.

## Method

- **MetaFine** introduces a compositional task graph with atomic skills and an extensible asset library, allowing unified task reconstruction from diverse benchmarks via adapters.
- It implements a three-dimensional diagnostic protocol to evaluate understanding (semantic interventions), perception (geometric/photometric perturbations), and controlled behavior (stage-wise success, trajectory stability).
- A hybrid real–sim evaluation combines limited real-world rollouts with scalable simulation, using 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== for environment registration and Prediction-Powered Inference for robust performance estimates.

## Results

- Binary success metrics inflated VLA model capabilities by up to **70%**, with top policies achieving **80%** on "Grasp Part" but only **12%** on "Rotate Along" when fine-grained constraints were enforced.
- VLA policies universally scored **0%** on semantically modified instructions, indicating reliance on instruction-scene correlations; policies with decoupled pathways showed frequent behavioral arrest in multi-stage tasks.
- OpenVLA-OFT's dual encoder (DINOv2+SigLIP) demonstrated better spatial fidelity for tasks like peg-in-hole compared to π0.5's single SigLIP encoder, and a multi-scale visual encoder causally improved grasp success (**39%** to **67%**) and alignment (**0%** to **32%**).
- Hybrid real–sim evaluation using PPI reduced the standard deviation of real-world performance estimates for grasping tasks from up to **11.5%** to around 2.6-**2.9%**, enabling more stable benchmarking with limited physical data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained manipulation requires a multi-dimensional evaluation beyond binary success to accurately assess understanding, perception, and controlled behavior.
> - The semantic grounding of VLA models primarily relies on instruction-scene correlations rather than genuine compositional understanding, leading to deficits in instruction following.
> - The visual encoder's ability to preserve local spatial structure is a critical bottleneck for precision in fine-grained tasks.
> - Action generation models face a dilemma between stability and expressiveness, where deterministic methods can be rigid and stochastic methods can suffer from spatial drift.
