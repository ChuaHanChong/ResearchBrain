# ContractionPPO: Certified Reinforcement Learning via Differentiable Contraction Layers

> [!summary] Summary
> ContractionPPO introduces a framework that augments Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a differentiable contraction metric layer, enabling certifiably robust planning and control for legged robots. The approach jointly trains a control policy and a stability certificate, demonstrating superior locomotion performance and resilience to high-magnitude external disturbances, achieving a 0% failure rate in demanding moving platform tasks on a quadruped robot where baseline methods failed.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning policies for legged robots achieve high performance but typically lack formal stability and robustness guarantees in unstructured environments.
- Classical contraction theory provides powerful stability assurances but has been challenging to apply to high-dimensional, nonlinear, and contact-rich systems like quadruped robots.
- There is a need to combine the empirical success of deep reinforcement learning with formal assurances of behavior for reliable and safe robot deployment.

## Method

- A **ContractionPPO** framework integrates a differentiable contraction metric layer directly into PPO, co-training a standard PPO policy and a Lipschitz-constrained contraction metric network.
- The contraction metric is learned as a state-dependent neural network using privileged information available only during simulation training, while the deployed control policy remains observation-based.
- Joint training uses a composite loss function, combining the standard PPO objective with a contraction loss (penalizing stability violations) and a regularization loss for the metric's positive definiteness.

## Results

- ContractionPPO achieved a **0%** failure rate across all tested control point settings in a demanding handstand task on a moving platform, significantly outperforming PPO (**99%** failure), RMA (**20%** failure), and TumblerNet (**100%** failure).
- The framework demonstrated strong robustness to untrained, high-magnitude external wind disturbances (up to **9.6** m/s) on a quadruped robot, maintaining stability and converging back to a steady posture within a safe region.
- ContractionPPO successfully generalized to a Booster T1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust stabilization on a moving platform and confirming its applicability across different robot morphologies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a state-dependent contraction metric simultaneously with the control policy acts as an implicit regularizer, guiding the policy towards robust, incrementally exponentially stable solutions.
> - Privileged information from simulation can be effectively utilized for learning the stability certificate without compromising the real-world deployability of the observation-based control policy.
> - Theoretical guarantees derived through Lipschitz constraints on neural networks provide explicit bounds for sim-to-real transfer of stability properties and ensure robustness against bounded disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of control policies for legged robots operating in unstructured environments presents a dual challenge: achieving high performance and ensuring formal guarantees of robustness and stability.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Robotics:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and other RL methods have demonstrated empirical success in training agile and versatile locomotion policies for quadruped robots. These methods leverage privileged information available during simulation training, such as full system state and dynamics, to enhance reward shaping and learning signals. However, a significant limitation of standard RL policies is the typical absence of formal guarantees regarding stability or robustness. While approaches like domain randomization can improve empirical robustness, they do not provide certified assurances of behavior under unforeseen conditions or large disturbances. Some works integrate RL with control-theoretic concepts but often rely on assumptions of full state observability, which is not always practical for high-dimensional real-world systems.

**Classical and Hybrid Control:** Traditional model-based control methods offer robustness through design but frequently struggle with generalization to unstructured settings. Hybrid approaches, which embed control-theoretic tools like Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) into learning frameworks, can provide local safety guarantees. However, these often necessitate manual design of functions and online optimization, which may limit scalability for robots with many degrees of freedom. Such methods typically do not offer trajectory-level robustness guarantees, where nearby trajectories are proven to converge.

**Contraction Theory:** Contraction theory provides a framework for analyzing the incremental exponential stability (IES) of nonlinear dynamical systems. It establishes conditions under which all system trajectories converge exponentially toward one another, offering a robust and general stability guarantee that is distinct from Lyapunov stability, which focuses on convergence to a single equilibrium point. Historically, applying contraction theory to high-dimensional, complex nonlinear systems like legged robots has been challenging, as it often requires hand-designed metrics and the solution of complex matrix inequalities. Furthermore, classical contraction analysis often requires specifying desired trajectories or setpoints, which can be difficult to construct for contact-rich systems.

**The Gap Addressed by This Work:** This research addresses the gap between the empirical performance of deep reinforcement learning policies and the need for formal stability guarantees in legged robotics. It seeks to combine the benefits of PPO (high performance, use of privileged information during training) with the formal assurances of contraction theory, overcoming the difficulties of applying classical contraction analysis to high-dimensional systems by learning the contraction metric using neural networks. This integration aims to produce control policies that are not only high-performing but also certifiably stable and robust, suitable for real-world deployment where safety and reliability are paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop ContractionPPO, a framework that augments Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a differentiable contraction metric layer to enable certified robust planning and control for legged robots. This overarching goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations:

- **Integrate Performance with Formal Guarantees:** The core motivation stems from the inherent trade-off in robotic control: achieving agile and dynamic behaviors (often best learned via RL) while simultaneously guaranteeing stability and robustness. Standard RL policies, despite their empirical success, typically lack formal guarantees, leaving their behavior under perturbations or unforeseen conditions uncertain. ContractionPPO aims to bridge this gap by enabling the policy to maximize performance metrics while concurrently generating a contraction metric that certifies the incremental exponential stability of the simulated closed-loop system.

- **Leverage Privileged Information for Certification without Compromising Deployment:** During RL training, simulations provide access to "privileged information" such as full system state and dynamics, which is not available to the robot at deployment time. The objective is to utilize this privileged information specifically for learning the contraction metric – the stability certificate – during training. This strategy allows the certification process to benefit from richer data while ensuring that the final *control policy* remains observation-based and deployable in the real world, relying only on sensor data.

- **Develop a Scalable Method for Contraction Analysis in Complex Systems:** Classical contraction theory, while powerful, has been difficult to apply to high-dimensional, nonlinear, and contact-rich systems like quadruped robots due to the complexity of hand-designing metrics and solving matrix inequalities. A key objective is to address this scalability challenge by parameterizing the contraction metric as a Lipschitz neural network, allowing it to be learned end-to-end alongside the control policy. This approach aims to make contraction analysis feasible for systems where traditional methods are intractable.

- **Derive Explicit Bounds for Sim-to-Real Generalization:** A crucial motivation is to provide theoretical guarantees that the learned stability certificate holds not just in simulation but also during real-world deployment. The objective includes deriving explicit upper bounds on the worst-case contraction rate. These bounds are expressed in terms of the Lipschitz constants of the neural networks and system dynamics, aiming to ensure that the certified properties generalize from the potentially idealized simulation environment to the complexities and uncertainties of the real world. This contributes to verifiable robustness under domain shifts and modeling errors.

- **Demonstrate Robustness to External Disturbances:** A practical motivation is to create policies that are inherently robust to external perturbations without requiring explicit training under such conditions. The goal is to demonstrate that by enforcing contraction conditions during training, the resulting policy exhibits resilient and stable behavior, ensuring safe recovery and bounded deviations even when subjected to strong disturbances not encountered during the learning phase.

In summary, the motivation is to advance the field of legged robotics by offering a framework that combines the empirical power of deep reinforcement learning with rigorous, verifiable stability guarantees, thereby moving towards "certified RL" for reliable and safe robot operation in complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ContractionPPO integrates a differentiable contraction metric layer directly into the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework. The approach involves co-training two neural networks: a standard PPO policy and a contraction metric network, both subject to Lipschitz constraints.

**System Dynamics and Control Policy:**
The robot's dynamics are modeled as a general nonlinear control-affine system: $\dot{x} = f(x) + B(x)u + d(x, t)$, where $x$ is the state, $u$ is the control input, $f(x)$ represents drift dynamics, $B(x)$ is the input matrix, and $d(x, t)$ signifies bounded disturbances. The agent observes $y = h(x)$ (e.g., proprioception, IMU).
The control policy operates in two layers:
1. **PPO Policy ($\Pi_{\text{PPO}}_\theta$)**: This high-level neural network receives raw sensor observations $y$ and outputs desired joint poses $q_{\text{ppo}_d}$.
2. **PD Controller ($\Pi_{\text{PD}}_\theta$)**: A low-level PD controller then generates torques based on these desired poses and current joint states: $\Pi_{\text{PD}}_\theta = K_p(q_{\text{ppo}_d} - q) + K_d(\dot{q}_{\text{ppo}_d} - \dot{q})$. The PPO policy effectively learns a nonlinear feedback term $\Delta q_\theta(y)$ that modifies a nominal desired trajectory $q_d$.

**Contraction Metric Network ($M_\phi$):**
A second neural network, parameterized by $\phi$, learns the state-dependent contraction metric $M_\phi(x)$.
- **Input**: The metric network receives a rich state vector $x = [q_{\text{ppo}}(t), \dot{q}_{\text{ppo}}(t), p(t), v(t)]$, which includes privileged information (COM position $p(t)$ and velocity $v(t)$) only available during simulation training. This privileged information is not used during real-world deployment.
- **Parameterization**: To ensure positive definiteness and symmetry, $M_\phi(x)$ is parameterized as $M_\phi(x) = \Theta(x)^\top\Theta(x)$, where $\Theta(x)$ is a lower-triangular matrix output by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP). Uniform bounds $0 < m_{\text{min}}I \preceq M(x) \preceq m_{\text{max}}I < \infty$ are imposed on the metric.
- **Lyapunov Function**: The geodesic distance in the metric space is approximated by a quadratic differential Lyapunov function $V_\phi(x, t):= e^\top M_\phi(x)e$, where $e(t):= x(t) - x_d(t)$ is the error between the current state and a desired trajectory.

**Joint Training and Loss Function:**
The policy and metric networks are trained jointly by minimizing a composite loss function, $L_{\text{total}}(\theta, \phi)$:
$L_{\text{total}}(\theta, \phi) = L_{\text{PPO}}(\theta) + w_{\text{contr}}L_{\text{contr}}(\phi) + w_{\text{PD}}L_{\text{PD}}(\phi)$.
1. **PPO Objective ($L_{\text{PPO}}(\theta)$)**: This is the standard PPO loss, which includes a clipped surrogate objective, value prediction error, and policy entropy regularization.
2. **Contraction Loss ($L_{\text{contr}}(\phi)$)**: This hinge loss penalizes violations of the contraction condition. The contraction condition requires that the time derivative of the Lyapunov function satisfy $\dot{V}_\phi(x, t) + \alpha V_\phi(x, t) \leq -\epsilon_\alpha$, where $\alpha > 0$ is the desired contraction rate and $\epsilon_\alpha > 0$ is a state-dependent approximation margin. The loss is formulated as $L_{\text{contr}} = \text{ReLU}\left(\frac{\dot{V}_\phi(x, t) + \alpha V_\phi(x, t)}{V_\phi(x, t)} + \bar{\epsilon}_\alpha\right)$, where $\bar{\epsilon}_\alpha$ is a scaled margin. $\dot{V}_\phi$ is computed using the variational dynamics of the closed-loop system and the time derivative of the metric $\dot{M}_\phi$, both computed via automatic differentiation.
3. **PD Loss ($L_{\text{PD}}(\phi)$)**: This term regularizes the learned metric $M_\phi(x)$ to enforce its uniform positive definiteness. It penalizes eigenvalues of $M_\phi(x)$ that fall below a minimum threshold ($m_{\text{min}}$) or exceed a maximum threshold ($m_{\text{max}}$): $L_{\text{PD}}(\phi) = \text{ReLU}(m_{\text{min}} - \lambda_{\text{min}}(M_\phi(x))) + \text{ReLU}(\lambda_{\text{max}}(M_\phi(x)) - m_{\text{max}})$.

**Theoretical Guarantees for Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robustness:**
The framework incorporates theoretical results to ensure that the learned stability properties generalize from simulation to real-world deployment and provide robustness against disturbances.
- **Lipschitz Constraints**: Both the PPO policy and the metric network are constrained to be Lipschitz continuous. Specifically, bounds are placed on the spectral norm of the policy's Jacobian ($\|J_\pi\| \leq L_\pi$) and the Frobenius norm of the metric's gradient ($\|\nabla M_\phi\|_F \leq L_M$).
- **Theorem 1 (Sim-to-Real Generalization Guarantee)**: This theorem provides an explicit upper bound for the maximum eigenvalue of a normalized contraction residual operator. If this bound is negative, it implies that the closed-loop system is incrementally exponentially stable within the compact state space $K$ explored during training. The bound is expressed in terms of the Lipschitz constants $L_\pi$, $L_M$, and bounds on the system dynamics and observation Jacobian.
- **Corollary 1 (Lower bound for $\alpha$)**: This corollary directly follows from Theorem 1 and specifies conditions on the Lipschitz constants and dynamic bounds that must be met to achieve a desired contraction rate $\alpha$.
- **Lemma 1 (Lower bound on $\epsilon_\alpha$)**: This lemma clarifies the state-dependent nature of the margin $\epsilon_\alpha$ in the contraction loss and its relationship with the contraction rate $\alpha$, highlighting the trade-off between a faster convergence rate and the complexity of satisfying the contraction condition.
- **Theorem 2 (Robustness)**: This theorem establishes that if the contraction conditions (derived in Theorem 1) are met, the system retains input-to-state incremental stability under bounded external disturbances. It provides an upper bound on the error $||e(t)||$ which exponentially decays and is influenced by the disturbance magnitude.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Simulation Environment**: IsaacLab with the Unitree Go1 quadruped robot.
- **Hardware Platform**: Unitree Go1 on a moving platform at Caltech's CAST Arena, tracked by an OptiTrack motion capture system.
- **Training Configuration**: 4,096 parallel agents, achieving full training within approximately two hours of wall-clock time on an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU.
- **Network Architectures**: Actor/Critic networks are MLPs (512, 256, 128), and the contraction layer MLPs are (128, 64). ELU activation and Adam optimizer are used. Spectral normalization is applied to enforce Lipschitz continuity.
- **Hyperparameters**: Learning rate $1 \times 10^{-3}$, 5 epochs per update, 15,000 max iterations, rollout length 24 steps, discount factor $\gamma = 0.99$. The contraction loss weight $w_{\text{contr}}$ is set to 0.01.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates ContractionPPO in both simulation and hardware, benchmarking its performance against several baselines: PPO, Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA), and TumblerNet. The results focus on locomotion performance (tracking error) and robustness to external disturbances.

**Locomotion Performance (Handstand on Moving Platform):**
- **ContractionPPO**: In a handstand task where the quadruped was commanded to remain within a 0.8m radius circle on a moving platform, ContractionPPO consistently maintained the robot within this boundary. It exhibited bounded trajectories and robust gait control, particularly evident in long rollouts where it prevented drift and loss of balance.
- **Baseline Methods**:
 - **PPO and TumblerNet**: These methods showed significant drift, causing the robot to move outside the commanded circular region and eventually lose balance.
 - **RMA**: While demonstrating stronger performance than PPO and TumblerNet, RMA also ultimately failed to remain within the designated stable region over time.
- **Humanoid Stabilization**: The framework's generalizability was demonstrated by successfully applying ContractionPPO to stabilize a Booster T1 humanoid robot on a moving platform (Figure 9), showcasing robust performance across different robot morphologies.
- **Failure Rate Analysis (Table II)**: A quantitative comparison using increasing "control points" (which correspond to faster and more aggressive platform movements) further highlighted the performance differences:
 - **ContractionPPO**: Achieved a 0% failure rate across all tested control point settings (10, 20, 30, 40, 50) and over combined episodes, indicating consistent, certifiably stable behavior.
 - **TumblerNet**: Exhibited a 100% failure rate across all settings, suggesting a lack of robustness and poor generalization.
 - **RMA**: Demonstrated moderate performance with an average failure rate of 20%.
 - **PPO**: Performed poorly with an average failure rate of 99%.
 - **Fixed Metric ($M_\phi = I$)**: Using a fixed identity matrix for the contraction metric resulted in a 100% failure rate, underscoring the necessity of a *learned, state-dependent* metric for complex dynamics.

**Robustness to External Disturbances:**
- **Wind Tunnel Experiments**: The robustness of ContractionPPO was assessed by subjecting the quadruped to increasing wind disturbances (4.8, 6.4, 8.0, and 9.6 m/s) during the handstand task.
- **Untrained for Disturbances**: A key finding is that ContractionPPO was *not trained* on these high-magnitude wind perturbations.
- **Result**: Despite the severe, unseen disturbances, ContractionPPO successfully maintained the handstand within the 0.8m circle. The robot exhibited transient deviations from its initial position but consistently converged back to a steady posture within the safe region, which is consistent with the incremental stability guarantees provided by Theorem 2. This behavior was visualized through trajectory plots (Figure 6 and Figure 8).

**Summary of Findings:**
ContractionPPO consistently outperformed baseline RL methods by formally enforcing incremental stability during training. The learned contraction metric acts as an implicit regularizer, guiding the policy towards robust solutions and ensuring recovery from disturbances, model mismatches, and observation noise. The inclusion of a conservative stability margin $\epsilon_\alpha$ in the loss function explicitly addresses approximation errors between simulation and real-world deployment, contributing to the framework's effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents ContractionPPO, a framework that advances the field of robotic control by systematically integrating formal stability guarantees into deep reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Certified Reinforcement Learning:** The work represents a notable step towards "certified RL" by enabling the joint learning of a high-performance control policy and a verifiable stability certificate. This moves beyond empirical robustness, which is common in many RL applications, to provide formal assurances about system behavior. This paradigm shift could increase confidence in deploying RL-trained policies in safety-critical applications where unpredictable behavior is unacceptable.

- **Enhanced Robustness and Safety in Real-World Deployment:** By guaranteeing incremental exponential stability, ContractionPPO ensures that trajectories of the closed-loop system converge exponentially to one another. This property implies strong robustness to external perturbations, modeling errors, and observation noise. The hardware experiments, particularly the resilience to untrained, high-magnitude wind disturbances, demonstrate that the learned policies maintain stable and safe operation even under severe domain shifts. This enhanced robustness is critical for deploying legged robots in dynamic, uncertain, and unstructured real-world environments.

- **Effective Utilization of Privileged Information:** The methodology strategically leverages privileged state information (full state, dynamics) available in simulation during training to learn the contraction metric. Crucially, the deployed policy remains observation-based, thereby retaining real-world applicability without requiring inaccessible information at runtime. This approach optimizes the training process by using rich data for certification while maintaining practical deployability.

- **Scalability of Contraction Theory:** Traditional application of contraction theory to high-dimensional, nonlinear systems like quadruped robots has been challenging due to the complexity of hand-designing metrics and solving matrix inequalities. By parameterizing the contraction metric as a differentiable neural network, ContractionPPO offers a scalable, learning-based approach to apply this powerful stability analysis framework, expanding its applicability to complex robotic systems.

- **Theoretical Guarantees for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The derivation of explicit bounds on the worst-case contraction rate, expressed in terms of Lipschitz constants and system dynamics, provides a theoretical basis for ensuring that stability certificates learned in simulation remain valid during real-world deployment. This addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics concerning the "sim-to-real" gap, contributing to more predictable and reliable robot behaviors.

- **Foundation for Future Robot Autonomy:** The demonstrated ability to achieve robust, certifiably stable control policies, even under challenging conditions, lays a foundation for future advancements in robot autonomy. The paper itself suggests future work involving integration with fault-tolerant systems, indicating potential for building more resilient and self-recovering robotic platforms. This could lead to robots that are not only agile but also inherently more trustworthy in their operation.

In conclusion, ContractionPPO represents a significant contribution by bridging the gap between high-performance reinforcement learning and formal control-theoretic guarantees, paving the way for more reliable, safe, and robust autonomous systems.
