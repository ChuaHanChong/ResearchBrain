# Differentiable Physics-based System Identification for Robotic Manipulation of Elastoplastic Materials

> [!summary] Summary
> A Differentiable Physics-based System Identification (DPSI) framework was developed to infer physics parameters of volumetric elastoplastic materials for robotic manipulation. It achieves accurate simulation-to-real alignment of material deformations and generalizes to unseen manipulation motions using minimal real-world interaction data and noisy 3D point clouds.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable materials presents challenges due to their infinite degrees of freedom and complex, unpredictable dynamics, making precise physics modeling difficult.
- Conventional robotic motion planning struggles without explicit, accurate physics models, while learning-based approaches require extensive and often unavailable realistic simulation data.
- Accurate physics parameters are hard to obtain from real-world observations, often involving incomplete 3D point clouds and lacking differentiable simulators for efficient, gradient-based optimization.

## Method

- A **DPSI** (==Differentiable Physics-based System Identification==) framework was developed that iteratively minimizes a loss function between real-world observations and physics simulations.
- The framework employs the ==mean-least-square Material Point Method (MLS-MPM)== for high-fidelity simulation of elastoplastic materials, incorporating ==von Mises plasticity== and frictional contacts.
- It leverages ==DiffTaiChi==, a GPU-accelerated programming language with ==automatic differentiation==, to enable efficient gradient-based optimization of key physics parameters (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, yield stress, density, friction coefficients) from noisy 3D point cloud data.

## Results

- The optimization process demonstrated convergence within **100 gradient updates**, with identified Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, and yield stress values aligning with reported ranges for clay-like materials.
- DPSI successfully recovered parameters that enabled accurate simulation of unseen, longer, and more complex manipulation motions, maintaining visual similarity between real and simulated deformed shapes.
- A typical optimization run (**100 iterations, 1 datapoint**) completed in approximately **10 minutes** on an Nvidia GeForce RTX 4090 GPU, with potential reductions to **2-5 minutes** with a good initial guess, indicating practical computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DPSI achieved accurate sim-to-real alignment and robust generalization to unseen manipulation motions with minimal real-world interaction data, demonstrating comparable performance with single-datapoint datasets.
> - Optimization converged effectively, especially with physically plausible initial parameter guesses, and the identified parameters offered transparent, physically intuitive interpretations.
> - The Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) loss, comparing a filled simulated particle system to a reconstructed target, proved most suitable for capturing correct spatial differences and achieving better sim2real alignment.
