# Iterative Grasp Pose Refinement: A Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach for 2D Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> DQN-based grasp refinement framework treats geometrically-labeled 2D grasp candidates as a starting state (x,y,θ,w) and applies small learned adjustments to convert failed grasps into stable, successful ones. Tested on 300 Dex-Net objects with a UR5, it fixes all 38 previously ungraspable objects and transfers to a real Delta parallel robot.

## Problem

- Geometric grasp-labeling algorithms evaluate candidates from fixed heuristics, overlooking viable grasp points and leaving some objects with no successful configuration at all.
- End-to-end deep learning grasp policies produce black-box representations lacking interpretability needed for generalizable, contact-rich manipulation control.
- Prior keypoint-based grasping methods rely on manual 3D annotation or geometric techniques for the entire pipeline, limiting scalability and adaptability across objects.

## Method

- ==Deep Q-Network== agent treats a 4D grasp pose (x,y,θ,w) as state and applies 9 discrete incremental actions (pixel/angle/width adjustments plus no-op) to refine it iteratively.
- ==Rotational-slip-based scoring function== computes reward in [-1,1] from roll/pitch/yaw deviation between initial and final object pose after each simulated grasp attempt.
- Refinement-only framing narrows the RL problem to local optimization around geometrically-labeled failed candidates rather than global grasp-pose search, using ==replay buffer== and ==target network== training.

## Results

- All **38 objects** out of 286 (after excluding 14 gripper-incompatible ones) that had zero successful geometric-label grasps were converted to successful grasps, a **100%** refinement success rate.
- Each refined grasp configuration was validated by requiring **3 out of 3** successful simulated trials before being labeled successful, confirming reliability over single-trial evaluation.
- Sim-to-real test on a Delta parallel robot: an initial grasp that failed in both simulation and reality was refined to a stable configuration achieving a success score of **0.86** on the physical object.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing RL as a local refinement module around existing heuristic candidates, rather than full grasp synthesis, cuts the search space and improves sample efficiency and tractability.
> - A simple rotational-slip reward signal, derived purely from pose deltas, is sufficient to shape stable grasp adjustments without dense visual or force feedback.
> - Sim-to-real transfer of refined low-dimensional grasp parameters (x,y,θ,w) can succeed even when the original geometric grasp fails identically in both domains, suggesting refinement generalizes across the sim-real gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic object manipulation represents a fundamental challenge in robotics, with direct applications in industrial automation and domestic assistance. Current robotic systems generally demonstrate limitations in achieving human-like dexterity and adaptability, particularly in unstructured environments. Data-driven approaches, such as deep Reinforcement Learning (RL), are increasingly utilized to develop robust and adaptable grasping strategies derived from sensory data.

Within this context, effective object representations are considered crucial for enhancing learned behaviors. Keypoint representations, which enable a robot to focus on salient geometric features, facilitate precise and efficient manipulation. The integration of keypoint detection with RL is an area of ongoing research aiming to develop reliable and versatile grasping systems capable of generalization across various objects.

Data-driven grasping pipelines typically employ either supervised learning or reinforcement learning. Supervised methods often rely on extensive, pre-labeled datasets for training grasp candidates, a process that can be computationally intensive despite yielding generalization capabilities. Reinforcement learning, conversely, has demonstrated efficacy in learning complex, end-to-end manipulation skills through environmental interaction. However, challenges such as sample efficiency and sparse rewards have spurred research into areas like demonstration-guided learning and graph-based RL frameworks.

While end-to-end deep learning is a prevalent data-driven approach for robotic manipulation, the resulting "black-box" representations frequently lack the interpretability necessary for generalizable control. Keypoint representations offer a structured alternative by providing a compact understanding of object geometry, which can improve generalization in contact-rich tasks. A limitation of these methods has been their dependence on manual 3D annotation for scalability. Furthermore, contemporary automated 2D keypoint generation techniques often utilize traditional geometric methods for the entirety of the grasping process, which may overlook viable grasp points and restrict adaptability. This highlights a requirement for more robust refinement strategies to complement existing methods.

The present work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the identified need for a robust refinement strategy. It seeks to bridge the gap between traditional geometric methods, which can generate initial grasp candidates, and data-driven learning, by employing a reinforcement learning framework specifically for iterative grasp pose refinement. This approach does not aim to replace end-to-end grasp synthesis but rather to complement it as a refinement module, focusing RL on local optimization around potentially promising but initially suboptimal or failed grasp candidates.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance robotic capabilities in object manipulation by enabling robots to achieve stable and successful grasps, particularly for objects that present challenges to conventional methods.

The primary motivations driving this work stem from several observed limitations in current robotic grasping approaches:
- **Addressing Incompleteness of Geometric Grasping Algorithms:** Traditional geometric-based algorithms, even those employing 2D vision and keypoint-based representations, are not universally successful. They can fail to identify any viable grasp configurations for certain objects or produce initial candidates that are unstable or lead to failure in physical execution. The motivation is to systematically improve these initially failed or suboptimal geometric candidates.
- **Enhancing Grasp Robustness and Success Rates:** The goal is to develop a method that can iteratively adjust grasp parameters, transforming failed grasp attempts into successful ones and improving the stability of already successful grasps. This directly contributes to enhancing the overall reliability and robustness of robotic grasping systems in varied scenarios.
- **Leveraging Deep Reinforcement Learning for Refinement:** Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) is motivated as a suitable paradigm due to its capacity to learn adaptive strategies through trial-and-error interaction. By framing grasp refinement as an RL problem, the system can discover optimal adjustments to grasp parameters without explicit programming for every possible object or situation.
- **Improving Interpretability and Generalization:** While end-to-end deep learning methods often provide "black-box" solutions, the use of keypoint-based object representations combined with a structured refinement approach offers a more interpretable framework. The work aims to achieve better generalization in contact-rich tasks by focusing on key geometric features.
- **Developing a Tractable and Scalable Solution:** Rather than attempting to learn grasp synthesis from scratch (global exploration), the motivation is to focus the RL problem on local optimization around initial candidates. This approach is posited as more tractable, efficient, and scalable for robotic grasping, allowing the system to refine existing knowledge rather than generating entirely new solutions.
- **Demonstrating Sim-to-Real Transferability:** A critical motivation is to validate the practical applicability of the proposed framework by demonstrating that refined grasp poses learned in a simulated environment can successfully transfer to and be executed by a physical robot in the real world. This helps to bridge the gap between simulation-based training and real-world robotic deployment.

The central objective is to propose a novel reinforcement learning-based framework, specifically employing a Deep Q-Network (DQN) agent, for iterative robotic grasp pose refinement using 2D overhead images. This framework aims to convert initial failed grasp candidates, generated by a geometric algorithm, into successful and stable grasp configurations by iteratively adjusting their pose parameters (x, y, θ, w).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a reinforcement learning-based framework designed for iterative refinement of robotic grasp poses. This framework integrates keypoint-based object representations with a Deep Q-Network (DQN). The process begins with initial grasp candidates generated by a geometric-based algorithm from 2D overhead images in a simulated environment. The framework's core task is to iteratively adjust the parameters of these candidates, particularly focusing on converting failed grasps into successful and stable ones.

**Simulation Environment and Dataset Generation:**
The CoppeliaSim environment served as the simulation platform, chosen for its physics-based modeling capabilities. A UR5 manipulator equipped with an RG2 gripper was simulated, alongside an overhead RGB-D vision sensor for capturing workspace images. The CoppeliaSim Python API facilitated real-time data interaction.

For evaluation, a random subset of 300 diverse object meshes from the Dex-Net dataset was utilized. These meshes were imported into CoppeliaSim. An initial labeling process was conducted using a geometric-based algorithm proposed by Sabzejou et al., which evaluated potential grasp configurations for each object. Out of the 300 objects, 52 were initially deemed ungraspable by this geometric method. Of these, 14 objects were excluded from further study due to their dimensions exceeding the physical opening width of the RG2 gripper. The remaining 38 objects, for which the geometric method found no successful grasps, became the specific target for the proposed refinement algorithm. For each of these 38 objects, one randomly selected failed grasp candidate was chosen as the starting point for refinement.

**Grasp Representation, States, and Actions:**
A grasp configuration is represented by a rectangle defined by four continuous parameters: the Cartesian coordinates of its center (x, y), its in-plane rotation (θ), and its width (w). This 4D vector (x, y, θ, w) constitutes the state (s) in the reinforcement learning formulation.

Actions are defined as discrete, small adjustments to these state parameters. The action space consists of nine predefined incremental changes: increasing or decreasing x, y, θ, or w by a small fixed amount, and a 'no operation' (no-op) action. Specifically, x and y are adjusted by ±1 pixel, θ by ±2 degrees, and w by ±2 units. The no-op action allows the agent to maintain the current state if it is deemed optimal.

**Scoring and Reward Structure:**
The success and stability of a grasp attempt are quantified by a scoring system, which also serves as the immediate reward (r) for the RL agent. Grasp stability is measured by the rotational slip (tilt) of the object between its initial and final orientations after a grasp attempt. The simulator provides roll, pitch, and yaw values for both stages. The rotational slip, |∆θ|, is calculated from these values.

The final score is normalized to a range of [−1, +1]:
- For successful grasps (object lifted), the score is `1 - (|∆θ| / π)`. A perfectly stable grasp (zero rotational slip) yields a score of +1.
- For failed grasps (gripper closes without lifting, or object drops), the score is `-1 + (|∆θ| / π)`. A completely missed grasp (no contact) results in -1, while failures where the object was partially manipulated receive scores closer to 0, reflecting some interaction.
This reward structure guides the agent towards stable and precise grasps.

**DQN Implementation and Training:**
A Deep Q-Network (DQN) was implemented to approximate the optimal Q-value function, Q(s, a | ϕ). A value-based DQN was chosen for its computational efficiency in this low-dimensional state space.

The Q-network is a fully connected neural network comprising three hidden layers with 256, 128, and 64 neurons, respectively. Each hidden layer utilizes a ReLU activation function and Layer Normalization. The network's parameters (ϕ) are optimized using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.005.

An ϵ-greedy policy was employed for exploration, with the exploration rate ϵ initialized at 1.0 and decaying by a factor of 0.999 per step, reaching a minimum of 0.01. The discount factor (γ) was set to 0.9. Transitions (s, a, r, s') are stored in an experience replay buffer, from which mini-batches of size 32 are sampled to update the network parameters by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss. A target network, Q_ϕ', is periodically updated to match the main Q-network's parameters to stabilize training.

The agent was trained for 25 episodes for each object, with each episode consisting of a maximum of 120 steps. An episode could terminate early if a perfect grasp (score = 1) was achieved. During training, the best grasp configuration found and its corresponding score were tracked.

**Grasp Validation and Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
To ensure the robustness of the refined grasps, each configuration was executed three times in the simulator. A grasp was labeled successful only if all three trials consistently succeeded.

For sim-to-real validation, an object (pliers) that failed to be grasped initially in simulation was selected. The initial failed grasp pose was attempted on a physical Delta parallel robot, which also resulted in failure. This failed configuration was then processed by the proposed RL refinement algorithm in simulation. The resulting refined grasp, which was successful in simulation, was subsequently transferred to the physical Delta robot and executed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed framework involved a rigorous training and testing procedure on a subset of the Dex-Net dataset and a physical robot.

**Initial Grasping Performance:**
From the initial 300 objects in the Dex-Net dataset, 14 objects were excluded from the analysis due to their geometries exceeding the physical limitations of the RG2 gripper. Of the remaining 286 objects, the geometric-based algorithm by Sabzejou et al. identified 38 objects for which no successful grasp configurations could be found. These 38 objects were therefore deemed ungraspable by the initial geometric labeling method and formed the challenging set for the refinement framework.

**Grasp Refinement Success Rate:**
The proposed iterative refinement framework was applied to these 38 challenging objects. For all 38 objects, the framework successfully identified refined grasp configurations that enabled the robot to grasp and lift them. This resulted in a 100% success rate for converting previously ungraspable objects into graspable ones, as shown in Figure 3. The robustness of these successes was ensured by requiring that each refined grasp configuration perform successfully across three independent trials in the simulator before being categorized as a stable grasp.

**DQN Training Dynamics:**
The effectiveness of the DQN training process was demonstrated by observing the agent's average reward per episode. Figures 4 and 5 illustrate the progressive improvement in grasp configurations.
- **Initial Stages:** Early episodes showed minimal rewards, reflecting the agent's initial lack of knowledge and the unsuccessful nature of the initial grasp candidates (Figures 4a, 5a).
- **Learning Progression:** As training advanced, the average reward obtained by the agent consistently increased (Figures 4b, 5b). During this phase, grasp configurations began to show minor adjustments, gradually moving away from the initial failed attempts.
- **Refined Grasps:** The configurations from episodes that achieved the highest average rewards represented significantly optimized and stable grasps (Figures 4c, 5c). These refined grasps were noticeably different from the initial configurations, indicating the DQN algorithm's effectiveness in optimizing grasp poses and meeting the success criteria.

**Sim-to-Real Transferability:**
A critical validation step involved testing the framework's ability to transfer refined grasps from simulation to a real-world physical robot. An object (pliers) that had an initial grasp configuration labeled as a failure in simulation was selected for this test. This initial failed grasp was first executed on a physical Delta parallel manipulator, and it similarly resulted in failure. Subsequently, the proposed refinement algorithm was applied to this failed simulated grasp, which yielded a refined grasp pose that proved successful in simulation. This refined grasp pose was then transferred to the physical Delta parallel manipulator. The robot successfully executed this refined grasp, resulting in a stable lift of the real object. This experiment (Figure 6) confirmed the direct transferability and practical effectiveness of the refinement method in a physical environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, with implications for enhanced autonomy and robustness in various applications.

**Addressing Limitations of Geometric Methods:** The primary significance lies in the framework's ability to address a notable limitation of conventional geometric-based grasping algorithms. By systematically refining initial failed grasp candidates, the proposed method demonstrates a capacity to transform objects previously deemed ungraspable into manipulable ones. This extends the operational envelope of robotic systems, allowing them to handle a broader spectrum of objects, including those with challenging geometries or orientations that static geometric analyses might misinterpret.

**Enhancing System Robustness and Adaptability:** The iterative refinement approach, driven by deep reinforcement learning, enhances the overall robustness of automated grasping systems. By optimizing grasp poses for stability and success, the framework contributes to more reliable robotic operation in varied and potentially unstructured environments. This adaptability is critical for applications in industrial automation, logistics, and domestic assistance, where objects are diverse and conditions can be unpredictable.

**Improved Efficiency and Scalability of Learning:** By focusing the reinforcement learning problem on local optimization (refinement) around promising, albeit initially failed, candidates, the method offers a more tractable and efficient approach compared to learning grasp synthesis from scratch through global exploration. This targeted learning can reduce the sample complexity and training time typically associated with end-to-end deep reinforcement learning in robotics, contributing to a more scalable solution for complex manipulation tasks.

**Bridging Methodological Gaps:** The work effectively bridges traditional geometric methods, which can generate initial candidates from 2D vision data, with data-driven deep reinforcement learning for refinement. This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of both paradigms: the initial structural understanding provided by geometry and the adaptive, optimization capabilities of RL.

**Demonstrated Practical Applicability:** The successful sim-to-real transferability, validated through physical experiments on a Delta parallel robot, underscores the practical utility and readiness of the refined grasp configurations for real-world deployment. This validation is a crucial step towards implementing learning-based manipulation strategies outside of simulation.

**Advancement in Robotic Manipulation:** This research contributes to the development of more versatile and capable robotic systems that can perform robust manipulation in contact-rich tasks. The ability to autonomously refine grasp poses based on feedback is a step towards achieving greater dexterity and autonomy in robotic systems. Future work, focusing on improved generalization to unseen data, would further enhance the adaptability of these systems to diverse and dynamic real-world scenarios.
