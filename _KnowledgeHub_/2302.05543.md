# Adding Conditional Control to Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ControlNet enables large, pretrained text-to-image diffusion models to accept fine-grained spatial and structural control through various image-based conditions, such as edge maps or human poses. The Stanford University team achieved this by adding a trainable copy of the model's U-Net blocks that learns to incorporate conditions, while the original model's parameters remain frozen, preserving its capabilities and allowing efficient training on smaller datasets.

## Problem

- Large text-to-image diffusion models lacked fine-grained spatial and structural control, making it challenging for users to dictate precise layouts, object poses, or specific geometric forms purely through textual descriptions.
- Existing methods often required training new models from scratch for each specific condition or direct finetuning, which could lead to catastrophic forgetting or overfitting of the large pretrained models.
- Providing image-based conditions (e.g., edge maps, human pose) to guide generation was not robustly supported by general text-to-image models, limiting creative and practical applications.

## Method

- **ControlNet** introduces a neural network architecture that creates a trainable copy of the large ==diffusion model=='s encoder and middle blocks, while crucially keeping the original model's parameters locked (frozen).
- It connects the trainable copy to the frozen backbone using 'zero convolutions,' which are 1x1 convolution layers initialized to zero, ensuring no initial impact on the pretrained model's output.
- During training, the trainable copy learns to integrate external image-based conditions (e.g., edge maps, pose skeletons) by adding its outputs to the corresponding skip-connections and the middle block of the original ==U-Net==.

## Results

- ControlNet effectively controls Stable Diffusion with diverse input conditions, including Canny edges, human keypoints, segmentation maps, and depth maps, demonstrating robustness even with insufficient or absent text prompts.
- In user studies, ControlNet consistently ranked highest in both 'result quality' and 'condition fidelity' for sketch-to-image generation compared to baseline methods.
- A ControlNet model trained on 200k samples with a single consumer GPU achieved results almost indistinguishable from industrial models trained on 12M images using large compute clusters, highlighting its exceptional data and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Zero convolutions, initialized to zero, prevent initial training noise from disrupting the pretrained backbone, allowing the model to learn conditional control progressively without harming pre-existing image generation capabilities.
> - Locking the parameters of the original large diffusion model and only training a copy effectively preserves its vast knowledge base, mitigating catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new conditions.
> - The architecture efficiently learns diverse spatial controls from relatively small datasets by leveraging the powerful, frozen backbone of the large pretrained model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of ControlNet emerges from a critical juncture in the evolution of generative AI, particularly in the domain of text-to-image synthesis. Prior to this work, large diffusion models like Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, and Midjourney had revolutionized image generation by producing visually stunning and diverse images from simple text prompts. However, a significant limitation persisted: the lack of fine-grained spatial and structural control. Users found it challenging to dictate precise layouts, object poses, or specific geometric forms purely through textual descriptions, often requiring extensive trial-and-error prompt engineering.

The broader research landscape was actively seeking solutions to this control problem. Existing approaches included:
- **Prompt Engineering & Manipulation:** Techniques like modifying attention layers or using CLIP guidance offered some control, but were often complex or limited in scope.
- **Image-to-Image Translation:** Conditional GANs and early diffusion models could translate one image type (e.g., edge map) to another (e.g., photo), but typically required training from scratch for each specific condition and lacked the text-to-image capabilities of large pretrained models.
- **Finetuning & Personalization:** Methods like Textual Inversion and DreamBooth enabled personalization of content (e.g., generating images of a specific object or style) by finetuning small parts of the model or learning new embeddings. However, direct spatial control was not their primary focus, and direct finetuning of large models could lead to catastrophic forgetting or overfitting with limited data.
- **Adapter Methods:** Concurrent works, such as T2I-Adapter, also explored adding new modules (adapters) to pretrained diffusion models for conditional control.

ControlNet distinguishes itself by offering a **general, efficient, and robust architecture** for adding *any type of spatial conditioning* (edges, depth, pose, segmentation, etc.) to *any large, pretrained text-to-image diffusion model* (demonstrated with Stable Diffusion). It directly addresses the challenges of:
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** By locking the parameters of the original large model and only training a copy, it preserves the vast knowledge encoded in the billions of images used for pretraining.
- **Limited Data for Specific Conditions:** It efficiently learns from relatively small datasets (e.g., 50k images for pose) by leveraging the powerful backbone of the frozen large model.
- **Generality and Composability:** Unlike task-specific models, ControlNet can handle a wide array of conditions and even combine multiple conditions simultaneously.

Its introduction marked a significant paradigm shift, democratizing high-quality, fine-grained control over generative AI. It moved beyond merely describing "what" to generate, to precisely controlling "how" it should look spatially, effectively merging the power of text-to-image models with the precision of image-to-image translation, without retraining the entire large model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable finer-grained spatial control over large, pretrained text-to-image diffusion models**, overcoming the limitations inherent in text-only prompting. The authors aim to allow users to provide additional image-based conditions (e.g., edge maps, human pose skeletons, depth maps, segmentation maps) to directly specify desired image compositions.

The motivations behind ControlNet are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Control Gap:** Text prompts, while powerful, are inherently limited in their ability to convey precise spatial information, complex layouts, or specific geometric forms. This often leads to a tedious trial-and-error process for users trying to match their mental imagery. The objective is to provide a more intuitive and direct way to guide image generation.
- **Leveraging Pretrained Model Strength:** Large diffusion models are "production-ready" and have learned deep, robust encoding layers from billions of images. The authors aim to reuse this immense knowledge as a strong backbone for learning diverse conditional controls, rather than training new models from scratch or risking the degradation of existing models through direct finetuning.
- **Addressing Finetuning Challenges:** Direct finetuning of large models with smaller, task-specific datasets often leads to catastrophic forgetting (where the model loses its general capabilities) or overfitting (where it performs well only on the specific training data). A core motivation is to design an architecture that is robust to these issues, allowing efficient training with limited data.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Training or finetuning massive models is computationally expensive. The design of ControlNet aims to be efficient in terms of GPU memory and training time, making it accessible for researchers and practitioners without industrial-scale computational resources.
- **Versatility and Composability:** The goal is to support a wide range of conditioning inputs and to allow for the combination of multiple conditions, increasing the model's flexibility and applicability across various creative and practical tasks.

In essence, the authors seek to augment the creative freedom offered by text-to-image models with precise, spatially-aware guidance, making these powerful tools more usable and predictable for a wider range of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ControlNet introduces a novel neural network architecture designed to "piggyback" on large, pretrained text-to-image diffusion models, specifically demonstrated with Stable Diffusion.

#### 3.1. The ControlNet Architecture

The core idea revolves around creating a trainable copy of the large model's encoder blocks and middle block, while crucially **locking (freezing) the parameters of the original pretrained model**.

- **Locked Backbone:** The original Stable Diffusion model (a U-Net structure with encoder, middle block, and decoder) remains untouched. Its parameters are frozen, ensuring that its extensive knowledge of image generation is preserved and not susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. This also means no gradient computation is needed for the locked parameters during finetuning, saving significant computational resources.
- **Trainable Copy:** For each neural network block in the original model that needs to be conditioned, a "trainable copy" is created. This copy has the same architecture as the corresponding original block but with its own set of parameters that are initialized and trained. This trainable copy receives the input feature map `x` as well as an external conditioning vector `c`.
- **Zero Convolutions:** This is the critical innovation for connecting the trainable copy to the locked model. ControlNet uses "zero convolutions," which are 1x1 convolution layers with both weight and bias parameters initialized to zero.
 - One zero convolution `Z(c; Θ_z1)` takes the external conditioning vector `c` and feeds its output into the trainable copy.
 - Another zero convolution `Z(F(x+Z(c; Θ_z1); Θ_c); Θ_z2)` takes the output of the trainable copy and adds it to the output of the *original* locked block `F(x; Θ)`.
 - **The key insight of zero convolutions:** At the very beginning of training, since their weights are zero, they effectively output zero. This means the trainable copy initially has no influence on the locked model's hidden states, and the overall output `y_c` equals the original model's output `y`. This prevents "harmful noise" or random initial gradients from damaging the pretrained backbone, allowing the trainable copy to progressively learn the conditional control from scratch without disrupting the pre-existing high-quality image generation capability.

#### 3.2. Application to Stable Diffusion

Stable Diffusion's U-Net architecture includes 12 encoding blocks and 1 middle block. ControlNet creates a trainable copy for each of these 13 blocks. The outputs of these ControlNet blocks are then added to the corresponding skip-connections and the middle block of the original Stable Diffusion U-Net.

- **Conditioning Input Encoding:** Input conditioning images (e.g., 512x512 Canny edge maps) are first converted into a 64x64 feature space vector (`c_f`) to match the latent space of Stable Diffusion. This is done via a small, jointly-trained network `E(·)` consisting of four convolution layers.

#### 3.3. Training Process

ControlNet is trained end-to-end using the same learning objective as the original diffusion model (predicting noise).

- **Random Prompt Replacement:** During training, 50% of text prompts are randomly replaced with empty strings. This encourages ControlNet to learn to infer semantics directly from the conditioning images, making it robust even when prompts are absent or insufficient.
- **"Sudden Convergence" Phenomenon:** Due to the zero convolutions, the model initially produces high-quality images (as it effectively behaves like the original Stable Diffusion). The authors observe that the model doesn't gradually improve its control but rather "abruptly succeeds" in following the input conditioning image, typically within 10,000 optimization steps. This indicates the stability and efficiency of the training process.

#### 3.4. Inference Considerations

Several techniques are employed during inference to enhance control and flexibility:

- **Classifier-Free Guidance Resolution Weighting (CFG-RW):** Stable Diffusion uses Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) for generating high-quality images. ControlNet can be added to both conditional (`ε_c`) and unconditional (`ε_uc`) paths of CFG. To prevent excessively strong guidance (which can lead to over-adherence to the condition at the expense of diversity) or loss of guidance, ControlNet introduces a weighting `w_i = 64/h_i` (where `h_i` is the resolution of the i-th block) to each connection between ControlNet and Stable Diffusion. This dynamically adjusts the influence of the condition based on the resolution, ensuring a balanced output.
- **Composing Multiple ControlNets:** To use multiple conditioning images simultaneously (e.g., Canny edges and human pose), the outputs of the corresponding ControlNets are simply added together and then fed into the Stable Diffusion model. No complex weighting or interpolation is required, simplifying multi-modal control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness, robustness, and versatility of ControlNet across various conditioning types and evaluation metrics.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Diverse Conditions:** ControlNet effectively controls Stable Diffusion using a wide range of input conditions, including Canny edges, Hough lines, user scribbles, human keypoints (OpenPose), segmentation maps (ADE20K), shape normals, and depth maps (Figure 1 & 7).
- **Prompt Flexibility:** The method works robustly with or without text prompts, and even with insufficient or conflicting prompts, demonstrating its ability to robustly interpret content semantics directly from the conditioning image.
- **Multi-Condition Composition:** ControlNet successfully composes multiple conditions (e.g., pose and depth) simultaneously to guide image generation (Figure 6).

#### 4.2. Ablative Studies

- **Importance of Zero Convolutions:** Replacing zero convolutions with standard Gaussian-initialized convolution layers significantly degrades performance, causing the "pretrained backbone of the trainable copy to be destroyed during finetuning" (Figure 8b). This validates the critical role of zero convolutions in protecting the pretrained model and ensuring stable training.
- **Strength of Trainable Copy:** A lightweight ControlNet-lite (replacing the full trainable copy with a single convolution layer) fails in scenarios with no or insufficient prompts (Figure 8c), indicating that the trainable copy needs the full depth and robustness derived from the pretrained model's architecture to effectively interpret complex conditioning images.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Evaluation

- **User Study:** In a user study comparing ControlNet against baselines (PITI, Sketch-Guided Diffusion) for sketch-to-image generation, ControlNet consistently ranked highest in both "result quality" and "condition fidelity" (Table 1), indicating strong subjective preference.
- **Comparison to Industrial Models:** A ControlNet for SD V2 trained on only 200k samples with a single RTX 3090Ti for 5 days yielded results almost "indistinguishable" from Stability AI's SDv2-D2I model, which was trained on 12M images using a large A100 cluster. This highlights ControlNet's exceptional data efficiency and accessibility.
- **Condition Reconstruction and FID:**
 - For semantic segmentation conditioning (ADE20K), ControlNet achieved a higher Intersection over Union (IoU) for reconstructed labels (0.35±0.14) compared to baselines like VQGAN, LDM, and PITI (Table 2), demonstrating superior fidelity to the input condition.
 - ControlNet also achieved the lowest Frechet Inception Distance (FID) (15.27) and competitive CLIP text-image and aesthetic scores compared to other segmentation-conditioned methods and even the base Stable Diffusion model without conditions (Table 3). This indicates high image quality and better alignment with textual prompts when conditions are applied.

#### 4.4. Additional Capabilities

- **Robustness to Dataset Size:** ControlNet training is robust even with small datasets (e.g., 1k images for a specific condition) and scales well with larger datasets (Figure 10).
- **Content Interpretation:** ControlNet can interpret semantics from ambiguous input conditioning images, attempting to generate plausible content even without specific text prompts (Figure 11).
- **Transferability to Community Models:** Pretrained ControlNets can be directly applied to various community-finetuned Stable Diffusion models (e.g., Comic Diffusion, Protogen 3.4) without additional training, extending their utility (Figure 12).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ControlNet represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly in making large text-to-image diffusion models more controllable and useful for a wider range of applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Democratization of Fine-Grained Control:** ControlNet moves beyond abstract text-based prompting, enabling users to exert precise spatial and structural control over image generation through intuitive visual inputs like sketches, pose skeletons, or depth maps. This unlocks new levels of creative expression and practical utility for artists, designers, and researchers alike.
- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** The "locked backbone" and "zero convolution" architecture elegantly solves the pervasive problem of catastrophic forgetting when finetuning large models. By preserving the vast knowledge of the pretrained model while incrementally learning new capabilities, ControlNet ensures that specialized control does not come at the cost of general image quality or diversity.
- **Exceptional Data Efficiency:** The ability of ControlNet to achieve competitive results with industrial-scale models using significantly less data (e.g., 200k images vs. 12M for depth-to-image) and less computational resources (a single consumer GPU vs. large clusters) is groundbreaking. This makes advanced conditional image generation accessible to a broader range of researchers and individual creators.
- **Versatility and Generalizability:** ControlNet is not limited to a single type of conditioning input. Its architecture is general enough to accommodate a wide variety of spatial controls, from low-level features like edges to high-level semantic maps. This versatility makes it a foundational tool for numerous image manipulation and generation tasks.
- **Composability of Conditions:** The simple additive mechanism for combining multiple ControlNets allows for highly complex and nuanced control over image generation, enabling multi-modal inputs that blend different types of structural guidance.
- **Catalyst for Downstream Applications:** ControlNet’s ability to faithfully translate structural inputs into diverse image outputs paves the way for numerous applications:
 - **Creative Arts:** Artists can now sketch out compositions, define character poses, or specify depth, and then let the diffusion model fill in the details.
 - **Design and Prototyping:** Designers can quickly generate variations of product designs or architectural layouts from simple structural inputs.
 - **Computer Graphics and VFX:** Automating the creation of assets based on specific structural requirements.
 - **Research:** Providing a robust framework for exploring new forms of conditional image generation and inter-modal translation.
- **Open-Source Impact:** The release of ControlNet has profoundly impacted the open-source community around Stable Diffusion, leading to an explosion of new tools, applications, and creative workflows that were previously impossible. Its design principles are likely to influence future architectures for controlling large foundation models in other modalities as well.

In summary, ControlNet represents a critical step towards truly controllable generative AI, bridging the gap between high-quality general generation and precise user-defined structure. Its efficiency and robust design have made it a widely adopted and highly influential contribution to the field.
