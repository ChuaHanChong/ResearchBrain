# Adding Conditional Control to Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ControlNet enables large, pretrained text-to-image diffusion models to accept fine-grained spatial and structural control through various image-based conditions, such as edge maps or human poses. The Stanford University team achieved this by adding a trainable copy of the model's U-Net blocks that learns to incorporate conditions, while the original model's parameters remain frozen, preserving its capabilities and allowing efficient training on smaller datasets.

## Problem

- Large text-to-image diffusion models lacked fine-grained spatial and structural control, making it challenging for users to dictate precise layouts, object poses, or specific geometric forms purely through textual descriptions.
- Existing methods often required training new models from scratch for each specific condition or direct finetuning, which could lead to catastrophic forgetting or overfitting of the large pretrained models.
- Providing image-based conditions (e.g., edge maps, human pose) to guide generation was not robustly supported by general text-to-image models, limiting creative and practical applications.

## Method

- **ControlNet** introduces a neural network architecture that creates a trainable copy of the large ==diffusion model=='s encoder and middle blocks, while crucially keeping the original model's parameters locked (frozen).
- It connects the trainable copy to the frozen backbone using 'zero convolutions,' which are 1x1 convolution layers initialized to zero, ensuring no initial impact on the pretrained model's output.
- During training, the trainable copy learns to integrate external image-based conditions (e.g., edge maps, pose skeletons) by adding its outputs to the corresponding skip-connections and the middle block of the original ==U-Net==.

## Results

- ControlNet effectively controls Stable Diffusion with diverse input conditions, including Canny edges, human keypoints, segmentation maps, and depth maps, demonstrating robustness even with insufficient or absent text prompts.
- In user studies, ControlNet consistently ranked highest in both 'result quality' and 'condition fidelity' for sketch-to-image generation compared to baseline methods.
- A ControlNet model trained on 200k samples with a single consumer GPU achieved results almost indistinguishable from industrial models trained on 12M images using large compute clusters, highlighting its exceptional data and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Zero convolutions, initialized to zero, prevent initial training noise from disrupting the pretrained backbone, allowing the model to learn conditional control progressively without harming pre-existing image generation capabilities.
> - Locking the parameters of the original large diffusion model and only training a copy effectively preserves its vast knowledge base, mitigating catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new conditions.
> - The architecture efficiently learns diverse spatial controls from relatively small datasets by leveraging the powerful, frozen backbone of the large pretrained model.
