# Realistic PointGoal Navigation via Auxiliary Losses and Information Bottleneck

> [!summary] Summary
> A new training paradigm, incorporating an information bottleneck and auxiliary losses, allows agents to perform PointGoal navigation in noisy, uninstrumented environments. This approach achieved a 19.86% SPL in realistic settings and significantly outperformed baselines while maintaining computational efficiency at inference.

## Problem

- Idealized assumptions (ground-truth localization, noiseless sensors, deterministic actuators) in simulated PointGoal navigation lead to poor transfer to real-world robotics.
- Agents fail to self-localize effectively without ground-truth GPS+Compass, a critical issue for robust navigation in novel environments.
- Learning self-localization and navigation simultaneously in end-to-end reinforcement learning creates an interdependent, detrimental learning cycle.

## Method

- Proposed an end-to-end architecture for **PointGoal** navigation, integrating an information bottleneck and specific auxiliary losses into a PPO training framework.
- Utilizes an information bottleneck to provide constrained access to ground-truth localization during training, guiding the agent without causing over-reliance at test time.
- Employs self-tracking and ego-motion estimation auxiliary losses to explicitly teach the agent robust self-localization from noisy visual inputs.

## Results

- Achieved **19.86%** SPL and **26.66%** Success in the challenging realistic setting, surpassing DD-PPO baseline (**2.83%** SPL) and prior work (Datta et al. [10]: **4.45%** SPL).
- Demonstrated a substantial +**57.92** SPL improvement over an end-to-end learning baseline in the semi-idealized setting.
- Showcased computational efficiency with an inference time of 2 hours 42 minutes, significantly faster than some other state-of-the-art methods with higher computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling self-localization and navigation learning via an information bottleneck and auxiliary losses is crucial for robust performance in realistic embodied navigation.
> - Providing constrained "privileged information" during training improves sample efficiency and final performance by offering a learning reference, even when unavailable during evaluation.
> - Explicit auxiliary losses for self-tracking and ego-motion estimation are fundamental for enabling precise self-localization and accurate goal-reaching.
