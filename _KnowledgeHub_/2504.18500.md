# Boxi: Design Decisions in the Context of Algorithmic Performance for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> ETH Zurich researchers present Boxi, an open-source multi-modal sensor payload for mobile robots that combines LiDAR, cameras, IMUs and GNSS, along with detailed empirical analysis of how design decisions impact algorithmic performance across seven diverse environments while providing comprehensive calibration and synchronization guidelines.

## Problem

- Existing autonomous driving sensor suites lack detailed information on calibration, synchronization and design rationale
- Limited understanding of how sensor payload design choices affect state estimation algorithm performance
- Need for well-documented, versatile sensor configurations that enable robust autonomous navigation

## Method

- Development of **Boxi**, a ==multi-modal sensor payload== with careful consideration of ==mechanical design, thermal management and timing==
- Systematic evaluation of ==different sensor modalities and configurations== across varied environments
- Creation of an open-source "cookbook" documenting design considerations and best practices

## Results

- Successfully enabled high-quality elevation mapping and traversability analysis for autonomous navigation
- Demonstrated that high-end IMUs provide significant benefits in dead-reckoning scenarios, backed by detailed performance comparisons across sensor types and environmental conditions
- Released open-source hardware/software designs and the GrandTour dataset for benchmarking

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LiDAR-inertial odometry consistently outperforms visual-inertial methods, with long-range LiDARs showing advantages in feature-limited environments
> - Global shutter cameras deliver superior visual odometry compared to rolling shutter alternatives
> - Time sync errors under 5ms have minimal impact while extrinsic calibration errors significantly affect performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and increasingly relevant challenge in robotics: the design and integration of multimodal sensor suites for robust autonomous navigation in complex environments. The research landscape surrounding this topic can be categorized as follows:

- **Autonomous Driving Payloads:** The authors acknowledge the significant work done in the autonomous driving sector, citing datasets like KITTI, NuScenes, Waymo, and Argoverse. However, they point out that while these datasets feature similar sensor configurations, detailed information on calibration, time synchronization, and sensor payload design is often missing or proprietary. Their work aims to address this gap by providing a more open and detailed analysis.
- **Compact Vision Payloads:** The paper highlights the development of smaller, more mobile payloads based on multi-camera setups, citing works like DLR 7, VersaVIS, and PIRVS. These payloads often focus on specific aspects like FPGA-based time synchronization or onboard SLAM processing. The authors argue that while these works emphasize the importance of calibration and time synchronization, they lack a systematic experimental evaluation of their impact on state estimation performance.
- **Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion:** The paper builds upon existing research that emphasizes the importance of time synchronization and extrinsic calibration between different sensor modalities for SLAM and state estimation. It extends this work by providing a comprehensive "cookbook" of design considerations and lessons learned, enabling the development of future perception systems beyond the specific Boxi platform.
- **State Estimation and Mapping Algorithms:** The paper leverages existing state-of-the-art algorithms for LiDAR, camera, proprioception, and GNSS-based state estimation. However, its primary focus is not on developing new algorithms but on evaluating the performance of existing algorithms under different sensor configurations, calibration errors, and environmental conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a state-of-the-art mobile robot sensor suite (Boxi):** The authors aimed to create a versatile, multi-modal sensor payload that could be used for a wide range of robotic applications, particularly for legged robots operating in challenging environments.
- **Foster a better understanding between payload design decisions and algorithmic performance:** The core motivation is to provide insights into how various design choices (sensor selection, time synchronization, calibration accuracy, etc.) affect the performance of downstream state estimation and mapping algorithms.
- **Allow the collection of large-scale multi-sensor datasets with high-quality calibration and time synchronization:** By creating a well-calibrated and synchronized sensor suite, the authors aim to facilitate the collection of high-quality datasets that can be used to benchmark and develop new algorithms.
- **Enabling autonomous mobile navigation:** The aim is to build a sensor payload that can enable robust and reliable autonomous navigation in complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research methodology can be summarized as follows:

- **Hardware Design and Integration:** The authors designed and built the Boxi sensor payload, carefully selecting and integrating a variety of sensors, including LiDARs, RGB cameras, RGB-D cameras, IMUs, and a GNSS system. The design process emphasized mechanical stiffness, thermal management, and accurate time synchronization.
- **Data Collection:** The authors collected data from seven real-world environments using the ANYmal legged robot equipped with Boxi. These environments were diverse in lighting, structure, and terrain, providing a challenging testbed for evaluating sensor performance.
- **Algorithmic Evaluation:** The authors evaluated the performance of different state estimation algorithms (OKVIS2, DLIO, TSIF, Inertial Explorer) using the collected data. They compared the performance of different sensor modalities (LiDAR, camera, IMU, GNSS) and conducted ablation studies to assess the impact of time synchronization errors and extrinsic calibration errors.
- **Cookbook Development:** Based on their design experience and experimental results, the authors compiled a comprehensive "cookbook" of design considerations and best practices for building sensor payloads for robotic platforms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The key findings and results of the research are:

- **Sensor Modality Comparison:** The performance of different sensor modalities varied significantly across the tested environments. LiDAR-inertial odometry (DLIO) consistently outperformed visual-inertial odometry (OKVIS2) and kinematic-based state estimation in terms of absolute trajectory error (ATE). GNSS provided highly accurate performance in environments with sufficient satellite coverage.
- **LiDAR Comparison:** The Hesai long-range LiDAR outperformed the Livox short-range LiDAR, especially in environments with limited geometric features.
- **Camera Comparison:** Global shutter cameras (CoreResearch) consistently delivered the best VIO accuracy compared to rolling shutter cameras (HDR, ZED2i). Rolling shutter cameras required careful compensation for the rolling shutter effect to achieve acceptable performance.
- **IMU Comparison:** While high-end IMUs (HG4930) did not directly lead to superior state estimation in DLIO, they significantly improved performance in dead-reckoning scenarios, mitigating drift more effectively than consumer-grade IMUs.
- **Ablation Studies:** Time synchronization errors below 5 ms had a negligible impact on DLIO performance. Extrinsic calibration errors, especially rotational offsets, had a significant impact on DLIO performance.
- **Mapping and Traversability Analysis:** The Boxi sensor payload enabled the generation of high-quality elevation maps and traversability analysis, demonstrating its potential for autonomous navigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has significant implications for the robotics community:

- **Practical Guidance for Sensor Payload Design:** The "cookbook" provides valuable guidance for researchers and engineers designing sensor payloads for mobile robots. It highlights the key design considerations, trade-offs, and best practices based on real-world experiments.
- **Data-Driven Insights into Algorithmic Performance:** The paper provides data-driven insights into how sensor selection, calibration accuracy, and time synchronization affect the performance of state estimation algorithms. This information can help researchers and engineers optimize their sensor configurations and algorithms for specific applications.
- **Open-Source Hardware and Software Design:** By making the hardware and software design of Boxi open-source, the authors are facilitating the development of new robotic platforms and algorithms.
- **High-Quality Dataset for Benchmarking:** The GrandTour dataset collected using Boxi provides a valuable resource for benchmarking and developing new state estimation and mapping algorithms.
- **Advancing Robust Autonomy:** By providing a versatile and well-characterized sensor payload, this research contributes to the development of more robust and reliable autonomous robots that can operate in complex and unstructured environments.

In conclusion, the paper "Boxi: Design Decisions in the Context of Algorithmic Performance for Robotics" presents a comprehensive study on the design and evaluation of a multi-modal sensor payload for mobile robots. The research provides valuable insights into the trade-offs involved in sensor selection, calibration, and time synchronization, and it offers practical guidance for researchers and engineers working on autonomous robotics systems. The open-source nature of the hardware and software design, along with the availability of the GrandTour dataset, will likely have a significant impact on the robotics community, fostering the development of more robust and reliable autonomous robots.
