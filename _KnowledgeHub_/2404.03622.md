# Mind's Eye of LLMs: Visualization-of-Thought Elicits Spatial Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from Microsoft Research introduces Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) prompting, a method that enables large language models to generate and manipulate internal text-based mental images for spatial reasoning tasks. This approach allows LLMs to outperform multimodal models on certain visual challenges, demonstrating an emergent 'mind's eye' capability.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often demonstrate underdeveloped spatial reasoning abilities, frequently relying on superficial linguistic cues rather than a deep, grounded understanding of space.
- Despite advancements in other reasoning domains, LLMs lack a genuine spatial awareness or a mechanism analogous to human mental imagery for simulating spatial interactions.
- Previous approaches in NLP for spatial reasoning improved performance on benchmarks but did not necessarily equip LLMs with a grounded understanding of spatial relationships.

## Method

- The researchers propose ==Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) prompting==, which explicitly prompts LLMs to 'Visualize the state after each reasoning step.'
- This strategy forces LLMs to generate an interleaved sequence of textual reasoning steps and ==text-based 2D grid visualizations==, simulating a =='visuospatial sketchpad.'==
- The approach was evaluated on three multi-hop spatial reasoning tasks: ==Natural Language Navigation, Visual Navigation==, and Visual Tiling.

## Results

- GPT-4 with Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) prompting consistently outperformed all other settings, including GPT-4 CoT, GPT-4 w/o Viz, and GPT-4 Vision, across multi-hop spatial reasoning tasks.
- VoT led to a **23.5%** accuracy improvement for GPT-4 in Natural Language Navigation (**59.00%** vs **35.50%** for CoT) and allowed GPT-4 to outperform GPT-4 Vision by over **14%** (**63.94%** vs **49.62%**) in Visual Navigation's next step prediction.
- The method significantly increased LLMs' 'visual state tracking' behavior (e.g., **96.2%** complete tracking rate for Visual Navigation-Route Planning), and when internal state visualizations were accurate, correct decisions were made in **65-77%** of cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models (LLMs), primarily trained on text, can exhibit an emergent capability akin to the human 'mind's eye' by generating and manipulating internal text-based spatial representations.
> - For certain abstract spatial reasoning tasks, internal spatial representation and manipulation via Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) in LLMs can be more effective than direct pixel-level perception in multimodal large language models (MLLMs).
> - The emergent visual state tracking ability in LLMs may stem from pre-training exposure to structured data such as tabular formats, city grids, or ASCII art in code comments.
