# Mind's Eye of LLMs: Visualization-of-Thought Elicits Spatial Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from Microsoft Research introduces Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) prompting, a method that enables large language models to generate and manipulate internal text-based mental images for spatial reasoning tasks. This approach allows LLMs to outperform multimodal models on certain visual challenges, demonstrating an emergent 'mind's eye' capability.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often demonstrate underdeveloped spatial reasoning abilities, frequently relying on superficial linguistic cues rather than a deep, grounded understanding of space.
- Despite advancements in other reasoning domains, LLMs lack a genuine spatial awareness or a mechanism analogous to human mental imagery for simulating spatial interactions.
- Previous approaches in NLP for spatial reasoning improved performance on benchmarks but did not necessarily equip LLMs with a grounded understanding of spatial relationships.

## Method

- The researchers propose ==Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) prompting==, which explicitly prompts LLMs to 'Visualize the state after each reasoning step.'
- This strategy forces LLMs to generate an interleaved sequence of textual reasoning steps and ==text-based 2D grid visualizations==, simulating a =='visuospatial sketchpad.'==
- The approach was evaluated on three multi-hop spatial reasoning tasks: ==Natural Language Navigation, Visual Navigation==, and Visual Tiling.

## Results

- GPT-4 with Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) prompting consistently outperformed all other settings, including GPT-4 CoT, GPT-4 w/o Viz, and GPT-4 Vision, across multi-hop spatial reasoning tasks.
- VoT led to a **23.5%** accuracy improvement for GPT-4 in Natural Language Navigation (**59.00%** vs **35.50%** for CoT) and allowed GPT-4 to outperform GPT-4 Vision by over **14%** (**63.94%** vs **49.62%**) in Visual Navigation's next step prediction.
- The method significantly increased LLMs' 'visual state tracking' behavior (e.g., **96.2%** complete tracking rate for Visual Navigation-Route Planning), and when internal state visualizations were accurate, correct decisions were made in **65-77%** of cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models (LLMs), primarily trained on text, can exhibit an emergent capability akin to the human 'mind's eye' by generating and manipulating internal text-based spatial representations.
> - For certain abstract spatial reasoning tasks, internal spatial representation and manipulation via Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) in LLMs can be more effective than direct pixel-level perception in multimodal large language models (MLLMs).
> - The emergent visual state tracking ability in LLMs may stem from pre-training exposure to structured data such as tabular formats, city grids, or ASCII art in code comments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen explosive growth and remarkable achievements in recent years, demonstrating impressive capabilities in language comprehension, generation, and various reasoning tasks such as mathematical, common sense, symbolic, and logical reasoning. However, despite these advancements, the paper identifies a significant gap: the **relatively underexplored ability of LLMs in spatial reasoning**. Human cognition heavily relies on spatial reasoning, allowing us to navigate, manipulate objects, and understand the physical world. While LLMs can process spatial information linguistically, their "understanding" of space has largely been inferential from text, often lacking a genuine spatial awareness or a mechanism analogous to human mental imagery.

Previous research in spatial reasoning within NLP primarily focused on extracting spatial semantics from text, converting spatial terms to logical forms, or pre-training models on synthetic spatial datasets. While these methods improved performance on benchmarks, they didn't necessarily equip LLMs with a genuine, grounded understanding of spatial relationships or the ability to simulate spatial interactions. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which can process both text and images, have emerged as a promising direction for bridging this gap, directly leveraging visual input.

This work critically intervenes in this landscape by proposing that LLMs, even *without* explicit pixel-level visual input, can develop a form of "Mind's Eye" – an internal capability to create and manipulate mental images. This concept is directly inspired by human cognitive science, specifically the idea of a "visuospatial sketchpad" and mental imagery in spatial tasks like navigation or mental rotation. By enabling LLMs to "visualize" their intermediate reasoning steps in a text-based grid format (like ASCII art), the paper aims to provide a *grounded* reasoning pathway that transcends purely linguistic inference. This approach directly challenges the assumption that visual input from MLLMs is always superior for spatial tasks, demonstrating that LLMs can internally generate and utilize spatial representations for enhanced reasoning. It positions itself as a bridge between cognitive science and LLM development, exploring how internal "world models" might be formed and utilized by language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The **key objective** of this research is to **enhance the spatial reasoning abilities of Large Language Models by enabling them to create and manipulate internal "mental images" through a novel prompting strategy called Visualization-of-Thought (VoT)**.

The **motivation** stems from several critical observations and inspirations:

- **LLMs' Limitations in Spatial Reasoning:** Despite their general prowess in many reasoning domains, LLMs' abilities in spatial reasoning remain underdeveloped and often rely on superficial linguistic cues rather than a deep, grounded understanding of space.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition (Mind's Eye):** Humans possess a remarkable cognitive capacity, the "Mind's Eye," which allows them to form mental images, simulate actions, and visualize unseen objects or scenarios. This ability is crucial for effective spatial reasoning (e.g., navigating a new environment, solving a puzzle mentally). The authors hypothesize that LLMs might possess an analogous, albeit emergent, capability.
- **Need for Grounded Reasoning:** Current LLM spatial reasoning often lacks grounding in a concrete spatial context. By visualizing intermediate states, VoT aims to ground the LLM's reasoning process in a "visuospatial sketchpad," allowing it to interpret its actions and their consequences within a simulated world.
- **Exploring Emergent Capabilities:** The research seeks to explore and elicit inherent or emergent spatial visualization capabilities within pre-trained LLMs, particularly those potentially developed through exposure to structured data like tabular formats, city grids, or even ASCII art in code comments.
- **Beyond Linguistic Semantics:** The goal is to move beyond mere linguistic understanding of spatial terms towards a more genuine "spatial awareness" that involves understanding relationships, directions, distances, and geometric shapes, essential for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) prompting**, a novel paradigm designed to elicit spatial reasoning in LLMs by having them visualize their reasoning traces.

- **VoT Prompting:** Instead of merely asking LLMs to "think step by step" (Chain-of-Thought, CoT), VoT explicitly prompts the model with "Visualize the state after each reasoning step." This forces the LLM to generate an interleaved sequence of textual reasoning steps (`z_i`) and textual visualizations (`v_i`), where subsequent reasoning steps are conditioned on the previously generated text and visualization. This process simulates a "visuospatial sketchpad," allowing LLMs to track changes in a "mental image" and inform subsequent decisions. The visualizations are rendered as text-based 2D grids using special characters/emojis.

- **Task Selection and Design:** To evaluate VoT's effectiveness, three multi-hop spatial reasoning tasks were chosen, requiring understanding of space, direction, and geometric shapes:
 - **Natural Language Navigation:** An existing task where LLMs navigate a 2D grid world described purely in natural language instructions, identifying objects at a final location.
 - **Visual Navigation (New Task):** LLMs are presented with a synthetic 2D grid world (using special characters for roads, obstacles, start/end points) and challenged to navigate from a start to a destination. This task has two sub-tasks:
 - **Route Planning:** Generating a sequence of navigation instructions to reach the destination.
 - **Next Step Prediction:** Identifying the correct direction for the next step given the map and previous movements.
 - **Visual Tiling (New Task):** Inspired by polyomino tiling puzzles, LLMs must select the correct variant (orientation) of a polyomino piece to fit into masked cells of a rectangle, demonstrating comprehension, organization, and reasoning with shapes.

- **Dataset Generation:**
 - **Natural Language Navigation:** 200 synthetic 3x3 square maps with landmarks and navigation instructions.
 - **Visual Navigation:** 496 navigation maps and 2520 QA instances generated synthetically. Difficulty (k-hop) increases with map size (up to 7x9). The generation process involved enumerating instruction sets, simulating paths with collision avoidance, and rendering maps as text grids.
 - **Visual Tiling:** 796 QA instances generated by creating valid polyomino tiling configurations (e.g., for a 5x4 rectangle with specific polyominoes) using algorithms like Dancing Links (for exact cover) or SAT solvers, then randomly masking pieces to form puzzles.
 - For visual tasks, textual grids were also rendered as images using color emojis for comparison with MLLMs.

- **Model Evaluation and Settings:**
 - **Models:** GPT-4, GPT-4 Vision (GPT-4V), GPT-3.5 turbo, LLAMA3-8B-Instruct, LLAMA3-70B-Instruct. All models accessed via Azure OpenAI API with greedy decoding (temperature 0, top p 1).
 - **Prompting Settings (Zero-shot):**
 - **GPT-4 CoT:** "Let’s think step by step."
 - **GPT-4 w/o Viz:** "Don’t use visualization. Let’s think step by step." (explicitly disabling visualization).
 - **GPT-4V CoT:** "Let’s think step by step." (for MLLM with image input).
 - **GPT-4 VoT:** "Visualize the state after each reasoning step."

- **Metrics:**
 - **Accuracy:** For all tasks except route planning, calculated as the proportion of correct answers (using substring or exact matching).
 - **Route Planning Metrics:**
 - **Average Completing Rate:** (normalized length of predicted path / ground truth length) averaged over all maps.
 - **Success Rate:** Proportion of instruction sequences that successfully reached the destination.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several significant findings:

- **Superior Performance of VoT:** GPT-4 with VoT prompting consistently and significantly outperformed all other settings, including GPT-4 CoT, GPT-4 with visualization explicitly disabled (GPT-4 w/o Viz), and even the multimodal GPT-4 Vision (GPT-4V CoT) in the visual tasks.
 - In Natural Language Navigation, GPT-4 VoT (59.00%) significantly outperformed GPT-4 w/o Viz (35.50%), showing a 23.5% improvement.
 - In Visual Navigation, VoT achieved 40.77% average completing rate and 14.72% success rate for route planning, and 63.94% accuracy for next step prediction, consistently outperforming baselines. Notably, GPT-4 VoT (63.94%) outperformed GPT-4V CoT (49.62%) in next step prediction, suggesting that internal visualization in an LLM can be more effective than direct external visual input for these specific spatial reasoning tasks.
 - In Visual Tiling, GPT-4 VoT achieved 55.28% accuracy, again surpassing other models.

- **Emergent Visual State Tracking:** The analysis of reasoning traces revealed that VoT markedly increased the "visual state tracking" behavior in LLMs.
 - **Complete Tracking Rate:** VoT achieved very high complete tracking rates (e.g., 96.2% for Visual Navigation-Route Planning, 92.9% for Next Step Prediction, 87.1% for Visual Tiling). This means LLMs frequently generated a visualization for each reasoning step.
 - In contrast, GPT-4 CoT (without explicit VoT prompt) still exhibited some partial tracking behavior (e.g., 59.2% for Visual Tiling), suggesting an inherent tendency for visual state tracking when spatiotemporal simulation is involved, but VoT significantly amplified this. Explicitly disabling visualization (GPT-4 w/o Viz) drastically reduced tracking.
 - The study found evidence that this emergent ability might stem from pre-training on tabular data, city grid navigation, maze exploration, and significantly, exposure to **ASCII art in code comments**, which trains LLMs to understand and manipulate 2D shapes and spatiotemporal causality through interleaved text and diagrams.

- **How Visualizations Enhance Answers:**
 - LLMs demonstrated a promising compliance rate (51-52%) in generating visualizations that adhered to spatial constraints (e.g., avoiding overlaps, navigating obstacles).
 - However, the accuracy of these state visualizations was relatively low (24-26%).
 - Despite this, when accurate internal state visualizations *were* generated, LLMs made correct decisions in 65-77% of cases, indicating that even imperfect visualization ability can significantly enhance groundedness and lead to performance gains.

- **VoT's Benefit to Less Powerful Models:**
 - VoT generally showed a benefit for less powerful models (GPT-3.5 turbo, LLAMA3-8B-Instruct, LLAMA3-70B-Instruct), though the improvements were less consistent or pronounced compared to GPT-4.
 - LLAMA3-70B VoT significantly outperformed its CoT baseline across most tasks, aligning with GPT-4's trends and suggesting scaling advantages.
 - Less capable models sometimes resorted to random guessing, which VoT helped minimize, but their performance trends across increasing difficulty levels remained irregular, indicating limitations in sustaining reliable reasoning.

- **Contexts Where VoT Might Underperform:** The paper notes an interesting exception: in a natural language navigation task within a ring (which could be solved via simple modular arithmetic), GPT-4 CoT (52.5%) slightly outperformed GPT-4 VoT (49.5%). This suggests that when a task can be efficiently reduced to pure logical or mathematical operations, explicitly forcing visual simulation might introduce unnecessary overhead or complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step towards understanding and enhancing the spatial reasoning capabilities of LLMs, with profound implications:

- **Emergence of "Mind's Eye" in LLMs:** The most striking significance is the demonstration that LLMs, primarily trained on text, can exhibit an emergent capability akin to the human "mind's eye." By effectively generating and manipulating internal "mental images" (as text-based 2D grids), LLMs can perform multi-hop spatial reasoning, mirroring a crucial aspect of human cognition. This provides empirical evidence for the existence of rudimentary "world models" within LLMs, even without explicit visual grounding.

- **Rethinking LLM vs. MLLM Capabilities:** The finding that a purely text-based LLM (GPT-4 VoT) can outperform a state-of-the-art multimodal model (GPT-4V CoT) on visual tasks is highly impactful. It suggests that for certain types of abstract spatial reasoning, the ability to *internally represent and manipulate* spatial states might be more critical than direct perception of pixel data. This could reshape research directions, encouraging more focus on internal representations and reasoning in LLMs rather than solely relying on multimodal fusion for spatial tasks.

- **Advancement in Prompt Engineering:** VoT introduces a powerful new prompting paradigm that extends beyond Chain-of-Thought by incorporating structured "visualization" as an integral part of the reasoning process. This opens new avenues for designing more effective prompts for complex, multi-step reasoning that benefits from state tracking.

- **Foundation for Future AI Systems:** This work lays a foundation for building more cognitively aligned AI systems that can reason about and interact with the physical world. If LLMs can develop robust internal spatial models, they could be integrated into robotic systems, autonomous navigation, and other applications requiring spatial intelligence, leading to more robust and explainable decision-making.

- **Cognitive Science Implications:** The paper provides computational insights into theories of mental imagery and spatial cognition. It offers a new tool for probing how large neural networks might implicitly learn and leverage spatial concepts, potentially shedding light on the mechanisms of human spatial reasoning.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper clearly outlines avenues for future work, including applying VoT to MLLMs to enhance their spatial awareness, exploring automatic data augmentation from real-world scenarios for learning generalized internal representations, and investigating more complex representations beyond 2D grids (e.g., 3D semantics). Addressing the current limitations of prompt sensitivity and performance on less advanced models will also be crucial.

In summary, "Mind’s Eye of LLMs" demonstrates a compelling new capability in large language models, pushing the boundaries of what is believed possible with text-only pre-training and offering a fresh perspective on how AI can mimic and achieve complex human-like spatial cognition.
