# Tuna-2: Pixel Embeddings Beat Vision Encoders for Multimodal Understanding and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta AI researchers introduced Tuna-2, a unified multimodal model that performs visual understanding and generation directly from pixel embeddings, eliminating the need for pretrained vision encoders. This encoder-free architecture achieved competitive performance across nine VQA benchmarks and state-of-the-art results among native UMMs for image generation and editing tasks.

## Problem

- Unified multimodal models (UMMs) traditionally rely on complex, modular designs with pretrained vision encoders (e.g., CLIP, VAE), leading to architectural complexity and potential representation mismatches between understanding and generation tasks.
- Reliance on compact latent spaces from VAEs or high-level semantic features from representation encoders can limit the model's ability to process and reason about fine-grained visual details.
- The use of pretrained, static encoders constrains the degree to which the entire multimodal system can be optimized end-to-end directly from raw pixel inputs.

## Method

- Developed **Tuna-2**, an ==encoder-free, monolithic architecture== that replaces pretrained vision encoders with simple ==patch embedding layers== to directly convert raw images into visual tokens.
- Employed an ==x-prediction and v-loss paradigm== based on ==rectified flow== for direct pixel-space image generation, removing the dependency on VAEs.
- Introduced a ==masking-based visual feature learning scheme== during training, where random image patches are masked, compelling the model to learn more robust unified representations for both understanding and generation.

## Results

- Tuna-2 achieved competitive results across **nine diverse VQA benchmarks**, outperforming earlier native UMMs and demonstrating stronger performance on pixel-centric tasks (V*, CountBench, VisuLogic).
- Attained **state-of-the-art performance among native UMMs** on **GenEval** and **DPG-Bench** for image generation, with LLM-judge evaluations indicating comparable quality to Tuna-R and superior diversity.
- Tuna-2 demonstrated more accurate vision-language alignment in attention map visualizations and greater robustness to misleading linguistic contexts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale end-to-end training of an encoder-free, pixel-space architecture can effectively learn visual representations that are competitive with, and often surpass, those derived from pretrained vision encoders for understanding tasks.
> - Direct pixel-space generation, when coupled with a robust flow matching paradigm and extensive training, can achieve state-of-the-art performance in image generation and editing without requiring latent-space VAEs.
> - Masking-based visual feature learning significantly enhances both multimodal understanding and generation, particularly for encoder-free models, by encouraging the model to develop robust representations and cross-modal alignment even with partial visual input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements, with a growing focus on developing Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) that integrate both visual understanding and generation capabilities within a single framework. Traditionally, UMMs have faced challenges in creating visual representations that effectively serve both tasks.

Early approaches in UMMs often employed decoupled visual representations. For instance, models might use vision encoders like CLIP for understanding tasks (e.g., visual question answering) and reconstruction-oriented encoders such as VQ-VAE for generative tasks (e.g., image synthesis). This architectural choice, however, introduced a mismatch between the representations used for different modalities, hindering fully end-to-end optimization.

More recent UMMs have progressed towards using unified visual representations derived from a shared vision encoder, aiming to bridge this gap. Despite this unification, a common characteristic of these models, including previous iterations like Tuna (Liu et al., 2025), is their reliance on pretrained vision encoders (such as VAEs or specialized representation encoders like SigLIP 2). These encoders process raw pixel data into a more compact latent space or extract high-level semantic features before feeding them into the main language model decoder.

In parallel, advancements in vision-language models for understanding and visual generation models have indicated a potential shift away from modular, encoder-based designs. In multimodal understanding, native vision-language models (e.g., Mono-InternVL, NEO) have begun to align images and natural language within unified, end-to-end architectures, discarding pretrained representation encoders. Similarly, in visual generation, pixel-space diffusion models (e.g., simple diffusion, JiT) have demonstrated increasing flexibility, scalability, and state-of-the-art performance, suggesting that pretrained VAE encoders might not be essential for high-fidelity image synthesis.

Tuna-2 positions itself within this evolving landscape by integrating these encoder-free trends into a unified multimodal model. It directly addresses the question of whether UMMs can move beyond pretrained vision encoders altogether, performing both understanding and generation directly from raw pixels. By proposing an architecture that progressively simplifies and ultimately removes these conventional encoder modules, Tuna-2 represents a move towards monolithic, end-to-end pixel-space learning in the context of UMMs, aiming to overcome the limitations of representation misalignment and fixed inductive biases imposed by pretrained encoders. Concurrent work like SenseNova-U1 and Neo-Unify also explores similar encoder-free architectures, indicating a broader trend in the research community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether a unified multimodal model can effectively perform both visual understanding and generation by operating directly on raw pixel embeddings, thereby completely bypassing the need for pretrained vision encoders. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

First, existing unified multimodal models typically rely on pretrained vision encoders, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) for generation or specialized representation encoders (e.g., based on CLIP or SigLIP) for understanding. This modular design often leads to several limitations:
- **Representation Misalignment:** The use of separate or even unified representations learned through different pretraining objectives can create misalignment between the understanding and generation tasks.
- **Hindrance to End-to-End Optimization:** The reliance on pretrained, fixed (or partially fixed) encoder components prevents true end-to-end optimization of the entire multimodal pipeline from raw pixels, limiting the model's ability to learn jointly optimal representations.
- **Built-in Inductive Biases:** Pretrained encoders often come with built-in inductive biases, such as fixed input resolutions or a focus on specific levels of visual detail. This can restrict the model's access to fine-grained visual information, potentially limiting performance on tasks requiring high-resolution perception.

Second, recent advancements in specific domains within multimodal AI have challenged the necessity of these encoders:
- **Encoder-free Understanding Models:** Newer native vision-language models have demonstrated the viability of removing pretrained representation encoders and aligning images and natural language within a unified, end-to-end architecture.
- **Pixel-space Generation Models:** Pixel-space diffusion models have shown increasing flexibility, scalability, and state-of-the-art performance in image synthesis, suggesting that VAEs might no longer be essential for high-fidelity image generation.

Motivated by these observations, the authors aim to introduce Tuna-2 as a native unified multimodal model that progressively simplifies its architecture by removing these encoder modules. Specifically, the objectives are:
1. **Develop an Encoder-Free Architecture:** To design a UMM, Tuna-2, that replaces both VAEs and representation encoders with simple patch embedding layers, enabling direct processing of raw image inputs by a single transformer decoder.
2. **Introduce Robust Feature Learning:** To implement a masking-based visual feature learning scheme to stabilize training and encourage the learning of robust pixel-space representations, which is particularly challenging in high-dimensional pixel space.
3. **Demonstrate Competitive Performance:** To show that this encoder-free, pixel-space modeling approach can achieve state-of-the-art performance across a diverse suite of multimodal understanding and generation benchmarks.
4. **Conduct Controlled Comparisons:** To perform systematic comparisons between Tuna-2 and an encoder-based pixel-space UMM variant (Tuna-R) to analyze the impact of removing encoders on training dynamics, understanding capabilities (especially fine-grained perception), and generation quality.
5. **Offer Design Insights:** To provide insights into the effectiveness and scalability of end-to-end pixel-space learning for developing stronger visual representations in future native unified multimodal models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around progressively simplifying the architecture of unified multimodal models, moving from latent-space processing to direct pixel-space interaction, and introducing a novel feature learning scheme.

#### 3.1. Towards Encoder-Free Unified Models

The design process of Tuna-2 is described as a progressive simplification of existing UMM architectures:

- **Tuna:** The baseline architecture, similar to previous work, consists of a VAE (Variational Autoencoder) and a representation encoder, which feed into an LLM decoder for joint vision-language modeling. Modality-specific heads (language modeling and flow matching) are used for text and image generation, respectively.
- **Tuna-R (Representation encoder-based architecture):** This intermediate design eliminates the VAE component but retains a pretrained representation encoder. Input images are encoded into visual tokens by the representation encoder, which are then combined with text tokens in the LLM decoder. Visual generation in Tuna-R is performed directly in pixel space using a flow matching head. This design aligns with established paradigms for vision-language models that emphasize strong understanding capabilities.
- **Tuna-2 (Encoder-free architecture):** This is the main contribution, further simplifying Tuna-R by entirely removing the representation encoder. In Tuna-2, pretrained vision encoders are replaced by simple patch embedding layers that convert raw images directly into visual tokens. These visual tokens are then processed jointly with text tokens by a single LLM decoder. This monolithic design avoids the inductive biases and fixed input resolutions associated with pretrained encoders, aiming for fully end-to-end native modeling from raw pixels.

#### 3.2. Pixel-Space Image Generation

With the removal of the VAE, Tuna-2 cannot rely on latent diffusion architectures for image generation. Instead, it adopts the x-prediction and v-loss paradigm from JiT (Li and He, 2025) for pixel-space flow matching.
- **Rectified Flow:** Given a source image $x_1$, a sampled noise $x_0 \sim N(0, I)$, and a timestamp $t \in$, a noisy sample $x_t$ is constructed as $x_t = tx_1 + (1 - t)x_0$.
- **Clean Image Prediction:** Tuna-2 is formulated to directly predict the clean image $x_\theta$ from the noisy image $x_t$, conditioned on contextual signals $c$ (e.g., text for text-to-image generation, text+image for image editing): $x_\theta = \pi_\theta(x_t, c, t)$, where $\pi_\theta$ represents the unified model (vision-language backbone and flow matching head).
- **Velocity Regression Loss:** While $x_\theta$ is predicted, it is transformed into a velocity term $v_\theta = (x_\theta - x_t) / (1 - t)$. The learning objective $L_{flow}$ minimizes the squared difference between $v_\theta$ and the ground truth velocity $v = x_1 - x_0$: $L_{flow} = E_{t,c,x_1,x_0} ||v_\theta - v||_2^2$.
- **Inference:** During inference, an Euler solver is used to predict a denoised image at $t'$ from an image at $t < t'$ based on the predicted velocity term $v_\theta$.

#### 3.3. Learning Robust Visual Representations via Masking

To address the challenge of learning unified representations in the high-dimensional pixel space, a masking-based visual feature learning scheme is introduced.
- **Masking Operation:** During training, a subset of image patches is randomly selected based on a masking ratio. The corresponding visual tokens are replaced with a learnable mask token before being fed into the LLM decoder.
- **Role in Generation:** For generation examples, the model is tasked with predicting the clean image patches in both masked and unmasked regions. This creates a more challenging denoising problem and encourages the mask token to absorb useful information for image reconstruction.
- **Role in Understanding:** For understanding examples, masking acts as a regularization mechanism. It forces the model to perform multimodal reasoning under partial visual observation, promoting the learning of more robust and less superficial visual representations.

#### 3.4. Training Pipeline

The training process for Tuna-2 is end-to-end and consists of two stages:

- **Stage 1: Full Model Pretraining:**
 - **Objective:** To establish a strong initialization for the flow matching head and adapt pixel-space visual inputs for unified multimodal understanding and generation.
 - **Tasks:** Jointly trained on image captioning (for understanding) and text-to-image generation.
 - **Data:** 550M in-house image-text pairs (30% image captioning, 70% text-to-image generation) and 20% text-only data from Nemotron.
 - **Optimization:** Trained for 300k steps using AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-4}$.
 - **Masking:** The masking-based feature learning strategy is applied during the final 40% of pretraining, with masking in 50% of training examples and masking ratio sampled from 0% to 50%.
 - **Tuna-R Specific:** For Tuna-R, an additional connector-alignment stage is performed before Stage 1, where only the connector layer between the representation encoder and LLM decoder is trained briefly (3k steps) with image captioning and text-to-image generation data. Tuna-2, being encoder-free, does not require this stage.

- **Stage 2: Supervised Finetuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** To refine Tuna-2's abilities, boosting performance and generalization across various multimodal tasks.
 - **Data:** A curated SFT corpus including 13M conversational examples from FineVision (image instruction-following) and approximately 2M examples from OmniEdit (image editing), plus high-quality image generation data.
 - **Optimization:** Trained for 50k steps with AdamW and a lower learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-5}$.
 - **General Parameters:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the LLM decoder, patch embedding size of 16.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted assess Tuna-2's capabilities across multimodal understanding, generation, and editing, alongside detailed ablations and analyses.

#### 4.1. Image Understanding

Tuna-2 was evaluated on nine Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks, including general benchmarks like GQA, RealWorldQA, MMVet, MMMU, and pixel-centric benchmarks such as MMVP, SEED-Bench2+, AI2D, ChartQA, and OCRBench, V*, CountBench, and VisuLogic.
- Both Tuna-R and Tuna-2 demonstrated improved performance over the baseline Tuna model and achieved competitive results among 7B-scale native UMMs across a majority of general VQA benchmarks (Table 1). This indicates the effectiveness of the pixel-space unified representations.
- Tuna-2 consistently outperformed Tuna-R on most understanding benchmarks, particularly on pixel-centric tasks requiring fine-grained visual perception (e.g., V*, CountBench, VisuLogic). This suggests that the encoder-free, monolithic architecture of Tuna-2, when trained at scale, can learn stronger fine-grained visual representations compared to models relying on pretrained representation encoders.

#### 4.2. Image Generation

Image generation capabilities were assessed using GenEval and DPG-Bench, along with an LLM-judge-based evaluation.
- On GenEval and DPG-Bench, Tuna-R and Tuna-2 achieved state-of-the-art results among native UMMs, demonstrating competitiveness with contemporary approaches (Table 2).
- Tuna-R generally exhibited slightly stronger performance in generation compared to Tuna-2. The authors hypothesize this is due to the semantic priors provided by the representation encoder in Tuna-R. However, this performance gap decreased after supervised finetuning.
- LLM-judge evaluation (using GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.7) for quality and diversity showed Tuna-2 to be competitive in image generation quality with Tuna-R and superior to Tuna. Notably, Tuna-2 was significantly preferred for image diversity (Table 3), indicating the encoder-free design's ability to produce varied outputs.

#### 4.3. Image Editing

Tuna-2's image editing capabilities were evaluated on ImgEdit.
- Tuna-2 demonstrated strong editing performance among unified models, surpassing several earlier baselines. While slightly behind Tuna and Tuna-R in overall editing scores, it remained competitive with established generation-only and unified editing systems (Table 4). This suggests the pixel-space unified modeling framework effectively supports instruction-guided image editing, though pretrained visual priors in encoder-based variants might offer a minor advantage in fine-grained editing fidelity.

#### 4.4. Image Reconstruction

To further analyze image generation capabilities, the models' ability to reconstruct input images from their pixel-space visual representations was evaluated after lightweight finetuning on ImageNet validation set.
- Both Tuna-R and Tuna-2 showed strong reconstruction performance, ranking highly among unified tokenizers and closely approaching specialized VAE models (Table 5, Figure 4). This indicates that the pixel-space unified visual representations can support high image reconstruction and generation quality without VAEs.

#### 4.5. Ablation: Model Training Dynamics

Ablation studies on the generation-to-understanding data ratio during pretraining (ranging from 8g2u to 2g8u) were conducted for Tuna-R and Tuna-2.
- Increasing the proportion of either generation or understanding data consistently reduced its corresponding training loss (MSE for flow matching, cross-entropy for language modeling) (Figure 5).
- The MSE loss for generation was found to be more sensitive to changes in the sampling ratio than the cross-entropy loss for understanding.
- A 7:3 generation-to-understanding ratio (7g3u) was identified as providing an effective trade-off, balancing performance between both objectives.

#### 4.6. Ablation: Masking-based Feature Learning

The effectiveness of the proposed masking-based feature learning strategy was evaluated.
- Applying masking during the later stages of pretraining consistently improved performance on both understanding (OCRBench, MMVP, CountBench) and generation (GenEval) benchmarks for both Tuna-R and Tuna-2 (Table 6).
- Tuna-2 benefited more from masked training than Tuna-R, potentially because the SigLIP 2 encoder in Tuna-R is already pretrained with a similar objective. This further supports masking as a representation enhancement strategy for pixel-space models.

#### 4.7. Analysis: Tuna-R vs. Tuna-2

Comparative analysis of performance trends across training data scales revealed insights into the two variants:
- **Understanding:** Tuna-R initially outperformed Tuna-2 due to its pretrained representation encoder's semantic priors. However, as training data scaled, Tuna-2 caught up and eventually surpassed Tuna-R on understanding benchmarks (Figure 6). This suggests the encoder-free, monolithic design of Tuna-2 is better suited for leveraging large-scale unified multimodal pretraining to develop stronger, fine-grained understanding capabilities.
- **Generation:** Tuna-R consistently maintained a slight lead over Tuna-2 in generation performance throughout training, indicating the continued value of semantic priors from the representation encoder. Nevertheless, this gap diminished significantly after SFT.

#### 4.8. Analysis: Attention Map Visualizations

Qualitative analysis of attention maps demonstrated Tuna-2's cross-modal alignment capabilities.
- Tuna-2 exhibited more accurate vision-language alignment in basic perception tasks (e.g., highlighting "shining" regions, localizing "purple object") compared to other encoder-based LMMs (Figure 7).
- In scenarios with misleading linguistic contexts (e.g., "dog cafe" with dogs in the image) or salient visual distractors (e.g., "football match" with a glass cup being kicked), Tuna-2 showed greater robustness, accurately localizing relevant visual regions rather than being misled by text or distractors. This indicates that Tuna-2 learns more reliable visual representations, leading to consistent cross-modal alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Tuna-2 represents a significant development in the field of unified multimodal models, challenging established architectural paradigms and offering several potential impacts:

- **Challenging the Necessity of Pretrained Encoders:** The primary significance of Tuna-2 is its demonstration that high-performance unified multimodal understanding and generation can be achieved without relying on pretrained vision encoders (such as VAEs or dedicated representation encoders). This finding suggests that these modular components, previously considered essential, may not be indispensable.
- **Advancing End-to-End Pixel-Space Learning:** Tuna-2 provides empirical evidence for the effectiveness and scalability of direct pixel-space modeling for UMMs. By processing raw pixels through simple patch embeddings, the model achieves true end-to-end optimization, which was previously limited by the latent-space operations or fixed inductive biases of modular encoders.
- **Improved Fine-Grained Visual Perception:** The results, particularly on pixel-centric understanding benchmarks and attention map analyses, indicate that encoder-free designs can lead to the learning of stronger, more fine-grained visual representations. This is crucial for tasks requiring detailed visual reasoning and robust cross-modal alignment, potentially enabling advancements in applications demanding precise visual understanding.
- **Architectural Simplification and Scalability:** By removing complex, multi-component vision encoder pipelines, Tuna-2 simplifies the overall model architecture into a single unified transformer. This simplification can reduce architectural overhead, potentially easing development, deployment, and future scaling of multimodal foundation models. The observed ability of Tuna-2 to catch up and surpass encoder-based variants at scale further supports its scalability potential.
- **Foundation for Future UMMs:** This work offers a promising direction for the development of future native UMMs. It suggests that investing in large-scale, end-to-end pixel-space pretraining for monolithic architectures can yield superior or competitive performance across a broad spectrum of multimodal tasks, encouraging further research into encoder-free designs and robust pixel-space feature learning techniques.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Diversity:** The finding that Tuna-2 generates images with significantly higher diversity, as judged by LLMs, indicates that direct pixel-space learning might foster a richer and more varied generative capability compared to latent-space approaches, which could be beneficial for creative applications.

In summary, Tuna-2 demonstrates the viability and advantages of an encoder-free, pixel-space approach for unified multimodal modeling, providing a concrete path toward more integrated, scalable, and robust multimodal AI systems.
