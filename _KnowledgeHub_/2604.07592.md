# Spatio-Temporal Grounding of Large Language Models from Perception Streams

> [!summary] Summary
> The Formally Explainable Spatio-Temporal Scenes (FESTS) framework, developed by Toyota Motor North America Research & Development, enables enhanced spatio-temporal reasoning in LLMs through automatically generated, verifiable supervision. This approach allowed a 3-billion-parameter LLM to achieve an 87.5% Frame F1 on complex spatio-temporal tasks, exceeding GPT-4.1's 84.8% on the proposed benchmark.

## Problem

- Frontier Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate limitations in fine-grained spatial reasoning, metric distance comprehension, and accurate temporal ordering for dynamic 3D environments.
- Existing spatio-temporal reasoning benchmarks often rely on simple queries like multiple-choice QA, lacking the depth and verifiability needed for robust real-world reasoning in complex scenarios.
- Manually annotating large datasets for complex spatio-temporal events is prohibitively expensive, posing a scalability challenge for training data critical to advanced reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- The **FESTS** (Formally Explainable Spatio-Temporal Scenes) framework automatically generates verifiable spatio-temporal supervision by matching complex queries against perception logs, yielding ==(query, frames, match, explanation) tuples==.
- The ==Spatial Regular Expression (SpRE)== language is extended with universal and existential quantifiers (==q-SpRE==) to enable persistent object tracking and the expression of more complex, multi-object relationships over time.
- A multi-stage fine-tuning approach, combining ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== on query-answer pairs and ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== with a custom ==hierarchical reward function==, is applied to a target LLM.

## Results

- A **3-billion-parameter** LLM, fine-tuned with the Q-SFT+RL pipeline, achieved an overall Frame F1 score of **87.5%** and an Exact Match score of **64.5%** on the FESTS benchmark.
- The fine-tuned Q-SFT+RL model slightly surpassed GPT-4.1's overall Frame F1 of **84.8%** on the same benchmark, demonstrating competitive performance with a model two orders of magnitude smaller.
- Performance gains scaled positively with increasing query lengths, with the Q-SFT+RL model achieving an **82.7%** Frame F1 score for the longest (16-frame) input sequences, marking a **17-point** increase over SFT alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automatically generated, self-verifiable spatio-temporal supervision, derived from formal methods, can substantially improve LLM reasoning capabilities without the need for manual, crowd-sourced labeling.
> - Extending formal pattern matching languages with quantifiers is crucial for enabling robust and nuanced spatio-temporal reasoning, supporting persistent object tracking and complex logical conditions over time.
> - Targeted, logically-grounded fine-tuning can imbue relatively small LLMs (3 billion parameters) with expert spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities that are competitive with, or even surpass, much larger frontier models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) agents for applications such as household robotics, autonomous driving, and assistive manipulation necessitates sophisticated reasoning capabilities regarding the dynamic, three-dimensional world. This includes comprehending how objects move, interact, and evolve over time within 3D space. Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual Language Models (VLMs) have shown utility in tasks like task-and-motion planning and annotation. However, a consistent observation across recent research is that frontier LLMs and VLMs exhibit limitations in fine-grained spatial reasoning, metric distance comprehension, accurate temporal ordering, and especially in integrating these aspects simultaneously. Specifically, these models may struggle with relative object ordering, fail to differentiate identical object instances, and inaccurately estimate metric distances.

Existing research has attempted to address these spatial reasoning deficiencies through various model enhancements. Approaches like SpatialVLM, SpatialBot, SpatialRGPT, 3D-aware SpatialLLM, and COMFORT have explored integrating geometric priors or object-centered prompts. However, many of these benchmarks and proposed solutions tend to focus on local or static spatial relationships. Similarly, while temporal understanding in video-LLMs has progressed from basic captioning to more complex challenges, studies like V-STaR highlight remaining difficulties in spatio-temporal reasoning, particularly concerning "when," "where," and "what" in dynamic contexts. Prior benchmarks for both spatial and temporal reasoning often rely on multiple-choice question-answering or short captions, which may not adequately stress the fine-grained, verifiable reasoning required for complex real-world scenarios. Notably, work by Li et al. suggested that temporal reasoning bottlenecks might reside within the LLM component itself rather than solely in the video encoder, implying that enhanced textual supervision could improve reasoning abilities.

This work addresses the identified gap by proposing a framework that injects rich, verifiable spatio-temporal supervision into LLMs. Unlike previous approaches that often rely on crowd-sourced labels or simpler query types, this research leverages formal methods to generate self-verifiable ground truth data. By extending an existing spatio-temporal pattern matching language with quantification, the work aims to enable more precise object tracking and complex relationship reasoning over time. This approach positions the research at the intersection of formal methods, natural language processing, and computer vision, seeking to bridge the performance gap of current LLMs in complex spatio-temporal understanding relevant for advanced embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models, particularly for application in embodied AI agents. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and is motivated by current limitations observed in state-of-the-art models.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Framework Development**: To introduce a general framework, Formally Explainable Spatio-Temporal Scenes (FESTS), capable of injecting verifiable spatio-temporal supervision into an LLM. This framework aims to enable LLMs to accurately answer and explain complex video queries.
2. **Automated Data Generation**: To develop a pipeline that can automatically synthesize diverse spatio-temporal queries, match them against structured perception logs, and export aligned (query, frames, match, explanation) tuples. This system is designed to generate arbitrarily large, self-verifiable training datasets without requiring manual labeling.
3. **Pattern Matching Language Extension**: To extend the existing Spatial Regular Expression (SpRE) language by incorporating universal and existential quantifiers. This extension aims to enable persistent object tracking and the expression of more complex reasoning patterns involving multiple objects over time.
4. **Benchmark Dataset Creation**: To release the FESTS benchmark dataset, which is automatically annotated and derived from verifiable spatio-temporal queries rather than traditional crowd-sourced labels.
5. **Empirical Validation**: To empirically demonstrate that the proposed framework and the resulting logically-grounded synthetic supervision can significantly improve the spatio-temporal reasoning skills of LLMs, enabling them to achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding much larger frontier models on complex tasks.
6. **Explainability Integration**: To produce natural language explanations for matched patterns, which can be incorporated into the training process or used in chain-of-thought reasoning frameworks.

**Motivation:**
The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations in current AI systems:
- **Deficiencies in Existing LLMs/VLMs**: Despite advances, frontier LLMs and VLMs frequently exhibit brittleness in comprehending fine-grained spatial relations (e.g., relative ordering, distinguishing instances, metric distances) and temporal dynamics. These shortcomings translate directly into potential failure modes for embodied AI systems operating in dynamic environments.
- **Need for Rich, Verifiable Supervision**: Current methods for training spatio-temporal reasoning often rely on limited multiple-choice QA or short captions, which lack the depth and verifiability necessary for robust reasoning. There is a recognized absence of datasets that couple complex queries with explicit spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities in models.
- **Scaling Training Data**: Manually annotating large datasets for complex spatio-temporal events is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. An automated, scalable method for generating ground-truth data is crucial for advancing reasoning capabilities.
- **Insights into LLM Bottlenecks**: Prior research suggests that temporal reasoning limitations may reside within the language model component itself, indicating that targeted textual supervision, rather than just improved video encoders, can lead to significant improvements.
- **Demand for Explainable AI**: For critical applications like autonomous driving, not only accurate reasoning but also the ability to explain *why* a particular event or pattern was detected is important for trust and debugging.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to provide a foundational advancement for LLMs to effectively interpret and reason about dynamic 3D environments, moving towards more intelligent and explainable embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the Formally Explainable Spatio-Temporal Scenes (FESTS) framework, designed to generate and utilize verifiable spatio-temporal supervision for fine-tuning Large Language Models.

**FESTS Framework Overview:**
The FESTS framework takes a data stream of perception-based object annotations as input. This data typically includes object classes, bounding box information, and may extend to unique object identifiers, pixel depth, or other relevant attributes. The framework processes this input to generate output tuples in the format (Q', D', M, S):
- **Q'**: The Natural Language (NL) version of a Spatio-Temporal Regular Expression (SpRE) query Q.
- **D'**: A sampled sub-stream of the original perception data, corresponding to the context of the query.
- **M**: The set of precise matches identified by the STREM (Spatio-Temporal Regular Expression Matching) engine.
- **S**: A set of Natural Language explanations, derived from formal explanations, detailing why the pattern was matched.

**Core Components and Process:**
1. **Spatio-Temporal Regular Expression Matching (STREM)**:
 - The framework utilizes and extends the STREM paradigm, which combines Regular Expressions (REs) with S4u spatial logic. This combination allows for expressing patterns that capture both temporal sequences and spatial relationships among objects within a perception stream.
 - The matching procedure is based on formal methods, employing Deterministic Finite Automata (DFA) to formally determine if a perception stream satisfies a given SpRE query.
2. **Extension of SpRE with Quantification (q-SpRE)**:
 - A significant contribution is addressing limitations in the original SpRE framework, which could not support complex queries requiring object persistence (e.g., tracking the *same* pedestrian over multiple frames) or universal/existential reasoning (e.g., "all cars are more than 500 units away").
 - The SpRE grammar was adjusted to include RE-level universal (∀) and existential (∃) quantifiers. These quantifiers introduce variables (e.g., `x`) that bind to specific objects, enabling tracking of entities across time. For instance, `∃x ← [car].xx` mandates that the *same* car appears in two consecutive frames.
 - The introduction of quantifiers necessitated a complete redevelopment of the semantic rules and the underlying query matching algorithms within the STREM framework.
3. **Pipeline Modules (Figure 1)**:
 - **SpRE Scene**: This module takes SpRE queries and the perception data stream, then processes them through the STREM engine to produce formal matches and explanations.
 - **Naturalizer**: The formal explanations generated by STREM are then converted into human-readable Natural Language explanations. This step enhances their utility for LLM training, particularly for enabling explainable reasoning.
 - **Packager**: This module aggregates the natural language query, the relevant sampled perception stream data, the formal matches, and the natural language explanations into the final (Q', D', M, S) tuples, ready for downstream training or evaluation.

**Dataset Composition and Generation:**
- **Perception Stream Source**: The Woven Perception dataset, an autonomous vehicle (AV) perception dataset, was chosen due to its comprehensive streams and high-quality, hand-labeled object annotations. It comprises 180 scenes, each with 126 frames from 7 monocular camera sensors, yielding over 1.2K perception streams.
- **Data Sampling**: To create training data of varying complexity, perception streams were sampled at increasing frame lengths (1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16 frames).
- **Query Templates**: Fifteen distinct templated queries were utilized, categorized into:
 - **Sequence**: Involving multiple temporally adjacent events.
 - **Spatial**: Involving operations like bounding box intersections.
 - **Temporal**: Involving eventual events over time.
 - **Metric**: Involving measurement-based constraints (e.g., distances).
 - **Existential**: Involving reasoning about the same or all objects over time, leveraging the new quantifiers.
- This process generated over 27,000 (query, frames, match, explanation) tuples for training.

**LLM Fine-Tuning and Evaluation:**
- **Target LLM**: The Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model (3 billion parameters) was selected for its public availability, relatively small size, suitability for task completion, and context length.
- **Fine-Tuning Configurations**:
 - **C1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (Q-SFT)**: The model was trained solely on the (query, match) outputs of the framework. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) using Low Rank Adaptation (LoRA) was applied to attention and MLP layers. The objective was causal language modeling, minimizing cross-entropy loss with non-result tokens masked.
 - **C2: Supervised Fine-Tuning with Reinforcement Learning (Q-SFT+RL)**: Building upon the C1 pre-trained model, Reinforcement Learning with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) was applied. A custom hierarchical reward function guided this stage, evaluating structural validity (e.g., XML format), match accuracy (mAP IoU, exact match), and reasoning fidelity (semantic similarity to ground-truth explanations, numerical IoU of referenced frames). Penalties were incurred for excessive response length, spurious text, and invalid formats.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Performance was assessed using Frame F1 (F1f), Exact Match (EM), and Segment F1 (F1s).
- **Comparison Models**: The fine-tuned models were benchmarked against the baseline Qwen2.5-Coder-3B-Instruct and a state-of-the-art GPT-4.1 model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the FESTS framework and its associated fine-tuning methodology significantly enhance the spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities of a 3-billion-parameter LLM.

**Overall Performance (Table 1):**
- The **Q-Baseline** model exhibited a Frame F1 score of 48.5% and an Exact Match score of 25.0%.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (Q-SFT)** on the generated query-answer pairs resulted in substantial improvement, boosting the overall Frame F1 to 80.4% and Exact Match to 56.6%. This improvement was primarily driven by an increase in recall, which rose by approximately 28 percentage points.
- Further training with **Reinforcement Learning (Q-SFT+RL)** on top of the SFT, incorporating explanations, pushed the performance even higher. The Frame F1 reached 87.5%, and Exact Match improved to 64.5%. This stage primarily contributed to an increase in precision, by approximately 2.2 percentage points.
- Comparing against a large frontier model, **GPT-4.1** achieved an overall Frame F1 of 84.8%. The Q-SFT+RL model, despite being two orders of magnitude smaller in parameter count, slightly surpassed GPT-4.1's overall Frame F1 performance on this benchmark. However, GPT-4.1 exhibited a lower Exact Match score of 35.0%.

**Impact of Query Length (Figure 2, Table 1):**
- The performance gains were observed to scale positively with increasing query lengths.
- For the longest input sequences of **16 frames**, the Frame F1 score for Q-SFT+RL reached 82.7%, representing a 17-point increase over Q-SFT (65.7%).
- On 16-frame inputs, the Exact Match for Q-SFT+RL was 64.5%, marking a substantial improvement of 39.5 points over the baseline and 29.5 points over GPT-4.1 (which scored 35.0% for 16-frame Exact Match).
- While SFT accounted for the majority of the initial improvement, RL further contributed approximately 7.9 points to the Exact Match score for 16-frame inputs.

**Per-Query Type Performance (Table 2):**
- **Sequence Queries**: Q-SFT improved F1 by 0.35, and Q-SFT+RL achieved an F1 of 0.962 and an Exact Match of 0.821, with recall exceeding 0.97.
- **Spatial Queries**: SFT increased F1 by 0.35, and Q-SFT+RL reached an F1 of 0.819 and an Exact Match of 0.552.
- **Temporal Queries**: F1 improved by 0.31 with SFT, and Q-SFT+RL achieved an F1 of 0.866 and an Exact Match of 0.615, with recall above 0.90.
- **Metric Queries**: F1 increased by 0.31 with SFT, and Q-SFT+RL recorded an F1 of 0.903 and an Exact Match of 0.706, with recall above 0.96.
- **Existential Queries**: F1 improved by 0.28 with SFT, and Q-SFT+RL reached an F1 of 0.828 and an Exact Match of 0.531, with recall exceeding 0.93.
- RL consistently improved recall across Sequence, Metric, and Existential queries, pushing them above 0.90. F1 scores for Sequence, Metric, and Temporal queries reached above 0.90 after RL. Spatial and Existential queries maintained F1 scores in the 0.82–0.83 range with RL.

In summary, the combined query-explanation–Reinforcement Learning pipeline consistently delivered improvements across various query complexities and frame lengths. The fine-tuned 3-billion-parameter model demonstrated competitive performance against GPT-4.1 on complex spatio-temporal reasoning tasks, with a significantly smaller model footprint.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that contributes to the field of embodied AI and large language models by addressing the challenge of robust spatio-temporal reasoning.

**Significance of the Work:**
- **Automated Verifiable Supervision**: The introduction of the FESTS framework provides a method for generating vast quantities of self-verifiable training data for spatio-temporal reasoning. This is significant because it bypasses the manual and often inconsistent process of crowd-sourced labeling, offering a scalable solution for complex tasks where ground truth can be formally defined. The dataset is derived from logically-grounded queries, ensuring the veracity of the labels.
- **Enhanced LLM Capabilities**: The empirical results demonstrate that a relatively small LLM (3-billion parameters) can achieve spatio-temporal reasoning performance (87.5% Frame F1) that is competitive with, and in some metrics, exceeds, a much larger state-of-the-art model like GPT-4.1 (84.8% Frame F1) on the proposed benchmark. This suggests that the quality and nature of the supervision are critical for specialized reasoning abilities.
- **Efficiency for Embodied AI**: Achieving high performance with a significantly smaller model has practical implications for embodied AI applications (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving) where computational resources and latency are often constrained. It enables the integration of sophisticated spatio-temporal intelligence into systems that might otherwise be limited by the size and cost of larger models.
- **Advancement in Pattern Matching Language**: The extension of SpRE with universal and existential quantifiers is a notable technical advancement. This enhancement allows for more expressive and nuanced queries, enabling persistent object tracking and the formulation of complex logical conditions that are crucial for understanding dynamic scenes.
- **Explainable Reasoning**: The framework's ability to produce natural language explanations for matched patterns contributes to the goal of explainable AI. These explanations can be leveraged during training (e.g., chain-of-thought prompting) to further refine model reasoning or provided directly to users for transparency and debugging.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems**: The improved spatio-temporal reasoning abilities of LLMs can directly benefit robotic systems and autonomous vehicles. This could lead to more accurate perception, better decision-making in dynamic environments, and more robust task planning, such as understanding complex interactions between vehicles and pedestrians.
- **Video Understanding and Annotation**: The framework can be applied to enhance Video LLMs by providing them with deeper spatio-temporal understanding beyond simple event detection or captioning. This could enable models to precisely locate, track, and explain intricate event sequences within video streams, potentially transforming video annotation processes.
- **Synthetic Data Generation for Research**: The methodology offers a blueprint for generating synthetic, high-fidelity datasets for a range of challenging reasoning tasks, thereby accelerating research and development in areas where real-world annotated data is scarce or difficult to obtain.
- **Development of Specialized LLMs**: This work supports the notion that focused, logically-grounded fine-tuning can imbue smaller LLMs with expert reasoning capabilities, paving the way for more efficient, task-specific models that do not require the computational overhead of general-purpose foundation models.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. The current framework lacks an automated component for translating natural language queries into their SpRE counterparts, requiring manual query curation.
2. The perception stream strictly requires pre-labeled data (e.g., object bounding boxes) as its input, although the subsequent spatio-temporal supervision is generated automatically.
3. The accuracy of the results is dependent on the quality of these sourced base labels.
4. The evaluation was conducted on a single LLM model (Qwen-3B).
5. Despite overall performance matching GPT-4.1, the generalization capabilities of the fine-tuned model still trail GPT-4.1, particularly for Existential and Spatial queries.

Future work includes developing methods for synthetic generation of queries and perception streams to improve generalizability, performing additional comparisons against a wider range of model configurations, and incorporating other spatial information such as point clouds or depth maps. These future directions aim to further generalize the framework's applicability and enhance its reasoning capabilities across more diverse data modalities.
