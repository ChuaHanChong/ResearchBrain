# How Should Vision-Language-Action Models Use Proprioceptive State?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and The Chinese University of Hong Kong conducted a controlled empirical study to establish design principles for integrating proprioceptive state into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The study found that short state histories (e.g., 8 frames) generally improve performance, with single-frame state best injected into the VLM and multi-frame history routed to the action head, achieving up to a 10.8 percentage point increase in success rate on composite tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack a standardized approach for integrating robot proprioceptive state, leading to varied and ad-hoc implementations.
- The impact of different proprioceptive state integration designs (representation, injection level, temporal scope) is confounded by other VLA system components, hindering direct empirical comparison.
- Proprioceptive state remains an underexplored input modality in VLAs, unlike vision and language, which have benefited from extensive pretraining.

## Method

- A controlled empirical study was conducted using a fixed **\pi_{0.5}** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model as a testbed to isolate design choices.
- The study systematically varied three aspects of proprioceptive state integration: ==representation (discrete vs. continuous)==, history length (1 to 96 frames), and ==injection site (VLM-side vs. action-side interfaces)==.
- A comprehensive evaluation protocol using atomic and composite tasks in the RoboCasa365 simulation platform was employed, including "no-state" and ==\"slot-matched repeat-current\"== baselines.

## Results

- All tested proprioceptive state interfaces improved atomic task success rates over a **54.6%** no-state baseline, with the "State Prompt" interface reaching **57.7%** (**+3.1**) for single-frame state.
- An eight-frame history consistently delivered performance improvements, enhancing composite task success rates by up to **10.8 percentage points** for action-side interfaces with EEF-pose state.
- For single-frame state, VLM-side injection showed superior performance on composite tasks (**34.4%** success with vp1), while multi-frame history was most effective when injected action-side, with ap8 achieving **39.0%** success on composite tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning on current proprioceptive state generally improves closed-loop control, with short state histories (e.g., 8 frames) providing consistent gains.
> - The optimal injection route for state is context-dependent: single-frame state is best integrated into the VLM, whereas multi-frame state history is more effective when routed to the action head.
> - Performance improvements from incorporating state history are attributed to its genuine temporal content, rather than merely increased conditioning capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have enabled robots to perform numerous manipulation tasks, generalizing across varied objects and scenes. These models are often inspired by biological motor control, where proprioception (e.g., joint angles, end-effector pose, gripper aperture) plays a crucial role in producing smooth, coordinated, and robust movements. Consequently, integrating robot proprioceptive state into VLA models is a common practice.

However, the field currently lacks a consensus on how this integration should be optimally performed. Unlike vision and language, which benefit from extensive pretraining, proprioceptive state in robotics has not undergone similar large-scale pretraining, leading to its status as a less understood input modality in VLAs. This absence of consensus has resulted in a diverse range of ad-hoc approaches across different VLA systems. These approaches vary significantly in:
1. **Representation:** Some models discretize state into text tokens (e.g., $\pi_{0.5}$), while others project it into continuous embeddings (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T N1).
2. **Injection Level:** State information is either fed into the Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone for multimodal context modeling or directly into the action generation module.
3. **Temporal Level:** The majority of existing methods utilize only the current state frame, with few exploring the benefits of state history.

A significant challenge in understanding the impact of these design choices is their entanglement with other system components, such as the VLA backbone architecture, pretraining strategies, specific datasets, and action representations. This entanglement makes direct, controlled comparisons across different state integration methods difficult, leading to a conflation of effects where observed performance differences cannot be solely attributed to the state integration design itself.

This paper addresses this gap by conducting a controlled empirical study. It aims to systematically disentangle the effects of different state integration designs by fixing other system components and varying only how proprioceptive state is represented, where it is injected, and how much history is considered. By providing a systematic comparison and deriving testable design principles, this work contributes to standardizing and optimizing the use of proprioceptive state in future VLA models, moving beyond ad-hoc solutions to a more evidence-based approach. The methodology also aligns with a growing trend in VLA research to isolate and empirically study the impact of individual architectural components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate and establish design principles for how Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models should incorporate robot proprioceptive state.

The motivation for this investigation stems from several factors:
- **Biological Inspiration and Expected Benefits:** Proprioception is critical for motor control in biological systems, implying a similar potential for improving robot control by enabling smoother movements, more stable motion phases, and precise contact-rich manipulation.
- **Underexplored Input Modality:** Despite its perceived importance, proprioceptive state remains the least examined and understood input in current VLA models. It lacks the large-scale pretraining that has benefited visual and linguistic modalities.
- **Lack of Standardization:** The absence of a clear understanding has led to a wide array of incompatible methods for integrating proprioceptive state into VLAs. These methods differ in how state is represented (e.g., discrete text tokens vs. continuous embeddings), where it is injected into the model (e.g., VLM backbone vs. action expert), and its temporal scope (e.g., current frame only vs. historical sequence).
- **Confounding Factors in Existing Work:** Current VLA research often embeds specific proprioceptive state integration designs within distinct backbones, pretraining strategies, datasets, and action representations. This entanglement makes it challenging to isolate the direct impact of proprioceptive state design choices on model performance. Without controlled experiments, it is difficult to determine which specific aspects of state integration genuinely improve decision-making.

To address these motivations, the study set out to answer three specific research questions:
1. **State Utility and Representation (RQ1):** Does conditioning on the current proprioceptive state actually enhance closed-loop control, and if so, on which tasks and through which representation (discrete or continuous)?
2. **Temporal Depth (RQ2):** What is the optimal length of state history that improves performance, and does this benefit derive from genuine temporal variation in the state or merely from increased conditioning capacity?
3. **Injection Route (RQ3):** Where should proprioceptive state be introduced into the VLA model architecture—specifically, into the vision-language backbone or directly into the action-generation module—and does this preferred route change with the amount of temporal context provided?

By systematically addressing these questions, the authors aim to provide actionable, evidence-based guidelines for designing state-aware VLA models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a controlled experimental analysis using a representative flow-matching VLA model as a testbed. The methodology is structured to isolate the effects of proprioceptive state integration design choices by keeping other model components constant.

#### 3.1. Base VLA Architecture

The base policy used for all experiments is $\pi_{0.5}$ (Intelligence et al. 2025), which consists of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) coupled with a flow-matching action expert. This setup ensures a fixed backbone network, training data, action representation, and evaluation protocol across all comparisons.

#### 3.2. Proprioceptive State Definition

Each raw state frame ($s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$) comprises the end-effector position and quaternion, the mobile-base position and quaternion in the world frame, and two gripper joint positions. The term "proprioceptive state" is used, although the inclusion of base pose incorporates world-frame localization information.

#### 3.3. Controlled Variables

The study systematically varies three key aspects of state integration:
1. **State Representation:**
 - **Discrete:** Current state is quantized into 256 bins per dimension and serialized into text tokens through the existing tokenizer (approximately 66 prompt tokens). This method adds no new trainable parameters for state encoding.
 - **Continuous:** Each state frame is independently processed through a two-layer projector (32 dimensions $\rightarrow$ host module hidden width $\rightarrow$ host module hidden width) to form a continuous state token. The projector parameters are shared across frames and history depths.
2. **History Length (K):** State history length is swept from 1 (current frame only) to 96 frames to assess the impact of temporal context.
3. **Injection Site / State Interfaces:** Five distinct interfaces, categorized by their injection point and mechanism, were implemented:
 - **State Prompt (sp):** Discrete state tokens (K=1 only) are appended to the language prompt in the VLM input, leveraging its native text entry. This is a VLM-side interface.
 - **VLM Prefix (vp):** Continuous state tokens are inserted into the bidirectional VLM prefix, after image and language tokens. State information influences multimodal context modeling and then indirectly conditions action generation. This is a VLM-side interface.
 - **Action Prefix (ap):** Continuous state tokens are placed directly in the causal action suffix, preceding noisy action tokens. This allows state to participate directly in the action expert's velocity-field prediction at each denoising step. This is an action-side interface.
 - **State Expert (se):** A dedicated state-processing stream, consisting of its own transformer stack, is added parallel to the VLM and action expert. State information exchanges with the action module during generation, representing a high-capacity, action-side design.
 - **Feature Modulation (fm):** State is maintained as a separate conditioning memory. Each layer of the action expert reads this memory via cross-attention and predicts per-feature scale ($\gamma$) and shift ($\beta$) parameters to modulate action features. This is an action-side interface.

#### 3.4. Experimental Protocol

- **Benchmark:** All experiments were conducted in the RoboCasa365 simulation platform, which provides diverse kitchen scenes, robot configurations, and manipulation tasks. Visual inputs consist of three camera views, and language instructions are used.
- **Atomic Benchmark:** 45 tasks were pre-partitioned into three families based on dominant manipulation semantics:
 - **A (Rearrangement/Pick-and-Place):** Tests large-range end-effector and mobile-base positioning.
 - **B (Articulated-Object Interaction):** Tests sustained contact and motion-phase modeling.
 - **C (Precision):** Tests tasks requiring high local precision (e.g., knob/switch control).
 Each family utilized a separate "category expert." Evaluation involved 50 closed-loop rollouts per task.
- **Composite Benchmark:** 20 task types from RoboCasa365's lifelong\_learning\_phase2, involving multi-stage behaviors (2-3 chained atomic subgoals). A single policy was trained jointly on all 20 types. Evaluation involved 25 closed-loop rollouts per task.
 - The composite task evaluations were performed with the default end-effector-pose state representation, and also repeated with joint-angle state representation (while maintaining end-effector delta action space) to assess robustness to coordinate system choice.
- **Metrics:** The primary metric was closed-loop task success rate (SR). For composite tasks, success required completing all constituent subgoals.
- **Controls:**
 - A "no-state" baseline was used for comparison.
 - A "slot-matched repeat-current" control was implemented for history length comparisons. This involved feeding the current state repeatedly for K slots, allowing the isolation of benefits from genuine temporal content versus merely added conditioning capacity.
 - All models were initialized from the same pretrained $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint and fine-tuned under a shared optimization recipe, ensuring matched data pipelines, action representations, learning-rate schedules, and training budgets.
 - Fixed random seeds were used for training and evaluation to control environmental randomness.
- **Probes and Case Study (Appendices):**
 - **Probe Experiments (Appendix A):** Fixed-checkpoint probes were used to trace data flow, measuring language-to-image attention redistribution and image-token representation changes for VLM-side state, and correction direction/magnitude for action-flow probes.
 - **Case Study (Appendix B):** A detailed analysis of the "PrepareToast" composite task was conducted, comparing $\text{ap}_1$ and $\text{ap}_8$ policies and using a fixed-policy counterfactual to gauge sensitivity to temporal variation at different task stages.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were organized around three core research questions, systematically comparing a no-state baseline, five state interfaces, and history lengths from 1 to 96 frames within the fixed $\pi_{0.5}$ framework.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Does Current State Improve Control, and How Do Interfaces Perform?

- **Overall Benefit:** Conditioning on current proprioceptive state generally improved closed-loop control. The no-state model achieved a macro success rate of 54.6% on atomic tasks. All five state interfaces showed improved point estimates, ranging from +1.1 ($\text{ap}_1$) to +3.1 ($\text{sp}_1$). Notably, $\text{sp}_1$ achieved 57.7%, with a paired task-bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [0.2, 6.1], indicating a statistically significant gain.
- **Task-Agnostic Best Interface:** No single interface was universally optimal across all task families.
 - **Family A (Rearrangement):** $\text{sp}_1$ showed the highest performance (68.7%, +7.0 over no-state).
 - **Family B (Articulated-Object Interaction):** $\text{vp}_1$ led (68.8%, +6.1), with $\text{se}_1$ and $\text{fm}_1$ performing similarly (+5.8 and +5.5).
 - **Family C (Precision):** $\text{se}_1$ achieved the highest success (42.8%, +3.3), while $\text{vp}_1$ showed a slight degradation (-1.2).
- **Computational Cost:** Interfaces differed significantly in computational cost. $\text{sp}_1$ was the most expensive (approximately 1114 training GFLOPs/sample), primarily due to serializing state into numerous VLM prompt tokens. Continuous interfaces were substantially cheaper; $\text{se}_1$ and $\text{fm}_1$ achieved comparable success rates to $\text{sp}_1$ with a fraction of the marginal compute, making them more efficient for scaling.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Is State History Useful, and How Much Is Needed?

- **Non-Monotonic Benefit:** On atomic tasks, short state histories improved performance over single-frame models. However, extending to deeper, uncompressed histories beyond a certain point provided no further benefit and, in some cases, degraded control. This degradation was particularly evident in Family C (precision tasks) with long histories.
- **Eight-Frame History:** An eight-frame history ($\text{K}=8$) emerged as a practical operating point, consistently providing strong and reliable gains across the atomic task sweep, balancing temporal information with context redundancy.
- **Transfer to Composite Tasks:** The benefit of short history extended to the more complex, longer-horizon composite tasks. Extending state input from a single frame to an eight-frame history improved overall performance for most interfaces, with the most pronounced benefit observed in action-side interfaces (e.g., $\text{ap}_{1 \to 8}$ showed a +10.8 percentage point increase with EEF-pose state).
- **Genuine Temporal Content:** A "slot-matched repeat-current" control, which provided the same number of conditioning slots but without genuine temporal variation, performed significantly worse than models with actual ordered history (e.g., $\text{ap}$ with genuine history gained +8.2 points over repeat-current for composite tasks, with the confidence interval excluding zero). This confirms that performance gains arise from the temporal information within the state sequence, not merely from increased conditioning capacity.
- **Robustness to State Representation:** The findings regarding the utility of short history were robust to the choice of state representation (end-effector-pose vs. joint-angle states), indicating that this is a secondary factor compared to history depth and injection location.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Where Should State Enter?

- **Single Frame (K=1): VLM-Side Preference:** For composite tasks, which demand stage tracking, VLM-side injection ($\text{vp}_1$) was the superior choice, outperforming $\text{ap}_1$ (e.g., 34.4% vs. 28.2% for EEF-pose state). On atomic tasks, the preference was less distinct.
- **Short History (K=8): Action-Side Advantage:** With an eight-frame history, the advantage shifted decisively to the action side. The performance gains from K=1 to K=8 were largest when history directly conditioned the action head ($\text{ap}$), while VLM-prefix injection with history ($\text{vp}$) showed smaller or negative gains. For K=8, $\text{ap}_8$ consistently performed best across all evaluations (59.6% atomic, 39.0% composite).
- **Routing Rule:** The results suggest a design rule: inject single-frame state into the VLM, but route multi-frame state history to the action head.
- **Probe Experiments (Appendix A):** Fixed-checkpoint probes supported a data-flow interpretation. VLM-side state visibly altered multimodal context early in the processing, while action-side state exerted its influence directly within the action expert, with temporal action-side state (ap) showing stronger alignment of its correction with the expert residual.
- **Case Study (Appendix B):** A case study on the "PrepareToast" task showed that $\text{ap}_8$'s performance advantage over $\text{ap}_1$ appeared specifically during the late-stage transition (e.g., closing a cabinet after placing items). Furthermore, the fixed $\text{ap}_8$ policy demonstrated the highest sensitivity to *genuine temporal variation* around native task progress boundaries, suggesting active use of history during crucial transitions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic and empirically grounded understanding of how proprioceptive state should be integrated into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a component that has been largely underexplored and inconsistently applied in the field. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Systematic Design Guidance:** The study moves beyond anecdotal evidence and ad-hoc implementations by providing a controlled experimental analysis. It decomposes the complex problem of state integration into measurable design axes (representation, temporal depth, injection route) and offers concrete, testable design principles. This systematic approach contributes to more rigorous VLA research and development.
- **Optimized VLA Performance:** The findings offer actionable insights for VLA model architects to make informed decisions, potentially leading to the development of more robust, precise, and efficient robotic control policies. For instance, the identification of a "short-window peak" for state history and the dynamic optimal injection route (VLM-side for single frame, action-side for history) can directly inform architectural choices.
- **Resource-Aware Model Design:** The analysis of computational costs for different interfaces highlights practical considerations for deployment. Identifying effective interfaces like $\text{se}_1$ and $\text{fm}_1$ that offer strong performance at a lower computational cost than, for example, $\text{sp}_1$, is valuable for real-world applications where compute resources may be constrained.
- **Reusable Evaluation Protocol:** The research establishes a comprehensive and reusable evaluation protocol, including task partitioning (rearrangement, articulation, precision), paired comparisons, and slot-matched controls. This framework can serve as a standard for auditing future state-aware VLAs, fostering consistency and comparability across studies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By delineating the current understanding and providing a robust experimental framework, the paper lays a foundation for future investigations. The acknowledged limitations—absence of real-robot validation and focus on kinematic state—directly point to important avenues for subsequent work. Extending the protocol to physical robots and incorporating other sensing modalities (e.g., force, tactile) would build upon this study's findings, moving towards more comprehensive embodied intelligence. The detailed analysis of *why* certain strategies work (e.g., through probe experiments and case studies) also encourages deeper mechanistic understanding in future VLA research.
