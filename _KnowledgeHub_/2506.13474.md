# Language Agents for Hypothesis-driven Clinical Decision Making with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A language agent system, LA-CDM, developed at Technical University of Munich and Munich Center for Machine Learning, enhances clinical diagnostic decision-making by iteratively requesting and interpreting diagnostic tests. This system, trained with a hybrid supervised and reinforcement learning approach, achieved an 81.3% mean accuracy and reduced average test costs to $1295.61 on the MIMIC-CDM dataset, while also improving confidence calibration.

## Problem

- Current LLM applications in medical diagnosis often assume immediate availability of all patient data, which is unrealistic in real-world clinical settings where information is uncovered incrementally.
- Existing LLM-based diagnostic methods typically rely on out-of-the-box LLM capabilities without specific fine-tuning for the complexities of iterative diagnostic decision-making, leading to suboptimal performance and efficiency.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for cost-efficient clinical decision-making have primarily focused on tabular data, limiting their ability to integrate rich textual information from clinical notes and imaging reports crucial for comprehensive diagnosis.

## Method

- Introduced **LA-CDM**, a two-agent system consisting of a ==hypothesis agent== and a decision agent, both sharing underlying Large Language Model (LLM) weights, to model iterative, hypothesis-driven diagnosis.
- Designed an RL environment that simulates a patient-doctor interaction, enabling step-wise information gathering and action selection, where test results are incrementally added to the patient's state.
- Employed a cyclic hybrid training paradigm combining supervised fine-tuning for accurate hypothesis generation and reinforcement learning (using GRPO) for uncertainty estimation and efficient, cost-aware action selection.

## Results

- Achieved an **81.3%** mean diagnostic accuracy and an **84.1%** micro F1-score on the MIMIC-CDM dataset, outperforming zero-shot baselines like OASST by nearly 30 percentage points in mean accuracy.
- Reduced average diagnostic test costs to 1295.61perpatient,demonstratingsignificantlygreaterefficiencycomparedtoasupervisedmodelusingalldata(1295.61 per patient, demonstrating significantly greater efficiency compared to a supervised model using all data (1295.61perpatient,demonstratingsignificantlygreaterefficiencycomparedtoasupervisedmodelusingalldata(3792.79) and the ReAct baseline ($1480.32).
- Improved confidence calibration, with the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) decreasing from **0.069** (untrained) to **0.037** (trained), indicating better alignment between the model's predicted confidences and actual correctness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training LLMs for iterative, hypothesis-driven decision-making with uncertainty awareness significantly enhances both diagnostic performance and efficiency in complex medical contexts.
> - The integration of reinforcement learning enables the model to learn optimal, cost-efficient test sequences that are challenging to capture with purely supervised methods, mimicking how human clinicians gain experience.
> - A two-agent architecture, separating the tasks of hypothesis generation and confidence estimation from clinical action selection (requesting tests or diagnosing), proves more effective for complex sequential diagnostic tasks.
