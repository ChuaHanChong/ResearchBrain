# MathCoder-VL: Bridging Vision and Code for Enhanced Multimodal Mathematical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CUHK introduce MathCoder-VL, a multimodal large language model that leverages code supervision and synthesized data to enhance mathematical reasoning capabilities, achieving state-of-the-art performance across mathematical benchmarks while outperforming GPT-4V on geometry tasks through a novel two-stage training approach combining image-to-code training and mathematical instruction fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Large multimodal models struggle with mathematical reasoning tasks that require integrating visual and textual information
- Existing datasets lack high-quality, math-specific cross-modal data needed for effective training
- Existing image-caption datasets lack the precision and detail required for solving mathematical problems, providing weak cross-modal alignment for visual reasoning

## Method

- Developed ==ImgCode-8.6M dataset== using ==model-in-the-loop== approach to translate math images into executable code
- Created ==MM-MathInstruct-3M dataset== combining collected K12 problems with synthetically generated figures and solutions
- Implemented ==two-stage training process== with ==image-to-code mid-training== followed by ==mathematical instruction fine-tuning==

## Results

- Outperformed GPT-4o by a significant margin on plane geometry tasks and on multi-step (two-step and three-step) problems from the We-Math benchmark
- Demonstrated competitive results against closed-source models like GPT-4-turbo and Claude3.5-Sonnet
- Successfully generated diverse new math figures and problems through the FigCodifier pipeline

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Code supervision provides precise alignment between visual and textual modalities
> - Combining real and synthetic data with verified solutions improves model performance
> - Two-stage training approach enables effective learning of both visual-code relationships and mathematical reasoning

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work fits into the rapidly evolving field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and specifically targets the challenging area of multimodal mathematical reasoning. Several key trends and related works provide context:

- **LMMs and their Limitations:** The paper acknowledges the recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), which have demonstrated human-level performance in various reasoning tasks. However, it highlights that LMMs still lag behind in mathematical capabilities, particularly in tasks requiring the integration of visual and textual information.
- **Importance of Multimodal Mathematical Reasoning:** The ability to reason mathematically with visual inputs is crucial for many real-world applications, including education, scientific research, and engineering. Geometry problem-solving, as emphasized in the paper, is a prime example of such a task.
- **Data Scarcity and Quality:** A major challenge in training effective LMMs for mathematical reasoning is the scarcity of high-quality, math-specific cross-modal data. Existing image-caption datasets often lack the precision and detail required for solving mathematical problems.
- **Code as Supervision:** The paper introduces the novel idea of leveraging code as supervision for cross-modal alignment. This approach is motivated by the fact that code inherently encodes all the information needed to generate corresponding figures, establishing a precise connection between the two modalities.
- **Data Synthesis Techniques:** The paper builds upon existing research on data synthesis, which has proven effective for improving the reasoning abilities of LLMs. However, it goes beyond traditional methods by generating both new questions and diverse new images, addressing the limitations of existing multimodal math datasets.

The paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical gap in high-quality, math-specific cross-modal data and proposing a novel solution based on code supervision and data synthesis. It leverages recent advancements in LMMs while acknowledging their limitations in mathematical reasoning and proposes a targeted approach to overcome these limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives and motivation of this research are:

- **Enhance the mathematical capabilities of LMMs:** The primary goal is to improve the ability of LMMs to solve mathematical problems that require integrating visual and textual information.
- **Address the scarcity of high-quality, math-specific cross-modal data:** A major motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing image-caption datasets, which often lack the precision and detail required for mathematical reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MathCoder-VL is developed through a two-stage process:

1. **Image-to-Code Mid-Training:**

 - **Dataset:** The researchers construct ImgCode-8.6M, a large image-code dataset, using a model-in-the-loop approach. They start with a seed dataset (DaTikZ-119K) and iteratively train an image-to-code model (FigCodifier) to translate math-related images into code.
 - **Image Collection:** They collect 3 million math-related images from various sources, including DaTikZ, K12 problem-solving datasets, mathematical textbooks, arXiv, and open-source datasets.
 - **Code Generation:** The image-to-code model translates the collected images into code, which is then executed to render new images. Only successfully generated image-code pairs are included in the dataset.
 - **Code Type:** They use TiKZ and Python. When TikZ code is available, they use GPT-4o mini to convert to Python.
 - **Model Architecture:** They start with InternVL-Chat-V1-2-40B and later adopt InternVL2-8B as the base model.
2. **Math Instruction Fine-Tuning:**

 - **Dataset:** The researchers create MM-MathInstruct-3M, a high-quality multimodal math instruction fine-tuning dataset.
 - **K12-2M Dataset:** They collect 4.6 million math problems with simple solutions and convert equations into LaTeX text.
 - **Synthetic Math Data:** They use the FigCodifier to generate new math figures from the images in the K12-2M dataset. They then use Qwen models to craft math reasoning questions and generate step-by-step solutions based on the newly synthesized images.
 - **Solution Verification:** Solutions are retained only if both Qwen models produce consistent answers.
3. **Training:**

 - Both the vision encoder and MLP projector are trainable during the image-to-code mid-training. The LLM backbone is frozen.
 - The entire model is fine-tuned during the math instruction fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the paper are:

- **MathCoder-VL achieves state-of-the-art performance:** The MathCoder-VL model achieves new state-of-the-art results across several mathematical benchmarks among comparable-size LMMs.
- **Significant improvements over the base model:** The MathCoder-VL model shows notable improvements over its base model, InternVL2-8B, on benchmarks such as MATH-Vision, MathVerse, and MathVista (GPS).
- **Competitive with closed-source models:** The MathCoder-VL model remains competitive with several proprietary models, outperforming GPT-4V and even exceeding GPT-4-turbo and Claude3.5-Sonnet in certain areas.
- **Strong performance on multi-step problems:** The MathCoder-VL model exhibits robust performance on multi-step problems, outperforming GPT-4o on both two-step and three-step problems on We-Math.
- **Outstanding ability in geometry:** The MathCoder-VL model excels in geometry, achieving an impressive average score on the plane geometry subsets from MATH-Vision, surpassing GPT-4o by a significant margin.
- **Image-to-code mid-training and math instruction fine-tuning are effective:** Ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of both image-to-code mid-training and math instruction fine-tuning in improving the model's mathematical reasoning abilities.

The MathCoder-VL project has significant potential impact in several areas:
