# AutoOdom: Learning Auto-regressive Proprioceptive Odometry for Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoOdom is a proprioceptive odometry system for legged robots that employs a two-stage training paradigm and an autoregressive enhancement mechanism. This method effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap by combining large-scale simulation data with limited real-world fine-tuning, leading to a 59.2% improvement in Absolute Trajectory Error (Umeyama alignment) over prior state-of-the-art on a humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Traditional proprioceptive odometry methods suffer from cumulative drift, modeling uncertainties, and require extensive manual tuning for complex legged robot dynamics.
- Pure learning-based odometry methods face a significant 'sim-to-real' gap and require substantial real-world data, hindering their practical deployment and generalization.
- Existing odometry systems often struggle with accuracy during rapid robot motions or in visually degraded environments where visual or LiDAR sensing is compromised.

## Method

- **AutoOdom** introduces a two-stage learning framework: initial pre-training on large-scale simulation data, followed by fine-tuning with limited real-world data.
- It implements an autoregressive training mechanism during real-world fine-tuning, feeding the model's own previous predictions back into the input sequence for subsequent time steps.
- The system uses an efficient MLP-based network architecture to process multi-modal proprioceptive sensor inputs (actions, velocities, joint states, rotation, incremental position change).

## Results

- AutoOdom achieved superior accuracy, with a **59.2%** improvement in Absolute Trajectory Error (Umeyama alignment) and a **36.2%** improvement in Relative Pose Error compared to the state-of-the-art Legolas method.
- The complete two-stage training framework demonstrated significantly better performance than single-stage training approaches, validating the design choice of combining simulation pre-training with real-world fine-tuning.
- The MLP network architecture provided comparable accuracy to LSTM while offering superior computational efficiency and lower inference latency, making it practical for real-time deployment on humanoid robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A two-stage training strategy effectively leverages abundant simulation data for initial learning of complex dynamics and then adapts to real-world conditions with minimal real-world data, successfully bridging the sim-to-real gap.
> - The autoregressive feedback loop is crucial for the model to learn robust predictions that inherently account for accumulated errors and enhance resilience against sensor noise in dynamic environments.
> - Optimal sensor input configurations vary between training stages; specifically, excluding simulated linear acceleration in Stage 1 but reintroducing real IMU acceleration in Stage 2 improves overall performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate state estimation, particularly odometry, is paramount for autonomous navigation and control of legged robots, especially in environments where traditional global positioning systems (GPS) are unavailable or unreliable, and visual sensing is compromised. The paper highlights that conventional visual odometry (VO) systems, while often precise, struggle during rapid motions (e.g., jumping, sharp turns) or in visually degraded conditions (e.g., motion blur, poor lighting), making proprioceptive odometry—using only onboard inertial and joint sensors—a critical alternative.

The broader research landscape of proprioceptive odometry for legged robots can be categorized into three main paradigms:

- **Analytical Filtering Methods:** These traditional approaches, such as Extended Kalman Filters (EKF) and Unscented Kalman Filters (UKF), fuse IMU data with leg kinematics, often relying on assumptions like zero-velocity at ground contact points. While providing a theoretical foundation, they suffer from significant limitations:
 - **Modeling Uncertainties:** Complex nonlinear dynamics, rapidly changing contact states, and foot-ground interactions are difficult to model accurately.
 - **Cumulative Drift:** Sensor noise and model inaccuracies lead to significant cumulative position drift, often exceeding 10% of the traveled distance.
 - **Extensive Tuning:** They require laborious manual parameter tuning for different robots and environments.
 - **Linearization Assumptions:** They struggle with the inherent nonlinearities of legged locomotion.

- **Hybrid Learning-Filtering Approaches:** These methods attempt to combine the strengths of analytical filters with the representational power of deep learning. Neural networks are often used to predict displacement corrections, contact states, or filter parameters, aiming to mitigate unmodeled uncertainties. While offering improvements, they remain constrained by the analytical backbone, still inheriting some of the inherent defects of their filtering components. Examples like TLIO use neural networks to regress 3D displacement and uncertainty estimates for joint pose, velocity, and IMU bias estimation.

- **Pure Learning-Based Methods:** These approaches directly learn complex motion patterns from proprioceptive data, bypassing explicit kinematic or dynamic models. They offer significant potential for handling nonlinear dynamics and avoiding continuous error propagation by using window-based formulations. IONet and Legolas are prominent examples, showing superior performance across diverse motion patterns. However, their main drawbacks include:
 - **Massive Data Requirements:** They demand extensive training data, which is time-consuming and resource-intensive to collect in diverse real-world scenarios.
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Training predominantly on simulated data often leads to a significant performance degradation when deployed on physical robots due to discrepancies between simulated and real-world sensor characteristics and dynamics.
 - **Generalization Issues:** They can struggle with generalization to unseen terrains or handling sensor biases.

AutoOdom positions itself at the forefront of pure learning-based methods, specifically addressing the critical "sim-to-real gap" and robustness challenges that have hindered their practical deployment. By introducing a novel two-stage training paradigm and an autoregressive enhancement mechanism, AutoOdom aims to combine the data efficiency and robustness of simulation-based training with the accuracy and noise resilience required for real-world application, thereby pushing the boundaries of reliable proprioceptive odometry for dynamic legged locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AutoOdom is to develop a highly accurate and robust proprioceptive odometry system for legged robots that can reliably operate in GPS-denied and visually degraded environments. The authors are specifically motivated to overcome the critical limitations faced by existing approaches:

- **Addressing Modeling Uncertainties and Cumulative Drift:** For analytical methods, AutoOdom aims to eliminate the reliance on explicit kinematic and contact models, thereby reducing cumulative drift and the need for extensive manual tuning.
- **Mitigating Constraints of Hybrid Methods:** By developing a pure learning-based approach, AutoOdom seeks to move beyond the inherent limitations imposed by analytical components in hybrid systems.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** A central motivation is to overcome the significant challenge of transferring models trained in simulation to real-world deployment, which often undermines accuracy for pure learning methods. This involves learning complex nonlinear dynamics from large-scale simulation data while effectively adapting to real-world conditions.
- **Enhancing Robustness to Sensor Noise and Dynamic Environments:** Legged locomotion involves rapidly changing contact states and substantial sensor noise. The system aims to be resilient to these factors, maintaining high accuracy across diverse and highly dynamic motion scenarios.
- **Reducing Real-World Data Dependency:** While pure learning methods are data-hungry, AutoOdom seeks a strategy that leverages large amounts of easily accessible simulation data while requiring only limited real-world data for fine-tuning.

In summary, the paper's key objectives are twofold:
1. **Develop a two-stage training scheme:** Integrate large-scale simulation data to learn complex dynamics and rapidly changing contact states, and then use limited real-world data to effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap.
2. **Introduce an autoregressive training mechanism:** Enable the model to learn from its own predictions, fostering resilience against sensor noise and improving robustness in highly dynamic locomotion scenarios.
These objectives converge on creating a precise, noise-resistant, and robust proprioceptive odometry estimator that converges quickly and maintains high accuracy across diverse and challenging robot motions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoOdom employs an innovative two-stage learning framework, integrating multi-modal proprioceptive sensing with a novel autoregressive training mechanism. The system is built upon Booster Gym, an open-source development platform for the Booster T1 robot.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
At each time step `t`, the system predicts the incremental robot motion (`∆p_t+1` ∈ R²) based on a historical window (H=50, corresponding to 1 second at 50Hz) of multi-modal observations `O_t`. The input observation `O_t` includes:
- `A_t` (R¹¹): Current actions/joint commands from the walking policy.
- `v_cmd_t` (R³): Desired linear and angular command velocities.
- `ω_t` (R³): Angular velocity from the gyroscope.
- `a_t` (R³): Linear acceleration measurements (used only in Stage 2).
- `q_t`, `˙q_t` (R¹²): Joint positions and velocities.
- `R_t` (R³ˣ³): Current rotation matrix.
- `∆p_t` (R²): Differential position change from the current position to 1 second ago (ground truth in simulation, model estimation in real-world deployment).

The model `f_θ` (a neural network) maps the historical observation sequence `O_t-H+1:t` to the predicted incremental position change `∆p_t+1`.

**B. Stage 1: Simulation-Based Pre-training:**
This initial stage leverages large-scale simulation data from the Isaac Gym simulator.
- **Data Generation:** Diverse training data is collected across various terrains (obstacles, flat, uneven) and motion patterns by executing parallel robot instances with robust locomotion policies.
- **Input Data:** All components of `O_t` are obtained as ground truth from the simulator. Crucially, `a_t` (linear acceleration) is *not* used in this stage. This is because simulated acceleration, derived from numerical differentiation, exhibits significant discrepancies compared to real IMU sensor measurements, and its inclusion would introduce an unnecessary sim-to-real gap early on.
- **Objective:** The goal of Stage 1 is for the model to learn the fundamental, complex nonlinear dynamics and rapidly changing contact states inherent in legged locomotion without the confounding factors of real-world noise, calibration errors, or hardware limitations.

**C. Stage 2: Real World Autoregressive Enhancement:**
This stage fine-tunes the pre-trained model from Stage 1 using limited real-world data collected from physical robots equipped with motion capture systems. This is the core innovation for bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Model Transfer:** Pre-trained model parameters from Stage 1 are transferred to an expanded network architecture using zero-padding techniques to accommodate new input dimensions.
- **Real-World Acceleration:** Real-world IMU linear acceleration data (`a_t`) is introduced as an additional input component. This is motivated by prior work demonstrating its richness in real-world scenarios, contrasting with its exclusion in simulation.
- **Autoregressive Training Mechanism:** This is the key novelty. Instead of using ground truth `∆p_t` in the input observation `O_t`, the model's *own previous predictions* (`∆p_pred_t`) (computed through cumulative integration of prior estimates) are fed back as input for subsequent time steps. This closed-loop learning process:
 - Forces the model to learn robust predictions that inherently account for accumulated errors.
 - Creates a feedback loop that enhances current input with historical prediction information.
 - Develops resilience to long-term noise relationships.
 - Significantly reduces sensitivity to sensor noise and improves robustness in highly dynamic real-world environments.

**D. Training Objective:**
The training objective uses a mean squared error (MSE) loss function, comparing predicted and ground truth incremental positions:
`L_MSE = (1/N) * Σ ||∆p_j - ∆p_hat_j||²`
where `∆p_j` is the ground truth increment and `∆p_hat_j` is the predicted increment. This relative pose prediction error formulation prevents error propagation to future predictions within the dataset, creating independent data points.

**E. Network Architecture:**
AutoOdom employs fully connected neural networks (`f_θ`) optimized for real-time deployment.
- **Input:** The network maps a multi-modal observation sequence (flattened) to a 2D incremental position prediction.
- **Variants:** Both MLP-based (`f_MLP_θ`) and LSTM-based (`f_LSTM_θ`) designs were implemented and compared.
- **Selection:** Experimental results showed comparable performance between MLP and LSTM. Given its superior computational efficiency and lower inference latency for real-time applications, the MLP architecture was chosen as the final design. The models typically contain between 0.5 million and 2.0 million parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of AutoOdom was conducted on the Booster T1 humanoid robot using real-world trajectories collected in an indoor soccer field environment, leveraging a motion capture system for ground truth.

**A. Quantitative Performance Comparison:**
AutoOdom was compared against Legolas, a state-of-the-art pure learning-based proprioceptive odometry system for quadrupedal robots, which has previously demonstrated superiority over traditional filtering and hybrid methods. The evaluation metrics included:
- **Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE):** `ATE_o` (first-frame alignment) and `ATE_u` (Umeyama alignment) for global accuracy.
- **Relative Pose Error (RPE):** For local accuracy, measuring incremental errors without cumulative drift.

The full AutoOdom system (using an MLP architecture, row 6 in Table I) achieved significantly superior performance:
- **`ATE_o`:** 0.5630 (57.2% improvement over Legolas's 1.3153)
- **`ATE_u`:** 0.2153 (59.2% improvement over Legolas's 0.5281)
- **RPE:** 0.0148 (36.2% improvement over Legolas's 0.0232)

These results unequivocally demonstrate AutoOdom's superior accuracy and robustness in highly dynamic environments compared to the leading baseline.

**B. Ablation Studies and Critical Insights:**

The ablation studies provided critical insights into the contribution of individual components and design choices:

1. **Two-Stage vs. Single-Stage Training (Table I):**
 - Stage 1 alone (simulation pre-training, rows 1-2) provided reasonable performance but was inferior to the full system.
 - Stage 2 alone (real-world training, rows 4-5) also showed the importance of real-world data but did not reach optimal performance.
 - The complete two-stage framework (row 6) achieved optimal results, validating the design philosophy of combining simulation for initial learning with real-world fine-tuning.

2. **Architectural Comparison (MLP vs. LSTM, Table I):**
 - Both MLP and LSTM architectures exhibited nearly identical performance in terms of accuracy metrics (e.g., AutoOdom MLP: `ATE_o` 0.5630, `ATE_u` 0.2153, RPE 0.0148; AutoOdom LSTM: `ATE_o` 0.6196, `ATE_u` 0.2544, RPE 0.0153).
 - Given this comparable accuracy and the necessity for real-time deployment, the MLP architecture was chosen due to its superior computational efficiency and lower inference latency.

3. **Impact of Autoregressive Mechanism (Table I, row 10 vs. row 6):**
 - Removing the autoregressive mechanism from AutoOdom (row 10) significantly degraded performance, especially in `ATE_o` (0.7876 vs. 0.5630) and `ATE_u` (0.2095 vs. 0.2153), though curiously not in RPE. This highlights the autoregressive feedback loop's crucial role in developing resilience against accumulated noise and improving global trajectory alignment.

4. **Input Modality Selection (Table I, row 9 vs. row 6 & Table II):**
 - **IMU Acceleration (`a_t`):** The ablation study revealed a "counterintuitive finding."
 - Excluding `a_t` during Stage 1 training (Table II, row 2) *improved* performance compared to including it (Table II, row 1), with `ATE_o` dropping from 1.4486 to 0.8176. This validates the decision to omit `a_t` in Stage 1 due to sim-to-real discrepancies.
 - However, when `a_t` was *reintroduced* during Stage 2 (real-world autoregressive enhancement), it contributed to the overall superior performance of the full AutoOdom system (Table I, row 6). This emphasizes the importance of stage-specific sensor configuration.
 - **Action History (`A_t`):** Removing action history (Table II, row 5) caused a significant deterioration in `ATE_o` (1.9190), indicating its critical role in predicting robot motion.
 - **Historical Position Data (`∆p_t` in input, Table II, row 3 vs 2):** Removing historical position data (Table II, row 3) also degraded `ATE_o` (1.1113 vs. 0.8176), showing its importance.
 - **Prediction Interval (`∆t`, Table II, row 6 vs 1):** Using a longer prediction interval (1.02s vs 0.02s) significantly worsened all metrics, emphasizing the need for high-frequency predictions.

These findings validate the systematic design choices of AutoOdom, particularly the two-stage training with specific sensor configurations for each stage and the autoregressive enhancement, as critical for robust proprioceptive odometry.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AutoOdom presents a significant advancement in the field of proprioceptive odometry for legged robots, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Reliability in Challenging Environments:** By achieving substantial improvements over state-of-the-art methods (57.2% in `ATE_o`, 59.2% in `ATE_u`, and 36.2% in RPE), AutoOdom offers a highly robust solution for robot navigation in environments where GPS is denied, and visual or LiDAR-based systems are degraded or fail (e.g., dark, smoky, dusty, cluttered, or highly dynamic scenarios). This is crucial for applications in disaster response, exploration of unknown terrains, search and rescue, and industrial inspection.

2. **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The two-stage training paradigm is a major contribution, effectively bridging the notorious sim-to-real gap. This approach maximizes the utility of readily available, large-scale simulation data for initial learning of complex dynamics, while strategically using limited real-world data for fine-tuning. This significantly reduces the time, cost, and complexity of data collection, accelerating research and development in real-world robotics.

3. **Robustness to Noise and Dynamic Motion:** The novel autoregressive training mechanism, where the model learns from its own predictions, instills resilience against sensor noise and improves robustness during highly dynamic locomotion. This is especially important for legged robots undergoing rapid movements, impacts, and complex foot-ground interactions, where conventional methods often struggle with error accumulation.

4. **Practical Deployment on Humanoid Robots:** The successful deployment and validation on the Booster T1 humanoid robot demonstrate the practical applicability of AutoOdom to complex robotic platforms. Humanoids are particularly challenging due to their high degrees of freedom and complex balance requirements. A reliable proprioceptive odometry system is fundamental for their autonomous operation and interaction with the environment.

5. **Insights for Future Research:** The comprehensive ablation studies provide valuable insights into optimal sensor modalities, temporal modeling, and architectural choices for proprioceptive odometry systems. The counterintuitive finding regarding IMU acceleration in simulation vs. real-world stages is particularly important, guiding future researchers in designing more effective training strategies.

6. **Foundation for Advanced Capabilities:** Highly accurate and robust odometry is a foundational component for more advanced robot capabilities such as Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), long-term autonomy, precise manipulation, and human-robot interaction. AutoOdom's performance can pave the way for more reliable and capable legged robots in a wider range of real-world scenarios.

Future work outlined by the authors, including extending to diverse environmental conditions, integrating uncertainty estimation for SLAM, and deployment on additional robotic platforms, underscores the significant potential for AutoOdom to impact the next generation of autonomous legged robots.
