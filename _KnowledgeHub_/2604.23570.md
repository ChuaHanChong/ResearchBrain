# EgoLive: A Large-Scale Egocentric Dataset from Real-World Human Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoLive is a large-scale egocentric dataset for robot manipulation learning, collected via a custom stereo head-mounted device (JoyEgoCam) in unconstrained real-world service scenarios, with an automated pipeline producing 6-DoF motion tracking, segmentation, and 3D reconstruction. It totals 1,680 hours across 65,866 episodes spanning 346 tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robot datasets have restricted environmental diversity and poor extensibility, blocking scalable acquisition of data that captures real-world manipulation complexity.
- Teleoperation and universal manipulation interface pipelines need specialized hardware, intensive human involvement, and are costly, limiting large-scale collection.
- Prior egocentric datasets stay confined to household, laboratory, or tabletop settings, leaving longer-horizon real service and business workflows largely uncovered.

## Method

- **JoyEgoCam**: a custom lightweight head-mounted stereo capture device with 130x130 degree field of view, 60fps 2160x2160 stereo RGB, and 200Hz IMU for minimal-intrusion in-the-wild collection.
- Automated annotation pipeline combining ==HaMeR==/==MANO==-based two-stage hand reconstruction, ==ORB-SLAM3== camera ego-motion, and stereo optimization for consistent 6-DoF motion tracking.
- Semantic and 3D reconstruction modules: detection/tracking/==SAM2== segmentation plus a fine-tuned ==Qwen3-VL-32B== captioner for sub-task instructions, and ==FoundationStereo==-based dense depth reconstruction.

## Results

- EgoLive reaches **1,680** hours, **65,866** episodes, and **346** tasks at 2160x2160/60fps, the largest open-source annotated real-world egocentric manipulation dataset to date.
- Depth reconstruction achieves mean errors of **3.0-8.8mm** within the typical 0.5-1.5m human operation range, with over **99%** of points under **20mm** error at 500-1300mm.
- t-SNE and word-frequency analysis show EgoLive's semantic label distribution lies above EgoDex (**829h**) and Xperience-10M (**1059h**) with longer tails across objects, actions, and attributes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimal-intrusion, low-cost head-mounted stereo hardware can match or exceed VR/glove-based capture in scale while preserving natural human hand morphology.
> - Sub-task captioning that explicitly decomposes hand, object, and action elements yields more consistent and verifiable annotations than free-form natural language description.
> - Deployment-scale in-the-wild collection in real service settings, not just households or labs, is needed to capture the long-tailed diversity generalist robot policies require.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of robot learning, particularly in achieving generalization capabilities for manipulation models, is subject to limitations related to data availability. Contemporary Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown progress, but their full potential is hindered by datasets that often exhibit restricted environmental diversity and limited scalability. These constraints impede the development of embodied systems capable of operating robustly in open-world scenarios.

Egocentric data collection, which involves capturing first-person visual perspectives using head-mounted wearable devices, has emerged as a method to address these data acquisition challenges. This approach is noted for its ability to enable scalable and natural "in-the-wild" data collection, embodying human-like visual perception. Advantages include inspiration from human learning processes, unrestricted movement for in-the-wild capture, and scalability due to relatively simple hardware.

Existing manipulation data collection paradigms include:
- **Real-robot teleoperation**: Provides high-fidelity trajectories but faces scalability issues due to specialized hardware, intensive human involvement, and high costs. Examples include ALOHA, AgiBot, and Tesla Optimus.
- **Universal Manipulation Interfaces (UMI)**: Employ instrumented grippers with cameras to align human-robot perceptual views. While enabling natural interaction and reducing visual domain gaps, these are often tailored to specific robot embodiments and may not easily adapt to dexterous hands. Examples include FastUMI and the Gen series.
- **Human egocentric video collection**: Offers a scalable, minimal-hardware approach for capturing natural interactions. This vision-only method bypasses hardware and spatial constraints of teleoperation and preserves natural human hand morphology better than UMI, supporting dexterous manipulation data collection. Examples include EgoScale, EgoDex, and EgoMimic.

Within human egocentric datasets, three trends are identifiable:
- **Generalist datasets** (e.g., EPIC-KITCHENS-100, Ego4D): Provide large-scale video coverage and language supervision for general egocentric visual understanding, but often lack manipulation-oriented geometric signals such as camera trajectories, 3D hand keypoints, and dense hand/object masks.
- **Manipulation-centric datasets** (e.g., EgoMimic, HOI4D, HOT3D, EgoDex): Focus on hand-object interaction, offering more detailed supervision including camera motion, 3D hand poses, and segmentation masks. However, these are largely collected in controlled environments (e.g., household, laboratory, tabletop settings), limiting their coverage of longer-horizon workflows in real service or business operations.
- **Deployment-scale datasets** (e.g., Egocentric-10K, Xperience-10M): Expand data collection towards greater operational diversity and longer-duration capture in real-world settings, with Xperience-10M providing substantial multi-modal sensory and annotation profiles.

EgoLive is positioned to address the limitations of existing egocentric datasets by providing a dataset that is large-scale, high-quality, and diverse, specifically focusing on real-world task-oriented human routines in under-explored scenarios such as home service, retail, and pharmacy. It integrates comprehensive multi-modal annotations with high-resolution, high-frame-rate stereo video, aiming to offer superior ecological validity and scene diversity compared to prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for introducing EgoLive stems from the identified bottleneck in the generalization capabilities of robot manipulation learning models. Current robotic datasets are often limited by two factors: restricted environmental diversity, which fails to represent real-world complexity, and poor extensibility, hindering large-scale data collection. These limitations collectively impede the development of generalizable embodied systems capable of operating in open-world scenarios.

The key objectives behind the development of EgoLive are:

1. **To establish the largest open-source annotated egocentric dataset focused on real-world task-oriented human routines.** This objective aims to overcome the scale limitations of existing datasets, providing a significantly larger volume of data relevant to practical human tasks.
2. **To deliver high data quality through specialized hardware and comprehensive, high-precision annotations.** This involves:
 - **Custom hardware**: Utilizing a customized head-mounted capture device, JoyEgoCam, designed to provide human-like stereo vision with a wide field of view (130° horizontal × 130° vertical), high resolution (2160×2160 per camera), and a high frame rate (60 frames per second).
 - **Rich multi-modal annotations**: Providing detailed annotations including 6-DoF motion tracking (camera pose, hand joints), fine-grained semantic segmentation (hand and object masks), 3D scene reconstruction (depth maps), and hierarchical language descriptions (sub-task boundaries and natural language captions). These annotations are generated with an emphasis on accuracy.
3. **To ensure superior diversity and ecological validity by collecting data exclusively in unconstrained real-world environments.** This objective differentiates EgoLive by focusing on "vertical field human working data," encompassing scenarios such as home service, retail, and other practical work environments. This approach aims to capture natural human behaviors and interactions without spatial constraints, enhancing the dataset's relevance for real-world robotic deployment.

The overarching goal with EgoLive is to provide the research community with a scalable, high-quality dataset that can accelerate breakthroughs in generalizable robotic models and facilitate the real-world deployment of robot systems by offering a rich foundation for understanding human manipulation in diverse, practical settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EgoLive dataset was constructed through a process involving custom hardware for data collection and an automated pipeline for multi-modal data annotation.

### 4. Data Collection

All data was collected using the JoyEgoCam, a custom-designed head-mounted device. This device features stereo RGB cameras capable of a 130° horizontal × 130° vertical field of view, capturing video at a resolution of 2160 × 2160 pixels per camera at 60 frames per second. An integrated Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) records data at 200 Hz, which supports accurate camera pose estimation. The device's design prioritizes minimal intrusion, allowing recruited operators to perform daily tasks naturally in real-world environments without significant discomfort or interference with hand motion.
The collected raw data streams include time-synchronized stereo RGB videos, camera calibration files, camera trigger frame timestamps, and IMU measurements. Each video segment typically ranges from 1 to 3 minutes, focusing on a single, continuous manipulation activity within a real-world setting.

### 5. Automated Data Annotation

A comprehensive in-house data annotation pipeline was developed to process the multi-modal data streams frame-wise. This pipeline consists of three main components:

- **Motion Tracking**: This component estimates 6-DoF trajectories for both the wrist and hand joints, which are then synchronized with camera ego-motion estimates to establish action reference frames. Hand motion is estimated using a two-stage method based on HaMeR, which first estimates MANO parameters from each monocular video to ensure precise 2D projections. Camera ego-motion is estimated using ORB-SLAM3, fusing binocular RGB images and IMU data, with initialization at the first frame of the left camera.

- **Semantic Understanding**: This component generates hierarchical semantic information, including hand-object interaction states and natural language descriptions of actions. Human hands and interacted objects are detected using a specified model and tracked via BoT-SORT. SAM2 is employed to generate segmentation masks for hands and interacted objects. Each episode is segmented into sub-tasks based on the results of hand-object detection and tracking. For instruction captioning, a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-32B model processes these segmented sub-task clips using a multi-stage reasoning strategy to generate fine-grained textual descriptions.

- **3D Reconstruction**: This component leverages binocular vision for accurate 3D hand reconstruction and depth estimation. In the second stage of hand motion estimation, stereo optimization is performed based on the initially estimated MANO parameters, leading to consistent 3D hand keypoint positions that satisfy 2D projection constraints across monocular videos. Depth maps with a resolution of 1152 × 1152 are reconstructed from calibrated stereo RGB videos using FoundationStereo.

This pipeline integrates geometric, kinematic, and semantic annotations from stereo RGB and IMU inputs, providing multi-modal supervision signals for various downstream tasks in robotics.

### 6. Main Findings and Results

EgoLive presents a dataset characterized by its scale, data quality, diversity, and validated accuracy across its annotations.

### 7. Dataset Scale and Characteristics

EgoLive comprises 1,680 hours of stereo video recordings, captured at 60 frames per second. The dataset includes 65,866 episodes, encompassing 346 distinct real-world tasks. The resolution for each camera is 2160×2160, with a field of view of 130° horizontal × 130° vertical. The paper asserts that EgoLive is the largest open-source annotated egocentric dataset specifically focused on real-world task-oriented human routines released to date.

### 8. Data Quality and Diversity

- **Collection Specifications**: The custom-designed head-mounted device, JoyEgoCam, provides high-resolution stereo video and an integrated IMU, supporting fine-grained motion tracking and accurate camera pose estimation.
- **Annotation Richness**: The dataset provides comprehensive multi-modal annotations, including 6-DoF motion tracking for hands and cameras, fine-grained semantic segmentation for hands and interacted objects, 3D scene reconstruction with depth maps, and hierarchical language descriptions for sub-tasks.
- **Environmental Diversity and Ecological Validity**: Data collection occurred exclusively in unconstrained real-world environments, covering various "vertical field human working data" scenarios such as home service, retail, and pharmacy. This approach aims to capture a broad range of natural human behaviors and interactions.

### 9. Dataset Distribution Analysis

- **Discrete Semantic Distribution**: An analysis of task categories and object-action-attribute labels from instruction captions indicates that EgoLive spans a wide spectrum of manipulation-intensive scenarios, including household services, organization, cleaning, and logistics. Word clouds demonstrate a rich variety of frequently occurring actions, objects, and object attributes. Comparative analysis with EgoDex and Xperience-10M, based on word frequency distribution, suggests that EgoLive exhibits broader semantic coverage and a more natural long-tailed distribution across semantic dimensions, indicating higher semantic diversity.
- **Continuous Feature Space Analysis**: T-SNE visualizations of image embeddings, extracted using Cosmos-Embed1-448p, illustrate the dataset's structure in a continuous feature space representing objects, environments, and actions. EgoLive occupies a broader region of this representation manifold and displays more locally coherent clusters compared to EgoDex and Xperience-10M. This suggests that EgoLive captures a wider range of interaction patterns while maintaining clear local semantic structures.

### 10. Accuracy Evaluation of Annotations

- **Hand Reconstruction**: Qualitative comparisons of 2D hand keypoint annotations indicate that EgoLive provides accurate keypoints that align closely with hands in images, in contrast to EgoDex which showed localization errors. 3D keypoint estimations were validated by projecting them onto depth-reconstructed scenes, demonstrating alignment with the point cloud representation and strong spatial consistency without significant wrist drift.
- **Depth Reconstruction**: Quantitative evaluation using a calibrated checkerboard method demonstrated low mean reconstruction errors. For instance, mean errors were approximately 3mm at distances of 500-700mm and increased to around 8-18mm at distances up to 3500mm. The model achieved high percentages of errors below 5mm and 10mm within typical human operation ranges (e.g., >79% within 5mm at 500-700mm). Qualitative visualizations across diverse real-world scenes showed effective recovery of spatial structures and 3D geometric information.
- **Instruction Captioning**: The instruction captioning module generates structured semantic annotations for sub-task clips, focusing on hands, manipulated objects, and actions. An LLM-as-a-Judge protocol was used to assess the consistency of key elements (hand, object, action, and global consistency) with ground-truth descriptions. While specific numerical metrics were not provided in the paper's extract, qualitative examples demonstrate the generation of fine-grained, structured descriptions reflecting observed behaviors.

### 11. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoLive is presented as a contribution to the field of robot learning, aiming to address existing limitations in dataset availability, quality, and diversity. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality**: The dataset is designed to mitigate the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse datasets that currently hinder the advancement of generalizable robot manipulation learning models. By offering a substantial volume of meticulously annotated data, EgoLive provides a resource for training more robust and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Enabling Generalizable Robotic Models**: The comprehensive nature of the annotations (including 6-DoF motion, semantic segmentation, 3D reconstruction, and hierarchical language descriptions) and its extensive coverage of real-world tasks can support the development of robot learning models with enhanced generalization capabilities. This is particularly relevant for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and embodied artificial intelligence that require rich, multi-modal input.
- **Facilitating Human-to-Robot Transfer**: The egocentric perspective and detailed human demonstration data are intended to bridge the embodiment gap between human and robot actions. This can aid research in human-to-robot alignment, enabling robots, including humanoid robots, to learn and execute complex manipulation policies directly from human observations.
- **Supporting Diverse Research Directions**: EgoLive is suitable for a range of research areas within robotics and computer vision, including:
 - Egocentric perception and understanding of human-object interactions.
 - Manipulation grounding, which involves mapping language commands to physical actions.
 - Workflow decomposition, breaking down complex tasks into manageable sub-tasks.
 - Downstream human-robot transfer tasks in real-world operational scenarios.
- **Promoting Real-World Deployment**: By collecting data exclusively in unconstrained real-world environments, encompassing various practical working scenarios (e.g., home service, retail, pharmacy), EgoLive enhances the ecological validity of learned policies. This focus is intended to facilitate the deployment of robotic systems in practical, utility-driven settings, moving beyond controlled laboratory environments.
- **Establishing a Growing Knowledge Base**: The methodology, including purpose-built hardware and a streamlined, automated annotation pipeline, is designed to support continuous growth in dataset scale and coverage. This framework positions EgoLive as an expanding resource for natural human behavioral priors, which could serve as a foundational knowledge base for future research in embodied intelligence.
