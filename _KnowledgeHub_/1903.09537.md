# Iterative Reinforcement Learning Based Design of Dynamic Locomotion Skills for Cassie

> [!summary] Summary
> An iterative deep reinforcement learning framework enables flexible policy refinement and consolidation for human-scale bipedal robots. This approach allowed the Cassie robot to acquire diverse and robust locomotion skills, including variable-speed walking and high-stepping gaits, with direct simulation-to-real transfer without dynamics randomization.

## Problem

- Iterative design of DRL policies is challenging, as modifying reward functions or network architectures can lead to unpredictable outcomes or "catastrophic forgetting" of previously learned skills.
- Deploying dynamic locomotion policies on physical human-scale bipedal robots from simulation often requires extensive tuning or reliance on dynamics randomization.
- Traditional behavior cloning for policy compression or distillation can suffer from covariate shift, where the student policy encounters states not covered by the expert data, leading to a lack of robustness.

## Method

- A DRL design methodology that integrates policy gradient ==reinforcement learning== with supervised learning, allowing reward functions to be fully redefined in each iteration while preserving prior skills.
- A novel Deterministic Action Stochastic State (DASS) tuple collection method that compactly represents policies by sampling states from stochastic execution and recording deterministic mean actions, suitable for robust supervised learning.
- An objective function that combines the standard RL objective with a supervised learning objective derived from DASS tuples, enabling new policies to be learned while maintaining desired behaviors from expert policies.

## Results

- Successfully compressed large expert policies into significantly smaller networks (e.g., (256, 256) to (16, 16) hidden layers) using as few as 600 DASS samples, maintaining robustness against perturbations.
- Distilled multiple specialized expert policies (e.g., walking forward, backward, stepping in place) into a single, unified policy capable of general locomotion at speeds from -**0.4** m/s to **1.2** m/s.
- Developed robust locomotion skills on the physical Cassie robot, including stable variable-speed walking, gaits with reduced joint accelerations for softer contact, and a high-stepping gait (up to 20 cm foot lift) demonstrating robustness to unmodeled obstacles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DASS tuple collection, by sampling states from the stochastic execution of an expert policy and recording deterministic mean actions, provides robust training data that mitigates covariate shift in imitation learning.
> - The combined RL and DASS-based supervised learning approach enables flexible and iterative policy refinement, allowing for targeted behavioral changes without causing catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills.
> - Successful direct sim-to-real transfer on a human-scale bipedal robot (Cassie) without dynamics randomization is achievable, suggesting that factors like learning stochastic policies at a lower control rate contribute to inherent robustness.
