# Mobi-$π$: Mobilizing Your Robot Learning Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Mobi-π, a framework developed by researchers from Stanford University, Toyota Research Institute, and the University of Cambridge, addresses the challenge of deploying pre-trained robot manipulation policies on mobile platforms by identifying optimal base poses for policy execution. It utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation and Bayesian Optimization to find poses that align with the manipulation policy's training viewpoint distribution, consistently outperforming baselines in simulated and real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Learned visuomotor manipulation policies are fragile to changes in robot base pose and camera viewpoint, limiting their deployment on mobile platforms.
- Existing approaches struggle with generalization: training view-robust policies requires vast data, end-to-end mobile manipulation is data-hungry and has generalization issues, and decoupled navigation lacks policy-awareness.
- Many pre-trained manipulation policies are trained under constrained, stationary conditions, making them unusable in dynamic mobile scenarios without extensive retraining or additional data.

## Method

- Formalizes the "policy mobilization problem" to find a mobile robot base pose that is "in-distribution" for a pre-trained manipulation policy in novel environments.
- Constructs a 3D Gaussian Splatting model of the environment for high-fidelity novel view synthesis from candidate robot poses.
- Uses Bayesian Optimization to maximize a hybrid score function, which combines in-distribution visual similarity (via DINO descriptors), task-relevant object visibility (via MiniCPM-v2), and collision avoidance.

## Results

- Consistently achieved superior task success rates across five challenging simulated mobile manipulation tasks on RoboCasa, significantly outperforming non-policy-aware navigation baselines and an end-to-end learning baseline.
- Demonstrated robust real-world deployment on a mobile robot across three complex tasks, achieving high success rates (70-100%) and 100% collision-free navigation, even surpassing human-driven initialization.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of DINO dense descriptors and the necessity of all components in the hybrid score function for effective policy mobilization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling manipulation policy training from policy-aware navigation, rather than training monolithic policies or passively detecting out-of-distribution states, is an effective strategy for mobile manipulation.
> - High-quality, real-time novel view synthesis (e.g., with 3DGS) combined with dense visual descriptors (e.g., DINO) can accurately assess a pose's suitability for a pre-trained manipulation policy.
> - A hybrid score function that considers visual similarity to training data, object visibility, and collision avoidance is crucial for robust policy mobilization, effectively optimized via gradient-free methods like Bayesian Optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has made significant strides, with visuomotor policies demonstrating impressive capabilities in complex manipulation tasks. However, a pervasive challenge is the poor generalization of these policies to novel conditions, particularly changes in the robot's base pose and camera viewpoint. This limitation severely constrains their deployment on mobile platforms, where precise tasks like opening drawers or turning faucets require specific and consistent viewing angles and reachability. Mobi-π directly addresses this fundamental challenge.

Historically, efforts to improve robot policy robustness can be categorized into several approaches:

- **Training View-Robust Policies:** This involves collecting vast and diverse datasets covering numerous viewpoints, often in simulation to mitigate cost, though this introduces the sim-to-real gap. Using 3D representations can inherently offer view-robustness, but requires meticulously calibrated camera extrinsics. Collecting multi-view data in the real world is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive.
- **End-to-End Mobile Manipulation:** Training a single, monolithic policy that handles both navigation and manipulation, often using large-scale datasets (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Octo). While promising for generalist robots, this approach is extremely data-hungry and still struggles with generalization to out-of-distribution environments, as highlighted by the paper's baselines.
- **Decoupled Navigation and Manipulation:** Chaining separate navigation and manipulation modules. Traditional navigation methods, however, typically focus on semantic goals (e.g., "go to the kitchen") or object localization, without considering the specific input distribution or workspace requirements of a downstream manipulation policy. This often leads to the robot arriving at a location where the manipulation policy fails due to an out-of-distribution viewpoint or unreachable object.
- **In-Distribution Detection (OOD Detection):** This area focuses on identifying when a robot's current observation is outside its training data distribution. While crucial, most prior work treats this as a passive detection problem, not an active problem of *how to bring the robot back into* an in-distribution state.

Mobi-π carves out a novel and complementary niche by formalizing the "policy mobilization problem." Instead of trying to make the *manipulation policy itself* robust to all possible viewpoints (which is difficult and data-intensive), it proposes making the *navigation* policy-aware. It aims to find an optimal mobile robot base pose that aligns with the *original training distribution* of a pre-trained manipulation policy. This strategy allows for the reuse of existing, potentially less robust, manipulation policies (trained on limited viewpoints) on mobile platforms without requiring additional demonstration data for the manipulation policy itself.

This work directly builds upon advancements in several areas:
- **Novel View Synthesis:** The use of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for rapid and high-quality scene representation and novel view generation is critical. 3DGS offers advantages over traditional NeRFs in terms of speed and ease of training, making it suitable for online deployment.
- **Foundation Models in Vision:** Leveraging models like DINO for dense visual descriptors and MiniCPM-v2 for vision-language object detection showcases the increasing utility of large pre-trained models for zero-shot generalization in robotics.
- **Sampling-based Optimization:** Bayesian Optimization is a well-established technique for optimizing expensive-to-evaluate, non-smooth black-box functions, which fits the problem of finding an optimal robot pose where success is hard to predict analytically.

By formalizing the "policy mobilization problem" and providing a framework to address it, Mobi-π offers a practical and data-efficient pathway to unlock the potential of numerous existing robot manipulation policies for broader deployment on mobile platforms, thereby expanding the applicability of robot learning to more diverse and unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Mobi-π stems from a critical bottleneck in deploying learned visuomotor manipulation policies: their inherent fragility to changes in the robot's base pose and camera viewpoint. While these policies can execute increasingly complex tasks, their training data is typically collected under constrained conditions, leading to a tight coupling between the policy's input distribution and the specific robot and camera configurations observed during training. When such policies are transferred to mobile robots, even slight shifts in the robot's position can cause visual inputs and reachable workspaces to fall outside the policy's training distribution, leading to severe performance degradation.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Formalize the Policy Mobilization Problem:** To define a new problem in mobile robotics: determining a mobile robot base pose in a novel environment that is "in-distribution" for a manipulation policy trained on limited camera viewpoints. This explicitly decouples navigation from manipulation policy training while ensuring navigation remains "policy-aware."
2. **Develop a Novel Method for Policy Mobilization:** To propose a practical approach that searches for suitable robot base poses without requiring extensive additional data collection or retraining of the core manipulation policy. This method aims to be effective in arbitrary, unseen room layouts.
3. **Introduce the Mobi-π Framework:** To provide a comprehensive framework for studying and benchmarking policy mobilization, which includes:
 - **Quantifiable Metrics:** Metrics to measure the difficulty of mobilizing a given policy, both spatially (how much perturbation a policy can tolerate) and visually (how prominent task-relevant objects are in the view).
 - **Simulated Task Suite:** A set of challenging mobile manipulation tasks based on RoboCasa to enable systematic evaluation and comparison of different mobilization methods.
 - **Visualization Tools:** Aids for qualitative analysis and debugging of the mobilization process.
4. **Validate the Approach in Simulation and Real-World:** To empirically demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing baselines (both policy-aware and non-policy-aware) in diverse simulated and real-world mobile manipulation scenarios, proving its effectiveness and practical applicability.

Ultimately, the overarching goal is to provide a robust and data-efficient pathway for leveraging the abundance of pre-trained, stationary robot manipulation policies on mobile platforms, thereby expanding their utility to larger, more dynamic, and varied environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Mobi-π framework introduces a novel method for policy mobilization, designed to be policy-aware and operate in unseen environments without extensive data collection.

**Assumptions:**
1. **Access to Initial Demonstrations:** The method assumes access to the initial observation frames from the training dataset used for the manipulation policy. This provides a reference for "in-distribution" viewpoints.
2. **Scene Map Generation:** Before policy execution, the robot is allowed to explore the scene to build a 3D representation.
3. **Fixed Robot Arm/Camera Configuration:** For simplicity, the initial robot arm pose is fixed, and a known, fixed relative transform exists between the robot base and its camera, simplifying pose optimization to the robot's base.

**Proposed Policy Mobilization Method:**

1. **Scene Representation with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):**
 - Upon entering a novel environment, the robot autonomously collects approximately 1,000 RGB-D images by driving around and varying camera viewpoints (takes <5 minutes).
 - These images are used to construct a high-quality 3D Gaussian Splatting model of the scene. 3DGS allows for efficient, high-fidelity novel view synthesis from arbitrary camera poses.
 - This representation enables rendering a realistic image from any candidate robot base pose, which is crucial for evaluating its suitability for policy execution.

2. **Hybrid Score Function (K(p)) for Policy Execution Viability:**
 - To evaluate how likely a manipulation policy will succeed from a candidate robot base pose `p`, a hybrid score function `K(p)` is defined, combining three critical factors:
 - **In-Distribution Score (K_id(p)):** This component assesses if the rendered view from pose `p` is visually similar to the initial observations from the policy's training dataset.
 - An image is rendered from `p` using the 3DGS model.
 - DINO dense descriptors are extracted from this rendered image.
 - These descriptors are compared (using K-Nearest Neighbors, KNN, with L2 distance) to precomputed DINO descriptors of the initial observation frames from the training dataset. The average L2 distance provides an "in-distribution" score, with lower distance indicating higher suitability. DINO dense descriptors are chosen for their robustness to scene texture variations and effectiveness in distinguishing in- from out-of-distribution views.
 - **Object Visibility Score (K_obj(p)):** To ensure the task-relevant object is actually visible (K_id alone might select texturally similar but irrelevant views), a vision-language model (MiniCPM-v2) is used. It checks if the object of interest is present in the rendered image. This is a binary score (1 if visible, 0 otherwise).
 - **Collision-Free Score (K_col(p)):** An occupancy map, constructed from the depth data used for 3DGS training, is consulted to verify that the robot's base pose `p` is collision-free. This is also a binary score (1 if collision-free, 0 otherwise).
 - **Combined Score:** `K(p)` is high only if `K_obj(p) = 1` and `K_col(p) = 1`. Otherwise, it defaults to 0, ensuring safety and task relevance before evaluating `K_id(p)`.

3. **Robot Pose Optimization with Bayesian Optimization (BO):**
 - Given the potentially non-smooth and complex nature of the `K(p)` landscape (due to occlusions, visibility changes), gradient-based optimization is unreliable.
 - Bayesian Optimization (BO), a sampling-based, gradient-free method, is employed to efficiently search for the optimal `p` that maximizes `K(p)`.
 - BO uses a surrogate model (e.g., Gaussian Process) to approximate `K(p)` based on observed samples and an acquisition function (e.g., Upper Confidence Bound) to balance exploration (sampling uncertain regions) and exploitation (sampling high-scoring regions).
 - The process involves an initial set of random samples, followed by iterative rounds where new poses are proposed, evaluated, and the surrogate model is updated, eventually yielding the highest-scoring pose for policy execution.

**The Mobi-π Framework Components:**

1. **Mobilization Feasibility Metrics:**
 - **Spatial Mobilization Feasibility (ϕ):** Quantifies how sensitive a policy is to base pose perturbations. It's the standard deviation (in meters) of Gaussian noise applied to the base pose that causes the policy's success rate to drop by half. A higher ϕ indicates easier mobilization.
 - **Visual Mobilization Feasibility (S_v):** Measures how prominently task-relevant objects appear in views from near-optimal poses. It's the average percentage of the image occupied by objects of interest in `N_v` sampled images. Higher S_v indicates easier visual cues for mobilization.

2. **Simulated Task Suite:**
 - A benchmark of five challenging mobile manipulation tasks built on RoboCasa (Close Door, Turn on Faucet, Turn on Microwave, Close Drawer, Turn on Stove).
 - For each task, a standard image-based imitation learning policy is trained on 300 demonstrations in 5 training room layouts.
 - The benchmark's goal is to deploy these policies into 5 *held-out* scenes with unseen layouts and textures, testing generalization capabilities.

3. **Visualization Tools:** For qualitative analysis of navigation paths, sampled poses, and score landscapes during the optimization process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in both simulation and real-world settings, demonstrate the significant effectiveness of the Mobi-π approach compared to existing baselines.

**Simulation Experiments:**

- **Superior Performance:** The proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines across the five challenging RoboCasa tasks (Close Door, Turn on Faucet, Turn on Microwave, Close Drawer, Turn on Stove). Its success rates often align with the performance of the manipulation policy when executed with only a small (5 cm) perturbation at initialization, indicating highly effective mobilization.
- **Failure of Non-Policy-Aware Baselines:** Baselines like LeLaN and VLFM, which navigate to an object's general vicinity without considering the manipulation policy's specific viewpoint requirements, perform poorly. This confirms that simply finding an object is insufficient for successful manipulation.
- **Generalization Challenges for End-to-End Learning:** The "BC w/ Nav" baseline, which trains an end-to-end mobile manipulation policy with 5x more data than the base manipulation policy, still struggles to generalize to unseen room layouts. This highlights the data-intensive nature and generalization limitations of training monolithic mobile manipulation policies.
- **Correlation with Mobilization Feasibility Metrics:**
 - Non-policy-aware baselines perform worst in tasks with low spatial mobilization feasibility (e.g., Turn on Stove, Turn on Faucet, Turn on Microwave), indicating that policies intolerant to pose perturbations are particularly difficult for such methods.
 - The proposed method's performance shows a correlation with visual mobilization feasibility, with a slight performance drop in the "Close Drawer" task, which has low visual feasibility. This is attributed to its reliance on visual cues for pose estimation.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Ablations confirmed the importance of DINO dense descriptors for `K_id`, showing that simpler DINO embeddings or policy encoder features perform significantly worse. DINO dense descriptors provide richer spatial and semantic information crucial for discerning suitable viewpoints, whereas policy encoders may focus on task-relevant features rather than comprehensive view suitability.
 - Each component of the hybrid score function (`K_id`, `K_obj`, `K_col`) was shown to contribute positively to the overall success rate, confirming the necessity of considering in-distribution alignment, object visibility, and collision avoidance simultaneously.

**Real-World Experiments:**

- **Robust Real-World Deployment:** The method was successfully deployed on a custom mobile robot in a mock grocery store environment across three complex tasks (Pick Chips, Bimanual Grab Paper Towels, Pour Tomatoes).
- **High Success Rates:** The proposed method achieved 100% navigation success and high full task success rates (70% for Pick Chips, 100% for Grab Paper Towels, 80% for Pour Tomatoes), crucially operating completely collision-free.
- **Outperforming Human Intuition:** A "Human" baseline, where experienced users manually drove the robot to what they believed were optimal poses, achieved high navigation success but significantly lower task success. This underscores that human intuition, while good for general navigation, lacks the precise policy-specific knowledge required for optimal mobilization.
- **Limitations of BC w/ Nav:** The real-world "BC w/ Nav" baseline also showed inconsistent performance, often placing the robot inappropriately (too close or poorly oriented), leading to failed manipulation attempts and collisions.
- **Qualitative Demonstrations:**
 - **Skill Sequencing:** Demonstrated chaining multiple "drawer-closing" policies in a real kitchen, showcasing its potential for multi-step tasks.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Successfully generalized to unseen scene layouts for the "Pick Chips" task without any modifications.
 - **Large-Scale Operation:** Operated effectively in a large (6x10 meters) grocery store scene for the "Pour Tomatoes" task, leveraging a large 3DGS model of the environment.
 - **Height Optimization:** Easily extended to optimize robot/camera height, allowing the robot to adapt to objects at different shelf heights for the "Bimanual Grab Paper Towels" task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Mobi-π: Mobilizing Your Robot Learning Policy" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of robot learning and mobile manipulation, with considerable potential impact:

1. **Novel Problem Formulation:** By formalizing the "policy mobilization problem," the authors introduce a new lens through which to view and address the challenges of deploying learned policies on mobile platforms. This formulation is distinct from merely training robust policies or semantic navigation, providing a critical missing piece in the ecosystem of robot learning.

2. **Data-Efficient Deployment of Existing Policies:** Perhaps the most impactful aspect is its ability to unlock the utility of countless pre-trained, "stationary" manipulation policies for mobile robots without the need for extensive, costly retraining or additional human demonstrations. This leverages the growing abundance of existing robot learning datasets and pre-trained models, accelerating the deployment of learned skills in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The method significantly improves the generalization capabilities of manipulation policies to novel environments and varying robot base poses. By actively finding an in-distribution viewpoint, it effectively mitigates the impact of visual and kinematic shifts that typically lead to policy failures, enhancing the robustness of robot operations.

4. **Complementary to End-to-End Learning:** Unlike approaches that advocate for massive end-to-end training of mobile manipulation policies (which can be prohibitively data-hungry and prone to generalization issues), Mobi-π offers a complementary strategy. It allows researchers and practitioners to immediately deploy existing manipulation expertise while future work continues to develop more broadly robust policies.

5. **Comprehensive Research Framework:** The introduction of the Mobi-π framework, including quantitative metrics (spatial and visual mobilization feasibility), a standardized simulation task suite based on RoboCasa, and visualization tools, provides a valuable resource for the wider research community. This framework will facilitate systematic benchmarking, development, and deeper understanding of policy mobilization methods.

6. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The strong performance in real-world mobile manipulation tasks, including skill sequencing, zero-shot generalization to unseen layouts, operation in large spaces, and adaptation to object heights, underscores its immediate practical relevance. This could pave the way for more dexterous and autonomous mobile robots in various applications, from logistics and manufacturing to assistive robotics in homes and stores.

7. **Bridging Navigation and Manipulation:** The work effectively bridges the gap between navigation and manipulation by introducing a "policy-aware" navigation objective. This is a crucial step towards creating truly intelligent mobile manipulators that can not only move to a location but also position themselves optimally for complex interactions.

In conclusion, Mobi-π offers an elegant and effective solution to a long-standing challenge in robot learning, providing a clear pathway for making learned manipulation skills more accessible and deployable on mobile platforms. Its significance lies in its novel problem formulation, data efficiency, and proven real-world applicability, positioning it as a foundational work for future advancements in general-purpose mobile manipulation.
