# Mobi-$π$: Mobilizing Your Robot Learning Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Mobi-π, a framework developed by researchers from Stanford University, Toyota Research Institute, and the University of Cambridge, addresses the challenge of deploying pre-trained robot manipulation policies on mobile platforms by identifying optimal base poses for policy execution. It utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation and Bayesian Optimization to find poses that align with the manipulation policy's training viewpoint distribution, consistently outperforming baselines in simulated and real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Learned visuomotor manipulation policies are fragile to changes in robot base pose and camera viewpoint, limiting their deployment on mobile platforms.
- Existing approaches struggle with generalization: training view-robust policies requires vast data, end-to-end mobile manipulation is data-hungry and has generalization issues, and decoupled navigation lacks policy-awareness.
- Many pre-trained manipulation policies are trained under constrained, stationary conditions, making them unusable in dynamic mobile scenarios without extensive retraining or additional data.

## Method

- Formalizes the "policy mobilization problem" to find a mobile robot base pose that is "in-distribution" for a pre-trained manipulation policy in novel environments.
- Constructs a 3D Gaussian Splatting model of the environment for high-fidelity novel view synthesis from candidate robot poses.
- Uses Bayesian Optimization to maximize a hybrid score function, which combines in-distribution visual similarity (via DINO descriptors), task-relevant object visibility (via MiniCPM-v2), and collision avoidance.

## Results

- Consistently achieved superior task success rates across five challenging simulated mobile manipulation tasks on RoboCasa, significantly outperforming non-policy-aware navigation baselines and an end-to-end learning baseline.
- Demonstrated robust real-world deployment on a mobile robot across three complex tasks, achieving high success rates (70-100%) and 100% collision-free navigation, even surpassing human-driven initialization.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of DINO dense descriptors and the necessity of all components in the hybrid score function for effective policy mobilization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling manipulation policy training from policy-aware navigation, rather than training monolithic policies or passively detecting out-of-distribution states, is an effective strategy for mobile manipulation.
> - High-quality, real-time novel view synthesis (e.g., with 3DGS) combined with dense visual descriptors (e.g., DINO) can accurately assess a pose's suitability for a pre-trained manipulation policy.
> - A hybrid score function that considers visual similarity to training data, object visibility, and collision avoidance is crucial for robust policy mobilization, effectively optimized via gradient-free methods like Bayesian Optimization.
