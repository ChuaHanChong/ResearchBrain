# Trajectory-Refined Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at McGill University, Mila Quebec AI Institute, and UT Austin developed Trajectory-Refined Distillation (TRD) to mitigate 'prefix failure' in on-policy distillation for large language models. TRD refines student-generated trajectories, leading to consistent performance improvements, including up to +4.6% Avg@16 on AIME24 and +12.8% Pass@16 on AMOBench for specific Qwen3 models, while also generating substantially shorter, more verifiable reasoning paths.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy distillation (OPD) and self-distillation (OPSD) methods often provide noisy or uninformative supervision signals to large language models.
- A structural limitation, termed 'prefix failure,' arises when a student model's generated sequence enters an incorrect reasoning path.
- This results in a bimodal teacher distribution and fragmented gradients, rendering traditional token-level loss interventions insufficient to correct the flawed trajectory.

## Method

- **Trajectory-Refined Distillation** (TRD) is introduced, which performs a ==trajectory-level correction== by refining student-generated rollouts.
- A teacher model generates a refined trajectory ('y_r'), conditioned on the initial student rollout ('y_o'), the problem, and optionally ground-truth answers for OPSD.
- The refined trajectory ('y_r') then serves as the high-quality supervision target for subsequent distillation, using forward KL divergence.

## Results

- TRD consistently surpasses strong dense-KL baselines across diverse benchmarks and model scales in both OPD and OPSD settings.
- The method yields performance gains up to +**4.6%** Avg@16 on AIME24 and a substantial +**12.8%** Pass@16 on AMOBench for specific Qwen3 models.
- TRD's refined trajectories are significantly shorter (up to **9x** compression) and achieve a higher verifier-pass rate, while dramatically reducing 'epistemic-token mass' (corrective teacher signals).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 'Prefix failure' leads to degraded supervision and fragmented gradients, with teachers repeatedly attempting to correct from problematic contexts.
> - Trajectory-level correction effectively mitigates prefix failure by providing a coherent, corrected reasoning path as the distillation target.
> - Refining trajectories not only fixes errors but also improves exploration, exposing the student to alternative, often more concise, correct derivations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated within the field of large language model (LLM) post-training, specifically focusing on knowledge distillation techniques. On-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as a significant method, providing dense, per-token supervision to a student model during its own generative rollouts. This approach has been incorporated into prominent industry models such as Qwen3, DeepSeek-v4, MiMo-v2, and GLM-5, often alongside supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) is a variant of OPD where the teacher and student models share parameters, with the teacher receiving additional privileged information (e.g., ground-truth answers).

Existing OPD and OPSD methodologies, despite their adoption, frequently exhibit failure modes that can lead to noisy or uninformative supervision signals. Prior attempts to address these issues have typically involved token-level loss interventions, such as reweighting or clipping individual token contributions to the loss function. However, these interventions operate at a granular level, leaving the problematic generated trajectory itself unchanged.

This paper identifies a structural limitation inherent in these token-level approaches, termed "prefix failure." Prefix failure occurs when a student model's rollout deviates into an incorrect reasoning path, rendering subsequent token-level corrections ineffective or fragmented. The current work positions itself as a development that moves beyond these token-level interventions by proposing a trajectory-level correction strategy. This aims to address the root cause of prefix failure, thereby enhancing the effectiveness and robustness of on-policy distillation paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from observed limitations in conventional on-policy distillation (OPD) and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) methods. While these methods are widely adopted for LLM post-training due to their dense supervision signals, empirical studies indicate they often underperform or exhibit specific failure modes, where the supervision signal becomes noisy or uninformative.

The authors identify a specific structural cause for these shortcomings, which they term "prefix failure." This occurs when the student model's generated sequence (rollout) embarks on an incorrect reasoning path. When this happens:
- **Bimodal Teacher Mixture:** The teacher model, faced with an erroneous prefix, struggles to provide a clear signal. Its optimal distribution becomes a mixture: one mode attempts to continue the student's flawed prefix for sequence consistency, while the other attempts to pivot towards a correct solution.
- **Fragmented Gradients:** Evaluating per-token Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence along the student's frozen, flawed rollout results in fragmented gradients. The teacher repeatedly recommends a "correction-onset" token, but the student's context continues along the incorrect path, preventing a coherent, multi-step recovery. This renders token-level loss truncation or reweighting insufficient, as they modify the loss signal on an already problematic trajectory rather than correcting the trajectory itself.

Given these identified limitations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Formalize Prefix Failure:** To formally describe and empirically validate the phenomenon of prefix failure, including its impact on teacher distributions and gradient signals under both forward and reverse KL divergences.
2. **Develop a Trajectory-Level Correction:** To propose a novel distillation method that intervenes at the trajectory level, correcting problematic prefixes before the distillation process. This aims to mitigate prefix failure at its source by providing a "refined trajectory" as a superior supervision target.
3. **Maintain On-Policy Support:** To ensure that the proposed trajectory-level correction remains within the student's on-policy support. This means the refined trajectories, while guided by a teacher, should still be plausible continuations that the student could eventually learn to generate.
4. **Enhance Exploration:** To improve the student model's exploration capabilities. Beyond correcting errors, the method aims to expose the student to alternative valid derivations of correct answers, even when its initial rollouts are already correct, thereby broadening its reasoning coverage.
5. **Empirical Validation:** To rigorously evaluate the proposed method, Trajectory-Refined Distillation (TRD), across a range of benchmarks, model scales, and both OPD and OPSD setups, comparing its performance against established baselines that use token-level loss interventions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is Trajectory-Refined Distillation (TRD), a strategy designed to move beyond token-level interventions by refining student-generated trajectories.

**Problem Formulation (Trajectory-Level Optimization):**
The authors frame the objective as maximizing the expected verifier-pass rate over the dataset D, subject to the constraint that the refined trajectory remains within the student's current policy support (πθ(y | x) > 0). This objective is not directly optimized during the KL update but rather guides an *upstream trajectory-construction task*.

**TRD Procedure (Algorithm 1):**
For each problem `x` from the training dataset:
1. **Initial Rollout:** The student model `πθ` first samples an initial, raw on-policy rollout, denoted `y_o ~ πθ(· | x)`. This is the same rollout used by standard OPD/OPSD baselines.
2. **Trajectory Refinement:** A refined trajectory `y_r` is then generated under the guidance of a teacher.
 - **In OPD:** The teacher `πT` generates `y_r` conditioned on the original problem `x` and the student's raw rollout `y_o`: `y_r ~ πT(· | x, y_o)`. This implicitly guides the teacher to correct `y_o` while grounding the refinement in the student's current generation patterns.
 - **In OPSD:** The student model's backbone (parameterized by `θ`) acts as the teacher. It generates `y_r` conditioned on `x`, `y_o`, and crucially, the ground-truth reference solution `y*`: `y_r ~ sg[πθ(· | x, y_o, y*)]`. A stop-gradient operator `sg[·]` is applied to prevent gradients from flowing back into the teacher branch during this sampling phase. This mechanism leverages privileged information to guide the refinement.
3. **Distillation:** The refined trajectory `y_r` then serves as the supervision target for the subsequent distillation process. The loss function used is the forward KL divergence with full vocabulary matching:
 `L(θ) = E[D(sg[πT(· | x, y_o, y_r,<t)] || πθ(· | x, y_r,<t))]`
 Here, the student model `πθ` is trained to match the (stop-gradient-enabled) teacher's distribution, but the conditioning context for both student and teacher (`x, y_r,<t`) now progresses along the *refined trajectory* `y_r`, rather than the potentially erroneous `y_o`.

**Rationale and Advantages:**
- **Mitigation of Prefix Failure:** By replacing `y_o` with `y_r`, TRD provides a corrected trajectory as the basis for supervision. This avoids the bimodal teacher mixture and fragmented gradients identified as core problems. The supervision contexts `(x, y_r,<t)` now genuinely follow a corrected path, allowing for effective multi-step learning.
- **Improved Exploration:** TRD enables the student to explore beyond its initial successful rollouts. If `y_o` is already correct, the teacher might still guide the generation of `y_r` to an alternative, potentially shorter or more robust, correct derivation. This expands the diversity of successful reasoning trajectories the student is exposed to.
- **On-Policy Support:** The conditioning on `y_o` during `y_r` generation ensures that the refined trajectory is still somewhat grounded in the student's current capabilities, preserving the on-policy nature of the distillation.
- **Forward KL Choice:** Forward KL is selected for its mode-covering properties and its empirical stability in previous distillation work.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Experiments utilize the Qwen3 model family, including Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, and Qwen3-8B.
- **Teacher Configuration:** In OPD, Qwen3-8B serves as a distinct teacher model. In OPSD, the same Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 or Qwen3-8B model acts as both student and (privileged) teacher.
- **Training Data:** DeepScaleR corpus for mathematical reasoning problems and TACO for algorithmic code generation.
- **Baselines:** Four strong dense-KL baselines are used for comparison, all training on `y_o`: Forward KL, Forward KL with Clipping (Zhao et al., 2026), Reverse KL, and Reverse KL with Top-K truncation (Fu et al., 2026).
- **Evaluation:** Performance is assessed using Avg@16 and Pass@16 metrics on five competition math benchmarks (AIME24/25, HMMT25, BeyondAIME, AMOBench) and three code generation benchmarks (HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across empirical validation, performance benchmarks, and trajectory analysis:

**Empirical Validation of Prefix Failure (Figure 3):**
- **Supervision Degradation:** Analysis of per-token KL loss on standard `y_o` rollouts in OPSD shows that both for correct and incorrect rollouts, the KL values remain near zero. This indicates an alignment between teacher and student distributions, implying a lack of informative corrective signal, particularly when the student is on a wrong path.
- **Perplexity Gap Shrinkage:** The perplexity gap between the teacher and student models remains minimal throughout training with `y_o`. This suggests that the privileged information provided to the teacher in OPSD offers little incremental supervision to the student on its unrefined rollouts.
- **Epistemic-token Mass:** The teacher places a notable portion (6-8‰) of its per-position probability mass on specific "epistemic onset tokens" (e.g., "Wait, Actually") when supervising `y_o`. This is interpreted as the teacher repeatedly attempting to initiate a correction from an already problematic context. In contrast, for `y_r` (TRD's refined trajectories), this mass decreases significantly (below 2‰), suggesting TRD successfully alleviates the fragmented gradient issue.

**OPD Results (Tables 1 and 2):**
- **Avg@16 (Exploitation):** TRD consistently outperforms prior baselines in single-attempt accuracy. For the Qwen3-1.7B student, TRD demonstrates gains of up to +4.6% on AIME24 compared to the base model. For the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 model, most `y_o`-based OPD variants show a regression or marginal improvement over the base model, while TRD achieves consistent gains across benchmarks (e.g., +2.3% on AIME24, +1.9% on HMMT25).
- **Pass@16 (Exploration/Coverage):** TRD's benefits are particularly pronounced on harder math benchmarks. On AMOBench, TRD shows a +5.1% improvement for Qwen3-1.7B and a substantial +12.8% for Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 relative to their respective base models, indicating an improved ability to find at least one correct solution among multiple attempts. On code generation tasks, TRD performs well on HumanEval+ and MBPP+, but generally performs similarly or slightly worse than the base model on LiveCodeBench, suggesting limitations in teacher refinement capabilities for this specific, harder code benchmark.

**OPSD Results (Tables 3 and 4):**
- **Avg@16:** TRD consistently achieves the best Avg@16 results across all evaluated math benchmarks at both Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B scales, and never falls below the performance of the base model. This contrasts with other `y_o`-based baselines, which frequently show regressions on specific benchmarks.
- **Pass@16:** TRD demonstrates significant improvements in Pass@16, especially on challenging benchmarks. For Qwen3-8B, TRD achieves a +20.5% gain on AMOBench and +9.6% on HMMT25 relative to the base model, representing a substantial relative improvement over the strongest baselines. Similar gains are observed for Qwen3-4B.

**Comparison of Refinement Signals (Table 5):**
- Comparing TRD in OPD (using a stronger Qwen3-8B teacher) and OPSD (using privileged information with the same Qwen3-4B backbone), OPSD generally shows stronger Pass@16 results across competition math benchmarks (winning on four of five). This suggests that direct guidance from the reference solution (`y*`) in OPSD can be more effective for broadening solution coverage and exploring alternative paths than relying solely on a larger, external teacher model in OPD.

**Trajectory Analysis (Figure 4, Appendix B.1, B.2):**
- **Training Trajectory (y_o vs. y_r):** In the OPSD setting, the refined trajectories (`y_r`) generated by TRD are substantially shorter than the raw on-policy rollouts (`y_o`). For Qwen3-8B with CoT, the median length of `y_r` is compressed by approximately 9x (0.88K tokens) compared to `y_o` (7.7K tokens), moving closer to the reference `y*` (0.49K tokens). The verifier-pass rate for `y_r` significantly improves (e.g., from 65.8% for `y_o` to 81.4% for `y_r` on Qwen3-8B). This refinement is predominantly corrective, as the "fail→pass" rate (y_o incorrect, y_r correct) is much higher than "pass→fail" (y_o correct, y_r incorrect), indicating TRD primarily fixes erroneous prefixes. Moreover, `y_r` also surfaces alternative, often shorter, correct derivations even when `y_o` was already successful, enhancing supervision diversity. This length compression also contributes to faster KL training, partially offsetting the `y_r` sampling cost.
- **Test Rollout Analysis (AMOBench):** On AMOBench test data (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, OPD setting), TRD's correct rollouts exhibit a bimodal length distribution with a distinct low-length mode, suggesting it finds more concise reasoning paths. Crucially, TRD significantly expands the coverage for the hardest questions (B0 bucket), which the base model consistently fails. TRD achieves a Pass@16 of 0.39 on these questions, nearly doubling the best baseline (0.22), directly demonstrating its ability to expand the reachable solution space of the policy.

**Ablations on Training Subsets (Appendix B.3):**
- Filtering the training corpus (e.g., using only `y_o` failures or successes) does not uniformly improve performance for any method. This indicates that both learning from corrected failures and exploring alternative successful paths (which can happen even when `y_o` is correct) are valuable contributions to the overall robustness and performance gains of TRD.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of large language model training and has the potential for notable impact.

**Theoretical and Methodological Advance:**
The paper's primary significance lies in its identification and formalization of "prefix failure" as a structural limitation of existing on-policy distillation (OPD) and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) methods. This provides a theoretical underpinning for many observed empirical shortcomings of dense per-token KL divergence training. By characterizing the bimodal teacher mixture and fragmented gradients, the work elucidates why token-level loss interventions are inherently limited. In response, Trajectory-Refined Distillation (TRD) introduces a novel trajectory-level intervention that addresses prefix failure at its source, representing a significant methodological shift from merely modifying loss signals to actively shaping the supervision trajectories themselves.

**Performance Enhancement and Robustness:**
TRD consistently demonstrates superior performance across a diverse set of challenging benchmarks (competition math and code generation), various model scales, and both OPD and OPSD settings. The observed gains in both Avg@16 (single-attempt accuracy) and particularly Pass@16 (solution coverage) highlight TRD's ability to improve both exploitation and exploration. The ability to achieve substantial gains on difficult problems like AMOBench, including solving questions previously unreachable by base models, indicates a genuine expansion of the student model's capabilities and reasoning coverage. This suggests TRD provides a more robust and effective distillation paradigm compared to existing baselines.

**Improved Exploration and Diversity of Solutions:**
Beyond correcting errors, TRD also contributes to better exploration. By exposing the student to refined trajectories (`y_r`) that can offer alternative, often more concise, valid derivations even when the initial student rollout (`y_o`) was correct, TRD broadens the student's understanding of diverse solution paths. This could lead to more robust and versatile LLMs capable of generating a wider array of high-quality responses.

**Efficiency Considerations:**
While TRD introduces an additional sampling step to generate `y_r`, the analysis indicates that the resulting refined trajectories are significantly shorter. This length compression can lead to faster KL training, partially offsetting the initial sampling overhead, especially for larger models. This suggests that the performance benefits of TRD do not necessarily come with a prohibitive increase in overall training time.

**Broader Implications for LLM Alignment and Self-Improvement:**
The findings regarding OPSD's effectiveness with privileged information offer insights for future LLM alignment research. The observation that specific reference guidance can sometimes be more impactful than simply using a larger, distinct teacher model in OPD highlights the value of quality, targeted feedback in self-improvement mechanisms. This could inform the design of more effective self-distillation and self-correction strategies for LLMs. The principles of trajectory-level refinement could also be extended to other sequential decision-making or generation tasks beyond language models.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations, including the added computational cost of `y_r` sampling (though partially mitigated by efficiency gains) and the reliance on the teacher's ability to provide effective refinement while staying within the student's support. Future work could explore methods to further optimize `y_r` generation for even greater efficiency, investigate adaptive strategies for teacher guidance, or extend the trajectory refinement concept to domains beyond text generation.

In summary, this work presents a foundational analysis of a critical failure mode in on-policy distillation and offers a principled, trajectory-level solution that demonstrably improves LLM performance and reasoning capabilities.
