# STATE-NAV: Stability-Aware Traversability Estimation for Bipedal Navigation on Rough Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> The STATE-NAV framework introduces a learning-based approach for bipedal robots to navigate rough terrain by predicting locomotion instability and its uncertainty. Utilizing a Transformer network (TravFormer) and a novel Body-to-Stance-Foot Angle (BSFA) instability metric, the system defines traversability as a stability-aware command velocity, enabling robust and efficient navigation in both simulations and real-world outdoor environments with a Digit robot.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based traversability methods are primarily designed for wheeled or quadrupedal robots, lacking specific models for bipedal instability.
- Bipedal robots rely heavily on manually designed rules for traversability, which often fail to accurately reflect locomotion stability and generalize across diverse, complex terrains.
- Navigation planning for bipedal robots frequently overlooks the critical aspect of motion aggressiveness and often fails to incorporate predicted instability and its uncertainty for risk-aware path selection.

## Method

- Identified the Body-to-==Stance-Foot Angle (BSFA)== as a reliable self-supervised metric for bipedal instability, demonstrating its strong correlation with fallover risk.
- Developed TravFormer, a Transformer-based neural network, to predict BSFA instability and its uncertainty from terrain elevation maps and robot command velocities.
- Defined traversability as a stability-aware command velocity, determined by an iterative search that ensures risk-sensitive predicted instability (using Value at Risk) remains below a user-defined limit, and integrated this into a hierarchical planning framework (TravRRT* global planner and MPC local planner).

## Results

- TravFormer achieved an RMSE of **0.7065** and accurate uncertainty estimation (|∆PICP| of **0.0007**) for instability prediction, outperforming simpler architectures.
- In simulation, STATE-NAV achieved **100%** success rates with lower maximum instability (**4.82**-**5.37**) on rough and ramp terrains, compared to baselines (70-**90%** success, **7.09**-**8.31** max instability) that often traversed hazardous areas.
- Successfully validated on a Digit humanoid robot in three diverse outdoor real-world environments, navigating goals while maintaining maximum BSFA instabilities between **1.7** and **3.4** in real time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Body-to-Stance-Foot Angle (BSFA) instability provides a highly effective and bipedal-specific self-supervised signal for training traversability estimators.
> - Integrating command velocity and terrain features via attention mechanisms in a Transformer architecture (TravFormer) significantly improves the prediction of bipedal instability and its associated uncertainty.
> - Defining traversability as a stability-aware command velocity, rather than a simple cost, enables more robust, environment-agnostic planning by allowing the robot to adapt its speed to maintain stability, reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning.
