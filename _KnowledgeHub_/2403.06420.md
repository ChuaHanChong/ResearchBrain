# RLingua: Improving Reinforcement Learning Sample Efficiency in Robotic Manipulations With Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RLingua prompts GPT-4 into a rule-based Python controller for a robot task, then uses that imperfect controller to collect rollout data with an exponentially decaying probability and to regularize TD3's actor loss via an imitation term. Across panda_gym and RLBench sparse-reward manipulation, it cuts sample complexity sharply and surpasses both the LLM controller and standard TD3, transferring to a real Panda arm.

## Problem

- LLM-for-RL work focuses on generating reward functions, yet the resulting policies still need millions of environment interactions, leaving RL's sample complexity largely unaddressed.
- Demonstration-augmented RL depends on pre-collected expert datasets gathered by humans, which is labor-intensive and often infeasible for new robot manipulation tasks.
- LLM robot planners assume pre-trained action primitives are available and never explain how to obtain low-level control policies from basic motions alone.

## Method

- Two-stage prompt design: task descriptions elicit task phases, then code guidelines, refined by non-expert human feedback or a code template, yield an imperfect rule-based Python controller (`π_LLM`).
- ==DAGGER==-style mixed rollouts: actions come from the LLM controller with probability `p_LLM` decaying exponentially per environment step, storing data in separate LLM and RL replay buffers.
- Modified **TD3** actor loss adds an imitation regularizer weighted by `λ_IM` that pulls the policy toward LLM-buffer actions, while critics train only on RL-buffer data; goal-oriented tasks also use ==Hindsight Experience Replay==.

## Results

- In panda_gym, **RLingua** reaches **0.8** success on PandaReach with under **25%** of standard TD3's environment steps, and under **50%** on PandaPush.
- Across 12 sparsely rewarded RLBench tasks RLingua exceeds **0.8** success rate, while standard TD3 fails completely on **10** of them at equal sample budgets.
- RLingua beats the GPT-4 controller everywhere except PandaStack, where that controller scores **0.44** and both RL variants fail; real Panda transfer succeeds on three task variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An imperfect zero-sample prior controller suffices to guide exploration; final policy quality comes from RL, so the generated controller need not be accurate.
> - Annealing the prior's sampling probability preserves early guidance while preventing a weak teacher from capping asymptotic performance, a general recipe for imperfect demonstrators.
> - Guidance still fails on very long-horizon tasks like stacking; splitting into sub-tasks restores learnability, and manual state extraction blocks end-to-end deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated its capability in solving a wide array of complex tasks, from board games to robotic control. However, a persistent challenge in real-world RL applications, particularly in robotics, is its low sample efficiency and the associated difficulty of reward engineering. RL agents typically learn from scratch through extensive trial-and-error, requiring a large number of interaction samples to achieve proficiency, a problem exacerbated in environments with sparse reward signals.

Current research efforts to mitigate RL's low sample efficiency include Deep RL from Demonstrations (DRLfD), where expert-collected data is used to preload replay buffers or regularize policy learning. While effective, these methods often rely on labor-intensive and not always feasible collection of expert demonstration datasets.

Concurrently, the rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have opened new avenues for aiding RL. LLMs, with their extensive general knowledge and code generation capabilities, have been explored for various roles in RL and robotics. Some studies have focused on leveraging LLMs to generate reward functions, using their in-context learning capacities or human interaction to formulate appropriate reward signals for RL or planning algorithms. Other approaches utilize LLMs for high-level motion planning, decomposing multi-stage tasks into actionable sub-tasks, or generating code for robot primitives. These methods often assume the availability of pre-trained action primitives or focus on higher-level task orchestration.

This work, RLingua, positions itself within this landscape by addressing a specific gap: the direct use of LLMs to generate *low-level, rule-based robot controllers* that can explicitly guide RL exploration and reduce sample complexity from scratch, without relying on pre-collected expert demonstrations or assuming existing high-level primitives. Unlike prior work that uses LLMs for reward design or high-level planning, RLingua focuses on extracting actionable robot motion knowledge to accelerate the core policy learning process, thus complementing existing LLM-assisted RL methodologies. It attempts to provide an initial, albeit imperfect, "warm start" for RL by leveraging LLM-generated code as a source of guiding behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the sample efficiency of Reinforcement Learning algorithms in robotic manipulation tasks by integrating the knowledge embedded within Large Language Models (LLMs). This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, driven by the existing challenges in RL and the emerging capabilities of LLMs.

**Detailed Objectives:**

1. **Extract Prior Knowledge from LLMs for Rule-Based Control:** Develop a methodology for eliciting and formalizing the extensive internal knowledge of LLMs regarding robot motions and coding into preliminary, rule-based robot controllers. This includes designing user-friendly prompt engineering techniques to facilitate the generation of task-specific Python code.
2. **Utilize Imperfect LLM Controllers for Data Collection:** Implement a mechanism where the LLM-generated, rule-based controllers, despite their potential imperfections, are actively used to collect action samples through interaction with the environment. This data is intended to provide initial guidance for the RL agent's exploration, thereby reducing the total number of environment samples required.
3. **Regularize RL Policy Learning with LLM Guidance:** Modify the standard RL policy update mechanisms (specifically the actor loss in TD3) to incorporate an imitation learning term. This term would regularize the policy learning process, nudging the RL agent's policy towards the behaviors demonstrated by the LLM-generated controller.
4. **Refine and Improve LLM-Generated Controllers via RL:** Leverage the reinforcement learning process itself to overcome the inherent imperfections of the rule-based controllers generated by LLMs. The RL agent is expected to learn to correct and optimize the preliminary behaviors, ultimately achieving higher performance than the LLM-generated controller alone.
5. **Demonstrate Effectiveness Across Diverse Robotic Tasks:** Validate the proposed framework's capability in a range of robotic manipulation scenarios, including those with sparse rewards and varying dynamic complexities, in both simulated and real-world environments.

**Motivation:**

The motivation for RLingua stems from several critical limitations in current RL and robot learning paradigms:

- **Low Sample Efficiency of RL:** A fundamental issue with RL is its need for a vast number of interactions with the environment to learn optimal policies. This "sample complexity" is a significant barrier, particularly in real-world robotics where each interaction can be time-consuming, costly, or even unsafe. The problem is compounded in environments offering sparse rewards, where agents struggle to find positive feedback signals.
- **Reliance on Expert Demonstrations:** Existing methods to improve sample efficiency, such as imitation learning or RL from demonstrations, often depend on high-quality, expert-collected data. The process of gathering such data is frequently labor-intensive, expensive, and not universally feasible for all tasks or robot platforms. There is a need for methods that can provide a "warm start" without this human expert bottleneck.
- **Untapped Potential of LLMs:** Large Language Models possess a vast repository of general knowledge and sophisticated reasoning capabilities, including code generation. While previous work has explored LLMs for high-level planning or reward specification in robotics, their potential to directly influence and accelerate low-level control policy learning, especially concerning sample efficiency, remained largely unaddressed.
- **Bridging the Gap between LLM Knowledge and Robotic Action:** Current LLM applications in robotics often involve abstract planning or require pre-existing robot primitives. The challenge is to translate LLM's high-level understanding into directly executable, low-level robot actions that can form a basis for effective RL, even if the initial translation is imperfect.
- **Addressing Imperfections of LLM-Generated Code:** While LLMs can generate code, this code may not always be perfectly suited for real-world robot dynamics or specific environmental constraints, leading to imperfect controllers. A robust framework should not only leverage LLM capabilities but also possess a mechanism to refine and overcome these imperfections through iterative learning.

By addressing these motivations, RLingua aims to make RL more practical and accessible for complex robotic manipulation tasks, reducing the dependency on extensive data and human expertise.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLingua is a framework designed to enhance the sample efficiency of Reinforcement Learning by integrating the knowledge of Large Language Models (LLMs). The methodology consists of two primary, interconnected stages: LLM Prompt Design and Reinforcement Learning with the LLM-Generated Controller.

**Overall Framework (Refer to Fig. 1):**
The core idea is to leverage an LLM to generate a preliminary, rule-based robot controller (`π_LLM`). This controller then assists an RL agent (`π_θπ`) by providing guided exploration data, which helps in faster policy learning. The RL process, in turn, refines the policy beyond the initial capabilities of the LLM-generated controller.

**A. LLM Prompt Design:**
This stage focuses on extracting the internal knowledge of LLMs in the form of an executable Python rule-based controller. The generated controller is typically imperfect due to the LLM's limited understanding of precise task dynamics and robot motion constraints. Two approaches for prompt design are proposed:

1. **Prompt Design with Human Feedback (Refer to Fig. 2):**
 This method is suitable for tasks with flexible dynamics but relatively simple control logic. It involves an iterative process:
 - **Task Descriptions (P-1 to P-5):** A sequence of prompts is provided to the LLM.
 - **(P-1) Robot Information:** Details about the robot type (Franka Emika Panda), control variables (e.g., 3-dimensional gripper position, Euler angles), and robot constraints.
 - **(P-2) General Task Descriptions:** A clear description of the specific task (e.g., pick and place a cube) and information about objects involved.
 - **(P-3) Question Description:** A direct question prompting the LLM to break down the long-horizon task into elementary phases of end-effector movement.
 - **(P-4) Response Template:** A standardized format for the LLM's response, guiding it to describe each phase (e.g., "Phase [NUM]: The gripper should move [CHOICE: to, around, above] [LOC]").
 - **(P-5) Rules:** Specific instructions on how to use the template and provide task hints.
 The output of this first step is a phase-by-phase description of the robot's movement.
 - **Code Guidelines (P-6 to P-7):** Building upon the phase descriptions, further prompts guide the LLM to generate Python code for the controller.
 - **(P-6) Control Inputs:** Clearly defined input variables for the controller (e.g., `gripper_position`, `object_position`, `gripper_finger_distance`, `target_position`). This allows the LLM to select relevant variables.
 - **(P-7) Rules:** Hints for generating a rule-based Python controller, including specific package usages (e.g., `numpy`) and considerations for motion constraints.
 - **Human Feedback (P-8):** After the preliminary Python controller is generated and executed (e.g., in a simulation or real robot), a non-expert user can provide natural language feedback based on observed performance failures. The LLM then uses this feedback to revise and improve the controller code. This iterative refinement process is designed to be user-friendly, not requiring expert knowledge in task planning.

2. **Prompt Design With A Code Template (Refer to Fig. 3):**
 This approach is employed for long-horizon tasks with high-dimensional state and action spaces where LLMs might struggle to generate executable code solely from human feedback.
 - **Task Descriptions and Phases:** Similar to the first method, task descriptions (P-1 to P-5) are used to elicit task completion phases.
 - **Code Template (P-9):** A pre-defined Python code template, based on the assumption of basic robot motions, is provided as part of the code guidelines (P-6, P-7). The LLM is instructed to follow this template when generating the controller code. This template includes pre-defined helper functions (e.g., `is_close`, `normalize_euler_angle`, `get_action`) and a structured `if-else` control flow to guide the LLM in constructing the low-level policy. This helps the LLM to navigate complex multi-stage tasks by cumulatively executing basic movements.

**B. Reinforcement Learning With the LLM Controller:**
This stage integrates the LLM-generated controller into an online RL training process to improve sample efficiency.

- **Dual Replay Buffers:** Two separate replay buffers are maintained:
 - `R_LLM`: Stores data generated by the LLM controller (`π_LLM`).
 - `R_RL`: Stores data generated by the policy currently being trained (`π_θπ`).
- **Modified Actor Loss:** The core RL algorithm used is TD3 (Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient). The standard TD3 actor loss (`ℓ_RL`) is augmented with an imitation learning loss (`ℓ_IM`):
 `ℓ_actor(θ_π) = ℓ_RL(θ_π) + λ_IM * ℓ_IM(θ_π)`
 - `ℓ_RL(θ_π)`: The standard TD3 actor loss, based on Q-values from data in `R_RL`.
 - `ℓ_IM(θ_π)`: An L2 loss penalizing the difference between the RL agent's action and the LLM controller's action for states sampled from `R_LLM`. This term acts as a regularization, guiding the RL policy towards the LLM-generated behaviors. `λ_IM` is a hyperparameter controlling its weight.
- **Combined Data Collection:** During rollouts, actions are sampled from either the LLM controller (`π_LLM`) with a probability `p_LLM`, or from the current training policy (`π_θπ`) with a probability `1 - p_LLM`. This allows the LLM controller to guide exploration and generate useful initial data.
- **Annealing `p_LLM`:** Since the LLM-generated controller is imperfect, its influence is gradually reduced over time. The probability `p_LLM` decays exponentially with each environment step: `p_LLM^(k+1) = λ_annl * p_LLM^k`, where `k` is the number of environment steps and `λ_annl` is the annealing rate. This strategy allows the RL agent to eventually surpass the limitations of the initial LLM guidance.
- **Base RL Algorithm:** TD3 is used, and for goal-oriented tasks, Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) is also incorporated to further improve learning from sparse rewards.
- **Control Assumption:** The approach assumes only a basic movement controller is available for the robot, allowing small 3D gripper position and Euler angle changes, precluding the use of high-level motion planners for long-distance movements.

The overall methodology aims to harness the broad knowledge of LLMs to provide a strong initialization and continuous guidance for RL, thereby making the learning process significantly more efficient and robust.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of RLingua was evaluated through extensive experiments in simulated environments (panda_gym and RLBench) and real-world robot experiments (Sim2Real). The findings demonstrate improvements in sample efficiency and success rates compared to standard RL methods.

**A. Simulations in the panda_gym Environment:**
Five goal-oriented tasks with the Franka Emika Panda robot were used: PandaReach, PandaPush, PandaPickAndPlace, PandaSlide, and PandaStack. These tasks varied in dynamic interactions. LLM-generated controllers for these tasks utilized the "prompt design with human feedback" approach.

- **PandaReach:** A simple task without dynamic interactions. The GPT-4 generated controller achieved a 100% success rate. RLingua matched this performance while using less than 25% of the environment steps required by standard TD3 to achieve a 0.8 success rate.
- **PandaPush:** RLingua reached a 0.8 success rate using less than 50% of the environment steps compared to standard TD3.
- **PandaSlide and PandaPickAndPlace:** These tasks involve sensitive interactions between the gripper and the cube, leading to imperfect GPT-4 generated controllers. RLingua achieved significantly higher success rates for the same number of environment steps than standard TD3, which showed slow improvement over millions of samples. This indicates RLingua's ability to overcome the imperfections of the LLM-generated controllers through RL.
- **PandaStack:** Both standard RL and RLingua struggled with this task, achieving low success rates, although the GPT-4 controller showed a 0.44 success rate. The authors hypothesize this is due to the task's long horizon and increased reward sparseness, suggesting potential for task decomposition.

Overall, in panda_gym, RLingua consistently demonstrated improved sample efficiency and surpassed or matched the performance of the LLM-generated controllers by integrating RL.

**B. Simulations in the RLBench Environment:**
Twelve diverse robot manipulation tasks from RLBench were selected, covering various interaction types and requiring both gripper displacement and rotation. For these more complex tasks, the "prompt design with a code template" was utilized for LLM controller generation.

- **High Success Rates for RLingua:** RLingua achieved success rates greater than 0.8 on all 12 tasks.
- **Failure of Standard TD3:** In contrast, the standard TD3 algorithm failed completely on 10 of these 12 tasks with the same number of environment steps. This highlights the substantial challenge posed by sparse rewards in these tasks for standard RL.
- **Overcoming Infeasible Paths:** The experiments also showed that RLingua was capable of overcoming issues like infeasible gripper paths generated by the LLM controllers, leveraging RL to refine the trajectories and achieve higher success rates.

These results indicate that RLingua effectively guides RL exploration in complex, sparsely rewarded environments, enabling successful policy learning where standard RL approaches fail.

**C. Real Robot Experiments (Sim2Real):**
The learned policies from RLingua were transferred to a real Franka Emika Panda robot for the `put_item_in_container` task from RLBench. An Azure Kinect camera was used for real-time object detection and state extraction.

- **Single Cube Pick-and-Place:** The robot successfully picked up a single cube and placed it into a container, demonstrating the foundational pick-and-place ability.
- **Single Cube with Interference:** The policy showed robustness by successfully picking up the cube and placing it, even when the cube's position was continuously altered with a stick by external interference. This indicated the policy's adaptability to dynamic, unexpected conditions.
- **Multiple Cubes Sequentially:** In a long-horizon task, the robot successfully picked and placed multiple labeled cubes ("B", "A", "I", "D", "U") into the container in a specific sequence.

The successes in these real-world tasks validate the transferability of policies learned using RLingua in simulation, confirming its practical deployment capabilities and robustness against real-world variability and interference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLingua framework represents an advance in addressing the persistent challenge of low sample efficiency in Reinforcement Learning for robotic manipulations. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

**Addresses a Core RL Challenge:** RLingua directly tackles one of the primary impediments to widespread RL adoption in robotics: the need for vast quantities of interaction data. By significantly reducing the sample complexity, the framework makes RL-based robot learning more feasible for real-world applications where data collection is costly, time-consuming, or risky.

**Novel Integration of LLMs with Low-Level Control:** The work proposes a novel approach to leverage Large Language Models by extracting their knowledge to generate imperfect, low-level, rule-based robot controllers. This differs from prior research that primarily used LLMs for high-level planning or reward function generation. It demonstrates a direct mechanism for LLM knowledge to provide initial behavioral guidance for RL agents learning from scratch, thereby bridging the gap between abstract language understanding and concrete robot actions.

**Reduced Reliance on Expert Demonstrations:** By generating preliminary controllers through LLM prompting, RLingua lessens the dependency on labor-intensive human expert demonstrations. This broadens the accessibility of advanced robot learning techniques, as it allows non-experts to initiate the learning process for new tasks with natural language instructions.

**Robustness through Iterative Refinement:** The framework exhibits the ability to refine and surpass the limitations of imperfect LLM-generated controllers. The integration of RL effectively corrects and optimizes the initial rule-based behaviors, leading to policies that outperform either the standard RL baseline or the LLM-generated controller alone. This adaptability is crucial for real-world deployment where initial LLM-generated code might not perfectly account for complex physics or environmental dynamics.

**Demonstrated Generalizability and Real-World Transferability:** The effectiveness of RLingua was shown across a diverse set of robotic manipulation tasks in two different simulation environments (panda_gym and RLBench). Furthermore, the successful transfer of learned policies to a real Franka Emika Panda robot, capable of handling interference and sequential multi-object tasks, underscores the practical utility and robustness of the approach in realistic settings.

**Implications for Future Research and Development:**
- **End-to-End Multimodal RL:** The identification of manual state information extraction as a limitation points towards a future where multimodal LLMs could enable end-to-end RLingua-like frameworks, processing raw sensor data directly for state estimation and action generation.
- **Combined LLM Benefits:** The approach is complementary to LLM-assisted reward design methods. Future algorithms could integrate both LLM-guided exploration and LLM-generated rewards for compounded benefits, maximizing the overall practicality of RL agents.
- **Personalized Robot Control:** By allowing non-experts to provide task descriptions and feedback in natural language, RLingua moves towards achieving more personalized robot controllers that can be obtained and refined without deep expert knowledge.

In summary, RLingua contributes to making reinforcement learning a more practical and accessible tool for complex robotic manipulation. By effectively integrating the cognitive capabilities of LLMs with the adaptive learning power of RL, it offers a pathway to accelerating the development and deployment of autonomous robotic systems.
