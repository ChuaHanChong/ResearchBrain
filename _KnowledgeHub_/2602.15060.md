# CLOT: Closed-Loop Global Motion Tracking for Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> A closed-loop global motion tracking framework, CLOT, enables drift-free whole-body humanoid teleoperation by integrating high-frequency localization feedback for long-horizon tasks. This system from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Lab shows an order of magnitude reduction in tracking errors compared to prior methods, coupled with robust performance on a full-sized humanoid.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body humanoid teleoperation systems suffer from accumulated global pose drift, limiting long-horizon task stability and safety.
- A fundamental trade-off exists between motion agility and long-term stability in current teleoperation frameworks, especially for full-sized humanoids.
- Collecting high-quality, long-horizon loco-manipulation data for training general-purpose humanoids is challenging due to these stability issues.

## Method

- A closed-loop global motion tracking system integrates high-frequency localization feedback from OptiTrack with an RL policy.
- Introduces "==Observation Pre==-shift," a data-driven randomization strategy, and a Transformer-based actor-critic network for smooth, stable global error correction.
- Leverages an Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) and a custom 20-hour human motion dataset to ensure natural and physically consistent robot behaviors.

## Results

- Reduced tracking error metrics by over an order of magnitude compared to prior state-of-the-art methods like TWIST2 in simulation.
- Maintained stable, drift-free teleoperation for 30 minutes continuously on the PNDbotics Adam Pro humanoid, with consistently low tracking errors.
- Successfully executed complex loco-manipulation tasks in the real world and demonstrated exceptional robustness to strong external disturbances, recovering balance smoothly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly addressing global pose drift through closed-loop feedback allows for stable, long-horizon humanoid teleoperation, crucial for practical applications.
> - The "Observation Pre-shift" technique effectively teaches RL policies to perform smooth global error corrections by implicitly learning compliant motion interpolation.
> - A Transformer architecture is vital for learning long-horizon spatiotemporal dependencies, enabling robust tracking and leveraging strategies like Observation Pre-shift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "CLOT" addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in the field of humanoid robotics: **achieving stable, long-horizon, whole-body teleoperation without accumulated global pose drift, especially for large, dynamically complex humanoids.** This work is situated at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL), whole-body control (WBC), human-robot interaction, and robust sim-to-real transfer.

Historically, humanoid teleoperation has been a cornerstone for generating complex, embodied demonstrations, which are crucial for advancing general-purpose humanoid robots. Early attempts often decoupled upper-body manipulation from lower-body locomotion, relying on external inputs like joysticks or exoskeletons. While stable, these methods limited the robot's dynamic potential and natural coordination.

Recent advancements, particularly those leveraging sim-to-real reinforcement learning, have enabled impressive agile skills in humanoids, from robust locomotion to highly dynamic maneuvers like parkour and dancing. These "motion-tracking" paradigms use human motion references to synthesize natural and dynamically feasible robot behaviors, often employing high-quality motion retargeting to bridge the morphological gap. Works like TWIST2, which the authors compare against, represent significant progress in portable whole-body teleoperation systems.

However, a fundamental trade-off has persisted: **motion agility versus long-term stability.** Many existing whole-body tracking systems primarily operate in the robot's *local frame*, neglecting *global pose feedback*. This leads to **accumulated global drift**, a severe limitation for extended operations, especially for full-sized humanoids with higher centers of mass and more challenging control dynamics. This drift not only degrades performance but also poses significant safety risks and can lead to task failure as the robot deviates from the operator's intended global trajectory. While some closed-loop systems have emerged, they often lack full whole-body tracking capabilities.

CLOT distinguishes itself by directly tackling this global drift problem. It proposes a novel approach that integrates **high-frequency localization feedback to achieve closed-loop global motion tracking** within a whole-body teleoperation framework. This is a significant step forward because it allows for "drift-free human-to-humanoid mimicry over long time horizons," enabling robots to maintain a consistent spatial relationship with the operator in the world frame.

Furthermore, CLOT recognizes that simply applying global tracking rewards in RL can lead to brittle or aggressive corrections. Therefore, it introduces **novel training strategies** like a data-driven randomization technique ("Observation Pre-shift") and an adversarial motion prior (AMP) to ensure smooth, stable, and natural-looking global corrections. The creation of a **custom 20-hour human motion dataset** specifically curated for humanoid teleoperation, addressing the limitations of existing animation-focused datasets, further strengthens its contribution to the practical deployment of humanoids.

In essence, CLOT pushes the boundaries of humanoid teleoperation by providing a robust, stable, and high-fidelity system that overcomes the long-standing issue of global pose drift, thereby facilitating the efficient collection of diverse and complex real-world data essential for the next generation of general-purpose humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the CLOT research is to **enable robust, high-fidelity, and drift-free whole-body humanoid teleoperation over long time horizons on full-sized humanoid robots.** This goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Advancing General-Purpose Humanoid Robots:** The paper states that "Collecting high-quality, long-horizon loco-manipulation data is a key prerequisite for advancing general-purpose humanoid robots." Teleoperation systems are crucial tools for efficiently generating these complex, embodied demonstrations that can then be used to train autonomous policies. Therefore, improving teleoperation directly contributes to the broader goal of more capable humanoids.

2. **Addressing the Trade-off between Agility and Stability:** Existing whole-body teleoperation systems struggle with a fundamental conflict: achieving agile, coordinated motions often comes at the cost of long-term stability due to accumulated global pose drift. The authors are motivated to resolve this trade-off, aiming for a system that provides both "whole-body flexibility and coordination" and "long-term stability."

3. **Mitigating Global Pose Drift:** The core problem CLOT seeks to solve is the "accumulated global pose drift, particularly on full-sized humanoids." This drift, which occurs when learning-based tracking methods operate solely in the robot's local frame, introduces severe safety risks, causes task failures, and limits the duration and complexity of teleoperated tasks. The motivation is to create a system that actively synchronizes operator and robot poses in a closed loop to eliminate this drift.

4. **Enabling Real-World High-Dynamic and Contact-Rich Tasks:** For humanoids to be truly useful, they must be able to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks in diverse, real-world environments, often involving dynamic movements and physical contact. Current systems' instability due to drift makes such tasks challenging or impossible over extended periods. CLOT aims to unlock these capabilities.

5. **Improving Learning-Based Control Strategies:** The authors acknowledge that directly imposing global tracking rewards in reinforcement learning can lead to "aggressive and brittle corrections." This motivates the development of novel training techniques, such as the data-driven randomization strategy ("Observation Pre-shift") and the adversarial motion prior, to facilitate smooth, stable, and natural global corrections within the RL framework.

6. **Curating Relevant Data:** Existing motion datasets, primarily designed for animation, often contain motions incompatible with the kinematic and dynamic properties of physical humanoids. This motivates the creation of a "carefully curated human motion data" set tailored specifically for humanoid teleoperation, ensuring physical consistency and usability.

In summary, CLOT's objectives are to build a robust teleoperation system that not only allows humanoids to precisely mimic human movements but also ensures they remain globally aligned with the operator over extended periods, enabling safer, more reliable, and more versatile real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CLOT system employs a comprehensive, multi-phase methodology integrating real-time motion capture, advanced reinforcement learning (RL), and a closed-loop control architecture for robust sim-to-real deployment.

**Phase 1: Data Pipeline**
1. **Human Motion Data Collection:** The team collected over 20 hours of high-fidelity full-body human motion data using an OptiTrack optical motion capture system (120 Hz). A strict protocol was followed to ensure physically consistent contacts and motion patterns compatible with humanoid kinematics, precluding unstable behaviors (e.g., tiptoeing, excessive Center of Mass deviation). The dataset includes diverse categories from basic locomotion to high-dynamic whole-body movements (e.g., martial arts, dance). Hi5 gloves were used to capture finger poses at 60 Hz.
2. **Real-time Motion Retargeting:** To bridge the morphological gap between human and robot, captured human motions are retargeted online using the **Pinocchio inverse kinematics (IK) solver**. This minimizes tracking errors between robot frames and corresponding human keypoints by solving for joint velocities. A post-optimization clamping step is applied for safety.

**Phase 2: RL Training Framework**
The core of CLOT is an RL-based policy trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within the MuJoCo simulation environment (specifically mjlab).

1. **Problem Formulation:** The task is framed as a goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the agent receives proprioceptive state information and a target motion goal, outputs PD controller targets for joints, and receives scalar rewards.
2. **Asymmetric Actor-Critic Observations:** The actor receives its proprioceptive state (base angular velocity, projected gravity, joint positions, joint velocities, previous action) and a *look-ahead* target motion goal. A history buffer (10 timesteps) is used to mitigate partial observability.
3. **Tracking-Oriented Reward Formulation:** A reward function is designed to encourage accurate teleoperation while penalizing unsafe behaviors. Tracking-related terms (e.g., body pose, joint kinematics, keypoint alignment, foot motion) use an exponentially decaying reward (r = exp(-err/σ)). Penalty terms discourage excessive torques, abrupt actions, contact instabilities, joint limit violations, and collisions. Adaptive error tolerance (σ) is used for stable optimization.
4. **Domain Randomization:** To improve sim-to-real transfer and robustness, physical parameters (body mass, inertia, joint damping, friction, actuator strength, contact properties) and observation conditions (noise, control delay) are randomized during training.
5. **Curriculum Learning:** A curriculum learning strategy is adopted, where penalties, noise, and randomization are initially reduced and then progressively increased as the policy's tracking performance improves. An adaptive sampling strategy prioritizes more challenging motion instances from the custom dataset.
6. **Key Design Components for Robust RL Policy Training:**
 - **Observation Pre-shift (Novel Strategy):** To prevent aggressive corrections often associated with direct global tracking, a randomized observation pre-shift strategy is introduced. During training, with a random probability, the *reference observation window* (future target goal `g_t+1+δ:t+10+δ`) is shifted forward by a temporal offset `δ`. Crucially, the *tracking reward* remains aligned with the *current time* (`g_t`). This decouples the observation trajectory from the reward trajectory, implicitly teaching the policy to smoothly interpolate motions and achieve compliant global corrections, even under large deviations. This is disabled during inference.
 - **Transformer-Based Actor-Critic Network:** Both the actor and critic networks are parameterized using a Transformer architecture. This design is chosen to effectively capture long-horizon spatiotemporal dependencies in the observations (historical states, current feedback, future goals), which is crucial for the stability and expressiveness required by the observation pre-shift mechanism and implicit interpolation.
 - **Motion Prior Reward (AMP):** To regularize policy behaviors towards human-like motion styles and suppress unnatural artifacts (e.g., overly aggressive joint torques), an Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) framework is incorporated. An adversarial discriminator (`D_phi`) is trained to distinguish between generated robot motions and real human motions from the dataset. The policy receives a reward (`r_amp = log D_phi(s_t)`) for generating motions that fool the discriminator, encouraging stylistic consistency while maintaining task-level tracking objectives.

**Phase 3: Closed-Loop Real-World Teleoperation System**
1. **Real-time Synchronization:** The system establishes a closed-loop by synchronizing the human operator and the robot in the global frame.
2. **Deployment Pipeline:** The OptiTrack system provides high-precision human motion and robot global pose (via markers, Fig. 5) feedback at 120 Hz. Retargeted human motions provide reference commands to the trained policy.
3. **Control Execution:** The policy, running at 50 Hz on an Intel Core i7-14700KF CPU, outputs joint-level position commands. These commands are executed by a low-level PD controller operating at 400 Hz on the 31-DoF PNDbotics Adam Pro 2 humanoid robot.
4. **Immersive Feedback:** First-person visual feedback from the robot's head-mounted camera is streamed to a Meta Quest 3 headset, enabling immersive human-in-the-loop control.

This comprehensive approach allows CLOT to combine the agility of learning-based whole-body control with the stability of closed-loop global pose feedback, facilitated by novel training techniques and a specialized dataset, culminating in a robust real-world teleoperation system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for CLOT, both in simulation and real-world, rigorously evaluate the system's performance, generalization, and robustness.

**A. Simulation Experiments (Unitree G1 & PNDbotics Adam Pro)**

1. **Comparison with Prior Methods (Q1 - Generalization):**
 - CLOT was compared against **TWIST2**, a leading whole-body teleoperation system, on the **Unitree G1** humanoid.
 - **Result:** CLOT achieved a significant reduction in all tracking error metrics (E_mgbp, E_mlbp, E_mgrp, E_mdp) by **more than an order of magnitude**. For example, E_mgbp dropped from 5.094 (TWIST2) to 0.056 (CLOT).
 - CLOT also demonstrated substantially smoother and more stable joint-level control (lower M_jt and σ_mjt).
 - **Finding:** This demonstrates CLOT's superior tracking accuracy, particularly due to its explicit closed-loop global pose feedback, and its generalization capability across different humanoid morphologies (even with a policy trained primarily for Adam Pro).

2. **Ablation Study (Q2 - Component Contribution):**
 - Ablations were performed on the **PNDbotics Adam Pro** to evaluate the contributions of the Transformer architecture, adversarial motion prior (AMP), and observation pre-shift.
 - **Transformer-only (without AMP and Pre-shift):** Showed substantially larger tracking errors (e.g., E_mgbp of 0.410 vs. 0.116 for full CLOT) and higher torque statistics, indicating less stable and less precise control.
 - **Observation Pre-shift:**
 - When simulating abrupt reference motion changes, policies *with* observation pre-shift consistently achieved **lower fluctuation and improved smoothness** for both Absolute Mean Joint Torque (AMJT) and Absolute Mean DOF Velocity (AMDV) during error recovery.
 - **Finding:** This highlights that observation pre-shift is crucial for learning smooth, stable trajectory error correction under large deviations, preventing aggressive and unstable recovery behaviors.
 - **Interaction between Pre-shift and Transformer:**
 - An **MLP-based policy** *failed to converge* and resulted in a fall when exposed to a significant pre-shift (2.0s).
 - A **Transformer-based policy**, however, successfully handled the large offset with stable tracking.
 - **Finding:** This conclusively demonstrates that the Transformer architecture's capacity to capture long-horizon spatiotemporal dependencies is *essential* for effectively leveraging the observation pre-shift mechanism and achieving stable implicit motion interpolation.

**B. Real-World Teleoperation (PNDbotics Adam Pro) (Q3 - Long-horizon, Drift-free Stability & Robustness)**

1. **Long-Horizon Loco-Manipulation Performance:**
 - CLOT was evaluated on three complex tasks: desktop manipulation (T1), table-to-shelf bin transfer (T2), and ground-to-table bucket placement (T3).
 - **Result:** The robot executed these tasks with **fluent and coordinated whole-body motion**, demonstrating high success rates (SR) and competitive average task success times (ATST). For T1 (desktop manipulation), CLOT achieved 27/30 SR in 25s, outperforming an MPC-based baseline (20/30 SR in 22s), thanks to its expanded workspace and whole-body coordination.
 - **Finding:** CLOT enables efficient and high-precision loco-manipulation over extended horizons in real-world settings.

2. **Extended-Duration Continuous Teleoperation:**
 - A **30-minute uninterrupted teleoperation session** was conducted.
 - **Result:** All tracking error metrics (E_mgbp, E_mgrp, E_mdp) remained **consistently low and stable** throughout the entire duration (Fig. 10).
 - **Finding:** This is a crucial result, demonstrating the effectiveness of the closed-loop global pose feedback in actively suppressing drift accumulation over long periods, thereby ensuring reliable and stable teleoperation.

3. **Robustness Performance under Strong External Disturbances:**
 - The robot was subjected to **strong external kicks** during teleoperation.
 - **Result:** Despite high-impulse posterior kicks, the robot **maintained balance and smoothly resumed its motion trajectory** (Fig. 9). Joint torques and velocities remained well within physical limits during both impact and recovery (Fig. 11).
 - **Finding:** CLOT exhibits exceptional robustness to real-world perturbations, a critical feature for safe and reliable humanoid deployment.

In summary, the results unequivocally validate CLOT's ability to achieve robust, high-fidelity, and drift-free whole-body teleoperation. The simulation experiments confirm the efficacy of its novel training components and architectural choices, while the real-world trials demonstrate its practical applicability for complex, long-duration, and dynamic tasks on a full-sized humanoid, even under significant disturbances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLOT system represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, with substantial implications for research and practical applications.

**1. Solving the Global Pose Drift Problem:** The most critical contribution of CLOT is its successful resolution of the long-standing challenge of accumulated global pose drift in whole-body humanoid teleoperation. By integrating high-frequency localization feedback into a closed-loop control system, CLOT enables truly drift-free human-to-humanoid mimicry. This is particularly impactful for full-sized, dynamically complex humanoids, which are highly susceptible to such drift and its associated safety risks and task failures. This breakthrough directly addresses a fundamental limitation that has hindered the widespread utility of advanced teleoperation systems.

**2. Enabling Long-Horizon, Complex Tasks:** With stable, drift-free operation, humanoids can now reliably perform complex loco-manipulation tasks over extended periods in contact-rich environments. This capability is crucial for scenarios requiring continuous human supervision or intricate sequential actions, such as assembly, maintenance, or disaster response.

**3. Accelerating Humanoid Data Collection:** A primary motivation for this work is the need for high-quality, long-horizon, embodied demonstration data to train general-purpose humanoid robots. CLOT provides an efficient and robust tool for generating this data. By allowing operators to fluently control a humanoid through diverse and dynamic motions, the system can rapidly build extensive datasets that are vital for advancing autonomous humanoid intelligence and developing more capable, generalized policies.

**4. Novel Methodological Contributions in RL:**
 - The **"Observation Pre-shift"** strategy is a novel and elegant solution for achieving smooth and stable global error corrections in RL-based motion tracking. It implicitly teaches the policy compliant motion interpolation, preventing the aggressive and brittle behaviors often seen when directly penalizing large deviations. This technique has potential applicability beyond teleoperation to other motion control problems where robust recovery from tracking errors is critical.
 - The effective integration of a **Transformer-based policy** to capture long-horizon spatiotemporal dependencies, especially in conjunction with the pre-shift strategy, demonstrates a powerful architectural choice for complex robotic control.
 - The inclusion of an **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP)** further refines the generated motions, ensuring they are naturalistic and human-like, which enhances both aesthetic quality and safety.

**5. High-Quality, Tailored Human Motion Dataset:** The creation of a 20-hour, carefully curated human motion dataset specifically for humanoid teleoperation is a valuable resource. Unlike existing animation-focused datasets, CLOT's dataset is optimized for the kinematic and dynamic constraints of physical humanoids, improving the realism and physical feasibility of learned behaviors. This dataset can potentially serve as a benchmark for future research in humanoid control.

**6. Demonstrated Robustness and Generalization:** The system's validated performance across different robot platforms (simulated Unitree G1 and real-world PNDbotics Adam Pro) and its exceptional robustness to strong external disturbances in real-world scenarios highlight its practical viability and reliability in unpredictable environments.

**Potential Future Impact:**
CLOT paves the way for a new generation of human-robot interaction and remote operation capabilities. It could enable:
- More effective telepresence robots for hazardous environments (e.g., disaster zones, space exploration).
- Enhanced capabilities for assistive humanoids in complex domestic or industrial settings.
- Faster iteration cycles for robotics researchers developing new humanoid skills, by providing a reliable platform for data collection and policy testing.

The acknowledged future work on exploring "lightweight, cost-effective global localization systems and VR-based human motion tracking" suggests a clear path towards making such advanced teleoperation systems more accessible and deployable in a broader range of real-world scenarios beyond high-end motion capture labs. This vision emphasizes the long-term goal of democratizing advanced humanoid control.
