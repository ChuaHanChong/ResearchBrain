# One-Step Diffusion Policy: Fast Visuomotor Policies via Diffusion Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> One-Step Diffusion Policy (OneDP) from NVIDIA and UT Austin introduces a method to accelerate visuomotor policies by distilling multi-step diffusion models into a single-step action generator. This approach enables real-time robotic control, achieving an action prediction frequency of 62.5 Hz in real-world settings, a 42x improvement over previous diffusion policies, while maintaining or improving success rates.

## Problem

- Traditional diffusion policies for robotics, like Diffusion Policy, suffer from slow inference speed due to iterative denoising steps (e.g., 1.49 Hz), limiting their real-time application.
- This slow inference is inadequate for dynamic tasks, human interaction, or rapidly changing environments that demand quick robotic responses.
- Previous attempts to accelerate diffusion policies for robotics still required multiple sampling iterations and faced performance degradation or slow convergence.

## Method

- **One-Step Diffusion Policy** (OneDP) distills knowledge from pre-trained, multi-step diffusion policies into a single-step implicit action generator (Gθ).
- The distillation minimizes a ==Kullback-Leibler (KL)== divergence-based loss across diffused action samples, approximating the score of the generator and the pre-trained policy.
- OneDP offers two variants: OneDP-S (Stochastic) which uses an auxiliary generator score network, and OneDP-D (Deterministic) which directly solves the score.

## Results

- OneDP achieved an action prediction frequency of **62.5** Hz in real-world settings (7 ms per prediction), a **42x** speedup compared to Diffusion Policy's **1.49** Hz (66 ms).
- In simulation, OneDP-S achieved an average success rate of **0.843**, slightly outperforming the pre-trained Diffusion Policy (**0.829** at 100 steps).
- OneDP demonstrated enhanced robustness in dynamic real-world tasks, achieving **100%** success in a task with human interference compared to **80%** for Diffusion Policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling diffusion policies through KL divergence over diffused samples can achieve single-step generation without sacrificing performance, and can even improve it by avoiding cumulative errors.
> - Warm-starting the distilled generator and its auxiliary network from the pre-trained teacher significantly accelerates convergence and preserves performance.
> - Stochastic policies (OneDP-S) generally demonstrate greater versatility and often higher success rates compared to their deterministic counterparts (OneDP-D) in complex robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of generative AI, particularly **diffusion models**, and **robotics control**, specifically **imitation learning** (behavior cloning).

Diffusion models (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020) have emerged as a dominant paradigm in generative AI, achieving remarkable success in diverse tasks such as text-to-image generation (e.g., DALL-E, Stable Diffusion), video generation, and various forms of reinforcement learning. Their strength lies in their ability to model complex data distributions by iteratively denoising data from a noisy input.

Recently, these powerful generative capabilities have been extended to robotics, leading to the development of "diffusion policies." Pioneering work like Chi et al. (2023)'s "Diffusion Policy" has demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation through imitation learning. By treating robot actions as data to be generated, diffusion models can learn robust and expressive policies from human demonstrations.

However, a fundamental limitation of traditional diffusion models is their **slow inference speed**. The generative process requires traversing a "reverse diffusion chain," involving numerous (tens to thousands) iterative evaluations of a denoising neural network. This iterative nature, while crucial for high-quality generation, poses a significant challenge for real-time robotic applications that demand rapid response times and often operate under computational constraints. For instance, the original Diffusion Policy runs at a mere 1.49 Hz, which is inadequate for dynamic tasks, tasks involving human interaction, or quickly changing environments where a robot needs to react instantaneously.

The broader research landscape has seen efforts to accelerate diffusion models, primarily in the image generation domain. These efforts generally fall into two categories:
1. **Improved Numerical Solvers:** Interpreting diffusion models as Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) or Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) and employing advanced numerical solvers to reduce the required number of sampling steps (e.g., Song et al., 2020a; Karras et al., 2022). While effective, these often still require several iterative steps for reasonable performance.
2. **Model Distillation:** Distilling knowledge from a large, slow "teacher" diffusion model into a smaller, faster "student" model that can generate high-quality samples in one or very few steps. This is typically achieved through techniques like Kullback-Leibler (KL) optimization or adversarial training (e.g., Salimans & Ho, 2022; Song et al., 2023).

Crucially, the application of these acceleration techniques specifically to **diffusion policies for robotic control** has been largely underexplored. While Consistency Policy (Prasad et al., 2024) attempted to accelerate diffusion policies using Consistency Trajectory Models (CTM), it still required multiple sampling iterations and faced challenges with performance degradation and slow convergence.

The present work, One-Step Diffusion Policy (OneDP), directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to significantly enhance the practicality of diffusion policies in robotics by achieving a truly *one-step* action generation process through diffusion distillation, thereby enabling real-time, responsive control without sacrificing performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the One-Step Diffusion Policy (OneDP) is to overcome the inherent slowness of existing diffusion policies in robotics, which severely limits their utility in real-time and dynamic environments. The paper explicitly states that the slow generation process, stemming from the necessity of iterative denoising steps (potentially thousands of times), "presents significant limitations for their application in robotic tasks."

Specifically, the authors highlight that policies like the Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), running at approximately 1.49 Hz, are insufficient for scenarios demanding quick responses and low computational latency. While static tasks like block stacking might tolerate slower inference, dynamic activities involving human interference or rapidly changing environments necessitate much quicker control responses.

Given this critical bottleneck, the key objectives of the OneDP research are:

1. **Achieve Single-Step Action Generation:** To distill the complex, multi-step knowledge of pre-trained diffusion policies into a compact model capable of generating actions in a single forward pass, maximizing inference efficiency.
2. **Significantly Accelerate Inference Speed:** To boost the action prediction frequency by an order of magnitude or more, making diffusion policies viable for real-time, computationally sensitive robotic applications.
3. **Maintain or Improve Performance:** Crucially, to achieve this acceleration without compromising (and ideally enhancing) the high success rates and robust behavior exhibited by the original, slower diffusion policies.
4. **Ensure Training Efficiency:** To ensure that the distillation process is computationally efficient, requiring minimal additional training cost beyond the initial pre-training of the teacher policy.

In essence, OneDP aims to make diffusion policies a practical, deployable solution for a broader range of real-world robotic tasks by addressing their core Achilles' heel: speed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The One-Step Diffusion Policy (OneDP) introduces a novel approach to accelerate visuomotor policies by distilling knowledge from pre-trained, multi-step diffusion policies into a single-step action generator. This distillation is achieved by minimizing a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence-based loss across the diffusion chain.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of Diffusion Policies

The paper first briefly reviews diffusion models, which operate by adding noise (forward diffusion) and then learning to reverse this process (denoising). For robotics, diffusion policies (e.g., Chi et al., 2023) predict an "action chunk" (sequence of actions) conditioned on visual observations. While powerful, sampling actions requires iteratively traversing the reverse diffusion chain, making it slow. The policy is represented as a conditional diffusion model $\pi_\theta(A_0|O)$, where $A_0$ is the action chunk and $O$ are observations.

#### 3.2. One-Step Diffusion Policy (OneDP)

The core idea of OneDP is to train a one-step implicit action generator, $G_\theta$, which directly outputs an action chunk $A_\theta$ from noise $z$ and observation $O$: $A_\theta = G_\theta(z, O)$. The goal is to align the distribution generated by $G_\theta$, $p_{G_\theta}$, with that of the pre-trained diffusion policy, $p_{\pi_\phi}$.

Inspired by score distillation techniques from text-to-3D generation (SDS, VSD), OneDP minimizes the reverse KL divergence $D_{KL}(p_{G_\theta}||p_{\pi_\phi})$. Directly computing this is intractable due to implicit distributions and complex integrals. However, its gradient with respect to $\theta$ (the generator parameters) can be expressed using score functions: $\nabla_\theta D_{KL} = E[(\nabla_{A_\theta} \log p_{G_\theta} - \nabla_{A_\theta} \log p_{\pi_\phi})\nabla_\theta A_\theta]$.

The key innovation is to optimize this KL divergence across *diffused action samples* rather than directly on the clean actions. This approach, similar to Diffusion-GAN, helps with misaligned supports and encourages mode-seeking behavior, which has shown empirical benefits in offline RL. The gradient for this diffused KL divergence is given by:
$\nabla_\theta E_{k\sim U} [D_{KL}(p_{G_\theta,k}||p_{\pi_\phi,k})] = E_{z,k,A_\theta,A^\theta_k} [w(k)(s_{p_{G_\theta}}(A^\theta_k) - s_{p_{\pi_\phi}}(A^\theta_k))\nabla_\theta A^\theta_k]$.
Here, $s_{p_{\pi_\phi}}(A^\theta_k)$ is the score of the pre-trained policy, obtainable from its noise prediction network $\epsilon_\phi$. To estimate $s_{p_{G_\theta}}(A^\theta_k)$, they introduce an auxiliary **generator score network** $\pi_\psi$, parameterized by $\epsilon_\psi$. This network is trained to predict the noise of diffused samples generated by $G_\theta$.

The training process for **OneDP-S (Stochastic)** involves iteratively updating:
1. **Generator Score Network ($\pi_\psi$):** Trained to minimize the denoising score matching loss (Equation 6), treating $G_\theta$'s output as the target data. This effectively estimates the score of $p_{G_\theta}$.
2. **Action Generator ($G_\theta$):** Updated using the score difference loss (Equation 5), approximating $s_{p_{G_\theta}}$ with $s_{p_{\pi_\psi}}$. The pre-trained policy $\pi_\phi$ remains fixed.

For **OneDP-D (Deterministic)**, the action generator $G_\theta$ outputs deterministic actions ($A_\theta = G_\theta(O)$, omitting noise $z$). In this case, the score of $p_{G_\theta}$ can be explicitly solved (Equation 7), eliminating the need for the auxiliary generator score network $\pi_\psi$. This simplifies the loss function (Equation 8).

#### 3.3. Policy and Distillation Discussion

The authors discuss the choice between stochastic (OneDP-S) and deterministic (OneDP-D) policies. While OneDP-D simplifies training, OneDP-S is generally more versatile, allowing for exploration and potentially better global optimum convergence in complex environments. The empirical results corroborate this, with OneDP-S often outperforming OneDP-D.

They emphasize the benefits of optimizing the *expectational reverse KL divergence* over diffused samples: it encourages mode-seeking behavior (improving empirical performance) and handles distributions with misaligned supports, both crucial for robust distillation.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Architecture:** Follows Chi et al. (2023), using a 1D temporal CNN U-Net for the diffusion backbone and a ResNet18 (without pre-training) as the vision encoder.
- **Noise Scheduling:** Tested with both DDPM (discrete-time, 100 steps, epsilon prediction) and EDM (continuous-time, x0 prediction), with EDM being a requirement for the baseline Consistency Policy.
- **Warm-Starting:** Both $G_\theta$ and $\pi_\psi$ are initialized by duplicating the neural network structure and weights from the pre-trained diffusion policy. This is a crucial step for preserving performance and accelerating distillation convergence.
- **Hyperparameters:** Distillation occurs over specific timesteps (e.g., for discrete). A reweighting function $w(k) = \sigma_k^2$ is used. Generator learning rate is 1e-6, while the generator score network learning rate is higher at 2e-5, allowing it to keep pace with the generator's distribution updates. Vision encoders are also trained during distillation.
- **Action Chunking:** Uses 16 actions per chunk, with two observations for vision encoding.

This comprehensive methodology allows OneDP to effectively distill complex visuomotor policies into a highly efficient single-step generator, designed for real-time robotic deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper provides extensive evaluation of OneDP in both simulated and real-world environments, consistently demonstrating its superior performance in terms of speed and often success rate compared to existing diffusion policies and distillation baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluated on 6 challenging tasks from the Robomimic benchmark (Square, Transport, ToolHang) and the PushT task, covering varying complexities.
- **Baselines:** Compared against the original Diffusion Policy (DP) with DDPM (100 steps) and DDIM (10, 1 steps), and Consistency Policy (CP) with EDM (1, 3 steps).
- **Key Findings (DDPM Setting - Table 1):**
 - **Vanilla DP at 1-step:** Fails completely (0% success rate), highlighting the fundamental challenge OneDP addresses.
 - **OneDP vs. DP:** OneDP-S achieves an average success rate of 0.843, slightly *outperforming* the pre-trained DP (0.829 at 100 steps and 0.836 at 10 DDIM steps). This suggests that single-step sampling can potentially avoid cumulative errors of iterative denoising.
 - **OneDP-S vs. OneDP-D:** OneDP-S (stochastic) generally achieves higher success rates (0.843 vs 0.826 for OneDP-D), supporting the idea that stochastic policies are more versatile.
- **Key Findings (EDM Setting - Table 2):**
 - **OneDP vs. CP:** OneDP-S (0.830 average success) significantly outperforms CP (0.712 average success for its best 3-step setting) with a single step. Even at 1-step, OneDP-S (0.830) vastly surpasses CP (0.251).
 - **Convergence Speed (Figure 4):** OneDP converges about **20x faster** than CP (20 epochs for OneDP vs. 450 epochs for CP). This is a critical advantage for practical deployment.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Setup:** Deployed on a Franka robot arm for four self-designed tasks: three pick-and-place (pnp) variants (pnp-milk, pnp-anything, pnp-milk-move) and a coffee machine manipulation task. Notably, pnp-milk-move involves human interference, demanding dynamic adaptability.
- **Baselines:** Compared against Diffusion Policy (DP) using DDIM with 10 steps, as the original DDPM is too slow.
- **Success Rate (Table 3):**
 - OneDP consistently outperforms DP across all tasks.
 - Most significantly, in the dynamic `pnp-milk-move` task (with human perturbation), OneDP (both D and S variants) achieves **100% success rate**, while DP achieves 80%. This highlights OneDP's superior responsiveness to environmental changes.
 - OneDP-S (0.98 average success) slightly outperforms OneDP-D (0.95 average success).
- **Time Efficiency (Task Completion Time - Table 4):**
 - OneDP completes tasks significantly faster than DP. For example, OneDP-S averages 25.81 seconds per task, compared to DP's 36.36 seconds. This speedup is attributed to quicker action predictions, reducing robot hesitation.
- **Inference Speed (Wall-Clock Time - Table 5):**
 - The wall-clock time for action prediction with OneDP (1 step) is **7 ms**, compared to 66 ms for DP (DDIM 10 steps).
 - This translates to an action prediction frequency of **62.5 Hz for OneDP** vs. 1.49 Hz for DP. This is an **order-of-magnitude improvement (approximately 42x faster)** in action prediction frequency.
 - The total time (Observation Encoding + Action Prediction) is 16 ms for OneDP versus 75 ms for DP.
- **Training Cost (Figure 1):** OneDP's distillation process requires only **2%-10% additional pre-training cost** for convergence compared to the original Diffusion Policy.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that OneDP is not only dramatically faster but also maintains or improves success rates, particularly excelling in dynamic real-world scenarios where rapid adaptation is crucial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The One-Step Diffusion Policy (OneDP) marks a significant advancement in the field of robotic control, particularly concerning the practical deployment of powerful generative models. Its core contribution lies in bridging the critical gap between the high performance of diffusion policies and the stringent real-time requirements of robotic systems.

The significance and potential impact of OneDP are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Real-Time Robotic Control:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is the drastic reduction in inference time. By achieving single-step action generation and boosting policy frequency from 1.5 Hz to 62 Hz, OneDP transforms diffusion policies from powerful but slow theoretical constructs into genuinely practical tools for real-world robotics. This speed is crucial for reactive behaviors, handling dynamic environments, and ensuring robot safety in human-robot collaboration scenarios.

2. **Enhanced Robustness to Dynamic Environments:** The compelling results in the `pnp-milk-move` task, where OneDP achieved 100% success despite human interference, demonstrate its superior ability to adapt quickly to unexpected changes. This robustness is vital for robots operating in unstructured and unpredictable environments, moving beyond fixed, classical manipulation tasks.

3. **Improved Task Completion Efficiency:** Beyond merely predicting actions faster, OneDP's rapid response translates to quicker task completion times in real-world scenarios. This efficiency gain is important for industrial applications where throughput is critical, and for complex, multi-stage tasks where hesitation can lead to failure.

4. **Optimal Performance without Compromise:** A remarkable finding is that OneDP not only accelerates inference but also often achieves comparable or even slightly *higher* success rates than the slower, pre-trained teacher policies. This suggests that single-step generation can, in some cases, mitigate cumulative errors inherent in iterative sampling, making the distilled policy superior. This "better and faster" outcome is a highly desirable, yet often elusive, goal in model distillation.

5. **Cost-Effective Training:** The fact that distillation for OneDP requires only a marginal increase (2%-10%) in training cost over pre-training the base diffusion policy makes it an economically viable and attractive solution for researchers and practitioners.

6. **Broader Adoption of Diffusion Policies:** By addressing the primary practical bottleneck of speed, OneDP significantly lowers the barrier to entry for using expressive diffusion policies in a wider array of robotic applications, potentially leading to more sophisticated and nuanced robot behaviors learned from demonstrations.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
The authors candidly discuss a few limitations:
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** OneDP was not tested on real-world tasks requiring very long sequences of actions or intricate planning.
- **Underutilized Potential:** In real-world experiments, the robot's operation frequency was capped at 20 Hz for stability, meaning OneDP's full 62 Hz potential was not exploited. This indicates even more room for future speed improvements.
- **Distillation Method Optimality:** The KL-based distillation, while effective, might not be the absolute optimal method for distribution matching, suggesting that incorporating discriminator terms (as in GANs) could potentially enhance distillation performance further.

Despite these limitations, OneDP represents a substantial leap forward for visuomotor policy learning. It transforms diffusion policies from a powerful academic concept into a practical, real-time solution, paving the way for more intelligent, responsive, and efficient robotic systems in dynamic real-world settings.
