# VLK: Learning Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from Synthetic Interactions in Reconstructed Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> An automated pipeline generates large-scale, paired vision-language-kinematics (VLK) data for humanoid robots by synthesizing interactions within 3D Gaussian Splatting-reconstructed real-world scenes. This approach enabled a Unitree G1 humanoid to perform object-directed navigation with up to 100% success and floor-level object manipulation with up to 100% success on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Enabling humanoid robots to perform perception-based loco-manipulation (interpreting visual input and language for whole-body movements) in human-centered environments.
- The lack of large-scale, synchronized datasets comprising egocentric observations, language instructions, and robot-compatible whole-body kinematic trajectories.
- Existing real-world data acquisition methods are costly, unscalable, or provide incomplete information needed for robust policy learning.

## Method

- Developed a ==real-to-sim-to-real== pipeline that synthetically generates paired **Vision-Language-Kinematics (VLK)** data by leveraging ==3D Gaussian Splatting== for realistic scene reconstruction.
- Synthesized diverse Unitree G1 humanoid behaviors for object-directed navigation and manipulation by adapting ==conditional diffusion== human-object interaction synthesis methods and ==motion retargeting== (OmniRetarget).
- Trained a high-level **VLK** policy (fine-tuned from π₀.₅ with a ==flow-matching== objective) on the generated data, mapping egocentric observations and language to short-horizon kinematic trajectories, and integrated it with a ==contact-aware whole-body tracker== for physical execution.

## Results

- The synthetic data generation pipeline efficiently produced **48,000 trajectories** in **600 GPU-hours**, enabling scalable data acquisition for humanoid loco-manipulation.
- The full system achieved high real-world success rates on a Unitree G1 robot: up to **100%** for object-directed navigation and up to **100%** for floor-level box manipulation tasks.
- Increasing the volume of synthetic training data consistently improved performance, and visual domain randomization boosted navigation success rates from **41%** (no randomization) to **90%** (full randomization).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the generation of robot behaviors from the rendering of visual observations allows for scalable production of comprehensive VLK data without manual human demonstrations.
> - Using 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct real-world scenes provides visually realistic and geometrically accurate synthetic environments essential for training grounded perception-based policies.
> - Extensive visual domain randomization during synthetic data rendering is critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap, particularly for making policies robust to real-world visual variations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the fundamental challenge of enabling humanoid robots to perform perception-based loco-manipulation, which involves interpreting egocentric observations and language instructions to execute coordinated whole-body movements in human-centered environments. This field requires policies that can map high-dimensional visual input and linguistic commands to complex kinematic behaviors.

Existing approaches to obtain the necessary paired data—synchronized egocentric observations, task instructions, and robot-compatible whole-body trajectories—face significant limitations. Real-world teleoperation systems, while capable of producing high-quality demonstrations, are costly and difficult to scale across diverse environments, objects, and interaction types. Human motion-capture datasets provide rich whole-body references but lack corresponding robot egocentric observations. Conversely, egocentric video datasets offer abundant visual experience but are not directly paired with robot-compatible kinematics or actions. This fragmented landscape signifies a bottleneck in acquiring the comprehensive vision-language-kinematics (VLK) data needed for scalable policy learning.

This research positions itself by aiming to overcome this data bottleneck through synthetic data generation. It leverages recent advancements in two key areas:
1. **Real2Sim:** The ability of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to efficiently reconstruct indoor environments with photorealistic detail and metric scale, offering a solution for creating realistic synthetic world assets.
2. **Sim2Real:** Progress in whole-body motion tracking, often trained in simulation, that can produce robot policies capable of executing kinematic reference trajectories on physical hardware.

The paper's contribution is in connecting these partial solutions into a unified "real-to-sim-to-real" pipeline. Unlike prior work on vision-language-action (VLA) policies for humanoids, such as WholeBodyVLA and Ψ0, which primarily rely on existing robot demonstrations or human videos, this work *generates* the primary vision-language-kinematics supervision synthetically. It adapts methods for human-object interaction synthesis from general human motion (SMPL-based) to the specific kinematic representation of the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, integrating it with motion retargeting and advanced whole-body tracking controllers. By decoupling the generation of behaviors from the rendering of observations and exploiting privileged simulation information, the authors aim to scale data production significantly without human demonstration effort.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to perform perception-based loco-manipulation tasks, specifically including object-directed navigation and object interaction (e.g., picking up and placing boxes), guided by egocentric visual observations and language instructions.

The central motivation stems from the recognition of a critical data bottleneck: the absence of a large-scale, synchronized dataset comprising egocentric images, language commands, and robot-compatible whole-body kinematic trajectories. Such a dataset is considered essential for training robust, perception-based humanoid policies. Current methods for data acquisition are either too expensive, not scalable, or do not provide the complete tuple of information required.

The specific objectives established to address this challenge are:
1. **Develop a Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline:** Create a system capable of automatically producing paired vision-language-kinematics (VLK) supervision. This pipeline aims to overcome the limitations of real-world data collection by synthesizing interactions in digital environments.
2. **Utilize Reconstructed Scenes:** Integrate 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to reconstruct real-world indoor environments. This provides visually realistic and geometrically accurate synthetic worlds, bridging the "Real2Sim" gap for environment assets.
3. **Synthesize Diverse Robot Behaviors:** Generate Unitree G1 humanoid trajectories for various tasks, including navigation and object manipulation. This synthesis leverages privileged information available in simulation, such as object poses, collision geometry, and walkable regions, to simplify the generation of valid whole-body movements.
4. **Render Paired Egocentric Observations:** After synthesizing robot behaviors, render corresponding egocentric RGB observations from the robot's viewpoint within the reconstructed 3DGS scenes. This ensures visual grounding consistent with robot interaction.
5. **Train a VLK Policy:** Develop and train a high-level policy that maps egocentric observations, language instructions, and the current robot state to short-horizon whole-body kinematic trajectories. This policy serves as the core decision-making component.
6. **Integrate with Whole-Body Tracking:** Couple the VLK policy with a whole-body tracker. The tracker is responsible for converting the high-level kinematic predictions into low-level robot actions for execution on the physical humanoid, ensuring dynamic stability and adherence to desired motions.
7. **Demonstrate Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Validate that policies trained purely on this synthetically generated data can effectively transfer to and perform tasks on a physical Unitree G1 robot in real-world environments.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to establish a scalable, automated methodology for generating the necessary training data, thereby enabling the development of advanced perception-based loco-manipulation capabilities for humanoid robots without extensive real-world teleoperation or human demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a "real-to-sim-to-real" pipeline that decouples the generation of robot behaviors from the rendering of visual observations. This involves generating synthetic Vision-Language-Kinematics (VLK) data in reconstructed scenes, training a high-level VLK policy on this data, and then deploying the policy with a whole-body tracker on a physical humanoid.

#### 3.1. Synthetic Data Generation in Reconstructed Scenes

The pipeline for synthetic data generation consists of three primary stages:

1. **Scene Reconstruction and Annotation:**
 - Indoor environments are reconstructed using Polycam scans from an iPhone 14 Pro, which combine RGB imagery and LiDAR-based depth for metric-scale scene representation.
 - A 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation is optimized for visual realism and spatial accuracy.
 - For motion synthesis, a scene point cloud is extracted and annotated. An interactive tool is used to manually place oriented 3D bounding boxes around task-relevant objects (e.g., chairs, tables, boxes) and to mark walkable regions on the floor as polygonal areas.
 - These annotations provide geometric and semantic constraints for sampling initial robot poses, target objects, and sparse waypoints for various tasks. Task instructions are automatically generated using simple templates (e.g., "walk toward the [object]", "pick up the box from the floor"), grounded in the semantic labels of the annotated objects.

2. **Interaction Synthesis in Reconstructed Scenes:**
 - Unitree G1 trajectories are synthesized for two task families: object-directed navigation and object interaction.
 - **Motion Representation:** G1 motions are represented as X = [p, R, sin(q), cos(q)], encoding global joint positions, 6D joint rotations, and joint angles (using sin/cos to avoid discontinuities). Object trajectories are represented as X_obj = [o, R_obj].
 - **Navigation Model:** Trained on G1 motions from BONES-SEED, it generates trajectories for walking towards an object, conditioned on language instructions, initial G1 states, and sparse scene-grounded waypoints. Target objects must be visible from the initial egocentric viewpoint to ensure visual grounding.
 - **Interaction Model:** This model is adapted from conditional diffusion formulations of human-object interaction synthesis methods (e.g., CHOIS,). It is trained on OMOMO sequences retargeted to the G1 morphology using OmniRetarget. It generates coupled G1-object trajectories, conditioned on initial object state, object geometry features, desired relative wrist poses (e.g., for grasping a box), and binary wrist-object contact labels (specifying contact timing and phase). A G1-specific forward-kinematics loss is used to improve end-effector position accuracy.
 - **Post-Processing:** Generated motions undergo post-processing to reduce common artifacts. Foot sliding is mitigated by constraining feet to remain fixed during contact phases using inverse kinematics. Hand-object contact quality is improved by an optimization-based approach that drives wrist positions and rotations towards desired relative poses within the object's local frame.

3. **Egocentric View Rendering:**
 - The synthesized G1 trajectories are replayed in Isaac Sim. The reconstructed 3DGS scene is loaded, and a Unitree G1 model equipped with a virtual ZED 2i camera (calibrated to match the physical robot's setup) is placed within it.
 - **Domain Randomization** is applied during rendering to enhance sim-to-real robustness. This includes perturbations to camera extrinsics and focal length, variations in dome-light intensity and yaw, and image-space augmentations (brightness, contrast, saturation, hue, Gaussian noise, Gaussian blur).
 - The output of this pipeline is a large dataset of paired VLK sequences, each containing an egocentric RGB observation, a task instruction, and the corresponding G1 whole-body kinematic trajectory.

#### 3.2. Vision-Language-Kinematics (VLK) Policy

The VLK policy maps current egocentric RGB observations, task instructions, and the current G1 kinematic state to a short-horizon (H=30 frames, 1 second at 30 Hz) future whole-body kinematic trajectory.
- **Kinematic State Representation:** Each G1 kinematic state (x_τ) includes heading-normalized root planar displacement, yaw change, root height, 6D root orientation, G1 joint angles (sin/cos encoding), and binary wrist-object contact labels.
- **Policy Formulation:** The policy (π_θ) is initialized from a pretrained π_0.5 model and fine-tuned on the generated VLK dataset, with its action space adapted to the G1 kinematic representation.
- **Training Objective:** An x_0-prediction objective within a flow-matching formulation. The primary reconstruction loss (L_traj) minimizes the squared difference between the predicted and ground-truth future trajectories.
- **Auxiliary Losses:** To improve physical realism and execution, additional losses are incorporated:
 - `L_foot-contact`: Focal loss for predicting foot-floor contact labels.
 - `L_acc-root`: Penalizes accumulated drift in the global root trajectory derived from relative displacements.
 - `L_fk`: Forward-kinematics loss on ankle and wrist positions to ensure accurate end-effector placement.
 - `L_foot`: Foot-skating regularization that penalizes horizontal foot velocity when a foot is predicted to be in contact with the ground.

#### 3.3. Whole-Body Motion Tracking

A contact-aware whole-body tracker, based on SceneBot, is responsible for executing the VLK policy's high-level kinematic predictions on the physical humanoid.
- The tracker operates independently of egocentric observations and language instructions, receiving only converted reference targets and current robot proprioception.
- At each control step, the predicted VLK state is converted into a tracker reference format, including lower-body joint targets, upper-body head and wrist 6D poses, root target pose, and binary wrist-object contact labels.
- The tracker then outputs joint-level PD targets, following the reference motion and using predicted contact labels to stabilize object transport by activating contact-aware wrist behavior.

#### 3.4. Deployment

For real-world deployment, a real-time VLA system utilizing Triton as an optimized inference backend is employed.
- VLK inference runs on an RTX 5090, while the whole-body tracker runs on an RTX 5000 Ada.
- To manage discontinuities in predicted trajectories, a client-side real-time chunking strategy (adapted from Ψ0) is used, with a 10-frame overlap between adjacent predicted action chunks.
- To address motion blur in egocentric observations during fast movements, a sharpness-based frame selection mechanism is implemented, where the sharpest frame from a buffered window is selected based on the variance of the Laplacian (Appendix B.3).
- The system operates with a multi-process runtime: a state estimator and whole-body tracker run at 50 Hz on a tethered laptop, communicating with a VLK inference client that offloads flow-matching trajectory sampling to an external GPU server. The VLK inference latency is 31 ms, and the total end-to-end replan latency is approximately 63 ms, providing sufficient headroom for real-time operation (Appendix B.4).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to address four primary research questions, providing quantitative and qualitative evidence for the proposed system's effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Synthetic Data Generation Efficiency

The pipeline's efficiency in generating diverse paired VLK data was evaluated. For each environment (4 lab layouts, 4 apartment layouts), 12 data-generation modes were used (6 evaluated tasks, 6 auxiliary modes for coverage).
- The pipeline synthesized 48,000 trajectories automatically within 600 GPU-hours on a single NVIDIA L40S GPU.
- Generating 1000 trajectories for one mode in one layout took approximately 4 hours for motion synthesis and 8.3 hours for egocentric observation rendering.
- **Finding:** The system can efficiently generate a large volume of paired VLK supervision after initial scene reconstruction and annotation, with processes parallelizable across layouts and modes.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Complete System

The full closed-loop system, comprising the VLK policy and whole-body tracker, was evaluated in both MuJoCo simulation (with IsaacSim rendering) and on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid.
- **Tasks:** Object-directed navigation ("Walk To," "Turn Around") and single-object transport ("Pick (Floor)," "Put (Floor)," "Pick (Surface)," "Put (Surface)"). Evaluation involved 20 real-world trials and 1000 simulation rollouts per mode, with varied scene layouts.
- **Real-world Success Rates:** The system demonstrated high success rates.
 - **Navigation:** "Walk To" achieved 100% in the lab and 95% in the apartment. "Turn Around" achieved 100% in the lab and 90% in the apartment.
 - **Floor-level Manipulation:** "Pick (Floor)" achieved 80% in the lab and 90% in the apartment. "Put (Floor)" achieved 100% in both lab and apartment.
 - **Surface-level Manipulation:** "Pick (Surface)" was 55% in the lab and 65% in the apartment. "Put (Surface)" was 40% in the lab and 75% in the apartment.
- **Simulation Success Rates:** Generally higher than real-world, indicating a remaining sim-to-real gap but strong policy performance.
- **Impact of Contact Labels:** An ablation showed "Pick (Floor)" without contact labels resulted in 0% success in 5 real-world trials, highlighting the importance of contact information for manipulation.
- **Finding:** The system achieved high success rates on object-directed navigation and floor-level box manipulation on the physical Unitree G1. Surface-level interactions proved more challenging, attributed to limited coverage in retargeted interaction data for diverse support-surface heights and resulting less reliable grasps.

#### 4.3. Effect of Synthesized Data Volume

An ablation study varied the amount of synthesized training data from 10% to 100% of the full dataset (1000 trajectories per mode per layout).
- **Finding:** Increased training data consistently improved performance across all evaluated modes.
 - Navigation tasks ("Walk To") showed high success rates even with limited data.
 - Manipulation tasks ("Pick (Floor)," "Pick (Surface)") benefited substantially from additional trajectories; for instance, "Pick (Surface)" success improved from 0% at 10% data to 46% with the full dataset.
- **Conclusion:** Contact-rich, perception-conditioned manipulation tasks require more scene-grounded supervision than navigation, underscoring the value of generating large volumes of synthetic data.

#### 4.4. Effect of Visual Domain Randomization

The impact of visual domain randomization during rendering on sim-to-real transfer robustness was evaluated, specifically for walking mode under visual perturbations (lighting and camera variations).
- **Success Rates (Walking Mode):**
 - No randomization: 41%
 - Camera randomization only: 48%
 - Lighting randomization only: 87%
 - Full randomization (lighting + camera): 90%
- **Finding:** Visual domain randomization significantly improved walking success. Lighting randomization contributed more substantially to robustness than camera randomization.
- **Conclusion:** Domain randomization is critical for bridging the visual sim-to-real gap, enhancing policies' robustness to real-world visual variations.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of generating large-scale synthetic vision-language-kinematics data in reconstructed scenes for training humanoid loco-manipulation policies. The trained VLK policy, when combined with a whole-body tracker, achieved reliable performance on a physical humanoid for both navigation and object transport, particularly for floor-level interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing the critical data acquisition bottleneck for perception-based loco-manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Scalable Data Generation for Humanoids:** The primary contribution is the development of an automated and scalable pipeline for generating paired vision-language-kinematics (VLK) data. This methodology bypasses the challenges and high costs associated with real-world teleoperation or human motion capture, offering a means to produce large, diverse datasets essential for learning complex humanoid behaviors. This could accelerate research and development in humanoid control by providing readily available, high-quality training data.

2. **Unified Real-to-Sim-to-Real Framework:** The work successfully integrates recent advancements in 3D scene reconstruction (3DGS), humanoid motion synthesis, and whole-body tracking into a cohesive "real-to-sim-to-real" framework. This demonstrates a practical and effective strategy for bridging the gap between simulation and real-world deployment, especially for perception-driven tasks in complex, unstructured environments. The use of reconstructed scenes enhances visual fidelity, which is crucial for perception-based policies.

3. **Enabling Complex Loco-Manipulation:** The system enables the Unitree G1 humanoid to perform a range of challenging tasks, including object-directed navigation and bimanual object transport (picking, carrying, and placing). These tasks require coordinated whole-body control, visual grounding of language instructions, and interaction with the environment. The demonstrated capabilities represent progress toward more autonomous and versatile humanoid robots capable of operating in human-centric spaces.

4. **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The effectiveness of domain randomization in bridging the visual sim-to-real gap is empirically demonstrated, particularly the impact of lighting variations. This reinforces the importance of incorporating systematic visual diversity in synthetic data generation for robust real-world policy deployment. The high success rates achieved on the physical robot validate the viability of training perception-based policies predominantly in simulation.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future advancements in humanoid robotics:
 - **Expanded Task Repertoire:** The current limitation to box-like object manipulation, stemming from the coverage of the OMOMO dataset, suggests a clear path for future work to incorporate richer interaction data for handling diverse objects (e.g., small objects, tools) and more precise grasping. This would necessitate development of low-level controllers for fine-grained manipulation.
 - **Reduced Manual Annotation:** The reliance on manual scene annotation (bounding boxes, walkable regions) could be reduced or automated through the integration of learned semantic scene understanding modules, making the data generation pipeline more autonomous and less labor-intensive.
 - **Longer-Horizon and More Abstract Tasks:** While the current system chains atomic prompts for longer tasks, integrating more sophisticated task planning and hierarchical control architectures could enable humanoids to tackle genuinely long-horizon, open-ended tasks with higher levels of abstraction.

In conclusion, the research provides a practical and scalable solution to a long-standing challenge in humanoid robotics, showcasing a powerful method for training complex perception-based loco-manipulation skills from synthetic data, and paving the way for more capable and autonomous humanoid agents.
