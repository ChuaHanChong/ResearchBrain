# SFT Conflicts, RL Coexists: A Theoretical and Empirical Analysis of Multi-Task Learning for LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> An analysis of multi-task LLM post-training showing SFT and RL differ geometrically: RL updates are sparse, tiny, and near-orthogonal across tasks, while SFT updates overlap and conflict. A gradient-interference theory bounds SFT interference by gradient norm and RL interference by rollout variance. The resulting Parallel-RL paradigm trains tasks independently then merges updates, retaining 103% of single-task RL accuracy.

## Problem

- Prior comparisons of supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning focus on single-task training, leaving their divergent behavior under multi-task and sequential training largely unexplained.
- Practice diverged without justification: supervised fine-tuning uses mixed-data training to avoid forgetting, while reinforcement learning commonly runs multi-stage curricula, with no mechanistic account of why.
- Existing forgetting analyses, such as implicit KL-minimal RL updates, explain single-task parameter deviation but never bound cross-task gradient interference, so multi-task conflict remains uncharacterized.

## Method

- ==Parameter-update geometry probe==: computes per-task update L2 norms and pairwise cosine similarities under ==LoRA== across math, science, logic, and code, quantifying sparsity and inter-task orthogonality.
- ==Gradient-interference theorem==: decomposes ==GRPO== and ==SFT== gradients by policy source and advantage function, proving SFT interference is ==norm-limited== while RL interference is ==variance-limited== by the intra-group rollout residual.
- **Parallel-RL**: trains each task in an independent RL run, then merges updates by sum, mean, ==TIES==, ==rank-1 SVD==, or a 5%-sample post-merge adaptation.

## Results

- Multi-stage training collapsed SFT to **23.1%** below base on average while RL gained **24.9%**; logic accuracy fell from **31.0%** to **9.0%** under sequential SFT.
- RL update norms averaged about **0.03** versus **7.4** for SFT, and cross-task cosine similarity was about **1e-5** for RL against **0.1** to **1.0** for SFT.
- Adapted Parallel-RL retained **103.2%** of single-task RL and beat base by **10.7** points; naive summed merging kept **94.2%**, whereas Parallel-SFT kept only **65.4%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Advantage normalization plus on-policy sampling cancels common-mode gradient components, so cross-task interference scales with rollout variance rather than raw gradient magnitude.
> - Near-orthogonal task updates make post-training embarrassingly parallel: train tasks separately, merge, and add or remove capabilities modularly instead of paying sequential-curriculum costs.
> - Decoupling is not universal: conflicting task pairs still interfere, so task selection, sparsified merging, and brief post-merge adaptation remain necessary.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) represents a significant area of current research. Two primary paradigms dominate post-training: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Previous studies have investigated the differences between SFT and RL in single-task training contexts. For instance, some research suggests that SFT primarily aids in memorization, while RL fosters generalization (Chu et al., 2025). Other works propose that RL tends to "squeeze" or consolidate policies into high-reward regions, whereas SFT "expands" the sampling space (Matsutani et al., 2025). Additionally, it has been observed that RL training often involves fine-tuning smaller subnetworks compared to SFT (Mukherjee et al., 2025). Hybrid approaches combining SFT and RL have also been explored to leverage the benefits of both.

While single-task comparisons are established, the behavior of SFT and RL in multi-task settings remains less explored. For multi-task SFT, the common practice involves training on mixed datasets (Dong et al., 2023; Park, 2025), with ongoing research addressing challenges like task conflicts and gradient imbalances through adaptive data selection and curriculum learning (Wu et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2025). In multi-task RL, various strategies are employed, including multi-stage training (Narvekar et al., 2020; Cho et al., 2024), curriculum learning, and mixed-data RL that addresses issues like gradient imbalance (Wu et al., 2025b). Recent studies also indicate that RL exhibits less catastrophic forgetting than SFT (Shenfeld et al., 2025; Lai et al., 2025). However, a systematic investigation into the fundamental divergence between SFT and RL specifically in multi-task training paradigms, beyond the aspect of forgetting, has been largely underexplored. This paper positions itself within this gap, aiming to provide a comprehensive empirical and theoretical analysis of SFT and RL in multi-task learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to systematically analyze the contrasting behaviors of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) when applied to multi-task learning for large language models (LLMs). The motivation stems from a preliminary observation that SFT exhibits severe task conflicts in multi-stage training, leading to performance degradation, whereas RL enables stable coexistence and cumulative performance growth across diverse tasks in similar settings.

The core research questions motivating this study are:
1. Why does SFT experience performance collapse under multi-stage training when RL achieves stable, cumulative performance growth across different tasks in a multi-stage context?
2. What are the underlying mechanisms, at both empirical (parameter level) and theoretical (gradient interference) levels, that explain this divergence in multi-task behavior?

To address these questions, the authors aimed to:
- Empirically quantify the performance differences between SFT and RL under mixed-data and multi-stage training paradigms.
- Investigate the generalization effects of single-task training, specifically how optimizing for one task impacts performance on other, untrained tasks for both SFT and RL.
- Conduct a parameter-level empirical analysis to observe the magnitude and directional interference of parameter updates (∆W) induced by SFT and RL across tasks.
- Develop a theoretical explanation for the observed parameter update dynamics, focusing on the roles of the advantage function and the on-policy nature of RL in shaping gradient interference.
- Leverage the theoretical and empirical insights to propose a novel multi-task training paradigm, Parallel-RL, designed to improve efficiency and flexibility by decoupling task-specific training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach combining empirical experimentation with theoretical analysis.

#### 3.1. Experiment Setup

- **Base Model:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B are used as the foundational LLMs.
- **Tasks:** Four reasoning domains are selected: Math, Science, Coding, and Logic.
- **RL Algorithm:** The Group-wise Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm (Guo et al., 2025) is primarily adopted, with some experiments also using PPO (Schulman et al., 2017).
- **Efficiency:** LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) is utilized for efficient fine-tuning and parameter analysis in some experiments (r=64, α=32).
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation is conducted using:
 - **Math:** MATH500 (Lightman et al., 2023) and AIME2025.
 - **Science:** MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021) and GPQA Diamond (Rein et al., 2023).
 - **Logic:** Knights & Knaves (Xie et al., 2024).
 - **Code:** LiveCodeBench (Jain et al., 2024).
- **Training Details:** SFT is implemented using LLaMA-Factory (Zheng et al., 2024) with a learning rate of 1e-5. RL training uses the VeRL framework (Sheng et al., 2024) with a learning rate of 3e-6, sampling temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, and G=16 rollouts per prompt for GRPO. KL divergence penalty is disabled for specific analysis. Multi-stage training follows the order: Math, Science, Code, Logic.
- **Evaluation Details:** The lighteval (Habib et al., 2023) toolkit is used for most tasks, with the Logic-RL suite for logical reasoning. Sampling temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95 are maintained during generation.

#### 3.2. Comparative Analysis of Training Paradigms

The study compares two multi-task learning paradigms:
- **Mixed-Data:** Aggregates datasets from all tasks for joint training.
- **Multi-Stage:** Sequentially trains the model on distinct tasks in separate phases.
Performance (accuracy) of SFT and RL is assessed under both strategies, with changes relative to a base model reported.

#### 3.3. Generalization of Single-Task Training

To understand the "SFT Conflicts" and "RL Coexists" phenomena, the model is trained on a single target task (e.g., Math SFT or Math RL) and then evaluated on its performance on that target task and all other untrained tasks. This quantifies the degree of positive or negative transfer.

#### 3.4. Parameter-Level Empirical Analysis

For each single-task training (e.g., Math SFT, Math RL), the parameter update vector (∆W) is computed. Two properties are analyzed:
- **Magnitude:** The L2 norm of ∆W for both SFT and RL updates.
- **Interference:** The pairwise cosine similarity between ∆W vectors from different tasks (e.g., ∆W\_Math and ∆W\_Code) for both SFT and RL.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The theoretical section aims to explain the empirical observations by examining the gradient descent directions of SFT and RL.
- **Gradient Formalization:** Expected gradients for SFT and RL are formalized (Eq 1), highlighting the "Policy Source" (off-policy for SFT, on-policy for RL) and "Advantage Function" (absent in SFT, present in RL).
- **Sparsity and Small Magnitude:** Proposition 4.1 (Shenfeld et al., 2025) is restated, which posits that on-policy RL implicitly minimizes KL-divergence to the initial policy, leading to conservative parameter updates.
- **Multi-Task Gradient Orthogonality:**
 - **GRPO Gradient Definition:** The GRPO gradient, including standardized advantages, is defined (Definition 4.2).
 - **Gradient Inner Product Decomposition (Lemma 4.3):** It is shown that the zero-sum property of GRPO advantages eliminates the contribution of the mean score function (common gradient direction), leaving only the residual score function for interference calculations.
 - **Mechanism Analysis:** Explains how the advantage function acts as a filter for the mean gradient, and how the on-policy nature ensures minimal and non-interfering residuals, especially in high-dimensional spaces due to the Concentration of Measure phenomenon (Vershynyn, 2018).
- **Upper Bound of Gradient Interference (Theorem 4.5):**
 - Assumption 4.4 (Bounded Expected Norm and Variance) defines bounds for SFT's score function norm ($M_i^2$) and RL's residual score function variance ($V_i^2$).
 - Theorem 4.5 derives that SFT interference is "norm-limited" ($|I_{SFT}(i,j)| \le M_i \cdot M_j$), while RL interference is "variance-limited" ($|I_{RL}(i,j)| \le V_i \cdot V_j$).
- **Quantification and Visualization:** Key metrics like ||S||², ||δS||², and CosSim(S_i, S_j) are quantified, and t-SNE is used to visualize score function distributions for different tasks.

#### 3.6. Parallel-RL Framework

Based on the insight of approximate orthogonality in RL updates, Parallel-RL is proposed:
- **Methodology:** Tasks are trained independently in parallel, producing task-specific update vectors (∆W_i). These updates are then merged to form the final model parameters: W_final = W_base + M(∆W_1..., ∆W_N).
- **Merging Strategies:**
 - **Naive Parallel-RL:** Direct summation (sum) or averaging (mean) of ∆W_i.
 - **Sparse Parallel:** Uses TIES (Yadav et al., 2023) or SVD (retaining rank-1 directions) for merging.
 - **Adapted Parallel-RL:** Naive Parallel-RL (sum) followed by rapid adaptation using a small subset (5%) of samples.
- **Ablation Study:** To assess decoupling, individual ∆W_i are selectively excluded from the merging process, and the impact on performance of target and other tasks is measured.
- **Task Selection and Single-Task Training:** Preliminary exploration into methods for identifying compatible tasks (e.g., via t-SNE of score functions) and optimizing single-task exploration while controlling multi-task interference (e.g., using sampling temperature) is also discussed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Divergent Multi-Task Training Behaviors

- **SFT Conflicts:** Multi-stage SFT resulted in significant performance degradation across tasks, averaging a 23.1% decline relative to the base model. When trained on a single task, SFT improved the target task by an average of 4.0% but concurrently caused an average decline of 5.1% on untrained tasks.
- **RL Coexists:** In contrast, multi-stage RL exhibited robust improvements, achieving an average gain of 24.9% over the base model. Single-task RL achieved an average gain of 6.8% on the target task while also yielding an average improvement of 2.3% on untrained tasks, indicating minimal negative transfer.

#### 4.2. Parameter-Level Mechanistic Differences

- **Magnitude and Sparsity:** RL-induced parameter updates (∆W) were observed to be minimal, with an average L2 norm of approximately 3e-2, which is more than two orders of magnitude smaller than SFT updates (average L2 norm of 7.4). RL updates also exhibited higher sparsity, with only 20% of parameters exceeding 10e-5 magnitude, compared to 93% in SFT.
- **Orthogonality of Updates:** The pairwise cosine similarity of ∆W between distinct tasks was found to be negligible for RL (around 10e-5), indicating approximately orthogonal optimization directions. For SFT, this similarity was substantial (ranging from 10e-1 to 1.0), with some updates pointing in conflicting directions.

#### 4.3. Theoretical Explanations for Divergence

- **Sparsity and Minimal Updates:** The theoretical analysis aligns RL's conservative updates with its implicit bias towards minimizing KL-divergence to the initial policy (Proposition 4.1), which constrains parameter deviation. SFT, conversely, needs larger updates to fit an extrinsic expert distribution.
- **Variance-Limited Interference in RL:**
 - The advantage function's zero-sum property (in GRPO) algebraically removes the common mean gradient direction, ensuring that interference is governed by the intra-group variance of residual score functions.
 - The on-policy nature of RL limits this intra-group divergence.
 - Theorem 4.5 formalized this distinction: SFT interference is "norm-limited" (scaling with absolute gradient magnitude), while RL interference is "variance-limited" (bounded by the small variance of multiple rollouts).
- **Empirical Validation of Theoretical Bounds:** Quantitative measurements showed that SFT's average score function norm (||S||²) was approximately 7.1, supporting the "norm-limited" view. For RL, the intra-group residual (||δS||²) was approximately 10e-2, confirming the "variance-limited" nature. The cross-task cosine similarity for RL gradients was approximately 10e-3, significantly lower than SFT's 10e-1. Visualizations using t-SNE also showed distinct, separable clusters for RL score functions across tasks, while SFT distributions exhibited significant overlap.

#### 4.4. Performance of Parallel-RL Framework

- **Effectiveness:** Naive Parallel-RL (sum) demonstrated efficient multi-task learning, largely preserving single-task performance (95% retention) and achieving an average 5.0% performance improvement over the base model. In contrast, Parallel-SFT (sum) suffered from severe task conflicts, retaining only 66% of single-task SFT performance.
- **Enhanced Strategies:** Sparsification methods like TIES Parallel-RL and SVD Parallel-RL further improved performance retention to 98% and 96% of single-task RL, respectively.
- **Optimal Performance:** Adapted Parallel-RL, which includes a light post-merge adaptation phase (5% samples), achieved the highest performance, with an average 10.7% gain over the base model and 103.2% retention compared to single-task RL, indicating both efficiency and efficacy.
- **Ablation Study:** Removing a specific task-specific update (∆W_i) from the Parallel-RL model resulted in a significant performance drop on that task (average 7.1%) but maintained or slightly improved performance on other tasks (average 0.6%), confirming the effective decoupling of capabilities across tasks.
- **Pass@k Analysis:** Pass@k evaluations showed that Parallel-RL models, while sometimes having a lower Pass@1 score, exhibited reasoning boundaries (Pass@k at higher k) comparable to single-task RL models, suggesting stability and preservation of learned capabilities.
- **Task Compatibility:** Experiments with a highly interfering "Game" task (SynLogic) showed that its score function distributions overlapped significantly with Math and Code tasks, leading to performance degradation when merged via Parallel-RL, thus validating the importance of task compatibility.
- **Exploration-Interference Trade-off:** Analysis of single-task training techniques revealed a trade-off where increasing exploration (e.g., higher sampling temperature) improves single-task performance but also increases intra-group variance and thus gradient interference. An optimal sampling temperature (e.g., τ ≈ 0.65 for Math) was identified that balanced these factors for the best Parallel-RL performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of multi-task learning for large language models by systematically elucidating the fundamental divergence between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **Fundamental Understanding:** The paper provides a comprehensive empirical and theoretical explanation for why SFT encounters task conflicts while RL exhibits task coexistence in multi-task scenarios. This includes detailed analysis at the parameter update and gradient interference levels, highlighting the roles of RL's advantage function and on-policy nature in promoting sparse and approximately orthogonal updates. The distinction between "norm-limited" SFT interference and "variance-limited" RL interference offers a mechanistic insight.
- **Novel Paradigm:** The introduction of Parallel-RL offers a new, efficient, and flexible paradigm for multi-task training of LLMs. By enabling independent, parallel training of tasks and subsequent merging of updates with minimal interference, it addresses a key challenge in scaling LLM capabilities.
- **Modular LLM Development:** The confirmed decoupling of task-specific capabilities through ablation studies suggests a pathway for more modular LLM architectures, allowing for the flexible composition and adaptation of specialized models for diverse real-world applications. This modularity could significantly streamline the development and deployment of LLMs tailored to specific needs without extensive retraining.
- **Advancement Towards AGI:** By improving the efficiency and effectiveness of multi-task learning, this work contributes to foundational research aimed at enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities and advancing the vision of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
- **Ethical Considerations:** The authors note that while striving for efficient multi-task training, it is crucial to maintain the safety and reliability of each individual task to ensure responsible deployment. The methodological study is not considered to introduce distinct ethical risks or societal impacts beyond general AI safety considerations.
