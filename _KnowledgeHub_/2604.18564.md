# MultiWorld: Scalable Multi-Agent Multi-View Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MultiWorld presents a framework for scalable multi-agent, multi-view video world modeling, integrating a Multi-Agent Condition Module (MACM) and a Global State Encoder (GSE). The model demonstrates improved action controllability, multi-view consistency, and scalability across varying numbers of agents and camera views, outperforming baselines on multi-player game and multi-robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing video world models predominantly focus on single-agent environments, limiting their applicability to complex real-world multi-agent scenarios.
- Current models struggle with multi-agent controllability, including distinguishing agent identities, managing diverse action strengths, and maintaining visual and geometric consistency across multiple camera views.
- Prior attempts at multi-agent world modeling often assume a fixed number of agents or predefined camera views, restricting scalability and generalizability.

## Method

- **MultiWorld** introduces a unified framework built on an ==action-conditioned diffusion== backbone, leveraging ==Flow Matching== with a Transformer architecture.
- A ==Multi-Agent Condition Module (MACM)== incorporates ==Agent Identity Embedding (AIE)== using RoPE for identity distinction and ==Adaptive Action Weighting (AAW)== to prioritize influential agent actions.
- A ==Global State Encoder (GSE)== utilizes a pretrained 3D reconstruction model (**VGGT**) to extract a shared, 3D-aware global environmental state, ensuring multi-view consistency and enabling parallel generation of views.

## Results

- MultiWorld consistently outperformed baselines on multi-player video-game and multi-robot manipulation datasets, achieving lower FVD (**179** vs. 207-245) and RPE (**0.67** vs. 0.72-0.75) while maintaining high action-following ability.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the MACM significantly improved action-following ability and FVD, and the GSE further enhanced multi-view consistency (RPE reduced from **0.76** to **0.67**) and overall video quality.
- Qualitative results demonstrated MultiWorld's ability to generate physically consistent videos across viewpoints, accurately simulate complex multi-agent interactions (e.g., pushing a board), and support stable long-horizon autoregressive generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing multi-agent and multi-view challenges allows for a framework that flexibly scales with variable numbers of agents and camera views without requiring architectural modifications.
> - Explicitly embedding agent identities and adaptively weighting individual actions within a shared action space enhances the model's ability to control specific agents and respond to their relative influence.
> - Extracting a unified 3D-aware global environmental state from multiple observations is crucial for maintaining visual and geometric consistency across different camera perspectives during video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video world models have demonstrated capabilities in simulating environmental dynamics, typically by generating future frames conditioned on historical frames and actions. These models often leverage diffusion-based generative architectures with Transformer backbones. However, a significant portion of the existing research in this domain primarily focuses on single-agent environments. This limitation restricts their applicability to real-world scenarios, which frequently involve multiple interacting agents and require observations from various viewpoints.

The current landscape of video world models exhibits limitations in addressing the complexities of multi-agent systems. These limitations include the inability to capture inter-agent interactions, manage diverse action strengths from multiple agents, and maintain visual and geometric consistency across different camera views. Approaches attempting to extend world models to multi-agent scenarios have often assumed a fixed number of agents or predefined camera views, thereby limiting their scalability and generalizability. Some prior works, such as MultiVerse, addressed two-player scenarios, while COMBO attempted to compose single-agent models, but these did not fully account for inter-agent interactions or scalable multi-view consistency. Concurrent work, like Solaris, also explored two-player video world models but with a fixed number of agents and views, and computational scaling challenges for more views.

MultiWorld addresses these identified gaps by proposing a unified framework designed for scalable multi-agent and multi-view world modeling. It aims to overcome the challenges of multi-agent controllability, multi-view consistency, and framework scalability, which are not comprehensively handled by existing single-agent or limited multi-agent approaches. By doing so, MultiWorld contributes to the development of more generalizable and realistic simulators for complex domains such as collaborative robotics and multi-player video games, moving beyond the single-agent paradigm prevalent in much of the current literature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of MultiWorld is motivated by the recognized limitations of existing video world models when applied to real-world multi-agent and multi-view scenarios. The paper identifies three primary challenges that current single-agent world models do not adequately address:

1. **Multi-Agent Controllability:** In environments with multiple agents, a world model must accurately associate specific actions with their corresponding agents and synchronize their executions. Existing models, designed for single agents, struggle to process simultaneous actions from multiple entities, particularly when actions are ambiguous (e.g., "mirror actions" where agents perform inverse movements) or when agents exhibit varying levels of activity (some moving, others stationary). A model that simply stacks actions together may fail to discern which agent performs which action or how to prioritize the influence of active agents. The objective is to enable precise control over each agent's behavior within a shared environment.

2. **Multi-View Consistency:** Real-world multi-agent systems often involve agents perceiving a shared environment from distinct viewpoints. For a simulated environment to be coherent, the observations generated for each view must be visually and geometrically consistent with each other. Previous models typically generate views independently or with limited cross-view awareness, leading to potential inconsistencies in object positions, interactions, or environmental state across different perspectives. The motivation is to ensure that the model synthesizes visually coherent videos across diverse perspectives, where observations from multiple agents remain geometrically consistent, reflecting a single underlying environmental state. This requires the model to maintain and leverage global 3D-aware environmental information.

3. **Framework Scalability:** Real-world applications of multi-agent systems, such as large-scale robotic fleets or complex multi-player games, can involve a variable number of agents and camera views. Existing models often assume a fixed number of agents or predefined camera setups, which restricts their applicability and requires architectural modifications for different configurations. The objective is to develop a framework that can flexibly scale with respect to both the number of agents and the number of views without requiring a fixed architecture or significant retraining. This implies the need for mechanisms that can handle variable-length inputs for actions and observations and process them efficiently.

In summary, the core motivation for MultiWorld is to bridge the gap between single-agent video world models and the demands of complex, interactive, multi-agent, and multi-view real-world environments. By addressing these three challenges, the authors aim to create a more robust, generalizable, and efficient world modeling framework capable of supporting advanced applications in areas like collaborative robotics and immersive gaming.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MultiWorld is a unified framework for scalable multi-agent, multi-view video world modeling. Its core architecture is built upon an action-conditioned diffusion backbone, specifically using Flow Matching with a Transformer. The framework integrates two primary novel components: the Multi-Agent Condition Module (MACM) and the Global State Encoder (GSE).

**Backbone and Notation:**
The model formulates multi-agent, multi-view world simulation as a collection of `C` image-action-conditioned video generation problems, where `C` is the number of camera views. Videos from different views are synthesized in parallel using a shared global environment state. The model processes joint actions `a_i = (a^1_i..., a^K_i)` for `K` agents at frame `i`. It models the conditional distribution of future video frames for each camera view `c` using Flow Matching, parameterizing a velocity network `v_theta(x_t^c, t, a, o)`. A frame-wise causal mask is applied to action cross-attention to ensure temporal causality and support long-horizon autoregressive generation.

**Multi-Agent Condition Module (MACM):**
The MACM addresses challenges related to distinguishing agent identities and managing variable action strengths. It comprises two main mechanisms:

- **Agent Identity Embedding (AIE):** To resolve identity ambiguity among agents, AIE assigns distinct identity embeddings to action tokens. It utilizes Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) to compute these embeddings, applying a rotation matrix to each agent's action embedding `a^i_f`. This approach ensures that action latents are made agent-specific, breaking the symmetry of the multi-agent action space. A self-attention block is then applied to these AIE-enhanced action tokens to explicitly model inter-agent interactions. The use of RoPE allows the framework to generalize to a variable number of agents without architectural changes, as relative embeddings can be extrapolated.

- **Adaptive Action Weighting (AAW):** To prioritize active agents whose actions drive significant environmental changes, AAW dynamically assigns weighting factors to each action token. This is implemented via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that predicts these weights. The action tokens are then multiplied by their respective weights and summed to form a unified action representation for each frame. This dynamic scaling mechanism guides the model to focus on more influential actions.

The aggregated action tokens from the MACM are subsequently injected into the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone through causal cross-attention.

**Global State Encoder (GSE):**
The GSE is designed to ensure multi-view consistency by providing a shared, 3D-aware global environmental state.

- **3D-aware Environmental State Extraction:** Given multiple input views `O = {O_c}^C_{c=1}`, the GSE uses a pretrained VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer), an end-to-end 3D reconstruction foundation model, as its backbone. VGGT extracts latent representations `H_vggt` that inherently contain 3D spatial information of the current global environment state from these variable-length observations.

- **Global Representation Fusion:** These latent features `H_vggt` are then processed by an MLP to align their dimensions with the DiT backbone, yielding `H`. This `H` is then injected into the DiT via cross-attention to condition the generation process. This design allows the diffusion model to generate multi-view-consistent partial observations based on a single, shared global environment state. This approach improves multi-view spatial consistency, supports an arbitrary number of views by compressing multi-view information into a unified 3D-aware state, and facilitates efficient parallel generation of different views.

**Scalable Framework:**
MultiWorld’s scalability is a fundamental design principle:

- **Agent Scalability:** Achieved through the MACM's AIE, which uses relative identity embeddings that extrapolate to accommodate varying numbers of agents without requiring architectural modifications.
- **Camera-View Scalability:** Enabled by the GSE, which compresses information from a variable number of partial observations into a unified global environmental state, allowing robust performance regardless of the input view count. This decomposition also permits parallel generation of views, significantly reducing inference latency compared to sequential methods.
- **Long-Horizon Autoregressive Generation:** MultiWorld supports stable, long-horizon multi-view simulation. It autoregressively generates multi-view video chunks, with the global environment state continuously updated by feeding the most recent observations (last frames of the generated chunk) back into the GSE for the subsequent chunk.

**Datasets:**
Two new multi-agent, multi-view datasets were constructed for evaluation:
- A real-player dataset from the multi-player game *It Takes Two*, featuring a dual-agent scenario with distinct viewpoints and interdependent actions. It includes 100 hours of preprocessed data (21M frames, 2560x1440 resolution).
- A multi-robot manipulation dataset using RoboFactory, encompassing 2-4 agents performing various tasks. This dataset includes thousands of episodes with variable agent and camera configurations, including successful and failure trajectories to reduce bias.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the multi-player video game and multi-robot manipulation datasets demonstrate the capabilities of the MultiWorld framework in terms of video fidelity, action-following ability, and multi-view consistency.

**Quantitative Evaluation:**
MultiWorld was compared against three baselines: Standard Image-Action-to-Video Model, Concatenated-View Video World Model (Concat-View), and COMBO. The evaluation used metrics including Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) for video quality (lower is better), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) (lower is better), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) (higher is better), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) (higher is better), Action-following ability (higher accuracy/normalized score is better), and Reprojection Error (RPE) for multi-view consistency (lower is better).

- **Overall Performance:** MultiWorld consistently outperformed all baselines across both the multi-player video game and multi-robot manipulation scenarios on most metrics.
 - **Multi-Player Video Game:** MultiWorld achieved the lowest FVD (179 vs. 207-245), lowest RPE (0.67 vs. 0.72-0.75), and highest action-following score (89.8 vs. 88.4-89.3).
 - **Multi-Robot Manipulation:** MultiWorld achieved the lowest FVD (96 vs. 99-106) and lowest RPE (1.52 vs. 1.54-1.60), and comparable action-following (88.7) to Combo (88.5). Concat-View had higher action-following (92.0) but was noted as not comparable due to being trained only on two camera views.

- **Ablation Study on Main Architectural Components:**
 - **MACM Contribution:** Adding the Multi-Agent Condition Module (+ MACM) to the standard baseline improved action-following ability significantly (from 88.4 to 89.7). It also led to an improvement in FVD (from 245 to 228), suggesting that enhanced controllability contributes to video quality.
 - **GSE Contribution:** When both MACM and GSE modules were applied, MultiWorld achieved its best performance (FVD 179, RPE 0.67, Action 89.8), demonstrating that integrating GSE improved multi-view consistency (RPE reduced from 0.76 to 0.67 after MACM alone) and further enhanced overall visual quality.

- **Ablation Study on MACM Design Choices:**
 - **Agent Identity Embedding (AIE) Base Frequency:** Experiments showed that reducing the RoPE base frequency from 10000 to 20 improved performance (FVD from 234 to 228, Action from 89.2 to 89.7). This indicates that a base frequency that better matches the number of agents leads to more distinguishable agent embeddings.
 - **Adaptive Action Weighting (AAW):** The inclusion of AAW improved visual quality and action-following performance (FVD from 245 to 236, Action from 88.4 to 88.6), supporting the mechanism's role in prioritizing influential actions.

- **Ablation Study on GSE Design Choices:**
 - **GSE Backbone Comparison:** Different backbones for the Global State Encoder were evaluated. The VGGT backbone (used in MultiWorld) yielded the best results (FVD 179, RPE 0.67) compared to a baseline without global state (FVD 228, RPE 0.75), Wan VAE (FVD 256, RPE 0.71), and DINOv2 (FVD 232, RPE 0.72). This result highlights the effectiveness of using a 3D reconstruction-aware model like VGGT for extracting shared global environmental states.

**Qualitative Results:**
Qualitative comparisons illustrated several advantages of MultiWorld:
- **Action Following and Consistency:** MultiWorld generated videos that adhered to specified actions, resulting in final states nearly identical to ground truth, outperforming baselines which exhibited inaccurate action execution, agent disappearance, or multi-view inconsistency.
- **Action Controllability:** The model demonstrated the ability to generate static videos in response to zero-action commands, avoiding spurious motion artifacts observed in other action-conditioned models.
- **Physical Consistency:** MultiWorld maintained consistent physical effects across viewpoints, such as consistent shadows cast by objects and correctly persistent snow footprints across different camera perspectives.
- **Multi-Agent Interactions:** The framework accurately simulated complex coordinated physical interactions, such as two agents simultaneously pushing and pulling a large board.
- **Failure Trajectory Simulation:** In multi-robot manipulation, MultiWorld was capable of simulating realistic cooperative failures, including inter-robot racing and collisions, which is relevant for enhancing failure trajectory data.
- **Long-Horizon Generation:** MultiWorld demonstrated stable autoregressive generation for sequences up to twice the training context length with minimal degradation, and up to four times with limited quality loss, enabling extended simulations.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges that the current scale of MultiWorld is limited, and large-scale training remains an unexplored area due to computational constraints.

**Future Work:**
Future research directions include investigating real-time multi-agent generation and exploring memory mechanisms for ultra-long multi-agent simulations to address substantial spatial and temporal memory demands.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MultiWorld presents a unified framework that addresses several limitations in existing video world models, specifically concerning multi-agent interactions, multi-view consistency, and scalability. This work has several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancement in World Modeling:** By providing a scalable framework for multi-agent, multi-view scenarios, MultiWorld advances the capabilities of video world models beyond predominantly single-agent settings. This moves the field closer to simulating complex real-world environments with interdependent agents and diverse observational perspectives.

2. **Enhanced Controllability and Consistency:** The introduction of the Multi-Agent Condition Module (MACM) provides a mechanism for precise multi-agent controllability, allowing the model to distinguish actions of individual agents and prioritize their influence. The Global State Encoder (GSE) ensures multi-view consistency by generating a shared, 3D-aware environmental state, leading to visually and geometrically coherent observations across different viewpoints. These capabilities are crucial for realistic simulations.

3. **Scalability for Diverse Applications:** The framework's ability to scale flexibly with respect to both agent and view counts without requiring architectural changes expands its applicability. This is particularly relevant for domains like collaborative robotics, where the number of robots can vary, and multi-player video games, which often feature dynamic player counts and camera setups.

4. **Improved Simulation Fidelity for Downstream Tasks:** The enhanced video fidelity, action-following ability, and multi-view consistency demonstrated by MultiWorld suggest that it could serve as a more robust simulator. This could benefit downstream tasks in artificial intelligence, such as:
 - **Robot Learning:** Providing a virtual environment for training and testing multi-robot policies, including the simulation of difficult-to-collect failure trajectories, which can inform more robust robot control strategies.
 - **Embodied AI:** Creating more realistic and interactive environments for embodied agents to learn and interact.
 - **Game Development:** Enabling the generation of complex multi-player game scenarios and dynamic environments, potentially aiding in game design and testing.

5. **New Benchmarking Resources:** The creation and release of two complementary datasets—one from a multi-player video game (It-Takes-Two subset) and another from a multi-robot manipulation simulator (RoboFactory)—provide valuable resources for future research. These datasets offer comprehensive benchmarks for evaluating multi-agent and multi-view configurations, fostering further development in this specialized area.

6. **Efficiency in Multi-View Generation:** The parallel generation of different views, enabled by the GSE, offers computational efficiency. This aspect could be impactful for applications requiring real-time or near real-time multi-view simulations, though the paper notes that real-time multi-agent generation is an area for future work.

In conclusion, MultiWorld represents a step towards developing more generalizable and capable world models by addressing inherent complexities of multi-agent and multi-view systems. Its contributions in controllability, consistency, and scalability lay foundational groundwork for future research in interactive AI and advanced simulation.
