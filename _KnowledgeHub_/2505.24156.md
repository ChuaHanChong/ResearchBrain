# Towards a Generalizable Bimanual Foundation Policy via Flow-based Video Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> CogRobot is a framework for learning generalizable bimanual manipulation policies that leverages pre-trained Text-to-Video (T2V) models by introducing optical flow as an intermediate representation. The framework achieves improved video generation quality (e.g., 22.663 PSNR on RDT) and higher success rates in bimanual tasks across both simulation (e.g., 100% for "Put Apple Cabinet") and real-world scenarios (e.g., 0.75 for "Pull box").

## Problem

- The large action space of bimanual manipulation makes it challenging to learn effective coordination policies for two robotic arms.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality bimanual manipulation data, making data collection complex and costly.
- Existing Text-to-Video (T2V) models lack "embodied domain knowledge," leading to physically implausible predictions (e.g., hallucinations, vague movements) when applied directly to robotics.

## Method

- CogRobot decomposes the problem into predicting future observation trajectories (videos) from language instructions and then deriving executable actions from these predicted videos.
- It employs a two-stage fine-tuning approach: a fine-tuned T2V model predicts future optical flow sequences from text, and another T2V model generates bimanual videos using the predicted flow as guidance.
- Optical flow is converted into a 3-channel "flow video" to enable pre-trained T2V models to process fine-grained motion, and a lightweight diffusion policy extracts robot actions from the generated video frames.

## Results

- Achieved superior video generation quality compared to direct fine-tuning, with 22.663 PSNR and 0.836 SSIM on the RDT dataset.
- Demonstrated improved success rates in simulation, reaching 100.0% for "Put Apple Cabinet" and 36.0% for "Block Handover" tasks on the RoboTwin benchmark.
- Showed enhanced real-world performance, achieving a 0.70 success rate for "Lift bag" and 0.75 for "Pull box" tasks, outperforming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While bimanual actions are complex, their resulting state trajectories can be uniformly represented through videos, a domain where large T2V models excel.
> - Optical flow serves as an effective intermediate representation, bridging the modality gap between text instructions and precise robotic movements by capturing fine-grained motion.
> - Adapting pre-trained large-scale generative models, like T2V models, for embodied tasks can reduce reliance on extensive domain-specific robot data and improve physical grounding.
