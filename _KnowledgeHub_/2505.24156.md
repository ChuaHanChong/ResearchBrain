# Towards a Generalizable Bimanual Foundation Policy via Flow-based Video Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> CogRobot is a framework for learning generalizable bimanual manipulation policies that leverages pre-trained Text-to-Video (T2V) models by introducing optical flow as an intermediate representation. The framework achieves improved video generation quality (e.g., 22.663 PSNR on RDT) and higher success rates in bimanual tasks across both simulation (e.g., 100% for "Put Apple Cabinet") and real-world scenarios (e.g., 0.75 for "Pull box").

## Problem

- The large action space of bimanual manipulation makes it challenging to learn effective coordination policies for two robotic arms.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality bimanual manipulation data, making data collection complex and costly.
- Existing Text-to-Video (T2V) models lack "embodied domain knowledge," leading to physically implausible predictions (e.g., hallucinations, vague movements) when applied directly to robotics.

## Method

- CogRobot decomposes the problem into predicting future observation trajectories (videos) from language instructions and then deriving executable actions from these predicted videos.
- It employs a two-stage fine-tuning approach: a fine-tuned T2V model predicts future optical flow sequences from text, and another T2V model generates bimanual videos using the predicted flow as guidance.
- Optical flow is converted into a 3-channel "flow video" to enable pre-trained T2V models to process fine-grained motion, and a lightweight diffusion policy extracts robot actions from the generated video frames.

## Results

- Achieved superior video generation quality compared to direct fine-tuning, with 22.663 PSNR and 0.836 SSIM on the RDT dataset.
- Demonstrated improved success rates in simulation, reaching 100.0% for "Put Apple Cabinet" and 36.0% for "Block Handover" tasks on the RoboTwin benchmark.
- Showed enhanced real-world performance, achieving a 0.70 success rate for "Lift bag" and 0.75 for "Pull box" tasks, outperforming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While bimanual actions are complex, their resulting state trajectories can be uniformly represented through videos, a domain where large T2V models excel.
> - Optical flow serves as an effective intermediate representation, bridging the modality gap between text instructions and precise robotic movements by capturing fine-grained motion.
> - Adapting pre-trained large-scale generative models, like T2V models, for embodied tasks can reduce reliance on extensive domain-specific robot data and improve physical grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Bimanual manipulation represents one of the most challenging frontiers in robotics, requiring precise coordination between two robotic arms to perform complex tasks that mirror human-like dexterity. This paper introduces CogRobot, a framework that addresses the fundamental challenges of learning generalizable bimanual manipulation policies by leveraging pre-trained Text-to-Video (T2V) models and introducing optical flow as an intermediate representation.

The research emerges from a collaboration between China Telecom's Institute of Artificial Intelligence (TeleAI), Northwestern Polytechnical University, and Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. This industry-academia partnership brings together substantial computational resources and cutting-edge research expertise to tackle the data scarcity and generalization challenges that have long hindered progress in bimanual robotics.

### 2. The Challenge of Bimanual Manipulation

Traditional approaches to bimanual manipulation face several critical limitations. The action space for coordinating two arms is substantially larger than single-arm systems, making it difficult to learn effective policies. High-quality bimanual manipulation data is extremely scarce due to the complexity and cost of data collection. Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often require training from scratch on vast, aggregated datasets, leading to substantial computational overhead while still struggling with the multimodality inherent in bimanual tasks.

Direct application of state-of-the-art T2V models to robot manipulation also proves problematic. While models like CogVideoX demonstrate strong generative capabilities, they lack "embodied domain knowledge," leading to physically implausible predictions including:

- **Physical Hallucination**: Generating human hands or fictitious robot motions that violate physical realism
- **Task Confusion**: Difficulty determining correct subtasks in long-horizon instructions
- **Vague Instructions**: Producing imprecise movements not grounded in feasible robot dynamics

### 3. CogRobot Framework

CogRobot addresses these challenges by decomposing the problem into two key components: predicting future observation trajectories (videos) from language instructions, and deriving executable actions from these predicted videos. The key insight is that while bimanual actions are complex, the resulting state trajectories can be uniformly represented through videos—a domain where T2V models excel.

The framework operates through three main stages:

1. **Text-to-Flow Generation**: A fine-tuned T2V model predicts future optical flow sequences given initial observations and language instructions
2. **Flow-to-Video Prediction**: Another T2V model generates actual bimanual manipulation videos using the predicted optical flow as guidance
3. **Action Generation**: A lightweight diffusion policy extracts executable robot actions from the generated video frames

### 4. Two-Stage Fine-Tuning Approach

The core innovation lies in using optical flow as an intermediate representation. Optical flow captures per-pixel displacement between image frames, effectively representing fine-grained motion of robot arms, end-effectors, and object interactions. This addresses the modality gap between text instructions and precise robotic movements.

#### 4.1. Stage 1: Text-to-Flow Generation

The first stage reformulates optical flow prediction as a video generation task. Since direct prediction of 2-channel optical flow by RGB-trained T2V models creates a modality gap, the authors convert optical flow into a 3-channel "flow video" by mapping flow direction to color and magnitude to saturation. This rule-based conversion allows leveraging pre-trained T2V expertise while maintaining computational efficiency.

The Text-to-Flow module learns: $v_F \sim p_{\theta_f}(v_F | o_0, l)$

where $v_F$ is the flow video, $o_0$ is the initial observation, and $l$ is the language instruction.

#### 4.2. Stage 2: Flow-to-Video Prediction

The predicted flow video serves as explicit motion guidance for generating actual bimanual manipulation videos. The model learns:

$v \sim p_{\theta_v}(\cdot | o_0, l, v_F)$

This approach provides higher-level semantic information about object and arm movements than raw text alone, significantly improving the model's ability to focus on relevant regions and motions.

### 5. Experimental Validation

The researchers developed a comprehensive experimental setup including:

- **Custom Bimanual Platform**: A real dual-arm system using two 7-DoF Realman robotic arms with external Intel RealSense camera
- **VR-based Teleoperation**: An efficient data collection system using Apple Vision Pro to capture 6D wrist poses and finger positions
- **Multi-domain Evaluation**: Testing on existing datasets (RDT, RoboMIND), custom simulation (RoboTwin), and real-world scenarios

### 6. Key Findings and Results

The experimental validation demonstrates significant improvements across multiple metrics:

#### 6.1. Video Generation Quality

CogRobot's flow-guided approach (CogVideoX-2B-Flow) substantially outperforms direct fine-tuning methods:
- **RDT Dataset**: 22.663 PSNR vs 20.502 PSNR for direct fine-tuning
- **Improved Structural Similarity**: 0.836 SSIM vs 0.806 SSIM
- **Better Motion Quality**: 760 FVD vs 1064 FVD

#### 6.2. Simulation Performance

On the RoboTwin benchmark, CogRobot consistently outperforms single-view baselines:
- **"Put Apple Cabinet"**: 100.0% success rate vs 82.0% for vanilla Diffusion Policy
- **"Block Handover"**: 36.0% vs 2.0% success rate

#### 6.3. Real-World Results

Real-world experiments show substantial improvements:
- **"Lift bag"**: 0.70 success rate vs 0.50 for baseline
- **"Pull box"**: 0.75 success rate vs 0.05 for baseline

The more pronounced advantage in complex tasks indicates that video prediction provides informative plans that effectively guide dual-arm coordination.

### 7. Significance and Future Directions

CogRobot represents a paradigm shift in robotic learning by efficiently adapting large-scale generative models for embodied tasks. The approach addresses fundamental challenges in bimanual manipulation:

1. **Data Efficiency**: Reduces the need for extensive domain-specific robot data by leveraging pre-trained models
2. **Physical Grounding**: Uses optical flow to ensure generated plans are physically executable
3. **Generalization**: Demonstrates effectiveness across simulation and real-world scenarios

The work opens new research directions for developing generalizable robot policies through foundation model adaptation. While current limitations include requiring separate policies for each task, the framework establishes a strong foundation for future developments in universal robotic intelligence.

The success of CogRobot suggests that the future of robot learning may increasingly rely on fine-tuning pre-trained foundation models rather than building robot-specific models from scratch, potentially accelerating progress toward more capable and generalizable robotic systems.
