# RoboTALES: Learning Reasoning-Guided Robot Policies via Task-Aligned Simulated Futures

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, RoboTALES, tightly integrates hierarchical reasoning with visual imagination and action generation, enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by learning task-aligned simulated futures. The system achieved a 64% average success rate on the RoboCasa benchmark and 97% on LIBERO10, surpassing prior methods with fewer demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing video generative models for robot control often produce simulated futures that lack semantic alignment with task intent and are not consistently action-conditional, hindering reliable planning.
- High-level reasoning from LLMs typically acts as an external module, loosely coupled with the robot's internal predictive models, preventing closed-loop alignment between imagination and action.
- Learning complex, long-horizon visuomotor tasks from high-dimensional observations with traditional methods is often sample-inefficient and lacks explicit reasoning.

## Method

- **RoboTALES** is a single-stage framework that jointly trains a ==video generator== and an ==action policy==, allowing for co-adaptation of internal representations and seamless integration of reasoning.
- An ==LLM planner== decomposes long-horizon tasks into a sequence of sub-tasks, which condition a pretrained ==Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)== backbone to guide future rollouts toward structured, milestone-driven simulations.
- A ==VLM critic== evaluates the semantic alignment and goal satisfaction of generated futures, providing a reward signal that optimizes the video generator through ==differentiable policy optimization (DDPO)==.

## Results

- RoboTALES achieved a **64%** average success rate on RoboCasa (vs VideoPolicy's **57.5%**, using just **50** vs. **300** demonstrations) and a **97%** mean success rate across **10** LIBERO10 tasks (vs VideoPolicy's **94%**).
- Qualitative evaluation revealed that RoboTALES maintained environmental integrity and physical plausibility throughout long-horizon task execution, effectively mitigating semantic drift.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the combined use of the LLM planner and VLM critic, along with joint training of the generator and policy, were crucial for robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning the video generator's imagination on hierarchical sub-task plans from an LLM planner creates milestone-driven, semantically coherent future simulations, preventing semantic drift.
> - Leveraging a VLM critic to provide reward-based feedback for semantic alignment and goal satisfaction guides the generative model towards producing task-faithful imagined futures.
> - Jointly optimizing the video generator and action policy allows action-level gradients to shape the world model's predictive features, ensuring they are directly informative for precise and purposeful control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous agents, particularly in visuomotor control, faces challenges in enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks from high-dimensional visual observations. Traditional model-free reinforcement learning approaches can achieve high task performance but often lack an explicit reasoning substrate and are sample-inefficient. Model-based approaches aim to address this by learning a "world model" that allows an agent to mentally simulate future states before acting.

Recent advancements have leveraged diffusion-based video generation models for robot control. Works such as Video-Policy, Gen2Act, and ViPRA have demonstrated the potential of these models as priors for policy learning, using generated rollouts to either supervise or directly condition action generation. However, these video generators are primarily optimized for visual realism or reconstruction, which can result in simulated futures that are weakly grounded in task intent and not consistently action-conditional. This limitation makes them challenging to use for reliable planning or policy extraction.

Concurrently, large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have shown capabilities in decomposing long-horizon tasks into subtasks and guiding robot behavior through natural language. Prior research has explored using LLMs for grounding plans in robot affordances, incorporating environment feedback for replanning, and decomposing navigation instructions. Nevertheless, a common limitation in these approaches is the loose coupling between language reasoning and the agent’s predictive representations. Language often influences action selection externally, rather than shaping the internal predictive dynamics of the world model. This decoupling can prevent a closed-loop alignment among imagined futures, high-level reasoning, and low-level actions.

RoboTALES aims to bridge these gaps by integrating hierarchical reasoning and semantic feedback directly into the process of video generation and policy learning. It seeks to develop a mechanism that explicitly imposes hierarchical structure on imagination and enforces the semantic faithfulness of simulated futures, which are critical for reliably benefiting robot action generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework, RoboTALES, that formalizes the alignment between high-level task logic and low-level visual dynamics to enable robots to execute complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks more effectively.

The motivation for RoboTALES stems from several limitations observed in existing approaches:

- **Addressing Semantic Drift in Video Generation**: Pretrained video generative models, while powerful for visual realism, often produce imagined futures that deviate from the actual task intent and may not reliably reflect the consequences of specific actions. This makes them difficult to leverage directly for robust planning or policy learning in robotic control, particularly for multi-step tasks where errors can compound over time. The objective is to anchor the video generator's imagination in abstract reasoning to produce temporally consistent and semantically coherent rollouts.
- **Integrating Hierarchical Reasoning with Predictive Models**: Current LLM-based planning often operates as an external module, providing high-level directives without directly shaping the internal predictive representations of the world model. This decoupling means the agent's internal "imagination" is not directly informed or constrained by the hierarchical plan, leading to a lack of closed-loop alignment between what the agent imagines and what it ultimately does. RoboTALES aims to integrate hierarchical subtask plans from an LLM planner directly into the latent space of the video generator, transforming rollout generation into a structured, milestone-driven simulation.
- **Ensuring Semantic Faithfulness of Imagined Futures**: Even with planner conditioning, a generative model might produce visually plausible futures that violate high-level task intent. The motivation is to introduce a mechanism that evaluates imagined futures against the task instruction and uses this feedback to guide the model's internal representations toward goal-aligned semantics. This involves a critic that provides reward-based feedback to keep the model's internal representations focused on the goal, rather than merely visual statistics.
- **Improving Policy Learning through Task-Aligned Features**: The lack of alignment between imagination, reasoning, and action can lead to erratic or less purposeful robot behaviors. The objective is to condition action generation on reasoning-aligned predictive features, thereby producing more precise and semantically consistent trajectories, especially in long and complex tasks. By enabling action-level gradients to flow back into the video generator, the framework encourages the world model to learn representations that are directly informative for downstream control.

In summary, RoboTALES is motivated by the need for a unified framework that combines the "imagine before acting" paradigm with explicit, language-grounded hierarchical reasoning and semantic evaluation, thereby enabling robots to perform purposeful and reliable actions in complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboTALES is presented as a single-stage framework designed to learn task-aligned simulated futures and subsequently use these to train robot policies. The approach employs a coupled, single-stage training strategy where the video generator and the action policy are optimized simultaneously, allowing for co-adaptation of representations.

The framework comprises four main components:

- **LLM Planner (F_P)**: An LLM, specifically Gemini-2.5-Pro, acts as the high-level reasoning engine. Given a natural language task instruction (τ) and the current observation (s_t), the planner decomposes the long-horizon task into a sequence of K semantically coherent sub-tasks (C = {c^(1)..., c^(K)}). These sub-tasks are then concatenated with the original instruction to form an augmented, reasoning-rich plan (C* = [τ; c^(1); c^(2); ...; c^(K)]). This augmented plan serves as a dense semantic signal to guide the video generator. The planner generates between 2 to 5 sub-tasks depending on complexity, and these decompositions are pre-generated and cached for computational efficiency and consistency.

- **Video Generator (G_θ)**: The framework utilizes a pretrained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) backbone as the next-state generator. This model is conditioned on the current visual state (s_t) and the augmented plan (C*) from the LLM Planner. It predicts a short-horizon future rollout of Δ frames (ŝ_{t+1:t+Δ}). During training, parameters of the VideoUNet are largely frozen, except for the cross-attention modules that ingest CLIP embeddings of C* and a targeted subset of decoder layers whose hidden states condition the action UNet. This selective unfreezing allows the model to become instruction-aware without compromising its pretrained generative stability.

- **VLM Critic (F_R)**: A frozen vision-language model, parameterized by ψ (e.g., SBERT-based embeddings), evaluates the generated future rollouts (ŝ_{t+1:t+Δ}) against the goal instruction (τ) in pixel space. It produces a scalar reward signal (r) that quantifies semantic alignment, realism, and goal satisfaction. This reward signal is used to optimize the trainable parameters of the Video Generator (G_θ*) via differentiable policy optimization (DDPO), reinforcing denoising transitions that lead to high-reward visual states. The denoising process is treated as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP), and a REINFORCE-style objective is optimized over the denoising trajectory, with a running mean baseline to compute the advantage.

- **Action Generator (π_ϕ)**: The policy is implemented as a 1D diffusion UNet, similar to Diffusion Policy. It is conditioned on initial action noise (a_i) and hidden state embeddings (f_{Gθ*}^{{l}}) extracted from the selected decoder layers of the video generator, as well as the augmented plan (C*). This component generates continuous executable action sequences (a_{t:t+H}).

The core of the methodology lies in the **joint optimization strategy**. Unlike decoupled approaches, RoboTALES does not impose a stop-gradient between the action generator and the video generator. This means action-level gradients from the action diffusion loss (L_action) propagate back through the conditioning features into the video generator's trainable decoder layers. This enables the video model to receive a direct training signal from the action objective, shaping its decoder features to be maximally informative for action prediction, in addition to being visually faithful and semantically aligned.

The total training loss combines three objectives:
- `L_video(θ*)`: Standard noise-prediction objective for visual fidelity.
- `L_DDPO(θ*)`: Policy optimization objective for semantic alignment, guided by the VLM Critic.
- `L_action(ϕ, θ*)`: Action diffusion loss for precise control.

This coupled design fosters a co-adaptation between imagination and action, where the video model learns to "imagine for acting" and the policy learns to "act from imagination," contributing to more purposeful and precise motor actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated RoboTALES on diverse manipulation tasks from the RoboCasa and LIBERO10 benchmarks, comparing its performance against existing methods.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **RoboCasa Benchmark (Table 1)**: RoboTALES achieved the highest average success rate of 64%, surpassing VideoPolicy (57.5%) and other baselines. The method demonstrated notable improvements across various task categories:
 - **Pick and Place tasks**: Exhibited strong performance, with success rates up to 66% (PnPStoveToCounter) and an average of 48%. This outperformed methods like VideoPolicy, which achieved 64% on PnPStoveToCounter and an average of 38.75% for Pick and Place tasks.
 - **Structure-sensitive tasks (Doors and Drawers)**: Showed significant margins, indicating improved execution of temporally ordered subgoals. For instance, achieving 100% success on "CloseDrawer" compared to VideoPolicy's 88%.
 - **Contact-rich behaviors (Twisting Knobs/Levers, Pressing Buttons)**: Demonstrated improved performance, suggesting that task-aligned imagination aids in disambiguating fine-grained action choices. For example, 96% success on "CoffeePressButton" and "TurnOnMicrowave," against VideoPolicy's 92% and 86%, respectively.
 - **Sample Efficiency**: RoboTALES achieved its results using 50 demonstrations, outperforming methods like GR00T and FPV, which employed 300 demonstrations.

- **LIBERO10 Benchmark (Table 2 & 3)**: RoboTALES achieved a mean success rate of 97% across all 10 tasks, outperforming all baselines. The strongest competitor, VideoPolicy, achieved 94%, followed by UVA at 90%. RoboTALES demonstrated near-perfect success on many tasks (e.g., 1.0 on "KITCHEN SCENE3 turn on the stove and put the moka pot on it"), with the lowest score being 0.88 on "KITCHEN SCENE8 put both moka pots on the stove." These results suggest robust generalization across diverse scene configurations and a strong capacity for sequential multi-step manipulation.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3)

- A comparison with VideoPolicy on RoboCasa manipulation tasks illustrated that RoboTALES maintained environmental integrity and physical plausibility throughout task execution. The baseline often exhibited semantic drift and physical distortions during long-horizon execution, leading to task failures (e.g., failing to pick up an object or losing consistency when moving it). RoboTALES, by conditioning the video model on reasoning-rich sub-goals, maintained a strong semantic anchor, preventing execution drift and ensuring goal-oriented trajectories.

#### 4.3. Ablations and Discussion

- **Effect of the Planner (Figure 4)**: The planner was shown to be necessary but not sufficient alone. While naively injecting structured sub-goals into an unadapted video model led to performance degradation, training the video model with planner conditioning alone improved performance. The full model, integrating both the planner and the VLM Critic, achieved the highest success rates, indicating the combined benefit of structured reasoning and semantic feedback.
- **Effect of Policy Optimization (Critic) (Figure 5 & 7)**: RL-based refinement via the VLM Critic demonstrably improved downstream control. RoboTALES exhibited lower variance in object-to-end-effector distance, indicating more consistent reaching behavior. It showed greater robustness to observation noise, maintaining significantly higher success rates where the baseline collapsed. Additionally, RoboTALES produced smoother latent dynamics and lower end-effector jerk, reflecting more physically plausible actions. The critic's reward signal was well-calibrated, with higher predicted rewards correlating monotonically with higher task success (AUROC of 0.72).
- **Effect of different VLM critic choices (Figure 8)**: An ablation comparing CycleReward and SBERT-based critics showed that the SBERT variant yielded a higher average success rate across tasks, demonstrating superior robustness for reasoning guidance.
- **Effect of changing action horizon (Figure 9)**: An action horizon of 16 steps yielded optimal performance, suggesting a balance between re-planning frequency and avoiding open-loop drift.
- **Joint Training vs. Stage-wise Training (Figure 10)**: The coupled, single-stage training approach of RoboTALES outperformed decoupled stage-wise training, confirming that the co-adaptation of the video generator and action policy is crucial for improved action prediction.
- **Planner Decomposition Quality (Figure 11)**: An LLM-judge evaluation indicated that planner-generated decompositions were preferred (60% wins) over prompt-only baselines and were more concise (2.80 vs 5.28 steps on average), highlighting improved plan efficiency.
- **Retaining and Improving General Video Generation Capabilities (Figure 12)**: Evaluation on the UCF101 dataset showed that joint task-aligned training did not compromise the underlying generative video prior. Instead, it improved general video generation quality, leading to lower Fréchet distance and MMD-RBF, higher cosine alignment, and better diversity/covariance matching compared to the original SVD baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboTALES presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by tightly integrating hierarchical reasoning with visual imagination and action generation. The framework's ability to learn task-aligned simulated futures, guided by an LLM Planner and a VLM Critic, addresses critical limitations of prior approaches that often suffer from semantic drift or a decoupling of high-level reasoning from low-level control.

The potential impact of RoboTALES is multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Performance in Complex Tasks**: By ensuring that imagined futures are semantically faithful and aligned with hierarchical sub-goals, RoboTALES enables robots to perform long-horizon manipulation tasks with higher success rates and greater reliability. This is particularly relevant for scenarios requiring sequential steps, fine-grained object interaction, and robustness to environmental variations.
- **More Coherent and Purposeful Robot Actions**: The framework’s mechanism for shaping the video generator's internal representations through action-level gradients results in more precise, smoother, and semantically consistent robot trajectories. This can reduce erratic behaviors and improve the overall fluidity of robot operations, making them more adaptable to unstructured environments.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency**: The reported results indicate that RoboTALES can achieve competitive or superior performance with fewer demonstrations compared to some prior methods. This is crucial for real-world robotic applications where obtaining large datasets of human demonstrations can be costly and time-consuming.
- **Advancement in World Modeling for Control**: RoboTALES refines the concept of using video generative models as world models by explicitly addressing the grounding of imagined futures in task intent. This moves beyond mere visual realism, transforming generative models into more effective tools for decision-making and control in robotics.
- **Path Towards More Intelligent Agents**: The integration of hierarchical reasoning directly into the predictive dynamics of the world model represents a step towards creating more cognitively capable autonomous agents that can "think" or "imagine" in a goal-directed manner, analogous to human cognitive processes.

Future work on RoboTALES includes exploring more sophisticated, task-aware reward functions or learnable reward models that can co-adapt with the generative and policy components. Addressing the computational costs associated with video diffusion models and extending the framework beyond simulation to real-world deployment are also critical next steps. These advancements could further enhance the framework's capabilities and broaden its applicability across various robotic domains.
