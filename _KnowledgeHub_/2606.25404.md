# HEART: Coordination of Heterogeneous Expert Agents for Physically Grounded Robotic Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> A token-aware multi-LLM framework coordinates specialized expert agents to generate robotic task plans that are both logically valid and physically executable, addressing a common challenge in LLM-based robotics. This approach significantly improved plan success rates for various LLM-based planners by 10-70 percentage points across 40 tasks in three household manipulation scenes, while also reducing token usage by 25-30% through agent specialization.

## Problem

- Single-LLM robotic task planning often generates linguistically plausible but physically inexecutable plans, failing to account for specific robot limitations or environmental realities.
- Existing LLM-based methods require extensive physical descriptions in prompts, leading to increased context lengths, higher computational costs, and potential degradation of reasoning quality.
- Multi-agent LLM frameworks for robotics frequently lack explicit coordination mechanisms under communication constraints, resulting in redundant processing, inefficient token usage, and inconsistent reasoning outcomes.

## Method

- The **HEART** framework introduces a ==token-aware multi-LLM system== that coordinates heterogeneous expert agents via a central allocator through four stages: Task Decomposition, Allocation, Expert Reasoning, and Plan Synthesis.
- It employs five role-specialized expert agents (Capability, Environment, Path, Feasibility, Constraint Reasoners) that process atomic subtasks with role-aligned, filtered environmental data to reduce context size and enhance precision.
- A central allocator assigns subtasks to agents using semantic scoring, a history-sensitive penalty for repeated failures, and capacity planning to adhere to a per-round token budget.

## Results

- The 5-agent HEART configuration achieved higher Plan Success Rates (e.g., **72.0**-**76.0%** on Beechwood and Benevolence scenes) and used approximately 25-**30%** fewer total tokens and half the allocation rounds compared to a single-agent setup.
- HEART's full allocation strategy, including the history-sensitive penalty and capacity planning, achieved approximately **99%** Subtask Success Rate with fewer allocation rounds, demonstrating effective resource management and improved task completion reliability.
- HEART significantly increased Plan Success Rates for both LLM-CoT (from **6.7**–**64.0%** to **60.7**–**77.3%**) and DELTA (from **6.7**–**64.0%** to **74.0**–**86.0%**) planners across 40 tasks in three household manipulation scenes by pre-validating task conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Role specialization significantly enhances planning robustness and efficiency, with the 5-agent configuration achieving higher plan success rates and improved token efficiency compared to fewer agents.
> - Systematic coordination under token budgets, via a history-sensitive penalty and capacity planning, is crucial for improving subtask success rates and reducing allocation rounds by preventing repetitive failures and optimizing resource use.
> - Pre-validation of physical conditions through specialized reasoning reduces common planning failure modes, such as precondition violations from hallucinated affordances, enabling downstream planners to generate more physically executable plans.
