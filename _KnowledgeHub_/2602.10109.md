# ST4VLA: Spatially Guided Training for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ST4VLA introduces a spatially guided training framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which preserves and leverages spatial priors from large vision-language models to enable robust and generalizable robot manipulation. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation and real-world tasks, demonstrating enhanced generalization to novel objects and instructions.

## Problem

- A critical gap exists in transferring high-level multimodal understanding of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to low-level, precise robot actions in 3D environments.
- Naive fine-tuning or joint training of VLMs for VLA tasks often leads to a degradation or "collapse" of spatial representations and "gradient conflicts" between perception and action objectives.
- Existing hierarchical robotic systems are rigid and rely on manual heuristics, while purely data-driven VLA models tend to overfit low-level motor patterns without fully exploiting VLM spatial priors.

## Method

- Introduces a ==dual-system== VLA framework comprising a VLM Planner (Qwen2.5-VL for spatial/semantic priors) and an Action Expert (Diffusion Transformer/DINOv2 for motor commands), connected by a lightweight querying transformer with gradient decay.
- Utilizes a two-stage training pipeline: spatial grounding pre-training (VLM on combined web-scale and robotic spatial data) followed by spatially guided action post-training (learning control while refining spatial priors).
- Employs spatial prompting during action training to explicitly activate the VLM's internal reasoning about scene geometry, guiding more reliable grounding and action generation.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average success rates on SimplerEnv (e.g., **84.6%** Visual Matching and **75.9%** Visual Aggregation on Google Robot) and LIBERO (**95.9%** average), significantly outperforming baselines like GR00T N1.5 and π0.
- Demonstrated robust generalization in large-scale simulated pick-and-place (e.g., **68%** for unseen objects, **78%** for unseen instructions) and real-world cluttered scenes (e.g., **62%** for unseen objects, **72%** for novel attribute-based instructions).
- Showed superior capabilities in complex, long-horizon real-world manipulation tasks, consistently surpassing baselines across in-distribution, physical interference, and task replanning settings (e.g., **59%** in-distribution on "Sorting Items into Drawers" versus GR00T N1.5's **42%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit spatial guidance during training prevents the degradation of spatial perception and leads to better alignment between spatial grounding and action policy optimization objectives, reflected in higher Projection-Space Similarity (PSS) scores.
> - A two-stage training approach, first pre-training spatial grounding with both general multimodal and targeted robotic data, then fine-tuning for action with spatial prompts, is critical for achieving robust and generalizable embodied control.
> - The performance gains from integrating spatial priors exhibit a non-linear scaling law, suggesting a "critical mass" of spatial grounding data is necessary to unlock the full potential of VLA models.
