# ST4VLA: Spatially Guided Training for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ST4VLA introduces a spatially guided training framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which preserves and leverages spatial priors from large vision-language models to enable robust and generalizable robot manipulation. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation and real-world tasks, demonstrating enhanced generalization to novel objects and instructions.

## Problem

- A critical gap exists in transferring high-level multimodal understanding of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to low-level, precise robot actions in 3D environments.
- Naive fine-tuning or joint training of VLMs for VLA tasks often leads to a degradation or "collapse" of spatial representations and "gradient conflicts" between perception and action objectives.
- Existing hierarchical robotic systems are rigid and rely on manual heuristics, while purely data-driven VLA models tend to overfit low-level motor patterns without fully exploiting VLM spatial priors.

## Method

- Introduces a ==dual-system== VLA framework comprising a VLM Planner (Qwen2.5-VL for spatial/semantic priors) and an Action Expert (Diffusion Transformer/DINOv2 for motor commands), connected by a lightweight querying transformer with gradient decay.
- Utilizes a two-stage training pipeline: spatial grounding pre-training (VLM on combined web-scale and robotic spatial data) followed by spatially guided action post-training (learning control while refining spatial priors).
- Employs spatial prompting during action training to explicitly activate the VLM's internal reasoning about scene geometry, guiding more reliable grounding and action generation.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average success rates on SimplerEnv (e.g., **84.6%** Visual Matching and **75.9%** Visual Aggregation on Google Robot) and LIBERO (**95.9%** average), significantly outperforming baselines like GR00T N1.5 and π0.
- Demonstrated robust generalization in large-scale simulated pick-and-place (e.g., **68%** for unseen objects, **78%** for unseen instructions) and real-world cluttered scenes (e.g., **62%** for unseen objects, **72%** for novel attribute-based instructions).
- Showed superior capabilities in complex, long-horizon real-world manipulation tasks, consistently surpassing baselines across in-distribution, physical interference, and task replanning settings (e.g., **59%** in-distribution on "Sorting Items into Drawers" versus GR00T N1.5's **42%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit spatial guidance during training prevents the degradation of spatial perception and leads to better alignment between spatial grounding and action policy optimization objectives, reflected in higher Projection-Space Similarity (PSS) scores.
> - A two-stage training approach, first pre-training spatial grounding with both general multimodal and targeted robotic data, then fine-tuning for action with spatial prompts, is critical for achieving robust and generalizable embodied control.
> - The performance gains from integrating spatial priors exhibit a non-linear scaling law, suggesting a "critical mass" of spatial grounding data is necessary to unlock the full potential of VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI aims to equip robots with the ability to understand and interact with the physical world, often guided by human language instructions. Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have revolutionized multimodal understanding, demonstrating remarkable capabilities in tasks like image captioning and visual question answering by learning from vast web-scale data. However, as the authors highlight, a critical gap exists when transferring these capabilities to embodied tasks. Robots must not only grasp the semantic meaning of an instruction but also translate it into precise "where" and "how" to act in a dynamic 3D environment, requiring low-level motor actions. This is challenging because natural language instructions are inherently sparse, while real-world actions demand continuous, finely-grained interactions, and corresponding text-to-action data is scarce.

The paper positions itself within the existing landscape by critiquing two primary categories of prior work:

1. **Hierarchical Robotic Systems:** These approaches explicitly encode spatial priors (e.g., bounding boxes, 3D scene graphs) using foundation models and decompose tasks into symbolic structures. While offering interpretability and leveraging spatial information, they often rely on rule-based task decomposition and manually designed planning heuristics. This rigid separation between symbolic planning and low-level motor control limits their scalability to diverse, complex tasks and impedes end-to-end policy learning.
2. **Data-driven Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Recent VLA models directly learn robot control by leveraging pre-trained VLMs and large-scale teleoperation datasets. These methods eliminate the need for manual task heuristics, moving towards end-to-end learning. However, they tend to overfit low-level motor patterns and often fail to fully exploit the spatial priors inherent in VLMs during execution. The authors' empirical analysis confirms that naive fine-tuning or joint training of VLMs for VLA tasks can lead to a "collapse" of spatial representations or "gradient conflicts," undermining spatial grounding.

ST4VLA emerges as an effort to bridge these two paradigms. It recognizes the critical role of "core spatial priors" (object recognition, affordance grounding, visual trajectory reasoning, relative localization) as transferable knowledge. By integrating these priors directly and explicitly into the VLA training pipeline, ST4VLA seeks to enable reliable and generalizable manipulation without the rigidness of hierarchical systems or the overfitting tendency of purely data-driven VLAs. The key innovation lies in its "spatially guided training" approach, which seeks to preserve and leverage spatial understanding from VLMs while learning embodiment-specific control. This work thus contributes to the evolving landscape of generalist robot learning, aiming for more robust and adaptable agents that can effectively reason about and interact with their physical surroundings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind ST4VLA is to overcome the fundamental disconnect between the high-level multimodal understanding capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the precise, low-level physical actions required for embodied robotics. While VLMs excel at interpreting complex visual and linguistic inputs, they typically lack the inherent spatial grounding necessary to accurately pinpoint *where* and *how* to act in a 3D environment to execute a given instruction. The authors observe that simply fine-tuning a VLM for action or co-training it with spatial data often leads to a degradation or "collapse" of its spatial priors, indicating a misalignment between the objectives of perception and action learning.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the ST4VLA framework are:

1. **Preserve Spatial Grounding:** To explicitly integrate and maintain the rich spatial grounding capabilities of VLMs throughout the policy learning process, preventing the degradation observed in naive VLA training.
2. **Align Optimization Dynamics:** To create a training paradigm that ensures consistent and stable optimization across both multimodal perception (spatial grounding) and action policy objectives, mitigating gradient conflicts.
3. **Enhance Generalization:** To significantly improve the robot's ability to generalize to novel situations, including unseen objects, paraphrased instructions, out-of-distribution environments, and complex long-horizon tasks in both simulation and real-world settings.
4. **Enable Robust Embodied Control:** To develop a VLA model that can reliably translate abstract linguistic instructions into concrete, low-level motor actions, effectively bridging the gap between semantic understanding and physical execution.

The authors propose that by strategically separating "where and what to act" (high-level spatial reasoning) from "how to act" (low-level motor control) and explicitly fostering this connection through spatial guidance, a more reliable and generalizable manipulation system can be achieved. This approach is geared towards building robust and scalable generalist robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ST4VLA introduces a novel dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework designed to explicitly integrate spatial priors into robot control through a two-stage training pipeline. The core idea is to foster alignment between spatial grounding and action policy objectives.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture: Dual-system, Dual-supervision

The framework is built upon a dual-system architecture, leveraging a robust VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) and a specialized action expert:

- **System 2 (The VLM Planner):** This component acts as a "slow but reliable reasoner." It employs Qwen2.5-VL as a multimodal encoder, tasked with capturing spatial and semantic priors from observations. It is aligned with a broad range of spatial grounding data, encompassing tasks like object detection, affordance recognition, and visual trajectory planning.
- **System 1 (The Action Expert):** This serves as a "fast embodiment-specific controller." It utilizes a compact Diffusion Transformer (DiT) and a DINOv2 visual encoder. Its role is to specialize the spatial priors from the VLM Planner into concrete embodiment-specific motor commands, trained on robot demonstration data.

To connect these two systems, a lightweight querying transformer (8.7 MB) is used. This transformer is conditioned on latent spatial grounding embeddings produced by the VLM Planner. It selectively attends to intermediate layers of the VLM via a k-layer cross-attention module, stabilizing expert learning and inference by mapping variable-length input tokens into a fixed set of learnable query tokens. To preserve the VLM Planner's semantic reasoning ability while enabling joint optimization, a gradient decay factor (e.g., 0.5) is introduced within the querying transformer to attenuate gradients propagated from the Action Expert back to the VLM.

#### 3.2. Training Recipe: Two-Stage Pipeline

ST4VLA's training proceeds in two distinct stages:

1. **Stage 1: Spatial Grounding Pre-training:**
 - **Objective:** To establish a foundational alignment between generic visuo-linguistic understanding and the specific spatial reasoning demands of robotics. This primes the VLM for subsequent co-adaptation with action objectives.
 - **Data Strategy:** The VLM is pre-trained on a strategic combination of large-scale internet vision-language grounding corpora (e.g., RefCOCO, LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL3) and targeted robot-specific datasets (e.g., RoboRefIt, A0 ManiSkill, ST4VLA's own synthetic data). This blend ensures that spatial priors are grounded in broad visual concepts and directly relevant to robotic tasks like bounding-box detection, affordance recognition, and trajectory prediction.
 - **Unified Format:** All robotic data is reformatted into a unified Question Answering (QA) structure, consistent with web-scale pre-training, allowing the VLM to develop a spatially-aware representation space under a standard supervised fine-tuning framework.

2. **Stage 2: Spatially Guided Action Post-training:**
 - **Objective:** To learn embodiment-specific control while simultaneously maintaining and refining the spatial priors acquired in Stage 1.
 - **Spatially Guided Action Learning:** Beyond standard co-training with spatial grounding data (where the VLM backbone is updated via next-token prediction), this stage introduces **spatial prompting** for action data. When processing an action sequence, the standard task instruction is augmented with a spatial prompt (e.g., "Identify all relevant toys and their spatial relationships to the container" for "store all toys into the toy box"). These prompts explicitly activate the VLM's internal reasoning about scene geometry, providing spatial cues that facilitate more reliable grounding and guide action generation.
 - **Gradient Alignment:** The dual-supervision strategy, coupled with the gradient decay in the querying transformer, establishes a cohesive link between high-level semantic perception and low-level motion control, ensuring a more stable and robust learning process.

This two-stage, dual-system approach allows ST4VLA to effectively leverage the VLM's powerful perception while specializing it for robotic control, ensuring spatial grounding is preserved and actively guides policy learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ST4VLA demonstrates significant advancements in robot learning, consistently outperforming existing baselines across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Study: Perception-Action Co-optimization

A key insight from the ablation study is the interaction between spatial perception and manipulation success during training.
- **Vanilla VLA:** Direct fine-tuning on action data alone causes rapid degradation of spatial perception (RefCOCO-g IoU drops significantly), indicating that action-only optimization disrupts crucial spatial representations.
- **Vanilla Co-training VLA:** Joint optimization with spatial data partially preserves perception but exhibits unstable oscillations in both metrics, suggesting gradient conflicts.
- **ST4VLA:** Achieves the best balance, maintaining strong spatial perception while achieving high manipulation success rates. This is substantiated by a higher **Projection-Space Similarity (PSS)** (0.42 compared to 0.25 for vanilla co-training), indicating better alignment between spatial grounding and action policy optimization dynamics.

#### 4.2. Performance on Public Benchmarks (SimplerEnv and LIBERO)

ST4VLA sets new state-of-the-art results on the SimplerEnv simulation suite, showing robust performance against visual appearance shifts and instruction-following tasks.
- **SimplerEnv (Google Robot):** ST4VLA achieves an average success rate of 84.6% on Visual Matching (VM) and 75.9% on Visual Aggregation (VA). This represents substantial gains over Vanilla VLA (66.1% VM, 63.5% VA) and outperforms strong baselines like CogACT, SpatialVLA, π0, and GR00T N1.5.
- **SimplerEnv (WidowX Robot):** ST4VLA attains an average success rate of 73.2%, a significant improvement over Vanilla VLA (54.7%) and leading all compared baselines including GR00T N1.5 (61.9%) and CogACT (51.3%).
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** ST4VLA achieves an impressive average success rate of 95.9%, outperforming GR00T N1 (93.9%) and π0 (94.2%). It shows particularly strong performance on Spatial (98.0%) and Long-Horizon (92.6%) tasks, highlighting its efficacy in complex, multi-step manipulation.

#### 4.3. Evaluation in Simulated Large-Scale Pick-and-Place

On a custom-built large-scale simulation benchmark (200 tasks, >3,000 objects in Isaac-Sim), ST4VLA consistently outperforms π0 and GR00T N1.5 across challenging generalization settings:
- **In-distribution:** 80% success rate.
- **Unseen Objects:** 68% success rate.
- **Unseen Instructions:** 78% success rate.
- **New Backgrounds:** 75% success rate.
These results demonstrate robust generalization in both vision and language under spatial guidance.

#### 4.4. Evaluation in Real-World Cluttered-Scene Pick-and-Place

On the Franka Research 3 robot, ST4VLA shows strong generalization to real-world complexities.
- Achieves 92% success rate in in-distribution tasks.
- Significantly outperforms baselines (π0, GR00T N1.5) across unseen objects (e.g., 62% vs 46% for GR00T N1.5), unseen object poses/orientations, new backgrounds, and novel instructions (e.g., 72% vs 40% for GR00T N1.5 on attribute-based instructions). This confirms the model's robust visual and linguistic generalization.

#### 4.5. Evaluation in Long-Horizon Manipulation

ST4VLA demonstrates superior capabilities in complex, reasoning-intensive tasks (e.g., desktop sorting, drawer organization, sandwich making) under dynamic real-world conditions.
- Consistently surpasses GR00T N1.5 and π0 across "in-distribution," "physical interference," and "task replanning" settings. For example, on "Sorting Items into Drawers," ST4VLA achieves 59% in-distribution, 54% with physical interference, and 57% with task replanning, compared to GR00T N1.5's 42%, 41%, and 45% respectively.
- This highlights its ability to decompose complex tasks, ground high-level goals into executable steps, adapt to disturbances, and dynamically revise plans.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Spatial Grounding Pre-training:** Integrating robotic grounding data (beyond general multimodal data) significantly improves robotic manipulation performance (e.g., WidowX VM: 54.9% -> 73.1%).
- **Co-train Loss Weight:** A 1:10 ratio between grounding and action loss is found to be optimal, suggesting a delicate balance is crucial.
- **Backbone-Agnostic Generalization:** ST4VLA's effectiveness is not solely dependent on backbone capacity. Even with a weaker Florence-2 VLM, ST4VLA surpasses GR00T N1.5, confirming the gains come from the proposed training method.
- **Scaling Laws of Spatial Priors:** Performance gains show a non-linear relationship with spatial grounding data volume; substantial improvements are observed only after a "critical mass" (e.g., >2.0M pairs), suggesting a threshold for unlocking full potential.
- **Spatial Prompt Formulations:** The default "Unified Prompting" (semantic, general) achieves the highest success rates, outperforming non-semantic padding or strictly constrained prompts (e.g., demanding specific box/point/trace outputs). This indicates that activating spatial awareness is key, but rigid formatting during inference is not necessary and can even be detrimental.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ST4VLA represents a significant step forward in the quest for generalist robot learning, offering a robust and scalable paradigm that addresses critical limitations of prior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The primary significance lies in its demonstrated ability to effectively bridge the gap between abstract multimodal understanding and precise, low-level embodied control by explicitly leveraging and preserving spatial priors.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** ST4VLA consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation and real-world benchmarks, exhibiting superior generalization to unseen objects, novel instructions (including paraphrased and attribute-based commands), and out-of-distribution environments. Its robustness to physical interference and dynamic replanning in long-horizon tasks is particularly impactful, indicating a higher degree of real-world deployability than previous models. This capacity for broad generalization is crucial for moving robotics beyond carefully curated lab settings.
2. **Scalable and Principled Training Paradigm:** The "spatially guided training" recipe, featuring two distinct stages (spatial grounding pre-training and spatially guided action post-training) and the intelligent use of spatial prompting and gradient decay, offers a principled way to integrate and preserve spatial knowledge within VLA models. The empirical finding on the scaling laws of spatial priors suggests a clear path for future improvements: investing in large-scale, high-quality spatial grounding data can yield substantial returns. This provides a clear research direction for the community.
3. **Improved Understanding of Perception-Action Alignment:** The preliminary study on perception-action co-optimization and the use of Projection-Space Similarity (PSS) offer valuable insights into the training dynamics of VLA models. Demonstrating that explicit spatial guidance leads to better alignment between spatial perception and action objectives (higher PSS) is a crucial finding that can inform the design of future embodied AI architectures. It highlights that maintaining spatial grounding is not merely a side benefit but integral to effective policy learning.
4. **Foundation for Generalist Robots:** By demonstrating strong performance on a wide range of tasks—from simple pick-and-place to complex, multi-step long-horizon manipulation requiring adaptation and replanning—ST4VLA lays a stronger foundation for the development of truly generalist robots. Such robots would be capable of understanding and executing diverse human commands in complex, unstructured environments.
5. **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to releasing source code, data, and models under the InternVLA-M1 framework will undoubtedly accelerate research and development in embodied AI. By providing validated models and datasets, the authors empower the broader robotics and AI community to build upon their advancements, foster replication, and explore new directions.

In conclusion, ST4VLA's innovative approach to integrating spatial priors fundamentally enhances the capabilities of VLA models. It represents a significant stride towards creating intelligent robots that can seamlessly translate human intent into robust and generalized physical actions, thereby accelerating progress towards practical, adaptable robotic systems in the real world.
