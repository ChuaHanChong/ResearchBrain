# FATE-VLA:Failue-aware test generation for vision-language-action models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed FATE-VLA, an adaptive test generation framework that integrates diversity-driven exploration with machine learning guidance to actively discover failure cases in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The approach increased failure rates by up to 29.7 percentage points and improved the diversity of discovered failures compared to existing static and purely diversity-based evaluation methods.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model evaluation relies on static benchmarks or random scene sampling, which often miss sparse and clustered failure regions in high-dimensional interaction spaces.
- This leads to an incomplete understanding of model fragilities and an underestimation of robustness risks in generalist robot policies.
- Existing robustness testing methods for VLAs typically use predefined perturbations, lacking adaptive learning to discover diverse and targeted failure scenarios.

## Method

- Proposed **FATE-VLA**, an adaptive, ==learning-guided test generation== framework to actively discover VLA model failures.
- It integrates diversity-driven exploration, based on ==Adaptive Random Testing (ART)==, with ==surrogate machine learning models== (Decision Trees or Random Forests) to predict failure likelihood.
- Developed two iterative hybrid algorithms that learn from executed scenes to generate test scenarios that are both failure-prone and diverse in object configurations and spatial layouts.

## Results

- FATE-VLA methods increased the failure rate for VLA models by up to **29.7 percentage points** over random sampling and **28.8 percentage points** over FSCSART on models like GR00T-N1.6.
- The approach preserved or improved trajectory coverage (TC) and significantly enhanced the diversity of discovered failure modes, as quantified by Trajectory Coverage under Failure (TCF) and Failed Object Coverage (FOC).
- Random Forest-guided algorithms demonstrated superior performance, achieving a better balance of high failure detection with diverse failure conditions compared to Decision Tree models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA model failures are not uniformly distributed but are clustered within specific, learnable regions of the input space.
> - Integrating machine learning guidance with diversity-driven testing is crucial for effective failure discovery; purely diversity-based methods offer limited gains over random sampling for failure detection.
> - Random Forest models provide a more balanced approach for failure prediction, enabling both high failure rates and broad diversity in discovered failure modes compared to single decision trees.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are developing as a paradigm for generalist robot policies, enabling robots to process natural language instructions and execute manipulation or navigation tasks. This progress has been supported by initiatives such as Open X-Embodiment and foundation models like OpenVLA, alongside standardized benchmarks like CALVIN, LIBERO, and ManiSkill2.

Despite these advancements, the evaluation of VLA models largely depends on static benchmarks that employ predefined or randomly sampled task scenes. The paper identifies a limitation in this approach, particularly in high-dimensional embodied interaction spaces where scene configurations vary across object identities, spatial layouts, orientations, and instruction semantics. In such complex environments, failures are often sparse, non-uniform, and clustered within specific regions of the input space. Consequently, traditional static benchmarking or random sampling methods may underestimate robustness risks and overlook behavioral fragilities, potentially leading to an incomplete understanding of a model's weaknesses.

Existing research has explored VLA robustness through perturbation- or adversarial-based methods, as seen in VLATest, Eva-VLA, Nebula, and DAERT, which investigate variations in environmental factors or seek worst-case failures. Benchmarks like VLABench and LIBERO-X also aim to reveal brittleness under distribution shifts or language variations. However, these methods typically rely on predefined perturbations or adversarial objectives rather than adaptively discovering diverse failure cases through learned guidance. Other related work focuses on failure prediction or discovery in robotic and safety-critical systems, but these are not specifically designed for multimodal VLA evaluation. Approaches like metamorphic testing and coverage-guided fuzzing emphasize evaluation or coverage, but lack the explicit goal of targeted discovery of diverse failure regions in VLA systems through automated test scene generation.

This work positions itself within this landscape by reframing VLA evaluation as an active failure discovery problem. It aims to address the limitations of static and passively exploratory methods by introducing an adaptive, learning-guided approach to test generation. The proposed FATE-VLA method seeks to identify failure-prone regions and generate test scenarios that expose diverse weaknesses across object configurations, spatial layouts, and behavioral trajectories, thereby moving beyond mere performance measurement to a more diagnostic understanding of model vulnerabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the identified shortcomings of current VLA model evaluation practices. While VLA models are increasingly deployed as generalist robot policies, their robustness evaluation primarily relies on static benchmarks that randomly sample task scenes. This approach is limited in high-dimensional embodied spaces where failures are sparse, non-uniform, and clustered, leading to an underestimation of robustness risks and an incomplete picture of model fragilities.

The authors articulate the following key objectives and motivations:

1. **Reframing VLA Evaluation:** To advocate for a shift from passive performance measurement on static benchmarks to an active failure discovery problem. This means that testing should progressively learn the structure of model weaknesses and generate scenarios that are both failure-prone and diverse.
2. **Addressing Sparse and Clustered Failures:** To develop a method capable of efficiently locating the sparse and clustered failure regions in high-dimensional VLA input spaces, which are often missed by random or uniform sampling.
3. **Enhancing Failure Diversity:** To move beyond simply identifying the number of failures to also consider the diversity of those failures. The motivation is that repeatedly exposing the same weakness offers limited diagnostic value, whereas discovering failures across different objects, spatial arrangements, and behavioral trajectories provides a more comprehensive understanding of model vulnerabilities.
4. **Proposing a Failure-Aware Test Generation Approach (FATE-VLA):** To introduce a novel method that combines diversity-driven exploration, inspired by Adaptive Random Testing (ART), with surrogate models that estimate the likelihood of failure. This approach is designed to iteratively learn from observed executions and guide test generation toward high-risk yet diverse scene regions.
5. **Introducing New Diversity Metrics:** To complement traditional success rate metrics with trajectory-level and object-level failure diversity metrics. These metrics aim to quantify the heterogeneity of discovered weaknesses, providing a richer characterization of model vulnerabilities.
6. **Empirical Validation:** To empirically evaluate the proposed FATE-VLA approach on state-of-the-art VLA models, demonstrating its effectiveness in uncovering substantially more failures and more diverse failure modes compared to random and diversity-only baselines.
7. **Informing Pre-Deployment Evaluation:** To provide evidence for the necessity of adaptive, diagnostic testing pipelines that can expose systematic robustness gaps in VLA models prior to their deployment in physical robot systems.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more effective and comprehensive evaluation strategies for VLA models, moving from a passive assessment of average performance to an active, diagnostic search for diverse and critical failure conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FATE-VLA approach proposes two hybrid test-generation algorithms that integrate Adaptive Random Testing (ART) with machine learning guidance to identify diverse, failure-inducing VLA scenes. Both algorithms operate iteratively, learning from executed scenes to progressively refine test generation.

**General Setup:**
The algorithms take as input:
- The VLA agent under test ($VLA_{UT}$).
- An object pool (O).
- Spatial constraints ($X_{range}, Y_{range}$) and orientation parameters ($\Omega$).
- A candidate set size (k) for scene generation.
- The total number of test scenes (N) to be generated.
Throughout the process, two primary archives are maintained:
- **Tested scene archive (S):** Stores all executed scenes.
- **Labeled archive (D):** Records each executed scene and its outcome (failure=1, success=0).
For Algorithm 2, an additional **Failure region archive (F)** stores scenes that specifically resulted in failures.

#### 3.1. Algorithm 1 - Hybrid Sorted Surrogate Assisted FSCS-ART Testing Algorithm

This algorithm augments the Fixed-Size Candidate Set Search (FSCS-ART) strategy with a surrogate model for failure prediction.

- **Initial Exploration Phase:** When no trained surrogate model is available (i.e., at the beginning or until both success and failure outcomes are observed), the algorithm generates a candidate set `C` of `k` random scenes, subject to predefined constraints. From this set, it selects the scene that maximizes the minimum Euclidean distance to previously executed scenes in `S`. This diversity-based selection ensures broad exploration of the input space. The selected scene is executed, and its outcome is recorded in `D` and `S`.
- **Learning-Guided Phase:** Once `D` contains examples from both outcome classes (success and failure), a surrogate model (Decision Tree or Random Forest) is trained on `D`. In subsequent iterations, candidate scenes `C` are first sorted based on their diversity with respect to `S`. The trained surrogate model then predicts whether each candidate is likely to cause a failure. If any candidate is predicted as a failure, the first such candidate from the diversity-sorted list is prioritized for execution. If no candidate is predicted as a failure, the candidate with the highest diversity score (first in the sorted list) is selected. After each execution, `D` and `S` are updated, and the surrogate model is retrained to incorporate new data, iteratively improving its predictive capability.
- **Balance of Exploration and Exploitation:** FSCS-ART ensures exploration by maximizing diversity, while the surrogate model provides exploitation by steering testing towards failure-prone regions.

#### 3.2. Algorithm 2 - Hybrid Adaptive RF-Guided FSCS-ART Testing Algorithm

This algorithm also uses an iterative, learning-guided approach, specifically employing a Random Forest (RF) model, and introduces an adaptive weighting mechanism.

- **Initial Exploration Phase:** Similar to Algorithm 1, in the absence of a trained RF model, the algorithm generates a candidate set `C` and selects the scene maximally distant from previously executed scenes in `S` using the FSCS-ART strategy. The outcome is recorded in `D` and `S`.
- **Learning-Guided Phase:** Once `D` contains both successful and failed cases, an RF model is trained on `D`. In this phase, candidate scenes are generated using a mix that considers the **failure archive (F)**, enabling exploration near previously discovered failures while maintaining global diversity. For each candidate scene `c`:
 - Its minimum distance `d` to previously executed scenes in `S` is computed and normalized ($norm_d$). This represents the diversity contribution.
 - The trained RF model estimates the failure probability ($p_{fail}$) for `c`.
 - A combined score is calculated: $score(c) = \alpha \cdot p_{fail} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot norm_d$.
 - The parameter $\alpha$ is an adaptive weight, $\alpha = \min(0.85, i/N)$, which gradually shifts the emphasis from exploration (lower $\alpha$) towards exploitation (higher $\alpha$) as more tests are conducted, while ensuring some diversity is always considered.
 - The candidate with the highest score is selected for execution.
- **Updates:** If the selected scene results in a failure, it is added to the `F` archive. `D` and `S` are updated with every execution, and the RF model is retrained at each iteration to improve its prediction capabilities.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
The study employs four metrics to assess the effectiveness of the approaches:
- **Failure Rate (FR):** Proportion of generated test scenes leading to unsuccessful task execution.
- **Trajectory Coverage (TC):** Extent to which generated test scenes explore novel target object positions within the manipulation platform.
- **Trajectory Coverage under Failure (TCF):** Trajectory coverage considering only executions that resulted in task failure, indicating behavioral diversity of discovered failures.
- **Failed Object Coverage (FOC):** Proportion of distinct manipulated objects for which at least one failure was observed, capturing object-specific failure modes.

**Subject VLAs and Baselines:**
- **Subject VLAs:** OpenVLA, π0, GR00T-N1.6, and EO-1.
- **Simulation Environment:** SimplerEnv, focused on the "Pick up" task.
- **Objects:** A pool of seven distinct manipulation objects (e.g., RedBull can, eggplant).
- **Surrogate Models:** Decision Tree (DT) and Random Forest (RF) classifiers.
- **Baselines:**
 1. **Random (B1):** Purely random scene generation.
 2. **FSCSART (B2):** Adaptive Random Testing without ML guidance.

**Experimental Procedure:**
Each experimental configuration was executed 10 times to account for stochasticity and ensure statistical validity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation addressed two research questions (RQ1 and RQ2), comparing the proposed FATE-VLA algorithms against random and diversity-driven baselines, and analyzing the impact of different machine learning (ML) models.

**RQ1 – Performance Against Baselines:**
The proposed ML-guided algorithms consistently achieved higher failure rates (FR) while generally maintaining or improving diversity metrics (TC, TCF, FOC) compared to the baselines.

- **Failure Rate (FR):**
 - On non-ceiling VLAs (EO1, GR00T-N1.6, and π0), the proposed methods substantially outperformed both baselines.
 - For GR00T-N1.6, Sorting_RF achieved an FR of 65.3%, representing a gain of +29.7 percentage points (pp) over the Random baseline (35.6%) and +28.8 pp over the FSCSART baseline (36.5%).
 - For EO1, Sorting_RF (60.0%) showed improvements of +23.3 pp over Random (36.7%) and +22.0 pp over FSCSART (38.0%).
 - For π0, Weighted_RF (73.7%) improved FR by +19.7 pp over Random (54.0%) and +18.2 pp over FSCSART (55.5%).
 - OpenVLA-7b was identified as a "ceiling case," with all methods achieving very high FR values (95-98%), indicating that failures are readily discoverable for this model. Even in this case, proposed methods modestly exceeded baselines (+0.8 to +2.2 pp over Random, +0.4 to +1.8 pp over FSCSART).
 - A notable observation was the minimal difference in FR between the Random (B1) and FSCSART (B2) baselines (0.9–1.5 pp difference on non-ceiling VLAs), indicating that diversity-driven ART alone provides little benefit over random sampling for failure detection without learned guidance. This suggests the ML guidance component is the primary driver of performance improvement.

- **Trajectory Coverage (TC):** The proposed methods generally maintained or improved TC, indicating that increased failure detection did not compromise the exploration of distinct object positions. Sorting_DT achieved the highest TC for EO1 (84.0%) and π0 (84.2%), exceeding both baselines. For GR00T-N1.6, most methods achieved similar high TC (~86%). Sorting_RF also showed improvement for OpenVLA-7b.

- **Trajectory Coverage under Failure (TCF):** This metric, reflecting the behavioral diversity of discovered failures, was also improved by the proposed methods, especially RF-based variants. Sorting_RF achieved 83.0% TCF for GR00T-N1.6, exceeding Random (78.2%, +4.8 pp) and FSCSART (79.9%, +3.1 pp). For π0, Sorting_RF reached 77.4% TCF, compared to 72.1% for Random (+5.3 pp) and 72.6% for FSCSART (+4.8 pp). DT-guided approaches occasionally exhibited a slight reduction in TCF, suggesting a tendency to over-focus on dominant failure regions.

- **Failed Object Coverage (FOC):** FOC, measuring the diversity of failed objects, was largely maintained or improved. On π0, Sorting_RF and Weighted_RF achieved 100% FOC, matching FSCSART and improving over Random. Weighted_RF achieved 97.1% FOC for GR00T-N1.6, surpassing both baselines.

**RQ2 – Impact of ML Model (DT vs. RF) on Failure Detection:**
Incorporating ML guidance significantly improved failure detection, and the choice of ML model influenced the balance between failure quantity and diversity.

- **DT Contribution:** Comparing FSCSART_DT against the FSCSART baseline revealed substantial FR gains (e.g., +18.6 pp on EO1, +28.3 pp on GR00T-N1.6), confirming that learned failure prediction is a primary driver of performance.
- **RF vs. DT:** RF-based variants (Sorting_RF, Weighted_RF) generally provided a better balance between exploitation and exploration. While DT guidance could achieve strong FR gains, it sometimes over-focused on dominant failure regions, leading to a slight reduction in TCF. RF models, by contrast, generated smoother and more robust failure-probability estimates through ensemble learning, which helped preserve broader exploration of the failure space, resulting in higher TCF while maintaining high FR. Weighted_RF, which blends RF failure probability with a diversity normalization term, provided the most consistent overall performance across FR, TCF, and FOC.

In summary, the research demonstrates that unguided exploration is insufficient for VLA evaluation, and that VLA failure regions exhibit learnable structure that can be effectively exploited by ML-guided test generation to yield both a higher quantity and greater diversity of discovered failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this research suggest a shift in the established paradigm for evaluating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Current practices, which predominantly rely on static benchmarks and random sampling, have been shown to provide an incomplete assessment of model robustness, particularly in high-dimensional embodied spaces where failures are sparse and clustered. FATE-VLA demonstrates that these limitations can be addressed by reframing VLA evaluation as an active failure discovery problem.

The key significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Robustness Assessment:** By uncovering substantially more failures (up to +29.7% over baselines) and revealing more diverse failure modes, FATE-VLA offers a more comprehensive and accurate assessment of VLA model robustness. This capability is critical for understanding the true limitations of these models before they are deployed in real-world applications where failures can have significant consequences.
2. **Improved Diagnostic Value:** The emphasis on identifying diverse failure modes, quantified by metrics such as Trajectory Coverage under Failure (TCF) and Failed Object Coverage (FOC), provides deeper diagnostic insights. This allows developers to understand not just that a model fails, but *how* and *under what conditions* it fails (e.g., specific object types, spatial arrangements, or behavioral patterns), facilitating targeted improvements.
3. **Foundation for Adaptive Testing:** The proposed methodology, combining diversity-driven exploration with machine-learning-guided failure prediction, offers a blueprint for future adaptive testing pipelines. Such pipelines can continuously learn from observed model behavior and intelligently steer test generation towards unexplored, high-risk regions, moving beyond fixed test suites.
4. **Implications for Deployment:** For VLA models intended for physical robots, where safety and reliability are paramount, an incomplete understanding of failure conditions poses considerable risks. FATE-VLA provides a mechanism to expose systematic weaknesses and robustness gaps proactively, which can inform design choices, model refinement, and safer deployment strategies.
5. **Guidance for Future Research:** The study highlights that VLA failure regions possess a "learnable structure." This insight can guide future research into interpretability, explainability, and the development of more robust and generalizable VLA architectures that are less susceptible to these identified failure patterns.
6. **Validation of ML-Guided Approaches:** The empirical results confirm that incorporating machine learning guidance (Decision Trees or Random Forests) into adaptive testing strategies is significantly more effective than pure random or diversity-only exploration for complex, high-dimensional systems like VLAs. This validates the utility of surrogate models in software testing and verification contexts.
7. **Contribution to Embodied AI Safety:** By providing tools and methodologies to systematically stress-test VLA models, this work contributes to the broader effort in ensuring the safety and reliability of embodied AI systems, especially in robotics.

While the work acknowledges limitations such as task specificity, object diversity, and simulation-based evaluation, it establishes a foundational argument for a paradigm shift in VLA model evaluation. The adoption of failure-aware, adaptive test generation holds the potential to significantly enhance the trustworthiness and reliability of generalist robot policies.
