# VL-Cogito: Progressive Curriculum Reinforcement Learning for Advanced Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VL-Cogito, developed by researchers from DAMO Academy, Alibaba Group, Hupan Lab, and Fudan University, introduces a Progressive Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (PCuRL) framework to enhance multimodal reasoning in large language models. The framework enables adaptive reasoning depth and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance on 6 out of 10 mainstream multimodal benchmarks, including significant gains over its Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B backbone model.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit unstable performance across diverse domains and difficulty levels due to the inherent complexity and heterogeneity of multimodal tasks.
- Existing MLLMs struggle with the semantic richness and varied problem formulations in multimodal contexts, leading to inconsistent performance.
- Conventional reasoning models often extend reasoning length indiscriminately, potentially compromising efficiency by over-thinking simple tasks.

## Method

- The paper introduces VL-Cogito, an MLLM trained with a novel ==Progressive Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (PCuRL)== framework built upon GRPO-based Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).
- PCuRL employs ==Online Difficulty Soft Weighting (ODSW)== to dynamically adjust the importance of prompts based on their difficulty across three progressive training stages (Easy, Medium, Hard).
- PCuRL incorporates ==Dynamic Length Reward (DyLR)== during the hard stage to incentivize the model to adapt its reasoning path length based on individual problem demands.

## Results

- VL-Cogito achieved state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance on **6 out of 10** mainstream multimodal benchmarks, including Geometry@3k, MathVista, LogicVista, ScienceQA, EMMA, and MMStar.
- The model demonstrated substantial absolute performance gains over its Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B backbone, with improvements of **7.6%** on Geometry@3K and **5.5%** on MathVista.
- Ablation studies confirmed that PCuRL's components (curriculum, ODSW, and DyLR) contribute to performance improvements, with DyLR effectively promoting adaptive reasoning length and depth when integrated within the progressive curriculum.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Progressive curriculum learning, when guided by online difficulty soft weighting, effectively stabilizes RL training and systematically fosters complex reasoning skills in MLLMs.
> - Dynamically incentivizing reasoning length, rather than fixed-length rewards, allows MLLMs to develop adaptive thought processes, generating concise responses for simple tasks and longer, in-depth reasoning for complex ones.
> - The stabilizing effect of the multi-stage curriculum is crucial for Dynamic Length Reward to function effectively, preventing unstable performance from premature over-lengthening on misclassified difficult tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically focusing on enhancing their reasoning capabilities through Reinforcement Learning (RL).

The paper acknowledges the proven effectiveness of RL in improving the "long-chain reasoning abilities" of LLMs, citing prominent examples such as OpenAI's o-series, Kimi-K1.5, and DeepSeek-R1. This success has motivated researchers to extend RL paradigms to the more complex domain of MLLMs, which integrate information from various modalities (e.g., text and images).

However, the authors identify a critical challenge in current MLLMs: their "unstable performance across various domains and difficulty levels" due to the "inherent complexity and diversity of multimodal tasks." Existing models often struggle with the semantic richness and varied problem formulations present in multimodal contexts, leading to inconsistencies in performance. The related work section further details these efforts, categorizing them into direct RLVR-style optimization for MLLMs (e.g., Vision-R1, R1-OneVision, OpenVLThinker) and advanced strategies for stability and effectiveness (e.g., data selection, reward design, advanced RL recipes like MM-Eureka, ThinkLite-VL, MMR1, R1-VL, VL-Rethinker, Praxis-VLM).

VL-Cogito directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel RL framework, PCuRL, which specifically targets the instability and lack of adaptive reasoning in MLLMs. By integrating curriculum learning and dynamic reward mechanisms, VL-Cogito aims to provide a more robust and generalized approach to training MLLMs for complex reasoning tasks. The paper positions its contribution as a novel RL recipe inspired by curriculum learning, distinct from existing approaches that might focus solely on data selection, specific reward designs, or iterative self-improvement without the explicit progressive difficulty and adaptive length mechanisms. This research pushes the frontier by providing a structured and systematic way to train MLLMs to not only perform well but also to adapt their reasoning depth and efficiency to the specific demands of diverse multimodal problems, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VL-Cogito stems from the observed limitations of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in handling the diverse and complex nature of multimodal reasoning tasks. While Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise in enhancing reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) and has been extended to MLLMs, existing models often exhibit "unstable performance across various domains and difficulty levels" due to the intrinsic complexity and heterogeneity of multimodal semantic content and problem formulations.

Against this background, the **key objectives** of this work are:

1. **Develop an advanced multimodal reasoning model**: The overarching goal is to create VL-Cogito, an MLLM that can reliably perform sophisticated reasoning across a wide spectrum of multimodal tasks.
2. **Introduce a novel multi-stage Progressive Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (PCuRL) framework**: This framework is designed to systematically guide the model's learning, enabling it to progressively master tasks of increasing difficulty and build robust reasoning capabilities.
3. **Enhance reasoning abilities across diverse multimodal contexts**: The aim is to improve the model's capacity to effectively tackle various domains (mathematics, science, logic, general understanding) and adapt to different levels of task complexity.
4. **Balance reasoning efficiency with correctness**: The authors seek to optimize the model's output by enabling it to adaptively regulate its reasoning path length according to task complexity, preventing both over-thinking on simple tasks and under-thinking on complex ones.

To achieve these objectives, the authors motivate two specific innovations within PCuRL:

- **Online Difficulty Soft Weighting (ODSW)**: This mechanism is motivated by the need to dynamically adjust training difficulty across successive RL training stages, allowing the model to incrementally transition from mastering simpler questions to effectively handling intricate problems. The goal is to provide a stable learning signal and optimal challenge for the model at each stage.
- **Dynamic Length Reward (DyLR)**: This mechanism is motivated by the observation that conventional reasoning models often extend reasoning length indiscriminately, potentially compromising efficiency. DyLR aims to explicitly incentivize the model to adapt its reasoning length based on individual problem demands, ensuring thorough reasoning for complex tasks while maintaining succinctness for simpler ones.

In essence, the motivation is to create an MLLM that not only performs well but also learns to reason in a more human-like, adaptive, and efficient manner, addressing the inherent variability in multimodal reasoning challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the VL-Cogito model lies in its novel **Progressive Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (PCuRL)** framework, which builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) scheme.

#### 3.1. Data Curation

To ensure robust and generalized performance, the authors curated an extensive dataset comprising 23 open-source multimodal datasets across six distinct task categories: Mathematical Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, Counting, Science Reasoning, Chart Understanding, and General Image Understanding.
To enhance the overall difficulty and coverage for RL training, two additional measures were implemented:
- **Open-ended Format**: Most multiple-choice samples were reformulated into an open-ended QA format to prevent reliance on superficial cues.
- **Difficulty Sampling**: Samples achieving above 50% accuracy over 8 trials using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct were removed to exclude questions not requiring genuine reasoning. The resultant dataset is detailed in Appendix A.

#### 3.2. Progressive Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (PCuRL) Framework

PCuRL is designed to incrementally train the MLLM on tasks of increasing difficulty, enabling it to acquire sophisticated reasoning skills and extend its reasoning chains while maintaining concise responses for simpler tasks. The framework consists of three key components:

##### 3.2.1. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based RLVR

The foundation is the GRPO algorithm, which improves training efficiency by estimating advantages based on comparing responses within a group for the same input. The advantage for a response is calculated using its reward relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the group. The model is optimized using a clipped surrogate objective, similar to PPO.
The reward function in RLVR combines:
- `r_acc(x, y_i)`: A binary accuracy reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect).
- `r_format(y_i)`: A strict binary format reward to constrain output structure.

##### 3.2.2. Online Difficulty Soft Weighting (ODSW)

ODSW is a curriculum learning strategy that dynamically adjusts the importance of prompts based on their difficulty. Unlike hard-weighted filtering, ODSW assigns a continuous weight to each prompt, allowing more prompts to be considered. The advantage values are adjusted at the prompt level using a predefined function `F` based on the rollout accuracy (average accuracy of `G` rollout responses for a prompt).
Inspired by learnability theory, prompts with rollout accuracy close to 0.5 are considered optimal for learning. The authors define three continuous piecewise functions `F` (Easy, Medium, Hard, as shown in Figure 2), integrating sine and constant components, to tailor the weighting for different difficulty stages. This allows the model to focus on a target difficulty level while preventing complete disregard of other difficulties.

##### 3.2.3. Dynamic Length Reward (DyLR)

Addressing the issue of indiscriminate reasoning length, DyLR determines an ideal reasoning length for each prompt. The target length for a prompt is defined as the average length of its correct rollout responses. If no correct responses are generated, the model is incentivized to extend reasoning up to a predefined maximum length.
The `r_len` reward function uses a `CosFn` to provide a cosine-shaped reward based on the generated response length (`L_i`) relative to the target length (`L_tgt`).
The overall reward `r(x, y_i)` combines accuracy, format, and dynamic length rewards with hyperparameters `α`, `β`, and `γ`. To mitigate excessive length growth for zero-accuracy prompts, an additional hyperparameter `w` is introduced to the advantage calculation when `Acc = 0`. DyLR encourages concise reasoning for simple tasks and longer, in-depth reasoning for complex ones.

##### 3.2.4. Progressive Curriculum RL Stages

The training process is structured into three consecutive stages: Easy, Medium, and Hard.
- The dataset remains consistent across stages, but the ODSW mechanism is employed in each stage to preferentially focus on data corresponding to the targeted difficulty level, using the dedicated `F(Acc)` function (Easy, Medium, or Hard variant).
- Data order is independently shuffled in each stage for generalization.
- The **Dynamic Length Reward (DyLR) mechanism is introduced exclusively during the hard stage**. This design allows the model to freely explore in earlier stages, adapt rapidly, and gain initial performance, while reserving the length reward for challenging questions in the final stage to drive deeper reasoning and exploration of complex chains. The hard stage takes more steps to converge due to increased reasoning complexity.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Backbone Model**: Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B.
- **Optimizer**: AdamW with a learning rate of 1e-6, using DeepSpeed-ZeRO3.
- **GRPO Configuration**: Rollout batch size 512, global batch size 128, max sequence length 4096, KL divergence loss coefficient 1e-3, 16 responses sampled per prompt (temperature 1.0).
- **Training Schedule**: 100 optimization steps for Easy and Medium stages (selecting optimal checkpoint for next stage), 1 epoch (approx. 200 steps) for the Hard stage.
- **Reward Hyperparameters**: α=1, β=0.5, γ=1, w=0.25. `r_min_len` = -1, `r_max_len` = 0 (DyLR acts as punishment for deviations from target length).
- **Evaluation**: Unified prompt instruction, final answers enclosed in "\box{}", vLLM for inference acceleration. Math-Verify and MathRuler for math, exact matching for non-math. GPT-4o used as an auxiliary judgment tool for challenging cases.

This comprehensive methodology aims to create an MLLM that not only learns from diverse data but also strategically adapts its reasoning process based on perceived task difficulty and required depth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate the effectiveness of VL-Cogito and its underlying PCuRL framework across a diverse range of multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison on Diverse Multimodal Benchmarks (Table 1)

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance**: VL-Cogito achieves either the best or highly competitive performance on 6 out of 10 mainstream multimodal benchmarks, spanning mathematics (Geometry@3k, MathVista, LogicVista), science (ScienceQA, EMMA), and general understanding (MMStar).
- **Significant Gains Over Backbone**: Compared to its backbone model, Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B, VL-Cogito shows substantial absolute performance enhancements: 7.6% on Geometry@3K, 5.5% on MathVista, 4.9% on LogicVista, 2.2% on ScienceQA, 4.5% on EMMA, and 3.8% on MMStar. This indicates robust and consistent improvements across domains, validating the generalization of the approach.
- **Superiority Over Reasoning-Oriented MLLMs**: VL-Cogito consistently outperforms or matches other dedicated reasoning-oriented MLLMs, even without a "cold-start warm-up phase" (supervised fine-tuning with meticulously curated datasets) that some baselines utilize. This underscores the efficacy of the direct RL pipeline.
- **Adaptive Reasoning Across Difficulties**: General-purpose MLLMs often underperform on mathematical benchmarks requiring advanced analytical skills. VL-Cogito, however, excels in rigorous mathematics and scientific tasks while maintaining strong performance on less complex tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study: Component-wise Performance Decomposition of PCuRL (Table 2)

- **Overall Contribution of PCuRL**: VL-Cogito (with all PCuRL components) significantly outperforms the vanilla GRPO baseline, particularly on demanding benchmarks like MathVision, LogicVista, MMMU, and EMMA, where reasoning depth is critical. The overall average score for VL-Cogito is 59.5%, compared to 58.1% for vanilla GRPO.
- **Impact of Multi-stage Curriculum**: The addition of the "Curriculum" strategy alone yields consistent gains across most tasks compared to vanilla GRPO, demonstrating the value of an easy-to-hard progression in stabilizing and improving learning.
- **Impact of Dynamic Length Reward (DyLR)**: When DyLR is applied *with* the curriculum (VL-Cogito), it further elevates scores on challenging mathematical datasets (MathVista, LogicVista), confirming its role in promoting deeper reasoning. However, when DyLR is applied directly to the GRPO baseline *without* the progressive curriculum ("+DyLR"), the model exhibits unstable and inconsistent performance. This crucial finding suggests that the curriculum's stabilizing effect is necessary for DyLR to function effectively, preventing premature over-lengthening on misclassified "difficult" tasks during early training.

#### 4.3. Impact of Online Difficulty Soft Weighting (ODSW) (Table 3)

- **Soft Weighting vs. Binary Weighting**: ODSW consistently achieves superior performance compared to binary weighting schemes across most benchmarks, especially when target difficulty is skewed. This suggests that soft weighting provides a more balanced optimization direction by dynamically emphasizing appropriate difficulty levels.
- **Importance of Challenging Queries**: The analysis shows that models trained with disproportionately more easy samples perform suboptimally. Emphasizing challenging problems, particularly through soft weighting, consistently improves outcomes, highlighting their essential role in advancing reasoning ability. This justifies prioritizing difficult tasks in the later stages of PCuRL.

#### 4.4. Impact of Dynamic Length Reward on Reasoning Efficiency and Performance (Figure 3)

- **Superiority of Dynamic Length Reward**: Models guided by DyLR achieve noticeably higher average accuracy across benchmarks, coupled with a more adaptive and context-appropriate response length distribution, compared to fixed-length cosine reward methods.
- **Adaptive Length for Complexity**: DyLR tends to generate shorter, more concise reasoning paths overall but exhibits flexibility, producing longer responses for difficult problems (e.g., mathematics) and shorter ones for simpler tasks (e.g., ScienceQA, MMStar). Fixed-length rewards, in contrast, indiscriminately inflate response lengths, often introducing unnecessary complexity and degrading accuracy.

#### 4.5. Comparison of Training Dynamics (Figure 4)

- **Reward Trajectory**: PCuRL's reward trajectory initially aligns with vanilla GRPO but drops slightly at the start of the hard stage (due to length penalties) before recovering and matching/exceeding the baseline as the model learns to generate longer, correct responses.
- **Validation Accuracy**: PCuRL's validation accuracy mirrors GRPO in easy/medium stages but surpasses it significantly in the hard stage, correlating with increased response length. This confirms that extended reasoning chains in the hard stage genuinely enhance problem-solving, not just output verbosity.
- **Average Response Length**: Vanilla GRPO's response length remains static (250-300 tokens). PCuRL, however, modulates length: similar to baseline in easy/medium stages, then a marked increase in the hard stage (from ~280 to 500 tokens), demonstrating effective curriculum guidance toward more sophisticated outputs.

#### 4.6. Case Studies (Figure 5)

- **Adaptive Reasoning Depth**: VL-Cogito dynamically adjusts its reasoning depth. For general or scientific multiple-choice questions, it systematically evaluates options. For mathematical problems, it either directly recalls properties (simple cases) or adopts a meticulous, stepwise approach (complex cases), demonstrating adaptive reasoning.
- **Self-Reflective Reasoning**: The model exhibits self-reflection, as shown in a geometry problem where it initially misapplied a theorem, recognized the error ("re-evaluate"), and corrected its calculation, underscoring the effectiveness of the RL-based training in instilling self-correction capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VL-Cogito research presents several significant advancements in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and their reasoning capabilities, with considerable potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization in MLLMs**: By systematically addressing the instability and inconsistent performance of MLLMs across diverse domains and difficulty levels, VL-Cogito contributes to the development of more robust and generally applicable multimodal reasoning agents. Its ability to perform consistently well across mathematics, science, logic, and general understanding benchmarks indicates a crucial step towards truly general-purpose multimodal AI.

2. **Pioneering Curriculum Learning in RL for MLLMs**: The introduction of the Progressive Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (PCuRL) framework is a significant methodological contribution. It demonstrates that a structured, multi-stage learning approach, incrementally guiding models from easier to harder tasks, can be highly effective in stabilizing RL training and fostering complex reasoning skills in MLLMs. This paradigm can inspire future RL frameworks for complex AI tasks.

3. **Novel Mechanisms for Adaptive Reasoning**: The two key innovations within PCuRL—Online Difficulty Soft Weighting (ODSW) and Dynamic Length Reward (DyLR)—are impactful.
 - **ODSW** provides a sophisticated way to manage the learning curriculum by dynamically adjusting the focus on different difficulty levels, ensuring the model is always challenged appropriately. This moves beyond simpler data filtering methods to a more nuanced, continuous weighting scheme.
 - **DyLR** is particularly significant as it tackles the critical problem of reasoning efficiency. By enabling the model to adapt its reasoning length based on task complexity, it prevents both superfluous verbosity for simple problems and inadequate depth for complex ones. This adaptive thought process is a hallmark of intelligent reasoning and a key step towards more human-like AI behavior.

4. **Advancement in "Slow Thinking" Systems for MLLMs**: The framework's ability to promote longer, more in-depth reasoning for difficult tasks, alongside observed self-reflection capabilities (as highlighted in the case studies), pushes MLLMs closer to becoming "slow-thinking" systems. These systems can engage in iterative refinement and deliberate exploration of reasoning paths, leading to higher accuracy on challenging problems, akin to human problem-solving.

5. **Practical Implications for Real-world Applications**: The improved reasoning capabilities of VL-Cogito have direct implications for various real-world applications requiring nuanced multimodal understanding. This could include advanced scientific discovery (interpreting complex diagrams and data), automated education (solving diverse math and science problems), intelligent assistants (answering questions about visual information), and complex decision-making systems that rely on combining textual and visual cues. The enhanced efficiency from dynamic length adaptation also makes these models more practical for deployment.

In conclusion, VL-Cogito significantly advances the state-of-the-art in multimodal reasoning by providing a stable, efficient, and adaptive RL training paradigm. It lays a strong foundation for developing more intelligent and robust MLLMs capable of tackling the inherent complexities of the multimodal world with greater precision and flexibility.
