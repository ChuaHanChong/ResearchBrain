# GesVLA: Gesture-Aware Vision-Language-Action Model Embedded Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> GesVLA integrates pointing gestures into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using a dual-VLM architecture and a scalable semi-synthetic data generation pipeline. This approach enhances target grounding accuracy and real-world robot manipulation success rates, particularly in complex, cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Current VLA models struggle with spatial ambiguity when relying solely on natural language for target specification in complex environments.
- Existing approaches for integrating gestures in human-robot interaction are often decoupled, leading to information loss, fragility, and reduced robustness.
- A lack of large-scale, high-quality multimodal datasets with precise gesture and action annotations hinders the development of gesture-aware VLA systems.

## Method

- Introduces a dual-VLM architecture that treats gesture as a first-class modality, enabling tight coupling between gesture, language, and action through latent space embeddings and ==cross-attention==.
- Developed a scalable semi-synthetic data generation pipeline to create abundant, accurately annotated multimodal data by synthesizing realistic gestures onto real-world scene images.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy, pre-training the intent reasoning VLM on synthetic gesture data and then freezing it while training the perception VLM and action expert on real robot demonstrations.

## Results

- VLM_int demonstrated strong intent reasoning, achieving **94.3%** accuracy in real-world target identification, significantly surpassing baseline MLLM and geometric pipeline approaches.
- The full GesVLA model achieved an **83.3%** average success rate in real-world manipulation tasks, representing a substantial improvement over text-only VLA models (**31.7%**) and those with shallow gesture integration.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of synthetic data diversity (e.g., coordinate jitter, hand appearance) and validated the two-stage training strategy with a frozen intent reasoning module for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling gesture and language within an end-to-end VLA framework significantly improves intention understanding and robustness over decoupled or rule-based methods.
> - Deep latent interaction, facilitated by cross-attention between gesture-aware intent reasoning and online perception modules, is critical for effective spatial grounding and action generation.
> - A scalable semi-synthetic data generation pipeline can successfully provide sufficient, high-quality multimodal data to pre-train gesture-aware intent reasoning models effectively for real-world transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to create general-purpose robots capable of interpreting human instructions and executing actions within a unified framework, often leveraging large-scale vision-language pretraining. Existing VLA systems, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and π-series models, primarily rely on textual instructions for human-robot interaction. While powerful in many scenarios, this reliance introduces inherent limitations in resolving spatial ambiguity, particularly in complex or cluttered environments where language alone might be insufficient to precisely specify a target object (e.g., "pick up this one").

Parallel to VLA development, research in human-robot interaction has explored integrating gesture perception to overcome the limitations of purely linguistic commands. Prior approaches to gesture integration can be broadly categorized:
- **Geometric Rule-based Systems:** These methods use traditional geometric rules and ray casting from hand keypoints (e.g., shoulder-wrist alignment) to infer pointing targets. While intuitive, they often lack robustness and deep coupling with language, leading to fragility against variations in pointing style or scene complexity.
- **Decoupled Modules with Text Conversion:** Some systems convert gestures into discrete textual descriptions, which are then fed to large language models (LLMs) for planning. This modular approach can lead to information loss, especially for precise spatial tasks, and cumulative errors across different processing stages.
- **Specialized Models and Visual Prompts:** Other efforts involve training specific models to predict pointing regions or using general Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate visual prompts for VLA policies. However, these often still involve multi-module pipelines that can suffer from similar issues of error propagation and lack of deep multimodal fusion.

GesVLA positions itself by addressing two key limitations simultaneously: the spatial ambiguity of language-only VLA models and the shallow integration or fragility of existing gesture interaction methods. It proposes to incorporate gesture as a "first-class modality" directly into the core reasoning and action generation process of VLA models, rather than treating it as an auxiliary signal or a pre-processed input. This aims to enable tighter coupling between gesture representations and action policies, providing fine-grained spatial reference that complements linguistic instructions, particularly for target localization in complex scenes. A major challenge in this area is the scarcity of large-scale, accurately annotated multimodal datasets (vision, language, gesture, action), which GesVLA also seeks to address through a scalable data generation pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for GesVLA stems from the observed limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-world robotic manipulation. Specifically, the authors identify two primary problems:

1. **Spatial Ambiguity in Text-Only Instructions:** Existing VLA systems predominantly rely on natural language instructions for human intent specification. While powerful for abstract tasks, language alone is often inadequate for precisely identifying target objects or locations in complex, cluttered environments. Phrases such as "pick up this one" or "place it there" are inherently ambiguous when multiple similar objects are present or when precise spatial relationships are crucial. This ambiguity necessitates overly detailed and cumbersome textual descriptions, which can reduce efficiency and intuitiveness of human-robot interaction. The paper aims to mitigate this by introducing a more direct spatial input.

2. **Insufficient Integration of Gestures in VLA Models and Lack of Training Data:** Humans naturally use deictic gestures, such as pointing, to resolve spatial ambiguities. While hand pose estimation has advanced, existing robotic systems that incorporate gestures typically treat them as auxiliary signals, converting them into discrete textual representations or using them in decoupled post-processing steps. This approach limits the depth of integration, potentially leading to information loss and reduced robustness, as gestures are not deeply factored into the VLA model's core reasoning process. Furthermore, a significant barrier to developing gesture-aware VLA models is the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality training datasets that align visual observations, language instructions, gesture signals, and precise spatial annotations for robot actions. Collecting such data in real-world settings is expensive and challenging to annotate accurately, especially for pointing targets.

To address these challenges, GesVLA establishes the following key objectives:
- **Introduce Gesture as a First-Class Modality:** To enable gestures to participate in both high-level reasoning and low-level action generation, integrating them directly into the latent space of the VLA model.
- **Design a Gesture-Aware Architecture:** Develop a VLA architecture that achieves tight coupling between gesture representations and action policies, allowing for seamless multimodal fusion without information loss from intermediate conversions. This involves decoupling intent reasoning from online perception and action generation while maintaining direct latent interaction.
- **Construct a Scalable Gesture Data Generation Pipeline:** Create a method for generating abundant and accurately annotated multimodal data (vision, language, gesture, spatial targets) by synthesizing gestures onto real-world scene images. This pipeline should reduce the sim-to-real visual gap and provide diverse motion patterns to effectively train gesture-aware models.
- **Develop an Effective Training Strategy:** Propose a multi-stage training approach that first equips the model with robust gesture perception and intent reasoning capabilities, and then integrates this with action prediction using real robot demonstrations.

By achieving these objectives, GesVLA aims to enhance the target grounding accuracy and overall efficiency of human-robot interaction, particularly in visually complex and cluttered environments, thereby advancing the capabilities of general-purpose robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GesVLA introduces a novel framework for gesture-aware robot manipulation, encompassing a specialized dual-VLM architecture, a scalable data generation pipeline, and a two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. A. Gesture-aware Dual-VLM Architecture

The architecture is designed to integrate gesture as a primary input modality, facilitating deep multimodal fusion. It consists of three main components:
- **VLM for Intent Reasoning (VLM_int):** This component is responsible for interpreting human intention from gesture and language inputs.
- **VLM for Online Perception (VLM_per):** This component processes real-time scene observations and language instructions, conditioned by the inferred intention from VLM_int.
- **Action Expert (F_θ):** This module generates robot actions based on the latent representations from VLM_per and the current robot state.

**Gesture Embedding:**
Gesture instructions are processed from video sequences. The system first identifies keyframes by detecting periods of hand motion stagnation, which typically correspond to pointing events. From these keyframes, four keypoints (wrist and three index finger joints) are extracted using MediaPipe. These keypoints, represented as (x, y, d) coordinates, are concatenated into a feature vector and projected into the model's latent space via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) module, enabling continuous representation of gesture information.

**Dual-VLM Architecture Flow:**
1. **VLM_int** takes gesture keyframes (and their embedded features) and the language instruction (T) to infer task-relevant targets. Its outputs include textual descriptions of inferred targets, visual prompts (e.g., highlighted regions on objects), and cached key-value states (K_int, V_int) that encapsulate the learned intent.
2. **VLM_per** receives multi-view scene observations (O), the language instruction (T), and the reasoning outputs (y) from VLM_int. Crucially, it uses cross-attention to directly attend to the latent representations (K_int, V_int) of VLM_int. This allows for direct propagation of intention information without intermediate discretization, maintaining a tight coupling between intent and perception.
3. The **Action Expert (F_θ)** then generates a sequence of actions (an action chunk) by iteratively denoising an initial noisy action trajectory (x_0). This process is conditioned on the latent representations from VLM_per and the current robot state (s_t). A flow-based policy is employed to produce smooth and precise continuous action trajectories.

**Asymmetric Interaction and Efficiency:**
The interaction between the modules is designed to be asymmetric, following a unidirectional flow: VLM_int → VLM_per → Action Expert. VLM_int does not attend to downstream modules, allowing its computations (including key-value states) to be performed once at the beginning of a task and then cached and reused throughout execution. This design optimizes computational efficiency during inference, with VLM_per executing at each control step and the Action Expert performing multiple denoising iterations per step.

#### 3.2. B. Scalable Gesture-aware Data Engine

To overcome the data bottleneck, a semi-synthetic data generation pipeline is developed:
**Target Selection and 3D Grounding:** Given a real-world RGB-D scene image, candidate objects are first detected using GroundingDINO. Task-relevant targets are then randomly sampled from these detections. For each selected target, its 2D center is perturbed with random jitter to simulate natural pointing variation. The corresponding 3D target point is then recovered using the depth map and camera intrinsics, providing precise 3D supervision. This process supports diverse task patterns involving multiple targets.

**Pointing Motion Generation:** Hand motion is simulated to naturally approach and point at a target. A virtual hand model, initialized from a random direction, smoothly moves towards the 3D target point. For multi-target tasks, the hand motion between targets incorporates a parabolic lifting term to simulate more human-like movements rather than straight-line interpolation. The hand mesh is then transformed and rendered onto the real scene images to create synthetic gesture video frames.

**Multimodal Dataset:** The pipeline generates a dataset of approximately 16,000 samples, each comprising: (1) a video of the pointing process, (2) a language instruction, (3) supervision signals for VLM_int (textual and coordinate-based reasoning outputs), and (4) metadata. This semi-synthetic approach combines realistic scene backgrounds with controllable synthetic gestures, providing accurate spatial labels at scale and enabling easy extension to new scenarios.

#### 3.3. C. Training Pipeline of GesVLA

GesVLA employs a two-stage training strategy:
1. **Intent Reasoning Pre-training (Stage 1):** VLM_int is trained on the large-scale semi-synthetic gesture dataset. This stage uses gesture keyframes, hand keypoint features, and language instructions as input, with supervision on reasoning outputs (textual descriptions and discretized coordinate tokens). The training objective is teacher-forced autoregressive cross-entropy, enabling VLM_int to jointly learn semantic reasoning and spatial grounding. PaliGemma-2B serves as the backbone, initialized from a pre-trained PaliGemma model.

2. **Joint Training for Action Generation (Stage 2):** After VLM_int is pre-trained, the full dual-VLM policy is trained on real robot demonstrations. In this stage, VLM_int is typically frozen to leverage its pre-trained reasoning capabilities without further modification. VLM_per and the action expert (F_θ) are optimized. Training samples consist of multimodal instructions, multi-view observations, robot states, and corresponding manipulation trajectories. The action expert is trained using a flow matching objective to model continuous action distributions, predicting the denoising direction for iterative action refinement. VLM_per is initialized from the VLM backbone of π_0, and the action expert is trained from scratch.

#### 3.4. D. Implementation Details

The PaliGemma-2B architecture is used as the backbone for both VLM_int and VLM_per. AdamW optimizer with a warmup cosine learning rate schedule and exponential moving average (EMA) of parameters are used for optimization. Training is performed on H20 GPUs, and inference on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GesVLA was evaluated through both intent reasoning experiments and real-world robot manipulation tasks, complemented by ablation studies and qualitative comparisons.

#### 4.1. A. Intent Reasoning Experiment Results

This experiment assessed VLM_int's ability to transfer gesture-conditioned intent reasoning from semi-synthetic training data to real-world scenarios.
- **Setup:** VLM_int was trained on 300 real RGB-D scenes augmented with synthetic gestures and evaluated on 88 new real scenes with varying object counts (≥ 7). A right-view camera provided input, and the task was to identify a pointed block and a pointed plate based on the instruction "Pick this up and put it there."
- **Baselines:**
 - **Baseline-1 (Prompted Native MLLM):** Qwen3.5-plus was prompted to identify pointing coordinates from key gesture frames. Achieved 38.6% accuracy. Error analysis indicated a tendency to select objects closest to the fingertip rather than those along the pointing direction, even with optimized prompts.
 - **Baseline-2 (Geometric Pipeline):** A non-end-to-end pipeline using MediaPipe for keypoints, GroundingDINO for object detection, and DepthAnythingV2 for depth estimation. Target determination involved minimizing distance to the index finger ray. Achieved 59.1% accuracy. This approach suffered from error propagation across its submodules and lacked deep language-gesture coupling.
- **GesVLA (VLM_int):** Achieved an accuracy of 94.3% (83/88 samples), outperforming Baseline-1 by 55.7% and Baseline-2 by 35.2%. This result indicated that joint modeling of gesture and language significantly improved intention understanding robustness, allowing the model to leverage context and language priors even with unstable local hand observations.

#### 4.2. B. Manipulation Experiment Results

Real-world robotic manipulation tasks were performed using a 7-DOF ARX5 robotic arm with three cameras.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Pick-and-Place Block:** Grasping a specified block from a cluttered arrangement and placing it onto a designated plate.
 - **Select Jelly:** Grasping one or more specified jelly cups in a given pointing order and placing them into a target plate.
 - **Select Fruit and Vegetable:** Grasping one or more specified objects (bell peppers, bananas) from produce bins in a given order and placing them into a basket.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Text-only VLA:** A VLA model trained with gesture sequences replaced by descriptive text. Achieved a total average success rate of 31.7%, confirming the insufficiency of language alone for precise spatial grounding in cluttered scenes.
 - **Prompted Native MLLM + VLA:** Used Baseline-1's intent reasoning to generate visual prompts for the VLA policy. Achieved a total average success rate of 31.7%.
 - **Geometric Pipeline + VLA:** Used Baseline-2's intent reasoning to generate visual prompts for the VLA policy. Achieved a total average success rate of 41.7%. Both baselines suffered from the low accuracy and robustness of their front-end gesture reasoning.
 - **GesVLA (Decoupled VLMs):** An intermediate variant of GesVLA where VLM_int and VLM_per were decoupled (no cross-attention), relying only on visual prompts for information transfer. Achieved a total average success rate of 61.7%. The performance gap with the full model suggests that visual prompts still introduce a modality bottleneck.
- **GesVLA (ours):** The full GesVLA model achieved a total average success rate of 83.3%. It significantly outperformed all baselines, with particular advantages in "hard" (cluttered, multi-object) scenarios: 95.0% for Pick-and-Place Block (Hard), 75.0% for Select Jelly (Hard), and 80.0% for Select Fruit and Vegetable (Hard). These results indicate that end-to-end latent interaction between gesture perception and action generation is critical for robust spatial instruction following.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Study

- **Intent Reasoning (Table III):**
 - Removing **coordinate jitter** during synthetic data generation severely degraded accuracy to 42.0%, indicating overfitting to fixed coordinate bins.
 - Removing **hand appearance augmentation** reduced performance to 76.1%, highlighting the importance of diverse visual variations for sim-to-real transfer.
 - Removing the **gesture MLP projection layer** lowered accuracy to 84.1%, showing that explicitly encoded pointing direction information is valuable beyond raw gesture images.
- **Training Strategy (Table IV):**
 - **Joint training** of all modules simultaneously resulted in only 45.0% average success, suggesting that large-scale synthetic reasoning data can negatively impact downstream action learning.
 - **Two-stage training** where VLM_int was fine-tuned in the second stage (with real-robot gesture annotations) achieved 80.0%.
 - **Two-stage training with VLM_int frozen** achieved the best performance of 83.3%, demonstrating that the semi-synthetic gesture data provided sufficient supervision for effective sim-to-real transfer without requiring additional real-robot gesture annotations.
- **Model Architecture (Table V):**
 - Removing the **visual prompt** from VLM_int to VLM_per significantly degraded overall success (66.6%), affirming its role in target grounding.
 - Adding **textual prompts** from VLM_int did not improve performance, suggesting that the cross-attention mechanism effectively conveys intent information.

#### 4.4. D. Qualitative Comparison

- **Real-world Rollout Visualization:** Demonstrations confirmed that gesture as an additional instruction modality simplifies human-robot interaction and improves intention understanding, particularly in cluttered environments requiring spatial disambiguation.
- **Intention Understanding:** Comparisons between a native MLLM (Qwen3.5-plus) and GesVLA's VLM_int showed that the MLLM often selected objects closest to the fingertip in image space without properly reasoning about the pointing direction. In contrast, VLM_int successfully identified intended targets along the pointing direction in complex scenes, providing more reliable intent understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GesVLA represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and human-robot interaction by systematically integrating pointing gestures into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **address the fundamental limitation of spatial ambiguity** inherent in language-only VLA systems. By allowing humans to intuitively point at objects, GesVLA simplifies interaction and reduces the cognitive load of providing complex, verbose textual descriptions, especially in cluttered or dynamic environments. This enhancement makes human-robot collaboration more natural, efficient, and robust for practical applications.

The proposed **gesture-aware dual-VLM architecture** is impactful because it moves beyond treating gestures as auxiliary signals. By embedding gesture features directly into the latent space and employing an asymmetric interaction design with cross-attention, GesVLA achieves a tight and deep coupling between gesture representations, language interpretation, and action generation. This deeper integration minimizes information loss and error propagation, leading to more accurate target grounding and reliable task execution compared to modular or decoupled approaches. The computational efficiency gained from the asymmetric design also makes the system more viable for real-time robotic deployment.

Furthermore, the development of a **scalable semi-synthetic gesture data generation pipeline** is a crucial methodological contribution. This pipeline effectively overcomes the challenge of acquiring large-scale, precisely annotated multimodal data for gesture-grounded interaction. By synthesizing realistic hand motions onto real-world scene images, it reduces the sim-to-real visual gap while providing diverse data for robust model training. This innovation lowers the barrier for future research in gesture-aware robotics, enabling more comprehensive pre-training of intent reasoning models.

The **two-stage training strategy** also demonstrates an effective approach to combine large-scale synthetic data pre-training with real-robot action learning, with the finding that freezing the pre-trained intent reasoning model (VLM_int) yields optimal performance without requiring additional real-robot gesture supervision. This suggests that the generated synthetic data is sufficiently rich and accurate for generalization.

The experimental results, demonstrating substantial improvements in target grounding accuracy and task success rates across various real-world manipulation scenarios (including multi-object and sequential tasks), confirm the practical utility of GesVLA. The most pronounced gains were observed in complex and cluttered environments, where the advantages of gesture-based spatial disambiguation are most evident.

Potential impacts include:
- **More intuitive robotic assistants:** Robots could become more user-friendly in domestic, industrial, or service settings, where natural pointing gestures are common for task specification.
- **Enhanced precision in complex tasks:** The ability to precisely identify targets via gesture could benefit applications requiring fine manipulation in cluttered spaces, such as assembly, sorting, or surgical assistance.
- **Foundation for richer human-robot communication:** GesVLA lays groundwork for exploring and integrating more diverse non-verbal cues and instruction modalities beyond basic pointing gestures, moving towards more sophisticated and human-like interactions.
- **Guidance for data generation:** The successful semi-synthetic data engine offers a blueprint for creating synthetic yet realistic datasets for other challenging multimodal robotics problems.

While the current system focuses on pointing gestures, the framework is extensible, indicating a path towards future work incorporating richer human instruction modalities and broadening the scope of manipulation tasks.
