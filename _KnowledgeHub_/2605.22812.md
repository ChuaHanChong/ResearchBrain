# GesVLA: Gesture-Aware Vision-Language-Action Model Embedded Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> GesVLA integrates pointing gestures into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using a dual-VLM architecture and a scalable semi-synthetic data generation pipeline. This approach enhances target grounding accuracy and real-world robot manipulation success rates, particularly in complex, cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Current VLA models struggle with spatial ambiguity when relying solely on natural language for target specification in complex environments.
- Existing approaches for integrating gestures in human-robot interaction are often decoupled, leading to information loss, fragility, and reduced robustness.
- A lack of large-scale, high-quality multimodal datasets with precise gesture and action annotations hinders the development of gesture-aware VLA systems.

## Method

- Introduces a dual-VLM architecture that treats gesture as a first-class modality, enabling tight coupling between gesture, language, and action through latent space embeddings and ==cross-attention==.
- Developed a scalable semi-synthetic data generation pipeline to create abundant, accurately annotated multimodal data by synthesizing realistic gestures onto real-world scene images.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy, pre-training the intent reasoning VLM on synthetic gesture data and then freezing it while training the perception VLM and action expert on real robot demonstrations.

## Results

- VLM_int demonstrated strong intent reasoning, achieving **94.3%** accuracy in real-world target identification, significantly surpassing baseline MLLM and geometric pipeline approaches.
- The full GesVLA model achieved an **83.3%** average success rate in real-world manipulation tasks, representing a substantial improvement over text-only VLA models (**31.7%**) and those with shallow gesture integration.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of synthetic data diversity (e.g., coordinate jitter, hand appearance) and validated the two-stage training strategy with a frozen intent reasoning module for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling gesture and language within an end-to-end VLA framework significantly improves intention understanding and robustness over decoupled or rule-based methods.
> - Deep latent interaction, facilitated by cross-attention between gesture-aware intent reasoning and online perception modules, is critical for effective spatial grounding and action generation.
> - A scalable semi-synthetic data generation pipeline can successfully provide sufficient, high-quality multimodal data to pre-train gesture-aware intent reasoning models effectively for real-world transfer.
