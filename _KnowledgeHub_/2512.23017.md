# Merge before Forget: A Single LoRA Continual Learning via Continual Merging

> [!summary] Summary
> SLAO (Single LoRA continual learning with Orthogonal initialization via continual merging) introduces a parameter-efficient method that enables large language models to continually learn new tasks using a single, constantly updated LoRA adapter. This approach mitigates catastrophic forgetting and achieves constant memory complexity by employing orthogonal initialization and time-aware asymmetric merging, outperforming existing data-free continual learning baselines across diverse benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models experience catastrophic forgetting when sequentially learning new tasks, leading to knowledge loss from previously acquired information.
- Existing LoRA-based continual learning methods face memory scalability issues, with memory footprint growing linearly as more tasks are learned, limiting long-term deployment.
- Current model merging techniques are not suitable for the sequential, incremental updates required in continual learning, nor do they account for LoRA's specific structural properties.

## Method

- SLAO incrementally integrates all task-specific LoRA updates into a single, shared LoRA adapter, ensuring a constant memory footprint.
- It initializes the new task's LoRA 'A' matrix using an orthogonal basis extracted from the previous task's fine-tuned 'A' matrix via QR decomposition.
- An asymmetric merging approach updates the LoRA 'B' component with a time-aware scaling factor (1/√i) while directly adopting the newly fine-tuned 'A' component for the merged LoRA.

## Results

- SLAO consistently surpassed all data-free continual learning baselines in average accuracy across standard, large-task, and SuperNI benchmarks on Llama-2-7B-chat and other LLMs.
- The approach maintained a constant memory footprint (O((m+n)r)) irrespective of the number of tasks, effectively addressing the scalability limitations of previous LoRA-CL methods.
- Its asymmetric merging strategy, particularly for the LoRA 'B' matrix with a time-aware scaling factor, proved crucial for superior performance, strong backward transfer, and robust stability across varying task orders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LoRA's 'A' and 'B' components exhibit intrinsic asymmetry, with the 'A' matrix showing higher cross-task similarity, suggesting distinct roles in learning dynamics.
> - Orthogonal initialization of the 'A' matrix, supported by Neural Tangent Kernel analysis, helps maintain geometric consistency across tasks and minimizes forgetting and intransigence errors.
> - A time-aware scaling coefficient (λ(i) = 1/√i) during the asymmetric merging process is crucial for adaptively balancing the retention of old knowledge with the integration of new information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has established them as foundational components in modern machine learning. However, their practical deployment faces significant challenges, particularly in **continual learning (CL)** scenarios. LLMs are prone to **catastrophic forgetting**, where knowledge from previously learned tasks is lost when adapting to new ones sequentially. Furthermore, the immense parameter count of LLMs makes full fine-tuning computationally expensive and memory-intensive, leading to a strong interest in **parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)** techniques.

**Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** has emerged as a leading PEFT method, injecting small, trainable low-rank matrices into pre-trained models. While LoRA mitigates the computational cost of adaptation, existing LoRA-based continual learning (LoRA-CL) methods still struggle with scalability and forgetting:
- **Memory Growth:** Many methods, such such as O-LoRA, InfLoRA, SAPT-LoRA, and SD-LoRA, address forgetting by retaining and freezing previously learned LoRAs or generating data representations. This strategy leads to memory consumption that grows linearly with the number of tasks, limiting scalability and storage capacity.
- **Task Interference:** The accumulation of multiple LoRAs, or the lack of effective merging mechanisms, can lead to interference between tasks.

This paper identifies a critical gap: existing LoRA-CL techniques are not designed for true *continual merging* where tasks arrive sequentially and updates need to be integrated into a *single, evolving adapter*. Current model merging techniques, like KnOTS and LoRA-LEGO, typically assume concurrent access to all task-specific models or LoRAs, which is not applicable to the sequential nature of CL. While some full-model continual merging methods exist (e.g., OPCM), they are not tailored for LoRA's unique structure and objectives.

The authors draw inspiration from model merging paradigms but extend it to frame continual learning as a sequential model merging problem for LoRAs. A crucial insight, supported by empirical analysis (Figure 1), is the **intrinsic asymmetry** between LoRA's `A` and `B` components. The `A` matrix (input-side) tends to exhibit higher cosine similarity across tasks than the `B` matrix (output-side), suggesting fundamentally different learning dynamics. This observation motivates an asymmetric treatment of these components during continual merging. By focusing on integrating updates into a single, unified LoRA continually, this work aims to provide a scalable, memory-efficient, and effective solution to LoRA-based continual learning, addressing the limitations of prior art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a novel parameter-efficient continual learning method for LLMs that effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting while ensuring constant memory complexity, irrespective of the number of tasks learned. This overarching goal is driven by two central questions identified in the paper:

1. **Can we enable continual learning using only a single shared LoRA, without learning or storing task-specific LoRAs or data representations?**
 - **Motivation:** Existing LoRA-CL methods lead to linear memory growth with each new task by freezing previous LoRAs or maintaining large data representations. This is unsustainable for LLMs learning from continuous data streams, imposing severe limitations on scalability and storage. The desire for a *constant memory footprint* is paramount for practical, long-term continual learning.

2. **How can we enable continual learning through LoRA-based continual merging?**
 - **Motivation:** While model merging combines multiple models into one, current approaches assume concurrent access to all models. Continual learning, by definition, involves sequential arrival of tasks. Therefore, a method is needed that can *incrementally integrate* new task knowledge into a single LoRA, adapting the merging mechanism to the sequential nature of CL. Furthermore, existing continual merging methods are not designed for LoRA, which has specific structural properties (e.g., asymmetry of A and B matrices) that need to be considered.

To address these questions, the authors are motivated by several key insights:
- **Reducing Forgetting and Intransigence Errors:** Theoretical analysis, particularly under the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime, shows that minimizing forgetting and intransigence errors (how much old knowledge is lost vs. how well new knowledge is acquired) is related to minimizing the Frobenius norm of differences between LoRA updates (`||B_t A_t - B_i A_i||_F` and `||B_i A_i - B*_i A*_i||_F`). This motivates architectural and initialization choices that maintain consistency and adaptability.
- **Orthogonal Initialization:** To minimize these errors, the authors propose an orthogonal initialization strategy for the `A` component of the LoRA. By extracting an orthogonal basis from the previous task's `A` and using it to initialize the new task's `A`, geometric consistency is maintained, facilitating alignment across tasks and reducing interference.
- **LoRA Asymmetry:** Empirical evidence and theoretical considerations reveal that the `B` component of LoRA (output-side) exhibits greater orthogonality to its initialization and more diverse learning dynamics across tasks compared to the `A` component (input-side). This suggests that `B` is a more suitable candidate for incremental merging to preserve task isolation and reduce interference.
- **Time-aware Scaling:** Inspired by model merging literature, a time-aware scaling mechanism (`λ(i)`) is crucial during the merging process. This adaptively balances the retention of old knowledge with the integration of new information, allowing the merged LoRA to evolve stably and effectively over time.

These motivations collectively form the foundation for the proposed SLAO algorithm, aiming to create a scalable, efficient, and robust solution for continual learning in LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **SLAO (Single LoRA continual learning with Orthogonal initialization via continual merging)**, incrementally integrates all task-specific LoRA updates into a single continually updated LoRA. This approach is built upon four core principles: Orthogonal Retention, Continual Merging, Asymmetry of LoRA, and Time-aware Scaling. The methodology can be broken down into two main operations performed for each new task:

**1. Orthogonal Basis Extraction for Initialization:**
When a new task *i* arrives, SLAO initializes the LoRA matrices (`A_ft,i` and `B_ft,i`) for this new task, crucially leveraging information from the *previously fine-tuned LoRA* (`A_ft,i-1`, `B_ft,i-1`) rather than the merged LoRA or random initialization.
- **A-matrix Initialization:** An orthogonal basis is extracted from the *transpose* of the previous task's fine-tuned `A` matrix (`(A_ft,i-1)^T`) using **QR decomposition**. This yields an orthogonal matrix `Q_i`. The new task's `A` matrix (`A_ft,i`) is then initialized as the transpose of `Q_i` (`Q_i^T`). This ensures that `A_ft,i * (A_ft,i)^T = I_r` (an identity matrix of rank `r`), thereby enforcing orthogonal rows. This orthogonal initialization for `A` is theoretically justified by the NTK analysis, which suggests it helps maintain geometric consistency across tasks and minimizes forgetting/intransigence errors.
- **B-matrix Initialization:** The new task's `B` matrix (`B_ft,i`) is initialized directly with the *previous task's fine-tuned `B` matrix* (`B_ft,i-1`). This leverages prior learned knowledge in `B` as a starting point.

After this initialization, the model is fine-tuned on the new task *i*'s training data, yielding updated task-specific LoRA matrices `B_ft,i` and `A_ft,i`.

**2. Asymmetrically Merging LoRA via Time-aware Scaling:**
Once fine-tuning for task *i* is complete, the updated task-specific LoRA (`B_ft,i A_ft,i`) needs to be merged into the single, continually updated LoRA (`B_i-1_merge A_i-1_merge`). Recognizing the inherent asymmetry of LoRA components, SLAO treats `A` and `B` differently during merging:
- **A-matrix Merging:** The *merged `A` matrix (`A_i_merge`)* is simply updated to be the *newly fine-tuned `A` matrix (`A_ft,i`)*. This approach implicitly reweights previous tasks' updates and allows `A` to remain adaptable to the latest task while its orthogonal initialization helps maintain consistency.
- **B-matrix Merging:** The *merged `B` matrix (`B_i_merge`)* is updated using a linear arithmetic composition. It combines the previously merged `B` (`B_i-1_merge`) with the difference between the new task's fine-tuned `B` (`B_ft,i`) and the previous merged `B`, scaled by a **time-aware coefficient `λ(i)`**:
 `B_i_merge = B_i-1_merge + λ(i) * (B_ft,i - B_i-1_merge)`
 The scaling factor `λ(i)` is set to `1/√i`. This choice is based on the observation that `B` task vectors tend to be approximately orthogonal and helps maintain a consistent magnitude of parameter changes across sequential merging steps, as supported by existing continual merging literature. This adaptive scaling ensures a balance between preserving past knowledge and integrating new information effectively.

**Theoretical Justification:**
The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Section 4.2 and Appendix A) to justify the design choices.
- **NTK Analysis:** It connects the minimization of forgetting and intransigence errors to minimizing the Frobenius norms of differences between LoRA products. The orthogonal initialization of `A` is shown to help achieve this by maintaining geometric consistency and alignment.
- **Dynamics of LoRA Components:** The analysis extends prior work on LoRA dynamics, suggesting that under orthogonal initialization of `A`, the `B` matrix may update across different initialization subspaces. This can effectively increase the rank of `B` in the merged LoRA, which aids generalization.

By combining these principles, SLAO aims to maintain a compact yet expressive representation of all learned tasks, offering a simple, scalable, and effective solution for LoRA-based continual learning with constant memory complexity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and efficiency of SLAO were rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments across diverse continual learning benchmarks using various LLM architectures.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Llama-2-7B-chat, Llama-2-13B-chat, Llama-3-2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B, and Qwen2.5-7B.
- **Benchmarks:**
 1. **Standard CL benchmark:** Consisting of 5 classification tasks (AG News, Amazon, Reviews, Yelp Reviews, DBpedia, Yahoo Answers).
 2. **Large number of tasks benchmark:** Includes the 5 standard CL tasks plus 4 GLUE tasks, 5 SuperGLUE tasks, and IMDB reviews (total 15 tasks).
 3. **SuperNI Benchmark:** A diverse collection of 15 NLP tasks with expert-written instructions (dialogue generation, information extraction, QA, summarization, sentiment analysis).
- **Baselines:**
 - **Continual Learning:** SeqLoRA, IncLoRA, O-LoRA, InfLoRA, SAPT-LoRA, LoRM (BA/AB), CorDA, MagMax.
 - **LoRA Merging:** KnOTS, LoRA-LEGO (adapted for sequential use).
 - **Continual Merging:** OPCM (adapted for LoRA).
 - **Upper Bound:** Multi-Task (joint training on all tasks).
- **Metrics:** Average Accuracy (AA), Backward Transfer (BWT), Maximum Order-Normalized Performance Disparity (MOPD), and Average Order-Normalized Performance Disparity (AOPD).

#### 4.2. Key Findings

1. **Superior Continual Learning Performance (Table 1):**
 - SLAO consistently **outperforms all data-free continual learning baselines** across all three benchmarks (Standard CL, Large Number of Tasks, SuperNI) when evaluated on Llama-2-7B-chat.
 - SAPT-LoRA achieved the highest performance among LoRA-based methods, but it relies on generating pseudo-samples from previous tasks, which is often impractical.
 - Basic LoRA CL methods like SeqLoRA perform poorly due to severe catastrophic forgetting. Freezing-based methods (IncLoRA, O-LoRA) show improvement but are limited by memory growth.
 - Existing LoRA merging (KnOTS, LoRA-LEGO) and full-model continual merging (OPCM) baselines perform significantly worse than SLAO, highlighting the need for a method specifically designed for *continual LoRA merging*.

2. **Constant Memory Footprint (Figure 2):**
 - SLAO demonstrably maintains a **constant memory overhead** (O((m+n)r)) regardless of the number of tasks, a critical advantage over freezing-based methods that incur linear memory growth (O(T(m+n)r)). This makes SLAO highly scalable for long-term continual learning.

3. **Impact of Initialization Strategies (Table 2):**
 - Initializing the new task LoRA from the **last fine-tuning point** (SLAO's approach) consistently yields the best performance across all benchmarks.
 - Initializing from the *last merging point* performs slightly worse.
 - Random (zero) initialization performs the worst, underscoring the importance of leveraging prior learned knowledge in the initialization phase.

4. **Asymmetric LoRA Merging is Crucial (Table 3):**
 - Experiments comparing various merging strategies confirmed the importance of **asymmetric treatment of LoRA components**.
 - Strategies that involve *merging only the B matrix* (`FTBA-MB`) consistently outperform those that merge `A` (`FTBA-MA`) or both (`FTBA-MBA`).
 - When freezing one component, *freezing A and merging B* (`FREA-MB`) performed better than the reverse (`FREB-MA`), aligning with the theoretical intuition and previous findings about LoRA asymmetry. SLAO's specific asymmetric merging strategy effectively leverages this property.

5. **Robustness across Models and Sizes (Table 4, Appendix B.1, B.2):**
 - SLAO's effectiveness was validated across different Llama variants (Llama-2-7B-chat, Llama-2-13B-chat, Llama-3-2-3B) and Qwen2.5 models.
 - Larger models generally achieved better accuracy and exhibited greater robustness to task order variations, confirming the benefit of increased capacity in CL. SLAO consistently performed well relative to baselines across these model scales.

6. **Adaptive Time-Varying Coefficient (Figure 4):**
 - The adaptive scaling factor (`λ(i) = 1/√i`) in SLAO consistently delivered the **highest average accuracy with lower variance** across different task orders and models, outperforming fixed scaling factors. This highlights the effectiveness of balancing new and old knowledge dynamically.

7. **Backward Transfer and Order Robustness (Appendix B.4, B.5):**
 - SLAO demonstrated **strong backward transfer** capability, indicating effective retention of knowledge from previous tasks.
 - It exhibited the **most stable performance across different task orders** (lowest MOPD and AOPD scores), showcasing its robustness to task sequence variability, a common challenge in CL.

8. **Training Efficiency (Appendix B.10):**
 - SLAO proved to be both **memory and training efficient**. It avoids additional computational costs during training by only performing a one-time QR matrix factorization at initialization. The orthogonal initialization also contributed to slightly faster training times.

In summary, the experimental results provide strong evidence that SLAO successfully addresses the core challenges of LoRA-based continual learning by achieving high performance, maintaining constant memory usage, and demonstrating robustness across various settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Merge before Forget" offers a significant advancement in the field of continual learning for Large Language Models, with profound practical and theoretical implications:

1. **Addressing the Memory Scalability Crisis:** The most critical contribution of SLAO is its ability to enable parameter-efficient continual learning with **constant memory complexity**. For LLMs, which are continuously growing in size and are expected to adapt to endless streams of new information, a linearly increasing memory footprint for each new task is unsustainable. SLAO breaks this barrier by maintaining a single, unified LoRA, making true long-term continual learning a feasible reality rather than a theoretical aspiration. This is crucial for deploying adaptive LLMs in dynamic environments without requiring prohibitive hardware upgrades or frequent, costly retraining.

2. **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting and Task Interference:** By leveraging orthogonal basis initialization and time-aware asymmetric merging, SLAO effectively reduces catastrophic forgetting and minimizes interference between past and new tasks. The theoretical grounding in NTK analysis provides a deeper understanding of how these mechanisms contribute to better knowledge retention and adaptation. This means LLMs can accumulate knowledge over time more reliably, leading to more robust and versatile models.

3. **Novel Approach to Continual LoRA Merging:** SLAO pioneers a novel paradigm for continual learning by explicitly framing it as a sequential LoRA merging problem. It uniquely adapts model merging concepts to the continual setting and, importantly, accounts for the intrinsic asymmetry of LoRA's A and B components. This innovative approach opens new avenues for research into how PEFT modules can be dynamically composed and updated in a streaming fashion.

4. **Enhanced Efficiency:** Beyond memory savings, SLAO also demonstrates efficiency in training time. The one-time orthogonal basis extraction during initialization avoids the iterative computations often required by other CL methods (e.g., orthogonalization during every training step), further contributing to its practical viability.

5. **Improved Robustness:** The method's superior performance in terms of backward transfer and order-robustness (lower MOPD and AOPD) signifies that models trained with SLAO are less susceptible to the sequence in which tasks are learned. This is a highly desirable property for real-world applications where the order of incoming data cannot always be controlled.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact of SLAO is on the development of more adaptive and scalable LLMs. It provides a blueprint for how large foundation models can continuously learn and evolve from new data without becoming computationally or memory-bound. This can lead to:
- **Ever-learning AI Agents:** LLMs that can truly learn throughout their operational lifespan, staying up-to-date with new information, trends, and user preferences without needing complete architectural overhauls.
- **Reduced Carbon Footprint:** By avoiding the need to store multiple large LoRAs or retrain entire models, SLAO contributes to a more environmentally friendly approach to AI development.
- **Broader Accessibility:** Making continual learning more efficient could democratize access to advanced LLM adaptation capabilities, as the memory constraints become less prohibitive for smaller-scale deployments.

In conclusion, "Merge before Forget" addresses a fundamental challenge in LLM continual learning with an elegant and effective solution. Its focus on constant memory, principled merging, and theoretical justification positions it as a significant step forward, promising to unlock new possibilities for the continuous adaptation of large-scale AI models.
