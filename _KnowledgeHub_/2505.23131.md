# DOPPLER: Dual-Policy Learning for Device Assignment in Asynchronous Dataflow Graphs

> [!summary] Summary
> DOPPLER, from researchers at Rice University, introduces a dual-policy learning framework for device assignment in asynchronous multi-GPU dataflow graphs, aiming to minimize execution time. It achieves substantial runtime reductions by effectively balancing load and minimizing communication, outperforming existing heuristics and learning-based methods.

## Problem

- Bulk-synchronous multi-GPU systems suffer from inefficiencies like GPU idle time and communication stalls, hindering overall throughput.
- Optimal device assignment is challenging in work-conserving (asynchronous, non-deterministic) systems due to dynamic execution order and stochastic completion times.
- Prior learning-based device placement methods often neglect asynchronous system characteristics and lack mechanisms for continuous, cost-effective refinement on real hardware.

## Method

- Formulated ==device assignment== as an episodic Markov Decision Process (MDP) where execution time is determined by a stochastic simulator mirroring a work-conserving scheduler.
- Developed a dual-policy Graph Neural Network (GNN) architecture: one policy for selecting the next node to assign (`SEL_theta`) and another for placing it on a specific device (`PLC_theta`).
- Introduced a three-stage training framework: imitation learning from an expert heuristic, simulation-based reinforcement learning (RL), and real-system RL for online fine-tuning during deployment.

## Results

- DOPPLER-SYS consistently achieved lower execution times, providing up to a **78.2%** runtime reduction compared to the CRITICAL PATH heuristic and up to **13.8%** over the strong ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER baseline.
- The dual-policy architecture demonstrated superior performance compared to single-policy variants, highlighting the benefits of explicit node selection and device placement learning.
- The three-stage training framework enabled faster convergence and led to better final policies compared to training solely on the real system, showcasing the value of pre-training and simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the device assignment problem into sequential node selection and device placement policies, guided by GNNs, effectively addresses the combinatorial complexity and system non-determinism.
> - A progressive training approach, combining expert-guided pre-training with simulated and then real-system RL, provides robust policies while minimizing expensive real-world exploration and enabling continuous adaptation.
> - Approximating GNN message passing once per MDP episode, rather than at every step, significantly reduces training cost without compromising the quality of learned assignments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the problem of optimizing the execution time of machine learning workloads on multi-GPU systems. Modern deep learning frameworks, such as PyTorch, TensorFlow, and JAX, typically operate in a bulk-synchronous manner. This means that computations proceed in a lock-step, level-wise fashion, often involving synchronization barriers like `all-reduce` operations between stages. While robust, this synchronous approach can lead to inefficiencies, as computing resources (GPUs, network) may sit idle waiting for all parts of a distributed operation to complete before proceeding, hindering overall throughput.

An alternative approach involves "work-conserving" (WC) dynamic schedulers, which aim to keep resources continually active by scheduling operations asynchronously as soon as their inputs are available. Such systems can potentially offer significant speedups by overlapping communication and computation. However, device assignment—the task of mapping operations in a dataflow graph to specific devices—becomes more complex in these non-deterministic WC environments. The stochastic nature of execution, where operation completion times can vary, makes it challenging to define and achieve optimal load balancing and communication minimization.

Prior learning-based methods for device assignment, such as PLACETO, have primarily focused on bulk-synchronous systems, often neglecting the specific characteristics and scheduling mechanisms of WC systems. Additionally, many reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for combinatorial optimization problems tend to rely solely on learning from scratch, potentially overlooking the valuable structural insights embedded in expert-designed heuristics.

DOPPLER contributes to this landscape by specifically targeting device assignment in asynchronous, work-conserving multi-GPU systems. It proposes a learning-based framework that aims to overcome the limitations of synchronous systems and the challenges of device assignment in non-deterministic environments. The approach incorporates insights from expert heuristics into its learning process and explicitly considers the dynamics of WC schedulers, distinguishing it from prior work that predominantly focused on synchronous systems or purely end-to-end RL without structural guidance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method for assigning operations within a dataflow graph to devices in a work-conserving multi-GPU system to minimize the total execution time. This objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Addressing Inefficiencies of Bulk-Synchronous Systems:** Current multi-GPU computing systems often employ bulk-synchronous execution models. This approach requires all distributed components of a computation to complete and synchronize (e.g., via `all-reduce` operations) before the next stage can begin. This synchronization can lead to periods of device idleness, as faster computations must wait for slower ones, and communication-intensive phases (like `all-reduce`) underutilize GPU compute cores. The authors demonstrate this inefficiency with examples like CHAINMM and FFNN, showing that a work-conserving system can achieve substantial runtime reductions (e.g., 33% for CHAINMM, 53% for FFNN) compared to synchronous systems. The motivation is to unlock these potential performance gains by enabling effective device assignment for WC systems.

- **Tackling the Complexity of Device Assignment in Work-Conserving Systems:** In a WC system, operations are scheduled dynamically and asynchronously as soon as their dependencies are met. This dynamic, non-deterministic execution order makes optimal device assignment an inherently difficult problem. Factors like PCIe bus prioritization or variable kernel launch times can alter the execution sequence across runs. It is challenging to precisely define and achieve "load balancing" or "communication minimization" when the temporal interplay of tasks is stochastic. The paper aims to find an assignment strategy that remains robust and efficient despite this non-determinism.

- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Learning-Based Methods:**
 - **System Awareness:** Many prior learning-based device placement methods were designed for or evaluated on bulk-synchronous systems. These methods may not effectively account for the distinct scheduling mechanisms and potential for overlap in WC systems, leading to suboptimal assignments.
 - **Scheduling Mechanism Ignorance:** Existing learning approaches often do not explicitly incorporate awareness of the underlying system's scheduling mechanism into their design. This can limit their ability to produce assignments that genuinely exploit the system's dynamic capabilities.
 - **Exclusivity of Reinforcement Learning:** Pure RL approaches, while powerful, can be data-intensive and slow to converge, especially for complex combinatorial problems with vast action spaces. They may also neglect the insights derived from expert-designed heuristics that reflect structural properties of the problem. The authors seek a method that can leverage both the power of RL and the wisdom of established heuristics.

- **Enabling Cost-Effective and Adaptive Training:** Directly training RL agents on real hardware can be prohibitively expensive and time-consuming due to the need for extensive exploration and real-time execution measurements. There is a need for a training framework that can accelerate convergence and reduce the risk of deploying low-quality policies, while also allowing for continuous improvement in production environments.

To address these motivations, the authors propose DOPPLER, a framework that employs a dual-policy learning approach (selecting an operation and then placing it) and a three-stage training methodology (imitation learning, simulation-based RL, and real-system RL) to produce effective and continually improving device assignments for work-conserving multi-GPU systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DOPPLER approaches the device assignment problem as a sequential decision-making process formulated within an episodic Markov Decision Process (MDP). The goal is to generate a device assignment `A`, a mapping from computation graph vertices `V` to devices `D`, that minimizes the execution time `ExecTime(A)` in a work-conserving system.

**Execution Time Model (`ExecTime(A)`):**
A key component of the methodology is the definition of `ExecTime(A)`. Due to the stochastic nature of WC systems, a closed-form formula for execution time is impractical. Instead, `ExecTime(A)` is determined via a stochastic simulation (Algorithm 1) that mimics a real WC scheduler. This simulation repeatedly identifies available tasks (executions or data transfers between devices) using `EnumTasks` (Algorithm 2) and stochastically chooses the next task to complete based on a probability distribution `P`. The simulation tracks the initiation and completion times of tasks, returning the total time until all vertices are processed. This simulation serves as a "digital twin" or, in the final training stage, is replaced by observation of actual hardware execution.

**Problem Reformulation as an Episodic MDP:**
The vast number of possible device assignments (`D^|V|` arms in a bandit problem) necessitates a structured approach. DOPPLER constructs an assignment sequentially, one vertex at a time, using two policies:
- `SEL_theta`: A selection policy that chooses the next vertex `v` to assign from a set of candidate nodes.
- `PLC_theta`: A placement policy that assigns the chosen vertex `v` to a specific device `d`.

Each episode in the MDP corresponds to the complete process of assigning all `|V|` nodes in the dataflow graph, with a reward computed at the end.

**MDP Formulation Details:**
- **States (`s_h`):** At each step `h`, the state is a tuple `(X_G, C_h, X_D,h)`.
 - `X_G`: Static graph features (e.g., node computation cost, communication costs to/from neighbors, t-level and b-level costs, which are sums of computation and communication costs along critical paths).
 - `C_h`: Dynamic set of candidate nodes available for assignment (initially entry nodes).
 - `X_D,h`: Dynamic device features (e.g., total computation time assigned to each device, earliest/latest start/end times for inputs on devices).
- **Actions (`a_h`):** An action `a_h = (v_h, d_h)` involves selecting a node `v_h` and assigning it to a device `d_h`.
- **Reward:** The reward `r_H` is calculated at the end of an episode (`h = H`, after all nodes are assigned) as `(-1) * ExecTime(s_H)`. Intermediate rewards are zero. A baseline reward (average execution time from previous episodes) is subtracted to improve stability.

**Dual Policy Graph Neural Network (GNN) Architectures:**
Both `SEL_theta` and `PLC_theta` are implemented using GNNs and feedforward neural networks (FFNNs). A message-passing GNN learns representations `h_v^(k)` for each node in the dataflow graph.
- **Node Policy Network (`SEL_theta`):**
 - Takes the graph state (`G`, `X_G`) as input.
 - Computes an embedding `h_v` for each candidate node `v` by concatenating: its GNN representation, aggregated GNN embeddings from its b-path and t-path (critical paths), and its raw features (`Z[v]`).
 - An FFNN processes these embeddings to produce `Q_G(v)` values, which are converted into probabilities using `softmax`.
 - Node selection uses an `epsilon`-greedy strategy based on these probabilities.
- **Device Policy Network (`PLC_theta`):**
 - Takes the selected node `v`, devices `D`, and the overall state (`G`, `X_D`, `X_G`) as input.
 - Computes an embedding `h_v,d` for placing node `v` on each device `d` by concatenating: `v`'s GNN representation, aggregated GNN embeddings of nodes already assigned to device `d`, `d`'s dynamic features (`Y[d]`), and `v`'s raw features (`Z[v]`).
 - An FFNN processes these embeddings to produce `Q_D(d)` values, converted to probabilities via `softmax`.
 - Device selection uses an `epsilon`-greedy strategy.

**Efficient Message Passing Approximation:**
To mitigate the high computational cost of GNN message passing on large graphs at every MDP step, DOPPLER performs message passing only once per MDP *episode*. Updated assignment information is then encoded into device features (`X_D,h`) without requiring further message passing within the same episode. This modification significantly reduces training time with minimal impact on convergence.

**DOPPLER's Three-Stage Training Framework:**
The training process is structured into three sequential stages:
1. **Imitation Learning (Stage I, Offline):** The dual policies are initially pre-trained in a supervised manner to imitate the decisions of an existing expert heuristic, CRITICAL PATH. This provides a warm-start, accelerating convergence in subsequent RL stages. The objective `J(theta)` maximizes the likelihood of reproducing the heuristic's actions.
2. **Simulation-Based Reinforcement Learning (Stage II, Offline):** The policies are further trained using RL, with rewards provided by the software simulator `ExecTime(A)`. This allows for extensive exploration in a cost-effective environment, refining the policies learned in Stage I without incurring real hardware execution costs. Policy gradient methods are used to maximize the expected reward.
3. **Real-System Reinforcement Learning (Stage III, Online):** The high-quality policies from Stage II are deployed in the real WC multi-GPU system. Here, the reward signal for further RL training is obtained "for free" by observing actual system runtimes (`ExecTime`) during deployment. This enables continuous refinement and adaptation of the policies to real-world system dynamics, functioning as an online fine-tuning mechanism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DOPPLER focused on dataflow graphs derived from various neural network architectures (CHAINMM, FFNN, LLAMA-BLOCK, LLAMA-LAYER) using a 4-GPU NVIDIA Tesla P100 system, with additional ablation studies on memory constraints and different GPU types (8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs).

**Q1: Comparison with Alternatives (Table 2):**
- **Superior Performance:** DOPPLER-SYS (DOPPLER trained through Stage III) consistently achieved lower execution times compared to all baseline methods: CRITICAL PATH (a non-learning heuristic), PLACETO (a single-policy RL method trained with simulation), and ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER (an expert-designed, structure-exploiting baseline developed by the authors).
- **Significant Runtime Reductions:** DOPPLER-SYS achieved up to a 78.2% runtime reduction compared to CRITICAL PATH and up to a 62.5% reduction compared to PLACETO. Against the strong ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER baseline, DOPPLER-SYS provided further runtime reductions of up to 13.8% (for LLAMA-LAYER).
- **Simulator Effectiveness:** DOPPLER-SIM (DOPPLER trained through Stage II) was frequently the second-best performing method, indicating the simulator's ability to approximate real-system performance for effective training.

**Q2: Contributions of Dual Policies (Table 3):**
- An ablation study investigated the individual contributions of the node selection (`SEL_theta`) and device placement (`PLC_theta`) policies. Replacing one of DOPPLER's policies with a CRITICAL PATH heuristic showed that both components contribute to the overall performance.
- Combining both learned policies (DOPPLER-SYS) generally yielded the best results, particularly for more complex models like FFNN, LLAMA-BLOCK, and LLAMA-LAYER. For CHAINMM, DOPPLER-PLC (only placement policy learned) marginally outperformed DOPPLER-SYS.

**Q3: Impact of Training Stages (Figure 3):**
- The three-stage training framework demonstrated benefits for convergence and performance. Training DOPPLER-SYS exclusively on the real system without pre-training (Stage I and II) resulted in slower convergence and less stable performance, attributed to initial exploration from a suboptimal model.
- The imitation learning and simulation-based RL stages (pre-training) were shown to enable faster convergence and lead to lower execution times in the final real-system RL stage.

**Q4: Assignment Analysis and Visualizations (Figure 4, Appendix E):**
- Analysis of the generated assignments indicated that DOPPLER is more effective at load balancing across GPUs and minimizing communication overhead.
- For the FFNN graph, DOPPLER's assignments typically co-located producer and consumer operations on the same device, minimizing inter-GPU data transfers, unlike PLACETO, which often scattered related operations across different GPUs. This difference translated to significantly reduced execution times, with PLACETO spending more time on data transfers.
- For CHAINMM, DOPPLER utilized all four GPUs more consistently throughout the computation, whereas ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER showed underutilization of devices towards the end.
- For LLAMA-BLOCK, DOPPLER achieved better load balancing and leveraged data locality more effectively, resulting in higher and more consistent GPU utilization compared to CRITICAL PATH and PLACETO.

**Q5: Transferability to New Graphs (Table 4):**
- DOPPLER demonstrated transfer learning capabilities. Models trained on simpler architectures (FFNN, CHAINMM) could be fine-tuned on more complex Llama-structured graphs.
- After 2,000 fine-tuning episodes, the transferred DOPPLER models generally adapted to the new architectures and outperformed all baselines.
- With 4,000 fine-tuning episodes (less than half of the full training duration), DOPPLER achieved assignments with execution times comparable to those obtained from full training on the target models, indicating efficient adaptation.

**Ablation Studies (Appendix H and I):**
- **Simulator Validation:** The simulator was found to be a cost-effective proxy for real system execution, exhibiting a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.79 and a Spearman coefficient of 0.69, despite tending to overestimate runtimes.
- **Training Consistency:** DOPPLER-SYS showed consistent performance across different random seeds for the CHAINMM graph, with low variance in execution times.
- **Message-Passing Efficiency:** Performing GNN message passing once per episode (instead of per MDP step) significantly reduced the number of message-passing operations by approximately 3000% during training, with only a marginal 0.7% increase in the best assignment's runtime, validating the efficiency optimization.
- **PLACETO Policy Design:** Even when PLACETO was pre-trained with imitation learning, DOPPLER's dual-policy design still yielded superior device assignments, suggesting the architectural advantage of the dual-policy approach over PLACETO's single policy.
- **Hardware Adaptability:** DOPPLER successfully adapted to different hardware configurations:
 - **Restricted Memory (4 P100 GPUs, 8GB/GPU):** DOPPLER maintained its performance advantage, achieving up to 63.7% runtime reduction over CRITICAL PATH and up to 18.6% over ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER, demonstrating its ability to handle memory constraints.
 - **Different GPU Types (8 V100 GPUs):** DOPPLER leveraged NVLink effectively and outperformed baselines in 3 out of 4 tested scenarios, achieving up to 67.7% reduction against CRITICAL PATH and up to 19.3% against ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DOPPLER, a framework for device assignment in asynchronous dataflow graphs that offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Performance in Work-Conserving Systems:** DOPPLER demonstrates substantial reductions in execution time for complex machine learning workloads on multi-GPU systems operating under work-conserving schedulers. By effectively assigning operations, it mitigates the performance bottlenecks inherent in bulk-synchronous systems, such as GPU idle time and communication stalls. This directly translates to faster training and inference times for machine learning models.

- **Novel Dual-Policy Learning Approach:** The proposed dual-policy architecture, separating node selection (`SEL_theta`) from device placement (`PLC_theta`), provides a structured way to decompose and address the complex device assignment problem. This design specifically accounts for the non-deterministic nature of work-conserving environments and allows for a more focused learning of strategies for critical path traversal and load balancing.

- **Cost-Effective and Adaptive Training Framework:** The three-stage training methodology (imitation learning, simulation-based RL, and real-system RL) is a practical approach for deploying and continuously improving RL-based schedulers. Imitation learning accelerates initial convergence by leveraging expert heuristics, simulation-based RL allows for extensive and cost-efficient exploration, and real-system RL enables online, cost-free fine-tuning. This framework reduces the risk associated with deploying nascent RL policies and makes such systems more viable for Machine Learning as a Service (MLaaS) applications.

- **Improved Resource Utilization:** By generating assignments that enable effective overlap of computation and communication, minimize data transfers, and achieve better load balancing across GPUs, DOPPLER optimizes hardware resource utilization. This implies that existing multi-GPU infrastructure can be used more efficiently, potentially reducing the need for hardware upgrades.

- **Generalizability and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability of DOPPLER to transfer learning to new graph structures (e.g., from simple FFNNs to Llama models) and adapt to varied hardware configurations (different GPU memory sizes, different GPU types, and numbers) suggests a robust and generalizable solution. This enhances its applicability across diverse deep learning workloads and hardware setups.

- **Broader Societal Implications:** The improved efficiency in executing deep learning computations on multi-GPU systems has potential societal benefits. More efficient use of computing resources can lead to reduced energy consumption and a lower carbon footprint associated with large-scale machine learning training and inference. Furthermore, by making deep learning computations faster and more accessible, it could expand the reach and applications of advanced AI technologies.
