# AeroMap3D: Anchoring Monocular UAV 6-DoF Localization to Visual-Geometric-Semantic Map Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> A monocular 6-DoF UAV localization system leverages public visual-geometric-semantic map priors, achieving 100% localization success with a 5.88 m mean 3D error over 55 km of flight in GNSS-denied urban environments. The system effectively overcomes cross-view discrepancies between UAV imagery and satellite maps and addresses structural inconsistencies arising from bare-earth Digital Elevation Models.

## Problem

- UAV localization in GNSS-denied environments is challenging due to accumulated drift from visual odometry and SLAM's lack of a persistent world-frame reference.
- Existing map-referenced localization methods are constrained by database sampling density or require extensive, resource-intensive site-specific 3D map construction.
- Using widely available public geospatial data introduces issues: large cross-view discrepancies between UAV images and satellite maps, and structural inconsistencies when lifting 2D matches on elevated structures to 3D with bare-earth Digital Elevation Models (DEMs).

## Method

- A lightweight, synthetic-trained ==Siamese CNN== adapter normalizes scale and yaw differences between UAV imagery and north-up satellite maps, enabling robust ==dense matching==.
- ==OpenStreetMap (OSM)== building footprints are used for ==semantic filtering==, rejecting matches on elevated structures before lifting 2D correspondences to 3D via bare-earth ==Digital Elevation Models (DEMs)==.
- A ==delayed-state Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)== fuses the discrete, map-anchored 6-DoF pose measurements with relative motion priors for continuous, drift-bounded trajectory estimation.

## Results

- The scale-yaw adapter significantly improved dense matcher performance, increasing the geometric-verification pass rate for RoMav2 from **62.4%** to **99.2%** on real UAV data.
- AeroMap3D achieved a **95.69%** single-frame localization success rate (**14.11 m** mean 3D error), and with the full PnP+EKF pipeline, **100%** continuous localization success (within **50 m**) at **5.88 m** mean 3D error (**11.25 m** P95) over **55 km** of flight.
- The UAV-Terra3D benchmark dataset was introduced, comprising **20 UAV trajectories** over **22.4 km^2** with co-registered public map layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient geometric normalization with a lightweight, synthetic-trained adapter can effectively bridge significant cross-view discrepancies for robust image matching.
> - Semantic information from public sources like OpenStreetMap is essential to mitigate systematic height biases when using bare-earth Digital Elevation Models for 3D lifting in urban environments.
> - Fusing discrete map-anchored metric poses with relative motion priors using an EKF provides a robust and continuous global localization solution, effectively bounding long-range drift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in AeroMap3D addresses challenges in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) navigation, specifically in environments where Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) signals are unavailable or unreliable (GNSS-denied scenarios). Current approaches to UAV localization typically rely on visual odometry (VO), which provides relative motion but accumulates drift over time, or Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), which reduces drift through loop closures within a locally constructed map. While these methods are effective for local consistency, they lack a persistent world-frame reference for long-duration trajectories, leading to metric drift.

To address this, geo-referenced imagery and elevation data offer an external anchor. However, existing map-referenced localization systems encounter specific limitations:
1. **Retrieval-based Localization:** Approaches like NetVLAD or those using vision foundation models match UAV images to geo-tagged satellite tiles for large-scale localization. While scalable, their accuracy is constrained by the sampling density of the reference database. Some methods also require offline pose-sampled RGB-D renderings, limiting their spatial scalability and rapid deployment.
2. **Metric 6-DoF Pose Estimation:** Methods that estimate 6-Degrees of Freedom (6-DoF) pose using Perspective-n-Point (PnP) over 2D-3D correspondences require explicit map geometry (elevation or depth). This geometry is often sourced from site surveys, custom 3D reconstructions, textured 3D models, or orthophoto-Digital Surface Model (DSM) pairs. Such high-fidelity map representations are accurate but demand substantial offline construction and specific data acquisition, which can restrict their deployability in previously unmapped or rapidly changing regions.

AeroMap3D identifies a gap in the landscape: the potential for accurate UAV localization using widely available public geospatial layers, such as satellite imagery, bare-earth Digital Elevation Models (DEMs), and OpenStreetMap (OSM) data. Leveraging such public data avoids the resource-intensive process of creating site-specific 3D reconstructions. However, this approach introduces two primary challenges that AeroMap3D specifically aims to resolve:
1. **Cross-view Discrepancy:** UAV imagery captured from varying altitudes, camera fields of view, and headings exhibits significant scale and yaw differences compared to north-up satellite maps. These geometric misalignments pose difficulties for modern dense matchers, which are not inherently designed for such large cross-view variations.
2. **Structural Inconsistency with Bare-Earth DEMs:** Bare-earth DEMs model only terrain height. When matching UAV image features on elevated structures (e.g., building roofs or facades) to a map pixel and then lifting this 2D correspondence to a 3D point using a bare-earth DEM, the resulting 3D point will have an incorrect, terrain-level elevation. These systematic height biases can propagate through the PnP solver and potentially form coherent "inlier" sets that RANSAC alone may fail to reject, leading to erroneous pose estimates.

By tackling these challenges, AeroMap3D seeks to provide a practical and deployable solution for robust UAV localization in GNSS-denied urban environments, leveraging existing public data infrastructure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AeroMap3D is to develop a monocular 6-DoF UAV localization system that can anchor onboard imagery to public visual, geometric, and semantic map priors, enabling navigation in GNSS-denied environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Leverage Widely Available Public Geospatial Data:** A core motivation is to move away from site-specific, high-fidelity 3D map requirements that necessitate extensive surveys, region-specific data acquisition, and offline construction. AeroMap3D aims to achieve accurate localization by utilizing readily accessible public data sources: geo-referenced satellite imagery (e.g., NAIP), bare-earth Digital Elevation Models (e.g., USGS 3DEP), and semantic annotations from OpenStreetMap (OSM). This approach reduces deployment costs and time, enhancing practical applicability in unexplored areas.

2. **Overcome Cross-View Discrepancy between UAV and Satellite Imagery:** UAV images often exhibit substantial differences in scale, orientation (yaw), and perspective compared to north-up satellite maps due to variations in flight altitude, camera field of view (FOV), and UAV heading. These discrepancies are challenging for many vision-based matchers. A key objective is to normalize these dominant geometric misalignments efficiently, enabling existing dense matchers, pre-trained on generic data, to reliably establish correspondences without requiring finetuning or relying on potentially inaccurate magnetometer readings for yaw.

3. **Address Structural Inconsistency of Bare-Earth DEMs in Urban Scenes:** Bare-earth DEMs are cost-effective and widely available but only represent ground elevation. In urban environments, UAVs frequently observe elevated structures like buildings. When 2D-2D matches are established between a UAV image and a map, and then lifted to 3D using a bare-earth DEM, any match on a building results in a 2D-3D correspondence with an incorrect Z-coordinate. This introduces a systematic height bias that can mislead standard PnP algorithms, even with RANSAC-based outlier rejection. A critical objective is to integrate semantic information from OSM to identify and filter out these structurally inconsistent matches before 3D lifting and pose estimation, thereby improving the geometric integrity of the correspondences.

4. **Enable Continuous, Drift-Tolerant Trajectory Estimation:** While visual odometry (VO) provides relative motion, it inevitably accumulates drift over long trajectories. The system aims to provide a persistent, metrically anchored world-frame reference. This is achieved by fusing the discrete, map-anchored 6-DoF pose measurements (derived from the PnP stage) with relative motion priors using a delayed-state Extended Kalman Filter (EKF). The objective is to produce a continuous, accurate, and globally consistent UAV trajectory estimate, bounding the accumulated drift.

5. **Demonstrate Robustness and Generalization:** A further motivation is to demonstrate the system's ability to generalize without extensive site-specific tuning or retraining. This includes showing that the scale-yaw adapter, trained purely on synthetic data, can transfer effectively to real UAV imagery from a different geographic region, and that the combined system performs robustly across diverse flight trajectories and urban scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AeroMap3D is a monocular UAV localization framework designed to derive 6-DoF pose estimates by anchoring onboard imagery to public visual, geometric, and semantic map priors. The system operates in several stages:

1. **Initialization:** The system assumes a coarse initial map pose, which can be obtained through methods like image retrieval or manual operator input.

2. **Cross-View Registration via Lightweight Adapter:**
 - **Scale-Yaw Normalization:** This component addresses the geometric discrepancies between UAV imagery and satellite maps. Instead of modifying or retraining a dense matcher, a lightweight Siamese Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) adapter is employed. This adapter uses a shared MobileNetV3-Small encoder for both the UAV image ($I_t$) and the north-up satellite tile ($I_{sat}$). Global-average-pooled descriptors ($g_t$, $g_{sat}$) are concatenated and further processed by a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The MLP predicts a log-scale ratio and a normalized yaw vector, which are then converted into a scale factor ($\hat{s}_t$) and a yaw offset ($\hat{\theta}_t$).
 - **Training:** The adapter is trained exclusively on synthetic UAV-map pairs. These pairs are generated by sampling plausible flight states, projecting camera footprints onto satellite imagery (from a different dataset, UAV-VisLoc), and rendering UAV-like views with known ground-truth scale and yaw. This synthetic training avoids the need for real UAV imagery, manual labels, or site-specific tuning.
 - **Dense Matching:** After the scale and yaw rectification by the adapter, a pre-trained dense matcher (e.g., RoMav2) is applied to the normalized UAV image and the rectified map tile. This process generates 2D-2D correspondences between UAV image pixels and map pixels ($C_{t}^{2D} = \{(x_i^t, m_i^t)\}_{i=1}^{N_t}$).

3. **Semantic Map Conditioning for DEM-PnP:**
 - **Problem:** Bare-earth DEMs provide terrain height but are inaccurate for elevated structures (buildings, bridges), leading to geometrically biased 2D-3D correspondences if matches occur on these structures.
 - **Semantic Filtering:** To counteract this, OpenStreetMap (OSM) building footprints are rasterized into a binary mask ($B$) within the map plane. Candidate 2D-2D matches are filtered by rejecting any map pixel ($m_i^t$) that falls within an OSM-annotated building region ($m_i^t \in B$). This produces a set of semantically reliable ground-level matches ($C_{t}^{gnd}$).
 - **3D Lifting:** Only the surviving map pixels from $C_{t}^{gnd}$ are then lifted to 3D points ($X_i$) using the bare-earth DEM elevation field ($h_{DEM}$). This creates a set of terrain-supported 2D-3D correspondences.
 - **Geometric Consensus (RANSAC-PnP):** Standard OpenCV RANSAC-PnP is applied to these terrain-supported 2D-3D correspondences to estimate the camera's 6-DoF pose ($\hat{T}_{t}^{CW}$). RANSAC is used to handle residual outliers that may arise from missing OSM annotations, off-nadir building lean, dynamic objects, or dense matcher inaccuracies. The resulting metric pose is then converted to the UAV body frame ($\hat{T}_{t}^{WB}$) for subsequent fusion.

4. **Continuous Trajectory Estimation via Map-Anchored EKF:**
 - **EKF State:** An Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) is employed to fuse the discrete, map-anchored 6-DoF pose measurements with relative motion priors. The EKF maintains a nominal state representing the UAV body pose in the world frame ($\hat{T}_k \in SE(3)$) and a local error state ($\delta\xi_k \in \mathbb{R}^6$) with an associated covariance matrix ($P_k$).
 - **Motion Prior:** Between map updates, the EKF propagates the state using relative pose increments ($\tilde{\Delta}T_k$). In the paper's experimental setup, this prior is derived from consecutive GNSS reference poses, perturbed with zero-mean noise proportional to translation and rotation, simulating realistic odometry uncertainty.
 - **Delayed Updates:** Visual pose measurements from PnP are timestamped at image acquisition. The EKF is designed to handle delayed measurements; a measurement obtained at time $k$ but processed at time $j \ge k$ updates the buffered state at $k$, and subsequent stored motion increments re-propagate the estimate to $j$.
 - **Mahalanobis Gating:** Each visual pose measurement is evaluated for consistency against the predicted state using its Mahalanobis distance ($\gamma_k$). A $\chi^2$ gate filters out measurements that are statistically inconsistent, preventing corrupt updates.
 - **EKF Update:** Accepted measurements are used to perform a standard EKF update, correcting the pose estimate and reducing uncertainty.

5. **UAV-Terra3D Benchmark:**
 - The authors also introduce UAV-Terra3D, a new public benchmark dataset. It comprises 20 continuous UAV trajectories (55 km of flight over 22.4 km$^2$) collected at eight sites in Austin, Texas. The dataset includes 30-Hz monocular UAV video (mostly nadir-view), calibrated camera intrinsics, GNSS reference trajectories, and co-registered public map layers: NAIP satellite imagery, USGS bare-earth DEMs (1-meter resolution, converted to ellipsoidal height), and OpenStreetMap semantic annotations. This dataset facilitates the evaluation of terrain-lifted 6-DoF localization, particularly concerning semantic validity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated AeroMap3D across four key questions, yielding the following main findings:

1. **Cross-Dataset Registration Performance and Adapter Effectiveness (Q1 & Q2):**
 - **Challenge with Direct Matching:** Without the scale-yaw adapter, pre-trained dense matchers (e.g., SIFT+LightGlue, RoMav2) exhibited limited performance when directly applied to real UAV-Terra3D imagery, achieving geometric-verification pass rates of 35.1% and 62.4% respectively. This indicates their difficulty in handling the significant scale, orientation, and appearance differences between synthetic training data and real cross-view UAV-map pairs.
 - **Adapter's Impact:** The lightweight scale-yaw adapter, trained solely on synthetic UAV-VisLoc data (from China) and evaluated on UAV-Terra3D (from the US) without any target-dataset specific training, substantially improved registration.
 - Adapter+RoMav2 achieved a pass rate of 99.2%, representing a 36.8-point increase.
 - Adapter+Tiny-RoMa increased its pass rate from 42.1% to 92.5%, a 50.4-point gain.
 - **Runtime:** The adapter introduced an overhead of approximately 35 ms. On an RTX 5090, Adapter+Tiny-RoMa provided a total runtime of 18 ms (adapter + matcher). For edge deployment on a Jetson Orin Nano, Adapter+Tiny-RoMa achieved 92.5% success within 130 ms, while RoMav2 alone exceeded the device's memory capacity.
 - **Conclusion:** The adapter effectively normalizes the dominant scale and yaw mismatches, enabling pre-trained dense matchers to reliably establish correspondences across different datasets and real-world conditions.

2. **Single-Frame Camera Pose Estimation (Q3):**
 - **Retrieval-based Baselines:** DINOv2-based retrieval and SelaVPR++ achieved low success rates (16.47% and 19.35%, respectively, for error below 50 m) with mean 3D errors around 29 m. This indicates that retrieval provides only coarse localization.
 - **Planar and Sparse PnP:** A planar 2D map homography achieved 62.20% success but retained a high mean error (30.80 m), demonstrating the inadequacy of a 2D assumption for 3D localization. Sparse DEM-lifted PnP from four UAV-footprint corners showed a mean error of 23.90 m but a low success rate (44.70%) due to inconsistent 3D points on elevated structures.
 - **Dense DEM-PnP (Ablation without OSM):** Without semantic filtering from OSM, dense DEM-lifted RANSAC-PnP achieved an 88.24% success rate and a mean 3D error of 16.17 m.
 - **AeroMap3D (Dense DEM-PnP + OSM):** The inclusion of OSM-based semantic filtering before 3D lifting significantly improved performance. AeroMap3D achieved a 95.69% success rate (for error below 50 m) and reduced the mean 3D error to 14.11 m, with a 95th-percentile error of 28.20 m.
 - **Conclusion:** Semantic conditioning with OSM prior to DEM lifting and RANSAC-PnP is crucial for robust single-frame 6-DoF pose estimation in urban environments, specifically by preventing geometrically biased correspondences from elevated structures.

3. **Continuous Trajectory Estimation (Q4):**
 - **Unanchored Methods:** Methods relying solely on motion propagation (Ref-Odom) or monocular visual odometry (ORB-VO, ORB-SLAM2) suffered from substantial drift. Ref-Odom achieved 14.90% success, ORB-VO 45.61% (mean error 28.24 m), and ORB-SLAM2 41.44% (mean error 14.71 m). The Relative Pose Error (RPE) analysis showed these methods accumulating significant drift over longer segments.
 - **GeoVINS-style Retrieval:** A DINOv2-based GeoVINS-style retrieval approach, which incorporates coarse map updates, achieved 100% success with a mean 3D error of 17.17 m. This demonstrated the benefit of map anchoring over unanchored methods.
 - **AeroMap3D (PnP+EKF):** By fusing map-based metric PnP updates with a reference-derived stochastic motion prior via the EKF, AeroMap3D achieved 100% localization success (within 50 m) with a mean 3D error of 5.88 m and a 95th-percentile error of 11.25 m over 55 km of flight. RPE analysis indicated that AeroMap3D remained below 6 m RPE even at 2 km segment lengths.
 - **Conclusion:** Fusing precise metric PnP-derived pose estimates with relative motion priors via an EKF effectively bounds long-range drift and provides accurate, continuous UAV localization, outperforming retrieval-based global anchoring methods.

4. **UAV-Terra3D Benchmark:** The release of the UAV-Terra3D dataset (55 km of flight, 22.4 km$^2$ mapped area, calibrated UAV imagery, reference trajectories, and aligned public map layers) provides a resource for future research in map-anchored UAV localization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AeroMap3D contributes to the field of autonomous UAV navigation by addressing practical limitations in GNSS-denied environments. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Democratization of UAV Localization:** By demonstrating that accurate 6-DoF UAV localization can be achieved using only widely available public geospatial data (satellite imagery, bare-earth DEMs, OpenStreetMap), AeroMap3D eliminates the need for expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive site surveys or the creation of specialized 3D textured maps. This lowers the barrier to entry for deploying autonomous UAVs in new or rapidly changing environments.

2. **Enhanced Rapid Deployment and Generalization:** The reliance on generic public data and the demonstrated cross-dataset generalization capability of the scale-yaw adapter imply that the system can be rapidly deployed in previously unexplored urban areas without requiring prior specific data acquisition or extensive finetuning for new target regions. This is particularly impactful for applications requiring on-demand or emergent operations.

3. **Improved Robustness of Geometric Pose Estimation:** The integration of semantic information from OpenStreetMap to filter geometrically inconsistent matches on elevated structures before DEM lifting addresses a fundamental challenge of using bare-earth DEMs in urban settings. This semantic conditioning leads to more reliable 2D-3D correspondences for PnP, significantly improving single-frame localization accuracy and success rates compared to purely geometric approaches. This methodological advancement can extend to other PnP-based localization systems that utilize imperfect 3D models.

4. **Practicality for Edge Computing and Onboard Deployment:** The lightweight design of the scale-yaw adapter allows for efficient execution, even on constrained edge computing platforms like the Jetson Orin Nano. This makes the system viable for real-time onboard deployment on UAVs, which often have strict size, weight, and power (SWaP) constraints.

5. **Advancement in Cross-View Matching:** The scale-yaw adapter offers a practical and effective solution to a persistent challenge in cross-view image matching: bridging large geometric differences (scale and yaw) between drone-level imagery and overhead maps. This technique could be applied to other domains facing similar cross-view matching problems.

6. **Contribution to the Research Community:** The release of the UAV-Terra3D benchmark dataset provides a valuable, standardized resource for researchers. This dataset, with its extensive coverage, calibrated imagery, reference trajectories, and aligned public map priors (visual, geometric, semantic), facilitates comparative evaluation and further development of map-anchored UAV localization algorithms, particularly those leveraging terrain geometry and semantic information.

In conclusion, AeroMap3D represents a step towards more practical, scalable, and robust UAV navigation in GNSS-denied environments, primarily by creatively leveraging existing public data infrastructure and addressing key challenges in cross-view perception and 3D geometric consistency.
