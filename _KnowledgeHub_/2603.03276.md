# Beyond Language Modeling: An Exploration of Multimodal Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR, Meta, and NYU developed a unified autoregressive Transformer framework, Transfusion, to explore native multimodal pretraining from scratch. This approach reveals that Representation Autoencoders (RAEs) effectively unify visual understanding and generation, enables the emergence of world modeling from general data, and scales efficiently with Mixture-of-Experts architectures.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are limited by their sole reliance on text, often described as a 'lossy compression of reality,' which hinders true world understanding.
- The design space for natively multimodal models trained from scratch is opaque, with existing approaches often adapting language models or using separate visual representations for different tasks.
- There is a lack of systematic, controlled experiments to isolate factors influencing multimodal capabilities and scaling properties without confounding effects from prior language-centric pretraining.

## Method

- Develops **Transfusion**, a single, ==autoregressive, decoder-only Transformer== backbone, pretrained from scratch with a ==unified objective== for both language and vision.
- Explores ==Representation Autoencoders (RAEs)== combined with ==frozen semantic visual encoders== to create a ==unified visual representation== suitable for both understanding and generation tasks.
- Systematically investigates the impact of diverse data sources, architectural choices (such as ==Mixture-of-Experts==), and ==scaling properties== through controlled experiments on multimodal performance.

## Results

- RAE-based visual representations leveraging semantic encoders consistently surpass VAE-based encoders across both visual understanding (VQA) and generation benchmarks.
- Multimodal co-training with diverse visual and language data yields synergistic improvements, with general pretraining outperforming scaling task-specific data for VQA, and raw video driving world modeling capabilities that transfer with minimal in-domain alignment.
- Mixture-of-Experts architectures demonstrate superior performance and efficiency in multimodal scaling compared to dense models, fostering natural modality specialization and harmonizing the identified scaling asymmetry where vision is more data-hungry than language. Systematic controlled experiments across multiple model scales confirm consistent trends.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representation Autoencoders (RAE) using semantic encoders provide an optimal unified visual representation, excelling in both visual understanding and generation, simplifying multimodal architectures.
> - Diverse visual and language data are complementary, with multimodal co-training yielding synergy that enhances downstream capabilities like VQA and naturally leads to world modeling.
> - Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures enable efficient and effective multimodal scaling, naturally inducing modality specialization and harmonizing the scaling asymmetry between vision and language.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself directly at the forefront of the evolution of foundation models, moving beyond the paradigm dominated by large language models (LLMs). The introduction explicitly frames LLMs as having "mastered the description of shadows on the wall without ever seeing the objects casting them," a poignant reference to Plato's Cave allegory, highlighting their inherent limitation due to reliance solely on text, a "lossy compression of reality." This perspective underscores a critical philosophical and pragmatic shift: to achieve more robust and intelligent AI, models must engage directly with the "raw dynamics of reality," particularly through the visual world.

Currently, the landscape of multimodal AI is characterized by several approaches:
- **LLM-centric multimodal models:** Many existing multimodal models primarily adapt pretrained language models to incorporate visual inputs, often relying on language pretraining as a starting point. This "paradigm prioritizes preserving existing language capabilities while adapting the model to become multimodal." The paper argues this approach confounds understanding of what is truly learned from multimodal training versus what is inherited from the language backbone.
- **Dual-representation models:** For tasks like visual generation and understanding, it has been common to use separate visual representations (e.g., VAEs for generation, semantic encoders for understanding), complicating model design.
- **Limited "from-scratch" exploration:** While interest in multimodal models is growing, the design space for *natively* multimodal models trained from scratch is "largely opaque," lacking systematic, controlled experiments to isolate influencing factors.

This work directly addresses these gaps by:
- **Focusing on from-scratch pretraining:** This is a key differentiator, aiming to disentangle the fundamental dynamics and scaling relationships of vision and language without the interference of inherited knowledge from pretrained LLMs. This allows for a clearer understanding of how multimodal capabilities truly emerge.
- **Systematically exploring the design space:** The paper conducts controlled experiments across crucial axes like visual representations, data composition, architecture, and scaling properties.
- **Advancing unified models:** By proposing a single, unified visual representation (RAE) for both understanding and generation and demonstrating its effectiveness, the paper simplifies multimodal architecture design.
- **Investigating Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for multimodality:** MoE has shown great promise in scaling LLMs, and this paper extends its application to the multimodal domain, studying how it can efficiently handle the diverse computational demands of different modalities.
- **Exploring world modeling emergence:** The work directly links general multimodal pretraining to the emergence of world modeling capabilities, moving towards Y. LeCun's vision of AI systems that build internal models of the physical world.

By providing empirical clarity through controlled experiments, this research offers foundational insights into how to build truly unified and natively multimodal foundation models, charting a path "beyond the shadows to model the source directly."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged limitations of language-only foundation models and the approaching "pragmatic ceiling" of high-quality text data exhaustion. The authors posit that the "visual world offers a critical axis for advancing foundation models beyond language," capturing the "raw dynamics of reality that language misses."

The overarching objective is to **provide empirical clarity to the opaque design space of native multimodal models** by conducting controlled, from-scratch pretraining experiments. This involves isolating factors that govern multimodal pretraining, ensuring that insights are derived from the unified training process itself, rather than being confounded by prior language-centric pretraining.

Specifically, the paper identifies and investigates five key axes:

1. **Visual Representation:** To determine the most effective visual representation for a unified model that can handle both understanding and generation tasks. This challenges the common assumption that separate representations are necessary.
2. **Data:** To understand how diverse data types (text, video, image-text pairs, action-conditioned video) contribute to or interfere with overall model performance and to identify synergistic effects.
3. **World Modeling:** To explore if physical prediction and planning capabilities can emerge naturally from general multimodal pretraining, even with minimal domain-specific data, by representing actions as text tokens.
4. **Architecture:** To study the effectiveness of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures in a unified multimodal setting for efficient and effective scaling, and to analyze how modality specialization emerges.
5. **Scaling Properties:** To derive scaling laws for both vision and language within unified pretraining, uncover any scaling asymmetries, and determine how architectural choices like MoE can harmonize these differences.

The ultimate goal is to pave the way for "truly unified multimodal models" that are not merely language models with tacked-on vision but are inherently capable of understanding and generating across modalities with a deep grasp of the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around training a **single, autoregressive, decoder-only Transformer backbone from scratch** using a unified framework called **Transfusion**. This framework is designed to handle both language and vision as first-class citizens, predicting both "next text token" and "next visual state."

Key components of the methodology include:

- **Unified Model and Tokenization:**
 - **Text:** Standard BPE tokenizer from LLaMA-3 is used, with next-token prediction as the objective.
 - **Vision:** A **frozen visual encoder** maps images or video frames to latent tokens. The paper explores a range of encoders:
 - **VAEs:** From Stable Diffusion (SD-VAE) and FLUX.1, traditionally used for generation.
 - **Semantic Encoders:** Such as SigLIP 2 So400m (language-supervised), DINOv2-L, and WebSSL-L (self-supervised), traditionally used for understanding.
 - **Raw Pixels:** Direct patchification of image pixels.
 - **Representation Autoencoders (RAE):** The paper leverages recent work on RAEs, which enable diffusion models to operate effectively in high-dimensional semantic latent spaces, challenging the VAE-necessity assumption.
 - **Simplified Projections:** Unlike some Transfusion variants, this work uses simple linear projection layers instead of U-Nets for visual input/output, promoting a more direct mapping into the Transformer's latent space.

- **Training and Inference Protocols:**
 - **Language Modeling:** Standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss.
 - **Image-wise Flow Matching for Visual Tokens:** A diffusion-based objective is used, where the model predicts a velocity field (v-pred) or directly the clean latent (x-pred) to denoise interpolated latents. This operates frame-by-frame for videos.
 - **Mixture Training:** Batches contain diverse examples from multiple sources, and a weighted combination of language modeling and flow matching objectives is optimized.
 - **Causal Vision and Language Masking:** A hybrid attention masking strategy is employed via FlexAttention. Language uses a standard causal mask, while visual data uses a block-wise causal mask where tokens within a frame attend bidirectionally, but only causally to previous tokens/frames.
 - **Inference:** Standard autoregressive next-token prediction for text; 25-step Euler sampler for visual denoising, with classifier-free guidance.

- **Diverse Data Sources:** The models are pretrained on a rich mixture of data:
 - Large-scale web text (DCLM).
 - Raw videos (YouTube, curated datasets like Kinetics, SomethingSomething V2) at 1 FPS.
 - Paired image-text data (MetaCLIP, Shutterstock).
 - Action-conditioned navigation trajectories (NWM, in-house annotated video data), formatted as `Image + Text (action) -> Image (next state)`.

- **Evaluation Metrics:** A comprehensive set of metrics is used to assess performance across modalities:
 - **Language:** Text perplexity (PPL) on DCLM validation and an in-house "Notes" corpus.
 - **Vision Generation:** Diffusion loss on CC12M validation, image generation scores on DPGBench and GenEval.
 - **Vision Understanding:** Average VQA accuracy on 16 Cambrian evaluation benchmarks (after 1 epoch of finetuning on Cambrian-7M).
 - **World Modeling:** Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) using zero-shot planning via the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) on the NWM setting.
 - **Knowledge-informed Generation:** WISE benchmark for factual understanding.
 - **Efficiency and Scaling:** IsoFLOP analysis for deriving scaling laws for parameters and tokens.

- **Architectural Exploration:**
 - **Modality-specific FFNs:** Initial exploration compares shared vs. modality-specific FFNs within the Transformer blocks.
 - **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):** Extensive study on MoE design space, including granularity (number of experts), sparsity (total vs. active experts), and shared/modality-specific expert designs.

The *controlled from-scratch* nature of the experiments, combined with the comprehensive evaluation, allows the authors to draw fundamental conclusions about the interplay between design choices and multimodal capabilities without confounding factors from inherited knowledge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper distills its extensive experimental findings into four key insights, supported by detailed empirical results:

**(i) Representation Autoencoder (RAE) Provides an Optimal Unified Visual Representation by Excelling at Both Visual Understanding and Generation.**
- The research challenges the conventional wisdom that VAEs are necessary for visual generation and semantic encoders for understanding.
- **Semantic encoders (e.g., SigLIP 2)**, when combined with RAE-style diffusion (high-dimensional latent spaces), consistently **outperform VAE-based encoders** (SD-VAE, FLUX.1) on *both* visual understanding (VQA) and generation (DPGBench, GenEval) benchmarks.
- This suggests a **single RAE-based encoder is sufficient** for unified multimodal models, simplifying architecture and improving performance across tasks.
- While raw pixels show competitive understanding performance, their generation quality still lags.

**(ii) Visual and Language Data Are Complementary and Yield Synergy for Downstream Capabilities.**
- Contrary to concerns about modality competition, adding diverse visual data (especially raw video) to text pretraining **minimally impacts or even slightly benefits text perplexity**. Image captions, however, can introduce slight degradation due to distributional shifts.
- **Image-text (I/T) data is essential** for acquiring visual generation and understanding capabilities. Different I/T sources (e.g., synthetic recaptions vs. high-aesthetic images) have complementary strengths, suggesting strategic data mixing.
- **Multimodal co-training yields positive synergy:** Adding text tokens consistently improves diffusion loss and image generation quality for a fixed vision budget. More importantly, general multimodal pretraining (with text, video, image-text) significantly **outperforms scaling task-specific data** for VQA, demonstrating a stronger foundation.

**(iii) Unified Multimodal Pretraining Leads Naturally to World Modeling, with Capabilities Emerging from General Training.**
- The model demonstrates **world modeling capabilities** (predicting future visual states conditioned on actions) without architectural modifications, by simply formatting navigation actions as standard text tokens.
- **Unsupervised video data** is a primary driver for world modeling performance, with general multimodal pretraining outperforming scaling domain-specific (NWM) data alone.
- World modeling capabilities **transfer with minimal alignment**, saturating at as little as 1% in-domain data, indicating that the core ability is largely acquired from general pretraining.
- Qualitative rollouts show the model can generate coherent trajectories and respond to **free-form natural language commands** as actions (e.g., "go closer to the house"), demonstrating emergent semantic understanding transfer.

**(iv) Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Enables Efficient and Effective Multimodal Scaling While Naturally Inducing Modality Specialization.**
- **MoE architectures are highly effective** for unified multimodal pretraining, outperforming simpler modality-specific FFNs and Mixture of Transformers (MoT).
- **High granularity (many smaller experts)** is critical for performance, with optimal granularities differing slightly for vision and language. The choice of diffusion prediction target (x-pred vs. v-pred) also depends on the visual representation, with x-pred benefiting RAE.
- **Increased sparsity** (more total experts at fixed active compute) consistently improves both language and vision performance, confirming MoE's scalability in this setting. Semantic representations (RAE) benefit more from sparsity than VAEs.
- **Natural modality specialization emerges:** The model automatically allocates more experts to language in early layers and more vision/multimodal experts in later layers, suggesting a "separate-then-integrate" processing strategy. Vision experts are largely general-purpose, processing tokens similarly across different diffusion timesteps and even sharing experts for both visual understanding and generation.

**Scaling Asymmetry:**
- **IsoFLOP analysis reveals a scaling asymmetry:** In dense models, language exhibits a balanced parameter-data trade-off (similar to Chinchilla), while **vision is significantly more data-hungry** (requiring more data relative to model size).
- **MoE harmonizes this asymmetry:** By providing high model capacity (total parameters) while accommodating the data-intensive nature of vision, MoE shifts language scaling towards a more data-hungry regime, effectively narrowing the gap in scaling exponents between modalities. MoE multimodal models also closely track unimodal MoE baselines in compute efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the development of truly unified multimodal foundation models, challenging several prevailing assumptions and offering clear guidance for future directions. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Multimodal AI:** By demonstrating the effectiveness of *from-scratch* multimodal pretraining, the paper provides a strong argument against the continued reliance on pretrained LLMs as the primary backbone for multimodal systems. This encourages the development of natively multimodal architectures that are not constrained by language-first biases, potentially leading to more robust and generalized AI.
- **Simplified and More Capable Architectures:** The finding that a single RAE-based visual representation can effectively handle both understanding and generation tasks simplifies multimodal model design significantly. This reduces architectural complexity and overhead, potentially making these models more efficient to train and deploy, while simultaneously enhancing their capabilities.
- **Unlocking True World Understanding:** The emergence of world modeling capabilities from general multimodal pretraining is a profound insight. It suggests that AI systems can learn a robust internal model of physical reality simply by observing how the world looks and moves, without needing extensive domain-specific engineering or data. This aligns with Yann LeCun's vision for "System 2" AI behaviors grounded in predictive models of the world. The ability to control these models with natural language further bridges the gap between high-level human intent and low-level physical interaction.
- **Efficient Scaling with MoE:** The systematic exploration of MoE architectures for multimodal settings, coupled with the revelation of scaling asymmetry between vision and language, provides crucial guidance for building larger, more capable multimodal models. MoE not only offers computational efficiency but also provides a structural solution to balance the divergent resource demands of different modalities. This will be critical as models continue to scale to hundreds of billions or even trillions of parameters.
- **Refined Understanding of Modality Interaction:** The paper demonstrates that "modality competition" is not an inherent flaw but a symptom of specific design choices and data distributions. By showing that raw visual data can be complementary to language, and that architectural separation (like MoE) can mitigate interference, the research encourages a more integrated approach to multimodal data utilization, opening up vast, untapped data sources like unlabeled video.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper implicitly or explicitly points to several exciting avenues for future work, including:
 - Developing even better generation-aware semantic visual representations to close the remaining gap with VAEs in fine-grained reconstruction.
 - Incorporating interleaved multimodal data to better capture real-world human-computer interaction patterns.
 - Exploring more advanced MoE designs, potentially addressing current hardware utilization bottlenecks.
 - Further investigation into the blurring boundary between multimodal models and world models, leveraging multimodal outputs for advanced reasoning and embodied AI.

In essence, this work provides a clearer, empirically-grounded blueprint for constructing the next generation of foundation models that can move "beyond language modeling" to genuinely understand and interact with the physical world in a unified and efficient manner.
