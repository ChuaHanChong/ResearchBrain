# Beyond Language Modeling: An Exploration of Multimodal Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR, Meta, and NYU developed a unified autoregressive Transformer framework, Transfusion, to explore native multimodal pretraining from scratch. This approach reveals that Representation Autoencoders (RAEs) effectively unify visual understanding and generation, enables the emergence of world modeling from general data, and scales efficiently with Mixture-of-Experts architectures.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are limited by their sole reliance on text, often described as a 'lossy compression of reality,' which hinders true world understanding.
- The design space for natively multimodal models trained from scratch is opaque, with existing approaches often adapting language models or using separate visual representations for different tasks.
- There is a lack of systematic, controlled experiments to isolate factors influencing multimodal capabilities and scaling properties without confounding effects from prior language-centric pretraining.

## Method

- Develops **Transfusion**, a single, ==autoregressive, decoder-only Transformer== backbone, pretrained from scratch with a ==unified objective== for both language and vision.
- Explores ==Representation Autoencoders (RAEs)== combined with ==frozen semantic visual encoders== to create a ==unified visual representation== suitable for both understanding and generation tasks.
- Systematically investigates the impact of diverse data sources, architectural choices (such as ==Mixture-of-Experts==), and ==scaling properties== through controlled experiments on multimodal performance.

## Results

- RAE-based visual representations leveraging semantic encoders consistently surpass VAE-based encoders across both visual understanding (VQA) and generation benchmarks.
- Multimodal co-training with diverse visual and language data yields synergistic improvements, with general pretraining outperforming scaling task-specific data for VQA, and raw video driving world modeling capabilities that transfer with minimal in-domain alignment.
- Mixture-of-Experts architectures demonstrate superior performance and efficiency in multimodal scaling compared to dense models, fostering natural modality specialization and harmonizing the identified scaling asymmetry where vision is more data-hungry than language. Systematic controlled experiments across multiple model scales confirm consistent trends.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representation Autoencoders (RAE) using semantic encoders provide an optimal unified visual representation, excelling in both visual understanding and generation, simplifying multimodal architectures.
> - Diverse visual and language data are complementary, with multimodal co-training yielding synergy that enhances downstream capabilities like VQA and naturally leads to world modeling.
> - Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures enable efficient and effective multimodal scaling, naturally inducing modality specialization and harmonizing the scaling asymmetry between vision and language.
