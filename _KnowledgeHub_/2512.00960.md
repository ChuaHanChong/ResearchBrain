# Efficient and Scalable Monocular Human-Object Interaction Motion Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborators developed an efficient framework for reconstructing 4D human-object interaction (HOI) motion from monocular videos, including a human-in-the-loop annotation engine and a two-stage optimization solver. This approach facilitated the creation of Open4DHOI, a diverse dataset comprising 131,000 frames, and enabled robust robotic imitation learning from the reconstructed motions.

## Problem

- Traditional multi-sensor systems for 4D HOI data capture are costly and lack the scalability and diversity needed for generalized, in-the-wild interactions.
- Existing monocular HOI reconstruction methods often yield physically inaccurate contacts and lack spatio-temporal consistency, while manual frame-by-frame annotation is prohibitively time-consuming.
- There is a critical need for large-scale, diverse, and temporally coherent 4D HOI datasets derived from unconstrained environments to train robust AI models.

## Method

- Developed a human-in-the-loop (HITL) data engine with a multi-modal contact predictor (InterPoint) for efficient sparse, temporally invariant interaction point annotation in monocular videos.
- Proposed "4DHOISolver," a two-stage optimization framework that reconstructs high-fidelity, physically plausible, and spatio-temporally coherent 4D HOI from sparse annotations.
- Constructed and released "Open4DHOI," a large-scale 4D HOI dataset sourced from in-the-wild monocular videos, using the developed annotation and reconstruction pipeline.

## Results

- The Open4DHOI dataset contains 131,000 frames across 451 sequences, showcasing high diversity in 135 object and 133 action categories, and was constructed with an average annotation time of approximately ten minutes per video.
- The 4DHOISolver demonstrated superior object tracking accuracy, achieving a per-frame Chamfer Distance of **4.67** on the BEHAVE dataset, outperforming baselines and yielding more physically plausible and temporally consistent HOI reconstructions.
- The InterPoint model effectively predicted human contact points (F1-score of **0.523**) and interaction point pairs (Recall of **0.303**), with its performance scaling positively with increased training data, thus accelerating the annotation process.
- An RL agent trained with the reconstructed data and contact-guided rewards significantly reduced MPJPE (from 151.82mm to 125.76mm) and contact error (from 43.94mm to 26.76mm), demonstrating the data's utility for robust robotic imitation learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparse contact annotations, guided by a multi-modal contact predictor and a human-in-the-loop approach, can significantly reduce the annotation burden while maintaining accuracy for 4D HOI.
> - A two-stage optimization process, incorporating contact, collision, and mask losses, effectively regularizes the ill-posed monocular 4D HOI reconstruction, ensuring physical plausibility and temporal consistency.
> - Reconstructed 4D HOI data from monocular videos, when coupled with contact-guided reward functions, is suitable for training robust reinforcement learning agents to imitate complex human manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction of high-fidelity 4D (3D + time) human-object interaction (HOI) data is a foundational requirement for developing generalized robot systems, particularly for tasks involving dexterous manipulation and for populating virtual environments with realistic digital humans. The broader research landscape for 4D HOI data acquisition can be broadly categorized into two approaches: multi-sensor systems and monocular video-based reconstruction.

Traditional 3D/4D HOI datasets, such as BEHAVE, rely on controlled studio environments equipped with multi-view camera setups, RGB-D systems, or wearable motion capture suits. These systems typically achieve high precision in reconstruction but incur substantial costs, limiting their scalability and diversity in terms of objects, actions, and environments. Such setups are often confined to indoor settings and struggle to capture common outdoor activities or complex industrial tasks. Object tracking in these systems often necessitates labor-intensive methods like manual annotation, physical markers, or sophisticated multi-camera trackers.

More recent research efforts have shifted towards reconstructing 3D HOI from widely available monocular images and videos, seeking to leverage the abundance and diversity of in-the-wild content. While off-the-shelf pose estimators can often recover human motion from these videos, handling object motion presents a significant challenge. Previous monocular methods often struggle with physically inaccurate contacts due to limitations in automated pose and depth alignment. Solutions like Open3DHOI rely on manual object pose adjustment in tools like Blender, while PICO proposes annotating contact regions. However, these frame-by-frame annotation strategies are resource-intensive for video sequences and fundamentally lack mechanisms to enforce spatio-temporal consistency across frames. Furthermore, existing contact prediction methods often treat human and object domains in isolation or rely on computationally expensive dense contact maps.

This work positions itself to address the limitations of both established multi-sensor systems and nascent monocular approaches. It aims to bridge the gap by developing an efficient and scalable method for extracting diverse, high-fidelity 4D HOI data from monocular internet videos. By introducing a sparse contact annotation paradigm and a human-in-the-loop data engine, the paper seeks to overcome the annotation bottleneck inherent in existing monocular methods. Concurrently, it proposes an optimization framework designed to ensure spatio-temporal coherence and physical plausibility in the reconstructed HOI, aspects often compromised in previous monocular reconstructions. The development of a large-scale dataset, Open4DHOI, further contributes to the growing need for diverse HOI data, aiming to provide a resource that is both cost-effective to produce and broadly applicable for downstream tasks such as robotic imitation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable generalized robot systems to learn from diverse and large-scale human-object interactions (HOI) by developing an efficient and scalable method for reconstructing 4D HOI motion from monocular internet videos. This addresses a fundamental need for data that can provide insights into human motor skills, intent, and physical reasoning, which are crucial for applications in robotics, gaming, and augmented/virtual reality.

The motivation for this work arises from several challenges in current 4D HOI data acquisition:

1. **High Cost and Limited Diversity of Traditional Capture Systems**: Existing multi-sensor capture systems, while offering high precision, are expensive to set up and operate. This limits data collection to controlled indoor environments and a narrow range of objects and actions, making them unsuitable for capturing the vast diversity of real-world human activities, such as outdoor sports or industrial tasks. The prohibitive cost prevents the creation of truly large-scale and generalized HOI datasets necessary for robust robotic learning.

2. **Annotation Bottleneck and Inaccuracy in Monocular Reconstruction**: Recent efforts to reconstruct 3D HOI from readily available monocular videos offer a potential solution to data diversity. However, purely automated methods often result in physically inaccurate contacts and poor alignment between humans and objects. Manual correction methods, such as adjusting object poses in Blender or annotating contact regions frame-by-frame, are prohibitively time-consuming and expensive for video sequences. Moreover, these approaches inherently struggle to maintain spatio-temporal consistency across frames, leading to jerky or implausible reconstructed motions.

3. **Lack of Scalable and Temporally Coherent 4D HOI Datasets**: There is a recognized need for large-scale 4D HOI datasets that accurately capture diverse interactions in unconstrained environments. Existing datasets either lack the scale, the diversity, or the temporal coherence required for training robust, generalized AI models. Creating such a dataset efficiently, without resorting to expensive multi-sensor setups or laborious per-frame manual annotations, represents a significant unmet need.

To address these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives:

- **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Annotation Paradigm**: Introduce a method to reduce the annotation burden for HOI in videos. This involves moving away from dense, per-frame labeling to a lightweight annotation of temporally invariant interaction points, guided by system-provided interactable reference points.
- **Build a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Data Engine**: Create a system, powered by a multi-modal contact predictor (InterPoint), that automatically proposes initial annotations and continuously improves its accuracy through a positive feedback loop with human verification. This aims to accelerate the annotation process for complex interactions.
- **Propose a Novel Optimization Framework for High-Quality Reconstruction**: Develop "4DHOISolver," a two-stage optimization framework capable of reconstructing high-fidelity, physically plausible, and spatio-temporally coherent 4D HOI from sparse annotations in monocular videos. This framework aims to constrain the ill-posed nature of monocular 4D HOI reconstruction.
- **Construct and Release a Large-Scale 4D HOI Dataset**: Leverage the developed pipeline to create "Open4DHOI," a new dataset featuring a diverse catalog of object types and actions captured from in-the-wild monocular videos. This dataset is intended to be publicly available.
- **Validate the Utility of the Reconstructed Data**: Demonstrate the effectiveness of the reconstructed motions for downstream applications, specifically by enabling a reinforcement learning (RL)-based agent to imitate the recovered HOI motions in a simulated environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a multi-stage pipeline designed for efficient and scalable monocular human-object interaction motion reconstruction, culminating in the creation of a large-scale dataset and its validation in a downstream application. The core idea revolves around annotating sparse, temporally invariant interaction point pairs and using these for an optimization-based reconstruction.

#### 3.1. Automated 4D Reconstruction (Initialization)

The initial stage focuses on obtaining coarse 3D reconstructions of humans and objects from in-the-wild monocular videos. This involves:
- **Preprocessing**: Detecting video shot transitions, removing non-interactive frames, and tracking masks for humans and objects using SAM2.
- **Human and Object Reconstruction**: Utilizing GVHMR for human motion recovery and SAM 3D Objects for object reconstruction. Hand reconstruction methods are integrated for detailed kinematics.
- **Spatial Alignment**: Estimating depth for humans and objects using DepthAnythingV2 and employing a depth-aware projection approach to spatially align human and object meshes and estimate object scales.

#### 3.2. Monocular HOI Annotation (Human-in-the-Loop Data Engine)

To overcome the annotation bottleneck, an efficient, human-in-the-loop annotation system is developed:
- **Contact Definition**: A hierarchical tree structure with 87 finely divided human keypoints is predefined (e.g., forearm subdivided into front, back, left, right). For objects, points can be freely selected on the surface.
- **Annotation App**: A custom interactive application facilitates the annotation process. Users perform two main types of annotations:
 - **3D-3D Point Pair Annotation**: Selecting a 3D point on the object's point cloud and assigning it to a corresponding human joint.
 - **3D-2D Projection Annotation**: Specifying a 3D object point and its corresponding 2D projection on the video frame to constrain object pose.
 - **Efficiency Features**: The app leverages a Point Tracking model to track annotated 2D points over time, requiring re-annotation only when stable points change. It also offers an option to fix the pose of static objects, reducing manual effort.
- **InterPoint Model (Multi-modal Contact Predictor)**: This model is introduced to accelerate annotation by proposing initial contact point pairs, forming a data flywheel.
 - **Problem Formulation**: Given an image frame and a 3D object point cloud, InterPoint predicts interacting human keypoints (from the 87 predefined) and their corresponding contact points on the object surface.
 - **Architecture**: It extracts 2D semantic embeddings for human and object from a Vision-Language Model (VLM, e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B) and 3D object features using a PointNet++ encoder. 3D human features are generated from learnable template parameters on the SMPL-X body model. A Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layer dynamically injects 2D semantics into 3D point features. An Interaction Transformer fuses these enriched human and object representations. Learnable queries then predict 87 human joint contact probabilities and localize object contact coordinates via an attention mechanism.
 - **Training Recipe**: Training uses a standard Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for human keypoint predictions. For sparse object-side correspondences, a bidirectional contrastive loss is formulated (keypoint-to-object and object-to-keypoint) to address sparsity challenges. The model is continuously fine-tuned with newly acquired and verified annotations.

#### 3.3. 4DHOISolver (Optimization Framework)

Following annotation, a two-stage optimization framework reconstructs high-quality, temporally consistent 4D HOI:
- **Stage 1: HOI Keypoint Solver**: This stage performs rapid geometric alignment.
 - **Point Matching**: Uses a weighted least squares method to solve two Points-Alignment problems: 3D-3D spatial alignment (based on annotated human-object contact pairs) and 3D-2D projection alignment (based on annotated object 3D-2D points). This optimizes the object's pose (rotation R_o and translation t_o).
 - **Inverse Kinematics (IK)**: After object pose optimization, IK is applied to refine human limb positions, which are critical for interaction.
- **Stage 2: HOI Optimizer**: This stage refines the physical plausibility of the interaction using gradient-based optimization of human pose parameters (θ_h), object rotation (R_o), and translation (t_o).
 - **Gradient Propagation**: Gradients are propagated along the kinematic chain based on joint-tree distances, focusing optimization on annotated contact regions while preserving stability in non-contact areas.
 - **Loss Function**: A composite loss function guides the optimization:
 - **Contact Loss**: Encourages annotated human-object point pairs to converge to a balanced contact configuration, assigning higher weights to pairs with larger initial distances.
 - **Collision Loss**: A bidirectional mesh-to-mesh penalty is applied to prevent interpenetration between human and object meshes.
 - **Mask Loss**: Uses pixel-wise Mean Squared Error (MSE) and an edge loss (based on distance transforms) between rendered and ground-truth masks of the human and object, handling mutual occlusions.
- **Post-Smoothing**: Optimization is performed every K frames, and intermediate frames are interpolated. A low-pass filter is applied to the entire optimized motion sequence to ensure temporal smoothness and coherence.

#### 3.4. Open4DHOI Dataset Construction

The developed pipeline is leveraged to construct Open4DHOI, a large-scale dataset comprising 451 sequences (131k frames) across 135 categories of rigid objects, sourced from mobile phone captures and TikTok videos. The data collection is designed to be cost-efficient, with an average annotation time of approximately ten minutes per video.

#### 3.5. HOI Simulation (Downstream Application Validation)

To demonstrate the utility of the reconstructed data, a policy is trained via reinforcement learning (RL) to control a humanoid agent to imitate the recovered HOI motions.
- **Noise-Robust Simulation**: Built upon InterMimic, the simulation incorporates contact annotations as strong priors to handle physical artifacts (e.g., interpenetration, floating) inherent in monocular reconstructions.
- **Reward Function**: A novel, contact-guided reward function is designed, comprising:
 - **Tracking Reward (R_tr)**: Standard reward for basic motion imitation, penalizing differences in joint positions, rotations, and velocities between the agent and reference motion.
 - **KP Label Reward (R_lr)**: Penalizes discrepancies between simulated force feedback and annotated ground-truth contact labels, specifically for active contacts.
 - **KP Contact Reward (R_cr)**: Uses an Interaction Graph to explicitly model dynamic contact maps. It enforces the global spatial distance between paired active contact points to be zero, compensating for reconstruction inaccuracies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several main findings and results across the development of the annotation and reconstruction pipeline, the created dataset, and the downstream application.

#### 4.1. Open4DHOI Dataset Characteristics and Efficiency

- **Scale and Diversity**: Open4DHOI comprises 131,000 frames across 451 sequences, featuring 135 object categories and 133 distinct action categories. The dataset demonstrates high diversity in both object types (spanning a wide range of sizes and rigid bodies) and human motions (covering a broader spectrum than comparable datasets, with a MoMask diversity metric of 3.465, exceeding BEHAVE and OMOMO). Interaction diversity is also high, including common and uncommon HOI types.
- **Cost Efficiency**: The data collection process, utilizing mobile phones and TikTok videos, is highly cost-efficient, requiring no specialized capture equipment. The annotation process takes approximately ten minutes per video on average (with 6.24 points per video), demonstrating strong scalability and significantly lower costs compared to methods like Open3DHOI's frame-by-frame Blender annotation or traditional multi-sensor systems (e.g., Vicon).

#### 4.2. 4DHOISolver Reconstruction Performance

- **Object Tracking Accuracy**: Quantitative evaluation of object reconstruction on the BEHAVE and IMHD^2 datasets, using Chamfer Distance (CD-o), shows that 4DHOISolver consistently outperforms existing baselines (PHOSA, CHORE, VisTracker, I'm HOI). This holds true for both per-frame CD-o (e.g., 4.67 on BEHAVE vs. 7.43 for I'm HOI) and sliding-window CD-o (e.g., 4.42 on BEHAVE vs. 4.82 for I'm HOI), indicating strong cross-dataset robustness.
- **Qualitative Reconstruction**: Visualizations on Open4DHOI demonstrate that the 4DHOISolver produces reconstructions with improved spatio-temporal alignment and physical plausibility compared to baselines. This includes reducing common artifacts such as object floating, severe interpenetration, and enhancing temporal consistency and faithful interaction geometry across frames.

#### 4.3. InterPoint Model Effectiveness

- **Interaction Point Prediction**: The InterPoint model achieves strong performance in predicting human contact points (F1-score of 0.523), object contact points (Recall of 0.476), and interaction point pairs (Recall of 0.303) on the Open4DHOI test set. This performance surpasses other interaction contact predictors (LEMON, InteractVLM) and human/object-specific models (DECO, 3DAffordance-LLM), demonstrating its feasibility for efficient annotation initialization.
- **Scalability with Data**: Experiments show that InterPoint's performance (F1 for human points, Recall for object and interaction points) consistently increases with larger training data ratios (from 20% to 100%). This validates the data flywheel concept, where more annotated data leads to improved model accuracy and, consequently, faster future annotation.
- **Annotation Acceleration**: Qualitative results illustrate that InterPoint provides accurate initial annotation proposals, reducing the need for manual refinement and significantly speeding up the overall annotation process.

#### 4.4. HOI Simulation Results (Downstream Application)

- **Imitation Performance**: An ablation study on an RL-based humanoid agent trained to imitate reconstructed HOI motions shows the effectiveness of the proposed reward functions.
 - Adding the KP Label Reward (TR+LR) to a basic Tracking Reward (TR only) improves contact scores (from 43.94mm to 39.53mm) and jitter (from 91.43 to 80.37).
 - The full reward function, including the KP Contact Reward (TR+LR+CR), further significantly reduces MPJPE (from 151.82mm to 125.76mm) and contact error (from 43.94mm to 26.76mm), while maintaining low jitter.
- **Physical Plausibility in Simulation**: Visualizations of the imitation results demonstrate that the RL agent, guided by the contact-aware reward function, can effectively optimize physically implausible issues present in the reference motion, such as interpenetration, foot floating, and contact misalignment. This indicates that the reconstructed HOI data is suitable for training robust robotic policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a scalable and efficient framework for reconstructing 4D human-object interactions (HOI) from diverse, in-the-wild monocular videos, addressing several long-standing challenges in the field. Its significance and potential impact span multiple domains:

1. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck for Generalized AI**: The primary impact is in overcoming the fundamental bottleneck of acquiring large-scale, high-fidelity 4D HOI data. Traditional multi-sensor systems are cost-prohibitive and confined to limited, controlled environments, hindering the development of generalized robot systems capable of operating in the complex real world. By leveraging readily available internet videos and an efficient annotation paradigm, this work offers a path to massively scale HOI data collection, which is crucial for training robust AI models.

2. **Enabling Open-Vocabulary and In-the-Wild HOI**: The framework allows for the capture and reconstruction of interactions involving a diverse range of objects and actions in unconstrained environments. This open-vocabulary and "in-the-wild" capability is a substantial step towards real-world applicability for embodied AI and robotics, moving beyond rigid, predefined interaction scenarios.

3. **Introduction of a Large-Scale, Diverse Dataset (Open4DHOI)**: The creation and public release of Open4DHOI represents a significant contribution to the research community. This dataset, with its scale and rich diversity in objects, actions, and human motions, provides a valuable resource for developing and evaluating new algorithms in 4D HOI reconstruction, understanding human motor skills, and training robotic agents. Its cost-efficient generation methodology provides a blueprint for future large-scale data collection efforts.

4. **Innovation in Annotation and Reconstruction Methodology**:
 - The proposed sparse contact annotation paradigm, focusing on temporally invariant interaction points, offers a more efficient alternative to dense, per-frame labeling.
 - The InterPoint-driven human-in-the-loop data engine introduces an intelligent annotation assistant that continuously improves through a data flywheel, marking a progression in semi-automated data curation.
 - The 4DHOISolver framework provides a robust solution for the ill-posed problem of monocular 4D HOI reconstruction, ensuring spatio-temporal coherence and physical plausibility through a two-stage optimization process, including novel contact, collision, and mask losses.

5. **Direct Impact on Downstream Robotic Applications**: The successful demonstration of training an RL-based humanoid agent to imitate the reconstructed HOI motions underscores the practical utility and quality of the generated data. This directly impacts the development of intelligent robotic agents capable of dexterous manipulation, enabling them to learn complex interaction skills from human demonstrations. The contact-guided reward functions developed for this purpose further highlight the data's richness for physics-based simulation and learning.

6. **Guidance for Future Research**: The discussion of limitations, such as challenges with extreme dynamic sliding contacts and highly deformable objects, and the need to bridge the sim-to-real gap, provides clear directions for future research in HOI reconstruction and embodied AI. This work lays a foundation for expanding the capabilities of generalized robots and digital human technologies.

In summary, this research offers a practical and scalable solution for a critical data acquisition problem, significantly advancing the state of the art in 4D HOI reconstruction and providing a valuable new resource for a wide array of AI-driven applications.
