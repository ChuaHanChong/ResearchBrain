# 2HandedAfforder: Learning Precise Actionable Bimanual Affordances from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> 2HandedAfforder extracts precise, bimanual affordance masks from egocentric human-activity videos via hand-inpainting and mask-completion, builds the 278K-image 2HANDS dataset, and trains a VLM+SAM segmentation model that predicts task-conditioned left/right-hand affordance regions, beating reasoning-segmentation and affordance baselines on a new human-aligned benchmark and enabling real bimanual robot grasping.

## Problem

- Existing affordance datasets label whole object parts rather than the precise interaction regions humans actually use, conflating affordance prediction with coarse part segmentation.
- Prior auto-labeled affordance methods only use hand bounding-boxes or heatmaps to mark task-agnostic interaction hotspots, ignoring bimanual coordination between two hands and objects.
- No existing benchmark evaluates prompt-based, precise, bimanual affordance segmentation against human judgments of interaction regions from observed video, leaving generalization untested.

## Method

- ==Affordance extraction pipeline==: video mask-propagation plus a video-based ==hand-inpainting model (VIDM)== uncovers occluded object regions, then ==SAM2==-based mask completion and hand-object mask intersection yield precise unimanual/bimanual affordance masks with narration-derived class labels, forming the 2HANDS dataset.
- **2HandedAfforder** model: a ==LoRA==-fine-tuned ==LLaVA-13b== VLM produces a ==[SEG] token== fused with a frozen SAM image encoder, feeding two SAM-style mask decoders that predict separate left-hand and right-hand affordance masks plus a taxonomy classifier for left/right/bimanual action type.
- **ActAffordance** benchmark: a new evaluation set built by showing annotators inpainted hand-object interaction sequences from EPIC-KITCHENS and Ego4D, asking them to mark all plausible bimanual interaction regions, with a cropped variant isolating segmentation from object-localization ability.

## Results

- On the combined ActAffordance benchmark 2HandedAfforder attains best scores across IoU (**0.058**), precision (**0.130**), Hausdorff distance, and mAP (**0.104**), outperforming LISA, LOCATE, and AffordanceLLM baselines.
- The AffExtract extraction pipeline itself reaches **0.420** combined precision against human annotations (**0.334** EPIC-KITCHENS, **0.541** Ego4D), confirming the auto-labeled masks align well with human-judged interaction regions.
- On the cropped benchmark isolating segmentation quality, 2HandedAfforder still leads with **0.269** precision and **0.240** mAP, and real-robot Tiago++ trials show affordance-guided bimanual grasping succeeds where plain object-segmentation grasps fail.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extracting affordances directly from observed hand-object contact via inpainting sidesteps costly manual annotation and yields regions that are actionable rather than merely conceptual object-part labels.
> - Video-based mask completion generalizes better than static image-inpainting completion because it naturally filters out frames where reconstruction fails, avoiding spurious 'ghost' affordance regions.
> - Treating affordance prediction as a low false-negative, high-precision segmentation problem (not IoU-maximizing) matters when ground truth is inherently multi-modal, i.e., many valid interaction regions exist per task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "2HandedAfforder" addresses a significant area within computer vision and robotics: affordance grounding. Affordance grounding refers to the ability of a system to understand which regions of an object are suitable for a particular action or task. This capability is considered fundamental for intelligent agents, particularly in robotics, where robots must interact with objects in unstructured, everyday environments.

Existing vision-based affordance prediction methods commonly reduce the problem to object part segmentation. Datasets used for training these methods, such as IIT-AFF, AGD20K, and 3DOI, often provide labels that represent general object parts rather than precise, actionable interaction regions. Consequently, models trained on such data tend to predict broad object areas (e.g., the entire bottom of a bottle for "pouring") instead of the specific, functional regions humans utilize (e.g., the precise grip area for pouring). Furthermore, a notable gap exists in the ability of current methods to account for bimanual actions, where two hands coordinate to interact with one or more objects, and the affordances of multiple objects are interconnected. While some previous works have explored bimanual affordances, a comprehensive dataset and model specifically addressing precise, task-specific bimanual affordance segmentation from human videos has been lacking.

Another limitation in the landscape is the focus on task-agnostic "hotspot" interaction regions in auto-labeled affordance detection methods. These approaches often use weak supervision from hand and object bounding boxes in egocentric videos but do not provide task-specific, precise segmentation masks. The challenge of hand occlusion during human-object interaction also complicates the direct extraction of affordance regions from videos.

This research contributes to the landscape by introducing a framework that moves beyond naive object part segmentation and task-agnostic hotspots. It specifically aims to extract and predict *precise*, *actionable*, *task-oriented*, and *bimanual* affordance regions. The introduction of the 2HANDS dataset, derived from human activity videos, offers a novel resource with auto-labeled, high-fidelity affordance masks and narration-based class labels. The proposed 2HandedAfforder model, a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based architecture, represents an advancement in reasoning about complex task prompts to predict these specific affordance regions. Finally, the ActAffordance benchmark provides a new standard for evaluating affordance prediction against human-observed interaction sequences, facilitating progress in this challenging domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while humans intuitively reason about which object regions afford specific actions, machines largely lack this capability, referred to as "affordance grounding." This limitation poses a significant hurdle for various intelligent systems, particularly in robotics, where precise object interaction is critical for task execution. For instance, a robot attempting to pour liquid needs to identify not just the bottle, but the specific region on the bottle that allows for stable pouring, often requiring coordinated bimanual action.

Current methodologies frequently fall short in several aspects. Existing labeled datasets often treat affordance prediction as a general object part segmentation problem, providing coarse labels that do not reflect the precision of human interaction. This imprecision results in models that predict conceptual rather than actionable regions. Additionally, many real-world tasks involve bimanual interactions, yet most affordance detection methods do not explicitly account for two hands coordinating with one or more objects, leading to an incomplete understanding of complex manipulation. The manual annotation of precise affordance regions is a laborious and often inconsistent process, motivating the need for automated extraction methods. However, automated extraction from human activity videos is complicated by the common problem of hands occluding the very object regions they interact with.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop a Method for Precise Affordance Extraction**: The first objective is to propose a novel methodology capable of automatically extracting precise, actionable, and task-oriented affordance regions directly from human activity video datasets. This method aims to overcome the challenge of hand occlusion by reconstructing the occluded object parts.
2. **Create a Comprehensive Bimanual Affordance Dataset**: A central objective is the generation of a large-scale dataset, named 2HANDS (2-Handed Affordance + Narration DataSet). This dataset aims to comprise a substantial number of images with automatically extracted unimanual and bimanual affordance segmentation masks, accompanied by task narrations as natural language affordance class labels.
3. **Propose a Task-Aware Bimanual Affordance Prediction Model**: The research aims to introduce a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based network, 2HandedAfforder, designed to predict pixel-wise affordance masks in images based on an input text prompt describing the intended task. This model is intended to reason about and identify specific, task-relevant bimanual affordance regions.
4. **Establish a Novel Evaluation Benchmark**: To rigorously assess the quality of the extracted affordances and the performance of the prediction model, an objective is to create a new benchmark, ActAffordance. This benchmark would use human annotations based on observing full interaction sequences, providing a more robust measure of alignment with human perception of actionable affordances.
5. **Demonstrate Actionability in Robotic Manipulation**: A final objective is to demonstrate the practical utility and "actionability" of the predicted affordance regions by integrating them into robotic manipulation scenarios. This involves showing that a robot can utilize these predictions to perform manipulation tasks more effectively than with conventional object segmentation methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research encompasses a multi-stage approach, including data extraction, dataset creation, model development, and experimental evaluation, with a focus on bimanual affordance prediction.

#### 3.1. Affordance Extraction from Human Videos (2HANDS Dataset Creation)

The foundation of this work is the automatic extraction of precise affordance regions from human activity videos, which form the 2HANDS dataset.
1. **Source Data**: The EPIC-KITCHENS dataset, consisting of egocentric videos of human activities in kitchens, was utilized. Initial annotations from VISOR provided sparse hand-object mask segmentations and contact labels.
2. **Dense Mask Propagation**: To obtain continuous hand and object masks across entire video sequences, a video-based mask propagation network was employed. This step ensures dense segmentation information for subsequent processing.
3. **Hand Inpainting**: A crucial step to address hand occlusion was the use of a video-based hand inpainting model, VIDM. This model reconstructs the object regions that are occluded by hands, using information from four surrounding frames in the sequence to provide a contextually accurate restoration of the objects.
4. **Object Mask Completion**: Following inpainting, the original object segmentation masks were propagated to the newly inpainted images using SAM2. This process "completes" the object masks, providing precise segmentations of the now un-occluded objects. This method was observed to yield more precise object masks compared to direct mask completion techniques.
5. **Affordance Region Calculation**: The final affordance region for a specific task was derived by computing the intersection of the completed, un-occluded object masks and the corresponding hand masks. This intersection delineates the precise area of interaction.
6. **Bimanual Taxonomy and Class Labels**: Affordances were classified into a bimanual taxonomy: unimanual left, unimanual right, and bimanual. The natural language narrations of the tasks performed in the videos were used directly as affordance class labels, making them task-oriented.
7. **Data Filtering and Augmentation**: Several filtering steps were applied to enhance data quality. This included erosion and dilation of masks to remove noise and fill gaps, removal of inconsistent data points (e.g., empty affordance masks for an action), and blacklisting of vague narrations. The dataset was augmented by horizontally flipping actions to balance left- and right-handed interactions and by applying standard techniques like color jittering and cropping.
The resulting 2HANDS dataset contains 278,160 datapoints from 25 kitchen environments, encompassing 160 object categories and 73 verb classes. Each datapoint includes an inpainted frame, two affordance masks (one potentially empty for unimanual actions), and the task narration.

#### 3.2. Task-oriented Bimanual Affordance Prediction (2HandedAfforder Model)

To predict these precise affordances, the 2HandedAfforder model was developed:
1. **Architecture**: The model leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for reasoning-based segmentation. It combines a VLM (LLaVa-13b) for joint text and image processing with a robust vision backbone (a modified, frozen SAM image encoder).
2. **Input and Output**: Given an input image and a task prompt (e.g., "Where would you interact with the objects to perform the action {action narration} in this image?"), the VLM generates language tokens and a segmentation `[SEG]` token.
3. **Affordance Decoders**: Two SAM-style mask decoders, initialized with pre-trained SAM weights, receive the encoded image and the `[SEG]` token. They are trained to predict separate pixel-wise segmentation masks for the left and right hands.
4. **Taxonomy Classifier**: A fully-connected classifier (MLP head) attached to one of the decoder output tokens predicts the bimanual taxonomy: 'unimanual left hand', 'unimanual right hand', or 'bimanual'.
5. **Training Procedure**: The SAM image encoder was frozen. The VLM was fine-tuned efficiently using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to preserve its pre-trained knowledge while specializing in segmentation. Mask prediction was optimized using a combination of Dice loss and Focal Cross-Entropy loss. Taxonomy prediction used Cross-Entropy loss. For unimanual tasks, the loss for the irrelevant hand's mask was set to zero.
6. **2HandedAfforder-CLIP**: An alternative, smaller model was also trained, replacing the VLM's `[SEG]` token with CLIP text features as input to the affordance decoders.

#### 3.3. ActAffordance Benchmark (Evaluation)

To evaluate both the quality of the extracted data and the model's performance, a novel benchmark, ActAffordance, was introduced:
1. **Annotation Process**: 10 human annotators used the TORAS platform. They were presented with pairs of inpainted (hands removed) and original (with hands) interaction images, along with the task description. Annotators were instructed to highlight *all possible* interaction regions for both the left and right hands, reflecting the multimodal nature of human affordance perception.
2. **Dataset Composition**: The benchmark comprises 400 activities from EPIC-KITCHENS and Ego4D. Crucially, there is no overlap between the EPIC-KITCHENS data used in 2HANDS and the benchmark.
3. **Benchmark Variants**:
 - **ActAffordance**: Evaluation on full images.
 - **ActAffordance-Cropped**: A modified version where images are cropped to a bounding box around the target objects. This helps to isolate the affordance region segmentation capability from object identification and enables fair comparison with baselines that use bounding boxes or query points.
4. **Evaluation Metrics**: Performance was assessed using Intersection over Union (IoU), Precision, Hausdorff Distance (HD), and Directional Hausdorff Distance (Dir. HD), and Mean Average Precision (mAP). Given that human annotations are multimodal (covering all possible interaction regions), Precision and Directional Hausdorff Distance were emphasized as key metrics to evaluate the precision of a single predicted affordance mode.

#### 3.4. Robotic Manipulation Demonstration

To demonstrate the practical applicability of the predicted affordances, experiments were conducted using a bimanual Tiago++ robot in a realistic kitchen environment:
1. **Perception Pipeline**: An RGBD camera captured the scene. LangSAM was used for initial object bounding box and mask generation based on text prompts.
2. **Affordance Integration**: The 2HandedAfforder model was applied to cropped object images to predict task-specific affordance regions. The intersection of these predicted masks with LangSAM's object masks was used, with an adjusted prediction threshold for larger, more optimistic masks.
3. **Grasping and Manipulation**: The segmented point clouds derived from the affordance regions informed a 6DoF grasp prediction network. Pre-designed manipulation primitives were then used to execute the manipulation tasks.
This setup allowed for a direct comparison of robot performance using affordance-guided grasping versus standard object or part segmentation approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments focused on evaluating the quality of the extracted affordances, the performance of the 2HandedAfforder model against baselines, its generalization capabilities, and its actionability in robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Affordance Extraction Quality

The quality of affordances derived from the extraction pipeline (`AffExtract`) was assessed by comparing them against the human-annotated ActAffordance benchmark. On the combined benchmark, `AffExtract` achieved a Precision of 0.420, with 0.334 for the EPIC-KITCHENS split and 0.541 for the Ego4D split. The combined IoU was 0.185, and the Directional Hausdorff Distance was 145. The relatively high precision and lower Directional Hausdorff Distance indicate a reasonable alignment between the extracted affordance masks and human-annotated regions, confirming that the pipeline generates meaningful and precise interaction areas. The lower IoU scores across the board highlight the inherent difficulty of the task, particularly when comparing a single extracted affordance mode against multimodal human annotations.

#### 4.2. Comparison against Baselines on ActAffordance Benchmark

The 2HandedAfforder model was evaluated against several baselines, including general object segmentation VLMs (LISA), weakly-supervised affordance detection methods (LOCATE, AffordanceLLM, VRB), and fully-supervised point-based methods (3DOI). Baselines were adapted where necessary to allow for inference on the benchmark, and their predictions were converted to segmentation masks for comparison.

- **ActAffordance Benchmark (Full Images)**:
 - **2HandedAfforder (2HAff)** consistently outperformed all baselines across all metrics. It achieved a combined IoU of 0.058, Precision of 0.130, HD of 262, Dir. HD of 202, and mAP of 0.104.
 - LISA was the second-best performing method, demonstrating the efficacy of reasoning segmentation in identifying relevant objects.
 - The CLIP-based version, **2HandedAfforder-CLIP (2HAffCLIP)**, ranked third, indicating that the VLM's advanced reasoning capabilities contributed to the superior performance of the full 2HandedAfforder model.
 - Performance on the Ego4D split was comparable to, and sometimes better than, the EPIC-KITCHENS split, suggesting reasonable generalization despite no Ego4D data being used in the 2HANDS training set. IoU scores remained generally low for all methods, underscoring the challenging nature of precise affordance prediction.

- **ActAffordance-Cropped Benchmark (Cropped Images)**:
 - Cropping images to target objects generally improved performance across all methods, as it simplified the initial object identification step.
 - **2HandedAfforder (2HAff)** maintained its top position, achieving a combined IoU of 0.086, Precision of 0.269, HD of 180, Dir. HD of 100, and mAP of 0.240. These scores represent significant improvements over the uncropped benchmark.
 - 3DOI showed reasonable performance in terms of precision on this cropped variant.
 - AffordanceLLM exhibited relatively good HD and Dir. HD scores, attributed to its tendency to always predict some affordance regions, avoiding penalties for no-mask predictions.
 - LISA remained competitive, while task-agnostic methods like VRB performed less effectively.

#### 4.3. In-the-wild Affordance Prediction and Robot Demonstration

The practical applicability of 2HandedAfforder was demonstrated through robotic manipulation experiments using a bimanual Tiago++ robot in a kitchen setting.
- **Integration with Robotic System**: The 2HandedAfforder model was deployed to infer affordance regions from RGBD camera images, guided by task prompts (e.g., 'pour into cup'). To enhance real-world performance, object bounding boxes and masks were first obtained using LangSAM, and the final affordance masks were computed as the intersection of 2HandedAfforder's predictions and LangSAM's object masks.
- **Actionability Demonstration**: The predicted affordance regions were used to guide a 6DoF grasp prediction network, enabling the robot to make informed grasping decisions. This approach demonstrated improved task success compared to methods relying solely on standard object or part segmentation (e.g., using LangSAM outputs alone).
- **Bimanual Task Execution**: Qualitative examples and videos (e.g., Figure 6, Figure 10) illustrate how the robot leverages the precise bimanual affordance predictions to perform complex tasks effectively. For instance, the robot could stably hold a pot with one hand while stirring its contents with a spatula held by the other hand, a task that proved challenging or impossible with less precise, unimanual object interaction understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer vision and robotics in several significant ways, with potential impacts on the development of more intelligent and autonomous agents.

First, the work introduces a **novel framework for automatically extracting precise, actionable, and bimanual affordance regions** from human activity videos. By leveraging advanced techniques such as video-based hand inpainting and object mask completion, this methodology successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of hand occlusion, which previously limited the fidelity of affordance data extracted from real-world interactions. This automated approach circumvents the labor-intensive and often imprecise nature of manual annotation, enabling the creation of large-scale, high-quality datasets.

Second, the creation of the **2HANDS dataset** represents a substantial contribution. As the first comprehensive dataset of its kind, offering precise, pixel-wise segmentation masks for unimanual and bimanual affordances, coupled with natural language narrations as task-specific labels, 2HANDS provides an invaluable resource. This dataset moves beyond generic object part segmentation to focus on specific, actionable interaction regions, which is critical for real-world applications and advancing the state of affordance grounding.

Third, the development of **2HandedAfforder, a VLM-based task-aware bimanual affordance prediction model**, marks a notable advancement. By integrating the reasoning capabilities of large language models with a robust vision backbone, the model can interpret complex task prompts and predict highly specific affordance regions. Its demonstrated superior performance against existing baselines in predicting both unimanual and bimanual affordances indicates a step forward in robustly understanding human-like object interactions. The ability to generalize to unseen data (e.g., Ego4D videos) further underscores its potential for broad applicability.

Fourth, the introduction of the **ActAffordance benchmark** provides a rigorous and realistic evaluation standard for affordance prediction. Unlike previous benchmarks that might rely on static, object-centric annotations, ActAffordance assesses model performance against human-annotated affordances derived from observing actual interaction sequences. This challenging, multimodal benchmark is essential for driving future research by highlighting the gap between current machine capabilities and human-level understanding of object affordances.

Finally, the **demonstration of 2HandedAfforder's actionability in real-world robotic manipulation** is a critical validation of this research. By integrating the predicted affordance regions into a bimanual robotic system, the work showcases how precise affordance grounding can lead to more informed grasping decisions and significantly improve task success compared to conventional object segmentation approaches. This direct application underscores the potential for 2HandedAfforder to enhance robotic intelligence and autonomy, enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments with greater dexterity and adaptability. The findings suggest a path toward robots that can reason more effectively about "how" to interact with objects, moving closer to human-like capabilities in daily tasks.
