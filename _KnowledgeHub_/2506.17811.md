# RoboMonkey: Scaling Test-Time Sampling and Verification for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboMonkey is a framework that scales test-time sampling and verification for Vision-Language-Action models, employing a learned action verifier trained on synthetic preference data. It demonstrated a 25% absolute improvement in success rates on real-world out-of-distribution tasks and established the existence of inference-time scaling laws for VLAs.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack robustness and generalization, exhibiting failures like imprecise grasping and collisions in unstructured real-world environments.
- Current efforts to improve VLA performance primarily focus on pre-training and post-training, with test-time compute scaling largely unexplored for robotics.
- The cost and difficulty of human annotation make training effective verifiers for robotic actions a significant challenge.

## Method

- **RoboMonkey** employs a two-stage framework, first training an action verifier using a novel automated pipeline for synthetic action preference data generation.
- At deployment, the system samples multiple diverse candidate actions from a VLA model, efficiently using Gaussian perturbation, and then selects the optimal action with the learned verifier.
- A VLA serving engine built on SGLang optimizes inference for both the base VLA and the verifier, significantly reducing computational overhead.

## Results

- RoboMonkey achieved a **25%** absolute improvement in success rates on real-world out-of-distribution tasks, reaching **60%** compared to **35%** for the base OpenVLA model.
- On in-distribution tasks, it delivered an average success rate of **47.5%**, an absolute improvement of **9%** over standalone OpenVLA.
- The system processed and verified 16 candidate actions in approximately 650 ms (**1.5** Hz) on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU, demonstrating practical real-time applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An inference-time scaling law exists for Vision-Language-Action models, where action error consistently decreases as the number of generated samples increases.
> - High-quality action verifiers can be trained scalably using automatically generated synthetic action comparison data, eliminating the need for expensive human labeling.
> - Efficient sampling methods like Gaussian perturbation, combined with optimized inference engines, enable practical test-time scaling for robotic control within real-time constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of robotics: ensuring the robustness and generalization of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in complex, unstructured real-world environments. Recent advancements in VLA models, often termed "foundation models for robotics" (e.g., OpenVLA, Octo, PaLM-E, RT-X), have leveraged large-scale datasets and scaled training compute to achieve remarkable visuomotor control capabilities. However, these models still exhibit diverse failure modes, such as imprecise grasping, task progression failures, and collisions, hindering their reliable deployment.

Traditionally, efforts to improve VLA performance have focused on two main stages:
1. **Pre-training:** Scaling up data collection, optimizing data mixtures, and developing advanced model architectures.
2. **Post-training:** Fine-tuning VLAs for multi-step reasoning with techniques like chain-of-thought or aligning them with human preferences.

This paper introduces a **paradigm shift** by focusing on a third, less explored, phase: **scaling compute at test-time (deployment).** The authors draw a direct analogy to the remarkable success of this approach in Large Language Models (LLMs), where repeatedly sampling candidate solutions and verifying them has significantly enhanced capabilities across tasks like mathematics, coding, and summarization. By applying this "generate-then-verify" strategy to robotics, the paper seeks to answer whether similar test-time scaling benefits can be realized for VLAs.

RoboMonkey thus positions itself at the intersection of generalist robotics, large multimodal models, and efficient AI inference systems. It contributes to the growing body of work on out-of-distribution robustness in robotics by proposing an inference-time solution rather than relying solely on pre- or post-training adjustments. It also directly compares against and builds upon related work, such as V-GPS, which similarly uses value functions to re-rank candidate actions, but RoboMonkey proposes a more scalable data curation and model architecture for its verifier. This work represents a significant step towards developing more reliable and adaptable general-purpose robot foundation models by demonstrating the overlooked potential of intelligent compute allocation during deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Despite their impressive capabilities stemming from large-scale pre-training, VLAs often fall short in robustness when deployed in unstructured real-world environments. They exhibit various failure modes, including:
- **Imprecise grasping:** Failing to accurately grip target objects.
- **Task progression failure:** Getting stuck or unable to complete sequential steps of a task.
- **Collision with surrounding objects:** Indicating a lack of fine-grained spatial reasoning or safety awareness.

Addressing these limitations is crucial for accelerating the practical deployment of robots. The authors observe that while significant research has focused on pre-training and post-training strategies to improve VLAs, the potential of allocating additional compute *during deployment* (test-time scaling) has been largely underexplored for robotics, unlike its proven efficacy in Large Language Models (LLMs). This observation leads to the central motivating question:

**Given an observation and task instruction, can the precision and robustness of VLAs be improved by repeatedly sampling and verifying actions at deployment?**

To answer this, the paper sets out two key objectives:

1. **To systematically investigate the existence of inference-time scaling laws for VLAs:** This involves analyzing whether action error consistently decreases as the number of generated samples increases, and identifying efficient sampling methods. This objective seeks to establish a fundamental principle for VLAs, akin to power-law scaling observed in LLMs.
2. **To develop and validate a practical test-time scaling framework, RoboMonkey, that leverages a learned action verifier:** This framework aims to capitalize on the observed scaling laws by generating diverse action candidates and employing a mechanism to select the optimal one. A critical sub-objective here is to devise a scalable method for training such an action verifier without costly human annotation.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between impressive VLA capabilities in controlled settings and their reliable performance in the unpredictable real world, by introducing an intelligent "generate-then-verify" mechanism at the point of action execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboMonkey is a two-stage framework: **(1) Training an Action Verifier** and **(2) Scaling Test-Time Compute (Deployment)**.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Training the Action Verifier

The core idea is to train a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based verifier that can evaluate the quality of a proposed robot action given the current state and task instruction.

- **Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline:** To avoid expensive human labeling, the authors propose an automated pipeline for generating synthetic action comparisons with preference labels.
 1. **Data Source:** An existing imitation learning dataset (e.g., Bridge V2) provides tuples of (state $s_t$, ground-truth expert action $a^*_t$, instruction $I$).
 2. **Candidate Action Generation:** For each tuple, a reference generalist VLA policy is used to generate $N$ candidate actions.
 3. **Clustering and Pairwise Comparisons:** These $N$ actions are then clustered to obtain $K$ representative actions, ensuring diversity. All $\binom{K}{2}$ pairwise comparisons are constructed from these $K$ actions.
 4. **Preference Labeling:** For each pair of actions $(a^i_t, a^j_t)$, a preference is assigned based on the Normalized Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) between each candidate action and the ground-truth expert action $a^*_t$. The action with the lower RMSE is labeled as the "winning" action ($a^W_t$), and the other as the "losing" action ($a^L_t$). This process creates a synthetic preference dataset $D_{comp}$.
- **Reward Modeling (Verifier Training):**
 1. **Verifier Architecture:** LLaVA-7B is used as the backbone VLM, with its final unembedding layer replaced by a reward head. This integrates a ViT-Large vision encoder and an MLP to map visual features.
 2. **Loss Function:** A modified Bradley-Terry model is used. The loss incorporates a margin component that accounts for preference levels. It minimizes the difference between the predicted preference level ($\hat{\Delta}_t = R_\phi(a^W_t, s_t, I) - R_\phi(a^L_t, s_t, I)$) and the ground-truth RMSE difference ($\Delta^*_t = \text{RMSE}(a^W_t, a^*_t) - \text{RMSE}(a^L_t, a^*_t)$). This margin term is found to improve accuracy, especially for distinguishing clearly different actions.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Scaling Test-Time Compute with RoboMonkey

During robot deployment, RoboMonkey employs a "generate-then-verify" pipeline to select robust actions.

- **Initial Action Sampling:** Given an observation ($s_t$) and instruction ($I$), RoboMonkey first samples $\hat{N}$ initial candidate actions from the base VLA model (e.g., OpenVLA) with a positive temperature $T$.
- **Gripper State Determination:** The discrete gripper action ($g_t$) is determined by majority voting among the gripper components of the $\hat{N}$ sampled actions.
- **Gaussian Perturbation for Proposal Distribution:** To efficiently generate a diverse set of action proposals, a Gaussian distribution $N(\mu_t, \Sigma_t)$ is fitted to the continuous translation and rotation components of the $\hat{N}$ initial actions. This "Gaussian perturbation" strategy is found to be more computationally efficient than repeatedly sampling from the VLA policy directly, while achieving similar performance to policy sampling in terms of action error reduction.
- **Augmented Action Sampling:** RoboMonkey then samples $\hat{K}$ new actions from this fitted Gaussian proposal distribution and appends the majority-voted gripper state $g_t$ to each. This forms a refined set of candidate actions $\tilde{A}$.
- **Verification and Selection:** Each action $\tilde{a}^i_t$ from $\tilde{A}$ is then scored by the pre-trained VLM-based action verifier $R_\phi(\tilde{a}^i_t, s_t, I)$. The action with the highest reward score is selected for execution.
- **Computational Efficiency:** To mitigate the overhead of repeated sampling and verification, the authors implemented a VLA serving engine on top of SGLang. This leverages KV Cache optimizations and batch processing, significantly speeding up inference for both the base VLA and the verifier.

This methodology combines scalable synthetic data generation, preference-based learning, and efficient inference techniques to effectively apply the test-time scaling paradigm to robotic control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the significant effectiveness of RoboMonkey across various evaluation scenarios.

- **Inference-Time Scaling Laws for VLAs:**
 - The paper establishes that, similar to LLMs, an "inference-time scaling law" exists for VLAs. Action error consistently decreases as the number of generated samples increases, following an exponentiated power law across a range of VLAs (OpenVLA, CogACT, Octo, SpatialVLA).
 - This effect is observed with various sampling methods: random sampling, policy sampling (repeatedly from the VLA), and Gaussian perturbation.
 - Crucially, **Gaussian perturbation** (sampling a few initial actions, fitting a Gaussian, and then sampling many from the Gaussian) achieves nearly identical performance to policy sampling but is significantly more computationally efficient. For OpenVLA, sampling 10,000 actions reduced RMSE by 59.3%.

- **Improved Precision on In-Distribution Tasks (SIMPLER Environment):**
 - RoboMonkey, when paired with OpenVLA, achieved an average success rate of **47.5%**, an **absolute improvement of 9%** over standalone OpenVLA (38.5%).
 - Specific tasks saw even greater gains; for instance, "put eggplant in basket" improved by 19%.
 - RoboMonkey consistently outperformed V-GPS, another verifier-based approach.

- **Enhanced Robustness on Out-of-Distribution Tasks (Real-World WidowX Robot):**
 - In real-world tasks involving unseen instructions, objects, distractors, and shapes, RoboMonkey delivered substantial performance gains.
 - It achieved an average success rate of **60%**, compared to 35% for OpenVLA and 30% for V-GPS, representing a **25% absolute improvement** over the base VLA.
 - RoboMonkey effectively mitigated common failure modes like imprecise grasping and collisions.
 - On tasks requiring strong visual and semantic generalization, such as "banana in basket" (where the robot had to differentiate between two yellow objects), OpenVLA achieved 0% success, while RoboMonkey significantly improved performance.

- **Scalability of Synthetic Training Data:**
 - Increasing the size of the synthetic action preference dataset (up to 20 million comparisons) consistently improved the RoboMonkey action verifier's performance and downstream closed-loop success rates on SIMPLER.
 - Task performance showed nearly log-linear growth with synthetic dataset size, highlighting the potential of large-scale synthetic data generation.

- **Adaptability to New Robot Setups (LIBERO-Long Benchmark):**
 - Fine-tuning both the VLA (OpenVLA) and the RoboMonkey action verifier on new tasks within the LIBERO-Long simulation environment yielded a **7% performance increase** in average success rate (56.5% vs. 49.8%) compared to fine-tuning the VLA alone. This demonstrates RoboMonkey's transferability and adaptability.

- **Practical Deployment and Efficiency:**
 - The authors implemented a VLA serving engine leveraging SGLang, which significantly optimized repeated sampling and batch processing.
 - Combined with Gaussian perturbation, RoboMonkey could sample and verify 16 candidate actions in approximately **650 ms (1.5 Hz)** on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU. This represents a 41.3% lower latency compared to naive policy sampling, making test-time scaling practically feasible.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboMonkey introduces a highly significant approach to enhancing the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several profound implications for the future of robotics:

- **A New Paradigm for Robot Control:** By demonstrating the existence of inference-time scaling laws for VLAs and effectively harnessing them through a "generate-then-verify" framework, RoboMonkey introduces a new paradigm in robot control. This shifts the focus beyond just pre-training and post-training optimizations to include intelligent compute allocation during deployment, mirroring successful strategies in Large Language Models. This perspective could fundamentally alter how robotic policies are designed and deployed.

- **Substantial Improvement in Robustness and Generalization:** The most immediate impact of RoboMonkey is its demonstrated ability to significantly improve VLA performance, particularly in challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) real-world tasks. Achieving a 25% absolute improvement on OOD tasks and mitigating common failure modes like imprecise grasping and collisions is a critical step towards deploying robots in complex, unstructured environments where current VLAs often struggle. This enhanced robustness is vital for practical, real-world robotic applications.

- **Scalable Verifier Training via Synthetic Data:** The proposed pipeline for automatically generating synthetic action preferences is highly impactful. It bypasses the prohibitive cost and scalability issues of human annotation, enabling the training of powerful VLM-based action verifiers on vast datasets (millions of comparisons). This makes preference-based learning a viable and scalable approach for improving robotic policies, opening avenues for continuously refining verifiers with more data.

- **Practical Deployment and Efficiency:** Addressing the computational overhead associated with test-time scaling is crucial for real-world applicability. RoboMonkey's optimizations, including the SGLang-based VLA serving engine and the efficient Gaussian perturbation sampling method, demonstrate that this approach can be made practically deployable within reasonable latency constraints (e.g., 1.5 Hz). This engineering contribution makes the theoretical benefits of test-time scaling accessible to real robot systems.

- **Foundational Implications for Generalist Robotics:** The findings suggest that existing generalist VLAs (like OpenVLA, Octo) can be made significantly more precise and robust with an external verification layer, rather than solely relying on improvements to the base policy itself. This separation of concerns—generating diverse candidates and then critically evaluating them—could be a cornerstone for building more reliable general-purpose robot foundation models that are less sensitive to distribution shifts and can adapt better to novel situations.

Despite its strengths, the paper also highlights future research directions. Reducing the computational overhead for high-frequency control tasks, scaling synthetic data generation to even larger, more diverse datasets across various robot embodiments, and broadening the evaluation scope remain important challenges. Nevertheless, RoboMonkey represents a significant advancement, offering a practical and effective pathway towards building more robust, general-purpose robotic systems through intelligent allocation of inference-time compute.
