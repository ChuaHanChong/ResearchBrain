# Beyond Imitation: Reinforcement Learning for Active Latent Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore developed Active Latent Planning (ATP-Latent), a two-stage method that combines a VAE-based Supervised Fine-Tuning stage with a Reinforcement Learning stage guided by a novel coherence reward. This approach enabled active reasoning in Large Language Models, leading to a 4.1% increase in average accuracy and a 3.3% reduction in token usage across mathematical reasoning tasks compared to imitation-based baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is computationally expensive and slow due to the generation of verbose language paths, leading to 'overthinking'.
- Existing latent reasoning methods primarily rely on imitating a single, arbitrary CoT trajectory, resulting in overfitted latent representations and suboptimal reasoning policies that do not generalize well.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to latent spaces has been challenging due to the lack of a smooth and well-defined state space, making effective exploration difficult and leading to unstable performance gains.

## Method

- ATP-Latent introduces a ==two-stage approach==: a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage followed by a Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage.
- The SFT stage utilizes a ==Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE)== to generate probabilistic latent tokens and a 'stop head' mechanism, creating a smooth, interpretable latent space with uniform information density suitable for RL exploration.
- The RL stage actively optimizes latent reasoning policies using a modified ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== framework, guided by a novel ==unsupervised coherence reward== that encourages logical, step-by-step reasoning.

## Results

- ATP-Latent achieved an average accuracy of **47.7%** and used **8.4** tokens across four mathematical reasoning benchmarks, representing a **4.1%** increase in average accuracy and a **3.3%** reduction in tokens compared to reimplemented SIM-CoT baselines.
- The RL stage contributed a **1.9%** accuracy improvement over the SFT-only version of ATP-Latent, confirming the effectiveness of active planning in optimizing latent reasoning policies.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the VAE, stop head, and RL stage are all crucial, and notably, the unsupervised coherence reward alone improved the SFT-only model by **0.9%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active planning through reinforcement learning can substantially improve latent reasoning policies, moving beyond the limitations of passive imitation and leading to more generalizable and robust solutions.
> - Designing a smooth and well-conditioned latent token representation space, achieved through a VAE framework and a stop-head mechanism, is critical for effective and stable RL exploration in LLM reasoning.
> - An auxiliary, unsupervised coherence reward, derived from the consistency of VAE-decoded latent tokens, provides a valuable signal for guiding RL towards optimal and logically coherent reasoning paths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper is positioned at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, and Reinforcement Learning (RL), with a specific focus on enhancing reasoning efficiency and generalizability through "latent planning."

The advent of CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022) significantly improved LLMs' reasoning abilities, particularly in complex tasks like mathematical reasoning. However, a major drawback of explicit language-based CoT is the generation of verbose reasoning paths, leading to increased latency and computational cost—a phenomenon often termed "overthinking" (Sui et al., 2025). This has spurred research into more efficient reasoning methods.

One promising direction is "latent CoT reasoning" (Chen et al., 2025b), which aims to condense the reasoning process into a continuous, high-dimensional latent space, represented by "latent tokens," rather than discrete natural language tokens. This approach promises higher information density per token and reduced inference costs. Existing latent reasoning methods, such as Coconut (Hao et al., 2024), SIM-CoT (Wei et al., 2025), and CoLaR (Tan et al., 2025), primarily rely on *imitation-based* strategies. They fine-tune LLMs to mimic human-annotated CoT labels, mapping parts of language CoTs to latent tokens. While successful in reducing token costs, these imitation methods suffer from a critical limitation: they typically learn from a single, arbitrary correct CoT trajectory for a given problem. As the paper highlights (Figure 1a), multiple equivalent but diverse CoT paths can exist for a single question. Passive imitation of one such path can lead to overfitted latent representations and biased policies, resulting in suboptimal reasoning performance and a gap between training and testing.

Furthermore, applying RL to latent reasoning is a relatively nascent but challenging area. Prior attempts (e.g., Butt et al., 2025; Tan et al., 2025) often introduce Gaussian noise for exploration but struggle with maintaining a smooth and well-defined latent state space, leading to unstable or limited performance gains. The paper specifically notes that a more advanced method like SIM-CoT, despite having better latent space supervision, surprisingly fails to show stable benefits with direct RL (Figure 3), potentially due to "sharpening" the latent token representation space, thus limiting exploration.

"Beyond Imitation" directly addresses these two major challenges. It proposes to overcome the limitations of passive imitation by introducing *active planning* via RL. It tackles the RL stability problem by carefully structuring the latent space through a Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE) and a stop-head mechanism. This work thus seeks to push the boundaries of efficient LLM reasoning by enabling LLMs to not just imitate but actively *explore and optimize* their reasoning processes in a continuous, interpretable, and well-constrained latent space, making a significant contribution to the evolving landscape of generalizable and efficient AI reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations of current state-of-the-art LLM reasoning paradigms, particularly within the context of "latent Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) methods.

**Motivation:**
1. **Inefficiency of Traditional CoT:** While explicit language-based CoT improves reasoning, it often generates excessively long reasoning paths ("overthinking"), which is computationally expensive and slow, hindering real-time applications of LLMs.
2. **Limitations of Imitation-Based Latent Reasoning:** Existing latent reasoning methods reduce token cost by shifting reasoning to a continuous latent space. However, they are typically trained by imitating *a single, arbitrary* language CoT label (Figure 1b). The core issue is that many equivalent but diverse CoT paths can exist for a single problem (Figure 1a). Passively imitating one specific path leads to:
 - **Overfitted latent token representations:** The model learns a rigid mapping tied to specific examples, rather than general reasoning principles.
 - **Biased and suboptimal latent reasoning policies:** The model cannot explore alternative, potentially more robust or efficient, reasoning trajectories.
 - **Performance gaps between training and testing:** The learned policy may not generalize well to unseen or slightly different reasoning contexts.
3. **Challenges in Applying RL to Latent Reasoning:** While RL offers a path to active planning, previous attempts to apply it to latent tokens have been largely unsuccessful in demonstrating clear accuracy improvements (Tan et al., 2025). This is attributed to two main problems:
 - **Lack of a Smooth State Space:** The latent token representation space, without proper regularization, may be too "sharp" or ill-conditioned for effective RL exploration, hindering gradient-based optimization.
 - **Unconfined and Unsupervised Exploration:** Without meaningful guidance, RL-based exploration in a continuous latent space can be random and inefficient, failing to discover optimal reasoning policies.

### 3. Key Objectives

To address these motivations, the paper sets out the following objectives:
1. **Emphasize Active Planning:** Highlight the critical importance of actively planning and exploring diverse reasoning paths within the latent token representation space, moving "beyond imitation" of single labels.
2. **Develop a Smoother and More Expressive Latent Space:** Design a mechanism that provides a well-defined and smooth latent token representation space, making it conducive for effective RL exploration. This includes enabling probabilistic latent token generation.
3. **Facilitate Optimal Latent Reasoning Policy:** Leverage reinforcement learning to actively optimize latent reasoning policies, allowing the model to discover more generalizable and robust reasoning strategies.
4. **Introduce Meaningful Guidance for RL:** Incorporate auxiliary rewards beyond mere answer correctness to provide soft constraints and beneficial training signals for RL, guiding the exploration of the latent space and preventing undesirable shortcuts.
5. **Achieve Superior Efficiency and Accuracy:** Demonstrate that the proposed method can achieve higher reasoning accuracy with fewer latent tokens compared to existing baselines, thereby providing a more efficient and effective LLM reasoning paradigm.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Active Latent Planning (ATP-Latent)**, is a two-stage approach comprising a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage and a Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage. It aims to create a smooth and well-defined latent space for active exploration, guided by a novel coherence reward.

#### 4.1. SFT Stage: Establishing a Smooth and Interpretable Latent Space

The SFT stage extends existing imitation-based latent reasoning by framing the latent token generation process as a Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE)-like training, ensuring a smoother latent space for subsequent RL and providing interpretability.

- **VAE Framework:**
 - **Encoder (Reasoning LLM, parameter θ):** The primary LLM acts as the encoder, taking the question and previous latent tokens to predict new latent tokens. Crucially, instead of deterministic latent tokens, ATP-Latent predicts the *mean (μ)* and *standard deviation (σ)* of a Gaussian distribution for each latent token (Equation 6), from which the latent token is then sampled (Equation 7). This probabilistic generation is key for introducing variability and a smoother space for RL exploration.
 - **Decoder (Auxiliary LLM, parameter ϕ):** An additional LLM serves as a decoder. Its role is to reconstruct segments of the original language CoT label from the generated latent tokens (Equation 9). This provides interpretability and is later used to calculate the coherence reward.
- **Stop Head for Uniform Information Density:**
 - A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), termed the "stop head" (π_θ^s), is integrated into the encoder. At each step, it predicts whether to continue generating latent tokens or to stop.
 - This mechanism replaces the fixed-length latent token generation of prior methods. It helps ensure that each latent token encodes a comparable amount of information and unifies the latent space, preventing inconsistencies in information density and addressing the "self-extending ability" problem of previous methods (Table 4).
 - A Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss (`L_Stop`) is used to train this head (Equation 8).
- **Loss Function:** The overall SFT loss (`L_SFT`, Equation 11) combines:
 - **Encoder Loss (`L_Enc`):** Standard cross-entropy loss for predicting the remaining language CoT steps and the final answer based on the latent tokens (Equation 5).
 - **Decoder Loss (`L_Dec`):** Cross-entropy loss for the decoder to reconstruct the original language CoT segments (Equation 9).
 - **Stop Loss (`L_Stop`):** BCE loss for the stop head (Equation 8).
 - **KL Divergence Loss (`L_KL`):** A β-VAE-style regularization term that encourages the latent space distributions to be close to a prior (e.g., a standard normal distribution) (Equation 10). A very small `β` is used to prioritize reasoning over strict decoding reconstruction.

#### 4.2. RL Stage: Active Planning with Coherence Guidance

The RL stage leverages the smooth latent space established by the SFT stage to actively explore and optimize latent reasoning policies using a modified Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.

- **RL Framework:** The core is based on the GRPO objective (Equation 2), but adapted for latent tokens and incorporating the stop policy (Equation 12). The reparameterization trick (Equation 13) allows gradient flow through the sampled latent tokens.
- **Novel Coherence Reward (`R_Coh`):** This is a key innovation. It addresses the lack of supervision for latent tokens beyond the final answer.
 - **Mechanism:** The VAE decoder (`ϕ`), trained in the SFT stage, is used to translate each generated latent token back into its corresponding natural language explanation (`R'`).
 - **Calculation:** `R_Coh(L)` measures the consistency of these decoded explanations. Specifically, it counts the ratio of language equations whose results (Right-Hand Side, RHS) are consistently used as inputs (Left-Hand Side, LHS) in subsequent decoded steps or the final answer (Equation 15).
 - **Purpose:** This provides an *unsupervised but beneficial* signal to judge the "reasonableness" of the latent CoT. It guides RL towards policies where latent tokens genuinely contribute to step-by-step reasoning rather than forming shortcuts (Zhang et al., 2025b).
- **Composite Reward Function:** The final reward `f(L, A)` for RL combines:
 - **Answer Correctness (`R_Correct`):** Binary reward for the final answer's accuracy.
 - **Coherence Reward (`R_Coh`):** Weighted by 0.1, it positively scales the reward for correct answers that also exhibit high coherence.
 - **Format Reward (`R_Format`):** Binary reward for adhering to the expected output format (Equation 16).
 - An alternative unsupervised setting is also explored, where `R_Correct` is removed, relying only on `R_Coh` and `R_Format` (Equation 17).

By combining these elements, ATP-Latent aims to create a robust and adaptive latent reasoning system that can actively discover optimal and diverse reasoning paths, moving beyond the limitations of purely imitative learning.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct across four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, GAM-hard, MultiArith, SVAMP) demonstrate the effectiveness of ATP-Latent.

#### 5.1. Overall Performance and Comparison to Baselines

- **Superior Trade-off:** ATP-Latent achieves the strongest overall trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. It reaches an average accuracy of **47.7%** across the four benchmarks while using only **8.4 tokens** on average (Table 1).
- **Accuracy Improvement:** Compared to the reimplemented SIM-CoT baseline, ATP-Latent demonstrates a notable **+4.1%** increase in average accuracy.
- **Token Efficiency:** It also achieves **-3.3%** fewer tokens compared to the reimplemented SIM-CoT, indicating improved efficiency without sacrificing performance.
- **Consistent Competitiveness:** ATP-Latent is consistently competitive across all datasets, achieving a particularly high **94.4%** accuracy on MultiArith.
- **RL Benefits:** When comparing with baselines boosted by RL fine-tuning (Table 3), ATP-Latent still demonstrates superiority. Its RL process yields a significant **+1.9%** improvement over its own SFT-only version, outperforming the RL-boosted Coconut + SIM-CoT by **+0.6%**. This highlights that the VAE, stop-head, and coherence reward collectively provide a better state space for RL.

#### 5.2. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies confirm the contribution of each proposed component (Table 1):
- **Importance of VAE:** Removing the VAE (i.e., deterministic latent tokens without `σ_t` prediction and KL loss) leads to an average accuracy drop of **-0.5%**, showing that the VAE framework is crucial for maintaining a smooth latent token representation space.
- **Importance of Stop Head:** Removing the stop head (reverting to fixed-length generation) results in an average accuracy drop of **-0.8%**, validating its role in achieving a more uniform and smooth latent space.
- **Importance of RL Stage:** Removing the RL stage (ATP-Latent-SFT model) leads to the largest degradation, an average **-1.9%** accuracy drop. This strongly emphasizes the significance of active planning via RL for improving latent reasoning policies.
- **Unsupervised Coherence Reward:** Using only the unsupervised coherence reward during RL training (without answer correctness) still improves the SFT-only model by an average of **+0.9%**. This is a powerful finding, demonstrating that coherence provides a useful, self-supervising signal for guiding latent reasoning policy optimization.

#### 5.3. Further Insights and Case Studies

- **Reward Function Impact:** Removing the coherence reward (`R_Coh`) in the RL stage leads to inferior in-domain performance (Figure 5a), underscoring its importance in guiding RL towards optimal and coherent latent reasoning policies.
- **VAE Training Smoothness:** The VAE training process in ATP-Latent shows smoother convergence on the validation loss during SFT compared to the SIM-CoT baseline (Figure 5b), indicating better regularization of the latent space.
- **Validity of Coherence:** The coherence reward correlates strongly with reasoning correctness. Correct examples exhibit a higher average group-normalized coherence (0.26) compared to incorrect ones (-0.33) (Figure 5c). This validates its utility as a proxy for reasoning quality.
- **Self-Extending Ability:** Unlike Coconut and SIM-CoT, ATP-Latent (both SFT and RL versions) demonstrates stable performance even when the maximum number of latent stages (tokens) is increased (Table 4). This showcases the effectiveness of the stop mechanism in handling variable reasoning lengths and mitigating instability issues.
- **Incentivizing Planning Capacity:** Pass@K evaluations (Figure 7) reveal that ATP-Latent, especially after RL, achieves significantly higher Pass@K values compared to baselines. This indicates that ATP-Latent's RL stage successfully incentivizes and develops a stronger capacity for active latent planning, enabling the model to explore and find a wider variety of valid reasoning paths for a given problem (Figure 8), leading to more generalizable solutions.

In summary, ATP-Latent effectively combines a VAE-based SFT stage for a smooth and interpretable latent space with an RL stage guided by a novel coherence reward. This synergistic approach leads to substantial improvements in both accuracy and efficiency for mathematical reasoning, addressing critical limitations of prior latent reasoning methods.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Beyond Imitation: Reinforcement Learning for Active Latent Planning" holds significant implications for the future development of efficient and robust Large Language Model reasoning.

#### 6.1. Overcoming Limitations of Imitation-Based Reasoning

The most immediate and impactful contribution is moving beyond the passive imitation paradigm prevalent in current latent reasoning methods. By recognizing that multiple correct reasoning paths exist and that imitating an arbitrary one leads to suboptimal, overfitted policies, ATP-Latent proposes an active learning framework. This shift is crucial for developing LLMs that are more adaptable and generalizable, capable of discovering optimal strategies rather than merely reproducing learned patterns.

#### 6.2. Enhancing Efficiency and Accuracy Simultaneously

ATP-Latent demonstrates a compelling achievement: it improves reasoning accuracy (average +4.1%) while simultaneously reducing the number of generated tokens (average -3.3%). This dual benefit is critical for practical LLM applications, especially those requiring real-time responses or operating under computational constraints. By achieving dense chain-of-thought reasoning in a continuous latent space, the method paves the way for more performant and deployable reasoning LLMs.

#### 6.3. Novel Framework for Reinforcement Learning in Latent Spaces

Applying reinforcement learning to continuous latent spaces for reasoning has been challenging due to issues with state space smoothness and effective reward signals. ATP-Latent addresses these with two key innovations:
- **VAE for Smooth Latent Exploration:** The integration of a VAE framework during SFT provides a probabilistically defined and smoother latent representation space. This is essential for RL algorithms to effectively explore and optimize policies, circumventing the "sharpening" problem observed in prior methods like SIM-CoT.
- **Coherence Reward for Unsupervised Guidance:** The introduction of an auxiliary coherence reward, derived from the consistency of VAE-decoded latent tokens, is a highly significant development. It provides a powerful, unsupervised training signal that guides the RL process towards more logical and step-by-step reasoning, preventing shortcuts and ensuring that latent tokens genuinely contribute to the reasoning process. This concept could be broadly applicable to other complex sequential decision-making tasks within LLMs.

#### 6.4. Improved Generalizability and Robustness

The ability of ATP-Latent to develop stronger "planning capacities" and explore diverse, valid reasoning paths (as evidenced by Pass@K evaluations and branch probability trees) translates directly into improved generalizability. Furthermore, the stop-head mechanism effectively solves the "self-extending ability" issue, ensuring stable performance across varying reasoning lengths, making the model more robust in deployment scenarios.

#### 6.5. Foundations for Future Research

ATP-Latent provides a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
- **Broader Task Application:** The authors explicitly mention plans to implement the method on more tasks, such as natural language and complex LaTeX reasoning. This indicates the potential for wide applicability of the core ideas beyond mathematical reasoning.
- **Interpretability of Latent Spaces:** The VAE decoder enables optional interpretability of latent tokens, allowing researchers and developers to gain insights into the model's internal reasoning steps. This interpretability is vital for debugging, understanding model failures, and building trust in complex AI systems.
- **Unsupervised Reasoning Improvement:** The success of the coherence reward as an unsupervised signal opens avenues for reducing reliance on expensive human-annotated CoT labels for RL, potentially making training more scalable and cost-effective.

In conclusion, "Beyond Imitation" represents a significant step forward in making LLMs not only more intelligent but also more efficient and robust in their reasoning capabilities. By actively shaping and exploring the latent space with intelligent reward signals, ATP-Latent pushes the boundaries of what's achievable in LLM-based reasoning, with clear implications for both research and practical applications.
