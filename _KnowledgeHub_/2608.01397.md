# SG-WAM: Self-Guided World Modeling in Geometry-Aware Policy Space

> [!summary] Summary
> SG-WAM develops a self-guided World Action Model that integrates geometry-aware future prediction directly into a policy's action generation, learning action-conditioned dynamics within its own representation space. It attained 98.5% average success on LIBERO and 73.0% on LIBERO-Plus for zero-shot out-of-distribution tasks, demonstrating improved robustness and generalization with a smaller model than many baselines.

## Problem

- Observation-space World Action Models (WAMs) often suffer from a high perceptual burden, dedicating excessive capacity to reconstructing irrelevant visual details instead of action-induced dynamics.
- Latent WAMs frequently encounter a target-policy mismatch, where prediction targets from auxiliary or externally defined latent spaces do not align well with the policy's action generation representations.
- Existing policy-coupled WAMs lack fine-grained geometric awareness, making it challenging for them to precisely capture 'where' and 'how' robot actions modify the scene.

## Method

- A ==self-guided world predictor (SGWP)== forecasts future latent states from current dynamics tokens and intervening actions, with prediction targets generated by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) copy of the online policy components.
- Geometric supervision is incorporated using a frozen 3D foundation model (VGGT-1B) to align the Vision-Language Model's visual tokens with spatially rich geometric features, grounding the policy's representations.
- A ==conditional flow-matching== action expert generates H-step action chunks, directly conditioned on the geometry-shaped visual tokens and dynamics tokens, ensuring action generation benefits from a spatially aware and future-predicting latent space that defines **SG-WAM**.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.5%** across the four standard LIBERO suites, matching or exceeding strong baselines despite utilizing a substantially smaller **0.9B** model and requiring no large-scale embodied pretraining.
- Demonstrated superior zero-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) transfer with the highest overall success rate of **73.0%** on the challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark.
- Consistently outperformed both VPP and VLA-JEPA baselines in real-world in-distribution (ID) and OOD tasks (e.g., Pick and Place, Towel Folding, Toolbox Organization), showing improved execution stability and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning action-conditioned dynamics within a policy's self-derived and geometry-aware representation space significantly enhances a robot's robustness and generalization capabilities in manipulation tasks.
> - Both self-guided world modeling and explicit geometric supervision provide complementary benefits, with the former having a larger impact on long-horizon tasks and the latter crucial for grounding policy representations in relevant spatial structures.
> - An optimal, moderate number of learnable dynamics tokens is crucial for efficiently representing complex interaction dynamics, as demonstrated by varied performance with different token counts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of robot manipulation, specifically focusing on World Action Models (WAMs). WAMs represent a paradigm shift from traditional policies by integrating action generation with explicit prediction of future environmental states. This future-oriented supervision is intended to encourage policy representations to capture action-induced dynamics more comprehensively than action imitation alone.

Existing WAMs broadly fall into two categories: observation-space and latent-space models. Observation-space approaches, such as those predicting future images, videos, or depth maps, provide dense supervision of scene evolution. However, they often require significant modeling capacity to reconstruct perceptual details (e.g., texture, background, viewpoint variations) that may be minimally relevant to manipulation tasks. This can divert computational resources from learning crucial action-induced dynamics.

Latent WAMs aim to overcome this by modeling future information in a compact representation space, thereby reducing the burden of reconstructing irrelevant appearance details. While this addresses the perceptual overhead, many latent WAMs still rely on externally defined or auxiliary target representations for future supervision. This can lead to a mismatch between the information prioritized by the predictive objective and the representations directly used for action generation by the action expert. Consequently, such models may not fully preserve the fine-grained spatial information critical for precise manipulation, as their geometric understanding often derives from perceptual reconstruction, predefined feature spaces, or auxiliary targets, rather than being intrinsically coupled to the policy's action-generation process.

SG-WAM seeks to address these limitations by proposing a framework that learns action-conditioned dynamics directly within the policy-derived representation space, ensuring this space is both aligned with action generation and explicitly structured for geometry awareness. This approach aims to create a future-modeling space that is intrinsically linked to action generation and capable of retaining the spatial information necessary to understand *where* and *how* actions modify the scene.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust World Action Model (WAM) for robot manipulation that overcomes the limitations of existing approaches by integrating geometry-aware future prediction directly into the policy's action generation mechanism.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges identified in current WAMs:
- **Perceptual Burden in Observation-Space WAMs:** Models that predict future images or videos often dedicate substantial capacity to capturing intricate perceptual details like textures, lighting, and background elements. While these details contribute to scene understanding, many are often tangential to the core dynamics of robot-environment interaction. This can lead to inefficient learning, as a significant portion of the model's resources is consumed by reconstruction tasks rather than focusing on manipulation-relevant state changes. The objective is to reduce this perceptual burden by shifting prediction to a more abstract, action-relevant space.
- **Target-Policy Mismatch in Latent WAMs:** Although latent WAMs mitigate perceptual overhead, they frequently rely on auxiliary or externally defined latent spaces for future prediction. This introduces a potential disconnect: the representations optimized for prediction might not perfectly align with, or be sufficiently coupled to, the representations that the action expert uses for generating actions. This mismatch can hinder the policy's ability to effectively leverage future-oriented supervision to refine its action-generation capabilities. The motivation is to create a seamless integration where prediction targets are derived from the same representational family used for action generation.
- **Lack of Geometry Awareness in Policy-Coupled Spaces:** While reducing perceptual burden and aligning latent spaces are important, successful robot manipulation fundamentally depends on precise spatial understanding – knowing *where* and *how* actions will impact the scene. Existing WAMs often lack a direct mechanism to structure their policy-coupled future-alignment space with fine-grained geometric information. They might capture "what" changes but struggle with the "where" and "how" due to insufficient spatial grounding within the action-relevant representations. The central challenge and key motivation for SG-WAM is to directly inject and maintain geometry awareness within the policy's internal representation space to ensure that the predicted dynamics are spatially meaningful for manipulation.

In summary, SG-WAM is motivated by the need for a WAM that combines the advantages of latent future prediction with explicit geometric grounding, all within a unified representation space that is directly utilized by the action expert for action generation. This aims to foster policies that are efficient, robust, and capable of spatially aware manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SG-WAM is structured as a self-guided framework that learns geometry-aware action-conditioned dynamics directly within the policy's representation space. The core design involves three interconnected elements: self-guided future prediction, geometric supervision, and conditional flow-matching for action generation, all optimized end-to-end within a shared Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone.

**Overall Framework:**
The system utilizes a 0.9B Qwen3.5-0.8B model as its VLM backbone, which jointly processes multi-view observations ($V_t$), language instructions ($L$), and learnable dynamics tokens ($Q$). This processing yields contextualized token representations $H_t = [H_{V_t}, H_L, H_{Q_t}]$, comprising visual, language, and dynamics token states.

**Learnable Dynamics Tokens:**
SG-WAM introduces $N_q$ learnable dynamics tokens ($Q$) which are appended to the visual and language token sequences before being fed into the VLM. After VLM processing, these become $H_{Q_t}$, serving as a compact, action-relevant latent representation of the scene state. These tokens are designed to capture the essence of scene evolution under robot actions.

**Self-Guided World Predictor (SGWP):**
The SGWP is central to the future prediction mechanism. It takes the current dynamics-token states ($H_{Q_t}$) and an intervening robot action sequence ($A_t^{(\Delta)}$) to forecast their future latent states.
1. **Current State Projection:** The current dynamics-token states ($H_{Q_t} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times D}$) are first projected into a compact latent prediction space ($z_{Q_t} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times d}$) by a projector $P_\phi$.
2. **Action Encoding:** The intervening action sequence ($A_t^{(\Delta)} = [a_t, \dots, a_{t+\Delta-1}]$) is encoded by an action encoder $A_\eta$ into an action-token sequence ($e_{A_t}$). This preserves the temporal order of actions.
3. **Future Prediction:** The SGWP ($F_\psi$), which consists of self-attention (for dynamics tokens) and cross-attention (dynamics tokens attending to action tokens), takes $z_{Q_t}$ and $e_{A_t}$ to predict the future dynamics representation ($\hat{z}_{Q_{t+\Delta}}$).
4. **Target Generation (EMA):** Crucially, the prediction target ($\bar{z}_{Q_{t+\Delta}}$) is generated by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) copy of the *same* online policy components (VLM, projector $P_\phi$, and learnable dynamics tokens $Q$). This EMA policy processes the future observation ($V_{t+\Delta}$) and language instruction ($L$) to produce $\bar{z}_{Q_{t+\Delta}}$. The EMA update rule ($\bar{\Theta} \leftarrow \mu \bar{\Theta} + (1-\mu)\Theta$) ensures stable, self-consistent targets derived from the policy's own representation family, minimizing target-policy mismatch.
5. **Prediction Loss:** The Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective, $L_{pred} = \frac{1}{N_q d} \|\hat{z}_{Q_{t+\Delta}} - \text{sg}(\bar{z}_{Q_{t+\Delta}})\|_F^2$, is used, where $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ denotes the stop-gradient operation to prevent gradients from updating the EMA target pathway.

**Geometric Supervision:**
To ensure the policy's internal representation retains spatial awareness, SG-WAM incorporates geometric supervision.
1. **Geometric Teacher:** A frozen 3D foundation model, VGGT-1B, acts as an auxiliary teacher. It processes the main-view observation ($V^m_t$) to extract spatially pooled geometric features ($Z^G_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N^m_v \times D_g}$).
2. **Policy Alignment:** A lightweight projector ($G_\gamma$) maps the VLM's main-view visual token states ($H^m_{V_t} \in \mathbb{R}^{N^m_v \times D}$) to the teacher's feature dimension ($\hat{Z}^G_t$).
3. **Geometric Loss:** A cosine similarity objective, $L_{geo} = \frac{1}{N^m_v} \sum_{j=1}^{N^m_v} (1 - \cos(\hat{Z}^G_{t,j}, Z^G_{t,j}))$, aligns $\hat{Z}^G_t$ with $Z^G_t$. This shapes the VLM's visual tokens with spatial structure, providing a grounded context for the dynamics tokens.

**Conditional Flow-Matching Action Expert:**
The action expert is conditioned on the complete contextualized VLM states ($H_t = [H_{V_t}, H_L, H_{Q_t}]$), ensuring that the geometry-shaped visual tokens and the dynamics tokens (optimized by future prediction) directly influence action generation. It utilizes conditional flow matching to generate an H-step action chunk, optimized via $L_{act} = \mathbb{E}_{A_t, \epsilon, \tau} [\|v_\omega(A^\tau_t, \tau, H_t) - (A_t - \epsilon)\|_2^2]$.

**Training Objective and Inference:**
The model is trained end-to-end with a combined loss $L = L_{act} + \lambda_{geo}L_{geo} + \lambda_{pred}L_{pred}$, where $\lambda_{geo}$ and $\lambda_{pred}$ are weighting coefficients. During inference, the frozen geometry teacher, the SGWP prediction branch, and the EMA target pathway are removed, leaving a streamlined online VLM, learnable dynamics tokens, and action expert. All trainable online components are jointly optimized in a single training stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SG-WAM was conducted across both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating its performance, robustness, and generalization capabilities compared to various baselines.

**Simulation Results (LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus):**
- **LIBERO (In-distribution performance):** SG-WAM achieved an average success rate of 98.5% across the four standard LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). This performance matched or exceeded strong baselines, despite SG-WAM utilizing a substantially smaller 0.9B model and requiring no large-scale embodied pretraining, unlike many competitor models (which often ranged from 3B to 7B parameters with extensive pretraining).
- **LIBERO-Plus (Zero-shot out-of-distribution transfer):** In this challenging benchmark designed to test robustness under various distribution shifts (camera viewpoint, robot embodiment, language, illumination, background, observation noise, scene layout), SG-WAM achieved the highest overall success rate of 73.0%. It demonstrated superior robustness by outperforming baselines that saw substantial performance drops under these perturbations. SG-WAM achieved the best performance in camera, language, illumination, and layout shifts, while remaining competitive in others. This suggests its learned policy representations are less sensitive to variations in visual and linguistic input distributions.

**Real-World Experimental Results:**
- **Tasks:** Evaluation included three in-distribution (ID) tasks: Pick and Place, Towel Folding, and Toolbox Organization. Out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization was assessed under Background Shift, Light Change, and Novel Object conditions.
- **Baselines:** SG-WAM was compared against VPP (an explicit WAM) and VLA-JEPA (an implicit latent WAM).
- **Performance:** SG-WAM consistently outperformed both VPP and VLA-JEPA in both ID and OOD conditions across all three tasks. This advantage was maintained even for the long-horizon Toolbox Organization task, which is prone to compounding errors. Subtask analysis further revealed SG-WAM's stronger performance in sequential deformable-object manipulation (Towel Folding) and in the later, more complex stages of multi-step tasks (Toolbox Organization). This indicates improved execution stability and robustness to unseen real-world conditions.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Geometric Supervision and Self-Guided World Modeling (Table 4):**
 - The full SG-WAM model (with both components) achieved 98.5% average success on LIBERO.
 - Removing self-guided world modeling reduced the average success rate by 1.9 percentage points (to 96.6%), with a notable drop on LIBERO-Long (from 96.2% to 92.2%).
 - Removing geometric supervision resulted in a 0.9 percentage point reduction (to 97.6%).
 - Removing both components led to an average success of 95.3%.
 - This indicates that both components contribute positively, with world modeling showing a larger impact, particularly for tasks requiring coherent long-horizon state transitions. They provide complementary benefits.
- **Number of Dynamics Tokens (Table 5):**
 - Performance varied non-monotonically with the number of dynamics tokens. Eight tokens yielded the best average success rate (98.5%). Increasing to 16 tokens or decreasing to 1 or 4 tokens resulted in lower performance, suggesting that a moderate number of tokens is optimal for representing interaction dynamics.
- **Qualitative Attention Maps (Figure 4 & 10):**
 - With geometric supervision, dynamics tokens exhibited more consistent attention to manipulation-relevant regions (robot end-effector, manipulated object, target receptacle).
 - Without geometric supervision, attention was often more dispersed or focused on visually salient but less task-critical objects. This suggests that geometric supervision effectively grounds policy representations in relevant spatial structures.
- **Effect of Intervening Action Conditioning (Table 7):**
 - Replacing the ground-truth intervening action sequence with an all-zero (null-action) sequence reduced the average success rate from 98.5% to 97.6%. This effect was most pronounced on LIBERO-Long tasks (1.6 percentage point reduction). This demonstrates that the specific action information provided to the SGWP contributes to more accurate prediction of future latent states, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SG-WAM represents a methodological advancement in the development of World Action Models for robot manipulation. By explicitly addressing the limitations of prior WAMs through self-guided world modeling and geometric grounding within a shared policy representation space, this work demonstrates a viable approach to learning robust and generalizable robotic policies.

The significance of SG-WAM is evidenced by several key aspects:
- **Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** SG-WAM achieves high performance (98.5% average success on LIBERO) with a comparatively compact 0.9B-parameter model and notably without reliance on large-scale embodied pretraining. This contrasts with many leading baselines that often require larger models and extensive pretraining, suggesting a more efficient learning paradigm for visuomotor policies.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The superior zero-shot transfer performance on LIBERO-Plus (73.0% overall success rate) and consistent outperformance in real-world evaluations under various out-of-distribution conditions (e.g., background shifts, light changes, novel objects) indicate that SG-WAM's learned representations are less susceptible to superficial visual cues and more focused on manipulation-relevant dynamics. This robustness is critical for deploying robotic systems in diverse and uncontrolled environments.
- **Improved Spatial and Temporal Coherence:** The integration of geometric supervision directly shapes the policy's visual tokens, allowing dynamics tokens to be informed by manipulation-relevant spatial structures. Coupled with self-guided future prediction, this enables the policy to model action-conditioned state transitions that are both spatially grounded and temporally coherent, particularly beneficial for long-horizon and multi-stage tasks where errors can accumulate. The ablation studies confirmed the complementary benefits of both components.
- **Unified and Streamlined Architecture:** By jointly optimizing latent future prediction, geometric grounding, and flow-matching action generation end-to-end within a single VLM backbone, SG-WAM creates a unified framework. This avoids separate pretraining stages or complex architectural overlays, simplifying deployment as auxiliary components are removed during inference.

The potential impact of SG-WAM lies in advancing the capabilities of generalist robot policies. By demonstrating that effective, robust, and generalizable robot manipulation can be achieved by learning action-conditioned dynamics in a policy-coupled, geometry-aware latent space, this research opens avenues for:
- **More Capable Robotic Systems:** Enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks more reliably in varied real-world settings.
- **Reduced Training Overhead:** Potentially lowering the computational resources and dataset sizes required for training high-performing manipulation policies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Providing a strong architectural and methodological foundation for exploring more complex tasks, diverse embodiments, and scalable world-modeling approaches in robotics.

Future work outlined by the authors includes scaling this self-guided world-modeling framework to larger policy backbones and broader cross-embodiment datasets, aiming to further enhance its applicability to a wider range of manipulation tasks and robot platforms.
