# ABot-M0.5: Unified Mobility-and-Manipulation World Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ABot-M0.5 unifies mobility and manipulation within a World Action Model by addressing structural misalignments in temporal granularity, action space, and train-test consistency. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on mobile manipulation benchmarks and demonstrates effective transfer to real-world robotic systems with limited demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) often use coarse video chunks for prediction, obscuring fine-grained contact dynamics crucial for precise manipulation.
- WAMs typically model navigation and manipulation actions as a single, entangled space, leading to conflicts and suboptimal specialization for distinct behaviors.
- Inverse dynamics models in WAMs are usually trained on ground-truth futures but must infer actions from their own predicted futures, causing exposure bias and error accumulation during long rollouts.

## Method

- **ABot-M0.5** uses ==frame-level latent actions==, extracted from an ==ALAM-pretrained encoder==, to bridge the gap between macroscopic world evolution and step-level control.
- A ==Dual-Level Mixture-of-Transformers (D-MoT)== disentangles action spaces into dedicated ==manipulation and mobility subspaces==, reducing gradient interference and supporting specialized learning.
- **Dream Forcing** trains the action predictor on ==self-dreamed video latents==, aligning the training conditions with the inference conditions to mitigate the train-test gap.

## Results

- ABot-M0.5 achieved an average success rate of **54.2%** on the RoboCasa365 mobile manipulation benchmark in the target setting, outperforming leading VLA and WAM baselines.
- The model demonstrated strong performance on pure manipulation tasks, including **94.10%** success on RoboTwin 2.0 and competitive zero-shot transfer with **83.4%** on LIBERO-Plus.
- Real-world deployments on an Agilex Piper robotic arm showed effective sim-to-real transfer with limited demonstrations, achieving **70%** success in fine manipulation and up to **80%** on long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bridging the temporal granularity gap with intermediate latent actions, which are embodiment-agnostic representations of local visual state transitions, is critical for achieving fine-grained control.
> - Explicitly decoupling heterogeneous action spaces (mobility and manipulation) significantly improves learning efficiency, specialization, and coordinated behavior.
> - Aligning the action prediction training context with the autoregressive inference context through 'Dream Forcing' is crucial for robust long-horizon performance and reduced error accumulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Mobile manipulation represents a significant area of development for general-purpose robotic systems, requiring robots to navigate complex environments, perform precise object interactions, and maintain task coherence over extended durations. Current embodied learning methodologies, however, exhibit limitations in addressing these requirements.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, a prominent class of methods, typically operate reactively, lacking explicit world modeling capabilities or mechanisms for maintaining long-horizon memory. This reactive nature often renders them insufficient for tasks demanding proactive planning or adaptation to evolving environmental states.

An alternative approach involves World Action Models (WAMs), which aim to address these limitations by jointly modeling future observations and actions. While WAMs offer potential for long-horizon planning, existing implementations often possess structural misalignments with the specific demands of mobile manipulation. These misalignments include:
- **Temporal Granularity:** WAMs frequently operate on coarse video chunks for world prediction, which can obscure fine-grained contact dynamics crucial for precise manipulation.
- **Action Space:** They tend to model navigation and manipulation actions as an entangled, homogeneous space, leading to potential conflicts in action distribution and suboptimal specialization between distinct behavioral modalities (e.g., low-frequency base movement versus high-frequency arm control).
- **Train-Test Consistency:** Inverse dynamics models in WAMs are typically trained using ground-truth future observations, yet during autoregressive inference, they must condition on their own potentially imperfect predicted futures. This discrepancy can lead to exposure bias and error accumulation over long rollouts.

The presented work, ABot-M0.5, positions itself within this landscape by proposing an alignment-aware WAM framework specifically designed to address these identified structural mismatches. It aims to advance the capabilities of WAMs beyond stationary manipulation to encompass the complexities of mobile manipulation, thereby contributing to the development of more capable and adaptable general-purpose robots.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a World Action Model (WAM) that overcomes the inherent structural misalignments limiting the effectiveness of current embodied learning methods in mobile manipulation tasks. The motivation stems from three core bottlenecks identified in existing WAM approaches when applied to the unified challenge of mobile manipulation:

1. **Temporal Granularity Mismatch:** Mobile manipulation tasks, such as grasping objects or performing fine alignments, often depend on subtle, short-duration dynamics. Current WAMs typically compress future observations into coarse temporal chunks for computational efficiency in video prediction. This coarse representation can obscure critical fine-grained details, such as contact onset or grasp closure, making it difficult for the policy to infer the precise motion intent required for successful execution. The objective is to bridge this gap by enabling the model to transition from macroscopic world evolution to step-level control.

2. **Action Structure Mismatch:** Mobile manipulation necessitates the simultaneous control of a robot's base for navigation and its arm for manipulation. These two types of actions possess fundamentally different characteristics: base movements are generally low-frequency and global, while arm manipulations are high-frequency, local, and interaction-rich. When treated as a single, entangled action space, these conflicting dynamics can lead to interference during optimization, preventing the model from specializing effectively in either mode and hindering coordinated behavior. The motivation is to disentangle these heterogeneous action spaces to facilitate specialized learning and improve compositionality.

3. **Rollout Condition Mismatch (Train-Test Consistency):** During training, inverse dynamics models typically predict actions based on ground-truth future observations. However, at deployment time, the robot must act based on its own model-predicted future observations, which may contain errors, noise, or visual artifacts. This discrepancy, referred to as exposure bias, causes a distribution shift between training and inference, leading to error accumulation and degraded performance in long-horizon autoregressive rollouts characteristic of mobile manipulation. The objective is to align the training conditions of the inverse dynamics model with the conditions encountered during actual inference.

By systematically addressing these three bottlenecks, the research aims to provide a unified framework for world-action learning that is structurally aligned with the requirements of mobile manipulation, thereby enabling more robust, accurate, and long-horizon control for general-purpose robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ABot-M0.5 is a video-action World Action Model constructed upon the Wan2.2 video diffusion backbone. Its methodology is centered on achieving alignment at three levels: temporal granularity, action space, and train-test consistency. The overall design employs a hierarchical generation process: `z_t+1` (future video latent) → `m_t` (frame-level latent action) → `a_t` (executable robot action), where `z_t+1` captures macroscopic world evolution, `m_t` represents fine-grained motion intents, and `a_t` translates these intents into embodiment-specific controls. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) serves as the unified generative objective across all stages, ensuring that training-time information flow mirrors autoregressive inference.

The architecture processes three parallel token streams: video latents (`X_z`), latent actions (`X_m`), and executable actions (`X_a`), with asymmetric information flow enforced to reflect causal dependencies.

#### 3.1. Intermediate Latent Action Modeling (Temporal Alignment)

To bridge the temporal and structural gap between coarse video latents and low-level actions, ABot-M0.5 introduces frame-level latent actions (`m_t`). These `m_t` serve as an intermediate, embodiment-agnostic representation of local visual state transitions.
- **Latent Action Extraction:** `m_t` are extracted from consecutive video frames (`I_t`, `I_t+1`) using a frozen, pretrained latent action encoder (`E_m`). This encoder is trained using the ALAM framework, which learns transition embeddings by enforcing algebraic consistency constraints (additivity `L_add` and reversal `L_rev`) on triplets of temporally ordered observations. This approach allows `E_m` to be trained on large-scale, action-free video datasets, providing a shared latent space for physical interactions independent of robot morphology.
- **Conditional Flow Matching:** The generation of latent actions (`m_t`) is formulated as a CFM problem, with an objective `L_m` that supervises the model to synthesize high-fidelity latent actions based on anticipated world dynamics.

#### 3.2. Dual-Level Mixture-of-Transformers (D-MoT) (Action Space Alignment)

The D-MoT architecture disentangles heterogeneity at both modality and action levels.
- **Modality-Level Disentanglement:** Each of the three token streams (`X_z`, `X_m`, `X_a`) is equipped with its own input projection, timestep embedding, and output head. This maintains distinct representational spaces while allowing cross-modal reasoning through shared Transformer trunk self-attention.
- **Action-Level Disentanglement:** Within the executable action stream (`X_a`), the action vector `a_t` is explicitly decoupled into manipulation (`a_manip_t`) and mobility (`a_move_t`) subspaces. Each subspace has a dedicated feed-forward network (FFN) and prediction head, specializing learning dynamics and reducing gradient interference.
- **Structured Joint Attention:** Despite FFN decoupling, the model supports coordinated reasoning via joint self-attention over concatenated token streams, governed by causal and conditional masks.
- **Subspace-Aware CFM Supervision:** The final action generation is supervised by CFM, with objectives `L_move_a` and `L_manip_a`. Both branches receive the noisy action of the other as input, enabling cross-subspace coordination, and share a single denoising timestep for inference efficiency. The total action loss `L_a` is a weighted sum.

#### 3.3. Dream Forcing (Train-Test Aligned Action Prediction)

To address the train-test gap where action prediction during inference conditions on model-generated futures, Dream Forcing trains the action predictor on self-dreamed video latents.
- **Two-Phase Forwarding Strategy:**
 - **Phase A (Parallel Rollout):** Synthesizes the dreamed conditioning latents (`_z_t+1`, `_m_t`) using a few-step denoising procedure in a single forward pass, rather than a sequential rollout.
 - **Phase B (Dream Forcing):** Executes a second forward pass to predict actions, conditioning on the dreamed latents from Phase A, instead of ground-truth latents. This aligns the training context of action prediction with the inference context.

#### 3.4. Training Paradigm

A progressive training paradigm is employed:
1. **Pretraining Data:** A diverse corpus of large-scale public and synthetic robot datasets (e.g., OXE, Agibot-Beta, RoboCasa365, RoboMind, Galaxea, InternData-A1) is used, standardized into a unified format.
2. **World Model Pretraining:** The visual world model (Wan2.2 5B backbone) is pre-trained as an action-unconditioned future video predictor using CFM loss (`L_pretrain_z`). A fixed semantic slot allocation strategy handles heterogeneous camera configurations across datasets, masking out zero-padded views.
3. **Latent Action Model Pretraining:** The latent action encoder (`E_m`) is pre-trained using the ALAM framework on large-scale video data, as described in Section 4.1. This encoder is then frozen.
4. **Progressive Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Stage I (SFT1):** Joint World Model and Inverse Dynamics Fine-Tuning. The model jointly predicts future video latents, latent actions, and executable actions. Latent action and executable action predictions are conditioned on *ground-truth* future video latents (`z_t+1`) and latent actions (`m_t`). Total loss `L_SFT1` combines `L_z`, `L_m`, and `L_a`. This stage stabilizes initial action learning.
 - **Stage II (SFT2):** Dream Forcing for Inference-Aligned Fine-Tuning. The future conditioning `z_t+1` and `m_t` for action prediction are replaced with model-predicted (`_z_t+1`, `_m_t`) counterparts. This uses the modified action loss `_L_a` within `L_SFT2`. This stage aligns the action learning regime with autoregressive rollout conditions.

#### 3.5. Efficient Structured Attention and Latent Augmentation

To enhance efficiency:
- **Efficient Structured Attention:** Sparse attention patterns are reformulated into dense sub-problems and implemented with variable-length FlashAttention, leading to a significant speedup.
- **Offset-Based Latent Augmentation:** A variable starting offset `s` is introduced for indexing precomputed video latent features, increasing temporal diversity and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ABot-M0.5 was conducted across various challenging benchmarks and real-world scenarios to assess its performance in mobile manipulation, general manipulation, and transferability.

#### 4.1. Main Results on Mobile Manipulation (RoboCasa365)

On the RoboCasa365 benchmark, which focuses on long-horizon household tasks requiring both navigation and precise manipulation, ABot-M0.5 demonstrated superior performance.
- **Overall Performance:** ABot-M0.5 achieved an average success rate of 40.4% in the pretraining scenario and 54.2% in the target setting, outperforming leading VLA and WAM baselines such as RLDX-1 (33.2%), Qwen-RobotManip (35.9%), Fast-WAM (43.5% in target), and GR00T-N1.5 (43.7% in target).
- **Composite Tasks:** The model showed particular strength in long-horizon composite tasks, which involve more severe error accumulation during rollout. For example, in the pretraining scenario, it achieved 38.3% on Composite-Seen tasks, significantly higher than GR00T-N1.5 (14.8%) and RLDX-1 (27.5%).
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations indicated that ABot-M0.5 could maintain coherent task progression across navigation and manipulation phases, while preserving fine-grained control during object interaction.

#### 4.2. Results on Manipulation Benchmarks (RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO/LIBERO-Plus)

The model's capabilities were also assessed on benchmarks focused purely on manipulation, demonstrating its broader applicability.
- **RoboTwin 2.0 (Bimanual Manipulation):** ABot-M0.5 achieved 94.00% success in clean settings and 94.20% in randomized settings, resulting in an average of 94.10%. This exceeded the performance of baselines like Fast-WAM (91.85%), Lingbot-VA (92.24%), and Qwen-RobotManip (93.85%), indicating effective generalization to multi-task, high-dimensional manipulation, even without mobility components.
- **LIBERO / LIBERO-Plus (Compositional Tabletop Manipulation):** ABot-M0.5 achieved competitive or leading performance, with an average success rate of 99.4% on LIBERO. In zero-shot evaluation on LIBERO-Plus, designed to test robustness to variations, ABot-M0.5 achieved 83.4% total success, outperforming other WAMs like ImageWAM (83.1%) and Cosmos-Policy (82.2%).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to determine the contribution of each core component:
- **Intermediate Latent Action Modeling:** Removing latent actions consistently degraded performance. For instance, on RoboTwin 2.0 (Clean), a baseline without latent actions achieved 87.60%, while ABot-M0.5 with 3-Stage Separate latent actions achieved 94.0%. The precise integration and causality of latent actions were shown to be crucial for fine-grained control.
- **Action-Decoupled MoT:** This component improved performance on RoboCasa365 Composite-Seen tasks, achieving a success rate of 0.48 compared to 0.34 for a Modality-level MoT. It also demonstrated faster convergence, indicating reduced gradient interference between mobility and manipulation action spaces.
- **Dream Forcing:** Activating Dream Forcing for only 5k additional steps on RoboCasa365 Atomic-Seen (Target 100%) boosted the success rate from a 50k-step baseline of 67.55% to 70.56%. Continuing training without Dream Forcing resulted in stagnation or slight degradation. This confirms Dream Forcing's role in bridging the train-test gap.
- **Pretraining and SFT:** Pretraining proved crucial for sample efficiency. On RoboCasa365 Atomic-Seen (Target 10%), pretraining followed by SFT achieved 49.0% success, significantly higher than SFT alone (17.8%). Visualizations of attention maps showed that pretraining established stronger visual and interaction priors, which SFT then refined for task-specific semantics.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

ABot-M0.5 was deployed on an Agilex Piper robotic arm using only 50 real-world demonstrations per task, assessing sim-to-real transferability.
- **Fine Manipulation (Peg Cylinder):** Achieved 70% success and 96% process score, outperforming π0.5 (50% success, 90% process) and Fast-WAM (30% success, 77% process).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks (Organize Plate, Arrange Fruits, Cup Stacking, Arrange Flower):** Achieved success rates between 60% and 80%, with process scores above 88%. Fast-WAM, for comparison, achieved 20-40% success in these scenarios.
The results indicated effective transferability, precision in manipulation, and robust long-term execution, highlighting the benefits of the Dual-level MoT and Dream Forcing in practical deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ABot-M0.5 represents a notable advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the deployment of World Action Models (WAMs) for complex mobile manipulation tasks.

The research's primary significance lies in its systematic approach to addressing structural misalignments inherent in existing WAMs when applied to mobile manipulation. By identifying and resolving the temporal granularity mismatch through intermediate latent actions, the action structure mismatch through a dual-level Mixture-of-Transformers, and the train-test inconsistency through Dream Forcing, ABot-M0.5 demonstrates that enhanced performance stems not merely from scaling model capacity or data volume, but also from deliberate architectural and training design.

The achieved state-of-the-art performance across diverse and challenging benchmarks, including mobile manipulation (RoboCasa365), bimanual manipulation (RoboTwin 2.0), compositional tabletop tasks (LIBERO/LIBERO-Plus), and real-world robotic deployments, validates the efficacy of the proposed framework. The strong results in real-world experiments with limited demonstrations underscore the model's robustness and transferability, indicating a practical pathway towards deploying general-purpose physical agents.

The potential impact of ABot-M0.5 extends to fostering the development of more capable and adaptable robots for a wide array of applications. By enabling precise, long-horizon control that effectively coordinates navigation and manipulation, this work contributes to foundational capabilities required for general-purpose robots operating in unstructured environments. The detailed ablation studies provide insights into the critical roles of each component, informing future research on architectural design for embodied agents.

Looking forward, the research suggests a practical trajectory for scaling WAMs beyond controlled laboratory settings, emphasizing the need for robust memory mechanisms for open-ended tasks and efficient inference for real-time edge device deployment. This work contributes to bridging the gap between current embodied learning methods and the requirements for truly generalist robotic systems.
