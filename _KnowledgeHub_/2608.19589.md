# OrthoSkillVLA: Continual Skill Learning via Gradient-Informed Skill Subspace Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> OrthoSkillVLA is a replay-free continual learning framework for pretrained VLA models that applies module-specific gradient-informed orthogonal subspace budgets to the VLM and ActionHead, plus a frozen-base feature-aware MoE velocity decoder with training-free routing, cutting forgetting (NBT 0.46 to 0.13) and lifting final success rate from 56.61% to 83.50% on LIBERO skill-incremental tasks.

## Problem

- Architecture-based skill-isolation methods prevent forgetting but grow inference footprint and compute as more skills accumulate, limiting resource-constrained real-time deployment.
- Prior subspace-constrained gradient-projection methods impose a single unified orthogonality constraint across the whole network, ignoring that VLM and ActionHead have different representational sensitivity.
- Existing methods freeze or update the shared velocity decoder uniformly, either bottlenecking output expressivity or overwriting previously learned velocity mappings for earlier skills.

## Method

- ==Module-Aware Skill Subspace Estimation==: separate energy thresholds (epsilon_VLM=0.99, epsilon_Head=0.9999) bound gradient-derived orthonormal bases per module, giving VLM broader plasticity and ActionHead stricter velocity-pattern protection.
- ==Gradient-Informed Orthogonal LoRA==: initializes and freezes the down-projection matrix from top singular vectors of the gradient projected orthogonal to historical bases, training only the up-projection to confine updates away from prior skills.
- ==Feature-Aware MoE Velocity Decoder==: freezes the base decoder and allocates a lightweight per-skill expert, using training-free projection-based routing onto gradient-derived bases for skill-affinity expert selection at inference.

## Results

- On LIBERO skill-incremental benchmark (OpenClose, Turn, PickPlace), OrthoSkillVLA reaches Final SR **83.50%** versus KeepLoRA 56.61%, SeqLoRA 32.44%, and EWC 25.17%, while cutting NBT to **0.13** from 0.46.
- Real-world 7-DoF xArm experiments across four skills (Flip, Pick, Push, Press) show OrthoSkillVLA achieving **86.25%** average final success rate versus KeepLoRA's lower retention, a **15.0%** absolute gain after all four skills learned.
- Ablations show module-aware budgeting and MoE decoding are complementary: separated threshold alone lifts Final SR to **60.44%**, MoE decoder alone to **67.94%**, combined to **83.50%**; training-free router hits **83.5%** versus oracle routing's 86.2%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Different VLA submodules exhibit distinct subspace occupancy behavior under continual learning, so uniform orthogonality constraints are a mismatch; module-aware budgeting should generalize to other multi-component policy architectures.
> - Freezing an output-stage readout layer trades stability for an expressivity bottleneck that burdens upstream constrained adapters; isolating cheap per-task output experts resolves this tension at negligible parameter cost.
> - Gradient-accumulated subspaces double as both interference-avoidance directions and reusable routing signatures, enabling accurate skill identification without any trainable or explicitly supervised router.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a foundational technology for robot learning, demonstrating capabilities in predicting conditional velocity fields and capturing diverse action distributions. Models such as π0.5, GR00T, X-VLA, and SmolVLA have advanced robotic control through robust generative objectives and architectural efficiency. However, a significant challenge in deploying these models for lifelong learning is catastrophic forgetting. When VLA models are sequentially adapted to acquire new skills, parameter updates can interfere with the representations and velocity mappings established for previously learned skills, leading to performance degradation. This issue impedes the scalable and practical deployment of VLA systems in dynamic environments where continuous skill acquisition is required.

Existing approaches to continual learning in robotics generally fall into two categories:
- **Architecture-based methods**: These strategies alleviate forgetting by isolating skills into separate computational paths, often through adapter expansion, expert routing, or evolving skill spaces. While effective at skill isolation, these methods typically lead to architectural growth and increased computational footprints as more skills are acquired. This can be undesirable for resource-constrained real-time robotic systems.
- **Subspace-constrained methods**: These methods, often based on gradient projection, aim to mitigate forgetting by restricting parameter updates to subspaces that are approximately orthogonal to the directions important for prior tasks. This approach maintains a constant architectural footprint, offering a more parameter-efficient alternative. However, the paper identifies limitations in directly applying these methods to VLA models. Previous subspace-constrained techniques typically impose uniform constraints across the entire network, which fails to account for the heterogeneous roles of different VLA components.

Specifically, the authors highlight two VLA-specific challenges that are not adequately addressed by existing methods:
1. **Module-level representational heterogeneity**: VLA architectures typically consist of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for broad semantic feature extraction, an ActionHead for refining these semantics into localized velocity patterns, and a final velocity decoder. The VLM's broad representations make it susceptible to capacity exhaustion, whereas the ActionHead's precise velocity patterns are highly sensitive to perturbations. A uniform constraint across these modules creates a trade-off: a stringent constraint needed for the ActionHead rapidly consumes the VLM's finite adaptation capacity, while a relaxed constraint preserving VLM plasticity fails to protect the ActionHead's delicate patterns.
2. **Output-stage expressivity bottleneck**: The final velocity decoder functions as a critical output layer, mapping latent representations directly to physical velocity fields. Freezing this layer to prevent overwriting prior velocity mappings creates an expressivity bottleneck, shifting the burden of fitting heterogeneous velocity fields onto preceding adapters. However, these adapters are themselves confined by orthogonal subspaces, intensifying the stability-plasticity tension. Conversely, updating a shared decoder risks directly corrupting previously learned velocity mappings.

This work aims to address these specific limitations by developing a continual skill learning framework tailored to the unique architectural and functional characteristics of VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to enable pretrained VLA models to continually acquire diverse robotic skills without incurring catastrophic forgetting, all while maintaining parameter efficiency and a compact architectural footprint. The authors specifically aim to overcome the two VLA-specific challenges identified in the broader research landscape: module-level representational heterogeneity and the output-stage expressivity bottleneck.

The key objectives are:
- **To preserve previously learned skills**: The framework should minimize the degradation of performance on prior skills when new skills are introduced, a core aspect of continual learning.
- **To effectively acquire new skills**: The model must maintain sufficient plasticity to learn new behavioral patterns and integrate them into its existing repertoire.
- **To ensure parameter efficiency**: The solution should avoid the architectural growth associated with many existing continual learning approaches, making it suitable for real-world robotic deployment with limited computational resources.
- **To implement module-aware adaptation**: The framework seeks to account for the distinct functional roles and varying sensitivity of VLM and ActionHead components by applying differentiated constraints or adaptation strategies. This involves balancing the need for the VLM's semantic plasticity with the ActionHead's requirement for precise velocity pattern retention.
- **To address the velocity decoder bottleneck**: A flexible mechanism for the final velocity decoder is required to ensure sufficient expressivity for learning diverse velocity mappings without overwriting prior knowledge or introducing excessive architectural overhead. This should ideally involve a mechanism that is robust and simple to implement.
- **To operate without demonstration replay**: The framework is designed for a replay-free setting, which is often crucial in real-world scenarios where storing and replaying past data may be impractical or undesirable.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a more robust and scalable continual learning solution specifically for VLA models, enhancing their applicability in lifelong robotic learning scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OrthoSkillVLA is a parameter-efficient continual learning framework designed for pretrained VLA models. It operates by estimating skill feature subspaces using gradient information and then adapting new skill knowledge in directions orthogonal to previously occupied subspaces. The methodology specifically addresses the identified VLA-specific challenges through a module-aware constraint strategy and a dedicated output decoder.

The methodology consists of three main components:

#### 3.1. Skill Subspace Estimation with Module-Aware Budgets

For each adapted layer within the VLA model, OrthoSkillVLA maintains an orthonormal basis set ($M_{k-1}$) that spans the feature subspace occupied by all skills learned prior to the current skill $S_k$. This basis accumulates basis vectors from previous skills, with $m_i$ representing the number of basis vectors allocated for skill $S_i$.

When a new skill $S_k$ is learned, its accumulated gradient ($G_k$) is used to identify novel feature directions. First, $G_k$ is projected onto the orthogonal complement of $M_{k-1}$ using the formula $\hat{G}_k = G_k - \text{Proj}_{M_{k-1}}(G_k)$, where $\text{Proj}_X(Y) = XX^\top Y$. This filtering process isolates the parts of the gradient that do not interfere with prior skills.

Subsequently, Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is performed on the residual gradient ($\hat{G}_k = U_k \Sigma_k V_k^\top$). To manage the finite representational space and avoid excessive storage, only a subset of the left singular vectors ($U_{k,1:m_k}$) are retained as new basis vectors. The number of retained vectors, $m_k$, is determined by an energy threshold $\epsilon \in (0, 1)$, ensuring that the selected novel features reconstruct the current skill's feature space with sufficient fidelity (Equation 2). The basis set is then cumulatively updated: $M_k = [M_{k-1}, U_{k,1:m_k}]$.

Crucially, OrthoSkillVLA implements a **module-aware budgeting strategy**. Recognizing the representational heterogeneity between the VLM and ActionHead, separate energy thresholds are applied:
- A relatively relaxed threshold ($\epsilon_{VLM}$) for the VLM, to preserve its broader semantic plasticity over the long term.
- A more stringent threshold ($\epsilon_{Head}$) for the ActionHead, to ensure precise retention of its localized and sensitive velocity patterns. This differentiation allows for a more tailored allocation of adaptation capacity.

#### 3.2. Gradient-Informed Orthogonal Low-Rank Adaptation

OrthoSkillVLA employs a parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) mechanism. For each adapted linear layer, the parameter update ($\Delta W_k$) is parameterized as a low-rank decomposition $AB$. The goal is to constrain these updates to the orthogonal complement of the historical bases $M_{k-1}$.

At the beginning of acquiring skill $S_k$:
1. The initial gradient ($G'_k$) for the current skill is computed.
2. $G'_k$ is projected away from the historical bases $M_{k-1}$ using $\hat{G}'_k = G'_k - \text{Proj}_{M_{k-1}}(G'_k)$ (Equation 3). This step identifies the "novel" directions for adaptation.
3. The down-projection matrix $A$ of the LoRA adapter is initialized with the top-$r$ left singular vectors of $\hat{G}'_k$ and subsequently *frozen*.
4. During fine-tuning for skill $S_k$, only the up-projection matrix $B$ is optimized. This ensures that all updates are confined to the orthogonal subspace defined by $A$, minimizing interference with previously learned skills.

After skill $S_k$ is learned, the low-rank adapters are merged into the base model's weights. Then, the subspace estimation procedure (Section 4.1) is performed to update the historical basis $M_k$ with information from $S_k$.

#### 3.3. Feature-Aware MoE Velocity Decoder

To address the output-stage expressivity bottleneck, OrthoSkillVLA introduces a Feature-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Decoder for the final velocity output layer.
1. The pretrained base velocity decoder ($W_{base}$) is *frozen* to prevent direct corruption of velocity mappings.
2. For each new skill $S_k$, a lightweight, skill-specific expert ($W_k$) is allocated. The predicted velocity $v_k$ is then calculated as $v_k = x_k (W_{base} + W_k)$, where $x_k$ is the latent representation (Equation 4). This allows each skill to have an isolated parameter space for its specific velocity mapping.

A key challenge with MoE architectures is routing. OrthoSkillVLA employs a **training-free gradient-informed routing strategy** based on feature-space affinity:
1. After learning skill $S_k$, the accumulated gradient of $W_k$ ($G_{Dec_k}$) serves as an approximation of the feature space occupied by the velocity representation $x_k$.
2. The left singular vectors of $G_{Dec_k}$ ($U_{Dec_k}$) are extracted and associated with the expert $W_k$.
3. During inference, given a test latent representation $x$, the expert selection is determined by $k^* = \text{arg max}_{i=1...,K} \|xU_{Dec_i}\|^2$ (Equation 5). This means the expert whose basis has the highest projection magnitude from the input feature is selected.
This projection-based routing mechanism is designed to be computationally efficient and to introduce minimal inference latency and memory footprint, making it suitable for real-time deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of OrthoSkillVLA was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, complemented by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulated Experiments

- **Setup**: Experiments were conducted using a pretrained X-VLA model on the LIBERO benchmark, reorganized into a "skill-incremental" protocol. This protocol involved three skills (OpenClose, Turn, PickPlace) with pronounced differences in action distributions. Performance was averaged over three skill orderings.
- **Metrics**: Forward Transfer (FWT, ↑), Negative Backward Transfer (NBT, ↓), Area Under the Curve (AUC, ↑), and Final Success Rate (Final SR, ↑).
- **Baselines**: SeqLoRA, IncLoRA, EWC, OLoRA, and KeepLoRA (a strong subspace-constrained baseline that uses a unified energy threshold and freezes the decoder).
- **Results**: OrthoSkillVLA consistently outperformed all baselines. It achieved the highest FWT (0.94), lowest NBT (0.13), highest AUC (0.88), and highest Final SR (83.50%).
 - SeqLoRA and IncLoRA showed high FWT but severe forgetting (high NBT, low Final SR).
 - EWC partially mitigated forgetting but at the cost of new-skill acquisition (low FWT).
 - OLoRA provided limited gains, suggesting its soft orthogonality was insufficient.
 - KeepLoRA improved retention over standard PEFT methods but its unified constraints were sub-optimal for VLA architectures. OrthoSkillVLA significantly reduced NBT (from 0.46 for KeepLoRA to 0.13) and improved Final SR (from 56.61% for KeepLoRA to 83.50%), indicating that both module-aware subspace budgeting and the Feature-Aware MoE decoder contribute to superior continual learning.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Setup**: Experiments were conducted on a 7-DoF xArm with a 6-DoF Inspire dexterous hand. Four distinct manipulation skills (Flip, Pick, Push, Press) were used. Each skill involved 50 expert demonstrations and was evaluated over 20 independent trials.
- **Results**: OrthoSkillVLA demonstrated more stable performance as new skills were introduced compared to KeepLoRA. After learning all four skills, OrthoSkillVLA achieved an average final success rate of 86.25%, which was 15.0% higher than KeepLoRA's performance. The improvements were primarily attributed to better retention of earlier skills while maintaining effective acquisition of later ones, supporting the framework's ability to balance stability and plasticity with heterogeneous action distributions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Disentangling Module-Aware Budgeting and MoE Decoding**:
 - Comparison of four variants (Unified-Frozen, Separated-Frozen, Unified-MoE, Separated-MoE) showed that both module-aware budgeting and the MoE decoder are crucial.
 - Separated-Frozen improved slightly over Unified-Frozen (KeepLoRA), indicating the benefit of differentiated budgets.
 - Unified-MoE showed larger gains over Unified-Frozen, validating the MoE decoder's role in output flexibility.
 - Full OrthoSkillVLA (Separated-MoE) achieved the best performance (Final SR 83.50%), confirming the synergistic effect of both designs.
- **Validating Module-Aware Budgeting Strategy**:
 - Analysis of Subspace Occupancy Ratio (SOR) under unified thresholds revealed that the VLM consistently occupied a much larger proportion of the subspace than the ActionHead, confirming representational heterogeneity.
 - Varying $\epsilon_{Head}$ while fixing $\epsilon_{VLM}$ showed that a more stringent $\epsilon_{Head}$ (e.g., 0.9999) significantly improved Final SR (from 59.83% to 84.83%) by protecting ActionHead's localized velocity patterns.
 - Conversely, varying $\epsilon_{VLM}$ while fixing $\epsilon_{Head}$ demonstrated that excessively relaxing $\epsilon_{VLM}$ (e.g., 0.9) drastically reduced Final SR (from 85.50% to 66.17%) due to insufficient capacity for VLM's semantic plasticity. This highlights the importance of balancing budgets.
- **Analysis of Training-Free Routing Mechanism**:
 - The routing confusion matrix demonstrated high accuracy in skill identification (e.g., 98.9% for OpenClose and Turn, 91.5% for PickPlace).
 - Comparison with Oracle Routing (where ground-truth skill identity is provided) showed that the training-free router achieved an average success rate of 83.5%, close to the oracle's 86.2%. The slight performance gap was primarily observed in the PickPlace skill, correlating with its slightly lower routing accuracy. This validates the reliability and efficiency of the feature-aware routing.
- **Effect of LoRA Rank**: An ablation on LoRA rank ($r \in \{32, 64, 96\}$) indicated that $r=64$ provided the best trade-off, balancing plasticity for new skills with the prevention of forgetting. Both smaller ranks (less plasticity) and larger ranks (noisy updates, degraded retention) performed less optimally.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OrthoSkillVLA framework contributes to the field of robotic continual learning by specifically addressing challenges inherent to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The findings demonstrate the importance of architectural awareness in designing continual learning solutions, particularly for complex, multi-modal models used in robotics.

The primary significance of this work includes:
- **Addressing VLA-specific Challenges**: The empirical identification and subsequent methodological treatment of module-level representational heterogeneity between the VLM and ActionHead, as well as the output-stage expressivity bottleneck of the velocity decoder, are key insights. This targeted approach offers a more effective solution compared to generic continual learning methods.
- **Enhanced Continual Skill Acquisition**: OrthoSkillVLA exhibits superior performance in terms of retaining previously learned skills (low NBT) while effectively acquiring new ones (high FWT and Final SR). This capability is critical for deploying robots in dynamic environments that require lifelong learning.
- **Parameter Efficiency**: By leveraging gradient-informed orthogonal low-rank adaptation and a lightweight Mixture-of-Experts decoder, the framework maintains a compact architectural footprint. This is a practical advantage for resource-constrained real-time robotic control systems, as it avoids the progressive model growth associated with many architectural expansion methods.
- **Robust and Efficient Output Decoding**: The introduction of a Feature-Aware MoE Decoder with a training-free projection-based routing mechanism provides necessary output flexibility without introducing complex, trainable components that might themselves suffer from forgetting. This enhances the practical applicability and reliability of the system.
- **Validation in Diverse Settings**: The consistent performance improvements observed in both simulated LIBERO tasks with distinct action distributions and real-world manipulation experiments demonstrate the robustness and generalizability of OrthoSkillVLA across various scenarios.

The potential impact of this research is substantial for robotic learning and deployment. By mitigating catastrophic forgetting in VLA models, OrthoSkillVLA facilitates the development of more adaptable and versatile robotic agents capable of acquiring a broad repertoire of skills over time without requiring constant retraining or extensive data replay. This moves closer to the vision of lifelong robot learning, making VLA models more scalable and practical for real-world applications in areas such as industrial automation, service robotics, and assistive technologies. The principles of module-aware adaptation and efficient output-layer handling could also inform future research in continual learning for other complex, multi-component neural architectures.
