# ReMA: Learning to Meta-think for LLMs with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ReMA framework leverages Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning to foster meta-thinking in Large Language Models by decoupling reasoning into a high-level meta-thinking agent and a low-level reasoning agent. This hierarchical approach yielded superior generalization, especially on out-of-distribution tasks, and consistent performance gains across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with robust problem-solving and generalization, especially on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, due to limited 'meta-thinking' abilities.
- Existing methods for inducing meta-thinking in LLMs, such as construction-based supervised fine-tuning or single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), lack flexibility, suffer from inefficient exploration, and exhibit poor generalization to unseen problems.
- Single-agent RL approaches force the LLM to learn both meta-thinking and detailed reasoning within a single autoregressive pass, which can lead to inefficient exploration and premature convergence to local optima.

## Method

- Introduces **ReMA**, a novel Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework that formally defines and optimizes a ==Multi-Agent Meta-thinking== Reasoning Process (MAMRP).
- Decouples the reasoning process into two specialized LLM agents: a high-level Meta-thinking Agent for strategic oversight and planning, and a low-level Reasoning Agent for detailed execution.
- Trains the MAMRP as a Markov Game, iteratively optimizing the high-level and low-level policies using efficient RL algorithms like GRPO, incorporating parameter sharing and a turn-level ratio for stable and efficient training in multi-turn settings.

## Results

- ReMA consistently outperformed single-agent RL baselines and basic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution test sets, showing significant generalization gains (e.g., **20%** on AMC23 and **13.33%** on AIME24 for **8B** models).
- Multi-turn ReMA demonstrated effective convergence on training sets and achieved an average performance gain of approximately **5%** on out-of-distribution test benchmarks, indicating stable improvements.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the proposed turn-level ratio clipping significantly improved sample efficiency during multi-turn training, achieving higher rewards with fewer steps, and parameter sharing led to noticeably faster convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling meta-thinking and reasoning into hierarchical agents through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) leads to improved generalization, particularly on out-of-distribution tasks, by cultivating more robust problem-solving strategies.
> - Specific MARL optimizations, such as turn-level ratio clipping and parameter sharing, are crucial for stable and efficient training of LLMs in multi-turn, long-horizon tasks.
> - Reward function design can lead to emergent behaviors and even 'role reversal' in multi-agent LLM systems, highlighting the complex interplay and adaptability of specialized agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas in artificial intelligence, primarily large language model (LLM) reasoning, meta-cognition, and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL).

**LLM Reasoning and Scaling Test-time Computation:** The introduction correctly identifies a crucial paradigm shift in LLM development: moving beyond simply scaling training-time computation to increasingly emphasize scaling test-time computation. This involves allowing LLMs to "think" or generate intermediate reasoning steps before producing a final answer, mirroring human cognitive processes. Recent advancements like OpenAI-o1, Deepseek R1, and Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking exemplify this trend, demonstrating performance boosts through explicit reasoning patterns (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, CoT).

**Meta-thinking (Metacognition) in LLMs:** A key concept is "meta-thinking," defined as the ability for models to monitor, evaluate, and control their own reasoning processes. This is inspired by human metacognitive skills and is seen as critical for robust problem-solving and generalization, especially to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks. The paper positions ReMA as a direct attempt to cultivate these meta-thinking patterns from LLMs themselves.

**Limitations of Existing Approaches:** The paper critically reviews current methods for inducing meta-thinking in LLMs:
- **Construction-based supervised approaches:** These fine-tune LLMs on structured reasoning trajectories (e.g., via SFT or DPO) derived from predefined meta-thinking templates. While offering some structure, they are criticized for lacking flexibility, leading to poor generalization to OOD problems and unstable performance.
- **Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning (SARL):** While adopted by models like DeepSeek R1, SARL attempts often require strong foundational models or extensive task-specific fine-tuning. More importantly, SARL forces the LLM to learn both meta-thinking and detailed reasoning within a single autoregressive pass, which can lead to inefficient exploration, reduced readability, and premature convergence to local optima.

**ReMA's Unique Contribution:** ReMA directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel MARL framework. Its distinct approach lies in **decoupling the reasoning process into two hierarchical agents**: a high-level meta-thinking agent for strategic oversight and planning, and a low-level reasoning agent for detailed execution. This multi-agent system is designed to distribute the exploration space, allowing each agent to specialize and explore more efficiently. The authors claim to be the "first to formally define and optimize a multi-agent meta-thinking reasoning process (MAMRP) through multi-agent reinforcement learning," marking a significant step in developing more sophisticated and generalizable LLM reasoning systems. This work extends beyond simple multi-agent debates or static generative reward models by actively training a tunable LLM to provide metacognitive instructions, making it a dynamic, collaborative learning system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the ReMA framework stem from the observed shortcomings of existing approaches to enhance LLM reasoning, particularly regarding meta-thinking capabilities:

1. **Enhance LLM Reasoning through Meta-thinking:** The core objective is to significantly improve LLMs' performance on complex reasoning tasks by endowing them with effective meta-thinking abilities. This involves enabling models to monitor, evaluate, and control their own problem-solving processes, leading to more adaptive and effective solutions.
2. **Overcome Efficacy Issues in Single-Agent Meta-thinking:** Current single-agent methods are deemed "low efficacy" because they lack a specialized design for acquiring meta-thinking. ReMA aims to address this by introducing a structured, multi-agent approach that naturally separates and specializes meta-thinking generation from reasoning execution.
3. **Improve Generalization and Robustness to OOD Tasks:** Construction-based supervised methods struggle with out-of-distribution generalization. A key motivation for ReMA is to foster meta-thinking patterns that are flexible enough to generalize well to unseen problems, ensuring more robust performance.
4. **Boost Exploration Efficiency in RL-based Reasoning:** Single-agent RL approaches for meta-thinking often suffer from inefficient exploration due to the vast search space of combined meta-thinking and reasoning. By decoupling these roles into distinct agents within a MARL framework, ReMA aims to enable each agent to explore its respective action space more structurally and efficiently during training.
5. **Increase Interpretability and Control over Reasoning:** By explicitly separating meta-thinking and reasoning into hierarchical agents, the framework aims to improve the interpretability of the LLM's thought process. This structured decomposition can offer clearer insights into how the model plans and executes solutions.
6. **Extend to Multi-turn Interactions:** Beyond single-shot reasoning, the motivation includes scaling ReMA to multi-turn interaction settings, allowing for more fine-grained cognitive switching and complex, long-horizon planning tasks, which are critical for human-like reasoning.

In essence, ReMA seeks to leverage the benefits of multi-agent collaboration and reinforcement learning to cultivate robust, generalizable, and interpretable meta-thinking capabilities in LLMs, pushing the boundaries of what these models can achieve in complex problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReMA introduces a novel framework that leverages Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) to foster meta-thinking in Large Language Models (LLMs). The core idea is to decouple the complex reasoning process into hierarchical roles, managed by distinct AI agents.

#### 3.1. Meta-thinking Reasoning Process (MRP) and Multi-Agent Meta-thinking Reasoning Process (MAMRP)

The paper first defines the general **Meta-thinking Reasoning Process (MRP)** for a single agent, where an LLM autoregressively generates a meta-thought (`m`) followed by a reasoning response (`y`).
ReMA then introduces the **Multi-Agent Meta-thinking Reasoning Process (MAMRP)** as its core innovation. This involves two specialized LLM agents:
- **High-level Meta-thinking Agent (π_h):** Responsible for strategic oversight, generating plans, monitoring progress, and revising strategies (the "think about thinking" aspect). Its output (`m`) guides the lower-level agent.
- **Low-level Reasoning Agent (π_l):** Tasked with executing detailed problem-solving steps (`y`) based on the instructions provided by the high-level agent.

#### 3.2. Single-turn vs. Multi-turn MAMRP

- **Single-turn MAMRP:** A straightforward approach where the high-level agent generates a complete meta-thinking plan (`m`) upfront, and the low-level agent executes the detailed reasoning (`y`) based on that single plan. This simplifies the interaction but limits dynamic adjustments.
- **Multi-turn MAMRP:** An extension to handle complex, long-horizon tasks. The high-level and low-level agents act in an interleaving manner. The high-level agent generates `m_t` at turn `t`, and the low-level agent generates `y_t` based on `m_t` and the interaction history. This allows for immediate and fine-grained cognitive switching, resembling a more iterative human problem-solving process.

#### 3.3. Training MAMRP via Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)

The training of ReMA is framed as a Markov Game between the two agents, aiming to optimize a joint hierarchical policy.
- **Objective Function:** The overall objective `J(θ_h, θ_l)` is to maximize the final reward `R(y, y*)`, which compares the generated answer `y` to the ground truth `y*`.
- **Iterative Optimization:** The high-level policy (π_h) and low-level policy (π_l) are optimized iteratively, with one agent's parameters fixed while the other is updated, akin to block coordinate ascent. The paper utilizes efficient RL algorithms like GRPO (Group-normalized Proximal Policy Optimization) or REINFORCE++ for parameter updates.
- **Reward Design:**
 - **Correctness Reward:** A primary reward of +1.0 for a correct answer. Incorrect answers incur penalties (e.g., -0.5 for incorrect but formatted, -1.0 for unformatted).
 - **Format Reward:** Specific penalties are applied if agents fail to adhere to required output formats (e.g., high-level agent penalized for including a final answer).
 - **Consistency Reward (Optional):** An alternative reward for the high-level agent, encouraging it to produce plans that lead to consistent responses from the low-level agent, regardless of correctness. This implicitly rewards plans that lead to reproducible reasoning.
- **Prompt Filtering:** During training, a prompt filtering mechanism is used to select training data where models have a "challenging but not impossible" success rate (between `ε_min` and `ε_max`), ensuring effective learning.

#### 3.4. Scaling to Multi-turn MAMRP

To improve efficiency and stability for multi-turn settings, ReMA introduces two key strategies:
- **Parameter Sharing:** Both high-level and low-level agents share the identical underlying model weights (`θ`), distinguished only by role-specific system prompts (`S_h` and `S_l`). This avoids inefficient model swapping and allows for larger batch sizes.
- **Turn-level Ratio for GRPO:** Instead of the standard token-level ratio in GRPO, a turn-level ratio is proposed. This treats all tokens within a turn as a single action, applying clipping at the turn level. This stabilizes training by preventing disproportionate influence from longer turns and mitigating issues like "Echo Trap" (model producing overly long repetitions or incoherent text).

Overall, ReMA's methodology strategically decomposes the LLM reasoning task, employs MARL for specialized learning, and incorporates practical optimizations for scalable and stable training, especially in complex multi-turn scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of ReMA yields several key findings, demonstrating its effectiveness across various tasks and configurations:

#### 4.1. Single-turn ReMA Performance

- **Superior Performance:** ReMA consistently outperforms single-agent RL baselines (VRP_RL and MRP_RL) and basic Chain-of-Thought (VRP/CoT) on both in-distribution (MATH500, RewardBench970) and out-of-distribution (GSM8K, AIME24, AMC23, GaoKao2023En, Minerva Math, Olympiad Bench, JudgeBench) test sets.
- **Strong Generalization:** The most significant gains are observed on out-of-distribution datasets, indicating that ReMA's meta-thinking mechanism confers superior generalization ability. For example, it achieved a 20% improvement on AMC23 for Llama3-8B-Instruct and 13.33% on AIME24 for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- **Instruction-Tuned LLMs:** While ReMA shows improvement across different LLMs (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), the accuracy gains on instruction-tuned models are sometimes smaller than on base models. This is attributed to their already higher initial performance and more fixed output distributions, limiting further RL improvement.

#### 4.2. Meta-thoughts Boost Low-level Generalization

- A motivating example using Qwen2.5-Math-7B demonstrated that "RL under Meta-thinking" (ReMA's approach) achieved better learning dynamics and significantly improved generalization to harder problems (like AIME24) compared to "RL from Base" or "RL from SFT" (fine-tuning with GPT-4o CoT answers). This highlights that guiding LLMs with meta-thoughts fundamentally changes their learning process, leading to more robust problem-solving strategies.

#### 4.3. Diverse Meta-thinking Characteristics of LLMs

- Experiments with structured JSON-format actions (DECOMPOSE, REWRITE, EMPTY) revealed that LLM scale impacts the quality and complexity of learned meta-thinking. Smaller models (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) tended to converge to simpler actions like "EMPTY" to avoid formatting penalties, suggesting limited capacity to simultaneously maintain complex output formats and explore diverse reasoning strategies. Larger models (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) exhibited more adaptive meta-thinking strategies based on problem difficulty.

#### 4.4. Multi-turn MAMRP Results and Ablations

- **Effective Convergence:** Multi-turn ReMA demonstrated effective convergence on training sets (e.g., accuracy increasing from 55% to 70% on MATH-Level3-5), with an average performance gain of ~5% on test benchmarks, showing stable improvements on OOD data.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The performance of multi-turn ReMA is highly sensitive to hyperparameters like maximum response length per turn and maximum number of turns. Improper configurations can lead to model collapse (massive repetitions or empty responses), pointing to the "Echo Trap" challenge in long-horizon RL.
- **Turn-level Ratio Efficacy:** Ablation studies confirmed that the proposed turn-level ratio clipping significantly improved sample efficiency compared to token-level variants, achieving higher training rewards with fewer steps. This aligns with the intuition that treating a turn as a single action stabilizes training.
- **Parameter Sharing Benefits:** Shared parameters with simultaneous updates converged noticeably faster than using separate model instances for each agent, highlighting the efficiency benefits of this architectural choice.
- **Reward Functions Shape Behaviors:** An interesting qualitative finding was that a "consistency-based reward" for the high-level agent, while promoting more detailed guidance, could lead to "jailbreak" behavior (high-level agent including final answer). Furthermore, it led to unexpected role reversal, where the low-level agent started performing more self-verification, suggesting that meta-thinking can emerge even in lower-level agents under specific reward structures.

In summary, ReMA consistently improves LLM reasoning, particularly generalization, by leveraging a novel multi-agent, hierarchical approach to meta-thinking. The paper also provides valuable insights into the dynamics of MARL for LLMs, demonstrating the importance of careful design choices like turn-level rewards and parameter sharing for stable and efficient training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReMA framework presents several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of large language models and artificial intelligence:

1. **Advancing LLM Reasoning through Hierarchical Meta-thinking:** ReMA's core innovation lies in formally defining and optimizing a Multi-Agent Meta-thinking Reasoning Process (MAMRP). By explicitly decoupling meta-thinking (planning, monitoring) from detailed reasoning (execution) into distinct, reinforcement-learned agents, it offers a more structured and cognitively inspired approach to complex problem-solving. This hierarchical design pushes LLMs beyond simple chain-of-thought, enabling them to "think about thinking" in a more deliberate and controllable manner.
2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate ReMA's superior performance, especially on out-of-distribution datasets. This indicates that the meta-thinking capabilities learned through MARL are not merely memorizing patterns but are cultivating more fundamental, generalizable problem-solving strategies, a crucial step towards more robust and adaptive AI.
3. **Improved RL for LLMs:** The paper addresses critical challenges in applying reinforcement learning to LLMs, particularly in multi-turn settings. The introduction of turn-level ratio clipping and the validation of parameter sharing strategies contribute directly to stabilizing and accelerating MARL training for language models. These technical contributions can benefit future research in long-horizon, multi-step LLM tasks.
4. **Insights into Multi-Agent Dynamics:** The ablation studies and qualitative analyses provide valuable insights into the emergent behaviors and evolving dynamics between the agents. The observation of "role reversal" where the low-level agent develops self-verification patterns under certain reward settings is particularly intriguing. This highlights the complex interplay in multi-agent systems and offers new avenues for understanding how specialized roles can emerge and interact.
5. **Blueprint for Complex AI Systems:** ReMA provides a compelling blueprint for designing more sophisticated AI systems that can tackle complex problems by orchestrating specialized components. The concept of hierarchical agents, each optimized for a specific cognitive function, can be extended to various domains beyond reasoning, such as planning, control, and multi-modal understanding.
6. **Interpretability of LLM Reasoning:** By separating the meta-thinking steps from the detailed reasoning, ReMA inherently offers a higher degree of interpretability compared to monolithic LLMs. Researchers can analyze the high-level plans generated by the meta-thinking agent, gaining insights into the model's strategic approach before delving into the execution details.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations and areas for future work:
- **Task Scope:** The current work is tested primarily on mathematical reasoning and LLM-as-a-Judge benchmarks. Future work could extend ReMA to tasks that naturally require more complex, multi-turn interactions between interleaved agents.
- **Understanding Multi-turn Dynamics:** A comprehensive understanding of the learning dynamics in multi-turn RL and multi-turn MARL for LLMs is still needed, particularly in mitigating issues like the "Echo Trap" and long-horizon credit assignment.
- **Inference-time Scaling:** While the framework proposes opportunities for inference-time computation scaling (e.g., balancing sampling budgets, hierarchical reranking), these aspects require further exploration and optimization through modern LLM speed-up techniques.

In conclusion, ReMA represents a significant stride in enhancing LLM reasoning by introducing a principled multi-agent approach to meta-thinking. Its empirical success, coupled with the insights into MARL dynamics for LLMs, paves the way for the development of more capable, generalizable, and transparent AI systems.
