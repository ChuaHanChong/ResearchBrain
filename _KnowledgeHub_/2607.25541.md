# P3: Probabilistic Policy Propagation for Stable VAE-Based Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> P3, a framework from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborators, enhances Variational Autoencoder-based Proximal Policy Optimization for robot learning by employing probabilistic policy propagation, leading to more stable training, faster convergence, and superior performance on humanoid parkour tasks and real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Existing VAE-PPO frameworks for robot locomotion exhibit slow convergence, training instability, and suboptimal asymptotic performance.
- The stochastic nature of VAE latent representations and single-sample approximations of the policy's probability ratio introduce bias into PPO's surrogate loss and increase gradient variance.
- This leads to biased KL divergence estimates, erroneous clipping, and instability, especially in high-dimensional tasks or when policies require fine-grained refinement.

## Method

- **P3** (Probabilistic Policy Propagation) introduces a ==distribution-aware optimization framework== with two complementary estimators: ==Moment Matching (MM)== and ==Monte Carlo (MC) latent sampling==.
- The MM estimator deterministically propagates the first two moments of the VAE's latent distribution through the actor network, providing a stable, reduced-noise optimization signal.
- A hybrid training schedule (==Latent Sample Fine-Tuning - LSFT==) uses MM for rapid initial convergence, followed by MC sampling to capture accurate distributional structures and refine robustness.

## Results

- P3 achieved **100.0%** data efficiency with the MM estimator, eliminating the **35.4%** spurious clipping observed in the single-sample VAE baseline due to estimation artifacts.
- P3 converged **20%** faster than the strongest sampling baseline (MC-only, N=50) and **33%** faster than the AutoEncoder baseline, reaching the highest terminal curriculum difficulty at **8,000 epochs**.
- The framework demonstrated superior sim-to-sim transfer with the highest total reward (**20.1**) and lifetime (**20.0**) in MuJoCo, and robust real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, achieving high success rates (**8-10 out of 10**) across diverse terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Single-sample approximations of the VAE's latent distribution in PPO cause significant bias in KL divergence estimation and introduce substantial gradient noise, hindering stable learning.
> - Accurately estimating the marginalized policy by considering the full latent distribution, rather than a single sample, is crucial for stable and efficient VAE-based policy optimization.
> - Combining a deterministic moment-matching approach for initial stability and rapid convergence with multi-sample Monte Carlo fine-tuning offers a balance of efficiency, stability, and accuracy for robust robot learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) has become a prevalent framework in reinforcement learning for robot locomotion, particularly for legged robots. This framework leverages VAEs to compress high-dimensional, noisy observations (e.g., proprioceptive and exteroceptive data) into compact latent representations. These latent states then serve as inputs for the PPO-trained policy, allowing the control system to operate effectively under partial observability and adapt to representation uncertainty. This approach has contributed to progress in complex-terrain locomotion and robust sim-to-real transfer for both quadrupedal and humanoid robots.

Despite its empirical successes, the existing VAE-PPO framework faces optimization challenges, including slow convergence, training instability, and suboptimal asymptotic performance. The core issue lies in the stochastic nature of the VAE's latent representation and its interaction with PPO's policy optimization. Previous implementations typically approximate the policy's probability ratio and KL divergence using only a single latent sample, which is identified as a fundamental theoretical cause of the observed optimization difficulties.

This work addresses this specific bottleneck by proposing a method that more accurately accounts for the probabilistic nature of the VAE's latent space within the PPO optimization loop. By improving the estimation of the marginalized policy, the research aims to enhance the stability, data efficiency, and overall performance of VAE-based robot learning, thereby strengthening a widely adopted robotics paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a distribution-aware optimization framework that addresses the inherent mismatch between stochastic VAE latent representations and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The authors identify a theoretical problem within the current VAE-PPO integration: a single-sample approximation of the latent distribution introduces significant variance and bias into PPO's surrogate loss function.

This problem manifests as follows:
- **Marginalized Policy Mismatch:** In VAE-based policies, each observation defines a latent distribution, and each sample from this latent distribution induces a distinct action distribution. The effective control policy is a marginalized distribution over the entire latent space. However, prior implementations of VAE-PPO typically use only one latent sample to estimate the probability ratio and KL divergence between the current and old policies. This single-sample approach fails to account for the aggregate, marginalized policy.
- **Biased KL Estimation and Erroneous Clipping:** Replacing the true marginalized policy with a single sampled component systematically distorts the log probability ratio. This distortion leads to biased KL divergence estimates, which are critical for PPO's trust-region enforcement. Erroneous clipping decisions can then occur, penalizing beneficial actions or failing to adequately constrain policy updates, resulting in inefficient and unstable training.
- **Increased Gradient Variance:** Finite latent sampling causes the probability ratio estimate to fluctuate across evaluations. This fluctuation increases the variance of the surrogate-loss gradients, destabilizing the optimization process. The authors' theoretical analysis shows that this additional noise, termed "Noise_latent", scales with the advantage, action dimension, VAE latent variance, and inversely with the number of latent samples and the square of the action standard deviation. Such noise can force Adam-style optimizers to adopt aggressively smaller step sizes, causing training stagnation, especially when fine-grained policy refinement is needed. For SGD-style optimizers, it can introduce a bias that prevents convergence to deterministic optimal policies.

Motivated by these identified issues, the research proposes P3 (Probabilistic Policy Propagation). The key objectives of P3 are:
1. To accurately estimate the marginalized policy by propagating the VAE's latent distribution through the actor network.
2. To provide complementary estimation methods that balance efficiency, stability, and accuracy in capturing latent uncertainty.
3. To improve data efficiency, accelerate convergence, enhance training stability, and achieve superior asymptotic performance compared to existing VAE-PPO methods.
4. To demonstrate the framework's effectiveness and robustness in challenging robot locomotion tasks, including sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Probabilistic Policy Propagation (P3), is a distribution-aware optimization framework designed to accurately estimate the marginalized policy in VAE-based PPO. P3 offers two complementary estimators: Moment Matching (MM) and Monte Carlo (MC) latent sampling, which can be used interchangeably or in a hybrid schedule.

**Theoretical Foundations:**

The methodology is grounded in a detailed theoretical analysis of the KL divergence and gradient noise introduced by single-sample approximations in VAE-PPO.
- **KL Divergence Analysis:** The authors decompose the policy KL divergence, demonstrating that the objective must constrain both the VAE estimator's distribution shift and the actor's consistency across the *entire* latent distribution (Equation 3). Previous methods often neglect the latter, focusing on a single latent sample, which distorts the PPO-clip objective.
- **Gradient Noise Analysis:** The analysis of gradient variance (Equation 6) reveals that latent sampling introduces additional noise ("Noise_latent") into the PPO gradient. This noise is proportional to the square of the advantage estimate, action dimension, VAE latent variance, and the Frobenius norm of the actor Jacobian, while being inversely proportional to the number of latent samples and the fourth power of the action standard deviation. This explains why high-DOF tasks or policies with reduced action standard deviations (common during convergence) suffer from increased instability.

**P3 Framework Components:**

1. **Moment-Matching (MM) Estimator:**
 - **Mechanism:** MM deterministically propagates the first two moments (mean and variance) of the VAE latent distribution through the actor network's layers. This is achieved using analytical expressions for linear layers and non-linear activation functions (specifically ELU, with derivations also provided for ReLU and Leaky ReLU). A diagonal-covariance approximation is employed for simplicity and efficiency.
 - **Properties:** MM provides a deterministic approximation of the marginalized policy, removing finite-sample fluctuations from the likelihood ratio. This results in a reduced-noise optimization signal (Equation 11), leading to stable and efficient learning. However, by discarding cross-unit covariance, MM can sometimes underestimate the true action variance, potentially yielding an overly narrow policy.

2. **Latent-Sampling (MC) Estimator:**
 - **Mechanism:** The MC estimator computes the marginalized policy by drawing multiple independent latent samples (N) from the VAE's latent distribution, evaluating the actor at each sample, and averaging their resulting action likelihoods (Equation 12).
 - **Properties:** As the number of samples (N) increases, the MC estimator consistently approaches the true marginalized policy. It is capable of capturing complex latent-induced correlations and exposing the actor to the full uncertainty of the latent distribution. This makes it robust to observation and dynamics shifts, which is beneficial for sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Trade-offs:** The primary drawback is its computational cost, as actor evaluation and memory usage scale linearly with N. A small N introduces significant ratio variance, while a large N (e.g., N ≥ 50) becomes computationally expensive for continuous training.

**Complementary Hybrid Schedule (Latent Sample Fine-Tuning - LSFT):**

P3 leverages the strengths of both MM and MC through a hybrid training schedule:
- **Initial Training with MM:** The policy is initially trained using the MM estimator. Its deterministic nature eliminates latent-sampling noise, providing a stable and efficient signal that facilitates rapid initial convergence and the acquisition of a strong baseline policy.
- **Latent Sample Fine-Tuning (LSFT) with MC:** Once the MM-trained policy reaches a plateau, the estimator is switched to MC with a moderate number of samples (e.g., N=15) for a shorter fine-tuning phase. LSFT allows the policy to capture more accurate distributional structures that diagonal MM might miss, improving robustness to latent uncertainty and further refining performance. Crucially, the encoder and actor parameters are shared, allowing for seamless switching between estimators.

**Experimental Setup:**

P3 is evaluated on humanoid parkour tasks in Isaac Sim, using an Actor-Critic framework with a VAE estimator. Baselines include SimpleActorCritic, SPR (Self-Predictive Representations), AutoEncoder (AE), and MC-only PPO with various sample counts (including N=1, which represents the standard VAE baseline). The training incorporates a curriculum-based terrain design and extensive domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer. Performance is assessed through data efficiency diagnostics, convergence analysis, and deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in MuJoCo and the real world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate P3 addressed several questions regarding data efficiency, approximation error, convergence, and performance in sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer.

**Q1: Data Efficiency (D_eff)**
- The standard single-sample VAE baseline (MC-only, N=1) exhibited a data efficiency (D_eff) of 64.6%, indicating that 35.4% of policy update samples were spuriously clipped due to estimation artifacts.
- Increasing the number of latent samples (N) in MC-only training improved D_eff: N=5 yielded 79.3%, N=15 yielded 89.8%, and N=50 reached 96.5%.
- The P3-MM estimator achieved 100.0% data efficiency by construction, as its deterministic propagation eliminates finite-sample fluctuations.
- The KL divergence analysis further supported this, showing that the single-sample VAE overestimated the KL by a factor of three compared to stable high-sample MC estimates, whereas MM closely matched high-sample accuracy. This confirms that single-sample KL bias contributes to erroneous clipping and reduced data utilization.

**Q2: Action Distribution Approximation Error**
- Analysis of the action distribution (Figure 4) at the P3-MM checkpoint showed that MM propagation, while preserving the action mean, tended to underestimate the action variance relative to an MC reference (direct latent sampling). This is attributed to MM's diagonal-covariance approximation, which does not explicitly model cross-dimensional correlations observed in the actor's output.
- After Latent Sample Fine-Tuning (LSFT) using the MC estimator, the agreement between the propagated and sampled distributions improved, indicating that LSFT mitigated MM's initial variance underestimation.
- Additional diagnostics (Appendix C.1) confirmed that the actor's output mean distribution is approximately Gaussian (Shapiro-Wilk test W=0.9995, p-value=0.422, 85.39% pass rate for action dimensions). The latent linearity test yielded an average R² of 0.9706, supporting the first-order Taylor approximation used in the theoretical gradient variance analysis.

**Q3: Convergence Analysis**
- **Comparison of Probability Estimators (Figure 5):**
 - P3, combining MM training with MC-based LSFT (N=15), achieved a high-difficulty plateau after 7,000 epochs of MM, followed by 1,000 epochs of LSFT, converging at 8,000 epochs.
 - MC-only (N=50), the strongest sampling baseline, converged at 10,000 epochs with a lower terminal difficulty, making P3 20% faster.
 - MC-only (N=15) converged slightly later than MC-only (N=50).
 - MC-only (N=5) and the single-sample VAE (N=1) did not converge within the 15,000-epoch budget, exhibiting significant training instability consistent with high gradient variance.
- **Comparison with Other Algorithms (Figure 6):**
 - P3 converged first at 8,000 epochs, reaching the highest terminal curriculum difficulty.
 - The AutoEncoder (AE) baseline converged at 12,000 epochs, reaching a lower terminal difficulty (approx. 4.65), meaning P3 was 33% faster.
 - SPR and SimpleActorCritic baselines did not meet the convergence criteria within 15,000 epochs, showing unstable training or stagnation.

**Q4: Performance Analysis**
- **Sim-to-Sim Transfer (MuJoCo, Table 2):**
 - P3 achieved the highest total reward (20.1) and lifetime (20.0) in MuJoCo, surpassing all baselines.
 - P3-MM, evaluated at its 7,000-epoch checkpoint, already outperformed MC-only (N=50). LSFT further enhanced P3's performance.
 - The deterministic AE baseline outperformed the stochastic VAE (Total Reward 17.9 vs. 16.2), while SPR showed the weakest transfer.
- **Real-world Deployment (Unitree G1 Robot, Table 3 & Figure 7):**
 - P3 demonstrated the highest overall success counts across stepping stones, stairs, and gaps, achieving 8, 9, and 10 successes out of 10 trials, respectively.
 - This performance was achieved despite challenges like elevation-map drift and kinematic discrepancies, indicating robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities.
- **Computational Cost (Table 8):** P3-MM maintained reasonable memory usage (15138 MB) and policy-learning time (0.96 s) per PPO epoch, comparable to VAE, while MC-only (N=50) incurred significantly higher costs (31851 MB, 1.22 s).

Overall, the findings indicate that P3 effectively addresses the identified optimization issues, leading to improved data efficiency, faster and more stable convergence, and superior task performance and robustness in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Probabilistic Policy Propagation (P3) as a framework that addresses a specific, yet impactful, optimization bottleneck within a widely adopted robotics framework: the joint optimization of stochastic VAE state estimators and PPO policies. By identifying and quantifying the sources of instability and inefficiency—namely, the biased KL estimation and increased gradient variance caused by single-sample latent approximations—the authors provide a foundational understanding of existing challenges.

The significance of P3 lies in its ability to reconcile the benefits of VAEs (e.g., handling partial observability and noise, promoting robust representations) with the requirements of stable and efficient PPO optimization.
- **Improved Optimization Reliability:** P3's deterministic Moment-Matching estimator significantly reduces gradient noise and eliminates erroneous clipping, leading to faster and more stable convergence. This allows policies to reach higher performance plateaus more consistently.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** By ensuring that a higher fraction of samples contribute effectively to policy updates, P3 makes the learning process more sample-efficient. This is crucial in robotics where real-world data collection is often costly and time-consuming.
- **Increased Robustness:** The Latent Sample Fine-Tuning component, utilizing Monte Carlo sampling, refines the policy's robustness to latent uncertainty and distributional shifts, which is particularly beneficial for challenging tasks and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Broader Applicability:** By demonstrating success on complex humanoid parkour tasks and achieving robust real-world deployment, P3 validates its applicability to high-degree-of-freedom robots and demanding environments.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. By transforming VAE-based policy learning into a more reliable and scalable foundation, P3 can facilitate its broader adoption across various robotic platforms and sensing modalities. Researchers and practitioners in robot learning may find P3 a valuable tool for developing more capable and robust controllers, particularly in scenarios involving high-dimensional sensory inputs and uncertain environments. This contributes to the advancement of learning-based control for autonomous systems, enabling more sophisticated and adaptable robotic behaviors.
