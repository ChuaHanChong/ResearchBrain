# CARE: Multi-Task Pretraining for Latent Continuous Action Representation in Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> CARE pretrains a Vision-Language-Action model to predict continuous latent actions from video-text pairs alone, no action labels needed. A shared VLM backbone with two decoders (next-frame, keypoint-trajectory) jointly learns richer latent actions via uncertainty-weighted multi-task loss, then a small labeled set fine-tunes an action head; beats prior label-free methods and rivals label-based OpenVLA on LIBERO.

## Problem

- VQ-VAE-based latent action models inherit quantization errors and codebook collapse, and VLA models built on them cannot correct these propagated biases downstream.
- Next-frame reconstruction objectives make latent actions merely compress inter-frame pixel differences, even when those differences are not action-caused, weakening action grounding.
- Single reconstruction-only training objectives let latent action models degenerate into pure future-frame predictors, a shortcut-learning failure mode rather than true action modeling.

## Method

- ==Latent VLM== built on Prismatic-7B (SigLIP+DinoV2 encoders, Llama backbone) predicts ==continuous latent-action tokens== directly from an image-instruction pair via placeholder tokens in the prompt.
- ==Multi-task pretraining== adds a ==point-track prediction== objective using CoTracker-derived keypoint coordinates alongside ==next-frame feature prediction==, both fused with latent actions via ==cross-attention decoders==.
- ==Uncertainty-Weighted Loss== combines the frame-prediction and point-track losses adaptively via learned task variances, then ==LoRA== fine-tuning maps latent actions to real actions with an ==L1 regression== head.

## Results

- On LIBERO, CARE (human+Bridge+RT-1) reaches **77.7%** average success rate, beating action-label pretrained OpenVLA by **1.4%** on Goal and **12.5%** on Long tasks.
- Against label-free baselines CARE outperforms LAPA (**64.3avg**) and CoMo (**69.2avg**) across all four LIBERO suites, and shows performance scaling with more pretraining data.
- Ablations show multi-task pretraining (**77.7%** SR) beats single-task frame-only (**67.8%**) or point-only (**54.3%**) variants, and CARE achieves lower LP-MSE and S-PCFC shortcut-learning scores than CoMo and LAPA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adding a geometrically-grounded auxiliary task like keypoint trajectory prediction to reconstruction objectives can directly counter shortcut learning in self-supervised latent action pretraining.
> - Continuous, VLM-native latent action representations trained without action labels can approach or beat action-labeled pretraining, narrowing the case for costly annotation at scale.
> - Uncertainty-weighted multi-task losses let heterogeneous auxiliary objectives (pixel-level and coordinate-level) be combined without manual loss-weight tuning, a transferable recipe beyond robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of **Embodied AI, Robotics, and Multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs)**, specifically addressing challenges in **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for robot control. VLA models represent a crucial step towards creating general-purpose robots capable of understanding high-level natural language instructions and executing complex tasks through low-level motion control.

The broader landscape is characterized by several key trends:
- **Rise of VLA Models:** Recent advancements have shown significant promise in integrating visual perception, language understanding, and motion control for tasks like grasping and manipulation. Models such as PaLM-E and RT-1 have demonstrated the potential of large-scale, multimodal models in robotics.
- **Data Scarcity for Robot Learning:** A fundamental bottleneck in developing scalable and generalizable VLA models is the immense cost and time required to collect large-scale, precisely action-annotated robot demonstration datasets. Obtaining accurate joint angles or end-effector trajectories for diverse tasks and robot morphologies is often impractical. This limitation significantly hinders scalability and transferability across different domains.
- **Leveraging Unlabeled Data with Latent Action Models (LAMs):** To circumvent the action-labeling problem, a promising direction involves utilizing abundant, action-unlabeled video data from diverse sources (e.g., internet videos, human demonstrations). This led to the development of Latent Action Models (LAMs), which aim to learn abstract "latent actions" from video observations.
 - Early LAMs like Genie and LAPA employed discrete latent actions, often using VQ-VAE objectives to compress visual information between frames.
 - Moto improved upon this by shifting the objective from pixel-level reconstruction to predicting high-level semantic embeddings, focusing on action-relevant features.
 - More recently, COMO introduced continuous latent action representations, moving beyond the limitations of fixed-size discrete codebooks to capture finer-grained variations.
- **Challenges in Existing LAMs:** Despite these advancements, several critical issues persist:
 - **Bias Propagation:** VQ-VAE-based LAMs can suffer from quantization errors and codebook collapse, which are then inherited by downstream VLA models.
 - **Lack of Explicit Action Encoding:** Many LAMs, especially those focused on next-frame reconstruction, tend to learn latent actions that merely compress visual differences between frames rather than explicitly encoding the underlying control actions causing those differences. This can lead to models that are good at predicting visual changes but not necessarily effective at controlling robots.
 - **Shortcut Learning:** Models can degenerate into simple future frame predictors, exploiting superficial correlations in the data rather than learning genuine action dynamics.

CARE directly addresses these challenges by proposing a novel unsupervised pretraining strategy. It builds upon the idea of continuous latent action representations from models like COMO but integrates LAM training directly into the **Vision-Language Model (VLM)** pretraining pipeline. Crucially, CARE aims to improve the *quality* of these latent action representations by introducing a multi-task learning objective that explicitly encourages the model to learn action-driven changes, thereby mitigating bias, enhancing explicit action encoding, and preventing shortcut learning. This positions CARE as a significant step towards more scalable, robust, and interpretable robot learning from weakly supervised data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the CARE framework is to overcome the critical bottleneck of **action supervision dependency** in training scalable and generalizable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot control. The high cost, time-consumption, and practical impracticality of acquiring large-scale, precisely labeled action data for diverse robotic tasks serve as the core motivation for this research.

Specifically, the paper articulates several key objectives:

1. **Eliminate the Need for Explicit Action Labels During Pretraining:** The foremost goal is to design a pretraining strategy that can effectively learn continuous latent action representations using only readily available **video-text pairs**, which are weakly aligned and lack explicit robot action annotations. This directly addresses the data scarcity problem.
2. **Develop a Multi-Task Pretraining Objective for Enhanced Latent Action Representation:** Recognizing the limitations of prior latent action models (LAMs) that often focused solely on next-frame reconstruction, CARE aims to introduce a novel multi-task learning approach. This approach is designed to:
 - **Improve Explicit Action Encoding:** Guide the VLM to generate latent actions that genuinely encode action-driven changes, rather than merely compressing visual differences or focusing on static features.
 - **Mitigate Bias Propagation:** By integrating LAM training into VLM pretraining and using a continuous representation, CARE implicitly aims to bypass the quantization errors and codebook collapse issues prevalent in VQ-VAE-based discrete LAMs.
 - **Prevent Shortcut Learning:** Design training objectives that make it harder for the model to degenerate into simple future frame predictors, instead forcing it to learn meaningful action dynamics.
3. **Seamless Integration of LAM Training into VLM Pretraining:** The authors aim to streamline the overall VLA training pipeline by embedding latent action model training directly within the pretraining phase of a Vision-Language Model. This reduces the complexity and number of stages (from four to three) traditionally required, making the process more efficient.
4. **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Interpretability:** Through experimental validation, the researchers aim to show that CARE can achieve high success rates in various simulation tasks, generate semantically interpretable latent actions, and effectively avoid shortcut learning compared to existing label-free and even some label-dependent approaches.

In essence, the motivation stems from the desire to unlock the full potential of VLA models for robotics by making them learn from the vast, largely unlabeled visual data available online, thereby accelerating the development of capable and adaptable robotic systems for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CARE (Continuous Action Representation) proposes a novel two-stage framework for training VLA models, primarily distinguished by its unsupervised pretraining strategy for learning latent continuous action representations.

**1. Latent Vision-Language Model (VLM) Architecture:**
The core of CARE is a Latent VLM built upon **Prismatic-7B VLM**. This foundation leverages a robust, large-scale multimodal architecture, comprising:
- **Visual Encoder:** A combination of two powerful pre-trained vision models, **SigLIP** and **DinoV2**. These encoders process the input image (x_img) to produce rich visual features.
- **Projector:** A two-layer MLP that projects the concatenated visual features into a unified visual feature vector (f_v).
- **LLM Backbone:** A 7B-parameter Llama Large Language Model.
The input to the VLM consists of an observation image (x_img) and a natural language task instruction (p). Latent-action placeholders are appended to the prompt, signaling the model to predict action dimensions. The text prompt is tokenized and embedded (f_T). The visual features (f_v) and text features (f_T) are then concatenated and fed into the LLM. The final hidden states (h_last) from the LLM, specifically the part corresponding to the action dimensions, are extracted as the **continuous latent action representation (z)**. This design positions the VLM to inherently understand both visual context and linguistic instructions, which is crucial for embodied AI.

**2. Continuous Latent Action with Multi-task Learning (Pretraining Stage):**
This stage is the core innovation of CARE, where the VLM learns to generate continuous latent action representations without explicit action labels. It employs a multi-task learning approach with two distinct training objectives, executed through an encoder-multi-decoder architecture:

- **Multi-Task Definition:**
 - **Frame Prediction Task:** Similar to previous LAMs, this objective aims to predict the vision feature of the next frame (f_t+1_v) given the current frame's vision feature (f_t_v) and the predicted latent action (z_t). A cross-attention mechanism computes the relationship between the current frame feature and the latent action (z_f), which is then fed into a **Frame Decoder** to predict the next frame feature.
 - **Point Track Prediction Task (Novel Contribution):** This is a key differentiator. Instead of solely relying on pixel-level frame reconstruction, CARE introduces the prediction of **keypoint trajectories**. Given the 2D coordinates (x and y) of 256 evenly distributed points in the current frame (k_t), and the latent action (z_t), the model predicts the 2D coordinates of these points in the next frame (k_t+1). The current and next frame point coordinates are acquired using **Co-Tracker**, a pre-trained model. This task forces the model to focus on *action-driven positional changes* of objects and entities, directly addressing the problem of models focusing on static background features or merely compressing frame differences. A separate **Point Decoder** is used for this objective after fusing k_t and z_t via cross-attention to obtain z_k.

- **Loss Function Definition:** To effectively combine these two objectives, CARE adopts the **Uncertainty-Weighted Loss (UWL) framework**. This adaptive weighting mechanism allows the model to learn the relative importance of each loss dynamically, maximizing a Gaussian likelihood under the assumption of task-independent uncertainty. The joint loss (L_t) is defined as:
 L_t = (1/σ1^2)L_t_f + (1/σ2^2)L_t_p + log(σ1) + log(σ2)
 where L_t_f is the Mean Squared Error (MSE) for frame prediction, L_t_p is the MSE for point track prediction, and σ1, σ2 are learnable parameters determining the adaptive weights.

- **Pretraining Datasets:** This stage leverages large-scale, action-unlabeled video datasets:
 - **Robot Data:** 140k trajectories from Open X-Embodiment.
 - **Human Videos:** Approximately 100k clips of daily activities from Something-Something v2.
 In total, around 240k trajectories/video clips are used, representing diverse actions and environments without the need for costly action labels.

**3. Fine-tuning VLA with Action Labels:**
After unsupervised pretraining, the Latent VLM has learned to represent actions implicitly. To enable direct robot control, a supervised fine-tuning stage is necessary to map these latent actions to actual robot commands.
- **Action Head:** A lightweight residual Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) is attached to the decoder output. This module takes the hidden representation from the last decoder layer and regresses real-valued actions (e.g., joint angles, end-effector poses).
- **Training Objective:** An L1 regression objective is used to minimize the difference between predicted and ground-truth actions.
- **Optimization:** **LoRA fine-tuning** is employed, a parameter-efficient method that updates only a small number of additional parameters, thus reducing computational cost and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.
- **Fine-tuning Dataset:** A small, 3% uniform sample from the **RT-1 dataset** is used, which *does* contain ground truth action labels. This demonstrates CARE's ability to achieve high performance with limited supervised data after extensive unsupervised pretraining.

This methodology ensures that CARE can leverage vast amounts of easily accessible data for learning robust latent action representations, which are then effectively translated into executable robot commands with minimal supervised data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CARE demonstrates its effectiveness across three key questions: performance, interpretability, and ability to prevent shortcut learning.

**Q1 (Performance): Can CARE efficiently learn real actions and improve policy performance during fine-tuning?**
- **Superiority over Label-Free Baselines:** CARE consistently outperforms other label-free pretraining approaches, LAPA and CoMo (both autoregressive and diffusion policy-based versions), across all four LIBERO benchmark task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). For instance, in the "Goal" task, CARE achieved 77.9% success rate, significantly higher than LAPA (57.1%) and CoMo (63.2%).
- **Competitiveness with Action-Label-Based Methods:** CARE achieves performance highly competitive with, and in some cases surpasses, methods that *do* utilize action labels during pretraining (e.g., OpenVLA, Octo). Notably, CARE outperforms OpenVLA by 1.4% in the "Goal" test (77.9% vs. 76.5%) and by a substantial 12.5% in the "Long" test (65.3% vs. 52.8%). The overall average success rate for CARE (human+Bridge+RT-1) is 77.7%.
- **Scaling Law Validation:** The results show that increasing the scale of pretraining data (e.g., "human only" vs. "human+Bridge+RT-1") leads to improved success rates, validating that CARE adheres to beneficial scaling laws.

**Q2 (Interpretability): Do CARE's continuous latent actions have better action representation capabilities?**
- **Linear Probing (LP-MSE):** CARE consistently achieves lower LP-MSE scores (meaning better predictability of ground-truth actions from latent actions, hence better interpretability) compared to CoMo and LAPA. For the "Goal" task, CARE showed a 22.8% reduction in LP-MSE compared to CoMo and a 47.8% reduction compared to LAPA. Similar improvements were observed across "Spatial" and "Long" tasks, indicating that CARE's latent actions more explicitly encode true robot actions.
- **Semantic Label Prediction:** A semantic label predictor trained on CARE's latent actions (from the initial frame and 9 subsequent frames) achieved 84.2% accuracy on LIBERO Goal tasks. This significantly outperforms LAPA (64.1%) and CoMo (71.2%) and is comparable to using 10 consecutive full frames. This result strongly suggests that CARE's latent actions possess strong semantic interpretability, effectively capturing the high-level intent and nature of the actions.

**Q3 (Shortcut Learning): Does CARE effectively prevent shortcut learning?**
- **S-PCFC Metric:** CARE's latent actions effectively circumvent shortcut learning, as evidenced by its lower S-PCFC scores compared to baselines (lower S-PCFC indicates weaker shortcut learning). LAPA, a VQ-VAE-based method, showed near-perfect S-PCFC scores (e.g., 0.992 in Spatial), indicating high susceptibility to shortcut learning. CoMo's Q-former approach partially mitigated this (0.892 in Spatial). CARE, through its VLM-based latent action extraction and multi-task pretraining, significantly reduces shortcut learning, achieving scores like 0.833 in "Goal" and 0.820 in "Long." This indicates that CARE encourages the model to learn genuine action dynamics rather than merely predicting future frames.

**Ablation Study on Multi-task Learning:**
- The ablation experiments clearly demonstrate the synergistic benefits of multi-task pretraining. The multi-task Prismatic-7B model (frame + keypoint prediction) achieved a 77.7% success rate. In contrast, single-task pretraining yielded significantly lower success rates: 67.8% for frame prediction only and 54.3% for keypoint trajectory prediction only. This confirms that the combination of objectives is crucial for CARE's strong performance, validating the hypothesis that multi-task learning enhances explicit action representation and avoids the pitfalls of single-objective training.

In summary, CARE not only achieves high-performance robot control comparable to label-intensive methods but also produces more interpretable and robust latent action representations by effectively addressing the problem of shortcut learning, all while drastically reducing the reliance on explicit action labels during pretraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CARE framework presents several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the fields of embodied AI and robotics:

1. **Breaking the Data Bottleneck in Robot Learning:** The most profound impact of CARE is its ability to reduce the dependency on costly and time-consuming action-labeled data for pretraining VLA models. By effectively leveraging readily available, weakly aligned video-text pairs, CARE opens the door to training more capable and general-purpose robots using the vast amount of internet video data and human demonstrations. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for developing complex robotic skills and accelerates the pace of research and development in robot learning.

2. **Enhanced Latent Action Representation Quality:** CARE's novel multi-task learning approach, particularly the integration of keypoint trajectory prediction, marks a significant step forward in learning more meaningful and explicit latent action representations. Unlike previous methods that might merely compress visual frame differences, CARE forces the model to focus on action-driven positional changes. This leads to latent actions that are more semantically interpretable, as demonstrated by the high semantic accuracy and lower LP-MSE scores. Higher quality latent representations are fundamental for robust and generalizable control policies.

3. **Mitigation of Shortcut Learning:** The successful prevention of shortcut learning is a critical advancement. If models merely learn to predict future frames rather than genuine action dynamics, their utility in real-world control is limited. CARE's methodological design, as validated by the S-PCFC metric, ensures that the learned representations are more reflective of the underlying control actions, making the derived policies more reliable and less susceptible to brittle, superficial correlations in the training data.

4. **Streamlined and Efficient VLA Training Pipeline:** By integrating LAM training directly into the VLM pretraining process, CARE simplifies the overall VLA training pipeline from four stages to three. This not only makes the process more efficient but also potentially fosters a more holistic learning of visual, linguistic, and action capabilities within a unified framework, reducing the chances of information loss or misalignment between separate stages.

5. **Accelerated Development and Broader Accessibility of Robotic Systems:** By reducing the need for specialized robot data collection, CARE can accelerate the development cycle for new robotic applications. Researchers and developers can quickly adapt and fine-tune generalist VLA models for specific tasks with smaller, task-specific labeled datasets. This could lead to a broader adoption of intelligent robotic systems in diverse domains, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and domestic assistance, where bespoke data collection for every new task is infeasible.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a strong foundation for future research directions. The authors explicitly mention plans to narrow the remaining performance gap with purely action-label-based pretraining by incorporating techniques like action chunking and multi-dimensional perceptual perspectives. This indicates ongoing efforts to push the boundaries of weakly-supervised robot learning further.

In conclusion, CARE represents a pivotal contribution by addressing critical challenges in data efficiency, representation quality, and learning robustness in robot control. Its innovative multi-task pretraining strategy has the potential to significantly scale up the development of capable, generalist robots that can learn effectively from the vast, unstructured data of the real world.
