# LoHoVLA: A Unified Vision-Language-Action Model for Long-Horizon Embodied Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University, ShanghaiTech University, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University develop LoHoVLA, a unified Vision-Language-Action model that integrates high-level task planning and low-level motion control within a single framework, achieving 85.1% success on complex reasoning tasks like "put-even-blocks-in-same-color-zone" versus 8.2% for hierarchical baselines through joint prediction of linguistic sub-tasks and discrete action tokens using an augmented PaliGemma-3B backbone, while introducing a hierarchical closed-loop control mechanism that triggers sub-task re-planning only after consecutive failures exceed a threshold (K=2), thereby reducing redundant high-level planning steps, and creating LoHoSet, a synthetic dataset of 20 diverse long-horizon tasks with explicit sub-task annotations that enables two-stage training for optimal performance across both planning and control capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models fall short in effective planning and reasoning for long-horizon tasks, relying on implicit sub-task planning that lacks interpretability and reliability
- Hierarchical architectures suffer from suboptimal coordination and limited generalization between distinct planning and control modules, leading to error propagation and communication inefficiencies

## Method

- Develop a unified VLA framework that jointly predicts both linguistic sub-tasks and robot actions using a shared VLM backbone (PaliGemma-3B) with augmented language head
- Implement hierarchical closed-loop control that intelligently distinguishes between planning errors and execution errors, re-planning sub-tasks only when consecutive failures exceed threshold K=2
- ==Create LoHoSet== synthetic dataset with 20 long-horizon tasks and explicit sub-task annotations to enable proper training of the unified model

## Results

- Achieves **85.1%** average reward and **81.0%** success rate on complex reasoning tasks versus **8.2%** for best hierarchical baseline
- Demonstrates strong generalization to unseen tasks while requiring fewer high-level planning steps compared to strategies that re-plan after every failure
- Shows that dataset expansion beyond original 4 LoHoRavens tasks is essential for preventing overfitting and enabling generalization to new task configurations

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified architectures with shared VLM backbones can effectively integrate planning and control while maintaining generalization capabilities across diverse tasks
> - Hierarchical closed-loop control reduces computational overhead by avoiding unnecessary sub-task re-planning for transient low-level execution errors
> - Two-stage training (first optimizing text loss for planning, then both text and action losses) is crucial for balancing abstract reasoning and concrete action generation

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI aims to develop agents that can perceive, reason, and act in physical environments to achieve complex goals. A significant challenge within this field is addressing "long-horizon tasks," which require multi-step planning, reasoning, and execution beyond a single action. This paper directly addresses this challenge by proposing a novel approach.

Historically, embodied AI for long-horizon tasks has largely followed two main paradigms:

1. **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** These models leverage large-scale pre-trained Vision Language Models (VLMs) as backbones, fine-tuning them on robotic demonstrations to map visual and linguistic inputs directly to executable robot actions. While effective for short-horizon tasks and adept at extracting semantic information, existing VLA models often "fall short in effective planning and reasoning," making them unsuitable for complex, multi-step tasks. They typically perform sub-task planning implicitly, leading to a lack of interpretability and reliability.
2. **Hierarchical Architectures:** To tackle long-horizon tasks, many seminal studies have employed hierarchical designs. These typically involve a high-level component (often an LLM-based planner) responsible for decomposing the overall goal into a sequence of sub-tasks, and a low-level component (a VLA-based controller) that translates these sub-tasks into precise robot actions. While offering modularity and explicit planning, these hierarchical systems frequently suffer from "suboptimal coordination and limited generalization" between their distinct modules. Errors can propagate, and communication between layers can be inefficient.

LoHoVLA positions itself as a critical bridge between these two paradigms. It seeks to overcome the limitations of both: the lack of explicit planning in vanilla VLA models and the coordination issues in hierarchical architectures. By proposing a *unified* VLA framework, LoHoVLA aims to integrate both high-level task planning and low-level motion control within a *single* model. This unified approach, leveraging a shared VLM backbone, seeks to foster better generalization across diverse tasks and enhance robustness by promoting a cohesive understanding of both linguistic sub-tasks and the corresponding physical actions. This work thus represents a step towards developing more generalizable and robust embodied intelligence systems that can handle real-world complexity more effectively.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind LoHoVLA stems from the inherent difficulties real-world embodied agents face when confronted with long-horizon, compositional, and dynamically changing tasks. Such tasks cannot be resolved with a single action and demand sophisticated reasoning, sequential execution, and adaptability to environmental changes or failures. The authors identify two critical components for success in these scenarios: high-level task planning (decomposing goals into atomic sub-tasks) and low-level motion control (generating precise robot actions).

The paper highlights the shortcomings of existing approaches:
- **Vanilla VLA models:** Despite their prowess in perception and language understanding, they "fall short in effective planning and reasoning," rendering them suboptimal for long-horizon tasks. Their implicit planning mechanism lacks interpretability and reliability.
- **Hierarchical architectures:** While explicitly addressing planning, their modularity often leads to "suboptimal coordination and limited generalization." This disjointed structure can cause inefficiencies and propagate errors across planning and execution layers.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the LoHoVLA research are:
1. **Develop a Unified Framework:** To introduce a novel, unified VLA framework capable of integrating both high-level task planning and low-level motion control within a single model. This aims to overcome the coordination issues of hierarchical approaches and enhance the planning capabilities of standard VLA models.
2. **Leverage Pretrained VLMs:** To build this unified model upon a large pretrained Vision Language Model (VLM) backbone (PaliGemma), augmenting its capabilities to jointly generate linguistic sub-tasks and discrete action tokens, thereby promoting shared representation and better generalization.
3. **Implement Robust Control:** To incorporate a hierarchical closed-loop control mechanism within the unified framework. This mechanism is designed to intelligently mitigate errors originating from both planning and control, preventing deadlocks and redundant re-planning.
4. **Create a Specialized Dataset:** To address the scarcity of finely annotated long-horizon demonstrations, the authors aim to synthesize a comprehensive dataset, LoHoSet, which provides the necessary visual observations, linguistic goals, explicit sub-tasks, and robot actions for training.
5. **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Generalization:** To empirically show that the proposed LoHoVLA model significantly outperforms both hierarchical baselines and standard VLA models on a diverse set of long-horizon embodied tasks, including generalization to unseen tasks, thereby validating the promise of unified architectures for embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LoHoVLA’s methodology is centered on creating a unified architecture that learns to jointly predict sub-tasks and actions, supported by a specialized dataset and a hierarchical closed-loop control mechanism.

#### 3.1. Modeling Configurations

The paper contrasts three modeling configurations:
- **Vanilla VLA:** Directly maps observation (`o_t`) and high-level goal (`g`) to a robot action (`a_t`). Planning is implicit.
- **Hierarchical Architecture:** Separates planning and control. A planner infers the sub-task (`ĝ_t`) from observation and goal, and a controller then generates the action based on the observation and the inferred sub-task.
- **LoHoVLA (Unified Paradigm):** Integrates both, formalizing the joint prediction of action and sub-task. It models `P(a_t, ĝ_t | o_t, g) = P(a_t | o_t, g, ĝ_t) * P(ĝ_t | o_t, g)`. This means it first infers the sub-task and then uses that sub-task as contextual guidance to predict the action, all within a single model.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

LoHoVLA builds upon a large pretrained Vision Language Model (VLM).
- **Base VLM:** PaliGemma-3b-mix-224 is chosen as the backbone due to its multimodal capabilities and effectiveness. It comprises a SigLIP-based image encoder, a Gemma-2B decoder-only language model, and a linear projection layer connecting visual features to the language model’s token space.
- **Augmented Language Head:** The crucial modification is augmenting the VLM's original language head. Instead of just generating text, this head is adapted to generate *both* linguistic sub-tasks and discrete action tokens. This shared backbone is hypothesized to enable learning generalizable representations across both planning and control.
- **Action De-Tokenizer:** Robot actions (e.g., Cartesian end-effector positions) are discretized into 1,024 uniform bins to allow joint training with language tokens. During inference, these discrete tokens are de-tokenized and de-normalized back into continuous actions.

#### 3.3. Hierarchical Closed-Loop Control Mechanism

To enhance robustness and handle execution failures in long-horizon tasks, LoHoVLA incorporates a hierarchical closed-loop control strategy:
- **Failure Types:** The system accounts for sub-task planning errors, incorrect action predictions despite correct planning, and failures due to external disturbances.
- **Adaptive Re-planning:** The core idea is to re-predict actions more frequently than re-planning sub-tasks. If the number of consecutive failures during the execution of a *single sub-task* exceeds a predefined threshold `K` (set to 2 in experiments), only then does the system trigger a re-planning of the sub-task. Otherwise, it merely re-predicts the action based on the new environment state. This intelligent re-planning mechanism avoids unnecessary high-level planning for transient low-level execution errors, improving efficiency.

#### 3.4. A Synthetic Dataset: LoHoSet

Recognizing the challenge of obtaining large-scale, fine-grained sub-task annotations for real-world scenarios, the authors introduce LoHoSet, a synthetic dataset built on the Ravens robot simulator.
- **Content:** LoHoSet comprises 20 diverse long-horizon tasks and 3 pick-and-place primitive tasks. 10 long-horizon tasks and all 3 primitives are adapted from the LoHoRavens benchmark for direct comparison, while an additional 10 long-horizon tasks are introduced to improve generalization.
- **Demonstrations:** Each task includes 1,000 expert demonstrations, featuring synchronized visual observations (RGB and depth), linguistic high-level goals, *explicitly annotated sub-tasks*, and low-level robot actions (pick and place positions).
- **Annotation Process:** Sub-tasks are collected using manually designed rules within the simulator, exploiting the full information about the scene. Object attributes (size, color, quantity, position) are varied, and sub-task descriptions are generated using templates.
- **Realism:** The simulator incorporates observation noise and a dropping probability to mimic real-world uncertainties.

#### 3.5. Training Configurations

LoHoVLA employs a two-stage training strategy:
1. **Stage 1 (High-Level Planning):** PaliGemma is fine-tuned on the 14 long-horizon tasks (4 LoHoRavens + 10 additional tasks). Only the *text loss* (for sub-task generation) is optimized, while the image encoder and linear projection layer are kept frozen. This stage focuses on enhancing the model's ability to perform high-level task planning. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to all linear layers to efficiently adapt the large model.
2. **Stage 2 (Low-Level Control):** The dataset is augmented with 10,000 demonstrations for each pick-and-place primitive task. In this stage, *both text and action losses* are optimized. This fine-tunes the model for precise low-level motion control while maintaining its planning capabilities.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with weight decay and gradient clipping. DeepSpeed is used for efficient distributed training on 8 NVIDIA 4090 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LoHoVLA focuses on its performance in high-level task planning, low-level motion control, and generalization across tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Comparison

LoHoVLA is compared against a vanilla VLA model and LoHoRavens (with both explicit and implicit feedback mechanisms) on both seen and unseen tasks from the LoHoRavens benchmark, augmented by the newly introduced tasks in LoHoSet.
- **Superior Performance:** LoHoVLA consistently achieves the highest average score and success rate across nearly all tasks. It demonstrates near-perfect accuracy on tasks like "put-block-into-matching-bowl."
- **Complex Reasoning:** For highly challenging reasoning tasks, such as "put-even-blocks-in-same-color-zone" (which demands integration of color recognition, counting, spatial reasoning, and logic), LoHoVLA scores 85.1% (average reward) and 81.0% (success rate), significantly outperforming all baselines which perform poorly (e.g., 8.2% for LoHoRavens implicit feedback).
- **Strong Generalization:** LoHoVLA exhibits strong generalization capabilities on unseen tasks, consistently surpassing all baselines despite having no prior exposure to these specific task configurations.
- **Task Type Nuances:** Interestingly, LoHoVLA occasionally outperforms on certain long-horizon tasks compared to simpler pick-and-place primitive tasks. This is attributed to differences in evaluation criteria: "zone-match" tasks (common in long-horizon scenarios) are more forgiving of minor spatial inaccuracies, which LoHoVLA handles well, whereas "pose-match" tasks (typical for primitives) require higher precision. Despite this, LoHoVLA remains robust across both types.
- **Vanilla VLA Weakness:** The vanilla VLA model performs the worst, often achieving zero success rates on several tasks. This is primarily due to its lack of explicit sub-task supervision, leading to overfitting to superficial patterns and disregarding crucial goal conditions (e.g., placing blocks in incorrect bowls).

#### 4.2. Closed-Loop Strategies Comparison

To assess the effectiveness of the hierarchical closed-loop control, three strategies are compared:
- **Strategy (a):** Re-predicts action only on failure. This performs the poorest, as it can get stuck in deadlocks if the underlying sub-task plan is flawed.
- **Strategy (b):** Re-plans the sub-task and re-predicts the action after *every* failure.
- **Strategy (c) (LoHoVLA's):** Hierarchical closed-loop control. Re-plans sub-task only if failures exceed a threshold (K=2); otherwise, re-predicts action.
- **Efficiency and Robustness:** Strategies (b) and (c) achieve comparable overall success rates. However, Strategy (c) requires *fewer high-level sub-task planning steps*. This crucial finding demonstrates that LoHoVLA's hierarchical approach is more efficient, avoiding redundant sub-task re-planning when failures are due to low-level execution errors or external disturbances, rather than a flawed plan. The optimal strategy can vary slightly depending on whether the task is reasoning-heavy (where prompt re-planning might help) or precision-heavy (where repeated low-level attempts are more beneficial).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies investigate the impact of dataset expansion and the two-stage training strategy on model performance, introducing a "sub-task planning success rate" metric to isolate planning ability.
- **Training Set Expansion:** Models trained *without* the expanded LoHoSet (i.e., only on the original 4 seen LoHoRavens tasks) show significantly poor generalization to unseen tasks. This is attributed to severe overfitting to the limited training data. For example, the model trained without expansion had 0% success on "put-block-into-mismatching-bowl" because it overfit to the similar "matching-bowl" task. The expanded dataset is vital for alleviating such overfitting and improving generalization.
- **Two-Stage Training:** Comparing one-stage vs. two-stage training (where primitive tasks and action labels are introduced later in the second stage): the one-stage approach yields lower sub-task planning success rates and consequently lower overall task completion success. This indicates that integrating action labels and primitive tasks too early in the training process hinders the effective optimization of the high-level task planning capabilities. The two-stage approach effectively balances the learning of abstract planning and concrete action generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LoHoVLA represents a significant stride in the development of embodied AI for complex, long-horizon tasks, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Advancement of Unified Architectures:** The most significant contribution is demonstrating the effectiveness of a unified Vision-Language-Action model. By integrating high-level planning and low-level control into a single framework with a shared VLM backbone, LoHoVLA addresses the inherent coordination issues of traditional hierarchical systems and the planning limitations of simpler VLA models. This work suggests that future research might increasingly move towards such end-to-end learning for complex robotic behaviors.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Robustness:** The empirical results clearly show LoHoVLA's superior performance and robust generalization capabilities, especially on unseen tasks. This generalizability, crucial for real-world deployment, is attributed to the shared representation learned through the unified architecture and the diversified LoHoSet dataset. The hierarchical closed-loop control further bolsters robustness by intelligently mitigating diverse types of failures, optimizing between re-planning and re-executing.
- **Efficient Error Recovery:** The hierarchical closed-loop control mechanism is a practical innovation. By distinguishing between high-level planning errors and low-level execution errors, it allows for more efficient error recovery by avoiding unnecessary and computationally expensive re-planning. This has direct implications for improving the reliability and efficiency of robotic systems in dynamic and uncertain environments.
- **New Dataset Contribution:** The creation of LoHoSet, a synthetic dataset with explicit sub-task annotations for long-horizon tasks, is a valuable resource. It addresses the significant challenge of data scarcity in this domain, providing a structured environment for training and benchmarking advanced embodied AI models. This dataset can accelerate research in multi-step robotic manipulation.
- **Insights into Training Strategies:** The ablation studies highlight the critical importance of a well-designed training strategy. The two-stage training approach, which first emphasizes high-level planning before fine-tuning for low-level control, is shown to be crucial for optimal performance. This provides valuable guidance for training future complex VLA models, especially when dealing with hierarchical reasoning and control.
- **Paving the Way for Generalist Robots:** By showcasing strong performance on complex, multi-step tasks, LoHoVLA pushes the frontier towards creating more generalist robot policies. The ability to reason about and execute long-horizon goals, combined with robustness to failures, brings embodied agents closer to functioning autonomously in unstructured, real-world settings.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as discrete robot actions and the assumption of single-timestep sub-task completion, LoHoVLA lays a strong foundation for future research in more sophisticated unified models, potentially incorporating continuous action spaces, more complex sub-task structures, and seamless transfer to physical robots.
