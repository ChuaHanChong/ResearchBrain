# Addressable Memory for Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldTrace is a training-free KV-cache memory framework for autoregressive video world models. It assigns every compressed summary slot a distinct, in-distribution virtual position fixed by slot rank, and compresses keys in canonical unrotated space, either as averaged history fields or as frozen scene-entry landmarks. Temporal consistency improves 15.5% and episodic recall 19.5% on the introduced LoopBench, with no retraining.

## Problem

- Once rollouts exceed the training horizon, temporal RoPE offsets fall out of distribution, so cached frames become unreadable by attention even though they remain stored.
- Naive cache compression averages keys in RoPE-rotated space, where mismatched phases partially cancel and strip the signal a summary slot was supposed to carry.
- Prior fixes handle only one side: block-relative reindexing collapses distinct summary slots onto one capped position, while EMA memory tokens scale to just two streams.

## Method

- **WorldTrace** ==slot indexing== assigns each summary slot a ==virtual position== fixed by its rank relative to the query, keeping all slots distinct and ==in-distribution== at any horizon.
- **WorldTrace-Field** applies ==canonical key averaging==: unrotate keys, average in ==canonical space==, re-rotate at the slot position, preserving the mean pre-softmax attention score of source frames.
- **WorldTrace-Landmark** detects ==scene entries== via ==cosine spikes between consecutive canonical keys==, storing those frames ==verbatim== as ==frozen canonical keys== re-rotated once per slot shift.

## Results

- On Matrix-Game-2, WorldTrace-Field reaches TempSSIM **0.545** at 48 chunks versus **0.472** for sliding window, a **15.5%** relative gain, plus the lowest scene drift **0.0295**.
- On LoopBench ABA loops, WorldTrace-Landmark raises Position-Aligned CLIP from **0.723** to **0.864**, roughly **19.5%**, and beats the baseline in every topology, length, orientation and multi-revisit tier.
- Ablations: canonical averaging cuts LatentDiff from **0.312** to **0.233** at four slots; verbatim landmarks sustain PAC near **0.99** at 256 chunks versus **0.610** for canonical anchoring.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Long-horizon memory failure is an addressing problem, not a capacity problem: content placed outside the trained positional range stays unreadable however well it was compressed.
> - Compress positionally encoded keys in canonical unrotated space; averaging rotated vectors cancels phases and silently biases attention away from distant history.
> - Coherence and recall demand different projections of history: averaged summaries blur specific scenes, while verbatim landmarks hinge on detecting the right scene transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on video generation and world modeling using transformer-based architectures. Recent advancements in autoregressive video world models aim to simulate interactive environments, providing capabilities for applications such as next-generation game engines and closed-loop robot simulators. A critical aspect of these models is their ability to maintain "visual persistence," meaning that when an agent revisits a previously generated scene, the visual output should be consistent with its original appearance.

Existing autoregressive video world models commonly utilize a Key-Value (KV) cache as a growing visual memory to carry forward previously generated frames. However, a significant challenge arises when generation rollouts extend beyond the length of the context window observed during training. The core problem identified is that these models rely on temporal Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) for encoding relative positions. When the temporal offsets between the current query and distant cached keys exceed the training distribution, the RoPE values become "out-of-distribution" (OOD). This renders stored content effectively unaddressable, as the attention mechanism struggles to retrieve relevant visual information. Furthermore, prior attempts at compressing the KV cache by naively averaging RoPE-rotated keys can corrupt memory through "phase cancellation," where incompatible positional phases average together and diminish the signal.

The paper positions itself by addressing these two coupled bottlenecks: the addressability of distant memories due to OOD temporal offsets and the informativeness of compressed memories due to naive compression methods. While other research has explored memory-augmented transformers, KV cache compression for Large Language Models (LLMs), and RoPE extrapolation, many focus on modifying content or addressing positional issues for individually retained tokens rather than compressed summaries within a fixed-budget cache for video. This work aims to provide a training-free solution specifically for interactive video world models, ensuring that compressed memories remain both addressable and informative over long generation horizons. It contrasts with training-time extensions that fine-tune models for longer contexts or methods that rely on external memory banks, by modifying the inference-time cache management of a fixed, pre-trained model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable visually persistent generation in interactive video world models over long horizons without requiring model retraining. The authors identify that current autoregressive video world models exhibit a rapid degradation in visual persistence once generation extends beyond their training context length. This limitation severely constrains their utility in interactive applications where agents explore and frequently revisit locations.

The motivation for this work stems from two specific, interconnected challenges:

1. **Addressability of Memory:** Current models predominantly use temporal Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) to encode the relative distance between a query and a cached key. During training, the model only encounters relative offsets within a specific bounded range (e.g., $\Delta t_{train}$). As generation progresses beyond this training horizon, the temporal offsets for older cached frames become increasingly large, falling outside the learned distribution. Consequently, even if these old frames are physically present in the KV cache, the attention mechanism, which relies on these positional encodings, cannot reliably "address" or retrieve them. This effectively makes distant memories unreadable, undermining visual persistence. The motivation is to devise a mechanism that ensures past observations remain within a temporal positional encoding range that the model was trained on, thereby keeping them retrievable regardless of the generation length.

2. **Informativeness of Compressed Memory:** To manage the linearly growing KV cache and mitigate the addressability problem, compressing old frames into a fixed-size memory is a common strategy. However, the authors observe that naive compression methods, such as simple averaging of RoPE-rotated keys, lead to "phase cancellation." Because each frame's key is rotated by a different angle based on its original timestamp, averaging these rotated vectors can cause their signals to cancel each other out, especially for distant frames where rotation angles are vastly different. This corruption renders the compressed memory uninformative, even if it is technically addressable. The motivation here is to develop compression techniques that preserve the semantic content of the summarized history, preventing the loss of critical visual information.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need to overcome the fundamental limitations of existing video world models in maintaining long-term visual consistency during extended interactive rollouts. It aims to achieve this by simultaneously ensuring that historical visual data is both retrievable (addressable) and meaningfully preserved (informative) within a fixed memory budget, without the computational expense and effort of retraining the underlying model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed solution, WorldTrace, is a training-free memory framework designed to enhance long-horizon visual persistence in autoregressive video world models. It operates by restructuring how the KV cache manages and accesses historical information.

**A. Cache Structure and Virtual Position Assignment:**
WorldTrace partitions the local attention window ($L_{attn}$) into two main components:
- **Recent Window ($\mathcal{R}$):** Stores the $N_r$ most recent verbatim latent frames.
- **Summary Cache ($\mathcal{S}$):** Contains $N_s$ slots for compressed distant history. The total size of the cache is $N_s + N_r = L_{attn}$, keeping it fixed and equal to the training context length.

The core of WorldTrace's addressability solution is **WorldTrace Slot Indexing (Definition 1)**. This scheme assigns a distinct, in-distribution virtual temporal position ($t_v^s$) to each summary slot $s$ relative to the current query frame $q$. Specifically, $t_v^s = q - (L_{attn} - 1 - s)$, where $s=0, \dots, N_s-1$. This design ensures four key properties:
1. **Linearity:** Simple, predictable mapping between slot rank and position.
2. **In-distribution Positions:** Summary slot positions always remain within the range $[t_{v,min}, t_{v,max}]$ (defined as $[\max(0, q - \Delta t_{train}), q - N_r]$), which the model was trained on.
3. **Horizon-stability:** Virtual positions do not shift out of distribution as the generation horizon ($N$) extends, as they depend only on slot rank and current query $q$, not absolute generation length.
4. **Distinct Positions:** Each summary slot maintains a unique position, preventing the positional collapse observed in methods like Block-relative RoPE, where multiple distant slots could map to the same OOD position.

**B. Canonical Keys and Compression Domain:**
To ensure informativeness and compatibility with the virtual position assignment, WorldTrace stores and processes keys in a **canonical (unrotated) key domain**. This addresses the phase cancellation issue (Sec. 2.2) by:
- Unrotating each source key to remove its original RoPE rotation.
- Performing compression (e.g., averaging) in this rotation-invariant canonical space.
- Re-rotating the compressed canonical key to the assigned virtual position $t_v^s$ only when attention scores are computed.
This process ensures that the compressed content does not carry leftover phase from its original timestamps, making it suitable for placement at any virtual position.

**C. Memory Compression Variants (Content Writers):**
WorldTrace proposes two complementary content writers for the summary slots, each optimized for different aspects of visual persistence:

1. **WorldTrace-Field (for Temporal Coherence):**
 - This variant aims to preserve the coarse field of past information for smooth temporal coherence.
 - It implements **Canonical Key Averaging (Definition 2)**: For each summary slot, all source frames assigned to that slot are first unrotated to their canonical (unrotated) key representation. These canonical keys are then averaged. The resulting mean canonical key is then re-rotated to the slot's assigned virtual position $t_v^s$ at attention time.
 - Values are similarly averaged.
 - The "projection matrix P" for WorldTrace-Field consists of rows that uniformly average keys over contiguous temporal groups. This is designed to be near-optimal when future attention spreads smoothly over history.
 - The system dynamically maintains these slot averages as the rollout proceeds, keeping evicted canonical keys in host memory for recomputation of group means.

2. **WorldTrace-Landmark (for Episodic Recall):**
 - This variant is designed for long-horizon recall of specific scenes.
 - It fills summary slots with **verbatim high-value past frames**, akin to Landmark Attention but applied training-free.
 - **Landmark Selection:** Scene-entry events are detected by monitoring the cosine distance between consecutive canonical keys; frames where this distance spikes above a threshold $\tau$ are marked as scene entries.
 - **Frozen Landmark Keys:** The most recent $N_s$ scene-entry frames are stored verbatim. Once a frame is selected as a landmark, its key is frozen in canonical form. At attention time, this single frozen canonical key is directly re-rotated to the summary slot's current virtual position $t_v^s$. This avoids accumulation of floating-point errors from repeated unrotate-rerotate cycles.
 - The "projection matrix P" for WorldTrace-Landmark consists of one-hot vectors, selecting a single scene-entry frame verbatim. This is near-optimal when recall queries concentrate on a few salient past frames.

**D. Position-Content Coupling Resolution:**
WorldTrace addresses the inherent coupling between position assignment and content compression (Sec. 2.1 and 2.2). The slot indexing (Def. 1) provides a consistent, in-distribution virtual position scheme. Concurrently, canonical key averaging (WorldTrace-Field) and frozen landmark keys (WorldTrace-Landmark) handle the source-to-virtual shift by operating in the rotation-invariant canonical space, preventing signal degradation and ensuring content accurately reflects the assigned virtual position.

**E. LoopBench Benchmark:**
To evaluate episodic recall under controlled conditions, the authors introduce **LoopBench**. This benchmark tasks the model with navigating a path that departs from a scene, visits other locations, and then returns to the initial scene. The performance is measured by comparing the regenerated return frame against the model's own generation of the original scene appearance at geometrically matched positions, eliminating the need for external ground truth. LoopBench varies path topology (e.g., ABA, ABCA, ABCDA loops), rollout length, camera orientation, and multi-revisit depth to systematically test memory capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate WorldTrace's ability to maintain visual persistence, specifically temporal coherence and episodic recall, across extended generation horizons without retraining.

**A. Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Temporal Coherence:** Measured by TempSSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure between consecutive decoded frames, higher is better) and Local Scene Drift (mean per-chunk CLIP feature distance to the preceding chunk, lower is better).
- **Episodic Recall:** Measured by Position-Aligned CLIP (PAC, CLIP-ViT-H/14 cosine similarity between geometrically paired return- and forward-leg frames in loops, higher is better).

**B. WorldTrace-Field: Temporal Coherence:**
- **Position Assignment:** WorldTrace's horizon-independent slot-rank position assignment was compared against Block-relative (clamps OOD offsets) and Centroid-linear (assigns positions based on average timestamp, $N$-dependent) variants, all using canonical key averaging.
 - At short horizons (N=8 and N=16 chunks), WorldTrace outperformed Block-relative (+5.9% and +2.8% TempSSIM) and Centroid-linear (+9.5% and +13.8% TempSSIM), demonstrating that accurate in-distribution position assignment is critical for effective compression.
 - Qualitatively (Fig. 4), baselines like sliding window, Block-relative, and Centroid-linear began to drift and generate incoherent geometry by 18 chunks, while WorldTrace-Field maintained coherence across longer horizons.
 - Quantitatively (Tab. 2), at N=48 chunks (24x training horizon), WorldTrace-Field achieved the highest TempSSIM (0.545) and lowest Scene Drift (0.0295), outperforming the sliding window baseline (0.472 TempSSIM) and other canonical averaging variants. This indicates a +15.5% relative gain in TempSSIM over the sliding window.

- **Canonical vs. Naive Key Compression:** An ablation study (Tab. 5) showed the benefit of canonical averaging in preventing phase cancellation.
 - Holding Block-relative positions fixed, switching from naive RoPE-space averaging to canonical averaging reduced LatentDiff (a measure of image difference in latent space) from 0.312 to 0.233 at $N_s=4$ (-25.3%).
 - Naive averaging's LatentDiff worsened with more summary slots, while canonical averaging remained more stable, confirming its role in preserving low-frequency content.

**C. WorldTrace-Landmark: Episodic Recall on LoopBench:**
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 5):** The sliding-window baseline consistently failed to reconstruct scene A upon revisiting, producing divergent scenes. WorldTrace-Landmark, however, successfully matched scene A across ABA, ABCA, and ABCDA loop geometries.
- **Quantitative Results (Tab. 3):** WorldTrace-Landmark consistently improved PAC (episodic recall) over the sliding-window baseline across all LoopBench difficulty tiers:
 - **Varying Topology:** For an ABA loop (N=16), PAC improved from 0.723 to 0.864. Similar gains were observed for ABCA (0.673 to 0.792) and ABCDA (0.666 to 0.799).
 - **Varying Rollout Length (ABA):** Gains were most significant at longer horizons. For N=32, PAC improved from 0.627 to 0.825 (+19.8% absolute gain).
 - **Camera Orientation:** Even in challenging scenarios like a full 360-degree camera pan, WorldTrace-Landmark showed an improvement (0.559 to 0.577 PAC).
 - **Multi-Revisit Patterns:** WorldTrace-Landmark also performed better in scenarios with multiple revisits (e.g., ABABA, ABCBA, ABCDBA).
- **Episodic Recall Sweep (Tab. 10):** A comprehensive sweep across N={16, 32, 48, 64, 128, 256} demonstrated that only verbatim landmark recall (WorldTrace-Landmark) scales effectively to very long horizons (N=256), maintaining a PAC of 0.989, whereas canonical-K anchoring methods dropped to 0.610.
- **Cross-Architecture Experiments (Tab. 11):** On LingBot-World (a 14B model), WorldTrace-Landmark improved PAC from 4x training horizon onwards, indicating the positional bottleneck is not specific to model scale or architecture.

**D. Memory and Runtime:**
- WorldTrace-Field's peak GPU memory matched the sliding-window baseline because its evicted canonical keys are offloaded to host memory.
- WorldTrace-Landmark added a constant overhead (approx. 0.6 GB) for storing frozen scene-anchor keys, which does not grow with rollout length.
- Both WorldTrace variants maintained runtime per chunk within 6% of the sliding-window baseline, indicating negligible practical overhead (Tab. 15).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work by Wu et al. presents WorldTrace as a contribution to the field of interactive video world models by addressing critical limitations in long-horizon visual persistence. The findings indicate that the degradation of visual persistence in such models is fundamentally a problem of memory addressability, exacerbated by out-of-distribution temporal positional encodings and inefficient compression strategies.

The primary significance of WorldTrace lies in its ability to extend visually persistent generation without requiring model retraining, which offers practical advantages in terms of computational cost and accessibility for existing pre-trained models. By ensuring that compressed memories remain both addressable (via slot-rank virtual positions) and informative (via canonical key operations), WorldTrace provides a framework that allows pre-trained models to function reliably over significantly longer interactive rollouts than previously possible.

Specific impacts include:
- **Enhanced Interactive Experiences:** The improvement in temporal coherence and episodic recall can enable more consistent and believable interactive environments in applications such as next-generation game engines, virtual reality, and robotics simulation. Agents can explore, deviate, and return to locations with a higher expectation of consistent scene appearance.
- **Reduced Computational Burden:** As a training-free inference-time solution, WorldTrace allows practitioners to leverage existing large-scale pre-trained world models for long-horizon tasks without the need for costly and time-consuming retraining or fine-tuning. This can democratize access to advanced generative capabilities.
- **Conceptual Clarity:** The research clarifies that memory for video world models should be designed with explicit consideration of how content is addressed and what type of visual traces are stored. The structured sparse attention framework and the two complementary memory writers (WorldTrace-Field for coherence and WorldTrace-Landmark for episodic recall) offer concrete approaches to this design problem.
- **Benchmark Contribution:** The introduction of LoopBench provides a standardized, objective method for evaluating episodic memory in autoregressive world models. This benchmark, which assesses a model's ability to reconstruct previously visited scenes after detours, can facilitate future research and comparisons in this domain.

The limitations, such as the lossy nature of compression and the reliance on a scene-entry detector for landmark recall, also point to areas for future development. These include exploring geometry-aware canonical keys, learning adaptive scene-entry policies, and integrating fine-tuning extensions for even greater flexibility. Overall, WorldTrace offers a methodological framework for improving memory management in autoregressive video world models, with implications for the development of more robust and immersive interactive AI systems.
