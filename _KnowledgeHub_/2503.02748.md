# Bridging VLM and KMP: Enabling Fine-grained robotic manipulation via Semantic Keypoints Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> The VL-MP framework integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Kernelized Movement Primitives (KMP) to enable fine-grained robotic manipulation. It uses semantic keypoint constraints for accurate VLM decision transfer and a local feature enhanced KMP (LFE-KMP) to preserve complex trajectory shapes, demonstrating improved success rates in pouring tasks under ambiguous conditions.

## Problem

- VLM-based robotic systems encounter information distortion when translating high-level semantic decisions into specific parameters for lower-level trajectory generation, hindering fine-grained manipulation.
- Traditional Movement Primitive (MP) methods, including KMP, often struggle to preserve the exact shape of complex movements, especially around critical start and end points, leading to trajectory deviations and task failures.
- Existing methods bridging VLMs and low-level control typically neglect the detailed parameter transfer process, resulting in suboptimal utilization of VLM insights for precise trajectory generalization.

## Method

- ==The VL-MP== framework establishes a Bridge Layer that uses 3D semantic keypoint representation and constraints to accurately derive task parameters from VLM decisions, minimizing information distortion.
- A Local Feature Enhanced Kernelized Movement Primitive (LFE-KMP) is introduced, which locally resamples and merges start and end points from reference trajectories to ensure shape-preserving generalization.
- The framework integrates a multi-turn dialogue system with GPT-4o and GroundingDINO for VLM-based perception and decision-making, generating standardized instructions and resolving ambiguities.

## Results

- VL-MP achieved a **90%** success rate in normal pouring environments and an **80%** success rate in ambiguous environments, outperforming baselines like VLM+Prim (**20%** ambiguous) and Diffusion Policy (**0%** ambiguous).
- In shape preservation simulations, VL-MP demonstrated superior performance with a topological similarity of **92.76%** and trajectory smoothness of **0.0663**, marking a **42.82%** improvement in topological similarity over standard KMP.
- Error analysis revealed that VL-MP's failures were mainly attributed to decision errors from target occlusion, while baseline methods frequently suffered from trajectory generation errors and collisions due to poor shape preservation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate representation of task decision parameters via 3D semantic keypoint constraints is crucial for effectively transferring high-level VLM information to low-level control, enabling precise manipulation.
> - Enhancing Movement Primitive models with local trajectory features, particularly around start and end points, significantly improves the preservation of complex trajectory shapes during generalization.
> - Combining VLM's adaptive perception and decision-making with KMP's precise trajectory generalization capabilities allows for robust fine-grained manipulation in ambiguous real-world environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robotic manipulation is an ongoing area of research that has evolved significantly, from early Movement Primitive (MP) techniques to modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These two approaches represent distinct paradigms with complementary strengths and weaknesses.

VLM-based methods, which leverage the adaptive perception of large language models, excel in zero-shot learning, open-world decision-making, and skill generalization. They can interpret complex natural language instructions and adapt to varied scenarios. However, these methods often encounter challenges with fine-grained motion planning, precision of manipulation, and inference efficiency. Their pre-training on caption-driven data may limit the retention of intricate visual details necessary for highly precise tasks.

Conversely, MP-based methods, such as Kernelized Movement Primitives (KMP), are proficient in the precise generalization and reproduction of skill trajectories from demonstrations. They provide a mathematical framework for modeling and reproducing complex joint behaviors with high fidelity. However, traditional MP methods typically lack autonomous decision-making capabilities, struggle to generalize across subtle variations within object instances, and can be sensitive to disturbances, often leading to shape distortion in generalized trajectories.

Existing research attempting to bridge VLMs and lower-level control often focuses on action function calls or task sequence decisions generated by large language models, neglecting the detailed parameter transfer process required for precise trajectory generalization. This gap results in information distortion when VLM decisions are translated into MP task parameters, leading to insufficient utilization of VLM insights and suboptimal fine-grained manipulation. Furthermore, current MP-based imitation learning methods frequently overlook critical local features around the start and end points of demonstration trajectories, which can cause significant deviations and failures in complex tasks.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms, proposing a framework to bridge VLM and KMP. It aims to integrate the decision-making and adaptive perception strengths of VLMs with the precise trajectory generalization capabilities of KMP. The objective is to overcome the limitations of each approach when applied in isolation, particularly in achieving fine-grained manipulation under ambiguous real-world conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, termed VL-MP, that integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Kernelized Movement Primitives (KMP) to enable fine-grained robotic manipulation. This framework is designed to function effectively in ambiguous environments by leveraging the strengths of both VLM-based decision-making and KMP-based trajectory generalization.

The motivation for this work stems from two main challenges observed in the current robotic manipulation landscape:

1. **Information Distortion in Task Parameter Transfer:** Contemporary VLM-based robotic systems excel at high-level decision-making and understanding complex instructions. However, when this high-level semantic information needs to be translated into specific, actionable parameters for lower-level trajectory generation algorithms like KMP, a significant loss or distortion of information can occur. This distortion prevents the underlying trajectory generalization methods from fully utilizing the rich decision context provided by the VLM, leading to inadequate precision for fine-grained manipulation tasks. The research aims to establish a low-distortion decision information transfer bridge to ensure the accurate transmission and utilization of VLM-derived decision parameters.

2. **Limitations of Traditional Movement Primitives in Shape Preservation:** Existing MP-based imitation learning methods, including KMP, are adept at reproducing general trajectories but often struggle to preserve the exact shape of complex movements, especially around critical start and end points. This limitation means that even if a VLM provides precise target parameters, the generalized trajectory might deviate from the desired path or shape, resulting in failed manipulation, particularly in tasks requiring high accuracy or intricate interactions. The motivation is to enhance KMP to support shape-preserving generalization of complex trajectories by addressing the neglect of local trajectory features.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the VL-MP framework include:

- Developing a method for accurate representation of task decision parameters using semantic keypoint constraints. This involves extracting and constraining 3D semantic keypoints from VLM decision information to generate precise task parameters.
- Introducing a local trajectory feature-enhanced KMP (LFE-KMP) that can achieve shape preservation for complex trajectories by emphasizing manipulation details near the start and end points of demonstration trajectories.
- Creating a cohesive framework that enables autonomous decision-making by VLMs and subsequent accurate, fine-grained manipulation generalization by LFE-KMP in real-world ambiguous environments.
- Validating the effectiveness of the proposed framework in practical robotic tasks, specifically focusing on pouring scenarios, and quantitatively assessing its ability to maintain complex trajectory shapes.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level precise control, facilitating more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VL-MP framework integrates a VLM for high-level decision-making, a Bridge Layer for accurate task parameter transfer, and a Local Feature Enhanced Kernelized Movement Primitive (LFE-KMP) for fine-grained trajectory generalization.

#### 3.1. Method Overview

The pipeline begins with the VLM processing environmental and language inputs for task decision-making. The Bridge Layer then extracts and constrains 3D semantic keypoints to represent task parameters accurately. These parameters are transmitted to the LFE-KMP, which generates fine-grained manipulation trajectories by resampling local features of the demonstration trajectory.

#### 3.2. VLM-based Perception and Decision-making

The framework utilizes a multi-turn dialogue system for robot decision-making, employing GPT-4o via the OpenAI API and GroundingDINO for visual perception.
- **Prompt Engineering:** The system adopts a prompt engineering framework based on recursively self-generated code, where Language Model Programs (LMPs) manage independent functions.
- **GI (Generate Instruction) and DI (Disambiguate Instruction) Modules:** GPT-4o is configured with specific system roles and prompts for GI and DI. GI processes natural language inputs to generate preliminary standardized instructions. GroundingDINO then uses these instructions as queries to detect candidate objects and generate detection masks. DI, sharing historical dialogue context with GI, resolves ambiguities from candidate retrieval data, ultimately selecting a unique target object for task execution.

#### 3.3. Task Parameter Representation via Bridge Layer

The Bridge Layer is designed to mitigate information distortion during the transfer of VLM decision information to MP task parameters by employing 3D semantic keypoint representation and constraints.
- **3D Keypoint Definition:** Three categories of keypoints—interaction, positional, and boundary—are defined to describe interactive details, geometric constraints, and the target object's characteristics (e.g., scale, shape, orientation). For instance, in a pouring task involving a mug, keypoints may include the rim (interaction), cup mouth center and base center (positional), and handle (boundary).
- **Keypoint Detection Network:** HRNet is used for keypoint generation. Its backbone extracts four multi-scale feature maps from an input RGB image. These features are then fused through unified upsampling and Conv2D-based processing, followed by a self-attention module to enhance global dependencies. Three prediction heads subsequently estimate center coordinates, 2D keypoint positions (u,v), and keypoint depth (z). 3D keypoints are computed from these outputs using the camera's intrinsic and extrinsic matrices.
- **Termination Task Parameter Derivation:** Interaction state information from the final frames of multiple human demonstrations in a skill library is crucial. The shape, size, and orientation of the target object in demonstrations impose constraints on the manipulated object.
 - **Position Constraint:** The termination position (`pos`) is constrained by the positional distribution of the manipulated object's interaction keypoints (K_i). To account for variations in target object shape and size across demonstrations, the termination position is normalized using a constant topological relationship between the keypoints of the target object and the manipulated object. This involves projecting keypoints onto an x-y plane and applying rotational and scaling corrections to derive the corrected interaction keypoint and thus the termination position.
 - **Pose Constraint:** The pose distribution of the manipulated object constrains the termination task pose. An orientation vector (Z) is constructed from K_i, and the angle (`α`) between the target interaction object's vector (Z_master) and the manipulated object's vector (Z_slave) is considered.
 - **Endpose Estimation (Algorithm 1):** This algorithm samples interaction keypoints and generates a series of candidate orientations based on `α`. The target pose (P_target) is selected as the one minimizing the distance relative to the initial pose (P_start).
- The initial pose and the derived skill termination pose collectively form the new task parameters `{ˆA_ξp, ˆb_ξp}` for the LFE-KMP.

#### 3.4. Trajectory Generalization via Local Feature Enhanced KMP (LFE-KMP)

LFE-KMP is introduced to improve KMP's ability to capture subtle variations and preserve trajectory shape, especially near start and end points.
- **Demonstration Trajectory Extraction:** Pose trajectories `ξ(t) = {s, q}` (position `s`, orientation `q`) are extracted from human demonstration videos. These trajectories are preprocessed by representing them on a Riemannian manifold to strengthen orientation constraints. A local coordinate system is constructed based on the manipulated object's keypoints to mitigate camera randomness.
- **GMM/GMR Modeling:** Demonstration trajectories, encoded in task-parameterized local coordinate systems, are modeled using GMM/GMR to obtain a probabilistic distribution `ˆξ(t)` for each task parameter, serving as the skill reference trajectory.
- **Kernel-based Refitting:** The KMP algorithm refits the probability distribution of the demonstration trajectory `ξ(t)` to the reference trajectory `ˆξ(t)` by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, ensuring minimal information loss.
- **Local Feature Enhancement:** To address convergence issues near start/end poses, the algorithm locally resamples the start and end points of the modeled reference trajectory `ˆξ` for each task parameter. These sampled points (P_e) are treated as desired trajectory points with minimal covariance (`εIo`) and merged with the original reference trajectory to form an extended reference trajectory. The KMP algorithm then applies to this extended trajectory. The KL divergence loss function for LFE-KMP integrates this enhancement (Equation 5).
- **Global Trajectory Generalization:** The local LFE-KMP results are transformed into the global coordinate system using a Gaussian linear transformation, yielding `˜ξ(t*)`. The final generalized global trajectory for new tasks is determined by maximizing the product of Gaussians under the new task parameters (Equations 6, 7), which is then sampled to generate the desired task trajectory.

#### 3.5. Experimental Design

- **Real-world Pouring Tasks:** Experiments were conducted in two environments: a "normal" environment with a single clear target and an "ambiguous" environment with multiple visually similar targets and vague initial instructions. A 6-axis collaborative robotic arm and a RealSense D435i camera were used. The success rate (transferring half a cup of water without spillage over 10 trials) was the primary metric.
- **Baselines for Pouring:** VLM+Prim (a Code as Policies variant) and Diffusion Policy (a generative trajectory imitation learning method).
- **Simulation for Shape Preservation:** The Handwriting dataset (GShape subset) was used to quantitatively evaluate trajectory shape preservation under significant changes in start/end point positions. Metrics included topological similarity (`c_s`) and trajectory smoothness (`κ_s`).
- **Baselines for Shape Preservation:** Kernel Movement Primitives (KMP) and Task-Parameterized Gaussian Mixture Models (TP-GMM).
- **Error Analysis:** Errors were categorized into Dynamics Error, Trajectory Generation Error, and Decision Error.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in real-world scenarios and simulations aimed to validate the VL-MP framework's effectiveness in achieving fine-grained manipulation and preserving trajectory shapes.

#### 4.1. Pouring Task Performance (Real-World)

The VL-MP framework demonstrated its capability in executing real-world pouring tasks, particularly under ambiguous conditions.
- **Success Rates (Table I):**
 - In the *normal environment* (one clear target), VL-MP achieved a 90% success rate (9/10 trials).
 - In the *ambiguous environment* (multiple similar targets, vague instructions), VL-MP achieved an 80% success rate (8/10 trials).
- **Comparative Performance:**
 - VL-MP significantly outperformed the baselines. In the ambiguous environment, VLM+Prim achieved a 20% success rate (2/10), and Diffusion Policy achieved 0% (0/10). This indicates VL-MP's effective utilization of VLM for logical reasoning and accurate conversion of decision information into task parameters.
 - In the normal environment, VL-MP (90%) also surpassed VLM+Prim (50%) and Diffusion Policy (40%), suggesting that its local feature enhancement contributes to improved precision in trajectory learning.

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiment Results and Error Analysis

Simulation experiments further explored the robustness and failure modes of the methods.
- **Robustness:** VL-MP exhibited robust performance in ideal simulation conditions, even with obstacles near the initial position (Fig. 9a).
- **Baseline Limitations:** VLM+Prim and Diffusion Policy frequently experienced collisions near skill initiation points (Fig. 9b). This was attributed to their lack of local trajectory feature enhancement. Additionally, these methods struggled to adapt to intra-class instance shape variations, leading to mug interference during the final pouring process (Fig. 9c).
- **Error Breakdown (Fig. 10):**
 - VL-MP demonstrated the lowest overall error rate. Its primary failures were due to *decision errors*, often caused by target occlusion in ambiguous environments.
 - Diffusion Policy and VLM+Prim showed frequent *trajectory generation errors*, primarily from collisions between mug rims, indicating issues in trajectory synthesis.

#### 4.3. Shape Preservation of Trajectories Generalization (Simulation)

To assess the ability to preserve complex trajectory shapes, simulation experiments were conducted using the Handwriting dataset (GShape subset), comparing VL-MP with KMP and TP-GMM.
- **Quantitative Metrics (Table II):**
 - VL-MP achieved a topological similarity (`c_s`) of 92.76% and a trajectory smoothness (`κ_s`) of 0.0663.
 - KMP showed `c_s` of 64.95% and `κ_s` of 0.4127.
 - TP-GMM recorded `c_s` of 84.89% and `κ_s` of 0.0911.
- **Comparative Analysis (Fig. 11):**
 - VL-MP exhibited the strongest shape preservation capabilities under disturbances (Fig. 11a), outperforming KMP and TP-GMM in both topological similarity and smoothness.
 - KMP demonstrated a complete loss of the original shape characteristics (Fig. 11b), with generated trajectories heavily influenced by absolute start/end point coordinates, leading to severe shape distortion. VL-MP showed a 42.82% improvement in `c_s` and an 83.03% enhancement in `κ_s` compared to KMP, highlighting the efficacy of its local feature enhancement.
 - TP-GMM partially retained shape features but resulted in significant deviations of the start and end points from the specified new task parameters (Fig. 11c), which would lead to task failure in real-world scenarios. This was attributed to TP-GMM treating all task parameters equally, leading to undesirable influences on local trajectory features. VL-MP mitigated this issue.

In summary, the results indicate that VL-MP effectively addresses the challenges of information distortion and shape preservation, enabling robust and precise fine-grained manipulation in both unambiguous and ambiguous settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VL-MP framework represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by effectively bridging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Kernelized Movement Primitives (KMP). This integration addresses two persistent challenges in autonomous manipulation: the precise transfer of high-level VLM decisions to low-level control and the preservation of complex trajectory shapes during generalization.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

- **Enhanced Fine-grained Manipulation:** By creating a low-distortion bridge for decision information transfer through semantic keypoint constraints, VL-MP enables robots to execute tasks requiring high precision and intricate movements. The ability to accurately interpret VLM decisions and translate them into specific task parameters for KMP’s generalization contributes to finer control and higher success rates in complex scenarios.
- **Improved Adaptability and Robustness:** The framework allows robots to perform effectively in ambiguous, open-set environments where task instructions may be vague and target objects visually similar. The VLM's decision-making capabilities, coupled with robust parameter transfer, enable the robot to disambiguate tasks and adapt to variations in the environment, which is a critical step towards more intelligent and flexible robotic systems.
- **Superior Trajectory Generalization:** The introduction of Local Feature Enhanced KMP (LFE-KMP) specifically addresses the issue of trajectory shape distortion prevalent in traditional MP methods. By emphasizing local features around start and end points and incorporating them into the trajectory learning process, LFE-KMP ensures that generalized trajectories maintain their original, desired shape, which is crucial for tasks where geometric accuracy is paramount.
- **Foundation for Future Dexterous Manipulation:** The demonstrated capabilities in tasks like pouring, which require precise control and interaction with objects, indicate a potential for VL-MP to be applied to a wider range of dexterous manipulation tasks. The authors' planned future work on VLM-guided whole-arm planning suggests a pathway towards even more complex and intricate robotic operations, moving beyond end-effector trajectories to holistic robot control.

The potential impact of this research includes:

- **Advancement in Robotic Skill Learning:** VL-MP provides a concrete method for learning and generalizing robotic skills that are both semantically intelligent and physically precise. This could lead to faster and more reliable deployment of robots in new and varied tasks.
- **Applications in Diverse Fields:** Industries requiring high-precision robotic operations, such as manufacturing, assembly, surgery assistance, and service robotics, could benefit from systems capable of fine-grained manipulation and adaptive decision-making. For example, robots could handle delicate objects, perform intricate assembly steps, or serve in unstructured human environments with greater accuracy and autonomy.
- **Mitigation of Information Loss in AI-Robot Integration:** The focus on reducing information distortion during the VLM-to-KMP transfer addresses a fundamental challenge in integrating high-level AI models with physical robotic systems. This aspect has broader implications for how cognitive AI capabilities can be effectively translated into physical actions.

By overcoming limitations in both VLM-based decision-making and KMP-based trajectory generation, VL-MP offers a step towards truly autonomous and dexterous robotic manipulation.
