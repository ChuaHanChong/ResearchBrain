# Squeeze the Soaked Sponge: Efficient Off-policy Reinforcement Finetuning for Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ReMix, an off-policy reinforcement finetuning framework, reduces the data volume needed for fine-tuning Large Language Models for reasoning tasks by over 30x for 1.5B models and over 450x for 7B models, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy compared to on-policy methods.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy Reinforcement Finetuning (RFT) methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) are sample-inefficient, requiring costly and time-consuming autoregressive rollouts for each optimization step.
- This sample inefficiency creates a significant computational bottleneck, hindering the scaling and economic finetuning of LLMs for complex reasoning tasks.
- Off-policy RFT, despite its potential for efficiency, remains underexplored and often lacks thorough comparisons or insights into its impact on LLM reasoning dynamics.

## Method

- Proposes Reincarnating ==Mix-policy== Proximal Policy Optimization (**ReMix**), an off-policy RFT framework using Mix-policy Proximal Policy Gradient (Mix-PPG) with an increased Update-To-Data ratio to build mini-batches from a mixture of historical and current on-policy data.
- Incorporates a KL-Convex Policy Constraint (KLC) for dynamic regularization, which balances deviation from the initial base model and the most recent historical policy.
- Employs Policy Reincarnation, a two-stage training approach that transitions from an efficient initial Mix-PPG stage to a stable on-policy Proximal Policy Gradient stage, mitigating off-policy bias for asymptotic improvement.

## Results

- ReMix-PPO achieved state-of-the-art Pass@1 accuracy (**52.10%** for **1.5B**, **63.27%** for **7B** models) on math reasoning benchmarks while reducing rollout data volume by over **30x** (**1.5B**) and over **450x** (**7B**) compared to leading baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three proposed components—Mix-PPG with increased Update-To-Data (UTD) ratio, KL-Convex Policy Constraint, and Policy Reincarnation—are crucial and synergistic for ReMix's superior performance.
- Analysis revealed that off-policy learning implicitly favors shorter responses and reduces self-reflection in LLMs, but ReMix demonstrated robustness to response length constraints and prompt template variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy Reinforcement Finetuning (RFT) can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance in LLMs with drastic reductions in data volume (30x-450x) when properly designed.
> - A multi-component approach, combining mixed-policy updates, dynamic KL constraints, and policy reincarnation, is essential for leveraging off-policy efficiency while maintaining training stability and asymptotic performance.
> - Off-policy learning can induce an 'implicit preference for shorter responses' (Whipping Effect) and suppress self-reflection, which needs to be managed for optimal reasoning ability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized AI capabilities, particularly in areas requiring complex reasoning. While Supervised Finetuning (SFT) has been a common approach to adapt pre-trained LLMs to specific tasks, its effectiveness is often limited by the availability and scalability of high-quality, task-specific data. This limitation has led to the rise of **Reinforcement Finetuning (RFT)**, also known as Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), as a powerful alternative. RFT treats the LLM as a policy model and learns directly from verifiable reward signals (e.g., correctness of answers) rather than relying solely on human feedback or extensive labeled datasets. This paradigm has shown significant promise in enhancing LLMs' reasoning abilities, particularly in fields like mathematics and coding, as exemplified by models such as DeepSeek-R1.

However, RFT inherits a long-standing challenge from traditional Reinforcement Learning: **sample inefficiency**. Most existing RFT methods, including popular ones like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), are inherently **on-policy**. This means they require generating fresh data (rollouts) from the current policy in each optimization iteration, discarding previously generated data. For LLMs, especially larger models engaged in long reasoning processes, these autoregressive rollouts are computationally expensive and time-consuming, forming a significant bottleneck for further scaling and wider adoption.

The paper, "Squeeze the Soaked Sponge," directly addresses this critical issue by advocating for the "renaissance of off-policy RL" in the context of RFT. Off-policy RL algorithms are naturally more sample-efficient as they can learn from data generated by past or different policies (i.e., experience replay). While some recent works have begun to incorporate off-policy data into RFT (e.g., using non-uniform replay strategies, asymmetric learning from signals, or learning from demonstrations), the authors argue that off-policy RFT remains underexplored. Specifically, previous attempts have often lacked thorough comparisons against state-of-the-art on-policy RFT models on diverse benchmarks and have not fully investigated the influence of off-policy learning on the dynamics of reasoning ability.

This work fits into the landscape by providing a comprehensive and practical solution for efficient off-policy RFT, demonstrating its superiority in terms of both performance and significant cost reduction. It moves beyond theoretical adaptations by systematically integrating multiple advanced off-policy RL techniques and rigorously evaluating their combined effects, also offering critical insights into the underlying learning dynamics, distinguishing it from prior, more narrowly focused explorations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are threefold:

1. **To overcome the sample inefficiency of existing on-policy RFT methods:** The authors are motivated by the high computational cost (in terms of compute and time) associated with on-policy RFT, which requires constant generation of new data from the current LLM policy. This bottleneck hinders the economic and efficient scaling of LLMs, especially for complex reasoning tasks that demand long reasoning processes.
2. **To explore and leverage the promise of off-policy RL for RFT:** Recognizing that off-policy algorithms are inherently more sample-efficient by utilizing historical data, the paper aims to adapt and integrate these principles into LLM finetuning. The goal is to make RFT more efficient without sacrificing performance.
3. **To develop a general and effective off-policy RFT approach:** The authors propose "ReMix" as a versatile framework that can enable existing on-policy proximal policy gradient methods (like PPO and GRPO) to effectively utilize off-policy data. This generality makes ReMix broadly applicable to current RFT pipelines.
4. **To gain deeper insights into the effects of off-policy learning on LLM reasoning abilities:** Beyond just performance, the paper aims to understand *how* off-policy training influences the learning process of reasoning behaviors in LLMs. This involves analyzing factors like response length, self-reflection, and the stability of training under varying degrees of "off-policyness."

In essence, the motivation is to make the powerful RFT paradigm more accessible and scalable for developing advanced reasoning LLMs by drastically reducing its computational footprint, while also advancing the fundamental understanding of off-policy RL's impact on LLM fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Reincarnating Mix-policy Proximal Policy Optimization (ReMix)**, a general approach designed to imbue on-policy proximal policy gradient methods (like PPO and GRPO) with the ability to leverage off-policy data efficiently. ReMix consists of three synergistic major components:

1. **Mix-policy Proximal Policy Gradient (Mix-PPG) with Increased Update-To-Data (UTD) Ratio:**
 - **Data Utilization:** This component directly addresses sample inefficiency by employing a hybrid sampling strategy. Training mini-batches are constructed from a mixture of data generated by *historical policies* (off-policy data) and *the current policy* (on-policy data). This balances data reuse for efficiency with distribution alignment for stability.
 - **Objective Function:** The conventional clipped surrogate objective (e.g., in PPO) is modified to `L_Mix-PPG_k(θ)`, which accounts for data from past policies (π_k-i) using importance sampling ratios. A key design choice is explicitly maintaining a sufficient portion of on-policy data to prevent training instability.
 - **Increased UTD Ratio:** Inspired by works like REDQ, ReMix increases the Update-To-Data ratio (`m`). This means performing multiple gradient updates (`m` times) on the same sampled data batches, further reducing the need for fresh, expensive environment interactions (autoregressive rollouts).

2. **KL-Convex Policy Constraint (KLC):**
 - **Dynamic Regularization:** Unlike conventional RFT that imposes a static KL-constraint solely on deviations from the initial base pre-trained model (π_base), KLC introduces a dynamic anchor.
 - **Dual Anchoring:** The KL-divergence penalty is modified to be a convex combination of two terms: one constraining deviation from the `base model (π_base)` and another from the `most recent historical policy (π_k-1)`.
 - **Balance of Stability and Flexibility:** This dual-anchoring strategy allows the policy to retain foundational capabilities (preventing catastrophic forgetting from π_base) while also adapting to its most recently acquired knowledge (iterative refinement from π_k-1). The parameter `λ` balances these two constraints, adapting dynamically throughout training.

3. **Policy Reincarnation:**
 - **Seamless Transition:** This component addresses the known limitation of off-policy RL, where while it accelerates early-stage training, its inherent bias can limit asymptotic performance. Policy reincarnation enables a smooth transition from efficient early-stage learning to stable asymptotic improvement.
 - **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1 (Mix-PPG Stage):** The initial policy model is trained for a predetermined number of steps (`T`) using the proposed Mix-PPG algorithm, focusing on rapid initial improvement.
 - **Stage 2 (Reincarnating On-policy PPG Stage):** After `T` steps, a "reincarnation" event occurs. The base reference model for the KL constraint is *reset* from the initial `π_base` to the current policy model `π_T`. Concurrently, the training switches from Mix-PPG back to a standard on-policy PPG method (e.g., PPO or GRPO). The historical policy set `H` (used for off-policy data) is also dropped. This effectively re-initializes the on-policy training with a much stronger starting policy, leveraging the efficiency gains of the first stage without inheriting its asymptotic limitations.

In practice, the authors implement ReMix-PPO and ReMix-GRPO using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and -7B as base models, with specific hyperparameters for the off-policy data portion, UTD ratio, historical policy window size, and reincarnation step point. The KL-Convex coefficient `λ` decays over training steps to adjust the balance between base model alignment and recent policy consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the significant superiority of ReMix across multiple dimensions:

1. **SOTA-Level Performance with Drastic Efficiency Gains:**
 - **1.5B Models:** ReMix-PPO achieved an average Pass@1 accuracy of 52.10% on five math reasoning benchmarks (AIME’24, AMC’23, Minerva, OlympiadBench, MATH500) with only 0.079M response rollouts and 350 training steps. This is comparable to the strongest baseline, DeepScaleR (52.14%), but with an **over 30x reduction** in rollout data volume (DeepScaleR used 2.519M rollouts).
 - **7B Models:** ReMix-PPO achieved 63.27%/64.39% Pass@1 accuracy with 0.007M/0.011M response rollouts and 50/75 training steps, respectively. This performance is slightly higher than the leading 7B baseline, AceReason-Nemotron (63.24%), but with an **over 450x reduction** in rollout data volume (AceReason-Nemotron used >3.584M rollouts).
 - **Comparison to PPO/GRPO:** ReMix-PPO and ReMix-GRPO consistently outperformed their standard on-policy counterparts (PPO and GRPO) with significantly fewer training steps and lower rollout data volume, showing 6x to 10x reduction in rollout data volume.

2. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Synergy:**
 - Removing any of the three core components (Mix-PPG with UTD, KL-Convex, Policy Reincarnation) resulted in lower final performance compared to the full ReMix, often comparable to or slightly better than standard PPO.
 - Mix-PPG (especially with increased UTD) was shown to provide a significant "early-stage boost" in accuracy. However, without policy reincarnation, this early gain could lead to slower asymptotic improvement or even degradation due to accumulated off-policy bias.
 - Policy reincarnation played a critical role in ensuring stable asymptotic improvement by seamlessly transitioning from the efficient Mix-PPG stage to a strong on-policy stage.

3. **Insightful Analysis of Off-Policy Learning Effects on Reasoning Behaviors:**
 - **Impact of Historical Sample Reuse:** Increasing the proportion of off-policy data in Mix-PPG led to larger policy distribution shifts (higher importance ratios) and faster initial accuracy gains. However, too much off-policyness resulted in inferior final performance or degradation, underscoring a trade-off between early efficiency and asymptotic stability.
 - **Implicit Preference for Shorter Responses (Whipping Effect):** Off-policy learning was observed to induce an implicit preference for shorter responses. This "Whipping Effect" occurs because when advantage estimates are negative (which is often the case when policy loss is positive), the model learns to minimize loss by steering towards a lower importance sampling ratio. Longer responses tend to accumulate larger distribution shifts over time, leading the model to prefer concise responses to reduce the average loss over long trajectories.
 - **Collapse Mode of Self-Reflection:** As off-policyness increased, there was a rapid decrease in response length and a significant suppression of self-reflection behaviors (e.g., use of 'verify', 're-examine' tokens). This shortening of responses and reduced self-reflection was directly correlated with a degradation in reasoning accuracy, as the model prematurely generated answers without adequate intermediate deliberation. ReMix, with its policy reincarnation, showed a merged dynamic: it initially reduced response length and self-reflection for early efficiency, then expanded responses and increased reflection for careful exploration and further improvement in later stages.
 - **Performance under Constrained Maximum Response Length:** ReMix demonstrated superior resilience to constraints on maximum response length (e.g., 4K tokens vs. 8K default). While all models suffered a performance drop under length constraints, ReMix-PPO exhibited the smallest decrease and performed best, validating its learned preference for concise reasoning.
 - **Impact of Guide Tokens in Prompt Format:** ReMix was more robust to variations in prompt templates. When explicit guide tokens (like `<think>`) were removed during evaluation, the base model and standard ReMix-PPO suffered significant drops in format correctness and accuracy. However, a variant of ReMix-PPO trained *without* these guide tokens showed high format correctness and strong performance under both standard and modified templates, suggesting it internalized reasoning behavior in a more general, less template-dependent manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Squeeze the Soaked Sponge" holds significant implications and potential impact for the development and deployment of advanced Large Language Models:

1. **Democratization and Scalability of LLM Finetuning:** The most immediate and profound impact is the dramatic reduction in computational cost for finetuning LLMs for complex reasoning tasks. The 30x to 450x reduction in rollout data volume means that SOTA-level reasoning capabilities can be achieved with significantly less GPU time and energy. This makes advanced RFT more accessible to researchers and organizations with limited computational resources, fostering broader participation in LLM development and accelerating research progress.
2. **Economic Viability of LLM Deployment:** For practical applications, the efficiency gains translate directly into cost savings. This can enable more frequent model updates, faster experimentation cycles, and potentially lead to the deployment of more specialized and performant LLMs in real-world scenarios where computational budgets are a concern.
3. **Advancement in Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The paper successfully launches a "renaissance of off-policy RL" for RFT. It demonstrates that with careful design (Mix-PPG, KL-Convex, Policy Reincarnation), the notorious instability and asymptotic performance issues of off-policy methods can be effectively mitigated, paving the way for more widespread adoption of these sample-efficient techniques in LLM training.
4. **Deeper Understanding of LLM Reasoning Dynamics:** Beyond empirical results, the detailed analysis of how off-policy learning affects response length, self-reflection, and overall reasoning behavior provides crucial mechanistic insights. The "Whipping Effect" and the observed collapse of self-reflection highlight a critical trade-off that future RFT methods must consider. This understanding is vital for designing more effective and robust finetuning strategies.
5. **Robustness and Adaptability of Finetuned Models:** The findings regarding resilience to response length constraints and prompt variations suggest that ReMix-trained models may be more robust and adaptable in deployment, requiring less strict adherence to training-time configurations. This enhances their practical utility in diverse inference environments.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, primarily related to computational resources and the scope of their investigation:
- The method was not tested on models larger than 7B, leaving its scalability to even larger LLMs unverified.
- The utilization of off-policy data employs fixed proportions, suggesting potential for adaptive control strategies to further optimize performance and stability.
- The work focuses on integrating off-policy techniques into PPO/GRPO and does not explore combinations with other advanced RFT methods (e.g., curriculum learning, advanced reward modeling, or specific architectural modifications).

These limitations naturally open up promising avenues for future research, including scaling ReMix to larger models, developing adaptive off-policy data utilization strategies, and exploring orthogonal integrations with other state-of-the-art RFT techniques to realize even more efficient and powerful LLMs.
