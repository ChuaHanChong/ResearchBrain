# gradSim: Differentiable simulation for system identification and visuomotor control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mila and NVIDIA developed ∇Sim, an end-to-end differentiable framework that integrates multiphysics simulation and differentiable rendering into a single computational graph. This framework accurately infers physical properties of objects and enables visuomotor control policies directly from video data, eliminating the need for 3D state supervision.

## Problem

- Accurately inferring the physical properties and dynamics of objects from raw visual observations (video sequences) without requiring precise 3D labels or state-space supervision.
- The limitation of 'white box' physics-based methods which traditionally depend on expensive and often infeasible 3D ground truth for training, especially for complex deformable objects.
- Challenges in learning visuomotor control policies that rely solely on visual input, typically needing complex 3D state perception modules or extensive trial-and-error.

## Method

- Unifies a differentiable multiphysics simulation engine with a differentiable rendering engine into a single, cohesive computational graph, allowing gradients to flow from pixels to physical parameters.
- Employs the discrete adjoint method for efficient gradient computation through complex physical dynamics and differentiable rasterizers (like SoftRas and DIB-R) for gradients through 3D-to-2D image formation.
- Enables end-to-end learning by computing a pixel-wise loss between a target video and a synthetic video generated by the model, then backpropagating this loss to adjust underlying physical attributes or control parameters.

## Results

- Achieved high accuracy in estimating physical attributes for rigid bodies (e.g., 9.01e-5 mean absolute relative error for mass) and deformable objects directly from video, outperforming or matching 3D-supervised baselines in various scenarios.
- Successfully trained neural network policies for visuomotor control of deformable objects towards a target visual pose using only pixel-wise MSE loss, demonstrating effectiveness for complex control tasks like cloth manipulation.
- Demonstrated impressive computational efficiency, achieving faster-than-real-time simulation rates (e.g., 1500 steps/second for 10000 tetrahedra) for both forward and backward passes of complex deformable geometries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The unified differentiable simulation and rendering pipeline produces remarkably smooth and well-behaved loss landscapes, which is highly conducive to gradient-based optimization for inverse problems.
> - Accurate physical modeling (e.g., correct object type) is more critical for robust parameter estimation than imperfections in the rendering model.
> - Including richer visual cues like shading and texture in the differentiable renderer significantly improves the convergence speed of parameter estimation by providing better gradients.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a long-standing and fundamentally challenging problem in computer vision and robotics: accurately inferring the physical properties and dynamics of objects from raw visual observations (video sequences). This is an "inverse problem" where the goal is to deduce underlying causes from observed effects, complicated by the inherent loss of information when a 3D physical world is projected onto 2D images.

Historically, solutions to predicting object dynamics from images have fallen into three categories:

1. **Black Box Methods:** These approaches, often deep learning models, learn an embedding of system states or observations without explicit physical priors. While flexible and requiring minimal assumptions, they typically lack interpretability and can be data-inefficient (e.g., Watters et al., 2017; Janner et al., 2019).
2. **Grey Box Methods:** These leverage partial knowledge of system dynamics to improve performance, striking a balance between learned models and physical priors (e.g., Mehta et al., 2020).
3. **White Box Methods:** These impose strong prior knowledge using explicit physics models, leading to more interpretable results and fewer learnable parameters (e.g., Degrave et al., 2016; Hu et al., 2020). However, their major limitation is the dependency on precise 3D labels or state-space supervision, which is labor-intensive to acquire and often infeasible for complex deformable objects or cloth.

∇Sim positions itself as a significant advancement within the **white box** paradigm, directly tackling its primary weakness: the need for 3D supervision. The broader research landscape has seen substantial progress in:

- **Differentiable Physics Simulators:** Efforts like DiffTaichi (Hu et al., 2020) and frameworks for contact/friction models (de Avila Belbute-Peres et al., 2018; Toussaint et al., 2018) have made it possible to compute gradients through physical dynamics. However, these typically provide gradients in the *state space* (e.g., 3D positions, velocities) and require 3D supervision.
- **Differentiable Rendering:** Techniques such as SoftRas (Liu et al., 2019) and DIB-R (Chen et al., 2019) have enabled backpropagation from 2D pixel losses to 3D scene parameters (geometry, camera, lighting). This is crucial for "inverse graphics" problems.
- **Physics-based Deep Learning:** Researchers have explored injecting physical structure (e.g., Lagrangian/Hamiltonian mechanics) into neural networks or using ground truth 3D supervision to learn dynamics.

The critical gap that ∇Sim addresses is the *lack of a unified, end-to-end differentiable framework* that spans the entire process from underlying physical attributes, through dynamic evolution, to 3D-to-2D image formation. While differentiable physics provides gradients through dynamics and differentiable rendering through image formation, these have largely been treated as separate components. ∇Sim bridges this by creating a single computational graph, allowing gradients to flow directly from image pixels in a video all the way back to the physical parameters that generated the motion. This places ∇Sim at the forefront of combining inverse graphics with inverse physics, moving towards truly holistic visual-physical reasoning without the prohibitive cost of 3D supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives of the ∇Sim project are twofold, addressing fundamental limitations in current approaches to understanding the physical world from visual data:

1. **To enable accurate system identification of an object's physical properties directly from video sequences, without requiring precise 3D labels or state-space supervision.**
 - **Motivation:** The task of inferring physical attributes (mass, friction, elasticity, material parameters) from observed motion is inherently ill-posed and challenging. Current "white box" methods, while offering interpretability and efficiency, are hamstrung by the immense difficulty and cost of gathering accurate 3D ground truth (e.g., 3D poses, trajectories) for training. This problem is particularly acute for complex systems like deformable solids or cloth, where 3D labeling is often infeasible. ∇Sim aims to eliminate this bottleneck by enabling learning directly from readily available 2D video data.

2. **To facilitate learning in challenging visuomotor control tasks, again without relying on state-based (3D) supervision, while achieving competitive or superior performance compared to 3D-supervised techniques.**
 - **Motivation:** For embodied AI agents, the ability to control objects and interact with the physical world based on visual input is paramount. Traditional control methods often require a precise understanding of the system's 3D state. If physical properties can be accurately inferred from pixels, then visuomotor control policies can be learned and optimized using visual feedback alone. This removes the need for complex perception modules to extract perfect 3D states, simplifying the control pipeline and making it more robust to real-world visual noise and ambiguity.

In essence, ∇Sim is motivated by the desire to build a physically grounded understanding of scenes from raw visual data, enabling both passive inference (what are the object properties?) and active control (how should I interact with this object?) in a data-efficient and interpretable manner, without the Achilles' heel of expensive 3D supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

∇Sim's core methodology lies in its novel unification of differentiable multiphysics simulation and differentiable rendering into a single, cohesive computational graph. This end-to-end differentiability allows gradients to be backpropagated from a pixel-level loss function in a video sequence all the way through the rendering and physics models to the underlying physical parameters.

The framework comprises two main, tightly integrated components:

1. **Differentiable Physics Engine:**
 - **Foundation:** The physics engine models the dynamic evolution of physical systems using Lagrangian mechanics, described by generalized coordinates (q), velocities (u), and design/model parameters (θ). The system's dynamics are governed by second-order ordinary differential equations (ODEs), M(s, θ)˙s = f(s, θ), where M is the mass matrix and f represents forces.
 - **Gradient Computation:** Instead of relying on black-box numerical integrators, ∇Sim treats the physics engine as a differentiable operation. Gradients are computed using the **discrete adjoint method**, which provides an implicit relationship between the state at the start (s⁻) and end (s⁺) of a timestep, and the model parameters (θ). This allows for efficient gradient calculation through time-integration schemes.
 - **Implementation:** The framework is agnostic to the specific physics engine, but the authors detail an efficient approach based on **source-code transformation** of parallel kernels, similar to DiffTaichi. This involves parsing Python syntax to generate C++/CUDA code for both forward and reverse (adjoint) modes, which are then wrapped as custom PyTorch autograd operations. This integration seamlessly embeds the physics into the larger PyTorch computational graph.
 - **Physical Models:** ∇Sim supports a variety of complex physical models:
 - **Deformable Solids:** Utilizes Finite Element Method (FEM) with constant strain tetrahedral elements and a stable Neo-Hookean constitutive model. It allows for optimization of Lamé elasticity parameters (μ, λ) and per-element actuation values (α).
 - **Deformable Thin-Shells (Cloth):** Employs constant strain triangular elements, applying the Neo-Hookean model for in-plane deformation, a bending energy term, and a lift/drag model to simulate air interaction.
 - **Rigid Bodies:** Models dynamics using Newton-Euler equations, allowing for the estimation of mass (m) and inertia matrix (I).
 - **Contact:** Implements a compliant contact model with penalty-based forces for non-interpenetration and a relaxed Coulomb friction model, parameterized by elastic stiffness (k_e), damping (k_d), frictional stiffness (k_f), and friction coefficient (μ).
 - Simpler systems like pendula, mass-springs, and incompressible fluids (smoke) are also supported.

2. **Differentiable Rendering Engine:**
 - **Function:** This component takes the physical states (geometry, material properties) from the physics engine, along with lighting and camera parameters, and generates 2D image outputs.
 - **Overcoming Non-Differentiability:** Traditional rasterization, with its discrete visibility and occlusion events, is non-differentiable. ∇Sim overcomes this by employing **differentiable rasterizers** such as SoftRas and DIB-R. These methods replace discontinuous triangle mesh edges with smooth sigmoids, effectively blurring boundaries and allowing for gradient computation. DIB-R specifically distinguishes foreground/background pixels for optimized rendering.
 - **Flexibility:** ∇Sim allows for independent and adjustable rates for physics simulation and rendering, enabling computational trade-offs (e.g., rendering fewer frames than physics updates).

**End-to-End Learning:** The combined system works by taking a target video sequence, initializing scene object properties randomly, running the differentiable physics simulation to generate a sequence of 3D states, passing these states to the differentiable renderer to produce a synthetic video, and then computing a pixel-wise loss (e.g., Mean Squared Error - MSE) between the synthetic and target videos. This loss signal is then backpropagated through the entire unified computation graph, adjusting the initial physical attributes or control parameters using gradient descent to minimize the difference. This novel architecture allows learning directly from pixels, circumventing the need for explicit 3D supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

∇Sim's evaluation demonstrates its effectiveness across various tasks and physical systems, highlighting its ability to learn complex physical parameters and control policies directly from video.

1. **Accurate Physical Parameter Estimation from Video:**
 - **Rigid Bodies:** ∇Sim achieved remarkable accuracy in estimating physical attributes of rigid objects. For mass estimation, it reported a mean absolute relative error of 9.01e-5, outperforming a strong ConvLSTM baseline by nearly two orders of magnitude and being highly competitive even with a "DiffPhysics (3D sup.)" baseline that required precise 3D ground truth. Similarly, it accurately estimated contact parameters like friction and elasticity solely from video observations.
 - **Deformable Bodies (FEM and Thin-Shells):** The framework successfully recovered per-particle masses and Lamé elasticity parameters for deformable solids (e.g., a hanging beam) and per-particle velocities for thin-shell cloth models. Notably, for cloth, where 3D supervision based on center-of-mass can be ambiguous, ∇Sim's image-based approach showed superior performance compared to its 3D-supervised counterpart.

2. **Smoothness of Loss Landscapes:**
 - A crucial finding was that despite the complexity and non-linearity of the combined simulation and rendering pipeline, ∇Sim produced remarkably smooth and well-behaved loss landscapes. This smoothness, demonstrated for rigid body mass and deformable body elasticity estimation, is highly conducive to gradient-based optimization and explains ∇Sim's superior performance compared to methods using black-box gradient estimation (like PyBullet + REINFORCE), which often encounter multiple local minima and convergence issues.

3. **Effective Visuomotor Control without 3D Supervision:**
 - ∇Sim demonstrated its utility for vision-based control tasks. It successfully trained neural network control policies to actuate deformable objects (e.g., a 2D walker, a 3D FEM gear) towards a target visual pose specified by a single image, using only pixelwise MSE loss. While taking more iterations than a heavily 3D-supervised baseline, it ultimately achieved the target states.
 - For cloth control, ∇Sim's image-based supervision again proved more effective than 3D state-based MPC (Model Predictive Control), as the latter struggled with the underdetermined nature of defining cloth configuration solely by its center of mass. This highlights the advantage of end-to-end visual feedback for complex deformable objects.

4. **Impact of Imperfect Dynamics and Rendering Models:**
 - The study investigated the robustness of ∇Sim to modeling errors. It found that the framework's performance was *more sensitive to imperfect dynamics models* (e.g., unmodeled friction or elasticity, incorrectly modeling a rigid object as deformable or vice-versa) than to imperfections in the rendering model. Incorrect object type modeling had the most severe impact. This suggests that accurate physical modeling is paramount for robust parameter estimation.
 - Using a photorealistic renderer (non-differentiable) for ground truth images, while using ∇Sim's differentiable rasterizer for optimization, still yielded reasonable parameter estimates, indicating some resilience to discrepancies between ground truth and rendered appearance.

5. **Impact of Shading and Texture Cues:**
 - Including richer visual cues like shading and texture in the differentiable renderer significantly improved the convergence speed of parameter estimation. Shading provides depth and orientation information, while textures offer non-uniform appearance cues, both enhancing the correspondence between rendered and ground-truth images and thus providing better gradients.

6. **Efficiency:**
 - ∇Sim demonstrated impressive computational efficiency, achieving faster-than-real-time simulation rates (e.g., 1500 steps/second for 10000 tetrahedra) for both forward and backward passes, even with complex deformable object geometries. This efficiency is crucial for practical applications in learning and control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

∇Sim represents a significant stride forward in the fields of computer vision, robotics, and machine learning, with profound implications for how AI agents can perceive and interact with the physical world.

1. **Breaking the 3D Supervision Barrier:** The most impactful contribution of ∇Sim is its ability to perform system identification and visuomotor control *directly from video pixels, without requiring precise 3D supervision*. This eliminates a major bottleneck that has traditionally limited the application of physically-grounded AI to real-world scenarios. Gathering accurate 3D labels for complex, deformable objects is extremely challenging and expensive, often rendering such approaches impractical. ∇Sim opens up possibilities for learning physics from vast amounts of unlabeled video data.

2. **Unified and Principled Framework:** By integrating differentiable physics and differentiable rendering into a single, cohesive computational graph, ∇Sim provides a principled and powerful framework for solving a wide array of inverse problems. This unified view simplifies the development of models that bridge the gap between low-level visual input and high-level physical understanding, offering a more holistic approach to embodied AI.

3. **Enhanced Interpretability and Debuggability:** As a "white box" method, ∇Sim leverages explicit physical models, making the learned parameters and behaviors inherently more interpretable than those derived from opaque, end-to-end neural networks. This interpretability is vital for building trust in AI systems, especially in safety-critical applications like autonomous vehicles or robotic manipulation. Discrepancies can be attributed to specific physical parameters or model assumptions, facilitating easier debugging and refinement.

4. **Accelerated Learning and Data Efficiency:** The availability of precise, analytical gradients from pixels to physical parameters can dramatically accelerate the training of reinforcement learning agents. By providing direct feedback on how physical attributes or control actions influence visual outcomes, ∇Sim can reduce the need for extensive trial-and-error exploration, leading to more data-efficient and energy-efficient learning.

5. **Bridging the Reality Gap:** ∇Sim offers a promising pathway for understanding and modeling the "unmodeled dynamics" present in real-world scenarios. By iteratively estimating physical parameters from real videos, researchers can refine simulation models to better match reality, thus bridging the persistent "reality gap" between simulation and the physical world. This is critical for sim-to-real transfer in robotics.

6. **Enabling Complex Interactions and Control:** The demonstrated success in visuomotor control for deformable objects, particularly outperforming 3D-supervised methods for cloth, signifies a new capability for robotic manipulation of highly complex, underactuated, and shape-changing objects. This could lead to advancements in tasks like robotic dressing, sorting laundry, or handling delicate biological tissues.

7. **Open-Source Contribution:** The availability of a PyTorch framework and the project website (gradsim.github.io) indicate an open-source spirit, encouraging further research and development within the community. This accelerates progress by providing a robust foundation for others to build upon.

In conclusion, ∇Sim is a landmark contribution that moves us closer to AI systems that possess an intuitive understanding of physics, learn directly from visual experience, and can interact intelligently with a dynamic, deformable world. While future work on contact-rich motion, articulated bodies, and higher-fidelity rendering remains, ∇Sim lays a crucial foundation for the next generation of embodied AI.
