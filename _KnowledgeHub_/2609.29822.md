# Self-Supervised Anchoring of Fingertip Sensing to Proprioception and Proactive Actions for Robot Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> PROPRA is a contrastive pretraining method for robot imitation learning with fingertip tactile and proximity sensors. Each sensor history is aligned independently to a sensorimotor anchor built from current proprioception plus a future action chunk, never to the other sensor. On four real UR5e manipulation tasks it lifts average success to 75.0% over vision-only (30.4%) and image-anchored pretraining (71.7%), while retaining pre-contact information.

## Problem

- External cameras miss fingertip-local cues like object proximity, contact onset and grasp state because of occlusion and limited temporal resolution near the fingers.
- Naively concatenating tactile and proximity histories to a policy can underperform vision-only baselines, since sparse phase-dependent signals are hard to exploit from limited demonstrations.
- Contrastive schemes anchored on images or on same-time sensor pairs give weak supervision: an anchor uninformative during a sensor's active phase teaches no temporal distinctions.

## Method

- Multimodal fingertip module retrofits ==force-sensitive resistors== (3 channels) and a reflective LED/phototransistor ==proximity circuit== (6 channels) onto a Robotiq 2F-140 gripper, sampled at 100 Hz.
- **PROPRA**'s ==sensorimotor anchor== concatenates current end-effector pose and gripper opening with a 10-step future action chunk (88-D), aligning each sensor's 50-step history to it via ==symmetric InfoNCE==.
- Per-modality four-layer pre-norm ==Transformer== encoders (no weight sharing) plus an MLP anchor encoder; only sensor encoders transfer into a ==Transformer diffusion policy== and fine-tune end-to-end, with batches upweighting low-proximity and tactile-transition steps.

## Results

- PROPRA reaches **75.0%** average success over four real tasks, versus **71.7%** image anchor, **72.1%** image-plus-direct-pair, **40.4%** no pretraining and **30.4%** vision-only.
- Time-to-contact decoding from frozen proximity embeddings is best on three of four tasks, with PickSponge R2 **0.440** against **0.377** for the image anchor and **0.327** raw.
- Without refitting on policy-execution recordings PROPRA stays positive on every task and seed, while raw proximity collapses to R2 **-8.0** and **-25.5**; tactile-to-proximity retrieval hits **6-12x** chance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchor choice matters more than the loss: a reference available throughout the episode that covaries with progress beats a modality only sporadically informative.
> - Cross-modal correspondence can emerge indirectly through a shared anchor; adding explicit same-time pairing between the two sensors changed average success by only 0.4 points.
> - Gains concentrate where sensor responses are temporally sparse; with rich contact throughout a trajectory such as lid rotation, image anchoring wins instead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

The robot must know what is happening at its fingertips while it approaches and grasps an object. Cameras can miss local interaction cues, and fingertip sensors are informative at different times: proximity before contact, touch after it. Take PickSponge, in which the robot grasps an object placed on a table and lifts it. Simply appending both sensor histories to the policy raises average success in the reported setup. It still makes performance worse than vision alone on some tasks.

The authors' approach, PROPRA, pretrains each sensor against the robot's own state and forthcoming actions. This reference is available throughout a manipulation. On four real-robot tasks, it has the highest reported average success among the compared pretraining methods, though not on every task.

PickSponge: the robot grasps an object on a table and lifts it.

### 2. A reference that stays available

The gripper carries a pressure-sensitive tactile sensor and a reflective optical proximity sensor. During approach, the proximity signal varies while the tactile signal remains near baseline. After contact, the tactile signal rises. In more than half of the pretraining samples, at least one fingertip sensor remains near its stationary value. Because of this, same-time alignment between the sensors can provide weak supervision when one sensor is nearly at baseline.

PROPRA avoids pairing the two sensor streams directly. At each time step, each sensor encoder sees its own recent history. A shared anchor encoder sees the robot's current end-effector pose and gripper opening, along with a chunk of forthcoming commanded actions. A contrastive objective aligns each sensor history with that anchor independently. In the implementation, histories span 0.5 seconds and the anchor uses ten future control actions. Only the pretrained sensor encoders transfer to the diffusion policy. At deployment the anchor is discarded, but the policy still uses images, robot state and fingertip histories.

(a) Sensor histories aligned with image embeddings at "now." (b) Separate tactile- and proximity-history encoders aligned with one anchor built from current proprioception and the future action chunk, with no direct sensor-to-sensor alignment objective.

### 3. What the robot tests show

The tests used a UR5e gripper on four real-robot tasks, with 50 demonstrations per task. The tasks were PickCup, PickSponge, MovePen and OpenLid, and each condition was run with three seeds and 20 trials per seed. On the unweighted four-task mean success rate, PROPRA reaches 75.0%. Image anchoring alone reaches 71.7%, and image anchoring plus a direct tactile–proximity pair reaches 72.1%. The authors describe the direct pair as bringing no clear benefit.

This is an average advantage, not a uniform win. The authors say task-level differences on PickCup and PickSponge are within seed variation. The image anchor scores 76.7% on OpenLid versus 73.3% for PROPRA. The comparison also does not cleanly isolate anchor choice. PROPRA uses non-uniform sensor-response sampling and hard negatives, while the image-anchor conditions use uniform sampling.

The second comparison is a representation probe. A frozen proximity representation was used to decode time-to-contact over the 2 seconds before contact. On PickSponge, PROPRA scores $R^{2}$ 0.440 ± 0.015 versus 0.377 ± 0.034 for image anchoring. Here $R^{2}$ is 1 for perfect prediction and 0 for prediction of the mean. PROPRA has the highest $R^{2}$ on three of four tasks, and image anchoring plus the direct pair is highest on MovePen. This is evidence about a representation probe. It does not show that better time-to-contact encoding causes the policy-success difference.

### 4. What the anchor changes

PROPRA relates each fingertip sensor's history to the robot's ongoing state and intended motion. It does not ask a temporarily quiet sensor to supervise the other. On these phase-sparse signals, it gives the highest average success among the compared pretraining methods. The scope is grasp-oriented imitation with two retrofitted sensors, and broader sensor configurations remain future work.
