# Embodied-R1: Reinforced Embodied Reasoning for General Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tianjin University developed Embodied-R1, a Vision-Language Model that employs reinforced fine-tuning to master four core "pointing" abilities, serving as an embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation. This approach enables robust zero-shot robotic manipulation, achieving an 87.5% success rate on real-world tasks and outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Bridging the "seeing-to-doing gap" in embodied AI, where high-level visual and linguistic understanding struggles to translate into effective low-level robotic actions in novel environments.
- Addressing performance degradation in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models due to data scarcity for grounding language and vision with physical actions, and embodiment heterogeneity complicating knowledge transfer across robot platforms.
- Overcoming the limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for reasoning, which uses rigid templates and generates incomplete guidance signals, leading to poor generalization to novel tasks or settings.

## Method

- Introduced "pointing" as a unified, embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation to abstract away robot-specific details and bridge high-level vision-language comprehension with low-level action primitives.
- Systematized embodied pointing into four distinct capabilities: ==Referring Expression Grounding (REG)==, Region Referring Grounding (RRG), Object Functional Grounding (OFG), and Visual Trace Generation (VTG).
- Developed **Embodied-R1**, a 3B-parameter Vision-Language Model, trained with a two-stage Reinforced Fine-tuning (RFT) curriculum and a specialized multi-task reward design using the Embodied-Points-200K dataset.

## Results

- Embodied-R1 achieved an **87.5%** zero-shot success rate on 8 real-world xArm manipulation tasks, representing a **62%** improvement over baselines like RoboPoint and FSD, and a **56.2%** success rate in SimplerEnv simulation, outperforming state-of-the-art end-to-end VLAs.
- The model demonstrated state-of-the-art performance among open-source models on five diverse spatial reasoning benchmarks (average rank **2.1**) and exhibited superior accuracy across all four defined embodied pointing capabilities.
- Embodied-R1 exhibited high robustness to zero-shot visual disturbances (e.g., background, lighting, height changes), maintaining success rates between **83%** and **100%**, supporting the efficacy of pointing as a universal representation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Pointing" effectively serves as an embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation, unifying semantic and spatial information to guide diverse robotic manipulation intents without predicting embodiment-specific actions.
> - Reinforced Fine-tuning (RFT) with a multi-task reward system allows for flexible, free-form reasoning and addresses the inherent multi-solution dilemma in pointing tasks more effectively than Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT), leading to stronger out-of-distribution generalization.
> - A comprehensively constructed dataset of "question-verification" pairs for various pointing tasks, combined with general reasoning data, is crucial for developing robust, generalized embodied pointing abilities in VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in embodied AI and robotic manipulation known as the "seeing-to-doing gap." This gap refers to the difficulty of translating high-level visual and linguistic understanding into effective low-level robotic actions, particularly in novel environments. Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while proficient in perception and imitation learning, often exhibit performance degradation when faced with unfamiliar settings. This issue is attributed to two primary factors: data scarcity, where insufficient embodied data hinders the grounding of language and vision with physical actions, and embodiment heterogeneity, where diverse robot morphologies complicate knowledge transfer across platforms.

Prior approaches have explored various methods to bridge this gap. End-to-end VLA models aim for direct policy learning but struggle with generalization and data requirements. Modular methods, which decompose tasks into perception, reasoning, and action, can suffer from cascading errors. Auxiliary visual signals like keypoints, affordance maps, bounding boxes, and visual trajectories have been used to abstract away embodiment-specific details and enhance cross-robot generalization. However, these often generate a single type of signal, leading to incomplete or simplistic guidance for complex decision-making. Specifically, methods relying on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for reasoning, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches, are noted to be constrained by rigid templates, limiting their generalization to novel tasks.

This research proposes "pointing" as a unified, embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation to address these limitations. It positions itself by integrating a comprehensive pointing mechanism with Reinforced Fine-tuning (RFT) within a VLM framework. This aims to overcome the "multi-solution dilemma" in pointing tasks (where multiple correct actions might exist for a given instruction) and enable flexible, free-form reasoning, which is argued to lead to stronger out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization compared to SFT-centric approaches. The work contributes to the advancement of general-purpose embodied AI by providing a method that aims to anchor reasoning within a scene and guide manipulation directly, without predicting embodiment-specific actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the "seeing-to-doing gap" in robotic manipulation, thereby enhancing the generalization capabilities of embodied AI systems in novel and complex environments. This objective is motivated by the persistent challenges of data scarcity and embodiment heterogeneity that hinder current VLM and VLA models.

Specifically, the key objectives include:
- **Developing a Unified, Embodiment-Agnostic Representation:** To overcome data scarcity and embodiment heterogeneity, the research aims to pioneer "pointing" as a singular intermediate representation that unifies semantic and spatial information into a compact, robot-independent format. This representation is intended to bridge high-level vision-language comprehension with low-level action primitives.
- **Defining Core Embodied Pointing Abilities:** To enable comprehensive manipulation, the work seeks to systematize embodied pointing into four distinct, yet interconnected, capabilities:
 - **Referring Expression Grounding (REG):** Localizing an object based on a linguistic description by generating a point within its segmentation mask. This addresses "What is this object?"
 - **Region Referring Grounding (RRG):** Identifying a spatial region from relational language (e.g., "the space between objects") by generating a point in a suitable free-space location. This addresses "Where should it be placed?"
 - **Object Functional Grounding (OFG):** Identifying functionally critical parts of an object (e.g., a handle for grasping) by marking a point on that area. This addresses "How to use/grasp it?"
 - **Visual Trace Generation (VTG):** Producing an ordered sequence of points to form a complete, object-centric manipulation trajectory. This implicitly conveys the execution process and addresses "How to complete the task?"
- **Constructing a Large-Scale, High-Quality Dataset:** To support the training of these embodied pointing capabilities, a comprehensive dataset, Embodied-Points-200K, is aimed to be constructed from diverse embodied and general visual reasoning sources, structured as "question-verification" pairs suitable for RFT.
- **Introducing a Reinforced Fine-tuning (RFT) Paradigm:** To enable flexible, free-form reasoning and address the inherent multi-solution dilemma in embodied pointing, the research aims to train a VLM using a two-stage RFT curriculum with a specialized multi-task reward design. This is motivated by the limitations of SFT in handling ambiguity and generalizing to novel tasks.
- **Achieving Robust Zero-Shot Generalization:** The ultimate motivation is to enable robots to perform manipulation tasks in unseen environments and with novel objects without task-specific fine-tuning, demonstrating high robustness against visual disturbances.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to demonstrate that a pointing-centric representation combined with an RFT training paradigm can provide an effective and generalizable pathway to closing the perception-action gap in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on developing Embodied-R1, a 3B-parameter Vision-Language Model (VLM), designed to perform general robotic manipulation through an intermediate "pointing" representation, trained via a Reinforced Fine-tuning (RFT) paradigm.

#### 3.1. The Architecture and Capabilities of Embodied-R1

Embodied-R1 is built upon the Qwen2.5-VL architecture, adapted to master four fundamental embodied pointing abilities. These abilities all generate image coordinates (p, q) but differ in their semantic purpose and output structure:
- **Referring Expression Grounding (REG):** Localizes an object from a linguistic description by predicting a point within its segmentation mask.
- **Region Referring Grounding (RRG):** Identifies a spatial region based on relational language (e.g., "between objects") by predicting a point in an appropriate free-space location.
- **Object Functional Grounding (OFG):** Identifies functionally critical parts of an object (e.g., a handle) by marking a point on that area.
- **Visual Trace Generation (VTG):** Produces an ordered sequence of points to represent an object-centric manipulation trajectory, providing a spatial plan.

#### 3.2. Enhancing Embodied Reasoning Abilities of VLM

Training involves three types of data to develop general embodied pointing capabilities:
- **General and Spatial Reasoning Data:**
 - **Embodied-Spatial-84K:** An embodied spatial awareness dataset aggregated from SAT and WhatsUp, converted into a unified multiple-choice format for objective evaluation.
 - **ViRL-subset-18K:** A diverse general-knowledge subset from the ViRL dataset, curated to prevent catastrophic forgetting and preserve general reasoning skills during specialized training.
- **Embodied Pointing Data (Embodied-Points-200K):** A dataset of approximately 200,000 high-quality instances, structured as "question-verification" pairs for RFT, addressing the multi-solution dilemma.
 - **REG Data:** Created by integrating web images (RefCOCO) and embodied data (RoboRef It, RoboPoint), requiring a single point output within the object's segmentation mask.
 - **RRG Data:** Generated via an automated pipeline processing a large corpus of open-source embodied datasets (approx. 1 million), extracting manipulated object final positions, referring regions relative to reference objects, and rendering this information onto initial images. Filtering ensures diversity in spatial relationships.
 - **OFG Data:** Constructed using the HandAL dataset, converting manipulable part annotations into bounding boxes. GPT-4o generated diverse function-related questions to promote generalization.
 - **VTG Data:** An object-centric visual trace dataset extracted via a three-step pipeline: (1) Key Object Proposal (using GPT-4o), (2) KeyPoint Identification (using self-supervised keypoint extractor and Grounded-SAM), and (3) Point Tracking and Projection (using Cotracker3 to compute dense temporal traces, then downsampling to 8 equidistant points on the initial image). Rigorous rule-based filtering and manual validation addressed noise from pre-trained models.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

Embodied-R1 is trained using a two-stage process optimized by the GRPO algorithm:
1. **Stage 1:** Focuses on enhancing spatial reasoning using Embodied-Spatial-84K and ViRL-subset-18K.
2. **Stage 2:** Trains embodied pointing capabilities using point-centric, multi-task mixed data from Embodied-Points-200K.

The GRPO algorithm generates multiple candidate responses, determines relative advantages by normalizing rewards, and optimizes the model using a clipped surrogate loss to maximize expected returns while maintaining stability.

#### 3.4. Multi-task Reward Design

A modular reward system is implemented to stabilize multi-task training, assigning a normalized weighted sum of components to each task. The reward function library includes:
- **Format Rewards (`r_format`):** Binary reward for adhering to structured output format (e.g., `<think>`, `<point>`).
- **Accuracy Rewards (`r_acc`):** Binary reward for general QA tasks, comparing predicted and ground-truth answers.
- **Point in Mask Reward (`r_mask`):** Binary reward for pointing tasks, checking if the predicted point falls within the ground-truth mask.
- **Point Distance Reward (`r_dis`):** Dense auxiliary reward based on Euclidean distance between predicted point and ground-truth mask center, scaled between 0 and 1 using thresholds.
- **Visual Trace Reward (`r_trace`):** Reward for VTG, derived from Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) between predicted and ground-truth trajectories, after interpolation to uniform point count, scaled between 0 and 1.
Total rewards (`R`) for each task are weighted sums of these components, normalized to be within to ensure balanced training across tasks.

#### 3.5. Action Executor of Embodied-R1

Embodied-R1's pointing mechanism can be integrated with various action executors for zero-shot robotic control through two primary pipelines:
- **Affordance Points Branch (-P):** Utilizes RRG and OFG capabilities to predict grasping and placement key points. These points serve as targets for a CuRobo motion planner, generating collision-free robot end-effector trajectories.
- **Visual Trace Branch (-V):** Employs object-centric visual traces from VTG. The 2D trace is mapped to 3D Cartesian coordinates using a pinhole camera model and initial depth. These discrete 3D points are then interpolated into a continuous SE(3) motion trajectory for robot execution, similar to the FSD methodology. This architecture allows for dynamic selection of pointing abilities and integration with motion planners.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Embodied-R1 encompassed "Seeing" (spatial reasoning and pointing capabilities) and "Doing" (robotic manipulation tasks) dimensions, utilizing 11 QA benchmarks, 4 simulated tasks (SIMPLEREnv), and 8 real-world XArm tasks. The model was initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and compared against leading open-source and closed-source models, as well as an SFT-trained variant (Embodied-SFT).

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Spatial Reasoning Capabilities

- Embodied-R1 demonstrated state-of-the-art performance among open-source models on five diverse benchmarks (CVBench, BLINK, CRPE, SAT, EmbSpatial-Bench) with an average rank of 2.1.
- It outperformed variants trained without common-sense data (Embodied-R1 w/o CS, Rank 3.4) and those trained with only SFT (Embodied-SFT, Rank 3.7).
- Embodied-R1 surpassed larger, specialized embodied models like RoboBrain-7B and FSD-13B, indicating the effectiveness of the RFT training strategy and diverse data.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Pointing Capabilities

- General VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL) performed poorly on specialized pointing tasks, emphasizing the need for targeted training.
- Embodied-R1 exhibited superior performance across all four defined pointing capabilities:
 - **REG (RoboRef It):** Achieved 85.58% accuracy.
 - **RRG (Where2Place, VABench-P):** Achieved 69.50% and 66.00% accuracy, respectively.
 - **OFG (Part-Afford Benchmark):** Achieved 56.63% accuracy, demonstrating generalization to OOD affordance prediction.
 - **VTG (VABench-V):** Achieved the lowest RMSE (77.8) and MAE (45.0) and highest LLM Score (7.3), indicating precise visual trace generation.
- Embodied-R1 significantly outperformed models trained solely with SFT (Embodied-SFT), validating the benefits of RFT for generalization in embodied pointing.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Embodied-R1 for Robot Manipulation

- **SimplerEnv Simulation:** Embodied-R1 achieved an average success rate of 56.2% on the WidowX arm using SimplerEnv, outperforming state-of-the-art end-to-end VLAs (e.g., $\pi_0$-fast 48.3%, OpenVLA-OFT 41.8%), modular methods (Sofar 53.8%), and affordance methods (FSD 40.6%). This indicates superior zero-shot generalization without domain-specific fine-tuning.
- **Real-World Robot Evaluation:** On an xArm 6 robot across eight tabletop manipulation tasks, Embodied-R1 achieved an 87.5% zero-shot success rate, representing a 62% improvement over baselines like RoboPoint and FSD. The Visual Trace Branch (Embodied-R1-V) generally produced more accurate annotations than the Affordance Points Branch (Embodied-R1-P). Failures in baselines were often attributed to poor spatial reasoning for tasks like "nearest object" or low grasping success for rigid objects.
- **Robustness against Visual Disturbances:** Embodied-R1 demonstrated high robustness to zero-shot visual disturbances, including changes in background, lighting, and height, maintaining high success rates (83-100%). This supports that pointing serves as a universal representation.
- **Qualitative Analysis in Complex Scenarios:** The model demonstrated versatility in long-horizon tasks (e.g., table organization), reasoning-intensive tasks (e.g., placing a sponge on a math problem's answer), and contact-rich manipulation tasks (e.g., blackboard wiping, bottle pressing) when integrated with high-level planners or impedance controllers.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis and Ablations

- **Embodied Reasoning Capabilities:** Embodied-R1 exhibited human-like, rational reasoning processes, progressively inferring target objects, analyzing spatial relationships, and determining target locations before providing coordinates (Figure 9).
- **Strong Generalization to OOD Scenarios:** Despite real-world data training, Embodied-R1 showed zero-shot generalization on VTG tasks in unseen simulations, across different robot embodiments, and even with hand-drawn sketches (Figure 5 right).
- **SFT vs. RL Ablation:** RL-based models consistently outperformed SFT counterparts on RRG benchmarks. The full model (RL w/ Think) performed best, confirming the efficacy of reward-driven reasoning.
- **Advantage of Mixed Training:** Joint multi-task training improved success rates across multiple pointing tasks (Part-Afford, Where2Place, VABench-P) compared to unmixed training, suggesting knowledge sharing and enhanced semantic understanding.
- **Integration with Learning-Based Pipeline:** When integrated as a conditional planner with Diffusion Policy on the LIBERO benchmark, Embodied-R1 boosted the average success rate from 64.4% to 79.4%, indicating effective bridging of high-level reasoning with low-level learning-based control.
- **3D RRG Capability (Embodied-R1-RGBD):** An RGB-D variant, Embodied-R1-RGBD, utilizing a Dual-Tower architecture and two-stage training, achieved 94.3% overall accuracy on the Open6DOR-Position benchmark, validating scalability to 3D RRG tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Embodied-R1, a VLM designed to address critical challenges in robotic manipulation, particularly the "seeing-to-doing gap." The work's significance lies in its proposal and empirical validation of "pointing" as a unified, embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation for bridging perception and action. This approach aims to overcome limitations related to data scarcity and robot heterogeneity that constrain current VLA models.

The primary impact areas include:
- **Enhanced Generalization in Robotics:** By enabling robust zero-shot robotic manipulation in both simulated and real-world environments, Embodied-R1 contributes to the development of more general-purpose robot agents. Its performance improvements over existing baselines, even against visual disturbances, suggest a pathway towards robots that can adapt to novel tasks and settings without extensive re-training.
- **Improved Reasoning Capabilities:** The application of Reinforced Fine-tuning (RFT) allows Embodied-R1 to develop flexible, free-form reasoning, which is demonstrated to be more effective for out-of-distribution generalization compared to traditional Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) methods that rely on rigid templates. This could influence future training paradigms for embodied AI models, encouraging the development of more adaptive cognitive processes.
- **Unified and Scalable Representation:** The systematic definition of four core embodied pointing abilities (REG, RRG, OFG, VTG) and their integration into a single model architecture provides a unified interface for diverse manipulation intents. This can simplify the development of robotic systems by abstracting away embodiment-specific details, facilitating knowledge transfer across different robot platforms.
- **Addressing Data Challenges:** The methodology for constructing the large-scale Embodied-Points-200K dataset, structured as "question-verification" pairs, offers a scalable approach to generate data suitable for RFT, which is crucial for mitigating data scarcity issues in embodied AI.

Potential future impacts and directions include:
- **Integration with Learning-Based Policies:** The research highlights the potential for integrating Embodied-R1's perception and reasoning with learning-based conditional policies, which could enhance execution efficiency and reactivity in dynamic environments.
- **Long-Horizon Task Capabilities:** While currently focused on single-step instructions, the framework could be extended to long-horizon tasks through hierarchical planning, with Embodied-R1 serving as a robust execution module for sub-tasks.
- **Richer Representations for Complex Interactions:** Acknowledging the limitations of 2D points for intricate tasks (e.g., precise force control, deformable object manipulation), future work may explore coupling high-level pointing commands with learnable downstream policies that can translate these targets into complex, dynamic actions.
- **Advanced 3D Understanding:** The preliminary integration of 3D information through an RGB-D variant suggests a promising path for robust 3D spatial reasoning, though further development is indicated for complex spatial relations.
- **Physical Common-Sense Alignment:** Mitigating physical common-sense hallucinations through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) could further align the model’s cognitive process with human intentions, leading to more physically plausible robotic behaviors.

Overall, the work contributes to the foundational understanding and practical application of embodied reasoning for robotic manipulation, offering a step towards more capable and general-purpose embodied AI.
