# Unlocking Generalization for Robotics via Modularity and Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Murtaza Dalal's doctoral research at Carnegie Mellon University developed a framework that integrates modularity with large-scale learning, enabling robots to perform zero-shot, text-conditioned manipulation in diverse, unstructured real-world environments. This approach achieves broad generalization by combining high-level planning, neural motion planning, and robust low-level policies, demonstrating high success rates across complex tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic systems are typically confined to narrow task sets in controlled environments, lacking the ability to generalize across varying conditions, object arrangements, and novel instructions.
- Applying large-scale data-driven learning directly to robotics is challenged by data scarcity, the need for high-dimensional generalization across semantic, geometric, and motor control axes, and stringent hardware safety constraints.
- Limitations exist in purely end-to-end reinforcement learning (data inefficiency, complex reward engineering) and classical planning methods (reliance on precise state estimation, vulnerability to cascading errors).

## Method

- Integrated modularity into learning through hierarchical structures, using parameterized action primitives for efficient low-level control and leveraging Large Language Models for high-level reasoning and sequencing with motion planning.
- Scaled robot learning by using procedural scene generation in simulation to create vast, diverse datasets, and employed planning algorithms as 'expert supervisors' to distill robust visuomotor policies.
- Unified these principles into a framework for generalist agents, combining Visual Language Model planning, fast reactive geometric motion planning, and a novel class of 'local policies' for zero-shot, text-conditioned, real-world manipulation.

## Results

- The RAPS framework demonstrated improved learning efficiency and success rates in sparse reward settings, outperforming raw action spaces and data-driven skill learning methods.
- The PSL framework achieved over **85%** success rates on diverse long-horizon tasks from raw visual input, significantly outperforming end-to-end RL and other baselines in sample efficiency.
- The ManipGen system demonstrated a **76%** success rate across 50 zero-shot real-world manipulation tasks (up to 8 stages), outperforming several state-of-the-art text-conditioned manipulation baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit modularity and hierarchical control significantly enhance learning efficiency and task success rates, especially in sparse reward settings and for long-horizon tasks, by decomposing complex problems into manageable sub-problems.
> - Large-scale data generation in simulation, coupled with expert planner distillation, provides a scalable methodology for training generalist visuomotor policies capable of robust sim-to-real transfer.
> - A synergistic integration of high-level VLM planning, reactive neural motion planning, and invariant low-level 'local policies' enables zero-shot generalization across diverse objects, poses, and task configurations in the real world.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: achieving generalization in complex, unstructured environments with under-specified instructions and novel situations. The broader research landscape has witnessed significant progress in fields such as computer vision and natural language processing, largely driven by large-scale, end-to-end learning with extensive datasets. However, applying this paradigm directly to robotics has presented several challenges, including the multi-modality of robotic tasks, the inherent scarcity of easily accessible, curated real-world data, and the critical safety and reliability concerns associated with deploying learning-based controllers on physical hardware.

Current successful robotic systems often rely on modular designs, where each module exhibits independent generalization capabilities. This thesis positions itself at the intersection of these two concepts, aiming to combine explicit modularity and hierarchical control with large-scale data-driven learning. It seeks to overcome the limitations of purely end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) approaches (e.g., data inefficiency, reliance on extensive reward engineering, difficulties with exploration in high-dimensional action spaces, and limited applicability to long-horizon tasks) and classical planning methods (e.g., dependence on accurate state estimation, susceptibility to cascading errors, and difficulties with contact-rich interactions). The research contributes to the development of generalist robot agents by integrating advances from vision, language, and planning with scalable robot learning in simulation, specifically targeting low-level control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research is the current limitation of robotic systems, which are typically confined to a narrow set of tasks within controlled environments. To enable robots to operate autonomously in dynamic, unstructured real-world settings, they must possess the ability to generalize across varying environments, object poses, arrangements, and low-level interaction skills, even when instructions are ambiguous or tasks are novel. This necessitates generalization across semantic, geometric, and motor control dimensions.

The author identifies several bottlenecks preventing the direct transfer of large-scale data-driven learning success from other AI domains to robotics:
- **Data Scarcity:** Manually collecting and curating large, diverse datasets for robotic tasks is labor-intensive and costly.
- **High-Dimensional Generalization:** The multitude of axes across which robots must generalize (semantics, geometry, control) makes data requirements impractically large, especially when learning direct mappings from visual observations to low-level control.
- **Hardware Constraints:** The imperative for safety, reliability, and deployability on physical hardware poses significant challenges for opaque, learned "black-box" controllers.

To address these challenges, the thesis proposes to integrate modularity with large-scale learning. The overarching objectives are structured around three key research questions:

1. **Integrating Modularity into Learning:** To determine optimal hierarchical structures and the balance between learned and planned components. This involves exploring:
 - Explicitly enforcing modularity via planning to improve learning efficiency and capability (Chapter 2).
 - Leveraging large language models (LLMs) for high-level reasoning and motion planning for sequencing, allowing reinforcement learning (RL) to focus on efficient online acquisition of low-level control (Chapter 3).

2. **Scaling via Procedural Scene Generation and Planner Distillation:** To establish a practical methodology for scaling robot learning. This is based on the insight that planning algorithms can supervise large-scale policy learning in simulation. Specific objectives include:
 - Using simulation for generating vast, diverse datasets through procedural scene generation.
 - Employing planning algorithms as "expert supervisors" to generate task-relevant data.
 - Distilling these planners into closed-loop visuomotor policies capable of operating in the real world solely from perceptual information.
 - Demonstrating this framework for end-to-end long-horizon manipulation (Chapter 4) and sim-to-real transfer for collision-free motion generation (Chapter 5).

3. **A Unifying Framework for Generalist Agents:** To combine the principles of modularity and large-scale learning into a single system capable of broad generalization. This aims to:
 - Build a robust Visual Language Model (VLM) planning system for combinatorial generalization over pre-trained skills.
 - Develop a fast, reactive geometric motion planner operating on noisy point cloud input.
 - Train a new class of "local policies" for sim-to-real transfer, emphasizing ease of transfer, pose invariance, and long-horizon chaining, ultimately enabling zero-shot, text-conditioned, real-world manipulation (Chapter 6).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The thesis systematically explores its objectives across three parts, each introducing and validating specific methodologies:

**Part I: Integrating Modularity into Learned Control**

- **Chapter 2: Accelerating Robotic Reinforcement Learning via Parameterized Action Primitives (RAPS)**
 - **Methodology:** Introduces a modular RL framework where a high-level policy learns to select and parameterize robot action primitives (RAPS).
 - **Modularity Design:** A high-level/low-level split is employed. The high-level policy, operating on visual inputs and sparse rewards, selects from a library of manually engineered, agent-centric primitives (e.g., lift, push, grasp). These primitives are parameterized by continuous arguments, which the policy also learns to output. A "dummy" primitive allows fallback to raw action space.
 - **Execution:** The selected primitive executes a fixed-horizon feedback control loop (e.g., end-effector position controller) to reach a target robot state, derived from its arguments.
 - **Evaluation:** Conducted across Kitchen, Meta-world, and Robosuite domains, using wall-clock time and training steps as primary metrics, comparing against raw actions, VICES, and skill learning methods (SPIRL, PARROT, Dyn-E).

- **Chapter 3: Plan-Seq-Learn (PSL)**
 - **Methodology:** A modular framework combining Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level planning, motion planning for sequencing, and RL for low-level control.
 - **Modularity Design:** A three-tiered hierarchy:
 - **Planning Module:** An LLM (GPT-4) generates a high-level task plan as a sequence of (target region, stage termination condition) tuples from a natural language task description.
 - **Sequencing Module:** Bridges the semantic gap by interpreting the LLM's plan. It uses off-the-shelf vision models (e.g., Segment Anything with calibrated RGB-D cameras) to estimate 3D poses of target regions. A sampling-based motion planner (AIT*) then computes and executes collision-free robot trajectories to these estimated poses. This module operates entirely from visual input.
 - **Learning Module:** A visual model-free RL algorithm (DRQ-v2) trains a single low-level control policy across all stages of the task. This policy leverages local observations (e.g., wrist camera views) and uses stage termination conditions (estimated visually) to progress through the plan.
 - **Evaluation:** Performance measured against end-to-end RL (E2E), hierarchical RL (RAPS), classical TAMP, and LLM planning (SayCan) on over 25 long-horizon tasks across four simulated benchmark suites (Meta-World, Obstructed Suite, Kitchen, Robosuite).

**Part II: Scaling via Procedural Scene Generation and Planner Distillation**

- **Chapter 4: Imitating Task and Motion Planning with Visuomotor Transformers (OPTIMUS)**
 - **Methodology:** A system for training visuomotor Transformer policies through large-scale imitation learning of a Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) expert.
 - **Data Generation:** A TAMP supervisor (PDDLStream, using AIT*, TRAC-IK, BiRRT) autonomously generates a diverse, large-scale dataset of successful trajectories in procedurally generated environments (e.g., block stacking, pick-and-place, shelf manipulation, articulated object manipulation with ShapeNet objects). Cost-sensitive TAMP and biased inverse kinematics (IK) are used to generate consistent, low-cost plans.
 - **Data Curation:** TAMP's joint-space demonstrations are converted to task-space actions. Trajectories are filtered based on task-space length and confinement within a visible workspace to remove sub-optimal or irrelevant data.
 - **Policy Architecture:** A Transformer network processes a history of multi-view RGB images (ResNet-18 + Spatial Softmax backbone) and proprioception (MLP). It predicts a continuous action using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) output distribution to handle the multi-modality of TAMP demonstrations.
 - **Evaluation:** Assessed on over 300 long-horizon manipulation tasks involving up to 8 stages and 72 diverse objects in simulation, comparing against BC-MLP, BC-RNN, and Behavior Transformer (BeT).

- **Chapter 5: Data Driven Neural Motion Planning (Neural MP)**
 - **Methodology:** Trains a generalist neural motion planner by distilling expert planner data generated in large-scale, procedurally diverse simulations, augmented with real-world deployment safety mechanisms.
 - **Data Generation:** Procedural scene generation combines parametric assets (shelves, microwaves, cabinets) with complex meshes from Objaverse. AIT* generates expert collision-free motion plans. Techniques like hindsight relabeling, reversibility, and in-hand object randomization are used to enhance data diversity. Trajectories are smoothed via cubic spline interpolation.
 - **Policy Architecture:** A sequence model (LSTM) takes as input segmented point clouds (PointNet++ encoder) of the robot, obstacles, and target robot, along with current and target joint angles. It predicts a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) distribution over delta joint angles.
 - **Deployment:** Integrates the learned policy with lightweight test-time optimization (TTO). N trajectories are sampled from the policy, and a linear model predicts future states. The trajectory with the minimal robot-scene intersection (computed via Signed Distance Function, SDF, on a spherical robot representation) is selected for execution. Real-world setup uses four calibrated Intel RealSense depth cameras and a PID joint controller.
 - **Evaluation:** Conducted on 64 real-world motion planning tasks across four environments (Bins, Shelf, Articulated, In-Hand), comparing against sampling-based (AIT*) and optimization-based (Curobo) planners, and a neural baseline (MπNets).

**Part III: A Unifying Framework for Building Generalist Agents by Combining Modularity and Scale**

- **Chapter 6: Local Policies Enable Zero-shot Long-horizon Manipulation (ManipGen)**
 - **Methodology:** Unifies the concepts of modularity and large-scale learning into a comprehensive system for zero-shot, text-conditioned, long-horizon manipulation in the real world.
 - **Framework Integration:**
 - **High-level Planning:** Utilizes a VLM (GPT-4o) to decompose natural language task prompts into a sequence of (object, skill) tuples.
 - **Pose Estimation:** Employs Grounded SAM with depth sensing to identify and localize target objects/regions. The VLM is used to refine object prompts for better detection.
 - **Motion Planning:** Leverages the Neural MP system (from Chapter 5) to generate fast, reactive, collision-free paths to the estimated target poses.
 - **Low-level Control:** Deploys a library of "local policies."
 - **Local Policies Design & Training:** A novel class of policies designed for pose, skill order, and scene configuration invariance.
 - **Training Phases:**
 1. **Behavior Acquisition:** Thousands of single-object, state-based RL experts (PPO) are trained in parallel in simulation (Isaac Gym) for core manipulation skills (pick, place, grasp handle, open, close) using diverse objects (UnidexGrasp, PartNet), randomized clutter, and carefully designed reward functions.
 2. **Distillation:** These experts are distilled into multi-object, generalist visuomotor policies using a modified DAgger approach.
 - **Observation Space:** Consists of wrist camera depth maps (clamped, normalized), the initial local depth map with a segmentation mask of the target object, and proprioception relative to the first time-step, including velocity information.
 - **Augmentations:** Depth maps are augmented with edge noise and random holes, alongside random camera cropping, to improve sim2real robustness.
 - **Actions:** Policies output delta pose targets for a Task Space Impedance (TSI) controller.
 - **Evaluation:** Comprehensive real-world evaluation on 50 long-horizon manipulation tasks across five environments, with up to 8 stages, assessing zero-shot capabilities. Compared against SayCan, OpenVLA, LLMTrajGen, and VoxPoser, and also benchmarked against Robosuite and FurnitureBench.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced a series of findings across its iterative development:

- **Chapter 2 (RAPS):**
 - Demonstrated that parameterized action primitives significantly enhanced learning efficiency and task success rates in sparse reward settings from image input, consistently outperforming raw action spaces and data-driven skill learning methods.
 - Showed robustness across different underlying RL algorithms (Dreamer, SAC, PPO) and improved performance in multi-task sequential control.
 - Enabled more efficient unsupervised exploration, leading to faster fine-tuning for new tasks.
 - Identified limitations in expressivity for dynamic/fluid motions and scalability to contact-rich tasks due to the fixed nature of hand-defined primitives.

- **Chapter 3 (PSL):**
 - Achieved state-of-the-art sample efficiency and high asymptotic success rates (over 85%) on a diverse set of over 25 long-horizon tasks (up to 10 stages) from raw visual input across four benchmark suites.
 - Outperformed end-to-end RL, hierarchical RL, classical TAMP, and LLM planning with pre-defined skills, particularly on challenging contact-intensive tasks like "NutAssembly."
 - Confirmed that local observations (wrist cameras) substantially accelerated policy learning (up to 4x speedup) and that explicit stage termination conditions were critical for effective plan sequencing.
 - Demonstrated robustness of the learned policies to moderate levels of noise in pose estimates.

- **Chapter 4 (OPTIMUS):**
 - Successfully distilled TAMP demonstrations into visuomotor Transformer policies, achieving high fidelity on single-step tasks (100% success on "PickPlace-1" and "Stack").
 - Enabled end-to-end policies to acquire long-horizon behaviors, solving tasks with up to 8 stages (e.g., "StackFive" at 70%, "PickPlaceFour" at 60%), outperforming alternative neural architectures.
 - Demonstrated capabilities for tasks requiring obstacle awareness (Shelf-1 at 91%) and articulated object manipulation (Microwave-1 at 86%).
 - Achieved strong generalization across diverse object sets in multi-task scenarios (e.g., "PickPlace-72" at 75% success), indicating the power of large-scale TAMP data and Transformer models.
 - Showed that the distilled policies not only operated from visual input but also surpassed the TAMP supervisor in average success rate (87% vs. 52%) and were significantly faster (5-7.5x speedup in execution).

- **Chapter 5 (Neural MP):**
 - Developed a state-of-the-art neural motion planner that achieved a 95.83% success rate on 48 diverse real-world free-hand motion planning tasks, marking a 23% improvement over sampling-based and 17% over optimization-based methods, and significantly outperforming prior neural motion planners (MπNets).
 - Operated with significantly lower planning time (3.9s vs. 80s for AIT*-80s).
 - Extended capabilities to in-hand motion planning tasks, achieving 87.5% success, and demonstrated dynamic obstacle avoidance.
 - Validated the effectiveness of procedural scene generation, multimodal (GMM) output distributions, and test-time optimization as key contributors to performance and real-world transfer.

- **Chapter 6 (ManipGen):**
 - Demonstrated a generalist robot agent capable of zero-shot long-horizon manipulation, achieving a 76% success rate across 50 real-world tasks (up to 8 stages) involving unseen objects, poses, and configurations, solely from natural language instructions.
 - Outperformed several state-of-the-art text-conditioned manipulation baselines (SayCan, OpenVLA, LLMTrajGen, VoxPoser) on these complex real-world tasks.
 - Showed that local policies, trained at scale in simulation, effectively transferred to real-world scenarios, outperforming methods relying on end-to-end transfer or real-world correction data (e.g., outperforming Transic on "Insert" with 80% vs. 45% success).
 - Ablation studies confirmed the importance of specific observation components (key-points, relative proprioception, velocity), initial observation encoding, and effective DAgger buffer management for multi-task policy distillation.
 - Identified cascading errors from modular components (VLM planning, pose estimation, motion planning) and limitations with specific object properties (shiny, transparent surfaces) as primary failure modes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of robotics by advancing the state of generalization for robotic systems. The key insight of combining modularity with large-scale learning offers a structured yet scalable approach to address long-standing challenges in robot perception, reasoning, and control.

The potential impacts include:

- **Enabling Generalist Robots:** The work demonstrates a viable path towards building generalist robot agents that can perform a wide variety of complex tasks in diverse, unstructured environments without requiring task-specific programming or extensive human demonstrations. This is a foundational step toward more autonomous and versatile robotic assistants in homes, warehouses, and other human-centric environments.
- **Reducing Development Costs:** By leveraging large-scale data generation in simulation and principled sim-to-real transfer, the methodology significantly reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection and manual reward engineering. This makes developing and deploying new robotic capabilities more economical and accessible.
- **Advancing Sim2Real Transfer:** The introduction and validation of "local policies" provide a robust paradigm for sim2real transfer. Their inherent invariance properties (pose, skill order, scene configuration) are crucial for generalization, allowing policies trained in simulation to perform effectively on real hardware without adaptation.
- **Synergistic Integration of AI Components:** The work effectively integrates state-of-the-art foundation models (VLMs) for high-level semantic reasoning with neural motion planners and low-level control policies. This modular yet interconnected framework provides a blueprint for leveraging advances across different AI sub-fields to create more capable robotic systems.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability:** The incorporation of planner distillation and test-time optimization in Neural MP (Chapter 5) contributes to safer and more reliable robot operation in the real world, mitigating collision risks that are common with purely learned controllers.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The thesis identifies several open problems and outlines potential future research directions, including enhancing closed-loop reactive control, improving long-horizon planning to address cascading errors, and developing continuous learning and adaptation mechanisms for robots operating in dynamic, evolving environments. The demonstrated scalability with compute offers a promising avenue for continued progress in these areas.
