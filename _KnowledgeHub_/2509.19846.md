# BoreaRL: A Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning Environment for Climate-Adaptive Boreal Forest Management

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced BoreaRL, a multi-objective reinforcement learning environment designed for climate-adaptive boreal forest management, featuring a physically-grounded simulator that models coupled energy, carbon, and water fluxes. The study revealed an asymmetric learning difficulty between carbon sequestration and permafrost protection objectives and demonstrated that adaptive episode selection significantly improves multi-objective RL performance and trade-off coverage.

## Problem

- Boreal forests are crucial for global carbon storage, yet permafrost thaw due to climate warming threatens to release vast greenhouse gases, necessitating effective climate-adaptive management.
- Optimizing boreal forest management involves complex, often conflicting, trade-offs, particularly between maximizing carbon sequestration and maintaining permafrost stability, which existing tools struggle to address.
- A gap exists in integrating realistic physics-based forest models with advanced reinforcement learning algorithms for multi-objective policy optimization, as current RL often uses simplified models and process-based models are primarily predictive.

## Method

- Developed "**BoreaRL**," a multi-objective reinforcement learning environment with a ==physically-grounded== simulator (BoreaRL-Sim) that models coupled energy, carbon, and water fluxes at detailed time steps, incorporating ecological processes and stochastic disturbances.
- Designed a flexible MORL environment wrapper (BoreaRL-Env) formalizing the management task as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process, offering a discrete action space for stand density and species mix, and a normalized vector reward for carbon and thaw objectives.
- Implemented and evaluated several multi-objective RL algorithms, including preference-conditioned EUPG and PPO variants (PPO Gated, Curriculum PPO), to learn sequential management policies for multi-decadal planning.

## Results

- Carbon-focused policies achieved higher average stem densities (around 1280 stems/ha) and maintained existing conifer fractions, while thaw-focused policies adopted more conservative densities (1000-1020 stems/ha) and promoted deciduous dominance.
- Curriculum PPO demonstrated superior performance in generalist settings, generating the most comprehensive trade-off coverage across the preference space with fewer λ-monotonicity violations (**50%**) compared to other methods.
- A strong positive correlation (r = +**0.65**) was observed between longer growing seasons and enhanced permafrost protection, mechanistically linked to increased transpiration cooling and canopy shading.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fundamental asymmetry in learning difficulty exists between the carbon and thaw objectives; carbon rewards provide clearer, more immediate feedback, while thaw rewards are noisier and more complex to optimize, especially with a risk-averse formulation.
> - Adaptive episode selection, as implemented in Curriculum PPO, significantly enhances MORL performance and trade-off coverage in generalist settings by strategically filtering training episodes and avoiding "trap sites" with conflicting gradients.
> - Learned management strategies showed that carbon-focused policies tend to maximize stand density and maintain conifer dominance, whereas thaw-focused policies adopt lower densities and promote deciduous species, revealing distinct approaches to the underlying trade-offs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Boreal forests constitute one of the largest terrestrial biomes, encompassing significant global carbon stores, much of which is contained within permafrost soils. The stability of these permafrost carbon pools is vulnerable to climate warming, presenting a potential positive feedback loop to global warming. Consequently, the management of boreal regions is considered relevant for global climate mitigation efforts.

Afforestation has been identified as a nature-based climate solution capable of sequestering atmospheric carbon and potentially providing co-benefits. In boreal contexts, afforestation strategies may contribute to both direct carbon dioxide removal and indirect protection of soil carbon stores by mitigating permafrost thaw. However, the unique characteristics of boreal ecosystems, including extensive permafrost coverage, extreme seasonal variability, and the distinct biogeophysical properties of coniferous and deciduous species, introduce complexities in optimizing afforestation for multiple objectives.

A central challenge in boreal forest management involves a trade-off between maximizing carbon sequestration and maintaining permafrost stability. Dense coniferous forests demonstrate high carbon uptake but possess low albedo, leading to increased solar radiation absorption, which can contribute to permafrost thaw. Conversely, deciduous forests allow for deeper snowpack, which insulates soil and may accelerate thaw, but their higher albedo when leafless can reduce energy absorption. These counteracting effects create a complex optimization landscape.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been identified as a suitable framework for discovering sequential policies in complex environments with long-term consequences and multi-decadal rewards, characteristics inherent to forest management. The inherently multi-objective nature of forest management problems, which may involve carbon sequestration, permafrost preservation, and other goals, positions this domain as an application area for Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL).

Prior applications of RL to environmental management, including forest management, have often relied on simplified growth models, static datasets, or adjacent domains. Concurrently, process-based forest models such as CLASSIC, CLM5, and CBM-CFS3 are primarily designed for prediction rather than policy optimization. This research addresses a gap in the literature by introducing an RL environment that integrates modern RL algorithms with realistic physics simulation and explicit multi-objective optimization capabilities relevant to climate-adaptive boreal forest management.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the critical role boreal forests play in the global carbon cycle and climate regulation. These forests store a substantial portion of terrestrial carbon, with a significant amount located in permafrost soils. The potential destabilization and thaw of permafrost due to climate warming could release vast quantities of greenhouse gases, initiating a positive feedback loop that exacerbates global warming. Therefore, effective management of boreal forests is crucial for climate mitigation.

However, current forest management practices and tools face challenges in addressing the complex, often conflicting, objectives inherent in boreal ecosystems. Specifically, optimizing for both carbon sequestration and permafrost preservation presents trade-offs that existing tools are not well-equipped to handle. For instance, dense coniferous stands are efficient at carbon uptake but can reduce surface albedo and alter snow dynamics, potentially impacting permafrost stability. The complex interplay between climate, species composition, stand density, management timing, and the relative importance of different objectives necessitates advanced decision-making tools.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is particularly suited for this challenge due to its ability to learn sequential policies through trial-and-error in complex, dynamic environments, accounting for long-term consequences and delayed rewards spanning multiple decades. The multi-objective nature of forest management, where various goals like carbon, permafrost, and potentially biodiversity or economic returns must be simultaneously considered, further highlights the relevance of Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL).

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are fourfold:

1. **Develop a Configurable Multi-Objective RL Environment:** To introduce "BoreaRL," a multi-objective reinforcement learning environment and framework for boreal forest management. This environment features a physically-grounded simulator that models coupled energy, carbon, and water fluxes, providing modular components for physics, rewards, agents, and evaluations, thereby enabling a systematic study of afforestation trade-offs within a MORL context.
2. **Design and Validate Training Paradigms:** To design and validate two distinct training paradigms within BoreaRL: a "site-specific mode" for controlled research studies with fixed environmental parameters, and a "generalist mode" for learning robust management policies under environmental stochasticity and diverse site conditions. A further objective is to evaluate the learning dynamics within these paradigms, specifically to identify potential asymmetries in learning difficulty between carbon and thaw objectives.
3. **Evaluate MORL Algorithm Performance and Management Strategies:** To benchmark multi-objective RL algorithms within BoreaRL, specifically demonstrating whether basic adaptive episode selection methods can achieve superior performance and trade-off coverage compared to standard preference-conditioned approaches, particularly in generalist settings. A related objective is to analyze the emergent management strategies discovered by these RL policies, identifying distinct approaches to density and species composition management that reflect the underlying carbon-permafrost trade-offs.
4. **Establish a Principled Testbed for MORL in Climate Applications:** To provide the research community with a scientifically credible and modular testbed for MORL in physically-grounded domains. This includes isolating specific challenges like asymmetric objective difficulties, facilitating the generation of testable scientific hypotheses, and offering a high-impact platform for developing solutions to climate change-related management problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development and evaluation of BoreaRL, a multi-objective reinforcement learning environment. The framework is designed for modularity and configurability, adhering to the `mo-gymnasium` API standard.

**BoreaRL Environment and Framework Structure:**
The core architecture comprises:
- **BoreaRL-Sim:** A physically-grounded simulator of boreal forest dynamics.
- **BoreaRL-Env:** A flexible MORL environment wrapper.

The framework provides compartmentalized modules for physics simulation (with selectable backend and temporal resolution), reward specification (customizable objective functions and normalization), agent interfaces (supporting single-policy and multi-policy MORL algorithms), environmental stochasticity (controllable weather generation and parameter sampling), and evaluation protocols (standardized metrics).

**Boreal Forest Simulator (BoreaRL-Sim):**
This component is a process-based simulator that models coupled energy, water, and carbon fluxes at an n-minute time step. It incorporates:
- **Multi-node energy balance model:** Spans canopy, trunk, snow, and soil layers, based on validated physical formulations from Land Surface Models like CLM5 and CLASSIC. The canopy energy balance is solved iteratively, while other nodes are prognostic.
- **Dynamic carbon cycle:** Tracks living biomass and soil organic carbon pools, including Gross Primary Production (GPP) based on a Light Use Efficiency model and respiration (autotrophic and heterotrophic) as a function of temperature.
- **Comprehensive water balance:** Includes canopy-intercepted water, ground snowpack (Snow Water Equivalent), and soil water content dynamics.
- **Stochastic modules for disturbances:** Fire probability conditioned on drought index, temperature, and species flammability; insect outbreaks dependent on winter temperature and stand density. Both cause fractional mortality and affect carbon pools.
- **Age-structured demography:** Five age classes (seedling to old), each with distinct canopy factors and light-use efficiency. Management actions (thinning, planting) modify age distribution.
- **Harvested Wood Products (HWP) accounting:** Tracks carbon stored in harvested timber, contributing to the carbon objective.
- **Operating Modes:**
 - **Generalist mode:** Each episode is driven by unique, stochastically generated weather sequences and site parameters sampled from realistic ranges. A context vector of site parameters is appended to the observation.
 - **Site-specific mode:** Utilizes deterministic weather patterns and fixed site parameters for reproducible, location-targeted optimization.

**Multi-Objective RL Environment (BoreaRL-Env):**
The management task is formalized as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process for MORL:
- **Observation Space (O):**
 - **Generalist mode:** 105 dimensions, encompassing current ecological state (year, stem density, conifer fraction, carbon stock), site climate parameters, historical information (disturbances, management, carbon flux trends), age distribution, detailed carbon stock values, penalty information, a preference input weight (w_C), and episode-level site parameters.
 - **Site-specific mode:** 43 dimensions, a reduced version without site-specific stochastic parameters.
 - All components are normalized to the range.
- **Action Space (A):** A discrete action space of 25 unique actions. These actions encode combinations of:
 - **Stand Density Change:** Five options: {-100, -50, 0, +50, +100} stems per hectare, representing thinning or planting.
 - **Species Mix Change:** Five options: {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} conifer fraction, ranging from purely deciduous to purely coniferous.
 - Actions are constrained; for example, thinning maintains a minimum density, and planting cannot exceed a maximum density (2000 stems/ha). Thinning preferentially removes oldest trees.
- **Vector Reward Function (R):** At each step `t`, the environment returns `R_t = [r_c,t, r_t,t]`, with components normalized to [−1, 1].
 - **Carbon Reward (r_c,t):** Based on the net ecosystem carbon change (ΔC_t, including HWP), with bonuses for total stock and HWP storage, and penalties for exceeding realistic biomass or soil carbon pools, maximum stand density, and ineffective actions.
 - **Thaw Reward (r_t,t):** Designed to protect permafrost by minimizing deep soil warming. It is calculated as an asymmetric function of conductive heat flux across the permafrost boundary (`f_cool - α * f_warm`), where `α = 2.5` heavily penalizes warming relative to cooling, reflecting the irreversibility of permafrost degradation.
- **Preference Conditioning:** A preference weight `w_C` for the carbon objective (ranging) is provided as part of the observation, allowing for training preference-conditioned policies.
- **Multi-Objective Formulation:** A linear scalarization approach is used, where the scalarized reward is `r_λ,t = w_C * R_carbon,t + (1 - w_C) * R_thaw,t`. Training can use either a fixed weight (λ) or a sampled weight (λ) across episodes.

**Multi-Objective RL Baseline Algorithms:**
The study evaluates four types of algorithms:
1. **Fixed Lambda EUPG (Expected Utility Policy Gradient):** Trains a single policy `π_θ(a|o)` on a scalarized reward with a fixed preference weight `λ`.
2. **Variable Lambda EUPG (Adaptive Preference Learning):** Trains a single policy `π_θ(a|o)` that adapts to preference weights by sampling `λ` for each episode and providing it as observation input.
3. **PPO Gated (Proximal Policy Optimization with Action Masking):** A standard PPO algorithm incorporating a gated architecture to separate planting from non-planting actions, ensuring only valid actions are considered.
4. **Curriculum PPO (Adaptive Episode Selection):** A preference-conditioned PPO agent that employs a two-level decision process. It uses an untrained random projection network (`f_ϕ(o_site) →`) to score episodes based on site characteristics. An adaptive threshold (`τ`) dynamically determines whether to accept an episode for training based on agent performance, expanding or contracting the training distribution.

**Experimental Design:**
- **Training:** For site-specific mode, 5 agents are trained with different random site seeds. For generalist mode, a single agent is trained, with each episode using a unique random seed for weather and site parameters to ensure robustness. Training duration varied based on mode and weight sampling.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is assessed using learning curves, reward metrics, and analysis of learned strategies. Key metrics include hypervolume and sparsity to quantify the quality and uniformity of the learned trade-off fronts, and λ-monotonicity to measure preference adherence. Heuristic baselines (e.g., zero density change, fixed density increase, target density, conifer restoration) are included for comparison.
- **Thaw Reward Formulations:** The impact of different thaw reward formulations (Asymmetric, Contrast, Raw Degree Days) on PPO Gated performance is also investigated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the BoreaRL environment and the performance of multi-objective RL algorithms in boreal forest management.

**Asymmetric Learning Difficulty:**
A fundamental asymmetry in learning difficulty was observed between the carbon and thaw objectives across both site-specific and generalist settings. Carbon-focused policies (λ = (1.0, 0.0)) consistently achieved rapid learning progress, whereas thaw-focused policies (λ = (0.0, 1.0)) demonstrated minimal or no improvement, often performing near baseline levels. This disparity is attributed to the nature of the underlying physics: carbon rewards provide more immediate and clear feedback through biomass accumulation, while thaw rewards depend on complex, often delayed, seasonal energy balance dynamics, resulting in noisier and less direct signals. The intentionally risk-averse formulation of the thaw objective, which heavily penalizes warming, further contributes to its optimization challenge.

**Emergent Management Strategies:**
Distinct forest management strategies emerged based on the objective preferences. Carbon-focused policies tended to aggressively increase stem density, reaching approximately 1280 stems/ha, and generally maintained the existing conifer fraction. Conversely, thaw-focused policies adopted more conservative densities (around 1000-1020 stems/ha) and promoted deciduous dominance. While these strategies indicate preference-specific behavior, the limited learning progress of thaw-focused policies suggests their strategies might be more a consequence of conservatism rather than optimized cooling.

**Curriculum Site Selection and Performance in Generalist Mode:**
In the generalist setting, where agents encounter environmental stochasticity, the Curriculum PPO algorithm, which employs adaptive episode selection, demonstrated superior performance compared to other preference-conditioned methods (Variable λ EUPG and PPO Gated). Curriculum PPO showed faster convergence and more stable performance in terms of scalarized rewards during training and evaluation. It also generated the most comprehensive trade-off coverage across the preference space, exhibiting fewer λ-monotonicity violations (50%) compared to other methods (e.g., PPO Gated with 100% violations, Variable λ EUPG with 66.7%). This indicates better adherence to user-specified preferences. Heuristic baselines achieved moderate rewards but did not effectively delineate the multi-objective front. The success of Curriculum PPO highlights that strategically selecting training episodes, even through a simple adaptive threshold based on a random projection of site characteristics, can significantly benefit learning by enabling agents to avoid "trap sites" with high warming potential where conflicting gradients destabilize learning.

**Impact of Thaw Reward Formulations:**
An evaluation of different thaw reward formulations revealed that the default Asymmetric Thaw formulation, with its risk-averse penalty for warming, was the most challenging to optimize. Alternative formulations like Contrast Thaw and Raw Degree Days yielded marginally better scalarized rewards but lacked the necessary strong penalty for irreversible permafrost degradation. This suggests that while easier to optimize, symmetric or linear formulations may not align with the ecological imperative of strongly avoiding permafrost thaw. All tested thaw formulations consistently created conflicts with the carbon objective, reinforcing the inherent trade-offs.

**Comparative Analysis of Algorithmic Management Strategies:**
Further analysis of the learned strategies revealed distinct management philosophies:
- **PPO Gated:** This algorithm developed an aggressive carbon-maximizing approach, characterized by achieving the highest average conifer fractions and rapid early planting followed by natural thinning. This strategy resulted in high-density, high-conifer stands, which maximized carbon but often led to significant permafrost warming due to the carbon objective's gradient overpowering the noisy thaw signal.
- **Curriculum PPO:** This algorithm learned a more balanced approach, demonstrating steady species optimization and maintaining moderate density strategies. It achieved better trade-offs between carbon gains and permafrost protection. The algorithm's ability to filter out challenging sites allowed it to learn more nuanced cooling strategies that standard methods missed, leading to improved permafrost outcomes.
- **Variable λ EUPG:** This algorithm adopted a conservative strategy, generally maintaining baseline species composition and limited growth. This was interpreted as a sign of limited learning, potentially due to conflicting gradients arising from the changing preference weights, which led to risk-averse inaction.

**Ecological Insights:**
The analysis of learned strategies also provided ecological insights. A notable correlation was observed between longer growing seasons and enhanced thaw protection (correlation coefficient r = +0.65). Mechanistically, longer growing seasons maintained higher Leaf Area Index (LAI) for more days, increasing transpiration cooling (r = +0.82) and canopy shading (r = −0.75), which reduces soil heating. This suggests that specific forest management practices leading to longer growing seasons can serve as a buffer against climate-induced permafrost degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of BoreaRL represents a contribution to the field of climate-adaptive forest management and multi-objective reinforcement learning. The framework's physically-grounded simulator, which models coupled energy, carbon, and water fluxes, provides a mechanism for studying complex biogeophysical trade-offs between carbon sequestration and permafrost preservation. This bridges a gap in existing research by offering an environment where realistic ecological dynamics can be directly integrated with modern reinforcement learning algorithms, moving beyond predictive models to support policy optimization.

The modular design of BoreaRL and its support for both site-specific and generalist training paradigms offer flexibility for researchers. It functions as a principled testbed, enabling controlled experiments to generate scientific hypotheses (e.g., regarding density-thaw relationships) and providing a standardized platform for benchmarking MORL algorithms in physically-grounded domains.

A significant finding is the identification and isolation of the "Asymmetric Multi-Objective" optimization challenge, where objectives like carbon and thaw have substantially different signal-to-noise ratios and timescales. The demonstrated limitations of current MORL methods in managing these asymmetric objectives, particularly the difficulty in optimizing for permafrost preservation, underscores the need for developing novel algorithmic approaches. The success of adaptive episode selection in BoreaRL, even in a naive form, highlights the potential of curriculum learning or site-selection strategies for improving MORL performance in complex, stochastic environments.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Accelerating Research:** By open-sourcing BoreaRL, the authors aim to accelerate research in multi-objective RL for climate applications, providing a readily accessible tool for the wider scientific community.
- **Developing Effective Management Strategies:** The environment can facilitate the development of more effective and robust climate-adaptive forest management strategies. These strategies could improve the stewardship of boreal carbon sinks, thereby contributing to global climate change mitigation efforts.
- **Advancing MORL:** The identified challenges within BoreaRL, particularly the asymmetric learning difficulties, serve as benchmarks for the development of more sophisticated MORL algorithms capable of handling complex, real-world climate problems.
- **Generating Ecological Insights:** The analysis of learned policies has already yielded ecological insights, such as the correlation between longer growing seasons and enhanced permafrost protection, suggesting specific avenues for forest management interventions.

While BoreaRL is currently a research tool for controlled experimentation rather than a deployment-ready decision support system, it lays foundational groundwork. Future work, including incorporating economic and biodiversity objectives, developing advanced MORL algorithms capable of handling environmental stochasticity, and rigorous validation against field data, could extend its utility towards practical applications in large geographic regions and real-time forest management.
