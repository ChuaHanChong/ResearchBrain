# Train-Once Plan-Anywhere Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Diffusion Trees

> [!summary] Summary
> DiTree, a framework from researchers at Technion–Israel Institute of Technology, enhances kinodynamic motion planning by integrating diffusion models as informed action samplers within sampling-based planners. This approach achieves superior performance in unseen environments, boosting success rates by 26% in CarMaze and 28% in AntMaze, while maintaining safety and dynamic feasibility.

## Problem

- Kinodynamic Motion Planning (KMP) remains computationally intensive for high-dimensional robots, with classical sampling-based planners (SBPs) suffering from slow, uninformed exploration.
- Purely learning-based motion planners, while fast, lack guarantees for collision avoidance, struggle with generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) environments, and often produce trajectories with predefined horizons.
- There is a need for motion planning solutions that combine the efficiency of learned policies with the reliability and theoretical guarantees of traditional search methods.

## Method

- **DiTree** integrates a ==Diffusion Policy== (DP) as an 'informed action sampler' within a generic ==Sampling-Based Planner== (SBP), replacing uninformed random action selection.
- The DP is conditioned on local, relative state and obstacle information, enabling generalization to diverse unseen environments rather than relying on global map context.
- Sampled action sequences are propagated via the robot's dynamics in an ==MPC-style== manner, with iterative re-sampling and explicit collision checking, ensuring dynamic feasibility and collision-free guarantees.

## Results

- DiTree achieved average success rates **26%** higher than baselines in OOD CarMaze environments and **28%** higher in the high-dimensional AntMaze domain, where classical SBPs completely failed.
- Generated trajectories were **25-50%** shorter on average compared to RRT, indicating improved solution quality and efficiency.
- Demonstrated successful deployment on a physical car robot, consistently avoiding collisions in all **10** trials while RRT experienced **8** collisions, and achieving significantly lower tracking deviation (**0.28m** vs. **0.5m**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging diffusion models as informed samplers within SBPs accelerates exploration and planning efficiency while preserving the probabilistic completeness of the underlying SBP.
> - Conditioning the diffusion policy on local, relative environment information allows a single trained model to generalize robustly to vastly different, unseen (OOD) environments.
> - A single diffusion denoising iteration, providing faster and coarser guidance, is more effective for guiding tree expansion than computationally expensive, high-fidelity trajectory generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Kinodynamic Motion Planning (KMP) is a fundamental and critical problem in robotics, concerned with generating collision-free trajectories that respect a robot's complex dynamic constraints (e.g., bounded velocity, acceleration, turning radius). It is essential for a wide range of real-world applications, from autonomous vehicles and drones to humanoid robots. However, KMP remains highly challenging due to the high-dimensional, non-linear nature of robot state and action spaces.

Historically, this problem has been largely tackled by **Sampling-Based Planners (SBPs)**, such as Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT) and Stable Sparse Trees (SST). SBPs explore the robot's state space by iteratively sampling actions, propagating dynamics, and building a search tree or graph. They offer strong theoretical guarantees, including probabilistic completeness (ability to find a solution if one exists, given enough time) and sometimes asymptotic optimality (convergence to an optimal solution). However, their performance can be severely hampered by slow exploration, especially in complex, high-dimensional, or cluttered environments, as they rely on uninformed, random sampling.

In recent years, **learning-based approaches**, particularly those leveraging deep generative models like Diffusion Models (DMs) and Flow Matching (FMs), have shown immense promise. These models excel at modeling complex, multi-modal distributions of expert behaviors and can generate high-quality trajectories very rapidly. They represent a significant advancement in generating diverse and sophisticated motions. Despite their speed, these learning-based planners suffer from critical limitations:
- **Lack of Guarantees:** They inherently lack guarantees on collision avoidance, often requiring external collision-checking or complex guided denoising, which might not ensure complete safety.
- **Limited Generalization (OOD Performance):** A major drawback is their poor generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios—environments significantly different from their training data. This "catastrophic failure" in unseen settings limits their practical deployability.
- **Predefined Trajectory Horizons:** Many diffusion-based planners generate fixed-length trajectories, requiring heuristic solutions like Model Predictive Control (MPC) for longer horizons, which can introduce temporal inconsistencies.
- **Lack of Differential Constraint Enforcement:** Some methods predict states directly, without explicitly enforcing dynamic feasibility, necessitating additional local planners or controllers to ensure physical adherence.

The paper "Train-Once Plan-Anywhere" directly addresses these limitations by proposing **DiTree (Diffusion Tree)**, a hybrid "learning-meets-search" framework. Inspired by the successes of combining learning and search in other AI domains (e.g., AlphaGo for strategic games or LLMs for reasoning), DiTree aims to merge the strengths of both paradigms. Its core contribution is using DMs not as direct trajectory generators, but as *informed action samplers* within the robust framework of SBPs. This approach seeks to accelerate SBP exploration using learned priors, while inherently retaining the safety, dynamic feasibility, and theoretical guarantees provided by the SBP backbone. By focusing on generalization across diverse environments with a single trained model, this work carves out a significant niche, aiming for a more practical and reliable solution for KMP in real-world robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the DiTree framework is to overcome the fundamental limitations of existing kinodynamic motion planning approaches by integrating the efficiency and generative power of diffusion models with the reliability and theoretical guarantees of sampling-based planners. Specifically, the authors aim to develop a planning system that can:

1. **Achieve High Efficiency:** Significantly reduce the runtime compared to traditional sampling-based planners (SBPs) by guiding the search more effectively.
2. **Ensure Robust Generalization:** Successfully plan in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments, meaning scenarios that are vastly different from the single environment used during training. This addresses a major weakness of purely learning-based methods.
3. **Guarantee Collision Avoidance and Dynamic Feasibility:** Inherit the safety guarantees of SBPs, ensuring that generated trajectories are collision-free and adhere to the robot's complex kinematic and dynamic constraints.
4. **Maintain Theoretical Soundness:** Preserve the probabilistic completeness and, potentially, asymptotic optimality properties of the underlying sampling-based planners.

The motivation for pursuing this hybrid approach stems from several critical observations in the field of robotic motion planning:

- **Ubiquity and Complexity of KMP:** Kinodynamic motion planning is foundational for most real-world robotic systems, yet it remains computationally intensive for high-dimensional, non-linear systems.
- **Limitations of Pure SBPs:** While robust and theoretically sound, classical SBPs like RRT and SST struggle with efficiency in complex or cluttered spaces due to their uninformed, exhaustive exploration strategy. Their "slow exploration due to uninformed action sampling" is a significant bottleneck.
- **Shortcomings of Pure Learning-Based Methods:** Advanced techniques like Diffusion Policies (DPs) can generate fast, high-quality trajectories. However, their practical deployment is severely limited by a "lack of collision avoidance guarantees," poor "generalization to unseen environments," and the need for "predefined trajectory horizons," among other issues.
- **Inspiration from Hybrid AI Successes:** The authors are motivated by the breakthroughs seen in other AI domains, such as AlphaGo, where combining deep learning with sophisticated search algorithms yielded unprecedented performance. They posit that a similar synergy can revolutionize KMP.

By leveraging a diffusion model as an "informed sampler" rather than a direct trajectory generator, DiTree aims to provide the necessary "guidance for state-space search within SBPs." This strategic integration is intended to "yield provably-safe solutions within a few action propagation iterations for complex dynamical systems," ultimately bridging the gap between speed, reliability, and generalizability in robotic motion planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of DiTree's methodology is to enhance a generic sampling-based tree planner (SBP) by replacing its uninformed random action selection with a learned, context-aware sampling procedure powered by a Diffusion Policy (DP). The framework is designed to be applicable to any SBP built around a fundamental iterative logic, as outlined in their Algorithm 1.

#### 3.1. A. Integration of Diffusion Policy for Action Sampling

The key innovation lies in the `Action Selector` component of the SBP. Instead of sampling a single action from a uniform distribution, DiTree samples a *sequence* of N actions, `u_1:N`, from a conditional diffusion policy. This policy is trained to learn the distribution of expert actions and is conditioned on three critical inputs:
1. **Current Tree State (`x_near`):** The robot's state at the node being considered for expansion.
2. **Target State (`x_target`):** This can either be the global goal state (`x_goal`) or a randomly sampled intermediate state (`x_rand`). The introduction of a "diffusion goal bias" (DGB) allows the policy to balance goal-directed exploitation with broader exploration.
3. **Local Obstacle Information (`X_near_obs`):** A localized representation of obstacles relative to `x_near`. This is crucial for guiding the policy away from collisions and improving generalization, as it abstracts away from global coordinates. The authors use an occupancy grid for this.

#### 3.2. B. Generalization-Enhancing Strategies

To foster generalization, particularly to unseen environments, DiTree employs two main strategies:
1. **Relative State Representation:** The `x_target` is represented relative to `x_near`, making the planning problem translation and rotation invariant, thus less dependent on specific global coordinates.
2. **Localized Obstacle Conditioning:** The DP is conditioned only on local obstacle information `X_near_obs` around `x_near`, rather than the entire global map. This simplifies the learning problem and makes the policy more robust to varied and complex unseen environments.

#### 3.3. C. Forward Propagation and Collision Checking

Once an action sequence `u_1:N` is sampled, it is executed via forward propagation using the robot's dynamic model. Importantly, DiTree uses the Diffusion Model in an **MPC-style (Model Predictive Control) manner** during forward propagation. This means the policy iteratively resamples actions based on updated local observations. This allows for:
- **Arbitrarily Long Edges:** The policy can generate long trajectory segments (edges) by perpetually sampling and propagating actions, even with only a local view of the environment.
- **Full Information Utilization:** Despite conditioning on local observations for sampling, the iterative resampling ensures that the latest local information is continuously integrated.
- **Collision-Free Guarantee:** The generated trajectory segment is subjected to explicit collision checking. Only collision-free segments are added to the search tree, thereby ensuring the inherent safety guarantees of SBPs are maintained.

#### 3.4. D. Theoretical Guarantees

A significant contribution of DiTree is its proof that it preserves the theoretical properties of the underlying SBP. Specifically, they prove that an RRT-based DiTree is **Probabilistically Complete (PC)**. This proof hinges on demonstrating that the diffusion models (DMs) and flow matching (FMs) used for action sampling possess "full support" – meaning they can, in principle, generate any dynamically feasible and collision-free action sequence with a non-zero probability. This ensures that the informed sampling does not prematurely prune the search space in a way that would prevent a solution from being found. They also discuss how this framework can be extended to achieve **Asymptotic Optimality (AO)** by pairing it with AO-SBPs like SST or AO-RRT.

#### 3.5. E. Implementation Details and Experimental Setup

- **SBP Backbone:** The implementation uses the popular RRT planner due to its simplicity and effectiveness.
- **Diffusion Model:** A single-step Flow Matching (FM) model is chosen for its favorable trade-off between speed and quality during inference, recognizing that fast, coarse samples are often preferable for guiding tree expansion.
- **Observation Encoder:** A UNet architecture is used to process the occupancy grid representing local observations.
- **Training:** The FM policy is trained on an offline dataset from a single environment (D4RL's Large maze) for 2 hours on an RTX 3090 GPU.
- **Robots:** Evaluated on a custom 6D non-holonomic CarMaze model and D4RL's 29D AntMaze quadruped robot.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against OMPL's RRT and SST, and a standalone Diffusion Policy (DP) (using the same model as DiTree but without the SBP framework).

In summary, DiTree's methodology meticulously integrates learning-based action priors into classical search, aiming to accelerate planning while retaining critical safety and theoretical assurances, and demonstrating strong generalization capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DiTree demonstrates significant improvements across several key metrics, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. The results consistently highlight the benefits of combining learned priors with structured search.

- **Superior Performance in OOD Scenarios:** DiTree was tested across 15 distinct, unseen maze environments with two different robot types (CarMaze and AntMaze).
 - **CarMaze Domain:** DiTree achieved an average success rate 26% higher than all other baselines (RRT, SST, and standalone DP). Furthermore, it matched the runtime performance of the standalone Diffusion Policy, being approximately four times faster than classical SBPs (RRT and SST).
 - **AntMaze Domain:** This domain, involving a 29D quadruped robot, proved particularly challenging. Classical SBPs (RRT and SST) completely failed to find any valid trajectories. In contrast, DiTree achieved a 28% higher success rate than the standalone Diffusion Policy, demonstrating its capability in high-dimensional and complex dynamic systems where traditional methods are ineffective.

- **Improved Trajectory Quality:** Beyond just finding a path, DiTree often found more efficient solutions. On average, DiTree discovered trajectories that were 25-50% shorter compared to those generated by RRT, indicating its strength in producing efficient and high-quality paths.

- **Real-World Applicability and Robustness:** A crucial finding was DiTree's successful deployment on a physical car robot. In a sharp-corner turning scenario, DiTree consistently avoided collisions across all 10 trials, while the RRT planner experienced 8 collisions. This real-world experiment, performed even under "severe sim-to-real gaps" (using a default dynamic model without system identification), showcased DiTree's robustness, superior trajectory quality, and practical viability. The average tracking deviation for DiTree was also significantly lower (0.28m vs. 0.5m for RRT).

- **Ablation Study Insights:** The ablation studies provided valuable insights into DiTree's design choices:
 - **Diffusion Denoising Iterations:** Surprisingly, using a *single* diffusion iteration yielded the best overall performance. This suggests that for DiTree's role as an informed sampler, faster, coarser guidance is more beneficial for tree expansion than computationally expensive, high-fidelity trajectory generation, which contrasts with traditional diffusion-based planners.
 - **Propagation Duration (Edge Length):** A fixed propagation duration of 64 steps for an action sequence per tree edge offered the best balance between covering ground and avoiding inefficient collisions.
 - **Diffusion Goal Bias (DGB):** The DGB, controlling the mix of conditioning on a random intermediate state versus the final goal, showed optimal performance at either 0% (pure goal-directed) or 85% (strong goal-bias with some exploration). The optimal setting depended on scenario complexity, balancing rapid expansion with sufficient exploration.

- **Shift in Computational Bottleneck:** The analysis revealed a significant shift in computational resource allocation. Traditional SBPs spent the majority of their time on collision checks and nearest-neighbor queries (approximately 798.6k collision checks per minute). DiTree, however, drastically reduced collision checks to 10.5k per minute, with 94.34% of its runtime allocated to diffusion model inference. This highlights a paradigm shift from low-level geometric operations to amortized, learned inference, opening new avenues for optimization.

In summary, DiTree delivers on its promise of efficient, generalizable, and provably-safe kinodynamic motion planning, significantly outperforming existing methods in challenging unseen environments and demonstrating real-world applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DiTree represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic motion planning, offering a compelling solution to long-standing challenges in kinodynamic motion planning (KMP). Its potential impact spans theoretical understanding, practical robotic applications, and future research directions.

#### 5.1. A. Bridging the Gap in Robotic Motion Planning

DiTree successfully integrates the speed and expressive power of modern generative models (diffusion models) with the reliability and theoretical guarantees of classical sampling-based planners (SBPs). This "learning-meets-search" paradigm provides a robust framework that simultaneously addresses the efficiency limitations of SBPs and the generalization and safety concerns of purely learning-based approaches. This synergistic approach could become a new standard for tackling complex planning problems.

#### 5.2. B. Enhanced Safety and Generalizability for Real-World Robotics

One of the most critical impacts is the ability to achieve "provably-safe solutions" that "generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios." The inherent collision-free nature (due to explicit collision checking within the SBP framework) and adherence to dynamic constraints make DiTree significantly more suitable for deployment on physical robots, especially in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, aerial navigation, and collaborative robotics. Its demonstrated performance in unseen environments drastically reduces the need for extensive environment-specific retraining, a major bottleneck for current learning-based systems.

#### 5.3. C. Improved Efficiency and Trajectory Quality

By leveraging diffusion models as "informed samplers," DiTree accelerates the exploration process within SBPs, leading to significantly faster planning times (e.g., 4x faster than classical SBPs on CarMaze) and higher success rates. The ability to find "25-50% shorter trajectories" also implies more efficient and potentially safer robot movements, which translates to better energy consumption, reduced wear, and more agile operation.

#### 5.4. D. Validation of Theoretical Guarantees in Learning-Enhanced Systems

The proof of probabilistic completeness for DiTree (and the discussion of extending to asymptotic optimality) is a crucial theoretical contribution. It demonstrates that integrating powerful, non-uniform learned priors does not necessarily compromise the fundamental guarantees of SBPs, provided the underlying generative model possesses "full support." This theoretical grounding builds confidence in the reliability of such hybrid systems.

#### 5.5. E. Paradigm Shift in Computational Bottlenecks

The observation that DiTree shifts the computational bottleneck from low-level geometric operations (collision detection) to diffusion model inference is highly significant. This redirection of computational resources opens new avenues for optimization, such as model distillation, quantization, and specialized hardware acceleration for diffusion models. Future research can focus on optimizing these AI components, potentially leading to even faster and more efficient planners.

#### 5.6. F. Future Research and Development

DiTree lays fertile ground for several exciting future research directions:
- **Optimization of Inference:** Further work on optimizing diffusion model inference speed (e.g., distillation, quantization) will directly improve planning runtime.
- **Learning Additional SBP Components:** The framework can be extended to learn other SBP components beyond action sampling, such as node selection strategies or goal biasing.
- **GPU Parallelization:** Exploiting the parallelizable nature of generative model inference with GPU-accelerated SBP frameworks promises substantial speed-ups.
- **Complex Environments:** Adapting DiTree to handle partial observability, dynamic environments, and richer 3D representations (e.g., depth images, point clouds) is a natural next step.
- **Learning from Scratch:** Exploring methods to train the diffusion policies using reinforcement learning rather than expert demonstrations could broaden its applicability to scenarios without pre-collected data.

In conclusion, DiTree is a transformative framework that pushes the boundaries of kinodynamic motion planning. By judiciously combining the strengths of learning and search, it offers a robust, efficient, and generalizable solution with strong theoretical backing and demonstrated real-world applicability, paving the way for safer and more intelligent robotic systems.
