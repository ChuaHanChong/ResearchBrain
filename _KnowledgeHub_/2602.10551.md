# C^2ROPE: Causal Continuous Rotary Positional Encoding for 3D Large Multimodal-Models Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new positional encoding, C2RoPE, enhances the ability of 3D Large Multimodal Models to perceive and reason about visual information by explicitly modeling spatio-temporal and causal relationships within visual tokens. Applied to the LLaVA-3D baseline, it boosted performance on the ScanQA benchmark with an +18.1 CIDEr score improvement and achieved a +1.2 EM@1 gain on SQA3D.

## Problem

- Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), designed for 1D text sequences, poorly handles the multi-dimensional spatial and causal relationships inherent in visual data, particularly in 3D Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).
- RoPE's raster-scan indexing for visual tokens leads to 'spatial locality loss' by assigning numerically distant positional indices to spatially adjacent tokens along vertical axes.
- The inherent temporal-causality assumption in RoPE causes 'visual tokens neglect,' where attention progressively weakens for earlier visual tokens in extended sequences, hindering comprehensive 3D scene understanding.

## Method

- ==C2RoPE== (Causal Continuous Rotary Positional Encoding) is proposed to explicitly model spatio-temporal and causal relationships for visual tokens, integrating into the LLaVA-3D-7B architecture.
- A ==spatio-temporal continuous positional embedding== mechanism combines 1D temporal indices with 2D Cartesian spatial coordinates (x, y) to form a hybrid positional index for each visual token, addressing spatial locality loss through a tailored frequency allocation strategy.
- ==Chebyshev Causal Masking== redefines causal dependencies based on spatial (Chebyshev) distance from an image's center, replacing temporal-based masking to mitigate visual token neglect and long-term attention decay.

## Results

- C2RoPE improved the LLaVA-3D baseline on the ScanQA benchmark, achieving **+4.3** EM@1, **+8.5** BLEU-4, **+13.4** METEOR, **+2.5** ROUGE, and **+18.1** CIDEr, reaching state-of-the-art among compared 3D LMMs.
- On the SQA3D benchmark (test set), C2RoPE enhanced LLaVA-3D by **+1.2** EM@1 and **+1.2** EM@R.
- Ablation studies confirmed C2RoPE's superior performance over other multimodal positional encoding methods like CCA and MCA on SQA3D validation and test subsets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct application of 1D-sequence-centric RoPE to 2D/3D visual data fundamentally limits LMMs' perceptual capabilities, causing issues like spatial locality loss and visual token neglect.
> - Explicitly integrating 2D spatial coordinates with 1D temporal information, using a strategic frequency allocation, is effective in preserving spatial continuity within visual token representations.
> - Defining causality based on spatial relationships (e.g., Chebyshev distance) rather than pure temporal distance better reflects visual information dependencies, enabling more comprehensive attention to spatially relevant visual tokens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of 3D Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) is an emerging research direction, aiming to integrate the generative and reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with 3D scene understanding. These models hold potential for applications in autonomous robotics, navigation systems, and human-robot interaction.

The dominant paradigm in both 2D and 3D LMMs involves aligning visual features with LLM representations. Many 3D LMMs adapt architectural components and mechanisms originally designed for natural language processing. One such inherited component is Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), a core method for positional modeling in LLMs. While effective for 1D text sequences, its direct application to visual data has been observed to introduce limitations.

Prior research on 2D LMMs and video LMMs has identified and addressed certain limitations of RoPE, such as long-range decay in attention or issues with multi-dimensional information encoding. Methods like CCA, MCA, HoPE, CoMemo, MiniCPM-V, VideoRoPE, and M-RoPE have proposed variants or adjustments to RoPE for image or video contexts. However, the specific implications and limitations of RoPE within the context of 3D LMMs, particularly those relying on 2D image tokens enhanced with 3D information, have remained less explored.

This paper contributes to the field by providing an in-depth analysis of the specific limitations that RoPE introduces when used in 3D LMMs. It then proposes a novel positional encoding method, C2RoPE, designed to address these limitations by explicitly modeling the spatio-temporal and causal relationships inherent in 3D visual data. This work aims to enhance the perceptual and reasoning capabilities of 3D LMMs, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and effective systems for understanding complex 3D environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify and address limitations of Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) when applied to 3D Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), which largely inherit their positional encoding mechanisms from Large Language Models (LLMs). The motivation stems from observations that RoPE, originally designed for 1D temporal sequences in text, does not optimally handle the inherent spatial and causal relationships within visual data, particularly in 3D contexts.

The authors identify two specific limitations:

1. **Spatial Locality Loss**: RoPE assigns one-dimensional temporal positional indices to visual tokens, typically following a raster-scan order (e.g., left to right, top to bottom across an image). This indexing scheme implicitly preserves continuity along the row dimension but disrupts it along the column dimension. Consequently, spatially adjacent image tokens along vertical axes are assigned numerically distant positional indices, which the paper refers to as "spatial locality loss" (illustrated in Figure 1a). This loss implies that the model may not effectively capture local spatial relationships essential for visual understanding.

2. **Visual Tokens Neglect**: RoPE's design assumes that temporally closer tokens are more causally related. This inductive bias leads to a "long-term decay" in attention allocation, where attention strength decreases as the temporal distance between tokens increases. When applied to visual tokens, especially in 3D LMMs that use multi-view images, this decay can cause the model to progressively neglect earlier visual tokens in the sequence. The authors demonstrate this through information flow analysis (Figures 1b and 1c), showing that only a small subset of visual tokens, typically those temporally close to the instruction tokens or the output, receive high attention, while the majority contribute sparsely. This "visual tokens neglect" is exacerbated by the extended sequence lengths resulting from multi-view image inputs, potentially hindering comprehensive 3D scene understanding.

Based on these identified issues, the key objectives of this work are:
- To develop a novel positional encoding method, C2RoPE, that explicitly models local spatial continuity for visual tokens.
- To integrate 1D temporal positions with Cartesian-based spatial coordinates to construct a hybrid positional index.
- To devise a frequency allocation strategy that encodes spatio-temporal positional information across the components of this hybrid index.
- To introduce a causal masking mechanism, Chebyshev Causal Masking, that defines causal dependencies based on spatial relationships (Chebyshev distance) to mitigate the long-term decay and visual token neglect.
- To validate the effectiveness of C2RoPE through quantitative evaluation on established 3D scene reasoning and 3D visual question answering benchmarks.

These objectives collectively aim to enhance the ability of 3D LMMs to perceive and reason about complex visual information by adapting positional encoding to better suit the multi-dimensional nature of visual data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, C2RoPE, aims to improve visual information perception in 3D LMMs by addressing the identified limitations of RoPE. It is integrated into the LLaVA-3D-7B architecture as the baseline. C2RoPE consists of two main components: a spatio-temporal continuous positional embedding mechanism and Chebyshev Causal Masking.

**A. Spatio-temporal Continuous Positional Embedding Mechanism**
This mechanism is designed to mitigate spatial locality loss by explicitly modeling both 1D temporal and 2D spatial positions of visual tokens.

1. **Hybrid Image Positional Indexing**:
 - The 2D image tokens, which are typically flattened into a 1D sequence in RoPE, are first projected onto a 2D Cartesian coordinate system.
 - The center of the image is defined as the origin (0,0). The x and y axes are aligned with the rows and columns, respectively, with coordinates incrementing by 1 in positive directions. This assigns spatial coordinates (x, y) to each image token.
 - These (x, y) coordinates are then combined with the original 1D temporal index (`m`) from RoPE to form a triplet hybrid positional index `(m, x, y)` for each image token.
 - The retention of the `m` component ensures compatibility with the 1D positional indices of text tokens and maintains consistency with original RoPE for relative position calculations, requiring no additional processing for text tokens.

2. **Frequency Allocation Strategy**:
 - The triplet hybrid index `(m, x, y)` needs to be encoded into the rotation matrix dimensions (e.g., d=128).
 - In C2RoPE, the dimensions of the rotation matrix are allocated as follows:
 - The last 32 dimensions (corresponding to lower frequencies, which are highly sensitive to variations) are used to interleave the `x` and `y` spatial coordinates.
 - The remaining 96 dimensions (corresponding to higher frequencies) are assigned to the temporal component `m`.
 - This allocation strategy is based on two considerations: (1) Assigning lower frequencies to `x` and `y` avoids over-emphasizing subtle spatial changes, which could disrupt the LLM's pre-trained temporal positional dependencies. (2) Maintaining a broad range of higher-frequency bands for `m` helps preserve the "massive values pattern" in query and key tokens, a property of RoPE crucial for capturing contextual semantics.
 - This approach differs from prior methods like M-RoPE and VideoRoPE, which define temporal components at the image or frame level, whereas C2RoPE defines it at the token level within a single image.

**B. Chebyshev Causal Masking**
This component addresses the visual tokens neglect problem and the long-term decay caused by RoPE's temporal-causality assumption.

1. **Spatial Causal Relationship**:
 - The method leverages the observation that visual information exhibits inherent 2D spatial structure, implying that neighboring image tokens are typically more causally related than distant ones.
 - Instead of temporal distance, causal correlation between visual tokens is determined by their Chebyshev distance from a reference point.
 - The origin of the Cartesian coordinate system (image center) is set as this reference point.
 - The principle is that the farther a token is from this origin (i.e., higher Chebyshev distance), the stronger its attention decay.
 - Tokens sharing the same Chebyshev distance are grouped, implying a correlated attention within visual self-attention.

2. **Application**:
 - This spatially-aware causal masking is applied to the self-attention matrix during the decoding phase.
 - It modifies the default causal attention masking, which is based solely on temporal position, to incorporate spatial causality. This aims to alleviate the neglect of visual tokens that are temporally distant but spatially relevant.

**Implementation Details**:
The experiments use LLaVA-3D-7B as the baseline. The training follows a two-stage process. Multi-view inputs (16 views) are used, with each image encoded into 576 2D patches, resulting in 9,216 patches in total. The same masking strategy and frequency allocation policy are applied uniformly to all views.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of C2RoPE was evaluated through experiments on 3D scene reasoning and 3D visual question answering benchmarks, using LLaVA-3D as the baseline model.

**A. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods**

The performance of C2RoPE was compared against LLaVA-3D, other expert 3D models, and 2D LLMs on the ScanQA and SQA3D benchmarks.

- **ScanQA Benchmark**:
 - C2RoPE demonstrated improvements across all evaluation metrics compared to the LLaVA-3D baseline.
 - LLaVA-3D: EM@1 (Exact Match @1) = 27.0, B-4 (BLEU-4) = 14.5, MET (METEOR) = 20.7, RGE (ROUGE) = 50.1, CIDEr = 91.7.
 - C2RoPE: EM@1 = 31.3 (an improvement of +4.3), B-4 = 23.0 (+8.5), MET = 34.1 (+13.4), RGE = 52.6 (+2.5), CIDEr = 109.8 (+18.1).
 - The C2RoPE-enhanced LLaVA-3D achieved performance superior to several specialized expert models (e.g., 3D-VLP, 3D-VisTA) and significantly outperformed 2D LLMs (e.g., InternVl2-8B, Qwen2-Vl-7B, LLaVA-Video-7B) on these 3D tasks. It also achieved state-of-the-art performance among the compared 3D LMMs on ScanQA.

- **SQA3D Benchmark (test set)**:
 - C2RoPE also showed improvements on this benchmark.
 - LLaVA-3D: EM@1 = 55.6, EM@R (Refined EM@1) = 53.1.
 - C2RoPE: EM@1 = 56.8 (+1.2), EM@R = 54.3 (+1.2).
 - The authors note that LLaVA-3D, by embedding point cloud features into 2D image tokens, may lack the richness of 3D information required for complex spatial reasoning in intricate scenes compared to some specialized 3D LMMs (e.g., ChatScene, Ross3D) that might process 3D information differently or more directly. While C2RoPE enhanced the LLaVA-3D baseline, its performance on SQA3D remained suboptimal compared to these few specialized models, as C2RoPE primarily improves image perception within LLaVA-3D's existing 3D information modeling paradigm.

**B. Ablation Study**

An ablation study was conducted on SQA3D subsets to compare C2RoPE with other multimodal image positional encoding methods, CCA and MCA, which aim to mitigate long-term decay by redirecting attention to central image regions via heuristic 1D positional indices.

- **Quantitative Results (Table II)**:
 - LLaVA-3D baseline: EM@1 (test) = 55.6, EM@R (test) = 53.1; EM@1 (val) = 51.5, EM@R (val) = 54.5.
 - LLaVA-3D + MCA: EM@1 (test) = 55.5, EM@R (test) = 53.7; EM@1 (val) = 50.9, EM@R (val) = 54.7.
 - LLaVA-3D + CCA: EM@1 (test) = 56.2, EM@R (test) = 53.9; EM@1 (val) = 50.9, EM@R (val) = 54.4.
 - LLaVA-3D + C2RoPE: EM@1 (test) = 56.8, EM@R (test) = 54.3; EM@1 (val) = 51.6, EM@R (val) = 55.7.
 - C2RoPE consistently achieved the best performance across both the validation and test subsets of SQA3D, indicating its effectiveness compared to alternative positional encoding modifications.

**C. Case Study**

A qualitative case study on SQA3D (Figure 5) demonstrated that for an identical instruction and multi-view scene images, the baseline LLaVA-3D generated a response containing hallucinated content. In contrast, C2RoPE produced a correct response, suggesting an improvement in accurately perceiving and interpreting visual information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and improvement of 3D Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by rigorously analyzing the impact of inherited Large Language Model (LLM) mechanisms on visual processing.

The significance of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Identified Fundamental Limitations**: The paper provides an in-depth analysis of specific issues arising from the application of Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) in 3D LMMs: spatial locality loss and visual token neglect. These identified limitations highlight a mismatch between the 1D temporal bias of RoPE and the multi-dimensional nature of visual information. This contributes to a deeper understanding of how inherent architectural choices in LLMs can affect their multimodal performance.

- **Novel Methodological Advance**: C2RoPE introduces a novel approach to positional encoding for visual tokens within LMMs. Its spatio-temporal continuous positional embedding mechanism, which combines 1D temporal indices with 2D Cartesian spatial coordinates, explicitly addresses the spatial locality loss. The frequency allocation strategy further refines this by judiciously distributing encoding dimensions. Furthermore, the Chebyshev Causal Masking represents a significant conceptual shift, moving from a purely temporal understanding of causality to one that incorporates spatial relationships, thereby mitigating the problem of visual token neglect.

- **Validated Performance Improvement**: The comprehensive experiments on ScanQA and SQA3D benchmarks demonstrate consistent and notable performance improvements over the LLaVA-3D baseline. The quantitative gains across various metrics (EM@1, BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE, CIDEr) support the efficacy of the proposed designs. The ablation studies further confirm that C2RoPE outperforms other RoPE variants adapted for multimodal contexts.

- **Broader Implications for LMM Design**: The findings and methodology presented could have broader implications for the design of future LMMs that process structured visual data, including static images, video sequences, and 3D point clouds. The principle of differentiating and explicitly modeling temporal and spatial causality, as well as the thoughtful allocation of frequency bands in positional embeddings, may be generalizable to other multimodal architectures.

- **Potential Impact on Applications**: By enhancing the 3D scene understanding and reasoning capabilities of LMMs, C2RoPE contributes to advancing the core technologies for various applications. These include autonomous robotics, where accurate spatial reasoning is critical for navigation and manipulation; advanced human-robot interaction systems requiring robust environmental perception; and other AI systems that need to interpret and interact with complex 3D environments. The ability to reduce visual hallucinations and improve perception, as demonstrated in the case study, is particularly important for deployment in real-world scenarios.
