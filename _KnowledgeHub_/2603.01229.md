# RMBench: Memory-Dependent Robotic Manipulation Benchmark with Insights into Policy Design

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MMLab@HKU, PKU, and collaborators developed RMBench, a benchmark for memory-dependent robotic manipulation, and Mem-0, a modular memory-oriented policy. This work established a principled metric for task memory complexity and demonstrated improved success rates on non-Markovian tasks in both simulation and real-world settings.

## Problem

- Most existing robotic manipulation policies assume a Markovian decision process, relying only on recent observations and struggling with tasks requiring extensive memory.
- Many real-world manipulation tasks are inherently non-Markovian, necessitating the retention and utilization of past observations over extended temporal horizons.
- A systematic experimental platform and standardized benchmark for evaluating and analyzing memory-dependent robotic policies have been absent, limiting targeted research.

## Method

- Introduced Task Memory Complexity (TMC), a principled metric to quantify the minimum amount of past information required for optimal decision-making in manipulation tasks.
- Developed **RMBench**, a comprehensive simulation benchmark comprising nine memory-dependent dual-arm tasks categorized by TMC, providing automated data synthesis and evaluation.
- Proposed Mem-0, a modular memory-oriented robotic policy with a dual-system architecture (Planning and Execution modules) and explicit memory components like key, anchor, and sliding memories.

## Results

- Mem-0 achieved substantial performance gains on RMBench, with an average success rate of **52.8%** on M(1) tasks (**38.4%** improvement) and **28.5%** on M(n) tasks (**21.2%** improvement) compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies showed that removing anchor memory led to a substantial reduction in success rates, and replacing the learned Subtask End Classifier with ground-truth signals improved M(n) task success from **28.5%** to **45.3%**.
- In real-world experiments on three tasks, Mem-0 achieved an average success rate of **22.50%**, outperforming ACT (**0.0%**) and Pi0.5 (**5.83%**), despite observed limitations in fine-grained manipulation precision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit memory mechanisms (anchor, sliding, and key memory) are crucial for addressing non-Markovian robotic manipulation tasks, enabling effective retention and retrieval of historical context.
> - The Subtask End Classifier is critical for coordinating high-level planning and low-level execution, but its current limitations in reliable termination detection can be a bottleneck for overall policy performance.
> - Persistent anchor memory is vital for maintaining context regarding a subtask's initial state, while sliding memory captures short-term temporal cues, both playing distinct roles in policy stability and effectiveness.
