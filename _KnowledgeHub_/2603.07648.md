# AtomicVLA: Unlocking the Potential of Atomic Skill Learning in Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> AtomicVLA is an end-to-end framework for robotic manipulation that integrates task planning and action execution through atomic skill abstraction and a Skill-Guided Mixture of Experts (SG-MoE) architecture. This approach demonstrated improved success rates on long-horizon tasks, achieved scalable continual learning, and mitigated catastrophic forgetting in both simulated and real-world robotic environments.

## Problem

- Existing Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limitations in structured task decomposition and planning for long-horizon, multi-step robotic tasks.
- Current VLA models rely on monolithic action decoders, leading to scalability issues, computational expense, and catastrophic forgetting when incrementally acquiring new skills.
- Decoupled planning and execution systems often result in suboptimal task coordination due to a lack of mutual awareness between high-level planners and low-level controllers.

## Method

- An end-to-end framework unifies task planning and action execution by adaptively switching between generating high-level language instructions ('think' mode) and low-level ==latent actions== ('act' mode).
- A ==Skill-Guided Mixture of Experts (SG-MoE)== architecture extends VLA foundation models, using ==atomic skill abstractions== to dynamically route inputs to specialized experts for precise action execution.
- Modular skill experts enable scalable ==continual learning== by adding new expert modules and extending the routing network for new skills without retraining existing components.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **96.6%** across four LIBERO task suites (**2.4%** improvement over baselines) and **95.2%** on LIBERO-LONG (**10%** improvement over π_0) in simulation.
- Outperformed baseline models by **20%** in average success rate on real-world long-horizon tasks, and maintained stable performance on previously learned tasks (only **1.3%** average decrease) during continual learning.
- Ablation studies showed the SG-MoE architecture significantly outperformed token-level MoE (by 6.6%) and denoising timestep-conditioned MoDE (by 5.7%) in terms of success rate, validating the effectiveness of skill-guided expert routing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decomposing complex tasks into semantically meaningful 'atomic skill abstractions' and dynamically activating specialized experts enhances task reliability and reduces interference among diverse skills.
> - A unified robot policy that can autonomously decide between high-level planning and fine-grained action generation avoids coordination issues common in decoupled systems.
> - A physics-informed trajectory-based atomic decomposition method, combined with VLM refinement, produces precise and interpretable annotations for robot actions, leading to higher quality training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "AtomicVLA" is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of robotic manipulation, particularly focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models represent an advancement from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), aiming to unify visual perception, language understanding, and direct action generation for robotic tasks. This paradigm has demonstrated progress in mapping diverse inputs to motor actions and generalizing to novel environments.

However, existing VLA models face recognized limitations, especially concerning complex real-world deployments. These limitations include challenges with long-horizon, multi-step problem-solving and the ability to generalize for continual skill acquisition. Current VLA models often employ monolithic action decoders trained on aggregated data, which can lead to issues with scalability, particularly when incrementally learning new skills. Such models may also suffer from interference among mixed skills and catastrophic forgetting when new skills are introduced, hindering lifelong learning capabilities.

The broader research landscape has seen various attempts to address these challenges:
- **Two-stage architectures:** Some approaches decouple high-level planning (often using VLMs) from low-level action execution (VLA-based controllers). This modularity, however, has been noted to potentially cause suboptimal task coordination due to a lack of mutual awareness between the planner and controller.
- **Integrated frameworks:** Other research explores unified models that jointly perform hierarchical reasoning and action generation to overcome the limitations of decoupled systems. AtomicVLA aligns with this direction by proposing a unified architecture.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures:** MoE has gained prominence in large language models for enhancing task specialization and representation capability through conditional computation. In robotics, MoE has been applied to address task heterogeneity, but often treats experts as interchangeable components. AtomicVLA reinterprets MoE through the lens of skill modularity, with each expert corresponding to a semantically meaningful action primitive.
- **Continual Learning:** Research in continual learning seeks to enable agents to adapt to new tasks without forgetting previously acquired skills. This involves methods like unsupervised skill discovery, hierarchical imitation learning, and latent action representations. Current VLA models, while focusing on generalizable skills, have limited dedicated investigations into scalable continual learning without single-decoder constraints.

AtomicVLA contributes to this landscape by proposing a framework that directly addresses the issues of scalability, long-horizon task execution, and continual learning in VLA models by introducing atomic skill abstraction and a dynamic expert composition mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome the scalability limitations and address the challenges of long-horizon and continual learning in existing Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation tasks. The specific motivations stem from several identified shortcomings:

1. **Inefficiency in Long-Horizon, Multi-Step Tasks:** Real-world robotic tasks frequently involve sequences of multiple steps and complex problem-solving over an extended duration. Existing VLA models, often trained on single actions or short-horizon skills, exhibit limitations in structured task decomposition and effective planning for such long-horizon tasks. This results in suboptimal performance and reduced reliability for real-world deployments.
2. **Lack of Scalability in Skill Acquisition:** Current VLA models typically rely on a monolithic action-decoding module. When new skills need to be acquired, this often necessitates fine-tuning the entire existing model, which is computationally expensive, requires substantial datasets, and can be inefficient. The authors note the scarcity of robot data, emphasizing the need for efficient leveraging of pretrained VLA model weights.
3. **Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Learning:** Incrementally learning new skills in existing VLA models frequently leads to catastrophic forgetting, where the acquisition of new knowledge degrades the performance on previously learned skills. This interference significantly hinders the ability of robots to continuously acquire and expand their skill repertoire over a lifetime of operation.
4. **Suboptimal Task Coordination in Modular Architectures:** While some approaches separate high-level planning from low-level execution, studies suggest that this modular decoupling can lead to a lack of mutual awareness between the planner and controller. This can result in misaligned decisions, the generation of outdated or irrelevant instructions, and compounding errors, thereby limiting overall task coordination.
5. **Ambiguity in Atomic Action Annotation:** Traditional methods for segmenting action sequences from video, such as those relying on Vision-Language Models for video understanding or optical flow, often produce ambiguous or noisy results that require extensive manual post-processing. A more precise and interpretable method for defining and annotating atomic actions is needed to support robust task planning.

To address these motivations, the authors propose AtomicVLA, a framework designed to:
- Enable both high-level task planning and fine-grained action generation within a unified architecture.
- Support scalable continual learning by modularizing skill experts.
- Mitigate catastrophic forgetting by allowing incremental skill acquisition without re-training existing experts.
- Provide a robust method for generating high-quality embodied reasoning data with precise atomic action annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AtomicVLA is presented as an end-to-end framework that unifies task planning and action execution by adaptively generating either natural language instructions or latent actions. The methodology comprises several interconnected components:

#### 3.1. Unified Task Planning and Action Execution

The framework designs a robot policy that integrates "thinking" (task planning) and "acting" (action execution) capabilities, with the ability to autonomously decide its output modality based on the current state.

- **Adaptive Thinking and Acting:** The model uses two special output tokens, `[think]` and `[act]`, to switch between modalities.
 - When `[think]` is predicted, the model enters a thinking mode. It takes multiple camera observations ($O_{1:n_t}$) and a language instruction ($\ell$) as input and outputs a high-level task plan ($C_{0-k}$), tracks current execution progress ($C_t$), and specifies an atomic skill abstraction ($\sigma$) in textual form. This mode is activated at key time steps, such as task initiation or transitions between sub-skills.
 - When `[act]` is predicted, the model switches to acting mode. It generates a low-level action chunk ($A_t$) conditioned on the robot's proprioceptive state ($S_t$) and the most recent atomic skill abstraction ($\sigma$).
- **End-to-end Framework:** This unified architecture aims to avoid the coordination issues associated with decoupled planner-controller systems by allowing the VLM to simultaneously perform high-level task planning and atomic action abstraction, directly guiding specialized action experts.

#### 3.2. Skill-Guided Mixture of Experts (SG-MoE) Architecture

AtomicVLA extends a VLA foundation model (specifically $\pi_0$ or $\pi_{0.5}$) with an atomic action abstraction-guided Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture to construct a scalable atomic skill library.

- **Atomic Skill Abstract Embedding:** Each atomic skill abstraction is mapped to a scalar "noise level" ($\sigma \in$), inspired by diffusion models. This scalar is then embedded into a high-dimensional vector $Z_\sigma = E(\text{norm}(\text{log}(\sigma)))$. This continuous and structured embedding space aims to facilitate semantic separation among skills and enable robust routing to corresponding skill-specific experts.
- **SG-MoE Components:** The skill library consists of:
 1. **Skill Router:** Responsible for selecting the appropriate expert.
 2. **Shared Expert:** Retains the pretrained action generation capabilities of the base VLA model (e.g., $\pi_0$).
 3. **Multiple Atomic Skill Experts:** Each expert specializes in executing a distinct atomic skill.
- **Skill-Guided Dynamic Routing:**
 - An atomic action abstraction is derived from high-level task instructions and environmental observations via the thinking pipeline.
 - This abstraction is deterministically mapped to a fixed high-dimensional embedding $Z_\sigma$.
 - The skill router computes a probability distribution over $K$ experts ($w_k = \text{Router}(Z_\sigma)$).
 - A sparse activation strategy is employed, where only the top-scoring expert is selected.
 - The final action chunk $A_t$ is a weighted combination of the shared expert and the selected atomic expert: $F_{out} = (1 - w_k) \cdot F_{\text{share}}(x_t) + w_k \cdot F_k(x_t)$, where $x_t$ is the current multimodal input. This mechanism allows for fine-grained execution of specific skills while retaining the generalization capability of the base model.

#### 3.3. Continual Learning with Skill Expansion

To enable continuous adaptation to new tasks and prevent catastrophic forgetting, AtomicVLA utilizes a modular skill-expert mechanism.

- **Modular Skill Experts:** Each atomic skill corresponds to a dedicated expert module. When a new atomic skill is introduced, a new expert module is added to the architecture, and the routing network is extended.
- **Initialization Strategy:** The expanded router is initialized by copying weights from the original router, while the new routing branch is initialized with small random values. This strategy aims to minimize fine-tuning requirements and preserve the performance of previously acquired skills. This design allows for efficient, interference-free expansion of the atomic skill library.

#### 3.4. Task Planning Embodied Data Generation

To acquire accurate and reliable annotations of atomic actions, the authors propose a trajectory-based atomic decomposition method.

- **Principal-Axis Analysis:** This method analyzes the kinematic dimensions of the end-effector trajectory (translational displacements, rotational changes, and gripper states) to segment atomic actions. Dominant modes of motion and gripper state transitions are identified to classify actions (e.g., "pick," "turn"). This physics-informed decomposition aims to produce temporally precise and semantically interpretable boundaries, reducing reliance on manual refinement.
- **Semantic Label Refinement with InternVideo2.5:** The decomposed atomic action segments are further refined and validated using the InternVideo2.5 model, which interprets corresponding video clips. This automatic correction and enrichment process aligns labels with the full trajectory, constructing a structured reasoning chain of executed atomic actions and associated high-level plans. This aims to improve annotation fidelity and provide interpretable, step-by-step execution guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments to evaluate AtomicVLA.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **LIBERO Benchmark:**
 - AtomicVLA achieved an average success rate of 96.6% across four task suites, demonstrating an improvement of 2.4% over baseline models.
 - On the LIBERO-LONG suite, which involves longer-horizon tasks, AtomicVLA attained a success rate of 95.2%, representing a 10% improvement over the $\pi_0$ baseline.
 - AtomicVLA* (built on $\pi_{0.5}$) further improved performance, reaching an average success rate of 97.8% and 96.2% on LIBERO-LONG.
 - The framework demonstrated error recovery capabilities; when an execution failure occurred (e.g., a dropped object), AtomicVLA could detect the anomaly, regenerate a new atomic skill abstraction, and attempt recovery.
- **CALVIN Benchmark (ABC-D split):**
 - AtomicVLA achieved an average task length of 4.09, outperforming the $\pi_0$ baseline by 0.22.
 - AtomicVLA* achieved an average task length of 4.27, outperforming the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline by 0.25.
 - AtomicVLA* showed relative improvements of 5.8%, 6.2%, and 6.6% on the last three stages of the evaluation sequence, indicating effectiveness in temporally extended and sequential manipulation tasks.
 - The paper noted that successful recoveries after failures were not considered valid completions under CALVIN's evaluation protocol, potentially underestimating the model's true capability. Performance on several "Push blocks right" tasks was notably lower (20-30% success rate) due to distribution shifts between training and evaluation object placements.

#### 4.2. Real-World Robot Results (Franka Robotic Arm)

- **Long-Horizon Tasks:**
 - In mixed training across three long-horizon tasks (Objects in plate, Object into drawer, Object into microwave), AtomicVLA and AtomicVLA* outperformed baseline models by 20% and 18.3%, respectively, in average success rate.
 - AtomicVLA* demonstrated robustness in tasks involving door-closing operations, reliably completing configurations where $\pi_{0.5}$ failed.
 - The authors observed that AtomicVLA effectively mitigated cross-task interference in mixed training scenarios, particularly where tasks involved significant changes in gripper states.
- **Continual Learning Skills:**
 - An experiment was conducted where "open" was introduced as a new skill after initial training on four other short-horizon tasks.
 - Baseline $\pi_{0.5}$ experienced an average success rate decrease of approximately 15% on previously learned tasks (e.g., a 20% decrease on the "stack" task), exhibiting catastrophic forgetting and skill interference.
 - AtomicVLA* maintained stable performance after continual learning, with only a 1.3% average decrease on base tasks, indicating that previously learned skills remained largely unaffected.
 - AtomicVLA* also acquired the new "open" skill more efficiently, achieving an overall improvement of 21% across all five tasks compared to $\pi_{0.5}$ under the same training steps.
 - Real-world error recovery capabilities were further illustrated, showing the system's ability to replan and correct behavior upon subtask failure.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- Ablation experiments on LIBERO-LONG compared AtomicVLA (SG-MoE) against a non-MoE $\pi_0$ baseline, a standard token-level MoE, and a timestep-conditioned MoDE variant.
- AtomicVLA achieved a 95.2% success rate, outperforming the token-level MoE by 6.6% and the MoDE variant by 5.7%.
- This suggested that routing experts based on semantically meaningful atomic skill abstractions (SG-MoE) leads to more coherent and efficient skill execution by ensuring experts specialize in distinct skills, reducing interference compared to token-level or denoising timestep-based routing.

#### 4.4. Parameter and Inference-Time

- AtomicVLA demonstrated practical inference latency: with 12 experts, the inference time on a single H20 GPU was 160 ms, indicating feasibility for real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of AtomicVLA carries several implications for the advancement of robotic manipulation and intelligent agents:

- **Enhanced Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:** By explicitly decomposing complex tasks into atomic skill abstractions and dynamically activating specialized experts, AtomicVLA demonstrates improved reliability and success rates for multi-step, long-horizon tasks in both simulated and real-world environments. This is a crucial step towards deploying robots in more complex, unstructured settings where sequential reasoning is essential.
- **Scalable Continual Learning:** The modular Skill-Guided Mixture-of-Experts (SG-MoE) architecture, coupled with the extensible routing encoder, provides a mechanism for robots to continually acquire new skills without experiencing significant catastrophic forgetting or severe interference with existing capabilities. This addresses a major limitation in lifelong learning for robotics, where constantly adapting to new operational requirements is paramount. The ability to add new experts and extend the router with minimal re-training can reduce computational costs and data requirements for skill expansion.
- **Mitigation of Skill Interference:** The explicit association of atomic skills with dedicated experts effectively reduces mutual interference that can arise during mixed training of heterogeneous tasks. This allows for more robust multi-task learning, particularly in scenarios involving diverse action types (e.g., grasping, turning, opening, closing) that might otherwise conflict in a monolithic architecture.
- **Unified Planning and Execution:** The end-to-end framework, which adaptively switches between "thinking" for high-level planning and "acting" for fine-grained control, represents an advancement in integrating these two modalities. This unified approach aims to improve coordination and reduce misalignments that can occur in modular, decoupled systems.
- **Improved Data Annotation and Reasoning:** The proposed trajectory-based atomic decomposition method, grounded in principal-axis analysis and refined by VLM interpretation, offers a more precise and semantically interpretable approach to annotating atomic actions. This can lead to higher-quality training data for VLA models, fostering better understanding and execution of elemental robot behaviors.

**Potential Impact:** The AtomicVLA framework has the potential to accelerate the development of more versatile, adaptable, and robust general-purpose robots. By enabling efficient continual learning and effective execution of complex, multi-stage tasks, it moves towards robotic systems that can operate effectively in dynamic, open-world environments. This could impact applications in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and service robotics, where adaptability and long-term operational capabilities are highly valued.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Dependence on VLM Fidelity:** The effectiveness of AtomicVLA relies on the VLM's ability to produce accurate atomic skill abstractions and task plans. Future work could explore integrating larger, more capable VLMs or advanced embodied reasoning models to enhance planning fidelity and the quality of the expert skill library.
- **Data Collection for New Skills:** While mitigating forgetting, acquiring new tasks still requires substantial human demonstration data for imitation learning. The authors suggest integrating pre-trained skill expert libraries with reinforcement learning (RL) to enable generalization to novel tasks under few-shot or zero-shot settings, potentially reducing the reliance on extensive human demonstrations.
