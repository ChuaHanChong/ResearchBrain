# DynaMem: Online Dynamic Spatio-Semantic Memory for Open World Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DynaMem introduces an online dynamic spatio-semantic memory architecture enabling open-vocabulary mobile manipulation in changing real-world environments. The system achieved a 70% pick-and-drop success rate on non-stationary objects, representing a more than 2x improvement over static state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Existing open-vocabulary mobile manipulation (OVMM) systems fundamentally rely on the assumption of static environments, severely limiting their applicability in dynamic real-world settings.
- Current robot memory structures lack the ability to adapt online by both adding new information and explicitly removing outdated data when objects move, appear, or disappear.
- Limitations within individual modules, such as navigation systems built on static maps, propagate and degrade the overall performance of modular OVMM architectures.

## Method

![[2411.04999-method.png]]
*An illustration of how our online dynamic spatio-semantic memory DynaMem responds to open vocabulary queries in a dynamic environment. During operation and exploration, DynaMem keeps updating its semantic map in memory. DynaMem maintains a voxelized pointcloud representation of the environment, and updates with dynamic changes in the environment by adding and removing points.*

- **DynaMem** introduces a novel dynamic 3D voxel memory that continuously updates its understanding of the environment by supporting both the online addition and removal of objects and obstacles.
- It employs a ray-casting mechanism to detect when existing voxels should be visible but are not, inferring that objects have moved or been removed and purging outdated information.
- A robust, two-stage querying process combines VLM-feature-based semantic similarity with Multimodal Large Language Model (mLLM-QA) for precise object localization, explicitly reporting if an object is 'not found' to prevent false positives.

## Results

- In real-world experiments with Stretch SE3 robots, DynaMem achieved a **70%** pick-and-drop success rate for non-stationary objects, demonstrating a more than **2x** improvement over static baselines (**30%** success rate).
- DynaMem drastically reduced failures due to inability to locate dynamic objects to **6.7%**, compared to **53.3%** for static systems, validating its core capability for dynamic environments.
- On the new DynaBench offline benchmark, a hybrid querying method (VLM-feature-based filtering followed by mLLM-QA) achieved the highest performance at **74.5%** accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enabling a memory system to dynamically add and remove object information online is crucial for adapting to real-world environment changes, such as moving objects, which is a major bottleneck for mobile manipulation.
> - A hybrid approach leveraging both VLM-feature similarity and mLLM-QA queries offers complementary strengths, leading to more robust and accurate open-vocabulary object localization than either method alone.
> - Explicitly handling and reporting when an queried object is not found in the environment prevents incorrect or wasted robot actions, enhancing the system's reliability in dynamic scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in the field of **Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM)**: operating in dynamic, ever-changing real-world environments.

**Current State of OVMM:** Recent years have seen significant strides in OVMM, allowing robots to understand natural language commands (e.g., "Pick up X from Y and put it in Z") and perform tasks in novel, unknown environments. The landscape is primarily divided into two approaches:
1. **Policies trained in simulation:** These methods (e.g., [2–4]) aim to transfer learning from simulated environments to the real world.
2. **Modular systems:** These combine open-vocabulary navigation (OVN) [5–8] with various robot manipulation skills [9–13]. Modular systems, particularly those leveraging advances in vision-language models (VLMs) and large language models (LLMs), have shown greater efficiency and success in real-world deployments due to their ability to generalize to diverse environments without extensive retraining.

**The Critical Gap Addressed by DynaMem:** A fundamental limitation across almost all existing OVMM and OVN systems (e.g., [1, 5–13]), regardless of their specific approach, is the **assumption of static environments**. These systems typically build a map of the environment and then operate under the premise that objects and obstacles remain in fixed locations. This assumption severely restricts their applicability in real-world settings, where environments are frequently altered by human activity or even the robot's own actions. The paper identifies this as a "primary challenge" where "limitations of a module propagate to the entire system." The absence of an effective, online-adaptable dynamic spatio-semantic memory is pinpointed as the root cause.

**DynaMem's Contribution to the Landscape:** DynaMem directly tackles this limitation by introducing a novel **dynamic spatio-semantic memory architecture**. It allows robots to continuously update their understanding of the environment, not just by adding new information but, crucially, by detecting and removing outdated information as objects move, appear, or disappear. This enables true "open-world" operation, where the robot's internal representation of the world evolves with the physical world.

**Connections to Related Fields:**
- **Spatio-semantic Memory:** DynaMem builds upon a rich history of spatio-semantic memory research [30–34], moving beyond limited category-specific semantic maps. More recent work [6, 7, 35–40] has integrated features from pre-trained VLMs (like CLIP), often using voxel maps or neural feature fields. DynaMem extends this by making the voxel-based memory *dynamic*, supporting both object addition and removal, a capability that distinguishes it from previous approaches focused mainly on pre-mapped scenes or static environments. It implicitly handles object changes at a voxel level, contrasting with more abstract, object-level scene graph approaches like DovSG.
- **SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping):** While SLAM is foundational for robot navigation, most practical SLAM methods (e.g., based on voxels, objects, NeRF, or Gaussian splats) still largely assume static environments. Although some sparse SLAM methods and approaches modeling moving objects [57–68] exist, DynaMem's focus is on building a *semantic memory* layer that can interpret and adapt to changes, relying on a stable underlying SLAM system but not replacing it.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (mLLMs):** DynaMem heavily leverages state-of-the-art foundation models. It uses VLMs like CLIP and SigLIP for embedding semantic features into its voxel map and mLLMs like GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro for complex open-vocabulary queries. The integration of these powerful models allows for flexible and open-ended object recognition and localization, which is a hallmark of modern embodied AI research.

**Novelty Claim:** The authors state, "To the best of our knowledge, DynaMem is the first spatio-semantic memory structure supporting both adding and removing objects." This claim highlights its unique position in the research landscape, directly addressing a gap that significantly hinders real-world robot deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable **open-vocabulary mobile manipulation (OVMM) in real-world, dynamic environments**, overcoming the limitations of current systems that assume static surroundings.

The authors clearly articulate their motivation and specific objectives through a "Problem Statement" in Section 3.1:

**Overarching Motivation:**
- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** Current OVMM systems, despite significant progress, are fundamentally limited by their reliance on the assumption of static environments. In contrast, real-world settings are inherently dynamic, with objects frequently moved, added, or removed by humans or the robot itself. This disconnect severely restricts the practical applicability and deployment of robots in homes, offices, and other human-centric spaces.
- **Addressing Module Limitations:** The paper emphasizes that in modular OVMM systems, the limitations of individual components (such as the navigation module's reliance on static maps) propagate and degrade the performance of the entire system. A robust, dynamic memory is essential to elevate the capabilities of the overall robotic stack.
- **Lack of Dynamic Memory Solutions:** A key motivation is the absence of an effective dynamic spatio-semantic memory that can adapt *online* to both the *addition and removal* of objects and obstacles. Existing memory structures are largely designed for static scenes or only handle new information without purging outdated data.

**Key Objectives for DynaMem:** To address the above motivations, DynaMem is designed to meet the following requirements for online, dynamic operation:

1. **Online Map Construction from Observations:** The robot must be able to build its environmental map in real-time by exploring and continuously observing posed RGB-D images, without any prior access to an existing map of the environment.
2. **Adaptation to Environment Dynamism:** The memory module must be capable of adapting to changes in the environment, including the movement, appearance, or disappearance of objects and obstacles, without prior knowledge of these changes. It needs to resolve contradictions arising from past and present observations.
3. **Robust Localization Queries:** Given a natural language query (e.g., "teddy bear"), the memory module must accurately return the 3D location of the object. Crucially, it must also be able to determine and explicitly state if the object is *not* present in the observed scene, rather than returning a "best guess" which can lead to false positives.
4. **Dynamic Obstacle Map Generation:** The memory module must continuously maintain an accurate representation of obstacles in space, adapting as objects move. This dynamic obstacle map is essential for safe and efficient navigation.

In essence, the core objective is to move beyond "static-world robotics" to "open-world robotics," where a robot's understanding of its environment is as fluid and adaptable as the environment itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DynaMem's methodology revolves around a novel **Dynamic 3D Voxel Memory** and sophisticated querying mechanisms to operate in dynamic environments.

#### 3.1. Dynamic 3D Voxel Map (DynaMem Architecture)

DynaMem maintains an *evolving sparse voxel map*. Each non-empty voxel stores critical information:
- **3D Location:** (x, y, z) coordinates in a global reference frame.
- **Observation Count (C):** How many times this voxel has been observed.
- **Source Image ID (I):** The ID of the latest image from which this voxel was backprojected.
- **Semantic Feature Vector (f):** A high-dimensional vector derived from a Vision-Language Model (VLM), representing the semantic content of the voxel.
- **Latest Observation Time (t):** The timestamp of the most recent observation contributing to the voxel.

##### 3.1.1. Updating DynaMem (Dynamism)

The key to DynaMem's dynamism lies in its mechanisms for adding and removing points:

- **Adding Points:**
 1. **Data Acquisition:** The robot captures new RGB-D images with global poses.
 2. **3D Reconstruction:** These images are converted to 3D points in the global frame using camera pose and depth information.
 3. **Semantic Feature Extraction:** Point-wise semantic features are generated. This involves using a segmentation model (e.g., SAM-v2) to identify object patches in the RGB images, followed by aggregating features from a VLM (e.g., CLIP or SigLIP) over these patches.
 4. **Voxel Assignment & Update:** New points are clustered and assigned to the nearest existing voxel grids. If a voxel is updated:
 - Its observation count (C) is incremented.
 - Its semantic feature vector (f) is updated as a weighted average of the old and new feature vectors.
 - Its latest observation time (t) and source image ID (I) are updated. If a voxel was previously empty, it's initialized.

- **Removing Points:** This is crucial for handling object movement or disappearance. DynaMem uses a *ray-casting* approach:
 1. **Principle:** If a voxel is within the camera's view frustum and *should* be visible (i.e., it's closer than the observed depth at its projected pixel location), but it is *not* present in the current point cloud, it implies the object at that voxel's location has moved or been removed.
 2. **Implementation:** Existing voxels are projected onto the current camera plane. A voxel is considered for removal if:
 - It falls within the camera's image bounds (Eq. 1).
 - Its 3D distance from the camera (`d`) is greater than 0, less than 2 meters (to mitigate depth noise), and critically, `d` is less than the observed depth `D[h,w]` at its projected pixel location `(h,w)` (Eq. 2). This `d < D[h,w]` condition identifies occluded or missing voxels.
 3. Voxels satisfying these conditions are then removed from the map or their metadata is updated to reflect their absence.

#### 3.2. Querying DynaMem for Object Localization

DynaMem implements a robust, two-stage process to answer natural language object localization queries, explicitly handling cases where the object is not found:

- **Stage 1: Finding Candidate Images:** Identify the most probable image(s) where the queried object might have been observed.
 - **Embedded Vision Language Features (VLM-feature based):**
 1. The language query (e.g., "red cup") is encoded into a latent embedding using a VLM's text encoder.
 2. The system finds the voxel(s) in DynaMem whose stored semantic feature vector (`f`) has the highest dot product similarity with the query embedding.
 3. The latest image ID (`I`) associated with this top-matching voxel is retrieved as a candidate. DBSCAN clustering can be used to return `k` candidate images from top `k` clusters.
 - **Multimodal Large Language Models (mLLM-QA based):**
 1. This approach frames object localization as a Visual Question Answering (VQA) problem.
 2. A sequence of recent (and not completely outdated) RGB images from the robot's observations (up to a model-specific context limit, e.g., 60 for Gemini 1.5 Pro) is fed to an mLLM (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro or GPT-4o) with a carefully crafted prompt.
 3. The mLLM is instructed to return the index of the latest image where the object was observed or "None" if not found.
 - **Hybrid Approach:** A combined method that leverages the strengths of both. The VoxelMap first identifies the top `k` candidate images using VLM-features. These `k` images are then passed to an mLLM for a more refined and robust localization decision. This method generalizes the individual approaches (k=1 for VLM-only, k->inf for mLLM-only).

- **Stage 2: Confirming Object Presence and Localization:**
 - The candidate image (or the chosen one from the hybrid approach) is then subjected to an **open-vocabulary object detector** (e.g., OWL-v2).
 - If OWL-v2 detects the queried object, its 3D location is determined from the median pixel of the object mask and returned.
 - Crucially, if OWL-v2 *fails to detect* the object, the system responds with "object not found" (∅), rather than a potentially incorrect best match. This is a significant improvement over prior work that often assumed object presence.

#### 3.3. Robot Navigation and Exploration

- **Dynamic Obstacle Map:** DynaMem generates a 2D obstacle map from its 3D voxel representation. Voxels above a certain height threshold (e.g., 0.2m) are projected as obstacles, while those below are considered navigable.
- **Exploration Primitives:** To build and update the map in unknown environments, DynaMem guides a frontier-based exploration using two value-based maps:
 - **Time-based value map (V_T):** Prioritizes exploring areas that have not been observed recently, helping to detect changes.
 - **Semantic-similarity-based value map (V_S):** Prioritizes exploring areas semantically similar to a current query, aiding in finding specific objects.
 - These can be linearly combined to balance exploration strategies.
- **Closed-loop Navigation:** To accommodate dynamics, the robot navigates in short, iterative steps (e.g., 0.7-1 meter). After each segment, it re-scans, updates DynaMem, and re-plans its path. This reactive approach ensures the navigation plan is always based on the most current environmental information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations across real-world environments and a newly developed offline benchmark, demonstrating the efficacy of DynaMem.

#### 4.1. Real-world Experiments (Stretch SE3 Robots)

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted in three distinct real-world lab environments (Kitchen, Game room, Meeting room, depicted in Figure 6). Each environment underwent three rounds of changes, and the robot executed 10 open-vocabulary pick-and-drop queries per environment, totaling 30 queries. The Hello Stretch SE3 robot, equipped with an Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera, was used. The full system leveraged AnyGrasp for grasping and a heuristic-based dropping system.
- **Baseline:** The static state-of-the-art OVMM system, OK-Robot (a previous work by some of the authors), was used as a comparative baseline to highlight the impact of dynamic memory.
- **Results (Figure 7a & b):**
 - **Overall Success:** DynaMem, using both VLM-feature-based and mLLM-QA-based queries, achieved an impressive **70% pick-and-drop success rate** on non-stationary objects.
 - **Improvement over Baseline:** This represents a significant **more than 2x improvement** over the static OK-Robot system, which only achieved a 30% success rate.
 - **Handling Dynamic Objects:** The most striking difference was in handling dynamic objects. OK-Robot failed in 53.3% of trials because it could not locate objects that had moved. In contrast, DynaMem experienced only 6.7% failures due to inability to navigate to dynamic objects, validating its core capability.
 - **Navigation to Static Goals:** For static goals, DynaMem with VLM-feature queries had a 10% failure rate, OK-Robot 13.3%, and DynaMem with mLLM-QA 20%. This suggests mLLM-QA, while powerful, might be slightly more prone to localization errors for static objects in real-world scenarios due to context window limitations or visual ambiguity.
- **Qualitative Failure Analysis (Figure 7a):**
 - **VLM-feature failures:** Often stemmed from poor localization due to semantic ambiguity (e.g., confusing "blue bowl" for "red bowl"), acting like a "bag-of-words" model.
 - **mLLM-QA failures:** Primarily due to the limited context window of mLLMs, causing objects to be missed if they were in outdated images or not within the provided image sequence.
 - The complementary nature of these failure modes suggests potential for a hybrid approach.

#### 4.2. Deployment in Home Environments

- **Setup:** DynaMem (using the hybrid querying method with k=3 images) was deployed in two real New York City apartments (one one-room, one two-room) for 17 long-horizon trials.
- **Results:** 9 successful trials out of 17.
- **Failure Modes (Figure 7c):** Analyzing failures provided crucial insights into real-world challenges:
 - **Navigation failures (4 out of 8 failures):** Half were due to objects not being found or confused with others; the other half were due to navigating to suboptimal locations for manipulation.
 - **Manipulation failures (3 out of 8 failures):** The robot's grasping or other manipulation skills failed.
 - **Placement failures (1 out of 8 failures):** Error in placing the object at the target destination.

#### 4.3. Ablations on an Offline Benchmark (DynaBench)

- **DynaBench:** A new offline benchmark was developed to efficiently evaluate dynamic 3D visual grounding algorithms. It comprises 9 dynamic environments (3 real-world robot-collected, 6 simulated iPhone Pro-collected), each with objects and obstacles changing across three rounds. Queries include positive (object present) and negative (object not yet observed or removed) cases. Evaluation measures 3D localization accuracy and correct "not found" responses.
- **Results (Table 1):**
 - **Human Baseline:** Average human performance was 81.9%.
 - **VLM-feature variants:** The default DynaMem (with adding and removing points) achieved 70.6%. Performance significantly dropped if only adding points (67.8%), or without OWL-v2 cross-checking (59.2%), confirming the importance of the dynamic update and verification mechanisms.
 - **mLLM-QA variants:** Gemini Pro 1.5 achieved 67.3%, outperforming Gemini Flash 1.5 (63.5%). VoxelMap-based image filtering marginally improved performance (67.3% vs. 66.8% without filtering).
 - **Hybrid Method (VLM-feature → mLLM, k=3):** This approach achieved the highest performance on DynaBench, at **74.5%**. This quantitatively confirms the real-world observation that the VLM-feature and mLLM-QA methods have complementary strengths, and their combination yields superior results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DynaMem represents a significant advancement in the field of mobile manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between controlled laboratory settings and the inherent unpredictability of real-world environments. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling True Open-World Mobile Manipulation:** The most critical contribution is overcoming the long-standing limitation of static environment assumptions in OVMM. By providing the first spatio-semantic memory structure that supports both the *online addition and removal* of objects and obstacles, DynaMem makes open-vocabulary robots viable in dynamic human-centric spaces. This is a foundational step towards general-purpose household or industrial robots.

2. **Substantial Performance Improvement:** The demonstrated >2x improvement in pick-and-drop success rates over state-of-the-art static baselines in real dynamic environments is a strong testament to DynaMem's practical utility. This performance gain, especially in handling non-stationary objects, directly translates to increased robustness and reliability for robotic tasks.

3. **Robust Object Localization and Absence Handling:** The two-stage querying process, combining the strengths of VLM-features and mLLMs, coupled with the explicit "object not found" response, significantly reduces false positives. This enhances the robot's intelligence by allowing it to reliably know when an object is genuinely absent or has moved, preventing wasteful or incorrect actions.

4. **Novel Exploration Strategies for Dynamic Environments:** The integration of value-based exploration (time-based and semantic-similarity-based) is crucial for robots operating in unknown and changing environments without prior maps. These strategies allow robots to intelligently prioritize areas for re-exploration or targeted search, improving efficiency and adaptability.

5. **Introduction of DynaBench – a Key Research Enabler:** The creation of DynaBench addresses a critical gap in the research community: the lack of dynamic benchmarks for evaluating 3D visual grounding algorithms. This offline benchmark provides a standardized, efficient, and reproducible means for researchers to test and compare dynamic memory solutions, accelerating future progress in this domain.

6. **Effective Integration of Foundation Models:** DynaMem effectively integrates state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (CLIP, SigLIP, OWL-v2) and Multimodal Large Language Models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro) into a practical robotic system. The hybrid querying approach showcases a powerful way to leverage the complementary strengths of these models for robust real-world performance, contributing to the broader understanding of how to operationalize advanced AI in robotics.

**Potential Future Impact:**
- **More Capable Household Robots:** The ability to adapt to changing home environments is fundamental for domestic robots, allowing them to perform tasks even as humans rearrange items.
- **Industrial and Service Robotics:** In factories, warehouses, or service settings where layouts and object locations can frequently change, DynaMem's principles could enable more flexible and autonomous operations.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots that can accurately perceive and react to dynamic environments will be safer and more intuitive to interact with, as they will better understand the current state of the shared workspace.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors):**
The authors acknowledge that current limitations include the potential for further performance improvement by merging VLM-feature and mLLM-QA queries more deeply, reasoning over both object and voxel-level abstractions, and integrating mLLMs for detecting and recovering from manipulation failures. Expanding the system to handle more complex skills like searching in cabinets or drawers would also enhance real-world applicability. These proposed extensions highlight clear pathways for continued research building upon DynaMem's strong foundation.

In conclusion, DynaMem pushes the frontier of robotics by addressing the dynamic nature of real-world environments, a challenge that has long constrained the deployment of truly autonomous mobile manipulation systems. Its robust methodology and demonstrated performance mark a significant step towards more adaptable, intelligent, and useful robots.
