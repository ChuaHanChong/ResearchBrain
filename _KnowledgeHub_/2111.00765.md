# Validate on Sim, Detect on Real -- Model Selection for Domain Randomization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Intel Labs and Technion introduced VSDR, a method for efficiently selecting the best sim2real policies for robotic tasks by combining simulation-based validation with out-of-distribution detection using minimal real-world observations. The approach achieved higher policy ranking accuracy than individual components or baselines, demonstrating robust performance with as few as 64 real-world observations.

## Problem

- The high cost, time, and safety concerns associated with evaluating numerous trained sim2real policies directly on physical robots, creating a bottleneck in development.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap using Domain Randomization (DR) leads to a combinatorial explosion of hyper-parameters and many candidate policies, making selection difficult.
- Existing policy selection methods often require extensive real-world data or are task-specific, lacking generalizability and data efficiency.

## Method

- A combined policy score, **VSDR** (Validate on Sim, Detect on Real), which integrates ==simulation validation== with real-world ==out-of-distribution (OOD) detection==.
- Policies are validated in simulation on a held-out set of ==domain-randomization (DR)==-generated environments to assess generalization capability.
- Real-world OOD scores are computed by fitting ==Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs)== to feature activations from simulated training data and then evaluating the ==log-likelihood== of minimal real-world observations under these GMMs.
- Normalized simulation validation and OOD scores are multiplied to produce a final VSDR score, favoring policies that perform well in both aspects.

## Results

- VSDR consistently outperformed individual components and baselines (OPC/SoftOPC) in ranking accuracy, achieving better Spearman correlation with ground-truth rankings in **86-100%** of evaluated cases.
- The method demonstrated robust performance across different real-world data collection protocols (Expert, Sparse-expert, Initial), effectively using as few as **64** real-world observations.
- Simulation validation with richer, more complex randomization (DR-heavy) settings led to superior VSDR rankings, showing average improvements of up to **53%** over sim validation alone when DR was off.
- The choice of neural network layer for GMM fitting significantly impacted OOD performance, with earlier layers (e.g., image encoder, `fc0`) showing average improvements of **38%** in Spearman correlation over sim validation only.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining simulation-based validation with real-world OOD detection provides a more accurate and robust policy ranking than either component used individually, leveraging their complementary information.
> - Policies that perceive the real world as 'in-distribution' (lower OOD score) relative to their diverse simulated training environments exhibit better sim2real transfer.
> - Using feature activations from earlier neural network layers (e.g., image encoder) for GMM fitting yields more effective OOD detection and better policy rankings.
> - The VSDR method is highly data-efficient, accurately ranking policies with merely dozens of easily obtainable real-world observations.
