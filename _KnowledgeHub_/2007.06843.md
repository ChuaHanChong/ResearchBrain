# Socially and Contextually Aware Human Motion and Pose Forecasting

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified end-to-end framework forecasts human global trajectory and body skeleton pose together instead of separately. A shared GRU encoder embeds each person's past motion, a permutation-invariant social pooling layer fuses all people's embeddings, and an I3D spatio-temporal video encoder supplies scene context; a GRU decoder emits future offsets under a single joint global-space MSE loss, beating baselines on PoseTrack and NTU RGB+D.

## Problem

- Pose forecasting methods predict body-centered joint offsets only, discarding the global body displacement that must stay consistent with the predicted skeleton motion.
- Trajectory forecasting methods reduce a person to a sequence of 2D locations, too coarse for detailed body-motion or activity anticipation in human-robot interaction.
- Prior models forecast each isolated person without scene context or the social interactions of surrounding people, and often need action labels at training time.

## Method

- Joint global-space formulation: one ==MSE== metric and offset loss over absolute joint coordinates couples trajectory and pose error into a single distance instead of two separate errors.
- ==Social pooling== module applies a permutation-invariant symmetric function (max, sum, or average) over all persons' **GRU** embeddings, yielding one shared social feature vector.
- Kinetics-pretrained **I3D** encodes video into a 1024-dim spatio-temporal context vector, compressed by a two-layer MLP to 256 and concatenated with each person's embedding before GRU decoding.

## Results

- On PoseTrack the full social plus context model reaches **43**/**80.1**/**152.3**/**188.4**/**243.2** pixel MSE at 80-560 ms, versus **72.4**/**114.7**/**217.8**/**253.1**/**311.5** for the local-pose plus zero-motion baseline.
- On NTU RGB+D the full model gets **13.1**/**19.8**/**29.7**/**34.1** cm at 80-400 ms, beating separately learned pose plus Social-GAN or Social-LSTM trajectories (about **14.5**/**22.4**/**34.7**/**40.9**).
- Max pooling beat average and sum pooling (**152.3** vs **153.6** and **154.1** at 320 ms); social gains concentrate in interactive actions such as pushing (**52.9** to **50.4** cm).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Correlated sub-tasks are better trained under one loss in the shared output space than stitched together from independently predicted components afterwards.
> - Adding a hand-designed constant-velocity trajectory to a body-centered pose model cannot recover the global motion term, evidence the two signals are genuinely coupled.
> - Scene context helps only where scenes vary: it improved crowded real-world video markedly but barely helped lab-recorded sequences with a fixed background.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion and pose forecasting is a critical area within computer vision and artificial intelligence, with applications spanning robotics, surveillance, autonomous driving, and human-computer interaction. Prior research in this domain typically addresses two distinct problems: forecasting human trajectories (global motion) or forecasting detailed body joint movements (local pose).

Many existing approaches to human skeleton pose forecasting, such as those by Martinez et al. (2017) and Jain et al. (2016), focus on predicting body joint movements relative to a body center, thus disregarding the overall global body displacement. This separation simplifies the problem but may yield incomplete information for real-world scenarios where consistent global motion is crucial (e.g., a running person's pose should align with their displacement). Conversely, a parallel line of research focuses solely on human trajectory forecasting, modeling the prediction of a sequence of 2D or 3D locations over time, as seen in works like Social-LSTM (Alahi et al., 2016) and Social-GAN (Gupta et al., 2018). While these methods are effective for path prediction in crowded environments, they lack the detailed body motion representation necessary for fine-grained activity analysis or precise human-robot interaction.

A notable gap in the existing literature is the lack of a unified framework that simultaneously predicts both global human motion and detailed body pose. Furthermore, many current pose forecasting models do not inherently account for external contextual cues, such as the physical environment or interactions with other individuals. While some models for trajectory prediction have incorporated "social forces" to model interactions in crowded scenes, these have typically not extended to detailed body pose prediction. Works like those by Walker et al. (2017) or Barsoum et al. (2017) explore video forecasting by generating pose futures or probabilistic motion, but still tend to separate local and global dynamics or operate on single individuals.

This paper positions itself by addressing these limitations, proposing a method that integrates global motion and local pose prediction into a single framework. It also explicitly incorporates scene and social contexts, which are often overlooked or treated separately in prior work, particularly within the pose forecasting domain. By developing an action-agnostic model, it also diverges from approaches that rely on pre-defined action labels, aiming for broader applicability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified, end-to-end framework capable of simultaneously forecasting both human global motion (trajectory) and detailed body skeleton pose. This unified approach aims to overcome the limitations of prior work that typically addresses these two aspects of human dynamics in isolation.

The motivation for this joint prediction framework is rooted in several practical considerations:
- **Correlation of Global and Local Motion:** The authors argue that human body pose changes and global body displacement are inherently correlated. For instance, the pose of a person running is intrinsically linked to their forward motion. Separating these two tasks can lead to inconsistent or unrealistic predictions. A model that predicts a running pose without a corresponding global displacement would be less useful for real-world applications.
- **Completeness for Real-world Applications:** For applications such as seamless robot navigation, collision avoidance in autonomous vehicles, or advanced surveillance systems, both the overall movement path and the detailed body posture are crucial. Predicting only one component provides an incomplete picture of future human behavior.
- **Incorporation of Scene Context:** Human movement and pose are often influenced by the physical environment. For example, a person on an ice-skating field is more likely to exhibit sliding motions than diving or jogging. Explicitly encoding spatio-temporal representations from video data (scene context) can provide important clues to guide predictions, making them more plausible and accurate within a given environment.
- **Inclusion of Social Context:** In multi-person scenarios, individuals' movements and poses are frequently influenced by those around them. Social interactions, such as an opponent in a boxing match adopting a defensive posture in response to an attack, provide strong predictive signals. Modeling these social dependencies can significantly enhance the accuracy and realism of forecasts for each individual.
- **Development of a Unified Evaluation Metric:** The existing separation of global motion and local pose prediction often leads to distinct evaluation metrics. A key objective is to introduce a single metric and loss function that jointly measures the error in both components in the original, absolute coordinate space, reflecting the combined real-world performance.
- **Action-Agnostic Modeling:** Many prior models rely on specific action labels during training. The authors aim to develop a single, action-agnostic model that can generalize across various activities without requiring prior semantic knowledge, enhancing its robustness and applicability in diverse, unconstrained environments.

By addressing these motivations, the research intends to advance the state-of-the-art in human behavior prediction towards more practical and comprehensive scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework is an end-to-end learning architecture designed for joint human motion and pose forecasting, incorporating both social and scene contextual information. It is built upon a sequence-to-sequence model utilizing Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs).

#### 3.1. Joint Prediction Formulation

Instead of predicting pose relative to a body center and global motion separately, the model directly forecasts the absolute future locations of body joints in the original 2D or 3D coordinate space. This approach naturally couples global and local movements.
- **Input:** The model receives the observed history of body joint locations for each person `j` up to time `t`, denoted as `X_j_o = (x_j_1, ..., x_j_t)`, where `x_j_t` is a vector of all `L` body joint coordinates at time `t`. The input to the encoder for each person is actually the *offset* values (`∆X_j_o`) rather than absolute positions, to facilitate learning.
- **Output:** The model predicts the future positions of these body joints for `T` future time steps, `X_j_f = (x_j_t+1, ..., x_j_t+T)`. Similarly, the decoder outputs *offset* values (`∆X_j_f`).
- **Evaluation Metric:** The performance is measured using the Mean Squared Error (MSE) or L2 distance between the ground-truth and predicted *absolute* future locations of all joints, averaged over all persons and future time steps. This metric intrinsically captures errors in both global motion and local pose.
- **Loss Function:** For training, the model minimizes the MSE between the predicted offset values (`∆X_j_f`) and the ground-truth offset values. This is described as making offset prediction an easier learning task.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The framework consists of four main modules:

- **Encoder Module:**
 - A dedicated GRU-based encoder processes the historical (past) offset sequence of poses for each individual `j`.
 - This encoder generates a person-specific latent representation, `h_j`, which encapsulates the historical pose and motion information for that individual. The hidden state dimension for GRU is fixed at 1024.

- **Social Pooling Module:**
 - To integrate social interactions, a permutation-invariant function `H(·)` is applied to the set of all person-specific embeddings `{h_1, ..., h_N}` from all individuals in the scene.
 - The purpose of this function is to generate a collective "social feature" `S` that is independent of the order of individuals.
 - The authors explore max, sum, and average pooling as symmetrical functions. Max pooling was empirically found to yield the best results.

- **Spatiotemporal Context Encoding Module:**
 - This module extracts scene context from the video stream.
 - It utilizes a pre-trained I3D (Inflated 3D ConvNet) model, trained on the Kinetics dataset, applied to the RGB video frames from which poses were extracted.
 - The resulting 1024-dimensional spatio-temporal feature vector is then processed by a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with a dropout probability of 0.7.
 - This MLP reduces the feature dimension to 256, producing a shared context feature `C` that is common to all persons in the scene.

- **Decoder Module:**
 - For each person `j`, a GRU-based decoder receives a concatenated feature vector.
 - This concatenated vector combines the person-specific embedding `h_j`, the shared social feature `S`, and the shared context feature `C`.
 - The decoder then forecasts the future `T` time steps of body motion and pose as offset values (`∆X_j_f`), which are subsequently converted to absolute coordinates for evaluation.

#### 3.3. Training and Datasets

- **Training Details:** The model is trained in an action-agnostic manner, meaning it does not incorporate action labels. The Adam optimizer is used, with an initial learning rate of 5e-4 and a decay factor of 0.95. Early stopping on a validation set is employed to select the best model.
- **Datasets:**
 - **PoseTrack:** A large-scale multi-person 2D dataset featuring diverse interactions and crowded scenarios. The model observes 15 past frames and forecasts 15 future frames. Poses consist of 14 joints, with results reported in pixels.
 - **NTU RGB+D 60:** A 3D dataset containing both single and mutual actions in a controlled laboratory setting. The model observes 40 past frames and forecasts 10 future frames. Poses consist of 13 major body joints, with results reported in centimeters.

#### 3.4. Baselines

The authors compare their model against several baselines, categorized into:
- **Non-learning Baselines:** ZeroPose-ZeroMotion (last observed pose repeated), ZeroPose-ConstantMotion (fixed pose, constant global velocity), ConstantPose-ConstantMotion (constant velocity for both pose and global motion).
- **Local Pose Forecasting with Fixed/Learned Global Motion:** Models that center poses (removing global motion) and use either fixed (zero/constant) or learned (Social-GAN, Social-LSTM) global motion. These include the vanilla model from Martinez et al. (2017) and an extension incorporating the social pooling module.
- **Ablation Studies of the Proposed Model:** Their `JointLearning` model (extension of Martinez et al. (2017) with joint loss) and versions integrating only context (`JointLearning+Context`) or only social information (`JointLearning+Social`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the PoseTrack and NTU RGB+D datasets demonstrate the performance of the proposed framework and the contribution of its components. The primary metric used for evaluation is MSE, measuring the L2 distance between predicted and ground-truth absolute joint locations.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Joint Motion and Pose Forecasting

- The baseline `Ours (JointLearning)`, which extends the method from Martinez et al. (2017) by applying a joint loss on both local pose and global motion, generally outperforms the non-learning baselines (ZeroPose-ZeroMotion, ZeroPose-ConstantMotion, ConstantPose-ConstantMotion) on both datasets. This indicates that jointly modeling these components, even without social or context information, yields improved predictions compared to trivial approaches or models that treat them separately.
- The `JointLearning` model also shows improved performance compared to methods that learn local pose separately and then combine it with either fixed (zero/constant) or learned (SGAN/SLSTM) global motion. This suggests that the inherent correlation between pose and global motion is better captured when learned jointly. This improvement is particularly pronounced on the more complex PoseTrack dataset. For NTU RGB+D, due to its controlled environment with less global motion, the difference between constant motion baselines and trajectory-learnable methods is less significant.

#### 4.2. Impact of Social and Contextual Information

- **Social Module:** The `Ours (JointLearning+Social)` model consistently demonstrates lower MSE values compared to `Ours (JointLearning)` (non-social) across all prediction horizons on both datasets. This indicates that incorporating social interactions among individuals provides valuable information for more accurate predictions of future movements and poses. For example, on PoseTrack at 560 milliseconds, `JointLearning` achieved 256.3 pixels, while `JointLearning+Social` achieved 246.7 pixels.
- **Context Module:** The `Ours (JointLearning+Context)` model also shows improvements over `Ours (JointLearning)`. This suggests that scene context contributes to prediction accuracy. For instance, on PoseTrack at 560 milliseconds, `JointLearning+Context` achieved 251.5 pixels, which is better than `JointLearning`'s 256.3 pixels. The impact of scene context on NTU RGB+D is less pronounced compared to PoseTrack, which the authors attribute to the controlled laboratory settings of the NTU dataset.
- **Combined Social and Context:** The full proposed model, `Ours (JointLearning+Social+Context)`, achieves the best performance across all time steps and both datasets, consistently reporting the lowest MSE. For example, on PoseTrack at 560 milliseconds, the full model achieved 243.2 pixels, outperforming all other baselines and ablations. Similarly, on NTU RGB+D at 400 milliseconds, it achieved 34.1 cm, compared to 35.1 cm for `JointLearning`. This indicates a synergistic effect where both social and contextual cues collectively enhance the model's predictive capabilities.

#### 4.3. Social Pooling Strategy

- An investigation into different social pooling strategies (average, sum, max) showed that **max pooling** yielded the best results within the `Social+Context` model, performing slightly better than average and sum pooling (e.g., Max Pooling MSE of 243.2 at 560ms on PoseTrack, compared to Average Pooling at 246.8 and Sum Pooling at 247.2).

#### 4.4. Action-Level Analysis (NTU RGB+D)

- A detailed breakdown of errors across 11 mutual action categories in the NTU RGB+D dataset (Table III) reveals that the `JointLearning+Social+Context` model generally outperforms `JointLearning` (non-social). The improvements are more significant in actions involving a high degree of social interaction, such as "punching" or "hugging," where understanding inter-person dynamics is critical. For instance, in "punching" at 400ms, the combined model achieved 44.1 cm versus 44.08 cm for `JointLearning`, while for "hugging" it was 54.9 cm versus 56.6 cm.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Observations

- Visualizations (Figure 4) demonstrate that the predictions from the full `JointLearning+Social+Context` model are visually closer to the ground-truth poses compared to the `JointLearning` (non-social) model, especially as the prediction horizon extends. The integration of scene context (e.g., a football pitch) aids in predicting appropriate leg movements, while social context helps in anticipating responsive poses in interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of human motion analysis by introducing a novel and integrated approach to forecasting human dynamics. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified and Comprehensive Prediction:** By proposing the joint prediction of both global human motion and detailed body skeleton pose within a single framework, the research addresses a notable gap in prior work that often treats these as separate problems. This unified approach is more representative of real-world human movement, where global displacement and local body articulation are intrinsically linked.
- **Enhanced Prediction Accuracy:** The systematic incorporation of both social interactions and scene context significantly improves prediction accuracy compared to models that disregard these cues or treat them in isolation. This advancement yields more plausible and context-aware forecasts of human behavior.
- **Development of a Relevant Evaluation Metric:** The introduction of a joint evaluation metric based on the absolute positions of body joints provides a more holistic and practical measure of performance, directly reflecting the utility of predictions in real-world coordinate space. This facilitates a more direct assessment of the model's ability to generate coherent and consistent human motion.
- **Action-Agnostic Generalizability:** Developing an action-agnostic model, which does not rely on predefined action labels, enhances the framework's robustness and applicability to a wider range of unpredictable human activities and diverse environments. This is particularly valuable for deploying such systems in unconstrained real-world settings.
- **Facilitation of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The ability to predict future human poses and trajectories with high accuracy and contextual awareness can enable more natural, smooth, and safe interactions between humans and robots. Robots can anticipate human intentions and movements, leading to better collaboration and collision avoidance.
- **Advancements in Autonomous Systems:** For autonomous vehicles and mobile robots operating in crowded or dynamic environments, accurate human motion and pose forecasting is crucial for predicting pedestrian intent, ensuring safety, and making informed navigation decisions.
- **Applications in Surveillance and Activity Anticipation:** The framework can be applied in intelligent surveillance systems to anticipate hazardous or anomalous human behaviors, potentially enabling proactive interventions. It also supports fine-grained anticipation of future activities, contributing to a deeper understanding of complex human actions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust baseline for future explorations in context-aware human dynamics prediction. The authors suggest directions such as integrating GAN-based social models, indicating a path for further improvements and exploration of multi-modal prediction.

In conclusion, this research presents a method that moves towards a more comprehensive and realistic understanding of future human behavior, with direct implications for the development of more intelligent and interactive artificial systems.
