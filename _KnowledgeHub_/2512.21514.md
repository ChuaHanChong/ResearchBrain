# DiverseGRPO: Mitigating Mode Collapse in Image Generation via Diversity-Aware GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University and Kuaishou Technology developed DiverseGRPO, an approach to mitigate mode collapse in image generation models trained with Group-level Policy Optimization (GRPO). This method employs a distributional creativity bonus in reward modeling and a structure-aware regularization strategy, leading to up to 18.8% higher perceptual diversity (DreamSim) and up to 23.3% lower FID scores, while maintaining or improving image quality.

## Problem

- Generative models, especially those tuned with RLHF methods like GRPO, frequently suffer from mode collapse, producing a limited range of similar outputs for diverse prompts.
- Existing GRPO-based reward functions optimize for single-sample quality, inadvertently driving models to converge on a narrow subset of 'high-reward' generation modes.
- The standard KL divergence regularization in GRPO misallocates diversity budget, weakly constraining early denoising steps that are crucial for structural diversity, and overly constraining later steps.

## Method

- Introduces a distributional ==creativity bonus== that quantifies visual diversity using DreamSim and spectral clustering, assigning higher exploration rewards to samples from underrepresented visual modes.
- Implements a structure-aware regularization that replaces the standard KL term with a consistent Wasserstein distance constraint during early, diversity-critical denoising steps.
- Removes regularization entirely in later denoising stages, allowing the model to focus on refining local details and maximizing quality without excessive constraints.

## Results

- Achieved substantial improvements in diversity metrics, including up to an **18.8%** increase in DreamSim and a **23.3%** reduction in FID score.
- Maintained or slightly enhanced image quality, with human-preference scores like PickScore and ImageReward seeing modest gains of **0.1%** to **3.4%**.
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrate a broader range of visual outputs for given prompts, effectively mitigating the repetitive generations seen in baseline GRPO methods and establishing a new quality-diversity Pareto frontier.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mode collapse in GRPO-tuned diffusion models stems from both short-sighted reward maximization and misaligned regularization, rather than solely from reward-driven optimization.
> - Rethinking the reward signal to include a 'distributional creativity bonus' can actively steer the model away from converging on a limited set of high-reward outputs.
> - Regularization needs to be 'structure-aware,' providing stronger constraints during early denoising phases critical for global diversity and relaxing them for later, fine-tuning stages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of generative AI, specifically focusing on the intersection of image generation, diffusion models, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

**Evolution of Generative Models:** Image generation has seen monumental progress with the advent of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and, more recently, diffusion models. Diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion and its variants, have achieved state-of-the-art results in generating high-quality and diverse images from text prompts.

**Rise of RLHF in Generative AI:** Inspired by its success in aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences (e.g., through methods like PPO and DPO), RLHF has been increasingly applied to text-to-image generation. Algorithms like Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO aim to refine diffusion models by optimizing them against human aesthetic judgments or task-specific preferences, leading to images that are not only high-fidelity but also more appealing or useful to humans.

**The Problem of Mode Collapse:** A long-standing issue in generative models, mode collapse, occurs when a model fails to capture the full diversity of the training data distribution, instead converging to generate only a limited subset of possible outputs. While initially prominent in GANs, this paper highlights that mode collapse is also a significant limitation for RLHF-tuned diffusion models, particularly those using GRPO-based approaches. This manifests as models producing homogenized outputs—similar compositions, styles, or subjects—despite varied prompts, severely restricting their utility in creative applications. Prior works on RL for diffusion models (e.g., MixGRPO, Flow-CPS, TempFlowGRPO, BranchGRPO) have largely focused on improving training efficiency, stability, or reward signal processing, often overlooking this critical diversity degradation issue.

**Current Approaches to Mitigate Mode Collapse:** In LLMs, efforts have included DivPO (online sample selection), entropy regularization, and multi-objective optimization (CPO). In image generation, methods like DiADM (diversity-aware modules) and techniques involving latent space projection and contrastive learning have been explored. However, these often introduce complexity or are not specifically tailored to the unique dynamics of GRPO-based diffusion models.

**This Paper's Novel Contribution:** "DiverseGRPO" directly tackles mode collapse in GRPO-based image generation by fundamentally rethinking both the reward modeling and the generation dynamics. It argues that diversity degradation is not an inherent byproduct of reward maximization but rather a symptom of misaligned learning objectives and regularization strategies. By proposing a distribution-aware reward mechanism and a structure-aware regularization tailored to diffusion process dynamics, DiverseGRPO offers a novel, two-pronged approach. This work directly builds upon and improves the state-of-the-art in RL-driven image generation by addressing a critical limitation that has hindered the practical deployment of these powerful models in diverse, creative scenarios, thereby establishing a new Pareto frontier for the quality-diversity trade-off.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **mitigate mode collapse in image generation models trained with Group-level Policy Optimization (GRPO), thereby enhancing the diversity of generated outputs while preserving or improving image quality.** The authors are motivated by the observation that despite the remarkable success of GRPO-based reinforcement learning (RL) in significantly improving image generation quality by aligning with human aesthetic preferences, these models exhibit a critical flaw: the degradation of generation diversity.

The specific motivations and underlying problems are dissected into two main perspectives:

1. **Reward Modeling Perspective: Short-sighted Reward Maximization Leading to Homogenization.**
 - **Problem:** Traditional GRPO relies on single-sample quality as the primary reward signal. This means the reward model assigns scores to individual images without considering the relationships or overall distribution of generated samples for a given prompt.
 - **Consequence:** This "short-sighted" approach drives the generative model to exploit and converge towards a narrow subset of "high-reward" generation modes. The model learns to reproduce "safe" or "high-score" patterns, inadvertently suppressing exploration of novel or unconventional visual interpretations.
 - **Formal Analysis:** The authors formally explain this through replicator dynamics, showing how modes with slightly higher average rewards gain disproportionately higher sampling probabilities, eventually leading to a unimodal distribution where only the dominant high-reward mode survives.
 - **Motivation for Change:** To counter this, the reward signal itself must be redesigned to encourage distribution-level diversity rather than just single-sample quality.

2. **Generation Dynamics Perspective: Misaligned Regularization Budget in Denoising Process.**
 - **Problem:** Conventional GRPO regularization, typically implemented as a KL divergence penalty, aims to constrain the policy updates relative to a reference model. However, this regularization mechanism fails to account for the uneven sensitivity of diversity throughout the diffusion model's denoising process.
 - **Observation:** The authors empirically observe that the early denoising phase (approximately the first one-third of steps) has a disproportionately large impact on the final diversity, accounting for roughly 66% of the overall diversity change. These early steps define the global structure and semantic modes.
 - **Consequence:** The standard KL penalty's strength is inversely proportional to the diffusion variance (σ²). In early stages, when variance is largest, the KL penalty becomes weakest, providing insufficient constraint exactly when diversity is most fragile. Conversely, in later stages, small variance amplifies the penalty, excessively constraining fine-grained detail refinement and potentially hindering reward optimization for quality. This creates a "structural mismatch" and an imbalanced "diversity budget" allocation.
 - **Motivation for Change:** To resolve this, a "structure-aware regularization" is needed that applies stronger constraints in the diversity-critical early stages and relaxes them in later stages to allow for effective reward optimization.

By addressing these two core issues, DiverseGRPO aims to unlock the full potential of RLHF in image generation, enabling models to produce both high-quality and highly diverse outputs essential for creative applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiverseGRPO proposes a novel two-pronged approach to simultaneously address the reward modeling and generation dynamics issues contributing to mode collapse in GRPO-based image generation. The method operates within the existing GRPO framework, specifically Flow-GRPO, by refining its reward calculation and regularization scheme.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Flow-GRPO Background

The paper first establishes the context of Flow-GRPO, which aims to maximize an expected cumulative reward objective. Flow-GRPO transforms deterministic ODE-based sampling into a stochastic SDE formulation to introduce exploration noise. The core optimization involves maximizing an objective function that combines a policy ratio term, an advantage term (calculated by normalizing group-level rewards), and a KL divergence regularization term (Equation 1, 2, 3, 6). The KL term constrains the divergence between the current policy and a reference policy at each denoising step.

#### 3.2. Distributional Creativity Bonus (Reward Level)

To address the "short-sighted" single-sample reward issue, DiverseGRPO introduces a **distributional creativity bonus** that incentivizes the model to explore a wider range of visual modes for a given text prompt. This mechanism comprises three stages:

1. **Perception Distance Calculation:** For a group of images generated from the same caption, the method first quantifies the visual differences between all pairs of images. This is achieved using **DreamSim**, a perceptual similarity model designed to align with human judgments of visual similarity. This results in a pairwise distance matrix `D`, where `D_ij` represents the perceptual distance between image `x_i` and `x_j`.

2. **Spectral Clustering:** The perceptual distance matrix `D` is then used to construct an affinity matrix `A` via a Gaussian kernel (Equation 8), which measures the similarity between images. A higher affinity indicates greater similarity. From `A`, a degree matrix `D_ii` (sum of affinities for each image) is computed, which is then used to form the normalized Laplacian matrix `L` (Equation 10). Eigenvalue decomposition of `L` yields eigenvectors that capture the intrinsic structure of the image data. These eigenvectors are then clustered using **k-means clustering** to partition the images into `k` distinct semantic clusters. Each cluster represents a distinct visual mode (e.g., different compositions, styles, or camera angles for the same subject).

3. **Reward Allocation:** After clustering, an **exploration reward** `E_i` is assigned to each image `x_i`. This reward is inversely proportional to the size of the cluster `n_k` to which `x_i` belongs, specifically `sqrt(N/n_k)` (Equation 11), where `N` is the total number of samples for the caption. This design ensures that images belonging to smaller clusters (representing novel or underrepresented visual modes) receive higher bonuses, actively encouraging the model to discover and generate such diverse outputs.

The final reward `R_i` for an image `x_i` is then a weighted sum of its intrinsic quality score `Q_i` (from the reward model like PickScore) and the computed exploration reward `E_i`, with `β` controlling the balance (Equation 12). This refined `R_i` is then fed into the GRPO advantage calculation (Equation 3), guiding the policy towards both high quality and high diversity.

#### 3.3. Structure-Aware Regularization (Generation Level)

To address the misaligned regularization budget during the denoising process, DiverseGRPO introduces a **structure-aware regularization** strategy that replaces the standard KL divergence term.

1. **Rethinking KL Penalty:** The authors highlight that the standard KL penalty (Equation 6) scales with `1/σ_t²`, where `σ_t²` is the diffusion variance at time `t`. In early denoising steps, `σ_t²` is large, making the KL penalty weak when diversity is most critical. In later steps, `σ_t²` is small, amplifying the penalty and excessively constraining fine-tuning.

2. **Structure-Aware Wasserstein Distance:** To correct this, DiverseGRPO replaces the KL term with a stage-dependent regularization. For the **first `K` denoising steps**, it applies a **Wasserstein Distance constraint** (Equation 13), specifically `||x_t+Δt,θ - x_t+Δt,ref||²`. Crucially, this removes the `1/σ_t²` variance denominator present in the KL term. This modification applies a *stronger and more consistent constraint* on early-stage updates, forcing the model to preserve structural diversity and semantic coverage when global image features are being formed.

3. **Relaxation in Later Stages:** For denoising steps **beyond `K` (t > K)**, the regularization term is **entirely removed** (Equation 14). This allows the policy to freely optimize for higher reward fidelity and refine local details without being excessively constrained, leveraging the full potential of the reward model for quality improvements.

By combining these two novel components, DiverseGRPO systematically addresses the root causes of mode collapse from both reward and generation dynamics perspectives, enabling the model to learn a policy that optimizes for both high quality and rich diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DiverseGRPO demonstrates significant improvements in mitigating mode collapse, consistently enhancing diversity while maintaining or even improving image quality across various experimental settings.

**Quantitative Results:**
- **Superior Diversity:** The method consistently and substantially improves all evaluated diversity metrics. For instance, across different backbones (SD3.5-M, Flux.1-dev) and reward models (PickScore, HPSv3), DiverseGRPO achieves:
 - **DreamSim (↑):** Up to **+18.8%** improvement, indicating significantly higher perceptual diversity aligned with human visual judgments.
 - **BeyondFID (↑):** Remarkable improvements, reaching up to **+184.2%**, confirming a much broader coverage of the semantic feature space and reduced mode collapse.
 - **FID (↓):** Significant reductions in FID score, with improvements up to **+23.3%**. Lower FID indicates better distributional alignment and less mode collapse.
 - **SSIM (↑):** Improvements up to **+25.6%**, further validating enhanced structural diversity.
- **Maintained/Improved Quality:** Crucially, these diversity gains are achieved without compromising, and often slightly enhancing, image quality metrics:
 - **CLIP (↑), ImageReward (↑), PickScore (↑), UnifiedReward (↑):** Consistent slight improvements across these human-preference-aligned reward scores, ranging from +0.1% to +3.4%. This suggests that DiverseGRPO not only prevents mode collapse but can also lead to the discovery of higher-quality, yet diverse, generation modes.
- **New Pareto Frontier:** The results collectively establish a new Pareto frontier, demonstrating that DiverseGRPO can achieve a superior trade-off between image quality and diversity compared to existing GRPO-based baselines.

**Qualitative Results (Figure 4):**
- Visual comparisons strikingly illustrate the effectiveness of DiverseGRPO. Baseline methods (Flow-GRPO) clearly suffer from mode collapse, producing highly repetitive outputs (e.g., nearly identical facial expressions, camera angles, or font styles) for diverse prompts.
- In contrast, DiverseGRPO generates visually distinct and creative images that maintain high quality and semantic consistency with the prompts. For example, given the prompt "A photo of a beautiful young woman, cute," the baseline produces highly similar faces, while DiverseGRPO offers a range of different looks and poses. This visually confirms the enhanced diversity across composition, stylistic details, and dynamic expression.

**Ablation Study (Figures 5 & 6):**
- **Individual Contributions:** The ablation study confirms that both proposed modules—the **Structure-Aware Regularization (SA-Reg)** and the **Distributional Creativity Bonus (Creativity Reward)**—contribute positively to the quality-diversity trade-off. Their combined application yields the optimal performance. SA-Reg alone improves diversity, but the addition of the creativity reward provides a significant further boost.
- **Training Dynamics:** Analysis of training process (Figure 6) reveals that DiverseGRPO successfully maintains a healthier level of diversity across the entire training duration, exhibiting a significantly slower decline in diversity compared to baseline methods, while achieving comparable final image quality. This confirms its role in preventing premature mode collapse.
- **Parameter Sensitivity:** Experiments on the creativity reward coefficient (β) and the number of SA-Reg steps (K) show that increasing these parameters generally enhances diversity, but with diminishing returns beyond certain points, suggesting an optimal balance between exploration and optimization efficiency.

**Impact of Exploration Bonus (Figure 7):**
- A compelling visualization demonstrates how the distributional creativity bonus functions during training. In later training stages (e.g., step 720), when a rare sample (e.g., a side-view elephant) appears, DiverseGRPO's spectral clustering identifies it as belonging to a small, underrepresented cluster. This triggers a higher exploration reward. Consequently, the model is incentivized to continue generating such rare, diverse samples and improve their quality. In contrast, baseline methods would likely suppress such rare modes in favor of more common, high-reward ones, leading to their eventual disappearance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "DiverseGRPO" holds significant implications for the advancement of generative AI, particularly in the realm of text-to-image synthesis, and its broader applications.

**Addressing a Critical Limitation of RLHF-tuned Generative Models:** The most significant impact of DiverseGRPO lies in its effective solution to mode collapse, a persistent and debilitating problem in generative models that, as this paper demonstrates, is exacerbated by conventional RLHF approaches like GRPO. By tackling this head-on, DiverseGRPO dramatically enhances the practical utility of high-quality, RLHF-tuned diffusion models. Without diversity, even perfect image quality quickly becomes monotonous and uninspiring, severely limiting real-world creative applications.

**Enabling Truly Creative AI Applications:** The ability to generate images that are not only high-quality but also diverse and creatively varied is paramount for numerous industries:
- **Digital Art and Design:** Artists and designers can leverage these models to rapidly iterate through diverse concepts, styles, and compositions, expanding their creative horizons.
- **Advertising and Marketing:** Generating a wide array of visual content for different campaigns, demographics, or A/B testing becomes more efficient and impactful.
- **Game Development:** Creating diverse characters, environments, and assets for virtual worlds becomes more feasible, enhancing immersion and replayability.
- **Content Creation Platforms (e.g., Kuaishou):** For platforms like Kuaishou, where user-generated content thrives on novelty and variety, DiverseGRPO could power advanced creative tools, allowing users to generate unique and engaging visual stories.

**Advancements in RL and Generative Model Theory:**
- **Novel Reward Design:** The introduction of a "distributional creativity bonus" through spectral clustering and adaptive exploration rewards marks a significant conceptual leap in RLHF. It moves beyond simplistic single-sample rewards to a more sophisticated, distribution-aware objective, offering a blueprint for designing more intelligent and context-aware reward functions in other generative AI domains, including LLMs.
- **Refined Regularization for Diffusion Models:** The "structure-aware regularization" leveraging Wasserstein distance is a subtle yet powerful insight into the dynamics of diffusion models. By recognizing and strategically addressing the uneven sensitivity of diversity across denoising steps, the method provides a more nuanced and effective way to constrain policy updates, demonstrating a deeper understanding of how to control the generative process for specific objectives. This could inspire similar temporally or spatially aware regularization schemes in other multi-step generative processes.
- **Pushing the Pareto Frontier:** Achieving a new Pareto frontier for the quality-diversity trade-off is a strong metric of advancement, demonstrating that previous assumptions about inherent trade-offs can be overcome with innovative methodological approaches.

**Generality and Robustness:** The demonstrated effectiveness across multiple diffusion backbones (SD3.5-M, Flux.1-dev) and various human-preference reward models (PickScore, HPSv3) suggests that DiverseGRPO is a robust and broadly applicable solution, not merely an artifact of specific model architectures or reward functions. This increases its potential for widespread adoption and further development.

**Future Research Directions:** This work opens several avenues for future research, including investigating more sophisticated clustering or diversity quantification methods, exploring adaptive scheduling of `β` and `K` during training, extending the approach to other RL algorithms, and applying similar principles to other multimodal generative tasks.

In conclusion, DiverseGRPO represents a significant step forward in making RLHF-tuned generative models more robust, versatile, and genuinely creative. By meticulously dissecting and intelligently solving the problem of mode collapse from dual perspectives, the authors have not only improved the state-of-the-art but also laid foundational groundwork for future innovations in intelligent content generation.
