# MoReGen: Multi-Agent Motion-Reasoning Engine for Code-based Text-to-Video Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> MoReGen introduces a framework for generating physically accurate videos from text prompts, leveraging a multi-agent LLM system to create simulation code for physics engines. This system achieves superior trajectory fidelity, demonstrated by a DTW score of 8.93 on the new MoReSet benchmark, outperforming models like Sora2 (11.21) and Veo3 (13.35), and includes novel metrics for evaluating physical consistency.

## Problem

- Current Text-to-Video (T2V) models prioritize visual fidelity over physical plausibility, often generating videos that violate fundamental physical laws like object counting or momentum conservation.
- Existing T2V evaluation metrics (e.g., FID, FVD) primarily assess aesthetic realism and pixel-level similarity, failing to quantitatively measure physical validity or temporal coherence.
- Data-driven T2V architectures struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios and lack causal reasoning, making them unable to consistently produce physically coherent outputs.

## Method

- **MoReGen** proposes a ==multi-agent== LLM system to convert natural language descriptions into executable simulation code for physics engines and rendering.
- The framework comprises a Text-Parser Agent for structured specification, a Code-Writer Agent for simulation code generation, and a Video-Render Agent for execution and rendering.
- It includes an iterative evaluator design that analyzes video outputs, compares estimated trajectories to ground-truth, assesses physical and semantic alignment, and provides feedback for code refinement.

## Results

- MoReGen achieved superior trajectory fidelity on the MoReSet benchmark with DTW scores of **8.93** ± **9.61**, DTW-N of **0.06** ± **0.07**, and Procrustes of **0.48** ± **0.30**, outperforming commercial models like Sora2 (DTW **11.21**) and Veo3 (DTW **13.35**).
- Existing physics-based metrics (e.g., Trajan, VideoPhy2) often misranked physically accurate simulator-rendered videos lower due to divergence from their training distributions, indicating limitations in assessing true physical validity.
- Qualitative analysis showed MoReGen accurately rendered complex physical phenomena, such as a Newton's cradle with correct object counting and momentum transfer, while other T2V models generated physically inconsistent motions and artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly generating executable code for physics simulators, guided by LLMs, enables the creation of videos with superior physical accuracy and reproducible motion, surpassing data-driven generative models.
> - Conventional "physics-based" T2V evaluation metrics are often flawed, biased towards aesthetic realism, or sensitive to training data distribution, leading to misleading assessments of physical validity.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of LLMs on phenomenon-specific structured schemas and iterative feedback from an evaluator are crucial for enhancing the physical precision and robustness of code-based video synthesis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-Video (T2V) generation has advanced significantly in recent years, with generative AI models achieving remarkable progress in producing photorealistic videos from natural language prompts. These advancements are largely driven by vision-transformer-based architectures and denoising diffusion probabilistic models, which leverage massive datasets and computational resources to generate high-fidelity visual content. However, this progress has primarily focused on visual fidelity and aesthetic realism, often at the expense of physical plausibility and adherence to fundamental laws of motion and dynamics.

Existing state-of-the-art T2V models frequently generate videos that, despite appearing realistic, violate basic physical principles such as object counting, momentum conservation, accurate representation of Newtonian forces, and correct velocity or pressure computations. This limitation stems from their data-driven design, which tends to prioritize pattern memorization and statistical correlations in training data over causal reasoning and an understanding of underlying physical laws. Consequently, these models often struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where generated videos deviate sharply from physical reality.

The evaluation methodologies for T2V models also reflect this bias towards aesthetics. Metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), and Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) quantify distributional similarity and pixel-level realism but provide limited insight into physical validity or temporal coherence. While human evaluations offer qualitative judgments, they are insufficient for assessing precise physical relationships quantitatively. Although some recent efforts have attempted to incorporate physics-informed generation and evaluation, they often remain constrained by the limitations of diffusion backbones or rely on LLM/VLM-based captioning and scoring, which can introduce issues like hallucination or bias.

MoReGen positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical gap between visual realism and physical plausibility in T2V generation. It shifts the paradigm from purely appearance-driven synthesis to a physics-grounded, motion-centric approach. By integrating multi-agent Large Language Models (LLMs) with physics simulators and rendering engines, MoReGen aims to generate reproducible, physically accurate videos directly from text. Furthermore, it introduces a novel benchmark (MoReSet) and evaluation metrics (MoRe Metrics) specifically designed to quantitatively assess physical validity and trajectory fidelity, moving beyond the limitations of existing aesthetics-focused or data-centric physics evaluation methods. This work seeks to establish a principled direction for the development and assessment of physically coherent video synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a text-to-video (T2V) generation framework that produces videos which are not only visually appealing but also strictly adhere to physical principles, specifically Newtonian motion. The work emphasizes physical precision and motion coherence, addressing a recognized deficiency in current T2V technologies.

The motivation for MoReGen stems from several limitations observed in the current state of T2V generation:

- **Deficiencies in Physical Reasoning:** Current advanced T2V models, despite their progress in photorealism, frequently fail to incorporate fundamental physics principles. Examples cited include inaccuracies in object counting, misrepresentation of momentum conservation, incorrect inference of Newtonian forces, and erroneous computations of velocity and pressure. This leads to videos that may appear realistic superficially but exhibit physically impossible or implausible motion trajectories.
- **Appearance-Driven vs. Physics-Grounded Generation:** Existing T2V approaches are predominantly appearance-driven, meaning they prioritize replicating visual patterns observed in training data. They lack an inherent understanding of motion dynamics, causality, or physical constraints. This design choice results in models that struggle to generalize beyond their training distribution and produce physically coherent outputs, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Limitations of Data-Driven Architectures:** The dominant paradigm in T2V, often relying on transformer-based architectures trained on massive datasets, optimizes for pattern memorization rather than causal reasoning. These models reproduce statistical correlations from the training data, leading to outputs that reflect what has been seen rather than what should logically occur according to physical laws.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Evaluation Metrics:** Conventional T2V evaluation metrics, such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), primarily assess the distributional similarity of generated pixels to ground-truth data. These metrics do not provide an indication of whether the generated videos obey underlying physical laws or maintain temporal coherence. While human-based evaluations are used, they are inherently qualitative and insufficient for precisely quantifying physical relationships. Existing physics-informed evaluation methods are often based on scene reconstruction metrics (e.g., PSNR) that overlook temporal consistency or distributional statistics (e.g., semantic adherence, average Jaccard) derived from fine-tuned encoders, which themselves can be vulnerable to OOD failures and may not reflect true physical validity.
- **Need for Reproducible and Accurate Motion:** There is a need for generative systems that can produce videos with reproducible and physically accurate motion directly from textual descriptions, especially in domains where quantitative accuracy is essential.

To address these motivations, MoReGen aims to introduce a framework that explicitly models Newtonian motion, ensures dynamic coherence, physical validity, and semantic alignment through a multi-agent system, and provides a robust, quantitative evaluation methodology for assessing these attributes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MoReGen is introduced as a multi-agent motion-reasoning engine designed for physics-grounded text-to-video (T2V) synthesis. The framework's core approach is to generate executable code for physics simulators and rendering engines, thereby enabling the synthesis of reproducible and physically accurate videos from natural language prompts. This contrasts with conventional diffusion-based pipelines that rely on probabilistic denoising.

The MoReGen framework comprises three cooperative LLM-based agents and a multi-component evaluator, orchestrated in an iterative feedback loop:

#### 3.1. Text-Parser Agent (A_text)

- **Function:** This agent is responsible for converting a free-form natural language text description of a Newtonian phenomenon into a structured simulation specification. The specification includes all necessary objects, physical parameters, and initial conditions required for simulation.
- **Training:** The agent addresses challenges like incomplete natural language descriptions and varying governing variables for different physical processes. To achieve precision, it is trained using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on phenomenon-specific structured schemas. These schemas define valid parameter ranges, physical units, essential physical entities (objects, anchors, constraints), geometric and mechanical consistency requirements, and physically coherent initial conditions.
- **Data:** A dataset of 1,200 curated text-specification pairs is used for SFT. Each output is manually validated for physical correctness and schema conformity.
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-Coder-14B is fine-tuned for five epochs using AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10−5. The model learns linguistic reasoning rules and infers missing parameters while avoiding hallucinations.

#### 3.2. Code-Writer Agent (A_coder)

- **Function:** This agent receives the structured specification from A_text and translates it into executable simulation code. This code simulates the underlying Newtonian motion process.
- **Mechanism:** It uses a unified prompt that provides a general Python class framework, outlining key simulation components such as space initialization, object creation, constraint setup, and the simulation loop. The agent populates this framework by reading fields from the structured specification, assigning numerical parameters, and selecting appropriate open-source physics engine APIs (e.g., Pymunk, Blender, Manim) to instantiate physical bodies, shapes, and constraints.
- **Output:** The generated code saves all object configurations and records system states (position, velocity, orientation) at each simulation step, forming complete temporal trajectories for each object. This ensures adherence to real-world mechanics rather than statistical approximations.

#### 3.3. Video-Render Agent (A_render)

- **Function:** This agent executes the simulation code (C_t) within a sandboxed environment and produces two synchronized outputs: telemetry data (recording each asset's spatial position, velocity, and orientation over time) and the final rendered video (v_t).
- **Mechanism:** The rendering script is generated by A_render using a single general-purpose prompt, without task-specific adaptations. It uses Pygame (which integrates with Pymunk) to interpret simulation configurations, reconstruct scene geometry, and replay recorded object states in real time.
- **Design:** The decoupled design between trajectory generation and video rendering allows for potential extension to more realistic 3D rendering engines in the future (e.g., Unreal Engine, Blender, Unity).

#### 3.4. Evaluator Design

- **Function:** The evaluator facilitates a multi-iteration refinement process to enhance the robustness and physical fidelity of the generated code and video. It analyzes the output videos and provides feedback to A_coder.
- **Process:**
 1. **Object Detection & Trajectory Estimation:** GroundedDINO, informed by object descriptors from A_text, detects object locations in the video. These locations guide CoTracker3 to estimate normalized object trajectories (T_est).
 2. **Trajectory Comparison:** An LLM (Qwen) evaluates the similarity and divergence between the estimated trajectories (T_est) and the ground-truth trajectories (T) obtained from the simulation process.
 3. **Physical & Semantic Assessment:** Qwen2.5-VL assesses the video for physical plausibility based on a set of physics rules and its alignment with the initial text prompt (x).
 4. **Feedback Synthesis:** An LLM synthesizes comprehensive feedback (F) by summarizing trajectory alignment, physical correctness, and prompt fidelity. This feedback is then provided to A_coder to guide iterative improvements in code generation and video synthesis.

#### 3.5. MoReSet Benchmark

- **Purpose:** To quantitatively assess physical validity, MoReGen introduces MoReSet, an open-source benchmark for evaluating T2V models through physics fidelity.
- **Composition:** It comprises 1,275 videos: 1,200 Blender-generated simulations for training and 75 real-world laboratory videos for testing.
- **Content:** Videos span nine fundamental categories of Newtonian physics experiments: gravity, acceleration, collision, oscillation, momentum, buoyancy, inertia, pendular motion, and pulley mechanics.
- **Annotations:** Each real-world test video is human-annotated with a natural-language description, object-level labels, environmental attributes, camera perspective, and full pixel-level ground-truth object trajectories (automatically extracted via CoTracker3 and human-corrected).

#### 3.6. MoRe Metrics

- **Purpose:** To directly measure the trajectory fidelity and physical coherence of key objects in motion, beyond pixel-level or latent-space feature similarities.
- **Metrics:**
 1. **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW):** Aligns estimated trajectories with ground-truth sequences of varying temporal lengths by projecting them into a non-linear space.
 2. **Normalized DTW (DTW-N):** Normalizes the DTW distance across clips of different scales and durations by centering and scaling trajectories to unit arc length.
 3. **Procrustes Analysis:** Assesses the geometric and structural alignment between predicted and ground-truth trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MoReGen and comparison with existing T2V models on the MoReSet benchmark yielded several findings regarding physical validity, trajectory fidelity, and the limitations of current evaluation metrics.

#### 4.1. Evaluation with MoRe Metrics

MoReGen demonstrated superior performance in generating physically accurate motion trajectories compared to both open-source and large-scale commercial T2V models.
- **Superior Trajectory Fidelity:** MoReGen consistently achieved the lowest (best) scores across all MoRe Metrics: DTW (8.93 ± 9.61), DTW-N (0.06 ± 0.07), and Procrustes (0.48 ± 0.30). This indicates a significantly better alignment with ground-truth object trajectories compared to all other evaluated models.
- **Performance of Commercial Models:** While commercial T2V engines like Veo3 (DTW: 13.35, DTW-N: 0.07, Procrustes: 0.57), Sora2 (DTW: 11.21, DTW-N: 0.08, Procrustes: 0.55), and Grok Imagine (DTW: 13.00, DTW-N: 0.08, Procrustes: 0.55) showed better visual aesthetics, this advantage did not translate to superior motion trajectory accuracy when assessed by MoRe Metrics. Their performance was comparable to, or sometimes worse than, smaller open-source models in terms of physical motion.
- **Physics-Based T2V Models:** NewtonGen (DTW: 17.88, DTW-N: 0.11, Procrustes: 0.62) and WISA (DTW: 12.67, DTW-N: 0.09, Procrustes: 0.68), which are designed to be physics-aware, did not show a clear advantage over general-purpose T2V models in motion accuracy. This suggests their fine-tuning approaches for physics validity were less effective than MoReGen's code-driven simulation.
- **Consistency:** MoReGen exhibited consistent performance across various physics principles, reflected by smaller standard deviations in its scores. In contrast, existing models displayed poor stability and large standard deviations, suggesting an imbalance in their training data and varying proficiency across different physical domains.

#### 4.2. Evaluation with Data-Driven Physics Metrics

Experiments using established physics-based evaluation frameworks, Trajan and VideoPhy2, revealed limitations in their ability to robustly assess physical validity, especially under Out-of-Distribution (OOD) conditions.
- **Inadequate Assessment:** Both Trajan (Average Jaccard, Occlusion Accuracy) and VideoPhy2 (Semantic Adherence, Physical Commonsense) frameworks frequently assigned lower scores to MoReGen's simulator-rendered videos. This outcome is attributed to the divergence of these videos from the training distributions of the metrics themselves.
- **Misleading Rankings by Trajan:** Trajan metrics awarded disproportionately high scores to models with low visual fidelity, such as LTX Video (AJ: 0.79, OA: 0.99), which struggled to generate objects resembling the prompt or produce accurate motion. Its performance according to Trajan even surpassed larger commercial models like Veo3 and Grok Imagine, which exhibited clearer physical plausibility.
- **Semantic Adherence Correlates with Visual Quality:** VideoPhy2's Semantic Adherence (SA) metric correlated strongly with visual quality rather than physical validity. It ranked visually appealing but physically inconsistent models (e.g., Sora2, Grok Imagine) higher.
- **Physical Commonsense (PC) Performance:** While VideoPhy2's Physical Commonsense (PC) metric better reflected actual dynamics and correctly favored MoReGen (PC: 4.53) and other simulator-based models, it still misranked some systems (e.g., ranking CogVideoX above more physically accurate models).
- **Conclusion:** These observations collectively indicate that current "physics-based" metrics do not reliably capture the physical validity of generated videos, highlighting a bias towards data- and aesthetics-driven approaches.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative comparisons, particularly using a Newton’s cradle prompt, reinforced the quantitative findings.
- **Object Counting and Collision Dynamics:** Most data-driven T2V models struggled with accurate object counting (e.g., generating four balls instead of five) and frequently misinterpreted collision dynamics and momentum transfer. Examples included balls being inserted perpendicularly, merging, splitting, or the system halting prematurely.
- **Motion Artifacts:** Other models introduced physically inconsistent behaviors and motion artifacts, such as unnatural string movements or objects merging and splitting.
- **MoReGen's Accuracy:** MoReGen produced a realistic Newton’s cradle with the correct number of balls and physics-valid motion, including subtle oscillations of the middle balls due to imperfect impact, details typically observed only in real-world scenarios. This demonstrated a superior physical understanding compared to other models.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study investigated the contributions of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and evaluator feedback.
- **Impact of SFT:** Incorporating SFT significantly enhanced the physical accuracy of generated videos. A medium-sized model (Qwen2.5-Coder-14B) with SFT (DTW: 8.93) outperformed a more powerful model (GPT-5 without SFT, DTW: 15.47) according to MoRe Metrics.
- **Impact of Evaluator Feedback:** Even a single iteration of evaluator feedback substantially improved model performance. The Qwen2.5-Coder-14B variant, initially unable to generate executable code, successfully produced runnable code after receiving feedback. GPT-5 also showed consistent performance gains across all evaluation metrics with feedback.
- **Metric Disagreement in Ablation:** Consistent with previous findings, Trajan (AJ, OA) and VideoPhy2 (SA) metrics continued to show disagreement with MoRe Metrics on physics validity, maintaining low scores for MoReGen's output, despite the accuracy of the physics engine. However, the Physical Commonsense (PC) metric remained consistently strong for code-driven entries, outperforming data-driven T2V models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MoReGen framework and its associated contributions represent a significant step in addressing the limitations of current Text-to-Video (T2V) generation, particularly concerning physical accuracy and motion coherence. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Bridging the Physical Plausibility Gap:** MoReGen provides a principled direction for T2V research to move beyond purely visual realism and toward physically coherent video synthesis. By demonstrating that current state-of-the-art models struggle with fundamental physics principles, the work highlights a critical research gap and offers a viable pathway to bridge the disconnect between aesthetic quality and physical validity.
- **Novel Generative Paradigm:** The introduction of a multi-agent, code-based, physics-grounded T2V framework is a departure from conventional data-driven diffusion models. By directly generating executable code for physics engines and renderers, MoReGen enables the creation of reproducible and inherently physically accurate motion sequences, offering a more robust approach to simulating dynamic phenomena.
- **Standardized Quantitative Evaluation:** The development of MoReSet, a benchmark suite with human-annotated videos across diverse Newtonian phenomena and ground-truth object trajectories, fills a crucial void in T2V evaluation. Coupled with MoRe Metrics (DTW, DTW-N, Procrustes Analysis), which directly quantify trajectory fidelity, the work establishes a standardized, motion-centric, and quantitative testbed for assessing physical validity, circumventing the biases and limitations of existing aesthetics-driven or flawed "physics-based" metrics.
- **Enhanced Model Interpretability and Controllability:** By grounding video generation in explicit physical simulations and code, MoReGen offers greater interpretability and controllability over the generated motion. This contrasts with black-box data-driven models where the underlying causes of motion are often opaque. The iterative feedback loop with an evaluator further refines the generated code and videos, contributing to more precise and robust outputs.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** MoReGen establishes a strong foundation for future research in physics-grounded T2V. The modular design, decoupling trajectory generation from video rendering, facilitates extensions to more advanced and photorealistic 3D rendering engines (e.g., Unreal Engine, Blender, Unity). This could lead to the synthesis of highly realistic videos that simultaneously adhere to complex physical laws.
- **Impact on Scientific and Creative Applications:** The ability to generate physically accurate videos from text has significant implications for various applications. In scientific domains, it could enable the simulation of complex physical experiments, aid in educational content creation for physics concepts, or assist in rapid prototyping of engineering designs. In creative industries, it could empower animators and content creators to generate dynamic scenes with consistent physics, reducing manual effort and improving realism in areas like visual effects.

In conclusion, MoReGen marks a substantial methodological contribution to T2V generation by emphasizing physical grounding and introducing robust evaluation methodologies. Its findings underscore the limitations of current T2V models in physical reasoning and offer a principled path toward synthesizing videos that are both visually compelling and physically coherent.
