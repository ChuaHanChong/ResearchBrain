# RL of Thoughts: Navigating LLM Reasoning with Inference-time Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> RL-of-Thoughts (RLoT) improves large language model reasoning at inference time by training a lightweight reinforcement learning "navigator" (under 3K parameters) to dynamically assemble problem-solving logical structures. This approach achieved a 13.4% performance boost for Llama3.1-8B-Instruct on the GPQA benchmark and enabled smaller LLMs to compete with much larger models, demonstrating computational efficiency and strong transferability.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex, multi-step reasoning due to their inherent token-level autoregressive generation, lacking sophisticated logical planning.
- Enhancing LLM reasoning through fine-tuning is computationally expensive and requires extensive specialized datasets.
- Current inference-time reasoning techniques (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) rely on predefined, task-agnostic logical structures, limiting their adaptability to specific problem characteristics or evolving reasoning states.

## Method

- The framework models long-sequence LLM reasoning as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP), defining states, actions, and rewards.
- A lightweight, three-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) "navigator" model, with under 3,000 parameters, is trained using the Double-Dueling-DQN algorithm.
- The navigator dynamically selects from five cognitive-inspired basic logic blocks (Reason, Decompose, Debate, Refine, Terminate) to guide the LLM in constructing adaptive reasoning structures.

## Results

- RLoT consistently surpassed established inference-time baselines across seven reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME, GPQA) and multiple LLMs, achieving an average performance improvement with comparable token consumption to top baselines.
- A lightweight navigator model (under 3,000 parameters) significantly enhanced sub-**10B** LLMs, enabling them to match or exceed the reasoning performance of larger models (e.g., **70B**, **200B**) using conventional methods.
- The trained navigator exhibited strong transferability, effectively improving reasoning across different LLMs and new, unseen reasoning tasks without requiring additional fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex LLM reasoning can be effectively framed as a Markov Decision Process, allowing a lightweight Reinforcement Learning agent to dynamically select reasoning steps.
> - Adaptive logical structures, constructed on-the-fly, provide superior guidance for LLMs in solving diverse and challenging reasoning problems compared to fixed, pre-defined templates.
> - A small, transferable RL navigator can significantly boost the reasoning capabilities of various LLMs, including smaller ones, while maintaining computational efficiency at inference time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities across numerous natural language processing tasks. However, their inherent token-level autoregressive nature presents limitations for complex reasoning tasks that necessitate sophisticated logical structures and long-term dependencies. The broader research landscape for enhancing LLM reasoning can be categorized into two primary approaches:

- **Fine-tuning approaches:** These methods involve updating the parameters of pre-trained LLMs to improve reasoning performance. While capable of substantial improvements, they typically require significant computational resources and large-scale datasets, rendering them costly. Recent advancements in this area include using Outcome Reward Models (ORM) and Process Reward Models (PRM) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) for reward-guided optimization of reasoning processes.
- **Inference-time techniques:** These methods enhance LLM reasoning without modifying the model's parameters, offering a more computationally efficient alternative. Prominent examples include Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT), and Graph-of-Thoughts (GoT). These techniques guide LLM reasoning through predefined external logical structures. However, a limitation of these established inference-time methods is their reliance on manually designed, task-agnostic frameworks that are applied uniformly across diverse tasks, lacking adaptability to specific problem characteristics or evolving reasoning states. More recent adaptive inference-time approaches have explored techniques like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to dynamically discover logical structures, but these often incur substantial computational costs due to their search-and-trial nature.

This work, "RL-of-Thoughts (RLoT)," positions itself within the inference-time techniques. It specifically aims to address the limitations of existing inference-time methods by introducing an adaptive mechanism for constructing task-specific logical structures. By leveraging Reinforcement Learning to train a lightweight "navigator" model, RLoT seeks to combine the cost-effectiveness of inference-time approaches with the adaptability lacking in static, predefined frameworks, while avoiding the high computational overhead of dynamic search-based methods. This approach contributes to the goal of improving LLM reasoning efficiency and flexibility, particularly for smaller LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the complex reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) at inference time by generating task-adaptive logical structures. The motivation stems from several identified limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Constraints of Token-Level Autoregressive Nature:** Despite advancements, the fundamental token-level autoregressive generation process of LLMs is not inherently optimized for complex, multi-step reasoning tasks like mathematical problem-solving or intricate question answering. These tasks often demand sophisticated logical planning and long-term coherence that go beyond simple sequential token prediction.
2. **Cost and Resource Demands of Fine-tuning:** While fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs can significantly improve reasoning, these methods typically necessitate substantial computational resources (e.g., GPUs) and extensive, often specialized, datasets. This makes them expensive and less accessible for many applications or researchers.
3. **Lack of Adaptability in Existing Inference-Time Techniques:** Current inference-time techniques, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT), and Graph-of-Thoughts (GoT), are recognized for their cost-effectiveness as they operate without modifying LLM parameters. However, their logical frameworks are often manually predefined and task-agnostic. This uniform application across diverse reasoning tasks (e.g., mathematics, STEM, commonsense) results in a lack of adaptability. They cannot dynamically adjust the reasoning structure based on the specific characteristics of a problem or the evolving problem-solving status, limiting their effectiveness in multi-step reasoning.
4. **Inefficiency of Search-and-Trial Adaptive Methods:** While some recent adaptive inference-time methods attempt to dynamically compose logical structures, many rely on search-and-trial mechanisms (e.g., Monte Carlo Tree Search). This iterative exploration and backtracking can incur significant additional computational costs during inference, diminishing their efficiency advantages.

To address these motivations, the authors propose **RL-of-Thoughts (RLoT)** with the following key objectives:
- To develop an inference-time technique that can adaptively construct logical structures tailored to specific reasoning tasks.
- To leverage Reinforcement Learning (RL) to train a lightweight "navigator" model that dynamically selects and combines basic cognitive-inspired logic blocks during the reasoning process.
- To demonstrate that this RL-trained navigator can significantly enhance the reasoning performance of various LLMs across diverse benchmarks, including challenging tasks.
- To achieve this enhancement efficiently, with a navigator model possessing a minimal number of parameters, making it computationally less demanding than fine-tuning or complex search-based methods.
- To investigate the transferability of the trained navigator model across different LLMs and unseen reasoning tasks, highlighting its generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RL-of-Thoughts (RLoT) framework enhances LLM reasoning at inference time by modeling long-sequence reasoning as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and training a lightweight Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent, referred to as the navigator model, to dynamically construct task-specific logical structures.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The RLoT framework operates by having the trained RL navigator dynamically select and combine predefined basic logic blocks throughout the reasoning process. This generates a task-specific logical structure that guides the LLM to solve complex problems. The LLM's parameters and a pre-trained Process Reward Model (PRM) remain frozen.

#### 3.2. LLM Reasoning as a Markov Decision Process (MDP)

To apply RL, long-sequence reasoning is formalized as an MDP, defined by its state space, action space, reward function, and state transition mechanism.

- **State Space (S):** The state is designed to capture the current problem-solving status, enabling dynamic adjustments to the logical structure. A self-evaluation mechanism is employed, where the LLM itself evaluates the current reasoning steps. This evaluation considers three major aspects: Correctness, Complexity, and Completeness, which are further broken down into seven detailed aspects (e.g., Correctness of modeling, Clarity for further reasoning, Complexity to the final answer). For each detailed aspect, the LLM assigns a score from 1 (False) to 3 (True), which are then aggregated to form a low-dimensional state vector. This approach summarizes complex textual reasoning into a structured, numerical representation.

- **Action Space (A):** Inspired by human cognitive strategies for problem-solving, five "basic logic blocks" are defined as the actions the RL agent can take:
 1. **Reason one step:** Instructs the LLM to perform a single reasoning step that contributes to the overall solution.
 2. **Decompose:** Directs the LLM to break down the current task into simpler subtasks, execute them sequentially, and summarize their results.
 3. **Debate:** Prompts the LLM to generate multiple plans or approaches for the current task, compare them, identify the most promising one, and then proceed with one reasoning step based on the selected plan.
 4. **Refine:** Instructs the LLM to review and revise the current reasoning step for clarity and correctness.
 5. **Terminate:** Signals the conclusion of the reasoning process, prompting the LLM to provide the final answer in a specified format.

- **Reward Function (R):** To evaluate the quality of intermediate reasoning results after each action, a pre-trained Process Reward Model (PRM) is utilized. The PRM assigns a score to the intermediate output, and this score serves as the single-step reward for the action taken. Math-Shepherd is specifically mentioned as the PRM used in this work.

- **State Transition (P):** The transition from one state to the next occurs after an action is executed. The selected action corresponds to a specific prompt that guides the LLM to continue reasoning. Following the LLM's response, the new problem-solving state is obtained by applying the self-evaluation mechanism to the updated reasoning trajectory.
 - **Restrictions:** To ensure logical coherence, simple restrictions are imposed:
 - Once an answer is produced, only the "Terminate" action is allowed.
 - "Refine" is converted to "Reason one step" if it is the first action.
 - A maximum number of actions is enforced, after which "Terminate" is automatically executed.

#### 3.3. Training of the Navigator Model

The navigator model is trained using the MDP framework.
- **RL Algorithm:** The training process is treated as a standard RL problem with a discrete action space. The Double-Dueling-DQN algorithm (combining Double Q-learning and Dueling Network architecture) is chosen for optimization, enhancing stability and mitigating value overestimation.
- **Navigator Architecture:** The navigator model is a three-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) containing less than 3,000 parameters. Its lightweight design aims for efficient training and inference.
- **Training Data and Efficiency:** Training focuses on "hard questions" extracted from the target task's training set (problems the LLM cannot solve directly). Only the navigator model's parameters are updated, while the LLM and PRM parameters remain fixed. This strategy significantly reduces computational costs.
- **Post-training Usage:** After training, the PRM is no longer needed during inference. The trained navigator directly selects actions based on the current state, guiding the LLM's reasoning path.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, efficiency, and transferability of RLoT across various reasoning benchmarks and LLMs.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

RLoT consistently outperformed established inference-time baselines (Direct QA, Zero-shot CoT, Few-shot CoT, CoT-SC, ToT) across nearly all evaluated reasoning tasks and LLMs.
- **Benchmarks:** Experiments were conducted on AIME (Olympic-level math), AMC (Olympic-level math), MATH (elementary math), GSM8K (elementary math), GPQA (STEM), MMLU-STEM (STEM), and StrategyQA (commonsense).
- **LLMs:** Tested LLMs included Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, GPT-4o-mini, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B.
- **Performance Gains:** RLoT achieved significant improvements, particularly in challenging scenarios. For instance, with Llama3.1-8B-Instruct on the GPQA benchmark, RLoT yielded a 13.4% performance boost. Across all LLMs, RLoT's average performance was higher than any baseline, with CoT-SC generally being the best-performing baseline.
- **Efficiency:** Despite being more effective, RLoT's token consumption was comparable to CoT-SC and notably lower than ToT, indicating efficient use of LLM interactions (e.g., 78% of ToT's token consumption). The navigator model itself, with fewer than 3K parameters, added negligible inference cost.

#### 4.2. Parameter Size Efficiency

RLoT demonstrated efficiency in enhancing smaller LLMs.
- A navigator model with less than 3,000 parameters was shown to significantly boost the performance of sub-10B LLMs (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, GPT-4o-mini) to levels comparable to, or even surpassing, much larger LLMs (e.g., 70B, 200B parameters) using standard Few-shot CoT. This suggests RLoT can largely compensate for the performance gap attributed to LLM parameter size in reasoning tasks.

#### 4.3. Transferability

The trained navigator model exhibited strong transferability.
- **Across Different LLMs:** A navigator model trained on one LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) on the MATH benchmark maintained consistent performance when applied to enhance other LLMs (e.g., Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, GPT-4o-mini) on the same benchmark, without further fine-tuning. In all tested cross-LLM scenarios, the RLoT-enhanced LLM outperformed the best baseline.
- **Across Different Reasoning Tasks:** A navigator trained on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct for one benchmark (e.g., MATH) effectively transferred its capabilities to other unseen reasoning tasks (e.g., GPQA, StrategyQA). While transferability was generally strong, better consistency was observed between mathematically-oriented (MATH, GPQA) tasks, with slightly more limited transfer to commonsense reasoning (StrategyQA).

#### 4.4. Typical Reasoning Patterns

Analysis of generated logical structures revealed interpretable task-specific patterns.
- **Common Patterns:** In MATH and GPQA, the "Reason-Refine" pattern frequently emerged, suggesting a strategy to perform a step and then immediately correct or clarify it. For more complex problems, "Decompose" and "Debate" operations were commonly employed to break down tasks or explore multiple solutions. "Refine" was often used before or after these complex steps to ensure coherence.
- **Case Study (Figure 5):** An example from the MATH dataset illustrated how RLoT's dynamic "Reason-Refine-Debate" sequence enabled a Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct LLM to correctly identify a geometric shape and choose an efficient solution strategy, leading to a correct answer, unlike a Few-shot CoT approach that made an error.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Analyses

- **Logic Block Effectiveness:** Ablation studies (removing individual logic blocks) confirmed that all five basic logic blocks ("Reason one step," "Decompose," "Debate," "Refine," "Terminate") contributed to the overall performance, as removing any block led to a decrease in accuracy.
- **Self-Evaluation Reliability:** Manual checks showed the LLM's self-evaluation was accurate in 82% of sampled cases. Further tests demonstrated the LLM could distinguish and classify different types of errors (modeling vs. calculation), supporting the reliability of the state representation. Robustness to noise in self-evaluation states was also shown, with performance degrading but still being comparable to baselines under 50% noise.
- **Role of RL:** Comparisons with alternative navigator training methods (fixed logic sequence, supervised-trained navigator, LLM as navigator) indicated that RL-trained navigators were superior, highlighting the importance of RL for flexible logic block selection.
- **PRM Impact:** Using a Process Reward Model (PRM) for reward signals was more effective than an Outcome Reward Model (ORM). The PRM showed a consistent positive correlation with final answer correctness across tasks and demonstrated generalization capability to out-of-domain tasks. Higher quality PRMs also led to better navigator performance, but the navigator remained effective even with degraded PRMs, given a directionally meaningful signal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents RL-of-Thoughts (RLoT) as a novel and impactful approach to enhance LLM reasoning, with several key significances:

1. **Adaptive Reasoning Enhancement:** RLoT addresses a fundamental limitation of many existing inference-time techniques by enabling LLMs to dynamically construct task-specific logical structures. This adaptability, guided by an RL navigator, allows LLMs to tailor their reasoning pathways to the unique characteristics of diverse problems, moving beyond rigid, predefined frameworks. This adaptive capability can lead to more robust and accurate solutions across a wide range of complex tasks.

2. **Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** By training a lightweight navigator model (under 3K parameters) with reinforcement learning, RLoT offers a computationally efficient alternative to parameter-heavy fine-tuning methods. The navigator guides reasoning without modifying the LLM's core parameters, making it a cost-effective solution for improving reasoning. This efficiency lowers the barrier to entry for advanced LLM reasoning, making it accessible to a broader range of applications and researchers with limited computational resources.

3. **Empowering Smaller LLMs:** A significant impact is RLoT's ability to boost the reasoning performance of sub-10B LLMs to levels comparable to, or even exceeding, much larger models (e.g., 100B-scale counterparts). This finding suggests that strategic inference-time guidance can partially mitigate the scaling law advantage, enabling smaller, more deployable LLMs to tackle complex problems typically reserved for their larger, more resource-intensive counterparts. This has implications for democratizing access to powerful reasoning capabilities and reducing the environmental footprint of large-scale AI.

4. **Strong Transferability and Generalization:** The demonstrated transferability of the trained navigator model across different LLMs and unseen reasoning tasks is noteworthy. This indicates that the learned policy for selecting logical blocks is not overfit to a specific model or task domain but captures generalizable reasoning strategies. This property reduces the need for extensive re-training, contributing further to efficiency and scalability.

5. **Interpretability of Reasoning Processes:** The analysis of typical reasoning patterns generated by RLoT (e.g., Reason-Refine, Decompose-Debate) offers insights into how the RL agent constructs effective problem-solving trajectories. These patterns align with human cognitive strategies, providing a degree of interpretability to the LLM's enhanced reasoning process.

In conclusion, RLoT contributes to the field by providing an efficient, adaptive, and transferable method for enhancing LLM reasoning at inference time. Its potential impact lies in making advanced reasoning more accessible, empowering smaller models, and paving the way for more flexible and intelligent LLM-based problem-solving systems.
