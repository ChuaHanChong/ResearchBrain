# RL of Thoughts: Navigating LLM Reasoning with Inference-time Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> RL-of-Thoughts (RLoT) improves large language model reasoning at inference time by training a lightweight reinforcement learning "navigator" (under 3K parameters) to dynamically assemble problem-solving logical structures. This approach achieved a 13.4% performance boost for Llama3.1-8B-Instruct on the GPQA benchmark and enabled smaller LLMs to compete with much larger models, demonstrating computational efficiency and strong transferability.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex, multi-step reasoning due to their inherent token-level autoregressive generation, lacking sophisticated logical planning.
- Enhancing LLM reasoning through fine-tuning is computationally expensive and requires extensive specialized datasets.
- Current inference-time reasoning techniques (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) rely on predefined, task-agnostic logical structures, limiting their adaptability to specific problem characteristics or evolving reasoning states.

## Method

- The framework models long-sequence LLM reasoning as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP), defining states, actions, and rewards.
- A lightweight, three-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) "navigator" model, with under 3,000 parameters, is trained using the Double-Dueling-DQN algorithm.
- The navigator dynamically selects from five cognitive-inspired basic logic blocks (Reason, Decompose, Debate, Refine, Terminate) to guide the LLM in constructing adaptive reasoning structures.

## Results

- RLoT consistently surpassed established inference-time baselines across seven reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME, GPQA) and multiple LLMs, achieving an average performance improvement with comparable token consumption to top baselines.
- A lightweight navigator model (under 3,000 parameters) significantly enhanced sub-**10B** LLMs, enabling them to match or exceed the reasoning performance of larger models (e.g., **70B**, **200B**) using conventional methods.
- The trained navigator exhibited strong transferability, effectively improving reasoning across different LLMs and new, unseen reasoning tasks without requiring additional fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex LLM reasoning can be effectively framed as a Markov Decision Process, allowing a lightweight Reinforcement Learning agent to dynamically select reasoning steps.
> - Adaptive logical structures, constructed on-the-fly, provide superior guidance for LLMs in solving diverse and challenging reasoning problems compared to fixed, pre-defined templates.
> - A small, transferable RL navigator can significantly boost the reasoning capabilities of various LLMs, including smaller ones, while maintaining computational efficiency at inference time.
