# RoboAgent: Chaining Basic Capabilities for Embodied Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The RoboAgent framework enhances embodied task planning by implementing a capability-driven pipeline within a single Vision-Language Model, decomposing complex tasks into five distinct vision-language capabilities. It achieved state-of-the-art performance on the EB-ALFRED and ALFWorld benchmarks, demonstrating improved task success rates and more interpretable decision-making.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face limitations in embodied task planning, particularly with multi-turn interactions, long-horizon reasoning, and managing extensive contextual dependencies.
- Intermediate reasoning traces, such as those from chain-of-thought methods, often lack principled formulation and direct supervision, making their utility and soundness for decision-making challenging to verify.
- Embodied task planning implicitly requires multiple cognitive processes like intent understanding, commonsense reasoning, and environment analysis, which existing VLM applications don't explicitly address.

## Method

- **RoboAgent** is a ==capability-driven planning architecture==, where a single Vision-Language Model acts as both a central scheduler and executes five distinct vision-language capabilities.
- Five specific capabilities (==Exploration Guidance==, ==Object Grounding==, ==Scene Description==, ==Action Decoding==, ==Experience Summarization==) are implemented.
- A ==three-stage training paradigm== is utilized, including supervised fine-tuning with expert trajectories, ==DAgger-style fine-tuning==, and reinforcement fine-tuning with the ==Expert-Induced Policy Optimization (EIPO)== algorithm.

## Results

- **RoboAgent** achieved state-of-the-art success rates on leading embodied task planning benchmarks, including **67.0%** on EB-ALFRED and **77.6%** on ALFWorld, surpassing prior open-source and many closed-source models.
- The framework demonstrated competitive performance in out-of-domain evaluations, achieving a **22.3%** success rate on EB-Habitat and a **22.1%** subgoal success rate on LoTa-WAH.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the multi-stage training and the capability-driven architecture are crucial, with the proposed approach consistently outperforming direct action generation by enabling more transparent reasoning and fine-grained supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly defining and supervising specialized vision-language capabilities within a VLM facilitates a more transparent and controllable reasoning process for embodied agents.
> - Decomposing complex embodied tasks into a sequence of simpler, capability-driven vision-language problems allows VLMs to leverage their understanding and reasoning abilities more effectively.
> - A progressive multi-stage training strategy, incorporating expert, model-generated, and augmented data with targeted reinforcement learning, systematically improves a VLM's performance and generalization in structured planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied agents has seen significant advancements, particularly with the emergence of foundation models such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs). A common approach to enable agents to handle complex tasks involves a hierarchical paradigm, where a high-level planner interprets instructions and decomposes tasks, and a low-level executor generates robot control sequences. This paper specifically addresses Embodied Task Planning (ETP), which focuses on the high-level planning aspect by abstracting navigation and manipulation into atomic actions.

While VLMs have demonstrated proficiency in multimodal understanding and reasoning, their application to embodied planning faces challenges. These challenges include the need for multi-turn interaction with environments, long-horizon reasoning, and the management of extensive contextual dependencies. Existing approaches to ETP with VLMs often involve generating intermediate reasoning traces through methods like chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning or reinforcement learning (RL) to encourage such traces. However, these traces can lack principled formulation and direct supervision, potentially limiting their soundness and utility in decision-making.

The broader landscape of embodied planning also includes efforts using large language models (LLMs) in text-based environments for plan representation, world modeling, error correction, and data construction. With visual observations, research has leveraged off-the-shelf closed-source models with enhancements such as scene graphs, multi-agent cooperation, memory modules, and replanning strategies. For open-source models, common training methodologies include supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with expert trajectories often enhanced with CoT, and RL algorithms with designed reward functions. Other methods involve collecting offline data via tree search and training models with preference optimization. There is also an ongoing effort to develop generalist foundation models for embodied intelligence and to create comprehensive evaluation benchmarks.

In reasoning with large models, researchers investigate progressive reasoning pipelines that decompose tasks into sub-tasks or sub-goals before action generation. Other studies explore guiding LLM decoding with learned reward or Q-models, enhancing RL for multi-turn interactions, and using preference optimization. Data-centric approaches focus on expanding datasets and leveraging self-generated trajectories for iterative self-improvement, or employing self-questioning mechanisms.

RoboAgent positions itself within this landscape by proposing a capability-driven planning framework that aims to bridge the gap between VLM's visual understanding and embodied planning. It differentiates from existing progressive planning frameworks by defining intermediate layers as specific, reusable vision-language capabilities rather than general sub-tasks. Unlike models that rely on self-questioning, RoboAgent introduces principled capability interfaces for fine-grained supervision. Furthermore, it implements all components (scheduler and capabilities) within a single, end-to-end trainable open-source VLM, thereby avoiding reliance on closed-source models or external tools, which is a common practice in some prior works. The paper's multi-stage training strategy is designed to systematically enhance the VLM's performance in this structured planning environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when applied to complex embodied task planning (ETP). While VLMs exhibit strong multimodal understanding, their performance in scenarios requiring multi-turn interaction, long-horizon reasoning, and extensive context analysis remains suboptimal. This gap is motivated by the inherent complexity of ETP, which implicitly demands multiple intermediate cognitive processes such as intent understanding, commonsense reasoning, environment analysis, action modeling, and progress monitoring.

The core motivation is to enable more reliable, transparent, and controllable reasoning processes in embodied agents. Existing approaches, particularly those employing chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, often generate intermediate reasoning traces that lack principled formulation and direct supervision. This can make it difficult to ascertain their soundness and utility for effective decision-making.

To address these issues, the key objectives for RoboAgent are:

1. **Decompose Complex Planning**: To break down an intricate embodied task into a sequence of simpler, more manageable vision-language problems. This decomposition is intended to allow VLMs to better address individual sub-problems.
2. **Leverage VLM Competencies**: To utilize the inherent capabilities of a VLM more effectively by explicitly invoking specialized sub-modules (capabilities) for different aspects of the planning process.
3. **Enhance Reasoning Transparency and Control**: To foster a more interpretable and controllable reasoning process. This transparency is crucial for applying fine-grained supervision during training and for diagnosing failure cases and performance bottlenecks during inference.
4. **Integrated Architecture**: To implement both the high-level scheduler and all low-level capabilities using a single, end-to-end trainable VLM, thereby eliminating the need for external tools or separate models. This design assumes that modern VLMs possess the intrinsic abilities for embodied reasoning, provided there is an appropriate mechanism to invoke these abilities.
5. **Develop an Effective Training Paradigm**: To design and implement a multi-stage training strategy that can progressively fine-tune the VLM for the proposed capability-driven pipeline. This strategy aims to leverage diverse data sources, including expert trajectories, model-generated data, and synthetic data, complemented by fine-grained supervision derived from environment simulators and reinforcement learning signals.

In essence, the work seeks to develop a robust and generalizable VLM-based agent that can perform complex embodied tasks through a structured, transparent, and trainable planning mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboAgent framework addresses Embodied Task Planning (ETP) by formulating it as a capability-driven planning problem where a single Vision-Language Model (VLM) serves as both a central scheduler and a set of specialized vision-language capabilities.

#### 3.1. Formulation

In ETP, an agent receives a text instruction `I` at the beginning of an episode. At each timestep `t`, it receives an egocentric RGB image `o_t` and generates an atomic action `a_t` from a predefined set `A`. The environment state evolves based on `s_{t+1} = E(s_t, a_t)`. Task completion is evaluated by goal conditions `{r_i}` at the episode's end.

#### 3.2. Capability-Driven Planning Architecture

The VLM `M` is responsible for generating capabilities and their queries, and then executing those capabilities. The scheduler generates queries `q_{j}^{i}` to invoke capabilities `g_{j}^{i}` suitable for the current context `c_{S}^{i}`. Each invoked capability `M(p_{g_{j}^{i}}, q_{j}^{i}, o_{j}^{i})` can either produce a sequence of atomic actions `a_{j}^{i}` or a textual feedback `f_{j}^{i}` to the scheduler. This feedback updates the scheduler's context.

Five distinct capabilities are implemented:
- **Exploration Guidance (EG)**: Given a target object, it predicts a promising exploration direction based on commonsense knowledge of scene layouts and object placements. It also uses a simple memory to avoid repeating failed exploration directions.
- **Object Grounding (OG)**: Performs open-vocabulary grounding to detect if a specific object is observable in the current view, outputting JSON-formatted bounding box annotations and an object label.
- **Scene Description (SD)**: Generates a textual description of a target object's state, based on the image observation, to inform subsequent manipulation.
- **Action Decoding (AD)**: Translates a navigation or manipulation command into an executable sequence of atomic actions. This capability is divided into two modes: one for exploration (navigational actions) and one for manipulation (control actions on target objects, considering agent inventory and location).
- **Experience Summarization (ES)**: Summarizes the outcome of the most recent action sequence, including success/failure and reasons for failure.

The scheduler maintains a history of queries and feedback, retaining feedback from OG (object found/not found) and ES (manipulation success/failure). Its output consists of a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process and a sequence of capability invocations with arguments.

#### 3.3. Multi-Stage Training Strategy

A three-stage training paradigm is employed to fine-tune the VLM:

- **Stage 1: Training with Expert Trajectories (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)**
 - **Scheduler Training**: Expert plans are segmented into exploration and manipulation sub-plans, based on key objects. An off-the-shelf LLM (GPT-4.1-mini) parses task instructions into object descriptions, which form queries for EG and OG. Rule-based approaches categorize manipulation sub-plans to generate queries for AD and ES. Template-based CoT traces are constructed, including task retelling, sub-plan lists, and next sub-plan targets. This process transforms expert plans into sequences of capability invocations.
 - **Capability Training**: Leverages internal information from the environment simulator for high-quality supervision:
 - **EG**: Ground-truth object locations from the scene graph (SG).
 - **OG**: Instance segmentation masks of target objects converted to JSON bounding box annotations.
 - **SD**: Textual descriptions of object states and relations extracted from filtered SG (based on observable objects).
 - **AD**: Directly from corresponding action sequences in expert trajectories.
 - **ES**: Environment messages indicating action success/failure.
 - To assess reliability, capability invocation sequences are executed with "perfect" (or perturbed for EG) capability implementations to filter for successful task instances.

- **Stage 2: Training with Model-Generated Data (DAgger-style SFT)**
 - The SFT-trained model is deployed to collect new trajectories and capability invocation sequences.
 - Corrective ground-truth outputs for capabilities are constructed from simulator information. For EG and OG, if model queries semantically match ground-truth queries, supervision for the target object is retrieved from SG/segmentation masks. For AD, ground-truth action sequences are constructed based on predefined sub-plan categories and SG object states.
 - **Data Augmentation**:
 - LLM-generated descriptive phrases/synonyms replace object references in EG/OG queries to improve open-vocabulary comprehension.
 - Atomic action formats are modified (e.g., "pick up" vs. "grasp") to enhance AD/ES robustness.
 - Synthetic samples are created for AD by substituting object categories.

- **Stage 3: Training with Expert Policy (Reinforcement Fine-Tuning - RFT)**
 - Aims to enhance scheduler reasoning, especially for multi-turn interactions.
 - **Expert-Induced Policy Optimization (EIPO)** algorithm is introduced. This algorithm optimizes the expert's advantage `A_π*(s,a)` to maximize the discrepancy between the current policy's return and the expert's return. It avoids Monte Carlo estimation of future returns by computing advantage directly from an expert policy `π*`. It incorporates importance sampling, PPO-style clipping, and GRPO-style group normalization to reduce variance.
 - **Scheduler as Policy**: The scheduler's input `s_t` is the history of queries and feedback, and its action `a_t` is the invoked capabilities and queries.
 - **Expert Scheduler (π*_S)**: Defined to monitor sub-plan completion and sequentially convert unfinished sub-plans into capability invocations.
 - **Reward Function**: A reward of +1 is assigned when an invoked capability completes a manipulation sub-plan. `V_π*` is computed based on remaining sub-plans.
 - **Offline Dataset**: An offline dataset `D` is constructed by synthesizing multi-turn interaction contexts. This involves using the expert scheduler to generate capability invocations and then injecting random errors into the capability feedback messages (e.g., OG reporting "not found," ES reporting "failure") to simulate real-world uncertainty and error recovery scenarios. This data is augmented with object descriptions, action spaces, and composite tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed RoboAgent framework across various benchmarks and simulators.

#### 4.1. Performance on Benchmarks

RoboAgent achieved leading performance on two widely used Embodied Task Planning (ETP) environments: ALFWorld and EB-ALFRED.

- **EB-ALFRED**: RoboAgent (using Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieved an average success rate (SR) of 67.0%, surpassing all existing fine-tuning-based methods and outperforming powerful closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) on the base, visual appearance, and long-horizon splits. The benchmark emphasizes generalization to diverse instruction styles, including complex syntax and referential expressions.
- **ALFWorld (Visual Observation)**: RoboAgent (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) demonstrated a significant improvement with an average SR of 77.6%, markedly outperforming existing RL-based methods (e.g., SEEA-R1, GFlowVLM, RL4VLM) which typically use larger models. This improvement is attributed to the explicit exploration process facilitated by the EG and OG capabilities, which learn to identify likely object locations through high-quality supervision, mitigating sparse reward challenges in exploration-intensive tasks.
- **ALFWorld (Textual Observation)**: When adapted to a text-only mode by modifying image-related capabilities, RoboAgent achieved a seen SR of 92.1% and an unseen SR of 94.0%. These results are competitive with recent LLM-based agents designed for text-only tasks and built upon larger backbones, suggesting that the approach acquires modality-agnostic planning skills.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Domain (OOD) Evaluation

Evaluations on EB-Habitat (visual environment) and LoTa-WAH (textual environment), built on different simulators (Habitat and VirtualHome respectively), were conducted to assess cross-domain generalization.

- **EB-Habitat**: RoboAgent achieved an SR of 22.3%, outperforming other open-source models trained on the ALFRED dataset (e.g., REBP, RoboGPT-R1) but still showed a noticeable gap compared to zero-shot, closed-source baselines (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet). This suggests that significant domain discrepancy between simulators remains a challenge for full generalization, especially in visual recognition due to less realistic rendering and novel object categories.
- **LoTa-WAH**: RoboAgent attained a subgoal success rate (SSR) of 22.1%, outperforming LLaMA-7B and LLaMA-30B, but falling short of GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies validated the contributions of each training stage and data source:

- **SFT on expert trajectories** established fundamental planning capabilities and formatted outputs.
- **Augmented expert data** in Stage 2 (DAgger-style SFT) improved performance on EB-ALFRED due to enhanced open-ended instruction understanding.
- **Model-generated data** (DAgger) provided significant performance gains on ALFWorld, highlighting the importance of fine-grained corrective supervision for capability learning.
- **RFT (EIPO)** stage, particularly with augmented and synthetic data, further enhanced scheduler performance, improving generalization to diverse states and complex tasks. Training solely on expert trajectories in RFT had limited effect due to the model's existing familiarity.
- **EIPO Algorithm**: A comparison with GRPO (a traditional RL algorithm) in an online action policy setting demonstrated that EIPO achieved higher SR within the same number of iterations, attributed to its more stable objective of maximizing expert advantage. Similarly, for the scheduler, EIPO consistently outperformed a reward-based baseline (which is analogous to GRPO on single-turn QA datasets) especially on longer-horizon tasks.

#### 4.4. Impact of the Capability-Driven Framework

A comparison with a plain VLM planner that directly generates actions (without explicit capability invocation) showed that the capability-driven approach consistently outperformed the direct planner. The plain planner exhibited higher frequencies of invalid actions, misinterpretations of task progress, and failures in object localization. This indicates that capabilities enable a clearer reasoning process and allow for more fine-grained supervisory signals during training.

#### 4.5. Efficiency

The capability-driven planning pipeline showed comparable overall runtime to action-based planners despite an increased number of VLM calls. This is due to the shorter, more concise prompts and outputs associated with individual capabilities.

#### 4.6. Error Analysis

Manual analysis of failed tasks revealed common failure modes:
- **EB-ALFRED**: Approximately half of failures stemmed from visual grounding (object recognition and open-vocabulary referring). Other significant sources included instruction parsing errors (~18%), low-level control errors (~17%), and instruction ambiguity (~9%).
- **ALFWorld**: Object recognition accounted for ~43% of failures, followed by failed exploration (~20%) and low-level control errors (~20%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboAgent framework presents a significant advancement in embodied task planning by introducing a capability-driven pipeline that systematically decomposes complex tasks into a series of basic vision-language understanding problems. This approach contributes to making the planning process more transparent, controllable, and interpretable, which is crucial for the development and debugging of intelligent agents.

The core impact of this work stems from several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced VLM Utility**: By formulating explicit vision-language capabilities (Exploration Guidance, Object Grounding, Scene Description, Action Decoding, Experience Summarization) and integrating them within a single VLM architecture, RoboAgent effectively leverages the inherent competencies of large foundation models for embodied reasoning. This demonstrates that VLMs can function as comprehensive cognitive engines for agents without relying on external, separate tools.
2. **Robust Training Paradigm**: The proposed multi-stage training strategy, which combines supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on expert data, DAgger-style SFT with model-generated data, and reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) using the Expert-Induced Policy Optimization (EIPO) algorithm, is a robust methodology. It systematically addresses challenges like out-of-distribution states, limited training data, and the need for high-quality intermediate supervision, contributing to the model's overall performance and generalization.
3. **Improved Generalization and Performance**: RoboAgent achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging embodied task planning benchmarks like EB-ALFRED and ALFWorld. Its ability to generalize across diverse instruction styles, novel scenes, and even different modalities (visual and textual environments) highlights a step towards more versatile and adaptable embodied AI agents. The approach's out-of-domain evaluation on EB-Habitat and LoTa-WAH, while showing room for improvement, indicates promising cross-simulator transfer capabilities compared to other open-source models.
4. **Principled Reasoning**: The explicit invocation of capabilities and the fine-grained supervision applied during training facilitate a more principled and verifiable reasoning process. This contrasts with less structured chain-of-thought methods, offering a clearer pathway for understanding agent decisions and pinpointing sources of failure. The error analysis further underscores this benefit, providing clear categories for future research directions.
5. **Practical Algorithm for RFT**: The Expert-Induced Policy Optimization (EIPO) algorithm offers a stable and effective method for offline policy optimization. By leveraging the expert's advantage, it bypasses complex estimations of returns and state values, which can be prone to variance, making it a valuable contribution to training large language and vision models for sequential decision-making.

The research opens avenues for future work, including scaling up training data for broader generalization, exploring dynamic and evolving sets of capabilities, and extending the framework to an even wider array of agentic tasks. Ultimately, RoboAgent's capability-driven design and systematic training approach contribute to the development of more intelligent, reliable, and interpretable embodied agents capable of navigating and manipulating in complex real-world environments.
