# Learning to Recover: Dynamic Reward Shaping with Wheel-Leg Coordination for Fallen Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research presents an asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework, incorporating Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping and curriculum learning, to achieve autonomous fall recovery for wheeled-legged robots. The approach demonstrates up to a 99.1% recovery success rate and quantifies a 15-26% reduction in joint torque consumption, alongside robust zero-shot transferability across different robot platforms.

## Problem

- Wheeled-legged robots lack robust and autonomous post-fall recovery mechanisms, impeding their deployment in unpredictable real-world environments.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for recovery often suffer from sparse reward signals, leading to inefficient exploration and unstable policy convergence, with limited transferability across diverse robot platforms.
- The full potential of active wheel-leg coordination for stable and energy-efficient recovery in hybrid robots remains largely unutilized and unquantified.

## Method

- An asymmetric actor-critic PPO framework is employed, leveraging privileged simulation information for the critic and real-world observable data for the actor, trained within a simulated environment using randomized initial fall states.
- Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED) is introduced to dynamically modulate task-related rewards, balancing initial exploration of diverse recovery maneuvers with later-stage refinement of the target upright posture.
- Curriculum learning and extensive domain randomization are integrated to enhance training efficiency, improve policy robustness against real-world uncertainties, and facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across different robot platforms.

## Results

- The Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED) policy achieved a **99.1%** recovery success rate, outperforming a fixed-reward baseline by **2.7** percentage points, and demonstrated a wider range of explored recovery maneuvers.
- Wheel-leg collaborative recovery reduced average joint torque consumption by **15.85%** on the KYON platform and **26.24%** on the Unitree Go2-W, while also enhancing base height stability during recovery.
- The policy successfully transferred to the Unitree Go2-W robot with a **97.8%** recovery success rate (**3.7** percentage points higher than its baseline), proving robust in real-world tests under repeated perturbations and exhibiting partial adaptability on unseen uneven terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The dynamic reward shaping (ED) mechanism effectively resolves the exploration-convergence dilemma in complex, sparse-reward tasks by progressively tightening reward constraints, enabling both broad action space exploration and precise posture control.
> - Active wheel-leg coordination significantly improves recovery stability and energy efficiency, demonstrating a 15-26% reduction in average joint torque by leveraging wheel actuation for centroidal adjustments and controlled sliding contacts.
> - The developed framework enables robust cross-platform generalization and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, allowing policies trained in simulation to operate effectively on distinct physical robots without platform-specific tuning.
