# Learning to Recover: Dynamic Reward Shaping with Wheel-Leg Coordination for Fallen Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research presents an asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework, incorporating Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping and curriculum learning, to achieve autonomous fall recovery for wheeled-legged robots. The approach demonstrates up to a 99.1% recovery success rate and quantifies a 15-26% reduction in joint torque consumption, alongside robust zero-shot transferability across different robot platforms.

## Problem

- Wheeled-legged robots lack robust and autonomous post-fall recovery mechanisms, impeding their deployment in unpredictable real-world environments.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for recovery often suffer from sparse reward signals, leading to inefficient exploration and unstable policy convergence, with limited transferability across diverse robot platforms.
- The full potential of active wheel-leg coordination for stable and energy-efficient recovery in hybrid robots remains largely unutilized and unquantified.

## Method

- An asymmetric actor-critic PPO framework is employed, leveraging privileged simulation information for the critic and real-world observable data for the actor, trained within a simulated environment using randomized initial fall states.
- Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED) is introduced to dynamically modulate task-related rewards, balancing initial exploration of diverse recovery maneuvers with later-stage refinement of the target upright posture.
- Curriculum learning and extensive domain randomization are integrated to enhance training efficiency, improve policy robustness against real-world uncertainties, and facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across different robot platforms.

## Results

- The Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED) policy achieved a **99.1%** recovery success rate, outperforming a fixed-reward baseline by **2.7** percentage points, and demonstrated a wider range of explored recovery maneuvers.
- Wheel-leg collaborative recovery reduced average joint torque consumption by **15.85%** on the KYON platform and **26.24%** on the Unitree Go2-W, while also enhancing base height stability during recovery.
- The policy successfully transferred to the Unitree Go2-W robot with a **97.8%** recovery success rate (**3.7** percentage points higher than its baseline), proving robust in real-world tests under repeated perturbations and exhibiting partial adaptability on unseen uneven terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The dynamic reward shaping (ED) mechanism effectively resolves the exploration-convergence dilemma in complex, sparse-reward tasks by progressively tightening reward constraints, enabling both broad action space exploration and precise posture control.
> - Active wheel-leg coordination significantly improves recovery stability and energy efficiency, demonstrating a 15-26% reduction in average joint torque by leveraging wheel actuation for centroidal adjustments and controlled sliding contacts.
> - The developed framework enables robust cross-platform generalization and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, allowing policies trained in simulation to operate effectively on distinct physical robots without platform-specific tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in robotics: enabling wheeled-legged robots to autonomously recover from falls. This capability is paramount for the practical deployment of such robots in real-world scenarios, where dynamic disturbances like collisions or slippage are inevitable. Wheeled-legged robots are gaining prominence for their unique mobility, combining the agility of legs for navigating uneven terrain with the speed and efficiency of wheels on flat surfaces, making them ideal for long-duration tasks like inspection and exploration.

Historically, post-fall recovery for legged and wheeled-legged robots has been tackled through several avenues:
- **Preplanned Motions and Simplified Dynamics:** Early approaches often relied on predefined motion sequences or simplified dynamic models to derive recovery actions. While effective on flat terrains, these methods typically lack robustness against diverse terrain geometries, are heavily dependent on model accuracy, and require significant manual effort for tuning, limiting their portability across different platforms. Examples include methods focused on pure legged recovery or using simplified models.
- **Optimization-Based Methods:** These approaches formulate recovery as an optimization problem, minimizing the discrepancy between current and target states. However, the highly nonlinear dynamics of wheeled-legged robots and the non-convex nature of the recovery task make deriving optimal action sequences extremely challenging.
- **Learning-Based (Reinforcement Learning - RL) Approaches:** In recent years, RL has shown immense promise due to its model-free nature and ability to learn complex policies directly through interaction. Notable RL-based works include hierarchical controllers, single controllers optimizing base height, and arm-assisted recovery for mobile manipulators. However, these RL methods frequently encounter issues such as sparse and delayed reward signals (hindering exploration), unstable convergence, and poor transferability across different robot platforms. Many also focus solely on flat terrains, leaving the full potential of wheel-leg collaboration largely unaddressed.

This paper positions itself directly within the learning-based paradigm, specifically aiming to overcome the limitations of existing RL-based recovery methods. It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Focusing on Wheeled-Legged Robots:** Explicitly leveraging the unique synergy of wheels and legs, which has been largely unexplored in full capacity for recovery.
2. **Addressing RL Challenges:** Introducing novel mechanisms like Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED) and integrating curriculum learning to mitigate sparse rewards, balance exploration and exploitation, and improve policy robustness and convergence stability.
3. **Achieving Cross-Platform Generalization:** Demonstrating robust performance across distinct robot platforms without platform-specific tuning, which is a significant step towards real-world deployability.
4. **Quantifying Wheel-Leg Synergy:** Systematically validating the critical role of wheel actuation in improving stability and reducing joint torque, providing deeper insights into hybrid locomotion control.

By tackling these gaps, the research contributes to making wheeled-legged robots more autonomous and resilient, moving the field closer to fully autonomous missions in complex, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the practical limitations of current post-fall recovery methods for wheeled-legged robots. While these robots offer unparalleled mobility, unexpected falls are a major impediment to their autonomous operation in dynamic and complex environments. Reliance on manual intervention or preplanned, terrain-agnostic motions severely restricts their operational efficiency and autonomy.

The paper identifies several critical challenges:
- **Complexity of Recovery:** Fall recovery is a highly nonlinear, discontinuous motion planning and control problem, involving precise posture adjustments and multiple contact events, often with uncertain ground geometry.
- **Limitations of Traditional Methods:** Optimization-based methods struggle with the highly nonlinear dynamics and require accurate models and state estimation, making them brittle.
- **Shortcomings of Existing RL Approaches:** Current RL methods for recovery often suffer from sparse reward signals, leading to inefficient exploration and unstable policy convergence. They also tend to be less transferable across different robot platforms and primarily focus on flat terrains.
- **Underutilization of Wheel-Leg Synergy:** The full potential of active wheel-leg coordination for robust recovery has not been adequately explored or quantified.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop an Adaptive Learning Framework for Robust Recovery:** Introduce a learning-based framework that integrates Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping and curriculum learning to balance the exploration of diverse recovery maneuvers with the refinement of precise final postures, thereby overcoming the challenges of sparse rewards and unstable convergence in RL.
2. **Leverage Asymmetric Actor-Critic Architecture:** Utilize privileged simulation information in an asymmetric actor-critic PPO framework to accelerate training and enhance Q-value estimation accuracy.
3. **Quantify the Benefits of Wheel-Leg Coordination:** Systematically validate and quantify how synergistic wheel actuation, alongside leg movements, can reduce joint torque consumption and improve stabilization during recovery, offering new insights into energy transfer mechanisms.
4. **Achieve Cross-Platform Generalization:** Demonstrate the framework's adaptability and robustness across distinct wheeled-legged robot platforms (KYON and Unitree Go2-W) without platform-specific tuning, ensuring high recovery success rates.
5. **Enhance Robustness to Uncertainty:** Incorporate noise-injected observations to improve the policy's resilience against state-estimation inaccuracies inherent in real-world deployment.

In essence, the research aims to create a highly robust, transferable, and energy-efficient autonomous fall recovery solution for wheeled-legged robots by advancing reinforcement learning techniques and fully exploiting their hybrid locomotion capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on an advanced reinforcement learning framework, specifically an asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) architecture, carefully designed to facilitate robust and efficient post-fall recovery for wheeled-legged robots. The training pipeline is implemented within the Isaac Lab framework, a unified simulation environment for interactive robot learning.

**A. State Initialization and Rollout:**
To ensure policy generalization, the robot's initial fallen states are highly randomized. Robots are initialized with random base orientations and joint angles, then allowed to free-fall from 1.1 meters for 2 seconds with zero joint torques. This procedure generates diverse and realistic initial collapse states, preventing unrealistic dynamics. Each training episode lasts for a fixed duration of 5 seconds without early termination.

**B. Privileged Information-based Actor-Critic:**
The framework employs an **asymmetric actor-critic architecture**. This design allows the critic network to access "privileged information" available only in simulation, enabling more accurate Q-value estimates and faster learning, while the actor operates solely on real-world observable information for deployment.
- **Actor Observations:** The actor receives proprioceptive feedback, including current and historical (0.01s interval) linear and angular velocities, body orientation, joint positions, joint velocities, and wheel speeds. The previous action is also provided. Crucially, Gaussian noise is added to most observations (especially joint velocities and base linear/angular velocities) to simulate real-world sensor inaccuracies and enhance robustness.
- **Critic Observations:** In addition to the actor's observations, the critic leverages privileged information such as collision states (to prevent damaging actions) and Vector-to-Goal (VTG) information (to accelerate learning by providing clearer guidance towards the target state).

**C. Actions:**
The policy network outputs scaled target values for joint positions and wheel velocities. These commands are then sent to the robot's onboard PD controllers for execution. Scaling factors (`s_p`, `s_v`) and default values (`q`, `q_v`) are used. A key observation from experiments led to setting `s_v < 1.0` to reduce sensitivity to velocity control changes, improving motion stability.

**D. Dynamic Exploration Strategy:**
This is a central innovation designed to overcome the exploration-convergence dilemma in RL for complex recovery tasks.
- **Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED):** This mechanism dynamically modulates the task-related reward signal throughout an episode. It introduces a factor `ed = (a * t/T)^k`, where `t` is the current step, `T` is the total episode steps, `k` is the growth rate, and `a` is a baseline coefficient. In early stages (t close to 0), `ed` is small, allowing for broad exploration of diverse and potentially counter-intuitive rolling/flipping maneuvers without strong penalties for deviation from the final pose. As the episode progresses (t approaches T), `ed` increases significantly, gradually tightening constraints and emphasizing precise posture control and refinement of the target pose. This balances exploration in early stages with convergence in later stages, making the policy more resilient.
- **Curriculum Learning (CW):** To further boost training efficiency and counteract potential slowdowns from dynamic reward shaping, curriculum learning is integrated. A curriculum weight `cw = cw^β` (where `β` is the progress rate) is applied to behavior rewards. This weight decays exponentially over training, ensuring rapid acquisition of task-related behaviors initially and promoting smoother, more refined motions in later training stages.

**E. Reward Function:**
The total reward `r_t` is a sum of task-related rewards and behavior-constraining rewards, carefully scaled and modulated by the dynamic factors:
`r_t = ed * (r_joint_pos + r_base_height + r_base_ori) + cw * (r_collision + r_action_rate + r_joint_vel + r_torque + r_joint_acc) + r_wheel_vel`
- **Task Rewards (modulated by `ed`):**
 - **Base Height:** Penalizes deviation from desired upright height.
 - **Base Orientation:** Penalizes misalignment of the robot's gravity vector with the ideal vertical.
 - **Joint Position:** Penalizes deviation from default joint angles.
- **Behavior Rewards (modulated by `cw`):**
 - **Body Collision:** Penalizes impacts to prevent damage.
 - **Action Rate:** Penalizes large changes in actions (excluding wheel actions for greater flexibility).
 - **Joint Velocity & Acceleration:** Penalizes high joint speeds and rapid accelerations for smoother motion.
 - **Torques:** Penalizes high joint torques to promote energy efficiency.
 - **Wheel Velocity:** Penalizes excessive wheel velocities (outside `cw` modulation).
- **Support State:** A reward for having all four wheels in contact, encouraging stable upright posture.

**F. Simulation Setup and Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
All training is conducted in the Isaac Sim simulator using models of the KYON and Unitree Go2-W robots. To enhance policy robustness and facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, extensive **domain randomization** is applied to various physical parameters (e.g., base mass, link masses, static friction, actuator stiffness/damping) and action noise. The trained policy is then directly deployed on the Unitree Go2-W hardware without any platform-specific tuning, demonstrating zero-shot transferability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several compelling findings, validated through extensive simulations and real-world experiments on two distinct robot platforms.

**A. Ablation of Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED) Mechanism:**
- The ED-Policy significantly improved recovery performance compared to a baseline (fixed reward function). The ED-Policy achieved a 99.1% success rate, a 2.7 percentage point improvement over the baseline's 96.4%.
- Analysis revealed that the baseline often failed in scenarios requiring substantial front leg motion, getting trapped in local optima due to strong penalties on joint angle deviations. The ED-Policy's dynamic modulation allowed for broader exploration of action space.
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA) of action sequences confirmed this: the ED-Policy exhibited significantly higher variance along principal components (e.g., σ²PC1 = 20.91 vs. 17.33 for baseline), indicating a wider range of explored recovery maneuvers and a less conservative policy.

**B. Quantified Wheel-Assisted Recovery:**
- A comparison between wheel-leg collaborative mode and a pure legged mode (fixed wheels) on the KYON platform demonstrated clear advantages of wheel actuation.
- **Enhanced Stability:** The wheel-leg collaborative group showed lower variance in base height trajectories during both the recovery and stabilization phases, indicating a more stable recovery process and reduced body oscillations.
- **Reduced Joint Torque Consumption:** The average joint torque in the wheel-leg collaborative group was 35.776 N·m, representing a 15.85% reduction compared to the pure legged group (42.515 N·m). This is attributed to the conversion of wheel kinetic energy into joint potential energy and the ability of active wheels to facilitate sliding contacts, which reduces internal forces and stress on leg joints.

**C. Cross-Platform Generalization:**
- The ED strategy was successfully deployed on the Unitree Go2-W, a platform with significantly different kinematic and dynamic parameters.
- **Robust Performance:** Despite hardware differences, the ED strategy maintained robust recovery performance, achieving a 97.8% success rate, a 3.7 percentage point improvement over the Unitree baseline (94.1%).
- **Torque Reduction Consistency:** Similar to KYON, wheel-leg coordination on Go2-W resulted in a substantial 26.24% reduction in average joint torque (7.123 N·m vs. 9.657 N·m). Active wheel rotation buffered posture adjustments, reducing the load on hip joints.
- **Feasibility Confirmation:** Quantified analysis of joint velocities and torques confirmed that the generated commands remained within the robots' operational limits, underscoring the real-world feasibility of the simulation-trained policy.

**D. Recovery Under Repeated Perturbations (Real-World Sim-to-Real):**
- Real-world tests on the Unitree Go2-W involved manual perturbations after the robot had initially recovered. The policy demonstrated autonomous and sustained recovery capabilities, correcting motions and initiating subsequent recovery attempts even after insufficient initial recovery, without being constrained by the fixed-step training regime.

**E. Recovery Adaptability on Unseen Terrains:**
- The policy was evaluated on five procedurally generated, uneven terrains (Random Boxes, Rough Terrain, Sloped Pyramid, Pyramid Stairs, Inverted Pyramid Stairs).
- **Partial Zero-Shot Generalization:** The robot exhibited partial adaptation, maintaining stability through controlled wheel-leg coordination despite the lack of explicit terrain sensing during training. The ED strategy still outperformed the baseline on non-flat terrains (78.6% vs. 61.8% success rate on KYON).
- **Limitations on Complex Terrains:** Recovery success rates declined significantly on these challenging terrains, particularly stairs, with frequent secondary falls. This highlights current limitations: lack of explicit terrain perception leads to delayed responses to abrupt contact changes, and posture adaptation is configuration-driven rather than truly terrain-aware, resulting in suboptimal stability margins.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes significant contributions to the field of robotics and holds considerable potential impact.

#### 5.1. Significance

1. **Breakthrough in Robust Fall Recovery:** The paper introduces a highly effective, learning-based framework that addresses the long-standing challenge of robust and autonomous post-fall recovery for wheeled-legged robots. It moves beyond brittle preplanned motions and the limitations of previous RL methods.
2. **Novel RL Methodologies:** The Episode-based Dynamic Reward Shaping (ED) mechanism is a crucial innovation that effectively resolves the exploration-convergence dilemma in sparse-reward RL. By dynamically adjusting reward emphasis, it enables both broad exploration of diverse recovery strategies and precise refinement of target postures, leading to more resilient policies.
3. **Quantified Wheel-Leg Synergy:** For the first time, this work systematically quantifies the substantial benefits of active wheel-leg coordination in recovery. Demonstrating 15-26% reduction in joint torque consumption and improved stability highlights the energy efficiency and dynamic capabilities unlocked by leveraging the wheels for centroidal adjustments and contact force regulation. This provides critical insights for the design and control of future hybrid locomotion systems.
4. **Exceptional Generalization Capabilities:** The remarkable cross-platform generalization (KYON and Unitree Go2-W) without platform-specific tuning is a testament to the framework's robustness and scalability. This "zero-shot" transferability is a highly desirable quality for real-world robotic systems, significantly reducing development and deployment costs.
5. **Enhanced Real-World Deployability:** The combination of dynamic reward shaping, curriculum learning, asymmetric actor-critic with privileged information, and extensive domain randomization directly translates to policies that are more robust to real-world uncertainties, sensor noise, and repeated perturbations.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Expanded Autonomy for Hybrid Robots:** The developed recovery capabilities are critical for enabling wheeled-legged robots to perform long-duration autonomous missions in complex, unstructured, and unpredictable environments (e.g., industrial inspection, disaster response, planetary exploration) without human intervention after a fall.
2. **Foundation for Advanced Hybrid Locomotion:** The quantified benefits of wheel-leg coordination could influence future robot designs and control strategies, promoting a deeper integration of wheeled and legged modalities for tasks beyond recovery, such as dynamic locomotion, manipulation, and interaction with the environment.
3. **Generalization to Other Complex Robotic Tasks:** The principles of dynamic reward shaping and adaptive curriculum learning, which effectively manage exploration and exploitation in environments with discontinuous contacts, could be generalized to a wide array of other challenging robotic tasks. This includes multi-robot collaborative recovery, manipulation-oriented posture adjustment, and adaptive locomotion in highly cluttered or deformable terrains, potentially leading to more versatile, robust, and energy-efficient robotic behaviors across the board.
4. **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer further validates the effectiveness of robust simulation training techniques (like domain randomization) and paves the way for accelerating robotic development by reducing the need for extensive real-world experimentation.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robust robot recovery, providing a powerful framework that leverages the unique advantages of wheeled-legged systems through innovative reinforcement learning techniques. Its findings pave the way for more autonomous, resilient, and energy-efficient robots capable of operating in challenging real-world scenarios.
