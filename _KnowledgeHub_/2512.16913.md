# Depth Any Panoramas: A Foundation Model for Panoramic Depth Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Insta360 Research and universities developed DAP (Depth Any Panoramas), a foundation model for panoramic metric depth estimation that leverages a 2-million-sample data engine and a three-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline. This model achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on public benchmarks, reducing AbsRel to 0.0921 on Stanford2D3D and 0.0781 on the new DAP-Test benchmark, providing robust and geometrically consistent depth across diverse environments.

## Problem

- Panoramic depth estimation significantly lags behind its perspective counterpart due to limited large-scale, diverse, and accurately annotated datasets.
- Existing panoramic depth models struggle with generalization to varied real-world scenes, particularly complex outdoor environments, and are hindered by equirectangular projection (ERP) distortions.
- Reliable metric (absolute scale) depth estimation for panoramas across arbitrary distances and diverse environments remains a challenge for practical applications.

## Method

- Introduced **DAP**, a panoramic metric depth foundation model, built upon a 'data-in-the-loop' paradigm with a massive data engine comprising 2 million synthetic and real panoramic images.
- Developed a three-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline to efficiently exploit unlabeled real-world data and bridge synthetic-to-real and indoor-to-outdoor domain gaps.
- Designed a geometry-consistent network with a DINOv3-Large backbone, a plug-and-play range mask head, and multi-loss optimization incorporating distortion map compensation, sharpness-centric, and geometry-centric losses.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on public benchmarks, with an AbsRel of 0.0921 on Stanford2D3D and 0.0781 on the newly introduced DAP-Test benchmark, outperforming previous metric methods by a considerable margin.
- Produced qualitatively superior results, demonstrating sharper object boundaries, smoother global geometry, and enhanced robustness in challenging areas like distant regions and sky areas compared to existing models.
- Ablation studies confirmed the incremental benefits of each proposed component, including distortion map compensation, geometry-centric losses (L_normal, L_pts), sharpness-centric losses (L_DF, L_grad), and the range mask head, validating their contribution to performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a foundation model on a massive, diverse dataset (synthetic, real, indoor, outdoor) through a 'data-in-the-loop' approach is crucial for achieving robust generalization in panoramic depth estimation.
> - A multi-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline, integrating a depth quality discriminator, effectively mitigates domain gaps between synthetic and real data, and between indoor and outdoor scenes.
> - Combining a powerful Vision Transformer backbone with specialized heads (like the range mask head) and a suite of distortion-aware, geometry-centric, and sharpness-centric losses ensures high-quality and consistent metric depth predictions for panoramas.
