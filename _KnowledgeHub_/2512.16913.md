# Depth Any Panoramas: A Foundation Model for Panoramic Depth Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Insta360 Research and universities developed DAP (Depth Any Panoramas), a foundation model for panoramic metric depth estimation that leverages a 2-million-sample data engine and a three-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline. This model achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on public benchmarks, reducing AbsRel to 0.0921 on Stanford2D3D and 0.0781 on the new DAP-Test benchmark, providing robust and geometrically consistent depth across diverse environments.

## Problem

- Panoramic depth estimation significantly lags behind its perspective counterpart due to limited large-scale, diverse, and accurately annotated datasets.
- Existing panoramic depth models struggle with generalization to varied real-world scenes, particularly complex outdoor environments, and are hindered by equirectangular projection (ERP) distortions.
- Reliable metric (absolute scale) depth estimation for panoramas across arbitrary distances and diverse environments remains a challenge for practical applications.

## Method

- Introduced **DAP**, a panoramic metric depth foundation model, built upon a 'data-in-the-loop' paradigm with a massive data engine comprising 2 million synthetic and real panoramic images.
- Developed a three-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline to efficiently exploit unlabeled real-world data and bridge synthetic-to-real and indoor-to-outdoor domain gaps.
- Designed a geometry-consistent network with a DINOv3-Large backbone, a plug-and-play range mask head, and multi-loss optimization incorporating distortion map compensation, sharpness-centric, and geometry-centric losses.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on public benchmarks, with an AbsRel of 0.0921 on Stanford2D3D and 0.0781 on the newly introduced DAP-Test benchmark, outperforming previous metric methods by a considerable margin.
- Produced qualitatively superior results, demonstrating sharper object boundaries, smoother global geometry, and enhanced robustness in challenging areas like distant regions and sky areas compared to existing models.
- Ablation studies confirmed the incremental benefits of each proposed component, including distortion map compensation, geometry-centric losses (L_normal, L_pts), sharpness-centric losses (L_DF, L_grad), and the range mask head, validating their contribution to performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a foundation model on a massive, diverse dataset (synthetic, real, indoor, outdoor) through a 'data-in-the-loop' approach is crucial for achieving robust generalization in panoramic depth estimation.
> - A multi-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline, integrating a depth quality discriminator, effectively mitigates domain gaps between synthetic and real data, and between indoor and outdoor scenes.
> - Combining a powerful Vision Transformer backbone with specialized heads (like the range mask head) and a suite of distortion-aware, geometry-centric, and sharpness-centric losses ensures high-quality and consistent metric depth predictions for panoramas.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Panoramic depth estimation is a crucial field within computer vision, offering a comprehensive 360° × 180° understanding of the surrounding environment. This spatial intelligence is vital for numerous applications, including autonomous navigation (e.g., omnidirectional obstacle avoidance for robots), augmented/virtual reality, 3D reconstruction, and virtual tours. Despite its importance, the paper identifies that panoramic depth estimation significantly lags behind its perspective (standard camera view) counterpart.

**Challenges in Panoramic Depth Estimation:**
1. **Limited Data:** The high cost and complexity of collecting and annotating large-scale, diverse panoramic datasets with accurate ground truth depth have been a major bottleneck.
2. **Generalization Issues:** Existing methods, both panorama-specific (e.g., Panda, Depth Anywhere, DA$^2$) and unified metric-depth frameworks (e.g., DAC, UniK3D), struggle to generalize effectively to diverse real-world scenes, particularly outdoors where lighting, scale, and complexity vary significantly.
3. **Equirectangular Projection (ERP) Distortions:** Panoramic images typically use ERP, which introduces severe geometric distortions, especially near the poles. This distortion poses unique challenges for neural networks trained on standard perspective images.

**Current Research Trends:**
- **Perspective Depth Estimation:** This area has seen rapid advancements due to abundant large-scale datasets and powerful deep learning models (eUniDepth, Metric3D, DepthPro, MoGe, DepthAnything). Some methods leverage pre-trained generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) for high-quality dense prediction.
- **In-domain Panoramic Depth:** Earlier works focused on specific datasets, employing distortion-aware designs or projection-driven strategies (e.g., projecting ERP to multiple perspective views) to mitigate ERP distortions. However, these often suffer from limited generalization.
- **Zero-shot Panoramic Depth:** Recent efforts aim for better generalization:
 - **Perspective Distillation:** Using pre-trained perspective depth models to generate pseudo-labels for panoramas (Depth Anywhere, PanDA).
 - **Data Expansion:** Generating training data through perspective-to-ERP conversion and diffusion-based out-painting (DA$^2$).
 - **Universal Camera Modeling:** Developing models that unify diverse imaging geometries under a common representation, such as ERP (DAC) or spherical coordinates (UniK3D).

**DAP's Position:**
This work directly addresses the fundamental limitations of data scarcity and poor generalization in panoramic depth estimation by introducing a **"foundation model"** paradigm. By focusing on a "data-in-the-loop" approach, DAP aims to replicate the success of foundation models in other vision tasks for the panoramic domain. It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Massive Data Scale:** Constructing the largest panoramic dataset (2M samples) to date, covering both indoor/outdoor and synthetic/real domains, thereby providing an unprecedented data foundation.
2. **Robust Generalization:** Explicitly designing a model and training pipeline to overcome domain gaps (synthetic-real, indoor-outdoor) and achieve strong zero-shot generalization across diverse real-world environments.
3. **Metric Depth Focus:** Providing metric depth estimates directly, which is crucial for real-world applications requiring absolute scale, rather than just relative depth.
In essence, DAP seeks to provide a unified, generalizable solution for panoramic depth estimation that can serve as a strong baseline for future research and applications, much like foundational models have transformed other areas of AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a panoramic metric depth foundation model, named DAP (Depth Any Panoramas), that demonstrates robust generalization across diverse scene distances and environments, encompassing both indoor and outdoor, and synthetic and real-world scenarios.**

The motivations driving this objective stem from critical challenges in the existing landscape of panoramic depth estimation:
- **Lack of Generalization in Existing Models:** Current panoramic depth estimation methods, whether optimized for relative or metric depth, frequently fail to generalize effectively to novel real-world scenes, especially complex outdoor environments. They often produce inconsistent or unrealistic depth predictions in varied conditions. This limits their practical utility in dynamic applications like robotics or AR/VR.
- **Scarcity of Diverse and Scalable Data:** A significant impediment to advancing panoramic depth estimation has been the prohibitive cost and difficulty associated with collecting and annotating large-scale, high-quality panoramic datasets with reliable ground-truth depth. This data limitation prevents models from learning robust, generalizable features.
- **Domain Gaps:** Even with available data, there are considerable domain gaps between synthetic data (which often has perfect annotations but lacks realism) and real-world data, as well as between indoor and outdoor scenes (due to differences in scale, lighting, and object distribution). Bridging these gaps is crucial for creating truly versatile models.
- **Need for Metric Consistency:** For many real-world applications (e.g., robotic navigation, 3D reconstruction), absolute metric depth is essential, not just relative depth. Existing metric depth frameworks still struggle with consistency across arbitrary distances and diverse scenes.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a "data-in-the-loop paradigm." This involves two key thrusts:
1. **Data Construction:** Creating a massive, diverse dataset with reliable ground truth by integrating public, synthetic, and unlabeled real-world panoramic data.
2. **Framework Design:** Developing a model architecture and training strategy that can effectively leverage such large-scale, varied data, adapt to its characteristics, and produce geometrically consistent, generalizable depth predictions.

By tackling these challenges head-on, the research aims to establish a foundational model that significantly pushes the boundaries of panoramic depth estimation, enabling more sophisticated spatial intelligence for a wide range of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DAP framework introduces a scalable panoramic depth foundation model built upon three core components: a large-scale data engine, a three-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline, and a geometry-consistent network design with multi-loss optimization.

#### 3.1. Data Engine

To overcome the limitations of existing datasets, DAP constructs a comprehensive data engine comprising approximately 2 million panoramic samples, the largest to date for this task, covering both synthetic and real-world, indoor and outdoor domains.
- **Simulated Outdoor Scene Data:** 90,000 high-quality outdoor panoramas with pixel-aligned depth annotations are synthesized using the UE5-based AirSim360 simulator. These samples are collected from diverse virtual environments (e.g., New York City, SF City) to simulate realistic drone flights.
- **Unlabeled Data:** A vast collection of 1.7 million high-quality real panoramic images are gathered from the internet and curated to filter out problematic samples. These are automatically categorized into indoor (~250K) and outdoor (~1.45M) scenes using Qwen2-VL. Additionally, 200,000 synthetic indoor panoramas are generated using DiT-360 to balance the indoor domain scarcity in real-world data.
- **Public Labeled Data:** Structured3D, an existing high-quality synthetic indoor dataset (20,000 samples), is incorporated to provide initial indoor supervision.

#### 3.2. Three-Stage Pseudo-Label Curation Pipeline

This pipeline efficiently exploits the large volume of unlabeled real-world data while bridging domain gaps between synthetic/real and indoor/outdoor scenes.
- **Stage 1: Scene-Invariant Labeler Training:** A base depth estimation model (Scene-Invariant Labeler) is trained on a balanced mix of *synthetic* indoor (Structured3D) and outdoor (AirSim360) labeled data. The goal is to develop a labeler that learns robust, physically grounded depth cues and generalizes across different scene types.
- **Stage 2: Realism-Invariant Labeler Training:**
 1. A depth quality discriminator (PatchGAN-based) is pre-trained to differentiate between high-quality synthetic ground truth and pseudo-labels generated by the Stage 1 labeler. This discriminator learns to assess the reliability of depth predictions.
 2. The Scene-Invariant Labeler (from Stage 1) then generates pseudo-labels for all 1.9 million unlabeled real images.
 3. The pre-trained discriminator is used to select the top 600,000 (300K indoor, 300K outdoor) highest-confidence pseudo-labeled samples. This step is crucial for mitigating the synthetic-to-real domain gap.
 4. A new Realism-Invariant Labeler is trained on these high-confidence pseudo-labels combined with the original synthetic datasets. This training makes the labeler more robust to appearance variations and realism-specific differences in real data.
- **Stage 3: DAP Training:** The final DAP foundation model is trained on *all* available data: the initial labeled datasets and the entire 1.9 million pseudo-labeled images refined by the Realism-Invariant Labeler. This large-scale semi-supervised learning enables the DAP model to achieve strong generalization across diverse real-world panoramic scenes.

#### 3.3. Model Design

The DAP network is engineered for high-quality metric depth estimation, addressing both robustness to distances and geometric consistency.
- **Backbone:** DINOv3-Large (a Vision Transformer, ViT) is adopted as the visual backbone, leveraging its powerful pre-trained visual priors for robust feature extraction.
- **Task-Specific Heads:**
 - **Metric Depth Head:** Predicts a dense metric depth map.
 - **Plug-and-Play Range Mask Head:** Outputs a binary mask *M* for specific distance thresholds (10m, 20m, 50m, 100m). This head helps mitigate uneven depth distributions by focusing on relevant distance ranges and is optimized with weighted BCE and Dice losses. The final depth output is the element-wise product of *M* and the predicted depth map *D*, ensuring physically valid and scale-consistent predictions.
- **Multi-Loss Optimization (with Distortion Map Compensation):** To ensure metric accuracy, edge fidelity, and geometric consistency, a set of loss functions are employed, all weighted by a distortion map to compensate for the non-uniform pixel distribution in ERP.
 - **L_SILog:** Standard scale-invariant logarithmic error loss for depth.
 - **Sharpness-Centric Optimization:**
 - **L_DF (Dense Fidelity Loss):** Inspired by, this loss enhances local sharpness and structural consistency by decomposing the panoramic depth map into 12 perspective patches (from virtual icosahedron cameras). Gram-based similarity is computed between predicted and ground-truth depth in these distortion-free views.
 - **L_grad (Gradient Refinement Loss):** Applied directly in the ERP domain, this loss uses Sobel operators to compute gradient magnitudes and focuses on ground-truth edge regions (thresholded gradient magnitudes) to explicitly improve the sharpness of object boundaries.
 - **Geometry-Centric Optimization:**
 - **L_normal (Normal Loss):** Computes the L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth surface normals, enforcing geometric consistency.
 - **L_pts (Point Cloud Loss):** Projects depth maps into 3D point clouds and calculates the L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth point clouds, further ensuring geometric accuracy.
- **Overall Objective:** The total loss is a weighted sum of all these loss components, modulated by a distortion map to balance gradient contributions across the spherical domain, preventing over-emphasis on regions near the poles.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DAP demonstrates its strong performance and generalization capabilities across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **Zero-shot Performance on Public Benchmarks:** DAP consistently achieves state-of-the-art results in zero-shot settings (without fine-tuning) on widely used indoor datasets (Stanford2D3D, Matterport3D) and outdoor datasets (Deep360).
 - On Stanford2D3D, DAP achieves AbsRel 0.0921, RMSE 0.3820, and δ1 0.9135, outperforming previous metric methods like Unik3D (AbsRel 0.1795, RMSE 0.4850, δ1 0.7823) and DAC (AbsRel 0.1366, RMSE 0.4509, δ1 0.8393).
 - Similar improvements are observed on Matterport3D and Deep360, where DAP shows superior AbsRel, RMSE, and δ1 metrics, confirming its robust generalization to unseen indoor and outdoor scenes.
 - Even when compared to scale-invariant methods (which benefit from post-alignment to ground truth scale), DAP's directly predicted metric depth is comparably strong, highlighting its inherent scale-awareness.
- **DAP-Test Benchmark Performance:** The newly introduced DAP-Test benchmark, consisting of 1,343 high-quality outdoor images with accurate depth annotations, further validates DAP's effectiveness.
 - DAP significantly reduces AbsRel from 0.2517 (Unik3D) to 0.0781 and RMSE from 10.56 (Unik3D) to 6.804, while increasing δ1 from 0.6086 (Unik3D) to 0.9370. This demonstrates the profound impact of the large-scale data engine and training strategies on outdoor metric depth estimation.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation

- **Visual Consistency and Detail:** Qualitative comparisons against DAC and Unik3D reveal that DAP produces sharper object boundaries, smoother global geometry, and superior robustness in challenging areas like distant regions and sky areas.
- **Indoor Scenes:** DAP accurately preserves fine structural details such as furniture edges and wall boundaries, which often appear over-smoothed or distorted in outputs from other models.
- **Outdoor Scenes:** Unlike existing methods that struggle to maintain stable depth structures in distant regions or sky areas (leading to distorted or collapsed predictions), DAP maintains strong robustness and metric consistency, generating smooth and realistic representations of both near and far spatial structures.
- **Scale-Awareness:** DAP exhibits stronger scale-awareness, with predicted depth and illumination distributions visually closer to the ground truth, particularly in distant wall and ceiling regions on datasets like Stanford2D3D.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Model Design Contribution:** The ablation study confirms the incremental benefits of each proposed component. Starting from a baseline with only L_SILog, adding the distortion map, geometry-centric losses (L_normal, L_pts), and sharpness-centric losses (L_DF, L_grad) progressively improves performance on both Stanford2D3D and Deep360, with the full model achieving the best results. This validates the importance of each loss term in ensuring metric consistency, structural fidelity, and edge sharpness.
- **Range Mask Head Effectiveness:** Evaluation of the plug-and-play range mask head at different distance thresholds (10m, 20m, 50m, 100m) shows its critical role. The 100m setting offers the best overall balance. Notably, removing the range mask head significantly degrades performance, confirming its effectiveness in filtering unreliable far-depth predictions and stabilizing training across diverse scene scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of DAP represents a significant leap forward in panoramic depth estimation, particularly through its "foundation model" approach and large-scale data strategy. Its potential impact spans several domains:

- **Establishment of a Foundation Model:** DAP successfully pioneers a panoramic metric depth foundation model, analogous to the transformative impact of foundation models in other computer vision tasks. This establishes a robust, generalizable, and highly capable baseline that can be fine-tuned or adapted for a multitude of downstream panoramic vision tasks, significantly accelerating future research and development.
- **Paradigm Shift in Data Utilization:** The research emphatically demonstrates the critical importance and effectiveness of large-scale, diverse data (synthetic, real, indoor, outdoor) for achieving robust generalization in panoramic depth estimation. The meticulously constructed "data engine" and the innovative three-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline offer a powerful blueprint for future efforts to leverage vast amounts of unlabeled data and overcome domain gaps in challenging vision problems. This data-centric approach is a key takeaway for the community.
- **Enhanced Spatial Intelligence for Applications:** By providing accurate, scale-consistent, and robust metric depth maps for full 360° environments, DAP directly benefits real-world applications:
 - **Robotics:** Greatly improves omnidirectional obstacle avoidance, path planning, and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) for autonomous robots.
 - **AR/VR:** Enables more realistic virtual object placement, scene understanding, and immersive experiences by accurately perceiving the geometry of the surrounding real world.
 - **3D Reconstruction:** Facilitates higher-quality and more complete 3D models of indoor and outdoor environments, crucial for architecture, surveying, and digital twinning.
 - **Virtual Tours:** Enhances the realism and interactivity of virtual tours by providing true depth information for panoramic images.
- **Robustness to Real-world Challenges:** DAP's demonstrated ability to maintain stable and realistic depth predictions in complex, diverse, and challenging real-world scenes—including distant regions and sky areas—addresses a long-standing limitation of previous methods. This robustness makes it highly practical for uncontrolled environments.
- **Technical Innovations:** The integration of DINOv3-Large as a powerful backbone, the novel plug-and-play range mask head, and a comprehensive suite of distortion-aware, sharpness-centric, and geometry-centric optimization losses provides a sophisticated and effective solution to the inherent complexities of panoramic vision. These components offer valuable methodological insights for other panoramic vision tasks.
- **Contribution to Benchmarking:** The introduction of the DAP-Test benchmark for outdoor panoramic scenes enriches the existing evaluation resources, providing the research community with a more relevant and challenging dataset for assessing model performance in real-world outdoor conditions.
- **Industry-Academia Synergy:** The successful collaboration between Insta360 Research and leading academic institutions exemplifies how combining industry's practical problems and data with academia's theoretical depth and cutting-edge algorithms can yield groundbreaking results with immediate practical relevance.

In conclusion, "Depth Any Panoramas" is a foundational work that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in panoramic depth estimation. It not only delivers a highly performant model but also provides a comprehensive framework for data scaling, domain adaptation, and geometrically-aware learning, paving the way for more intelligent and capable 360-degree vision systems.
