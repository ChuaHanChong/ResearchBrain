# WorldCycle: Self-Verifiable Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldCycle introduces a self-verifiable reinforcement learning framework that leverages reversible action cycles to improve the long-horizon physical and geometric consistency of interactive video world models. It also presents CycleBench, a diagnostic benchmark for evaluating state-returning consistency, demonstrating up to a 44% reduction in state-returning drift and a 4x improvement in composite-action accuracy.

## Problem

- Interactive video world models suffer from compounding errors in autoregressive generation, leading to a loss of physical and geometric consistency over long rollouts.
- Existing RL-based post-training methods for world models are limited to short-horizon improvements due to the 'verification bottleneck'—the absence of ground-truth supervision for long-term action sequences.
- State-of-the-art world models fail basic reversibility checks, exhibiting spatial drift and temporal inconsistency, particularly when handling composite actions.

## Method

- The framework employs the algebraic property of ==reversible action cycles== to generate dense, annotation-free, and self-verifiable long-horizon supervision signals.
- Two complementary trajectory-level rewards are introduced: a ==Spatial Closure Reward==, which densely verifies mirrored states within a cycle, and a ==Temporal State Consistency Reward==, which enforces consistent action behavior across repeated cycles.
- **WorldCycle** utilizes a cycle-consistent RL training approach with a warm-up schedule, multi-scale cycle sampling, and a negative-aware objective for efficient optimization of long rollouts and enhanced generalization to composite actions.

## Results

- WorldCycle reduced state-returning drift metrics (ESC, RPS, RCS) by up to **44%** on short-term actions and **34%** on long-term actions compared to leading baselines.
- The framework achieved a **4x** improvement in action accuracy on out-of-domain composite actions (from **0.136** to **0.553**) and reduced state drift in these challenging scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both spatial and temporal rewards are essential and complementary, with the 'warm-up-and-combine' training schedule proving optimal for stability and performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reversible action cycles provide an intrinsic, annotation-free, and precise algebraic target for verifying long-horizon correctness in learned world model dynamics.
> - Densely comparing mirrored states within a cycle and phase-aligned states across repeated cycles transforms a sparse endpoint signal into rich, localized, and long-range supervision.
> - Optimizing for cycle closure compels the model to learn actions as consistent state operators rather than memorized patterns, which significantly improves generalization to out-of-distribution composite actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Interactive video world models (IWMs) represent a developing area in generative artificial intelligence, aiming to predict future visual observations based on an initial state and a sequence of user or agent actions. These models are designed to function as interactive simulators, supporting applications in embodied interaction, robot learning, visual planning, and game-like environments. They commonly employ an autoregressive generation paradigm, where each newly generated observation serves as context for the next prediction. A known challenge with this approach is the accumulation of errors over extended rollouts, which can compromise the physical and geometric consistency required for long-horizon tasks.

Recent efforts have focused on improving IWMs through post-training methods based on reinforcement learning (RL). However, existing RL approaches primarily optimize for short-horizon visual quality or per-step action alignment. A fundamental limitation for long-horizon improvement is the "verification bottleneck": for an arbitrary sequence of actions, there is typically no ground-truth future state available to measure the accumulated error. This absence of long-term supervision has restricted RL-based enhancements to local, short-duration improvements. For example, methods like WorldCompass attempt to reward clip-level action following, but such objectives are often insufficient for correcting fine-grained spatial drift over extended periods.

This work addresses the verification bottleneck by introducing a paradigm that leverages the algebraic properties of reversible action trajectories. While existing video world models (e.g., Genie, GameNGen, Oasis, WorldPlay, HY-World) prioritize fidelity along a single forward direction and do not typically assess whether a rollout has achieved a specific target state dictated by action composition, WorldCycle focuses on a specific class of dynamics: those involving reversible actions. For such actions, a composed sequence and its inverse should analytically return the system to its initial state, providing an intrinsic, annotation-free reference for long-horizon correctness. This approach contrasts with classical world models that often learn dynamics through compact internal representations and with other video generation models that focus on appearance or geometry-level coherence.

Furthermore, WorldCycle distinguishes itself from other RL-based post-training methods (such as those applied to language models like RLHF or diffusion models like DDPO) by constructing rewards whose targets are fixed by the algebraic identity of reversible cycles. This provides a label-free objective without requiring external annotation, unlike methods that rely on external scorers for individual clips or short segments.

The paper also acknowledges concurrent efforts in cycle-based consistency, such as Cycle-World and World Models as Group Actions. While these approaches also explore cyclic constraints, WorldCycle specifically frames the cyclic structure as a source of self-verifiable reward functions for *reinforcement learning post-training*, directly targeting the verification bottleneck in scenarios where ground-truth future videos are unavailable. This positions WorldCycle as a method that leverages algebraic structure not merely as a regularizer, but as a mechanism for generating dense, long-horizon supervisory signals for RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust method for improving the long-horizon consistency of interactive video world models, particularly in the face of compounding errors inherent in autoregressive generation. The motivation stems from several identified limitations in current IWMs and their post-training methodologies:

- **Compounding Errors in Autoregressive Models:** Interactive video world models, which generate future observations sequentially, are susceptible to small, single-step errors that accumulate and propagate. This error accumulation progressively erodes the physical and geometric consistency of generated long-horizon rollouts, rendering them unsuitable for tasks requiring precise state transitions like embodied interaction or robotic control.

- **The Verification Bottleneck for Long-Horizon Supervision:** Existing RL-based post-training methods for IWMs predominantly optimize short-horizon objectives, such as visual quality or local action alignment. The fundamental obstacle to achieving long-horizon improvements is the absence of effective supervision. For an arbitrary action sequence, there is no ground-truth future state available to measure the accumulated error over a long trajectory. This "verification bottleneck" prevents direct penalization of trajectory-level errors.

- **Failure of Existing Models in Basic Reversibility Checks:** The authors observe that even state-of-the-art IWMs, such as WorldPlay and WorldCompass, fail minimal sanity checks involving reversible action cycles. Specifically, they exhibit:
 - **Spatial Closure Failure:** An inverse action sequence (e.g., forward-then-backward) does not consistently return the model to its initial view, indicating accumulated spatial drift.
 - **Temporal Consistency Failure:** The same action, when executed from what should be identical states at different rollout positions, produces inconsistent displacements, reflecting autoregressive accumulation and a failure to treat actions as consistent state operators. These failures are invisible to short-horizon rewards.

- **Challenges with Reward Sparsity and Temporal Drift:** While the insight of reversible action cycles provides an exact, label-free target for long-horizon trajectories, two challenges remain:
 - **Reward Sparsity:** An endpoint-only signal from a cycle provides insufficient gradient information to localize and correct intermediate errors within the trajectory.
 - **Temporal Drift:** Autoregressive accumulation can cause the same action to behave differently depending on its depth within the rollout.

- **Poor Handling of Composite Actions:** Pre-trained IWMs often exhibit a significant drop in accuracy when faced with composite actions (e.g., moving forward while turning) compared to simple actions. This is attributed to such combinations being out-of-distribution relative to typical pretraining data, which rarely provides ground-truth video demonstrations of complex composite trajectories.

To address these motivations, WorldCycle sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
1. **Introduce Self-Verifiable Supervision:** Leverage reversible action cycles as a novel, annotation-free, and precise long-horizon supervision signal to overcome the verification bottleneck.
2. **Develop Dense Trajectory-Level Rewards:** Construct two complementary rewards:
 - **Spatial Closure Reward:** Densify supervision by comparing mirrored forward and reverse segments within a single cycle, localizing where drift begins.
 - **Temporal State Consistency Reward:** Penalize drift of identical actions over time by comparing phase-aligned states across repeated executions of a cycle.
3. **Facilitate Generalization:** Force the model to learn actions as consistent state operators rather than memorized temporal patterns, thereby enabling generalization to out-of-distribution composite action cycles without ground-truth video supervision.
4. **Establish a Diagnostic Benchmark:** Introduce CycleBench, a new benchmark suite, to systematically evaluate the state-returning consistency of IWMs under various reversible, repeated, and composite action structures, thereby shifting evaluation beyond short-horizon generation quality to simulator capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldCycle is a self-verifiable Reinforcement Learning framework designed to improve the long-horizon consistency of interactive video world models by transforming closed action programs into dense, annotation-free supervision.

**Preliminaries and Core Insight:**
The framework models an interactive video world model as a learned state-transition system, `M_θ = (S, A, f_θ)`, where `f_θ` maps a state `s_t` and action `a_t` to `s_{t+1}`. The fundamental challenge is that learned transformations `ˆT_a` only approximate true transformations `T_a`, leading to compounding errors `E_a` over a trajectory `ˆT_γ = T_γ ◦ E_γ`. The key insight is that for reversible actions, whose induced transformations form a group, an action `a` has an inverse `a^-1` such that `T_a ◦ T_a^-1 = I` (identity). Thus, for a closed action cycle `γ`, `T_γ = I`, meaning `f_θ(s, γ)` should ideally return `s`. This provides an analytically known, content-independent, and annotation-free target for long-horizon trajectories.

**State Discrepancy Measurement:**
Since the true physical state `s` is latent, WorldCycle uses dense point tracking in image space as an observable proxy for state consistency. Given two generated frames `o_i` and `o_j`, `N` valid correspondence points are tracked (e.g., using CoTracker during training) and their mean squared displacement is normalized by image dimensions to calculate `d(o_i, o_j)`.

**Self-Verifiable Reinforcement Learning Objectives:**
WorldCycle constructs two complementary trajectory-level rewards:

- **Spatial Closure Reward (`R_spatial`):**
 - A symmetric cycle `γ_c` is constructed by a forward action sequence `(a_1, ..., a_m)` followed by its inverse `(a_m^-1, ..., a_1^-1)`. Analytically, `T_γ_c = I`.
 - Instead of only supervising the final endpoint, this reward densely verifies mirrored states within the cycle. The state reached after `i` forward actions (`o_i`) should match the state reached at the mirrored phase `2m-i` of the reverse trajectory (`o_{2m-i}`).
 - `R_spatial = (1/(m+1)) * Σ_{i=0}^m exp[-αd(o_i, o_{2m-i})]`. This localizes drift throughout the rollout, providing dense credit assignment.

- **Temporal State Consistency Reward (`R_temporal`):**
 - This reward enforces that `s_t = s_t'` implies `f_θ(s_t, a) = f_θ(s_t', a)`. To achieve this, the cyclic action program is repeated for `K` cycles.
 - The reward compares phase-aligned observations across cycles. `o_i^k` (observation at step `i` of cycle `k`) is compared to `o_i^1` (observation at step `i` of the first cycle). The first cycle acts as a stable anchor.
 - `R_temporal = (1/((K-1)(L+1))) * Σ_{k=2}^K Σ_{i=0}^L exp[-αd(o_i^k, o_i^1)]`. This penalizes the drift of identical actions over time across extended horizons.

**Cycle-Consistent RL Training:**

- **Warm-up-and-Combine Schedule:** To ensure stability, the temporal consistency reward is activated only after a `t_w` warm-up phase where only the spatial reward is active (`1[t ≥ t_w]` term). This grounds individual cycles before enforcing cross-cycle consistency, avoiding initial gradient noise and catastrophic forgetting.
- **Multi-Scale Cycle Sampling:** To prevent the model from memorizing fixed temporal positions for reversal, hierarchical cycles of varying lengths (`H/2^k`) are sampled randomly during training.
- **Reward Composition:** The cycle rewards (`R_cycle = λ_s R_spatial + 1[t ≥ t_w] λ_t R_temporal`) are combined with an action-following reward (`R_act`) and a visual quality score (`R_HPS`) to ensure visual fidelity and local action accuracy are maintained. `R_total = R_cycle + λ_a R_act + λ_v R_HPS`.
- **Trajectory-Level Optimization:** WorldCycle uses a DiffusionNFT-style negative-aware objective, which performs supervised regression towards reward-weighted positive and negative velocity directions. This allows for efficient optimization of long rollouts by backpropagating only through the trailing chunk while keeping previous chunks as frozen context. Training involves 4,000 image-caption pairs on an 8B WorldPlay AR checkpoint, with rollouts chunked into 16-frame clips, up to K=8 repeated cycles, and training for 3 days on 8 H200 GPUs.

**Analysis on Composite Action Generalization:**
The framework directly addresses composite actions. A composite action sequence paired with its inverse still forms a closed cycle, providing annotation-free supervision for these out-of-domain trajectories. By optimizing cycle closure, WorldCycle reduces local transition errors and their propagation, encouraging the model to learn the underlying action composition algebra rather than memorizing specific trajectories, thereby enhancing generalization.

**CycleBench Benchmark:**
A new benchmark, CycleBench, is introduced to measure error accumulation in IWMs as state-transition simulators.
- **Tasks:** Four tasks probe different aspects of error:
 - **T1 (Reversible-Cycle):** Forward sequence followed by inverse; evaluates mirrored frame agreement densely.
 - **T2 (Closed-Cycle):** Non-retracing closed path (e.g., rectangle); evaluates endpoint closure.
 - **T3 (Repeated-Cycle):** Same closed cycle repeated `K` times; tracks endpoint error growth and phase-aligned drift.
 - **T4 (Cascaded-Cycle):** Two different closed cycles `C^(1) ◦ C^(2)` sequentially; probes cross-cycle error propagation.
- **Scenario Settings:** Each task is evaluated under:
 - **Short-term (125 frames):** Basic in-domain case.
 - **Mid-term (253 frames):** Tests error amplification under longer generation.
 - **Long-term (381 frames):** Primary stress test for compounding error.
 - **Composite-action (125 frames):** Out-of-domain multi-component controls.
- **Metrics:** Uses RoMa (an independent dense correspondence estimator) to calculate:
 - **ESC↓ (Endpoint State Closure):** Distance between generated endpoint and initial frame.
 - **RPS↓ (Reverse-Path Symmetry):** Average distance between mirrored forward-reverse pairs.
 - **RCS↓ (Repeated-Cycle Stability):** Average phase-aligned distance across cycles.
 - Auxiliary metrics: Action Accuracy↑ and HPSv3↑ for visual quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WorldCycle was conducted using the newly introduced CycleBench benchmark against several baseline interactive video world models, including Lingbot World v2, WorldPlay, and WorldCompass.

**Quantitative Performance (Table 1):**
- **State Consistency Improvements Across Horizons:** WorldCycle consistently reduced state-returning drift metrics (ESC, RPS, RCS) compared to both WorldPlay (the base model) and WorldCompass (a leading RL post-trained baseline).
 - On **short-term (125 frames) simple actions**, WorldCycle reduced Endpoint State Closure (ESC) by 32% and Reverse-Path Symmetry (RPS) by 44% compared to WorldCompass.
 - These gains persisted with longer horizons: for **mid-term (253 frames) actions**, ESC dropped by 21% and RPS by 28%.
 - For the **long-term (381 frames) stress test**, Repeated-Cycle Stability (RCS) showed the largest relative gain, falling by 34%, reflecting the direct contribution of the temporal consistency reward.
- **Action Accuracy and Visual Quality:** WorldCycle maintained or slightly improved Action Accuracy (e.g., 0.833 vs. 0.829 for WorldCompass on short-term actions) and HPSv3 visual quality across these settings, indicating that consistency gains did not compromise other performance aspects.
- **Superior Generalization to Out-of-Domain Composite Actions:**
 - On **composite actions (125 frames)**, where the base WorldPlay model's action accuracy collapsed to 0.136, WorldCycle achieved an accuracy of 0.553. This represents an approximate 4x improvement over the base model and an 11% improvement over WorldCompass.
 - WorldCycle also significantly reduced ESC (22%), RPS (29%), and RCS (35%) on composite actions, demonstrating effective generalization to unseen action compositions.
- **Scaling Alone is Insufficient for State Consistency:** Lingbot World v2 (14B parameters), while achieving high HPSv3 visual quality, significantly underperformed all RL post-trained baselines on cycle-consistency metrics (ESC, RPS, RCS were 1.4 to 8.3 times worse than WorldCycle), indicating that increased model capacity alone does not resolve state-returning consistency issues.

**Qualitative Observations (Figures 4 and 6):**
- **Drift in Baselines:** Qualitative comparisons on CycleBench (Figure 4) revealed that while baselines generated locally plausible frames, they accumulated visible drift upon returning to the initial state. This manifested as color shifts, geometric distortions, and structural details diverging, particularly in composite-action settings.
- **WorldCycle's Stability:** WorldCycle recovered states substantially closer to the starting view in both simple and composite action settings.
- **Long-Horizon Stability (Figure 6):** In the 381-frame, five-repeated-cycle setting, LingBot World v2 showed pronounced color drift (e.g., oversaturated blue sky). WorldPlay and WorldCompass developed visible visual artifacts (whitening, unnatural linear patterns in the sky). In contrast, WorldCycle maintained stable colors, textures, and scene content, producing a final return frame that closely aligned with the initial observation.

**Ablation Study (Table 2):**
- **Complementary Rewards:** Both spatial and temporal rewards were found to be essential. Removing the temporal signal primarily degraded long-range stability and composite-action generalization. Conversely, removing the spatial signal led to broader failures, as temporal supervision relies on individual cycles approximately closing.
- **Importance of Warm-up-and-Combine Schedule:** The "warm-up-and-combine" training schedule consistently outperformed "joint-from-scratch" and "sequential" training strategies. Joint-from-scratch underperformed because the temporal reward was noisy without an initially grounded spatial reference. Sequential training risked catastrophic forgetting of the spatial constraint.
- **No Degradation in Visual Quality:** The full WorldCycle model achieved the highest HPSv3 score among all variants (10.42), a 16% gain over the base model (8.98), demonstrating that improvements in cycle consistency did not come at the expense of visual fidelity.

**VBench Video Quality Evaluation (Table 3):**
- WorldCycle consistently matched or exceeded WorldCompass on most VBench dimensions (Aesthetic Quality, Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Imaging Quality, Temporal Flickering, and Motion Smoothness) across all four scenario settings. It also consistently outperformed the base WorldPlay model, reinforcing that cycle-consistency training improved overall perceptual video quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WorldCycle framework and its accompanying CycleBench benchmark represent a significant contribution to the field of interactive video world models, with several implications and potential impacts:

- **Addressing the Verification Bottleneck:** The primary significance lies in providing a novel solution to the "verification bottleneck" in long-horizon world model training. By transforming reversible action cycles into dense, annotation-free, and self-verifiable supervision signals, WorldCycle enables effective RL post-training without relying on scarce or non-existent ground-truth future video data. This opens avenues for improving IWMs in scenarios where explicit labels for complex long-term trajectories are impractical to obtain.

- **Towards Physically Grounded World Models:** The framework pushes models to learn actions as consistent state operators rather than merely memorizing temporal patterns. This approach promotes the development of more physically and geometrically consistent simulators, which is crucial for applications requiring accurate and predictable environmental responses, such as robotics, embodied AI, and advanced simulation environments. The reduction of state-returning drift by up to 44% and significant improvements in composite-action accuracy underscore this impact.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** WorldCycle demonstrates strong generalization to out-of-distribution composite actions. By optimizing for the underlying action composition algebra through cycle closure, the model becomes more robust to combinations of actions it may not have frequently encountered in its pretraining data. This is a vital step towards creating world models that can handle the complexity and diversity of real-world interactions.

- **New Evaluation Paradigm with CycleBench:** The introduction of CycleBench establishes the first diagnostic benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the *state-returning consistency* of interactive video world models. This shifts the evaluation paradigm from solely assessing short-horizon generation fidelity to systematically probing a model's capabilities as a long-horizon state-transition simulator. CycleBench provides critical metrics (ESC, RPS, RCS) that directly measure accumulated error, offering a more rigorous assessment for future research.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The principles established by WorldCycle—leveraging algebraic properties of actions for self-supervision—offer a foundation for several future research directions. These include extending self-verifiable supervision to:
 - **Approximately reversible processes:** Dynamics where exact reversal is not possible but certain conservation laws or equivalent terminal states can be analytically verified.
 - **Irreversible dynamics:** Exploring alternative constraints for processes like object deformation or contact-rich interactions.
 - **Diverse action modalities:** Applying similar self-supervision strategies to more complex action spaces, such as robotic manipulation, where precise control and consistent state transitions are paramount.

In conclusion, WorldCycle advances interactive video world modeling by providing a method to train models for long-horizon consistency, enhancing their reliability and realism. The work not only introduces a practical RL framework but also proposes a new benchmark that could guide future development in building more robust and physically grounded interactive simulators.
