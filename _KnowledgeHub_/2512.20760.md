# Generalization of RLVR Using Causal Reasoning as a Testbed

> [!summary] Summary
> This research empirically investigates the generalization of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in formal causal inference tasks using a new RLCausal dataset. It demonstrates that RLVR enhances LLM performance and cross-level generalization on associational and interventional queries for sufficiently capable models by refining reasoning strategies and reducing specific error types.

## Problem

- The robustness and boundaries of RLVR generalization in complex reasoning tasks, especially beyond training data, were not fully understood.
- Existing causal reasoning benchmarks often conflate natural language understanding with formal reasoning, obscuring LLMs' pure derivation and calculation capabilities.
- LLMs showed limitations in performing numerically challenging formal counterfactual reasoning problems, even with explicit instructions.

## Method

- Developed **RLCausal**, a custom dataset of formal ==causal inference== problems based on abstract causal graphs, to isolate derivation and calculation aspects of reasoning.
- Conducted an empirical study comparing RLVR and Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) across various LLM scales (3B, 7B, 32B parameters) on associational, interventional, and counterfactual query types.
- Analyzed reasoning traces using an LLM judge to categorize marginalization strategies and types of errors, such as abstract probability derivation and calculation errors.

## Results

- RLVR generally outperformed SFT on both within-level and across-level generalization for associational and interventional queries with **7B** and **32B** parameter models.
- RLVR fine-tuning led to a notable reduction in abstract probability and causal derivation errors, shifting problem-solving towards more systematic strategies for complex queries.
- Both RLVR and SFT showed limited success on counterfactual queries in the RLCausal dataset, with no consistent performance gains even when explicit 'twin-network' hints were provided.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLVR's generalization effectiveness is contingent on the LLM's initial reasoning competence, showing limited benefits for models with poor baseline abilities.
> - RLVR training encourages LLMs to adopt more systematic 'incremental marginalization' strategies and significantly reduces abstract probability and causal derivation errors.
> - Current LLMs, even with RLVR and explicit prompting, exhibit fundamental difficulties with complex formal counterfactual reasoning involving abduction and marginalization.
