# SCOPE: Semantic Conditioning for Sim2Real Category-Level Object Pose Estimation in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> SCOPE is a diffusion-based category-level object pose estimator that drops discrete category labels, instead conditioning an attention U-Net on continuous DINOv2 semantic features via cross-attention to regress NOCS images from RGB plus depth-derived normals, with TEASER++ recovering 6D pose and scale. Trained only on synthetic data, it improves 5°5cm accuracy by 31.9% and grasps unseen objects at up to 100% success.

## Problem

- Fixed category labels are human conventions, not geometric or appearance similarity, so they impose arbitrary structure that blocks generalization to unseen instances and categories.
- Prior pose models merely concatenate DINOv2 features to inputs, unlike image synthesis models that propagate semantics through cross-attention, leaving geometric-semantic spatial relationships unmodeled.
- Synthetically trained category-level estimators lag mixed real-plus-synthetic methods badly, and transfer from known to unknown categories remains largely unexplored in the literature.

## Method

- **SCOPE**'s conditional ==DDPM== with attention ==U-Net== regresses ==NOCS== images from concatenated RGB and surface normals, then ==TEASER++== registers the predicted normalized cloud with depth for 6D pose plus scale.
- ==Cross-attention conditioning== injects frozen ==DINOv2== patch and CLS tokens as keys and values at the fifth down-sampling and second up-sampling blocks, alongside ==self-attention== on U-Net features.
- **CAMERA-BPR**: ==BlenderProc== ==domain-randomized== re-render (50,000 images, 600,000 instances) with Perlin depth noise, plus rectangular and pixel-wise normal dropout, to close the Sim2Real gap.

## Results

- On REAL275 synthetic-only training, SCOPE beats DPDN by **31.9%** at 5°5cm (**49.2** vs **37.3**) and **9.6%** at 10°5cm, and GS-Pose by **31.3%** at IoU75.
- Cross-attention conditioning beats concatenation-with-category-labels by **27.7%** ADD-S on YCB-V and **66.0%** ADD(-S) on TYOL; DINOv2 beats CLIP-ViT/L14 (**49.0** vs **28.1** at 5°5cm).
- Grasping with a Toyota HSR reached **90-100%** success on box-shaped and foam-brick objects outside known categories, worst case **60%** on the pitcher base; runtime **0.193 s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - How a semantic prior enters the network matters more than which prior: attention-based conditioning preserves spatial geometry-semantics correspondence that feature concatenation destroys.
> - Continuous embedding spaces generalize past the label taxonomy they were never trained on, so discrete category vocabularies can be dropped without losing category-level accuracy.
> - Diffusion refinement bought 2.4% accuracy for 746% runtime; cheap sampling settings usually suffice, and remaining Sim2Real gap still favors real depth-noise statistics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate object pose estimation is a fundamental requirement for autonomous manipulation and scene understanding in robotics. The choice of algorithm often depends on the availability of object priors. When geometry priors are known, retrieving the 6D pose (rotation R and translation t) is a common approach. However, in open and unstructured environments, robots frequently encounter novel or unknown objects, making it impractical to have precise geometric models beforehand. This necessitates methods that can generalize based on broader object characteristics.

Category-level object pose estimation has emerged as a dominant approach to address this challenge. In this paradigm, only general semantic category information, rather than instance-specific CAD models, is assumed to be available during inference. Traditional training for such systems often utilizes real-world data, which can introduce biases specific to the dataset, thereby limiting general applicability. Consequently, synthetic training data is increasingly preferred due to the availability of 3D CAD models, the automatic generation of pose annotations through rendering engines, and the efficiency of parallel processing on GPUs.

A significant limitation in current category-level approaches is the reliance on discrete category labels. These labels, derived from human semantic conventions, may not always correlate with geometric or appearance similarities that are relevant for robotic tasks. This arbitrary structuring can hinder generalization across diverse object instances and categories. Recent advancements have therefore explored the use of continuous embedding spaces, learned directly from data, to represent object semantics in a more adaptive and task-relevant manner. Models like DINOv2 have been employed to extract robust visual features that can encode meaningful visual concepts, facilitating zero-shot reasoning.

Prior works incorporating DINOv2 features for category-level pose estimation, such as, and, typically concatenate these semantic features directly to the model's input. This contrasts with mechanisms observed in state-of-the-art image synthesis models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, InstructPix2Pix), which leverage cross-attention to condition the generation process based on semantic information without requiring explicit category labels. The application of diffusion models to pose estimation is also an emerging area, with methods like DiffusionNOCS demonstrating their utility in managing symmetry and uncertainty.

This research aims to advance category-level pose estimation by exploring an alternative method for incorporating semantic information, specifically drawing inspiration from the cross-attention conditioning mechanisms in modern image synthesis models. By leveraging continuous semantic priors derived from DINOv2 and integrating them via cross-attention, the work seeks to improve generalization capabilities, particularly in transferring knowledge from known to previously unseen or unknown object categories, and to further reduce the Sim2Real gap in pose estimation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust category-level object pose estimation model that can accurately estimate the 6D pose and scale of objects, even those belonging to unseen or unknown categories, by effectively bridging the Sim2Real gap. This is achieved through a novel approach to incorporating semantic information.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Discrete Category Labels:** Traditional category-level pose estimation relies on predefined, discrete semantic categories. These categories are often based on human conventions, which may not align with the underlying geometric or textural similarities relevant for a robot's interaction with objects. For instance, a mug, a teapot, and a pitcher might have similar handling properties but belong to different human-defined categories. The research is motivated by the need to move beyond these arbitrary distinctions and utilize continuous semantic embeddings that can group semantically similar objects, regardless of their conventional labels. This aims to enable more natural and adaptive generalization.

2. **Leveraging Continuous Semantic Priors for Generalization:** The work aims to employ advanced vision foundation models, specifically DINOv2, to extract continuous semantic priors. DINOv2 features are known for their ability to encode meaningful visual concepts and provide strong priors for zero-shot reasoning. By conditioning the pose estimation model directly on these continuous embeddings, the objective is to allow the model to learn a feature space where semantically similar objects are represented adjacently, thereby facilitating continuous transitions between object categories and adaptation to unknown instances.

3. **Introducing Cross-Attention for Semantic Conditioning:** A core motivation is to explore a more effective mechanism for integrating semantic information compared to the concatenation strategies employed by prior works. Inspired by the success of semantic conditioning via cross-attention in state-of-the-art image synthesis models, this research proposes injecting DINOv2 features into a U-Net diffusion model via cross-attention. This mechanism is hypothesized to enable the model to dynamically learn semantic relationships and explicitly model mutual spatial correspondences between geometric features (from RGB and normals) and semantic patches (from DINOv2), leading to more robust pose estimation and enhanced generalization.

4. **Reducing the Sim2Real Gap:** The research aims to improve the transferability of models trained solely on synthetic data to real-world scenarios. This is addressed by combining photorealistic synthetic training data with specific noise models for point normals and by using a domain randomization strategy during synthetic data generation. The objective is to make the model less sensitive to differences between synthetic training environments and real-world deployment.

5. **Enabling Robust Robotic Manipulation of Unseen Objects:** Ultimately, the motivation extends to practical robotic applications. By improving generalization to unknown object instances and categories, the research seeks to enable robots to manipulate objects they have not encountered during training. The final objective is to demonstrate this capability through real-world grasping experiments with a physical robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SCOPE (Semantic Conditioning for Object Pose Estimation) is a diffusion-based category-level object pose estimation model. It integrates continuous DINOv2 semantic features into an attention U-Net diffusion model via cross-attention to estimate object poses.

**Overall Framework:**
The model operates on the principle of regressing a Normalized Object Coordinate Space (NOCS) image, which maps object points to a canonical coordinate system. The estimated NOCS image is then used to retrieve the 6D pose (rotation and translation) and scale of the object. The function `F` maps an input image `I` and semantic input `S` to an estimated NOCS image `X̂`, denoted as `X̂ = F(I, S)`.

**Input Modalities:**
- **Image Input (`I`):** This consists of a local object crop, which is the RGB image (`I_RGB`) concatenated with surface normals (`I_normals`) computed from depth data. These crops are segmented from full-scene images using masks from an object detector. The combined input `I` has dimensions `H x W x 6`.
- **Semantic Input (`S`):** DINOv2 features serve as the continuous semantic priors. These features include both patch tokens and a CLS token, providing a comprehensive representation for zero-shot semantic grounding.

**Core Model - Conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) with U-Net:**
SCOPE approximates the function `F` using a conditional DDPM, which is responsible for generating the NOCS image (`x_0`).
- **Forward Diffusion Process:** During training, noise `ϵ` is gradually added to the ground truth NOCS image (`x_0`) over `T` timesteps according to a fixed variance schedule `β_t`. This results in a noisy image `x_t`, described by `x_t = √ᾱ_t x_0 + √(1 - ᾱ_t) ϵ`, where `ᾱ_t` is the cumulative noise factor.
- **Reverse (Denoising) Process:** An attention U-Net is trained to estimate the noise `ϵ` that was added to `x_t`. The U-Net's prediction is conditioned on the noisy image `x_t`, the current timestep `t`, the image input `I` (RGB + normals), and the semantic conditioning `S`. The training objective minimizes the L2 loss between the true noise `ϵ` and the predicted noise `ϵ_θ(x_t, t, I, S)`.

**Pose Retrieval:**
Once the NOCS image `X̂` is estimated, a 6D pose and a scaling scalar are retrieved. This is achieved by integrating TEASER++, a point cloud registration algorithm. TEASER++ efficiently registers the normalized point cloud (derived from the NOCS image) with the depth data to solve for the 6D pose and scale.

**Semantic Conditioning with Cross-Attention:**
This is a core differentiating aspect of SCOPE. Instead of concatenating semantic features, SCOPE integrates DINOv2 embeddings (`S`) into the U-Net encoder features through cross-attention.
- **Mechanism:** Cross-attention layers are employed at specific blocks (fifth down-sampling and second up-sampling) within the U-Net. In these layers, the U-Net feature map generates the `Query (Q)`, while the frozen semantic embedding `S` is projected to form the `Key (K)` and `Value (V)` matrices. The attention mechanism is calculated as `softmax((QK^T) / √d_k) V`.
- **Bidirectional Correspondence:** This design allows each pixel in the geometric feature maps from the input (RGB + normals) to attend to every semantic patch within the DINOv2 embeddings, and vice-versa. This enables the network to reason about fine-grained geometric structures in the context of broader semantic meaning.
- **Self-Attention:** In parallel, SCOPE also utilizes self-attention layers where Q, K, and V are all derived from the U-Net's own feature maps, further processing intra-geometric relationships.

**Object Symmetry Disambiguation:**
Diffusion models implicitly handle rotational symmetries without requiring handcrafted symmetry annotations or specialized loss functions, a characteristic observed in previous works. This property simplifies the model design for objects with symmetries.

**Synthetic Data Generation and Augmentation:**
To facilitate Sim2Real transfer and robust training, two synthetic datasets are utilized:
- **CAMERA:** Used for direct comparison with existing benchmarks.
- **CAMERA-BlenderProc Randomized (CAMERA-BPR):** A custom dataset rendered using BlenderProc with extensive domain randomization. This includes fully randomized object rotations and increased scene complexity (two random objects per category, 12 objects per scene, 2,000 scenes, resulting in 50,000 images and 600,000 object instances).
- **Online Data Augmentations:** Applied to both CAMERA variants:
 - **Depth Data:** Augmented with Perlin noise to simulate real-world depth sensor characteristics.
 - **RGB and Normals:** Cropped based on rendered segmentation masks, with 2D visibility below 50% being a criterion. Normals are further augmented with random rectangular dropout (50% probability, 5% of crop size) and pixel-wise dropout (0-10% of pixels set to 0).

**Training and Inference Details:**
- **Training:** Conducted for 50 epochs with 1000 denoising timesteps. The Adam optimizer is used with a learning rate of 0.0001 and a warm-up phase of 1000 steps.
- **Inference:** The fast DPM++ solver is employed with 5 denoising steps. For TEASER++, a noise bound of 0.02 and a maximum of 1000 iterations for rotation estimation are set.
- **Input Image Size:** `160 x 160` pixels for direct comparison with prior work.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several findings across category-level pose estimation, generalization capabilities, ablation studies, and real-world grasping experiments.

**Category-Level Pose Estimation Results on REAL275:**
- **Comparison with Synthetic-only Training:** SCOPE demonstrated improved performance against state-of-the-art methods trained exclusively on synthetic data. SCOPE (CAMERA) achieved the highest IoU75 among methods reporting all three IoU thresholds, outperforming GS-Pose by 16.5% at IoU50 and 31.3% at IoU75. For rotation and translation accuracy, SCOPE surpassed DPDN, the leading synthetic benchmark, by 31.9% on the 5°5cm metric and 9.6% on the 10°5cm metric.
- **Comparison with Mixed-data Training:** While falling short of GenPose and SecondPose (which utilize real-world data during training), SCOPE outperformed several mixed-data trained methods such as ShaPO, SSP-Pose, and CenterSnap. Notably, SCOPE improved upon the mixed-data trained SecondPose with a 25.6% increase in IoU50 and a 48.3% increase in IoU75.
- **Impact of Domain Randomization:** The two SCOPE variants (trained on CAMERA and CAMERA-BPR) showed similar overall performance, though CAMERA-BPR exhibited a slight decrease in IoU75. This was attributed to the CAMERA variant's stronger domain adaptation to the specific characteristics of REAL275, whereas CAMERA-BPR aimed for broader generalization.

**Category-Level Pose Estimation Results on YCB-V and TYOL:**
- **Performance on Known Categories:** On the TYOL dataset, SCOPE (CAMERA-BPR) surpassed all synthetic-only and mixed-data trained benchmarks across the 5°5cm, 10°5cm, and 15°5cm metrics, confirming the effectiveness of domain randomization for varied object poses. On YCB-V, both SCOPE variants outperformed synthetic-only models, and SCOPE reduced the Sim2Real gap to 1.1% on the 5°5cm metric when compared to mixed-data trained benchmarks.

**Feature Extractor Comparison (DINOv2 vs. CLIP):**
- An ablation study comparing DINOv2 and CLIP-ViT/L14 as semantic feature extractors demonstrated DINOv2's superior performance. DINOv2 conditioning resulted in a 49.0% recall on the 5°5cm metric, compared to 28.1% for CLIP. This indicates that DINOv2 provides stronger spatial consistency and fine-grained object representations critical for pixel-aligned NOCS regression.

**Beyond Category-Level Pose Estimation (Generalization to Unknown Categories):**
- To assess generalization, SCOPE's cross-attention conditioning was compared against a concatenation-based approach (similar to DiffusionNOCS) with discrete category labels, using all objects in YCB-V and TYOL.
- SCOPE with cross-attention consistently outperformed the concatenation method. On YCB-V, it achieved relative improvements of 27.7% in ADD-S and 23.1% in ADD(-S). On TYOL, the gains were 36.1% in ADD-S and 66.0% in ADD(-S). This indicates that cross-attention conditioning fosters improved semantic awareness and stronger generalization capabilities to objects outside the predefined training categories.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Number of Sampled Points (for TEASER++):** Maximum performance for IoU75 and 10°5cm metrics was observed with 500 sampled points. Reducing points led to negligible performance loss while improving runtime.
- **Number of Denoising Steps (DPM++ solver):** 10 denoising steps yielded maximum performance on 10°5cm and 15°5cm metrics, while 5 steps were optimal for IoU75. A balanced setup of 5 denoising steps was chosen for speed-accuracy trade-off.
- **Number of Refinement Steps:** Multiple noise samples (refinement) resulted in a minor 2.4% performance increase on the 5°5cm metric but increased runtime by 746%. This suggested that refinement was not efficient for the observed performance gains.
- **Optimal Configuration:** The determined optimal balance between speed and accuracy involved 5 denoising steps, 500 sampled points, and a single inference step, resulting in an average runtime of 0.193 s.

**Grasping Experiments:**
- Experiments with a Toyota HSR robot using YCB-V objects demonstrated SCOPE's ability to enable manipulation of both known and unknown object categories.
- For box-shaped objects from unknown categories (e.g., foam brick), SCOPE achieved grasp success rates between 90-100%.
- For known object categories, success rates also ranged from 90-100%, indicating robustness to intra-category variations.
- The lowest success rate (60%) occurred for the pitcher base, attributed to the sensitivity of grasping handles where minor rotation errors led to failure.
- These results suggest that SCOPE is applicable for real-world robotic scenarios involving unseen objects from both known and unknown semantic categories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, SCOPE, contributes to the field of robotic manipulation and computer vision by advancing category-level object pose estimation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Sim2Real Transfer:** SCOPE demonstrates progress in bridging the Sim2Real gap for category-level object pose estimation. By employing photorealistic synthetic data combined with effective noise models for point normals and comprehensive domain randomization, the model achieves performance levels comparable to, and in some cases surpassing, methods trained on a combination of synthetic and real data. This reduces the dependency on labor-intensive real-world data collection and annotation, accelerating the development and deployment of robotic systems.

2. **Enhanced Generalization to Unknown Objects and Categories:** A key innovation is the use of continuous DINOv2 semantic priors conditioned via cross-attention. This mechanism allows SCOPE to move beyond the limitations of discrete, human-defined category labels, enabling the model to generalize effectively to novel object instances and even entirely unknown categories. This capability is critical for robots operating in unstructured, open-ended environments where they frequently encounter objects not seen during training. The demonstrated performance on out-of-category objects in the YCB-V and TYOL datasets highlights this generalization capacity.

3. **Methodological Innovation in Semantic Conditioning:** The approach of integrating semantic features through cross-attention in a U-Net diffusion model for NOCS regression offers a novel paradigm for leveraging high-level semantic information. Unlike previous methods that often rely on simple feature concatenation, cross-attention facilitates a more dynamic and fine-grained understanding of geometric structures in the context of semantic meaning. This explicit modeling of geometric-semantic correspondences can lead to more robust and accurate pose predictions.

4. **Practical Applicability in Robotics:** The successful real-world grasping experiments with a physical robot (Toyota HSR) demonstrate the practical utility of SCOPE. The ability to reliably estimate poses and enable grasping of objects from unknown categories is a direct step towards more autonomous and versatile robotic systems. This has implications for various applications, including logistics, domestic assistance, and manufacturing, where robots need to handle diverse and sometimes unfamiliar items.

5. **Implicit Symmetry Handling:** The inherent properties of diffusion models to implicitly handle rotational symmetry simplify the modeling process. This eliminates the need for complex, handcrafted symmetry annotations or specialized loss functions, making the system more streamlined and potentially more broadly applicable to objects with various forms of symmetry.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays the groundwork for further investigations. Suggestions such as increasing training category diversity to achieve truly universal pose estimation and exploring cross-attention conditioning for geometric features from foundational models on point clouds or meshes indicate directions for continued advancement. This research contributes a significant component to the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic perception systems.
