# Neural Unsigned Distance Fields for Implicit Function Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Neural Unsigned Distance Fields (NDF) introduces a neural implicit representation that learns the unsigned distance to a surface, overcoming the limitation of prior methods to only represent closed surfaces. The approach achieves state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction performance on benchmark datasets while also demonstrating successful capture of open surfaces, complex internal geometries, and general mathematical functions.

## Problem

- Existing neural implicit representations (like Occupancy Networks and Signed Distance Functions) are fundamentally restricted to modeling closed surfaces, which divide space into a clear 'inside' and 'outside'.
- Many real-world objects and scenes, such as garments, car interiors, or architectural scans, inherently feature open surfaces or complex internal structures that existing methods cannot represent natively.
- The requirement for closed surfaces necessitates tedious and often lossy pre-processing steps for training data, introducing artifacts and potentially removing fine details.

## Method

- The authors propose **Neural Distance Fields (NDF)**, a neural network that learns to approximate the ==Unsigned Distance Field (UDF)== of a shape, predicting the absolute distance to the closest surface point without needing an 'inside/outside' distinction.
- A ==3D CNN encoder== processes a sparse input point cloud into multi-scale deep features, which a decoder then uses to regress the unsigned distance for any continuous 3D point.
- Novel algorithms for dense point cloud extraction (==iterative gradient-based projection==) and surface rendering/function evaluation (==modified sphere tracing== with damped marching and Taylor approximation) are developed to visualize and utilize the UDFs effectively.

## Results

- NDF achieves state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction accuracy on closed surface datasets like ShapeNet cars, outperforming methods such as IF-Net and OccNet (e.g., **Chamfer-L2 of 0.055** vs. IF-Net's **0.127** for 3000 input points).
- The model successfully reconstructs complex inner structures of cars and open garment surfaces from unprocessed raw data, a task where SDF-based baselines like SAL failed to capture internal details.
- NDF demonstrates a generalized capacity to approximate 2D mathematical functions and manifolds from sparse samples, using ray tracing to naturally find multiple outputs for multi-target regression tasks (e.g., spirals).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By learning Unsigned Distance Fields (UDFs) instead of Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) or occupancy, NDF can represent a far broader class of shapes, including open surfaces and those with complex internal structures, without requiring artificial data closures.
> - The analytical computation of spatial gradients from the learned UDF enables robust iterative projection for extracting dense point clouds and provides a direction towards the surface.
> - Modified sphere tracing, traditionally a graphics technique, can be repurposed as a machine learning tool for direct rendering and even multi-target regression for complex functions and manifolds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a fundamental problem in computer graphics, vision, and robotics: the reconstruction of continuous, high-resolution 3D surfaces from incomplete and unstructured 3D point clouds. The choice of shape representation is central to effective learning in this domain. Historically, 3D shapes have been represented explicitly using:
- **Voxels:** These discretize space into a 3D grid, allowing for convolutional operations but suffering from cubic memory scaling with resolution, limiting detail.
- **Meshes:** These represent surfaces as a collection of vertices and faces. While common, they often require a fixed topology (e.g., deforming a template) or struggle to guarantee surface continuity and topological flexibility when generated directly.
- **Point Clouds:** These are simple, raw collections of 3D points. While powerful for input, their output resolution is typically fixed, and they lack inherent surface connectivity.

In recent years, **Implicit Function Learning (IFL)** has emerged as a promising alternative. IFL methods train neural networks to represent surfaces implicitly by defining a function $f(p)$ that maps a continuous 3D point $p$ to a value indicating its relationship to the surface. The surface itself is then defined as the zero-level set of this function (e.g., $f(p)=0$). Two main types of implicit representations dominate:
- **Occupancy Networks (OccNet):** These predict whether a point $p$ is "inside" or "outside" the surface, typically yielding a binary classification or a probability.
- **Signed Distance Functions (SDFs):** These predict the signed distance from a point $p$ to the closest point on the surface, with the sign indicating whether $p$ is inside (negative) or outside (positive).

IFL methods offer significant advantages over explicit representations, including the ability to represent continuous surfaces of arbitrary resolution, handle diverse topologies, and generate smooth outputs. However, a critical limitation shared by nearly all existing IFL approaches, including OccNet and SDFs (e.g., DeepSDF, IF-Net), is that they are inherently designed for **closed surfaces**. A closed surface divides 3D space into a distinct "inside" and "outside." Many real-world objects, such as a car with internal structures (seats, steering wheel), clothing, or scanned architectural scenes (walls, open doorways), are not closed. This poses substantial challenges:
1. **Data Pre-processing:** Training data often needs to be artificially closed, which can lead to loss of detail, introduction of artifacts, and removal of crucial internal structures. This is a tedious and often lossy process.
2. **Representational Limitation:** These models cannot natively represent open surfaces or shapes with complex internal configurations that don't clearly define an "inside/outside" separation.

This paper's contribution, **Neural Distance Fields (NDF)**, directly addresses this fundamental limitation. By proposing to learn **Unsigned Distance Fields (UDFs)** instead of SDFs or occupancies, NDF breaks the dependency on closed surfaces. A UDF simply predicts the absolute distance from any point to the closest point on the surface, regardless of whether it's "inside" or "outside." This simple yet powerful shift expands the class of representable shapes beyond closed volumes to include open surfaces, shapes with intricate internal geometries, and even abstract mathematical manifolds and functions. The work positions itself as a significant generalization within the IFL paradigm, aiming to unlock its full potential for a broader range of real-world applications where complex, open geometries are prevalent.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a learnable 3D shape output representation that can handle continuous, high-resolution outputs of **arbitrary and diverse shapes**, specifically overcoming the limitation of existing neural implicit representations to only generate closed surfaces.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Broaden the Class of Representable Shapes:** Existing neural implicit models are limited to shapes that cleanly divide space into an "inside" and "outside" (i.e., closed surfaces). This is a severe constraint for many real-world objects and scenes, such as garments, car interiors, and scanned room environments, which inherently feature open surfaces or complex internal structures. The authors aim to enable the representation and reconstruction of these more general shapes.
2. **Eliminate Artificial Data Pre-processing:** The requirement for closed surfaces in prior IFL methods necessitates tedious and often lossy pre-processing steps. Artificially closing surfaces can introduce artifacts, remove fine details, and destroy valuable internal structures (e.g., the interior of a bus or car). A key motivation is to allow direct learning from raw, unprocessed 3D scan data, preserving fidelity and reducing data pipeline complexity.
3. **Enhance Expressive Power and Generalization:** By moving to an Unsigned Distance Field (UDF) representation, the authors aim to create a more general shape learning model. This includes not only open 3D surfaces but also the ability to approximate open curves, manifolds, and even mathematical functions, demonstrating a wider applicability beyond traditional 3D object reconstruction.
4. **Develop Compatible Visualization and Extraction Algorithms:** Learning with UDFs presents new challenges for visualizing and extracting surfaces, as traditional methods like Marching Cubes rely on detecting sign changes (inside-to-outside flips). Therefore, a crucial objective is to develop new, effective algorithms for generating dense point clouds, meshes, and rendered images directly from the UDF representation.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** While extending the representational capacity, the authors also aim to demonstrate that NDF can achieve or surpass state-of-the-art reconstruction accuracy even on conventional closed surface datasets like ShapeNet, showing that the generalization does not come at the cost of performance.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of neural implicit representations by making them more versatile and applicable to the complexities of real-world 3D data, thereby simplifying data pipelines and enabling the reconstruction of richer, more detailed geometric information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the Neural Distance Fields (NDF) method lies in training a neural network to predict the **unsigned distance field (UDF)** to a surface, rather than the signed distance field (SDF) or occupancy.

#### 3.1. Shape Encoding and Decoding

- **Encoder:** Similar to IF-Nets, the input is a sparse 3D point cloud, which is first voxelized. A 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) then processes this voxelized input to generate a multi-scale grid of deep features ($F_1, \dots, F_n$). These features effectively encode the input shape information at different levels of detail.
- **Decoder:** The decoder's task is to predict the UDF for any continuous point $p \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (where $d=3$ for 3D shapes). Given a point $p$, a neural function $\Psi_x(p)$ extracts features from the multi-scale grid generated by the encoder. A subsequent learned function $\Phi$ then regresses the unsigned distance from these deep features. This function $\Phi$ is designed with ReLU non-linearities in its layers to ensure that its output, representing the distance, is always non-negative ($\Phi \ge 0$). The combined function $f_x(p) = (\Phi \circ \Psi_x)(p)$ is the NDF approximator, mapping points to their predicted unsigned distance to the surface.
- **Spatial Gradient:** A critical aspect of NDF is that its spatial gradient $\nabla_p f_x(p)$ can be computed analytically using standard back-propagation, which is leveraged for surface extraction and normal estimation.

#### 3.2. Learning Process

- **Training Data:** The model is trained on pairs of input point clouds $X_i$ and their corresponding ground truth surfaces $S_i$. Importantly, these surfaces can be open 3D meshes, mathematical functions, or curves. Ground truth UDF values are computed by sampling points in the vicinity of the surface and finding their minimum Euclidean distance to $S_i$.
- **Loss Function:** The training uses a mini-batch L1 loss:
 $L_B(w) = \sum_{x \in B} \sum_{p \in P} |\min(f^w_x(p), \delta) - \min(\text{UDF}(p, S_x), \delta)|$
 Here, $B$ is a mini-batch of inputs, $P$ is a sub-sample of points, and $\delta$ is a clamping parameter (set to 10 cm). Clamping concentrates the model's capacity on accurately representing points close to the surface, similar to how it's used in SDF learning.

#### 3.3. Surface Visualization and Extraction Algorithms

Learning with UDFs presents a challenge because traditional surface extraction methods (e.g., Marching Cubes) rely on the sign changes of SDFs. The authors propose novel algorithms for NDFs:

- **Dense Point Cloud Extraction (Algorithm 1):**
 1. **Initial Sampling:** Start with a large number of points uniformly sampled within the bounding box of the shape.
 2. **Initial Filtering:** Keep only points $p$ where $f(p) < \delta$, indicating they are relatively close to the surface.
 3. **Iterative Projection:** For each selected point $p$, it is moved towards the surface using the equation: $q := p - f(p) \cdot \frac{\nabla_p f(p)}{\|\nabla_p f(p)\|}$. This projects the point along the negative gradient direction (which points towards the closest surface point) by a distance equal to its predicted UDF value. This projection is repeated a few times (e.g., 5 steps) to refine accuracy, as NDF is an approximation.
 4. **Dense Resampling:** To obtain a denser point cloud, points are randomly sampled (with replacement) from the initially projected sparse cloud. Gaussian noise (with variance $\delta/3$) is added to these points, effectively creating new points around the sparse surface.
 5. **Second Iterative Projection:** These newly sampled points are again iteratively projected to the surface using the same gradient-based method.
 6. **Final Filtering:** Only points with $f(p) < \delta$ (after projection) are returned as the dense point cloud. This process is highly parallelizable on GPUs.
 - **Meshing:** Once a dense point cloud is extracted, off-the-shelf meshing algorithms like Ball-Pivoting Algorithm (BPA) can be used to generate high-quality meshes.

- **Ray Tracing for Rendering and Function Evaluation (Algorithm 2):**
 1. **Modified Sphere Tracing:** For direct rendering or evaluating functions (finding the zero-level set along a ray), a modified sphere tracing algorithm is employed. Sphere tracing typically marches along a ray by steps equal to the distance field value.
 2. **Damped Marching:** To prevent over-shooting due to NDF inaccuracies, the algorithm first marches with a damped step size ($\alpha \cdot f(p)$, where $\alpha \in (0, 1]$) until the ray point is closer than a threshold $\epsilon_1$ to the surface.
 3. **Taylor Approximation Refinement:** If over-shooting occurs or for fine-grained convergence near the surface ($f(p) < \epsilon_1$), a refinement step uses a first-order Taylor approximation to estimate the intersection point $\gamma$ along the ray: $f(p) + \gamma \cdot r^T \nabla_p f(p) \approx 0$. The ray then steps by $\beta \cdot \gamma$ ($\beta \in (0, 1]$), allowing for backward steps if needed. This iterates until $f(p) < \epsilon_2$.
 - **Multi-target Regression:** This ray tracing technique is uniquely extended for general function/manifold regression. To find $y$ for a given $(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ in $f(x_1, \dots, x_n, y)=0$, a ray is cast from $(x_1, \dots, x_n, 0)$ along the $y$-axis (i.e., $r = (0, \dots, 0, 1)^T$). NDF can naturally find multiple $y$ values, unlike traditional regression which tends to average them.

- **Surface Normals and Differentiability:** UDFs are theoretically non-differentiable exactly at the surface and at the "cut locus" (points equidistant to multiple surface points). To address this, NDF computes gradients for normals slightly offset from the surface ($\epsilon=5$ mm back along the ray) where the UDF is differentiable and the cut locus is avoided, provided the surface curvature is within limits.

This comprehensive methodology allows NDF to effectively learn and visualize a broader class of shapes, leveraging the inherent properties of unsigned distance fields while addressing their unique challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate NDF's capabilities across various tasks, focusing on 3D shape reconstruction from sparse point clouds and demonstrating its ability to represent more general shapes and functions.

#### 4.1. 3D Shape Reconstruction of Closed Surfaces

- **Comparison with SOTA:** NDF was first evaluated on ShapeNet cars, pre-processed to be closed, to enable direct comparison with methods like OccNet, IF-Net, PSGN, and DMC.
- **Results:** NDF achieved state-of-the-art performance, outperforming all baselines quantitatively. For example, with 3000 input points, NDF achieved a Chamfer-L2 score of 0.055 (median) compared to IF-Net's 0.127. Qualitatively, NDF produced reconstructions of closed surfaces with precision comparable to or better than existing SOTA methods (Figure 5, Table 1 Left).

#### 4.2. 3D Shape Reconstruction of Complex Shapes (Open Surfaces and Inner Structures)

- **Core Advantage Demonstration:** This is where NDF truly differentiates itself. The model was trained on unprocessed ShapeNet cars (5756 models, retaining inner structures) and open garment surfaces (307 models from MGN), as well as 35 real-world 3D scanned spaces.
- **Baselines:** SAL was used as a baseline because it also claims to learn from raw data without needing closed surfaces. However, SAL still outputs SDFs, limiting it to closed surfaces. IF-Net (trained on closed data) served as a reference for typical IFL performance on pre-processed data.
- **Results:**
 - **Inner Structures:** NDF successfully reconstructed complex inner structures of cars (e.g., seats, steering wheel), which SAL, despite being trained on raw data, failed to capture because its output is an SDF and thus limited to closed surfaces (Figure 7). IF-Net, trained on closed data, naturally missed these structures. Quantitatively, NDF achieved significantly lower Chamfer-L2 scores (e.g., 0.041 median for 10000 points on raw data) compared to SAL (5.79) and even the ground truth *without* inner structures (1.996), illustrating its superior ability to capture detail (Table 1 Right).
 - **Open Surfaces:** NDF effectively completed open garment surfaces from sparse input point clouds, visualized through dense output point cloud generation (Algorithm 1) and direct renderings (Algorithm 2) (Figure 8). This confirmed its ability to represent non-manifold, open geometries.
 - **General Class Reconstruction:** NDF also demonstrated the ability to reconstruct all ShapeNet object classes (Figure 6) with superior quantitative results compared to baselines, further highlighting its generalization capacity.
 - **Multi-Class Training:** Unlike SAL, which diverged when trained on multiple object classes jointly, NDF successfully handled this setting.

#### 4.3. Functions and Manifolds

- **Beyond 3D Shapes:** NDF was tested on its ability to represent arbitrary 2D mathematical functions and manifolds. A single NDF model was trained on sparse 2D point samples from linear functions, parabolas, sinusoids, and spirals.
- **Results:** NDF successfully interpolated sparse data points to approximate these functions and manifolds accurately. The ray-tracing algorithm (Algorithm 2) was employed to extract the surfaces, demonstrating its utility beyond 3D rendering for general regression tasks. Notably, for multi-target regression (e.g., a spiral where a single x can map to multiple y values), NDF naturally produced multiple outputs, unlike standard regression methods that would average them (Figure 9). This marks the first application of sphere tracing for regression, transforming a graphics technique into a core machine learning tool.

#### 4.4. Visualization Algorithms

- The developed algorithms for dense point cloud extraction (Algorithm 1) and direct rendering via modified sphere tracing (Algorithm 2) were shown to be effective in visualizing the NDF reconstructions, producing millions of points and high-quality images.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Neural Unsigned Distance Fields (NDF) represents a significant advancement in the field of implicit function learning for 3D shape representation and reconstruction. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

#### 5.1. Technical Advancements and Generalization

- **Unlocking Open Surfaces and Inner Structures:** The most impactful contribution is NDF's ability to represent open surfaces and shapes with intricate internal structures. This breaks a fundamental barrier of previous implicit models (SDFs, Occupancy Networks) which were restricted to closed, manifold surfaces. This allows for a more faithful representation of real-world objects and scenes that are often open or contain internal components.
- **Direct Learning from Raw Data:** By eliminating the need for artificial closing of surfaces during pre-processing, NDF streamlines data pipelines. This not only saves tedious manual effort but also preserves the original detail and integrity of raw sensor data (e.g., LiDAR scans, depth camera captures), leading to more accurate and complete reconstructions.
- **Broader Applicability:** NDF's demonstrated capacity to represent not just 3D shapes but also 2D curves, manifolds, and mathematical functions highlights its generalizability. This opens doors for its application in diverse fields beyond 3D object modeling, such as scientific data visualization or learning complex functional relationships.
- **Novel Visualization Techniques:** The development of efficient algorithms for dense point cloud extraction and modified sphere tracing for rendering UDFs is crucial. These innovations solve the challenge of visualizing implicit surfaces that lack an "inside/outside" distinction, making NDF practical for applications requiring explicit surface outputs or rendering.
- **Multi-Target Regression:** The novel use of sphere tracing for multi-target regression is a clever repurposing of a graphics technique for a core machine learning task. This allows NDF to naturally model data with multiple outputs for a single input, where traditional regression methods might struggle or average responses.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact on Applications

- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** NDF can enable the creation of highly detailed and realistic 3D environments and objects for AR/VR applications, especially when dealing with complex scanned scenes or digital humans and clothing.
- **Autonomous Vehicles and Robotics:** Accurate 3D reconstruction and completion of environments from sparse sensor data (e.g., LiDAR) are critical for navigation, object recognition, and interaction. NDF's ability to handle complex, open scene elements (like road infrastructure, open doors, or varied terrain) could significantly improve perception systems.
- **Virtual Humans and Garments:** In digital human modeling, accurately representing clothing (which is inherently an open surface) and complex body shapes with fine details is crucial. NDF offers a promising approach for high-fidelity garment reconstruction and simulation.
- **Cultural Heritage and Fabrication:** For tasks like digital preservation of artifacts or quality assurance in manufacturing, creating precise 3D models from scans is essential. NDF can help capture the intricate details and potentially open features of real-world objects.
- **Research in 3D Representation Learning:** NDF provides a new paradigm for implicit shape representation, likely inspiring further research into more generalized distance fields and their applications in generative models, shape editing, and semantic understanding.
