# RMGS-SLAM: Real-time Multi-sensor Gaussian Splatting SLAM

> [!summary] Summary
> RMGS-SLAM presents a real-time multi-sensor (LiDAR-Inertial-Visual) SLAM system for generating photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting maps. It achieves the best overall localization accuracy (ATE RMSE) and superior rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across challenging large-scale outdoor datasets, maintaining global consistency through explicit loop closure and efficient mapping via cascaded Gaussian initialization.

## Problem

- Integrating computationally intensive 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for photorealistic mapping into real-time SLAM while maintaining low-latency pose estimation.
- Achieving long-term global consistency and suppressing accumulated drift in large-scale 3DGS maps due to the lack of explicit loop closure mechanisms in existing systems.
- Improving the quality and accelerating the convergence of 3D Gaussian optimization by addressing suboptimal initialization strategies in current LiDAR-assisted methods.

## Method

- Developed a tightly coupled ==LiDAR-Inertial-Visual (LIV)== SLAM framework for ==non-blocking dense mapping==, executing pose estimation and Gaussian initialization in parallel with global map optimization.
- Introduced a **cascaded 3D Gaussian initialization** strategy that combines ==feed-forward model predictions== with ==voxel-PCA== derived geometric priors to improve primitive quality and accelerate optimization.
- Implemented a **Gaussian-based loop closure** method for large-scale scenes, utilizing ==Gaussian GICP registration== for loop constraint estimation and ==GTSAM-based pose graph optimization== for global consistency.

## Results

- **RMGS-SLAM** achieved the best overall localization accuracy (ATE RMSE) and superior rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all tested sequences compared to existing 3DGS-based SLAM systems.
- Demonstrated near-real-time performance, with Real-time factors close to **1** on most sequences, successfully balancing computational demands for pose estimation, continuous mapping, and loop closure.
- Validated the effectiveness of cascaded Gaussian initialization via voxel-PCA and the Gaussian-based loop closure mechanism, which significantly improved rendering quality and global consistency in large-scale scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly integrating LiDAR, IMU, and camera data enables robust real-time ego-motion estimation and high-quality photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction.
> - A cascaded Gaussian initialization strategy, leveraging both learned predictions and geometric priors from voxel-PCA, significantly improves the quality of initial 3D Gaussians and accelerates optimization convergence.
> - Explicit loop closure through Gaussian-based GICP registration and pose graph optimization effectively maintains long-term global consistency and reduces drift in large-scale 3DGS maps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental capability for autonomous systems, enabling robots to estimate their pose while concurrently constructing a map of the surrounding environment. Historically, SLAM systems predominantly generated geometry-centric maps, which provided structural information but often lacked the photorealistic detail necessary for immersive visual applications and certain robotic tasks, such as precise manipulation or human-robot interaction in visually rich environments.

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents a significant advancement in scene representation. 3DGS models scenes using explicit anisotropic Gaussian primitives, allowing for efficient differentiable rasterization. This approach yields an appearance-aware, continuous, and directly renderable map, making it suitable for applications requiring high-fidelity visual outputs. Consequently, integrating 3DGS into SLAM systems offers the potential to combine accurate pose estimation with photorealistic environment reconstruction.

However, developing real-time 3DGS-based SLAM presents several challenges. High-quality 3DGS reconstruction typically involves iterative optimization, which can be computationally intensive and time-consuming. Real-time SLAM, conversely, requires both pose estimation and map updates to keep pace with continuous, high-frequency sensor data streams. Existing 3DGS-based SLAM methods can be broadly categorized:
- **Visual SLAM systems (e.g., GS-SLAM, MonoGS, SplaTAM):** These primarily rely on visual input and often struggle in unconstrained real-world environments due to factors like viewpoint variation, illumination changes, and imperfect geometry, affecting tracking robustness and reconstruction quality. Many also operate closer to "online" rather than "real-time" due to computational constraints, requiring significant processing time after data acquisition.
- **LiDAR-assisted 3DGS-based SLAM:** To enhance geometric perception and pose robustness, LiDAR sensors have been integrated.
 - **Loosely coupled methods (e.g., VPGS-SLAM, LIV-GaussMap, LIV-GS):** These typically prioritize a dominant sensing modality or perform asynchronous multi-sensor pose estimation, simplifying integration. However, the loose or asynchronous interaction between tracking and mapping can hinder real-time performance under high-rate sensor inputs.
 - **Tightly coupled methods (e.g., Gaussian-LIC, LVI-GS, FusionGS-SLAM, GS-LIVM, GS-LIVO):** These aim to fuse multi-modal measurements within a unified estimator and integrate them more deeply with Gaussian mapping to improve spatiotemporal consistency. Despite progress, maintaining both real-time performance and global consistency in large-scale environments remains a challenge. Issues include reliance on local optimization, limited real-time iteration budgets for fine details, or insufficient geometric assumptions during Gaussian initialization. Furthermore, explicit loop closure, crucial for long-term global consistency in large environments, is often omitted in these real-time systems to preserve computational efficiency.

RMGS-SLAM positions itself within the landscape of tightly coupled LiDAR-Inertial-Visual (LIV) 3DGS-based SLAM systems. It addresses the limitations of existing methods by proposing a framework designed to simultaneously achieve real-time performance, continuous dense mapping, high-quality Gaussian initialization, and robust global consistency through explicit Gaussian-based loop closure, particularly in large-scale real-world scenes. The paper aims to fill the gap where current systems either lack real-time capability, robust multi-sensor integration, effective initialization, or global consistency mechanisms suitable for expansive, looped environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) system that leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for photorealistic environment reconstruction in large-scale real-world settings. This objective is motivated by several identified challenges in the current state of 3DGS-based SLAM:

1. **Simultaneous Achievement of Real-time Performance, Continuous Reconstruction, and Global Consistency:** Existing 3DGS-based SLAM methods often struggle to concurrently deliver low-latency pose estimation, continuous updates to a dense 3D Gaussian map, and long-term global consistency, especially when operating in large-scale environments with high-frequency sensor inputs. The iterative optimization inherent to 3DGS reconstruction poses a significant computational burden for real-time systems.
2. **Improving Gaussian Primitive Initialization Quality and Optimization Convergence:** Many current LiDAR-assisted 3DGS-SLAM systems rely on LiDAR observations with limited geometric assumptions for Gaussian initialization. This often necessitates extensive online refinement, slowing down the mapping process and impacting real-time performance. A more robust and efficient initialization strategy is needed to accelerate the convergence of the subsequent Gaussian optimization.
3. **Ensuring Global Consistency in Large-Scale Environments:** For SLAM systems to operate reliably over extended periods and across large areas, mechanisms for correcting accumulated drift are essential. Loop closure is a standard technique for this, but it is frequently omitted in real-time 3DGS-based systems to maintain runtime efficiency. This omission limits their ability to build globally consistent maps in large-scale environments where drift can accumulate significantly. The paper seeks to integrate an explicit and robust loop closure mechanism directly operating on the Gaussian map.
4. **Addressing the Limitations of Loosely vs. Tightly Coupled Systems:** While tightly coupled multi-sensor fusion (LiDAR-Inertial-Visual) has shown promise for robust pose estimation, its effective integration with continuous 3DGS mapping, especially with loop closure, remains an area requiring further development to meet real-time and large-scale requirements.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to fully exploit the benefits of 3DGS for photorealistic mapping in real-time SLAM, while overcoming the computational, initialization, and global consistency hurdles that limit the applicability of existing approaches in demanding, large-scale real-world scenarios. The goal is to provide a system that offers both accurate localization and a high-fidelity, continuously updated, and globally consistent 3D environment representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed RMGS-SLAM system employs a four-module design for real-time localization and photorealistic mapping.

#### 3.1. A. LIV Front-end

The system begins with a LiDAR-Inertial-Visual (LIV) front-end, built upon a Sequential Iterative Error State Kalman Filter (IESKF) system. This module is responsible for real-time ego-motion estimation and providing time-synchronized data streams. Its outputs include corrected pose estimates, RGB images, projected depth images, and structured geometric priors derived from voxel-based Principal Component Analysis (voxel-PCA).

#### 3.2. B. Voxel-PCA Geometric Fitting

To provide geometric priors for Gaussian initialization, a voxel-PCA geometric fitting scheme is implemented. The latest colored point cloud, processed by the front-end, is partitioned into voxels using a hash-indexed octree. For each voxel, the mean and covariance of the points are computed, followed by eigen-decomposition to obtain eigenvalues (λ_min ≤ λ_mid ≤ λ_max) and corresponding eigenvectors (v_min, v_mid, v_max).
- **Geometry Classification:** Structures are classified as planar if λ_min is below a threshold (τ_p) with normal v_min, or linear if λ_max/λ_min exceeds a threshold (τ_l) with direction v_max. Otherwise, the structure is deemed unreliable.
- **Geometric Descriptor:** A descriptor `g = [v_k^T, μ^T]^T` is defined, where v_k is v_min for planar structures and v_max for linear structures.
- **Uncertainty Propagation:** The uncertainty of the descriptor (Σ_g) is computed by propagating point-level covariances (Σ_W_i) from points within each voxel using a Jacobian-based approach.
- **Output:** Each point is assigned attributes (p_W_i, R_pca, s_pca, c_i, m_i) including its world-frame position, PCA-derived rotation and scale (from eigenvectors and log-square-root of eigenvalues), color, and a reliability indicator. This module generates a compact geometric representation with explicit orientation, scale, and reliability.

#### 3.3. C. Cascaded 3D Gaussian Primitive Initialization

This module initializes new Gaussian primitives. Keyframes are selected periodically, and loopframes are designated when relative motion exceeds thresholds. Observations from keyframes between successive loopframes are aggregated to initialize new Gaussians.
- **Base Initialization:** For each aggregated 3D point, a Gaussian is instantiated with its world-frame mean at the point's location and its DC component from the point's color.
- **Cascaded Attribute Initialization (Θ(p)):** The remaining attributes (scale, rotation, opacity, Spherical Harmonics coefficients) are initialized via a three-tiered strategy:
 1. **Model-based (Θ_model(p)):** If a valid prediction is available, a pre-trained feed-forward model (FFM), specifically G3Splat, processes the current and previous loopframe images to predict per-pixel Gaussian attributes. For each 3D point, it is projected into both views, and attributes from the closer, valid view are selected via bilinear interpolation. Predicted camera-frame rotations are transformed to the world frame. Predicted anisotropic scale ratios (a) are normalized and combined with LiDAR depth (d) and camera focal length (f) to determine the world-frame log-scale `s_out = log((d/f) * a)`.
 2. **PCA-based (Θ_pca(p)):** If model prediction is invalid but the point is geometrically reliable (from voxel-PCA), rotation is directly from PCA eigenvectors. The PCA-derived scale `s_pca` is adjusted by a dynamic factor `log β`, where β relates the number of unreliable model predictions to total points, yielding `s_out = s_pca + log β`.
 3. **Heuristic-based (Θ_heur(p)):** If neither model nor PCA initialization is applicable, an isotropic scale `s_out = log(d/f)` is used, and rotation is set to identity.
- **Default Attributes:** For PCA and heuristic branches, opacity is constant, and higher-order SH coefficients are zero. This cascaded design aims to provide robust and high-quality initialization, facilitating faster optimization convergence.

#### 3.4. D. Optimization

Newly initialized Gaussians are inserted into the global map after a spatial proximity test to filter duplicates. Optimization is supervised by keyframe images.
- **Optimization Windows:** Two temporal windows (recent and history) are maintained for sampling views, balancing fast adaptation with long-range consistency.
- **Gaussian Update Scope:** Only Gaussians in the most recent K segments are updated; older segments remain frozen to preserve stability.
- **Stable Supervision:** A geometric support mask (M_geo) from accumulated opacity and a conservative interior mask (M_int) from morphological erosion are used. Supervision terms are evaluated only within M_int to avoid unstable gradients in under-observed regions.
- **Rendering:** Color rendering uses differentiable Gaussian splatting via alpha compositing. Depth is computed using the same compositing weights.
- **Loss Function:** The overall objective `L` combines photometric (L1 loss `L_rgb`), structural (1-SSIM `L_ssim`), and depth consistency (L1 loss `L_depth`) terms, weighted by λ_rgb, λ_ssim, and λ_depth, respectively.
- **Refinement and Pruning:** Visible and unfrozen Gaussians are refined using a sparse gradient-based optimizer. Low-opacity Gaussians are pruned to remove weak or redundant primitives.

#### 3.5. E. Gaussian-based Loop Closure

To ensure global consistency in large-scale scenes, loop closure is performed directly on the 3D Gaussian map.
- **Loop Candidate Search:** For each current loopframe, spatially proximate historical loopframes (sufficiently separated in time/index) are identified as loop candidates.
- **Gaussian Set Construction:**
 - **Source Set (G_src_i):** Gaussians associated with the current loopframe.
 - **Target Set (G_tar_j):** Gaussians extracted from the optimized global map around the historical loopframe `j`, specifically those within the camera frusta of its neighboring keyframes `K_j`. Further filtering removes distant Gaussians and those already in G_src_i.
- **Gaussian-based Generalized Iterative Closest Point (GICP):** Relative pose estimation for loop constraints is performed using an extended GICP.
 - **Covariance Regularization:** Gaussian covariances (Σ_src_m, Σ_tar_n) are regularized to a planar form (eigenvalues diag(1, 1, 10^-3)) to improve registration robustness.
 - **Registration:** The rigid transform `T = (R, t)` that aligns G_src_i to G_tar_j is found by minimizing a sum of Mahalanobis distances between corresponding Gaussian centers, weighted by their regularized covariances.
- **Loop Acceptance and Pose Graph Optimization:** Loop candidates are accepted if registration converges and the residual error is below a threshold. Accepted alignments create loop edges, which are then jointly optimized with odometry constraints in a GTSAM-based pose graph.
- **Map Update:** After pose-graph optimization, updated corrected poses are propagated to associated Gaussian segments and supervisory camera views in the global map, enforcing global consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated RMGS-SLAM against several baselines on both public datasets (FAST-LIVO2, MARS-LVIG) and a newly collected large-scale looped outdoor dataset (Driving1, Driving2).

#### 4.1. A. Evaluation of Rendering Quality

- **Overall Performance:** RMGS-SLAM achieved high rendering quality, demonstrating the best or near-best performance in terms of PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across almost all evaluated sequences (Table I). It produced sharper RGB images with fewer artifacts under novel views (Fig. 3).
- **Impact of Loop Closure:** The integrated Gaussian-based loop closure contributed to improved global Gaussian map consistency in large-scale, revisited scenes. This was evident in more consistent reconstructions, such as road markings, when compared to methods lacking explicit loop closure (e.g., Gaussian-LIC2), which showed overlapping structures.
- **Impact of Cascaded Initialization:** The cascaded Gaussian initialization strategy, combining feed-forward predictions with geometric priors, facilitated more faithful recovery of view-dependent lighting and shading effects.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Gaussian-LIC2:** Showed a favorable trade-off but its lack of loop closure limited global consistency in large scenes.
 - **GS-LIVM:** Demonstrated degraded pose estimation and reconstruction quality due to its reliance on sweep reconstruction assumptions, which did not consistently hold.
 - **MonoGS and SplaTAM:** Evaluated offline (LiDAR converted to depth images). SplaTAM was sensitive to the quality of sparse and noisy projected depth, leading to inferior reconstruction. MonoGS, relying more on photometric residuals, achieved competitive quality but incurred substantially higher runtime.

#### 4.2. B. Evaluation of Localization Accuracy

- **Overall Performance:** RMGS-SLAM consistently achieved the best overall localization accuracy, as quantified by Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) in RMSE, across all tested sequences (Table II).
- **Impact of Loop Closure:** The Gaussian-based loop closure significantly improved global consistency and reduced accumulated drift, particularly noticeable along the vertical (z-axis) direction (Fig. 4). This led to better trajectory closure and geometric consistency upon revisiting previously mapped areas compared to baselines, which often exhibited residual offsets or heading inconsistencies.

#### 4.3. C. Runtime Analysis

- **System-level Runtime:** RMGS-SLAM achieved Real-time factors close to 1 on most sequences (Table I), indicating near-real-time full-pipeline processing while maintaining strong rendering quality. In contrast, several baseline methods had significantly larger factors or failed to complete sequences due to high optimization overhead.
- **Module-level Runtime:** Analysis on small (HKU Campus) and large (Driving1) sequences (Fig. 5) revealed varying computational demands:
 - In small-scale scenes, runtime was primarily shared between state estimation and Gaussian primitive optimization.
 - In large-scale, looped scenes, loop closure became the dominant computational cost once triggered, accompanied by increased GPU memory usage.
- **Conclusion:** Despite these variations, the system maintained real-time requirements in both scenarios (e.g., Real-time factors of 1.03 for HKU Campus and 1.01 for Driving1), confirming stable real-time performance across different scene scales.

#### 4.4. D. Ablation Study

The ablation study (Table III) demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed components:
- **Cascaded Initialization:** The full system outperformed variants without voxel-PCA, the Feed-Forward Model (FFM), or both, validating the cascaded strategy. Removing the FFM led to a larger drop in SSIM and LPIPS than PSNR, suggesting FFM primarily improves structural and perceptual fidelity.
- **FFM Choice:** G3Splat was found to deliver better overall performance compared to DepthSplat as the FFM instantiation.
- **Loop Closure:** The loop closure mechanism significantly improved rendering performance in large-scale looped scenes (e.g., Driving1) without causing noticeable degradation in smaller sequences, despite the additional computational overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RMGS-SLAM system presents a notable advancement in the field of real-time 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) SLAM, particularly for large-scale, complex environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Technical Contribution to Real-time 3DGS-SLAM:** The paper addresses critical limitations of existing 3DGS-based SLAM methods, specifically the challenge of simultaneously achieving real-time performance, continuous dense mapping, and long-term global consistency. By tightly coupling LiDAR-Inertial-Visual (LIV) sensors and introducing novel components for initialization and loop closure, RMGS-SLAM contributes a robust framework capable of operating under demanding real-world conditions.
2. **Enhanced Photorealistic Mapping:** The proposed cascaded Gaussian initialization strategy, which intelligently combines feed-forward model predictions with geometry-aware priors (voxel-PCA), facilitates the generation of high-quality 3D Gaussian primitives. This leads to faster optimization convergence and ultimately results in more photorealistic and visually consistent 3D maps compared to prior art. The ability to render sharp RGB images with fewer artifacts under novel views is a direct outcome.
3. **Improved Localization Accuracy and Global Consistency:** The integration of a 3DGS-based loop closure mechanism, utilizing Gaussian-based Generalized Iterative Closest Point (GICP) registration and pose-graph optimization, is a significant development. This component effectively mitigates accumulated drift and enforces global consistency across large trajectories, leading to superior localization accuracy and map integrity, particularly in looped environments.
4. **Novel Benchmark Dataset:** The creation and release of a new large-scale looped outdoor dataset (Driving1, Driving2) with hardware-synchronized LiDAR-camera-IMU streams and ground-truth trajectories provide a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset enables more realistic and comprehensive evaluation of 3DGS-based SLAM systems in challenging scenarios, fostering further development and benchmarking in this area.
5. **Broadened Applicability for Robotic Systems:** The capability to generate real-time, accurate poses and high-fidelity, renderable 3D maps has direct implications for various robotic applications. These include, but are not limited to, autonomous navigation, augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) systems requiring highly detailed scene representations, and advanced human-robot interaction where photorealistic environmental understanding is beneficial. The framework lays a foundation for future integration into downstream robotic tasks that demand both precise localization and rich environmental perception.
6. **Direction for Future Research:** While demonstrating strong performance, the paper acknowledges the current system's reliance on high-performance hardware. This highlights a clear avenue for future work focused on improving computational efficiency, potentially through more optimized Gaussian representation, adaptive optimization strategies, or hardware acceleration. Such advancements would broaden the applicability of real-time 3DGS-SLAM to a wider range of platforms and tasks.
