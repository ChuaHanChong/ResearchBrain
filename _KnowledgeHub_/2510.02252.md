# Retargeting Matters: General Motion Retargeting for Humanoid Motion Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from Stanford University's Department of Computer Science demonstrates that the quality of motion retargeting critically influences the performance and robustness of humanoid motion tracking policies. Their proposed General Motion Retargeting (GMR) method generates physically plausible and perceptually faithful reference motions, leading to improved policy success rates and reduced tracking errors compared to existing open-source approaches.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to robustly track human motion due to the 'embodiment gap' (differences in morphology, kinematics, and dynamics) between humans and robots.
- Existing motion retargeting methods introduce artifacts like ground penetration, self-intersection, and physically infeasible movements, which are often overlooked or assumed to be corrected by downstream reinforcement learning (RL) policies.
- The unproven assumption that RL policies can easily compensate for poor retargeting without extensive reward engineering leads to inefficient policy development and reduces robustness.

## Method

- Systematically evaluated the impact of various retargeting methods (PHC, ProtoMotions, Unitree) on the performance of humanoid motion tracking policies, specifically isolating the effect by ==suppressing excessive reward tuning==.
- Developed **GMR** (==General Motion Retargeting==), a novel ==two-stage optimization-based pipeline== that includes non-uniform local scaling and robustly handles physical constraints.
- Trained motion imitation policies using an independent framework (**BeyondMimic**) on retargeted data and evaluated their performance across diverse motions and rigorous simulated environments, including conditions mimicking real-world deployment.

## Results

- GMR consistently achieved higher success rates and significantly lower tracking errors compared to PHC and ProtoMotions, often matching or closely approaching the performance of a high-quality closed-source baseline (Unitree).
- Policies trained on GMR-retargeted data demonstrated enhanced robustness, particularly for dynamic and long motion sequences like 'Dance 1' and 'Run (stop & go)', where other methods often resulted in **0% success rates**.
- A user study confirmed that GMR-retargeted motions were perceived as more faithful to the original human movements than those generated by other open-source techniques, indicating high perceptual fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of retargeted motion is a fundamental upstream factor that critically impacts the success rates and robustness of humanoid motion tracking policies, particularly for dynamic and complex movements.
> - Specific physical artifacts in retargeted data, such as ground penetration, self-intersection, and sudden joint value jumps, severely hinder the ability of RL policies to learn and perform effectively.
> - Investing in high-quality motion retargeting can significantly reduce the need for laborious reward engineering and domain randomization, streamlining the development of more robust and generalizable humanoid controllers.
