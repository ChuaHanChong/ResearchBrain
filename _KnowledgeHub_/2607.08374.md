# Large-Language-Models-as-a-Judge in Theory-Agnostic Adaptive Metric-Alignment for Prototypical Networks in Personality Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> The JAM framework develops a theory-agnostic approach for personality recognition, integrating a graph prototypical network, cross-theory harmonization, and an LLM-as-a-judge mechanism for data quality assessment. It achieves over 9% improvement in balanced accuracy on the Essays dataset and 8x lower training FLOPs, offering a more generalizable and efficient model.

## Problem

- Personality recognition models are constrained by specific psychological theories (e.g., Big-5, MBTI), limiting generalization across diverse datasets and cultural contexts.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, consistently annotated public datasets, and existing data is often heterogeneous, leading to partial or inconsistent views of latent traits.
- Many current methods treat personality recognition as a static classification task, which may not fully capture the complexity and underlying shared structures of personality.

## Method

- A theory-agnostic **JAM** framework combining an ==Attention-Pooled Graph Prototypical Network==, using a ==Longformer== backbone, learns structured representations by clustering embeddings.
- **Cross-Theory Harmonization (CTH)** bridges heterogeneous psychological annotations and derives latent "pseudo-facets" via ==Prototypical Fine-tuning==, ==Human-Guided Linkage==, and ==Machine-Induced Consensus==.
- An **LLM-as-a-Judge (LAJ)** mechanism, operating either pre-loop or in-the-loop, assesses training sample quality, identifies hard examples, and guides adaptive metric learning to refine confidence weights.

## Results

- The full JAM framework consistently outperformed baselines, achieving an average improvement of over **9%** in Balanced Accuracy on the Essays dataset, demonstrating enhanced cross-framework generalization.
- The LLM-before-the-loop (LBL) LAJ pipeline consistently outperformed the LLM-in-the-loop (LIL) approach, providing an additional **2.4%** Balanced Accuracy gain on the Essays dataset and faster convergence.
- The JAM framework exhibited higher computational efficiency with approximately **8x** lower training FLOPs and reduced inference time compared to prior approaches, suggesting suitability for real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Theory-agnostic learning, facilitated by cross-theory harmonization, can discover unified latent "pseudo-facets" that capture shared psychological structures across different personality theories.
> - Leveraging LLMs as auxiliary evaluators for data quality, rather than primary predictors, effectively mitigates noise in training datasets and improves model robustness, especially for lower-quality data.
> - Integrating human-defined mappings (Human-Guided Linkage) with data-driven consensus (Machine-Induced Consensus) within the CTH module is essential for effectively aligning and learning from heterogeneous personality datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of personality recognition is characterized by efforts to infer an individual's personality traits from their digital footprints, particularly textual data. This capability is considered valuable for applications such as recommendation systems, personalized human-AI interactions, and addressing data sparsity or cold start problems in user modeling.

Historically, personality recognition models have been primarily built around specific psychological theories, such as the Big-5 (OCEAN) or Myers–Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI). This theory-dependent approach has led to limitations in model generalization across diverse datasets and cultural contexts, as different theories employ distinct dimensions and terminologies, and their annotations may reflect partial or inconsistent views of latent traits. Furthermore, the scarcity of large-scale, consistently annotated public datasets, exacerbated by privacy concerns leading to the discontinuation of resources like the myPersonality dataset, presents a significant challenge. Existing methods often frame personality recognition as a static classification task, which may not fully capture the complexity and context-dependency of personality.

Recent advancements in natural language processing (NLP) and representation learning have seen the adoption of Transformer architectures and large language models (LLMs). Encoder-only models, like Sentence Transformers (e.g., MiniLM, MPNet, Sentence-T5), are used to generate semantic embeddings, while decoder-only autoregressive LLMs (e.g., GPT-3, LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, GPT-4, o1, DeepSeek R1) have demonstrated few-shot and zero-shot capabilities for direct personality inference through prompting. Prior works have explored various modeling techniques, including BiLSTM-based models, graph neural networks (e.g., KGrAt-Net, TranSentGAT), and generative AI for data augmentation and robust learning in low-resource settings.

This work contributes to the evolving landscape by attempting to address the fundamental limitation of theory-dependence. It proposes a framework that shifts the focus from adapting to predefined psychological taxonomies towards discovering unified latent "pseudo-facets" that are intended to capture shared psychological structure. By integrating heterogeneous datasets and leveraging LLMs for data quality assessment rather than direct inference, the research aims to improve cross-framework generalization and robustness, particularly for personality theories with limited data resources. It also seeks to balance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs with the computational efficiency of encoder-based architectures for representation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a *theory-agnostic* framework, named JAM ((J)udge for (A)daptive (M)etric-Alignment), for personality recognition. This framework is designed to learn generalizable psychological representations and infer an individual's latent psychological profile directly from textual samples, without requiring adherence to specific, predefined personality taxonomies or theory-specific labels during training or inference.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations observed in traditional and contemporary personality recognition approaches:

1. **Constraint of Theory-Dependent Models:** Current personality recognition models are largely built upon specific psychological theories (e.g., Big-5, MBTI). This dependence limits their capacity to generalize effectively across datasets that might use different theoretical frameworks or across diverse cultural contexts. Personality itself is posited to be theory-invariant, arising from stable psychological patterns that should manifest consistently regardless of the specific framework used to describe them.
2. **Heterogeneous and Scarce Data:** The availability of large-scale, consistently annotated data for personality recognition is limited. Existing public datasets are often small and use varied or sometimes inconsistent annotation schemes, reflecting only partial views of latent traits. The discontinuation of major datasets due to privacy concerns further exacerbates this data scarcity. Integrating these heterogeneous data sources while maintaining consistency is a significant challenge.
3. **Static Classification Paradigm:** Many existing methods treat personality recognition as a static classification task. This approach may not fully leverage deeper psychological insights or capture the underlying, shared structures of personality that could lead to more robust and generalizable models.
4. **Need for Unified Latent Representations:** The research is motivated by the idea of moving beyond simply fitting models to predefined personality theories. Instead, the goal is to discover unified latent "pseudo-facets" that represent shared psychological structures common across different theoretical frameworks. This would allow for a more flexible and robust understanding of personality.
5. **Improving Generalization and Low-Resource Support:** By learning theory-agnostic representations, the framework aims to improve generalization across existing personality theories and facilitate personality inference even for "low-resource" theories where large annotated datasets are unavailable.
6. **Addressing Data Quality and Robustness:** Human-annotated personality datasets can contain ambiguities, mislabels, or boundary samples. The research is motivated by the need to develop mechanisms to identify and mitigate the impact of such noisy data, thereby improving the robustness and quality of the learning process.
7. **Balancing LLM Capabilities with Practicality:** While large language models (LLMs) offer powerful reasoning capabilities, their direct use for inference can be computationally expensive and introduce risks of data leakage. The motivation includes finding an efficient way to leverage LLMs' evaluative strengths for data quality assessment while keeping core representation learning efficient and controllable via encoder-based models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The JAM framework proposes a theory-agnostic approach to personality recognition by integrating a language embedding backbone with an Attention-Pooled Graph Prototypical Network, a Cross-Theory Harmonization (CTH) module, and an LLM-as-a-Judge (LAJ) mechanism.

#### 3.1. Datasets

The study utilized two publicly available datasets:
- **Essays Dataset:** Annotated with Big-5 personality traits, collected in a controlled writing environment. It contained 1578 training, 395 validation, and 494 test samples.
- **Kaggle Dataset:** Annotated with MBTI personality types, sourced from an online community forum (PersonalityCafe). It comprised 5552 training, 1388 validation, and 1735 test samples.
Both datasets were split using Tan’s stratified algorithm to ensure an even distribution of personality traits across splits. The task was formulated as a multi-label classification problem, where each sample text `x` has associated binary labels `y` (for `|d|` personality dimensions) and sample-specific confidence weights `z`.

#### 3.2. Attention-Pooled Graph Prototypical Network

This component learns structured representations by clustering in an embedding space:
- **Language Embedding Backbone:** The Longformer model was chosen as the backbone due to its ability to process long textual sequences efficiently using a combination of local and global attention mechanisms.
- **Graph Construction:** The layer-wise hidden representations `H` from the Longformer encoder (L layers, `v` dimensions each) are treated as nodes in a graph (`H ∈ R^(L×v)`). A fully connected weighted graph is constructed using a normalized adjacency matrix `Ã`, where all nodes are interconnected, allowing symmetric information exchange across different abstraction levels.
- **Graph Neural Network (GNN) Layers:** Node embeddings are iteratively updated (`H^(k) = σ(ÃH^(k-1)W^(k))`), where `σ` is the LeakyReLU activation and `W^(k)` is a trainable weight matrix. This aggregates information from neighboring nodes.
- **Attention-based Pooling:** An attention mechanism is applied to the final GNN layer's node representations. A trainable query vector `q` computes importance scores for each node, normalized by a softmax function to obtain attention weights `α_i`. These weights are used to compute a weighted sum of node representations, yielding the final graph representation `h_graph`. This allows the model to learn which layer-wise representations are most relevant for personality prediction.

#### 3.3. Cross-Theory Harmonization (CTH)

CTH is designed to bridge heterogeneous psychological annotations and derive latent "pseudo-facets":
- **Prototypical Fine-tuning (PF):** The model is fine-tuned using a meta-learning approach. Class prototypes `s_i` are computed as weighted centroids of embeddings from a support set. The objective is to minimize the Euclidean distance between a query embedding and its ground-truth class prototype while maximizing distance to other prototypes. A weighted negative log-likelihood loss is used, which helps mitigate class imbalance by organizing the representation space around theory-aware prototypes.
- **Human-Guided Linkage (HGL):** External, human-defined mappings (Table I) are introduced to explicitly align shared facets across different personality theories (e.g., Big-5 and MBTI). This component encourages the representation space to converge towards a more unified structure. The authors acknowledge that these mappings might be imperfect but serve to gently steer representations towards shared semantic regions.
- **Machine-Induced Consensus (MIC):** A cross-entropy objective guides cross-dataset adaptation through a lightweight adapter layer that projects embeddings into a shared space. This component aims to infer shared structure from data-driven agreement signals across datasets, refining the space established by PF. It is noted to prioritize high-confidence correspondences.
- **Full CTH:** The final CTH module integrates PF, HGL, and MIC. This hybrid design leverages the initial structural organization from PF, the explicit (though soft) guidance from HGL, and the data-driven refinement from MIC, to progressively reshape the representation space into a coherent manifold that encodes both distinct and overlapping pseudo-facets.
- **Overall Loss Function:** The joint loss `L_JAM` combines weighted terms for PF from both datasets, HGL, and MIC (Eq. 10).

#### 3.4. LLM-as-a-Judge (LAJ)

LAJ is incorporated to assess training sample quality, identify hard examples (ambiguous, mislabeled, boundary cases), and guide adaptive metric learning. LLMs are used as auxiliary evaluators, not primary predictors.
- **LLMs Employed:** OpenAI’s ChatGPT (gpt-4o), Alibaba Qwen (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B), Meta Llama (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), and DeepSeek (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) were used.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** LLMs were prompted with a system prompt (Fig. 4) to evaluate the consistency of written content with specified personality traits (e.g., Big-5 framework). They provide an analysis and a judgment of 'Possible', 'Impossible', or 'Ambiguous' in a structured JSON format.
- **Data Augmentation:** Ambiguous samples were synthesized for LLM training by combining sentences from distinct personality profiles, exposing the LLMs to borderline cases.
- **Two Pipelines:**
 - **LLM-before-the-loop (LBL, Algorithm 1):** The LLM assesses and refines the entire training dataset `D_standard` *prior* to model training. Based on the LLM's judgment (`ζ`), the sample-specific confidence weights `z` are updated: `z=1.0` for 'Possible', `z=0.2` for 'Ambiguous', and `z=0` for 'Impossible' (Eq. 9).
 - **LLM-in-the-loop (LIL, Algorithm 2):** The LLM dynamically evaluates samples *during* training. For query samples `(x, y, w^(Q))` from the query set `D^(Q)` whose loss `L_JAM` exceeds a predefined threshold `τ`, the LLM judges their quality, and the corresponding `z` values are updated. This process iteratively refines the dataset.
- The updated `z` values influence the contribution of each example during prototype computation and training, thereby guiding the model to focus on higher-quality, more consistent samples.

#### 3.5. Experiment Design and Evaluation

- **Training Configurations:** Models were trained on Essays-only, Kaggle-only, and combined datasets. Ablation studies were conducted for individual CTH components.
- **Hyperparameters:** Batch size 32, learning rate 1e-5, max 30,000 training episodes with early stopping, random seed 42. Four NVIDIA A100 GPUs were used.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Regular Accuracy (RA), Balanced Accuracy (BA), and F1 Score were used to assess overall performance, performance on imbalanced datasets, and model bias, respectively.
- **Statistical Analysis:** McNemar test was employed to assess the statistical significance of performance differences between approaches.
- **Visualization:** t-SNE was used to visualize personality embeddings and demonstrate the structural clarity achieved by the proposed method.
- **Computational Analysis:** Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) and estimated computational costs were compared against prior approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings regarding the JAM framework's performance, the effectiveness of its components, and its computational characteristics.

#### 4.1. Baseline Acquisition

- **Cross-Entropy (CE):** Achieved good performance when trained and evaluated on the same dataset. However, a substantial performance drop was observed in cross-dataset generalization (e.g., Essays-trained on Kaggle), indicating poor transferability. Combining datasets for CE led to performance degradation.
- **Off-the-shelf Prototypical (PO):** Showed poor performance on the Essays dataset but performed better on the Kaggle dataset, which was attributed to the nature of data collection (constrained vs. diverse social media). This suggested the potential of few-shot learning and its inherent ability to mitigate class imbalance.
- **Fine-tuned Prototypical (PF):** Demonstrated significant performance improvements when fine-tuned on individual datasets. Notably, a model trained on Kaggle generalized to Essays more effectively than vice-versa, indicating the potentially higher quality and diversity of the Kaggle dataset. PF on Kaggle achieved results comparable to CE on Essays, supporting the effectiveness of prototypical networks in capturing personality features.

#### 4.2. Cross-Theory Harmonization (CTH) Performance

- The full CTH [Both] approach consistently outperformed representative baselines. For the Essays dataset, an average improvement of over 9% in Balanced Accuracy (BA) across all traits was observed compared to the best baseline.
- **Ablation Study:**
 - **Human-Guided Linkage (HGL) alone:** This component, when used in isolation, led to degraded performance on both datasets. This was interpreted as an indication that imperfect human-defined mappings or the constrained nature of the Essays data could introduce noise or suboptimal supervision, hindering the learning objective.
 - **Machine-Induced Consensus (MIC) alone:** MIC showed performance gains on the Essays dataset but not on Kaggle, suggesting its effectiveness can be context-dependent and that combining datasets might introduce additional noise that MIC alone cannot fully address.
 - **CTH (HGL + MIC):** The integration of both HGL and MIC restored performance to levels comparable with the baseline on Kaggle and further improved results on the Essays dataset. This suggests that the complementary strengths of these components allow the model to effectively merge different knowledge sources and achieve theory-agnostic modeling.

#### 4.3. LLM-as-a-Judge (LAJ) Performance

- **LBL vs. LIL Pipelines:** The **JAM(LBL)gpt [Both]** (LLM-before-the-loop) approach consistently outperformed the **JAM(LIL)gpt [Both]** (LLM-in-the-loop) approach across personality traits in both datasets. LBL also exhibited faster convergence (approximately 3000 episodes vs. 7000 for LIL). This indicates that pre-evaluating and refining the dataset before training is generally more effective than dynamic, iterative assessment during training, as an initially noisy dataset can lead the model to suboptimal local minima.
- **Impact on Datasets:** LAJ mechanisms further improved performance on the **Essays dataset** by an additional 2.4% BA over CTH [Both]. This improvement was attributed to the effective filtering of noisy samples. For the **Kaggle dataset**, LLMs generally did not significantly improve over the CTH baseline, which aligns with its observed higher data quality and the CTH stage's established robustness on this dataset.
- **LLM Judgment Distribution:** The Essays dataset had a higher proportion of 'Possible' judgments (85.7%) compared to Kaggle (70.8%). The modest performance gain of ~2% on Essays by LAJ was considered reasonable given the estimated proportion of noisy data.
- **LLM Model Comparison and Hyperparameters:** GPT (gpt-4o) generally performed best among the tested LLMs. The study found that `z_ζ∈{Impossible} = 0` (muting 'Impossible' samples) often led to better performance on Essays, supporting the generalization claim, but its effect was inconsistent and LLM-dependent on Kaggle. `z_ζ∈{Ambiguous}` (down-weighting 'Ambiguous' samples) was found to be a less sensitive hyperparameter. Fine-tuning open-source LLMs did not yield statistically significant improvements and sometimes degraded performance, suggesting that powerful, pre-trained LLMs are sufficiently capable for the judging task without additional fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Statistical Findings

- McNemar tests confirmed statistically significant improvements in performance from PF to MIC, MIC to CTH, and CTH to JAM(LBL)gpt on the Essays dataset.
- For the Kaggle dataset, significant gains were observed from PF to MIC and MIC to CTH. However, the improvement from CTH to JAM(LBL)gpt was not statistically significant (p-value = 0.1011), reinforcing the observation that LAJ's benefits were more pronounced in the lower-resource/noisier Essays dataset.

#### 4.5. Embedding Visualization

- t-SNE visualizations showed that JAM(LBL)gpt [Both] produced more structured embeddings with clearer separation between clusters corresponding to distinct personality trait combinations compared to the off-the-shelf PO approach. This indicated improved capture and preservation of personality cues from text, with the effect being more pronounced in the Kaggle dataset due to its larger sample size and higher classification accuracy.

#### 4.6. Computational Analysis

- The JAM framework achieved lower inference time and approximately 8x lower training FLOPs compared to prior approaches like PICEPR(Embeddings). This efficiency is attributed to its encoder-based architecture for core representation learning and the strategy of using LLMs as evaluators rather than primary inference engines. The negligible overhead from prototype retrieval contributed to maintaining low latency, suggesting improved environmental sustainability and suitability for large-scale deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces JAM, a framework that represents a step towards theory-agnostic personality recognition, aiming to overcome several limitations of existing methods.

**Advancement in Personality Recognition:** The framework shifts the paradigm from models constrained by specific psychological theories to those capable of discovering unified latent "pseudo-facets." This approach facilitates the understanding of shared underlying behavioral structures that might manifest across different theoretical frameworks (e.g., Big-5 and MBTI), fostering a more holistic and generalizable view of personality in computational models.

**Improved Generalization and Low-Resource Support:** The Cross-Theory Harmonization (CTH) module, by integrating Human-Guided Linkage and Machine-Induced Consensus, demonstrates the capacity to align and learn from heterogeneous datasets annotated under different personality theories. This capability is significant for improving generalization across frameworks and offers a pathway to support "low-resource" personality theories, where extensive, theory-specific annotated data is often unavailable.

**Enhanced Data Quality and Robustness:** The integration of the LLM-as-a-Judge (LAJ) mechanism provides a method for implicitly refining training data quality. By identifying and down-weighting ambiguous, mislabeled, or boundary samples, particularly in datasets with inherent noise like the Essays dataset, LAJ contributes to more robust model training. This mechanism leverages the reasoning capabilities of LLMs without incurring the high computational costs or data leakage risks associated with using LLMs as direct predictors.

**Practical Applicability and Efficiency:** The framework's ability to maintain low inference latency and computational cost (lower FLOPs) makes it a viable solution for real-world deployment. Applications such as personalized recommendation systems, AI agents, and robots that require real-time personality inference can benefit from this efficiency. Reducing computational overhead also translates into lower energy consumption and a smaller carbon footprint, aligning with efforts towards more environmentally sustainable AI.

**Foundations for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for several future research directions. These include further validation on a broader array of personality theories and culturally diverse datasets to confirm the robustness of CTH. The authors also suggest exploring the integration of JAM with federated learning frameworks to address critical concerns regarding data privacy, informed consent, and fairness, especially given the sensitive nature of personality data. Additionally, investigating adaptive or learned graph structures within the GNN component could lead to more nuanced modeling of inter-layer dependencies.
