# VLAW: Iterative Co-Improvement of Vision-Language-Action Policy and World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The VLAW framework iteratively co-improves vision-language-action (VLA) policies and action-conditioned world models for robot manipulation. It leverages limited real-world interaction to ground the world model, which then generates abundant synthetic data to boost policy performance, achieving a 39.2% absolute success rate increase on contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Collecting sufficient real-world interaction data for post-training VLA models is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, limiting their scalability.
- Existing learned world models often lack physical fidelity, exhibiting over-optimism (trained on successful demonstrations) and struggling to accurately model diverse physical interactions, including failure cases.
- Continuously improving VLA policies in the real world is challenging due to the data scarcity bottleneck and the difficulty of optimizing expressive policies without explicit action likelihoods.

## Method

- **VLAW** introduces an ==iterative co-improvement== framework that alternately refines a VLA policy and an ==action-conditioned world model==.
- A pre-trained world model is fine-tuned using a small, fixed budget of real-world online policy rollouts (==D_real==), crucially including failure cases, to improve its physical fidelity and reduce ==over-optimism==.
- The improved world model then generates a large amount of ==synthetic trajectories==, which are automatically filtered for success by a ==vision-language reward model==, providing high-quality data to post-train the VLA policy.

## Results

- The world model, post-trained with online rollouts, demonstrated substantial improvements in video prediction quality (e.g., FVD reduced from **225.13** to **64.12**) and accurately modeled interaction outcomes, effectively mitigating over-optimism.
- VLAW achieved a **39.2%** absolute success rate improvement, increasing the mean success rate of the VLA policy from **0.46** to **0.868** on challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks, outperforming baselines that do not use world models.
- Training with world model-generated synthetic data contributed **11.6%** absolute success rate improvement, highlighting the significant value of scalable, high-quality synthetic data for policy enhancement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Limited real-world interaction, when strategically used to include diverse outcomes like failures, can effectively ground a pre-trained world model, leading to significantly higher physical fidelity.
> - High-fidelity synthetic data, generated by a grounded world model and filtered by an accurate reward model, provides a scalable and effective alternative to expensive real-world data for improving VLA policies.
> - An iterative feedback loop between policy exploration (generating new real-world data) and world model refinement (generating better synthetic data) enables continuous and scalable performance improvement for robot learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, largely driven by the emergence of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. These models represent a significant leap towards generalist robots capable of understanding complex language instructions and executing diverse tasks based on visual observations. By leveraging large-scale demonstration datasets, VLA models (such as the *π0.5* model used as a base in this work) have achieved remarkable success in various robotic manipulation tasks.

However, a fundamental challenge in pushing the boundaries of VLA models lies in **data acquisition for continuous improvement**. While initial training on large offline datasets is effective, fine-tuning or "post-training" through online interaction in the real world is crucial for robustness and adapting to novel scenarios. Unfortunately, collecting online policy rollouts in real-world robotic settings is notoriously **expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive**. This cost significantly limits the amount of real-world interaction data available, thereby restricting the scalability and effectiveness of online fine-tuning for VLA models.

This limitation has spurred interest in using **learned simulators, or "world models,"** to generate synthetic rollout data in imagination, a concept explored in model-based reinforcement learning. World models predict future states given current observations and actions, offering a seemingly cost-effective way to generate vast amounts of training data. However, the paper identifies a critical shortfall in existing world models: they often **lack the physical fidelity** required for effective policy improvement. This deficiency stems from several factors:
1. **Over-optimism:** World models are frequently trained on demonstration datasets predominantly consisting of successful trajectories, leading them to be overly optimistic in their predictions and struggle with accurately modeling failure cases.
2. **Limited Coverage of Interactions:** These models often lack exposure to the diverse physical interactions, particularly contact-rich manipulation and deformable objects, that are common in real-world robotic tasks.
3. **Critical Physical Details:** They struggle to accurately model small yet critical physical details, resulting in blurry visual predictions or unrealistic dynamics.

Consequently, while many model-based reinforcement learning approaches have been proposed (e.g., DayDreamer, SOLAR, World4rl), they often focus on simpler tasks or require task-specific dynamics models. Recent advances in video diffusion models have enabled more realistic multi-task action-conditioned world models, but the challenge of accurately modeling complex, contact-rich physical dynamics and diverse failure scenarios persists.

VLAW positions itself precisely at this intersection, aiming to bridge the gap between expensive real-world data and the imperfect fidelity of synthetic environments. It seeks to develop a scalable framework that overcomes the limitations of both purely real-world online learning (cost) and purely synthetic learning (fidelity) by enabling a synergistic, iterative co-improvement process. This work contributes to the broader landscape by proposing a practical solution for efficiently grounding world models in real-world physics, thereby unlocking their potential to generate high-quality synthetic data for robust and scalable VLA policy learning, pushing towards more generalist and capable robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to **improve the performance and reliability of vision-language-action (VLA) models** through iterative online interaction with the real world, while addressing the inherent scalability challenges.

The paper is motivated by two primary observations:
1. **The high cost of real-world data:** While VLA models benefit significantly from post-training on online interaction rollouts, collecting this data in real robotic environments is extremely expensive and time-consuming. This limits the amount of data available for policy improvement and hinders the scalability of VLA models.
2. **The fidelity gap in learned world models:** Learned action-conditioned world models offer a promising alternative for generating synthetic data. However, existing world models often lack the physical realism necessary for effective policy improvement. They tend to be overly optimistic (trained on successful demonstrations), struggle with diverse physical interactions (especially failures), and fail to capture critical small details in contact-rich or deformable object manipulation, often leading to unrealistic or blurry predictions.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of the VLAW framework are:

1. **To develop a physically-grounded world model:** The first objective is to train an action-conditioned world model that accurately captures the complex dynamics of real-world robotic tasks, including both successful executions and, crucially, failure cases. This grounding is achieved by fine-tuning a pre-trained world model using a limited amount of real-world online rollout data.
2. **To leverage synthetic data for VLA policy improvement:** The second objective is to demonstrate that the high-fidelity synthetic data generated by this improved world model can effectively be used to generate large-scale, automatically annotated training data, which in turn significantly enhances the performance of a state-of-the-art VLA policy.
3. **To establish an iterative co-improvement pipeline:** The ultimate objective is to propose and validate a simple, scalable iterative algorithm that jointly and continuously improves both the VLA policy and the world model. This feedback loop ensures that as the policy gets better at exploring, the world model becomes more accurate in the explored regions, and vice-versa.
4. **To minimize real-world interaction:** Implicit in the above objectives is the motivation to maximize the utility of limited real-world interaction by strategically using it to refine the world model, which then generates abundant synthetic data, thereby reducing the overall real-world data collection budget for policy improvement.

By achieving these objectives, VLAW aims to provide a powerful and practical solution for scaling up generalist robot learning in real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLAW (Iterative Co-Improvement of Vision-Language-Action Policy and World Model) proposes a simple yet scalable iterative framework designed to jointly enhance a VLA policy and an action-conditioned world model. The methodology revolves around a closed-loop system where limited real-world interaction is used to ground the world model, which then generates abundant synthetic data to improve the VLA policy. The process is outlined in Algorithm 1 and Figure 3 of the paper.

The core components and iterative steps are:

**A. Components:**
1. **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Policy (πθ):** VLAW starts with a pre-trained VLA policy. In the experiments, this is the *π0.5* model (Intelligence et al., 2025b), which is a state-of-the-art flow-matching-based policy designed for open-world generalization in robot manipulation. This policy maps current visual states and language instructions to robot actions.
2. **Action-conditioned World Model (Mφ):** The framework utilizes a pre-trained action-conditioned world model, specifically Ctrl-World (Guo et al., 2025a), which is a diffusion-based video generation model capable of predicting future visual observations conditioned on current state and actions.
3. **Vision-Language Reward Model (R):** To automate the labeling of synthetic trajectories, a general-purpose vision-language model, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct (Team, 2025a), is employed. This model assesses whether a task instruction has been successfully completed by analyzing a trajectory video.

**B. Iterative Pipeline (K_iter iterations, two in experiments):**

**Step 1: Real-World Policy Rollouts (Grounding Data Collection)**
- The current VLA policy (πθ) is deployed on the real robot to collect a *small, fixed budget* of online rollout trajectories (Dreal). For each task category, a limited number of trajectories (e.g., 50 per task category in experiments) are collected.
- During this process, each trajectory is sparsely labeled as either a success or a failure. This step is crucial for collecting diverse interaction data, including failure cases, which are typically under-represented in existing demonstration datasets.

**Step 2: World Model and Reward Model Post-training (Fidelity Improvement)**
- **World Model Fine-tuning:** The pre-trained world model (Mφ) is fine-tuned using the newly collected real-world rollout data (Dreal). This fine-tuning adapts the world model to the specific dynamics encountered during policy execution in the target tasks. To prevent overfitting to the limited Dreal and maintain broad generalization, the world model is co-trained with its original, larger dataset (DDROID). The training objective uses a standard diffusion loss (Eq. 1 & 2). This grounding process significantly improves the world model's ability to accurately predict outcomes for both success and failure cases, particularly for contact-rich interactions.
- **Reward Model Fine-tuning:** In the first iteration, the vision-language reward model (R) is fine-tuned using the success labels from Dreal. This improves the accuracy of the reward model for the specific robot tasks. The reward model takes a trajectory video and language instruction as input and outputs the probability of success, using a predefined threshold (e.g., 0.8) to classify successful trajectories (Eq. 3).

**Step 3: Synthetic Data Generation (Scalable Data Augmentation)**
- Using the *updated and grounded* world model (Mφ), a *large number* of synthetic trajectories (Dsyn) are generated in imagination. This involves running the VLA policy (πθ) in a closed-loop with the world model, starting from initial states sampled from real trajectories.
- The fine-tuned reward model (R) then automatically evaluates these synthetic trajectories, filtering them to create a dataset of *only successful synthetic trajectories* (D+syn). This filtering step is vital for providing high-quality supervision for policy learning.

**Step 4: VLA Policy Post-training (Policy Improvement)**
- The VLA policy (πθ) is fine-tuned using a supervised learning objective, specifically a flow-matching loss (LFM, Eq. 4). The training data for this step comprises both the successful real-world rollouts (D+real) and the newly generated and filtered successful synthetic rollouts (D+syn).
- The policy update can be interpreted as an approximation of policy optimization under a regularized reinforcement learning framework, resembling Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) but adapted for flow-matching policies that do not provide explicit action likelihoods (Appendix A). By training on successful trajectories, the policy learns to repeat successful behaviors.

**Step 5: Iteration**
- Steps 1-4 are alternated, creating a continuous feedback loop. As the policy improves, it explores new states, which in turn provides more diverse and relevant data for the world model to further improve its fidelity, leading to better synthetic data and even better policies.

This iterative co-improvement approach allows VLAW to efficiently leverage limited real-world interaction to bootstrap the fidelity of the world model, then scale policy improvement through high-quality synthetic data, effectively addressing the data scarcity bottleneck in real-world robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the DROID real-robot platform, involving a Franka Panda arm with a Robotiq gripper and observations from multiple cameras. Five categories of contact-rich tasks were used: Stacking, Open Book, Erase Marks, Scooping, and Drawing, which are known to be challenging for traditional simulations due to complex physical interactions and deformable objects. The base VLA policy was *π0.5*, and the base world model was Ctrl-World. A small number of expert demonstrations (25 per task) were used to warm-start *π0.5*. VLAW performed two iterative rounds, collecting 50 real-world rollouts and generating 500 synthetic trajectories per task category per round.

The main findings and results can be summarized as follows:

**1. Accurate Action-Conditioned World Model for Contact-Rich Tasks (Q1):**
- **Quantitative Video Quality:** Finetuning the Ctrl-World model with online rollout data (including failures) substantially improved video prediction quality. All evaluated metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, FVD) showed significant improvements compared to the pretrained Ctrl-World and a version finetuned only on expert demonstrations (Table 1). For instance, FVD, a key perceptual metric, dropped from 225.13 (pretrained) to 64.12 (Ours), indicating much more realistic video generation.
- **Reduced Over-Optimism and Improved Interaction Modeling:** The most critical finding for policy improvement was the world model's ability to accurately model interaction outcomes. After training on online rollout data (which includes failure cases), the world model largely eliminated the "over-optimistic bias" seen in models trained only on expert demonstrations. The false-positive rate for interaction predictions significantly decreased, and the true-negative rate increased (Table 1, Event Confusion Matrix). This indicates that the grounded world model can reliably predict both successful and failed physical interactions.
- **High Fidelity in Policy-in-the-Loop Rollouts:** Qualitatively (Figure 5, 6), the post-trained world model maintained high visual fidelity and physical plausibility for long-horizon rollouts (up to 20 seconds) when the policy interacted in a closed-loop within the model. This long-horizon stability is crucial for generating useful synthetic trajectories for policy learning.

**2. Improvement of VLA Policy Performance using World Model-Generated Data (Q2):**
- **Superior Performance over Baselines:** VLAW consistently and substantially outperformed two baselines that do not use a world model: Filtered BC (finetuning on successful real-world rollouts only) and DSRL (optimizing policy noise space). VLAW achieved a 39.2% absolute success rate improvement over the initial base policy (from 0.46 to 0.868 mean success rate after two iterations, Table 2).
- **Significant Contribution of Synthetic Data:** Training with the world model-generated synthetic rollouts contributed 11.6% absolute success rate improvement (comparing VLAW Ours-2 (0.868) to Filtered BC-2 (0.752)), highlighting the value of the large-scale synthetic data.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablations (Figure 9) confirmed the importance of both components: reducing the amount of synthetic data or removing the real-world success trajectories during policy fine-tuning led to noticeable performance degradation. This underscores that both real-world grounding and synthetic data amplification are critical.
- **Effective Reward Model:** The fine-tuned vision-language reward model with a confidence threshold (p(yes) > 0.8) proved effective in filtering successful trajectories from world model-generated rollouts, significantly reducing false positives compared to a direct binary output (Table 3, Appendix C). This ensures that the policy learns from high-quality, positively labeled synthetic data.

**3. Continuous Improvement through Iterative Training (Q3):**
- The results show consistent performance gains across tasks from the first round of VLAW training ("Ours-1") to the second round ("Ours-2") (e.g., mean success rate increased from 0.744 to 0.868). This empirically validates that the iterative co-improvement process successfully and continuously enhances both the world model's fidelity and the VLA policy's performance in a multi-task setting.

In summary, VLAW successfully demonstrates that a physically-grounded world model, cultivated through limited real-world online interaction (including failures), can generate high-fidelity synthetic data. This synthetic data, combined with a robust reward model, effectively and scalably improves the performance of state-of-the-art VLA policies on complex real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLAW presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the critical challenges of data efficiency and generalization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **Bridging the Real-World Data Gap:** The most immediate and impactful contribution of VLAW is its ability to mitigate the prohibitive cost and time associated with collecting real-world robot interaction data. By strategically using a small amount of online real-world data to "ground" a world model, and then generating massive amounts of synthetic data from this improved model, VLAW offers a scalable pathway for policy learning that is otherwise unfeasible for complex tasks. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and refining advanced robot policies.

2. **Enhancing World Model Fidelity for Decision Making:** VLAW directly tackles the long-standing problem of insufficient physical fidelity in learned world models. By actively incorporating failure cases and diverse interaction dynamics from online rollouts, the world model in VLAW becomes less optimistic and more accurate in predicting real-world outcomes, including complex contact-rich and deformable object interactions. This enhanced fidelity is crucial, as it ensures that the synthetic data generated is actually useful and reliable for improving robot policies, rather than reinforcing unrealistic behaviors.

3. **Scalable Policy Improvement for State-of-the-Art VLA Models:** The framework enables the iterative improvement of expressive, flow-matching-based VLA policies (like *π0.5*), which are difficult to optimize with traditional reinforcement learning methods due to the absence of explicit action likelihoods. By formulating policy improvement as an advantage-weighted supervised learning objective on filtered successful trajectories (both real and synthetic), VLAW provides a stable and scalable mechanism for post-training these powerful models.

4. **Enabling Generalist Robotics:** The capacity to continuously improve policies on challenging, multi-task, contact-rich manipulation scenarios pushes the boundaries toward truly generalist robots. As base video models and large-scale robot interaction data continue to expand, VLAW's approach of iteratively refining world models and policies in a data-efficient manner could become a cornerstone for learning highly capable and adaptive robotic agents across a vast array of tasks.

5. **Reduced Risk and Increased Safety:** Training predominantly in a synthetic environment generated by a high-fidelity world model can significantly reduce wear and tear on physical robots and minimize potential damage or unsafe behaviors that might occur during extensive real-world exploration. This translates into more cost-effective and safer robot development cycles.

**Potential Future Impact:**
- **Faster Iteration Cycles:** VLAW could dramatically accelerate the research and development cycles for robot learning, allowing researchers to quickly test and refine policies without extensive hardware setup or human supervision.
- **Broader Task Capabilities:** The improved ability to model contact and deformable objects opens doors for robots to perform more intricate and human-like manipulation tasks, from delicate assembly to handling soft goods, which are currently major challenges.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By reducing the need for massive real-world data collection, VLAW could make advanced robot learning more accessible to smaller research groups and individual developers who lack the resources of large industrial labs.

While the current evaluation is limited to five task categories, the authors rightly point out that scaling online rollout data to a broader and more diverse set of tasks is a promising direction. VLAW lays a strong foundation, suggesting that a future where robots learn complex skills largely in their own "imagined" worlds, grounded periodically by real-world feedback, is increasingly within reach. This paradigm shift could profoundly impact how generalist robotic policies are developed and deployed.
