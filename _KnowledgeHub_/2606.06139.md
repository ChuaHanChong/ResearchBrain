# MotionDisco: Motion Discovery for Extreme Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionDisco presents a framework for autonomous discovery and execution of complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation skills for humanoid robots, eliminating reliance on human demonstrations or teleoperation. It couples an LLM-guided evolutionary search for discrete contact plans with a kinodynamic trajectory optimizer, enabling robots to generate and execute novel, physically feasible behaviors on real hardware, achieving robust performance across challenging tasks.

## Problem

- Humanoid loco-manipulation tasks involve high-dimensional, contact-rich action spaces with combinatorial complexity, making traditional exploration methods computationally prohibitive.
- Existing methods like human motion retargeting are limited by demonstration availability, restrict robot behaviors to human-like capabilities, and struggle with accurate contact recovery.
- Teleoperation lacks autonomy and scalability, preventing robots from independently reasoning or synthesizing new behaviors in novel environments.

## Method

- **MotionDisco** integrates an ==LLM-guided evolutionary search== for discrete contact-mode sequences with a ==contact-explicit motion planning== module.
- The LLM iteratively mutates Python programs representing contact plans, using a scene description, goal, and detailed feedback from the motion planner.
- The motion planning module performs rapid kinematic feasibility checks and, for valid plans, full kinodynamic trajectory optimization, providing numerical scores and structured text feedback (especially for failures) back to the LLM to guide subsequent mutations.

## Results

- MotionDisco successfully solved all eight challenging long-horizon loco-manipulation scenarios, finding first valid solutions within minutes.
- The full MotionDisco framework achieved lower trajectory optimization costs and a higher percentage of valid contact plans compared to versions without text feedback.
- Autonomously discovered motions were successfully deployed zero-shot on a physical humanoid robot, robustly executing tasks like 'Under-Table Pick & Place' and 'Climb Table w/ Box' with multiple consecutive successes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative, LLM-guided evolutionary search is crucial for discovering complex, long-horizon motions, outperforming single LLM calls.
> - Providing structured text feedback to the LLM on specific failure points (e.g., kinematically infeasible contact modes) significantly improves the discovery of optimal and feasible solutions.
> - The evolutionary search mechanism naturally generates diverse, qualitatively different contact plans and motions for the same task, offering an efficient source of varied behavioral data.
