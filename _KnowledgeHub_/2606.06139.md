# MotionDisco: Motion Discovery for Extreme Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionDisco presents a framework for autonomous discovery and execution of complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation skills for humanoid robots, eliminating reliance on human demonstrations or teleoperation. It couples an LLM-guided evolutionary search for discrete contact plans with a kinodynamic trajectory optimizer, enabling robots to generate and execute novel, physically feasible behaviors on real hardware, achieving robust performance across challenging tasks.

## Problem

- Humanoid loco-manipulation tasks involve high-dimensional, contact-rich action spaces with combinatorial complexity, making traditional exploration methods computationally prohibitive.
- Existing methods like human motion retargeting are limited by demonstration availability, restrict robot behaviors to human-like capabilities, and struggle with accurate contact recovery.
- Teleoperation lacks autonomy and scalability, preventing robots from independently reasoning or synthesizing new behaviors in novel environments.

## Method

- **MotionDisco** integrates an ==LLM-guided evolutionary search== for discrete contact-mode sequences with a ==contact-explicit motion planning== module.
- The LLM iteratively mutates Python programs representing contact plans, using a scene description, goal, and detailed feedback from the motion planner.
- The motion planning module performs rapid kinematic feasibility checks and, for valid plans, full kinodynamic trajectory optimization, providing numerical scores and structured text feedback (especially for failures) back to the LLM to guide subsequent mutations.

## Results

- MotionDisco successfully solved all eight challenging long-horizon loco-manipulation scenarios, finding first valid solutions within minutes.
- The full MotionDisco framework achieved lower trajectory optimization costs and a higher percentage of valid contact plans compared to versions without text feedback.
- Autonomously discovered motions were successfully deployed zero-shot on a physical humanoid robot, robustly executing tasks like 'Under-Table Pick & Place' and 'Climb Table w/ Box' with multiple consecutive successes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative, LLM-guided evolutionary search is crucial for discovering complex, long-horizon motions, outperforming single LLM calls.
> - Providing structured text feedback to the LLM on specific failure points (e.g., kinematically infeasible contact modes) significantly improves the discovery of optimal and feasible solutions.
> - The evolutionary search mechanism naturally generates diverse, qualitatively different contact plans and motions for the same task, offering an efficient source of varied behavioral data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is actively pursuing the development of autonomous agents capable of performing complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks in diverse and unstructured environments. A significant challenge in this domain is the exploration of high-dimensional, contact-rich action spaces, which traditionally leads to prohibitive computational costs.

Current state-of-the-art approaches primarily address this challenge through two main strategies:
1. **Human Motion Retargeting:** This involves transferring human motion data to humanoid robots. While successful in many scenarios, this approach is limited by the availability of relevant human demonstrations and inherently constrains robot behaviors to human-like capabilities, potentially preventing the discovery of physically optimal or unique robot-specific motions. Recovering accurate contact information from kinematic retargeting for loco-manipulation is also difficult.
2. **Teleoperation:** This method bypasses the exploration problem by allowing human operators to directly control the robot. While effective for immediate task execution, it lacks autonomy and scalability for generalized real-world deployment in novel situations.

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have introduced new possibilities for robotic planning by leveraging their common-sense reasoning and code generation capabilities. LLMs have been used for high-level procedural knowledge, translating natural language into robot policy code, and integrating with planning and optimization methods to generate skill sequences or evolve reward functions. Similarly, evolutionary algorithms have demonstrated effectiveness in program search and scientific discovery, providing mechanisms for exploring complex solution spaces.

MotionDisco positions itself at the intersection of these trends, aiming to overcome the limitations of retargeting and teleoperation. It seeks to autonomously generate novel, dynamically feasible, and contact-rich humanoid behaviors without reliance on human demonstrations. By coupling LLM-guided evolutionary search with kinodynamic trajectory optimization, MotionDisco contributes to the landscape of autonomous motion discovery, offering a method that can explore combinatorial interaction spaces and synthesize behaviors that leverage the full physical capabilities of humanoid robots, moving beyond imitation paradigms. This work builds on prior research in LLM-assisted trajectory optimization and program search but extends it to directly generate programs over contact plans, thereby encompassing both locomotion and manipulation within a unified framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named MotionDisco, capable of autonomously discovering contact-rich, long-horizon loco-manipulation motions for humanoid robots from an initial state, without any dependence on human demonstrations, motion retargeting, or teleoperation.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several challenges and limitations in current humanoid robot control strategies:

1. **Complexity of Loco-Manipulation:** Humanoid robots operating in the real world must execute tasks that involve intricate sequences of movement (locomotion) and interaction with objects (manipulation), often simultaneously. These behaviors are contact-rich, meaning they involve frequent and varied physical interactions with the environment, and can span long temporal horizons.
2. **Combinatorial Explosion:** The space of possible contact interactions grows combinatorially with the task horizon and the number of objects in the scene. This high-dimensionality and the discrete nature of contact modes make exploration and planning extremely challenging for traditional optimization or reinforcement learning methods alone.
3. **Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - **Human Motion Retargeting:** While useful, this approach is constrained by the availability and quality of human demonstrations. It is impractical to generate demonstrations for every conceivable scenario, and retargeted motions may not fully exploit the robot's unique morphology or dynamic capabilities, limiting the discovery of novel or optimal robot-specific behaviors. Furthermore, accurately recovering contact information for loco-manipulation from kinematic human data is difficult.
 - **Teleoperation:** This method provides direct control but lacks autonomy and scalability. It does not enable the robot to independently reason about its environment or synthesize new behaviors.
4. **Need for Autonomous Discovery:** For humanoid robots to become truly versatile and adaptive in novel and unstructured environments, they need the ability to autonomously reason about their surroundings and generate novel behaviors. This requires a departure from purely imitation-based or human-controlled paradigms.
5. **Leveraging Advanced AI:** The recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) offer unprecedented reasoning capabilities, particularly in generating structured code or plans from high-level prompts. The motivation is to harness these capabilities to guide the exploration of discrete contact sequences.
6. **Efficient Evaluation of Discrete Plans:** Given the vast number of potential contact plans, an efficient mechanism to evaluate their physical feasibility (kinematic and dynamic) is crucial. This necessitates a robust and layered motion planning module that can provide rapid feedback to guide the search.

The research aims to address these motivations by making the following contributions:
- Developing a framework that integrates LLM-guided evolutionary search for contact plans with a kinodynamic trajectory optimizer, which provides both numerical and structured language feedback to the LLM.
- Demonstrating, through extensive analysis, that this search-based approach, especially with structured feedback, is essential for successful motion discovery and outperforms non-iterative LLM approaches. It also aims to show that the evolutionary nature of the search can efficiently generate diverse solutions for the same task.
- Achieving real-world deployment of these autonomously discovered, long-horizon loco-manipulation behaviors on a physical humanoid robot, without any reliance on human data or teleoperation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionDisco operates as a framework that couples discrete contact-plan search with continuous motion planning. It iteratively proposes candidate contact sequences and evaluates their physical feasibility and optimality. The framework comprises two primary interacting modules: an LLM-guided evolutionary search and a contact-explicit motion planning module.

#### 3.1. Motion Planning Module

This module serves two functions: evaluating candidate contact plans generated by the LLM-guided search and, for feasible plans, generating a detailed robot-object motion trajectory.

1. **Sequential Kinematic Feasibility Check:**
 - **Purpose:** Acts as a rapid geometric feasibility filter for proposed contact sequences. It determines if a given sequence of contacts can be kinematically realized without collisions or exceeding configuration limits.
 - **Process:** Similar to, it performs a sequential kinematic optimization for each contact mode in the sequence.
 - **Output:** If a sequence is found infeasible, it is rejected, and the module returns textual feedback indicating the precise failure point (e.g., which contact assignment or transition caused the kinematic optimization to fail). This stage does not consider dynamics, making it computationally efficient for initial pruning.

2. **Contact-Explicit Trajectory Optimization (TO):**
 - **Purpose:** If a contact sequence passes the kinematic feasibility check, this stage solves for the full dynamically feasible motion.
 - **Process:** It finds the optimal state trajectory x(·), control trajectory u(·), contact wrenches, and stage durations $\bar{T}_{0:K-1}$ for the robot and objects. This involves solving a continuous optimization problem (Equation 3) that minimizes a cost function (ϕ and ϕ_T) subject to:
 - Robot dynamics.
 - Contact constraints induced by the contact mode $c_k$.
 - Collision avoidance (ensuring no unintended contacts).
 - State and control limits.
 - Initial and goal state constraints.
 - Minimum and maximum stage durations.
 - **Formalism:** A contact mode `c` is defined as a complete assignment of contact states for all active interfaces `a ∈ I`. The set `M(c)` denotes the feasible constraint manifold satisfying dynamics, contact, collision, and limits for a given mode. The continuous optimization problem aims to find trajectories that stay within `M(c)` for each mode in a sequence `C = (c_0..., c_{K-1})`.
 - **Implementation:** Both kinematic optimization and trajectory optimization utilize direct multiple shooting transcriptions, solved by temporal structure exploiting solvers like and.

#### 3.2. Motion Discovery via LLM-guided Evolutionary Search

This module iteratively explores the combinatorial space of contact-mode sequences, guided by an LLM and feedback from the motion planner.

1. **Search Representation:**
 - The search space is represented as an evolutionary tree `T = (N, E)`.
 - Each node `n ∈ N` represents a candidate contact plan, stored as a Python program `p_n`. Executing `p_n` yields a discrete contact-mode sequence `C_n`.
 - Each node also stores a scalar score `F_n` (from TO cost), optional text feedback `τ_n` (from motion planning failures), and an offspring count `o_n`.
 - Edges `e ∈ E` represent mutations from a parent program to a child program.

2. **Initialization and Iteration:**
 - The search starts from a root node representing the initial contact state, given a scene representation `S` (text-based description of objects, positions, dimensions) and a goal `G` (discrete contact goals and continuous target states).
 - The search iteratively performs the following steps until a budget is exhausted: selects a parent node, mutates its program using an LLM, evaluates the resulting sequence via the motion planning module, and adds the evaluated candidate as a child node to the tree.

3. **Tree Expansion Strategy (based on ShinkaEvolve):**
 - **Expansion Sampling:** Nodes are partitioned into "island subpopulations." An island is uniformly sampled, and then a parent node `i` is sampled from that island with a probability `p(i | k)` favoring high-scoring nodes that have not been expanded excessively (`w_i` incorporates score `F_i` and offspring count `o_i`).
 - **Diversity Maintenance:** Separate island subpopulations help prevent premature convergence, and elites are protected from migration.
 - **Novelty-Based Rejection Sampling:** Before adding a new candidate to the tree, its program is embedded and compared to existing nodes in the selected island. If too similar (cosine similarity above a threshold), an LLM "novelty judge" decides if it's meaningfully different; otherwise, it's rejected.
 - **Inspiration Sampling:** The LLM prompt includes additional "inspiration" nodes from the tree (a mix of high-performing and random nodes) to encourage diverse solutions and recombination.
 - **Mutation-Type Sampling:** Three types of mutations are sampled with fixed probabilities: targeted diff edits (0.6), full rewrites (0.3), and crossover (0.1).

4. **Contact Plan Mutation:**
 - An LLM (Claude Opus 4.7) is prompted with the scene `S`, goal `G`, parent program `p_n`, parent score `F_n`, parent feedback `τ_n`, and valid interfaces `I`. (Appendix A.1 details the extensive prompt structure, including planning rules, helper functions, and example patterns).
 - The LLM generates a modified Python program `p'` that, when executed, produces a new contact-mode sequence `C'`. The LLM focuses solely on mutating the discrete contact plan; continuous motion is handled by the motion planner.
 - Syntactic validity and consistency checks are applied to the proposed program before evaluation.

5. **Contact Plan Scoring and Feedback:**
 - **Kinematically Feasible Plans:** If the sequential kinematic feasibility check passes, the trajectory optimization problem is solved. The score `J(C')` is calculated as the sum of constraint residuals and a jerk regularization term (Equation 5). The text feedback reports these numerical values.
 - **Kinematically Infeasible Plans:** If the kinematic check fails, the node receives a score of zero. The system identifies the longest kinematically feasible prefix of the sequence ($\hat{K}$) and provides specific text feedback to the LLM about the first failing mode or transition ($c'_{\hat{K}}$ or $c'_{\hat{K}-1} \rightarrow c'_{\hat{K}}$). This detailed feedback enables the LLM to make local repairs to the contact plan in subsequent mutations rather than discarding the entire sequence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MotionDisco was evaluated on eight challenging long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks designed to assess its ability to discover complex whole-body behaviors, integrating locomotion, manipulation, and environmental interaction (details in Appendix B.1, Figure 4).

#### 4.1. LLM-guided Search Enables Motion Discovery

A quantitative analysis (Table 1) compared three strategies:
- **Single LLM Call (SC):** A non-iterative approach where the LLM generates a single contact plan.
- **MotionDisco without Text Feedback (MD w/o TF):** The evolutionary search framework but without the structured failure feedback to the LLM.
- **MotionDisco (MD):** The full framework with LLM-guided evolutionary search and text feedback.

The findings were:
- **Necessity of Iterative Search:** SC failed to solve most scenarios, confirming that a single LLM query is insufficient for complex motion discovery and that an iterative search process is crucial for refining initial proposals.
- **Benefit of Text Feedback:** Both MD w/o TF and MD successfully solved all scenarios. However, MD (with text feedback) consistently achieved lower trajectory optimization (TO) costs and a higher percentage of valid contact plans compared to MD w/o TF. This indicates that the structured text feedback is effective in guiding the LLM to discover more optimal and feasible solutions.
- **Practical Efficiency:** MD found a first valid solution within a few minutes across all scenarios. This demonstrates the practical applicability of the approach, despite the combinatorial nature of the contact-plan space.

#### 4.2. LLM-guided Search Produces Diverse Motions

Beyond merely finding feasible solutions, the evolutionary search mechanism inherently produced multiple distinct and qualitatively different solutions for the same task in a single run.
- **Variety of Contact Plans:** For tasks like "Parkour Pick & Place 2" (Figure 3), the search discovered varied contact plans, such as alternative assignments of end-effectors to environmental features or different sequences of steps and grasps.
- **Emergent Diversity:** This diversity in motion (e.g., varied hand and foot trajectories, distinct whole-body postures) arose naturally from the search process without requiring re-prompting or restarting the pipeline. This contrasts with single LLM calls, which tend to converge on a single mode.
- **Data Generation Source:** This characteristic provides an efficient source of diverse data for humanoid loco-manipulation, which can be valuable for downstream policy training.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment

The feasibility and robustness of the discovered motions were validated through real-world deployment on a physical humanoid robot.
- **Policy Training:** Motion tracking policies were trained using reinforcement learning (DeepMimic-style rewards) coupled with domain randomization to enhance robustness.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** The trained controllers were deployed zero-shot (without further training or fine-tuning for the real robot) on the hardware.
- **Successful Execution:** The robot robustly reproduced the discovered motions, achieving multiple consecutive successful executions across all attempted tasks. Specific examples of real-world executions include "Under-Table Pick & Place" (Figure 5), "Move Through Clutter" (Figure 6), "Parkour Pick & Place 2" (Figure 7), "Long-Distance Pick & Place" (Figure 8), and "Climb Table w/ Box" (Figure 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MotionDisco framework represents a notable advancement in autonomous humanoid robot control, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering Autonomous Motion Discovery:** This work is identified as the first to autonomously discover and execute long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation skills on real hardware using an entirely automated evolutionary search, critically without relying on motion retargeting from human demonstrations or teleoperation. This signifies a shift towards more robot-centric skill generation.

2. **Overcoming Data Dependency and Generalization Limitations:** By eliminating the reliance on human demonstrations, MotionDisco addresses a key limitation of many existing approaches. This enables the robot to generate behaviors for novel scenarios where human demonstrations might be unavailable, impractical, or even suboptimal for the robot's unique morphology and capabilities. It liberates humanoid robots from human-like behavioral constraints, potentially unlocking a wider array of physically optimal or unconventional motions.

3. **Synergistic Integration of LLMs and Optimization:** The framework effectively bridges high-level symbolic reasoning from LLMs with low-level continuous kinodynamic optimization. The LLM provides common-sense reasoning and structured plan generation, while the motion planner provides physically informed feedback, creating a powerful closed-loop system for efficient exploration of complex action spaces. This demonstrates a practical and effective method for combining modern AI techniques with established robotic control principles.

4. **Efficiency and Scalability for Complex Tasks:** MotionDisco's ability to rapidly discover feasible solutions (within minutes) for challenging long-horizon tasks and generate diverse behaviors in a single run highlights its efficiency. This scalability is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments requiring adaptive and varied responses.

5. **Foundation for Future Autonomous Robotics:** The success in real-world deployment underscores the robustness and practical viability of the discovered motions. This work lays a foundation for future advancements in truly autonomous humanoid robots that can reason, plan, and execute complex interactions in unstructured environments without direct human intervention. This could significantly impact fields such as disaster response, logistics, manufacturing, and assistance in human environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also represent areas for future impact:
- **Contact Model and Object Geometry:** Currently restricted to unilateral sticking contacts on rectangular patches and rigid box-like geometries. Expanding to sliding, non-unilateral contacts, articulated, and free-form shapes would dramatically broaden applicability.
- **Perception Integration:** MotionDisco assumes a known scene representation. Integrating a vision module for on-the-fly scene reconstruction from sensor observations (as hinted by references) would be a critical step toward deployment in truly unstructured environments.
- These areas for improvement suggest that while MotionDisco is a significant step, continuous development will be required to realize its full potential across the breadth of real-world robotic challenges.
