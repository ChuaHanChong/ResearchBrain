# AgentEvolver: Towards Efficient Self-Evolving Agent System

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentEvolver presents a self-evolving agent system that leverages large language models for autonomous task generation, efficient exploration, and fine-grained credit assignment. The framework demonstrated an average performance improvement of 29.4 percentage points for its 7B model and reduced training steps for convergence by up to 67% on standard benchmarks.

## Problem

- The high cost and scarcity of manually crafted, diverse task datasets for training LLM-powered agents.
- Inefficient exploration strategies in reinforcement learning (RL) for LLM agents, leading to slow convergence in complex, tool-augmented environments.
- Low sample utilization in RL, where sparse terminal rewards fail to provide fine-grained credit assignment for individual actions within a trajectory.

## Method

- ==Self-Questioning==: Enables autonomous generation of diverse training tasks and reference solutions by leveraging LLMs for curiosity-guided environment exploration and adaptive task synthesis.
- ==Self-Navigating==: Boosts exploration efficiency by distilling and reusing structured natural language 'experiences' from past successful or failed trajectories to guide future rollouts.
- ==Self-Attributing==: Improves sample efficiency through LLM-based retrospective analysis to assign ==fine-grained, step-wise attribution rewards== to individual actions within a trajectory.

## Results

- Achieved a **29.4** percentage point improvement in average task goal completion (avg@8) for the 7B model and **27.8** percentage points for the 14B model on AppWorld and BFCL v3 benchmarks.
- Reduced training steps for convergence by **55%** on AppWorld and **67%** on BFCL v3 compared to baselines, demonstrating significantly enhanced sample efficiency.
- Synthetic training data generated by the system proved as effective as, or superior to, human-curated data, facilitating cross-domain generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models possess sufficient reasoning capabilities to autonomously drive their own learning process, encompassing task generation, exploration guidance, and reward attribution.
> - High-quality synthetic training data, generated by LLMs with robust curation and a reference-based LLM Judge, can effectively replace or augment human-curated datasets for agent training.
> - Combining curiosity-driven task generation, experience-guided exploration, and fine-grained credit assignment mechanisms creates a synergistic system for efficient and scalable agent self-evolution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous agents powered by large language models (LLMs) represents a frontier in artificial intelligence, promising to significantly augment human productivity by enabling systems to reason, utilize tools, and execute complex tasks across various environments. This domain broadly follows two paradigms: workflow-based orchestration, where human developers explicitly design action sequences or tool-calling rules, and learning-based optimization, where agents adapt their policies through iterative feedback and interaction. AgentEvolver firmly situates itself within the latter, particularly focusing on reinforcement learning (RL) as a principled approach to imbue agents with adaptive and generalizable behaviors.

Despite the promise of RL-driven agents, their practical deployment has been hampered by several persistent challenges. Firstly, the manual construction of high-quality, diverse task datasets, especially for novel or dynamic environments where tool functionalities are unknown, is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive. Secondly, existing RL methods for LLM-based agents, which often rely on policy optimization algorithms like PPO or GRPO, suffer from severe sample inefficiency. Each interaction (rollout) in complex, tool-augmented environments is computationally and financially costly, and current exploration strategies (e.g., intrinsic rewards, UCB methods) often fall short in providing effective guidance for long-horizon tasks. This leads to approximate brute-force exploration, generating redundant rollouts and limiting the learning value derived from interactions.

AgentEvolver directly addresses these critical bottlenecks. It posits a paradigm shift from rigid, human-engineered learning pipelines to an LLM-guided self-improvement process. By leveraging the LLMs' inherent semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities, AgentEvolver aims to empower the models themselves to autonomously generate training tasks, guide exploration more efficiently, and refine performance through targeted feedback. This positions AgentEvolver as a significant contribution to the field by proposing a more scalable, cost-effective, and autonomous pathway for the continual evolution of LLM-powered agent capabilities, bridging the gap between theoretical RL potential and practical application challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of AgentEvolver is to develop a **self-evolving agent system** that can autonomously and efficiently improve its capabilities through continuous interaction with diverse environments, minimizing reliance on human intervention.

The core motivation stems from the recognition that while Large Language Models possess formidable semantic understanding and reasoning prowess, current methods for training LLM-powered agents remain inefficient and costly. The authors pose a fundamental question: "why not entrust the model itself with greater autonomy in driving its own learning process?" This vision drives the specific objectives:

1. **Reduce dependence on handcrafted task datasets:** Traditional agent training necessitates extensive, manually curated datasets, which are expensive to create and lack diversity, especially in novel or uncharacterized environments. AgentEvolver aims to overcome this "task scarcity" by enabling curiosity-driven, autonomous task generation.
2. **Improve exploration efficiency:** Current RL methods for LLMs often involve broad, inefficient exploration, resulting in redundant trajectories and slow convergence, particularly in long-horizon, tool-augmented tasks. The system seeks to enhance "inefficient exploration" by guiding agents to reuse past experiences and employ more targeted search strategies.
3. **Enhance sample utilization:** Traditional reward mechanisms in RL, like sparse terminal rewards, often assign credit uniformly across an entire trajectory, failing to distinguish the contribution of individual actions or states. AgentEvolver aims to address "low sample utilization" by providing fine-grained, differentiated rewards to make learning from each interaction more effective.

By addressing these three bottlenecks, AgentEvolver seeks to establish a framework that allows for the **scalable, cost-effective, and continual improvement** of agent capabilities, ultimately paving the way for more robust and intelligent autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AgentEvolver proposes a unified, self-evolving agent system built upon three synergistic mechanisms: self-questioning, self-navigating, and self-attributing, all integrated into a continuous self-training loop. The framework also includes a robust infrastructure to support its operations.

#### 3.1. Self-Questioning (Proxy Task Generation)

This module tackles task scarcity by enabling curiosity-driven, autonomous task generation.
- **Curiosity-Guided Exploration with Environment Profiles:** The agent first develops a structured understanding of unknown environments. Environment profiles (summarizing entities, attributes, operations) act as action priors, guiding a high-temperature LLM (e.g., Qwen-Plus) in stochastic exploration. A two-phase strategy (breadth-first then depth-first) ensures diverse state-action space coverage. This process transforms the environment into a distribution of exploration trajectories.
- **Adaptive Task Synthesis:** Explored trajectories are combined with user preferences (difficulty, style) to synthesize candidate queries (tasks) and extract reference solutions. An LLM analyzes the context to generate tasks aligned with desired constraints. Reference solutions, derived from the successful exploration paths, serve as proxy ground-truths for later evaluation.
- **Task Curation and Distributional Hybrid:** A multi-layer filtering pipeline refines the generated tasks. Real-time filtering uses lexical and semantic deduplication to prevent redundancy. Post-generation filtering strictly verifies task executability against the environment using reference solutions, removing hallucinatory tasks. Optionally, a hybrid distribution can combine synthetic tasks with available target task data to maintain stable capabilities.
- **Synthetic Task Reward with LLM Judge:** To provide reliable learning signals, a general-purpose, reference-based LLM Judge (e.g., Qwen3-235B-A22B or Qwen-Max) is employed. It follows principles of relevance/repetition checking and continuous scoring, comparing agent trajectories against reference solutions to assess task completion quality and provide a trajectory-level reward.

#### 3.2. Self-Navigating (Efficient Exploration)

This mechanism enhances exploration efficiency by leveraging past experiences.
- **Notion of Experience:** Experiences are structured natural language insights (e.g., skills, tactics, failure notes) distilled from past trajectories. Each experience has a "When to use" (retrieval trigger) and "Content" (guidance).
- **Experience Acquisition:** An offline Pool Construction process builds a repository of high-quality experiences from initial policy rollouts on proxy tasks, involving trajectory preprocessing, experience extraction (from successful/failed/comparative pairs), LLM-based validation, and vector store updating. Online Experience Retrieval dynamically draws top-k relevant experiences for a given task using query embeddings, followed by re-ranking and re-writing.
- **Experience-Mixed Rollout:** To balance exploration and exploitation, rollouts interleave vanilla (unguided) and experience-guided trajectories. For guided rollouts, retrieved experiences are injected into the LLM's prompt. Standardized advantages are computed for all trajectories.
- **Experience Incorporation:** During policy optimization, an experience stripping strategy removes experience tokens from training samples to prevent overfitting, ensuring the model learns intrinsic reasoning. A selective boosting strategy is applied to positive advantage samples from experience-guided rollouts, relaxing the PPO/GRPO clipping threshold (ε_high) to preserve valuable gradient signals and mitigate off-policy learning problems.

#### 3.3. Self-Attributing (Fine-Grained Credit Assignment)

This module improves sample efficiency by assigning differentiated, step-wise rewards.
- **Step-wise Attribution via LLM-based Reasoning:** An LLM (e.g., Qwen-Max) retrospectively analyzes entire trajectories, including task, intermediate steps, and final outcome, in a single pass. It outputs binary labels (GOOD/BAD) for each action, measuring its contribution to the final outcome ("process quality"). This leverages LLM reasoning to replace rigid heuristics with flexible, context-aware analysis.
- **Attribution-Based Reward Construction:** GOOD/BAD labels are quantified as +1/-1 respectively, providing a clear directional signal. These step-wise attribution rewards (r_attr) are then trajectory-level normalized to ensure numerical stability and consistent scale, regardless of trajectory length.
- **Composite Reward Construction:** The normalized attribution signal (r̂_attr) is fused with a separately normalized outcome-based reward (r̂_out), derived from the terminal reward provided by the LLM Judge. The outcome reward is applied only at the terminal step. A hyperparameter α controls the relative contribution of attribution vs. outcome.
- **Advantage Computation:** Step-level advantage (A_t) is computed as the undiscounted cumulative future reward, integrating both process correctness and outcome effectiveness.
- **Policy Optimization:** Step-level advantages are broadcast to token-level advantages for GRPO-style policy gradient optimization. This integrated signal is used for both vanilla and experience-guided rollouts, working synergistically with the selective boosting mechanism.

#### 3.4. Framework and Infrastructure

AgentEvolver provides a practical, service-oriented system:
- **Training Framework:** A Master Orchestrator drives a four-stage loop: Task Synthesis (Task Manager), Trajectory Rollout (parallel workers), Experience Summarization (Experience Manager), and Sample Construction & Model Optimization (Trainer Workers).
- **Context Manager (CM):** Manages historical context using a mutable Live Context Timeline (LCT) and an immutable Timeline Snapshot Recorder (TSR). It defines four **Context-Managing Templates (CMTs)**: Basic Causal (efficient, deterministic), Reasoning-Augmented (think-before-act), Sliding Context Window (scalable for long horizons via summarization), and Self-Context Managing (agent actively controls memory via preserve/delete/compress operations).
- **Environment Service:** A scalable, Gym-compatible execution backend using Ray for high-concurrency, isolated environment instances. It supports benchmarks like AppWorld, BFCL, WebShop, and Crafter with a unified HTTP interface.

This comprehensive methodology aims to create an agent that learns not just *what* to do, but also *how* to learn and evolve its capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AgentEvolver demonstrates superior performance and efficiency across various experimental evaluations using Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-14B backbone models on the AppWorld and BFCL v3 benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- Integrating all three mechanisms (Self-Questioning, Self-Navigating, Self-Attributing) into AgentEvolver significantly boosts task goal completion (TGC) compared to a Vanilla GRPO baseline.
 - For the **7B model**, the overall average performance (avg@8) improved by 29.4 percentage points, with AppWorld seeing a +30.6% gain and BFCL v3 a +28.1% gain. Best performance (best@8) saw even higher increases.
 - For the **14B model**, similar improvements were observed, with avg@8 increasing by 27.8 percentage points (AppWorld +30.7%, BFCL v3 +24.9%).

#### 4.2. Individual Mechanism Contributions (Ablation Studies)

- **Self-Questioning (SQ):** This module provides the most substantial initial performance gain. For the 7B model, adding SQ alone raised avg@8 from 15.8% to 36.1%.
 - **Effectiveness of Synthetic Data:** Agents trained on SQ-generated synthetic data showed significant improvements over zero-shot baselines, performing comparably to agents trained on human-curated original data. When combined (hybrid data), synthetic data often surpassed original data performance, demonstrating its ability to enhance diversity and expand agent capabilities.
 - **Influence of Data Quantity:** Even a small volume (100 samples) of synthetic data yielded high performance (40.3% avg@8 for 14B on AppWorld), with gains diminishing but continuing as data volume increased, indicating high diversity and training efficiency.
 - **Capability Generalization:** Models trained on synthetic data exhibited cross-domain generalization, with only a marginal performance drop when evaluated on tasks from a different environment, suggesting that AgentEvolver fosters both general and domain-specific capabilities.
 - **LLM Judge Efficacy:** The LLM Judge's scoring principles and, crucially, the incorporation of reference solutions significantly improved its ability to provide high-quality synthetic rewards, bringing performance close to human-curated data.
- **Self-Navigating (SN):** Building on SQ, SN further improved exploration efficiency.
 - **Experience-Guided Inference:** Even without additional training, incorporating retrieved experiences during inference led to average gains of +5.4% avg@4 and +6.7% best@4, stabilizing rollouts and raising performance.
 - **Implicit vs. Explicit Learning:** RL-based implicit learning (internalizing experience through training) substantially outperformed ICL-only explicit learning and vanilla RL baselines (e.g., +34.2% avg@4 and +28.1% best@4 over ICL-only), demonstrating better generalization and scalability. The selective boosting mechanism was crucial for its effectiveness.
 - **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** While higher proportions of experience-guided rollouts (η) benefited immediate inference performance, training dynamics showed that an optimal balance (η=0.5) was necessary for sustained long-term improvement, preventing excessive exploitation from hindering exploration.
 - **`hat{epsilon}_high` Parameter:** A moderate relaxation of the GRPO clipping threshold (`hat{epsilon}_high = 0.6`) was found to balance rapid early learning with stable long-term optimization, preventing overfitting seen with overly aggressive values.
- **Self-Attributing (SA):** This mechanism provided notable gains in sample efficiency and final performance.
 - **Effectiveness:** The complete SA method, combining attribution (r̂_attr) and outcome (r̂_out) rewards, consistently outperformed ablations relying on only one type of reward. Both channels were found to be complementary and necessary for optimal learning.
 - **Sample Efficiency:** SA significantly accelerated convergence, requiring 55% fewer training steps on AppWorld (40 vs. 90) and 67% fewer on BFCL v3 (20 vs. 60) to reach 90% of the baseline's best performance. It also achieved higher Area Under the Curve (AUC) scores, indicating more efficient overall learning.
 - **Hyperparameter α:** Analysis of the weighting hyperparameter α revealed a trade-off: larger α values accelerated initial learning but could lead to reduced long-term performance due to overfitting to LLM judge heuristics. Optimal α values (e.g., 0.10-0.20) achieved both rapid convergence and robust final performance.
- **Context-Managing Templates (CMTs):**
 - The **Self-Context-Managing Template** demonstrated the best overall performance, especially for long-horizon tasks (TGC@8), highlighting the value of dynamic context compression and discarding in environments with extensive tool APIs. The **Reasoning-Augmented Template** was the second best, emphasizing the benefit of explicit "think-before-act" steps.

In summary, the experiments robustly validate that AgentEvolver, through the synergistic operation of its three core mechanisms and supported by its flexible infrastructure, achieves more efficient exploration, better sample utilization, and faster adaptation compared to traditional RL-based baselines, resulting in superior agent performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AgentEvolver represents a significant step forward in the development of autonomous, LLM-powered agents, offering solutions to several long-standing challenges in reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing Core Bottlenecks in RL-driven LLM Agents:** The framework systematically tackles the three major limitations plaguing current LLM agent training: the high cost and scarcity of manually crafted task datasets, the inefficiency of exploration in complex environments, and the low utilization of samples due to sparse reward signals. By providing automated, LLM-driven solutions for task generation, exploration guidance, and fine-grained credit assignment, AgentEvolver makes agent development more practical and scalable.
2. **Pioneering a Self-Evolving Paradigm:** AgentEvolver champions a paradigm shift from human-engineered learning pipelines to an autonomous, LLM-guided self-improvement process. This empowers LLMs to actively drive their own learning, leading to agents that can continually evolve and adapt their capabilities without constant external supervision. This move towards greater agent autonomy is crucial for building truly intelligent systems that can operate in dynamic, real-world settings.
3. **Enhanced Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness:** By drastically reducing the reliance on expensive human data annotation and improving training efficiency through better exploration and sample utilization, AgentEvolver makes the development of sophisticated LLM agents more scalable and financially viable. This could democratize access to advanced agent capabilities, allowing more researchers and developers to build and deploy intelligent systems.
4. **Robust and Generalizable Agent Capabilities:** The self-questioning mechanism generates diverse and high-quality synthetic tasks, which, when combined with self-navigating's experience reuse, leads to agents with improved reasoning and task execution capabilities that generalize well across different domains. This lays the groundwork for agents that are not only proficient in specific tasks but also adaptable to novel scenarios.
5. **Practical and Extensible Infrastructure:** Beyond its algorithmic contributions, AgentEvolver provides a modular and developer-oriented infrastructure. The Context Manager offers flexible context evolution strategies, including self-managed memory, while the Gym-compatible Environment Service provides a scalable backend for diverse environments. This practical foundation serves as a valuable resource for future research and application development in the field of LLM-powered agents.
6. **Future Directions for AI Research:** The paper explicitly outlines ambitious next steps, including targeting more complex, real-world applications (e.g., enterprise workflows, safety-critical tool chains), scaling the framework to even larger LLM policy models, and pursuing "LLM-level self-evolving" systems where a single model (or family of models) unifies exploration, experience abstraction, self-attribution, and inference. These directions hint at the potential for AgentEvolver to inspire further foundational research towards truly autonomous and continually learning AI.

In essence, AgentEvolver contributes significantly to making LLM-powered autonomous agents a reality by providing a coherent, efficient, and scalable framework for their self-evolution, with profound implications for human productivity and the future of AI.
