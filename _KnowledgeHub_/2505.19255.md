# VTool-R1: VLMs Learn to Think with Images via Reinforcement Learning on Multimodal Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> VTool-R1 trains Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate multimodal chains of thought by dynamically interleaving textual and visual reasoning steps. This framework enables VLMs to strategically use visual editing tools through reinforcement learning, resulting in improved reasoning accuracy on visual question answering tasks involving tables and charts.

## Problem

- While LLMs have advanced in generating complex textual chains of thought and using external tools via RFT, VLMs lack a comparable capability for multimodal reasoning, typically producing only text-based outputs even for visual tasks.
- Existing methods for incorporating visual steps (e.g., Visual Sketchpad) operate at inference time and rely on large, pre-trained models, without providing a mechanism for smaller VLMs to learn to generate and use these visual steps.
- VLMs currently do not dynamically "think with images" as part of their reasoning process, limiting their ability to actively interact with and derive insights from visual elements in a human-like manner.

## Method

- VTool-R1 introduces a ==Reinforcement Learning Finetuning (RFT) framework== that trains VLMs to generate multimodal chains of thought.
- VLMs learn to strategically invoke ==external Python-based visual editing tools==, with the modified image from tool execution fed back into the VLM for continued reasoning.
- The training employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with an outcome-based reward function that assesses the correctness of the final answer.

## Results

- VTool-R1 significantly improves reasoning accuracy on challenging visual question answering tasks over tables and charts.
- The Qwen-VL 3B model, for instance, increased its accuracy from approximately **24%** to **57.9%** on TableVQA and **64.0%** on ChartQA with VTool-R1.
- Models learn to generate and interpret multimodal chains of thought, dynamically incorporating visual edits like highlighting or masking into their reasoning processes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can be explicitly trained to generate and integrate intermediate visual reasoning steps into their chains of thought using reinforcement learning.
> - An outcome-based reward function, focused solely on the correctness of the final answer, effectively guides VLMs to strategically utilize visual tools without leading to reward hacking.
> - Models learn to adaptively decide when to invoke visual tools and when to rely on direct reasoning, demonstrating a nuanced understanding of task requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs) has been significantly influenced by Reinforcement Learning Finetuning (RFT). Techniques such as generating long chains of thought, multi-turn self-correction, and effective tool integration (e.g., search engines, code interpreters) have been demonstrated in models like DeepSeekR1 and GPT-4o.

However, the application of RFT to vision-language models (VLMs) for enhancing multimodal reasoning has encountered specific challenges. Existing VLM RFT efforts primarily focus on text-only reasoning, where models process visual inputs initially but generate reasoning chains exclusively in text, conditioned on fixed input image tokens. This approach often overlooks the potential for intermediate visual reasoning steps within the response generation process. Current VLMs, even advanced ones, may rely on linguistic shortcuts, leading to errors in scenarios where visual details contradict textual common sense (e.g., a hand with six fingers being incorrectly identified as five based on language priors).

To address the limitations of text-dominant reasoning, prior inference-time frameworks like Visual Sketchpad and Refocus have explored incorporating intermediate visual steps. These methods typically involve models generating visual artifacts or using external tools to modify images during inference. While effective in some contexts, these approaches typically rely on highly capable, often commercial, models (e.g., GPT-4o) to produce meaningful visual steps and tend to underperform with smaller, open-source models due to the absence of specific training mechanisms for such visual interactions.

VTool-R1 positions itself in this landscape by introducing an RFT framework designed to explicitly train VLMs to generate multimodal chains of thought. It aims to bridge the gap between text-only RFT for VLMs and inference-time visual reasoning methods that lack a training component. By integrating Python-based visual editing tools into the RFT process and using outcome-based rewards, VTool-R1 seeks to enable VLMs to strategically learn when and how to incorporate visual reasoning steps to improve task performance, rather than merely relying on inference-time prompting. The work is presented as the first RFT framework to enable VLMs to learn to interleave text and intermediate visual reasoning steps in their generated responses. The paper also acknowledges concurrent works such as Deepeyes and OpenThink-IMG, which similarly integrate visual steps, but VTool-R1 demonstrates performance advantages on structured image data, attributed to its specific tool and task design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Motivation

The motivation for VTool-R1 stems from observed gaps in the reasoning capabilities of current vision-language models (VLMs), particularly in how they engage with visual information during complex tasks.

**Motivation:**
1. **Limited Multimodal Reasoning in VLMs:** While Reinforcement Learning Finetuning (RFT) has significantly advanced text-based reasoning in large language models (LLMs), a comparable breakthrough for multimodal reasoning in VLMs has not been widely achieved. Existing VLM RFT initiatives primarily focus on training models to generate textual chains of thought from an initial visual input, without incorporating dynamic intermediate visual reasoning steps in their responses.
2. **Prevalence of Language Shortcuts:** Current VLMs, when primarily guided by language, can exhibit "language shortcut" behaviors. This means they might generate textually plausible answers based on linguistic patterns or common knowledge, even when the visual evidence contradicts it. The paper illustrates this with an example of a VLM potentially misidentifying six fingers in an image as five, solely based on the linguistic prior that "a hand has five fingers," rather than visually verifying the input. This highlights a critical failure mode of purely language-based reasoning in multimodal contexts.
3. **Limitations of Inference-Time Visual Reasoning:** Approaches like Visual Sketchpad and Refocus have demonstrated the benefits of introducing intermediate visual reasoning steps during inference. However, these methods typically require highly capable, often proprietary, models (e.g., GPT-4o) to effectively generate and utilize these visual steps. Open-source or less capable models generally underperform in such inference-only settings because they lack specific training to learn when and how to integrate visual tools into their reasoning process. This points to a need for a training mechanism to imbue VLMs with this capability.

#### 2.2. Key Objectives

1. **Develop an RFT Framework for Multimodal Chains of Thought:** Introduce VTool-R1, a novel RFT framework specifically designed to train VLMs to generate reasoning chains that interleave both textual and intermediate visual steps, effectively teaching them to "think with images."
2. **Integrate External Visual Editing Tools:** Equip VLMs with the ability to interact with and invoke Python-based external visual editing tools as part of their reasoning process. This integration aims to allow models to modify images dynamically to aid their understanding and decision-making.
3. **Learn Strategic Visual Tool Use via Outcome-Based Rewards:** Train VLMs using outcome-based rewards, meaning the model is rewarded solely for the correctness of its final answer. The objective is to enable the VLM to autonomously learn *when* and *how* to generate and incorporate visual reasoning steps, without relying on explicit process-based supervision or penalties for specific tool usage.
4. **Enhance Performance on Structured Visual Reasoning Tasks:** Validate the effectiveness of VTool-R1 on challenging structured image reasoning tasks, specifically those involving charts and tables. The goal is to demonstrate that by learning to strategically use visual tools, VLMs can significantly improve their reasoning performance on these tasks.
5. **Enable Generation of Multimodal Chain of Thoughts:** Make VLMs capable of producing a coherent sequence of reasoning that naturally blends textual explanations with dynamically generated visual outputs, simulating how humans might use visual attention to process information before forming conclusions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VTool-R1 is designed to train Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate multimodal chains of thought by integrating external visual editing tools into a Reinforcement Learning Finetuning (RFT) framework.

**VLM Preliminaries:**
The framework considers a VLM policy, denoted as π_θ, which generates a text response (y) given a text prompt (x) and an image (I). A key requirement is for VLMs to process multiple image inputs, accommodating both the original image and intermediate visual steps generated during reasoning.

**VLM Inference and Rollout with Visual Chains of Thought:**
The inference and rollout process in VTool-R1 is iterative, building upon the prompting format introduced by Refocus.
1. **Initial Prompting:** The VLM receives an initial input consisting of the original image, a system prompt, Python code templates for visual editing tools, and task-specific goals. In-context examples guide the VLM to generate a "Thought 0" (initial reasoning) followed by an "Action 0."
2. **Tool Invocation:** "Action 0" can either be a "no action needed" statement or a Python-like pseudocode snippet that calls an external visual editing tool.
3. **External Execution:** If a tool is invoked, the code is executed in an external Python environment, generating a modified image (I').
4. **Second-Round Reasoning:** The modified image (I') is then fed back into the *same* VLM instance as an *additional* image input, alongside the original image (I) and the initial prompt (x). The VLM then continues its reasoning, forming a "Thought 1" over the combined visual and textual context, leading to a final answer.
5. **Single-Turn Tool Use:** For the scope of this work, the model is allowed to call a tool at most once per question. If no tool is invoked, the answer is generated directly in the first round. This design ensures that the VLM learns to process and reason over dynamic visual outputs.

**RFT VLM to Generate Visual Chains of Thought:**
VTool-R1 adopts RFT to train VLMs to autonomously decide when and how to use visual tools.
1. **Outcome-Based Optimization:** The training objective focuses solely on optimizing the final reasoning response (y), rather than intermediate tool-invoking outputs. This design encourages the model to learn tool use that directly contributes to final task performance.
2. **Objective Function:** The RFT objective aims to maximize the expected reward of the generated response while regularizing with a KL-divergence penalty against a reference policy (π_ref). The objective is defined as:
 `max_{π_θ} E_{[I,x]∼D, y∼π_θ(·|I,x;T)} [ r_ϕ(I, x, y) − β D_KL [ π_θ(· | I, x; T) ∥ π_ref (· | I, x; T) ] ]`
 where `y ∼ π_θ(·|I,x;T)` represents the two-stage rollout (original image `I` concatenated with edited image `I'` if a tool is used, otherwise just `I`).
3. **GRPO Algorithm:** The training approach is built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a critic-free policy gradient method. GRPO estimates advantages from a group of sampled responses `{y_i}` to update the policy, improving stability and resource efficiency compared to methods requiring a separate critic.
4. **Training Rollout Structure:** The same iterative inference template used for evaluation is employed during training rollouts, ensuring that the model learns to structure its output with "Thoughts," "Actions," "Tool Use," and "Final Answer" sections, reinforced by few-shot examples.

**Reward Modeling:**
1. **Outcome-Based Reward:** The reward model `r_ϕ` is outcome-based, assigning a score of 1 for a correct final answer and 0 otherwise. For free-form answers in structured visual understanding tasks (e.g., ChartQA), a lightweight LLM-based judge assesses the match between the predicted and ground truth answers.
2. **Avoidance of Process-Based Rewards:** The authors explored, but rejected, process-based rewards (e.g., penalizing incorrect tool use or rewarding successful invocations). These were found to lead to "reward hacking," where models either avoided tools entirely or generated superficial tool calls that met success criteria without genuinely improving reasoning.
3. **No Format-Based Rewards:** Format-based rewards were deemed unnecessary as the models learned to follow the structured output format effectively through clear instruction templates.

**Dataset:**
Evaluation is conducted on structured image reasoning tasks, specifically chart and table-based reasoning, following the data splits from Refocus.
- **Table Split:** Includes VWTQ (Wikipedia tables), VWTQ_syn (synthetic tables with randomized styles), and VTabFact (entailment classification from TabFact content). Datasets are split 70/30 for train/test.
- **Chart Split:** Derived from ChartQA, comprising 444 horizontal and 382 vertical bar chart QA pairs for testing, and 14,344 examples for training.

**Visual Editing Toolset T:**
A set of Python-implemented visual editing tools simulates visual attention by modifying table or chart images.
- **Table Tools:** `Highlight Column/Row`, `Mask Column/Row`, `Draw Column/Row`. These apply semi-transparent highlights, white masks, or solid bounding boxes.
- **Chart Tools:** Analogous operations for highlighting, masking, or drawing bounding boxes around individual bars based on x/y-axis positions.
These tools leverage external libraries like OpenCV for image manipulation and region identification. The model can call multiple tools simultaneously within a single action.

**Experiment Setup:**
- **Base Models:** State-of-the-art open-source VLMs, Qwen-VL 2.5, at 3B, 7B, and 32B parameter scales.
- **Training Infrastructure:** VeRL training infrastructure.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW.
- **Hyperparameters:** Initial learning rate of 1e−6, weight decay of 1e−2, global batch size of 32 (table) or 256 (chart), rollout group size of 5, KL divergence coefficient of 1e−2, decoding temperature of 1.0, and bf16 precision.
- **Hardware:** H100 and H200 GPUs.

**Baseline Models:**
To benchmark VTool-R1, several baselines are used:
- **GPT-4o:** A powerful commercial model representing an upper bound, known for its inference-only tool-use capabilities.
- **Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B/32B) without RFT:**
 - *Prompted Tool-Use Inference:* The VLM is prompted to use tools (following VTool-R1's template), but without RFT. Failed tool calls lead to regeneration attempts.
 - *Direct Inference (No Tools):* Only the image and question are provided, without any tool-related prompting.
- **R1-VL:** An open-source, RL-tuned VLM trained on general visual reasoning tasks, serving as a weaker academic baseline.
- **Deepeyes:** A concurrent RL-trained tool-use VLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that VTool-R1 enhances the reasoning capabilities of VLMs by enabling them to integrate intermediate visual reasoning steps via reinforcement learning on multimodal tool use.

**Qualitative Tool-use Example:**
Figure 2 illustrates a qualitative instance where the VTool-R1 3B model successfully generates and utilizes an intermediate visual step to arrive at a correct answer. This highlights the model’s ability to interleave visual and textual reasoning effectively.

**RFT for Improved Tool Use and Reasoning:**
- A significant improvement in tool use capability and reasoning accuracy is observed in VTool-R1 models compared to non-trained baselines (Table 1).
- The 3B and 7B Qwen-VL models, which initially demonstrated limited ability to generate meaningful tool use (thoughts, actions, or tool calls), learned to use tools effectively after VTool-R1 training. This indicates that the RFT process not only improves final answer accuracy but also helps models internalize structured reasoning patterns involving visual steps.
- VTool-R1 significantly outperforms the direct inference baseline for the 3B and 7B models, indicating that RFT-driven tool use as an additional reasoning step provides substantial benefits to overall reasoning capabilities.

**Non-Monotonic Tool Use Frequency:**
- The study found that tool call frequency and success rate do not monotonically increase throughout training as accuracy improves (Figure 3).
- Instead, models exhibit adaptive behavior, initially tending to overuse tools due to prompt exposure, but later learning to invoke them more selectively and cautiously. For instance, the 3B model showed decreased tool use over time while achieving higher reasoning accuracy, implying it learned when tools were unnecessary and could proceed with direct reasoning. The 32B model (Appendix) displayed similar adaptive behavior. This adaptive and context-aware tool-use is identified as a key outcome of the RFT strategy.

**Tool Call Success Rate:**
- Using a proxy metric (successful Python execution with no exceptions and valid image return), the success rate of tool calls increased steadily for table tasks, while for chart tasks, it fluctuated throughout training (Figure 3). The authors highlight the need for future work to incorporate human-annotated oracle verifiers for more accurate evaluation of tool-use success.

**Training Dynamics:**
- Model accuracy generally improved steadily throughout training, with minor fluctuations, and converged within approximately 50 training steps (around one epoch), depending on the configuration.

**Reward Design Effectiveness:**
- Experiments with alternative reward settings confirmed the robustness of outcome-based rewards. Process-based rewards (e.g., penalizing failed tool calls or giving extra rewards for successful tool use) led to reward hacking. Models either entirely avoided tool use to prevent penalties or exploited success criteria to trigger "success" signals without contributing to reasoning. This supports the claim that outcome-based rewards tied solely to final task correctness are the most reliable design for VTool-R1.

**Benefits from External Tool Use:**
- VTool-R1 models demonstrated clear advantages over R1-VL models, which are RL-tuned on general visual reasoning tasks but lack an explicit tool-use step for generating intermediate visual outputs (Table 2). This underscores the impact of learning to "think with images" on enhancing final performance.
- VTool-R1's 7B model achieved significantly higher performance (80.7% accuracy) compared to a concurrent RL-trained tool-use VLM, Deepeyes (60.0% accuracy), on structured image data. This is attributed to the intrinsic design of VTool-R1's tools, training recipe, and well-designed structured image tasks.

**Failure Analysis:**
The paper presents four categories of failure cases:
- The model correctly generates an intermediate visual step but fails to make the correct inference in the subsequent rollout.
- The model identifies the necessary visual augmentation, but the augmentation itself is flawed (e.g., numbers obstructed by bounding boxes), leading to incorrect readings in the second rollout.
- The model determines that no image reasoning step is needed in the first rollout but subsequently provides an incorrect answer.
- The model generates tool-use code, but the code execution fails, resulting in no intermediate visual step.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Significance

VTool-R1 represents a advancement in the training of vision-language models (VLMs) by demonstrating that reinforcement learning finetuning (RFT) can effectively teach these models to integrate visual reasoning steps directly into their response generation.

- **Novel RFT Framework:** VTool-R1 is presented as the first RFT framework that successfully trains VLMs to generate multimodal chains of thought by interleaving textual and intermediate visual reasoning steps. It enables models to actively interact with and modify visual inputs via external visual editing tools.
- **Strategic Tool Use without Process Supervision:** A key contribution is showing that outcome-based rewards are sufficient to incentivize VLMs to learn *when* and *how* to use visual tools strategically, without requiring explicit process-level supervision or hand-crafted rewards for tool invocation. This approach allows the model to autonomously determine if and how visual modifications enhance final reasoning accuracy.
- **Beyond Text-Dominant Reasoning:** The work addresses a limitation in existing VLM RFT efforts, which largely focus on text-only reasoning from fixed image inputs. By enabling "thinking with images," VTool-R1 moves beyond language shortcuts and allows VLMs to dynamically engage with visual information throughout the reasoning process.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Multimodal Reasoning:** The ability for VLMs to generate and reason over intermediate visual steps could significantly enhance their performance on complex multimodal tasks, particularly those requiring detailed visual analysis and interpretation, such as structured image understanding (charts, tables).
- **Unlocking New Capabilities:** This framework opens a new direction for multimodal AI research, potentially enabling VLMs to acquire capabilities that extend beyond what is intrinsically encoded in their parameters. By leveraging external tools, VLMs can perform operations that might be difficult or impossible through purely parametric reasoning.
- **Generalization to Broader Tasks and Toolsets:** While the current work focuses on selective attention in structured image reasoning with a specific toolset, the VTool-R1 framework has the potential to scale to a wider array of visual editing tools and generalize to more diverse multimodal datasets and reasoning challenges.
- **Foundation for Agentic Systems:** The iterative nature of visual tool use and reasoning positions VTool-R1 as a foundational step towards more sophisticated agentic VLM systems. Future extensions could include multi-turn execution, integration of more advanced generative models as tools (e.g., for arbitrary visual modifications), and incorporation of model-internal feedback mechanisms for continuous self-correction and refinement, similar to developments in LLM-based agents.
