# Revisiting Parameter Redundancy in Vision-Language-Action Models: Insights from VLM-to-VLA Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers revisited parameter redundancy in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, demonstrating that post-pruning recovery masks the removal of vital components rather than truly redundant ones. They introduced a recovery-free pruning scheme leveraging VLM-to-VLA parameter divergence, which achieved significant model compression for OpenVLA and π0.5 while retaining high performance.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often built upon large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), suffer from substantial computational and storage overhead.
- Existing VLA pruning methods frequently lead to a "performance cliff," requiring extensive post-hoc fine-tuning or low-rank adaptation for recovery, which is computationally expensive.
- The reliance on recovery mechanisms during pruning raises questions about whether genuinely redundant parameters are being removed, or if critical components are being indiscriminately cut.

## Method

- Reframed ==parameter pruning== as a diagnostic tool, evaluating the direct impact of parameter removal on policy behavior *without* any post-pruning recovery.
- Quantified the ==relative parameter divergence== (∥WVLA−WVLM∥2/∥WVLM∥2\|W_{VLA} - W_{VLM}\|_2 / \|W_{VLM}\|_2∥WVLA−WVLM∥2/∥WVLM∥2) between the initial VLM and the adapted VLA to identify structured signals of functional reorganization.
- Designed a ==multi-module, differentiated pruning scheme== that selectively removes parameters based on their module-specific divergence signals, enabling ==recovery-free compression==.

## Results

- Post-pruning LoRA fine-tuning could restore model performance from near **0%** success rate to baseline levels, indicating recovery masks indiscriminate parameter removal.
- The recovery-free pruning scheme reduced OpenVLA parameters from **7.5B** to **6.2B** (memory from **14.9GB** to **12.4GB**) while maintaining **62.3%** average success rate, outperforming traditional methods (e.g., LLM-Pruner at **1.0%** SR) at comparable memory.
- For π0.5, the proposed scheme reduced memory from **7.3GB** to **5.6GB**, achieving an average success rate of **89.0%** (compared to baseline **96.9%**) without any post-pruning recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Post-pruning performance recovery in VLA models often compensates for the removal of vital parameters rather than solely identifying and eliminating intrinsic redundancy.
> - Parameter divergence introduced during the VLM-to-VLA adaptation process contains structured and module-heterogeneous signals that effectively distinguish functionally important parameters from redundant ones.
> - The utility of these divergence signals is specific to each module (e.g., projector modules are critical and non-selective, while SigLIP vision backbones are robust to extensive pruning).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in embodied intelligence, integrating large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robotic action and control tasks. This approach enables VLAs to leverage rich cross-modal representations from VLMs, offering generalization capabilities across varying environments, objects, and task compositions. Examples include models like RT-2 and PaLM-E, with OpenVLA and π$_{0.5}$ serving as prominent open-source baselines built upon VLMs like Prismatic and PaLI-Gemma, respectively.

However, the "inheritance" of VLM parameters contributes to a significant computational and storage overhead in VLA models, a concern that has prompted research into efficiency optimization. Existing methods to address this challenge include various forms of pruning (parameter, token, layer), quantization, and lightweight architectural design. A recurring observation in VLA models is their sensitivity to parameter removal, where even moderate pruning can lead to a substantial performance degradation, often referred to as a "performance cliff."

Current strategies for addressing this sensitivity typically involve post-hoc recovery mechanisms, such as fine-tuning or low-rank adaptation (e.g., LoRA), to compensate for the performance loss after pruning. Methods like RLRC and GLUESTICK exemplify this approach, where a pruning method is often deemed successful if the model's performance can be restored to an acceptable level during a recovery phase. This reliance on recovery suggests that pruning in VLAs often entails repairing induced damage rather than solely removing functionally redundant components. Prior work, such as VLM4VLA and "Actions as Language," has highlighted the complex relationship between VLM pre-training quality and VLA task performance, and the potential for catastrophic forgetting of VLM knowledge during VLA fine-tuning.

This paper challenges the conventional view that post-pruning performance degradation is an inevitable cost requiring subsequent recovery. Instead, it re-examines the concept of parameter redundancy in VLAs, questioning whether the necessity of recovery implies that the removed parameters were, in fact, critical rather than truly redundant. It posits that VLA parameter redundancy may differ from that in models trained end-to-end due to the structured adaptation process from general vision-language understanding to robotic control. The work aims to shift the focus from engineering fixes for recovery to a more fundamental understanding of parameter functionality and redundancy identification during the VLM-to-VLA adaptation process.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to revisit the concept of parameter redundancy in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by analyzing the VLM-to-VLA adaptation process. The motivation stems from a critical observation: VLA models, despite their large parameter count, exhibit extreme sensitivity to pruning. Current efficiency paradigms often treat post-pruning performance degradation as an unavoidable consequence, relying on subsequent fine-tuning or low-rank corrections to restore efficacy. The authors question this premise, arguing that such reliance on recovery may mask the indiscriminate removal of vital parameters rather than true redundancy.

Specifically, the paper is motivated by a fundamental question: if a pruned VLA model consistently requires re-learning or explicit performance recovery to function effectively, were the removed parameters genuinely redundant, or were they critical components whose removal necessitated compensatory repair? This inquiry leads to a re-evaluation of parameter redundancy in the context of VLA models, which are constructed through a structured adaptation from general vision-language understanding to robotic control, unlike models trained end-to-end on a single task. During this adaptation, parameters are selectively reused, re-weighted, or stabilized to support action-centric computation paths.

The key objectives are structured around four hypotheses:

- **Hypothesis I**: To investigate whether the necessity of post-pruning performance recovery in VLA models indicates that the removed parameters were unlikely to be truly redundant. This objective aims to demonstrate that current recovery-dependent pruning paradigms primarily compensate for performance degradation rather than identifying intrinsic redundancy.
- **Hypothesis II**: To determine if the parameter differences introduced during the VLM-to-VLA adaptation process contain useful signals for identifying redundant parameters. This involves quantifying the spatial distribution of parameter divergence between the initial VLM and the adapted VLA to reveal structured patterns across different modules.
- **Hypothesis III**: To explore whether the utility of VLM-VLA parameter differences for identifying redundancy varies across different modules (e.g., vision encoders, language backbones, cross-modal projectors). This objective seeks to uncover module-specific functional reorganization patterns.
- **Hypothesis IV**: To ascertain if, when properly utilized, VLM-VLA parameter differences can facilitate structured pruning without requiring post-pruning performance recovery. The ultimate goal is to design a pruning scheme that can reduce VLA parameter scale while maintaining performance directly, thus providing a path for efficient deployment in resource-constrained environments.

By systematically addressing these objectives, the research aims to establish a causal link between adaptation-induced parameter divergence and functional contributions, thereby developing a principled, recovery-free pruning methodology for VLA models.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research employs an analysis framework that reframes VLA parameter pruning from an optimization problem into a diagnostic tool. This framework aims to decouple the identification of redundant parameters from the repair of pruning-induced damage.

**Reframing VLA Parameter Pruning as an Analysis Problem:**
The paper posits that if a pruning intervention necessitates explicit performance recovery, it indicates that the removed parameters were not truly redundant but rather vital. Therefore, pruning *without* subsequent performance recovery is treated as a controlled intervention to directly reveal the impact of parameter removal on policy behavior.

**VLM–VLA Parameter Difference as a Structural Signal:**
VLA models are typically initialized from pre-trained VLMs and adapted. The authors quantify the relative parameter divergence, $\Delta W_{rel} = \frac{\|W_{VLA} - W_{VLM}\|_2}{\|W_{VLM}\|_2}$, between the VLM ($W_{VLM}$) and the adapted VLA ($W_{VLA}$) parameters. This divergence is hypothesized to contain structured information regarding the functional reorganization of weights during adaptation.
To verify this, the study visualizes weight divergence distributions for two VLM-VLA pairs: `⟨Prismatic, OpenVLA⟩` and `⟨PaLI-Gemma, π$_{0.5}$⟩`.
- **OpenVLA (Prismatic-based) analysis**:
 - **Language Model (Llama 2)**: Divergence shows a "three-stage" vertical distribution—dense calibration in initial layers, stability in middle layers, and fluctuations in terminal layers related to action semantic mapping. Feed-Forward Network (FFN) modules exhibit inter-layer fluctuations and "strip-like" sparsity in channel dimensions, suggesting embodied knowledge is encoded in specific sub-channels.
 - **Vision Backbone (DINOv2 & SigLIP)**: DINOv2 shows updates concentrated in shallow layers (Attention and FFN) for low-level visual cues. SigLIP exhibits stronger responses in deeper layers, with higher FFN channel updates, indicating a role as a semantic supplement.
 - **Projector**: Parameter changes show a monotonic increase, with later-stage mapping layers fluctuating more than initial up-sampling layers, implying higher update priority for semantic transformation near the language model entrance.
- **π$_{0.5}$ (PaLI-Gemma-based) analysis**:
 - **Language Model (Gemma)**: Multi-Query Attention (MQA) shows high discriminative signals in head dimensions. Low layers retain text-parsing priors (minimal divergence), middle layers exhibit large divergence (functional reorganization for embodied tasks), and high layers show decreased divergence (stability after semantic-to-action mapping).
 - **Vision Tower**: Attention modules show mild changes, preserving visual priors. FFN modules in middle and high layers show strong divergence, serving as a basis for identifying redundancy.
These visualizations suggest that parameter updates during VLM-to-VLA adaptation are structured and heterogeneous, providing an empirical basis for identifying VLA redundancy.

**Controlled Pruning as a Diagnostic Instrument:**
To establish a causal link between parameter divergence and functional importance, the authors introduce controlled pruning. Given a module and a pruning ratio, two complementary strategies are defined based on the magnitude of $|\Delta W|$:
- **Highest-difference pruning**: Removes the top-r% parameters with the largest $|\Delta W|$.
- **Lowest-difference pruning**: Removes the bottom-r% parameters with the smallest $|\Delta W|$.
Pruning is applied directly without any fine-tuning or recovery. Algorithm 1, "Module-Aware VLA Mask Construction," details how $\Delta W$ is aggregated across multi-matrix modules (e.g., LLM FFNs) and how masks are constructed module-wise, ensuring reproducibility and systematic application of the diagnostic probe.

**Unified Pipeline for Causal Analysis:**
The methodology follows a systematic pipeline:
1. Selection of correlated `⟨VLM, VLA⟩` pairs.
2. Calculation of $\Delta W$ across sub-modules.
3. Analysis of spatial distribution patterns of $\Delta W$.
4. Application of controlled pruning interventions (Highest-diff, Lowest-diff, Random).
5. Evaluation of direct inference behavior *without post-pruning recovery*.
6. Performance of cross-module and cross-model comparisons.

**Experimental Settings:**
- **Dataset**: Experiments are conducted on the LIBERO benchmark, which tests robotic manipulation skills across four sub-datasets (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), using Success Rate (SR) as the primary metric.
- **Baselines**: The study uses OpenVLA and π$_{0.5}$ as the VLA models. For the final algorithm evaluation, it compares against established pruning criteria like LLM-Pruner, FLAP, and Wanda, evaluated under recovery-free constraints.
- **Implementation**: NVIDIA A100 GPUs are used. Diagnostic experiments are performed via direct inference. Control experiments involving recovery utilize LoRA fine-tuning under FSDP.

### 4. Main findings and results

The study's findings are presented through systematic verification of the proposed hypotheses:

**Validation of Hypothesis I: Recovery Masks Indiscriminate Pruning**
Experiments on the Prismatic-OpenVLA pair, targeting the LLM's FFN intermediate layers, demonstrated that post-pruning LoRA fine-tuning enabled recovery to or beyond baseline performance across all pruning strategies (Lowest-diff, Highest-diff, Random), even when initial performance after pruning was near 0% Success Rate (SR). This "strong compensation" effect was observed across all LIBERO sub-datasets. Causal analysis further indicated that the number of convergence steps required for recovery scaled with the pruning ratio, suggesting deeper structural perturbations at higher pruning levels. These results support Hypothesis I, indicating that relying on recovery masks the indiscriminate removal of vital parameters.

**Validation of Hypothesis II: VLM-to-VLA Parameter Differences as Redundancy Signals**
Evaluation of Highest-diff and Lowest-diff pruning across OpenVLA and π$_{0.5}$ revealed that parameter divergence ($\Delta W$) is not a uniform importance metric but exhibits significant *Module Heterogeneity*.
- **OpenVLA (Prismatic-based)**:
 - **DINOv2 Vision Backbone**: Demonstrated a "sensitivity reversal." Pruning the highest-difference attention heads caused performance collapse (e.g., 1.6% SR), while pruning the lowest-difference FFN channels led to collapse (e.g., 0.0% SR). This indicates that functional importance is highly path-dependent within the same backbone.
 - **Llama 2 Language Model**: Removing the lowest-difference attention heads or FFN channels resulted in total degradation (e.g., 0.0% and 2.7% SR, respectively), whereas pruning the highest-difference components maintained higher performance.
 - **SigLIP Vision Backbone**: Showed minimal sensitivity to $\Delta W$ across pruning strategies, consistent with its supplementary semantic role.
- **π$_{0.5}$ (PaLI-Gemma-based)**:
 - Pruning highest-difference FFN channels in both vision and language modules had minimal impact on performance (∼95.0% SR), while removing lowest-difference channels caused significant drops.
 - Lowest-difference Gemma attention heads also led to collapse (0.0% SR) upon removal.
These cross-model observations confirm Hypothesis II, establishing that VLM-to-VLA parameter divergence contains structured signals effective for distinguishing vital from redundant parameters, with varying patterns across different computational roles.

**Validation of Hypothesis III: Module-Specific Utility of Divergence Signals**
Further investigation into the boundaries of $\Delta W$ signal utility revealed unequal contributions of VLA components to final performance.
- **Projector module (OpenVLA)**: Was identified as "fragile and non-selective." Both highest-diff and lowest-diff pruning strategies led to performance collapse at moderate pruning ratios (e.g., 0.0% SR at 0.3 ratio for highest-diff, 54.0% for lowest-diff), implying its critical role as a cross-modal alignment bottleneck that must be largely protected.
- **SigLIP (OpenVLA)**: Exhibited extreme robustness. Even with 100% parameter removal, the model maintained moderate success (e.g., 47.0% SR for attention, 70.0% for FFN), contrasting with DINOv2, where slight pruning caused failures. This reinforces SigLIP's role as providing supplementary semantic information.
These results validate Hypothesis III, demonstrating that the signal's usability depends on the module's functional role, necessitating differentiated allocation logic in joint pruning.

**Validation of Hypothesis IV: Recovery-Free Structured Pruning**
Based on the identified module heterogeneity and signal utility, the authors designed a multi-module joint pruning scheme with "Light," "Moderate," and "Aggressive" configurations. This scheme was compared against traditional pruning criteria (LLM-Pruner, FLAP, Wanda) under identical *recovery-free* conditions.
- **OpenVLA (Table 5)**: Traditional methods suffered catastrophic failure without performance recovery (e.g., LLM-Pruner achieved 1.0% SR at 12.4GB memory, compared to OpenVLA baseline of 76.5%). In contrast, the proposed "Ours-Moderate" configuration reduced parameters from 7.5B to 6.2B (14.9GB to 12.4GB memory) while maintaining 62.3% average SR, a 60% higher performance than LLM-Pruner at comparable memory. Even the "Ours-Aggressive" config (5.7B parameters, 11.3GB memory) retained 52.5% average SR.
- **π$_{0.5}$ (Table 6)**: The "Ours-Moderate" scheme reduced memory from 7.3GB to 5.6GB, with average SR shifting from 96.9% to 89.0%. The "Ours-Aggressive" configuration (2.5B parameters, 5.0GB memory) maintained 82.1% average SR.
These results strongly support Hypothesis IV, confirming that by correctly interpreting structural importance from VLM-to-VLA adaptation, significant model compression can be achieved without requiring expensive post-pruning recovery mechanisms. Appendix B, C, and D further detail generalizability across all LIBERO sub-datasets and for π$_{0}$ on RoboTwin2.0 tasks, reinforcing the consistency of these findings.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research offers several significant contributions and has notable potential impacts on the field of embodied intelligence and efficient AI deployment:

- **Reconceptualization of Parameter Redundancy and Pruning**: The study fundamentally challenges the conventional paradigm in VLA pruning, which often relies on post-hoc recovery mechanisms. By reframing pruning as a diagnostic tool rather than solely an optimization problem, it highlights that the necessity of recovery often masks the removal of critical parameters, rather than merely redundant ones. This shift in perspective can lead to a more precise understanding of which parameters are truly dispensable.

- **Mechanistic Understanding of VLM-to-VLA Adaptation**: The research provides insights into the parameter evolution mechanism during the VLM-to-VLA adaptation process. By quantifying and visualizing parameter divergence, it uncovers structured patterns and significant module heterogeneity within VLAs (e.g., in language models, vision backbones, and projectors). This deeper mechanistic understanding of how general VLM knowledge is reorganized for specific embodied action tasks is valuable for future model design and optimization.

- **Development of Recovery-Free Pruning Strategies**: A key practical impact is the demonstration that substantial VLA model compression is achievable without the need for post-pruning fine-tuning or other compensatory mechanisms. This is a considerable advancement, as it reduces the computational cost and complexity associated with deploying pruned models. By leveraging adaptation-induced divergence signals and applying differentiated pruning logic across modules, the proposed approach allows for direct deployment of efficient policies.

- **Enabling Efficient VLA Deployment in Resource-Constrained Environments**: The ability to reduce VLA parameter scale and memory usage (e.g., 12%–30% parameter reduction while retaining approximately 90% of original performance for OpenVLA and π$_{0.5}$) without performance recovery is critical for real-world robotic applications. This directly addresses the computational burden of large VLAs, making it feasible to deploy robust robotic policies on edge devices and in environments with limited computational resources.

- **Guiding Future Research**: The findings suggest new avenues for research in efficient embodied AI. Future work could explore more sophisticated ways to interpret adaptation signals, develop dynamic pruning strategies based on task context, or integrate these insights into novel lightweight architectural designs that are inherently more efficient from the ground up, reducing the reliance on massive pre-trained models.

In summary, this research provides both a critical re-evaluation of current VLA pruning practices and a data-driven approach for identifying true parameter redundancy. Its impact lies in advancing the fundamental understanding of VLA model internals and offering a practical, recovery-free method for achieving efficient and robust robotic intelligence.
