# Deriving Hyperparameter Scaling Laws via Modern Optimization Theory

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at King Abdullah University of Science and Technology, ETH Zürich, and Mila derived explicit power-law scaling predictions for learning rate, momentum, and batch size by minimizing a convergence bound for modern optimizers. The analysis consistently shows an optimal performance decay rate of T^(-1/4) with respect to the total token budget T, offering a theoretical basis for empirically observed hyperparameter rules.

## Problem

- Empirical hyperparameter tuning for large-scale deep learning models is computationally expensive and resource-intensive.
- Existing empirical hyperparameter scaling laws often lack a unified theoretical explanation and can sometimes appear contradictory.
- Prior theoretical work on hyperparameter transfer typically assumes fixed batch sizes, momentum, and training horizons, leaving a gap for other critical dimensions.

## Method

- The study leverages a ==non-convex convergence bound== from modern optimization theory for ==Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO)== methods as a proxy for optimizer performance.
- The proxy objective is analytically minimized across various ==tuning regimes== (fixed momentum, fixed batch size, or joint tuning) with respect to ==learning rate==, ==momentum==, and ==batch size==.
- Asymptotic ==power-law schedules== for optimal hyperparameters and the resulting performance rate are derived as functions of training iterations or total token budget.

## Results

- Under fixed momentum, optimal batch size scales as **T^(1/2)** and learning rate scales as **T^(-1/4)** for a fixed token budget, recovering empirically observed relationships.
- When batch size is fixed, optimal momentum scales as **b * T^(-1/2)** and learning rate as **b * T^(-3/4)**, aligning with timescale preservation arguments.
- With joint tuning of learning rate, momentum, and batch size, the optimal scalings are **b*T^(1/6)**, **eta*T^(-7/12)**, and **alpha*T^(-1/3)**, leading to the consistent **T^(-1/4)** performance rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal performance for LMO-based optimizers consistently scales as O(T^(-1/4)) with respect to the total token budget T across different hyperparameter tuning strategies.
> - Momentum tuning is crucial for achieving optimal performance when batch size growth is constrained by hardware, preventing a non-vanishing noise floor.
> - The batch-size landscape is relatively flat; multiple batch-size scaling laws can achieve the T^(-1/4) optimal rate, with the specific optimal batch size heavily influenced by lower-order terms and the assumed noise model.
