# BendTwin: Robust Dense-to-Sparse Physical Reconstruction with Bending-Aware Differentiable Spring-Mass Models

> [!summary] Summary
> BendTwin introduces bending stiffness and damping into differentiable spring-mass models for robust physical reconstruction, creating more mechanically faithful digital twins from sparse-view RGB-D videos. This framework achieves a 19.0% reduction in Chamfer Distance during reconstruction and a 16.8% reduction for future prediction, particularly enhancing stability with sparser physical representations.

## Problem

- Existing spring-mass models primarily use axial springs, oversimplifying structural mechanics by not explicitly accounting for bending deformations.
- Axial-only models often become mechanically under-constrained and unstable with sparse physical graphs, leading to non-physical local deformations and limited mechanical expressiveness.
- Accurate physical model reconstruction from sparse visual inputs typically requires prior object geometry and mechanical property knowledge, or extensive controlled experiments, hindering generalization.

## Method

- **BendTwin** augments traditional axial-only spring-mass models with explicit ==bending stiffness and viscous bending damping==, defined over local node triplets.
- The framework formulates ==bending energy== as proportional to the squared deviation from a rest angle, with restoring forces derived to minimize angular differences.
- An efficient ==O(N) triplet construction== method is employed for high-valence nodes to ensure scalability while preserving bending expressivity.

## Results

- BendTwin reduced Chamfer Distance by **19.0%** for reconstruction and **16.8%** for future prediction, along with improvements in tracking error and visual rendering metrics, compared to axial-only baselines.
- It demonstrated enhanced robustness to physical graph sparsity: with a **0.05** sampling ratio, BendTwin reduced Chamfer Distance by **26.5%** for reconstruction and **18.3%** for future prediction over the baseline.
- The framework effectively reconstructed and predicted object dynamics using only surface nodes, reducing Chamfer Distance by approximately **6.9%** for reconstruction and **5.3%** for prediction compared to an axial-only system with interior nodes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling bending mechanics significantly improves the mechanical fidelity and stability of spring-mass systems, especially when the physical graph discretization is sparse.
> - Bending constraints effectively penalize non-physical surface distortions, enabling purely surface-based models to reproduce volumetric deformations with comparable or superior accuracy to axial-only systems with interior nodes.
> - The differentiable formulation of bending forces allows for robust inverse optimization of mechanical parameters from observational video data, enhancing the accuracy of reconstructed digital twins.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "BENDTWIN" addresses the challenge of creating physically consistent digital twins of real-world objects from observational data, specifically video. This field is critical for applications requiring prediction of object dynamics under interaction, such as robotic manipulation, policy learning data generation, and vision-based world models.

Historically, precise physical model reconstruction has relied on classical methods like finite element analysis (FEA), material point methods (MPM), or position-based dynamics (PBD). While these methods are capable of high accuracy, they typically require prior knowledge of object geometry and mechanical properties, or extensive controlled experiments, making them labor-intensive and difficult to generalize.

Recent advancements in 3D reconstruction, including neural radiance fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting, have enabled detailed geometric modeling from visual inputs. However, these techniques primarily focus on geometric and appearance fidelity, often assigning material properties manually or generatively, which may not accurately reflect true physical dynamics.

This gap has been partially addressed by differentiable physics, which allows the optimization of unknown physical parameters by matching simulated dynamics to observed data. Methods in differentiable physics have been developed for rigid and soft bodies, but often operate on implicit geometry or do not reconstruct a complete digital twin encompassing geometry, material behavior, and appearance simultaneously from sparse visual inputs.

A related line of work, exemplified by Spring-Gaussian and PhysTwin, integrates spring-mass models with reconstructed geometry (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) to create physical digital twins. PhysTwin, in particular, reconstructs deformable objects from sparse-view RGB-D videos and optimizes a differentiable spring-mass system with learnable parameters. However, these existing spring-mass formulations predominantly rely on axial springs, which model stretching and compression but do not explicitly account for bending mechanics. This axial-only approach can oversimplify structural mechanics, leading to mechanically under-constrained systems, particularly when the physical graph is sparse. Such under-constrained models may struggle to preserve stable local deformations, limiting their mechanical expressiveness.

BendTwin positions itself within this landscape by directly addressing the limitations of axial-only spring-mass models in physics-driven 3D reconstruction. It extends these models by incorporating explicit bending stiffness and damping, aiming to improve mechanical fidelity and system stability, especially in scenarios with sparser physical discretizations. This work contributes to the field by enhancing the physical realism and robustness of differentiable spring-mass models for creating digital twins from visual data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and mechanically expressive framework for reconstructing deformable objects from video observations, capable of physically consistent dynamic prediction. The authors aim to overcome limitations identified in existing spring-mass based physical reconstruction methods.

The key motivations for BendTwin are:

1. **Addressing Mechanical Oversimplification:** Existing spring-mass models, such as PhysTwin, primarily use axial springs. While effective for stretching-dominated deformations, this formulation does not explicitly model bending mechanics. This oversimplification leads to an incomplete representation of an object's structural behavior, as bending deformations must be implicitly approximated through axial coupling.
2. **Mitigating Under-Constrained Systems:** When the physical graph representing an object (e.g., the number of nodes in a spring-mass system) is coarsened or made sparse, axial-only formulations can become mechanically under-constrained. This occurs because there are insufficient axial connections to regulate local angular deformations explicitly. As a result, the model may fail to preserve stable local shapes and can exhibit non-physical deformations or instability.
3. **Improving Fidelity for Complex Deformations:** Many real-world objects exhibit complex deformations that involve significant bending, in addition to stretching and compression. An explicit model for bending stiffness is motivated by the need to capture these higher-order deformation modes more accurately, leading to more faithful digital twins.
4. **Enabling Efficient and Robust Reconstruction:** The authors aim to improve the mechanical stability and accuracy of physical reconstruction even with sparse discretizations. This would allow for more efficient simulations (fewer nodes) without compromising the physical fidelity of the reconstructed object. By maintaining the simplicity of explicit spring-mass dynamics, the approach aims to remain computationally tractable and differentiable for inverse parameter optimization.
5. **Enhancing Prediction Capabilities:** A more mechanically accurate digital twin is expected to lead to better predictions of future dynamic states under interaction, which is crucial for downstream applications like robot manipulation planning and physically grounded world models.

In summary, the core objective is to augment the differentiable spring-mass modeling paradigm with explicit bending constraints, thereby improving mechanical expressiveness, system stability, and overall accuracy in reconstructing and predicting the behavior of deformable objects from video observations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BendTwin proposes a bending-aware differentiable spring–mass framework for reconstructing deformable objects and predicting their future dynamics from sparse-view RGB-D videos. The methodology integrates inverse mechanical parameter estimation with a refined physical model.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as an inverse problem where the goal is to estimate the mechanical parameters (α) of a deformable object by matching its simulated dynamics to partial 3D observations (X_obs_t) derived from multi-view RGB-D videos.

The object is discretized as a graph G = {V, E, T}, where:
- V is a set of surface mass nodes.
- E represents axial springs connecting nodes.
- T denotes bending triplets, a novel component introduced in this work.
Each node *i* ∈ V has a position x^t^i and velocity v^t^i at time *t*. The system state at time *t* is X^t^ = {x^t^i, v^t^i}.

The physical parameters (α) to be estimated include:
- Axial stiffness (k_ij) and dashpot damping coefficients (γ_ij) for each axial spring (i,j) ∈ E.
- **Bending stiffness (k_θ,(i,j,k)) and viscous bending damping (c_b,(i,j,k))** for each bending triplet (i,j,k) ∈ T.
- Contact model parameters (α_contact), such as restitution and Coulomb friction coefficients for interactions with other objects or the ground.

The optimization objective minimizes the discrepancy between the simulated state (X_hat_t) and the observed state (X_obs_t) over a sequence of *T* frames:
min_α, G_t L(X_hat_t, X_obs_t) = Σ_[t=0 to T] [L_geo(X_hat_t, X_obs_t) + L_trk(X_hat_t, X_obs_t)]
subject to the constraint that the simulated state evolves according to a differentiable explicit Euler integrator: X_hat_t+1 = F_exp_α,G_t(X_hat_t).
- L_geo measures geometric discrepancy using Chamfer distance.
- L_trk measures point-wise tracking error.
- F_exp facilitates efficient forward simulation while maintaining differentiability.

#### 3.2. Bending-Enhanced Spring–Mass Dynamics

The core innovation of BendTwin is the augmentation of the traditional axial-only spring–mass model with explicit bending stiffness and damping.

- **Motivation for Bending Enhancement:** Standard spring–mass models rely solely on axial springs, which only resist stretching and compression. This approach is insufficient for accurately modeling bending-dominated deformations and can lead to under-constrained local deformation modes, especially in sparse discretizations.

- **Total Internal Potential Energy:** The total internal potential energy E_int(X) is defined as the sum of axial spring energy (E_axial) and the newly introduced bending energy (E_bend):
 E_int(X) = Σ_((i,j)∈E) E_axial(i, j) + Σ_((i,j,k)∈T) E_bend(i, j, k).
 Nodal forces are derived by differentiating E_int with respect to node positions: f = −∇_x E_int. The paper focuses on the derivation of forces from E_bend.

- **Bending Stiffness:**
 - Bending springs are introduced over local triplets (i, j, k), where *j* is the center node and *i*, *k* are its neighbors.
 - The bending energy E_bend(i, j, k) is defined as 0.5 * k_θ,(i,j,k) * (θ - θ_0)^2, where θ is the current angle between edges (i,j) and (k,j), and θ_0 is the rest angle.
 - Differentiating E_bend with respect to nodal positions yields restoring forces (f_bend_i, f_bend_k, f_bend_j) that act to reduce the angular deviation. These forces are proportional to (θ - θ_0) and are directed by geometrically derived unit-scaled directional vectors d_(i,j,k) which ensure torque consistency.

- **Bending Damping:**
 - To dissipate energy associated with angular motion and prevent oscillations, viscous bending damping is introduced.
 - For each triplet, an angular rate ˙θ_(i,j,k) is computed, representing the relative angular motion of edges (i,j) and (k,j).
 - Damping forces (f_rot_i, f_rot_k, f_rot_j) are modeled as a viscous torque proportional to this angular rate, with a damping coefficient c_b,(i,j,k). The force directions are identical to those for elastic bending forces.

- **Efficient Triplet Construction for High-Valence Nodes:** To manage computational cost for nodes with many neighbors (high valence), BendTwin constructs O(N) triplets instead of O(N^2) by selecting a reference neighbor for each node *j* and forming triplets with other neighbors. This preserves bending expressivity while maintaining scalability.

- **Total Force Computation:** The total force acting on any node *j* is the sum of forces from axial springs and damping, bending stiffness and damping, and any external forces (e.g., gravity, contact forces).

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings

- **Dataset:** Non-cloth deformable object sequences from the PhysTwin benchmark, excluding fabric-like materials which exhibit minimal intrinsic bending resistance. Sequences are split 7:3 for training (reconstruction & re-simulation) and testing (future prediction).
- **Baseline:** PhysTwin, an axial-only spring–mass formulation.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** 3D metrics (Chamfer Distance (CD), tracking error) and 2D rendering metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, silhouette IoU) from 3D Gaussian Splatting renderings.
- **Implementation:** PyTorch and Warp for GPU-accelerated simulation. Adam optimizer for α. Explicit Euler integrator with fine sub-steps (5x10^-5 s). Specific collision parameters (restitution, friction) are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that BendTwin consistently improves upon the axial-only PhysTwin baseline in terms of mechanical fidelity, simulation stability, and prediction accuracy.

#### 4.1. Reconstruction & Re-simulation

Quantitative results (Table 1) show BendTwin's performance during the training phase where physical parameters are optimized to match observed dynamics:
- **Geometric Accuracy:** BendTwin reduced Chamfer Distance (CD) from 0.0058 to 0.0047, representing a 19.0% improvement. Tracking error decreased from 0.0091 to 0.0078, a 14.3% improvement. These reductions indicate that the added bending constraints assist the physical graph in better preserving object structure.
- **Visual Quality:** Improvements were also observed in rendering metrics. PSNR increased from 28.429 to 28.605 (+0.18 dB), silhouette IoU improved from 79.5% to 80.6%, and LPIPS decreased from 0.022 to 0.021. This suggests that the improved mechanical consistency translates to more accurate visual renderings.

#### 4.2. Future Prediction

The ability of the optimized model to predict dynamics in unseen future frames (Table 1) also showed improvements:
- **Geometric Accuracy:** BendTwin reduced CD from 0.0095 to 0.0079 (16.8% reduction) and tracking error from 0.0166 to 0.0153 (7.8% reduction). This indicates that bending-aware constraints reduce the accumulation of geometric drift when the model is rolled out beyond the training data.
- **Visual Quality:** Similar improvements in rendering metrics were observed for future prediction. IoU increased from 68.7% to 71.5% (+2.8 percentage points), and PSNR improved from 26.412 to 26.755 (+0.34 dB).
- **Qualitative Observations:** Figure 3 visually supports these quantitative findings, showing BendTwin providing a closer match to ground-truth deformation patterns during long-horizon prediction.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Downsampled Physical System Robustness (Table 2):** This study investigated the robustness of BendTwin and PhysTwin to sparser physical graphs by reducing the sampling ratio (r).
 - **At r = 0.1:** BendTwin consistently outperformed PhysTwin. For reconstruction, CD decreased by 9.6% and tracking error by 10.8%, with PSNR improving by 0.45 dB and IoU by 1.6 percentage points. For future prediction, CD decreased by 7.2% and tracking error by 8.5%, with PSNR improving by 0.50 dB and IoU by 2.5 percentage points.
 - **At r = 0.05:** As the physical graph became significantly sparser, BendTwin's advantage substantially increased. For reconstruction, CD reduced by 26.5% and tracking error by 25.6%, with PSNR improving by 1.07 dB and IoU by 4.9 percentage points. For future prediction, CD reduced by 18.3% and tracking error by 16.3%, with PSNR improving by 1.14 dB and IoU by 7.0 percentage points.
 - These results demonstrate that bending constraints effectively stabilize the spring-mass system when axial-only formulations become under-constrained due to reduced connectivity.

- **Removal of Interior Nodes (Table 3):** This ablation assessed whether BendTwin could function effectively using only surface nodes, in contrast to PhysTwin which relies on interior nodes for mechanical support.
 - A surface-only BendTwin configuration (r=1.0, without interior nodes) was compared against the standard PhysTwin (with interior nodes).
 - For reconstruction and re-simulation, the surface-only BendTwin reduced CD by approximately 6.9% and tracking error by 3.3%, while increasing IoU by 1.1 percentage points.
 - For future prediction, CD decreased by approximately 5.3% and LPIPS by 6.7%, with PSNR increasing by 0.570 dB and IoU by 3.3 percentage points.
 - These findings indicate that the explicit bending constraints in BendTwin sufficiently penalize non-physical surface distortions, allowing a purely surface-based system to reproduce volumetric deformation with comparable or superior fidelity to an axial-only system that explicitly supports its interior. This potentially offers efficiency gains by simulating fewer mass points.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BendTwin represents an advancement in the field of physics-informed 3D reconstruction and dynamic prediction, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Mechanical Fidelity:** By explicitly incorporating bending stiffness and damping into spring-mass models, BendTwin provides a more mechanically faithful representation of deformable objects. This allows the model to capture higher-order deformation modes beyond simple stretching and compression, leading to more accurate and physically consistent digital twins.

2. **Improved Robustness and Stability for Sparse Systems:** A key limitation of axial-only spring-mass models is their tendency to become mechanically under-constrained and unstable when the physical graph is sparse. BendTwin's bending constraints significantly mitigate this issue, maintaining system stability and accuracy even with substantial downsampling of the physical graph. This robustness is critical for applications where computational efficiency or simplified representations are desired.

3. **Increased Efficiency through Surface-Only Models:** The demonstrated ability of BendTwin to perform effectively using only surface nodes, without the need for interior discretizations, is significant. This suggests that complex volumetric objects could be represented and simulated with fewer mass points, leading to more efficient computations while maintaining or even improving accuracy. This efficiency can be particularly beneficial for real-time applications or large-scale simulations.

4. **Broader Applicability for Deformable Objects:** By addressing a fundamental aspect of deformable body mechanics (bending), BendTwin extends the range of objects and deformation behaviors that can be accurately modeled using differentiable spring-mass systems. This makes the framework more versatile for a wider variety of real-world materials and interactions.

5. **Impact on Robotics and Interaction Planning:** Creating physically consistent digital twins is fundamental for intelligent robotic systems. BendTwin's improved accuracy in predicting dynamic states under interaction can directly benefit:
 - **Robot Manipulation Planning:** Robots can plan more precise and physically aware interactions with deformable objects.
 - **Policy Learning:** More realistic simulation data can be generated for training robot policies, improving sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Vision-Based World Models:** Enhanced physical grounding enables more accurate and stable rollouts for predicting future states in complex environments.

6. **Advancement in Differentiable Physics:** This work contributes to the broader field of differentiable physics by providing a more expressive and robust differentiable physical model. It demonstrates how incorporating specific mechanical priors (like bending) can significantly enhance the performance of gradient-based inverse parameter estimation from visual data.

In conclusion, BendTwin offers an effective and more mechanically complete foundation for building digital twins of deformable objects, promising improvements in efficiency, accuracy, and stability for a range of downstream applications requiring physically-informed dynamic prediction.
