# Stronger-MAS: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Collaborative LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> STRONGER-MAS, from UCSD and Intel, introduces an algorithm and system for training collaborative Large Language Model (LLM) agents using multi-agent reinforcement learning. This approach enables LLMs to learn specialized roles and coordinate effectively, achieving up to 99.5% accuracy in long-horizon planning tasks and consistent performance gains across various agentic domains.

## Problem

- Current Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) for LLMs primarily rely on 'prompt-only' designs, lacking mechanisms for iterative improvement through training.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to LLM agents is largely confined to single-agent scenarios, without robust frameworks for training multi-agent policies.
- Standard group-based RL objectives assume identical prompts for candidate actions, which is often violated in MAS due to role-specific and turn-dependent prompts.

## Method

- Developed **AT-GRPO** (==Agent- and Turn-wise== Grouped RL), an algorithm that adapts group-based RL for MAS by using tree-structured sampling and agent/turn-wise grouping.
- Designed a novel distributed training system with independent LLM resource pools and CPU-based environment execution, supporting concurrent on-policy training for multiple LLM policies.
- Implemented agent-wise credit assignment using a mixed global and local reward design to balance individual specialization with overall team objectives.

## Results

- MAS + AT-GRPO achieved substantial performance gains, increasing accuracy from 14.0–**47.0%** to 96.0–**99.5%** in long-horizon planning tasks and delivering 3.87–**7.62%** gains in coding.
- Role-specialized policies learned distinct functions, evidenced by a performance drop from **96.0%** to **6.0%** when trained agents' roles were swapped.
- The framework demonstrated scalability, showing continuous performance gains (from **18.2%** to **47.7%** on AIME24) as more collaborative agents were added to the system.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL training within a MAS environment robustly reinforces and leads to the emergence of specialized, non-interchangeable roles among LLM agents.
> - The choice between a single role-sharing policy and multiple role-specialized policies for MAS depends on the functional distinctness and overlap of roles within a given task.
> - Directly applying single-agent group-based RL to multi-agent settings typically leads to performance degradation due to state heterogeneity and biased advantage estimations.
