# Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution of Coding-Agent Harnesses

> [!summary] Summary
> Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE) is a closed-loop system for automatically evolving the external components (harness) of coding agents, such as system prompts, tools, and middleware. AHE enhances `pass@1` scores on Terminal-Bench 2 from 69.7% to 77.0%, outperforming human-designed and other automated baselines, and demonstrates transferability to new benchmarks and base models.

## Problem

- Current coding agents rely on manually engineered harnesses (prompts, tools, middleware), which are slow to adapt and struggle to keep pace with rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs).
- Automated agent optimization typically focuses on single components, failing to jointly evolve the full, interdependent harness due to challenges like heterogeneous action spaces and difficulty in extracting actionable signals from voluminous trajectories.
- The manual harness design process creates a gap between raw LLM capabilities and their effective utilization in complex software engineering tasks.

## Method

- Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE) implements a closed-loop system where an evolution agent autonomously optimizes all editable harness components through an observability-driven design.
- AHE utilizes NexAU, an editable and decoupled harness substrate, to expose seven orthogonal component types as explicit, file-level representations, providing a clear and localized action space for modifications.
- An 'Agent Debugger' processes multiple task rollouts into layered trajectory evidence and structured analysis reports, distilling actionable root causes for the evolve agent, while the 'Evolve Agent' makes evidence-driven, auditable edits recorded as 'falsifiable contracts'.

## Results

- AHE improved the `pass@1` score on Terminal-Bench 2 from a baseline of **69.7%** to **77.0%** over ten iterations, outperforming human-designed harnesses (**71.9%**) and other self-evolving baselines (**68.9%** and **72.3%**).
- The evolved AHE harness demonstrated transferability, achieving the highest aggregate success rate of **75.6%** on SWE-bench-verified and yielding consistent `pass@1` gains ranging from +2.3 to +10.1 percentage points when applied to five different base models.
- Ablation studies showed that long-term memory (+5.6 pp), tools (+3.3 pp), and middleware (+2.2 pp) were the primary contributors to performance gains, while the evolved system prompt resulted in a slight regression (-2.3 pp).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary drivers of coding agent performance gains originate from structural harness components (tools, middleware, long-term memory) rather than solely from prose-level strategic instructions in the system prompt.
> - An observability-driven framework, with decoupled components and layered evidence, is effective in managing the complexity of joint harness evolution, enabling autonomous improvements to an agent's operational context.
> - Evolved harnesses encode general engineering experience and coordination patterns, proving portable across different benchmarks and base models, suggesting a more efficient way to encode behavior than constant re-derivation from prompts.
