# An Aerial Manipulator for Perception-Driven Flower Targeting Toward Contactless Pollination in Vertical Farming

> [!summary] Summary
> An aerial manipulator for perception-driven flower targeting in vertical farming integrates real-time RGB-D vision and a deep-learning-based flower detector with an MPPI controller for robust UAV flight. This system achieved centimeter-level end-effector positioning accuracy in both simulations and real-world experiments, preparing for contactless pollination.

## Problem

- Declining natural pollinators and their absence in vertical farms create a high demand for autonomous pollination solutions.
- Existing robotic contact-based pollination methods risk damaging delicate floral structures, potentially reducing crop quality and yield.
- Confined and dense crop canopies in vertical farms necessitate high-precision robotic navigation and manipulation to avoid plant damage.

## Method

- A lightweight aerial manipulator platform combining a quadrotor UAV with onboard RGB-D perception and a 2-DoF manipulator was developed, featuring a modular end-effector interface to facilitate future integration of various contactless pollination mechanisms.
- A deep learning-based perception system was implemented for real-time flower detection and 3D localization using RGB-D data.
- An integrated control framework featuring a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) controller was used for robust UAV flight and precise end-effector positioning.

## Results

- Simulation experiments in MuJoCo demonstrated stable trajectory tracking and end-effector placement within a 5 cm error margin, with the MPPI controller ensuring smooth, stable UAV motion that enabled precise local end-effector adjustments.
- Real-world experiments achieved centimeter-level end-effector positioning accuracy, with approximately 40 mm difference along the X-axis matching the planned standoff.
- The system successfully performed real-time perception-driven flower detection and stable end-effector alignment in a laboratory environment mimicking vertical farming.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An MPPI-based controller manages UAV dynamics for stable flight and precise trajectory tracking in constrained environments, while decoupling coarse positioning from fine manipulator adjustments enables high-precision targeting without aggressive maneuvers.
> - Real-time RGB-D perception can reliably guide an aerial manipulator to delicate targets in complex agricultural settings.
> - A modular system design supports the future development and integration of various contactless agricultural manipulation tools.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the fields of agricultural robotics, vertical farming, and autonomous aerial manipulation, particularly in the context of crop pollination. The global decline of natural pollinators, such as bees, poses a substantial threat to agricultural productivity and food security. This issue is amplified in controlled indoor agricultural settings, specifically vertical farms, where natural insect pollination is absent. Consequently, there is a recognized need for autonomous robotic systems to perform pollination tasks.

Existing robotic pollination methods predominantly involve physical contact with flowers, utilizing mechanisms such as mechanical brushes, vibrating sticks, or micro-drones equipped with ionic liquid gels. While these contact-based approaches can achieve pollination, they introduce a significant risk of damaging delicate floral structures, potentially leading to reduced crop quality and yield. The current research landscape indicates a demand for robotic solutions that can navigate complex agricultural environments and interact with plants with minimal physical interference.

Drone-based and robotic pollination is an emerging area, with various techniques explored, including dry pollen sowing, liquid spray application, airflow disturbance, and contact-based delivery. However, an integrated aerial platform capable of precisely targeting flowers and facilitating *contactless* pollination methods has remained a specific research gap.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by developing an aerial manipulator designed for perception-driven flower targeting, localization, and approach, specifically optimized for the confined and dense canopies of vertical farms. By focusing on high-precision positioning and alignment as a precursor to contactless pollination, the authors aim to mitigate the risks associated with physical contact, thereby contributing to the development of more sustainable and less invasive agricultural robotic technologies. The platform is presented as a foundational step towards integrating advanced contactless end-effectors, such as those utilizing acoustic manipulation, into autonomous pollination systems, thereby addressing limitations of current robotic methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two interconnected global challenges: the decline of natural pollinators and the increasing demand for food security, particularly through innovative agricultural methods like vertical farming.

- **Motivation:**
 - **Pollinator Decline and Food Security:** The global decline of natural pollinators significantly jeopardizes crop production and food security. With the world population projected to reach nearly 10 billion by 2050, innovative agricultural technologies are required to ensure stable crop yields.
 - **Vertical Farming Challenges:** Vertical farming offers a solution for year-round production in controlled environments. However, the absence of natural insect pollinators in these indoor settings creates a significant manual labor demand for pollination, or requires the development of autonomous alternatives.
 - **Limitations of Existing Robotic Pollination:** Current robotic pollination methods largely rely on physical contact. These contact-based approaches, while functional, carry a high risk of damaging delicate floral structures (e.g., petals, stigmas). Such damage can compromise the flower's ability to be successfully pollinated or reduce the quality of the resulting crop, thereby counteracting the intended benefits of robotic intervention. This necessitates the development of robotic systems that can perform precise flower-targeting tasks while minimizing physical interference.
 - **Need for Precision in Confined Spaces:** Vertical farms often feature dense crop canopies and constrained spatial requirements, demanding high-precision robotic navigation and manipulation to avoid plant damage and achieve effective pollination.

- **Key Objectives:**
 - **System Design:** To design and develop a lightweight, high-precision aerial manipulator platform specifically optimized for the constrained spatial requirements and dense canopies characteristic of vertical farming environments.
 - **Perception-Driven Targeting:** To implement a perception system capable of real-time flower detection and localization using onboard RGB-D data, enabling the aerial manipulator to identify and target individual flowers accurately.
 - **Integrated Control Framework:** To develop and integrate a robust control pipeline combining deep-learning-based perception with an advanced Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller for the Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV). This framework aims to achieve stable flight dynamics and precise trajectory tracking, ensuring accurate end-effector positioning near floral targets.
 - **Precise End-Effector Positioning:** To enable centimeter-level end-effector positioning accuracy, allowing the manipulator to approach flowers with minimal physical interference. The system's goal is to achieve stable aerial positioning and precise local alignment of an end-effector relative to the target flower.
 - **Contactless Pollination Foundation:** To establish a practical and modular robotic platform that serves as a foundation for the future integration of various contactless pollination end-effectors, such as acoustic-based pollen manipulation modules.
 - **Experimental Validation:** To conduct comprehensive experimental evaluations in both simulated (MuJoCo) and real-world (UAV lab) environments to demonstrate the platform's stability, reliability, and scalability as a framework for perception-guided targeting and positioning in agricultural contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system is an aerial manipulator platform designed for perception-driven flower targeting, localization, and approach, integrating real-time visual perception, UAV flight control, and a lightweight manipulator.

#### 3.1. System Overview and Architecture

The platform consists of a lightweight quadrotor UAV integrated with onboard RGB-D perception, a 2-DoF manipulator, and a control stack built on the open-source PX4 autopilot. The system operates autonomously, coordinating flower detection, target-relative positioning, and end-effector alignment. A modular design is employed, organizing the architecture into three main components: Sensing, Perception & Localization; UAV Control & Navigation; and Manipulator & End-Effector.

- **Sensing, Perception & Localization Module:**
 - **Sensing:** An onboard RGB-D camera acquires RGB image frames and depth measurements. Camera calibration and frame transformation ensure geometric consistency between the camera, UAV, and global reference frames.
 - **Detection:** A lightweight deep learning model, such as a YOLO-based flower detector pre-trained on sunflower datasets, processes incoming image frames to identify flower regions in real time.
 - **Localization:** Detected flower observations are combined with depth measurements and UAV pose information through a depth-association and pose-fusion process. This computes the 3D position of the target flower, first in the UAV frame and then in the global frame, providing spatial information for downstream control.

- **UAV Control & Navigation Module:**
 - **State Estimation:** IMU measurements, odometry, and PX4 state feedback are fused to provide robust localization and motion feedback for indoor flight.
 - **Mission Management:** A finite-state machine manages the task progression through operational states including search, detection, approach, alignment, and mission continuation.
 - **Desired Pose Computation:** Once the flower target position is available, the system computes a desired UAV standoff pose. This pose maintains a safe operating distance from the flower while ensuring the target remains within the sensing and manipulation workspace.
 - **UAV Control:** A Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller generates closed-loop control actions. MPPI is employed for its robustness in handling nonlinear dynamics and indoor disturbances. These commands are transmitted to the PX4 autopilot via an offboard interface, executing low-level flight control actions to achieve stable hover near the flower.

- **Manipulator & End-Effector Module:**
 - **Local Positioning Refinement:** After the UAV achieves a stable hover, this module performs fine positioning. The desired end-effector pose is determined from the target pose, UAV pose, and manipulator kinematics.
 - **Manipulator:** A lightweight 2-DoF manipulator, mounted beneath the UAV, performs fine positioning corrections to complement the global mobility of the aerial platform. The UAV provides coarse positioning, while the manipulator improves targeting precision through local adjustments, avoiding aggressive UAV maneuvers in constrained environments.
 - **Modular Interface:** The aligned end-effector connects via a modular interface, designed to facilitate future integration of various pollination mechanisms, including contactless acoustic-based pollen manipulation modules. The current implementation focuses on perception-guided targeting, UAV standoff control, and precise end-effector positioning.

#### 3.2. Design, Model, and Control

- **Mechanical Design:** A custom L-shaped mounting structure is designed to integrate the RGB-D camera and the modular end-effector (e.g., a phased array for acoustic manipulation) beneath the UAV. The bracket is rigidly connected to the UAV frame, emphasizing lightweight construction, structural stability, and minimal aerodynamic interference to enable accurate positioning of sensing and actuation components.

- **UAV and Robotic Arm Modeling:**
 - **System Configuration:** A 2-DoF robotic arm is integrated with the UAV. While the manipulator provides 2-DoF, the UAV offers full 6-DoF mobility, enabling the combined system to achieve sufficient reachability and positioning.
 - **UAV Dynamics:** The UAV is modeled as a rigid-body quadrotor with 6-DoF, described by standard translational and rotational dynamics equations. These equations relate the UAV's position, velocity, acceleration, angular velocity, inertia, and control forces/torques.
 - **UAV-Manipulator Coupling:** The combined system dynamics are represented by a general form of rigid-body dynamics, considering the inertia matrix, Coriolis and centrifugal effects, and gravity for the combined UAV and manipulator state. To manage computational complexity, the manipulator is often treated as a quasi-static subsystem, assuming slower motion relative to UAV dynamics, enabling a decoupled control strategy.
 - **End-Effector Kinematics:** The position of the end-effector in the inertial frame is computed using forward kinematics, considering the UAV's position and rotation matrix, and the manipulator's position relative to the UAV body frame (which depends on joint angles).

- **UAV and Robotic Arm Control:**
 - **MPPI-Based UAV Control:** An MPPI controller is employed for the UAV. At each control step, MPPI samples multiple control trajectories and minimizes an expected cost function. This cost function penalizes position error relative to the target, velocity deviation, and control effort. The controller generates smooth control inputs, which are then sent to the PX4 autopilot via an offboard interface.
 - **Robotic Arm Control:** The control objective is to maintain a stable UAV pose while simultaneously aligning the end-effector with the desired flower position. The desired UAV position includes a computed offset to maintain a safe standoff distance from the flower. The manipulator performs fine adjustments to compensate for residual positioning errors, enhancing precision without requiring aggressive UAV maneuvers. The control pipeline integrates real-time feedback from perception, planning, and actuation components.

#### 3.3. Development

The system is implemented using ROS 2 (Robot Operating System 2) in Ubuntu 22.04 with ROS 2 Humble. This facilitates modular, real-time communication between sensing, perception, control, and manipulation components. The software stack includes:
- Sensor driver and state bridge nodes for RGB-D data and UAV feedback.
- A perception and target-localization pipeline for flower detection and estimation.
- A mission manager and MPPI-based control pipeline for trajectory generation and UAV positioning via PX4.
- A manipulator and end-effector interface pipeline for fine adjustment.
- Logging and visualization nodes.
The design supports both MuJoCo simulation and real-world PX4-based UAV platforms using consistent ROS 2 interfaces, allowing algorithms validated in simulation to be deployed in the real world by switching launch files and parameter configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed aerial manipulator platform was conducted through both simulation experiments in MuJoCo and real-world experiments in a UAV lab environment. The assessments focused on system stability, perception-guided positioning, and end-effector alignment accuracy.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments in MuJoCo

- **Setup:** A 1:1 scale model of the UAV aerial manipulator system, including geometry, mass distribution, and onboard sensing components, was implemented in a physics-based MuJoCo simulation environment. The full perception-to-control pipeline was replicated for closed-loop evaluation.
- **Metrics:** The primary evaluation metric was the end-effector position error relative to the target flower. A task was considered successfully aligned if the end-effector distance error was within 5 cm. Orientation error was not modeled separately, prioritizing spatial alignment.
- **Results:** The MPPI-based control framework demonstrated stable trajectory tracking and consistent convergence toward the target position. The UAV maintained a stable hover, while the manipulator performed fine adjustments, resulting in accurate end-effector placement across multiple trials within the specified 5 cm error margin. These results established the theoretical control performance and positioning accuracy under idealized conditions.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments in UAV Lab

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted in a 6x6 meter indoor laboratory environment configured to mimic a vertical farming setup. Sunflower targets were arranged on vertical stands. The UAV operated autonomously using its onboard perception system and the MPPI-based control on a PX4 platform. PhaseSpace motion capture markers were attached to both the end-effector and target flowers for accurate measurement of position and orientation deviations. The UAV maintained a standoff distance of approximately 10-30 cm from the flower during operation.
- **Results:**
 - **Perception and Alignment:** The perception module successfully detected and localized flower targets in real-time, enabling reliable alignment of the end-effector.
 - **Positioning Accuracy:** Measured positioning showed a difference of approximately 40 mm along the X-axis. This deviation matched the +40 mm forward standoff specified by the controller, indicating that the system intentionally positioned itself in front of the flower as planned. Discrepancies observed in the Y and Z axes were attributed to lateral and vertical standoffs, as well as minor axis misalignment between the coordinate frames.
 - **Orientation Error:** Minor deviations in orientation were observed, which the authors attributed to varying lighting conditions and sensing noise inherent in the real-world environment.
 - **Trajectory Stability:** The MPPI controller facilitated smooth and stable UAV motion, even in the presence of minor disturbances. The UAV maintained hover stability, allowing the manipulator to perform local adjustments. This demonstrated effective coordination between the aerial and manipulator subsystems in a physical environment.
 - Qualitative results, presented through images, further illustrated stable UAV positioning and accurate end-effector alignment near the target flowers.

#### 4.3. Discussion

The experimental results from both simulation and real-world trials collectively demonstrated the functionality and stability of the proposed aerial manipulator platform for flower-targeted positioning in vertical farming environments. The MPPI-based control framework provided robust aerial positioning, the perception system enabled reliable flower localization, and the manipulator enhanced fine alignment accuracy without destabilizing the UAV. This work focused on validating the robotic aerial manipulator and its positioning framework as a critical prerequisite for future contactless pollination systems. While the current implementation demonstrated precise positioning, it also identified future research directions, including improving perception robustness under varying lighting conditions, enhancing calibration between sensing and actuation modules, and integrating specific contactless actuation mechanisms, such as acoustic-based pollen manipulation using algorithms like GS-PAT.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The developed aerial manipulator platform represents a significant contribution to the field of agricultural robotics, offering a foundation for addressing critical challenges in modern food production.

- **Addressing Pollinator Decline and Food Security:** By proposing a robotic alternative for pollination, particularly in controlled environments, the work directly contributes to mitigating the impact of natural pollinator decline on crop yields. This has implications for enhancing food security, especially as global population continues to grow.

- **Enabling Contactless Pollination:** The primary significance lies in establishing a robust robotic platform that facilitates *contactless* flower targeting and positioning. This approach fundamentally mitigates the risks of physical damage to delicate floral structures, a major drawback of existing contact-based robotic pollination methods. By minimizing interference, the platform can potentially improve crop quality and yield compared to physically intrusive techniques.

- **Advancement in Vertical Farming Automation:** The platform is specifically optimized for vertical farming environments, where dense canopies and confined spaces necessitate high-precision robotic interaction. Its demonstrated ability to achieve centimeter-level end-effector positioning in such environments represents an advancement in the automation capabilities for indoor agriculture, moving beyond basic environmental control to direct plant interaction.

- **Integration of Advanced Robotics and AI:** The system integrates advanced components such as real-time RGB-D perception, deep learning for flower detection, and a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller for robust UAV dynamics. This integration showcases the potential of combining cutting-edge robotics and artificial intelligence to solve complex agricultural tasks, contributing to the broader development of intelligent agricultural systems.

- **Modular and Scalable Framework:** The modular design of the system and its ROS 2-based implementation provide a flexible and scalable framework. This allows for the straightforward integration of future contactless end-effectors (e.g., acoustic manipulators) and facilitates further research and development in autonomous agricultural tasks beyond just pollination, such as targeted spraying, phenotyping, or pest detection.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work explicitly serves as a foundational step for future development in contactless pollination technologies. By validating the precision targeting and positioning capabilities, it lays the groundwork for subsequent research into the efficacy and practical application of specific contactless actuation mechanisms, such as acoustic pollen transfer.

In summary, this research provides a practical, robust, and scalable aerial manipulator platform for perception-driven flower targeting. Its focus on enabling contactless interaction offers a pathway toward more sustainable and less damaging autonomous pollination systems, with broad implications for controlled environment agriculture and global food security.
