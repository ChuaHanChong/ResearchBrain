# Action100M: A Large-scale Video Action Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Action100M is a 147-million-segment video action dataset built from 1.2M HowTo100M instructional videos via a fully automated pipeline: V-JEPA 2 hierarchical temporal segmentation, multi-level Tree-of-Captions generation, and GPT-OSS-120B Self-Refine aggregation into structured brief/detailed action and caption annotations. Training VL-JEPA on it yields consistent data-scaling gains and strong zero-shot action recognition and retrieval, beating much larger CLIP/SigLIP2/Perception-Encoder models.

## Problem

- Existing video action datasets are siloed to narrow domains like cooking or toy assembly and stay under one million labeled action instances, limiting open-vocabulary coverage.
- Manual and semi-manual annotation pipelines bottleneck dataset scale and restrict activity diversity, while internet-mined datasets lack fine-grained hierarchical labels.
- Prior LLM-captioned video datasets assign one caption per segment without encoding temporal hierarchy, giving weak supervision for multi-scale world modeling.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical temporal segmentation== applies ==agglomerative Ward-linkage clustering== over V-JEPA 2 frame embeddings with local temporal connectivity, producing a multi-scale segment tree per video.
- ==Tree-of-Captions== generation pairs Llama-3.2-Vision mid-frame captions for leaf nodes with Perception-LM-3B segment captions for higher nodes, capturing spatial and temporal detail jointly.
- ==LLM Aggregation== prompts GPT-OSS-120B under three rounds of ==Self-Refine== to fuse tree evidence and video metadata into five structured fields: brief/detailed action, actor, brief/detailed caption.

## Results

- Pipeline produced **147,092,653** annotated segments totaling **21.27B** words from **1.2M** videos spanning **14.6 years**, using **1.3M** V100 and **0.3M** H100/H200 GPU hours.
- VL-JEPA (ViT-L, **3B** samples seen) reaches **52.5%** average zero-shot action recognition, beating Perception Encoder ViT-G (**44.7%**, **86B** samples) and SigLIP2 ViT-g (**39.8%**, **40B** samples).
- Controlled ablation at **20k** steps shows Action100M brief actions outperform direct PLM-3B pseudo-labeling, and semantic resampling with smaller k-means cluster counts improves zero-shot accuracy over unresampled data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Converting raw video into hierarchical text representations before applying costly reasoning models keeps large-scale structured annotation computationally tractable.
> - Aggregating evidence across multiple caption granularities before final labeling reduces hallucination compared to single-pass captioning of whole videos.
> - Long-tailed action-concept frequency in internet video demands explicit semantic resampling; naive uniform sampling biases models toward dominant but uninformative actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of Action100M is motivated by a critical gap in current machine intelligence: while models have made rapid strides in "world state perception" (e.g., object recognition) due to large-scale image-text datasets, their capability to "understand physical actions" remains comparatively limited. This limitation stems largely from the absence of robust data foundations that are adequately scaled, diverse, and semantically rich for action-centric understanding.

#### 1.1. Limitations of Existing Video Action Datasets

Prior work in video action datasets has significant constraints that Action100M aims to overcome:

- **Scale and Scope:** Existing datasets like COIN, CrossTask, and YouCook2 are relatively small, often containing fewer than a million action instances. They are typically collected for narrow domains (e.g., cooking, toy assembly), limiting their ability to support open-domain and open-vocabulary action recognition.
- **Annotation Bottlenecks:** Many high-quality datasets (e.g., Breakfast, Assembly101) rely heavily on manual annotation, making them expensive to scale and restricting their size and diversity. While internet-mined datasets offer broader coverage, they often lack the fine-grained, hierarchical labels crucial for complex action understanding.
- **Egocentric Limitations:** Egocentric datasets like EgoProceL and Ego4D Goal-Step provide unique first-person perspectives and dense annotations but remain largely domain-specific.

Action100M directly addresses these issues by leveraging 1.2 million instructional videos to generate over 100 million action instances. Its fully automated pipeline bypasses manual annotation bottlenecks, enabling an unprecedented scale and broad coverage of real-world activities with rich, multi-level annotations, distinguishing it significantly from prior efforts summarized in Table 1(a) of the paper.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Existing Video Captioning Datasets

The landscape of video captioning datasets, though growing in scale, also presents challenges for action understanding:

- **Noisy and Generic Captions:** Large-scale datasets like InternVid and HD-VILA-100M often derive captions from Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) transcripts or video metadata. These captions are frequently short, generic, noisy, and only weakly aligned with underlying physical actions, making them unsuitable for learning fine-grained action representations.
- **Limited Coverage of Richer Captions:** Recent efforts have leveraged powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate richer, more detailed, and instruction-like captions (e.g., OpenVid-1M, UltraVideo). However, these datasets are typically built on a relatively small number of source videos compared to ASR-based corpora, thus limiting their coverage of diverse environments and long-horizon activities. Koala-36M, while scaled, is primarily oriented towards video generation rather than structured action understanding.
- **Lack of Temporal Hierarchy:** Even advanced perception-centric datasets like PE Video and PLM-Video-Auto generally assign a single caption per segment and do not explicitly encode temporal hierarchies. This limitation hinders the modeling of multi-scale structures in activities (e.g., steps, sub-tasks, overarching tasks), which is vital for developing world models that reason over extended action sequences and their context.

Action100M innovatively combines the scale of ASR-driven corpora with the descriptive richness of LLM-captioned datasets. Its key distinguishing feature is the introduction of a "Tree-of-Captions" structure derived from hierarchical temporal segmentation, providing multiple levels of captions that capture both fine-grained actions and broader contextual narratives. This design preserves temporal hierarchy and dense-captioning capability, positioning Action100M as particularly suitable for training large-scale world models and action-centric video understanding systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Action100M**, a novel, large-scale dataset specifically designed to advance machine intelligence in understanding and interacting with the physical world. The core motivation stems from a recognized deficiency in current AI capabilities: the ability to robustly infer physical actions from visual observations.

The paper highlights several critical problems that Action100M aims to address:

1. **Bridging the Gap in AI Capabilities:** While AI has excelled in "world state perception" (recognizing objects and their attributes), "understanding physical actions" and their impact on world state transitions remains a significant challenge. This gap limits AI's utility in real-world scenarios.
2. **Lack of Robust Data Foundations for Action Understanding:** Existing video action datasets are insufficient in several key aspects:
 - **Limited Scale:** Most datasets comprise less than a million action instances, which is inadequate for training highly capable, generalized models.
 - **Narrow Domain Coverage:** Datasets are often developed for specific, constrained domains (e.g., cooking, toy assembly), preventing the development of truly open-domain action recognizers.
 - **Manual Annotation Bottlenecks:** The reliance on laborious and expensive manual annotation restricts the scalability and diversity of existing datasets.
 - **Lack of Open-Vocabulary Supervision:** Existing annotations often use predefined taxonomies, limiting the models' ability to understand novel or unseen actions.
3. **Need for Multi-Level and Structured Annotations:** Traditional video captioning datasets either offer generic, noisy captions (ASR-based) or, if richer, lack the multi-scale temporal hierarchy and explicit action-centric detail required for sophisticated reasoning about complex activities.

By addressing these limitations, Action100M is motivated to enable the development of:

- **Open-Domain and Open-Vocabulary Video Action Recognizers:** Models capable of understanding a vast range of actions without prior knowledge of specific categories.
- **Embodied Learning:** Allowing AI agents to learn physical skills and behaviors from observing human actions.
- **Wearable Assistive Applications:** Supporting devices that can understand and guide users through complex physical tasks.
- **Advanced Physical World Modeling:** Enabling AI systems to reason about the effects of actions, anticipate future states, and plan long-horizon procedures, moving towards a more comprehensive understanding of cause and effect in the physical world.

Ultimately, Action100M seeks to provide the necessary data infrastructure—large-scale, high-quality, diverse, and hierarchically structured—to accelerate research in these frontier areas of AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Action100M is constructed through a fully automated, scalable pipeline designed to overcome the limitations of manual annotation while ensuring high-quality, structured output. The pipeline processes 1.2 million Internet instructional videos (from HowTo100M), totaling 14.6 years of duration, and involves three distinct stages:

#### 3.1. Temporal Segmentation

The first stage focuses on decomposing each video into a hierarchy of temporally coherent segments, capturing actions at multiple levels of abstraction.

1. **Visual Embedding Generation:** Each video's frames are uniformly sampled (one out of every four raw frames) and then transformed into dense visual embeddings using the **V-JEPA 2 ViT-g-384 encoder**. To maintain temporal consistency and robust representation, overlapping temporal windows (64 sampled frames with an 8-frame stride) are processed independently. Spatial average pooling is applied to produce per-frame features, and representations for shared frames from overlapping windows are accumulated and averaged.
2. **Hierarchical Clustering:** A **hierarchical agglomerative clustering** algorithm (using Ward linkage) is applied to these frame-level representations. A crucial aspect is the "local temporal connectivity constraint," which ensures that merges only occur between contiguous time spans. The clustering proceeds bottom-up, iteratively merging adjacent segments to minimize intra-cluster variance. This process generates a hierarchical tree decomposition of the video, where each node corresponds to a semantically coherent temporal segment. Lower levels of the hierarchy represent fine-grained atomic motions, while higher levels capture coarser activities. Segments shorter than 0.5 seconds are discarded to ensure semantic meaningfulness.

#### 3.2. Caption Generation

Following segmentation, the second stage annotates each node in the generated "Tree-of-Captions" with descriptive text, capturing both fine-grained spatial details and broader temporal dynamics.

1. **Mid-frame Captioning (for leaf nodes):** For the smallest, most fine-grained segments (leaf nodes), a key frame at the midpoint of its temporal span is extracted. This frame is then processed by **Llama-3.2-Vision-11B**, prompted with "Describe this image in detail," to generate highly descriptive frame-level captions.
2. **Video-segment Captioning (for higher-level nodes):** For longer temporal segments (higher-level nodes), 32 evenly spaced frames are sampled. These are then fed into **Perception-LM-3B**, prompted with "Describe this video in detail," to generate comprehensive segment-level captions. Both captioning models are designed to be efficient, capable of running on a single NVIDIA V100 32GB GPU with a generation limit of 1024 tokens.

#### 3.3. LLM Aggregation

The final stage leverages a powerful Large Language Model (LLM) to aggregate information from the multi-level captions and extract structured action annotations, a process designed to reduce hallucinations and enhance quality.

1. **Information Aggregation:** For each node in the Tree-of-Captions, the LLM receives an input prompt combining the current node's caption, its children's captions (formatted in depth-first order), and global video context (including root captions within a limited depth, video title, description, and ASR transcript).
2. **Structured Annotation Extraction:** **GPT-OSS-120B** is queried to extract five structured fields:
 - Brief action description
 - Detailed action description
 - Actor (performing the action)
 - Brief video caption
 - Detailed video caption
3. **Self-Refine Procedure:** To ensure high consistency and quality, this extraction is performed through three iterative rounds of a "Self-Refine" procedure. In each round, the model revisits its previous output alongside the original context, iteratively correcting factual errors, resolving inconsistencies, and removing unsupported statements. Segments shorter than four seconds are discarded at this stage. The prompts emphasize visual observability and conservative reasoning to avoid hallucinations.

The computational resources deployed for this pipeline are substantial, involving approximately 1.3 million V100 GPU hours for segmentation and captioning, and 0.3 million H100/H200 GPU hours for LLM aggregation, demonstrating the significant investment in this large-scale data generation effort.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the Action100M dataset itself and empirical results demonstrating its utility for training advanced video understanding models.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Annotation Characteristics

- **Massive Scale:** Action100M is built from 1,199,096 face-blurred instructional videos from HowTo100M, accumulating to a total duration of approximately 14.6 years. From these, the automated pipeline generates an impressive 147,092,653 temporally localized video segments.
- **Rich Textual Content:** The dataset contains an estimated total of 21.27 billion English words across its four annotation types: brief action, brief caption, detailed action, and detailed caption. The average word counts clearly show a hierarchical level of detail: brief action (3.2 words), brief caption (19.2 words), detailed action (27.8 words), and detailed caption (95.3 words).
- **Segment Duration Distribution:** The segmentation process yields a majority of shorter segments, with 64% having durations between 0-3 seconds, 23.8% in the 3-10 second range, and 10.2% between 10 seconds and 1 minute. Only about 2% of segments exceed one minute. A small fraction (3.23%) of segments are labeled "N/A," typically corresponding to non-action content.
- **Content Diversity:** A word cloud of video titles confirms the dataset's instructional and procedural nature (e.g., "make," "recipe," "DIY"). N-gram distributions reveal that action descriptions are verb-centric ("add," "stir"), while video captions are more descriptive with adjectives and object-centric terms. The analysis also highlights a long-tailed distribution of action concepts, motivating specific training strategies.
- **Storage Footprint:** The entire dataset, including annotations, metadata, and the full Tree-of-Captions structure, requires approximately 205 GB of disk space.

#### 4.2. Experimental Results on VL-JEPA Training

To demonstrate Action100M's utility, the authors trained a **VL-JEPA** (Vision-Language Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) model, a state-of-the-art vision-language model, in a three-stage procedure: image pretraining, main Action100M pretraining (8 frames), and fine-tuning with more frames (32 frames) and an unfrozen encoder.

- **Consistent Data Scaling:** Experiments show that VL-JEPA's performance consistently improves as the amount of Action100M training data increases, indicating the dataset's scalability benefits for learning robust representations.
- **Strong Zero-shot Performance:**
 - **Action Recognition:** VL-JEPA (ViT-L at 256px, having seen 3.3 billion samples) achieved an average Top-1 accuracy of 52.5% across eight diverse action recognition benchmarks (including SSv2, EPIC-KITCHENS-100, Kinetics-400). This performance significantly surpasses that of highly sampled and often larger models like CLIP (30.7%, 12.8B samples), SigLIP2 (39.8%, 40B samples), and PE-Core (44.7%, 86B samples). VL-JEPA demonstrated particular strength in motion-focused and step-centric tasks.
 - **Text-to-Video Retrieval:** VL-JEPA also established strong vision-language alignment, achieving an average Recall@1 of 63.7% on eight text-to-video retrieval benchmarks, again outperforming the compared foundation models.
- **Impact of Video Pretraining:** A significant performance jump from the image-only pretraining stage to the video pretraining stage with Action100M confirms that image-only data is insufficient for action recognition and highlights the critical value of Action100M's video content.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of the Action100M Pipeline

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the effectiveness of specific components and annotation types within the Action100M pipeline:

- **Brief Action Descriptions:** These LLM-aggregated descriptions proved highly effective in improving zero-shot action recognition, outperforming a direct pseudo-labeling baseline (PLM-3B). This confirms the superior quality and utility of annotations generated through hierarchical captioning and sophisticated LLM aggregation.
- **Detailed Captions:** Action100M's detailed captions showed advantages over PLM-Video-Auto captions on most benchmarks, further validating the comprehensive nature of the generated textual data.
- **Domain Specificity:** While Action100M generally excels, integrating domain-specific data like Ego4D atomic action descriptions provided additional benefits for egocentric action recognition (e.g., on EK-100 and EgoExo4D), indicating that specialized data can still complement a broad foundation.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Semantic Resampling

To mitigate the challenge of long-tailed action distributions, a semantic resampling strategy was explored:

- Embedding brief action descriptions and applying k-means clustering (k = {10^3, 10^4, 10^5}) to deduplicated actions for balanced sampling.
- Results showed that resampling with a smaller number of clusters led to improved average zero-shot action classification performance. This indicates that strategically down-sampling frequent actions and up-sampling rare ones, guided by semantic similarity, is an effective approach to enhance sample efficiency and model robustness against data imbalance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Action100M represents a significant leap forward in the field of video understanding and multimodal AI, establishing itself as a crucial new data foundation with far-reaching implications:

- **Enabling Scalable, Open-Vocabulary Action Understanding:** The most direct impact of Action100M is its provision of an unprecedented scale (147 million segments) of high-quality, open-vocabulary action annotations. This addresses a long-standing bottleneck in training general-purpose models capable of understanding diverse, real-world physical actions, moving beyond the limitations of small, narrow-domain, and manually annotated datasets.
- **Advancing World Models and Embodied AI:** The dataset's rich, hierarchical annotations—the "Tree-of-Captions" structure with brief/detailed actions and captions—is specifically designed to support the development of sophisticated world models. These models can learn to reason over multi-scale procedural structures, understand complex sequences of actions, predict outcomes, and infer causal relationships in the physical world. This is fundamental for progressing embodied AI agents towards more intelligent and autonomous interaction with their environment.
- **Fueling Research in Long-Horizon Planning and Action Anticipation:** The instructional video nature of Action100M, combined with its detailed procedural annotations, makes it an ideal resource for research into long-horizon planning and action anticipation. Models trained on Action100M can learn to decompose complex tasks into steps, understand the intent behind actions, and predict future actions, which are critical for robotics, assistive technologies, and human-computer collaboration.
- **Methodological Blueprint for Large-Scale Data Curation:** The fully automated pipeline, leveraging frontier models like V-JEPA 2, Llama-3.2-Vision, Perception-LM, and GPT-OSS with techniques like Self-Refine, offers a robust and cost-effective blueprint for generating high-quality, structured annotations at scale. This methodology can inspire and be adapted for curating other complex multimodal datasets, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in data-driven AI research.
- **Setting New Benchmarks for Zero-shot Generalization:** The empirical results demonstrate that models (specifically VL-JEPA) pretrained on Action100M achieve superior zero-shot transfer performance across a diverse array of action recognition and text-to-video retrieval benchmarks. This underscores the dataset's quality and generalizability, establishing it as a new standard for evaluating foundation models in video understanding.
- **Mitigating Data Imbalance with Semantic Resampling:** The successful implementation of semantic resampling highlights an effective strategy for addressing the prevalent long-tail problem in large, naturally distributed datasets. This approach ensures that models learn effectively from both frequent and rare actions, leading to more balanced and robust representations.

In conclusion, Action100M is poised to be a foundational resource that will significantly accelerate progress in video understanding, world modeling, and embodied AI, paving the way for more capable and context-aware machine intelligence in the physical world.
