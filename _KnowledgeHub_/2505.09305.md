# Embodied intelligent industrial robotics: Framework and techniques

> [!summary] Summary
> This work defines and proposes a knowledge-driven technical framework for Embodied Intelligent Industrial Robotics (EIIR) to overcome the limitations of general embodied AI in industrial settings. It synthesizes existing and emerging techniques across five core modules to enable flexible and autonomous production systems for intelligent manufacturing.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Intelligent Robotics (EIR) predominantly focuses on daily-life applications and lacks the specialized industrial knowledge, process information, and adherence to specific constraints required for manufacturing.
- Current multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) often fail to meet the stringent efficiency, accuracy, reliability, and safety standards of industrial environments.
- There is an absence of a systematic framework and comprehensive synthesis of embodied intelligence tailored specifically for industrial robotics (EIIR).

## Method

- A novel knowledge-driven technical framework for EIIR is proposed, comprising five interconnected modules: ==World Model==, High-Level Task Planner, Low-Level Skill Controller, Simulator, and Physical System.
- The framework centralizes a World Model to integrate general, working-environment, and operating-object knowledge, including industrial-specific knowledge graphs and semantic maps.
- It combines LLM-based high-level task planning with a 'skill-language' architecture for low-level control, supported by a bidirectional digital-twin loop between the simulator and physical system for continuous adaptation and improvement.

## Results

- The proposed EIIR framework is conceptually validated through a case study on a flexible assembly system for valves, demonstrating its potential for integrating industrial knowledge and enabling adaptive task execution.
- The research systematically reviews techniques for each framework module, highlighting that a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach, combining LLMs with industrial semantic maps and knowledge graphs, shows promise for robust industrial task planning.
- It identifies the need for composite simulation architectures that fuse production-line simulators with deep learning-oriented robot simulation nodes to bridge the gap between virtual commissioning and AI-driven agent training, mediated by industry-standard protocols.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating industrial-specific knowledge, such as detailed product and process information, directly into embodied intelligence systems is essential to address the 'knowledge blind spot' of general MLLMs in industrial applications.
> - A modular, knowledge-driven framework provides a structured and adaptable approach to transition from traditional, rigid industrial automation to more flexible, autonomous, and robust production systems.
> - The synergy between advanced LLM-based high-level planning, a standardized 'skill-language' for low-level control, and comprehensive simulation capabilities through a digital twin loop is critical for developing advanced industrial robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence (EI), also referred to as embodied artificial intelligence, has experienced significant growth, particularly when integrated with robotics to form embodied intelligent robotics (EIR). Existing research in EIR primarily focuses on applications in daily-life contexts, such as home service and social interaction. However, industrial environments present distinct and more stringent requirements concerning efficiency, accuracy, reliability, and safety. Current EIR techniques, often reliant on multi-modal large language models (MLLMs), are noted to lack specialized industrial knowledge, process-specific information, and an understanding of industrial constraints, which poses challenges for their direct application in manufacturing scenarios.

This paper identifies a gap in the systematic study and synthesis of embodied intelligence within the context of industrial robotics (EIIR). While existing reviews cover general embodied AI, EIR, or the integration of large language models with robotics, this work distinguishes itself by specifically addressing embodied intelligence in industrial settings. It aims to provide a focused analysis of the challenges encountered when transitioning EIR from general to industrial applications and to propose a framework tailored to these unique demands.

The authors observe a rising trend in industrial embodied intelligence research since 2018, with significant growth peaking in 2024. This trend is linked to advancements in EIR, pre-trained models, and MLLMs, which have enhanced robotic perception and cognition. Geographically, China, the United States, Italy, the United Kingdom, and Germany are identified as leading contributors to this research, with a substantial portion of publications originating from computer science and engineering disciplines, indicating an interdisciplinary research network. This paper positions itself as the first comprehensive review to specifically address EIIR, summarize its challenges, propose a dedicated technical framework, and outline future research directions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the integration of embodied intelligence (EI) with industrial robotics (IR), defining this synergistic field as Embodied Intelligent Industrial Robotics (EIIR). The motivation stems from the observation that while the combination of embodied intelligence and robotics holds considerable promise, its direct application in industrial scenarios faces substantial technical hurdles.

The paper outlines several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Address Limitations of Existing EIR**: Current EIR techniques, predominantly designed for daily-life applications, often fall short of the high standards required in industrial settings regarding efficiency, accuracy, reliability, and safety. A core deficiency identified is the lack of industrial-specific knowledge within existing multi-modal large language models (MLLMs), which limits their ability to understand and adhere to industrial standards, process information, and operational constraints.
2. **Define Knowledge Requirements for Industrial Robots**: The authors argue that for effective operation in industrial environments, robots must possess three types of knowledge:
 - **General knowledge**: For natural human-robot communication.
 - **Working-environment knowledge**: To understand the operational space, enabling efficient locomotion and interaction.
 - **Operating-object knowledge**: To facilitate precise manipulation and ensure compliance with industrial specifications.
3. **Propose a Knowledge-Driven EIIR Framework**: To overcome the identified challenges, the paper aims to introduce a novel knowledge-driven technical framework for EIIR. This framework is intended to be adaptable to various industrial environments and to provide a structured approach for integrating embodied intelligence into industrial robots.
4. **Systematically Review and Organize Relevant Techniques**: A significant objective is to thoroughly review the development of techniques related to each module of the proposed EIIR framework. This includes discussing recent progress and detailing how these techniques can be adapted for industrial applications, thereby organizing a fragmented body of literature.
5. **Demonstrate Applicability through a Case Study**: The paper seeks to provide a practical illustration of the proposed EIIR framework's utility and potential through a real-world assembly system case study. This aims to validate the conceptual framework in a tangible industrial context.
6. **Identify Challenges and Future Directions**: Finally, the research aims to summarize the key challenges facing EIIR in industrial scenarios and to suggest avenues for future research. This is intended to guide scholars and engineers in their efforts to advance EIIR technology.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between general embodied intelligence research and the specific demands of intelligent manufacturing, thereby fostering a new technological paradigm for industrial automation characterized by flexibility, autonomy, and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research involves proposing a novel **knowledge-driven technical framework for Embodied Intelligent Industrial Robotics (EIIR)** and then comprehensively reviewing existing and emerging techniques that align with each component of this framework. The framework is designed to address the unique requirements of industrial environments.

The proposed EIIR framework comprises five interconnected modules:

1. **World Model**: This module is positioned as the central knowledge source. It is responsible for providing robots with **general knowledge** (for natural language understanding), **working-environment knowledge** (e.g., semantic maps of production lines, equipment orientations, operational boundaries), and **operating-object knowledge** (e.g., domain-specific knowledge graphs detailing product processes, part topologies, and parameters). The world model described here is a symbolic formulation, not a generative visual scene model. The framework posits that an industrial task-oriented fine-tuned Large Language Model (LLM) can extract information from process documents and CAD models to populate the operating-object knowledge graph.

2. **High-Level Task Planner**: This module's function is to interpret natural language commands from users (e.g., "assemble 1,000 pressure-reducing valves") and decompose them into a sequence of subtasks. It leverages an LLM-based encoder and integrates external knowledge from the world model to ensure that task plans comply with industrial specifications and constraints.

3. **Low-Level Skill Controller**: This module translates the abstract subtasks generated by the high-level planner into specific, physical-executable sequential skills. It accesses a library of pre-defined industrial skills and converts them into device-independent standardized instructions. An interpreter then dynamically converts these instructions into protocol-specific commands for various target controllers (e.g., robots, PLCs), enabling physical execution. The reusability of skill sets across different product configurations is a key feature.

4. **Simulator**: This module serves to model the kinematics, control logic, and environmental interactions of industrial robots and production lines. It enables algorithm development, virtual commissioning, and the creation of digital twins at both single-robot and full-production-line scales. The simulator and physical system are envisioned to form a bidirectional digital-twin loop, where real-time data from the physical system updates the simulator, and virtual models infer additional states, allowing for continuous strategy re-evaluation and adjustment within the perception-decision-action loop.

5. **Physical System**: This module represents the real-world industrial environment where tasks are physically executed. It interacts with the simulator in a digital-twin loop, providing feedback and serving as the operational environment for the EIIR.

**Detailed Review of Techniques for Each Module:**

- **World Models**: The paper reviews methods for representing working-environment knowledge, including geometric maps, scene graphs, and semantic maps. It differentiates between non-LLM (e.g., GNNs, point clouds) and LLM-based approaches for semantic map construction. For operating-object knowledge, it discusses ontologies and knowledge graphs, emphasizing their role in structuring product, process, and resource information to support task planning and execution.

- **High-Level Task Planner**: The review distinguishes between general task planning (LLM-based with prompts, visual modules, security modules; and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models) and industrial task planning. For industrial contexts, it covers knowledge and skill-based methods (e.g., knowledge graph-based reasoning, Domain Specific Language (DSL)-based PDDL planning), learning-based methods (deep learning, reinforcement learning, imitation learning), and LLM-based methods. The paper proposes a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach combined with LLMs, semantic maps, and knowledge graphs to infuse industrial knowledge.

- **Low-Level Skill Controller**: This section details a "skill-language" architecture. It categorizes industrial skills into handling, joining, inspecting, and special operations, providing a taxonomy and outlining key parameters. It also discusses DSLs as intermediate languages to bridge skill abstraction and hardware instructions, evaluating them based on criteria like kinematics, path planning, real-time control, Perception-Action capabilities, PLC interoperability, tool chain support, and industrial application.

- **EIIR Simulators**: Simulators are categorized into robot simulators (e.g., Gazebo, Isaac Sim for single robots) and production-line simulators (e.g., KUKA.Sim, Tecnomatix for entire lines). Evaluation criteria include high-fidelity motion simulation, rich robot libraries, Python API, multi-sensor simulation, RGB-D camera support, PLC integration, and multi-device interaction. The paper also reviews sim-to-real techniques, grouping them into tuning simulation (e.g., domain randomization, system identification), aligning data (e.g., domain adaptation, natural language as a bridge), and merging policies.

The methodology involves a systematic literature review and synthesis guided by the proposed framework. A case study on an EIIR-based flexible assembly system is presented to conceptually validate the framework, illustrating how its modules would function in an integrated industrial application.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research identifies significant limitations in applying existing embodied intelligent robotics (EIR) directly to industrial scenarios. These limitations are attributed primarily to the absence of industrial-specific knowledge, process understanding, and adherence to industrial constraints in current general-purpose MLLMs. To address this, the paper proposes a **knowledge-driven technical framework for Embodied Intelligent Industrial Robotics (EIIR)**, which represents a structured approach to integrating embodied intelligence into industrial settings.

The key findings and results are presented through the proposed framework and the synthesis of existing research for each module:

- **Framework Proposal**: A five-module framework (World Model, High-Level Task Planner, Low-Level Skill Controller, Simulator, Physical System) is presented as a paradigm shift from traditional, rigid industrial automation to flexible, autonomous production. This framework emphasizes knowledge integration as its core differentiator.

- **World Model Module**: The paper establishes the necessity of a comprehensive world model that integrates general, working-environment, and operating-object knowledge. It finds that existing methods for semantic map construction (both non-LLM and LLM-based) and knowledge graph creation (focused on products, processes, and resources) are foundational. However, it notes a gap in industrial foundation models capable of robustly supporting industrial object recognition and functional semantic understanding.

- **High-Level Task Planner Module**: A review of task planning techniques reveals that general LLM-based and VLA-based methods, while powerful, lack the precision and constraint adherence required for industrial tasks. Existing industrial planning methods (knowledge and skill-based, learning-based) offer solutions but suffer from manual rule definition, data dependency, or limited generalization. The paper suggests that a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach, combining LLMs with industrial-specific semantic maps and knowledge graphs, offers a promising path to overcome the "knowledge blind spot" in industrial task planning.

- **Low-Level Skill Controller Module**: The paper defines a "skill-language" architecture for physical execution. It presents a taxonomy of industrial skills, categorizing them into handling, joining, inspecting, and special operations, identifying "Pick and place," "Transport," "Press-fit," "Screw," "Measure," and "Check" as indispensable. The analysis of Domain Specific Languages (DSLs) highlights their role in abstracting skills and enabling hardware-independent control, with a focus on kinematics, path planning, real-time control, PLC interoperability, and tool chain support. This structured approach aims to enable LLM-driven EI through standardized skill bases and programming interfaces.

- **EIIR Simulator Module**: The research evaluates both robot simulators (e.g., Gazebo, Isaac Sim) and production-line simulators (e.g., Tecnomatix, Visual Components) against industrial-specific criteria. It finds that while robot simulators excel in single-robot deep learning, they often lack compatibility with PLC protocols and MES integration. Conversely, production-line simulators are mature for virtual commissioning but frequently lack open APIs for AI-oriented agent training. The paper concludes that bridging this gap requires a composite simulation architecture, fusing digital twin bases from production-line simulators with dedicated robot simulation nodes that support deep learning, often mediated by middleware like ROS or OPC servers. It also categorizes sim-to-real techniques into tuning simulation, aligning data, and merging policies to facilitate the transfer of learned policies to real industrial environments.

- **Case Study: EIIR-based Flexible Assembly System**: A conceptual validation of the EIIR framework is presented through a flexible assembly system for valves.
 - **World Model Application**: Semantic maps for working areas (part/tool libraries, assembly units) are automatically constructed, enabling mobile robots to understand locations and retrieval tasks. An assembly knowledge graph, populated by an LLM fine-tuned on process documents and CAD models, provides detailed product and process information.
 - **Task Planning Application**: When instructed (e.g., "assemble 1000 pressure-reducing valves"), the high-level task planner utilizes knowledge embedding and multi-agent interactions to generate optimized subtask lists for different assembly units and material flow, considering metrics like production line balance.
 - **Skill Control Application**: The low-level skill controller translates subtasks into executable programs, matching processes to skill invocations and populating parameters based on the world model and visual observations. It leverages skill models (VLA models) for learning new skills from demonstrations and open-vocabulary grasping for mobile robots, all guided by semantic maps for navigation.
 - **Simulator Application**: A digital twin of the assembly system enables virtual rehearsal, verification of task plans, path optimization, and program testing. During operation, the simulator forms a bidirectional digital-twin loop with the physical system, facilitating real-time monitoring, state inference, and triggering replanning or error recovery based on continuous feedback, embodying a perception-decision-action loop.
 - **Broad Applicability**: Beyond assembly, the framework's applicability is demonstrated across diverse industrial scenarios, including CNC machining, logistics warehousing, 3C electronics, LED manufacturing, and new-energy production, for tasks such as transport, machining, and assembly, indicating its potential across the industrial chain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper carries significance for the advancement of industrial automation and intelligent manufacturing, primarily by proposing a structured framework for Embodied Intelligent Industrial Robotics (EIIR). Its potential impact spans several domains:

- **Paradigm Shift in Industrial Automation**: The paper posits that EIIR is poised to reshape traditional industrial automation by moving beyond rigid, manually programmed controls to flexible, autonomous production systems. This transition could enable industrial robots to perceive, make decisions, and interact within dynamic industrial environments, similar to human workers.

- **Foundation for Intelligent Factories**: The long-term vision positions industrial embodied intelligence as the "cognitive center" of intelligent factories. This implies a shift from "program solidification" to systems capable of "independent evolution," where robots can adapt to changing production needs, handle unexpected situations, and continuously improve their performance through learning and interaction.

- **Addressing Current Gaps**: The proposed knowledge-driven EIIR framework directly addresses the limitations of existing EIR techniques when applied to industrial contexts, particularly their lack of industrial-specific knowledge, process understanding, and adherence to strict operational constraints. By emphasizing a robust World Model and knowledge-driven planning, the framework provides a pathway to overcome these deficiencies.

- **Enhanced Flexibility and Adaptability**: The framework is designed to enable higher task-level flexibility in industrial robots, allowing them to perform a variety of tasks (e.g., loading, unloading, assembly) and adapt to diverse product configurations and small-batch manufacturing demands. This is crucial for modern intelligent manufacturing paradigms that prioritize customization and responsiveness.

- **Guidance for Research and Development**: By systematically reviewing methodologies across the World Model, High-Level Task Planner, Low-Level Skill Controller, and Simulator modules, the paper provides a comprehensive reference for scholars and engineers. It offers insights into the latest EIIR developments and aids in the selection of appropriate techniques for specific industrial applications.

- **Practical Implementation Roadmap**: The case study of an EIIR-based flexible assembly system demonstrates the practical applicability and potential of the proposed framework in a real-world setting. This illustration provides a tangible blueprint for implementing EIIR in complex manufacturing systems, including cross-category products and multi-unit collaboration.

- **Identification of Future Research Avenues**: The paper concludes by outlining key challenges and future research directions. These include:
 - **Development of Industrial Foundation Models**: To combat "industrial illusion" in LLMs and provide deep domain-specific knowledge across product life cycles.
 - **RAG-like High-Level Task Planning**: Integrating LLMs with semantic maps and domain knowledge graphs for deterministic planning under industrial constraints.
 - **Generalizable Low-Level Skills and Control Language**: Research into open-vocabulary object detection, 6D pose estimation for parametric parts, and the creation of a unified Domain Specific Language (DSL) for heterogeneous industrial devices (robots and PLCs) to eliminate "protocol walls."
 - **Advanced EIIR Simulators**: Developing open-source simulators that support multi-domain coupling, virtual industrial agents, and simulation data engines for foundation model training, forming a virtual-real fusion environment.

Overall, the significance of this work lies in its systematic approach to defining and structuring the emerging field of embodied intelligent industrial robotics. It provides both a conceptual framework and a synthesis of technical components, aiming to drive the rapid advancement and practical application of EIIR, ultimately contributing to a more intelligent and adaptable manufacturing ecosystem.
