# Exploring the Limits of Vision-Language-Action Manipulations in Cross-task Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces AGNOSTOS, a novel benchmark for evaluating zero-shot cross-task generalization in robotic manipulation, and proposes X-ICM, a method that leverages large language models (LLMs) with dynamics-guided in-context learning. X-ICM significantly outperforms existing vision-language-action models on the AGNOSTOS benchmark, achieving an average success rate of 30.1% on unseen tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with zero-shot generalization to entirely unseen manipulation tasks, despite excelling in within-task variations.
- A lack of a rigorous, reproducible benchmark specifically designed to evaluate true zero-shot cross-task generalization for robotic manipulation.
- Existing approaches do not effectively leverage the in-context learning capabilities of LLMs to generate action plans for genuinely novel manipulation challenges.

## Method

- Introduced **AGNOSTOS**, a novel simulation benchmark on ==RLBench== comprising 23 distinct unseen manipulation tasks, categorized into two difficulty levels, for rigorous evaluation of cross-task generalization.
- Proposed **Cross-Task In-context Manipulation (X-ICM)**, a method that uses a ==Large Language Model (LLM)== to predict action sequences for unseen tasks.
- **X-ICM** integrates a ==dynamics-guided sample selection== module, which employs a ==diffusion model== to retrieve the most relevant ==in-context demonstrations== from seen tasks based on predicted future visual states.

## Results

- Existing VLA models achieved an average success rate of at most **17.5%** on AGNOSTOS, with all models failing completely on at least **8** of the 23 unseen tasks.
- X-ICM (72B) achieved an average success rate of **30.1%** on AGNOSTOS, outperforming the best baseline by **12.6%** and successfully addressing all **23** unseen tasks.
- Real-world experiments with an xArm7 robot demonstrated X-ICM's practical applicability, achieving success rates ranging from **10%** to **70%** on five zero-shot cross-task manipulation challenges.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLA models, including foundation models, exhibit severe limitations in zero-shot cross-task generalization, frequently failing on novel manipulation scenarios (0% success on multiple tasks).
> - Effective cross-task generalization with LLMs heavily relies on selecting highly relevant in-context demonstrations; random selection significantly degrades performance.
> - Larger LLM backbones generally improve zero-shot cross-task generalization, and an optimal number of in-context demonstrations exists to maximize performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is undergoing a transformative period, largely driven by advances in **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. These models integrate visual perception, language understanding, and action generation, moving towards more intelligent and adaptable robots. Previous generations of VLA models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in generalizing across visual variations within *known* tasks, such as handling objects in novel scenes or with altered properties (termed "within-task generalization"). This has been largely facilitated by large-scale pre-training on diverse datasets, including human videos and cross-embodiment robotic demonstrations.

However, this paper identifies a critical and largely *underexplored gap*: the ability of VLA models to generalize to *entirely unseen tasks*—a capability termed **zero-shot cross-task generalization**. This is a fundamental requirement for deploying robots in real-world, open-ended environments, where unforeseen tasks are the norm. Most prior work, while sometimes involving real-world environments, often lacks reproducibility and rarely systematically targets this specific type of generalization. While an increasing number of simulated benchmarks have emerged to address generalization, they primarily focus on within-task variations, such as changes in object appearance, lighting, or minor environmental perturbations. Benchmarks that do include some form of cross-task evaluation are often limited in scope (e.g., fewer tasks, only in-domain models) and do not push the boundaries of "novelty" as rigorously.

"AGNOSTOS" directly confronts this limitation by introducing the first benchmark specifically designed for rigorous, reproducible evaluation of zero-shot cross-task generalization. This directly addresses the need for standardized evaluation criteria for a crucial aspect of generalist robotics. Furthermore, the proposed "X-ICM" method leverages the rapidly advancing capabilities of **Large Language Models (LLMs)**, particularly their **in-context learning (ICL)** abilities, to tackle this challenge. While ICL has been explored in robotics for within-task generalization, this work is novel in extending this paradigm to the more difficult zero-shot *cross-task* setting, identifying and addressing the unique challenges of relevant demonstration selection across tasks. By doing so, this paper aims to push the boundaries of what current VLA models can achieve and pave the way for more truly general-purpose robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from the recognized inadequacy of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in generalizing to entirely new and unseen manipulation tasks, a crucial capability for real-world robotic deployment. The authors highlight that while VLA models excel at within-task generalization (e.g., handling novel object appearances within a familiar task), their performance on zero-shot cross-task generalization remains significantly underexplored and limited.

To address this critical gap, the paper sets out three key objectives:

1. **Introduce AGNOSTOS: A Novel Zero-shot Cross-task Generalization Benchmark.** The primary objective is to create a robust, reproducible, and challenging simulation benchmark specifically tailored to evaluate the zero-shot cross-task generalization abilities of VLA models. This benchmark, built on RLBench, comprises 23 unseen manipulation tasks—distinct from common training task distributions—categorized into two levels of difficulty to systematically assess generalization robustness.
2. **Systematically Evaluate Existing VLA Models on AGNOSTOS.** The authors aim to conduct an extensive evaluation of a diverse range of leading VLA models, including those pre-trained on large-scale robotic datasets (Foundation VLA models), human action videos (Human-video VLA models), and purely on in-domain data (In-domain VLA models). This evaluation seeks to uncover the fundamental limitations of current approaches when faced with genuinely novel tasks.
3. **Propose Cross-Task In-Context Manipulation (X-ICM): A Novel Method for Enhanced Generalization.** Motivated by the observed limitations of existing models, the final objective is to develop and validate a new method that leverages the strengths of Large Language Models (LLMs) and in-context learning to improve zero-shot cross-task generalization. X-ICM is designed to predict action sequences for unseen tasks by drawing on relevant in-context demonstrations from seen tasks, incorporating a novel dynamics-guided sample selection strategy.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for general-purpose robots capable of operating in open-world settings. By creating a rigorous benchmark and proposing a method to significantly improve performance on this benchmark, the authors aim to advance the state of the art in robotic manipulation and catalyze future research towards truly adaptable and intelligent robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces both a novel benchmark, **AGNOSTOS**, and a new method, **X-ICM**, to tackle zero-shot cross-task generalization in robotic manipulation.

#### 3.1. AGNOSTOS: Zero-shot Cross-task Generalization Benchmark

AGNOSTOS is built upon the RLBench simulation environment and is designed to provide a reproducible and rigorous evaluation of cross-task generalization.

- **Task Selection:** The benchmark uses a standard set of 18 widely used RLBench tasks for *training* (seen tasks). For *testing*, it introduces 23 held-out unseen tasks, specifically curated to have semantics disjoint from the training set.
- **Difficulty Levels:** The 23 unseen tasks are categorized into two difficulty levels to probe different aspects of generalization:
 - **Level-1 (13 tasks):** These tasks share partial semantic similarity with seen tasks, either in object types (e.g., 'cups') or motion primitives (e.g., 'put').
 - **Level-2 (10 tasks):** These tasks introduce entirely novel scenarios with no overlapping objects or actions, requiring broader compositional reasoning and semantic extrapolation.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** For each unseen task, three test runs, each consisting of 25 rollouts, are performed, and success rates are reported.
- **Benchmarked Models:** AGNOSTOS evaluates three broad categories of open-source VLA models:
 1. **Foundation VLA models:** Trained on large-scale real-world robotic demonstrations or built on multimodal LLMs/VLMs (e.g., OpenVLA, $\pi^0$, VoxPoser). These are typically fine-tuned on the 18 seen tasks to bridge embodiment gaps.
 2. **Human-video VLA models:** Pre-trained on large-scale human action videos (e.g., R3M, D4R) and then fine-tuned on the 18 seen tasks.
 3. **In-domain VLA models:** Trained from scratch on RLBench's 18 seen tasks with task-specific architectures (e.g., PerAct, RVT). These serve as baselines without domain mismatch.

#### 3.2. X-ICM: Cross-task In-context Manipulation Method

X-ICM is a novel approach that leverages in-context learning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for zero-shot cross-task generalization. It consists of two main modules:

1. **Dynamics-guided Sample Selection Module:**
 - **Motivation:** The core idea is that selecting *relevant* in-context demonstrations from seen tasks is crucial for LLMs to generate effective action plans for unseen tasks. Random selection often fails.
 - **Diffusion-based Dynamics Modeling:** A dynamics diffusion model (initialized from InstructPix2Pix) is trained on all 3,600 seen demonstrations. This model takes an initial visual observation and language description as input and predicts the *final* visual observation of the completed task. By predicting the future state, the model learns to capture the inherent dynamics or "flow" of the task.
 - **Dynamics-guided Retrieving Stage:** Once trained, this diffusion model is used to extract a multi-modal "dynamic feature" (combining visual features of the predicted target observation and textual features of the language description) for each seen demonstration and for the target unseen task. Cosine similarity between these dynamic features is computed to measure cross-task dynamics similarity. The top-K (K=18 in experiments) seen demonstrations with the highest similarity scores are retrieved to serve as in-context examples.

2. **Cross-task In-context Prediction Module:**
 - **Textualizing Key Information:** For each selected seen demonstration and the target unseen task, key information is textualized. This includes:
 - 3D positions of object centers: Detected using GroundingDINO, depth information, and normalized into a 100x100x100 grid coordinate system.
 - Key-action sequence: Selects actions when gripper state changes or joint velocities are near zero, representing end-effector 7-dimensional states (3D position, roll-pitch-yaw angles, gripper open/close). End-effector positions are also normalized to the grid, and angles are discretized.
 - **Constructing In-context Prompt:** A prompt is constructed for the LLM, concatenating:
 - A system prompt providing instructions to the robot.
 - The textualized K selected seen demonstrations (ordered by descending similarity score), formatted as `[language_description, {object_context}] -> [action_sequence]`.
 - The textualized target unseen task, formatted as `[language_description, {object_context}] ->`.
 - **LLM Action Prediction:** An off-the-shelf LLM (Qwen2.5-Instruct 7B/72B) then processes this prompt to predict the key-action sequence for the unseen task.

#### 3.3. Real-world Experiments

To demonstrate practical applicability, X-ICM is tested on five real-world manipulation tasks using an xArm7 robot. For each task, 20 demonstrations are collected. Zero-shot cross-task generalization is evaluated by using demonstrations from four tasks as in-context prompts to solve the fifth, unseen task. Ground-truth object positions are used in simulation for a fair comparison with baselines, while the real-world setup relies on visual perception for object localization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings from its systematic evaluations on AGNOSTOS and real-world experiments:

1. **Significant Limitations of Existing VLA Models:** The comprehensive benchmarking on AGNOSTOS reveals that current VLA models, despite being trained on diverse and large-scale datasets, **struggle significantly with zero-shot cross-task generalization**.
 - None of the existing models (including sophisticated foundation models like $\pi^0$ and VoxPoser, human-video pre-trained models, and in-domain trained models) generalize effectively to the unseen tasks.
 - All prior models completely fail (0% success rate) on at least eight of the 23 unseen tasks, indicating a severe lack of generalization capability to truly novel scenarios. This underscores the difficulty of Level-2 tasks in particular, where many models score near zero.
 - The highest-performing baseline, $\pi^0$, achieved an average success rate of 17.5% across all unseen tasks, with VoxPoser at 15.6%.

2. **X-ICM Achieves Superior Zero-shot Cross-task Generalization:** The proposed X-ICM method demonstrably outperforms all existing VLA models on the AGNOSTOS benchmark.
 - **X-ICM (7B)** achieved an average success rate of **23.5%**, improving over $\pi^0$ by 6.0%.
 - **X-ICM (72B)** achieved an even higher average success rate of **30.1%**, marking an improvement of 7.9% over VoxPoser and 12.6% over $\pi^0$.
 - Notably, X-ICM (72B) succeeded on *all 23 unseen tasks* (i.e., did not have 0% success on any task), demonstrating a much broader generalization capability compared to baselines, which failed on multiple tasks.
 - Performance gains were observed across both Level-1 (e.g., X-ICM 72B at 37.6% vs. $\pi^0$ at 21.7%) and Level-2 tasks (e.g., X-ICM 72B at 20.3% vs. SAM2Act at 15.9%), indicating robustness across varying degrees of novelty.

3. **Effectiveness of Dynamics-guided Sample Selection:** Ablation studies confirmed the crucial role of the dynamics-guided sample selection module.
 - X-ICM (72B) with the selection module achieved 30.1% overall success, while disabling it (using random sampling, denoted as "w/o sel") resulted in a significant drop to 25.2%. This validates the strategy's ability to identify relevant demonstrations, thereby enhancing the LLM's cross-task generalization.
 - Further analysis showed that dynamic features derived from predicting the *target* observation (f\_vis.out) were most effective for similarity-based retrieval.

4. **Impact of LLM Backbone and Number of Demonstrations:**
 - **LLM Size:** Larger LLM backbones generally lead to better performance, with X-ICM (72B) outperforming X-ICM (7B) and X-ICM (14B), although the gains showed diminishing returns at the largest scale.
 - **Number of Samples:** Performance improved rapidly as the number of in-context demonstrations increased from 1 to 12, then plateaued, suggesting an optimal range beyond which additional samples might introduce noise.
 - **LLM Choice:** Significant variability in performance was observed across different 7B/8B LLM backbones, emphasizing the importance of selecting a powerful and well-aligned LLM.

5. **Promising Real-world Applicability:** X-ICM demonstrated strong zero-shot cross-task performance in real-world experiments with an xArm7 robot on five diverse manipulation tasks (e.g., "put block into bin," "stack cups"). Success rates ranged from 10% to 70%, showcasing the method's ability to transfer learned concepts to physical robots in novel scenarios without fine-tuning on the target task. Evaluation on a long-horizon task ("clean the table") also showed some success (5% overall success via sub-tasks), highlighting both the potential and the cumulative challenges of such tasks in a zero-shot cross-task setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the pursuit of general-purpose robotic manipulation, addressing a critical, yet previously underexplored, aspect of VLA models: zero-shot cross-task generalization. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Setting a New Standard for Generalization Evaluation:** The introduction of **AGNOSTOS** provides the scientific community with the **first comprehensive and reproducible benchmark** specifically designed to evaluate zero-shot cross-task generalization. By curating 23 unseen tasks across two distinct difficulty levels, AGNOSTOS offers a rigorous testbed that pushes the limits of current VLA models beyond mere within-task variations. This benchmark fills a crucial gap, enabling standardized comparisons and driving focused research toward truly adaptable robotic systems. The open-source nature of AGNOSTOS and its associated code will likely foster rapid advancements in the field.

2. **Unveiling Fundamental Limitations of Current VLA Models:** The systematic evaluation on AGNOSTOS conclusively demonstrates that even the most advanced VLA models, despite their pre-training on vast datasets, fundamentally struggle with genuinely novel tasks. This finding is crucial as it clarifies where current approaches fall short, providing clear motivation and direction for future research efforts. It highlights that simply scaling data or model parameters is insufficient for achieving robust cross-task generalization.

3. **Pioneering a Novel Approach for Cross-task Generalization:** The proposed **X-ICM** method offers a compelling and effective solution to enhance zero-shot cross-task generalization. By cleverly integrating the in-context learning capabilities of LLMs with a novel **dynamics-guided sample selection strategy**, X-ICM significantly outperforms state-of-the-art VLA models. This methodology opens new avenues for leveraging LLMs in robotics, particularly by demonstrating how carefully selected demonstrations can unlock LLMs' abilities to reason about unseen task dynamics. The dynamics diffusion model, which learns to predict future observations, is a key innovation in effectively bridging the gap between seen and unseen task characteristics.

4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Novel Tasks:** The promising results from real-world experiments, albeit on a limited set of tasks, demonstrate X-ICM's potential for practical deployment. Its ability to perform zero-shot cross-task manipulation on a physical robot validates the approach's real-world applicability and scalability beyond simulation. This is a crucial step towards robots that can adapt on-the-fly to novel instructions in unstructured environments.

5. **Inspiring Future Research Directions:** The work explicitly discusses limitations and points to rich areas for future investigation. These include:
 - **Enhanced Multi-modal Reasoning:** Integrating richer visual context beyond textualized object information into LLMs to improve generalization to novel semantics.
 - **Abstract Concepts and Generalizable Primitives:** Leveraging concepts like object trajectories as a bridge between MLLMs' perception and dynamic action sequence prediction to reduce cross-task generalization complexity.
 - **Scaling to Diverse Embodiments:** Expanding X-ICM's versatility across varied robotic platforms and sensor configurations.
 - **Long-horizon Tasks:** Addressing the compounding challenges of zero-shot cross-task generalization in long-horizon tasks, where sub-task success is sequential.

In conclusion, "Exploring the Limits of Vision-Language-Action Manipulation in Cross-task Generalization" provides both a much-needed diagnostic tool (AGNOSTOS) and a powerful new method (X-ICM) that significantly advances the field. It clarifies the current limitations of VLA models, offers a concrete path forward, and will undoubtedly inspire a new wave of research focused on building truly general-purpose robots capable of operating in the open, unpredictable real world.
