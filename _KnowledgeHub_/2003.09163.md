# Detection in Crowded Scenes: One Proposal, Multiple Predictions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and MEGVII Technology introduce a "Multiple Instance Prediction" (MIP) paradigm for object detection, enabling each proposal to predict a set of correlated instances rather than a single one, which improves detection in crowded scenes. This approach, incorporating an Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) Loss and Set NMS, boosts Averaged Precision by 4.9% on the CrowdHuman dataset and reduces the log-average Miss Rate by 1.0% on CityPersons.

## Problem

- Traditional proposal-based object detectors struggle to distinguish highly overlapped instances in crowded scenes due to their similar feature representations.
- The standard Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) algorithm often mistakenly suppresses valid, highly overlapping detections, leading to missed instances in dense environments.
- Existing advanced NMS strategies or specialized loss functions for crowded scenes often add complexity but do not fully resolve the problem of recalling heavily occluded objects.

## Method

- The **Multiple Instance Prediction (MIP)** scheme allows each proposal box to predict a set of K correlated instances instead of a single instance, simplifying the learning task in crowded scenarios.
- A novel ==Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) Loss== supervises the prediction of instance sets by finding an optimal one-to-one matching between predicted and ground-truth sets within each proposal.
- **Set NMS**, a modified ==Non-Maximum Suppression== algorithm, prevents valid detections originating from the *same* initial proposal from being suppressed, while still managing inter-proposal duplicates effectively.

## Results

- Achieved a **4.9%** increase in Averaged Precision (AP) and a **1.5%** improvement in log-average Miss Rate (MR^-2) on the heavily crowded CrowdHuman dataset compared to the FPN baseline.
- Demonstrated improved recall for crowded objects by **8.9%** on CrowdHuman and **29.6%** on CityPersons, effectively addressing the issue of missed instances due to high overlap.
- Showed generalizability and robustness with a **1.0%** AP gain on the general COCO dataset and compatibility across various detector architectures, including FPN, Cascade R-CNN, and RetinaNet.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The fundamental challenge in crowded scene detection arises from the 'one proposal, one prediction' paradigm, which forces models to differentiate highly similar features for individual, overlapping objects.
> - Allowing proposals to predict a *set* of instances simplifies the learning process, as nearby proposals can then be trained to infer the same set of objects, intrinsically handling high overlap.
> - Standard NMS is a major bottleneck in crowded scenarios, necessitating a modified NMS (Set NMS) to preserve multiple valid detections that originate from a single proposal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object detection systems based on proposal-based frameworks have achieved considerable success across various datasets, including COCO and PASCAL VOC. These frameworks generally involve a two-step pipeline: first, generating an overcomplete set of object proposals (either handcrafted, like predefined anchors, or learned, like Region Proposal Networks); and second, predicting a single instance (including its confidence score and refined location) corresponding to each proposal box. Post-processing methods, such as Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), are then typically employed to eliminate redundant predictions.

Despite their general effectiveness, these traditional proposal-based approaches encounter challenges in crowded scenes where instances frequently overlap. Common failure modes include instances being mistakenly suppressed by NMS due to their proximity or similar features, leading to missed detections. This issue is particularly pronounced when instances exhibit high overlap, making it difficult for detectors to generate distinct predictions for highly similar proposals.

Prior research has attempted to address these limitations through several avenues:
- **Advanced NMS strategies**: Modifications like Soft-NMS or neural network-based NMS aim to improve duplicate removal in crowded contexts, but often increase complexity or may not always be effective in distinguishing heavily overlapping boxes.
- **Specialized loss functions**: Losses such as Repulsion Loss or Aggregation Loss have been introduced to improve the quality of crowded detections by adding penalties or enforcing certain proposal behaviors. However, these often retain traditional NMS, limiting their ability to recall highly overlapped instances.
- **Re-scoring methods**: Approaches like RelationNet attempt to encourage a one-to-one relationship between proposals and ground truths to reduce the reliance on NMS. However, these can struggle in highly crowded environments where proposals are very dense and features are highly similar, leading to ambiguity in distinguishing individuals.
- **Part-based detectors**: These are specialized for certain object types, like pedestrians, and often do not generalize well to varied object sizes and shapes.

This paper positions itself by arguing that the fundamental difficulty in crowded scenes stems from the paradigm of predicting only one instance per proposal box. It proposes a novel approach that deviates from this traditional paradigm, aiming to offer a more robust and effective solution that is applicable to existing proposal-based frameworks without introducing significant complexity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a proposal-based object detector capable of effectively detecting highly-overlapped instances in crowded scenes. The authors identify two main motivations for this objective and the proposed approach:

1. **Difficulty in distinguishing highly overlapped instances**: In crowded scenarios, multiple objects, such as people standing close together, often occupy nearly identical spatial regions in an image. Consequently, the feature representations extracted from proposal boxes encompassing these objects can be very similar. Traditional proposal-based detectors are designed to associate each proposal with a *single* ground-truth instance. This forces the network to generate distinct predictions for proposals that share highly similar features, which is an intrinsically difficult learning task. The authors illustrate this with an example (Figure 2a) where multiple overlapping proposals are expected to predict different single instances despite their feature similarity.

2. **Mistaken suppression by Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)**: NMS is a crucial post-processing step in most object detection pipelines, designed to remove redundant bounding box predictions. It operates by iteratively selecting the prediction with the highest confidence score and suppressing (discarding) other predictions that heavily overlap with it (e.g., IoU > a threshold). In crowded scenes, valid instances are often in close proximity and heavily overlap. When a ground truth instance is heavily occluded by another, its associated proposal and subsequent prediction may have a high Intersection-over-Union (IoU) with a prediction corresponding to another, nearby instance. This can lead to the legitimate detection of one instance being erroneously suppressed by NMS, resulting in missed detections (Figure 1a). Current NMS variants attempt to mitigate this but often introduce complexity or fail to distinguish truly distinct but overlapping objects.

Motivated by these challenges, the authors propose a paradigm shift: instead of each proposal predicting a single instance, it should predict a *set* of correlated instances that might be highly overlapped within its region. This new scheme is expected to simplify the learning process, as nearby proposals can then be trained to infer the same set of instances, rather than needing to differentiate individual, highly similar objects. This approach aims to improve recall for crowded instances without substantially increasing false positives or computational cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method is to enable each proposal box to predict a set of correlated instances rather than a single one. This "Multiple Instance Prediction" (MIP) scheme is augmented with a new loss function (EMD Loss) and a modified post-processing algorithm (Set NMS). An optional Refinement Module is also introduced to address potential false positives.

#### 3.1. Multiple Instance Prediction (MIP)

- **Instance Set Prediction**: For each proposal box `b_i`, instead of predicting a single `(class, location)` pair, the system predicts a set of `K` instances: `P(b_i) = {(c_i^(1), l_i^(1)), ..., (c_i^(K), l_i^(K))}`. `K` is a predefined constant representing the maximum cardinality of ground-truth instances (`G(b_i)`) that significantly overlap with `b_i` (defined by an IoU threshold `θ`). If fewer than `K` instances overlap, dummy boxes (classified as background with no regression loss) fill the remaining slots. This is implemented by adding `K` parallel prediction branches to the existing detection head of proposal-based frameworks. The authors primarily set `K=2` for their experiments, finding it sufficient for most crowded scenarios in the datasets.

#### 3.2. EMD Loss

- **Earth Mover's Distance Loss**: To supervise the prediction of a *set* of instances, a novel Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) loss function is introduced. This loss minimizes the "distance" between the predicted set `P(b_i)` and the target ground-truth set `G(b_i)`. The EMD loss is defined as:
 `L(b_i) = min_(π∈Π) Σ_(k=1)^K [L_cls(c_i^(k), g_πk) + L_reg(l_i^(k), g_πk)]`
 where `π` is a permutation matching predicted instances to ground-truth instances, `g_πk` is the `πk`-th ground-truth box in `G(b_i)`, `L_cls` is the classification loss, and `L_reg` is the bounding box regression loss. This formulation searches for the optimal one-to-one matching between predicted and ground-truth instances within the set, minimizing the overall loss. This approach generalizes traditional single-instance prediction loss when `K=1`.

#### 3.3. Set NMS

- **Modified Non-Maximum Suppression**: Since a single proposal can now generate multiple detections, applying naive NMS could lead to valid detections from the *same* proposal being suppressed. Set NMS is a modification to the standard NMS pipeline. Before a box `A` suppresses a box `B` based on their overlap, Set NMS includes an additional check: if `A` and `B` originate from the *same initial proposal box*, suppression is skipped. This prevents intra-proposal duplicates from being mistakenly eliminated, while still allowing inter-proposal duplicates (predictions for the same ground truth from different proposals) to be handled by the NMS mechanism.

#### 3.4. Refinement Module (RM)

- **Optional Post-Processing Module**: To mitigate a potential increase in false positives due to multiple predictions per proposal, an optional Refinement Module is proposed. This module takes the initial set of predictions from a proposal as input, combines them with the proposal's feature, and performs a second round of prediction. Its purpose is to correct potentially false predictions generated in the first stage. It concatenates feature information with the box coordinates to produce refined outputs.

#### 3.5. Network Architecture Integration

The proposed method is designed to be applicable to most state-of-the-art proposal-based detectors. For evaluation, the authors chose an FPN (Feature Pyramid Network) with a ResNet-50 backbone as the baseline. The MIP scheme, EMD Loss, and Set NMS are integrated into the RCNN (or RoI) branch of FPN, which is responsible for instance prediction. The only extra hyper-parameter introduced is `K` (set to 2), which determines the maximum number of instances predicted per proposal. The refinement module is an optional addition to this architecture. The paper also demonstrates its applicability to Cascade R-CNN and one-stage detectors like RetinaNet.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across three datasets with varying degrees of crowdedness: CrowdHuman (heavily crowded), CityPersons (moderately crowded), and COCO (slightly crowded/general object detection).

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

- **Averaged Precision (AP)**: Standard metric reflecting both precision and recall. Higher AP indicates better performance.
- **MR^-2 (log-average Miss Rate on FPPI in [10^-2, 100])**: Commonly used in pedestrian detection, highly sensitive to false positives (FPs). Lower MR^-2 indicates better performance.
- **Jaccard Index (JI)**: Evaluates the counting ability of a detector by comparing the overlap between the prediction set and ground truths, often reported at the best confidence threshold. Higher JI indicates better performance.

#### 4.2. Experiments on CrowdHuman Dataset

CrowdHuman is a heavily crowded dataset with an average of 22.64 objects and 2.40 overlaps per image (IoU > 0.5).

- **Ablation Study (Table 2)**:
 - The FPN baseline achieved 85.8% AP, 42.9% MR^-2, and 79.8% JI.
 - Integrating Multiple Instance Prediction (MIP) with EMD Loss alone improved AP to 87.4% and JI to 80.8%, with MR^-2 remaining similar (42.8%). This suggests improved recall without a significant increase in FPs.
 - Adding Set NMS in conjunction with MIP further boosted AP to 90.3% and JI to 82.0%, while also improving MR^-2 to 42.2%. This indicates the combined effect of predicting multiple instances and correctly handling their suppression is significant.
 - Including the optional Refinement Module (RM) provided additional gains, reaching 90.7% AP, 41.4% MR^-2, and 82.3% JI. The RM primarily contributed to reducing false positives, as indicated by the MR^-2 improvement.

- **Comparisons with various NMS strategies (Table 3)**:
 - Varying the IoU threshold for naive NMS showed trade-offs: a higher threshold (0.6) could increase AP (88.1%) but worsened MR^-2 (45.4%), indicating more FPs.
 - Soft-NMS showed improvements in AP (88.2%) but no change in MR^-2 or JI compared to baseline NMS with a 0.5 IoU threshold.
 - The proposed method consistently outperformed all NMS variants across all metrics, achieving 90.7% AP, 41.4% MR^-2, and 82.3% JI with the Refinement Module.

- **Comparisons with previous crowded detection methods (Table 4)**:
 - Re-implementations of RelationNet and GossipNet, two approaches for crowded detection, performed worse than the FPN baseline on CrowdHuman. RelationNet achieved 81.6% AP and 48.2% MR^-2, while GossipNet yielded 80.4% AP and 49.4% MR^-2. This suggests these methods struggled with the high density and feature similarity in CrowdHuman, leading to many false positives.
 - The proposed method achieved 90.7% AP and 41.4% MR^-2, significantly outperforming these previous works.
 - When applied to a Cascade R-CNN baseline, the method further improved AP from 86.2% to 90.6% and MR^-2 from 40.2% to 38.7%, demonstrating its compatibility and effectiveness with stronger baselines.

- **Analysis on Recalls (Table 5)**:
 - The method significantly improved recall for crowded objects on CrowdHuman by 8.9% (from 54.4% for FPN baseline to 63.3% for the proposed method based on JI optimized thresholds, the values in table 5 are different due to using percentage of total ground truths as recall, actual recall improved from 64.9% to 74.6% for crowded objects and 80.8% to 83.7% for total objects). It also moderately improved recall for uncrowded objects. This confirms the method's effectiveness in detecting difficult, overlapped instances.

#### 4.3. Experiments on CityPersons Dataset

CityPersons is a moderately crowded pedestrian detection dataset with 6.47 objects and 0.32 overlaps per image.

- The proposed method improved AP by 0.9% (from 95.2% to 96.1%) and MR^-2 by 1.0% (from 11.7% to 10.7%) over the FPN baseline.
- Recall analysis (Table 6) showed a substantial improvement in crowded object recall (from 59.3% to 88.9%), reinforcing the method's capability in crowded scenarios.

#### 4.4. Experiments on COCO Dataset

COCO is a general object detection dataset with low crowdedness (9.34 objects and 0.015 overlaps per image).

- The method achieved moderate improvements on COCO, with a 1.0% increase in AP (from 37.5% to 38.5%) over the FPN baseline.
- Notably, large objects (AP_L) showed the most significant improvement, increasing from 48.6% to 50.1%, suggesting that even in less crowded settings, the handling of potential overlaps is beneficial.
- This demonstrates the method's robustness and generalization ability to multi-class and less crowded scenes without degrading performance.

#### 4.5. Additional Experiments (Appendix)

- **Backbone Scaling**: Replacing ResNet-50 with SEResNeXt101 further boosted mJI on the CrowdHuman test set from 76.60% (with ResNet-50) to 77.74%, outperforming challenge baselines.
- **Number of Heads (K)**: Experiments showed that K=2 yielded optimal results (90.7% AP, 41.4% MR^-2, 82.3% JI) compared to K=1 (equivalent to baseline) or K=3, indicating K=2 is sufficient for the target datasets.
- **One-Stage Detector**: The method was applied to RetinaNet, a one-stage detector, resulting in improvements from 82.0% to 82.7% AP and 56.3% to 54.7% MR^-2 on CrowdHuman, indicating its applicability beyond two-stage frameworks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a modification to proposal-based object detectors that addresses a fundamental limitation in detecting highly overlapped instances in crowded scenes. By shifting from a single-instance-per-proposal paradigm to a multiple-instance prediction scheme, the proposed method offers a direct approach to handling object ambiguity and NMS-related suppression errors in dense environments.

The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Improved Performance in Crowded Scenes**: The method demonstrates notable improvements (e.g., 4.9% AP gain on CrowdHuman and 1.0% MR^-2 improvement on CityPersons), directly addressing a long-standing challenge in object detection. This is particularly impactful for applications in surveillance, autonomous driving, and crowd analysis, where accurately detecting individuals in dense groups is critical.
- **Generalizability and Robustness**: The consistent performance gains across datasets with varying degrees of crowdedness (from heavily crowded CrowdHuman to moderately crowded CityPersons and general COCO) suggest the method is robust. It not only excels in its target crowded scenarios but also maintains or improves performance in less crowded settings, indicating it does not negatively impact isolated object detection.
- **Simplicity and Flexibility**: The proposed approach is described as simple and almost cost-free, primarily involving the addition of prediction branches and the EMD loss. It is designed to be applicable to most existing state-of-the-art proposal-based detectors (two-stage like FPN and Cascade R-CNN, and one-stage like RetinaNet), offering a modular enhancement without requiring a complete overhaul of established frameworks. This ease of integration could accelerate its adoption in practical systems.
- **Addresses Core Limitations**: By rethinking the basic premise of "one proposal, one prediction," the work tackles the root causes of missed detections in crowded scenes—the difficulty of distinguishing highly similar features and the limitations of NMS. The EMD Loss facilitates learning sets of instances, and Set NMS prevents unwarranted suppression of valid, highly overlapping detections.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The success of the multiple instance prediction paradigm could inspire further research into set-based prediction mechanisms in other computer vision tasks beyond bounding box detection. It provides a new perspective on how object detectors can be designed to intrinsically handle object relationships and densities.

In conclusion, this work provides an effective and flexible solution for a critical problem in object detection, offering substantial performance improvements in challenging crowded environments while maintaining compatibility with current architectural designs.
