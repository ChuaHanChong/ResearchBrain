# MoSA: Motion-constrained Stress Adaptation for Mitigating Real-to-Sim Gap in Continuum Dynamics via Learning Residual Anisotropy

> [!summary] Summary
> The MoSA framework improves real-to-sim dynamics learning for deformable objects by augmenting isotropic physics models with learned residual anisotropy and heterogeneity, guided by motion-constrained optimization. It achieved superior accuracy on synthetic data and a mean PSNR of 31.35 on real-world generalization, enabling higher success rates in robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Classical physics-based system identification struggles with real-world objects exhibiting mild anisotropy and heterogeneity, leading to a "real-to-sim" gap due to simplified isotropic material models.
- End-to-end neural network models for dynamics often discard strong physical priors, resulting in poor data efficiency, unstable training, and overfitting when learning complex dynamics from scratch.
- Learning dynamics from visual observations typically relies on indirect and ill-conditioned image reconstruction losses, which provide ambiguous supervision and hinder stable optimization.

## Method

- A structured, physics-informed neural network progressively adapts an isotropic constitutive prior by learning bounded residual stress corrections, including pre-linear, microplane-based redistribution, and post-linear terms, to capture mild anisotropy.
- Heterogeneity is modeled by modulating a global material parameter with a continuous implicit field, η(x)\eta(x)η(x), providing local spatial variations in material properties.
- A motion-constrained optimization strategy uses higher-order supervision derived from dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions, incorporating derivative-based regularization (flow, scale, and rotation losses) in addition to image/mask rendering losses.

## Results

- MoSA achieved the lowest Chamfer Distance (**13.5**) and Earth Mover Distance (**8.8**) on the synthetic PAC-NeRF dataset for 3D trajectory prediction, demonstrating superior accuracy over baselines.
- On a real-world dataset, MoSA consistently outperformed all baselines in generalization, achieving a mean PSNR of **31.35** and SSIM of **92.3**, compared to GIC's **30.02** PSNR and **91.5** SSIM.
- The learned dynamics from MoSA enabled robot policies to achieve substantially higher real-world success rates in manipulation tasks (e.g., **68%** for elastic-rabbit placement vs. **42%** for baselines), demonstrating practical utility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted learning of residual continuum effects (anisotropy and heterogeneity) on top of a physics prior is more effective for bridging the real-to-sim gap than purely physics-based or purely data-driven approaches.
> - Higher-order motion constraints derived from dynamic 3D reconstructions provide more direct and stable supervision for dynamics learning from videos compared to indirect image-level reconstruction losses.
> - Decomposing anisotropic stress adaptation into structured, progressive corrections (pre-linear, microplane-based redistribution, post-linear) improves learning stability, efficiency, and offers physical interpretability for the learned residual dynamics.
