# FD-VLA: Force-Distilled Vision-Language-Action Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FD-VLA introduces a force-distilled vision-language-action model that enables robots to perform contact-rich manipulation without requiring physical force sensors during inference, by learning force representations from visual and robot state inputs. This approach achieved a mean success rate of 61.1% across various contact-rich tasks, outperforming existing vision-language-only and raw force-augmented baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-only Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack the precision and robustness needed for contact-rich manipulation tasks due to their inability to infer subtle physical interactions from visual data alone.
- Directly integrating physical force sensors into VLA frameworks is costly, fragile, and risks disrupting the carefully aligned vision-language semantics of pre-trained large language models.
- Existing methods for force integration in VLAs face challenges such as modality misalignment, training instability, increased inference complexity, or limited fine-grained force-vision-state interactions due to late fusion strategies.

## Method

- The **FD-VLA** framework integrates force awareness by ==distilling force representations== from visual observations and robot proprioceptive states, eliminating the need for physical force sensors during inference.
- A ==Force Distillation Module (FDM)== predicts a latent force token from visual and state inputs, supervised by actual raw force signals during training via an ==L2 loss==.
- A ==directional attention masking== mechanism is employed within the VLA's transformer layers to robustly fuse the predicted force and robot state tokens with vision and language, while preserving the pre-trained semantic priors of the backbone VLM.

## Results

- FD-VLA achieved the highest mean success rate of **61.1%** across three real-world contact-rich tasks (Clean Whiteboard, Press Emergency Button, Insert Plug) compared to baselines.
- The proposed model significantly outperformed vision-language-only baselines (e.g., SmolVLA at **23.3%**) and those augmented with direct raw force input (e.g., SmolVLA with raw force at **38.9%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Force Distillation Module's architecture and the use of a learnable token contributed to a substantial improvement in task success rates, moving from **38.9%** without FDM to **61.1%** with the full FD-VLA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Force information can be effectively distilled from readily available visual and robot state inputs, enabling robust force-aware manipulation without the cost and complexity of physical sensors at inference.
> - Employing a learnable query token within the force distillation module is more effective at capturing task-relevant force representations than directly encoding raw, potentially noisy, force measurements.
> - A selective, directional attention mechanism facilitates early and fine-grained cross-modal fusion of force, vision, and state within a pre-trained VLM, enhancing perception-action coupling without degrading its core vision-language capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is positioned within the evolving field of robot imitation learning, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures. VLA models, such as RT-1, RT-2, Octo, and OpenVLA, have gained prominence by integrating large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and visual observations for generating low-level robot commands. These models benefit from the semantic priors and generalization capabilities of their VLM backbones, facilitating scalable manipulation skills across various tasks.

However, a known limitation of vision-only robot policies, including many VLA implementations, is their susceptibility to real-world complexities such as occlusions, lighting variations, depth ambiguities, and nuanced contact effects. These factors can degrade performance in tasks requiring precise physical interaction. This highlights a need for multimodal VLAs that can integrate additional sensory signals beyond vision and language.

Among these additional modalities, force and tactile sensing are considered particularly critical for contact-rich manipulation. These modalities provide direct information about contact dynamics, compliance, and physical interactions, which are often not discernible from visual data alone. Recent research has started to incorporate force/tactile signals into VLA frameworks. Examples include Tactile-VLA, which directly encodes raw force signals into latent embeddings, and ForceVLA, which injects a dedicated force token post-VLM via a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module. While these approaches aim to enrich visuomotor reasoning with contact awareness, they face challenges such as the risk of disturbing the VLM's pre-trained vision-language semantics, potential training instability, increased inference complexity, or limitations in fine-grained force-vision-state interactions due to late fusion.

This paper addresses these challenges by proposing a novel approach, Force-Distilled VLA (FD-VLA), which integrates force awareness without requiring physical force sensors during deployment. It leverages cross-modal knowledge distillation, a technique primarily used for aligning heterogeneous sensory streams, to implicitly learn force representations. While cross-modal distillation has been explored for other modalities like depth priors, its application to contact-rich manipulation, particularly for distilling force information, has received less attention, especially given the expense and fragility of force/tactile sensors. FD-VLA distinguishes itself by distilling force information from readily available visual and robot state inputs, offering a solution that is both hardware-agnostic at inference and designed to enhance cross-modal fusion without disrupting the VLM's core capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and data-efficient method for integrating force awareness into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically targeting contact-rich manipulation tasks. This integration is sought without the reliance on physical force sensors during the inference phase.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges observed in current VLA frameworks and the integration of force sensing:

1. **Limitations of Vision-Only VLAs:** While VLA models demonstrate strong generalization, their reliance on vision alone often proves insufficient for tasks requiring precise physical interaction. Occlusions, varied lighting conditions, depth ambiguities, and subtle contact dynamics are difficult to infer solely from visual inputs, leading to reduced robustness and precision in contact-rich scenarios.
2. **Importance of Force/Tactile Sensing:** Force and tactile signals provide direct, unambiguous information about physical contact, crucial for tasks involving pushing, inserting, or maintaining compliant contact. Integrating this modality can significantly enhance a robot's perception-action coupling.
3. **Challenges with Direct Force Integration:**
 - **Cost and Complexity of Hardware:** Physical force-torque sensors are often expensive and fragile, restricting their deployment across a wide range of robotic platforms. This limits the scalability and practical applicability of force-aware robotic systems.
 - **Impact on Pre-trained VLMs:** Directly incorporating an additional modality like raw force signals into a large, pre-trained VLM can be computationally expensive due to the need for extensive re-training. Furthermore, it risks disturbing the VLM's carefully aligned vision-language semantics, potentially leading to "catastrophic interference" and degradation of its existing capabilities.
 - **Noise in Raw Force Signals:** Raw force measurements from physical sensors are frequently subject to high-frequency artifacts and low-frequency drift. Such noisy data may not be optimal for direct policy learning, potentially hindering the model's ability to extract relevant contact information.
4. **Architectural Limitations of Existing Force-Integrated VLAs:**
 - **Early Fusion Risks (e.g., Tactile-VLA):** Approaches that directly encode and fuse raw force signals early in the VLM pipeline risk modality misalignment and interference with pre-trained visual-linguistic semantics.
 - **Late Fusion Limitations (e.g., ForceVLA):** Methods that inject force tokens late in the pipeline, often through complex modules like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), may increase training instability and inference complexity. More importantly, late fusion can limit the degree of fine-grained force-vision-state interactions, potentially reducing tight perception-action coupling and generalization.

Motivated by these challenges, the researchers proposed FD-VLA, guided by three core design principles:

1. **Leverage Predicted Force Tokens:** To overcome the issues with raw sensor data and hardware dependency, FD-VLA aims to distill force representations from visual observations and robot states. These *predicted* force tokens are used during inference, while actual force signals are only required during training for supervision.
2. **Maximize Feature-Level Cross-Modal Fusion and Alignment:** The framework is designed to facilitate robust cross-modal fusion and alignment of force, vision, and state information *before, within, and after* the VLM. This aims to preserve the semantic richness of the pre-trained VLM while effectively enriching action representations with physical context.
3. **Enable Sensor-Free Inference:** A key practical motivation is to ensure that the trained policy can be deployed on diverse robotic platforms that lack dedicated force sensors, thereby reducing hardware costs and complexity.

By addressing these points, the work aims to advance the capabilities of VLA models in real-world contact-rich manipulation, making them more robust, versatile, and accessible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FD-VLA framework is designed to integrate force awareness into a VLA model for contact-rich manipulation without requiring physical force sensors during inference. The core of its approach involves a Force Distillation Module (FDM) and a specific attention masking mechanism within a pre-trained VLM.

**A. Overall Framework Architecture:**

The system takes multimodal inputs: language instructions (`L`), visual observations (`V_t`), robot proprioceptive states (`S_t`), and, during training, raw force measurements (`F_t`).

1. **Input Encoding:**
 - Visual observations (`V_t`), language instructions (`L`), and robot states (`S_t`) are encoded into corresponding feature representations (`f_V_t`, `f_L`, `f_S_t`). This is achieved by leveraging the pre-trained VLM's tokenizer and vision encoder (for vision and language) and a lightweight single-layer MLP (for robot state). These embeddings are projected to match the hidden dimension (`D`) of the VLM.
2. **Force Distillation Module (FDM):**
 - Instead of directly feeding raw force, the FDM predicts a latent force representation (`f_pF_t`) from the visual observation and robot state. This module is active during both training and inference.
 - During training, the FDM's predicted force token is supervised by aligning it with a latent representation of *actual* force signals (`f_aF_t`), which are derived from raw sensor measurements via a lightweight MLP.
 - During inference, the FDM operates entirely without actual force measurements, producing `f_pF_t` solely from `f_V_t` and `f_S_t`.
3. **Multimodal Fusion into VLM:**
 - The encoded visual, language, state, and *predicted force* tokens (`f_V_t`, `f_L`, `f_S_t`, `f_pF_t`) are concatenated along the token dimension.
 - This combined set of tokens (`f_fs_t`) is then passed into a pre-trained VLM (specifically, SmolVLM-2 with SignLIP is used as the backbone).
4. **Action Expert:**
 - Following the VLM, an action expert, implemented as a transformer-based policy head with a conditional flow-matching decoder, maps the fused multimodal embeddings from the VLM to a sequence of robot actions (`a_t` to `a_t+H-1`).

**B. Force Distillation Module (FDM) Design:**

The FDM is a critical component for generating a compact, state-aware force representation. It operates on the principle that force information is inherently coupled with robot state and visual context.

1. **Actual Force Branch (Training Only):**
 - Raw force measurements from the robot's force/torque sensor are fed into a lightweight MLP. This MLP projects the raw force into an embedding space, yielding `f_aF_t`, which serves as the ground-truth supervisory signal.
2. **Prediction Branch:**
 - A learnable query token `p` (of dimension 1x_D_) is used to predict the latent force feature.
 - A context matrix `C_t` is formed by concatenating the query token `p`, the image embeddings `f_V_t`, and the robot state embeddings `f_S_t`.
 - A single-query multi-head attention mechanism is employed where `p` acts as the query, and `C_t` provides the keys and values. The output `Z` represents the aggregated information relevant to the force query.
 - A Feed-Forward Network (FFN) with a Layer Normalization (LN) layer processes `Z` and the query token `p` (via a residual connection) to produce the final predicted force token `f_pF_t`. This design allows `p` to self-condition, injecting learned contact priors directly.
3. **Distillation Loss:**
 - During training, the predicted force token `f_pF_t` is aligned with the actual force token `f_aF_t` using an L2 loss: `L_dist = ||f_pF_t - f_aF_t||^2_2`.

**C. Directional Attention Masking Mechanism:**

To integrate state and predicted force tokens into the frozen pre-trained VLM without corrupting its existing semantic priors, a directional attention masking mechanism (inspired by SmolVLA) is used.

1. **Stream Separation:** Input tokens are divided into two streams:
 - **Perceptual Stream (`X_percept`):** Comprises frozen visual (`f_V`) and language (`f_L`) tokens.
 - **Control Stream (`X_control`):** Comprises robot state (`f_S`) and predicted force (`f_pF`) tokens.
2. **Attention Mask (`M`):** A mask is applied within the VLM's transformer layers.
 - Perceptual tokens can only attend to other perceptual tokens, preserving their pre-trained vision-language alignment.
 - Control tokens can attend to all perceptual tokens (for contextual awareness) and also to previous control tokens (to model their temporal dependencies).
 - This one-way information flow ensures that the VLM's core knowledge remains intact while the control stream gains necessary contextual information.

**D. Action Expert and Overall Training Objective:**

1. **Action Expert:** The action expert `π_θ` is a transformer that predicts a chunk of actions `A_t = [a_t..., a_t+H-1]` based on the VLM's fused features `X_t`.
2. **Conditional Flow-Matching Objective (`L_τ`):** The action expert is trained using a conditional flow-matching loss, where it learns to output a velocity field `u(A_τ_t | A_t)` to map noisy actions `A_τ_t` back to the ground-truth actions `A_t`. This is suited for chunked action prediction.
3. **Overall Training Loss:** The total loss function `L` combines the policy learning loss (`L_τ`) and the force-distillation loss (`L_dist`), weighted by a hyperparameter `λ`: `L = L_τ(θ) + λL_dist`.

This methodology allows FD-VLA to leverage the semantic capabilities of large VLMs while robustly integrating force awareness through a distilled, sensor-free approach, facilitating effective perception-action coupling in contact-rich tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated FD-VLA against several baselines on real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, focusing on success rates and generalization capabilities.

**A. Experimental Setup:**

- **Hardware:** A UR5e robotic arm was used as the manipulation platform. Visual inputs were captured by an Azure Kinect camera (main view) and a RealSense D405 camera (gripper view).
- **Data Collection:** Expert demonstrations (50 per task) were collected via teleoperation using a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse. Raw force information was recorded from the UR5e's internal force sensor during data collection.
- **Evaluation:** Each task was evaluated over 30 independent test trials, with success criteria defined specifically for each task (e.g., complete erasure for whiteboard, full depression for button, full seating for plug).

**B. Experimental Tasks:**

Three contact-rich manipulation tasks were used for evaluation:

1. **Clean Whiteboard:** Requires continuous, consistent contact and motion planning across a surface.
2. **Press Emergency Button:** Demands precise vertical pressing with sufficient force to overcome resistance and spring tension.
3. **Insert Plug:** Involves precise positional alignment and regulated force application during engagement.

**C. Main Results (Performance Comparison):**

FD-VLA was benchmarked against three policies: DP3, π0, and SmolVLA. For SmolVLA and π0, variants both with and without raw force inputs were evaluated.

**D. Ablation Study:**

An ablation study was conducted on the Force Distillation Module (FDM) using three variants:

1. **Without FDM (MLP for force encoding):** A direct MLP layer was used to encode force. This variant yielded a mean success rate of 38.9%.
2. **FDM with force token (learnable token replaced by encoded ground-truth force):** The FDM architecture was used, but the learnable query token was replaced with the actual force token (encoded by an MLP). This resulted in a mean success rate of 51.1%.
3. **FDM with learnable token (FD-VLA's proposed method):** This is the full FD-VLA model, achieving a mean success rate of 61.1%.

- **Findings from Ablation:**
 - The introduction of the FDM (FDM with force token vs. Without FDM) led to a notable improvement in success rate (51.1% vs 38.9%), indicating the benefit of deep cross-modal interactions in early fusion provided by the FDM architecture.
 - The "FDM with learnable token" variant (FD-VLA) outperformed the "FDM with force token" (61.1% vs 51.1%). This result suggested that a learnable token for distilling force information is more effective than directly using latent features encoded from raw ground-truth force measurements. This highlights that learning a denoised, task-relevant proxy for force (via the learnable token) can mitigate the challenges of noisy, high-dimensional raw signals.

**E. Model Generalization (Visual):**

The model's visual generalization ability was assessed in two scenarios:

1. **Novel Background:** Scenes with different layouts and textures than those used during training.
2. **Visual Perturbations:** Changes in lighting, contrast, and color distributions.

- **Findings:** Qualitative results indicated that FD-VLA maintained robust task performance across both scenarios. The robot appeared to leverage structural cues instead of overfitting to textures in novel backgrounds, and it adapted smoothly to illumination and appearance changes under photometric perturbations. These observations suggested the model's capacity to extract task-relevant features that remain invariant to superficial visual shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FD-VLA framework introduces a novel approach to integrate force awareness into Vision-Language-Action models, offering several contributions and potential impacts on robotic manipulation research and deployment:

1. **Addressing Hardware Dependency and Cost:** A key significance of FD-VLA is its ability to enable force-aware reasoning without requiring physical force-torque sensors during inference. This addresses a major practical limitation in robotics, as force sensors are often expensive, fragile, and complex to integrate across diverse platforms. By eliminating this hardware dependency, FD-VLA reduces the overall cost and complexity of deploying advanced manipulation systems, potentially broadening the applicability of VLA models in real-world settings.
2. **Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate that FD-VLA consistently achieves higher success rates in contact-rich manipulation tasks compared to vision-language-only baselines and even those that directly incorporate raw force measurements. This highlights the effectiveness of the force distillation approach in providing robust and precise control, which is crucial for tasks requiring delicate physical interaction.
3. **Improved Cross-Modal Fusion and Robustness:** The Force Distillation Module (FDM) and the directional attention masking mechanism contribute to a more effective cross-modal fusion. The FDM distills a task-relevant and denoised force representation from visual and robot state inputs, serving as a cleaner signal for policy learning than raw, potentially noisy, sensor data. This early, integrated force-vision-state fusion, prior to the VLM, is suggested to improve cross-modal alignment and enhance the overall perception-action robustness.
4. **Preservation of VLM Semantics:** By utilizing a frozen pre-trained VLM and a directional attention masking strategy, FD-VLA ensures that the integration of force and state information does not interfere with or degrade the VLM's existing vision-language semantic representations. This allows the framework to leverage the extensive pre-trained knowledge of VLMs while selectively enhancing it with physical interaction cues, minimizing the need for costly and disruptive re-training.
5. **Advancement in Multimodal Robot Learning:** This work provides a method for implicitly learning and integrating difficult-to-sense modalities like force into VLA frameworks. This approach could be extended to other modalities where direct sensing is challenging or undesirable, paving the way for more comprehensive multimodal learning in robotics.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper suggests future work on extending this framework to multi-robot coordination and toward scalable, general-purpose manipulation systems. The sensor-free nature of FD-VLA at deployment could be particularly impactful in these complex scenarios, where adding more physical sensors would further escalate complexity and cost.

In summary, FD-VLA contributes a practical, effective, and robust solution for force-aware manipulation within VLA paradigms, addressing critical limitations of hardware dependency and raw sensor noise while maintaining the benefits of large pre-trained models.
