# FD-VLA: Force-Distilled Vision-Language-Action Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FD-VLA introduces a force-distilled vision-language-action model that enables robots to perform contact-rich manipulation without requiring physical force sensors during inference, by learning force representations from visual and robot state inputs. This approach achieved a mean success rate of 61.1% across various contact-rich tasks, outperforming existing vision-language-only and raw force-augmented baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-only Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack the precision and robustness needed for contact-rich manipulation tasks due to their inability to infer subtle physical interactions from visual data alone.
- Directly integrating physical force sensors into VLA frameworks is costly, fragile, and risks disrupting the carefully aligned vision-language semantics of pre-trained large language models.
- Existing methods for force integration in VLAs face challenges such as modality misalignment, training instability, increased inference complexity, or limited fine-grained force-vision-state interactions due to late fusion strategies.

## Method

- The **FD-VLA** framework integrates force awareness by ==distilling force representations== from visual observations and robot proprioceptive states, eliminating the need for physical force sensors during inference.
- A ==Force Distillation Module (FDM)== predicts a latent force token from visual and state inputs, supervised by actual raw force signals during training via an ==L2 loss==.
- A ==directional attention masking== mechanism is employed within the VLA's transformer layers to robustly fuse the predicted force and robot state tokens with vision and language, while preserving the pre-trained semantic priors of the backbone VLM.

## Results

- FD-VLA achieved the highest mean success rate of **61.1%** across three real-world contact-rich tasks (Clean Whiteboard, Press Emergency Button, Insert Plug) compared to baselines.
- The proposed model significantly outperformed vision-language-only baselines (e.g., SmolVLA at **23.3%**) and those augmented with direct raw force input (e.g., SmolVLA with raw force at **38.9%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Force Distillation Module's architecture and the use of a learnable token contributed to a substantial improvement in task success rates, moving from **38.9%** without FDM to **61.1%** with the full FD-VLA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Force information can be effectively distilled from readily available visual and robot state inputs, enabling robust force-aware manipulation without the cost and complexity of physical sensors at inference.
> - Employing a learnable query token within the force distillation module is more effective at capturing task-relevant force representations than directly encoding raw, potentially noisy, force measurements.
> - A selective, directional attention mechanism facilitates early and fine-grained cross-modal fusion of force, vision, and state within a pre-trained VLM, enhancing perception-action coupling without degrading its core vision-language capabilities.
