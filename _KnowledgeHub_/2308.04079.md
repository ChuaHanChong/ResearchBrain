# 3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Inria, Université Côte d'Azur, and Max-Planck-Institut für Informatik developed a novel scene representation using 3D Gaussians for real-time radiance field rendering. This method achieves photorealistic novel view synthesis at over 100 frames per second at 1080p resolution while maintaining visual quality comparable to or exceeding state-of-the-art offline methods like Mip-NeRF360.

## Problem

- Traditional Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) achieve high visual quality but suffer from slow rendering speeds, making them impractical for real-time applications.
- Existing faster NeRF variants or point-based methods often compromise on visual quality, failing to simultaneously achieve both high fidelity and real-time performance at high resolutions.

## Method

- Represents scenes using a collection of optimizable ==3D Gaussians==, initialized from sparse ==Structure-from-Motion (SfM)== points.
- Employs an interleaved optimization process with ==adaptive density control==, which dynamically clones, splits, or shrinks Gaussians based on rendering quality.
- Renders novel views using a fast, ==differentiable, visibility-aware splatting algorithm== that projects 3D Gaussians to 2D screen space and sorts them by depth for efficient ==tile-based rasterization==.

## Results

- Achieves photorealistic rendering at over **100 frames per second (fps)** at **1080p** resolution, significantly faster than Mip-NeRF360 (**<1 fps**).
- Demonstrates visual quality comparable to or exceeding Mip-NeRF360, which was previously the state-of-the-art for quality, on various complex scenes.
- Reduces training times from hours to minutes for complex scenes, making the process more practical.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Utilizing anisotropic 3D Gaussians with full covariance matrices allows for efficient and accurate representation of complex scene geometry and appearance, including thin structures.
> - An adaptive density control mechanism, which clones or splits Gaussians based on rendering quality, enables efficient allocation of representational capacity to areas requiring more detail.
> - A differentiable tile-based rasterizer for 3D Gaussians allows for fast training and real-time rendering, achieving high frame rates without sacrificing visual quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have revolutionized the field of novel view synthesis by enabling photorealistic rendering from a set of input images. However, the computational demands of NeRFs have limited their practical application in real-time scenarios. The paper "3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering" introduces a groundbreaking approach that achieves real-time rendering speeds while maintaining high visual quality.

#### 1.1. Background and Context

Novel view synthesis has long been a challenging problem in computer graphics and computer vision. Previous approaches fall into several categories:

1. **Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs)**: These methods represent scenes as continuous volumetric functions learned by neural networks. While they achieve impressive visual quality, they typically suffer from slow rendering speeds.

2. **Point-Based Rendering**: These approaches represent scenes as collections of discrete points, offering faster rendering but often with lower visual quality.

3. **Image-Based Rendering (IBR)**: These techniques directly use captured images to synthesize novel views without explicit 3D reconstruction.

Recent NeRF methods have improved rendering speed (InstantNGP, Plenoxels) or visual quality (Mip-NeRF360), but none have successfully achieved both simultaneously at the level required for real-time applications at high resolutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Research Team and Contribution

The work, conducted by researchers from Inria and Université Côte d'Azur in France, along with collaborators from the Max-Planck-Institut für Informatik in Germany, presents a novel representation based on 3D Gaussians that are rendered through a fast, differentiable splatting algorithm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 3D Gaussian Splatting approach combines the best aspects of both neural and point-based rendering techniques. The method consists of three key components:

1. A scene representation using 3D Gaussians
2. An interleaved optimization and density control process
3. A fast, visibility-aware rendering algorithm

#### 3.1. 3D Gaussian Representation

Unlike traditional point-based methods that require dense Multi-View Stereo (MVS) reconstructions, 3D Gaussian Splatting only requires sparse Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points, which are already produced during camera calibration. Each 3D Gaussian is defined by:

- A 3D position
- A 3D covariance matrix representing anisotropic shape
- Opacity
- Spherical harmonic coefficients for view-dependent appearance

The anisotropic nature of the Gaussians allows them to better represent complex geometries like thin structures. Each Gaussian's covariance matrix is defined as:

$$\Sigma = RSS^TR^T$$

Where R is a rotation matrix and S is a scaling matrix. This representation allows the Gaussians to stretch and orient in 3D space to efficiently model surfaces.

#### 3.2. Optimization and Density Control

The optimization process iteratively refines the properties of each Gaussian to minimize the difference between rendered and ground truth images. A key innovation is the adaptive density control mechanism, which dynamically adds or removes Gaussians based on rendering quality.

When a region is under-reconstructed (insufficient density), Gaussians are cloned to increase detail. For over-reconstructed regions, large Gaussians are split into smaller ones. This adaptive approach ensures that computational resources are focused on areas requiring more detail.

The paper also introduces a "shrinking" approach for Gaussians that don't contribute significantly to the final image.

#### 3.3. Fast Visibility-Aware Rendering

The rendering algorithm is a critical component of the system, enabling both fast training and real-time rendering. The process involves:

1. Projecting 3D Gaussians to 2D screen space
2. Sorting Gaussians by depth
3. Applying a tile-based differentiable rasterizer
4. Implementing an efficient backward pass for optimization

For each pixel, the rendered color is computed as:

$$C = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$$

Where $c_i$ is the color of Gaussian $i$, $\alpha_i$ is its opacity, and $\mathcal{N}$ is the set of Gaussians affecting the pixel.

The differentiable nature of the entire pipeline allows for end-to-end optimization. The rendering algorithm achieves impressive speeds of over 100 frames per second at 1080p resolution, making it suitable for real-time applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The method was evaluated on several standard datasets, comparing against state-of-the-art NeRF approaches. The results demonstrate that 3D Gaussian Splatting achieves:

1. **Superior visual quality**: Often matching or exceeding Mip-NeRF360, which was previously considered the state-of-the-art in terms of quality.
2. **Real-time rendering**: Achieving 30+ frames per second at 1080p resolution, compared to less than 1 fps for Mip-NeRF360.
3. **Competitive training times**: Training in minutes rather than hours for complex scenes.

The method was also evaluated with different initialization strategies, and additional ablation studies demonstrate the importance of its various components. It shows excellent view consistency between adjacent frames, and the anisotropic nature of the Gaussians proves critical for accurately representing complex scenes, including challenging scenarios with high-frequency details, specular reflections, and indoor scenes with complex lighting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The 3D Gaussian Splatting approach represents a significant advancement in neural rendering, effectively bridging the gap between the high quality of NeRFs and the speed requirements of real-time applications. Its contributions include:

1. **Enabling real-time NeRF applications**: By achieving 30+ fps rendering at high resolutions, the method opens the door for applications in virtual reality, augmented reality, and interactive visualization that were previously impractical with NeRF approaches.

2. **Improved representation efficiency**: The anisotropic 3D Gaussian representation efficiently models complex surfaces with relatively few primitives (1-5 million Gaussians versus billions of samples in traditional NeRFs).

3. **Practical training times**: With training times measured in minutes rather than hours or days, the method makes neural rendering more accessible for practical applications.

4. **New direction for point-based graphics**: The work demonstrates how neural techniques can be effectively incorporated into traditional point-based rendering pipelines.

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive results, the method has some limitations:

1. **Memory requirements**: The point-based nature of the representation can require significant memory for very complex scenes.

2. **Dynamic scenes**: The current implementation focuses on static scenes. Extending to dynamic scenes would be valuable for many applications.

3. **Extremely specular surfaces**: While the method handles moderate specular effects well, extremely reflective surfaces remain challenging.

Future work might explore:

- Compression techniques to reduce memory requirements
- Extensions to dynamic scene rendering
- Integration with other rendering techniques for specialized effects
- Hardware-accelerated implementations for even faster rendering

#### 5.2. Conclusion

3D Gaussian Splatting represents a significant advancement in neural rendering, effectively solving the long-standing challenge of achieving both high-quality and real-time performance in novel view synthesis. By combining the principles of point-based graphics with neural optimization techniques, the method creates a practical solution that could enable widespread adoption of neural rendering in real-world applications.

The method's ability to render photorealistic novel views at over 100 frames per second while maintaining quality comparable to the best offline methods marks a pivotal moment in the evolution of neural rendering technologies. As the first approach to truly achieve real-time, high-quality neural rendering at high resolutions, 3D Gaussian Splatting opens new possibilities for applications in virtual reality, augmented reality, gaming, and interactive visualization.
