# Reason Through the Latent! Making Latent Visual Reasoning Necessary

> [!summary] Summary
> Causal Visual Recurrent Reasoning (CVRR) makes latent visual reasoning causally necessary rather than merely informative. It initializes a recurrent state from a frozen VLM's image-conditioned question hidden state at a patched layer boundary, refines it through a shared LoRA-adapted decoder layer that re-reads fixed visual evidence, then deletes visual rows and multimodal KV caches before decoding. It reaches 81.2% on V* under this no-bypass interface.

## Problem

- Existing latent visual reasoners leave the original multimodal KV cache intact, so image information reaches the answer through a bypass around the proposed latent state.
- Interventions on prior latent states barely change predictions: UniVLR shifts at most 0.5 points under blank-image, matched-swap, and norm-matched-noise replacement, showing weak behavioral reliance.
- Benchmark accuracy therefore cannot establish that a model reasons through its latent path; informativeness of a hidden state is conflated with causal utilization.

## Method

- **Causal visual-read boundary**: ==layer-wise activation patching== on contrastive image pairs locates a boundary at layer 20 of Qwen2.5-VL-7B; the next layer becomes the shared recurrent transition.
- **Persistent visual recurrence**: one shared ==LoRA==-adapted decoder layer (rank 32, 0.035% of parameters) updates the question state over T=4 steps with mixing coefficient 0.5 while re-reading fixed visual rows.
- **Strict causal decoder interface**: visual rows and multimodal prefix caches are discarded before decoding, leaving only the final recurrent state plus image-free text caches, trained with answer cross-entropy alone.

## Results

- Under the no-bypass interface CVRR reaches **81.2%** V*, **52.7%** MMVP pair, **55.2%** BLINK overall; retrained latent baselines reach at most **39.8%**, **2.7%**, and **38.8%**.
- Ablations: replacing native initialization with a text-only anchor drops V* to **37.2%**; removing visual re-reading costs **12.0** V* points and **34.7** MMVP pair points.
- Corrupting the recurrent state cuts V* from **81.2%** to **14.0%**; crossed clean/wrong evidence yields plus or minus **14.8**-point difference-in-differences, largely removed when question-to-visual attention is blocked.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Probing a latent state while bypass routes remain cannot establish reliance; remove the alternative image-conditioned paths first, then intervene.
> - Path necessity must be built in architecturally, since imposing it post hoc on existing latent reasoners destroys their pretrained visual competence.
> - Recurrence needs both a competent starting state and persistent re-reading; influential image regions shift across steps, so latent evidence is not a static packet.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position in the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses latent visual reasoning, an emerging area in multimodal artificial intelligence. Conventional vision-language models often reason through explicit textual chains of thought, while latent reasoning methods perform intermediate computation in continuous hidden representations that are not verbalized. This approach can reduce the need to generate long textual explanations and may allow models to preserve visual information that is difficult to express accurately in language.

Recent latent visual reasoning systems, including LVR, Monet, SkiLa, Laser, UniVLR, HyLaR, and PEARL, differ in how they construct and update hidden visual states. Some use recurrent latent updates, some combine textual and continuous reasoning, and others represent visual reasoning through predictive embeddings or latent sketches. Parallel work has explored explicit visual search, pixel-level operations, self-reflection, and repeated image consultation.

The paper identifies a methodological issue within this research landscape. A latent state may contain useful visual information without being causally necessary for the final answer. If the decoder retains access to the original image-conditioned hidden states, visual tokens, or multimodal key-value caches, the model can answer through an alternative route. In that situation, replacing or corrupting a proposed latent state may have little effect even when the state is informative. The authors describe this alternative route as a “bypass.”

The paper therefore shifts the evaluation question from whether a latent state is informative to whether prediction actually depends on computation through that state. This places latent visual reasoning in closer relationship with mechanistic interpretability and causal analysis, where researchers distinguish information encoded in a representation from information that influences behavior. The proposed method, Causal Visual Recurrent Reasoning (CVRR), combines architectural path control with causal interventions. Rather than merely measuring reliance after training, it removes alternative image-conditioned routes by design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper has three central objectives.

First, it aims to determine whether existing latent visual reasoning methods behaviorally rely on their proposed latent states. The authors test this by replacing latent content with blank-image states, states taken from examples with different answers, or norm-matched noise. If predictions remain stable, the latent state may not be necessary because the original multimodal answer context still supplies sufficient visual information.

Second, the work seeks to preserve the visual competence already acquired by a pretrained vision-language model. Simply forcing a model to answer through a newly introduced latent bottleneck can damage performance, especially if the bottleneck begins from a text-only representation or raw visual features that do not yet contain the model’s integrated visual interpretation. The authors’ preliminary experiments suggest that the question-token representation formed during a native multimodal pass already contains most of the visual competence needed for answering.

Third, the paper aims to make recurrent latent computation itself necessary for prediction. The desired system should not merely carry an informative image-conditioned state to the decoder. It should update that state using visual evidence, and the final answer should be produced only from the resulting recurrent representation.

The motivation is both scientific and architectural. Scientifically, claims about latent reasoning require evidence that the proposed computation is actually used, rather than simply correlated with the answer. Architecturally, eliminating bypasses provides a controlled interface for testing this claim. The authors do not argue that redundant visual pathways are inherently harmful for practical systems. Instead, they argue that such pathways make it difficult to establish causal claims about latent reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Preliminary Diagnostics

The authors first evaluate existing latent visual reasoning methods using latent-state replacement experiments. For each method, they compare normal predictions with predictions after replacing the latent content while leaving the original multimodal answer context intact. Some methods, particularly UniVLR in the reported example, show changes of no more than approximately 0.5 percentage points across interventions. This indicates weak behavioral dependence on the modified latent content under the ordinary inference interface.

They then compare four representations from a frozen pretrained vision-language model:

1. The full multimodal hidden state containing visual and question tokens.
2. Only the question-token rows from the multimodal pass.
3. Question-token rows from a text-only pass.
4. Text-only question rows concatenated with raw visual features.

The full multimodal state achieves 75.9% on V*, while the multimodal question rows achieve 75.4%. Text-only conditions achieve 35.6%. The small gap between the first two conditions suggests that the question representation formed after visual processing retains most of the relevant visual competence.

#### 3.2. Localizing the Visual-Read Boundary

CVRR begins recurrence at a point where visual information has already entered the question representation but where visual states still exert downstream influence. The authors locate this point using layer-wise activation patching on the frozen base model.

They construct pairs of examples with the same question but different images and answers. At each candidate layer, visual activations from one example are substituted into the other, and the reduction in preference for the original answer is measured. The first sustained decline in this visual influence profile identifies a candidate boundary.

For Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, the selected boundary is layer 20, with layer 21 used as the recurrent transition. The lower backbone produces a multimodal hidden state at this boundary. The multimodal state is used to initialize the recurrent pathway, while a separate text-only pass supplies image-free decoder context.

#### 3.3. Persistent Visual Recurrence

The layer-21 decoder block is first applied in its original pretrained form to the multimodal boundary state. The resulting question-token rows form the initial recurrent state, denoted \(h_1\), and the visual-token rows form persistent visual evidence, denoted \(v\).

At each later recurrent step, the previous question state replaces the original question rows, while the visual rows remain fixed. The shared decoder layer processes the evolving question state together with the same visual evidence. A mixing coefficient of 0.5 combines the previous state and the new transition output. The default recurrent depth is four steps.

This design allows the model to repeatedly reread visual information without introducing a separate cross-attention module. It preserves the original token ordering, causal mask, and positional structure. The recurrent transition uses shared low-rank adaptation parameters. The frozen backbone remains unchanged, and only approximately 2.88 million LoRA parameters are trained out of roughly 8.295 billion total parameters.

#### 3.4. Strict No-Bypass Answer Interface

Before answer decoding, CVRR discards the visual rows and the original multimodal key-value cache. The decoder receives the final recurrent question state \(h_T\) and image-free text-only prefix caches. Consequently, \(h_T\) is the only remaining source of image-conditioned information.

Training uses answer-token cross-entropy on 438,000 Visual CoT question-answer pairs. The model is trained for three epochs using AdamW, with a learning rate of \(2 \times 10^{-5}\), bfloat16 precision, and a global batch size of 128. The main experiments use Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and evaluate on V*, MMVP, BLINK, and MME-RealWorld-Lite.

The paper also retrains compatible latent reasoning baselines under the same strict no-bypass interface. These controls preserve the baselines’ original latent mechanisms and training objectives while removing alternative multimodal answer routes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance

Under the strict interface, CVRR achieves:

- **V***: 81.2% overall accuracy, including 82.6% on Direct Attributes and 79.0% on Relative Position.
- **MMVP**: 74.7% item accuracy and 52.7% pair accuracy.
- **BLINK**: 55.2% overall accuracy, with 70.0% on Counting, 28.0% on IQ-Test, 62.7% on Jigsaw, 42.5% on Relative Reflectance, and 90.9% on Spatial Relation.
- **MME-RealWorld-Lite**: 46.7%.

The comparable latent reasoning baselines retrained without bypasses perform substantially worse. Their V* accuracies range from 35.6% to 39.8%, and their MMVP pair accuracies range from 0.0% to 2.7%. Their BLINK scores reach at most 38.8%, and their MME-RealWorld-Lite scores reach at most 33.1%.

These results indicate that eliminating bypasses alone is insufficient. The model also needs an initialization that preserves the visual computation learned during pretraining.

#### 4.2. Ablation Findings

The component ablations separate native multimodal initialization from recurrent refinement.

Replacing the native multimodal initial state \(h_1\) with a text-only anchor reduces V* accuracy from 81.2% to 37.2% and MMVP pair accuracy from 52.7% to 2.0%. Persistent visual evidence and a learned transition cannot reconstruct the visual competence already integrated into the native multimodal question representation.

Using \(h_1\) without recurrent updates produces 75.4% on V* but only 22.7% MMVP pair accuracy. Removing visual rereading while retaining the initial state and learned transition reduces V* accuracy by 12.0 points and MMVP pair accuracy by 34.7 points. Removing the learned transition also reduces performance, by 4.7 and 20.0 points on those metrics, respectively.

These results support a division of labor: native multimodal initialization transfers pretrained visual competence into the required path, while recurrent visual rereading and learned transitions further refine that representation.

#### 4.3. Causal Intervention Results

Corrupting the final recurrent state causes V* accuracy to fall from 81.2% to 14.0% under matched-state replacement, 36.1% with a blank-image state, and 35.6% with norm-matched noise. Restoring the original multimodal prefix partially compensates for the corruption, showing that an alternative visual pathway can recover some performance.

Crossed-state experiments pair the question state from one image with visual evidence from another. Clean visual evidence improves an incorrect initial state, whereas incorrect evidence degrades a correct initial state when question-to-visual connections remain active. Blocking these connections largely removes the effects. The reported difference-in-differences effects are approximately +14.8 and −14.8 percentage points.

On MMVP pairs with identical questions, replacing the final state with the state from the alternative image reduces accuracy from 73.3% to 26.7%, even though the question representation remains fixed. This indicates that the model is sensitive to image-conditioned content rather than simply to disruption of the question encoding.

The authors also find that the spatially influential visual regions change across recurrent steps. Consecutive causal maps have mean cosine similarities of 0.37 and 0.35, suggesting that the recurrent process does not use the visual representation as an unchanging information packet.

#### 4.4. Efficiency and Generalization

At four recurrent steps, CVRR increases latency by approximately 9.9% relative to its one-step version and increases decoder computation by 13.4%, while adding only 0.12 GiB of peak memory. Under a matched four-step comparison, it has lower measured latency and incremental memory than the evaluated latent reasoning baselines.

Experiments with Gemma-3, Gemma-4, and InternVL3 indicate that the general strategy transfers across backbones, although the degree of strict-path performance retention and intervention sensitivity varies by architecture.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

The paper’s main contribution is a causal framing of latent visual reasoning. It distinguishes a representation’s informational content from its behavioral necessity and proposes an architectural solution for removing bypasses. CVRR demonstrates that a model can retain substantial pretrained visual competence while routing image-conditioned prediction through a recurrent latent state.

This approach could influence future evaluation protocols by encouraging researchers to report path-controlled results in addition to standard benchmark accuracy. It may also support more precise studies of hidden-state reasoning, including analyses of when recurrent computation changes visual focus or revises an initial interpretation.

The findings have practical implications for efficient multimodal systems. CVRR reuses a single adapted decoder layer rather than repeatedly processing the full multimodal backbone, which limits additional computation and memory. However, the paper does not establish that strict path necessity improves general-purpose deployment performance, interpretability in a complete sense, or robustness outside the tested tasks.

The authors identify several limitations. The visual-read boundary is backbone-specific and must be localized separately. Most mechanistic analyses use multiple-choice or restricted first-token readouts, which may not fully represent open-ended generation. The method rereads fixed visual evidence rather than adaptively acquiring new evidence. Finally, the study is based on a single training run for each configuration, so reported confidence intervals capture evaluation-sample uncertainty rather than variation across training runs. Further work is needed to test adaptive visual acquisition, open-ended tasks, more backbones, and broader causal evaluation settings.
