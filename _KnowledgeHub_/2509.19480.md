# OmniVLA: An Omni-Modal Vision-Language-Action Model for Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniVLA introduces an omni-modal Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed for robot navigation, capable of interpreting goal specifications from natural language, 2D poses, and egocentric images simultaneously. The system, trained on over 9,500 hours of diverse real-world data, demonstrates superior performance over single-modality baselines and exhibits strong adaptability as a foundation model.

## Problem

- Robots are typically limited to single input modalities for navigation, lacking the human-like flexibility to combine diverse goal specifications (e.g., language, GPS, visual cues).
- Existing navigation policies struggle to integrate and leverage complementary information from different modalities effectively for more robust and adaptable navigation.
- The robotics community lacks a unified framework to efficiently utilize vast amounts of disparate navigation datasets, often curated for specific tasks or goal representations, for large-scale model training.

## Method

- Developed **OmniVLA** by extending a 7B-parameter pre-trained VLA backbone (OpenVLA) to process and fuse current observations with egocentric goal images, 2D poses, and natural language prompts.
- Compiled an extensive training dataset comprising over 9,500 hours of real-world navigation data collected across 10 diverse robot platforms from 13 publicly available datasets.
- Employed randomized modality sampling and attention masking during training to enable cross-modal learning and ensure the policy can handle scenarios with missing or combined goal information.

## Results

- OmniVLA consistently outperformed state-of-the-art specialist baselines, achieving a **73%** success rate in language-conditioned navigation and a **95%** success rate in 2D pose-conditioned navigation.
- The model successfully followed compositional goals, achieving an **80%** success rate when integrating 2D goal poses with out-of-distribution behavioral language instructions.
- OmniVLA-edge demonstrated efficient adaptability by improving satellite imagery navigation from **19%** to **62%** SR after fine-tuning and transferring zero-shot to new robot platforms like the VizBot and Unitree Go1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single, high-capacity VLA model can effectively learn to interpret and compose goal specifications from diverse modalities, offering a unified approach to robot navigation.
> - Training on an unprecedentedly large and varied dataset of real-world navigation data, leveraging cross-modal information, leads to richer geometric, semantic, and visual understanding.
> - The policy demonstrates qualities of a foundation model, showing strong generalization capabilities, efficient adaptation to new modalities/environments, and out-of-the-box transfer to new robot embodiments.
