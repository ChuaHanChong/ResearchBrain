# Learning from Trials and Errors: Reflective Test-Time Planning for Embodied LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Reflective Test-Time Planning, a framework that allows embodied Large Language Models to learn from their mistakes and adapt during deployment by converting verbal reflections into self-supervised training signals for model parameter updates. The system achieved a 33.65% average success rate on the Long-Horizon Household benchmark, outperforming baselines like 3DLLM-Mem (11.13%), and demonstrated robust adaptation in fitting tasks and generalization to new environments.

## Problem

- Existing embodied LLMs are "brittle static oracles" that struggle to adapt to unforeseen circumstances and cannot learn from execution failures during deployment.
- Current AI reflection mechanisms often store critiques as context, influencing future actions indirectly without updating underlying model parameters, leading to repeated mistakes.
- Addressing long-horizon temporal credit assignment is challenging, where immediate successful actions might lead to long-term task failures.

## Method

- A ==Reflective Test-Time Planning framework== integrates three interacting multimodal LLMs (action generation, internal evaluator, external evaluator) to unify reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action.
- ==Reflection-in-action== uses an internal evaluator to score and select the best action from multiple candidates before execution, simulating outcomes.
- ==Reflection-on-action== employs an external evaluator to provide immediate feedback, and crucially, retrospective reflection to re-evaluate past actions with hindsight, generating self-supervised training signals for parameter updates via LoRA.

## Results

- The full Reflective Test-Time Planning framework achieved an average **33.65%** success rate on the Long-Horizon Household benchmark, significantly outperforming the strongest baseline (3DLLM-Mem) at **11.13%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action components are mutually dependent and essential for performance, with removing either leading to significant degradation.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization to photorealistic Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) scenes, achieving **19.5%** success on preparation tasks despite being trained only on synthetic data, showcasing transferability under domain shift.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying 'reflection-in-action' (pre-execution mental simulation) and 'reflection-on-action' (post-execution learning from outcomes) creates a robust, adaptive learning cycle for embodied agents.
> - Converting verbal reflections into self-supervised parameter updates enables "double-loop learning," where agents correct not just actions but also the underlying assumptions and policies guiding those actions.
> - Retrospective reflection, by re-evaluating past decisions with full hindsight, effectively solves the challenging problem of temporal credit assignment in long-horizon tasks, distilling long-term foresight into actionable learning signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Large Language Models (LLMs) represent a significant development in equipping robotic agents with high-level task reasoning capabilities. These models can interpret complex instructions and generate action plans, addressing challenges in long-horizon tasks. However, a limitation of many existing embodied LLM systems is their static nature during deployment. They often function as fixed "oracles" that, once deployed, cannot internally reflect on errors, understand why a failure occurred, or update their internal models based on real-world interaction outcomes. This can lead to repeated mistakes and a lack of cumulative learning experience.

Prior research has explored various forms of AI reflection and self-improvement. One area involves LLM-based verbal reflection, where agents generate natural-language critiques of past behaviors to inform future actions. While these methods enable a form of "reflection-on-action" at the reasoning trace level, they often treat reflections as contextual text rather than persistent signals for parameter updates. Another line of work focuses on "reflection-in-action" by utilizing internal world models to anticipate outcomes and guide action selection. However, these methods frequently encounter the challenge of updating internal beliefs when actual execution outcomes diverge from predictions.

Classical planning and reinforcement learning (RL) approaches offer different mechanisms for behavior revision. Planning methods optimize action sequences over fixed dynamics but typically lack deployment-time parameter updates, confining learning to a "single-loop" where actions are corrected without revising underlying assumptions. Model-free RL learns from scalar reward signals aggregated over many episodes, which can be noisy, non-linguistic, and amortized, making it difficult to diagnose specific failures within a single unfolding interaction episode. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) aims to adapt models to distribution shifts during inference, but its application to embodied settings with self-supervised verbal assessments for continuous parameter updates remains an area of active investigation.

This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to unify and operationalize both reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action within a single, systematic test-time planning framework. It addresses the gap where embodied LLMs lack the capacity for "double-loop learning"—not merely from outcomes, but from diagnosing and correcting the underlying causes of their errors through adaptive parameter updates during deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop "Reflective Test-Time Planning," a systematic framework that enables embodied LLMs to learn from failures and adapt their behavior and internal models during real-time deployment. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations of current embodied AI systems:

1. **Brittleness and Stagnation:** Existing embodied LLMs, despite their high-level task planning abilities, tend to be brittle. They often treat deployment as a sequence of independent trials, repeating mistakes rather than accumulating experience and improving over time. This lack of persistent learning prevents robust operation in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.
2. **Lack of Deeper Reflective Capacity:** Unlike humans, who fluidly alternate between reflecting on planned actions and learning from actual outcomes, most AI agents cannot articulate what went wrong or why. Current reflective mechanisms in AI are often isolated, either focusing on pre-execution simulation without post-execution model updates, or generating verbal critiques that do not translate into lasting parameter changes.
3. **Ineffective Credit Assignment:** Many failures in embodied tasks are non-local; an action that appears successful immediately might later impede progress, or a seemingly suboptimal action could enable future success. Traditional feedback mechanisms, such as immediate scalar rewards or step-wise external reflections, are often insufficient to diagnose and address these long-horizon credit assignment problems effectively within a single episode.
4. **Absence of "Double-Loop Learning":** The researchers draw inspiration from Donald Sch¨on's framework of reflective practitioners, highlighting that effective learning involves not just correcting actions (single-loop learning) but also revising the underlying beliefs, assumptions, and decision-making processes that led to errors (double-loop learning). Current embodied LLMs typically lack this deeper form of adaptive learning during deployment.
5. **Converting Reflections into Actionable Learning Signals:** A core motivation is to operationalize linguistic reflections (human-interpretable critiques and scores) into concrete self-supervised training signals. This would enable dynamic parameter updates during test time, making the learning process interpretable and efficient.

To address these motivations, the proposed framework aims to integrate reflection-in-action (internal simulation and scoring of candidate actions before execution) and reflection-on-action (updating beliefs and action policies based on execution outcomes). Furthermore, it introduces retrospective reflection to handle long-horizon credit assignment and facilitate test-time training that updates both the internal reflection model and the action policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Reflective Test-Time Planning framework integrates three interacting Large Language Models (LLMs) and three types of reflection during deployment to achieve adaptive learning.

**Model Components:**
The framework employs three distinct embodied LLMs:
1. **Action Generation LLM ($\pi_\theta$):** Responsible for producing natural language actions given observations.
2. **Internal Reflection LLM ($V_{\phi_i}$):** Generates pre-action evaluations and scores for candidate actions.
3. **External Reflection LLM ($V_{\phi_e}$):** Provides post-execution assessments of actual outcomes.

These LLMs are initially fine-tuned on a small set of tasks to establish basic capabilities in understanding action formats, generating reflections, and processing 3D observations.

**Reflection Mechanisms:**

1. **Reflection-in-Action (Pre-action Scoring):**
 - **Candidate Action Generation:** Instead of generating a single action, the agent uses the action LLM ($\pi_\theta$) with a high sampling temperature (T) to generate *N* diverse candidate actions ($a_t^k$) at each decision step. This encourages exploration of multiple options.
 - **Internal Reflection Scoring:** For each candidate action, the Internal Reflection LLM ($V_{\phi_i}$) evaluates it, generating a natural language reflection ($f_{t,k}^i$) and a numerical score ($s_{t,k}^i \in$). This process mentally simulates the potential outcome of each action.
 - **Best Action Selection:** The agent executes the candidate action with the highest internal reflection score ($a_t^*$).

2. **Reflection-on-Action (Post-execution Learning):** This mode uses test-time training to update the action policy ($\pi_\theta$) and the internal reflection model ($V_{\phi_i}$).
 - **External Reflection Generation:** After executing $a_t^*$ and observing the environmental feedback (e.g., success/failure, next observation), the External Reflection LLM ($V_{\phi_e}$) generates an immediate, language-based external reflection ($f_t^e$) and score ($s_t^e$) assessing the outcome. This grounds beliefs in reality.
 - **Working Memory Buffer:** A buffer ($W$) stores a window of recent experiences (observations, actions, external reflections, scores) for a fixed number of steps ($K$) or until a key milestone is reached.

3. **Retrospective Reflection (Hindsight Re-evaluation):**
 - **Temporal Credit Assignment:** To address the non-local nature of many failures, retrospective reflection is triggered periodically (e.g., at room transitions or after accumulating $K$ steps).
 - **Hindsight Re-evaluation:** The External Reflection LLM ($V_{\phi_e}$) re-evaluates *earlier* actions ($a_j$) from the working memory or a retrospective buffer ($D_{retro}$) with the full context of current observations and accumulated experience. This generates a hindsight-corrected reflection ($f_j^r$) and score ($s_j^r$), providing a more accurate assessment of long-term consequences.
 - These retrospective reflections serve as self-supervised signals.

**Test-Time Training (TTT):**
The retrospective reflections are converted into training data to update the agent's models during deployment, enabling "double-loop learning."
1. **Training Dataset Construction ($D_{train}$):**
 - **Retro-supervised Pairs:** Actions re-evaluated by retrospective reflection form pairs $(a_j, f_j^r, s_j^r)$.
 - **Regularization Pairs:** To prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain existing knowledge, randomly sampled unexplored actions are included, with $V_{\phi_i}$'s current predictions as targets.
2. **Internal Reflection LLM ($V_{\phi_i}$) Training:** Updated via supervised learning (minimizing negative log-likelihood) using the combined $D_{train}$ dataset. This teaches $V_{\phi_i}$ to anticipate what hindsight will reveal.
3. **Action LLM ($\pi_\theta$) Training:** Updated using policy gradient (REINFORCE loss) with the retrospective scores ($s_r$, mapped to a reward $r \in [-1, 1]$) as reward signals. This reinforces actions that lead to favorable long-term outcomes and discourages those that lead to failures.

The use of verbal reflections throughout the process is inspired by "double-loop learning," aiming to abstract generalizable lessons that are transferable and interpretable, thereby providing effective supervisory signals for parameter updates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Reflective Test-Time Planning on two newly designed benchmarks and conducts various ablation studies and real-world experiments.

**Long-Horizon Household Tasks:**
- **Performance Gains:** The full Reflective Test-Time Planning model achieved an average success rate of 33.65%, significantly outperforming all baseline models. For instance, it achieved 44.7% on "Fitting" tasks compared to 10.6% for the next best baseline (3DLLM-Mem), and 32.4% on "Selection" tasks. Baselines like Reflexion, Self-Refine, ReflectVLM, PPO, and DreamerV3 yielded average success rates between 3.53% and 11.20%.
- **Ablation Studies (Mutual Dependency):** The ablations demonstrated that both reflection-in-action (RIA) and reflection-on-action (ROA) are interdependent and crucial for performance. Removing either component (e.g., "w/o RIA" at 11.45% or "w/o ROA" at 12.52% average success) led to substantial performance degradation, often performing worse than removing both ("w/o RIA/ROA" at 10.4% average success). This suggests a synergistic relationship where RIA provides higher-quality actions for ROA to learn from, and ROA refines the internal model for better future action selection. Similarly, ablating the action model loss or internal reflection loss individually also degraded performance, confirming their mutual dependency.
- **Retrospective vs. Immediate External Reflection:** Retrospective reflection (33.7% success) was found to be more effective for credit assignment than using immediate external reflection as the sole TTT signal (15.6% success), while also incurring lower computational overhead (1x vs. ~4x).
- **SFT Initialization and External LLM Reliability:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) initialization was necessary for the models to produce structured outputs, but excessive SFT data led to overfitting. The improvements were primarily attributed to reflection during test-time interaction, not solely to the initialization. Human evaluations of the External Reflection LLM ($V_{\phi_e}$) confirmed its high factual correctness, causal quality, and usefulness as a learning signal, even in out-of-domain scenarios, performing comparably to a human oracle.
- **Compute-Matched Experiments:** Baselines given a 3x increased action budget (to match the approximate wall-clock time overhead of the full model) did not show significant performance improvements (e.g., 8.79% average success for vanilla baseline with matched time vs. 33.65% for the full model). This indicates that the performance gains arise from the quality of reflective computation and model adaptation, rather than merely increased inference time.
- **Efficiency Improvements:** Through parallelization, batching, QLoRA, and multi-GPU parallelism, the effective test-time overhead of the framework was reduced from approximately 3x to 0.45x, with minimal performance degradation.

**MuJoCo Cupboard Fitting Task:**
- **Superior Performance:** The full method achieved a 60.2% fit rate and 25.3% correct rate, significantly outperforming all baselines on this controlled geometric placement task.
- **Reflection Mechanisms Essential:** Ablations confirmed the necessity of both RIA (dropping to 53.5% fit rate without it) and ROA (dropping to 45.2% fit rate without it).
- **LoRA Effectiveness:** LoRA-based TTT (60.2% fit rate) achieved comparable performance to full base-weight training (57.4% fit rate) while significantly reducing trainable parameters and memory footprint.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:** Optimal performance was observed with N=6 candidate actions for RIA, a sampling temperature of 1.25-1.5, and a LoRA configuration of (r=8, $\alpha$=16). A moderate action budget (50 steps) was found to be optimal, with excessive budgets sometimes leading to slight performance degradation.
- **Single-Step vs. Receding Horizon Planning:** The single-step action generation approach of the proposed framework (60.2% fit rate) was found to be more effective and computationally efficient (5x lower compute) than a receding horizon planning variant (57.8% fit rate). The paper attributes this to wasted computation on unpredictable futures and conflicts between learning from imagination (in sequence planning) and learning from reality (in TTT). Retrospective reflection implicitly provides long-horizon foresight.

**Generalization and Real-Robot Experiments:**
- **Zero-Shot Generalization to HM3D:** The model demonstrated zero-shot generalization to photorealistic HM3D environments, outperforming baselines by a large margin (19.5% for "Preparation" tasks vs. 7.32% for the next best baseline) despite being trained exclusively on synthetic BEHAVIOR-1K scenes.
- **Real-Robot Performance (Franka Panda):** Experiments on a Franka Panda arm for the Cupboard Fitting task showed that the full model generalized substantially better than the ablation without RIA and ROA. This performance gap widened in the real-world setting, suggesting that active failure recovery is crucial in the face of real-world uncertainties. Even without fine-tuning, the zero-shot full model outperformed a fine-tuned ablation, highlighting the importance of adaptive reflection.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Qualitative examples illustrated how reflection mechanisms enable continual learning, active perception, and correction of behavioral mistakes, such as re-evaluating placement decisions mid-episode to avoid blocking larger objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Reflective Test-Time Planning framework presents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI by addressing the limitations of static, non-adaptive embodied LLMs. Its primary significance lies in operationalizing "double-loop learning" for AI agents, enabling them to move beyond merely executing plans to truly learning from their mistakes and adapting their internal models and policies during deployment.

**Key impacts include:**

- **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** By integrating reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action, the framework allows agents to recover from unforeseen failures and adapt to novel or uncertain environmental conditions without requiring costly retraining or explicit reward engineering. This is crucial for deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments like homes.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Reasoning and Credit Assignment:** The introduction of retrospective reflection effectively tackles the challenging temporal credit assignment problem. Agents learn not just from immediate outcomes but from the long-term consequences of their actions, leading to more strategic and foresightful decision-making.
- **Human-Inspired Learning Paradigm:** Drawing inspiration from human reflective practitioners, the framework establishes a more human-like learning paradigm for AI, where internal deliberation and post-action analysis are integral to continuous improvement. The use of verbal reflections also enhances the interpretability of the agent's reasoning process.
- **Strong Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to photorealistic environments (HM3D) and successful real-robot deployments highlight the framework's potential to bridge the sim-to-real gap and enable effective transfer of learned behaviors to diverse, real-world scenarios.
- **Practical Implications for Robotics:** The ability of robots to learn from their own trials and errors in real-time could pave the way for more autonomous, reliable, and user-friendly robotic systems in various applications, from household assistance to industrial automation. It reduces the reliance on extensive pre-training data covering all possible failure modes.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated reflective mechanisms, the integration of richer sensory modalities (e.g., tactile feedback for reflection), and the development of self-improving AI systems that continuously evolve their capabilities through interaction.

Overall, the Reflective Test-Time Planning framework contributes a novel and effective method for integrating continuous, self-supervised learning into embodied LLMs, making them more resilient, intelligent, and capable of operating in dynamic real-world settings.
