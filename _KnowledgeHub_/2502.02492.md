# VideoJAM: Joint Appearance-Motion Representations for Enhanced Motion Generation in Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoJAM, a framework from Meta GenAI and Tel Aviv University, improves motion coherence and physical plausibility in generative video models through joint appearance-motion representations and a novel inner-guidance mechanism. Human evaluators preferred VideoJAM-30B's motion over Kling 1.5 in 72.7% of comparisons, demonstrating enhanced realism and overall video quality despite efficient fine-tuning on limited data.

## Problem

- Generative video models often struggle with motion coherence, dynamic interactions, and adherence to physical laws, leading to unnatural movements or physics violations.
- Existing models prioritize appearance fidelity due to the pixel reconstruction objective, neglecting temporal dynamics and physical plausibility.
- Issues include unnatural gaits, objects passing through each other, and severe deformations during complex actions.

## Method

- Introduced **VideoJAM**, a framework that instills an ==explicit motion prior== through a modified training objective and an ==inner-guidance== mechanism.
- Developed a ==joint appearance-motion representation== by modifying ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== architecture to jointly predict ==video== and ==optical flow latents== during training.
- Implemented ==Inner-Guidance== during inference, which dynamically leverages the model's self-predicted motion to guide generation towards temporal coherence.

## Results

- **VideoJAM** consistently outperformed open-source and proprietary models in motion coherence across benchmarks, with human evaluators preferring VideoJAM-30B's motion over **Kling 1.5** in **72.7%** of comparisons.
- It substantially improved motion coherence for its base DiT models, preferred by human raters in **82.0%** of cases for **DiT-4B** and **72.7%** for **DiT-30B** against the base models on **VideoJAM-bench**.
- The framework also enhanced overall perceived visual quality and text alignment, achieving these results with **efficient fine-tuning** on a smaller dataset and lower resolution than comparable models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conventional pixel-based reconstruction objectives bias generative video models towards appearance fidelity, causing them to neglect crucial temporal dynamics and motion coherence.
> - Learning a joint appearance-motion representation and using a dynamic self-guidance mechanism enables models to intrinsically acquire a strong motion prior.
> - Effectively integrating appearance and motion learning is complementary, leading to improvements in both motion coherence and overall visual quality/text faithfulness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI has witnessed remarkable progress in recent years, particularly with the advent of diffusion models. Initially revolutionizing image generation, these models have rapidly extended their capabilities to video synthesis. Notable advancements, such as OpenAI's Sora and Kuaishou's Kling, demonstrate an impressive ability to produce high-quality, long-duration video clips that are visually compelling and often adhere to user prompts. The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, which replaces the traditional U-Net backbone, has become a standard for scalable and efficient video generation, serving as the foundation for many state-of-the-art models.

Despite these tremendous strides, a persistent and critical challenge remains: **motion coherence, dynamic interactions, and adherence to physical laws in generated videos.** As the paper aptly points out, even highly advanced models frequently struggle with accurately portraying complex or even basic motions. Examples include unnatural gaits (e.g., jogging with repetitive leg movements), objects passing through each other (e.g., a hula hoop through a person), or complex actions leading to severe deformations (e.g., extra limbs during gymnastics). This limitation is particularly perplexing because such models are trained on vast datasets that contain abundant examples of real-world motion.

VideoJAM directly addresses this fundamental limitation. The authors argue that the root cause lies in the conventional pixel reconstruction objective commonly used in training, which prioritizes appearance fidelity (color, texture) at the expense of temporal dynamics. This bias causes models to overlook subtle but crucial temporal inconsistencies.

This work fits into a growing body of research that seeks to inject a deeper understanding of motion and physics into generative video models. Previous attempts have explored various avenues:
- **Motion/Physics-based Signals as Input/Guidance:** Some methods incorporate optical flow, motion trajectories, or physics-based simulations as explicit inputs or guidance signals during generation. VideoJAM differs by aiming to teach the model an *internal* temporal prior rather than relying solely on external, fixed inputs.
- **Decoupling Content and Motion:** Another line of work proposes separating the generation of static content from dynamic motion, allowing for more granular control and potentially better temporal consistency. VideoJAM takes a different approach by seeking a *joint* representation, implying that appearance and motion are fundamentally intertwined and can be learned synergistically.
- **Enhancing Image-to-Video Generation:** Some recent efforts use motion representations specifically to improve coherence when generating videos conditioned on a static image. VideoJAM, however, focuses on text-to-video generation, which presents a broader challenge in synthesizing both content and motion from scratch.

By proposing a generic framework that explicitly instills a strong motion prior through a joint appearance-motion representation and a dynamic inference guidance mechanism, VideoJAM carves out a significant niche. It posits that motion coherence is not just an auxiliary enhancement but a core component that can be learned intrinsically, leading to more realistic and physically plausible video generations without requiring massive data or architectural overhauls. This work represents a crucial step towards creating "world simulators" that not only look real but also behave realistically, a long-standing goal in AI research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The **key objective** of this research is to significantly enhance motion coherence, dynamics, and adherence to physical laws in generative video models. The authors aim to enable these models to produce videos that are not only visually high-quality but also temporally plausible and realistic, overcoming the common pitfalls of flickering, unnatural movements, and violations of basic physics.

The **motivation** behind VideoJAM stems from a critical observation about the limitations of existing state-of-the-art video generation models. Despite their impressive progress in visual fidelity, these models frequently struggle with capturing real-world motion and dynamics. The paper highlights several specific examples of these struggles:
- **Basic Motion Incoherence:** Even for common actions like jogging, models can exhibit unnatural or repetitive movements (e.g., stepping on the same leg repeatedly).
- **Complex Motion Failures:** For intricate actions such as gymnastics, models often produce severe deformations or static representations.
- **Physics Violations:** Objects may pass through each other (e.g., a hula hoop passing through a person) or appear/disappear unrealistically.
- **Rotational Motion Difficulties:** Models struggle to replicate simple, repetitive patterns of movement, such as a spinning object maintaining consistent rotation.

The authors hypothesize that this fundamental limitation arises from the **conventional pixel reconstruction objective** used during model training. Their core argument is that this objective inherently biases models towards learning appearance-based features (like color, texture, and static content) because pixel-wise differences are largely dominated by these aspects. Temporal information, such as motion, dynamics, and physical interactions, contributes comparatively less to the overall pixel-based loss, leading the model to deprioritize its learning.

To validate this hypothesis, the paper presents a compelling **motivation experiment** (Fig. 3). They compare a "vanilla" DiT model with their fine-tuned VideoJAM model by evaluating their loss functions on both original videos and videos with randomly permuted frames (thus destroying temporal coherence). The results unequivocally show that the "vanilla" DiT model is nearly invariant to temporal perturbations, especially in the early denoising steps (t ≤ 60) that are crucial for defining coarse motion. This insensitivity indicates that the model fails to differentiate between a temporally coherent and an incoherent video based on its pixel-focused objective. In stark contrast, the VideoJAM model exhibits extreme sensitivity to these perturbations, demonstrating that its training objective has successfully incorporated an awareness of temporal consistency. This finding strongly supports their claim that the conventional objective is a key impediment to motion modeling, thus motivating the need for VideoJAM's novel approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoJAM is a novel framework designed to instill an explicit motion prior into video generative models. It comprises two complementary phases: a modified training objective and a new inference-time guidance mechanism. The framework is designed to be generic and easily adaptable to existing video models with minimal architectural changes.

#### 3.1. Joint Appearance-Motion Representations (Training Phase)

The core idea during training is to encourage the model to learn a unified, joint latent representation that encapsulates both appearance and motion information simultaneously.
- **Motion Representation:** The authors choose optical flow as their motion representation. Optical flow computes dense displacement fields between successive frames, effectively encoding pixel-level movement. It is flexible and can be represented as an RGB image, allowing it to be processed by existing Temporal Auto-Encoders (TAEs) without requiring additional training. The optical flow is normalized to capture motion magnitude, with a parameter (σ = 0.15) to ensure that even subtle movements remain distinguishable.
- **Architectural Modifications:** VideoJAM modifies the architecture of a pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model to accommodate dual input and dual output. This involves:
 - **Extended Input Projection (W_in+):** The initial linear projection layer (W_in) is extended to accept two inputs: the noised video latent (x_t) and the noised optical flow latent (d_t). This is achieved by adding zero-rows, ensuring that at initialization, the modified network is equivalent to the pre-trained DiT and initially ignores the motion signal. The model must learn to combine these two signals into a single, joint latent representation.
 - **Extended Output Projection (W_out+):** The final linear projection layer (W_out) is extended to predict both the appearance (video) and the motion (optical flow) from this learned joint latent representation.
- **Modified Training Objective:** The conventional Flow Matching objective (which optimizes the model to predict velocity from a noised video) is extended. The new objective function, L, now includes an explicit motion term:
 L = E<sub>[x1,d1],[x0,d0],y,t</sub> [ ||u+([x_t, d_t]), y, t; θ') - v+t ||²₂ ]
 Here, [x_t, d_t] denotes the concatenation of noised video and flow latents, u+ = [u_x, u_d] represents the dual output (video and motion predictions), and v+t = [v_x_t, v_d_t] are the target velocities for both appearance and motion. By jointly fine-tuning all network weights, the model is compelled to learn a unified latent space from which both signals can be predicted accurately.

#### 3.2. Inner-Guidance (Inference Phase)

During inference, the primary goal is video generation, with the predicted motion serving as an auxiliary signal to guide the model towards temporally coherent outputs.
- **Challenge with Existing Guidance:** The authors identify a key limitation with traditional guidance mechanisms like Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) or compositional guidance (e.g., InstructPix2Pix). These methods assume that auxiliary conditions are independent of each other and the model's weights. However, in VideoJAM, the motion prediction (d_t) is dynamically generated by the model itself and thus inherently dependent on the model's evolving state and the text prompt (y). This dependency invalidates the independence assumption of prior approaches.
- **Novel Inner-Guidance Formulation:** To address this, VideoJAM introduces "Inner-Guidance," which directly modifies the sampling distribution. The proposed modified distribution ˜p(x_t, d_t | y) is formulated to leverage three terms: the original joint sampling distribution p(x_t, d_t | y), a term encouraging prompt fidelity p(y | x_t, d_t), and crucially, a term maximizing the likelihood of the noisy motion prediction p(d_t | x_t, y).
- **Practical Implementation:** By taking the log derivative, this translates into a combined score function:
 ˜u+([x_t, d_t], y, t; θ') = (1 + w₁ + w₂) · u+([x_t, d_t]), y, t; θ')
 - w₁ · u+([x_t, d_t], ∅, t; θ') - w₂ · u+([x_t, ∅], y, t; θ')
 This formulation leverages conditional and unconditional predictions (on text (y) and optical flow (d)) obtained by randomly dropping out these signals during training. The guidance scales (w₁ and w₂) control the influence of text and motion, respectively. Notably, motion guidance is applied only during the first half of denoising steps (50 steps), as these steps are found to be critical for determining coarse motion.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

The framework was fine-tuned on pre-trained DiT-4B and DiT-30B models, which are state-of-the-art text-to-video Diffusion Transformers. Fine-tuning was efficient, using only 3 million random samples (less than 3% of the original training data) and RAFT for optical flow estimation. Models generated 128-frame (5-second) videos at 24 FPS with a spatial resolution of 256x256.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoJAM's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments, benchmarked against various state-of-the-art models.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Experiments

- **Enhanced Motion Diversity and Complexity:** Figures 1, 5, and 9 showcase VideoJAM-30B's ability to generate a wide variety of challenging motion types, including complex gymnastics (e.g., "air splits," "acrobatics"), physically plausible interactions (e.g., "fingers pressed into shimmering slime ball," "basketball landing in a net"), and consistent rotational motions.
- **Superior Motion Coherence vs. Baselines:** Figure 6 provides direct qualitative comparisons with leading proprietary models like Sora and Kling 1.5, as well as the base DiT-30B. The comparisons vividly illustrate the motion issues prevalent in these competitive models:
 - **Unnatural Motion:** "Backward motion" (Sora), unnatural movements (Kling, DiT-30B) in basic actions like a running giraffe.
 - **Static or Deformed Outputs:** Static videos (Sora, Kling) or body deformations (Kling) for complex human actions like pull-ups or headstands.
 - **Physics Violations:** Objects appearing/disappearing unrealistically (Sora, DiT-30B) for rotational motion (e.g., a roulette wheel).
- In contrast, VideoJAM consistently produces temporally coherent videos that better adhere to the laws of physics across all these challenging scenarios, highlighting its core advantage.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Experiments

The evaluation used both automatic metrics (VBench) and human evaluations (2AFC protocol) on two benchmarks:
- **VideoJAM-bench:** A novel benchmark specifically constructed to evaluate motion coherence in challenging real-world scenarios, addressing limitations of existing benchmarks that might prioritize static scenes or unusual activities. It includes prompts across basic motion, complex motion, rotational motion, and physics.
- **Movie Gen (MGen) benchmark:** Used to demonstrate the robustness of VideoJAM's results on a broader, established benchmark.

**Key Quantitative Results (Tables 1, 2, 7):**
- **State-of-the-Art Motion Coherence:** VideoJAM consistently outperforms all baselines (both open-source and proprietary) in terms of motion coherence across all model sizes on both benchmarks, as measured by both human evaluation and VBench.
 - For example, on VideoJAM-bench, human raters preferred VideoJAM-30B's motion over Kling 1.5 by a significant margin (63.8% preference for Kling vs. 72.7% for VideoJAM).
- **Significant Improvement over Base Models:** In direct "apples-to-apples" comparisons, VideoJAM substantially improves the motion coherence of its base DiT models. Human raters preferred VideoJAM's motion in 82.0% of cases for DiT-4B and 72.7% for DiT-30B on VideoJAM-bench.
- **Enhanced Overall Quality:** Beyond motion, VideoJAM also improves the perceived visual quality and text faithfulness of generations. Human raters favored VideoJAM in quality (e.g., 74.2% preference for VideoJAM-30B over DiT-30B) and text alignment (71.9% preference for VideoJAM-30B). This suggests that effectively integrating appearance and motion is complementary, enhancing both aspects.
- **Efficiency and Scale-agnostic:** VideoJAM achieves these results despite being fine-tuned at a significantly lower resolution (256x256) and on a much smaller dataset (3 million samples) compared to competitive proprietary models (768 and higher resolution, O(100M) videos). This demonstrates its efficiency and generalizability across different model scales. VideoJAM-4B even outperforms a larger baseline (CogVideo5B).
- **VBench Breakdown (Tables 4, 5, 8):** VideoJAM strikes a better balance between "Dynamic Degree" (amount of motion) and "Motion Smoothness" (coherence of motion) compared to baselines. While some models might generate more motion, it's often incoherent; VideoJAM ensures motion is both present and plausible.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3)

The ablation study confirms the critical contribution of VideoJAM's key components:
- **Impact of Inner-Guidance:** Removing either text guidance or motion guidance from Inner-Guidance significantly degrades motion coherence. The removal of motion guidance is more detrimental, underscoring its importance.
- **Necessity of Optical Flow Prediction:** Completely dropping the optical flow prediction during inference (i.e., not leveraging the joint representation) yields the most harmful effect on motion coherence, substantiating the benefits of the dual output structure.
- **Superiority of Inner-Guidance Formulation:** Replacing Inner-Guidance with a compositional guidance approach (like InstructPix2Pix, which assumes conditional independence) results in substantially worse motion coherence, validating the need for VideoJAM's tailored formulation that accounts for the dynamic, model-dependent nature of the motion signal.
- The ablations also show that VideoJAM's full framework benefits overall visual quality and text alignment, reinforcing the idea that appearance and motion are complementary.

#### 4.4. Limitations

Despite its successes, VideoJAM has limitations:
- **"Zoomed-out" Scenarios:** The use of limited training resolution and RGB motion representation (optical flow) hinders the model's ability to capture subtle motion when moving objects occupy a small portion of the frame (e.g., a skydiver deploying a parachute). The relative motion magnitude is reduced, making the representation less informative.
- **Complex Physics of Object Interactions:** While motion and physics are intertwined, the optical flow representation lacks explicit physics encoding. This limits the model's ability to handle highly complex physical interactions, such as precise object collisions or deformable body dynamics (e.g., a soccer player's foot not realistically touching the ball before it changes trajectory).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoJAM represents a significant advancement in the field of generative video modeling by directly addressing the pervasive and critical challenge of **motion coherence**. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that by explicitly instilling a strong motion prior, models can overcome the inherent bias of pixel-based objectives towards appearance fidelity.

The **significance** of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Overcoming a Fundamental Limitation:** VideoJAM tackles one of the most glaring weaknesses of current state-of-the-art video generation models: their struggle with natural, physically plausible motion. By providing a generic and effective solution, it pushes the boundaries of what generative video AI can realistically depict.
- **Innovative Joint Representation:** The intuitive design of learning a single, unified latent representation that captures both appearance and motion is a novel approach. This "joint appearance-motion representation" allows the model to deeply integrate these two complementary aspects of video, leading to more holistic and realistic outputs.
- **Efficient and Generalizable Framework:** VideoJAM's ability to achieve state-of-the-art motion coherence with minimal architectural modifications (just two linear layers) and without requiring additional training data (only fine-tuning on a small subset of existing data) makes it highly practical and adaptable. This efficiency means it can be readily integrated into existing pre-trained models, accelerating progress in the field.
- **Novel Inference Guidance:** The introduction of "Inner-Guidance" is a key methodological innovation. By dynamically leveraging the model's own evolving motion prediction as a guidance signal, it elegantly sidesteps the limitations of prior guidance mechanisms that assume static, independent conditions. This approach offers a powerful paradigm for self-correcting and refining generative processes.
- **Complementarity of Appearance and Motion:** The findings underscore a crucial insight: appearance and motion are not mutually exclusive or conflicting objectives. When effectively integrated, enhancing one (motion) can synergistically improve the other (perceived visual quality and text faithfulness), leading to a higher overall quality of generated video.

The **potential impact** of VideoJAM is substantial:
- **More Realistic Content Creation:** By enabling the generation of videos with more accurate and natural motion, VideoJAM can significantly elevate the quality of AI-generated content for various applications, including entertainment, advertising, game development, and digital art.
- **Foundation for Advanced World Models:** The paper notes that "video generation poses a unique challenge, requiring the modeling of both spatial interactions and temporal dynamics." By explicitly teaching models about motion, VideoJAM lays groundwork for more sophisticated "world models" that can not only generate visuals but also accurately simulate complex real-world interactions and physics. This could have profound implications for robotics, virtual reality, and scientific simulation.
- **Paving the Way for New Priors:** The success of integrating motion suggests a broader pathway for incorporating other "real-world priors" into generative models. Future work could extend this approach to explicitly encode complex physics, material properties, causality, or even abstract concepts like intent, further bridging the gap between generated content and real-world understanding.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper's insights into the limitations of pixel-based objectives and the effectiveness of joint representations could inspire new research directions in diffusion models and beyond, encouraging a shift towards objectives that more holistically capture the multi-faceted nature of real-world data.

The authors also include an **Impact Statement**, acknowledging the potential for misuse inherent in any content generation technology. They emphasize that VideoJAM does not introduce new specific risks beyond those already discussed in the research community for generative AI. This reflects a commitment to responsible AI development, advocating for the continuous development and application of tools to detect biases and mitigate malicious use cases, ensuring the safe and fair deployment of these powerful generative capabilities.
