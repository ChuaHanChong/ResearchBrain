# VideoJAM: Joint Appearance-Motion Representations for Enhanced Motion Generation in Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoJAM, a framework from Meta GenAI and Tel Aviv University, improves motion coherence and physical plausibility in generative video models through joint appearance-motion representations and a novel inner-guidance mechanism. Human evaluators preferred VideoJAM-30B's motion over Kling 1.5 in 72.7% of comparisons, demonstrating enhanced realism and overall video quality despite efficient fine-tuning on limited data.

## Problem

- Generative video models often struggle with motion coherence, dynamic interactions, and adherence to physical laws, leading to unnatural movements or physics violations.
- Existing models prioritize appearance fidelity due to the pixel reconstruction objective, neglecting temporal dynamics and physical plausibility.
- Issues include unnatural gaits, objects passing through each other, and severe deformations during complex actions.

## Method

- Introduced **VideoJAM**, a framework that instills an ==explicit motion prior== through a modified training objective and an ==inner-guidance== mechanism.
- Developed a ==joint appearance-motion representation== by modifying ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== architecture to jointly predict ==video== and ==optical flow latents== during training.
- Implemented ==Inner-Guidance== during inference, which dynamically leverages the model's self-predicted motion to guide generation towards temporal coherence.

## Results

- **VideoJAM** consistently outperformed open-source and proprietary models in motion coherence across benchmarks, with human evaluators preferring VideoJAM-30B's motion over **Kling 1.5** in **72.7%** of comparisons.
- It substantially improved motion coherence for its base DiT models, preferred by human raters in **82.0%** of cases for **DiT-4B** and **72.7%** for **DiT-30B** against the base models on **VideoJAM-bench**.
- The framework also enhanced overall perceived visual quality and text alignment, achieving these results with **efficient fine-tuning** on a smaller dataset and lower resolution than comparable models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conventional pixel-based reconstruction objectives bias generative video models towards appearance fidelity, causing them to neglect crucial temporal dynamics and motion coherence.
> - Learning a joint appearance-motion representation and using a dynamic self-guidance mechanism enables models to intrinsically acquire a strong motion prior.
> - Effectively integrating appearance and motion learning is complementary, leading to improvements in both motion coherence and overall visual quality/text faithfulness.
