# EditDuet: A Multi-Agent System for Video Non-Linear Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> EditDuet automates video non-linear editing with two LLM agents. An Editor uses timeline tools (search, add, remove, move clips) and a Critic gives natural-language feedback until it renders. Synthetic in-context demonstrations found by self-supervised exploration teach the agents to communicate. It cuts failures to 8.2% and beats retrieval and agent baselines on human and VLM-judged preference.

## Problem

- Earlier video editing work focused on retrieval or user interfaces and left the actual editing to the user, so the core task of assembling timelines from raw footage stayed manual.
- Single-agent or one-pass systems such as VisProg and Transcript2Video cannot track multiple constraints across steps or self-correct, producing incomplete outputs, repeated clips, and frequent failures.
- Multi-agent setups fail because Editor and Critic misread each other, hallucinating unsupported actions, and handcrafting demonstrations is infeasible; the creative task also has no ground truth and costly human evaluation.

## Method

- ==Editor-Critic architecture== on Llama3.1-8B with ==structured generation==: the Editor calls search, add, remove, swap, move and DONE tools; the Critic reads only the timeline and either gives feedback or triggers RENDER.
- ==Test-time in-context learning== of agent communication: Explorer, Labeler, Scorer and Self-Reflecting agents ==synthesize demonstrations==, keeping only score-5 examples, five each for Editor and Critic, with no fine-tuning.
- NLE environment with hierarchical ==TW-FINCH== clip segmentation, captions, shot type and camera motion labels, plus ==CLIP search==; a GPT-4o ==keyframe-grid judge== yields a ==PreferenceRate== metric for automatic evaluation.

## Results

- EditDuet reaches an **8.2%** failure rate, **89.8%** time coverage and **0.174** repetitions per sequence, versus VisProg at **34.8%** failure and **44.8%** coverage, and BAGEL at **14.3%** failure.
- Ablations: an Editor-only agent fails **23.8%** of runs, and adding the Critic without exploration fails **19.5%**. Exploration cuts system-breaking failures from **19.5%** to **8.2%**.
- Humans preferred EditDuet over Transcript2Video **86.9%**, VisProg **83.9%** and BAGEL **81.8%**. The GPT-4o judge agreed with human majority votes **80.6%** of the time, versus **78.7%** among humans (PABAK **0.61** vs **0.57**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting a creative task into specialized agents with narrow toolsets and a feedback loop beats one-pass or single-agent systems, because iterative self-correction is impossible in one pass.
> - Self-generated, score-filtered demonstrations can replace handcrafted examples for inter-agent communication, cutting hallucinated functions and unsupported feedback with no fine-tuning or human labeling.
> - For open-ended creative tasks without ground truth, a rubric-prompted VLM judge validated against human agreement can serve as a scalable proxy metric for evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of AI-assisted content creation, specifically targeting the automation of video non-linear editing (NLE). This domain has seen increasing interest due to the proliferation of video content across various platforms, from professional filmmaking to social media.

Traditionally, video NLE software, such as Adobe Premiere Pro, Avid Media Composer, and DaVinci Resolve, offers powerful tools but demands significant training and experience. Previous AI-assisted efforts in this area have largely focused on either facilitating video retrieval or improving user interfaces, leaving the core, creative decision-making of editing to the human user. For example, some approaches formulate NLE as a sequence modeling task (Leake et al., 2017) or a text-to-video alignment problem (Xiong et al., 2022), primarily focusing on selecting and arranging pre-defined clips. Other works introduce "point technologies" for specific editing sub-tasks like multi-camera alignment (Arev et al., 2014) or transition prediction (Guhan et al., 2025), or develop new user interfaces for human-AI interaction (Huber et al., 2019). While valuable, these methods often operate in constrained scenarios, lack broad user input adaptability, or fail to capture the iterative and subjective nature of the editing process.

EditDuet distinguishes itself by formulating video NLE as a *sequential decision-making process* and proposing a *multi-agent system* to automate the "core task of video editing." It directly addresses the limitations of single-pass or retrieval-only systems by introducing an iterative refinement loop. This approach draws significant inspiration from recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and their capabilities as intelligent agents, particularly in tool-use and complex task execution (Gupta and Kembhavi 2023; Surís et al. 2023). The paper also builds on the success of LLMs in iterative refinement (Madaan et al. 2024) and agentic workflows (Yao et al. 2022), extending these concepts to the visual domain of video editing.

Furthermore, the research tackles the inherent challenge of evaluating creative AI outputs, where ground truth is ambiguous and multiple valid solutions exist. By proposing and validating an "LLM-as-a-judge" metric, EditDuet contributes to the broader field of AI evaluation, demonstrating a scalable and reliable method for assessing subjective quality that correlates well with human preferences (Zheng et al. 2024).

In essence, EditDuet pushes the boundaries of AI-assisted video editing by moving beyond mere assistance to comprehensive, language-driven automation, adopting a sophisticated multi-agent, iterative paradigm, and providing a robust evaluation framework for this complex creative task. It represents a significant step towards more autonomous and intelligent creative AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **automate the core task of video non-linear editing (NLE) from high-level natural language instructions**, specifically focusing on generating B-roll sequences that complement an A-roll (voice-over or interview footage).

The motivation for this ambitious goal stems from several critical observations and challenges in current video production workflows:

- **Complexity and Inaccessibility of Professional NLE Software:** Modern NLE applications are incredibly powerful but demand extensive training and experience, creating a significant barrier for aspiring content creators, small businesses, or even seasoned professionals facing tight deadlines. The authors envision a more accessible workflow where users can simply articulate their creative vision in plain English.
- **The Iterative Nature of Video Editing:** Video NLE is inherently an iterative process (Murch 2001), involving continuous refinement, feedback, and adjustments. Most existing AI solutions for video editing are single-pass or retrieval-based, failing to capture this fundamental aspect. A key motivation for EditDuet is to build a system that can engage in iterative refinement, exploring solutions and progressively improving them based on feedback.
- **Handling Large Shooting Ratios:** In documentary filmmaking, reality TV, and other genres, the amount of raw footage shot often dwarfs the final edited content (e.g., 100:1 shooting ratio). Manually sifting through hundreds of minutes of footage is time-consuming and tedious. An automated system could significantly expedite this process.
- **Need for Versatile Co-Creation Modalities:** The ideal system should offer flexibility, supporting everything from fully automated video assembly to providing intelligent assistance within a human-driven editing process.
- **Challenges in Interpreting Multimodal Information:** Automating NLE requires overcoming several technical hurdles:
 - **Understanding the Video Collection:** The system must comprehend the content, style (e.g., cinematographic shot types, camera motion), and artistic intent (e.g., pacing, mood) conveyed in raw footage.
 - **Proficiency with NLE Tools:** The AI needs to "think" like a professional editor, understanding how and when to use various editing operations (e.g., trimming, cutting, sequencing).
 - **Natural Language Understanding:** Interpreting high-level, often subjective, natural language instructions that include domain-specific vocabulary and artistic directives is crucial.
 - **Effective Multi-Agent Communication:** For complex, open-ended tasks, a single agent often struggles with managing multiple constraints and long-term planning. The motivation for a multi-agent system is to specialize roles (e.g., Editor and Critic) to handle different aspects of the task, requiring robust communication between them.
- **Lack of Ground Truth and Scalable Evaluation for Creative Tasks:** Evaluating the "quality" of an edited video is highly subjective, making it difficult to collect ground truth data for training and evaluation. Human preference studies are reliable but costly and unscalable. This motivates the development of an automated, yet human-aligned, evaluation metric.

In summary, EditDuet seeks to democratize and accelerate video editing by creating an intelligent, autonomous system capable of understanding complex creative requests, operating professional-level editing tools, and iteratively refining its output, all while addressing the inherent challenges of multimodal interpretation and subjective evaluation in creative AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EditDuet is designed as a multi-agent system that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) for iterative non-linear video editing. Its architecture comprises an NLE Environment, an Editor Agent, a Critic Agent, a novel approach for In-Context Learning (ICL) to facilitate multi-agent communication, and a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based evaluation framework.

#### 3.1. Non-Linear Editing Environment (Ω)

The environment simulates a professional NLE software interface, providing agents with observations and tools to manipulate a video timeline.
- **Inputs:**
 - **A-roll (A):** The primary audio/video track, typically an interview or voice-over, for which B-roll is to be created.
 - **Video Collection (V):** A large repository of raw footage.
- **Observations (o):** Agents receive a 4-tuple observation:
 - `o_A`: Text transcription of the A-roll audio.
 - `o_V`: A summarized description of the entire video collection, generated by Llama3.1-70B-Instruct from hierarchical video segment descriptions.
 - `o_search`: Search results (up to five video sub-clips) returned by a `search_collection` tool.
 - `τ`: The current state of the NLE timeline, an ordered sequence of video sub-clips.
- **Video Collection Processing:** Raw videos are hierarchically segmented into sub-clips using TW-FINCH clustering. Each sub-clip is enriched with metadata:
 - Duration, start/end times.
 - Content description (captions generated by Llava-NeXt from 16 frames).
 - Cinematographic shot type (e.g., close-up, wide shot) using a MobileNet V3 classifier.
 - Camera motion classification (e.g., static, zoom-in, panoramic) using an LSTM classifier.
- **Search Engine:** A CLIP-similarity-based search engine allows agents to query the video collection with natural language, returning relevant sub-clips. This mimics how a human editor navigates large video libraries.

#### 3.2. Editor Agent (E)

The Editor Agent is responsible for executing concrete editing actions on the timeline based on natural language feedback from the Critic.
- **LLM Backbone:** Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, enhanced with structured generation to ensure valid function calls.
- **Actions (API functions):** The Editor has access to a suite of typical NLE operations:
 - `search_collection(query_string)`: Queries the video collection for relevant clips.
 - `add_to_timeline(video_id, index, start_time, end_time)`: Inserts a specified sub-clip into the timeline.
 - `remove_from_timeline(index)`: Deletes a clip from the timeline.
 - `switch_clip_positions(index_k, index_l)`: Swaps two clips.
 - `move_clip(source_index, target_index)`: Relocates a clip within the timeline.
 - `DONE()`: Signals to the Critic that it has completed its current task based on the feedback.

#### 3.3. Critic Agent (C)

The Critic Agent acts as an overseer, evaluating the Editor's progress against the initial user request and providing iterative feedback or signaling completion.
- **LLM Backbone:** Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, also using structured generation.
- **Actions:**
 - `give_feedback(feedback_string)`: Provides natural language instructions and suggestions to the Editor for improving the timeline. This feedback must be actionable and guide the Editor toward the user's request.
 - `RENDER()`: Signals that the current timeline fully satisfies the user request and should be rendered as the final output.

#### 3.4. In-Context Learning (ICL) of Multi-Agent Communication

A critical component of EditDuet is a self-supervised exploration process to generate synthetic in-context examples, addressing the challenge of effective communication and "hallucinations" between agents. This process does not involve fine-tuning the models.
- **Motivation:** Manually crafting diverse and high-quality interaction examples is infeasible. This exploration helps agents learn appropriate feedback types and how to interpret/act on them.
- **Two-Stage Exploration:**
 1. **Stage 1: Editor Demonstrations (Tool Usage & Feedback Following):**
 - **Editor Explorer:** Randomly takes actions to modify a timeline.
 - **Editor Labeler:** Generates natural language feedback that *could have* elicited the Explorer's actions.
 - **Editor Scorer:** Scores the alignment and efficiency of the Explorer's actions given the Labeler's feedback (1-5).
 - **Self-Reflecting Editor:** Refines actions for scores of 4 to achieve optimal demonstrations.
 - Only perfect-score (5) examples are kept as synthetic demonstrations for the Editor Agent.
 2. **Stage 2: Critic Demonstrations (Guiding Editor for B-roll):**
 - **Critic Explorer:** Interacts with the (Stage 1-trained) Editor Agent, iteratively providing feedback until `RENDER()` is called.
 - **Critic Labeler:** Generates a user request that *would have* led to the Critic Explorer's feedback history and the final timeline.
 - **Critic Scorer:** Scores the final timeline against the generated user request (1-5).
 - Only perfect-score (5) examples are kept as synthetic demonstrations for the Critic Agent.

#### 3.5. Automatic Non-Linear Editing Evaluation

Given the subjective nature of video editing, the authors propose an automated evaluation framework using a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to approximate human preferences.
- **NLE Judge (J):** GPT-4o is employed as the VLM. It takes the user request and two timelines (represented as a grid of keyframes from sub-clip midpoints along with durations) and outputs its preferred choice. This allows it to assess visual coherence, relevance, pacing, and overall aesthetic quality.
- **Metric:** `PreferenceRate` quantifies how often one method's output is preferred over another's by the judge.
- **Validation:** A user study with 35 participants showed an 80.6% agreement between the GPT-4o judge and human preferences, comparable to the 78.7% agreement among human evaluators themselves (PABAK Kappa of 0.61 for judge-human vs. 0.57 for human-human). This validation makes the automatic judge a reliable and scalable evaluation tool.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EditDuet and its comparative analysis against baselines and ablations reveal its superior performance in automating video non-linear editing.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Dataset and Criteria

- **Dataset:** EditStock, a collection of real-world raw footage and final cuts from 56 filmmaking projects. The evaluation focused on five documentary titles ("The Scramble King," "The Rock Climber," "The Ovens of Cappoquin," "Shores of this Bay," and "Built by Life"), with 1458 minutes of raw video condensed into 21.5 minutes of B-roll sequences (up to 1.25 minutes each, comprising up to 27 sub-clips). Each sequence was manually annotated with high-level user prompts.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Preference Rate:** (Human and Automatic) – Percentage of times a method's output is preferred by humans or the VLM-based judge.
 - **Time Coverage:** Ratio `min(desired_duration, actual_duration) / max(desired_duration, actual_duration)`, aiming for 100%.
 - **Repetitions:** Average number of sub-clips with at least 80% overlap within the output sequence (lower is better).
 - **Failure Rate:** Percentage of runs where a system fails to render a valid video (e.g., invalid function calls, out-of-bounds indices, file hallucinations).

#### 4.2. Baselines and Competing Methods

- **Transcript2Video (T2V) [Xiong et al. 2022]:** A non-LLM, retrieval-based method adapted for duration matching using CLIP similarity.
- **VisProg [Gupta and Kembhavi 2023]:** An LLM-based method that extracts executable programs for vision tasks, extended to use NLE functions in a single-pass manner.
- **BAGEL [Murty et al. 2024]:** An agentic approach that uses a single Editor agent augmented with demonstrations from exploration.
- **Ablations:**
 - **Editor Only:** A single Editor agent without a Critic.
 - **Editor Critic:** The multi-agent Editor-Critic system, but *without* the proposed self-supervised exploration phase for multi-agent communication.

- **Overall Performance:** EditDuet consistently outperforms all baselines and ablations across most metrics. It achieves the highest human and automatic preference rates, indicating that its outputs are consistently judged as higher quality and more aligned with user requests.
- **Failure Rate:** EditDuet's failure rate of 8.2% is the lowest among all LLM-based methods. This is a significant improvement over VisProg (34.8%), Editor Only (23.8%), and Editor Critic (19.5%), demonstrating the robustness provided by the multi-agent design and, critically, the self-supervised exploration for communication. T2V has a 0.0% failure rate as it’s a non-LLM retrieval method that doesn't "fail" in the same way (it always produces an output, albeit potentially a poor one).
- **Time Coverage:** EditDuet achieves excellent time coverage (89.8%), closely matching the target duration, which is crucial for satisfying user requests. This is significantly better than VisProg (44.8%) and Editor Only (68.5%). T2V achieves slightly higher coverage (92.6%) but at the cost of high repetitions and low quality.
- **Repetitions:** EditDuet exhibits the lowest average number of repetitions (0.174), indicating that it produces diverse and non-redundant B-roll sequences. This is a major advantage over T2V (2.696 repetitions), which frequently repeats clips due to its retrieval-only nature.
- **Ablation Insights:**
 - **The Critic's Role:** Comparing "Editor Only" (23.8% failure, 68.5% coverage) to "Editor Critic" (19.5% failure, 82.7% coverage) highlights the benefit of the Critic agent. The Critic's iterative feedback significantly improves the timeline's quality and constraint satisfaction, even without explicit communication training.
 - **Importance of Exploration:** The most striking finding is the impact of the self-supervised exploration for multi-agent communication. "Editor Critic" (without exploration) has a 19.5% failure rate and only 29.4% automatic preference, whereas "EditDuet" (with exploration) drastically reduces the failure rate to 8.2% and boosts automatic preference to 77.8%. This strongly validates that teaching agents *how to communicate effectively* is paramount for complex, collaborative tasks, not just giving them tools.
- **Qualitative Results:** Example outputs confirm that EditDuet produces B-roll sequences with better aesthetic quality, appropriate pacing, and closer semantic alignment to user requests compared to baselines. T2V often outputs irrelevant or low-quality footage, while VisProg struggles with repetitions and sometimes generates empty timelines due to its single-pass nature. Agentic approaches (BAGEL, EditDuet) fare better, but EditDuet is clearly superior due to its iterative refinement and robust inter-agent communication.

#### 4.4. Limitations

The authors transparently acknowledge limitations:
- While exploration significantly reduces system-breaking failures (from 19.5% to 8.2%), some issues like function hallucination (agents inventing non-existent tools) and unsupported feedback (Critic requesting actions the Editor cannot perform) persist.
- The current NLE environment is simplified, lacking advanced features like transitions, pixel-level content editing, or audio manipulation.
- Current VLM context sizes limit the use of full VLMs as agent backbones, necessitating a text-based representation of video content for the LLM agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EditDuet represents a significant advancement in the field of AI-assisted content creation, with substantial scientific and practical implications.

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Novel Multi-Agent Architecture for NLE:** The work is pioneering in formulating the complex, iterative task of non-linear video editing as a multi-agent decision-making process. By specializing LLM agents into an "Editor" and a "Critic," the system effectively deconstructs the problem, allowing each agent to focus on specific sub-tasks and constraints.
- **Innovative Multi-Agent Communication Strategy:** The self-supervised exploration process for generating in-context learning examples is a crucial scientific contribution. It addresses a fundamental challenge in multi-agent systems: how to enable effective and actionable communication between specialized agents without extensive manual engineering or fine-tuning. The results clearly demonstrate that this approach drastically improves agent robustness and output quality.
- **Validating LLM-as-a-Judge for Creative Tasks:** The successful validation of GPT-4o as an automatic NLE judge, demonstrating high correlation with human preferences, is a key methodological contribution. This provides a scalable, cost-effective, and reliable evaluation metric for subjective creative tasks, which has been a major bottleneck in AI research for domains like image/video generation and editing.
- **Bridging Vision and Language for Complex Action:** EditDuet effectively bridges the gap between high-level natural language understanding and low-level, tool-based visual manipulation. It demonstrates how LLMs, even without native visual processing, can orchestrate complex visual tasks by interpreting structured visual metadata and executing predefined functions.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Democratization of Video Editing:** The most immediate and profound impact lies in making professional-quality video editing accessible to a much broader audience. By allowing users to articulate their creative vision in natural language, EditDuet could empower amateur filmmakers, social media content creators, small businesses, and educators to produce compelling videos without needing extensive training in complex software. This lowers the barrier to entry for video content creation, fostering greater creativity and participation.
- **Assistant for Professional Editors:** While not replacing human creativity, EditDuet can serve as a powerful assistant for professional editors. It can automate tedious, repetitive tasks (e.g., sifting through hundreds of hours of B-roll, assembling first drafts based on general directives), allowing human editors to focus on the more artistic, nuanced, and culturally sensitive aspects of storytelling. This can significantly accelerate production workflows and free up creative bandwidth.
- **Foundation for Future Creative AI Tools:** The multi-agent, iterative refinement paradigm, coupled with the robust evaluation methodology, establishes a strong blueprint for other complex creative generation tasks. This approach could be adapted to areas like 3D modeling, graphic design, music composition, or interactive narrative generation, where human-like iterative feedback and multi-faceted problem-solving are crucial.
- **Advancing Human-AI Collaboration:** EditDuet exemplifies a sophisticated form of human-AI co-creation, where the AI interprets high-level intent and executes the technical steps, while the human provides the overarching creative direction and ultimate approval. This model has the potential to redefine how humans interact with AI in creative domains.
- **New Research Avenues:** The authors themselves highlight several exciting future directions:
 - **Expanding the Editing Environment:** Integrating more sophisticated NLE features (transitions, pixel-level edits, audio mixing) into the agent's action space. This would require designing new tools and possibly specialized agents.
 - **Leveraging Advanced VLMs:** As Vision-Language Models become more powerful and capable of handling larger video contexts, they could be directly integrated as agent backbones, potentially eliminating the need for extensive metadata pre-processing and enabling more nuanced visual understanding.
 - **Reinforcement Learning for NLE:** The formulation of NLE as a decision process, combined with AI-based evaluation, opens the door for using reinforcement learning to train agents that can autonomously learn optimal editing strategies directly from user preferences or expert demonstrations.

In conclusion, EditDuet represents a significant leap forward in bringing autonomous, intelligent agents to the creative domain of video editing. Its robust performance, innovative multi-agent communication, and practical evaluation methodology set a new standard for AI-powered creative tools and lay crucial groundwork for future advancements in human-AI collaboration for content generation.
