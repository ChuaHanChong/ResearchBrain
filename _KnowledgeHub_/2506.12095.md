# DoublyAware: Dual Planning and Policy Awareness for Temporal Difference Learning in Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Texas at Arlington, TU Darmstadt, TU Wien, and Austrian Institute of Technology developed DoublyAware, a framework that addresses planning and policy uncertainties in TD-MPC for humanoid locomotion by integrating conformal prediction and Group-Relative Policy Optimization. This approach enabled the Unitree H1-2 humanoid to achieve faster convergence and higher episode returns on various locomotion tasks in HumanoidBench.

## Problem

- Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) for humanoid locomotion is hindered by pervasive aleatoric (environmental) and epistemic (model/policy) uncertainties, impacting exploration efficiency and learning stability.
- Current Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) approaches exhibit poor sample efficiency, unstable policy updates, and compounding errors in high-dimensional humanoid control.
- Existing robot learning methods often address uncertainty in a monolithic way, failing to differentiate and mitigate its distinct components effectively.

## Method

- **DoublyAware**, an extension of the TD-MPC framework, explicitly decomposes total uncertainty into planning (aleatoric) and policy (epistemic) components.
- Conformal prediction is integrated into the planning phase to statistically filter candidate trajectories, providing distribution-free validity and robustly handling aleatoric uncertainty.
- ==Group-Relative Policy Constraint (GRPC)== with an adaptive trust-region is used in the learning phase to ensure stable and data-efficient policy updates by leveraging relative action preferences and controlling policy divergence.

## Results

- DoublyAware demonstrated faster convergence and higher episode returns on most humanoid locomotion tasks from HumanoidBench, including standing, walking, running, and navigating poles.
- Ablation studies showed that the synergistic combination of conformal trajectory prediction and group-relative policy constraint yielded superior performance and data efficiency.
- Qualitative evaluation revealed DoublyAware generated stable, coherent, and kinodynamically feasible motions for complex tasks, outperforming baselines which often produced unstable or infeasible behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing overall uncertainty into distinct planning (aleatoric) and policy (epistemic) components enables more effective and targeted mitigation strategies in MBRL.
> - Conformal prediction offers a robust, distribution-free statistical mechanism for filtering high-confidence trajectories, crucial for safe planning under environmental stochasticity.
> - Utilizing group-relative policy optimization with an adaptive trust-region leads to more stable and sample-efficient policy updates by reducing variance in gradients and bounding policy divergence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning, particularly for complex systems like humanoids, frequently encounters challenges related to uncertainty. Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) offers a framework for robots to learn dynamic behaviors by leveraging learned models of the environment. However, the inherent stochasticity of real-world environments (aleatoric uncertainty) and the incomplete knowledge within learned models and policies (epistemic uncertainty) can impede efficient exploration, learning stability, and the generation of robust, safe behaviors. This is particularly pronounced in high-dimensional action spaces with complex contact dynamics, characteristic of humanoid locomotion.

Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) has emerged as a promising approach within MBRL, combining the short-horizon optimization capabilities of Model Predictive Control (MPC) with the sample-efficient, value-driven learning of Temporal Difference (TD) methods. Prior iterations like TD-MPC and TD-MPC2 have demonstrated success in continuous control tasks by learning latent world models and integrating TD learning with MPC-style planning. Despite these advancements, existing TD-MPC frameworks often face limitations such as poor sample efficiency, unstable policy updates, compounding errors during planning, and biases in learning, especially when dealing with the high-dimensional control requirements of humanoid robots.

This work situates itself at the intersection of robust MBRL, uncertainty-aware planning, and stable policy optimization. It builds upon TD-MPC by explicitly addressing the dual nature of uncertainty. In uncertainty-aware planning, previous research has explored conformal prediction and information-theoretic decomposition to enhance robustness. Conformal prediction provides statistical guarantees for risk assessment in various robotic contexts, including learned manifold learning and motion planning under dynamic uncertainty. This work extends this by applying conformal prediction to latent trajectory selection within TD-MPC, specifically to manage aleatoric uncertainty in the planning phase.

Concurrently, the paper addresses challenges in policy-aware optimization for robot learning. Policy mismatch and bootstrapping error accumulation are known issues in off-policy reinforcement learning, particularly for long-horizon tasks. Methods like offline RL, actor regularization (e.g., LOOP), and in-sample learning techniques have been developed to mitigate distributional shifts and improve policy stability. This research contributes to this area by integrating Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with an adaptive trust-region, aiming to provide more stable and data-efficient policy updates by focusing on relative action preferences and bounding policy divergence.

By proposing a decomposition of uncertainty into distinct planning (aleatoric) and policy (epistemic) components and addressing each with tailored mechanisms (conformal prediction for planning and GRPO for policy learning), the paper aims to enhance the reliability and efficiency of TD-MPC-based humanoid locomotion learning beyond the capabilities of current state-of-the-art methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance robust robot learning for humanoid locomotion within model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) frameworks. The authors specifically aim to address the significant challenges posed by environmental stochasticity and the inherent uncertainties during the learning process.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing MBRL and TD-MPC approaches when applied to complex, high-dimensional humanoid locomotion tasks:

1. **Pervasiveness and Impact of Uncertainty:** Humanoid locomotion involves complex whole-body coordination, where observations and dynamics can be inherently noisy and stochastic (aleatoric uncertainty), and the learned models or policies possess incomplete knowledge (epistemic uncertainty). This dual nature of uncertainty can hinder efficient exploration, destabilize learning, and compromise the safety and robustness of robotic behaviors. Traditional MBRL methods often treat uncertainty monolithically, which may not be sufficient for comprehensive mitigation.
2. **Limitations of Existing TD-MPC:** While TD-MPC and its variants like TD-MPC2 have demonstrated capabilities in short-horizon decision-making through real-time trajectory optimization, they frequently suffer from drawbacks in high-dimensional control settings. These include:
 - **Poor Sample Efficiency:** The extensive exploration required in complex action spaces can necessitate large amounts of interaction data, slowing down the learning process.
 - **Unstable Policy Updates:** Policy networks, especially during early training, can be prone to large, destabilizing updates due to noise or out-of-distribution actions.
 - **Compounding Errors:** Errors in learned dynamics models can accumulate over longer planning horizons, leading to inaccurate predictions and sub-optimal control.
 - **Learning Biases:** Policies might struggle with distributional shifts between actions sampled during planning and actions executed by the learned policy, affecting generalization.
3. **Need for Distinct Uncertainty Handling:** The authors argue that aleatoric uncertainty (from environmental randomness and system dynamics, e.g., ground contact, observation noise) cannot be eliminated and requires robust planning. Conversely, epistemic uncertainty (from the policy network's incomplete knowledge) can be reduced through targeted exploration and sophisticated learning strategies. Explicitly decomposing and addressing these distinct types of uncertainty is hypothesized to lead to more effective and reliable robot learning.
4. **Leveraging Advanced Techniques:** The work is motivated by the potential of integrating advanced statistical and optimization techniques:
 - **Conformal Prediction:** To provide distribution-free statistical guarantees for filtering candidate trajectories in the planning phase, making trajectory selection robust against stochastic dynamics without strong distributional assumptions.
 - **Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** To enhance the learning phase with a policy-aware optimization strategy that uses relative action preferences and adaptive trust regions, aiming for more stable and efficient policy updates.

In summary, the key objectives articulated by the authors are threefold:
1. To formally decompose the overall uncertainty in MBRL for humanoid locomotion into planning uncertainty (aleatoric) and policy uncertainty (epistemic) and propose distinct mechanisms to address each.
2. To integrate these mechanisms—a planning-aware component (conformal quantile filtering) with a policy-aware learning optimizer (GRPO with adaptive trust-region)—to create a unified framework, DoublyAware. This framework aims to enable uncertainty-calibrated trajectory filtering and leverage policy rollouts as informative learnable priors.
3. To evaluate the efficacy of DoublyAware on standard humanoid locomotion tasks, demonstrating improvements in sample efficiency, convergence speed, and kinodynamically feasible motions compared to existing reinforcement learning baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, DoublyAware, extends the Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) framework by explicitly decomposing and addressing two distinct types of uncertainty: planning uncertainty (aleatoric) and policy uncertainty (epistemic). This is achieved through a principled combination of conformal prediction for trajectory filtering in the planning phase and Group-Relative Policy Constraint (GRPC) optimization with an adaptive trust-region in the learning phase.

**A. Vanilla Planning with Model-Predictive Control (TD-MPC Foundation)**
The foundation of DoublyAware is a standard MBRL formulation for infinite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), aiming to learn a policy $\Pi_\theta(s)$ that maximizes the expected discounted cumulative reward. The underlying TD-MPC framework leverages an MPC-like local trajectory optimizer, specifically MPPI (Model Predictive Path Integral), within a learned latent dynamics model.
During planning, action sequences of length *H* are sampled as latent trajectories. Each trajectory's cumulative return is estimated using a trained value estimator $V_\theta(z_t, a_t)$, composed of a reward estimator $R_\theta(z_t, a_t)$ and a Q-value estimator $Q_\theta(z_H, a_H)$. MPPI samples actions from a Gaussian distribution, which is iteratively updated based on the returns of sampled trajectories using a softmax weighting scheme. This *H*-step look-ahead policy aims to iteratively maximize the first step's expected return.

**B. Planning as Exploration with Conformal Trajectories (Addressing Aleatoric Uncertainty)**
To enhance the robustness and safety of planning exploration, DoublyAware integrates conformal prediction into the MPPI planner. This mechanism statistically filters candidate trajectories without making parametric assumptions about the return distribution, addressing aleatoric uncertainty.

1. **Trajectory Generation:** At each planning step, two sets of candidate latent trajectories are generated:
 - **Policy-guided trajectories ($A_\pi$):** Sampled from the current policy network by simulating trajectories under the learned world model. These act as prior knowledge, reflecting learned behavior.
 - **MPPI-sampled trajectories ($A_{mppi}$):** Generated via the MPPI planner, exploring the action space heuristically and reweighting based on expected cumulative rewards.
2. **Nonconformity Score Calculation:** The core idea is to quantify the agreement between candidate trajectories (from MPPI) and prior trajectories (from the policy network). Nonconformity scores $\bar{s}^{(i)}$ are computed directly from the normalized costs (estimated using the TD-based cost function $V_\theta(z, a)$) of all trajectories in the combined set $A = \{A_\pi \cup A_{mppi}\}$. A higher cost corresponds to a higher nonconformity score.
3. **Conformal Quantile Filtering:** A quantile threshold $\hat{q}$ is determined from the set of all nonconformity scores at a pre-defined risk level $(1-\alpha)$. This threshold defines an acceptance region.
4. **Prediction Set Construction:** The conformal prediction set $A_{conformal}$ is constructed by including only those trajectories whose nonconformity scores $\bar{s}^{(j)}$ are less than or equal to $\hat{q}$. This set contains "statistically significant" trajectories.
5. **Guarantees:** Conformal prediction provides distribution-free validity, ensuring that the true optimal trajectory is within the prediction set with a probability of at least $(1-\alpha)$. Even with an imperfect (weak) value estimator that improves over time, the method maintains asymptotic weak validity, as long as the prior model improves on a sub-linear teaching schedule. The filtered trajectories are then used to update the MPPI's action distribution parameters ($\mu_j, \sigma_j$).

**C. Learning with Group Relative Policy Constraint (Addressing Epistemic Uncertainty)**
To enhance policy stability and sample efficiency during the learning phase, DoublyAware adopts and improves upon Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which addresses epistemic uncertainty arising from the policy network's incomplete knowledge.

1. **Group-Based Action Sampling:** For a given state $s_i$, a group of $G$ actions $\{a_1^i, \ldots, a_G^i\}$ is sampled from the current policy $\pi_\theta(s)$.
2. **Soft Attention Advantage Scores:** Instead of relying on absolute advantage estimates, which can be sensitive to errors, GRPO computes group-based soft attention advantage scores $A_i(q)$ for each action. These scores are derived by applying a softmax function to the Q-values $\{q_1^i, \ldots, q_G^i\}$ of the sampled actions, effectively providing a relative preference distribution across the group. This approach is demonstrated to yield smaller variance in policy gradients compared to standard normalized advantages, leading to more stable policy updates.
3. **Improved GRPO Objective:** The policy objective $L_\pi(\theta)$ combines the soft attention advantage scores with the log-probability of actions under the policy: $L_{GRPO}^\pi(\theta) = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^G A_i(q) \log \pi_\theta(a_i | s)$.
4. **Adaptive Trust-Region Regularization:** To prevent large, destabilizing policy updates and ensure consistency, a residual-style regularization term is added to the policy objective. This term is based on the KL divergence between the current policy $\pi_\theta$ and an MPPI-based prior $\mu(a|s)$ (derived from the MPPI distributions used in planning). This effectively imposes an adaptive trust-region, bounding the divergence from prior behavior and stabilizing policy learning. The combined objective is $L_\pi(\theta) = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^G A_i(q) \log \pi_\theta(a_i | s) + \beta \log \mu(a | s)$, where $\beta$ is a weighting coefficient.
5. **Concurrent Model Optimization:** Simultaneously, the latent dynamics model $d_\theta$, encoder $h_\theta$, reward network $R_\theta$, and value network $Q_\theta$ are optimized. The model objective consists of mean squared error terms for predicting the next latent state, the reward, and the Q-value (bootstrapped with the target Q-value of the next state and policy output).

The overall methodology integrates these components such that conformal prediction refines the planning trajectories, which then inform the policy learning process through GRPC, leading to a synergistic approach for robust and efficient humanoid locomotion learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DoublyAware was conducted on the Unitree 26-DoF H1-2 humanoid using the HumanoidBench locomotion suite, which comprises nine distinct tasks. The performance was compared against several reinforcement learning baselines: SAC, BC-SAC, AWAC, TD-MPC2, and TD-M(PC)$^2$. The analysis focused on episode returns (quantitative), qualitative aspects of motion, and the individual contributions of the uncertainty-aware modules through ablation studies.

**A. Episode Returns of Locomotion Tasks (Quantitative Performance)**
DoublyAware demonstrated advantages in terms of convergence speed and overall reward acquisition across a range of locomotion tasks:
- **Foundational Tasks:** For tasks such as standing, walking, running, and sitting, DoublyAware achieved significantly faster convergence. For instance, standing was learned in fewer than 100,000 training iterations, walking in approximately 300,000, running in 450,000, and sitting in 200,000 iterations. Competing baselines exhibited slower convergence and often struggled to achieve comparable performance within the same training budget, particularly for walking and running.
- **Complex Coordination Tasks:** On tasks requiring more precise whole-body coordination, such as navigating around standing poles, hurdling over obstacles, and walking across inclined slides, DoublyAware maintained a consistent performance advantage. It surpassed a reward threshold of 600 for pole navigation within 500,000 iterations, achieved approximately 150 for hurdling, and around 300 for walking over slides. While other methods eventually learned these tasks, their convergence rates were slower, and performance variability was higher.
- **Highly Challenging Tasks:** DoublyAware exhibited relative underperformance on the most challenging tasks, specifically crawling through a tunnel and stair-climbing, compared to other methods in terms of reward acquisition within the given training iterations. However, most baselines also struggled with these tasks.
- **Data Efficiency:** Overall, DoublyAware showed improved training efficiency and data efficiency compared to the competing baselines.

**B. Ablation Studies on Uncertainty-Aware Modules**
An ablation analysis was performed to evaluate the individual and combined contributions of conformal trajectory prediction (CP) and group-relative policy constraint (GRPC) within the TD-MPC framework. Three variants were compared: TD-M(PC)$^2$ (baseline), TD-M(PC)$^2$ + CP, and DoublyAware (TD-M(PC)$^2$ + CP + GRPC).
- **Complementary Benefits:** DoublyAware (combining both CP and GRPC) consistently outperformed the ablated variants in terms of final performance and sample efficiency across most locomotion tasks, including standing, walking, pole navigation, hurdling, and walking over slides. This suggests that CP and GRPC offer complementary benefits.
- **Specific Contributions:** CP improved robustness and uncertainty-awareness during trajectory exploration, leading to better generalization across some task domains. GRPC enhanced policy-aware optimization, contributing to more stable and data-efficient learning. The integration of both components in DoublyAware led to a more robust and data-efficient locomotion policy.
- **Limitations:** In complex coordination tasks like crawling and stair-climbing, TD-M(PC)$^2$ + CP sometimes performed better than the full DoublyAware, indicating that while CP alone offers advantages in specific challenging scenarios, its combination with GRPC might not provide a universal advantage across all task complexities, or tuning may be required.

**C. Visualization of Sequential Behaviors (Qualitative Results)**
Qualitative evaluation of the generated behaviors revealed further insights:
- **Stable Poses:** DoublyAware enabled the H1-2 robot to achieve stable and plausible poses for standing and sitting, demonstrating coordinated control of limbs.
- **Coherent Locomotion:** For walking and running tasks, DoublyAware consistently resulted in coherent forward locomotion. In contrast, some baselines like TD-M(PC)$^2$ induced backward locomotion or dynamically infeasible motions.
- **Navigating Obstacles:** In the slide-walking task, DoublyAware guided the robot to progressively ascend and reach the top of the incline. For navigating through poles, it generated trajectories that effectively avoided collisions while maintaining forward progress, a task where competing methods often failed or got stuck.
- **Challenging Task Limitations:** DoublyAware struggled with crawling through a tunnel, exhibiting sub-optimal postures and stalled progress, similar to most other baselines. Only SAC and TD-MPC2 were noted to sufficiently complete this task. Similarly, for hurdling and stair-climbing, DoublyAware and other baselines failed to teach the robot to accomplish these tasks.

**Summary of Solving Abilities (Table I):**
The combined quantitative and qualitative results indicate that DoublyAware successfully solves most locomotion tasks (standing, walking, running, sitting, navigating poles, walking on inclined slides). However, like most other methods evaluated, it was unable to sufficiently solve crawling, hurdling, and stair-climbing tasks, which are characterized by highly complex full-body movements and dynamic skill refinement requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DoublyAware, a framework designed to enhance the robustness and sample efficiency of humanoid locomotion learning within the Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) paradigm. Its significance and potential impact stem from its structured approach to addressing inherent uncertainties in complex robotic systems.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
1. **Principled Uncertainty Decomposition:** The work's primary contribution is the explicit decomposition of total uncertainty into distinct planning (aleatoric) and policy (epistemic) components. This reframing allows for targeted mitigation strategies, moving beyond monolithic uncertainty handling in MBRL. This can lead to a more nuanced understanding of where failures occur and how to address them specifically.
2. **Robust Planning with Conformal Prediction:** The integration of conformal prediction for trajectory filtering in the planning phase provides a method for statistically grounded, distribution-free risk assessment. This is significant because it allows the robot to select high-confidence trajectories robustly against environmental stochasticity and model inaccuracies, without relying on strong parametric assumptions about the underlying dynamics or reward distributions. This can enhance safety and reliability in real-world deployments where environmental conditions are unpredictable.
3. **Stable and Efficient Policy Learning:** The adoption and improvement of Group-Relative Policy Constraint (GRPC) with an adaptive trust-region directly addresses the challenges of policy instability and sample inefficiency in high-dimensional control. By learning from relative action preferences and regularizing policy updates, DoublyAware can achieve more stable and data-efficient learning. This is crucial for humanoid robots, where the cost and time associated with real-world training data collection are substantial.
4. **Demonstrated Performance on Humanoid Locomotion:** The evaluations on the Unitree 26-DoF H1-2 humanoid across the HumanoidBench tasks demonstrate practical improvements. DoublyAware exhibited faster convergence, higher episode returns, and generated more kinodynamically feasible and coherent motions for various locomotion tasks compared to several established baselines. This suggests that the proposed mechanisms are effective in practice for complex, whole-body control.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** By improving sample efficiency and convergence speed, DoublyAware could significantly reduce the time and computational resources required to train complex locomotion skills for humanoid robots. This facilitates faster development cycles for new robotic capabilities.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Safety:** The robust handling of planning uncertainty via conformal prediction contributes to more reliable decision-making, which is critical for humanoid robots operating in unstructured or human-proximate environments. Reduced policy instability further contributes to predictable and safer behaviors.
- **Broader Applicability of TD-MPC:** The work expands the applicability and performance of TD-MPC frameworks to more challenging, high-dimensional control problems like humanoid locomotion, which have historically posed significant difficulties for model-based approaches.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The explicit decomposition of uncertainty provides a new perspective for future research. It opens avenues for investigating more sophisticated methods for modeling and mitigating each uncertainty component, potentially leading to breakthroughs in tasks that DoublyAware currently struggles with (e.g., highly complex full-body coordination like crawling or stair-climbing). Further exploration of how to adapt the balance between planning and policy awareness for different task complexities or stages of learning could also be fruitful.
- **Methodological Transferability:** The principles of uncertainty decomposition, conformal filtering for planning, and group-relative policy optimization could be transferable to other complex robotic platforms or control domains beyond humanoid locomotion, wherever model uncertainty and high-dimensional action spaces are prevalent.

In conclusion, DoublyAware contributes to the scientific understanding of uncertainty management in MBRL and provides a practical framework for developing more capable and reliable humanoid robots.
