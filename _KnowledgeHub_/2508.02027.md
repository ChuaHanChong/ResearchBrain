# An Evolving Scenario Generation Method based on Dual-modal Driver Model Trained by Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-modal driver model, trained using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning by researchers at Tongji University, efficiently generates evolving safety-critical scenarios for autonomous vehicles. This model produces scenarios with over 85% fidelity to naturalistic driving data, while increasing test efficiency by 195% and complexity by up to 32.35% compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Current scenario-based autonomous vehicle (AV) testing methods, particularly predefined scenarios, restrict background vehicle (BV) interactions, limiting the generation of complex, dynamic safety-critical situations.
- Existing evolving scenario methods struggle to simultaneously achieve high behavioral fidelity in BVs and effective adversarial behavior against the subject vehicle (SV), often leading to either unrealistic collisions or inefficient testing.
- Single-vehicle adversarial models limit the complexity and diversity of generated safety-critical scenarios, failing to capture intricate multi-vehicle interactions common in real-world challenges.

## Method

- A Dual-Modal Driver Model (Dual-DM) for background vehicles (BVs) is developed, capable of switching between non-adversarial (naturalistic) and adversarial driving behaviors.
- The adversarial modality focuses on progressively compressing the subject vehicle's (SV) drivable space through single or multi-vehicle cooperative actions, ensuring a controlled reaction time and avoiding irrational direct collisions.
- The Dual-DM is trained using a two-stage Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework, starting with basic driving capabilities and then refining adversarial behaviors in an SUT-in-the-loop environment with a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) architecture.

## Results

- The generated scenarios demonstrated high fidelity to naturalistic driving data (HighD dataset), with Jensen-Shannon divergence values of 0.0074 for vehicle velocity and 0.1323 for Time-to-Collision during lane changes, indicating over 85% similarity.
- Testing efficiency increased by 195% with Dual-DM, generating 429 safety-critical scenarios in 500 rounds (efficiency E=0.86) compared to 145 for Social-DRL (E=0.29) and 129 for Nilsson (E=0.26).
- Scenario complexity improved, achieving a C score of 0.45 for Dual-DM, which is 32.35% higher than the Nilsson model (C=0.34) and 12.5% higher than the Social-DRL model (C=0.40).
- Analysis of cooperative rewards and case studies confirmed the generation of diverse adversarial interaction patterns, including 23 distinct two-BV and 16 distinct three-BV position combinations, through progressive compression of the SV's drivable space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Balancing behavioral fidelity and adversarial intent within a single dual-modal driver model allows for the generation of realistic yet challenging safety-critical scenarios.
> - Multi-agent cooperative adversarial behaviors, learned via MARL, are crucial for creating complex and diverse safety-critical scenarios that involve intricate interactions among multiple vehicles.
> - Adversarial behavior that focuses on progressively compressing the SV's drivable space, rather than initiating direct collisions, yields more ecologically valid and valuable test cases for AV decision-making systems.
