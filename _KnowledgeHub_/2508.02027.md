# An Evolving Scenario Generation Method based on Dual-modal Driver Model Trained by Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-modal driver model, trained using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning by researchers at Tongji University, efficiently generates evolving safety-critical scenarios for autonomous vehicles. This model produces scenarios with over 85% fidelity to naturalistic driving data, while increasing test efficiency by 195% and complexity by up to 32.35% compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Current scenario-based autonomous vehicle (AV) testing methods, particularly predefined scenarios, restrict background vehicle (BV) interactions, limiting the generation of complex, dynamic safety-critical situations.
- Existing evolving scenario methods struggle to simultaneously achieve high behavioral fidelity in BVs and effective adversarial behavior against the subject vehicle (SV), often leading to either unrealistic collisions or inefficient testing.
- Single-vehicle adversarial models limit the complexity and diversity of generated safety-critical scenarios, failing to capture intricate multi-vehicle interactions common in real-world challenges.

## Method

- A Dual-Modal Driver Model (Dual-DM) for background vehicles (BVs) is developed, capable of switching between non-adversarial (naturalistic) and adversarial driving behaviors.
- The adversarial modality focuses on progressively compressing the subject vehicle's (SV) drivable space through single or multi-vehicle cooperative actions, ensuring a controlled reaction time and avoiding irrational direct collisions.
- The Dual-DM is trained using a two-stage Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework, starting with basic driving capabilities and then refining adversarial behaviors in an SUT-in-the-loop environment with a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) architecture.

## Results

- The generated scenarios demonstrated high fidelity to naturalistic driving data (HighD dataset), with Jensen-Shannon divergence values of 0.0074 for vehicle velocity and 0.1323 for Time-to-Collision during lane changes, indicating over 85% similarity.
- Testing efficiency increased by 195% with Dual-DM, generating 429 safety-critical scenarios in 500 rounds (efficiency E=0.86) compared to 145 for Social-DRL (E=0.29) and 129 for Nilsson (E=0.26).
- Scenario complexity improved, achieving a C score of 0.45 for Dual-DM, which is 32.35% higher than the Nilsson model (C=0.34) and 12.5% higher than the Social-DRL model (C=0.40).
- Analysis of cooperative rewards and case studies confirmed the generation of diverse adversarial interaction patterns, including 23 distinct two-BV and 16 distinct three-BV position combinations, through progressive compression of the SV's drivable space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Balancing behavioral fidelity and adversarial intent within a single dual-modal driver model allows for the generation of realistic yet challenging safety-critical scenarios.
> - Multi-agent cooperative adversarial behaviors, learned via MARL, are crucial for creating complex and diverse safety-critical scenarios that involve intricate interactions among multiple vehicles.
> - Adversarial behavior that focuses on progressively compressing the SV's drivable space, rather than initiating direct collisions, yields more ecologically valid and valuable test cases for AV decision-making systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The safety validation of autonomous vehicles (AVs), particularly their decision-making and planning systems, is a critical challenge. Scenario-based testing is a prominent methodology for addressing this challenge. Within scenario-based testing, two main approaches exist: testing based on predefined scenarios and testing based on evolving scenarios.

Predefined scenarios often involve defining a logical scenario and then sampling concrete test cases within its parameter space. A limitation of this approach is that background vehicles (BVs) typically follow a pre-scripted behavior, which limits their interactive capabilities with the subject vehicle (SV) under test. This can hinder the generation of complex, highly interactive, and safety-critical situations.

Evolving scenario methods, in contrast, deploy BVs controlled by sophisticated driver models within a continuous traffic environment. These models possess decision-making capabilities, allowing scenarios to dynamically evolve based on bidirectional interactions between the SV and BVs. The driving style of these driver models, especially their adversarial characteristics, significantly influences the efficiency and criticality of the generated scenarios.

Existing research in evolving scenario generation has explored various driver model types, including function-based models (e.g., IDM, MOBIL, Nilsson) and learning-based models (imitation learning, reinforcement learning). Function-based models offer interpretability but often simplify vehicle behavior, making it difficult to generate complex scenarios. Imitation learning models achieve high fidelity by learning from naturalistic driving data but are data-dependent and may lack versatility. Reinforcement learning (RL) models, while potentially lacking data guidance for fidelity, offer flexibility in designing reward functions to achieve specific testing objectives, such as adversarial behavior.

Within RL-based driver models, different dimensions exist: adversariality (non-adversarial vs. adversarial) and collaboration among BVs (single-vehicle non-cooperative vs. multi-vehicle cooperative). Non-adversarial models yield high fidelity but low critical scenario efficiency. Single-vehicle adversarial models improve criticality but may lack complexity and diversity. Multi-vehicle cooperative models can generate complex and diverse scenarios, but existing models often serve only the BVs' own driving ability rather than adversarial testing, or lack generalizability in complex situations.

This paper positions itself to address the gap in balancing behavioral fidelity with adversariality and generating complex, diverse safety-critical scenarios efficiently, especially those arising from multi-vehicle cooperative adversarial interactions. It builds upon the concept of multi-vehicle collaborative adversarial driver models, aiming to develop a more universally applicable model that achieves these goals without sacrificing realism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to design and implement an evolving scenario generation method based on a dual-modal driver model trained by Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL). This method aims to efficiently generate complex, diverse, and highly interactive safety-critical scenarios for testing the decision-making and planning systems of autonomous vehicles.

The motivation behind this work stems from several critical needs and limitations observed in current autonomous driving testing methodologies:

1. **Ensuring Safety of High-Level Autonomous Driving Systems:** Autonomous vehicles, particularly those operating at L3-L5 autonomy, rely heavily on their decision-making and planning systems. Deficiencies in these systems can lead to inappropriate behaviors in complex traffic, posing significant safety risks. Effective testing is therefore paramount to ensure the safety of these "brains" of AVs.
2. **Limitations of Predefined Scenarios:** Traditional scenario-based testing, which uses predefined logical scenarios, often restricts background vehicles to follow fixed "scripts." This approach prevents BVs from genuinely interacting with the Subject Vehicle (SV) under test, thus limiting the generation of dynamic and interactive safety-critical situations that might expose subtle system vulnerabilities.
3. **Balancing Fidelity and Adversariality in Evolving Scenarios:** While evolving scenarios allow for continuous interaction, existing driver models struggle to simultaneously achieve high behavioral fidelity (realism) and effective adversariality (challenging the SV). Overemphasis on adversarial behavior can lead to unrealistic, irrational collisions (e.g., BVs directly crashing into the SV), diminishing the testing value. Conversely, over-reliance on fidelity, without adversarial intent, results in scenarios indistinguishable from naturalistic driving data (NDD), yielding very low efficiency in generating safety-critical scenarios. The motivation is to find a balance where BVs behave realistically while still actively challenging the SV.
4. **Increasing Complexity and Diversity of Safety-Critical Scenarios:** Relying solely on single-vehicle adversarial behaviors limits the complexity and diversity of generated safety-critical situations. Many critical scenarios in the real world involve intricate interactions among multiple vehicles. The motivation is to enable multi-vehicle cooperative adversarial behaviors to generate more complex and varied scenarios that better reflect real-world challenges and comprehensively test the SV's decision-making system.
5. **Improving Testing Efficiency:** The goal is to maximize the rate at which safety-critical scenarios are generated within a given simulation time. By guiding the scenario evolution towards critical situations through adversarial interactions, the testing process can become more efficient, allowing for faster identification of system flaws.

To address these motivations, the authors propose developing a Dual-Modal Driver Model (Dual-DM) capable of switching between non-adversarial and adversarial driving modalities. This model is intended to:
- Make adversarial interactions with the Subject Under Test (SUT) through both single-vehicle and multi-vehicle cooperative behaviors.
- Generate safety-critical scenarios by progressively compressing the SV’s drivable space, rather than initiating irrational direct collisions, ensuring a controlled reaction time window for the SUT.
- Ensure the generated scenarios maintain high fidelity, complexity, and diversity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a Dual-Modal Driver Model (Dual-DM) and a two-stage Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) training framework for generating evolving safety-critical scenarios.

#### 3.1. Design of Dual-DM

Dual-DM functions as a Non-Player Character (NPC) model for background vehicles (BVs). It operates with two driving modalities:
- **Non-adversarial driving modality:** When a BV is far from the Subject Vehicle (SV) (controlled by the System Under Test, SUT), it makes decisions based on its own driving goals, exhibiting naturalistic behaviors.
- **Adversarial driving modality:** When a BV is within a defined "adversarial driving area" (a 45-meter longitudinal range around the SV, spanning five lateral lanes), it adopts confrontational behaviors. These behaviors can be single-vehicle or multi-vehicle cooperative. The adversarial strategy focuses on progressively compressing the SV's drivable space (forward and lateral lane-changing) while ensuring a controlled reaction time window for the SUT, avoiding direct, irrational collisions.

#### 3.2. Training Framework of Dual-DM

The training of Dual-DM involves a two-stage process:
- **Stage 1: Basic Driving Capabilities (Non-Adversarial Modality):** A Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) method, derived from previous work and based on an Actor-Critic structure, is used to train the driver model. This stage equips the model with fundamental single-vehicle driving skills such as tracking, car-following, and lane-changing, along with improved interaction ability through hierarchical training (level-k theory).
- **Stage 2: Adversarial Driving Modality:** Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is employed for this stage. It builds upon the pre-trained non-adversarial capabilities from Stage 1. This stage uses an SUT-in-the-loop approach, where the specific SUT being tested (e.g., the Stackelberg model) is connected to the training environment. The driver model learns adversarial behaviors by iteratively interacting with this SUT, dynamically updating its policy to achieve strong confrontation. The MARL architecture uses a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) structure. All agents share the same Actor and Critic networks and a shared cooperative reward. This design aims to guide cooperative behavior learning while efficiently utilizing training data from multiple agents.
The Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) algorithm is used for training in both stages.

#### 3.3. Dual-DM Formulation

- **Observation Space:** The model uses an ego-centric perspective, observing a 100m range ahead and 20m behind, across five lanes. This area is discretized into a 25x5 grid. The observation comprises five layers for each grid: spatial drivability (P), relative longitudinal velocity (Δvs), relative lateral velocity (Δvt), relative yaw angle (Δh), and an adversarial driving determination factor (M). Layer M indicates the SV's position relative to the BV when the BV is in the adversarial driving area, determining the driving modality.
- **Action Space:** Actions are continuous variables for longitudinal acceleration/deceleration (A_long ∈ [-1, 1], mapped to throttle/brake) and lateral lane change (A_lat ∈ [-1, 1], discretized into D_LaneChange ∈ {-1, 0, 1} for right, keep, left lane change).
- **Reward Function:**
 - **Individual Reward (r_ind):** Guides fundamental driving. It includes a negative reward for collisions or road boundary violations (r_c). Otherwise, it sums three components: r_cf for car-following (quantifying adherence to target distance), r_lc for lane-changing (rewarding increased drivable space while penalizing unnecessary changes), and r_rv for illegal/irrational behaviors.
 - **Cooperative Reward (r_coop):** Guides adversarial behavior. Awarded to BVs within the adversarial driving area when they compress the SV's left lane-changing space (r_l), right lane-changing space (r_r), or forward drivable space (r_f). This reward is shared evenly among participating BVs and added to their individual rewards.
- **Behavioral Constraints:** Five rules are implemented to prevent unrealistic or unsafe behaviors: prohibiting lane changes out of bounds, prohibiting unsafe lane changes (within 10m of other vehicles front/rear in target lane), and prohibiting lane changes from main road to merging ramps. These constraints contribute to the r_rv negative reward.

#### 3.4. Evolving Scenario Generation and Critical Scenarios Filtering

The trained Dual-DM is deployed as an NPC model in a CARLA-based simulation environment. The map is a 2400m highway with four lanes, including curves and an on-ramp. The Stackelberg model is used as the SUT for the SV. Two baseline NPC models are used for comparison:
- **Nilsson driver model:** A function-based lane-changing decision model.
- **DRL-based social driver model (Social-DRL):** A DRL-based model (similar to Dual-DM's Stage 1 training) incorporating social rewards to exhibit cooperative, neutral, or competitive attributes.

The simulation runs for 500 rounds, with 1 SV and 15 BVs. A safety-critical situation is defined as a collision or a Time-to-Collision (TTC) less than 0.5 seconds. For each critical situation, trajectory data 3.5 seconds prior to the event is extracted as a safety-critical scenario.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation framework assessed the generated evolving scenarios across four dimensions: fidelity, test efficiency, complexity, and diversity.

#### 4.1. Fidelity Evaluation

Fidelity measures the similarity between generated scenarios and naturalistic driving data (NDD). The HighD dataset served as the NDD benchmark. Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence was used to quantify the similarity of probability distributions for vehicle velocity and Time-to-Collision (TTC) during lane-change maneuvers.
- **Results:** The JS divergence for vehicle velocity was 0.0074, and for TTC under lane-change behaviors, it was 0.1323. Both values are below 0.15, indicating over 85% similarity to real-world driving characteristics. This suggests that Dual-DM generates evolving scenarios with high fidelity, despite its adversarial capabilities.

#### 4.2. Test Efficiency Evaluation

Test efficiency quantifies the rate of generating safety-critical scenarios per simulation round.
- **Metric:** E = (number of critical scenarios / number of simulation rounds), capped at 1 if the ratio exceeds 1.
- **Results:**
 - Dual-DM: 429 safety-critical scenarios in 500 rounds (E = 0.86).
 - Social-DRL: 145 scenarios (E = 0.29).
 - Nilsson: 129 scenarios (E = 0.26).
 Dual-DM demonstrated a substantial efficiency improvement, generating approximately 195% more safety-critical scenarios compared to the two baseline models.

#### 4.3. Complexity Evaluation

Scenario complexity evaluates the challenge level for the SUT, considering the number and distribution of BVs, and the complexity of their actions.
- **Metrics:**
 - `C_pos`: Information entropy based on BV distribution in 8 areas around the SV (adjusted for single-area concentration).
 - `C_act`: Action complexity based on the number of BVs performing significant acceleration/deceleration (≥4m/s²) or lane changes.
 - Overall complexity `C` is a normalized product of `C_pos` and `C_act`.
- **Results:**
 - Dual-DM: C = 0.45.
 - Social-DRL: C = 0.40.
 - Nilsson: C = 0.34.
 Dual-DM generated scenarios with higher complexity, showing a 32.35% increase over Nilsson and a 12.5% increase over Social-DRL.

#### 4.4. Diversity Evaluation

Diversity assesses the richness of adversarial interaction patterns between BVs and the SV, specifically by analyzing the internal model information (cooperative reward values).
- **Statistical Analysis:**
 - **Number of BVs in adversarial interaction:** Out of 429 safety-critical scenarios, 414 involved BVs receiving positive cooperative rewards. The number of BVs participating varied from 1 to 3 (64.49% with 1 BV, 30.68% with 2 BVs, 4.83% with 3 BVs). This distribution indicates varied group sizes for adversarial interactions.
 - **Position combinations of cooperative adversarial behaviors:** Among 127 two-BV cooperative adversarial scenarios, 23 distinct position combinations (adversarial patterns) were identified. For 20 three-BV scenarios, 16 distinct adversarial patterns were observed. This confirms the model's ability to generate diverse interaction configurations.
- **Case Studies:** Visualizations of scenario fragments demonstrated different adversarial behaviors:
 - **Single-vehicle adversarial behavior:** A BV in a neighboring lane compresses the SV's lateral space, then changes lanes to further reduce forward drivable space.
 - **Two-vehicle cooperative adversarial behavior:** Two BVs coordinate; one compresses lateral space, then the other takes its place while the first compresses forward space, leading to an SV collision during an evasive maneuver.
 - **Three-vehicle cooperative adversarial behavior:** Three BVs constrain the SV's lane-changing space from both sides, followed by a BV reducing forward drivable space, forcing the SV into a collision due to inadequate decision-making.
 These cases qualitatively confirmed that Dual-DM BVs challenge the SV by progressively compressing drivable space rather than direct collisions, aligning with the design objectives and illustrating diversity in interaction patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous vehicle safety validation by introducing an effective method for generating challenging and realistic test scenarios.

The primary significance lies in the **enhanced efficiency of safety-critical scenario generation**. By developing a dual-modal driver model capable of both naturalistic and adversarial driving, the authors have substantially increased the probability of encountering critical situations during simulation. The reported 195% improvement in efficiency over baseline models suggests that this approach can significantly accelerate the identification of vulnerabilities in AV decision-making and planning systems, which is crucial for their development and deployment.

Furthermore, the work addresses the critical challenge of **balancing scenario fidelity with adversariality**. The Dual-DM successfully generates scenarios that are highly similar to naturalistic driving data (over 85% fidelity), ensuring that the test cases are representative of real-world conditions. Simultaneously, it effectively orchestrates adversarial interactions, demonstrating that challenging AVs does not necessitate unrealistic or irrational maneuvers by background vehicles. Instead, BVs progressively compress the SV's drivable space, providing a more ecologically valid test of the SUT's reactive capabilities.

The emphasis on **multi-vehicle cooperative adversarial behaviors** is another notable impact. Traditional adversarial testing often focuses on single-agent challenges. This research demonstrates how Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning can enable coordinated actions among multiple background vehicles to create complex and diverse safety-critical scenarios. This capability allows for the generation of more sophisticated interaction patterns, better reflecting the complexity of real-world traffic and potentially uncovering deficiencies in AV systems that single-vehicle interactions might miss.

The proposed **SUT-in-the-loop training framework** offers practical value, allowing the driver model to be trained specifically against the decision-making logic of a particular autonomous driving system. This adaptability means Dual-DM can be tailored to expose specific weaknesses of different AV implementations, rather than relying on generic adversarial behaviors.

In summary, this research provides a robust, efficient, and versatile methodology for testing autonomous vehicles. Its potential impact includes:
- **Accelerating AV Development:** By rapidly generating critical test cases, developers can iterate on AV system designs more quickly and cost-effectively.
- **Improving AV Safety:** More thorough testing with diverse and complex safety-critical scenarios can lead to the identification and mitigation of more potential failure modes before deployment.
- **Advancing Testing Standards:** The framework could contribute to developing more comprehensive and challenging test protocols for highly automated driving systems.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as training the Dual-DM in more diverse road topologies and incorporating advanced SUTs, indicates a path towards even greater generalizability and practical application of this method.
