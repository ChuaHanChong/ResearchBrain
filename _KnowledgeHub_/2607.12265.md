# DiffRadar: Differentiable Physics-Aware Radar SLAM with Gaussian Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> A new radar SLAM system, DiffRadar, from Clemson University, models radar observations as a differentiable, physics-aware Gaussian field to enable robust, real-time localization and mapping by directly incorporating anisotropic resolution and Doppler dynamics. This approach achieved a 6x reduction in translational ATE on the Radarize benchmark and over a 6x reduction in translational ATE on controlled stress tests while maintaining real-time performance.

## Problem

- Existing SLAM systems primarily use optical sensors, which are unreliable in adverse conditions (e.g., fog, low light) or privacy-sensitive environments.
- Conventional radar SLAM approaches process observations as discretized images, ignoring radar-specific physics like anisotropic resolution and Doppler shifts, leading to unstable pose estimation and mapping.
- Traditional modular radar SLAM pipelines struggle with robustness and accuracy in feature-poor, degenerate, or dynamically changing environments.

## Method

- **DiffRadar** formulates ==radar SLAM== as a ==differentiable optimization== problem, directly incorporating radar sensing physics into a unified framework.
- The environment is represented by a continuous radar scene field composed of ==Gaussian primitives==, each parameterizing spatial geometry and physics-aware radar scattering behavior (==anisotropic covariance==, ==directional backscatter==).
- A ==differentiable renderer== projects this Gaussian scene into radar measurement space (==Range-Azimuth== and ==Doppler-Azimuth==), enabling joint optimization of robot pose and primitive parameters against raw radar measurements.

## Results

- Achieved a **6x** reduction in translational Absolute Trajectory Error (**ATEt**) on the Radarize benchmark (from **0.606m** to **0.103m**) and over **6x** improvement across controlled stress tests compared to baselines.
- Showcased enhanced robustness in challenging scenarios, including a **20x** improvement in ATE in feature-poor corridors and a reduction in peak translational error from **7.72m** to **0.36m** during rapid speed transitions.
- Operated in real-time at **70 FPS** on commodity hardware with a compact memory footprint of **40 MB**, while also improving map consistency from **43%** to **95%** on stress tests.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling radar observations as a differentiable, physics-aware Gaussian field effectively captures anisotropic spatial resolution and directional backscatter, providing a richer scene representation than discretized methods.
> - Directly incorporating Doppler measurements into a unified optimization objective provides crucial translation-sensitive gradients, significantly enhancing motion estimation robustness, especially during rapid velocity changes or in degenerate conditions.
> - Physics-conditioned visibility gating is essential for correctly associating radar primitives with observed measurements, which helps manage dynamic clutter and ensures physically plausible gradient updates for both pose and map.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a foundational capability for autonomous mobile systems. The majority of modern SLAM systems rely on optical sensors such as cameras or LiDAR, which provide high accuracy under optimal conditions. However, these sensors demonstrate degraded performance in challenging environments characterized by poor lighting, adverse weather conditions (e.g., fog, glare), or privacy concerns that preclude optical sensing.

Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar presents an alternative sensing modality for robust localization in such environments. Radar operates effectively irrespective of lighting and can penetrate common obscurants like smoke and dust. Furthermore, modern mmWave radars are compact, cost-effective, and increasingly integrated into mobile platforms. Despite these advantages, radar perception fundamentally differs from optical sensing. Radar observations are typically sparse, noisy, and anisotropic, offering high resolution in range but limited resolution in azimuth.

Existing radar SLAM systems commonly process radar observations as discretized range-azimuth (RA) heatmaps, subsequently estimating motion through modular pipelines such as scan matching or Doppler-based odometry. This approach, while functional in some contexts, limits the exploitation of radar sensing physics. Discretization can compromise geometric continuity and often assumes isotropic radar intensity, neglecting directional backscatter and Doppler shift characteristics. This modularity can lead to unstable pose estimation and degraded mapping, particularly in feature-poor, degenerate, or dynamically changing environments where individual sensing cues may become unreliable. Recent advancements in visual scene representation, such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), demonstrate the potential of differentiable modeling for tasks like view synthesis and visual SLAM. While some work has explored neural or Gaussian representations for radar, radar signal formation, primarily involving additive reflections rather than optical occlusion, necessitates a distinct forward model.

This research addresses a perceived "abstraction mismatch" in conventional radar SLAM, where radar observations are treated as images rather than physical signals. It aims to integrate radar-specific properties, such as anisotropic measurement uncertainty, directional scattering, and Doppler dynamics, directly into a unified, differentiable optimization framework, moving beyond discretized image representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time radar SLAM system, termed DiffRadar, that models radar observations as a differentiable, physics-aware Gaussian field. This approach aims to enable joint optimization of robot pose and scene structure directly from raw radar measurements, thereby addressing the limitations of existing radar SLAM systems.

The motivation for DiffRadar stems from several identified shortcomings in current radar SLAM methodologies:

- **Reliability in Adverse Conditions:** While radar is inherently more robust to adverse lighting and weather than optical sensors, existing radar SLAM systems often struggle with unstable pose estimation and degraded mapping in challenging scenarios. This limits their applicability in critical mobile systems requiring continuous and accurate localization under diverse environmental conditions.
- **Abstraction Mismatch in Radar Processing:** A core motivation is the observation that most radar SLAM systems treat radar observations as discretized images (e.g., range-azimuth heatmaps) for processing. This discretization, according to the authors, breaks geometric continuity and oversimplifies radar intensity, neglecting fundamental physics such as anisotropic measurement uncertainty, directional backscatter, and Doppler dynamics. This abstraction mismatch hinders the ability to fully leverage the rich information contained in radar signals.
- **Limitations of Modular Pipelines:** Traditional radar SLAM pipelines typically separate motion estimation (e.g., scan matching, Doppler odometry) and mapping into distinct modules. This modularity can lead to reduced robustness when individual sensing cues are weak or ambiguous. For instance, scan matching can become unstable in feature-poor or repetitive environments.
- **Need for Physics-Aware Modeling:** The authors argue for a radar SLAM formulation that is directly grounded in radar sensing physics. Such a formulation would inherently incorporate radar-specific properties, facilitating a more coherent and robust interpretation of radar data. This includes modeling anisotropic spatial resolution (high range, coarse azimuth), directional reflectivity, and Doppler shifts arising from ego-motion.
- **Inspiration from Differentiable Scene Representations:** Recent advancements in visual SLAM, particularly those using differentiable scene representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting, demonstrate the benefits of continuous scene modeling and gradient-based optimization. The motivation is to adapt these principles to radar, while fundamentally altering the rendering model to align with radar physics rather than optical principles.
- **Addressing Degenerate and Dynamic Environments:** The research specifically targets common radar SLAM failure modes, such as corridor degeneracy (lack of lateral features), rapid motion regime transitions, and the presence of dynamic clutter, where current systems exhibit significant performance degradation.

By addressing these points, DiffRadar aims to establish a unified, physics-aware, and differentiable optimization framework for radar SLAM that enhances robustness, accuracy, and consistency in diverse and challenging environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffRadar reformulates radar SLAM as a differentiable optimization problem that is grounded in radar sensing physics. Instead of the conventional modular processing stages for signal processing, motion estimation, and mapping, DiffRadar integrates these elements within a unified optimization framework. The system operates in three tightly coupled stages: Radar Observation Processing, Differentiable Radar Rendering, and a Joint Optimization Backend.

#### 3.1. Radar Observation Processing

Raw radar I/Q measurements from a Frequency-Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) radar are initially processed using standard signal processing techniques to generate Range-Azimuth (RA) and Doppler-Azimuth (DA) radar maps. From these processed maps, initial Gaussian primitives are instantiated based on Constant False Alarm Rate (CFAR) detections, providing an initial representation of the scene.

#### 3.2. Physics-Aware Radar Representation

DiffRadar represents the environment as a continuous radar scene field using a collection of Gaussian primitives. Each primitive, $G_i = \{\mu_i, \Sigma_i, \beta_i, A_i(\phi), \alpha_i\}$, parameterizes both spatial geometry and radar scattering behavior:
- $\mu_i$: Spatial mean of primitive $i$ in the global map frame.
- $\Sigma_i$: Covariance matrix, capturing anisotropic spatial uncertainty.
- $\beta_i$: Reflectivity scalar of the primitive.
- $A_i(\phi)$: Models the directional dependence of radar backscatter with respect to azimuth angle $\phi$, often represented as a low-order harmonic expansion.
- $\alpha_i$: A reliability weight derived from the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), ranging from 0 to 1.

**Radar Anisotropy Modeling:** To account for the anisotropic spatial resolution of mmWave radars (high range, coarser angular resolution), the covariance $\Sigma_i$ is modeled using a rotation-scale decomposition: $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i^2 R_i^\top$. Here, $R_i$ rotates the local range-cross-range coordinate system, and $S_i = \text{diag}(\sigma_r, \sigma_\theta r_i)$ encodes anisotropic spatial scales. $\sigma_r = \frac{c}{2B}$ (range resolution) and $\sigma_\theta = \frac{\lambda}{D}$ (angular resolution) are derived from radar intrinsic parameters, ensuring primitives have narrow radial extent and cross-range uncertainty that increases with distance.

**Directional Backscatter:** Radar reflections are viewpoint-dependent. DiffRadar models this with $A_i(\phi)$, a harmonic expansion that captures the azimuth-dependent reflectivity profile. This allows the system to learn continuous angular scattering patterns, providing informative gradients as the sensor pose changes.

#### 3.3. Differentiable Radar Rendering

The Gaussian scene representation is projected into radar measurement space using a physics-consistent differentiable renderer. Unlike optical rendering, radar returns are additive.

**Range-Azimuth (RA) Rendering:** The rendered intensity $I(\phi, r, t)$ at a specific azimuth-range bin $(\phi, r)$ at time $t$ is calculated as the sum of contributions from all visible primitives:
$I (\phi, r, t) = \sum_{i} A_t(i) \alpha_i \beta_i A_i(\phi) G_i(r; \mu_i, \mathbf{T}_t)$, where $G_i(r; \mu_i, \mathbf{T}_t)$ is a Gaussian kernel centered at the projected range of primitive $i$ transformed by the current pose $\mathbf{T}_t$. $A_t(i)$ is an association mask ensuring only physically plausible scatterers contribute.

**Doppler Rendering:** Doppler shift, arising from relative motion, is also predicted. For a primitive at $\mu_i$ and ego-motion $\mathbf{v}(t)$, the Doppler shift is $f_{D,i}(t) = \frac{2}{\lambda} \hat{\mathbf{r}}_i(t)^\top \mathbf{v}(t)$. Since radar aggregates Doppler per azimuth beam, the predicted Doppler-azimuth (DA) profile $\hat{f}_D(\phi, t)$ is an aggregation of contributions from all primitives overlapping each beam, weighted by their spatial proximity and radiometric strength.

**Unified Optimization Objective:** DiffRadar jointly optimizes robot pose and Gaussian primitive parameters by minimizing residuals between rendered and measured radar observations:
$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{T}_t, \{G_i\}) = \lambda_{RA} \|I_{rend} - I_{meas}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{DA} \|\hat{f}_D - f_{D,meas}\|_2^2$. The RA term ensures geometric consistency, while the Doppler term provides strong translation-sensitive gradients, effectively coupling velocity and primitive geometry.

#### 3.4. Online SLAM System

The system integrates these components into an online SLAM backend (Algorithm 1).

**Primitive Lifecycle:** CFAR detections initialize Gaussian primitives. These primitives are activated only after repeated observation support, while low-confidence or stale primitives are pruned. Redundant scatterers are merged.

**Visibility-Conditioned Association (VDRF):** For each incoming radar frame, a visibility function $V_t(i)$ determines which primitives can physically contribute to the measurement. A primitive is deemed visible if its geometry, angular scattering, and Doppler-consistent motion jointly yield sufficient likelihood under the radar forward model. This likelihood is factorized into range consistency, angular scattering support, and Doppler-consistent motion terms. This explicit physics-conditioned visibility prevents gradients from propagating through spurious scatterers, a departure from RGB-based 3DGS.

**Joint Pose-Map Optimization:** Given the set of associated primitives, DiffRadar jointly refines the radar scene representation and robot trajectory by minimizing the objective function. Gradients from RA intensity residuals primarily update primitive means ($\mu_i$) and covariances ($\Sigma_i$), shifting them towards configurations that better explain observed returns while preserving anisotropy. They also refine radiometric parameters ($\beta_i, A_i(\phi)$). Doppler residuals refine the velocity estimate $\mathbf{v}(t)$ and simultaneously adjust primitive positions, providing strong translation-sensitive kinematic gradients that complement geometric constraints.
Optimization proceeds through alternating pose and map refinement. A sliding window of 8–12 frames is used with Adam optimizer and a robust Huber loss. Primitive covariances are reprojected after each update to maintain the radar-consistent anisotropic structure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DiffRadar was conducted across four aspects: SLAM accuracy, robustness under radar degeneracy, map fidelity, and runtime efficiency. Experiments were performed on the public Radarize benchmark and a controlled Radar Degeneracy Stress-Test (RDST) suite.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Evaluation on Radarize

- **End-to-End SLAM Accuracy:** DiffRadar achieved improvements in global trajectory accuracy. It reduced translational Absolute Trajectory Error (ATEt) from 0.606m (Radarize) to 0.103m, representing approximately a 6x improvement. Rotational ATE ($\text{ATE}_\psi$) was also the lowest among evaluated methods (0.084 rad vs. 0.116 rad for Radarize). The system exhibited slightly higher relative translation error (REt) compared to Radarize, a trade-off noted by the authors as prioritizing global consistency over short-term odometry.
- **Qualitative Results:** Trajectory comparisons on sequences featuring feature-poor corridors, L-shaped corridors, and hallway intersections demonstrated that DiffRadar maintained close alignment with ground truth, whereas Radarize accumulated noticeable drift. Reconstructed radar maps showed that DiffRadar produced sharper and more geometrically consistent layouts compared to the blurred and duplicated structures from Radarize, especially in challenging corridor and loop closure scenarios.

#### 4.2. Controlled Stress Tests on RDST

The RDST suite was designed to isolate specific radar SLAM challenges, including motion degeneracy, rapid motion changes, dynamic clutter, and long-horizon trajectory drift.

- **E1. Motion Observability (Zig-zag Excitation):** In feature-poor corridors with lateral motion, DiffRadar improved global translational ATE by over 20x for cart trajectories (from 0.830m to 0.039m) and substantially for handheld runs (0.536m to 0.085m). Qualitatively, DiffRadar produced spatially consistent scatterers and followed ground-truth trajectories closely, while Radarize exhibited streak artifacts and drift.
- **E2. Motion Dynamics (Speed Transitions):** Under scripted slow-fast-slow and fast-slow-fast motion schedules, DiffRadar significantly reduced transient errors. For handheld trajectories, peak post-transition translation error decreased from 7.72m to 0.36m, and overall ATE dropped from 3.93m to 0.16m. This indicates stable motion estimation under rapid velocity changes.
- **E3. Degenerate Motion (In-place Rotation):** During pure in-place rotations where translational Doppler cues are absent, DiffRadar substantially improved orientation estimation. Final yaw error decreased from 2.69 rad to 0.051 rad, and yaw drift rate dropped from 0.098 rad/s to 0.002 rad/s. This suggests spatial consistency constraints derived from the joint optimization stabilize orientation in degenerate conditions.
- **E4. Environmental Interference (Dynamic Clutter):** With increasing numbers of moving pedestrians, DiffRadar maintained stable performance. Its ATE increased marginally (0.085m to 0.103m with four pedestrians), and map consistency remained high (approx. 94%). Radarize showed larger trajectory errors and lower map consistency (40-47%). This indicates the visibility-conditioned radar field representation helps in suppressing clutter.
- **E5. Long-horizon Consistency (Loop Closures):** For trajectories with loops exceeding 100m, DiffRadar dramatically reduced global drift. Loop drift for cart-mounted runs decreased from 20.44m/100m to 0.51m/100m, and ATE dropped from 2.92m to 0.017m.
- **Overall SLAM Performance on RDST:** Averaged across all RDST sequences, DiffRadar achieved over a 6x reduction in translational ATE and a 3x reduction in rotational ATE compared to Radarize. Map consistency more than doubled (from 43% to 95%), and vertical artifacts were reduced.

#### 4.3. Runtime and Ablation Analysis

- **Runtime:** DiffRadar achieved real-time performance at 70 FPS in steady state on commodity hardware, compared to 55 FPS for Radarize. It also maintained a smaller memory footprint (40 MB vs. 162 MB). The efficiency is attributed to its compact Gaussian representation and fixed-lag optimization window.
- **Ablation Study:**
 - Removing Doppler residuals substantially degraded translational accuracy.
 - Removing RA residuals degraded both translation and orientation.
 - Replacing anisotropic covariance with isotropic covariance or removing visibility gating increased trajectory errors.
 - The system demonstrated robustness to moderate calibration noise (±5% perturbation in D, B parameters caused only moderate degradation).
 These findings confirmed the complementary roles of Doppler and spatial radar measurements and the importance of physics-aware modeling components for accurate and stable radar SLAM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DiffRadar represents an advancement in radar SLAM by departing from traditional image-based processing towards a physics-aware, differentiable approach. The core significance lies in its ability to directly incorporate the fundamental characteristics of radar sensing—such as anisotropic resolution, directional backscatter, and Doppler shifts—into a unified optimization framework. This shifts the paradigm from treating radar data as images to processing it as physical signals, which allows for a more comprehensive and robust interpretation of the sensor's measurements.

The key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy:** DiffRadar demonstrates substantial improvements in trajectory accuracy and robustness across a range of challenging environments. Its ability to effectively handle corridor degeneracy, rapid motion changes, dynamic clutter, and long-horizon loop closures, where conventional radar SLAM systems often fail or accumulate significant drift, indicates its potential for deployment in demanding real-world applications. This can lead to more reliable autonomous navigation in conditions where optical sensors are impractical.
- **Physics-Consistent Scene Representation:** The introduction of a differentiable, physics-aware Gaussian field representation for radar scatterers provides a continuous and geometrically consistent model of the environment. This representation, which accounts for radar-specific anisotropies and directional scattering, allows for gradient-based optimization of both scene structure and sensor pose, leading to more accurate and coherent mapping.
- **Unified Optimization Framework:** By integrating radar measurement processing, scene representation, and trajectory estimation into a single differentiable optimization loop, DiffRadar eliminates the need for heuristic scan matching and modular estimation pipelines. This unified approach enables complementary cues from range-azimuth intensity and Doppler measurements to jointly constrain motion and map building, leading to more stable and consistent SLAM.
- **Real-time Performance on Commodity Hardware:** The system maintains real-time performance at 70 FPS on commodity mmWave radar platforms, coupled with a compact memory footprint. This efficiency, achieved through a compact Gaussian scene representation and fixed-lag optimization, makes DiffRadar practical for embedded systems and widespread adoption in mobile and edge devices.
- **Foundation for Future Radar Perception:** This work provides a foundation for next-generation radar perception systems by demonstrating the power of differentiable modeling grounded in sensing physics. It could inspire further research into integrating other radar phenomena, such as multipath effects, or extending these principles to other non-optical sensing modalities.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors include:**
- Evaluation specific to a particular commodity FMCW radar configuration, suggesting that adaptation to substantially different radar hardware may require additional calibration.
- While showing robustness under moderate dynamic clutter, the system may still face challenges in environments with dense moving objects that produce highly inconsistent radar returns.

Despite these limitations, DiffRadar's demonstrated gains in accuracy, robustness, and consistency, coupled with its real-time performance, indicate a significant step towards enabling reliable radar-only SLAM for mobile platforms operating in diverse and challenging conditions where traditional sensing modalities are insufficient.
