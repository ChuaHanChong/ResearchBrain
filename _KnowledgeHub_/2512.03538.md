# AdaPower: Specializing World Foundation Models for Predictive Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaPower introduces a lightweight adaptation framework that transforms World Foundation Models (WFMs) into specialist models for predictive robotic manipulation. This approach enhances zero-shot generalization and long-horizon consistency by adding minimal trainable parameters, leading to improved performance in both simulated and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- World Foundation Models (WFMs) excel at simulating visual dynamics but lack the control-oriented precision required for robotic manipulation tasks.
- Current methods for adapting WFMs to robotics are computationally expensive, involve lengthy adaptation cycles, and do not fully leverage pre-trained generalist policies.
- Deployment of WFMs in closed-loop robotic control suffers from test-time distribution shifts and long-horizon inconsistency, where prediction errors accumulate.

## Method

- **AdaPower** employs a parameter-efficient adaptation strategy by introducing minimal trainable layers (less than 10% of the base WFM's parameters) into a frozen WFM backbone.
- A Temporal-Spatial Test-Time Training (TS-TTT) module is integrated to allow self-supervised optimization during inference, adapting to novel distribution shifts by leveraging the low-rank properties of 4D video features.
- A Memory Persistence (MP) module is introduced to maintain historical context via cross-attention with object-centric features, ensuring long-horizon consistency and combating error accumulation during multi-step rollouts.

## Results

- AdaPower achieved an average success rate of **41.5%** on ten unseen LIBERO manipulation tasks, demonstrating a **41%** improvement over baseline VLA models and outperforming other adapter methods.
- Qualitative evaluations confirmed AdaPower generates visually stable and physically consistent long-horizon trajectories for unseen tasks, unlike other methods that suffer from error accumulation and prediction degradation.
- The framework universally empowered various pre-trained VLA policies, increasing CogACT's success rate by **41%** and π0's by **27%**, and showed over **30%** improvement in average success rate in real-world manipulation experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specializing large, pre-trained World Foundation Models for robotic control can be achieved efficiently with a small fraction of trainable parameters, preserving generalist knowledge.
> - Test-time adaptation tailored for the spatiotemporal characteristics of video data (TS-TTT) significantly improves model generalization to unseen environments and objects.
> - Integrating historical context through a memory module is crucial for maintaining physical consistency and preventing error propagation in long-horizon, autoregressive predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of large-scale generative AI and practical robotics: the application of World Foundation Models (WFMs) for precise robotic control. WFMs, such as Cosmos, WAN, GAIA-1, and GENIE, represent a significant paradigm shift, demonstrating unprecedented capabilities in simulating visual dynamics and generalizing across diverse environments due to their training on internet-scale video datasets. These models excel at generating visually plausible future predictions conditioned on multimodal prompts, effectively capturing physical and semantic regularities.

However, the paper highlights a fundamental disconnect: the remarkable generative realism of WFMs does not directly translate to the control-oriented precision demanded by robotic manipulation. Robotic tasks require continuous, fine-grained interactions and action-conditioned, precisely executable dynamic forecasts, which general-purpose WFMs are not inherently designed for.

Existing state-of-the-art approaches, exemplified by methods like DreamGen, typically attempt to bridge this gap through a synthetic data generation paradigm. This involves fine-tuning WFMs on limited task-specific data, generating massive synthetic video datasets, extracting pseudo-action sequences via inverse dynamics models, and subsequently training new visuomotor policies from these neural trajectories. While promising, this conventional pipeline suffers from three major limitations that AdaPower seeks to overcome:
1. **Prohibitive computational costs:** Generating large datasets and training new policies is resource-intensive.
2. **Lengthy adaptation cycles:** The entire process must be repeated for each new task or environment.
3. **Underutilization of pre-trained policies:** This approach bypasses the potential to enhance existing powerful generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in favor of training new specialist policies.

AdaPower distinguishes itself by proposing an *efficient model adaptation* framework that *empowers existing pre-trained VLA policies* directly, rather than generating synthetic data or training new policies. This positions AdaPower within the broader landscape of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and adapter-based methods (e.g., AdaptFormer, Visual Prompt Tuning, LoRA, VACE, ControlNet) that aim to specialize large pre-trained models with minimal computational overhead.

Furthermore, the work integrates with the field of model-based planning and Model Predictive Control (MPC), where learned dynamics models are central (e.g., TD-MPC, MBOP, MoPA). AdaPower leverages WFMs as high-fidelity, action-conditioned dynamics predictors for MPC, enabling more robust and accurate planning. The introduction of Temporal-Spatial Test-Time Training (TS-TTT) also extends the concept of Test-Time Training (TTT) (e.g., TENT, CoTTA, EATA), which aims to adapt models to distribution shifts during inference, specifically tailoring it for the unique spatiotemporal characteristics of video data in world models. By addressing these limitations and building upon existing strengths in various subfields, AdaPower offers a novel and more efficient approach to making generalist WFMs practical for specialized robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the inherent gap between the high visual dynamics simulation capabilities of World Foundation Models (WFMs) and the stringent control-oriented precision required for robotic manipulation tasks. The authors explicitly aim to transform general-purpose WFMs into *specialist world models* (SWMs) that can be directly and effectively used for predictive control.

The core motivations driving AdaPower are:
1. **Overcoming computational inefficiency of existing methods:** Current approaches that rely on generating vast amounts of synthetic data and subsequently training new visuomotor policies are computationally expensive and time-consuming. AdaPower seeks a lightweight and efficient alternative.
2. **Maximizing the utility of pre-trained VLA policies:** Instead of discarding or side-stepping powerful pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, AdaPower aims to *empower* these existing policies, enhancing their zero-shot generalization capabilities without the need for extensive retraining. This preserves the valuable generalist knowledge already encoded in VLAs.
3. **Addressing real-world deployment challenges:** The deployment of WFMs in closed-loop robotic control faces two critical challenges:
 - **Test-time distribution shift:** When models encounter novel initial states or environmental configurations during inference, predictions can rapidly degrade without mechanisms for self-supervised optimization.
 - **Long-horizon inconsistency:** Minor errors in multi-step autoregressive rollouts tend to accumulate, leading to physically implausible predictions that destabilize planning over longer time horizons.
 AdaPower's design specifically targets these issues to ensure reliable and consistent performance in dynamic environments.
4. **Promoting zero-shot improvement:** The framework is designed to enable true zero-shot performance improvement on novel environments and tasks, making robotic systems more adaptable and requiring less specific prior training data for new scenarios.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of large, pre-trained generative models for practical, precise robotic control, doing so in a computationally efficient and adaptable manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaPower (Adapt and Empower) is a lightweight adaptation framework designed to transform a general-purpose World Foundation Model (WFM) into a specialist world model (SWM) for robotic manipulation. The core idea is to introduce minimal, trainable parameters into a frozen WFM backbone, specializing its dynamics prediction without compromising its generalist knowledge or incurring high computational costs.

**Base World Foundation Model:**
The framework utilizes **Cosmos-Predict2-2B**, a Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based world model, as its backbone. This model is pre-trained on large-scale datasets to learn general physical priors, making it an ideal candidate for adaptation.

**Adapter Design and Parameter Efficiency:**
During adaptation, only the newly introduced layers—comprising the Memory Persistence (MP) module, the Temporal-Spatial Test-Time Training (TS-TTT) module, and an action encoder—are trained. The vast majority of the pre-trained WFM parameters remain frozen. This approach ensures high computational efficiency and prevents catastrophic forgetting, preserving the WFM's foundational understanding of physical dynamics while specializing it for manipulation. The added parameters amount to approximately 150 million, which is less than 10% of the base model size, demonstrating a highly parameter-efficient adaptation strategy. To ensure training stability, the outputs of these new layers are zero-initialized, allowing them to gradually learn their contributions without perturbing the pre-trained representations.

**Key Components of AdaPower:**

1. **Temporal-Spatial Test-Time Training (TS-TTT) Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To enable self-supervised optimization during inference, allowing the model to adapt to novel test-time distribution shifts and improve generalization.
 - **Mechanism:** Unlike conventional TTT designed for 2D sequence data (channel-wise low-rank priors), TS-TTT extends adaptation to exploit the intrinsic low-rank structure of 4D video features (Temporal x Height x Width x Channel). It recognizes that video data exhibits both spatial low-rankness (adjacent pixels are similar) and temporal low-rankness (background and gradual changes between frames).
 - **Implementation:** TS-TTT comprises two branches for self-learning optimization:
 - **Temporal-Spatial Branch:** The video input feature `v` (T x H x W x D) is flattened into a 2D sequence `v_ts` (D x THW). A neural network `f` with parameters `W` is optimized during inference to reconstruct `v_ts` from a low-rank projection `Θ_K v_ts`, minimizing `ℓ(W; v_ts) = ||f(W; Θ_K v_ts) - Θ_V v_ts||^2`. This allows the model to learn correlations across the fused temporal and spatial dimensions.
 - **Channel-wise Branch:** The video feature `v` is reshaped into `v_c` (THW x D), and a similar low-rank projection and reconstruction process is applied to learn channel-wise correlations.
 - **Output:** The final output `v_o` is obtained via a residual connection: `v_o = v + z_ts + z_c`, where `z_ts` and `z_c` are the outputs of the respective branches. This joint optimization significantly amplifies the self-supervised learning signal and enhances generalization.

2. **Memory Persistence (MP) Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To maintain historical context and ensure long-horizon consistency in predictions, combating error accumulation and knowledge forgetting during multi-step autoregressive rollouts.
 - **Mechanism:** At each prediction step, the MP module retrieves historical memory features from prior frames and integrates them with the current DiT block features using cross-attention.
 - **Feature Extraction:** Object-centric information, crucial for environmental dynamics, is extracted from historical frames using a DINOv2 encoder, yielding dense patch tokens. These tokens are reshaped into a 2D sequence to form the memory feature.
 - **Integration:** In the cross-attention mechanism, the memory feature provides the key and value representations, while the current DiT feature provides the query. This allows the DiT feature to directly incorporate historical context, stabilizing predictions and preserving critical object relationships over time.
 - **Placement:** Both TS-TTT and MP modules are strategically inserted every seven blocks of the Diffusion Transformer backbone, striking a balance between effectiveness and computational overhead.

**Deployment with Model Predictive Control (MPC):**
After adaptation, the specialist world model is integrated into an MPC framework for robotic manipulation.
- **VLA as Planner:** A pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model (e.g., CogACT, π0) serves as a high-level planner. Given an initial state and a task instruction, it generates multiple candidate action sequences. To enhance exploration, these are augmented with noisy sequences from a low-variance Gaussian distribution.
- **SWM as Dynamics Predictor:** The adapted SWM simulates these candidate action sequences, performing rollouts to generate imagined trajectories and forecast future states.
- **Reward Model:** A reward model, based on approaches like ReWind, estimates task completion progress from the imagined trajectories and task instructions.
- **Optimal Action Selection:** The action sequence that yields the highest predicted reward is selected for execution by the robot.

This cooperative architecture synergistically combines the VLA's general reasoning and high-level planning capabilities with the SWM's accurate physical simulation, enabling robust and precise robotic control. Crucially, the VLA operates in a zero-shot manner without fine-tuning, while the SWM's improved generalization allows adaptation to unseen scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AdaPower demonstrates significant advancements in specializing World Foundation Models for predictive manipulation across both simulated and real-world environments.

**Quantitative Performance in Simulation (LIBERO Benchmarks):**
Evaluations on ten unseen LIBERO manipulation tasks highlight AdaPower's superior performance:
- **Overall Improvement:** AdaPower achieves an average success rate of 41.5%, a substantial **41% improvement** over the baseline pre-trained VLA (CogACT, which scored 0.5% success rate without a specialized world model).
- **Comparison with Other Adapters:** AdaPower consistently outperforms other parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and adapter methods:
 - VACE: 20.5% average success rate.
 - LoRA: 23.5% average success rate.
 - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT): 32.5% average success rate.
This indicates that AdaPower's unique combination of memory persistence and test-time adaptation provides more accurate predictions for unseen environments.

**Qualitative Performance in Simulation:**
Qualitative comparisons of long-horizon rollouts (Figure 4) reveal that AdaPower generates visually stable and physically consistent trajectories for unseen tasks. In contrast, other methods (LoRA, SFT, VACE) tend to accumulate small errors over time, leading to blurry objects, loss of physical consistency, and prediction degradation. AdaPower's ability to maintain stable object trajectories, model contact dynamics, and propagate forces correctly over extended horizons underscores its enhanced generalizable prediction capabilities.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation experiments confirm the complementary contributions of AdaPower's key modules:
- **Module Effectiveness (Table 2):**
 - Removing the Memory Persistence (MP) module resulted in a 1.5% drop in success rate (from 41.5% to 40.0%), indicating faster degradation during rollouts.
 - Removing the Temporal-Spatial Test-Time Training (TS-TTT) module led to a 3.5% decrease (from 41.5% to 38.0%), highlighting its role in generalizing to unseen objects and environments.
 - The best performance is achieved when both modules are combined, validating their synergistic relationship.
- **TS-TTT Variants (Table 3):**
 - The chosen design, fusing temporal and spatial dimensions while having a separate channel dimension (TS+C), yielded the best results (41.5% Avg.SR, 0.0205 Test MSE).
 - Separate self-learning for temporal, spatial, and channel dimensions (T+S+C) performed worse (35.0% Avg.SR), suggesting inherent correlations between spatial and temporal aspects.
 - Fusing all three dimensions (TSC) failed to converge (NaN loss), underscoring the importance of the specific factorization and fusion strategy in TS-TTT.

**Cross-Policy Evaluation:**
AdaPower demonstrates strong architecture-agnostic compatibility, enhancing the performance of different pre-trained VLA models:
- With CogACT: Increased average success rate from 0.5% to 41.5% (+41%).
- With π0: Increased average success rate from 21.0% to 48.0% (+27%).
This shows that the specialized world model can universally empower various VLA policies, improving their zero-shot generalization.

**Real-world Experiment:**
Deployment on a Franka Research 3 robotic arm for five physical manipulation tasks in real-world environments confirms AdaPower's transferability and robustness:
- **Real-world Success:** AdaPower improved the average success rate of the pre-trained CogACT by over 30% in tasks like stacking bowls, grasping into plates, and grasping into baskets (Figure 6).
- **Robustness:** The adapted world model performs reliably under visual noise and domain shifts inherent in real-world settings, validating its practical applicability.

In summary, AdaPower consistently delivers significant performance improvements, particularly in zero-shot generalization, while maintaining computational efficiency. Its core components, TS-TTT and MP, are empirically validated to be critical for achieving robust and accurate long-horizon predictions in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AdaPower marks a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation and the application of large foundation models. Its primary significance lies in establishing a **new paradigm for leveraging World Foundation Models (WFMs) in robotic control**. Instead of the computationally expensive and rigid approach of synthetic data generation and full policy retraining, AdaPower proposes an efficient adaptation framework that directly bridges the gap between general world knowledge and specialist robotic requirements.

The potential impact of AdaPower is multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization for Robotics:** By significantly improving the zero-shot generalization capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies (demonstrated by 30-41% success rate increases), AdaPower reduces the need for extensive task-specific data collection and policy fine-tuning. This is crucial for deploying robots in novel or constantly changing environments, making robotic systems more versatile and adaptable.

2. **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The framework's lightweight nature, adapting only a small fraction of parameters (less than 10% of the base WFM), drastically cuts down on computational costs and training time compared to conventional methods. This efficiency makes advanced robotic capabilities more accessible and scalable, particularly for research labs or industries with limited computational resources.

3. **Robustness and Reliability in Dynamic Environments:** The introduction of the Temporal-Spatial Test-Time Training (TS-TTT) and Memory Persistence (MP) modules directly addresses two critical challenges in closed-loop control: adapting to test-time distribution shifts and maintaining long-horizon consistency. This leads to more stable, accurate, and physically plausible predictions, which are indispensable for reliable robotic operation in unpredictable real-world scenarios.

4. **Democratization of Advanced AI in Robotics:** By making it easier and more efficient to adapt powerful generalist WFMs, AdaPower could accelerate the integration of cutting-edge AI research into practical robotic applications. This could enable a broader range of users and organizations to develop sophisticated robotic behaviors without deep expertise in foundation model architecture or extensive resources for training from scratch.

5. **Foundation for Future Intelligent Systems:** The demonstrated success in both simulation and real-world manipulation tasks positions AdaPower as a strong foundation for future developments in intelligent systems. The principles of efficient WFM specialization and integration with MPC could be extended to other domains requiring accurate predictive models, such as autonomous driving, multi-agent interaction, and complex industrial automation.

While the paper acknowledges moderate test-time overhead as a current limitation, suggesting avenues for future work in real-time efficiency and domain transfer robustness, AdaPower's core contributions represent a significant step forward. It offers a practical and powerful method to unlock the potential of large generative models for precise, adaptable, and efficient robotic control, paving the way for more capable and intelligent autonomous systems.
