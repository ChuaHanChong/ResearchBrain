# FAPO: Flawed-Aware Policy Optimization for Efficient and Reliable Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> FAPO introduces a novel framework for large language model (LLM) reasoning, Flawed-Aware Policy Optimization, that adaptively penalizes unreliable reasoning paths to boost process reliability and efficiency. It leverages a specialized generative reward model (GenRM) for fine-grained error detection, leading to improvements of up to +4.7% in outcome correctness on reasoning benchmarks and significantly reducing the ratio of flawed-positive rollouts.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs often reinforce 'flawed-positive' reasoning (e.g., guessing, logical jumps) because they only reward based on a correct final answer, regardless of the reasoning process.
- This indiscriminate positive reinforcement can hinder the development of genuinely robust reasoning capabilities, potentially trapping the optimization process in unreliable patterns.
- Traditional LLM-as-a-judge approaches for process-level feedback are either too inefficient for online RL training or lack the precision to accurately detect fine-grained flawed positives without over-criticizing.

## Method

- **FAPO** introduces an adaptive policy optimization framework that dynamically adjusts the ==penalization== of flawed-positive rollouts throughout the RL training process.
- A specialized Generative Reward Model (FAPO-GenRM-4B) is developed and trained with a novel step-wise RL reward formulation to accurately and efficiently detect flawed positives and localize reasoning errors.
- The FAPO framework integrates this GenRM into policy optimization via an adaptive reward mechanism that applies a penalty (-λ) to positively rewarded rollouts identified as flawed positives, with λ set to 1 for a majority-guided strategy.

## Results

- FAPO achieved consistent improvements in outcome correctness, boosting performance by up to +**4.7%** on AIME24 for the **7B** model and +**3.5%** on AIME24 for the **32B** model, across various reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework significantly enhanced process reliability, reducing the ratio of flawed positives in model responses (e.g., from **15.5%** to **7.1%** on AIME24 for FAPO-**32B**).
- FAPO demonstrated improved training stability with smoother learning curves, avoiding performance drops seen in baseline models, while maintaining efficient token usage without increasing average response length.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flawed positives play a dual role in RL training: they can act as 'stepping stones' for early capability acquisition but become detrimental to robust learning in later refinement stages.
> - An adaptive strategy is crucial to effectively manage flawed positives, allowing their utility in warm-up phases while gradually shifting optimization to reliable reasoning as the model matures.
> - A small, specialized generative reward model, trained with a process-level reward that encourages precise error localization, can outperform larger, general LLM-as-a-judge models in both accuracy and efficiency for detecting flawed reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of large language model (LLM) reasoning, specifically within the paradigm of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR has been recognized as a method for improving LLM capabilities in verifiable tasks such as mathematical reasoning and code generation. In this paradigm, LLMs generate reasoning trajectories, and those leading to correct final answers are typically rewarded positively, guiding policy optimization.

However, existing RLVR approaches frequently encounter "flawed-positive rollouts." These refer to instances where an LLM reaches a correct final answer but through an unreliable or logically invalid reasoning process, such as answer-guessing or jump-in-reasoning. Previous studies have identified the prevalence of such flawed patterns in LLMs. The current challenge in RLVR is that rule-based outcome rewards assign identical positive signals to both genuinely correct and flawed-positive rollouts, thereby inadvertently reinforcing unreliable reasoning patterns.

This paper addresses a gap in the literature by systematically investigating the impact of these flawed positives throughout the RL training process and by proposing a method to mitigate their negative effects. While prior work has documented the existence of these issues, their mechanistic influence on the RL process and the development of targeted mitigation strategies remain areas requiring further exploration. FAPO differentiates itself by introducing an adaptive optimization strategy that leverages a specialized generative reward model (GenRM) to identify and differentially reward reasoning processes, moving beyond simple binary outcome feedback to encourage more reliable reasoning. This contrasts with purely discriminative reward models or general LLM-as-a-judge approaches, which often exhibit limitations in efficiency or fine-grained error detection for this specific task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and reliability of large language model (LLM) reasoning capabilities within the reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) framework.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical observation regarding the limitations of current RLVR paradigms:
- **The Flawed Positive Problem:** Existing RLVR systems typically use rule-based outcome rewards that provide a binary signal (correct/incorrect) based solely on the final answer. This approach fails to distinguish between a final correct answer achieved through a sound reasoning process and one achieved through a "flawed-positive" pattern, such as guessing or skipping logical steps. Consequently, unreliable reasoning patterns can be reinforced, potentially limiting the model's overall reasoning robustness and performance ceiling.
- **Preliminary Study Insights:** The authors conducted a preliminary investigation into the prevalence and impact of flawed positives during RL training, which yielded two key insights:
 - **Early Stage Utility:** In the initial phases of training, when LLMs may not yet be capable of consistently generating fully correct reasoning paths, flawed positives can act as "stepping stones." They provide a pathway to reach correct answers, thereby accelerating early capability gains.
 - **Later Stage Detriment:** As the model's capabilities advance and it becomes capable of producing fully correct rollouts, the continued reinforcement of flawed positives can hinder further learning. These unreliable patterns may trap the optimization process, preventing the model from developing robust, genuine problem-solving abilities. The study observed that in standard RL, the flawed-positive ratio can remain consistently high, indicating a struggle to shift towards reliable reasoning.
- **Need for Adaptive Optimization:** Based on these findings, the motivation arose to develop an optimization strategy that could adaptively manage the role of flawed positives. Such a strategy should allow the model to leverage these shortcuts during a warm-up phase but then gradually shift the optimization focus towards reliable, error-free reasoning in later refinement stages.
- **Need for Accurate and Efficient Flawed Positive Detection:** To implement such an adaptive strategy, an accurate and computationally efficient method for detecting flawed-positive rollouts is required. Existing general-purpose LLM-as-a-judge models were found to be either too slow for online RL integration or prone to over-criticizing minor errors, thus necessitating a specialized and precise detection mechanism.

In summary, the research aims to address the challenge of unreliable reasoning in RL-trained LLMs by understanding the dynamic role of flawed positives and designing an adaptive policy optimization framework supported by a fine-grained, efficient generative reward model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Flawed-Aware Policy Optimization (FAPO), is designed to address the challenge of flawed-positive rollouts in RLVR by integrating a mechanism for detecting and adaptively penalizing such patterns. FAPO consists of two main components: a generative reward model (GenRM) for flawed-positive detection and an adaptive policy optimization algorithm for penalization.

#### 3.1. Flawed Positive Detection (FAPO-GenRM)

The first component focuses on accurately identifying flawed-positive reasoning trajectories.
- **Motivation for Custom GenRM:** Existing LLMs or discriminative reward models were evaluated and found to be either inefficient for online RL training (e.g., strong LLMs like Qwen3-32B) or insufficiently accurate, often exhibiting an "over-critic" phenomenon (high recall but low precision) on lightweight models. This highlighted the need for a compact, effective, and efficient specialized reward model.
- **FAPO-GenRM Training:** A generative reward model, FAPO-GenRM-4B, is trained to detect flawed positives and localize reasoning errors.
 - **Dataset Construction (FAPO-Critic-85K):** To train FAPO-GenRM, a specialized dataset was created. This involved generating multiple responses to mathematical reasoning questions using various LLMs (from LLaMA and Qwen families, 7B to 70B). Positive samples (those with correct final answers, including flawed ones) were then selected. A stronger LLM, Qwen3-32B, was used to identify step-level error locations within these selected responses. To mitigate label noise, a "consensus filtering" strategy was employed, retaining only samples where three independent generations yielded consistent error locations.
 - **Step-wise RL Optimization for GenRM:** The GenRM is trained using a customized RL reward formulation:
 `R_FAPO-GenRM = R_Outcome + R_Process`
 - `R_Outcome`: A binary reward (+1 for correctly identifying a flawed positive/fully correct, -1 otherwise). This guides the model towards overall correctness in detection.
 - `R_Process`: A fine-grained, distance-sensitive penalty applied specifically to flawed-positive cases. It is defined as `- |t_hat - t_star| / n`, where `t_hat` is the predicted error index, `t_star` is the ground-truth error index, and `n` is the total number of reasoning steps. This penalty encourages precise error localization rather than mere guessing of the "flawed positive" label.
 - **Natural Reward Shift for GenRM:** This reward design intrinsically promotes a natural learning progression. In early stages, `R_Outcome` (with a larger reward magnitude from -1 to 1) dominates, encouraging the GenRM to correctly classify responses. As the GenRM becomes proficient at classification, `R_Process` (with a smaller range of [-1, 0]) gains relative importance, shifting the focus towards fine-grained error localization without requiring additional hyperparameters for control.

#### 3.2. Flawed Positive Penalization (FAPO-Reasoning)

The second component integrates the FAPO-GenRM's detection capabilities into the main LLM policy optimization.
- **Adaptive Reward Mechanism:** For each reasoning rollout `o` generated by the policy, FAPO-GenRM-4B is used to determine if it is a flawed positive (`y_hat = FP`). The reward function for policy optimization is then modified:
 `R_FAPO(o, a*) = R_RLVR(o, a*) + R_Δ(o, a*|θ)`
 - `R_RLVR`: The standard rule-based binary outcome reward (+1 if final answer is correct, -1 otherwise).
 - `R_Δ`: An additional penalty term. If `R_RLVR` is positive (correct final answer) AND FAPO-GenRM identifies the rollout as a flawed positive (`y_hat = FP`), a penalty of `-λ` is applied. Otherwise, `R_Δ` is 0.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** FAPO integrates this modified reward into the GRPO framework, which uses group-relative advantage estimation to update the policy.
- **Theoretical Analysis and λ Parameter:** A theoretical analysis (Appendix A) demonstrates how the parameter `λ` controls the optimization dynamics. It shows that FAPO naturally enables an optimization shift:
 - In early stages (warm-up), when negative samples dominate, flawed positives contribute positively to policy updates, accelerating initial gains.
 - As the model improves, and the ratio `ρ = α/β` (fully correct rollouts `α` to negative rollouts `β`) exceeds `2/λ - 1`, the optimization objective shifts towards penalizing flawed positives, promoting reliable reasoning (refinement stage).
 - Further, when `ρ > 4/λ - 1`, the scaling factor in advantage estimation increases, leading to more conservative updates that stabilize training.
- **Majority-Guided Strategy for λ:** Based on the theoretical analysis, the authors adopt a majority-guided strategy, setting `λ = 1`. This configuration ensures that the optimization shifts to prioritize reliable reasoning when fully correct rollouts begin to outnumber incorrect ones (i.e., `ρ_shift = 1`).
- **Implementation Details:**
 - FAPO is implemented using the GRPO baseline with strategies like clip-higher, token-level loss, and overlong reward shaping.
 - Base models for reasoning experiments are Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Qwen2.5-32B.
 - An asynchronous architecture decouples GenRM inference from rollout generation and actor training. FAPO-GenRM-4B is deployed as an external service to manage computational overhead and enhance training efficiency, thereby addressing infrastructure challenges associated with adding an extra generative reward model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings from preliminary studies and the evaluation of the FAPO framework.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Study on Flawed Positives in RL Training

- **Prevalence:** An initial assessment of pre-trained and instruction-tuned LLMs (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama3.3-70B-Instruct, Qwen3-1.7B) revealed that flawed positives constitute 20%–40% of correct rollouts across various models, indicating their widespread presence.
- **Role as Stepping Stones:** Analysis showed that flawed positives are most prevalent in early learning stages, diminishing as model confidence and overall accuracy increase. This supports the hypothesis that they initially act as useful shortcuts for models to achieve correct answers before developing fully reliable reasoning.
- **Persistence and Twofold Effects:** During standard RL training, while overall rollout accuracy improved, the ratio of flawed positives remained nearly constant (~30%). This suggested that the existing optimization struggled to transition from unreliable to genuine problem-solving. A trial penalizing flawed positives from the start showed performance gains, but with slower early-stage improvement, reinforcing the need for an adaptive strategy.

#### 4.2. FAPO-GenRM Performance (Flawed Positive Detection)

- **Enhanced Detection Capability:** FAPO-GenRM-4B achieved an F1 score of 89.4 on the newly constructed FlawedPositiveBench and 83.3 on the public ProcessBench. This performance surpassed several state-of-the-art discriminative and generative models, including the larger Qwen3-32B teacher model.
- **Efficiency:** The GenRM achieved these results with shorter response lengths compared to larger models, which is crucial for maintaining inference efficiency during online RL training.
- **Impact on RL Performance:** An ablation study demonstrated a direct correlation: a stronger FAPO-GenRM (trained with the proposed method) led to improved performance in the final RL reasoning task compared to using a baseline GenRM (Qwen3-4B-Instruct), validating the importance of accurate detection.

#### 4.3. FAPO-Reasoning Performance (RL Optimization)

- **Outcome Correctness:** FAPO models consistently outperformed baseline models across various reasoning benchmarks:
 - AIME24: FAPO-7B showed a +4.7% improvement, and FAPO-32B showed a +3.5% improvement.
 - AIME25: FAPO-32B improved by +3.1%.
 - GPQA-Diamond: FAPO-32B showed a +1.5% improvement.
 - Additional evaluations across AMC, MATH, and LiveCodeBench also showed consistent improvements for FAPO-32B. These gains were observed across nearly all intermediate evaluation checkpoints during training.
- **Process Reliability:** FAPO significantly reduced the flawed-positive ratio in model responses. For FAPO-32B, the ratio decreased from 15.5% to 7.1% on AIME24, from 10.9% to 1.7% on AIME25, and from 45.7% to 42.0% on GPQA-Diamond. These reductions were corroborated by both LLM-as-a-judge evaluations and a manual human verification, demonstrating that FAPO promotes genuinely reliable reasoning processes.
- **Training Stability:** FAPO exhibited smoother learning curves and enhanced training stability, without the performance drops in later stages observed in baseline models. This indicates that FAPO effectively mitigates the negative impact of reinforcing unreliable patterns.
- **Token Budget:** The performance improvements achieved by FAPO did not require an increase in the average response length, which differentiates it from some prior work that achieves gains by scaling up output length.
- **Self-Correction Analysis:** FAPO demonstrated an adaptive self-correction behavior. While initially leveraging self-corrected rollouts, it gradually shifted towards generating fully correct and shorter rollouts, indicating a progression towards more efficient and direct problem-solving.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Hyperparameter λ

- All tested `λ` configurations for flaw-aware learning consistently improved performance over the baseline.
- Optimal performance required tuning `λ`, with a more aggressive penalization strategy (corresponding to `ρ = 1/2`) yielding the largest gains in the 7B setting. This suggests that while the theoretical framework provides guidance, empirical adjustment of `λ` can further optimize performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Flawed-Aware Policy Optimization (FAPO) framework presents several contributions and potential impacts on the development of reasoning capabilities in large language models.

- **Enhancing Reasoning Reliability:** FAPO directly addresses the issue of unreliable reasoning patterns (e.g., guessing, logical jumps) that can be inadvertently reinforced in standard RLVR. By penalizing flawed-positive rollouts, FAPO promotes the internalization of robust and logically sound reasoning processes, leading to higher process reliability and outcome correctness. This moves beyond simply achieving correct answers to ensuring *how* those answers are derived, fostering more trustworthy AI systems.
- **Adaptive Learning Trajectory:** The framework introduces an adaptive learning mechanism that leverages the dual nature of flawed positives. It allows models to use flawed positives as "stepping stones" for rapid capability acquisition in early training stages, while subsequently shifting the optimization focus towards genuine, reliable problem-solving. This adaptive approach optimizes training efficiency by preventing early-stage stagnation and late-stage performance degradation.
- **Novel Generative Reward Model:** FAPO introduces a specialized Generative Reward Model (GenRM) with a process-level reward formulation. This GenRM is designed to accurately and efficiently detect fine-grained reasoning errors, overcoming limitations of general-purpose LLM-as-a-judge approaches in terms of both precision and computational cost for online RL training. The design of the reward signal specifically aims to mitigate "guessing" behavior by encouraging precise error localization.
- **Robustness against Reward Hacking:** The theoretical analysis and empirical results suggest that FAPO's reward structure, particularly with the majority-guided `λ=1` setting, exhibits robustness against reward hacking. This is a significant advantage, as complex, fine-grained reward models often risk policies exploiting spurious shortcuts rather than learning intended behaviors.
- **Practical Scalability for Large-Scale RL:** The proposed asynchronous infrastructure design, which decouples GenRM inference from other RL components, makes FAPO practical for integration into large-scale RL systems. This design minimizes the additional computational burden and training time, thereby facilitating the adoption of more sophisticated reward modeling in industrial settings without substantial overhead.
- **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated in mathematical reasoning, the underlying principles of FAPO, which distinguish between process reliability and outcome correctness, are potentially applicable to other complex reasoning tasks, including multi-choice questions, multi-turn interactions, and agent-based RL settings where flawed processes are prevalent.

In conclusion, FAPO offers a principled and practically viable approach to advancing LLM reasoning beyond mere outcome accuracy, paving the way for more reliable, efficient, and robust AI agents.
