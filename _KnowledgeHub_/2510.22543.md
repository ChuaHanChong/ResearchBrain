# FAPO: Flawed-Aware Policy Optimization for Efficient and Reliable Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> FAPO introduces a novel framework for large language model (LLM) reasoning, Flawed-Aware Policy Optimization, that adaptively penalizes unreliable reasoning paths to boost process reliability and efficiency. It leverages a specialized generative reward model (GenRM) for fine-grained error detection, leading to improvements of up to +4.7% in outcome correctness on reasoning benchmarks and significantly reducing the ratio of flawed-positive rollouts.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs often reinforce 'flawed-positive' reasoning (e.g., guessing, logical jumps) because they only reward based on a correct final answer, regardless of the reasoning process.
- This indiscriminate positive reinforcement can hinder the development of genuinely robust reasoning capabilities, potentially trapping the optimization process in unreliable patterns.
- Traditional LLM-as-a-judge approaches for process-level feedback are either too inefficient for online RL training or lack the precision to accurately detect fine-grained flawed positives without over-criticizing.

## Method

- **FAPO** introduces an adaptive policy optimization framework that dynamically adjusts the ==penalization== of flawed-positive rollouts throughout the RL training process.
- A specialized Generative Reward Model (FAPO-GenRM-4B) is developed and trained with a novel step-wise RL reward formulation to accurately and efficiently detect flawed positives and localize reasoning errors.
- The FAPO framework integrates this GenRM into policy optimization via an adaptive reward mechanism that applies a penalty (-λ) to positively rewarded rollouts identified as flawed positives, with λ set to 1 for a majority-guided strategy.

## Results

- FAPO achieved consistent improvements in outcome correctness, boosting performance by up to +**4.7%** on AIME24 for the **7B** model and +**3.5%** on AIME24 for the **32B** model, across various reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework significantly enhanced process reliability, reducing the ratio of flawed positives in model responses (e.g., from **15.5%** to **7.1%** on AIME24 for FAPO-**32B**).
- FAPO demonstrated improved training stability with smoother learning curves, avoiding performance drops seen in baseline models, while maintaining efficient token usage without increasing average response length.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flawed positives play a dual role in RL training: they can act as 'stepping stones' for early capability acquisition but become detrimental to robust learning in later refinement stages.
> - An adaptive strategy is crucial to effectively manage flawed positives, allowing their utility in warm-up phases while gradually shifting optimization to reliable reasoning as the model matures.
> - A small, specialized generative reward model, trained with a process-level reward that encourages precise error localization, can outperform larger, general LLM-as-a-judge models in both accuracy and efficiency for detecting flawed reasoning.
