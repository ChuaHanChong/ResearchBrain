# Explosive Output to Enhance Jumping Ability: A Variable Reduction Ratio Design Paradigm for Humanoid Robots Knee Joint

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a variable reduction ratio knee joint (EVRR-K) for electrically driven humanoid robots, addressing the mismatch between motor characteristics and kinematic demands during explosive jumping. The system enabled a 1-DOF platform to achieve a 63 cm vertical jump, showing a 28.1% improvement over optimal fixed-ratio joints, and allowed a full-scale humanoid to perform 0.5 m vertical and 1.1 m forward jumps.

## Problem

- Electrically driven humanoid robots exhibit limited jumping capabilities (0.3-0.5 m) compared to hydraulic systems (>0.6 m) and humans.
- During a jump, the increasing knee-to-center-of-mass transmission ratio conflicts with electric motor characteristics, which perform optimally at mid-speeds but degrade significantly at high speeds due to power losses.
- Fixed-ratio knee joints struggle to provide both high initial torque and sustained high power output during the entire takeoff phase, leading to inefficient motor utilization.

## Method

- A novel ==Explosive Variable Reduction Ratio Knee (EVRR-K)== joint design paradigm was proposed, featuring a reduction ratio that dynamically decreases as the knee joint extends.
- A compact linear actuator-driven guide-rod mechanism was engineered to implement the variable reduction ratio with high parametric tunability.
- An optimization framework was developed to select mechanical parameters that maximize takeoff energy and jump height while keeping the motor operating within its high-power, low-loss region.

## Results

- A 1-DOF leg-like platform equipped with the EVRR-K joint achieved a vertical jump height of 63 cm, demonstrating a **28.1%** theoretical improvement over optimal fixed-ratio joints.
- A full-scale 45 kg humanoid robot (BHR8-J1) successfully performed a **0.5** m vertical jump, a **1.1** m forward jump, and a **0.5** m box jump.
- The EVRR-K design kept the knee motor speed under 3000 rpm, avoiding high power-loss regions, while enabling peak knee joint torque of 286 Nm and peak power of **1.5** kW during box jumping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically decreasing the reduction ratio during joint extension effectively aligns the motor's operating characteristics with the kinematic demands of jumping, optimizing power delivery.
> - Maintaining the motor speed below 3000 rpm (outside the high power-loss region) is crucial for maximizing sustained power output and achieving higher jump performance in electrically driven systems.
> - The proposed guide-rod mechanism offers a compact and efficient method to achieve a variable reduction ratio, allowing for precise control over the knee joint's torque-speed profile.
