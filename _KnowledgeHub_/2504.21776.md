# WebThinker: Empowering Large Reasoning Models with Deep Research Capability

> [!summary] Summary
> The WebThinker framework, developed by researchers from Renmin University of China and collaborators, equips Large Reasoning Models with autonomous deep web exploration and an interleaved think-search-and-draft strategy for comprehensive report generation. It achieved state-of-the-art performance among 32B models on challenging reasoning benchmarks like GAIA and Humanity's Last Exam, and produced more complete and thorough scientific reports than existing methods.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) are limited by reliance on static, internal knowledge, making them suboptimal for complex, knowledge-intensive tasks requiring up-to-date external information.
- Existing open-source Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) agents often use predefined workflows, restricting LRMs' ability to deeply explore the web and dynamically interact with search results.
- There is a need for systems that can conduct in-depth web information retrieval, mining, and integration beyond shallow search to generate comprehensive and accurate reports.

## Method

- WebThinker introduces a =='Deep Web Explorer' module==, allowing LRMs to autonomously search, navigate web pages (via link-clicking), and extract information to address knowledge gaps.
- An =='Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft' strategy== enables LRMs to seamlessly interleave reasoning, information gathering, and report writing, using specialized tools for drafting, checking, and editing content.
- The framework employs ==iterative online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)== to refine the LRM's ability to perceive information needs, reason, and effectively utilize research tools.

## Results

- WebThinker-32B-RL achieved State-of-the-Art (SOTA) performance among **32B** models on complex reasoning benchmarks, including a **+21.5%** gain over its base version on Humanity's Last Exam (HLE) and surpassing stronger proprietary models like o3-mini (High) on HLE.
- On scientific report generation tasks from Glaive, WebThinker-32B (RL version) achieved the highest overall average score (**8.1**), outperforming RAG baselines and advanced non-proprietary systems like Grok3 DeeperSearch.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the Deep Web Explorer, especially its link-clicking capability, and the 'Autonomous Report Draft' component, with RL training being vital for complex problem-solving performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving reasoning with dynamic information gathering and iterative report refinement is crucial for tackling complex, knowledge-intensive tasks, surpassing static RAG workflows.
> - Empowering LRMs with hierarchical web exploration capabilities, including deep navigation beyond initial search results, is vital for comprehensive information acquisition.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically iterative online DPO, effectively optimizes an LRM's strategic use of research tools, leading to significant performance gains in complex problem-solving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the forefront of research aimed at enhancing Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by overcoming their inherent limitation: reliance on static internal knowledge. While LRMs such as OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have demonstrated remarkable long-horizon reasoning capabilities across domains like math, code, and science, their performance degrades significantly when confronted with complex, knowledge-intensive tasks requiring up-to-date or comprehensive external information.

The broader research landscape has seen the emergence of **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** as a dominant paradigm to address this. RAG enhances generative models by integrating retrieval mechanisms, allowing them to access external knowledge. However, the paper critiques existing open-source RAG agents for typically employing predefined workflows (e.g., standard RAG or iterative RAG with query planning), which limit the LRM's ability to deeply explore the web and closely interact with search engines. This often results in "shallow search" and hinders the generation of truly comprehensive and accurate reports requiring multi-step reasoning and synthesis of diverse web information.

This limitation has spurred a "deep research" initiative, as acknowledged by prominent industry players like OpenAI ("OpenAI Deep Research"), xAI Grok3 ("Grok3 DeeperSearch"), and Google Gemini ("Gemini2.0 Deep Research"). These efforts highlight a critical, unmet demand for systems that can conduct in-depth web information retrieval, mining, and integration. WebThinker aims to bridge this gap by offering a *universal, flexible, and open-source* deep research framework, distinguishing itself from proprietary solutions and more constrained open-source RAG approaches.

WebThinker specifically differentiates itself by moving beyond predefined RAG workflows. Instead of merely retrieving documents based on a query, it empowers LRMs to *autonomously* decide when and how to search, navigate web pages (by clicking links or buttons), extract information, and, crucially, *interleave* these actions with reasoning and report drafting. This "agentic" approach, where the LRM acts as an intelligent agent making dynamic decisions about information gathering and synthesis, represents a significant advancement over static retrieval pipelines. The integration of Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based training further refines the LRM's ability to effectively utilize these research tools, pushing towards more human-like, adaptive information exploration and knowledge synthesis. In essence, WebThinker seeks to enable LRMs to perform "system-2" thinking in dynamic, real-world information environments, a capability that traditional LRMs and basic RAG systems often lack.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind WebThinker is to overcome the limitations of current Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) which, despite their impressive internal reasoning capabilities, are constrained by their reliance on static, pre-trained knowledge. This static nature renders them suboptimal for complex, knowledge-intensive tasks that require dynamic, up-to-date, or extensively synthesized information from the vast and ever-changing internet. Furthermore, existing open-source deep search agents often employ rigid, predefined Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) workflows, preventing LRMs from truly "diving deep" into web exploration or intricately interacting with search results and web pages.

The paper identifies a revolutionary objective: to enable users to conduct profound searches, mine disparate information, and integrate comprehensive and reliable research from the internet through simple queries. This capability would significantly reduce the time and cost associated with information gathering for researchers in knowledge-intensive fields such as finance, science, and engineering.

To achieve this overarching goal, WebThinker sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Empower LRMs with Autonomous Web Exploration:** The core objective is to allow LRMs to autonomously search the web, navigate dynamically between web pages (e.g., by clicking links or buttons), and extract relevant information when they encounter knowledge gaps during their reasoning process.
2. **Integrate Deep Web Exploration:** To facilitate truly in-depth information gathering, the paper aims to design a **Deep Web Explorer** module. This module enables the LRM to go beyond simple keyword searches, allowing it to recursively search and traverse deeper links based on evolving information needs, ensuring comprehensive data acquisition.
3. **Implement an Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft Strategy:** A crucial objective is to seamlessly interleave the LRM's reasoning process with information gathering and report writing. This strategy moves beyond generating a report in one go after all information is supposedly collected; instead, it allows the model to draft sections, check the current report, and edit content in real-time as new information is discovered and integrated, ensuring comprehensiveness, coherence, and adaptability.
4. **Enhance Research Tool Utilization via Reinforcement Learning:** To unlock the full potential of these deep research capabilities, the paper aims to develop an RL-based training strategy. Specifically, iterative online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is used to refine the LRM's ability to perceive information needs, reason, and effectively interact with the various research tools (search, navigation, drafting, checking, editing).

By addressing these objectives, WebThinker seeks to establish a more capable and versatile deep research system, bridging the gap between advanced LRM reasoning and dynamic, real-world information environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WebThinker is designed as an autonomous deep research agent that enhances Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) with capabilities for deep web exploration and comprehensive report generation. Its methodology is structured around a novel framework operating in two primary modes, supported by a sophisticated RL-based training strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The research frames the complex reasoning task as a mapping from an instruction (*I*), a task query (*q*), and a set of available tools (*T*) to a logical reasoning chain (*R*) and a final output (*y*). The generative process is decomposed into two parts: reasoning with tools, where the LRM dynamically calls tools and incorporates their outputs, and final output generation.

#### 3.2. WebThinker Framework Overview

WebThinker operates in two distinct modes, as illustrated in Figure 3:

- **Problem-Solving Mode:** In this mode, the LRM is equipped with a **Deep Web Explorer** module. When the LRM encounters a knowledge gap, it can autonomously initiate web searches, navigate through web pages (e.g., by clicking interactive elements like links or buttons), and extract relevant information. This allows for in-depth information gathering beyond conventional shallow search mechanisms.
- **Report Generation Mode:** This mode employs an **Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft** strategy. Here, the LRM seamlessly interleaves its reasoning processes with information seeking (via the Deep Web Explorer) and report generation. It utilizes a set of specialized tools for drafting, checking, and editing sections of a research report. These writing tools are executed by an assistant LLM that leverages a dynamically updated document memory containing all explored web pages, ensuring the report is comprehensive, coherent, and grounded in evidence.

#### 3.3. Deep Web Explorer

The Deep Web Explorer module is central to WebThinker's ability to interact with the web. It is driven by the main LRM and operates under a specific instruction. The explorer utilizes two fundamental tools: a **search engine** (to retrieve web pages based on a generated query) and a **navigation tool** (to interact with elements on a viewed page, like clicking links). Upon a search request from the main LRM, the explorer executes its own internal reasoning chain to decide whether to perform further searches or navigate deeper into web content. Its ultimate goal is to return a concise summary (O_exp) that addresses the knowledge gap to the main reasoning chain. This hierarchical delegation allows the main LRM to focus on high-level reasoning while the explorer handles the complexities of web interaction.

#### 3.4. Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft Strategy

For report generation, the main LRM orchestrates the entire process, deciding when to gather more information via the Deep Web Explorer and when to modify the report. It uses three key report writing tools:
- **T_draft (Drafting content):** Instructs an assistant LLM to write specific sections of the report based on current guidelines and gathered information.
- **T_check (Checking report):** Requests an outline of the current report, allowing the LRM to assess completeness and structure.
- **T_edit (Editing report):** Provides instructions to an assistant LLM to revise existing report content.

These writing tools are implemented by an assistant LLM, which retrieves relevant documents from a cumulative document memory (M) containing all previously explored web pages. This separation of concerns allows the main LRM to maintain overall control and reasoning flow, while the assistant LLM handles the detailed text generation and manipulation. The process continues until the main LRM decides the report is complete.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning for Tool Utilization

To optimize the LRM's effective use of these research tools, WebThinker employs an on-policy Reinforcement Learning (RL) training strategy, specifically **iterative online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**.

1. **Preference Data Construction:** The process begins by sampling multiple diverse reasoning trajectories from WebThinker on complex tasks (e.g., SuperGPQA, WebWalkerQA). For each task, pairs of trajectories (R_w, R_l) are constructed, where R_w is preferred over R_l. The preference criteria are hierarchical:
 - **Overall Correctness/Report Quality:** Correct answers/higher quality reports are always preferred.
 - **Tool Efficiency:** Among equally correct trajectories, those with fewer total tool calls are preferred.
 - **Thinking Conciseness:** Among equally correct and efficient trajectories, shorter trajectories (if significantly shorter) are preferred.
2. **Iterative Online DPO Training:** The constructed preference dataset (D) is used to train the LRM. The DPO loss function reinforces preferred trajectories while penalizing dispreferred ones relative to a reference policy. This training is performed iteratively: the model is trained, new trajectories are sampled using the updated model, a new preference set is generated, and the process repeats. This iterative, on-policy approach allows the LRM to continuously refine its tool usage strategy by learning from its own interactions and generated outcomes.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The backbone for WebThinker is QwQ-32B, with Qwen2.5-Instruct serving as the assistant LLM. The Bing Web Search API is used for web searches, and Crawl4AI handles web page content fetching. Training involves two iterations of online DPO with a maximum sequence length of 32,768 tokens. For baselines, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting is applied where appropriate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WebThinker underwent extensive evaluation across two main categories of tasks: complex reasoning benchmarks and scientific report generation. The results consistently demonstrate its superior performance compared to existing methods and strong proprietary systems.

#### 4.1. Results on Complex Problem-Solving

Experiments were conducted on GPQA (PhD-level science QA), GAIA (general AI assistant tasks), WebWalkerQA (deep web navigation), and Humanity's Last Exam (HLE, extremely challenging interdisciplinary problems). The key findings were:

- **Limitations of Baselines:** Direct reasoning models (e.g., QwQ-32B) often struggle with knowledge-intensive tasks, despite their internal reasoning strengths. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods, while providing improvements, show inconsistent gains, especially on highly complex tasks like HLE which demand deep reasoning and search integration.
- **Advantage of Autonomous Search:** Methods incorporating autonomous search (e.g., Search-o1) demonstrated notable gains over direct reasoning and basic RAG. For instance, Search-o1 outperformed RAG-QwQ-32B by a significant margin on GAIA, WebWalkerQA, and HLE.
- **WebThinker's Superiority (Base Version):** The training-free WebThinker-32B-Base, which inherently utilizes its Deep Web Explorer for more profound web exploration, consistently surpassed all prior methods, including Search-o1, across all benchmarks. For example, it showed a +22.9% improvement on WebWalkerQA and +20.4% on HLE compared to Search-o1.
- **Impact of RL Training:** The RL-trained WebThinker-32B-RL achieved State-of-the-Art (SOTA) performance among 32B models on all complex reasoning benchmarks. It substantially improved upon its Base version, with gains of +8.5% on GAIA and +21.5% on HLE. Remarkably, on the extremely challenging HLE, WebThinker-32B-RL even surpassed stronger proprietary models like o3-mini (High).

#### 4.2. Results on Scientific Report Generation

WebThinker was evaluated on the open-ended reasoning tasks from Glaive, assessing report quality across completeness, thoroughness, factuality, and coherence.

- **Overall Performance:** WebThinker-32B (both Base and RL versions) achieved the highest overall average score (8.0 and 8.1 respectively), outperforming RAG baselines and advanced non-proprietary systems like Grok3 DeeperSearch and Gemini2.0 Deep Research (7.9).
- **Qualitative Aspects:** WebThinker particularly excelled in Completeness and Thoroughness, matching top scores in Factuality and Coherence. A t-SNE visualization of content embeddings revealed that WebThinker's reports formed broader sub-clusters within topic groups, suggesting a more diverse and deeper exploration and synthesis of information. This highlights the effectiveness of its Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft strategy in dynamically adapting information gathering and writing to produce richer, more unique reports.

#### 4.3. Adaptability Across LRM Backbones

WebThinker's adaptability was tested with different sizes of DeepSeek-R1 models (7B, 14B, 32B). After cold-start SFT and RL training, R1-based WebThinker consistently outperformed direct reasoning and standard RAG across all tasks and model sizes. For instance, WebThinker-R1-7B showed relative gains of 174.4% on GAIA and 422.6% on WebWalkerQA over direct generation, underscoring its general applicability and effectiveness in enhancing diverse LRMs.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided crucial insights into the contribution of WebThinker's core components:

- **RL Training:** Iterative online RL training was vital for complex problem-solving performance, demonstrating significant improvements over the base (untrained) version and even offline DPO. Its impact on report generation was less pronounced, indicating the base framework's strong foundation in this area.
- **Deep Web Exploration:** Removing the Deep Web Explorer module severely degraded performance in both problem-solving and report generation, confirming its criticality. Disabling only the link-clicking capability within the explorer also impaired problem-solving, highlighting the necessity of deeper web traversal beyond initial search results.
- **Report Generation Components:** The "Autonomous Report Draft" component was found to be the most critical for report quality, with its removal leading to the largest performance drop. The "Report Check & Edit" tools also played a significant role, particularly in maintaining report coherence. These findings underscore the importance of the interleaved thinking, searching, and iterative refinement capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WebThinker represents a significant step forward in empowering large reasoning models with capabilities beyond static knowledge retrieval and predefined task execution. Its comprehensive framework, which integrates autonomous deep web exploration with real-time, interleaved reasoning and report generation, addresses a critical gap in the current landscape of AI research.

#### 5.1. Significance

The primary significance of WebThinker lies in its ability to:
- **Advance Agentic AI:** It pushes the boundaries of agentic LLMs by enabling autonomous decision-making in complex, dynamic environments (the web). This move from simple tool-use to deep, self-directed research capability is a substantial contribution.
- **Overcome LRM Knowledge Limitations:** By dynamically searching, navigating, and synthesizing information, WebThinker effectively mitigates the inherent limitation of LRMs relying solely on their internal, static knowledge base.
- **Enhance Real-World Problem Solving:** The demonstrated superior performance on challenging benchmarks like GAIA, WebWalkerQA, and Humanity's Last Exam validates its effectiveness in tackling complex, real-world problems that demand extensive external knowledge and multi-step reasoning.
- **Facilitate Comprehensive Knowledge Synthesis:** The "Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft" strategy fundamentally changes how LLMs can generate comprehensive reports, making them more thorough, factual, and coherent by integrating information gathering and writing in real-time.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** As an open-source framework, WebThinker provides a valuable resource for the research community, promoting further innovation and democratizing access to advanced deep research capabilities.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The potential broader impact of WebThinker is substantial, both positively and negatively:

**Positive Societal Impacts:**
- **Accelerated Research and Discovery:** Researchers in knowledge-intensive fields (science, engineering, finance) can significantly reduce the time and cost associated with information gathering, potentially accelerating scientific breakthroughs and innovation.
- **Improved Information Access and Synthesis:** It can democratize access to advanced research capabilities, enabling individuals and organizations with fewer resources to conduct in-depth investigations.
- **Enhanced Decision-Making:** By providing comprehensive and reliable research reports, WebThinker can support more informed decision-making in critical sectors, from policy formulation to business strategy.
- **Journalism and Education:** It could aid investigative journalists in gathering background information more efficiently and provide students with powerful tools for learning and essay writing based on dynamic web content.

**Negative Societal Impacts and Risks:**
- **Misinformation and Disinformation:** The ability to autonomously gather and synthesize vast amounts of online information could be misused to generate and disseminate sophisticated misinformation or propaganda, making it harder to discern factual content.
- **Bias Amplification:** If the underlying web data or the training preferences contain biases, WebThinker could inadvertently amplify these biases in its generated reports or problem solutions, leading to skewed or unfair outcomes.
- **Data Privacy Concerns:** Autonomous web crawling and information extraction raise questions about data privacy, especially concerning how personal or sensitive information encountered online is handled and synthesized.
- **Verifiability and Trust:** While aiming for factuality, the opaque nature of LLM reasoning might make it challenging to fully verify the provenance and accuracy of every piece of information in an AI-generated report, potentially eroding trust in information sources.
- **Displacement of Human Expertise:** In the long term, highly capable deep research agents might impact roles requiring extensive human information gathering, analysis, and report writing.

The authors acknowledge these limitations and broader impacts in their conclusion and dedicated section. They specifically point out current limitations such as the inability to process multimodal information (images, videos), a limited set of tools (calling for improved scalability and generalization), and the absence of GUI-based web exploration for more complex real-world interactive tasks. Addressing these will be crucial for the responsible development and deployment of future deep research systems. The emphasis on ethical guidelines, robust validation, and transparency is paramount to mitigate potential harms and ensure such powerful tools serve the broader public good.
