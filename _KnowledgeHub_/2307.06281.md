# MMBench: Is Your Multi-modal Model an All-around Player?

> [!summary] Summary
> MMBench is a bilingual benchmark that evaluates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) across a hierarchical taxonomy of 20 fine-grained abilities, including perception and reasoning tasks. It incorporates a robust evaluation framework with techniques like CircularEval and LLM-involved choice extraction to provide objective and scalable performance assessments in both English and Chinese.

## Problem

- Existing objective benchmarks for VLMs suffer from "false negative" issues and lack fine-grained analysis of model capabilities.
- Subjective human evaluations are costly, time-consuming, and lack scalability and objectivity for frequent VLM assessments.
- The rapid progress of VLMs has created a gap in comprehensive and reliable evaluation methodologies, hindering systematic development and comparison.

## Method

- The authors developed **MMBench**, a ==bilingual (English and Chinese) multiple-choice== benchmark featuring a ==hierarchical taxonomy== of 20 distinct fine-grained multimodal abilities.
- Rigorous ==quality control== mechanisms were implemented to filter "text-only" and "wrong" questions using state-of-the-art LLMs and VLMs.
- Novel evaluation strategies, including ==CircularEval== for robust assessment and ==LLM-involved choice extraction== for handling free-form VLM outputs, were introduced.

## Results

- MMBench questions were validated to require multimodal understanding, as text-only LLMs like GPT-4 achieved random-level accuracy (**2.9%**).
- InternLM-XComposer2 achieved the highest overall performance among open-source VLMs (**78.1%**) and showed minimal performance drop (**<1%**) on the MMBench-CN version, demonstrating strong bilingual capability.
- Evaluated VLMs consistently demonstrated weaknesses in recognizing low-level visual features, interpreting structuralized visual inputs, and reasoning about complex inter-object spatial relationships.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CircularEval provides a more rigorous evaluation, revealing larger and more meaningful performance gaps and consistency issues in VLMs compared to traditional methods.
> - The capabilities of the underlying Large Language Model backbone significantly influence VLM performance, especially for complex reasoning tasks.
> - Proprietary VLMs exhibit advantages in structuralized image-text understanding and external knowledge-based tasks, but top open-source models can compete in other areas of perception and reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of MMBench is situated at a critical juncture in the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence, specifically in the domain of multimodal learning. The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) like OpenAI's ChatGPT and GPT-4 has revolutionized natural language processing, showcasing unprecedented reasoning and comprehension capabilities. Inspired by this success, the research community has made significant strides in extending these architectures to handle multimodal inputs, particularly vision, leading to the emergence of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) such as GPT-4v, Gemini-Pro-V, and LLaVA. These LVLMs exhibit remarkable abilities in integrating visual perception with language understanding and reasoning.

However, the rapid advancement of these models has outpaced the development of effective, comprehensive, and scalable evaluation methodologies. This gap creates a bottleneck, hindering systematic progress and making it difficult to objectively compare and understand the true capabilities and limitations of different LVLMs. The existing evaluation landscape suffers from several critical drawbacks:

- **Traditional Objective Benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2, COCO Caption, GQA):** While providing quantitative performance metrics (like accuracy, BLEU, CIDEr), these benchmarks were often designed for earlier generations of models and specific tasks. When applied to advanced LVLMs, they face "false negative" issues, where semantically correct but lexically different answers are penalized. More critically, they lack fine-grained ability assessment, offering limited insights into *why* a model succeeds or fails, and thus provide insufficient feedback for targeted improvements across diverse cognitive capabilities (e.g., spatial reasoning, attribute comparison, social understanding).
- **Subjective Human-involved Benchmarks (e.g., OwlEval, LVLM-eHub):** These approaches leverage human annotators for more comprehensive and accurate evaluations, allowing for flexible interpretation of model outputs. However, they suffer from inherent limitations:
 - **Scalability:** Human evaluation is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, making it impractical for frequent model iterations or large-scale evaluations.
 - **Bias and Reproducibility:** Human judgments can be subjective and vary between annotators, leading to potential biases and challenges in reproducing results across different evaluation campaigns.
 - **Statistical Instability:** Smaller datasets often used in human evaluations can result in statistically unstable performance metrics.

MMBench directly addresses these challenges by carving out a unique niche in the research landscape. It aims to deliver a **systematically designed objective benchmark** that combines the comprehensiveness and fine-grained analysis sought in subjective evaluations with the scalability and objectivity of automated metrics. By introducing a hierarchical ability taxonomy, rigorous quality control, and novel evaluation strategies like CircularEval and LLM-assisted choice extraction, MMBench positions itself as a robust, scalable, and insightful tool. Furthermore, its bilingual nature (English and Chinese) adds a crucial dimension, enabling the assessment of VLMs in diverse linguistic contexts, which is increasingly vital for global AI development. In essence, MMBench seeks to provide the research community with a more reliable compass to navigate the complex multimodal AI landscape, guiding the development of truly "all-around" and general-purpose VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind MMBench is to resolve the significant limitations of current evaluation practices for large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), thereby facilitating more effective and guided progress in multimodal AI research. The authors articulate several key objectives to achieve this:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive and Fine-grained Evaluation:** The primary goal is to move beyond task-specific or qualitative assessments and provide a holistic, in-depth understanding of VLM capabilities. The authors aim to achieve this by defining a hierarchical taxonomy of 20 distinct fine-grained abilities, spanning both perception (e.g., object localization, image quality) and various forms of reasoning (e.g., physical property reasoning, social relation reasoning, structuralized image-text understanding). This fine-grained analysis is crucial for identifying specific strengths and weaknesses of models, guiding targeted improvements.

2. **Ensure Robustness and Accuracy of Evaluation:** MMBench seeks to overcome the "false negative" problem prevalent in traditional objective benchmarks (where semantically correct but textually varied answers are penalized) and the biases/scalability issues of subjective human evaluations. This objective drives the development of novel evaluation strategies.

3. **Provide an Objective and Scalable Benchmark:** Recognizing the cost and subjectivity of human evaluations, MMBench is designed to be an objective benchmark. This means it must be amenable to automated evaluation, even for free-form VLM outputs, to ensure scalability for frequent and extensive testing.

4. **Enable Bilingual Assessment:** A unique objective of MMBench is to support evaluation in both English and Chinese. This allows for an "apples-to-apples" comparison of VLM performance in a bilingual context, addressing the growing need for globally competent multimodal AI models and investigating language-specific biases or strengths.

5. **Facilitate Future VLM Development:** Ultimately, the benchmark aims to serve as a valuable resource for the research community. By providing clear performance metrics across diverse abilities and highlighting challenging areas, MMBench intends to offer actionable insights that can drive future architectural innovations, training methodologies, and data curation efforts for VLMs.

In summary, MMBench is motivated by the critical need for a next-generation evaluation tool that is simultaneously comprehensive, robust, objective, scalable, and culturally inclusive, thereby accelerating the journey towards truly general-purpose multimodal artificial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MMBench project employs a systematic and multi-faceted methodology focused on high-quality data curation and robust evaluation strategies.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

1. **Hierarchical Ability Taxonomy:**
 - The benchmark establishes a comprehensive, three-level hierarchical taxonomy to categorize multimodal capabilities.
 - **Level 1 (L-1):** Divides abilities into "Perception" (gathering information from sensory inputs) and "Reasoning" (drawing conclusions from information).
 - **Level 2 (L-2):** Further breaks down L-1 into six broader categories: Coarse Perception, Fine-grained Perception (Single-instance), Fine-grained Perception (Cross-instance), Attribute Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, and Relation Reasoning.
 - **Level 3 (L-3):** Consists of 20 fine-grained "leaf abilities" (e.g., Object Localization, Celebrity Recognition, Image Emotion, Future Prediction, Structuralized Image-Text Understanding). Each L-3 ability definition is detailed in the appendix.

2. **Question Collection:**
 - **Format:** Questions are designed as multiple-choice problems, each comprising a quadruple (Question, Choices, Image, Correct Answer). Most questions have 2-4 choices.
 - **Sources:** Data is primarily collected manually by a group of undergraduate and graduate student volunteers. Approximately 80% of questions are gathered from the Internet, while the remaining 20% are derived from the validation sets of existing public datasets (e.g., COCO, ScienceQA, TextVQA) with self-constructed questions to ensure they are not used for training.
 - **Distribution:** Efforts were made to ensure a balanced distribution, with at least 125 samples for each of the 20 L-3 abilities, totaling 3,217 questions.

3. **Rigorous Quality Control:**
 - **"Text-Only" Filtering:** To ensure multimodal understanding is required, questions answerable by text alone are identified. State-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4, Gemini-Pro, and Qwen-Max are used for text-only inference. If more than half of these LLMs correctly answer a question without visual input, it undergoes manual verification and is removed if deemed "text-only."
 - **"Wrong" Question Filtering:** To identify ambiguous, flawed, or incorrectly answered questions, a set of state-of-the-art VLMs (both open-source and proprietary, e.g., GPT-4v, InternLM-XComposer2) are prompted. If *all* these VLMs fail to answer a question correctly, it is flagged for manual review and removed if problematic. This paradigm can also be applied to other benchmarks to improve data quality.

4. **MMBench-CN (Bilingual Version):**
 - All English questions and choices are translated into Chinese using GPT-4, carefully preserving proper nouns, symbols, and code in their original English form.
 - All GPT-4 translations are subsequently verified by human annotators to ensure validity and accuracy. This enables direct, "apples-to-apples" performance comparisons of VLMs in both linguistic contexts.

5. **Data Splits:** The dataset is divided into a development set (40%) and a test set (60%). Ground truth answers for the test set remain confidential, requiring submissions to an evaluation server for official results.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Strategy

MMBench introduces two key innovations to ensure robust and accurate evaluation:

1. **LLM-involved Choice Extraction:**
 - **Problem:** Many VLMs, especially those not specifically tuned for multiple-choice formats or general-purpose proprietary models, output free-form text instead of explicit choice labels (A, B, C, D). Rule-based exact matching can lead to "false negatives."
 - **Approach:** An initial heuristic matching attempts to extract choice labels. If unsuccessful, a powerful LLM (GPT-4-0125 by default) is employed as a "choice extractor." Given the question, options, and the VLM's free-form prediction, GPT-4 is prompted to identify the most semantically similar choice. If no choice aligns, it returns a pseudo-choice 'Z'.
 - **Validation:** Extensive experiments confirm GPT-4's effectiveness, achieving 91.5% alignment with human judgment in choice extraction, significantly improving accuracy compared to purely heuristic methods.

2. **CircularEval Strategy:**
 - **Problem:** Multiple-choice questions can be susceptible to random guessing (e.g., 25% for 4-choice questions) and model biases towards certain choice labels, potentially masking true performance differences.
 - **Approach:** Each question is presented to the VLM *N* times (where N is the number of choices). In each "pass," the order of the choices (and thus the correct answer's label) is circularly shifted.
 - **Success Condition:** A VLM is considered to have *successfully solved a question only if it correctly predicts the answer in all N circular passes*.
 - **Benefits:** This stricter criterion significantly reduces the impact of random chance and label bias, revealing larger and more meaningful performance gaps between VLMs, thus providing a more rigorous assessment.

Models are evaluated in a zero-shot setting, using a consistent prompt template. A comprehensive set of 21 mainstream VLMs, encompassing open-source and proprietary models across various architectures and parameter sizes, are tested. The evaluation code is integrated into VLMEvalKit for community access.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of a diverse range of 21 Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on MMBench yielded several significant findings:

1. **Impact of CircularEval:** The CircularEval strategy proved to be a substantially more rigorous evaluation method than traditional single-pass (VanillaEval). For most VLMs, adopting CircularEval led to a considerable drop in reported accuracy (e.g., OpenFlamingo v2 dropped from 36.7% to 2.6%, and even state-of-the-art proprietary models saw ~10% accuracy drops). This indicates that CircularEval is more effective at exposing genuine performance differences and consistency issues, making it a more robust measure of VLM reliability.

2. **MMBench Requires Multimodal Understanding:** A sanity check with GPT-4 (text-only input) resulted in random-level overall accuracy (2.9%), confirming that MMBench questions cannot be solved through linguistic understanding alone and genuinely require multimodal capabilities.

3. **Top-Performing Models:**
 - Among **open-source VLMs**, **InternLM-XComposer2** achieved the best overall performance (78.1% on the test set), significantly outperforming other open-source and even some proprietary models. This highlights its advanced multimodal understanding capabilities.
 - LLaVA-based architectures (LLaVA series and Yi-VL series) also demonstrated strong overall performance, positioning them just behind the leading closed-source models like GPT-4v and Qwen-VL-Max.
 - **Proprietary models** (GPT-4v, Qwen-VL-Max, Gemini-Pro-V) generally occupied the top tiers, though InternLM-XComposer2 closed the gap considerably.

4. **Role of the Underlying LLM:** The choice and scale of the Large Language Model (LLM) serving as the language backbone significantly impacted VLM performance. Models built on more powerful LLMs (e.g., LLaVA-InternLM2-20B over LLaVA-v1.5-7B) showed consistent improvements across all L-2 capabilities, especially for reasoning tasks. This underscores the critical importance of strong linguistic reasoning for advanced multimodal understanding.

5. **Bilingual Performance (MMBench-CN):** Most VLMs exhibited a noticeable performance drop when evaluated on MMBench-CN compared to MMBench (English). This suggests an imbalance in English vs. Chinese training corpora for many models or their underlying LLMs. Notably, InternLM-XComposer2 showed a minimal performance drop (less than 1%), indicating superior bilingual capabilities or more balanced cross-language multimodal training.

6. **Proprietary vs. Open-Source: A Fine-grained Analysis:**
 - Proprietary VLMs showed a distinct advantage in **structuralized image-text understanding** (e.g., interpreting charts, code, diagrams) and tasks requiring **external knowledge** (e.g., celebrity recognition, physical property reasoning).
 - However, they did *not* show a significant advantage over top open-source models in other perception or reasoning capabilities, suggesting specific areas where their proprietary training data or architectures excel.

7. **Content Moderation in Proprietary VLMs:** GPT-4v, Gemini-Pro-V, and Qwen-VL-Max were observed to reject answering a small percentage of questions (1.8%, 1.6%, and 0.1% respectively), primarily due to content moderation policies (e.g., GPT-4v refusing celebrity recognition). While this impacts their evaluated accuracy, the effect was relatively minor (up to 2.4% estimated accuracy difference), indicating that their overall performance is still high.

8. **Hard Cases and Common VLM Limitations:** MMBench identified several persistent weaknesses across almost all evaluated VLMs:
 - **Low-level Visual Features:** Poor ability to accurately recognize and compare subtle visual attributes like brightness, sharpness, contrast ratio, or image artifacts.
 - **Structuralized Visual Inputs:** Difficulty in understanding and extracting information from complex structural elements like tables, diagrams, or intricate layouts, even in relatively simple cases.
 - **Inter-object Spatial Relationships:** Weak performance in reasoning about complex 2D or 3D spatial relationships between objects.

These findings provide a clear roadmap for future VLM research, highlighting both the impressive progress and the critical areas that require further attention for achieving truly "all-around" multimodal intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MMBench marks a significant step forward in the evaluation paradigm for Vision-Language Models, with broad implications for the advancement of multimodal AI.

1. **Establishing a New Standard for Robust Evaluation:** MMBench directly addresses the critical need for a more comprehensive, objective, and scalable evaluation framework. By overcoming the limitations of both traditional objective benchmarks (false negatives, lack of fine-grained analysis) and subjective human evaluations (cost, bias, scalability), MMBench offers a more reliable and reproducible measure of VLM performance. The novel **CircularEval strategy** sets a higher bar for model consistency and understanding, effectively differentiating truly capable models from those that might achieve high scores due to chance or biases. The **LLM-involved choice extraction** intelligently handles diverse VLM output formats, ensuring fair assessment across models with varying instruction-following capabilities.

2. **Driving Fine-grained Model Improvement:** The detailed **hierarchical ability taxonomy** is perhaps one of MMBench's most impactful contributions. By dissecting multimodal intelligence into 20 fine-grained skills, the benchmark provides invaluable diagnostic information. Researchers can now pinpoint specific areas where their models excel or falter (e.g., "Image Quality," "Structuralized Image-Text Understanding," "Social Relation"), rather than just receiving a single aggregate score. This fine-grained feedback is crucial for guiding targeted model development, architectural innovations, and data curation efforts, enabling a more scientific and efficient approach to improving VLMs.

3. **Promoting Bilingual and Cross-cultural AI Research:** The inclusion of a meticulously translated **MMBench-CN** version is a pioneering effort. It facilitates the direct comparison of VLM performance in both English and Chinese contexts, shedding light on potential linguistic biases in current models and training datasets. As AI becomes increasingly global, benchmarks that evaluate cross-lingual and cross-cultural capabilities are essential for developing truly universal and inclusive AI systems.

4. **Enhancing Data Quality and Benchmark Integrity:** The proposed **rigorous quality control mechanisms** (filtering "text-only" and "wrong" questions using SOTA LLMs and VLMs) are highly significant. These techniques are generalizable and can be adopted by the broader community to improve the quality and integrity of other existing and future benchmarks, ensuring that evaluation accurately reflects genuine multimodal understanding.

5. **Fostering Open Science and Collaboration:** By making the development set data and evaluation code (integrated into VLMEvalKit) publicly available, MMBench actively encourages community engagement, transparency, and collaborative research. This fosters a shared understanding of VLM capabilities and challenges, accelerating collective progress.

6. **Informing the Quest for AGI:** Fundamentally, MMBench contributes to the long-term goal of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). By systematically evaluating models on core perception and reasoning abilities, it provides a benchmark against which progress towards human-like multimodal intelligence can be measured. The identified "hard cases" and common VLM limitations offer clear directions for foundational research needed to overcome current bottlenecks and build more robust, intelligent, and versatile AI systems.

In essence, MMBench is more than just a dataset; it is a meticulously designed analytical tool that provides clarity and direction in the complex landscape of multimodal AI research. Its widespread adoption is poised to significantly impact how VLMs are developed, evaluated, and ultimately improved, moving the field closer to realizing the full potential of multimodal artificial intelligence.
