# Object Motion Guided Human Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> OMOMO synthesizes full-body human manipulation motion from object motion alone. It splits generation into two conditional diffusion stages: the first predicts hand joint positions from Basis Point Set object geometry features, the second generates full-body SMPL-X poses from those hands, with a contact-constraint rectification between stages. It reaches a 0.72 contact F1 on 15 objects, generalizes to unseen objects, and drives a smartphone-only motion capture system.

## Problem

- Prior contextual motion synthesis models static scenes or static objects, leaving dynamic full-body interaction with large moving objects largely unaddressed.
- Reinforcement-learning manipulation policies stay tied to the specific geometry used for training, typically boxes, so they fail on diverse everyday objects.
- Existing human-object datasets cover small grasped objects or low-quality multi-view captures, offering too little per-object data to train motion generative models.

## Method

- Two-stage **conditional diffusion** with hand positions as the intermediate representation: stage one predicts left and right hand joints, stage two synthesizes full-body poses.
- Object geometry encoded with ==Basis Point Set== features, 1024 sampled ball points plus centroid location, compressed by an MLP into a 256-dimensional per-frame condition.
- A ==hand contact constraint== post-process detects first contact under a 0.03 threshold and propagates the hand-to-vertex offset via object rotation across later frames.

## Results

- On 15 objects OMOMO achieves **24.01 cm** hand JPE and **0.72** contact F1, versus **0.64** without contact constraints, **0.51** single-stage, and **0.32** for the GOAL baseline.
- On 5 unseen objects it holds **0.61** F1 and **25.12 cm** hand JPE, beating single-stage **0.47** and GOAL **0.29**, showing generalization to new geometry.
- Perceptual study: **85%** preferred OMOMO over GOAL, **65%** over single-stage, **31%** over ground truth; feeding ground-truth hands cuts MPJPE from **12.42** to **7.01 cm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing a low-dimensional intermediate variable that is causally decisive, here hand position, lets you inject hard geometric constraints that end-to-end diffusion cannot enforce.
> - Contact realism and joint-position accuracy trade off: the constraint raised contact recall from 0.58 to 0.70 while slightly worsening MPJPE.
> - Rigid-offset contact propagation breaks on intermittent contact, gluing hands to objects and causing penetration; kinematic pipelines still need explicit contact-state prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of contextual human motion synthesis is critical for applications in character animation, embodied AI, virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), and robotics. Existing research has made progress in modeling human interactions with static 3D environments or stationary objects, such as navigating scenes or sitting on chairs. These efforts often rely on large-scale human motion datasets that capture interactions with immutable scene elements.

However, a significant gap exists in the synthesis of full-body human motion when interacting with dynamic, moving objects. Datasets containing comprehensive full-body human-object interaction with actively manipulated, large-sized objects are scarce. Prior attempts to model such dynamic behaviors frequently employ reinforcement learning, but these methods are often limited to specific object geometries used during training and may not generalize well. Furthermore, much of the existing work involving dynamic objects focuses primarily on dexterous hand motions rather than full-body manipulation. The challenge intensifies when considering the precise coordination required between the entire human body, particularly the hands, and a moving object.

This work addresses this specific gap by focusing on synthesizing full-body human motion for the manipulation of large-sized objects, guided solely by the object's motion. It moves beyond interactions with static environments or small, hand-held objects to tackle the complexity of full-body engagement with dynamic, larger items. The authors leverage conditional diffusion models, a generative approach that has recently shown success in motion modeling, to handle the inherent uncertainty in generating diverse human motions from a single object trajectory. A key contribution is the development of a framework that explicitly enforces contact constraints, a crucial aspect for generating physically plausible human-object interactions that has not been adequately addressed by naive application of generative models. Additionally, the paper introduces a new, large-scale dataset specifically tailored for dynamic human-object interactions, directly contributing to alleviating the data scarcity issue in this domain. This positions the work as an advancement in creating more realistic and generalizable models for human behavior in dynamic, interactive environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for synthesizing full-body human motion that is guided by the motion of an object being manipulated. This core objective is driven by several detailed motivations and aims:

1. **Generate Full-Body Manipulation Behaviors from Object Motion:** The central aim is to predict plausible full-body human poses based solely on a sequence of object states. This is motivated by the observation that in real-world scenarios, human actions are often a direct response to, or cause of, object movement. The ability to synthesize the full body's motion, rather than just hand or partial body movements, is crucial for realistic character animation and embodied agents.

2. **Address the Challenge of Uncertainty:** Human motion exhibits inherent variability. Multiple plausible full-body movements can lead to the same observed object motion. The research is motivated to develop a generative model capable of capturing this one-to-many mapping, providing diverse yet realistic human responses. Diffusion models are identified as a suitable framework for this task due to their capacity for generating varied outputs.

3. **Ensure Physically Plausible Contact Constraints:** A significant challenge in synthesizing human-object interaction is maintaining accurate and realistic contact between the human and the object. Naive application of generative models often fails to enforce these contact constraints precisely, leading to interpenetration or detachment artifacts. A key motivation is to devise a mechanism within the synthesis pipeline that explicitly ensures consistent and physically plausible contact, particularly between the hands and the manipulated object.

4. **Enhance Generalizability to Unseen Objects:** Prior methods, especially those based on reinforcement learning, often struggle to generalize beyond the specific geometries or object types used during training. This work aims to develop an approach that can synthesize plausible manipulation motions for objects not encountered during the model's training phase, thereby increasing its practical utility and robustness.

5. **Overcome Data Scarcity for Dynamic Human-Object Interaction:** A critical motivation stems from the recognized scarcity of high-quality datasets that pair 3D object geometry, object motion, and corresponding full-body human motion for dynamic manipulation tasks. The authors are motivated to collect and contribute a large-scale dataset to facilitate further research and development in this underserved area. This dataset aims to capture diverse interactions with various large-sized objects, resembling real-world scenarios.

6. **Develop an Accessible Motion Capture System:** The research is also motivated by the desire to create a user-friendly and affordable method for capturing human-object interaction motions. This involves exploring the feasibility of using readily available consumer devices, such as smartphones, to capture object motion that can then drive full-body human motion synthesis. This aims to democratize the creation of interactive motion data.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for more advanced, realistic, and generalizable methods for synthesizing full-body human-object interactions with dynamic objects, driven by accurate contact enforcement, new data resources, and accessible capture techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Object MOtion guided human MOtion synthesis (OMOMO), is a conditional diffusion framework designed to generate full-body human poses from a sequence of object states. The approach addresses the inherent challenges of uncertainty in pose prediction and the necessity of precise contact constraints through a two-stage pipeline.

### 4. Data Representation

- **Human Pose Representation:** Human poses at each timestep are represented using the SMPL-X parametric human model. The state includes global joint positions ($\mathbf{J}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{24 \times 3}$) and global joint rotations ($\mathbf{Q}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{22 \times 6}$), with rotations encoded using a 6D continuous representation.
- **Object Representation:** For a sequence of 3D object geometries ($\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K \times 3}$), a Basis Point Set (BPS) representation is employed. This method encodes object geometry into a lightweight, fixed-length vector ($\in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 3}$) by calculating the difference between sampled points within a sphere (centered at the object's centroid) and their nearest neighbors on the object mesh. The global 3D location of the object's centroid is concatenated with the BPS representation. Finally, a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) projects these high-dimensional features into a lower-dimensional object geometry feature vector ($\mathbf{O}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$).

### 5. Conditional Diffusion Formulation

OMOMO utilizes a standard Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) framework.
- **Forward Diffusion Process:** This process gradually adds Gaussian noise to the data representation ($\mathbf{x}_0$) over $N$ timesteps, forming a Markov chain. Each step is governed by a fixed variance schedule ($\beta_n$).
- **Reverse Diffusion Process:** This process learns a neural network ($p_\theta$) to denoise a noisy input ($\mathbf{x}_n$) iteratively, starting from random noise, to reconstruct the desired data ($\mathbf{x}_{n-1}$). The network predicts the mean ($\mu_\theta$) of the reverse distribution, which is reparameterized to predict the original data ($\hat{\mathbf{x}}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_n, n, \mathbf{c})$). The training objective is an L1 reconstruction loss between the predicted original data and the ground truth. The conditions ($\mathbf{c}$) are incorporated into the denoising network. The denoising network architecture is a Transformer model, consisting of four self-attention blocks, each with a multi-head attention layer and a position-wise feedforward layer. Noise level embedding is an additional input to the transformer.

### 6. Two-Stage Pipeline for Human Motion Synthesis

The core of OMOMO is a two-stage approach:

1. **Stage 1: Generating Hand Positions from Object Geometry:**
 - The conditional diffusion model is trained to generate a sequence of right and left hand joint positions ($\mathbf{H}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times 6}$) using the object geometry features ($\mathbf{O}_t$) as conditions.
 - The objective is to predict plausible hand placements on or near the object throughout the manipulation sequence.

2. **Apply Hand Contact Constraints (Post-processing):**
 - The hand joint positions generated in Stage 1 may not consistently maintain precise contact with the object. To address this, a post-processing step is introduced.
 - It first calculates the minimum distance from hand joints to the object mesh at each timestep.
 - An empirical contact threshold (0.03m) is used to identify frames where contact is inferred.
 - For a frame $k$ where contact is detected, a difference vector ($\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{H}_k - \mathbf{V}_{i_k}$) is computed between the hand joint position and its nearest vertex on the object mesh.
 - This difference vector, adjusted by the object's rotation, is then used to update hand positions ($\hat{\mathbf{H}}_t = \mathbf{V}_{i_t} + \mathbf{R}_t \mathbf{R}_k^{-1} \mathbf{p}$) for subsequent timesteps, ensuring consistent relative positioning and maintaining contact.
 - The system infers single or two-handed manipulation based on whether one or both hands are in close proximity to the object (Euclidean distance < 0.03m).

3. **Stage 2: Generating Full-body Poses from Rectified Hand Positions:**
 - The conditional diffusion model in this stage generates full-body poses ($\mathbf{X}_t$) using the rectified (contact-constrained) hand joint positions ($\hat{\mathbf{H}}_t$) as conditions.
 - This stage focuses on synthesizing the complete body's movement, conditioned on the more reliable and physically plausible hand-object interaction. The model is trained purely on human motion data.

### 7. Dataset Collection

To support this research, a new large-scale dataset was collected:

- **Objects:** 15 everyday, large-sized objects (e.g., tables, chairs, mops, suitcases).
- **Object Geometry Capture:** 3D object geometry was reconstructed from monocular videos using Luma AI, followed by manual cleaning and downsampling using Meshlab.
- **Motion Capture:** A Vicon system with 12 cameras (120 FPS) was used to simultaneously capture human and object motion. 5 markers were attached to each object. 17 subjects performed guided interactions with verbal instructions for meaningful actions, resulting in approximately 10 hours of motion data across all objects.
- **Data Processing:** Object SDFs were computed using a public library or SIREN for noisy cases. Raw human mocap files were processed with Mosh++ to extract SMPL-X parameters. Object transformations were derived from marker positions using the orthogonal Procrustes problem, followed by manual verification.

### 8. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Dataset Splits:** Two evaluation settings were used: (1) training and testing on all 15 objects, and (2) training on 10 objects and testing on 5 unseen objects to assess generalization.
- **Baselines:** An adapted version of GOAL, an autoregressive model for object-reaching motion synthesis, was used as a baseline.
- **Metrics:** A comprehensive set of metrics was employed, including:
 - Pose/Joint Errors: HandJPE (hand joint position error), MPJPE (mean per-joint position error), MPVPE (mean per-vertex error).
 - Root Motion Errors: $\mathbf{T}_{root}$ (root translation error), $\mathbf{O}_{root}$ (root orientation error).
 - Physical Plausibility: FS (foot sliding), Collision Percentage (human-object interpenetration).
 - Contact Performance: $\mathbf{C}_{prec}$ (precision), $\mathbf{C}_{rec}$ (recall), F1 Score for hand-object contact detection.
- **Ablation Study:** Variants of OMOMO were tested, including a single-stage model and a two-stage model without contact constraints, as well as a model using ground truth hand positions (OMOMO-GT) to evaluate the impact of each component.
- **Human Perceptual Study:** A study involving Amazon Mechanical Turk workers evaluated the naturalness and contact realism of generated sequences compared to baselines and ground truth.
- **Application Tests:** The pipeline was tested with manually animated object trajectories in Blender and object motion captured using an iPhone's ARKit API.

This methodical approach, combining a two-stage diffusion framework with explicit contact enforcement and a dedicated dataset, aims to achieve robust and realistic full-body human motion synthesis for dynamic object manipulation.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provided several key findings regarding the effectiveness, realism, and generalizability of the OMOMO framework:

1. **Superior Quantitative Performance:** OMOMO consistently outperformed the adapted GOAL baseline and its own single-stage variant across most quantitative metrics for both training/testing on all 15 objects and the more challenging unseen object scenario (Tables 2 and 3).
 - Specifically, OMOMO demonstrated lower mean hand joint position errors (Hand JPE), mean per-joint position errors (MPJPE), mean per-vertex errors (MPVPE), and reduced root translation and orientation errors ($\mathbf{T}_{root}$, $\mathbf{O}_{root}$) compared to the baselines. For instance, on all 15 objects, OMOMO achieved a Hand JPE of 24.01 cm and MPJPE of 12.42 cm, significantly lower than GOAL's 49.90 cm and 15.64 cm, respectively.

2. **Enhanced Contact Realism:** The two-stage design, particularly the inclusion of the explicit contact constraints, significantly improved the realism of hand-object interactions.
 - OMOMO achieved substantially higher contact precision ($\mathbf{C}_{prec}$), recall ($\mathbf{C}_{rec}$), and F1 Score (0.82, 0.70, and 0.72 respectively for all 15 objects) compared to the OMOMO variant without contact constraints (0.36, 0.83, 0.58, 0.64) and the OMOMO-single-stage (0.38, 0.78, 0.42, 0.51). This indicates that the contact constraint mechanism effectively enforced plausible hand-object contact, mitigating issues like non-contact or interpenetration.
 - While the collision percentage for OMOMO (0.22%) was higher than GOAL (0.06%), this was attributed to GOAL's tendency to generate motions where the character often did not engage with the object, thus avoiding collisions but failing the task.

3. **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** When tested on 5 unseen objects, OMOMO maintained its quantitative advantage over the baselines. Although error metrics slightly increased compared to testing on seen objects, the performance degradation was controlled, and contact metrics remained strong (e.g., F1 Score of 0.61 for unseen objects vs. 0.72 for all objects), demonstrating the model's capacity to generalize to new object geometries.

4. **Ablation Study Confirmation:** The ablation study (Table 4) confirmed the importance of accurate hand position prediction. When the second stage of OMOMO was supplied with ground truth hand positions (OMOMO-GT), the overall performance, including MPJPE, $\mathbf{T}_{root}$, and contact F1 score, improved further (e.g., MPJPE of 7.01 cm for OMOMO-GT vs. 12.42 cm for OMOMO). This suggests that while OMOMO's first stage for hand position prediction is effective, there is still room for improvement in this sub-task, which directly impacts the quality of the full-body synthesis.

5. **Positive Human Perceptual Evaluation:** A human perceptual study involving 20 workers per question (Figure 7) demonstrated a clear preference for OMOMO over both the GOAL baseline and the OMOMO-single-stage variant, specifically regarding motion naturalness and contact realism.
 - Compared to GOAL, 85% of workers preferred OMOMO.
 - Compared to OMOMO-single-stage, 65% preferred OMOMO.
 - When compared against ground truth, 31% of workers preferred OMOMO, indicating that while ground truth remains the gold standard, OMOMO generates highly competitive and often indistinguishable results for some sequences. The paper acknowledges that some artifacts in generated motion (e.g., penetration) led to ground truth preference in other cases.

6. **Functional Applications Demonstrated:**
 - The system successfully synthesized plausible human motion when provided with manually animated object trajectories created in Blender (Figure 9), highlighting its utility for animation artists.
 - A novel application was demonstrated where object motion captured by an iPhone's ARKit API (using ARWorldTrackingConfiguration) could drive the synthesis of full-body human motion (Figure 10). This showcased an affordable and user-friendly method for generating human interaction motions from real-world object movements.

7. **Dataset Contribution:** The collection of a 10-hour, high-quality dataset covering 15 distinct objects with paired 3D object geometry, object motion, and full-body human motion represents a significant resource that addresses a critical data gap in the field.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that OMOMO is an effective and robust framework for object motion-guided human motion synthesis, providing more realistic and physically plausible interactions than previous approaches, particularly in handling contact constraints and generalizing to new objects. The contributions of the dataset and the smartphone-based application further enhance the practical relevance of this work.

### 10. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and carries substantial potential for impact across various domains:

1. **Advancement in Human-Object Interaction Modeling:** The work significantly advances the capability to model full-body human interaction with dynamic, large-sized objects, a previously under-explored and challenging area. By shifting focus from static environments or small-object dexterous manipulation to full-body engagement with moving objects, it pushes the boundaries of contextual human motion synthesis.

2. **Enhanced Realism and Physical Plausibility:** The introduction of a two-stage conditional diffusion framework, coupled with explicit hand contact constraints, directly addresses the critical issue of physical plausibility. By ensuring consistent and realistic contact between the human and the object, the generated motions are less prone to artifacts like interpenetration or detachment, leading to more believable and immersive experiences. This is a crucial step towards generating "physically aware" virtual characters and agents.

3. **Addressing Data Scarcity with a High-Quality Dataset:** The creation and release of a large-scale, high-quality dataset comprising 3D object geometry, object motion, and full-body human motion for 15 diverse objects is a substantial contribution. This resource is poised to catalyze future research in human-object interaction, providing a standardized benchmark and rich training data for developing more sophisticated models in computer graphics, robotics, and embodied AI. It lowers the barrier to entry for researchers in this domain, which often suffers from a lack of comprehensive real-world data.

4. **Democratization of Motion Capture for Interactions:** The demonstrated application of using a standard smartphone (iPhone ARKit) to capture object motion and then synthesize corresponding human motion offers an accessible and affordable alternative to traditional, expensive motion capture systems. This could democratize the creation of interactive motion data, enabling animators, game developers, VR/AR content creators, and even robotics researchers to generate human-like manipulation sequences without specialized hardware, making such technology more widely available.

5. **Impact on Character Animation and Virtual Agents:** The ability to generate realistic full-body manipulation motions from object cues has immediate implications for character animation. Animators could more easily create complex interactive scenes by only specifying object trajectories, with the human motion being automatically generated. This can lead to more dynamic and believable virtual characters in films, video games, and simulations. In embodied AI, it could enable agents to learn and perform complex manipulation tasks more naturally and efficiently.

6. **Applications in VR/AR and Robotics:** In VR/AR, the technology can enhance immersion by generating contextually appropriate human motions for virtual avatars interacting with dynamic virtual objects. For robotics, understanding and synthesizing human manipulation can provide valuable insights for designing more adaptive and human-like robot control strategies, particularly for collaborative robotics where robots need to interact with objects alongside humans.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a robust framework that can serve as a foundation for future advancements. The identified limitations, such as handling dexterous hand movements, intermittent contacts, and integrating physics-based components, outline clear avenues for subsequent research. The two-stage approach provides a modular architecture where improvements in hand pose prediction or contact modeling can directly enhance the overall full-body synthesis.

In conclusion, "Object Motion Guided Human Motion Synthesis" makes significant strides in an under-researched area, offering both a novel methodological framework and a critical dataset. Its potential to improve realism, lower barriers to content creation, and inform intelligent systems across multiple fields positions it as an impactful contribution to the scientific community.
