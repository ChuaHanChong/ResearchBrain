# Scaling Cross-Embodiment World Models for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego and collaborators developed a particle-based world model capable of learning generalizable dynamics for dexterous manipulation across diverse robot hands and human interaction data. This model enables zero-shot transfer for model-based control on novel robotic hardware, effectively reshaping deformable objects and pushing rigid ones.

## Problem

- Achieving generalist robot capabilities across diverse physical embodiments is challenging, as most robotic solutions are often embodiment-specific.
- Transferring data and control policies between robots with different action spaces and kinematics for complex dexterous manipulation tasks remains difficult.
- Developing unified state and action representations is crucial for abstracting away embodiment-specific details and enabling cross-embodiment learning.

## Method

- A unified particle-based representation describes both end-effectors (human or robot hands) and objects, with actions defined as end-effector particle displacement fields.
- A Graph Neural Network (GNN) world model (DPI-Net) is trained on diverse interaction data (simulated robots, real human hands) to predict future states based on this unified representation.
- Model-Predictive Control (MPC) using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) integrates the learned world model for real-world control on novel dexterous robotic hardware.

## Results

- Training on diverse simulated hands enabled zero-shot generalization to unseen robotic embodiments, often matching or exceeding performance of models trained directly on target-specific data.
- A 1:1 ratio of simulated robot data to real human interaction data yielded the lowest prediction error across tasks, establishing an optimal co-training strategy.
- The learned world models successfully enabled model-based control for challenging tasks like deformable object reshaping on novel 6-DoF and 12-DoF robot hands in the real world without fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Environment dynamics are largely 'embodiment-invariant,' and a unified particle-based world model can effectively capture these dynamics across diverse manipulators.
> - Cross-embodiment training exhibits a positive scaling law, where increasing diversity in training embodiments leads to improved generalization and robust performance on unseen hardware.
> - Co-training with a balanced mixture of simulation and real human data significantly reduces the sim-to-real gap, outperforming either data source alone for real-world robotic deployment.
