# Scaling Cross-Embodiment World Models for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego and collaborators developed a particle-based world model capable of learning generalizable dynamics for dexterous manipulation across diverse robot hands and human interaction data. This model enables zero-shot transfer for model-based control on novel robotic hardware, effectively reshaping deformable objects and pushing rigid ones.

## Problem

- Achieving generalist robot capabilities across diverse physical embodiments is challenging, as most robotic solutions are often embodiment-specific.
- Transferring data and control policies between robots with different action spaces and kinematics for complex dexterous manipulation tasks remains difficult.
- Developing unified state and action representations is crucial for abstracting away embodiment-specific details and enabling cross-embodiment learning.

## Method

- A unified particle-based representation describes both end-effectors (human or robot hands) and objects, with actions defined as end-effector particle displacement fields.
- A Graph Neural Network (GNN) world model (DPI-Net) is trained on diverse interaction data (simulated robots, real human hands) to predict future states based on this unified representation.
- Model-Predictive Control (MPC) using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) integrates the learned world model for real-world control on novel dexterous robotic hardware.

## Results

- Training on diverse simulated hands enabled zero-shot generalization to unseen robotic embodiments, often matching or exceeding performance of models trained directly on target-specific data.
- A 1:1 ratio of simulated robot data to real human interaction data yielded the lowest prediction error across tasks, establishing an optimal co-training strategy.
- The learned world models successfully enabled model-based control for challenging tasks like deformable object reshaping on novel 6-DoF and 12-DoF robot hands in the real world without fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Environment dynamics are largely 'embodiment-invariant,' and a unified particle-based world model can effectively capture these dynamics across diverse manipulators.
> - Cross-embodiment training exhibits a positive scaling law, where increasing diversity in training embodiments leads to improved generalization and robust performance on unseen hardware.
> - Co-training with a balanced mixture of simulation and real human data significantly reduces the sim-to-real gap, outperforming either data source alone for real-world robotic deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses one of the most significant challenges in modern robotics: achieving **generalist robot capabilities** that can operate across diverse physical embodiments and learn from heterogeneous data sources. The vision of "generalist embodied agents" is a major driving force in current robotics research, aiming to create robots that are adaptable and can perform a wide range of tasks in unstructured environments.

Historically, robotics has made significant strides in specific domains (e.g., industrial manipulators for repetitive tasks, legged robots for locomotion), but these solutions are often embodiment-specific, requiring extensive re-engineering or retraining for new hardware. **Cross-embodiment learning** is a paradigm shift aiming to overcome this limitation. Prior work has demonstrated embodiment-level generalization in areas like locomotion and manipulation with parallel grippers, where the action spaces and kinematics are relatively simpler. However, extending such generalization to **dexterous manipulation** – involving multi-fingered hands with high degrees of freedom (DoF) and complex, fine-grained contact interactions – has remained a formidable challenge. Most dexterous manipulation efforts have been confined to grasping and in-hand reorientation. This paper seeks to expand this generalization to more complex domains like non-prehensile object pushing and deformable object manipulation.

Within this context, the paper positions itself as a **model-based approach**, contrasting with prevalent model-free methods like Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Behavior Cloning (BC). While model-free methods have shown success, they often require extensive simulation training (facing the sim-to-real gap) or large datasets of expert demonstrations. Model-based approaches, which learn **world models** to explicitly predict the effects of actions, are gaining traction for tasks requiring fine-grained control and reasoning about physics. This work explicitly builds upon the strengths of world models by hypothesizing that they can capture environment dynamics that are "embodiment-invariant," thus providing a unified interface.

A specific sub-area of world models involves **particle-based models**, which are more physically grounded than pixel-based models and incorporate stronger inductive biases about local interactions. Previous work explored particle-based models for human-object interactions, but their direct application for model-based control or transfer to novel embodiments was limited. This paper significantly advances this by demonstrating that particle-based world models can be effectively scaled across *multiple* embodiments, including both simulated robots and real human hands, and deployed for model-based control on novel robotic hardware.

The paper also addresses the crucial issue of **data efficiency and diversity**. By proposing a method to co-train on both simulated robot data and real human data, it taps into two valuable resources: the scalability and control of simulation, and the realism and naturalness of human interaction data. This strategy is vital for bridging the "reality gap" and "embodiment gap" that often plague robotics research, contributing to the broader effort of leveraging large, diverse datasets (e.g.,) for robot learning.

In summary, this work pushes the boundaries of cross-embodiment learning by tackling the complex domain of dexterous manipulation using a novel particle-based world model approach. It leverages the strengths of diverse data sources (simulated robots, real humans) and advanced neural network architectures (GNNs) to create a more generalizable and capable robotic system, addressing fundamental questions about data scaling and knowledge transfer in robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to contribute to the vision of **building generalist embodied agents** capable of operating across diverse physical morphologies. To achieve this, the authors identify several key objectives and motivations:

1. **Overcome Data and Policy Transfer Barriers:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent difficulties in sharing data and transferring control policies across robots with different action spaces, kinematics, and physical dimensions. This "cross-embodiment" problem is exacerbated by the natural variations in hardware, manufacturing tolerances, and upgrades common in large-scale robot deployments.
2. **Identify Embodiment-Invariant Knowledge:** A central question driving this research is: "Is there any invariance that allows actions to transfer across embodiments?" The authors hypothesize that **environment dynamics** (i.e., how objects respond to interactions) are fundamentally embodiment-invariant. If a model can capture these universal dynamics, it could serve as a unified interface for diverse manipulators.
3. **Develop a Unified State and Action Representation:** To operationalize the idea of embodiment-invariant dynamics, the crucial challenge is to design **state and action representations that abstract away embodiment-specific details** while retaining control relevance. This representation must be capable of mapping heterogeneous data (from human hands, various robot hands) and control spaces into a shared domain.
4. **Learn a Unified World Model:** The core objective is to learn a single predictive model – a **world model** – from diverse cross-embodiment data. This model would predict future states given current states and actions in the unified representation, thereby capturing the conjectured embodiment-invariant dynamics.
5. **Enable Model-Based Control on Novel Hardware:** Beyond learning, a key objective is to demonstrate that the learned world model can facilitate effective model-based control on previously unseen or distinct robotic hardware, without the need for extensive re-training or motion retargeting. This requires converting robot-specific joint actions into the unified action representation for planning.
6. **Investigate Scaling Laws and Data Synergies:** The paper aims to empirically study how generalization performance scales with the diversity and quantity of training embodiments. Furthermore, it seeks to determine the optimal strategies for combining different data sources – specifically, simulated robot interaction data and real-world human interaction data – to leverage their complementary strengths and bridge the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Q1: Embodiment Scaling:** Does training on more diverse embodiments improve generalization to unseen ones?
 - **Q2: Co-training Recipe:** How can simulation and real-world data be effectively combined for training?
 - **Q3: Real-World Deployment:** Can the learned dynamics model enable effective model-based planning for dexterous manipulation on actual robot hardware?

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to lay a foundational step towards more adaptable, data-efficient, and generalist robots capable of dexterous manipulation in complex, contact-rich scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on creating a **unified particle-based representation** for hands and objects, training a **graph-based world model** on diverse data, and integrating this model with **model-predictive control (MPC)** for cross-embodiment deployment.

**A. Unified Particle-Based State and Action Space:**
The core innovation is representing both the robotic/human end-effector and the manipulated object as sets of 3D particles.
- **State Representation:** An end-effector (hand) is represented by $N_e$ particles ($X_t^{(e)}$) and an object by $N_o$ particles ($X_t^{(o)}$). The full world state at time $t$ is $X_t = (X_t^{(e)}, X_t^{(o)})$. This abstraction allows for diverse hands (e.g., human, 6-DoF, 12-DoF, 24-DoF robot hands) and objects (rigid, deformable) to be described uniformly.
- **Action Representation:** Actions are defined as the end-effector particle displacement field, $a_P_t = \Delta X_t^{(e)} = \{ \delta_{i,t} \in R^3 \}_{i=1}^{N_e}$. This means the action is not joint angles but rather the desired change in position for each particle on the hand. This is crucial for making actions embodiment-agnostic. Robot joint actions ($u_t$) are mapped to this particle displacement field via forward kinematics ($\Phi_e$).

**B. Perception Module:**
A multi-view camera setup is used for state estimation during data collection and deployment.
- **Hand Perception (human data):** Multi-view images are used to reconstruct human hand meshes with POEM-v2, from which particles are sampled using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to preserve geometry.
- **Object Perception:** Multi-view point clouds are fused, followed by Poisson surface reconstruction to obtain a smooth surface, and then FPS for particle sampling.
- **Robot Perception (deployment):** Robot state is acquired from proprioception, while object perception follows the same procedure as data collection.

**C. World Model Architecture:**
A Graph Neural Network (GNN), specifically DPI-Net, is chosen as the world model.
- **Graph Construction:** The world state (hand and object particles) forms the vertices of a graph, with edges connecting particles within a certain radius. Particle attributes include position and group information (robot or object).
- **Message Passing:** The GNN models local particle interactions through message passing, where node and edge features are propagated hierarchically through multiple steps (L steps).
- **Inductive Biases:** GNNs provide strong inductive biases:
 - **Spatial Locality:** Message passing is restricted to local neighborhoods, mirroring short-range physical interactions.
 - **Equivariance:** Relative coordinates and shared update functions ensure invariance to global translations, rotations, and particle permutations, which aids generalization.
- **Training:** The world model $f_\theta$ is trained via supervised learning to predict the next state $\hat{X}_{t+1}$ given the current state $X_t$ and particle action $a_P_t$. The loss function $L(O_t, \hat{O}_t)$ uses mean squared error (MSE) when point-level correspondence is available (in simulation) or Chamfer distance (CD) / Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) for unpaired point sets (e.g., real-world data). Critically, this training does not require expert demonstrations; random interaction data suffices.

**D. Model-Based Planning (MPC):**
For control, the learned world model is integrated with a Model-Predictive Control (MPC) framework, specifically using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM).
- **Action Parameterization:** To enable efficient planning in high-dimensional dexterous manipulation, the action space is constrained to low-dimensional motion primitives. For example:
 - **Object Pushing:** Constrained to a fixed x-y plane with global translations and variable finger contact.
 - **Plasticine Reshaping:** Defined by three primitives: FingersPinch, PalmPress, and ThumbPinch, each involving specific joint rotations and translations.
- **Planning Loop:**
 1. Sample sequences of robot joint actions $\{u_t\}_{t=0}^{H-1}$ (for a planning horizon H) from the parameterized action space.
 2. Map these joint actions to particle displacement fields $a_P_t$ using the robot's forward kinematics.
 3. Roll out the particle actions with the learned world model to predict future states.
 4. Evaluate the predicted final state against the target state (specified as a point cloud) using a cost function (e.g., CD or EMD).
 5. Optimize the sampled action sequence to minimize the cost function, and execute the first optimal action.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks:** Non-prehensile rigid object pushing (box reorientation) and deformable object reshaping (molding plasticine into target shapes).
- **Simulated Environments:** SAPIEN for rigid-body tasks and Rewarped (a differentiable multiphysics simulator) for deformable tasks. Six diverse robot hands (Ability, Allegro, XHand, Leap, Shadow, Shadow variant) are used to collect 100 random interaction trajectories per task.
- **Real-World Hardware:** A 7-DoF UFACTORY XArm 7 robot arm equipped with two different dexterous hands: PSYONIC Ability Hand (6-DoF) and Robot Era XHand (12-DoF). Multi-view Intel RealSense cameras provide perception.
- **Human Data:** 30 minutes of real human demonstrations for each plasticine reshaping primitive (ThumbPinch, FingersPinch, PalmPress) are collected.

This comprehensive approach combines robust data representation, advanced neural network modeling, and effective planning strategies to tackle the complexities of cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were structured to answer the three key questions (Q1, Q2, Q3) regarding embodiment scaling, co-training strategies, and real-world deployment.

**Q1: Does cross-embodiment training of the world model improve generalization on unseen embodiments? (Simulation Study)**

- **Embodiment Scaling Law:** The results clearly show a positive scaling trend. As more distinct embodiments are included in the training data, the prediction error on unseen, held-out embodiments consistently decreases (Figure 2). Concurrently, the variance in performance across different training subsets shrinks, indicating that greater diversity leads to more stable and robust models.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization Strength:** A remarkable finding is that when training on five out of six available simulated hands (effectively "zero-shot" to the sixth held-out hand), the model's performance often approaches or even surpasses that of a model trained *directly and solely* on data from the target embodiment itself. This strongly suggests that a diverse cross-embodiment dataset can effectively substitute for target-specific data, enabling deployment to novel hardware with minimal or no prior exposure.
- **Benefits of Co-training:** Even when data from the target embodiment is available and included in the training (denoted by x=6*), adding data from other embodiments still yields further performance gains over training only on the target hand. This highlights the synergistic effect of diverse data, even for known embodiments.
- **Task-Specific Differences:** The scaling effect was more pronounced in deformable object manipulation compared to rigid object pushing. The authors hypothesize that this is because deformable tasks involve larger and more varied contact surfaces, making end-effector geometry and diverse contact patterns more critical for accurate dynamics prediction. Rigid pushing, often relying on fewer contact points, is less sensitive to cross-embodiment variations.
- **Embodiment-Specific Trends:** Smaller hands (e.g., Ability Hand) achieved competitive performance with fewer training embodiments, while larger, more complex hands (e.g., Shadow Hand, Leap Hand) showed more significant improvements with increasing training diversity. This is attributed to graph density: smaller hands have more compact geometries and denser particle connections in the GNN, allowing better information propagation with less data. Larger hands, with sparser connections, require more diverse data to cover interaction patterns.

**Q2: What is the co-training recipe to leverage simulation and real-world data? (Sim-to-Real Study)**

- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Training the world model exclusively on simulation data resulted in the highest prediction error on real human interaction data (Figure 5), clearly demonstrating the pervasive sim-to-real gap.
- **Human-Only Baseline:** Training solely on real human data provided a stronger baseline performance, as it captures the true dynamics.
- **Optimal Co-training Balance:** Co-training with a mixture of simulation and human data significantly reduced prediction error, outperforming either simulation-only or human-only training. Specifically, a 1:1 ratio of simulation to human data yielded the best performance across both rigid and deformable tasks. This suggests that simulation data acts as a valuable regularizer and provides broader coverage of interaction patterns, complementing the realism of human data rather than simply substituting it. The authors note that evaluating on human data only serves as an approximation, necessitating real-robot validation.

**Q3: Does the learned dynamics model enable effective planning for dexterous manipulation? (Real-World Deployment)**

- **Zero-Shot Transfer to Robot Hardware:** Both the human-only and co-training models successfully enabled zero-shot transfer for model-based control on novel robot hands (Ability Hand and XHand) for the challenging Plasticine Reshaping task. This confirms that the unified particle-based state and action representations effectively abstract away hardware specifics.
- **Co-training Superiority:** Quantitatively (Table I), the co-training model demonstrated superior performance, achieving lower Chamfer Distance (CD) and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) errors when molding plasticine into target shapes compared to the human-only model. This confirms the benefits of combining simulated and real data for real-world robotic deployment.
- **Qualitative Success:** Qualitatively (Figure 4), both the 6-DoF Ability Hand and the 12-DoF XHand, despite their kinematic differences, were able to successfully reshape deformable clay into target letters (e.g., "X", "R", "T", "A") by composing the learned motion primitives (FingersPinch, PalmPress, ThumbPinch). This visual evidence strongly validates the model's ability to facilitate effective model-predictive planning on diverse robot hardware without any fine-tuning.

In summary, the main findings establish that world models, when built upon a cross-embodiment particle-based representation, can effectively learn generalizable dynamics, benefit significantly from scaling and co-training with diverse data, and enable robust model-based control on novel dexterous robot hands in the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions to the field of robotics and has considerable potential impact:

1. **Advancing Generalist Robotics:** The most profound impact lies in its contribution to building **generalist robots**. By demonstrating that a single world model, trained on diverse human and robot data, can control various dexterous hands, the research takes a crucial step towards robots that are not hard-coded for specific hardware but can adapt and operate across a spectrum of morphologies. This reduces the need for costly, time-consuming retraining or redesign for every new robot variant.

2. **Unified Interface for Heterogeneous Data and Control:** The introduction of a **unified particle-based state and action representation** is a key conceptual and practical breakthrough. This abstraction provides a common language for describing interactions across vastly different embodiments, making it possible to aggregate and learn from heterogeneous data sources (e.g., human demonstrations, varied robot simulation data, real robot interaction data) and to apply a single control policy. This is critical for scaling data collection and knowledge transfer in robotics.

3. **Data Efficiency and Leverage of Human Expertise:** The finding that diverse cross-embodiment data (including human data) can substitute for or significantly augment target-specific robot data is highly impactful. It suggests that robots can learn complex dexterous skills from readily available human interaction data and simulated environments, thereby **reducing the extensive and costly data collection burden** traditionally associated with robot learning. The optimal co-training recipe (1:1 sim-to-real ratio) provides practical guidance for practitioners aiming to bridge the sim-to-real gap effectively.

4. **Enabling Complex Dexterous Manipulation:** This research extends cross-embodiment generalization to previously challenging domains, specifically **non-prehensile pushing of rigid objects** and **dexterous manipulation of deformable objects**. These tasks require fine-grained contact control and intricate reasoning about physics, areas where model-based approaches, as demonstrated here, offer a strong advantage. The success in deformable manipulation, a notoriously difficult area, is particularly noteworthy.

5. **Strengthening Model-Based Control for Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment of the learned world models on real robot hardware (Ability Hand and XHand) for complex tasks highlights the practical utility of this approach. It validates world models as a **promising abstraction for effective model-predictive control** in contact-rich and high-DoF scenarios, demonstrating their ability to operate without fine-tuning on novel hardware.

6. **Insights into Embodiment Scaling Laws:** The empirical study of embodiment scaling laws provides valuable insights into how performance improves with increasing morphological diversity in the training data. This offers a principled way to think about dataset design and resource allocation in future robot learning efforts, indicating that "more diverse data" is indeed beneficial, particularly for complex tasks and larger embodiments. The observation about graph density also opens avenues for designing more robust GNN architectures.

**Future Directions and Broader Implications:**
This work serves as a foundational step. Future research could explore:
- Expanding the scope to even broader tasks and morphologies (e.g., different types of end-effectors, multi-arm systems).
- Developing more advanced world model architectures that are less sensitive to variations in graph density across embodiments.
- Investigating more sophisticated ways to balance and integrate diverse data sources, potentially using adaptive weighting or curriculum learning strategies.
- Applying this cross-embodiment framework to real-world tasks that require continuous learning and adaptation in dynamic environments.

Ultimately, this research moves the field closer to realizing truly adaptable and intelligent robots that can learn from a wide range of experiences, including human interaction, and seamlessly transfer their knowledge across different physical forms, paving the way for more versatile and impactful robotic applications in manufacturing, healthcare, and service industries.
