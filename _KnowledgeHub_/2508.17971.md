# Neural Algorithmic Reasoners informed Large Language Model for Multi-Agent Path Finding

> [!summary] Summary
> Beihang University researchers developed LLM-NAR, a framework integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with Graph Neural Network-based Neural Algorithmic Reasoners (NARs) for Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF). This approach improves LLMs' ability to generate collision-free and efficient paths by leveraging GNNs for spatial and algorithmic reasoning, achieving higher success rates and reduced execution times.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit limited capabilities in complex Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) tasks, particularly in understanding intricate spatial constraints and coordinating multiple agents precisely.
- Traditional MAPF algorithms, while often optimal, suffer from high computational costs and scalability issues as the number of agents and map complexity increase.
- Existing learning-based MAPF methods typically demand extensive training data and computational resources, hindering their efficiency and broad applicability.

## Method

- The ==LLM-NAR== framework combines an LLM for high-level understanding, a pre-trained ==GNN-based Neural Algorithmic Reasoner (NAR)== for spatial comprehension, and a ==cross-attention mechanism== for information fusion.
- A GNN-based NAR is pre-trained using optimal path data generated by ==Conflict-Based Search (CBS)==, enabling it to learn and represent map information and agent relationships effectively.
- A cross-attention mechanism efficiently fuses the linguistic outputs from the LLM with the spatial representations from the pre-trained GNN-NAR, with only the cross-attention network being trained on optimal actions in a few thousand steps.

## Results

- LLM-NAR consistently achieved higher success rates and produced paths with fewer average steps compared to baseline LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5-turbo, LLaMA3) across various simulated MAPF scenarios.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance in real-world experiments with LIMO mobile robots, successfully guiding all agents with shorter paths in scenarios where other LLM baselines failed.
- LLM-NAR exhibited significant efficiency gains, requiring only **5,000 training steps** (compared to **300,000** for RL methods) and achieved substantially faster execution times than classical optimal planning algorithms like CBS (e.g., **~2.0s vs ~32.3s** for **16 agents**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating GNN-based Neural Algorithmic Reasoners provides LLMs with the structured spatial knowledge and algorithmic reasoning capabilities essential for precise multi-agent path planning.
> - A focused training strategy for the cross-attention mechanism allows for highly efficient fusion of LLM and GNN-NAR outputs, drastically reducing the required training steps compared to end-to-end learning methods.
> - Augmenting LLMs with explicit spatial reasoning enables them to overcome inherent limitations in combinatorial planning, leading to more accurate, collision-free, and efficient paths in complex multi-agent environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) and Large Language Models (LLMs), situating itself within the rapidly evolving fields of AI, robotics, and multi-agent systems.

**Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF):** MAPF is a foundational problem in artificial intelligence and robotics, crucial for applications ranging from warehouse automation and logistics to autonomous vehicle coordination and swarm control. The core task involves navigating multiple agents from their respective start positions to unique goal positions within an environment, while avoiding collisions and often optimizing for metrics like total travel time. Traditionally, MAPF has been tackled by two main categories of methods:
1. **Classical Planning Algorithms:** Exemplified by Conflict-Based Search (CBS) and its variants, these methods are known for computing optimal or near-optimal solutions but often suffer from high computational costs, especially as the number of agents or map complexity increases.
2. **Learning-Based Approaches:** This category includes Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), which aim to learn effective policies directly from data or experience. While offering scalability benefits over classical methods, they often demand extensive training data and computational resources.

**Large Language Models (LLMs) in Multi-Agent Systems:** In recent years, LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding, generation, and reasoning across a wide spectrum of tasks. Their success has naturally led to explorations of their utility in multi-agent systems, particularly for enhancing inter-agent communication, coordination, and high-level decision-making. LLMs can interpret complex instructions, facilitate natural interaction, and even simulate multi-agent environments. However, their application to tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning and complex combinatorial planning, such as MAPF, has been limited. Existing studies highlight that LLMs struggle with understanding intricate spatial constraints and formulating collaborative strategies, leading to "less than satisfactory" performance. This paper directly tackles this identified gap.

**Neural Algorithmic Reasoning (NAR):** The concept of Neural Algorithmic Reasoning provides a crucial bridge between the adaptable nature of neural networks and the logical precision of classical algorithms. GNNs, due to their inherent ability to process graph-structured data and represent relationships, are particularly well-suited for NAR tasks. They can effectively approximate classical algorithms and learn to reason over complex graph data. This makes GNN-based NAR an ideal candidate to augment LLMs, which traditionally lack strong native spatial and algorithmic reasoning capabilities. The paper's novelty lies in being the *first work to propose using a neural algorithmic reasoner to integrate GNNs with map information for MAPF, thereby guiding LLM to achieve superior performance.* This approach aims to leverage the strengths of both LLMs (for high-level planning and understanding task descriptions) and GNNs (for detailed spatial and relational reasoning).

In essence, this research attempts to overcome the LLMs' inherent weaknesses in exact algorithmic execution and spatial comprehension by embedding algorithmic reasoning capabilities via GNNs, thereby creating a more robust and capable AI agent for complex multi-agent planning tasks like MAPF.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly improve the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) in Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) tasks, a domain where their inherent capabilities have proven insufficient. The authors aim to achieve this by integrating LLMs with Neural Algorithmic Reasoners (NARs), specifically those based on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs).

The key motivations driving this work are:
1. **Address LLM Limitations in MAPF:** Existing LLM-based approaches for MAPF have shown unsatisfactory performance, primarily due to their difficulties in understanding intricate spatial constraints, managing multi-agent coordination, and formulating precise, collision-free paths. The authors explicitly acknowledge this gap.
2. **Leverage Strengths of Both LLMs and GNNs:** LLMs excel at understanding natural language prompts, generating coherent text, and general high-level reasoning. GNNs, on the other hand, are powerful tools for processing graph-structured data, making them adept at representing spatial relationships, obstacles, and agent interactions in a MAPF environment. The motivation is to combine these complementary strengths.
3. **Enhance LLM's Spatial and Algorithmic Reasoning:** The goal is to "inform" the LLM with structured spatial knowledge and algorithmic reasoning capabilities, which are naturally handled by GNN-based NARs. This augmentation is hypothesized to enable LLMs to produce more accurate, collision-free, and efficient paths.
4. **Develop an Efficient and Adaptable Framework:** The authors aim for a framework that is not only effective but also efficient in terms of training time and adaptable to various LLM models. The use of a cross-attention mechanism, trainable with fewer steps than traditional learning methods, addresses these efficiency concerns.
5. **Contribute to Practical Multi-Agent Coordination:** By improving LLM performance in MAPF, the research seeks to pave the way for more robust and intelligent multi-agent systems in real-world applications such as warehouse management, traffic control, and swarm robotics.

In summary, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between LLM's impressive linguistic and general reasoning abilities and their current deficiencies in exact, spatially-aware algorithmic problem-solving for complex multi-agent coordination tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose LLM-NAR, a novel framework designed to enhance Large Language Models (LLMs) for Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) by integrating them with Neural Algorithmic Reasoners (NARs). The framework consists of three core components: an LLM for MAPF, a pre-trained GNN-based NAR, and a cross-attention mechanism for fusion.

**A. Overview of LLM-NAR Framework (Algorithm 1):**
The overarching strategy involves:
1. **GNN-NAR Pre-training:** Utilizing Conflict-Based Search (CBS) as an expert policy to generate optimal path data for MAPF tasks. This data is then used to pre-train a GNN-based NAR network, enabling it to accurately represent map information and agent relationships.
2. **LLM Prompting:** A specially designed prompt mechanism feeds scenario-specific information to the LLM at each timestep, enabling it to generate token outputs representing potential actions.
3. **Cross-Attention Fusion:** The outputs from the pre-trained GNN-NAR (spatial representation) and the LLM (linguistic action suggestions) are fused via a cross-attention mechanism.
4. **Cross-Attention Training:** The cross-attention network is trained by minimizing the loss between its fused action output and the optimal actions provided by the CBS expert policy, while keeping the LLM and GNN parameters frozen.

**B. LLM for MAPF Component:**
This module focuses on an improved prompt engineering strategy for LLMs in MAPF tasks, building upon previous work:
- **System and User Prompts:** A system prompt defines the LLM's role as a MAPF solver. User prompts provide detailed, step-by-step scene descriptions at each timestep, including current agent positions, target locations, a text-based map description, and coordinates of agents and obstacles. This detailed input helps the LLM maintain awareness of the current state and goals.
- **Simplified Workflow:** Unlike previous methods that might prompt with feasible actions, this approach simplifies the interaction by directly feeding scene information and integrating conflict checking into the environment transition. Invalid LLM actions are automatically corrected to a "stay" action.
- **Reset Mechanism:** To combat information loss or confusion (e.g., forgetting goals, generating invalid actions) often observed in LLMs over long sequences, a reset mechanism is introduced. The LLM is reset (its context cleared, and the task restarted from the current position) if it generates only invalid solutions for three consecutive rounds or after a fixed number of rounds (m=5 in experiments). This "reboot" helps maintain LLM performance.

**C. GNN-based NAR Component:**
This component is a pre-trained Graph Neural Network designed to extract and integrate spatial and relational dynamics from the MAPF environment:
- **Observation Processing:** At each timestep, each agent's local environmental observations (o_i^t) are collected into a matrix (O^t) and processed by a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to generate feature vectors (X^t).
- **Graph Convolution:** A connectivity structure between agents is defined by an adjacency matrix (C^t). Graph convolutions aggregate observational data from neighboring agents, enhancing the understanding of potential interactions (e.g., collisions).
- **Multi-Layer GNN:** The GNN comprises 'L' layers, where each layer updates its state by integrating outputs from the previous layer via graph convolution and a non-linear activation function (σ).
- **Action Prediction and Pre-training:** The final features (X_L^t) from the GNN layers are fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict actions (Û^t). The GNN is pre-trained by minimizing the loss between these predicted actions and the optimal actions ((U^t)*) obtained from CBS, effectively learning an "expert policy" for spatial reasoning.

**D. Cross-Attention Mechanism:**
Inspired by multi-modal integration frameworks like Flamingo, this component fuses the linguistic outputs from the LLM with the spatial representations from the GNN-based NAR:
- **Input Streams:** The LLM's output token (Θ^t) and the GNN's graph representation (X_L^t) serve as the two input modalities.
- **Self-Attention and Gated Cross-Attention:** The LLM tokens (Θ^t) are first refined through a Self-Attention block. Subsequently, they interact with the graph representations (X_L^t) via a Gated Cross-Attention block. Here, the LLM tokens form the *queries*, while the GNN representations provide the *keys* and *values*. This allows the LLM's linguistic understanding to query and integrate spatial knowledge from the GNN.
- **Multi-Layer Interaction:** This cross-attention process is repeated over N_l layers (N_l=3 in the paper), allowing for deeper integration of information.
- **Training Strategy:** During the training of the cross-attention component, the parameters of both the LLM and the pre-trained GNN-based NAR are frozen. Only the cross-attention network is optimized by minimizing the loss between its final action output and the CBS-derived optimal actions. This focused training approach is highly efficient, requiring only a few thousand training steps.

This modular design allows the LLM-NAR framework to leverage the strengths of each component, with the GNN providing robust spatial and relational understanding to guide the LLM's high-level planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and real-world scenarios consistently demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed LLM-NAR framework compared to existing LLM-based methods and provide insights into its efficiency against traditional and reinforcement learning approaches.

**A. Experiment Setup:**
- **Environment:** Experiments were performed on grid maps of varying sizes (8x8, 20x20, 28x28) with different numbers of agents (2, 4, 8, 10, 16) and obstacle densities (empty or 10% obstacles).
- **Baselines:** Comparative LLM baselines included Qwen2, Gemma2, LLaMA3, and GPT-3.5-turbo.
- **Metrics:** Performance was assessed using:
 - **Success Rate (R):** Percentage of agents reaching their goals.
 - **Average Step (δ):** Mean path length for all agents, normalized by the maximum allowed steps, indicating path efficiency.
- **Training Efficiency:** The cross-attention mechanism required only 5,000 training steps using 100 execution cases, a significantly lower number compared to typical reinforcement learning methods.

**B. Simulation Results:**
1. **Success Rate (Table I):**
 - **General Trend:** Across all map sizes and obstacle conditions, success rates decreased as the number of agents increased, indicating higher task complexity.
 - **LLM-NAR Superiority:** LLM-NAR consistently achieved significantly higher success rates than all baseline LLMs. For instance, on a 20x20 empty map with 10 agents, GPT-3.5-turbo had a 62.50% success rate, while LLM-NAR reached 80.00%. With 16 agents, where baselines often dropped below 60%, LLM-NAR maintained 65.63%.
 - **Obstacle Environments:** In environments with 10% obstacles, Qwen2 performed poorly, and LLaMA3 and GPT-3.5-turbo showed varied performance depending on agent numbers. However, LLM-NAR consistently outperformed all baselines, demonstrating its robustness in complex, constrained environments (e.g., 20x20 map with 10% obstacles, 16 agents: LLM-NAR 59.13% vs. GPT-3.5-turbo 48.44%).
2. **Average Step (Table II):**
 - **Path Efficiency:** LLM-NAR consistently required the fewest average steps to complete tasks, indicating more efficient and shorter paths. For example, on a 20x20 empty map with 10 agents, LLM-NAR achieved an average step of 0.54, whereas other methods required at least 0.69.
 - **Robustness:** Even with obstacles, LLM-NAR maintained its advantage in path efficiency across all map sizes and agent counts.
3. **Trajectory Visualization (Fig. 6):** Visual comparisons on a 20x20 map with 10% obstacles and 10 agents clearly showed that LLM-NAR enabled all agents to reach their goals with shorter and more efficient paths, unlike GPT-3.5-turbo where one agent failed and paths were less optimal.

**C. Practical Analysis (Comparison with Traditional and RL Methods):**
- **Training Efficiency (Table III):** LLM-NAR required only 5,000 training steps, drastically fewer than reinforcement learning methods like PRIMAL, DHC, and SCRIMP (each requiring 300,000 steps). This highlights a significant advantage in terms of computational resources and time needed for model deployment.
- **Execution Time (Table IV):** LLM-NAR achieved significantly lower execution times compared to the traditional optimal planning algorithm CBS. The runtime efficiency gap widened with an increasing number of agents (e.g., for 16 agents on a 20x20 map, LLM-NAR took ~2.0s while CBS took ~32.3s), demonstrating the scalability of LLM-NAR.

**D. Real-world Results:**
- **LIMO Mobile Robots:** Experiments with 2-4 LIMO mobile robots on a 5x4 map using a motion capture system validated the method in a physical environment.
- **Consistent Superiority:**
 - With two agents, all methods succeeded, but LLM-NAR produced shorter paths.
 - With four agents, GPT and LLaMA3 failed to guide some robots to their targets, while LLM-NAR successfully guided all robots with shorter paths. (Fig. 7 visually confirms this for 2, 3, and 4 robots).

The comprehensive experimental results strongly support the claims that LLM-NAR significantly improves LLM performance in MAPF tasks, offering higher success rates, greater path efficiency, faster training, and better scalability, making it a robust solution for complex multi-agent coordination.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LLM-NAR framework represents a significant advancement in bridging the gap between the high-level reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the precise, spatially-aware algorithmic demands of Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF). This work has several key significances and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced LLM Capabilities for Spatial Reasoning:** The most direct impact is providing LLMs with a robust mechanism for spatial and algorithmic reasoning. By explicitly integrating a GNN-based Neural Algorithmic Reasoner, LLM-NAR mitigates the inherent weaknesses of LLMs in tasks requiring precise combinatorial optimization and understanding of physical constraints. This could unlock LLMs for a broader range of real-world planning and control problems that demand more than just linguistic understanding.
2. **Efficient and Scalable Multi-Agent Planning:** The framework demonstrates remarkable efficiency in training (only 5,000 steps for the cross-attention mechanism) compared to traditional reinforcement learning methods (hundreds of thousands of steps). It also shows significantly faster execution times than classical optimal planners like CBS, especially with increasing agent numbers. This efficiency and scalability are crucial for practical deployment in dynamic and large-scale multi-agent systems.
3. **A Novel Hybrid AI Architecture:** LLM-NAR proposes a pioneering hybrid architecture that effectively combines the strengths of different AI paradigms: LLMs for high-level understanding and goal formulation, GNNs for structured spatial and relational reasoning, and a cross-attention mechanism for seamless information fusion. This paradigm could inspire similar multi-modal or multi-component AI systems that leverage specialized modules for different aspects of complex problem-solving.
4. **Broader Applicability to Robotics and Automation:** MAPF is fundamental to many robotics and automation applications, including automated warehouses, drone swarms, and autonomous vehicle coordination. LLM-NAR's improved performance and efficiency could lead to more intelligent, adaptable, and robust control systems for these domains, potentially reducing development costs and operational complexities.
5. **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of an open-source dataset for LLM-NAR interactions in MAPF tasks is a valuable contribution to the research community. It will facilitate further research, benchmarking, and development in this emerging area, enabling other researchers to build upon this work and explore new avenues.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens doors for future research, such as exploring temporal GNNs to further optimize performance, suggesting continuous improvements in the reasoning capabilities of such hybrid models. The framework's adaptability to various LLM models (e.g., potential for even greater gains with GPT-4 or future advanced models) also highlights its forward-looking design.

In conclusion, LLM-NAR represents a pragmatic and effective solution to a critical challenge in multi-agent AI. By intelligently augmenting LLMs with algorithmic reasoning, it paves the way for more capable, efficient, and broadly applicable intelligent agents in complex, spatially-constrained environments.
