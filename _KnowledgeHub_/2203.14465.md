# STaR: Bootstrapping Reasoning With Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The STaR (Self-Taught Reasoner) algorithm enables large language models to iteratively improve their reasoning capabilities by generating and filtering their own rationales, overcoming the need for extensive human-annotated datasets. This method, which includes a novel 'rationalization' step, allowed a 6B-parameter GPT-J model to achieve performance on arithmetic, commonsense, and math word problems comparable to or surpassing much larger state-of-the-art models.

## Problem

- Acquiring large, high-quality datasets of human-annotated rationales is prohibitively expensive and often infeasible for training LLMs in step-by-step reasoning.
- Existing methods like few-shot in-context learning for rationale generation often lead to suboptimal performance compared to fine-tuning on extensive rationale datasets.
- LLMs typically receive no direct training signal for problems they fail to solve, limiting their ability to learn from mistakes in complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- **STaR** (Self-Taught Reasoner) is an ==iterative bootstrapping algorithm== where a pre-trained LLM generates rationales for a problem set, and only those leading to correct answers are used to fine-tune the model.
- The algorithm incorporates ==rationalization==, which involves prompting the model with failed problems and their correct answers as hints, encouraging it to generate rationales that justify the correct solution.
- The model is repeatedly fine-tuned on the combined dataset of self-generated correct rationales and rationalized correct answers, leading to continuous improvement in rationale generation and task performance.

## Results

- On arithmetic tasks, STaR achieved **89.5%** accuracy, significantly outperforming a baseline fine-tuned directly on answers (**76.3%**) and showing improved generalization to unseen digit lengths.
- For CommonsenseQA, STaR with GPT-J achieved **72.5%** accuracy, comparable to a 30x larger GPT-3 model (**73.0%**) and outperforming a 137B LaMDA model prompted with few-shot CoT (**55.6%**).
- STaR generated rationales were perceived as higher quality than few-shot prompts and some human-generated rationales, indicating an improved coherence in reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can iteratively improve their reasoning capabilities by generating and filtering their own rationales, effectively creating a self-improving loop.
> - Providing the correct answer as a 'hint' (rationalization) for initially failed problems significantly boosts the learning process, allowing the model to 'reason backward' and expand its training data.
> - The approach enables smaller LLMs (e.g., GPT-J 6B) to achieve reasoning performance comparable to or exceeding much larger models (e.g., GPT-3, LaMDA) that lack this self-improvement mechanism.
