# EvolveR: Self-Evolving LLM Agents through an Experience-Driven Lifecycle

> [!summary] Summary
> EVOLVER enables large language model agents to autonomously learn and improve from their own experiences by distilling raw interaction trajectories into strategic principles. This framework achieves an average Exact Match score of 0.382 across seven complex question-answering benchmarks, surpassing various state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- LLM agents suffer from "operational amnesia," failing to systematically learn from their own experiences and iteratively refine problem-solving strategies.
- Existing approaches either discard experiences, store raw un-distilled trajectories, or rely on external teacher models, rather than allowing agents to autonomously distill principles.
- Current LLM agents lack a self-contained lifecycle for continuous self-improvement, treating each task as an isolated episode.

## Method

- **EVOLVER** introduces a closed-loop experience lifecycle comprising Offline Experience Self-Distillation, Online Interaction, and Policy Evolution.
- Agents self-distill raw interaction trajectories into abstract strategic principles (guiding or cautionary) using their own policy model, storing them in a dynamic, quality-controlled experience base.
- Policy evolution leverages ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a ==composite reward function== to update the agent's policy, learning to effectively utilize distilled wisdom from its experiences.

## Results

- EVOLVER achieved the highest average Exact Match (EM) score of **0.382** across seven diverse question-answering benchmarks using the Qwen2.5-3B model.
- The framework consistently outperformed a range of strong baselines, including advanced prompting, SFT, and state-of-the-art RL agents, demonstrating robust generalizability.
- Performance scaled positively with model size, improving monotonically from **0.150** for the 0.5B model to **0.382** for the 3B model, and self-distillation by the 3B model outperformed external teacher distillation (**0.382** vs. **0.370** avg. EM).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Principles self-distilled by the agent's own policy model are more effective for larger models due to better "cognitive alignment" than those provided by external teacher models.
> - Experience retrieval from the dynamically curated experience base is an indispensable component, confirming that agents learn not just to generate good trajectories, but how to effectively apply their accumulated wisdom.
> - Direct internalization of principles into model parameters without a dynamic quality filter for retrieved principles can degrade performance, emphasizing the importance of the explicit, curated experience base.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Model (LLM) agents has progressed significantly, enabling them to perform diverse tasks through advanced reasoning and tool use. However, a limitation in these agents is their tendency to treat each interaction as an isolated event, leading to "operational amnesia." This means they frequently fail to systematically learn from past successes or mistakes, hindering their progression towards greater autonomy and intelligence. This contrasts with human learning, which involves a continuous lifecycle of interaction, reflection, and abstraction to refine strategies and build expertise over time.

Prior research has attempted to address components of this challenge. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks, for instance, mitigate external knowledge gaps by allowing agents to query external databases. However, RAG does not inherently enable agents to systematically learn from the consequences of their own actions or to refine their internal problem-solving strategies. Other approaches, such as those involving natural language reflections, often store these reflections in external memory, sometimes using a powerful external LLM as a "scribe" or "teacher model." While this can be resource-efficient, these reflections typically serve as transient hints, leaving the agent's intrinsic policy unchanged. Alternatively, some systems attempt to learn by recalling raw past trajectories; however, this method often struggles with generalization and fails to abstract reusable strategic principles, leading to mimicry rather than genuine learning.

Within the realm of continual learning (CL), efforts have focused on enabling models to learn sequentially while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Nevertheless, most CL paradigms assume predefined task boundaries and primarily focus on knowledge preservation rather than active knowledge acquisition in open-ended environments. Similarly, existing LLM agent frameworks, including those leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) like ReAct and Reflexion, are often prompt-based and can be considered stateless in terms of long-term strategic knowledge accumulation. While some RL methods have successfully optimized LLM interaction with external factual knowledge, they typically do not address the broader challenge of an agent's self-improvement through its own internal experience.

EvolveR is introduced to address these identified shortcomings by presenting a framework that enables agents to systematically learn and evolve from their own experiences. It specifically aims to move beyond episodic problem-solving and transient hints by integrating a closed-loop lifecycle for generating, distilling, and applying abstract strategic principles, thereby enabling continuous self-improvement of the agent's intrinsic policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce EvolveR, a novel framework that enables Large Language Model (LLM) agents to self-improve through a complete, closed-loop experience lifecycle. This framework aims to allow agents to systematically learn from their own experiences and iteratively refine their problem-solving strategies, thereby moving towards greater autonomy and intelligence.

The motivations for developing EvolveR stem from several limitations identified in existing LLM agent paradigms:

- **Addressing Operational Amnesia:** Current LLM agents often treat each task independently, failing to retain or leverage insights from past interactions. This "operational amnesia" hinders their ability to build expertise over time, a fundamental aspect of intelligent behavior observed in humans. The work is motivated by the need to equip agents with a mechanism to learn from both successes and failures, accumulating a repository of refined strategies.
- **Beyond External Knowledge Gaps:** While Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) effectively addresses factual knowledge gaps by accessing external data, it does not fundamentally enhance the agent's strategic reasoning capabilities. The motivation is to tackle a more foundational limitation: the agent's inability to systematically learn and refine its internal problem-solving methodologies.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Prior Self-Improvement Attempts:**
 - Previous approaches that store natural language reflections (e.g., via external "teacher" LLMs) often treat these as transient hints. This does not lead to a lasting change in the agent's intrinsic policy. EvolveR seeks a deeper integration where learning directly impacts the agent's internal decision-making process.
 - Learning by recalling raw, un-distilled past trajectories has been shown to struggle with generalization. Such methods tend to mimic past solutions rather than abstracting reusable strategic principles. EvolveR aims to enable agents to distill abstract principles, making their learning more generalizable and efficient.
- **Establishing a Continuous Learning Lifecycle:** The research is motivated by the aspiration to bridge the gap between episodic problem-solving and sustainable self-improvement, mirroring the continuous learning process observed in humans. This involves creating a system where interaction, reflection, abstraction, and policy refinement occur in a self-perpetuating cycle.
- **Policy Evolution through Reinforcement Learning:** Many LLM agent frameworks are stateless or rely on simple prompting. A key motivation is to integrate a reinforcement learning mechanism that allows the agent to update its policy based on the utility and success of its actions, thereby internalizing learned experiences and evolving its behavior.

By addressing these motivations, EvolveR aims to provide a comprehensive blueprint for agents capable of learning not only from external data but also from the direct consequences of their own actions, fostering more autonomous and adaptable systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvolveR introduces a novel framework for self-evolving LLM agents, structured around a closed-loop, experience-driven lifecycle. This lifecycle consists of three core, interconnected components: Offline Experience Self-Distillation, Online Interaction, and Policy Evolution.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Formalizing Agent Interaction

At each step $t$, the agent selects an action $a_t$ from an action space $A$ based on its policy $\pi_\theta$. The action space is designed for complex, knowledge-intensive tasks and includes three operations:
- `<search experience>`: Queries the internal experience base (E) to retrieve relevant distilled principles.
- `<search knowledge>`: Queries an external knowledge base (e.g., a search engine) for factual information.
- `<answer>`: Outputs the final answer and concludes the interaction.

#### 3.2. The EvolveR Lifecycle: From Interactions to Principles: Offline Experience Self-Distillation

This phase focuses on transforming raw interaction data into strategic principles while the agent's policy parameters are frozen.
- **Principle from Self-Distillation:** The agent's own policy model ($\pi_\theta$) analyzes its past interaction trajectories. Using specially designed prompts, the model reviews each trajectory and distills a concise natural language statement reflecting a core strategic insight. This results in either a "guiding principle" from a successful outcome or a "cautionary principle" from a failure. Each principle includes a natural language description and structured knowledge triples (e.g., (subject, predicate, object)).
- **Deduplication and Integration:** To maintain a high-quality experience base (E), a rigorous integration process is applied to new principles.
 - **Deduplication:** For principles originating from the same problem, semantic equivalence checks are performed using the agent model to prevent redundancies, retaining only one representative per cluster.
 - **Integration:** Each unique candidate principle ($p_{cand}$) undergoes a two-stage matching process against existing principles in E. First, embedding similarity identifies the most similar existing principles. Second, the agent model provides a binary "yes/no" judgment on semantic equivalence. If a principle is novel (similarity below $\theta_{sim}$), it is added to E. Otherwise, the new trajectory ($\tau_{src}$) is merged under its best matching existing principle ($p^*$), enriching it without redundancy (Equation 1).
- **Quality Control via Dynamic Scoring:** Each principle in E tracks its usage and success counts. A metric score $s(p) = (c_{succ}(p) + 1) / (c_{use}(p) + 2)$ quantifies its historical effectiveness (Equation 2). Principles with scores falling below a threshold $\theta_{prune}$ are periodically pruned to ensure the experience base remains high-quality and compact.

#### 3.2.2. Online Interaction

In this phase, the agent applies its distilled principles to solve problems and generate new interaction data.
- **Experience as a Strategic Principle:** The agent operates within a deliberative reasoning loop (e.g., Think-Act-Observe). When the agent issues a `<search experience>` action, retrieved principles ($P_k$) provide heuristic guidance. These principles influence the agent's internal monologue (`<think>`) and guide subsequent actions (e.g., `<search knowledge>`), making problem-solving more efficient.
- **Generating High-Quality Trajectories for Future Distillation:** The online phase aims to produce high-quality trajectories ($\tau_{new}$) for the next offline reflection cycle. Since the agent's actions are guided by proven principles, these trajectories are structured, experience-guided records of problem-solving, capturing the interplay between distilled principles, internal reasoning, and external tool use.

#### 3.3. Policy Evolution: Closing the Loop with Reinforcement Learning

A reinforcement learning framework enables the agent to learn from its actions and evolve its policy $\pi_\theta$.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function $R(\tau)$ for a trajectory $\tau$ balances task success and procedural correctness: $R(\tau) = w_o R_{outcome}(\tau) + w_f R_{format}(\tau)$.
 - **Outcome Reward ($R_{outcome}$):** A sparse, binary reward based on the Exact Match (EM) of the final answer with the ground truth (Equation 3).
 - **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** A dense shaping reward that evaluates the quality of the reasoning process (Equation 4). It is composed of:
 - $R_{think}$: Rewards a balanced number of `<think>` actions.
 - $R_{search}$: Promotes the use of both `<search experience>` and `<search knowledge>` (diversity) and a reasonable quantity of search actions.
 - An indicator function $I(\tau_{complete})$ ensures only structurally complete trajectories receive a format reward.
- **Policy Optimization:** The policy $\pi_\theta$ is updated using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO uses the average reward of sampled trajectories as a baseline, maximizing an objective function that considers the importance sampling ratio and applies a KL-divergence penalty to constrain updates (Equation 5). This process reinforces the effective utilization of distilled wisdom to produce high-reward trajectories, thereby closing the learning loop.

#### 3.4. Experimental Implementation Details

- **Tasks and Datasets:** Evaluated on seven QA benchmarks: Natural Questions (NQ), HotpotQA (in-domain), TriviaQA, PopQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, and Bamboogle (out-of-domain).
- **Baseline Methods:** Compared against various prompting-based (Direct Inference, CoT, RAG, IRCoT, Search-o1), Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT, Rejection Sampling), and RL-based methods (R1-base, R1-instruct, Search-R1-base, Search-R1-instruct) using Qwen2.5 foundational models.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Primarily Exact Match (EM); F1 Score also used for generalizability analysis.
- **Implementation:** Qwen2.5 model family (0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B). A cold-start SFT stage initialized the model on a curated dataset of CoT interaction trajectories (700 samples from NQ/HotpotQA). LoRA was used for fine-tuning. GRPO optimized the agent, with 128 prompts per RL step, generating G=8 trajectories each. Adam optimizer with a reduced learning rate (1e-6). Training conducted on 8 A100 GPUs. BGE-M3 used as the embedding model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EvolveR across various benchmarks and model scales yielded several findings regarding its effectiveness and the contributions of its components.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

EvolveR demonstrated superior performance on complex multi-hop question-answering benchmarks.
- **Qwen2.5-3B:** EvolveR achieved an average Exact Match (EM) score of 0.382, outperforming all baselines, including strong reinforcement learning agents like Search-R1-instruct (0.325). This performance was consistent across in-domain (NQ, HotpotQA) and out-of-domain datasets (PopQA, Bamboogle).
- **Qwen2.5-7B:** EvolveR similarly achieved an average EM score of 0.417, surpassing Search-R1-instruct (0.385) and other baselines.
This consistent, high-level performance across diverse benchmarks indicates that EvolveR's systematic approach to distilling, managing, and utilizing experiences enables agents to develop more generalizable problem-solving strategies.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Model Scales Generalizability

EvolveR's performance was evaluated across Qwen2.5 models of 0.5B, 1.5B, and 3B parameters. A clear positive correlation was observed: as the parameter count of the base model increased, EvolveR's performance improved significantly on every benchmark. The average performance monotonically rose from 0.150 (0.5B) to 0.270 (1.5B) and further to 0.382 (3B). This demonstrates EvolveR's ability to effectively leverage the superior reasoning and instruction-following capabilities of larger foundational models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies: Dissecting the EvolveR Framework: Validating the Self-Distillation Mechanism

A comparison between EvolveR (self-distill) and EvolveR (teacher-distill), which uses GPT-4o-mini for distillation, revealed a scale-dependent relationship:
- For the 0.5B model, the external teacher model provided a clear benefit.
- For the 3B model, EvolveR (self-distill) (0.382 avg.) outperformed the teacher-guided variant (0.370 avg.). This suggests that for more sophisticated agents, principles distilled from their own internal policy lead to better "cognitive alignment" and are ultimately more effective.

#### 4.3.2. The Role of Experience Retrieval

Disabling access to the distilled principles during inference (EvolveR w/o exp-retrieve) resulted in a substantial performance degradation across all model scales. For the 3B model, the average performance dropped from 0.382 to 0.340. This finding emphasizes that the ability to access and condition on retrieved principles is a critical and indispensable component for EvolveR's optimal performance.

#### 4.3.3. Component-Level Attribution and Memory Configuration

- **Component Attribution:** Analysis on the Qwen2.5-3B model showed that while reinforcement learning (RL) alone provided a baseline improvement (0.325), it was insufficient for optimal performance. Experience retrieval contributed a substantial direct gain, and RL further enhanced experience-conditioned training. The full EvolveR framework, combining principle distillation, retrieval, and RL, achieved the highest score of 0.382, indicating complementary roles where retrieval guides reasoning and RL optimizes the policy to utilize these principles effectively.
- **Memory Configuration:** Self-distilled experience (0.382) outperformed teacher-distilled experience (0.370) at the 3B scale, suggesting that an experience base aligned with the student model's own policy and failure modes provides more actionable guidance.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Principle Quality and Scalability: Characterizing Principle Quality and Failure Modes

Manual annotation of distilled principles indicated that the dynamic scoring mechanism effectively filters principles. Low-score principles frequently contained vague advice (50%), while high-score principles were predominantly ideal and actionable (82%). The mechanism also significantly reduced the proportion of incorrect/misleading principles (from 24% to 8% in high-score tier).

#### 4.4.2. Long-Term Noise Accumulation and Scalability

The Qwen2.5-3B agent's performance and search latency were tracked as the experience base grew. Even with nearly 50,000 principles, retrieval latency remained low (~0.20s). The agent's performance remained highly stable and even peaked at the 45k scale, demonstrating robustness against substantial experience growth and noise accumulation due to semantic deduplication, dynamic scoring, and RL-conditioned policy.

#### 4.5. Necessity of the RL (GRPO) Stage (Appendix A.2.1)

An ablation experiment compared RL-based EvolveR with an SFT-only variant using the same successful trajectories. The RL-based variant substantially outperformed the SFT-only version (0.382 vs. 0.357 average EM for 3B model). This highlights that RL, by leveraging both successful and failed trajectories, enables the agent to learn robust retrieval and reasoning strategies beyond merely reproducing surface-level actions.

#### 4.6. Longitudinal Analysis of Learning Dynamics (Appendix A.2.3)

- **Evolution of Action Frequencies:** During RL training, the agent learned to increase the frequency of `<think>` and `<search knowledge>` actions, eventually converging to an efficient range (e.g., ~2 experience searches, ~4 knowledge searches, ~6 reasoning steps).
- **Intrinsic Improvement in Principle Distillation:** Principles generated in later training stages achieved a higher average metric score (0.500 from Interval 4 vs. 0.462 from Interval 1), supported by substantial usage counts. This suggests that the agent's intrinsic ability to distill high-quality strategies improved over time.

#### 4.7. Exploring the Influence of Experience Internalization (Appendix A.3)

An experiment allowing the agent to "absorb" retrieved experience by unmasking loss for `<experience>` tokens resulted in a slight performance degradation. This suggests that direct internalization without more sophisticated filtering mechanisms, such as an auxiliary model to weigh principle relevance, might introduce noise into the learning signal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EvolveR framework represents a notable contribution to the field of LLM agents, shifting the paradigm from purely reactive or knowledge-retrieval-focused systems to agents capable of continuous, experience-driven self-evolution.

One significant impact is the **establishment of a comprehensive, closed-loop lifecycle for LLM agent learning**. This moves beyond the episodic problem-solving prevalent in many current agents, addressing the critical limitation of "operational amnesia" by enabling agents to systematically transform raw interaction trajectories into abstract, reusable strategic principles. This capacity for self-improvement provides a foundation for truly adaptive and continuously evolving artificial intelligence systems.

The research demonstrates **enhanced problem-solving capabilities** in complex, knowledge-intensive tasks. EvolveR's consistent superior performance across diverse multi-hop question-answering benchmarks indicates that agents can develop more generalized and robust problem-solving strategies when learning from their own experiences. This suggests potential for broader applicability in domains requiring dynamic strategy adjustment and complex reasoning.

A key finding is the **validation of the self-distillation mechanism and the concept of "cognitive alignment."** The observation that self-distilled principles become more effective than those provided by a stronger external teacher as the agent's base model scales highlights the importance of internal consistency and alignment between the agent's policy and its learned strategic wisdom. This insight could guide future development towards more self-sufficient and integrated learning architectures for AI.

Furthermore, the work provides a **blueprint for building agents that actively construct and evolve expertise.** Rather than merely accessing external knowledge or mimicking pre-defined behaviors, EvolveR agents actively build a curated repository of strategic principles, demonstrating a form of active learning from the consequences of their actions. This approach could lead to more autonomous systems that are less reliant on constant human supervision or extensive pre-curated datasets for domain adaptation.

The explicit nature of the distilled principles, comprising natural language descriptions and structured triples, offers potential avenues for **improved interpretability and steerability** of agent behavior. Understanding the "wisdom" an agent has acquired could facilitate debugging, ensure alignment with human values, and enable more precise guidance.

However, the increased autonomy of self-evolving agents also raises **critical safety considerations**. An agent that develops its own principles could, if not guided by robust, value-aligned reward functions, potentially evolve undesirable or harmful strategies. This underscores the necessity for continued research into alignment techniques and ethical safeguards for such systems. While the current work focuses on specific QA tasks, extending EvolveR to broader applications such as embodied interaction or creative generation will be important for fully understanding its boundaries and applicability. The long-term scalability of the experience base and noise accumulation remain open challenges for truly lifelong learning agents, despite the demonstrated robustness of EvolveR's curation mechanisms.
