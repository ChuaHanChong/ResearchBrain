# ManipLVM-R1: Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Embodied Manipulation with Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ManipLVM-R1, developed by researchers from MBZUAI and collaborating institutions, introduces a reinforcement learning framework that uses verifiable rewards to train Large Vision-Language Models for robotic manipulation. This approach reduces reliance on human-annotated datasets and demonstrates enhanced generalization and sample efficiency in affordance perception and trajectory prediction tasks, outperforming supervised baselines on in-domain and out-of-domain datasets.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for robotic manipulation heavily rely on costly, human-annotated datasets, which limits their scalability and adaptability.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) based models often struggle to generalize robustly to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios or novel environments.
- LVLMs frequently learn superficial patterns rather than deep, systematic physical reasoning, leading to difficulties in low-level decision-making and grappling with physical dynamics.

## Method

- The paper introduces **ManipLVM-R1**, a reinforcement learning framework that utilizes Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to train Large Vision-Language Models for embodied manipulation.
- It designs rule-based reward functions tailored for two key robotic manipulation subtasks: affordance perception and trajectory prediction.
- For affordance perception, rewards combine format adherence with spatial accuracy (Intersection-over-Union), and for trajectory prediction, rewards integrate format adherence, path similarity (Discrete Fréchet, Hausdorff, RMSE), and endpoint distance.

## Results

- ManipLVM-R1-3B achieved an IoU of 31.0 for affordance perception on the in-domain ShareRobot dataset, significantly outperforming RoboBrain-7B (11.79) using only **50%** of the training data.
- In out-of-domain tests, ManipLVM-R1-3B demonstrated superior generalization, achieving the highest Grasp-IoU (34.65) on UMD Part Affordance and the lowest average trajectory error (131.99) on VAIT, surpassing larger baseline models.
- Qualitative analysis revealed emergent multi-step reasoning abilities, such as identifying object attributes and matching slots, suggesting a deeper understanding of tasks without explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verifiable, rule-based rewards can significantly reduce the need for extensive human-annotated datasets in robotic manipulation, leading to improved sample efficiency and reduced data collection costs.
> - Structured reward functions, designed for specific manipulation subtasks, compel large vision-language models to develop a more systematic understanding of physical interactions and geometric constraints.
> - Aggregating multiple complementary metrics for trajectory rewards (e.g., Frechet, Hausdorff, RMSE, endpoint distance) provides a more stable and reliable learning signal, fostering deeper understanding of spatial structure and action consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Executive Summary

The paper introduces a novel framework that leverages Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to overcome the limitations of human-annotated datasets in training Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for robotic manipulation. ManipLVM-R1 proposes structured, rule-based reward functions for affordance perception and trajectory prediction, enabling the model to learn physical reasoning without extensive supervision. The framework demonstrates significant improvements in generalization, sample efficiency, and out-of-domain (OOD) performance, offering a promising path toward more adaptable and scalable robot learning systems.

### 2. Introduction

#### 2.1. Paper Identification

The paper, titled "ManipLVM-R1: Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Embodied Manipulation with Large Vision-Language Models," is a preprint (arXiv:2505.16517v2 [cs.RO] 24 May 2025) currently under review.

### 3. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 3.1. LVLMs in Robotics: Current State and Challenges

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) represent a significant leap towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), excelling in visual perception and language understanding across tasks like visual question answering and image captioning. Their application in robotics, particularly for complex manipulation tasks requiring fine-grained control and physical reasoning, has been a recent frontier. Existing research largely focuses on high-level planning, action sequencing, or general visual reasoning, as seen in works like VoxPoser, RoboFlamingo, and OpenVLA. While these models leverage LVLMs for semantic understanding and task decomposition, they often fall short in low-level decision-making and grappling with physical dynamics.

#### 3.2. Shift from Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

A critical limitation of most current LVLM-based robotic manipulation systems is their heavy reliance on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using costly human-annotated training datasets. Projects like RoboBrain, which is a close predecessor to ManipLVM-R1, construct SFT datasets by combining expert demonstrations with language model-generated reasoning. While this enhances alignment with instructions, SFT-based methods inherently suffer from high data collection costs and limited generalization, leading to performance degradation in out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios. This data dependency impedes scalability and adaptability in diverse, unseen environments.

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a promising paradigm, particularly in domains where correctness can be objectively verified, such as mathematics and programming. Unlike Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), RLVR derives its reward signals from a rule-based, verifiable function, eliminating the need for human labeling during the reinforcement learning phase. While RLVR has been explored for general visual reasoning (e.g., Vision-R1, LMM-R1, VLM-R1), its application to robotic manipulation is nascent. The challenges in this context include the sparsity and delayed nature of traditional manipulation rewards, and the difficulty in designing rewards that impose spatial and logical constraints to encourage true physical reasoning rather than superficial pattern matching. ManipLVM-R1 directly addresses these challenges by adapting RLVR for robotic manipulation.

### 4. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ManipLVM-R1 is to address the fundamental limitations of existing LVLM-based robotic manipulation systems:
1. **Reduce Reliance on Costly Human Annotations:** Current methods require extensive, meticulously labeled datasets, which are expensive and time-consuming to acquire, limiting their scalability.
2. **Enhance Generalization and Adaptability:** Supervised models often struggle to perform robustly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios or novel environments not explicitly covered in their training data.
3. **Improve Physical Reasoning:** Many LVLMs excel at high-level planning but lack a deep, systematic understanding of physical interactions and low-level dynamics necessary for robust manipulation. They tend to learn superficial patterns rather than grounded reasoning.
4. **Provide Timely and Informative Feedback:** Traditional robotic RL often suffers from sparse and delayed rewards, making learning inefficient. ManipLVM-R1 aims to provide immediate, structured feedback to guide the learning process effectively.

To achieve these, ManipLVM-R1 sets out to:
- Propose a novel reinforcement learning framework that replaces traditional supervision with Reinforcement Learning using Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).
- Design rule-based reward functions specifically tailored for key robotic manipulation subtasks: affordance perception and trajectory prediction.
- Enable models to learn deeper, more systematic reasoning about physical interactions by imposing spatial-logical constraints through these rewards.
- Demonstrate enhanced generalization and physical plausibility while achieving competitive performance with significantly less training data.

### 5. Methodology and Approach

ManipLVM-R1 introduces an RLVR-based framework designed to provide timely and verifiable feedback for robotic manipulation, tackling the challenges of sparse rewards and lack of spatial-logical constraints. The framework decomposes the overall manipulation task into two distinct subtasks: affordance perception and trajectory prediction, with separate, tailored reward mechanisms.

#### 5.1. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

ManipLVM-R1 builds upon the RLVR paradigm, which optimizes a policy model ($\pi_\theta$) by maximizing a verifiable reward function ($R_{RLVR}$) while regularizing with a KL-divergence term to a reference model ($\pi_{ref}$). Unlike RLHF, $R$ is derived programmatically from objective correctness, not human preference.

#### 5.2. ManipLVM-R1 Framework Overview

Given an input state (visual image encoding $x$ and textual instruction $q$), the policy model generates multiple candidate responses. These responses are then evaluated by one of two task-specific verifiable reward functions. The framework's core novelty lies in the design of these structured rewards, which go beyond simple task success/failure to provide rich, immediate feedback on specific aspects of manipulation.

#### 5.3. Affordance Perception Reward Design

For the affordance perception task, which involves identifying the interactive regions of an object, ManipLVM-R1 employs an **Affordance Perception Reward ($R_{spatial}$)** composed of two parts:
1. **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** Ensures that the model's output adheres to a predefined structured format: `<think>Reasoning</think><answer>bbox: (w, x, y, z)</answer>`. This guides the model to organize its output logically and makes reward extraction reliable.
2. **Affordance Reward ($R_{aff}$):** Focuses on the spatial accuracy of the predicted bounding box (`b*`) against the ground-truth box (`b̂`) using the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) metric. By optimizing $R_{aff} = \text{IoU}(b^*, \hat{b})$, the model is incentivized to generate spatially precise and semantically grounded predictions crucial for interaction planning.
 The total affordance reward is $R_{spatial} = R_{format} + R_{aff}$.

#### 5.4. Trajectory Match Reward Design

For trajectory prediction, where the model outputs a sequence of 2D coordinates representing an end-effector path, ManipLVM-R1 uses a **Trajectory Match Reward ($R_{trajectory}$)** with three components:
1. **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** Similar to affordance perception, this verifies structural correctness and enforces trajectory-specific constraints, such as the number of predicted waypoints (e.g., 3-10), ensuring conciseness and interpretability.
2. **Path Similarity Reward ($R_{path}$):** This is a crucial innovation to address the limitation of supervised methods that often overemphasize endpoint accuracy while neglecting the quality and feasibility of the entire path. $R_{path}$ is a weighted aggregation of rewards derived from three geometric similarity metrics:
 - **Discrete Fréchet Distance (DFD):** Quantifies overall shape similarity and temporal alignment.
 - **Hausdorff Distance (HD):** Captures the maximum pointwise deviation between predicted and ground-truth trajectories.
 - **Root Mean Square Error (RMSE):** Measures the average pointwise discrepancy.
 These metrics capture different aspects of trajectory quality, encouraging the model to generate physically plausible and goal-aligned trajectories.
3. **Endpoint Distance Reward ($R_{end}$):** Measures the Euclidean distance between the final predicted point and the ground-truth endpoint, ensuring goal achievement.
 The overall trajectory match reward is defined as $R_{trajectory} = R_{format} + R_{path} + R_{end}$.

#### 5.5. Policy Optimization

ManipLVM-R1 adopts a policy update mechanism inspired by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The policy model generates multiple responses, and task-specific rewards are assigned to each. These rewards are then normalized into "advantages" ($A_i$) to assess the quality of each response relative to others. The policy is updated to increase the likelihood of high-quality responses while suppressing lower-quality ones. A KL divergence constraint is applied between the updated policy and a reference model to ensure training stability.

### 6. Main Findings and Results

ManipLVM-R1 demonstrates substantial performance gains across multiple manipulation tasks, showcasing its effectiveness in sample efficiency, task success, and generalization.

#### 6.1. Quantitative Performance

**In-Domain Dataset (ShareRobot):**
- **Affordance Perception:** ManipLVM-R1-3B achieved an IoU score of 31.0, a significant improvement over the best baseline, RoboBrain-7B (11.79), and Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct (7.40). This remarkable performance was achieved using only 50% of the training data that baselines were trained on.
- **Trajectory Prediction:** ManipLVM-R1-3B achieved an average distance error of 110.87, outperforming most open-source and supervised fine-tuned models, including Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct (107.86) which is a much larger model, reinforcing the effectiveness of its trajectory reward design.

**Out-of-Domain (OOD) Dataset (UMD Part Affordance and VAIT):**
- **Affordance Perception (UMD):** ManipLVM-R1-3B showed robust generalization, achieving the highest Grasp-IoU (34.65) and strong performance across other subtasks (Cut-IoU: 25.58, Pound-IoU: 23.50, Scoop-IoU: 28.27). This significantly surpassed all open-source and SFT baselines, including RoboBrain-7B.
- **Trajectory Prediction (VAIT):** ManipLVM-R1-3B achieved the lowest average trajectory error (131.99), outperforming all baselines, including the larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct (164.14) and RoboBrain-7B (202.70).
These OOD results are particularly impactful, demonstrating ManipLVM-R1's ability to generalize effectively under distribution shifts, a critical requirement for real-world robotic deployment.

#### 6.2. Sample Efficiency and Generalization

A core finding is ManipLVM-R1's exceptional sample efficiency. By using only 50% of the training data compared to fully supervised baselines, it not only matches but frequently surpasses their performance. This validates the paper's central hypothesis that verifiable rewards can significantly reduce annotation dependency while enhancing the model's inherent reasoning capabilities and generalization to unseen scenarios.

#### 6.3. Qualitative Analysis: Successes and Failure Modes

- **"Aha Moments" and Emergent Reasoning:** Qualitative examples showcased ManipLVM-R1's emergent multi-step reasoning abilities without explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data. For instance, when instructed to "Insert round object into its slot," the model identified object attributes (color, shape) and located the matching slot, implying implicit planning. This suggests a deeper, systematic understanding of tasks beyond shallow pattern matching.
- **Failure Cases:** The analysis also highlighted limitations, primarily related to:
 - **Reasoning Hallucinations/Lack of Commonsense:** The model could generate incorrect reasoning paths due to a lack of real-world commonsense knowledge, e.g., misinterpreting an uncommon doorknob.
 - **Perceptual Errors:** Misperceiving the gripper's spatial position could lead to faulty reasoning and ultimately incorrect action trajectories, indicating a need for more robust internal belief verification.

#### 6.4. Ablation Study on Reward Design

An ablation study on the Trajectory Match Reward confirmed the effectiveness of the multi-metric reward design. The full reward configuration (DFD + HD + RMSE + Endpoint Error) consistently achieved the highest performance. This highlights that aggregating multiple complementary metrics provides a more stable and reliable learning signal for trajectory alignment, encouraging a deeper understanding of spatial structure and action consistency compared to single metrics or less effective combinations (e.g., DTW + Endpoint).

### 7. Significance and Potential Impact

ManipLVM-R1 represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, offering a new paradigm for training LVLMs that addresses critical bottlenecks.

#### 7.1. Contribution to Robot Learning

- **Reduced Supervision Costs:** By demonstrating that complex robotic skills can be learned effectively with rule-based, verifiable rewards rather than costly human annotations, ManipLVM-R1 opens avenues for more scalable and economical robot learning systems. This can accelerate research and deployment by removing a major data acquisition burden.
- **Enhanced Physical Reasoning:** The structured reward mechanisms, specifically designed for affordance perception and trajectory prediction, compel the model to develop a more systematic understanding of physical interactions. This moves beyond rote memorization of demonstrations towards true physical reasoning and problem-solving.
- **Improved Generalization:** The superior performance in OOD scenarios is a crucial step towards robust and adaptable robots that can operate in diverse and novel real-world environments. This is a key challenge for current embodied AI systems.

#### 7.2. Implications for Real-world Robotics

The ability of ManipLVM-R1 to generalize with limited supervision makes it highly relevant for real-world robotic applications. Robots often encounter unforeseen situations, and a system that can reason and adapt without requiring constant re-training on new data will be invaluable for industrial automation, domestic assistance, and hazardous environment exploration. The framework's focus on verifiable rewards also lends itself to explainability, as the reward functions directly encode desired behaviors and constraints.

#### 7.3. Future Directions and Limitations

The paper acknowledges several limitations and outlines future work:
- **Scope of Tasks:** The current framework focuses on 2D affordance perception and trajectory prediction. Real-world manipulation often involves more intricate, long-horizon tasks, fine-grained force control, manipulation of deformable objects, and dynamic interaction. Future work will aim to extend the paradigm to these complex challenges, possibly leveraging larger and more comprehensive expert datasets.
- **2D to 3D Translation:** The translation of predicted 2D image-space trajectories to precise and safe 3D robot movements is a challenge not fully captured by current metrics. However, the authors point to advancements in 3D shape understanding and reconstruction from 2D images, suggesting potential for direct 6DoF pose prediction to bridge this gap.
- **Robustness to Commonsense and Perceptual Errors:** The identified failure cases regarding reasoning hallucinations and misperception highlight the need for incorporating external commonsense knowledge bases or self-monitoring modules to improve robustness in ambiguous or unfamiliar scenarios.

In conclusion, ManipLVM-R1 presents a compelling case for the efficacy of RLVR in robotic manipulation, paving the way for more sample-efficient, generalizable, and physically grounded robot learning systems.
