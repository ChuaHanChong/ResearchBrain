# ManipLVM-R1: Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Embodied Manipulation with Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ManipLVM-R1, developed by researchers from MBZUAI and collaborating institutions, introduces a reinforcement learning framework that uses verifiable rewards to train Large Vision-Language Models for robotic manipulation. This approach reduces reliance on human-annotated datasets and demonstrates enhanced generalization and sample efficiency in affordance perception and trajectory prediction tasks, outperforming supervised baselines on in-domain and out-of-domain datasets.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for robotic manipulation heavily rely on costly, human-annotated datasets, which limits their scalability and adaptability.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) based models often struggle to generalize robustly to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios or novel environments.
- LVLMs frequently learn superficial patterns rather than deep, systematic physical reasoning, leading to difficulties in low-level decision-making and grappling with physical dynamics.

## Method

- The paper introduces **ManipLVM-R1**, a reinforcement learning framework that utilizes Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to train Large Vision-Language Models for embodied manipulation.
- It designs rule-based reward functions tailored for two key robotic manipulation subtasks: affordance perception and trajectory prediction.
- For affordance perception, rewards combine format adherence with spatial accuracy (Intersection-over-Union), and for trajectory prediction, rewards integrate format adherence, path similarity (Discrete Fréchet, Hausdorff, RMSE), and endpoint distance.

## Results

- ManipLVM-R1-3B achieved an IoU of 31.0 for affordance perception on the in-domain ShareRobot dataset, significantly outperforming RoboBrain-7B (11.79) using only **50%** of the training data.
- In out-of-domain tests, ManipLVM-R1-3B demonstrated superior generalization, achieving the highest Grasp-IoU (34.65) on UMD Part Affordance and the lowest average trajectory error (131.99) on VAIT, surpassing larger baseline models.
- Qualitative analysis revealed emergent multi-step reasoning abilities, such as identifying object attributes and matching slots, suggesting a deeper understanding of tasks without explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verifiable, rule-based rewards can significantly reduce the need for extensive human-annotated datasets in robotic manipulation, leading to improved sample efficiency and reduced data collection costs.
> - Structured reward functions, designed for specific manipulation subtasks, compel large vision-language models to develop a more systematic understanding of physical interactions and geometric constraints.
> - Aggregating multiple complementary metrics for trajectory rewards (e.g., Frechet, Hausdorff, RMSE, endpoint distance) provides a more stable and reliable learning signal, fostering deeper understanding of spatial structure and action consistency.
