# FoMoVLA: Bridging Visual Foresight and Motion Guidance for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from LiAuto, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, and Tsinghua University developed FoMoVLA, a training-only framework that unifies visual foresight and motion guidance for Vision-Language-Action models. It enhanced robot manipulation performance, achieving 98.8% success on LIBERO and 56.9% on RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop, while also improving zero-shot generalization by 6.4 percentage points on LIBERO-Plus compared to a strong baseline.

## Problem

- Reactive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies struggle with anticipating future scene states and modeling long-horizon object dynamics, limiting complex task performance.
- Current visual foresight methods are either computationally expensive due to dense pixel prediction or lack granular motion details for guiding robot trajectories.
- A gap exists in VLA models for a unified mechanism that combines both high-level future state prediction and continuous motion path guidance.

## Method

- Introduced **FoMoVLA**, a training-only framework that augments Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with explicit ==spatio-temporal supervision==.
- Integrated two complementary branches: ==point tracking== (supervised by an off-the-shelf tracker) to learn continuous 2D motion trajectories for image tokens, and ==future feature prediction== (via learnable ==`<Foresight>` tokens== and an ==EMA teacher==) to encode the task's final visual state.
- Developed a ==Future-Conditioned Cross-Attention (FCCA)== module to explicitly condition motion prediction on the anticipated future visual state, ensuring consistent foresight.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.8%** on the LIBERO benchmark, showing an improvement over the **96.5%** baseline and confirming the synergy of combined foresight and motion guidance via FCCA.
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization on LIBERO-Plus with an **80.5%** success rate, surpassing its StarVLA backbone by **6.4 percentage points** on unseen language and background perturbations.
- Attained a **56.9%** average success rate on the RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop benchmark, marking a **9.1 percentage point** improvement over the StarVLA-GR00T backbone for humanoid robot manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Future visual state prediction and sparse point tracking are complementary for comprehensive spatio-temporal reasoning, providing "where to go" and "how to get there" guidance respectively.
> - Explicitly conditioning local motion prediction on a global future state via a cross-attention mechanism enhances consistency and overall performance beyond simple multi-task training.
> - Integrating these auxiliary objectives into VLA models during training allows for improved latent representations and action policy learning without incurring significant inference overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robot learning have established Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as a paradigm for robotic manipulation, demonstrating capabilities across various tasks. These models typically operate reactively, mapping current visual observations and natural language instructions directly to actions. This reactive approach, while effective in many canonical tasks, inherently limits the model's ability to anticipate future scene evolution, model long-horizon object dynamics, or infer the continuous motion path required to achieve a goal.

A segment of research has attempted to integrate foresight capabilities into VLA models by predicting future visual states. However, existing visual foresight methods encounter specific limitations:
- **Dense pixel-level prediction**: Such methods generate rich information about multi-frame future visual states but often capture static content irrelevant to control, leading to representational redundancy and high computational overhead. They primarily indicate "where to go" in a visual sense.
- **Compact keyframe-level future representations**: These approaches are more efficient by predicting future states at key moments but generally only characterize the target state. They often lack the granular details of dynamic interactions necessary for guiding the robot's trajectory, thus failing to sufficiently explain "how to get there."

Concurrently, other research efforts have explored motion tracking and geometric information, such as optical flow or sparse 2D point trajectories, to provide action-aligned spatio-temporal representations. These methods capture continuous local movements and interaction details throughout task execution. However, they typically focus on the *how* of motion without explicitly providing a holistic, high-level representation of the *where*—the anticipated future visual state.

The work presented in this paper identifies a gap in existing VLA models: the lack of a unified mechanism that jointly encodes both the target future state (visual foresight) and the continuous motion dynamics leading to it (motion guidance). It argues that future feature prediction and sparse point tracking are complementary, with the former providing the goal state and the latter capturing the continuous path. FoMoVLA aims to bridge this gap by integrating both forms of spatio-temporal supervision within a VLA framework, thereby moving beyond purely reactive policies towards those that can explicitly reason about future world dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby improving their performance and generalization in robotic manipulation tasks. The authors identify a fundamental limitation in current VLA models, which are described as "fundamentally reactive." This means they primarily learn direct mappings from instantaneous observations and language instructions to immediate actions, without explicit mechanisms for anticipating future world states or understanding continuous motion dynamics over a task horizon.

This reactive paradigm leads to specific challenges and motivates the following detailed objectives:

1. **Overcome the limitations of reactive policies**: Current VLA models struggle with tasks requiring long-horizon planning or complex interactions where understanding the sequence of changes and the eventual goal state is critical. The authors aim to transition VLA models from purely reactive to proactively foresight-guided.

2. **Integrate holistic future state prediction**: Existing visual foresight methods, whether dense pixel-level or sparse keyframe-level, are deemed either too computationally intensive or insufficiently detailed for actionable control. The objective is to develop a compact yet comprehensive representation of the future visual state that encapsulates global scene changes relevant to the task, effectively answering "where to go." This is addressed by predicting future features rather than raw pixels.

3. **Incorporate continuous motion guidance**: While future visual states indicate the goal, they do not prescribe the specific movements required to reach it. The motivation is to provide explicit guidance on "how to get there" by modeling continuous motion dynamics. This is achieved through sparse 2D point tracking, which captures local movements and interaction details of task-relevant objects.

4. **Establish consistency between foresight and motion**: A central motivation is the hypothesis that future state prediction and motion tracking are inherently complementary and should not be treated as independent auxiliary tasks. The authors propose that motion prediction can be significantly improved if it is explicitly conditioned on the anticipated future state. Therefore, a key objective is to design a mechanism that actively couples these two forms of spatio-temporal supervision, ensuring that the predicted future state and the point trajectories leading to it are jointly consistent.

5. **Enhance action policy learning and generalization**: By providing explicit spatio-temporal supervision, the goal is to ground the VLA model's latent representations more effectively, enabling the policy to learn actions that are both future-oriented (aiming for the predicted goal) and physically constrained (following plausible motion paths). This is expected to lead to improved performance on benchmark tasks and stronger zero-shot generalization to novel scenarios.

6. **Maintain inference efficiency**: A practical objective is to achieve these enhancements without introducing additional computational overhead during inference. The proposed auxiliary branches are designed to be training-only, allowing the model to absorb spatio-temporal knowledge into its core parameters and then discard the auxiliary components at deployment.

In summary, the motivation behind FoMoVLA is to equip VLA models with a more comprehensive understanding of world dynamics by explicitly modeling both the target future state and the continuous motion required to reach it, and crucially, to ensure these two forms of foresight are consistently integrated to directly inform action generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FoMoVLA is a training-only framework designed to augment Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with explicit spatio-temporal supervision. It integrates three complementary training-time branches into a foundation model: a point tracking branch, a future feature prediction branch, and a Future-Conditioned Cross-Attention (FCCA) module.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: VLA Policy Formulation and Flow Matching Action Head

The framework builds upon a typical VLA model where a policy maps a visual observation ($o_t$) and a natural language instruction ($l$) to a chunk of $T$ consecutive actions ($a_{t:t+T}$). A pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) serves as the backbone, tokenizing $o_t$ into $M$ spatial image tokens and processing them with $l$ to produce contextual representations ($c$). The action head is trained using conditional flow matching, where a DiT network $v_\theta$ is trained to predict the constant conditional velocity $x_1 - x_0$ between Gaussian noise $x_0$ and the ground-truth action chunk $x_1$, optimizing the action loss $L_{action}$. At inference, the action chunk is recovered by integrating this velocity.

#### 3.2. Point Tracking Branch

To ground the VLM's image token representations in explicit spatial motion, FoMoVLA supervises them to predict 2D point trajectories over the action chunk horizon.
- **Goal-Aware Representations**: To ensure image tokens encode task-relevant motion guided by language, the input sequence is reordered so that text tokens precede image tokens. This enables each image token's hidden state to be conditioned on the complete language instruction through causal attention.
- **Sparse Point Selection**: The vision encoder outputs an $H' \times W'$ spatial feature map (e.g., $8 \times 8$ for $M=64$ image tokens). A sparse set of $N$ query points is defined by mapping the center of each compressed patch cell back to its original image coordinate, establishing a one-to-one correspondence between image tokens and spatial query locations. This provides a lightweight geometric supervision signal.
- **Supervision via Point Tracking**: A frozen, off-the-shelf point tracker (CoTracker-v3) acts as a teacher. Given the current frame $o_t$ and the $N$ grid points, it generates sparse ground-truth 2D displacement targets ($d^\star \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T \times 2}$) and visibility labels ($v^\star \in \{0,1\}^{N \times T}$) for all $T$ frames of the action chunk. This teacher is used exclusively during training.
- **Alignment Heads**: From the VLM hidden states at the $N$ image token positions, latent features $h$ are extracted. Two lightweight projection heads (two-layer MLPs) predict the point displacements ($\hat{d}$) and visibility ($\hat{v}$).
- **Motion Losses ($L_{track}$)**: The motion alignment objective combines three terms:
 - $L_{disp}$: Mean squared error for displacement regression.
 - $L_{vis}$: Binary Cross-Entropy for visibility classification.
 - $L_{smooth}$: L2 loss on the second-order temporal difference of predicted displacements, promoting physically plausible and smooth trajectories. All losses are masked by ground-truth visibility.

#### 3.3. Future Feature Prediction Branch

This branch focuses on encoding a compact, holistic representation of the final goal state.
- **<Foresight> Tokens**: $K$ special learnable tokens (e.g., $K=16$) are appended to the input sequence after the image and instruction tokens. Their hidden states are trained to encode the visual features of $o_{t+T}$, the final frame of the action chunk. This bottleneck encourages capturing global scene-level changes rather than fine-grained temporal details.
- **EMA Teacher**: A shadow copy of the vision encoder, updated via exponential moving average (EMA) with momentum $\mu=0.999$, serves as a stable teacher. It provides target visual features ($z^\star \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$) for the target frame $o_{t+T}$.
- **MAE Decoder**: A lightweight MAE decoder reconstructs the $M$-patch target features from the $K$ <Foresight> hidden states. It concatenates the $K$ tokens with $M-K$ learnable mask tokens and processes them through a 2-layer ViT decoder.
- **Loss ($L_{foresight}$)**: A cosine-similarity loss is used to match the reconstructed features ($\hat{z}$) with the EMA teacher's target features ($z^\star$).
- **Inference**: The $K$ <Foresight> tokens are retained in the input sequence at inference, allowing the action head to access future-informed hidden states as an explicit prior for the target visual state.

#### 3.4. Future-Conditioned Cross-Attention (FCCA) Module

To explicitly couple the future feature prediction and geometric point tracking objectives, a lightweight FCCA module is introduced.
- **Purpose**: It conditions the motion prediction on the predicted future state, thereby providing a global foresight prior to anchor local trajectory estimation. The hypothesis is that knowing the future visual state makes predicting point movements a better-constrained problem.
- **Mechanism**: The FCCA module is inserted between the VLM's visual token extraction and the displacement projector. It uses multi-head attention (MHA) where the visual features at grid positions ($H_{vis}$) query the VLM hidden states at <Foresight> positions ($H_{fut}$).
- **Initialization**: The output projection of the MHA is zero-initialized, causing the module to initially behave as an identity mapping. This preserves the pretrained VLM features without perturbation at the start of training and gradually learns to inject future information into spatial tokens.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference

- **Overall Training Objective**: The total loss $L$ is a weighted sum of the action prediction loss ($L_{action}$) and the two auxiliary objectives: $L = L_{action} + \lambda_1 L_{foresight} + \lambda_2 (L_{disp} + L_{vis} + \lambda_{smooth} L_{smooth})$.
- **Teacher Roles**: Both the CoTracker-v3 point tracker and the EMA teacher operate in a supervisory capacity, providing targets without receiving gradient updates themselves.
- **Inference**: All auxiliary branches (point tracking heads, MAE decoder) are discarded. The only modification is the inclusion of the $K$ <Foresight> tokens in the input sequence to the VLM, incurring minimal additional latency (e.g., 9.4 ms) and GPU memory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates FoMoVLA across three benchmarks: LIBERO, RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop, and LIBERO-Plus for zero-shot generalization. Comparisons are made against generalist VLA/WAMs, future prediction methods, and point tracking methods.

#### 4.1. LIBERO Evaluation (Table 1)

- **Performance Gains from Auxiliary Objectives**: Both the future feature prediction branch and the point tracking branch individually contribute to improved performance over the Base VLA model. The "Future Prediction" variant achieved an average success rate of 97.5%, while the "Tracking" variant reached 97.8%, compared to the Base Backbone's 96.5%. This indicates that both predictive and geometric supervision provide complementary inductive biases.
- **Synergy through FCCA**: When combining both objectives, simply adding their losses ("+ Future Prediction + Tracking") yielded an average of 98.3%. However, the full FoMoVLA model, which incorporates the Future-Conditioned Cross-Attention (FCCA) module to explicitly couple these objectives, achieved the highest average success rate of 98.8%. This suggests that explicit interaction between foresight and motion prediction leads to gains beyond simple multi-task training. The advantage was particularly notable on LIBERO-Long tasks (97.6%), which require extended temporal reasoning.

#### 4.2. LIBERO-Plus Zero-Shot OOD Robustness (Table 2)

- FoMoVLA demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization capabilities, achieving an overall success rate of 80.5% without any fine-tuning on perturbed data. This matched the performance of Abot-M0, a method that leverages additional pretraining, while significantly outperforming its base backbone, StarVLA (74.1%), by 6.4 percentage points.
- **Areas of Strong Generalization**: The most significant improvements were observed on language (+5.5% over StarVLA) and background perturbations, indicating that FoMoVLA's enhanced spatio-temporal representations contribute to better understanding of task instructions and scene context under variations.
- **Modest Gains in Some Perturbations**: Gains on camera and robot state shifts were more modest. The authors suggest this may be due to the model's training on fixed viewpoints and initial poses, making it challenging to generalize to entirely novel configurations using purely 2D motion cues.

#### 4.3. RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop Evaluation (Table 3 and 7)

- FoMoVLA achieved an average success rate of 56.9% on the RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop benchmark, which features 24 pick-place tasks for a humanoid robot with egocentric observations. This represents a 9.1 percentage point improvement over the StarVLA-GR00T backbone (47.8%).
- **Consistent Component Benefits**: Ablation studies on RoboCasa revealed a similar pattern to LIBERO: adding future prediction alone improved performance to 54.4%, adding tracking alone to 55.6%, and the combination with FCCA yielded the highest 56.9%. These results confirm the consistent benefits of the dual-objective training and the FCCA module across different robot embodiments and camera setups.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 4)

- **Future Conditioning (FCCA)**: Quantitative results confirmed that the FCCA module consistently yielded gains over variants without explicit future conditioning (e.g., +1.8% on LIBERO-Long in Table 1). Qualitative visualizations (Fig. 3) illustrated that FCCA helps align predicted point trajectories with actual action dynamics, rectifying incorrect displacement directions observed in models without it.
- **Sparse Supervision Design**:
 - **Point Grid Density**: A sparse 8x8 grid (64 points) was found to be optimal, achieving the best overall performance, especially for long-horizon tasks. Denser grids (e.g., 16x16 with 256 points) led to degraded performance, validating the choice of lightweight, sparse supervision.
 - **Foresight Architecture**: The MAE bottleneck design (16 to 64 tokens) outperformed a direct 64-token approach for long-horizon tasks. This supports the concept that a compact bottleneck encourages global goal-state encoding rather than patch-level memorization.
- **Auxiliary Task Coupling**: Different coupling designs were tested. Sharing goal tokens between branches led to severe performance degradation due to gradient interference. Assigning separate query tokens recovered performance. The final design, "Image + Goal CrossAttn" (FoMoVLA's approach), which uses image tokens for motion decoding while injecting goal-oriented context via cross-attention from foresight tokens, achieved the highest performance (98.8%), demonstrating effective cooperative information flow without representational interference.

#### 4.5. Scalability Across Different Frameworks (Table 5)

- FoMoVLA demonstrated its compatibility and generalizability by consistently improving two alternative StarVLA variants: StarVLA-π and StarVLA-OFT. It boosted StarVLA-π from 95.7% to 97.9% average success rate (with a notable +8.2% on LIBERO-Long) and StarVLA-OFT from 96.6% to 98.0%. This indicates that FoMoVLA's benefits are complementary to various policy head designs, rather than being tied to a specific action formulation.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4 and 7)

- Visualizations confirmed that the predicted foresight accurately reconstructs ground-truth future features, indicating the K-token bottleneck's ability to capture global scene changes.
- Predicted point trajectories closely aligned with ground-truth motion, signifying successful absorption of geometric supervision.
- Crucially, the predicted change heatmap concentrated on task-relevant regions (e.g., manipulated object, end-effector path) while suppressing static backgrounds, demonstrating spatially grounded foresight.
- On RoboCasa with egocentric views, the model successfully handled non-stationary viewpoints, disentangling ego-motion from task-relevant object motion and focusing predictive attention on dynamic elements.

#### 4.7. Inference Cost (Table 8)

- FoMoVLA incurs minimal inference overhead. The addition of K=16 <Foresight> tokens increases median latency by only 9.4 ms and GPU memory by 0.1 GB, confirming its practical deployability without significant resource demands.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FoMoVLA represents an advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Its significance lies in addressing a fundamental limitation of existing reactive VLA policies and providing a comprehensive framework for incorporating explicit spatio-temporal foresight.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Bridging Foresight Modalities**: The paper's core contribution is the integration of compact future feature prediction and sparse 2D point tracking into a single VLA framework. By arguing that these two forms of foresight are complementary—one defining "where to go" (goal state) and the other "how to get there" (motion path)—FoMoVLA moves beyond approaches that treat them as separate, implicitly related objectives.
- **Enhanced Latent Representations**: FoMoVLA injects explicit spatio-temporal supervision directly into the latent representations of the VLM backbone. This allows the model to learn more predictive and spatio-temporally grounded visual representations, leading to a deeper understanding of scene dynamics and state transitions.
- **Improved Manipulation Performance**: The consistent improvements observed across diverse benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop) and task complexities (including long-horizon tasks) demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach in leading to more precise and robust robotic manipulation.
- **Strong Zero-Shot Generalization**: FoMoVLA's competitive performance on the challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark for out-of-distribution robustness highlights its ability to generalize to unseen environmental perturbations, language variations, and background changes, which is crucial for real-world robotic applications.
- **Efficient Deployment**: A significant practical impact is that all auxiliary components (point tracking heads, EMA teacher, MAE decoder) are utilized exclusively during training. This ensures that the learned spatio-temporal knowledge is absorbed into the VLM backbone, and the model incurs no additional parameter overhead or substantial latency during inference. This characteristic makes FoMoVLA appealing for real-time robotic systems where computational efficiency is critical.
- **Cooperative Information Flow**: The introduction of the Future-Conditioned Cross-Attention (FCCA) module is impactful as it enables explicit interaction and consistency between anticipated visual states and geometric motion dynamics. This mechanism prevents gradient interference and fosters a synergistic learning environment, leading to more coherent spatio-temporal reasoning than naive multi-task training.

**Potential Impact:**

The capabilities demonstrated by FoMoVLA could have several potential impacts on the development of robotic systems:
- **More Autonomous and Proactive Robots**: By enabling robots to explicitly anticipate future states and motion paths, FoMoVLA could contribute to the development of more autonomous robots capable of proactive planning and execution, reducing reliance on constant human intervention.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Environments**: The enhanced spatio-temporal understanding could lead to robots that are more robust in dynamic and unstructured environments, better able to react to unforeseen changes while still working towards a long-term goal.
- **Foundation for Complex Tasks**: The framework's ability to ground visual representations in explicit motion cues and future states could serve as a foundation for tackling more complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks, assembly, or dexterous operations.
- **Reduced Data Requirements**: While not explicitly stated, models with better inductive biases regarding world dynamics might require less demonstration data to achieve competent performance, a crucial aspect for scaling robot learning.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The paper acknowledges a limitation: current 2D tracking supervision primarily models view-dependent motion in image space and does not explicitly capture the 3D geometry of a dynamic scene. This limits robustness to significant viewpoint and depth variations.

A promising future direction identified by the authors is to complement 2D point tracking with 3D motion prediction. Integrating 3D geometric reasoning could enhance the model's understanding of physical interactions, leading to greater robustness against changes in camera perspective and depth variations, and ultimately contributing to more versatile and generalized robotic manipulation capabilities. This suggests a pathway for future research to build upon FoMoVLA's foundations to develop even more advanced and general-purpose robot intelligence.
