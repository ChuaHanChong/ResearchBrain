# Boosting Robotic Manipulation Generalization with Minimal Costly Data

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboTron-Craft is an Isaac Sim pipeline that auto-generates physically realistic, stage-divided manipulation trajectories; RoboTron-Platter splits demonstrations into a cheap target-agnostic Spatial Reasoning Phase and a costly Physical Interaction Phase, then trains VLA models on a mixture dominated by automatically collected SRP data. Mixing raises zero-shot success by up to 41% and transfers picking skill to unseen target objects.

## Problem

- Teleoperated demonstration collection dominates existing robot datasets and is prohibitively slow and costly, one dataset needing 17 months for 130k episodes, leaving scenario coverage sparse.
- Prior VLA training treats an episode as one undifferentiated trajectory, ignoring that approach and contact sub-tasks differ in difficulty and require different data volumes.
- Existing generalization routes, domain randomization, meta-learning, world-model pretraining, still improve little on out-of-distribution target objects without auxiliary information supplied at inference.

## Method

- **RoboTron-Craft**: an ==Isaac Sim== generation pipeline with two-level object-centric then agent-centric planning, sampling collision-free manipulation poses and motion-planned trajectories over varied objects, scenes and photo-realistic renders.
- SRP-only collection truncates each trajectory at an approaching pose formed by applying a random SE(3) offset to the ground-truth grasp pose, skipping contact simulation costing over 70% of generation time.
- **RoboTron-Platter**: segment full trajectories by end-effector-to-target distance into ==Spatial Reasoning Phase (SRP)== and ==Physical Interaction Phase (PIP)==, then train on a mixed set of all full-stage episodes plus extra independent SRP episodes.

## Results

- Adding SRP data lifts zero-shot mean success by up to **41%** over the SRP-free reference and matches full-stage-data performance; gains saturate once SRP episodes exceed twice the full-stage count.
- At 66% SRP proportion, RoboMM improves **0.28 to 0.58** and RoboFlamingo **0.13 to 0.28** on the paper's benchmark, and **0.80 to 0.88** and **0.74 to 0.78** on CALVIN.
- Novel targets given SRP-only data reach **0.40** success versus **0.05** with no data, and repeating only PIP segments during training raises zero-shot success from **0.46 to 0.58**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sub-task difficulty is asymmetric: spatial search scales with workspace and scene diversity, contact scales with object geometry diversity, so each stage deserves its own data budget.
> - Cheap partial trajectories act as a catalyst, not a substitute, with a saturation ceiling of a few times the full-stage volume before extra ones stop helping.
> - Shared approach behaviour can bridge skills to unseen objects, yet partial-data generalization stays weak, so contact demonstrations remain the binding constraint on absolute performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI), specifically focusing on enhancing the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation. The rapid advancement of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has amplified their application in real-world interactions, driving the development of VLA models. A significant challenge in training these models is the demand for large quantities of diverse demonstration trajectories, which are costly and time-consuming to collect, whether in simulation or real-world environments. This limitation often results in insufficient data coverage across various scenarios, hindering model performance and generalization to novel agent configurations or tasks.

Prior research has attempted to address these issues through several avenues, including cross-agent training, spatial cognition enhancement, and task logical extraction via chain-of-thoughts. Recent studies have also explored scaling laws relating operational workspace volume, data quantity, and VLA model generalization. A common premise underlying these approaches is the understanding of the compositional nature of embodied tasks. Existing robot datasets, while extensive, are often collected via laborious teleoperation or manual labeling, presenting challenges in reproducibility and diversity. Algorithm-driven data collection methods, primarily in simulators, frequently suffer from limited task diversity and simplified physics.

Furthermore, current EAI models often struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, meaning they cannot effectively perform tasks on objects or in environments not explicitly encountered during training. Efforts to improve this have ranged from domain randomization and meta-learning to data augmentation, world model building, and spatial reasoning. The present work positions itself within this landscape by proposing a data mixture method that aims to reduce reliance on expensive interaction data, thereby offering a partial solution to the data scarcity and generalization challenges facing VLA models in robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that the high costs associated with collecting diverse robot manipulation demonstrations often lead to insufficient data coverage, which consequently limits the generalization performance of VLA models. The authors identify that failures in robotic manipulation frequently occur during the Spatial Reasoning Phase (SRP), where the robot navigates a large workspace without close interaction with targets. Conversely, the Physical Interaction Phase (PIP) requires precise actions and object interaction, making its data collection significantly more labor-intensive and costly.

Given this distinction, the core question guiding this work is whether inexpensive SRP data can be strategically leveraged to enhance the value of scarce and costly PIP data, thereby reducing the overall effort required for data collection.

Specifically, the key objectives are:
- **Develop a Cost-Effective Data Generation Pipeline:** To introduce RoboTron-Craft, a stage-divided pipeline designed to generate physically realistic manipulation trajectories with photo-realistic observations. This pipeline also serves as an evaluation environment.
- **Produce Diverse Trajectories and Identify Scaling Laws:** To utilize RoboTron-Craft to generate diverse trajectories across various stages, objects, and environments, and to reveal the logarithmic scaling law governing VLA model performance in relation to target object diversity, the amount of training trajectories, and environmental diversity.
- **Propose a Decoupled Training Framework:** To introduce the RoboTron-Platter method, a framework that explicitly decouples training trajectories into distinct SRP and PIP stages. This framework aims to utilize abundant, easily collectible SRP data to specifically enhance the spatial reasoning abilities of VLA models.
- **Demonstrate Performance Enhancement and Cost Reduction:** To demonstrate through analysis and experimentation that sub-task-specific training, incorporating additional SRP data in appropriate proportions, can act as a performance catalyst for robot manipulation. This approach aims to maximize the utilization of costly PIP data and significantly reduce the manual effort traditionally required for extensive data collection.
- **Improve Generalization to Novel Scenarios:** To achieve substantial improvements in success rates for zero-shot scenes and to enable the transfer of manipulation skills to novel target objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a two-pronged approach: the **RoboTron-Craft** benchmark for data generation and evaluation, and the **RoboTron-Platter** method for stage-decoupled model training.

#### 3.1. RoboTron-Craft Benchmark

RoboTron-Craft is a cost-effective pipeline built upon the NVIDIA Isaac Sim platform, which provides physics simulation and photo-realistic rendering capabilities. It is designed to generate physically realistic manipulation trajectories and serves as an environment for model evaluation.

- **Trajectory Generation Pipeline:** The pipeline is structured as a two-level hierarchy:
 - **Scene Initialization:** Randomly samples a pre-defined environment and manipulatable objects, placing them in collision-free poses. The agent is initialized with a random arm pose.
 - **Object-Centric Planning:** Samples a target object and a feasible manipulation pose (g = (R, t) ∈ SE(3)) from a set of pre-generated pose candidates (G_cand_M). These candidates are automatically generated and undergo collision detection. Pre-grasp offset poses are also incorporated as keyframes.
 - **Agent-Centric Planning:** A task manager supervises task execution stages using known 'g' and task templates. Collision-free robot trajectories are planned using motion planners (e.g., CuRobo) informed by the target's geometry and scene spatial occupancy. Path points are generated at 120Hz.
 - **Trajectory Collection:** Records comprehensive data at 30Hz, including language descriptions, gripper poses, action targets, robot joint information, gripper status, RGB and depth camera images, and object status, for training and reproduction.

- **SRP Data Collection:** To specifically generate inexpensive SRP data, the method modifies the trajectory generation process. Instead of planning to the exact manipulation pose 'g', an offset transform (∆T) with random orientation and translation is applied to 'g' to obtain an "approaching pose" (g_app) that is just before direct interaction. This approach eliminates the need for precise narrow-path planning and complex physics simulation during interaction, significantly reducing data collection time. The approach is designed to be applicable to real-world robot data collection using sparse ground truth labels and object pose estimation, thereby reducing human teleoperation.

#### 3.2. RoboTron-Platter: Training with Data Mixture

RoboTron-Platter is a method that segments robot manipulation trajectories into distinct SRP and PIP stages based on the agent's interaction with objects.

- **Trajectory Segmentation:** Full-stage trajectories (τ_F) from a dataset (D_F) are segmented into SRP and PIP based on the distance between the end-effector and the target object. The PIP segment (τ_PIP) begins when the end-effector is at the approaching pose (g_app) and ends after the interaction-rich stage. Remaining segments, such as the initial approach, are classified as SRP (τ_SRP).
- **Dataset Construction:** An independently collected SRP dataset (D_SRP_ind) is incorporated alongside the segmented D_F. A mixed dataset (D_Mix) is constructed by sampling N1 full-stage trajectories from D_F and N2 independent SRP trajectories from D_SRP_ind.
- **Training Procedure:** The VLA model (RoboMM is used as a baseline) is trained on D_Mix. This implicitly creates a sub-task-specific training procedure for D_PIP and (D_SRP ∪ D_SRP_ind), allowing for flexible control over each sub-task's performance by varying the proportions (p_SRP = N2 / (N1 + N2)).
- **Data Balancing:** During training, to prevent SRP features from dominating, PIP segments within the full-stage trajectories (τ_PIP ⊂ τ_F) are duplicated by a factor of floor(N2/N1). This strategy aims to maintain distribution consistency across sub-tasks and stabilize the model optimization process.
- **Evaluation:** Models are evaluated in the RoboTron-Craft benchmark using IsaacSim. Success is defined as the agent successfully picking up the specified target. Evaluations are performed in both "test scenes" (encountered during training) and "zero-shot scenes" (randomly initialized novel environments) to assess generalization. Inference does not use depth images, relying on language instruction, RGB observations, and camera parameters.
- **Ablation Studies:** The research conducts ablation studies on aspects of demonstration generation (e.g., approaching strategies, randomization, action label generation, camera viewpoints) and the necessity of stage decoupling, as well as data balancing strategies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis yielded several findings regarding the impact of SRP data and data scaling laws on VLA model performance:

- **Generalization with SRP Data:** Incorporating additional, automatically collected SRP data significantly improved the VLA model's success rate in zero-shot scenes. A maximum improvement of 41% over a reference model (trained without additional SRP data) was observed. This demonstrates that inexpensive SRP data can achieve performance equivalent to costly full-stage data under certain conditions. However, performance gains plateaued when the ratio of N2 (independent SRP trajectories) to N1 (full-stage trajectories) exceeded 2:1, indicating a bottleneck where excessive incomplete trajectories without sufficient operational data may hinder effective manipulation learning. Increasing the quantity of full-stage data (N1) could delay this bottleneck.
- **Universality of SRP Data:** The benefits of adding SRP data were observed across different VLA models (RoboMM, RoboFlamingo) and datasets (the authors' dataset and CALVIN). The improvement was more pronounced in tasks with larger, more complex spatial reasoning challenges, such as approaching targets from random orientations (authors' dataset), compared to simpler tasks with relatively fixed approach poses (CALVIN).
- **Generalization on Novel Targets:** Models trained with only SRP data for novel, out-of-distribution (OOD) target objects could successfully perform manipulation tasks, especially for objects with geometric similarities to those seen during training. This suggests that SRP data can act as a "bridge" for transferring generalized skills, enabling instance-wide generalization without requiring additional auxiliary information. However, overall performance on novel targets remained relatively lower than on seen targets, indicating room for further research in this area.
- **Data Scaling Laws:**
 - **Object Instance Capability:** Performance improved with both increasing the quantity of training data per instance and increasing the diversity of target geometries. Augmenting target geometry diversity was found to be more effective than merely increasing the number of trajectories for each instance, implying that the policy can abstract manipulation-suitable geometric features from diverse shapes.
 - **Environment Capability:** VLA model performance positively correlated with the diversity of training scenes, both in environments encountered during training and in novel environments. Increasing environmental diversity yielded greater benefits than increasing object diversity for VLA models.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Demonstration Generation:** A "relaxed linear approach" for target approach was found most beneficial, avoiding wrist camera occlusion and rigid path constraints. Randomized manipulation poses were crucial for generalization. Using a table-mounted camera combined with a wrist camera provided optimal observation, minimizing self-occlusion. "State subtraction" for action label generation was more effective than "target pose subtraction."
 - **Stage Decoupling Necessity:** The SRP stage, due to its vast exploration space and inherent difficulty, significantly reduced overall performance when not decoupled. Learning the two stages in a decoupled manner, with potentially different data volumes and learning speeds, was deemed advisable. The performance bottleneck for manipulation-only tasks occurred earlier than for full-stage tasks, indicating distinct data requirements for spatial reasoning (workspace coverage) and physical interaction (target shapes).
 - **Data Balancing Strategies:** Repeating only the PIP segments of full-stage data during training yielded optimal results compared to repeating full-stage trajectories or no repetition. This approach maintained sub-task distribution consistency and stabilized the model optimization process. A logarithmic relationship was identified between the proportion of SRP data (p_SRP) and model success rate, suggesting that approximately 4 times more additional SRP data (p_SRP up to 66%) can be effectively added to full-stage data before performance rapidly declines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the development of more efficient and generalized robotic manipulation systems. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a practical method for reducing the prohibitive costs associated with collecting large-scale, diverse robot demonstration data. By strategically leveraging easily collectible Spatial Reasoning Phase (SRP) data, the RoboTron-Platter method provides a pathway to amplify the value of scarce Physical Interaction Phase (PIP) data.

The potential impacts include:
- **Reduced Data Collection Costs:** The ability to achieve substantial performance improvements (up to 41% in zero-shot scenes) by integrating inexpensive SRP data directly translates into a reduced dependency on labor-intensive human teleoperation, making robotic learning more accessible and scalable.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The method significantly boosts the generalization performance of VLA models, particularly in novel, zero-shot environments and with unseen target objects. This addresses a critical limitation of current EAI models that struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Novel Instance-Wide Generalization:** The finding that SRP-only trajectories can act as a "bridge" for transferring generalized skills to novel objects opens new research avenues for instance-wide generalization without requiring explicit demonstrations for every new object.
- **Foundation for Stage-Aware Learning:** The introduction of a stage-decoupled training paradigm, coupled with identified scaling laws for different task stages, provides a new conceptual framework for future research. This could lead to more sophisticated stage-aware curriculum learning approaches in embodied AI, allowing for optimized data allocation and learning rates based on the inherent difficulty and data requirements of each task phase.
- **Improved Model Robustness:** Insights from the ablation studies, particularly regarding optimal data generation strategies and camera placements, can guide the development of higher-quality datasets and more robust VLA model architectures.

In summary, this work advances the field by offering a data-efficient and cost-effective approach to training VLA models, paving the way for more capable and adaptable robotic systems that can operate effectively in diverse, unconstrained real-world environments.
