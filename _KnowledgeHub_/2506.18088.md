# RoboTwin 2.0: A Scalable Data Generator and Benchmark with Strong Domain Randomization for Robust Bimanual Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboTwin 2.0 introduces a scalable simulation framework and benchmark designed to generate high-quality, domain-randomized data for robust bimanual robotic manipulation, addressing limitations in existing synthetic datasets. Policies trained with RoboTwin 2.0 data achieved a 24.4% improvement in real-world success rates for few-shot learning and 21.0% for zero-shot generalization on unseen backgrounds.

## Problem

- The prohibitive cost and complexity of collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world datasets for bimanual robotic tasks.
- Existing synthetic datasets often lack automated quality control, leading to execution failures or suboptimal grasps that hinder policy learning.
- Current simulation environments employ superficial domain randomization, oversimplifying real-world complexities and limiting robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

![[2506.18088-method.png]]
*RoboTwin 2.0 pipeline: MLLM-based expert code generation with simulation-in-the-loop feedback synthesizes task programs over RoboTwin-OD and a skill API, then produces domain-randomized trajectories for downstream policy training.*

- An ==automated expert data generation pipeline== leveraging ==multimodal large language models (MLLMs)== and ==closed-loop simulation-in-the-loop feedback== to synthesize and refine high-quality task execution code.
- Comprehensive ==domain randomization== systematically applied across five dimensions: scene clutter, background textures, lighting variations, tabletop heights, and diverse language instructions.
- ==Embodiment-aware grasp adaptation== mechanisms that account for heterogeneous ==dual-arm kinematics==, generating robot-specific action candidates and manipulation strategies.

## Results

- Automated expert code generation achieved an average success rate of **71.3%** with multimodal feedback, alongside reduced token costs for code refinement.
- Policies trained with RoboTwin 2.0's domain-randomized data showed relative improvements of up to **31.9%** in average success rate for generalization on unseen, randomized conditions in simulation.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated policies augmented with RoboTwin 2.0 data achieved a **24.4%** improvement in average real-world success rate in few-shot scenarios and **21.0%** in zero-shot generalization on unseen backgrounds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal feedback from a vision-language model, combined with execution logs, substantially improves the reliability and success rate of MLLM-generated robotic task code.
> - Extensive and systematic domain randomization across visual, physical, and linguistic factors is critical for training policies that exhibit strong robustness and generalization to real-world complexities.
> - Accounting for robot-specific kinematic differences through embodiment-aware grasp adaptation is essential for efficient manipulation across diverse robotic platforms, especially those with fewer degrees of freedom.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with a strong focus on developing generalizable policies that can perform complex tasks in diverse real-world environments. A critical challenge in this area is the prohibitive cost and complexity of collecting large-scale, high-quality, and diverse real-world datasets, especially for bimanual tasks. This has led to a growing reliance on simulation-based data generation.

RoboTwin 2.0 directly addresses the limitations of existing simulation pipelines for robotic manipulation, particularly in the context of bimanual tasks and sim-to-real transfer. Prior work, such as ManiSkill2, Meta-World, CALVIN, and RoboVerse, has made strides in providing benchmarks and datasets for various aspects of manipulation. Large-scale real-world datasets like Open X-Embodiment and Bridge Data have also emerged to bridge the sim-to-real gap. RoboTwin 1.0 previously introduced a dual-arm benchmark with simulated replicas of real demonstrations.

However, RoboTwin 2.0 identifies three critical shortcomings in the current landscape:
1. **Lack of automated quality control:** Many existing synthetic datasets suffer from execution failures or suboptimal grasps due to a lack of expert-level validation loops, degrading policy learning.
2. **Superficial domain randomization (DR):** Current simulations often oversimplify environments, omitting crucial real-world complexities like clutter, varied lighting, and ambiguous language instructions, which are essential for robust sim-to-real transfer.
3. **Overlooking cross-embodiment variation:** Different robotic platforms have distinct kinematic capabilities and optimal grasp strategies, but existing synthetic datasets rarely account for these embodiment-specific affordances.

RoboTwin 2.0 positions itself as a significant advancement by integrating state-of-the-art techniques to overcome these challenges. It leverages the power of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for automated task plan generation, a trend seen in works like GenSim2 and RoboGen, but extends it with closed-loop simulation-in-the-loop feedback for higher quality. Its comprehensive domain randomization strategy builds upon prior work in DR for sim-to-real transfer (e.g.,), but applies it systematically across five critical dimensions and with a bidirectional feedback loop. Furthermore, its embodiment-aware adaptation mechanism specifically targets the problem of generalizing across heterogeneous robotic hardware, a key aspect for developing universal robot policies.

By providing a scalable framework, a large-scale object library (RoboTwin-OD), a diverse benchmark of 50 bimanual tasks across five robot embodiments, and a pre-collected dataset of over 100,000 expert trajectories, RoboTwin 2.0 directly contributes to the development and evaluation of generalizable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, RDT-1B, Pi0, OpenVLA). It provides the robust, diverse, and high-fidelity synthetic data that these models require to effectively learn from and generalize to real-world complexities, thereby closing a critical gap in the current robot learning ecosystem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RoboTwin 2.0 stems from the recognized need for high-quality, diverse, and large-scale datasets to train robust and generalizable bimanual robotic manipulation policies, particularly VLA foundation models. The paper identifies two primary challenges that existing synthetic datasets fail to adequately address:

1. **Scalable and Efficient Data Generation for Novel Tasks:** Current methods struggle to efficiently generate expert-level data for a broad spectrum of novel bimanual tasks without extensive manual intervention. This leads to bottlenecks in scaling up datasets for training generalist policies. The objective is to automate the creation of high-quality expert trajectories, minimizing human supervision and ensuring semantic alignment with task objectives.
2. **Oversimplified Simulation Environments:** Existing simulation environments often lack the complexity and variability found in the real world. This "oversimplification" results in policies that overfit to narrow data distributions and fail to generalize effectively to real-world scenarios characterized by clutter, varied lighting, diverse backgrounds, and different robot kinematics. The motivation here is to develop a simulation framework that generates data with rich visual, linguistic, and physical diversity to bridge the sim-to-real gap and enhance policy robustness.

Specifically, RoboTwin 2.0 aims to:
- Develop an automated expert data generation pipeline that leverages MLLMs and simulation-in-the-loop feedback to iteratively validate and refine task execution code, ensuring high-quality demonstrations.
- Implement comprehensive domain randomization over language instructions, object clutter, background textures, lighting conditions, and tabletop configurations to improve sim-to-real transfer and policy generalization.
- Incorporate embodiment-aware adaptation mechanisms that account for heterogeneous dual-arm kinematics, generating robot-specific action candidates and manipulation strategies.
- Release robust resources including a large-scale object library (RoboTwin-OD), an automated data generator, a pre-collected multi-embodiment domain-randomized trajectory dataset, and a standardized benchmark for evaluating policy generalization.

Ultimately, the overarching objective is to provide the robotics community with a powerful, scalable, and open-source platform that accelerates research in robust and generalizable bimanual robotic manipulation by addressing the fundamental data generation and simulation fidelity challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboTwin 2.0 proposes a scalable simulation framework built upon three integrated key components: an automated expert data generation pipeline, comprehensive domain randomization, and embodiment-aware grasp adaptation.

#### 3.1. Automated Expert Code Generation via MLLMs and Simulation-in-the-Loop Feedback

This module automates the synthesis of executable task plans from natural language instructions, ensuring expert-level quality without extensive human supervision.
- **Closed-Loop Architecture:** The system employs a closed-loop architecture with two agents: a **code-generation agent** (based on MLLMs like DeepSeek-V3) and a **vision-language model (VLM) observer** (moonshot-v1-32k-vision-preview).
- **Input Specification:** Tasks are defined by natural language instructions, a general API list for robotic actions, example function calls, and hierarchical constraint specifications.
- **Initial Code Generation:** The MLLM-based code agent synthesizes an initial Python program, treating it as a structured prediction problem over available API calls, using natural language understanding and few-shot prompting.
- **Simulated Execution and Logging:** The generated program is executed multiple times (ten per iteration) in a simulated environment to account for stochasticity. A structured execution log records successes, failures, and their causes (e.g., grasp failure, incorrect placement).
- **Multimodal Observation and Error Localization:** The VLM observer inspects the robot's behavior frame-by-frame across trials. It detects failures, localizes their occurrence, and diagnoses the root cause (flawed logic, incorrect API usage, etc.). This multimodal feedback goes beyond simple execution logs.
- **Code Repair and Iterative Refinement:** The code-generation agent receives both quantitative execution logs and qualitative VLM diagnostics. It uses this combined feedback to revise the program iteratively. The process continues until a success rate above 50% is achieved across ten runs or after five refinement iterations, yielding robust, expert-level task code.

#### 3.2. Domain Randomization for Robust Robotic Manipulation

To enhance policy robustness and sim-to-real transfer, RoboTwin 2.0 systematically randomizes five dimensions:
- **Scene Clutter:** Task-irrelevant distractor objects from RoboTwin-OD (731 objects across 147 categories) are placed in scenes, ensuring physical plausibility via collision-aware placement. Similar objects to task-relevant ones are excluded to prevent confusion.
- **Diverse Background Textures:** A large curated texture library (11,000 high-quality textures generated via LLM prompting, web crawling, and Stable Diffusion v2 with human filtering) is applied to tabletop surfaces and backgrounds, reducing overfitting to clean synthetic environments.
- **Lighting Variation:** Lighting conditions are randomized across color, type, intensity, and position to mimic real-world illumination shifts, which drastically alter object appearance and reflections.
- **Tabletop Heights:** Table heights are uniformly randomized within a plausible range, introducing variability in viewpoints and spatial relations between the robot and objects.
- **Trajectory-Level Diverse Language Instructions:** A multimodal LLM generates diverse task templates and multiple object descriptions, capturing varied attributes. For each trajectory, instructions are composed by sampling from these linguistic pools, creating a large set of linguistically varied instructions.

#### 3.3. Embodiment-Aware Grasp Adaptation

This component addresses the differences in kinematic structures and manipulation strategies across various robotic arms.
- **Object Annotation:** Each object in RoboTwin-OD is richly annotated with candidate manipulation poses, covering multiple grasp axes and approach directions. This captures both manipulation diversity and robot-specific preferences.
- **Angular Perturbations:** To expand the feasible space, angular perturbations biased towards directions with higher arm reachability are applied.
- **Motion Planning:** Candidate grasps are generated by combining preferred operation directions, randomized pose perturbations, and parallelized motion planning attempts, leveraging Curobo, a GPU-accelerated motion planner.
- **Multi-Embodiment Support:** The framework supports five distinct robot embodiments (Franka, Piper, UR5, ARX-X5, Aloha-AgileX) and flexible dual-arm pairings, including heterogeneous manipulators.

#### 3.4. RoboTwin 2.0 Data Generator, Benchmark, and Large-Scale Dataset

Building on these methodological components, RoboTwin 2.0 introduces three key resources:
- **RoboTwin-OD:** A large-scale object library with 731 instances across 147 categories. Objects are sourced from in-house RGB-to-3D reconstruction, Objaverse, and SAPIEN PartNet-Mobility. Each object has rich semantic (15 language annotations per object) and manipulation-relevant annotations (key points, axes, functional points).
- **Automated Data Generation and Pre-Collected Dataset:** A pipeline for generating expert trajectories across 50 dual-arm tasks, supporting data collection on five distinct robot platforms. Over 100,000 expert trajectories are pre-collected and released.
- **Benchmark:** A unified evaluation benchmark for policy generalization to cluttered environments and open-ended language goals, designed to reflect real-world complexity and diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrate its effectiveness across automated code generation, adaptive grasping, policy robustness in simulation, and sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Automated Expert Code Generation

- **Improved Success Rates with Multimodal Feedback:** On a suite of 10 tasks, multimodal feedback (MM FB) significantly improved the Average Success Rate (ASR) compared to one-shot (Vanilla) or execution-log-only feedback (FB). In RoboTwin 2.0, ASR increased from 62.1% (Vanilla) to 71.3% (MM FB), while RoboTwin 1.0 showed gains from 47.4% to 63.9%. Top5-ASR also saw consistent improvements, indicating that perceptual feedback benefits the best programs.
- **Enhanced Efficiency:** RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrated faster convergence (e.g., 1.76 vs. 2.42 CR-Iter for MM FB) and substantially reduced token costs (569.4 vs. 1236.6 tokens for Vanilla) compared to RoboTwin 1.0, suggesting stronger initial priors and more efficient refinement.
- **Robustness and Reliability:** Feedback mechanisms, particularly vision-language diagnostics, narrowed the success-rate distribution and raised the median, leading to more compact and reliable task execution, centered above 80% success for RoboTwin 2.0 with MM FB.

#### 4.2. Evaluating Efficiency with and without Adaptive Grasping

- **Significant Gains for Low-DoF Robots:** The embodiment-aware grasp augmentation strategy led to an average improvement of 8.3% in task success rates across all five robot embodiments. This benefit was most pronounced for lower-DoF platforms like Aloha-AgileX (+13.5%), Piper (+22.7%), and ARX-X5 (+5.6%), where the method provides crucial additional feasible grasp options.
- **Marginal Impact for High-DoF Robots:** For high-DoF arms such as Franka and UR5, success rates remained largely unchanged, indicating that robots with ample kinematic flexibility benefit less from expanded grasp candidates.

#### 4.3. Assessing the Impact of RoboTwin 2.0 on Policy Robustness (Simulation)

- **Domain Randomization Enhances Generalization:** Models (RDT, Pi0) pre-trained with RoboTwin 2.0's domain-randomized data showed significant relative improvements in average success rate (31.9% for RDT, 29.3% for Pi0) when evaluated on unseen, domain-randomized conditions. This effect persisted even when downstream tasks were fine-tuned using only clean, non-randomized data.
- **Limitations of Clean Data:** Models trained solely on clean data or using existing pre-trained VLAs without further fine-tuning showed negligible improvements, highlighting that data without domain randomization is insufficient to handle environmental variations. This also ruled out a "Real-to-Sim" gap as the primary cause for low VLA performance in simulation.

#### 4.4. Evaluation on Sim-to-Real Performance

- **Robustness Gains in Real-World Settings:** On four bimanual tasks executed on a COBOT-Magic platform, policies augmented with RoboTwin 2.0 data achieved substantial gains. In the few-shot setting (10 real + 1k synthetic domain-randomized trajectories), the average real-world success rate improved by 24.4%.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Even in a zero-shot setting, trained solely on 1,000 domain-randomized synthetic trajectories, policies showed notable improvements of 21.0% and 20.5% on unseen-background scenarios.
- **Effectiveness in Complex Scenes:** Performance gains were more pronounced in visually complex scenes (cluttered environments, unseen backgrounds), confirming RoboTwin 2.0's efficacy under challenging real-world conditions. This indicates that high-fidelity, domain-randomized synthetic data, combined with minimal real-world data, effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap.

#### 4.5. RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark

- **Highlighting Robustness Challenges:** Evaluation of five VLA policies (ACT, DP, RDT, Pi0, DP3) on 50 tasks showed a significant drop in success rates from "Easy" (clean) to "Hard" (domain-randomized) conditions. RDT's average success rate dropped by 20.8%, and Pi0's by 30.1%, underscoring that robustness to domain shifts remains a major challenge for current models.
- **Value of Pre-training:** Pre-trained models (RDT, Pi0) showed stronger resilience under hard conditions compared to non-pretrained ones (ACT, DP, DP3), indicating that vision-language-action pre-training provides useful priors for generalization.
- **Impact of 3D Information:** DP3 achieved the best few-shot performance, suggesting the importance of 3D information, although its performance benefited from ideal point clouds and clean segmentation in simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboTwin 2.0 represents a significant contribution to the fields of robotic manipulation, sim-to-real transfer, and the development of generalist robot policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable and High-Quality Data Generation:** By introducing an automated expert data generation pipeline powered by MLLMs and a closed-loop simulation-in-the-loop feedback mechanism with VLM observation, RoboTwin 2.0 drastically reduces the manual effort and cost associated with creating high-quality, diverse robot datasets. This scalability is crucial for training increasingly complex VLA foundation models that demand vast amounts of data.
- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robustness:** The comprehensive and systematic domain randomization across visual (clutter, background, lighting), physical (tabletop height), and linguistic dimensions is a critical advancement. The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that training with RoboTwin 2.0's domain-randomized data significantly improves policies' robustness to real-world environmental variations and enhances their zero-shot and few-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities. This directly addresses one of the most persistent challenges in robot learning.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The embodiment-aware grasp adaptation mechanism, tailored for heterogeneous dual-arm kinematics, enables policies to generalize across different robot platforms. This is vital for developing truly generalist robots that are not restricted to a single hardware configuration, thereby broadening the applicability of learned skills.
- **Valuable Open-Source Resources:** The release of the RoboTwin-OD asset library (with 731 annotated objects), the automated data generator, a pre-collected large-scale multi-embodiment domain-randomized trajectory dataset (over 100,000 trajectories), and a standardized 50-task benchmark provides invaluable resources to the entire robotics research community. These tools and datasets will accelerate research, facilitate reproducible comparisons, and lower the barrier to entry for developing and evaluating advanced manipulation policies.
- **Advancing VLA Foundation Models:** RoboTwin 2.0 provides the essential high-fidelity, diverse, and domain-randomized data needed to train and benchmark the next generation of Vision-Language-Action models. By explicitly addressing the limitations of prior synthetic datasets, it enables these models to learn more robust and generalizable representations, ultimately pushing closer to deploying truly intelligent robots in unstructured real-world environments.

In conclusion, RoboTwin 2.0 is not just an incremental update but a comprehensive platform that tackles fundamental challenges in robot learning by integrating state-of-the-art AI techniques. Its open-source nature and the provision of rich datasets and benchmarks are poised to significantly impact and propel future research in robust and generalizable bimanual robotic manipulation.
