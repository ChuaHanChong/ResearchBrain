# G2DP: Diffusion Planning with Spatio-Temporal Grid Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> G2DP introduces a diffusion-based motion planner that uses differentiable spatio-temporal grid guidance to integrate dense environmental constraints directly into trajectory generation for autonomous vehicles. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance among imitation-based planners on nuPlan, improving reactive driving scores by 8.4 points on Test14-hard and showing significant gains in collision avoidance (+10.15 points) and progress (+10.5 points) on interPlan and DeepScenario benchmarks.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving motion planners struggle to generate safe and efficient trajectories in complex, dynamic environments due to the limitations of traditional rule-based and existing learning-based methods.
- Diffusion-based generative models for motion planning, while capable of diverse trajectory generation, lack explicit mechanisms to guarantee safety and compliance with driving rules in closed-loop execution.
- Current guidance strategies for diffusion planners are either sparse (entity-centric geometric queries) or post-hoc (rule-based refinement), leading to limited situational awareness, rigidity, or deviations from learned behaviors.

## Method

- Proposes **G2DP**, a grid-guided diffusion planner that integrates a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== for trajectory generation with a U-Net for spatio-temporal occupancy grid prediction.
- Constructs a differentiable spatio-temporal cost volume by fusing predicted future occupancy grids with route desirability maps, providing a continuous cost landscape.
- Employs classifier guidance to inject dense, analytical gradients derived from the fused cost volume directly into the diffusion model's denoising loop, proactively steering trajectory generation towards safe and progress-optimal pathways.

## Results

- G2DP achieved state-of-the-art performance among purely imitation-based planners on the nuPlan benchmark, with its history-free variant (G2DPext†^	ext{\dagger}ext†) improving reactive driving scores by **8.4** points on Test14-hard.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on the interPlan benchmark, outperforming Diffusion Planner by **9.65** points overall and improving collision avoidance by **10.15** points and Time-to-Collision by **10.75** points.
- In a zero-shot evaluation on the DeepScenario urban dataset, G2DP improved collision avoidance by approximately **11.0** points and progress by **10.5** points compared to Diffusion Planner, showcasing robustness to distribution shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Densely represented spatio-temporal cost volumes, fusing probabilistic occupancy with route progress, enable more proactive and continuous steering of generative planning compared to sparse geometric guidance.
> - Injecting energy-based guidance during the later stages of the diffusion denoising process is crucial for refining macroscopic trajectory topology towards safety and progress without disrupting the model's initial behavioral intent.
> - A Top-K aggregation strategy for the ego vehicle's footprint on the cost grid provides a robust and differentiable mechanism for calculating trajectory costs, balancing safety coverage with robustness to noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving motion planning aims to generate safe and efficient trajectories for a self-driving vehicle in complex, dynamic environments. Traditional rule-based methods offer interpretability and safety guarantees but often struggle with intricate multi-agent interactions and long-tail scenarios, frequently necessitating extensive manual tuning.

Learning-based approaches, particularly imitation learning, have emerged as a method to replicate human-like driving behaviors from large datasets. While these methods demonstrate improved expressiveness, they are susceptible to distribution shifts and generally lack explicit mechanisms to guarantee safety or strict adherence to traffic rules. Consequently, many contemporary learning-based planners employ a hybrid strategy, utilizing learning for initial trajectory generation followed by optimization-based refinement to rectify unsafe or kinematically infeasible plans. This reliance on post-hoc rule enforcement can reintroduce the rigidity of traditional systems and potentially deviate trajectories from the learned distribution.

Recently, diffusion-based generative models have been adopted for motion planning, reframing the task as a conditional generation problem. These models are capable of representing diverse and multi-modal trajectory distributions and offer flexibility during the inference process, providing a potential advantage over autoregressive planners in terms of long-horizon stability. However, a significant challenge within diffusion-based planning pertains to ensuring safety and compliance with driving rules in closed-loop execution. The inherent stochasticity of these models often requires explicit guidance during the denoising process.

Existing guidance strategies for diffusion planners typically fall into two categories:
1. **Sparse Geometric Guidance:** This involves evaluating constraints based on discrete, entity-centric geometric queries (e.g., Euclidean distances to lanes or other agents). Such methods are often confined to instance-anchored avoidance and may result in less smooth or reactive trajectories due to their limited situational awareness and inability to capture future uncertainties.
2. **Post-hoc Refinement:** Similar to hybrid learning-based planners, this involves applying rule-based corrections after trajectory generation. As noted, this can reintroduce rigidity and move trajectories away from the learned distribution.

The current work, G2DP, addresses the limitations of these existing guidance mechanisms by proposing a novel approach that integrates dense, distributional spatio-temporal constraints directly into the differentiable generative loop of diffusion-based planners. It leverages Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) occupancy grids, which naturally encapsulate rich spatial information, to construct a differentiable cost landscape. This approach aims to provide proactive and continuous steering of trajectory generation, moving beyond sparse, deterministic queries and post-hoc rectification, thereby enhancing safety and robustness in interactive traffic scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a diffusion-based motion planner for autonomous driving that can effectively and robustly enforce dense environmental constraints during trajectory generation, particularly in dynamic and interactive traffic scenarios.

The motivation for G2DP stems from several identified limitations in the current state of diffusion-based planning:

- **Inherent Stochasticity and Safety Concerns:** While diffusion models excel at capturing diverse driving behaviors and generating multi-modal trajectories, their stochastic nature means that naive sampling often lacks explicit mechanisms to guarantee safety and strict adherence to traffic rules, especially when dealing with imperfect or noisy demonstration data.
- **Limitations of Existing Guidance Mechanisms:**
 - **Sparse Geometric Guidance:** Current guidance methods often rely on sparse, entity-centric geometric queries (e.g., distances to a few surrounding agents or lane boundaries). These provide limited situational awareness, filter out crucial spatial uncertainty, and typically result in reactive rather than proactive steering. This can lead to abrupt maneuvers or fragility in complex interactive scenes.
 - **Post-hoc Refinement:** Applying rule-based optimization after trajectory generation, while common, can reintroduce the inflexibility of traditional systems. It also risks shifting the generated trajectories away from the learned distribution, undermining the benefits of generative modeling.
- **Unexplored Potential of Dense Spatial Representations:** Despite the natural fit of Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) occupancy grids for representing dense spatial information and potential collision risks, their direct and differentiable integration as continuous cost landscapes within the generative denoising loop of diffusion planners remained largely unexplored.
- **Need for Proactive Steering:** An effective planner requires the ability to proactively steer trajectories toward safe and optimal regions, rather than merely reacting to impending collisions. This necessitates guidance that can anticipate future risks and provide continuous, well-conditioned gradients.

Therefore, the key motivation is to bridge the gap between rich spatio-temporal grid representations and continuous generative planning. G2DP aims to overcome the fragilities of sparse guidance and the rigidity of post-hoc refinement by constructing a differentiable spatio-temporal cost volume. This volume fuses probabilistic future occupancy distributions with route-progress information, allowing for the injection of dense, analytical gradients directly into the denoising loop. This active steering mechanism is designed to guide trajectory generation towards collision-free and progress-optimal pathways, thereby enhancing the safety, robustness, and generalization capabilities of diffusion-based autonomous driving planners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

G2DP proposes a grid-guided diffusion planner that integrates trajectory generation with explicit safety constraints through a differentiable spatio-temporal cost volume. The approach consists of three main components: a diffusion-based trajectory generator, a cost grid map constructor, and a grid-guided sampling procedure.

**A. Diffusion-Based Trajectory Generation**

- **Trajectory Representation:** The ego vehicle's motion plan is formulated as conditional trajectory generation over a fixed horizon of $T_{fut}$ steps. The ego state at each timestep is represented by its 2D position and heading $(x_\tau, y_\tau, \cos \theta_\tau, \sin \theta_\tau)$. The complete ego trajectory $x \in \mathbb{R}^{(T_{fut}+1) \times 4}$ is the primary target for the diffusion model.
- **Scene Encoding:** Surrounding scene information, including past neighbor states, static objects, and lane segments, is encoded into a set of compact scene context tokens $S$ using dedicated fusion encoders.
- **DiT-Based Diffusion Model:** A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone is employed, operating directly in the trajectory space. It models a variance-preserving diffusion process where a neural network learns to denoise trajectories in the reverse process. At each diffusion step $t$, the noisy trajectory $x_t$ is embedded along with an agent-type token and an embedding of the diffusion step $t$ and navigation route. The denoising network comprises DiT blocks with adaptive layer normalization, applying self-attention to ego trajectory tokens and cross-attention to scene context.
- **Sampling and Ego-Only Generation:** During inference, the process is initialized by concatenating the current ego state with Gaussian noise for future steps. A DPM-Solver iteratively solves the reverse diffusion. A constraint corrector ensures the first state of $x_t$ matches the current ego state. The model generates a single ego trajectory proposal $\hat{x}_0$, while past neighbor trajectories are used only for conditioning.

**B. Cost Grid Map Construction**

A U-Net-based model predicts spatio-temporal occupancy grids from observed history and static scene context.

- **Input Representation:** Inputs are ego-centric BEV rasterizations. Dynamic history is represented by agent footprints over $T_{hist}$ past timesteps ($P \in \mathbb{R}^{C_{dyn} \times T_{hist} \times H \times W}$), while static map features (lane geometry, drivable areas) are encoded in $M \in \mathbb{R}^{C_{stat} \times H \times W}$.
- **Occupancy Prediction:** The U-Net outputs future occupancy grids $O \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{occ} \times H \times W}$, where $O_\tau(u, v) \in$ represents the occupancy probability at future timestep $\tau$. This serves as a dense, differentiable cost field.
- **Route-Aligned Progress:** The navigation route polyline is rasterized into ego-centric grids, and a distance transform to the route centerline is computed. A route desirability map $R(u, v)$ is defined, assigning lower costs to cells near and ahead along the route, encouraging forward progress.
- **Fused Cost Grids:** For each future timestep $\tau$, occupancy and progress are fused into a single cost grid $\psi_\tau(u, v) = \gamma \cdot O_\tau(u, v) + (1 - \gamma) \cdot R(u, v)$, where $\gamma$ balances collision avoidance and route progress. The stack of these grids $C = \{\psi_\tau\}_{T_{occ}}^{\tau=1}$ constitutes the spatio-temporal cost volume.

**C. Trajectory Generation Guided by Cost Grids**

To enable proactive obstacle avoidance and progress maintenance, G2DP employs a spatially continuous and differentiable guidance signal derived from the fused cost grids.

- **Spatio-Temporal Energy Formulation:** For an ego future trajectory sample $x = \{x_\tau\}_{T}^{\tau=0}$, an energy field is constructed. For each timestep $\tau$, the ego vehicle's oriented rectangular footprint $K_{\theta_\tau}$ is used to bilinearly sample the cost grid $\psi_\tau$. To balance safety coverage and robustness to noise, a "Top-K" footprint aggregation strategy is applied, where the scalar cost $c^{(\tau)}$ is the mean of the $K$ highest values within the sampled region. The cumulative trajectory cost, serving as the potential energy $E(x)$, is defined as the sum of $c^{(\tau)}$ over the planning horizon: $E(x) = \sum_{\tau=1}^{T} c^{(\tau)}$. The gradient $\nabla_x E(x)$ provides a holistic spatio-temporal signal for trajectory optimization.
- **Gradient-Based Denoising Steering:** During inference, classifier guidance is used. The unconstrained marginal distribution $p_t(x_t | S)$ learned by the DiT is reweighted with the safety energy $E(x_t; C)$ to define a refined posterior distribution $p^\star_t(x_t | S, C)$. The guided sampling update is derived from the score of this distribution: $\nabla_{x_t} \log p^\star_t = \nabla_{x_t} \log p_t - \lambda_t \nabla_{x_t} E(x_t; C)$, where $\lambda_t$ is a guidance scale. The term $-\lambda_t \nabla_{x_t} E$ acts as an energy-based correction, steering the generative process towards low-energy basins (safe, progress-aligned trajectories) while preserving learned kinematic constraints.
- **Guidance Injection Window:** Guidance is applied during the late stages of the denoising process (e.g., steps 8-9 out of 10) to refine the macroscopic trajectory topology without disrupting the model’s initial behavioral intent, which is established in earlier steps.

**D. Implementation Details**

- **Training Strategy:** The occupancy U-Net and diffusion model are trained separately on the nuPlan 1M training split. The U-Net is trained offline with focal loss and remains frozen during inference. The DiT backbone is trained with MSE loss.
- **Planning and Sampling:** A planning horizon of 3 seconds at 10 Hz is used, with a 10-step DPM-Solver for inference.
- **Occupancy Prediction:** The occupancy network predicts 30 future occupancy grids (10 Hz) on a 400x400 BEV raster (100 m x 100 m, 0.25 m/px).
- **Cost Fusion:** The occupancy weight $\gamma$ is fixed at 0.95.
- **Top-K Aggregation:** $K=15$ is chosen for Top-K footprint aggregation, representing approximately 8% of the footprint cells.
- **Guidance Scale and Window:** Guidance is restricted to denoising steps 8–9 with a fixed scale of $\lambda_t = 0.5$.
- **Inputs:** G2DP uses 2 seconds of neighbor histories and current ego state. G2DP$^\dagger$ removes all history inputs, using only current ego and neighbor states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

G2DP was evaluated on three closed-loop planning benchmarks: nuPlan, interPlan, and DeepScenario, comparing against rule-based, hybrid, and learning-based methods, including other diffusion planners.

**A. nuPlan Main Results (Table I)**

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance:** G2DP demonstrated the strongest performance among purely imitation-based planners (those without post-hoc refinement). It consistently outperformed baselines such as Diffusion Planner and Flow Planner across Val14, Test14-hard, and Test14 benchmarks in both non-reactive (NR) and reactive (R) settings.
- **Reactive Test14-hard Improvement:** On the challenging reactive Test14-hard benchmark, G2DP$^\dagger$ (the history-free variant) achieved notable gains, improving the reactive driving score by +8.4 points compared to Diffusion Planner. This variant (G2DP$^\dagger$) also showed a +3.7 improvement over the full G2DP, suggesting that removing temporal correlations from history inputs can benefit reactive performance.
- **Reduced Refinement Dependency:** When comparing with hybrid methods that utilize post-hoc refinement (+ref.), G2DP$^\dagger$ exhibited a substantially reduced dependency on this module (+4.1 points) compared to Diffusion Planner (+12.8 points). This indicates that the grid-guided sampling intrinsically aligns with rule-based safety criteria, leading to more robust initial plans.

**B. interPlan Results (Table II & III)**

- **Zero-shot Generalization:** In a zero-shot transfer setting on interPlan (a benchmark of highly interactive, adversarial scenarios), G2DP significantly outperformed Diffusion Planner by +8.84 points in overall score. G2DP$^\dagger$ further increased this to +9.65 points.
- **Enhanced Safety:** The improvement was primarily attributed to larger safety margins. Collision avoidance improved by +8.06 points (G2DP) and +10.15 points (G2DP$^\dagger$) relative to Diffusion Planner. Time-to-Collision (TTC) also saw substantial gains, with +9.55 points (G2DP) and +10.75 points (G2DP$^\dagger$). This indicates the effectiveness of dense probabilistic cost-grid guidance in complex, interactive scenarios.

**C. DeepScenario Results (NR) (Table IV)**

- **Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** In a zero-shot evaluation on DeepScenario (a drone-recorded urban dataset with real-world dense traffic), G2DP improved both safety and efficiency compared to Diffusion Planner. Collision avoidance increased by approximately +11.0 points, and progress by +10.5 points. G2DP also surpassed PDM-Closed in progress and route-aligned progress metrics within the high-density urban subset.

**D. Qualitative Analysis (Fig. 3, 4, 5)**

- **Safe and Efficient Navigation:** Qualitative comparisons showed that G2DP maintained safe and efficient forward progress in crowded urban streets and executed anticipatory lane changes to bypass stopped vehicles, outperforming baselines that either suffered collisions or inefficient navigation.
- **Proactive Steering:** Ablation studies comparing execution with and without grid guidance demonstrated that guided trajectories consistently steered away from obstacles and conflict zones while maintaining kinematic feasibility and forward progress. The guidance gradients, derived from the time-varying cost volume, showed how high-risk regions generated repulsive gradients, actively pushing trajectory points towards safer and more goal-consistent areas.

**E. Ablation Studies**

- **Effect of Grid Guidance (Table V):** Grid guidance achieved the highest overall score (+3.35) and improved safety (Collision +3.59, TTC +4.94) compared to an unguided approach. In contrast, sparse guidance (instance-level distance functions) led to a degradation in overall score (−1.96) and comfort (−10.13), suggesting that dense, probabilistic risk distributions enable smoother and more anticipatory collision avoidance.
- **Occupancy Weight ($\gamma$) (Fig. 6):** Performance peaked at $\gamma = 0.95$. Lower values weakened safety enforcement, while removing the progress term ($\gamma=1.0$) induced overly conservative braking, leading to increased rear-end collisions in reactive scenarios.
- **Guidance Scale ($\lambda_t$) and Injection Window (Fig. 7):** Applying guidance during the late stages of denoising (Steps 8–9 out of 10) was consistently more effective. An optimal guidance scale of $\lambda_t = 0.5$ was identified. Stronger gradients ($\lambda_t \geq 0.8$) or extending the guidance window to earlier steps negatively impacted performance, confirming that early guidance can interfere with trajectory stabilization.
- **Cost Aggregation (Table VI):** The Top-K strategy (K=15) for footprint cost aggregation balanced capturing hazard boundaries and mitigating sensitivity to single-pixel noise, outperforming average pooling (which diluted collision signals) and max pooling (which was sensitive to localized artifacts).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The G2DP research introduces a significant advancement in diffusion-based motion planning for autonomous driving by demonstrating the efficacy of spatio-temporal grid guidance. Its key contributions and potential impacts are as follows:

- **Enhanced Safety and Robustness:** The core innovation of injecting dense, distributional cost field gradients directly into the denoising loop provides a proactive and continuous mechanism for steering trajectory generation. This has been empirically shown to significantly improve safety-critical metrics, such as collision avoidance and time-to-collision, across diverse and challenging benchmarks. This approach offers a more robust framework for safety enforcement compared to sparse geometric queries or post-hoc refinement.
- **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The model's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance on nuPlan and robust zero-shot generalization on interPlan and DeepScenario benchmarks indicates its adaptability to complex, interactive, and previously unseen scenarios. This suggests a potential for safer deployment in real-world urban environments characterized by dense traffic and varied driving situations, where traditional methods or less sophisticated learning models might falter.
- **Advancement in Generative Planning:** G2DP bridges a gap between high-level environmental context (via spatio-temporal grids) and continuous trajectory generation, leveraging the strengths of diffusion models for multi-modal behavior modeling while addressing their inherent safety challenges. By integrating explicit constraints directly into the generative process, it moves towards more autonomous and adaptable end-to-end learned planning pipelines, reducing the reliance on brittle, hand-engineered fallback mechanisms.
- **Flexible and Differentiable Guidance:** The formulation of a differentiable spatio-temporal energy functional, which evaluates dynamic collision risks over the ego geometric footprint, provides analytical gradients for guidance. This allows for fine-grained steering without altering the generative backbone, preserving the learned behavioral priors of the diffusion model.
- **Potential for Real-World Autonomous Driving Systems:** The demonstrated improvements in reactive robustness and collision avoidance in challenging scenarios position G2DP as a relevant development for practical autonomous driving applications. Its ability to maintain forward progress safely amidst dense pedestrians and execute anticipatory maneuvers underscores its utility in complex urban topologies.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges that G2DP currently relies on a simplified vehicle footprint model and requires tuning for guidance scheduling parameters. Future work suggests exploring an end-to-end framework where raw sensor data directly informs cost volume generation, aiming to unify perception and guided generative planning within a single, more integrated loop. This could further enhance the model's adaptability and performance by removing the dependency on intermediate representations and hand-tuned components.
