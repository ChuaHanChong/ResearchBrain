# Coding the Visual World: From Image to Simulation Using Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Sagi Eppel from the Weizmann Institute AI Hub developed the Im2Sim methodology and Im2Sim2Im evaluation framework to assess Vision Language Models' capacity for mechanistic understanding by generating simulation code from images. The research shows VLMs can grasp high-level generative processes and build models but often struggle with replicating fine details and exact spatial layouts.

## Problem

- Current Vision Language Model (VLM) benchmarks primarily assess descriptive and discriminative abilities, failing to probe deeper "world understanding" beyond pattern recognition.
- A key challenge is determining if VLMs can infer the underlying causal mechanisms or generative processes responsible for visual patterns.
- Existing visual-to-code generation approaches are often limited to structured, man-made visuals, not complex, emergent natural phenomena.

## Method

- The ==Im2Sim methodology== prompts Vision Language Models (VLMs) to generate executable simulation code that recreates the underlying generative processes observed in natural images.
- An ==Im2Sim2Im evaluation framework== quantitatively assesses the similarity between VLM-generated simulations and original images, complemented by qualitative analysis of code and descriptions.
- The approach evaluates leading VLMs' ability to explain and reproduce ==emergent patterns== across diverse domains, including physical phenomena, biological structures, and urban layouts.

## Results

- Leading Vision Language Models achieved **50-80%** matching accuracy between their generated synthetic images and original inputs in quantitative evaluations, exceeding random chance.
- VLMs successfully generated executable code capable of simulating a wide array of emergent patterns from images, including physical phenomena and biological structures.
- Qualitative analysis indicated that while VLMs grasp high-level processes, they sometimes resort to visual pattern replication using procedural functions instead of true physical simulation, especially for intricate details.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs exhibit high-level conceptual understanding, identifying fundamental generative mechanisms and selecting appropriate modeling frameworks (e.g., L-systems for vegetation, Perlin noise for textures).
> - Models can decompose complex systems into components and apply multi-level abstraction, balancing detailed physics with simpler approximations.
> - A notable asymmetry reveals strong conceptual grasp of principles but limited capacity to precisely reproduce fine-grained details, exact spatial arrangements, and integrate multi-component simulations with high fidelity.
