# Coding the Visual World: From Image to Simulation Using Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Sagi Eppel from the Weizmann Institute AI Hub developed the Im2Sim methodology and Im2Sim2Im evaluation framework to assess Vision Language Models' capacity for mechanistic understanding by generating simulation code from images. The research shows VLMs can grasp high-level generative processes and build models but often struggle with replicating fine details and exact spatial layouts.

## Problem

- Current Vision Language Model (VLM) benchmarks primarily assess descriptive and discriminative abilities, failing to probe deeper "world understanding" beyond pattern recognition.
- A key challenge is determining if VLMs can infer the underlying causal mechanisms or generative processes responsible for visual patterns.
- Existing visual-to-code generation approaches are often limited to structured, man-made visuals, not complex, emergent natural phenomena.

## Method

- The ==Im2Sim methodology== prompts Vision Language Models (VLMs) to generate executable simulation code that recreates the underlying generative processes observed in natural images.
- An ==Im2Sim2Im evaluation framework== quantitatively assesses the similarity between VLM-generated simulations and original images, complemented by qualitative analysis of code and descriptions.
- The approach evaluates leading VLMs' ability to explain and reproduce ==emergent patterns== across diverse domains, including physical phenomena, biological structures, and urban layouts.

## Results

- Leading Vision Language Models achieved **50-80%** matching accuracy between their generated synthetic images and original inputs in quantitative evaluations, exceeding random chance.
- VLMs successfully generated executable code capable of simulating a wide array of emergent patterns from images, including physical phenomena and biological structures.
- Qualitative analysis indicated that while VLMs grasp high-level processes, they sometimes resort to visual pattern replication using procedural functions instead of true physical simulation, especially for intricate details.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs exhibit high-level conceptual understanding, identifying fundamental generative mechanisms and selecting appropriate modeling frameworks (e.g., L-systems for vegetation, Perlin noise for textures).
> - Models can decompose complex systems into components and apply multi-level abstraction, balancing detailed physics with simpler approximations.
> - A notable asymmetry reveals strong conceptual grasp of principles but limited capacity to precisely reproduce fine-grained details, exact spatial arrangements, and integrate multi-component simulations with high fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly computer vision and natural language processing, has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision Language Models (VLMs). These models, trained on extensive visual question-answering and image-to-text datasets, have demonstrated capabilities in tasks such as image classification, segmentation, and complex question answering. However, the extent to which VLMs possess a genuine understanding of the world, beyond pattern recognition, remains a subject of ongoing discussion within the research community. Some studies indicate VLM proficiency in general visual intelligence, while others highlight limitations in addressing seemingly straightforward tasks or perceiving fine visual details.

This work addresses a more profound aspect of visual understanding: the ability to construct a representative model of the system depicted in an image, which necessitates an understanding of the underlying generative mechanisms. Traditional computer vision tasks often focus on descriptive or recognition-based approaches, such as identifying objects or segments within an image. In contrast, this research explores VLMs' capacity to infer the *process* that forms a visual pattern and then simulate that process using code. This moves beyond mere pattern identification to pattern generation based on inferred principles, aligning with perspectives that equate deeper understanding with the ability to model and predict phenomena.

The methodology employed, Im2Sim (Image-to-Simulation), builds upon the concept of generating code from visual inputs, an area with growing interest. Prior research has explored related tasks such as converting diagrams to code (e.g., UML diagrams, block diagrams), generating vector graphics from images or text, creating CAD models from various inputs, or synthesizing procedural materials. This paper extends these ideas by focusing on the simulation of dynamic, emergent real-world systems (e.g., physical phenomena, biological growth, urban development) rather than primarily static designs or abstract representations. By tasking VLMs with generating executable simulation code, this study positions itself at the intersection of visual understanding, generative AI, and computational modeling, aiming to probe the mechanistic comprehension capabilities of current VLM architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the capacity of Vision Language Models (VLMs) to comprehend and simulate the systems and mechanisms depicted in natural images. This overarching goal is supported by several detailed motivations and specific aims:

- **Probing Deep Visual Understanding:** The work is fundamentally motivated by the hypothesis that a deep form of visual understanding is represented by the ability to construct a representative model of a system shown in an image. Unlike superficial tasks such as classification or segmentation, creating a simulation requires identifying and understanding the underlying mechanisms and generative processes that formed the observed visual patterns. The research seeks to determine if VLMs can move beyond pattern recognition to achieve this level of mechanistic comprehension.

- **Evaluating VLM World Understanding:** There is an ongoing debate within the AI community regarding whether VLMs truly possess "world understanding" or if their capabilities are primarily based on sophisticated pattern matching. By challenging VLMs to not only describe but also simulate complex emergent patterns—ranging from physical systems like waves and clouds to biological structures like vegetation, and social systems like cities—the research aims to provide empirical evidence contributing to this discussion. The capacity to infer and implement generative processes is presented as a strong indicator of deeper understanding.

- **Introducing and Applying the Im2Sim Methodology:** A core objective is to introduce and rigorously apply the Im2Sim (Image-to-Simulation) methodology. This framework provides a structured approach for evaluating VLMs by tasking them with transforming a natural image into a descriptive text, executable simulation code, and a synthetic image generated by that code. This method yields multiple outputs (process description, code, synthetic image) that can be analyzed to assess the VLM's understanding.

- **Comparative Analysis of VLM Capabilities:** The study aims to qualitatively evaluate different leading VLMs (e.g., GPT, Gemini) across a diverse range of visual domains and system complexities. This comparison seeks to identify common strengths, specific weaknesses, and varying strategies employed by different models when tasked with system simulation.

- **Generating Useful Assets:** Beyond evaluation, a practical motivation is the generation of a diverse collection of pattern and texture generators. The executable code produced by the VLMs, if successful, can serve as a resource for applications requiring synthetic visual assets, such as computer graphics, game development, or design.

In summary, the research is driven by a desire to assess the depth of VLM visual intelligence by challenging them with a constructive, generative task—simulating the world from an image—rather than solely a discriminative one.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs the Im2Sim (Image-to-Simulation) methodology to evaluate the understanding capabilities of Vision Language Models (VLMs). This approach involves a multi-step process, which can be summarized as follows:

#### 3.1. Im2Sim Workflow

The core Im2Sim methodology (illustrated in Figure 1 and 2, left) consists of three main stages:
1. **Image Input:** A VLM is provided with a natural image depicting a real-world system or emergent pattern. These images represent a wide variety of domains, including physical phenomena (e.g., waves, clouds, light reflections, geological formations), biological structures (e.g., vegetation, trees), urban environments (e.g., cities, settlements), textual elements (e.g., handwriting, printed pages), and general visual patterns (e.g., tiling, textures).
2. **VLM Processing:** The VLM is tasked with two primary outputs based on the input image:
 - **Process Description:** A textual description of the underlying mechanisms or processes that are hypothesized to have formed the visual pattern in the image.
 - **Simulation Code Generation:** Executable code that implements the identified generative process. The specific programming language for the code is not explicitly stated but is typically Python for simulation tasks.
3. **Code Execution and Image Generation:** The code generated by the VLM is then executed to produce a synthetic image. This simulated image is intended to represent the VLM's interpretation and generative model of the original input.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Methods

The research employs both quantitative and qualitative evaluation methods for assessing VLM performance.

- **Quantitative Evaluation (Im2Sim2Im):**
 - This method, discussed in prior work and briefly summarized here (Figure 2, right), evaluates the accuracy of the VLM-generated simulation by comparing the synthetic image to the original input.
 - A multiple-choice image matching task is performed: an evaluator (either a human or another VLM, such as GPT-5) is presented with the original real image as a reference. This is paired with 10 test images, comprising the VLM's own synthetic image and 9 decoy images generated by other simulations.
 - The evaluator's task is to identify which of the 10 test images best matches the system of the reference image. The underlying assumption is that a more accurate simulation will yield a synthetic image that is perceptually more similar to the input.
 - Table 1 presents quantitative results for various VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5-flash/pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Llama-4, Grok-4) evaluated by GPT-5 (color/grayscale) or human evaluators. Accuracies ranged from 50% to 82% for leading models, compared to a 10% random baseline.

- **Qualitative Evaluation (Focus of this work):**
 - This study primarily focuses on a qualitative analysis. This involves direct examination of the three outputs of the Im2Sim method: the process description, the generated simulation code, and the synthetic images.
 - The generated synthetic images are visually compared against the original real-world images.
 - The analysis delves into the strategies employed by the VLMs for different types of systems, examining how they decompose systems into components, select modeling approaches, handle various levels of abstraction, and manage parameter tuning.
 - The paper organizes this qualitative analysis into sections covering diverse system categories: physical patterns, trees and vegetation, cities and settlements, text and symbols, and general visual patterns (e.g., tiling).

#### 3.3. Specific VLM Instances and Approaches

The paper mentions utilizing leading VLMs, primarily GPT (referred to as GPT-5 in qualitative examples) and Gemini (specifically Gemini 2.5-flash and Gemini 2.5-pro in quantitative results), along with other models like Qwen2.5, Llama-4, and Grok-4. The qualitative sections often detail how these models, particularly GPT, address specific image types:

- **Physical Systems:** VLMs recognized underlying processes and represented them across domains, sometimes using coarse approximations for certain components (e.g., sinusoidal waves for water surface) and accurate physics for others (e.g., Snell's law for light reflection), or by bypassing complex simulations in favor of statistical distributions (e.g., Boltzmann-like distribution for Chladni plates).
- **Vegetation:** VLMs leveraged well-known methods such as L-systems for branching structures and reaction-diffusion or fractal Brownian motion (fBM) for textures.
- **Cities:** VLMs employed multi-layered simulations, combining procedural functions (e.g., Perlin noise for geography), network growth models, and grid subdivision algorithms, with layers guiding subsequent ones (e.g., geography guiding population, population guiding road networks).
- **Text and Symbols:** VLMs demonstrated varied approaches, from simulating individual brush strokes for handwriting to replicating printing processes with standard fonts or generating random letter-like shapes using Markov chains.
- **General Visual Patterns:** For computationally expensive or complex systems (e.g., sun's granular cells, clouds, galaxies), VLMs often bypassed direct physical simulation, instead using simplified procedural functions like modified Voronoi cells, fBM, or Perlin noise to replicate visual outcomes. For static patterns like tiling, VLMs attempted to replicate underlying structures, including imperfections.

The methodology systematically applies the Im2Sim framework to a broad array of visual inputs, providing both quantitative metrics and in-depth qualitative observations to characterize VLM understanding of generative processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research reveals several key findings regarding the capabilities and limitations of Vision Language Models (VLMs) in understanding and simulating real-world systems from images:

#### 4.1. Demonstrated Capacity for System Understanding and Modeling

- **Broad Domain Coverage:** Leading VLMs, particularly GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5, exhibited the ability to understand and model complex, multi-component systems across a diverse range of domains. These included physical phenomena (e.g., weather-eroded dunes, Chladni plates, light caustics, fireplace flames), biological patterns (e.g., trees, vegetation), urban structures (e.g., cities), and various textures and patterns.
- **Identification of Core Mechanisms:** VLMs were able to identify the underlying core patterns, structures, and generative mechanisms present in images. For instance, they recognized branching in plants, convective processes in flames, or grid layouts in cities.
- **Approximation and Simulation:** The models demonstrated the capacity to approximate and simulate these identified mechanisms, producing synthetic images that, while often rough, captured the dominant structures and dynamics of the original systems. This indicates a form of deep mechanistic understanding rather than solely superficial pattern recognition.

#### 4.2. Multi-level Abstraction and Component Decomposition

- **System Decomposition:** A significant finding was the VLMs' ability to decompose a complex system into distinct components. For example, when simulating light reflection from a wavy water surface, the VLM modeled the water surface and the light reflection as separate but interacting elements.
- **Variable Abstraction Levels:** VLMs frequently simulated different components of a system at varying levels of abstraction. They might use coarse approximations for some parts (e.g., a simple sinusoidal function for a wavy water surface or a scalar heat field for fire) while applying more principled or accurate physics for others (e.g., Snell’s law for light reflection) or bypass direct physical simulation (e.g., for Chladni plates, using Boltzmann-like distributions for particle scattering). This strategic choice suggests an ability to prioritize and simplify complex dynamics.

#### 4.3. Reliance on Known Modeling Frameworks

- The simulation strategies and models employed by the VLMs were typically based on established frameworks. Examples include L-systems for botanical structures, reaction-diffusion or fractal Brownian motion (fBM) for textures, multi-component cellular models for geological erosion, network growth models for urban development, and procedural functions like Perlin noise or Voronoi cells for various natural patterns (e.g., clouds, solar granulation).
- The ability to select appropriate models, set relevant parameters and rules, and combine multiple models within a single simulation suggests an understanding of both the system and the applicability of various computational modeling techniques.

#### 4.4. Limitations in Fine Detail and Precision

- **Lack of Fine-grained Spatial Reproduction:** A consistent limitation observed across domains was the VLMs' struggle to replicate fine details, low-level spatial arrangements, and exact visual patterns. Examples include failing to capture specific wave modes for Chladni patterns, precise leaf shapes or bark textures, the exact layout and spatial correspondence of input cities, or intricate details of complex tapestry designs.
- **Imprecise Parameter Tuning and Integration:** While VLMs selected appropriate models for subsystems, the parameter tuning for these models and their precise integration often proved imprecise, resulting in generated images that captured general structures but lacked high fidelity.
- **"Cheating" or Pattern Replication vs. Physics Simulation:** In a notable proportion of cases, particularly for computationally expensive or challenging physical systems (e.g., solar granular cells, cloud formation), VLMs opted to bypass explicit physical simulation. Instead, they generated code that used simpler procedural functions (e.g., fBM, Perlin noise, Voronoi cells) to directly replicate the *visual appearance* of the pattern. While sometimes yielding visually more similar outputs, this strategy circumvents the explicit modeling of underlying physics, indicating a preference for visual approximation in some contexts. This behavior was more common in smaller VLM models.

#### 4.5. Observed Asymmetry in Understanding

- The findings highlight an intriguing asymmetry in VLM capabilities: a strong capacity for high-level, conceptual understanding of the generative structures and underlying principles of systems, coupled with a limited ability to perceive and accurately reproduce the fine visual details and precise low-level patterns within those same systems. VLMs demonstrated an understanding of *how* a system generally works but struggled with the exact visual manifestation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of Vision Language Models (VLMs) and has several potential impacts across various domains:

#### 5.1. Advancement in VLM Understanding and Evaluation

- **Validation of Deep Understanding:** The study provides empirical evidence supporting the claim that VLMs can achieve a form of "deep mechanistic understanding" of visual information. By successfully tasking VLMs to infer and simulate generative processes, the work suggests that these models can go beyond mere pattern recognition, classification, or description, indicating a more profound comprehension of how visual patterns are formed in the real world. This contributes to the ongoing debate about the nature and extent of VLM intelligence.
- **Novel Evaluation Methodology:** The Im2Sim framework offers a new and distinct methodology for evaluating VLM capabilities. Unlike traditional benchmarks that focus on recognition, detection, or question-answering, Im2Sim assesses a VLM's ability to synthesize and generate, thereby probing a higher-order form of intelligence related to modeling and causality. This can lead to the development of more comprehensive evaluation metrics for future VLM research.

#### 5.2. Contributions to Generative AI and Content Creation

- **Intelligent Content Generation:** The ability of VLMs to generate executable code for diverse patterns and systems represents a step towards more "intelligent" generative AI. This moves beyond simple image generation to procedural content generation guided by inferred principles, enabling the creation of complex synthetic environments, textures, and assets.
- **Practical Asset Creation:** The directly usable pattern and texture generators produced by the VLMs have immediate practical applications in fields such as computer graphics, game development, architectural visualization, and design. Artists and developers could leverage such VLM-generated code to create varied and procedurally controlled visual elements.

#### 5.3. Implications for Scientific and Computational Modeling

- **Automated Scientific Modeling:** The demonstrated capacity of VLMs to infer scientific processes from visual data hints at a future where AI could assist in scientific discovery and modeling. VLMs might be used to propose initial simulation models from observational data, generate hypotheses about underlying mechanisms, or even help in education by visually explaining complex phenomena through interactive simulations.
- **Cross-Domain Knowledge Transfer:** The fact that VLMs employ known modeling frameworks (e.g., L-systems, cellular automata) across diverse domains suggests a form of abstract reasoning that identifies common generative principles. This could lead to AI tools that facilitate knowledge transfer between scientific disciplines by recognizing analogous processes in different visual contexts.

#### 5.4. Identifying Areas for Future Research

- **Addressing Fine-grained Limitations:** The identified asymmetry—high-level understanding contrasted with limitations in fine detail replication and precise parameter tuning—highlights critical areas for future VLM development. Research can focus on improving VLMs' capacity for meticulous spatial reasoning, accurate integration of complex physical models, and sophisticated parameter optimization.
- **Bridging Abstraction Gaps:** Future work could explore how to better bridge the gap between a VLM's high-level conceptual understanding and its ability to instantiate those concepts with precise, low-level visual fidelity, potentially leading to more robust and accurate simulation capabilities.
- **Understanding "Cheating" Behavior:** The observation that VLMs sometimes bypass physics simulation in favor of visual pattern replication warrants further investigation. Understanding when and why VLMs choose this strategy can inform the design of prompts and VLM architectures that encourage deeper mechanistic reasoning over superficial visual matching.

In summary, this research not only validates certain advanced cognitive abilities in VLMs related to generative understanding but also opens avenues for practical applications in content creation and scientific modeling, while simultaneously pinpointing specific challenges for the ongoing development of more capable and genuinely intelligent AI systems.
