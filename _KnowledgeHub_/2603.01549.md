# Pri4R: Learning World Dynamics for Vision-Language-Action Models with Privileged 4D Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Pri4R introduces a framework to equip Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with an implicit understanding of world dynamics by leveraging privileged 4D geometric representation, specifically 3D point tracks, during training. This approach significantly improves VLA robustness and physical interaction capabilities without adding inference-time computational overhead, yielding up to a +13.2% average success rate increase on challenging simulation benchmarks like RoboCasa and +13.4% on real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack an implicit understanding of world dynamics, leading to brittle performance in physically interactive tasks.
- Current VLA training, primarily through action imitation, teaches 'how to move' but provides limited information about the physical consequences of actions on the environment.
- Prior approaches to incorporate predictive capabilities often introduce computational overhead at inference time or use signals that are spatially redundant or temporally sparse.

## Method

- **Pri4R** augments VLA models with a ==lightweight point track head== during training to predict future 3D trajectories of scene points, leveraging the VLM backbone's shared representations.
- It employs ==temporally dense and metrically grounded 3D point tracks== as a ==privileged auxiliary supervision signal==, directly aligning with the spatiotemporal metric space of robot actions.
- After training, the point track head is ==discarded==, allowing the refined VLA model to operate with no additional inference-time architecture changes, inputs, or computational overhead.

## Results

- Pri4R consistently improved task success rates for various VLA models across simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks.
- On the challenging RoboCasa benchmark, OpenVLA-OFT enhanced with Pri4R achieved a **+13.2%** absolute increase in average success rate (from **33.1%** to **46.3%**), with gains up to **+30.7%** on specific tasks like 'Pressing buttons'.
- In real-world evaluations, Pri4R led to an average **+13.4%** improvement in success rates for OpenVLA-OFT across four interactive manipulation tasks, including scenarios with obstacles, randomized placements, and moving objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporally dense and metrically grounded 3D point tracks are a superior supervisory signal for learning world dynamics compared to goal-only prediction, 2D tracks, or dense depth maps, especially when tracking both robot and scene points.
> - Equipping VLA models with an implicit understanding of spatiotemporal dynamics via privileged training-time supervision significantly enhances robustness without altering the inference pipeline.
> - Pri4R's lightweight point track head and its integration strategy effectively refine the VLM backbone's internal representations to encode causal relationships between actions and scene geometry evolution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, often built upon large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), aim to enable robots to perform complex tasks by understanding natural language instructions and visual observations to predict actions. A common approach involves adapting VLMs by adding a lightweight action head, leveraging their high-level semantic understanding for robotic control.

However, a noted limitation in many existing VLA models is their inadequate grasp of *world dynamics*—how the environment physically responds to a robot's actions. Traditional VLA training, often based solely on action imitation (behavior cloning), teaches a robot *how* to move but not *what will happen* as a consequence of those movements. This can lead to actions that are semantically plausible but physically inaccurate or brittle, hindering robust interaction with objects and environments. For instance, a robot might attempt to grasp a door handle without accounting for the door's kinematic constraints, leading to task failure.

Prior research has addressed this by incorporating predictive capabilities into robot policies. Some methods involve using separate generative models to predict future images or states, which then guide the action policy. While these approaches can enhance anticipatory reasoning, they often introduce additional computational latency during inference and are constrained by the accuracy of the predicted signals. Other strategies train a uniform policy to predict actions alongside auxiliary signals, such as high-level abstractions (language, feature embeddings, object-centric representations) or dense observations (images, depth maps).

The authors argue that these auxiliary signals often fall short because they are not directly aligned with the *spatiotemporal metric space* in which physical action-world interactions occur. High-level abstractions may lack explicit metric geometry, while dense observations can be spatially redundant and may not effectively capture the causal evolution of the scene geometry over time. Furthermore, many of these methods still require additional computation during inference, making them less suitable for real-time robotic applications.

Pri4R distinguishes itself by proposing a method to imbue VLA models with an *implicit* understanding of world dynamics through *privileged 4D geometric supervision* during training. This approach leverages detailed 3D point tracks—representing how scene geometry evolves over time—as a supervisory signal. Crucially, Pri4R is designed to modify the VLA's internal representations during training without imposing any changes to the model architecture, inputs, or computational overhead during inference. This situates Pri4R as an approach that aims to enhance VLA robustness and physical awareness while maintaining the efficiency and simplicity of existing VLA deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the physical interaction capabilities and robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by enabling them to implicitly understand how the world dynamically responds to their actions. The authors identified a significant gap in current VLA models: despite their advancements in semantic understanding and action imitation, they often exhibit a limited grasp of spatiotemporal dynamics, leading to brittle performance in physically interactive tasks.

The motivation for Pri4R stems from several observations:
- **Limitations of Action-Only Supervision:** Existing VLA models are predominantly trained via behavior cloning, using only action labels. This training signal informs the model *how* to move but provides limited information about the *consequences* of those movements on the surrounding environment. This deficiency prevents policies from developing an intuitive understanding of physical interactions, making them prone to errors when dealing with kinematic constraints, object contacts, or dynamic environments.
- **Human Analogy:** Humans learn motor control by not only understanding their body movements but also by anticipating how the world will react. This involves an internal model of geometry and dynamics, allowing for anticipatory reasoning about object movement, deformation, and contact. The authors seek to imbue robots with an analogous capability.
- **Deficiencies of Existing Predictive Signals:** While some prior works have incorporated predictive signals (e.g., future images, latent states, language, depth maps) to improve VLAs, these often have limitations. They can be temporally sparse (predicting only a final state), lack explicit spatial structure (language or latent embeddings), or be spatially redundant (dense images/depth maps that largely duplicate input observations). Furthermore, many of these approaches introduce additional computational overhead or complexity at inference time, which is undesirable for real-world robotic deployment.
- **Need for Metric and Temporally Grounded Representation:** The authors hypothesize that truly modeling world dynamics requires a representation that is both metric (preserving 3D geometry) and temporally grounded (showing evolution over time)—a "4D" representation. They propose that 3D point tracks, which capture the precise spatiotemporal evolution of scene points, naturally fit this requirement and are directly aligned with the metric space of robot actions.
- **Zero Inference Overhead Requirement:** A key practical motivation is to achieve this enhanced understanding of world dynamics without altering the VLA's inference-time architecture, inputs, outputs, or computational load. This ensures that the improved policies can be seamlessly integrated into existing VLA deployment pipelines.

In summary, the key objectives are:
1. To develop a simple and effective approach, Pri4R, that equips VLA models with an implicit understanding of world dynamics.
2. To leverage privileged 4D geometric information (3D point tracks) during training as an auxiliary supervision signal.
3. To refine the VLM backbone's shared representation, enabling it to encode the causal relationship between actions and scene geometry evolution.
4. To achieve these improvements without introducing any additional inference-time complexity, inputs, or computational overhead.
5. To validate the effectiveness of 3D point track prediction as a supervision target and demonstrate its consistent performance gains across diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks, with state-of-the-art VLA baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Pri4R proposes a framework that enhances VLA models by incorporating privileged geometric information during training to foster an implicit understanding of world dynamics. The core idea involves using high-fidelity 4D signals—specifically, 3D point tracks—as an auxiliary supervision target. This supervision is applied *only during training* to refine the VLM backbone's internal representations, thereby equipping the VLA with a physically-aware context for robot control. Crucially, the auxiliary components are discarded after training, ensuring zero additional inputs, outputs, or computational overhead at inference time.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Learning from Privileged Point Track Head:**
1. **Augmented Architecture:** Pri4R augments the VLM backbone with a lightweight *point track head*. This head is designed to predict future 3D trajectories of scene points.
2. **Shared Representation:** The VLM backbone's multi-modal embeddings (derived from observations like images, language, and proprioception) are fed into this point track head. This setup ensures that the gradients from the point tracking task backpropagate into the VLM backbone, encouraging it to encode spatiotemporal constraints and world dynamics within its shared feature space.
3. **Lightweight Design:** The point track head consists of two small Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a Point MLP and a Fusion MLP.
 - The **Point MLP** embeds the current set of Np 3D points (P_t) into per-point features (e_t).
 - The **Fusion MLP** takes a sequence of multi-modal embeddings (z_t) from the VLM backbone (representing scene context over the action horizon) and concatenates it with the per-point features (e_t). It then predicts per-step 3D displacements (dΔP_t:t+H) for the point set over the action horizon.
4. **VLA Backbone Integration:** The integration of the point track head is tailored to different VLA architectural patterns:
 - **OpenVLA-OFT (Backbone-centric VLAs):** For models like OpenVLA-OFT, which use an MLP regression head directly on action-query token embeddings, Pri4R sets z_t to these final-layer hidden states. This directly ties the dynamics-aware representation to the action prediction mechanism.
 - **π Series (Expert-style VLAs):** For models like the π series, which use a dedicated transformer action expert, Pri4R introduces a lightweight transformer *embedding module*. This module takes learnable query tokens and applies cross-attention over the VLM backbone's final-layer image and language tokens to produce the action-horizon embedding sequence z_t. This z_t is then processed by the point track head.

**B. Why 3D Point Tracks as Privileged Supervision?**
The choice of 3D point tracks is justified by their superior alignment with the requirements for learning world dynamics compared to other common predictive representations:
- **Temporally Dense:** They capture fine-grained world interaction by matching the action horizon (predicting per-step displacements).
- **Geometric:** They provide explicit metric 3D structure, promoting spatial awareness.
- **Spatially Sparse:** They focus on a compact, informative set of points, making the learning objective efficient without the redundancy of dense predictions like images or depth maps.
- **Aligned with Actions:** 3D point tracks exist in the same spatiotemporal metric space as robot actions, providing a naturally aligned supervisory signal for control.

**C. Construction of 3D Point Track Supervision:**
To obtain the privileged 4D supervision:
- **Simulation:** In simulated environments, ground-truth scene meshes are accessible. The authors initialize Np query points on object surfaces at the first frame, track their corresponding face indices and barycentric coordinates, and then retrieve their 3D locations at every subsequent timestep by rolling out the action sequence. This yields the sequence of 3D point sets {P_τ} for the entire demonstration window.
- **Real World:** For real-world data, an off-the-shelf 3D point tracking model is used to generate pseudo-labeled tracks. To ensure relevance, points are sampled with a bias towards foreground regions (robots and objects) using a segmentation model, while background points are sampled uniformly. Stable world coordinates are recovered due to a fixed camera setup.
- **Displacement Prediction:** Instead of directly predicting absolute positions, the point track head is supervised to predict *per-step 3D displacements* (Δp_τ,j = p_τ+1,j - p_τ,j) over the action horizon. This is temporally aligned with action prediction.

**D. Training:**
- **Combined Objective:** The total loss function combines the original VLA's action training objective (e.g., ℓ1 regression for OpenVLA-OFT, flow matching for π models) with an auxiliary ℓ1 loss on the predicted 3D point track displacements. A weighting factor, ω_pt, controls the balance between these two loss terms.
- **End-to-End Optimization:** The entire model, including the VLM backbone and the point track head (and embedding module for π series), is trained end-to-end.
- **Inference-Time Discard:** After training, the point track head and its associated embedding module are discarded. The original VLA architecture runs unchanged, benefiting from the refined internal representations without any added complexity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate Pri4R's ability to inject knowledge about world dynamics, its effectiveness across diverse tasks and architectures, and the impact of its design choices.

**A. Multitask Simulation Benchmarks:**
Pri4R was evaluated on two multi-task simulation benchmarks: LIBERO and RoboCasa.
- **LIBERO:** Pri4R consistently improved the average success rates of all tested VLA baselines (Diffusion Policy, Octo, DiT Policy, OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, π0, π0.5).
 - OpenVLA-OFT + Pri4R achieved a 96.3% average success rate, a **+3.6%** absolute improvement over baseline (92.7%).
 - Notable gains were observed on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite, where OpenVLA-OFT + Pri4R yielded a **+9.8%** absolute improvement (95.3% vs 85.5%), suggesting enhanced modeling of action-world interactions over longer horizons.
 - π0.5 + Pri4R showed a +1.4% average gain and a +3.8% gain on LIBERO-Long.
- **RoboCasa:** On the more complex RoboCasa benchmark, requiring robust execution under randomized scene configurations, Pri4R achieved even larger gains.
 - OpenVLA-OFT + Pri4R resulted in a **+13.2%** absolute increase in average success rate (46.3% vs 33.1%).
 - Significant improvements were seen across various task categories, including "Open/Close drawers" (+16.0%), "Twisting knobs" (+21.0%), "Turning levers" (+17.0%), and "Pressing buttons" (+30.7%).
 - π0.5 + Pri4R improved average success rate by +4.1%, with notable gains in "Open/Close drawers" (+17.5%) and "Twisting knobs" (+14.0%).
- **Training Dynamics:** Analysis of training dynamics showed that while Pri4R initially learns slower due to the added point track prediction objective, it then improves rapidly, reaching the baseline's peak performance 2.7x faster.

**B. Analysis of 3D Point Track Supervision:**
Ablation studies explored the critical characteristics of 3D point tracks.
- **Temporality & Spatiality:**
 - Using a "goal-only" objective (predicting only the terminal point set) yielded only a +0.7% improvement over the OpenVLA-OFT baseline on RoboCasa (33.8% vs 33.1%).
 - Using 2D point tracks (preserving temporal density but removing metric 3D structure) yielded a modest +3.9% improvement (37.0%).
 - **Conclusion:** Supervising with temporally dense and metrically grounded 3D tracks over the full horizon provided substantially larger gains (+13.2%), indicating that both temporality and spatiality are crucial.
- **Spatial Redundancy:**
 - Replacing 3D point track prediction with future depth map prediction (using VAE latent codes) improved performance by +8.3% (42.3%) but consistently underperformed Pri4R's 3D point tracks (+13.2%).
 - **Conclusion:** This gap is attributed to the spatial redundancy of depth maps and their lack of identity registration across time, making 3D point tracks a stronger signal for learning scene evolution.
- **Scene-Robot Interaction:**
 - Tracking only scene points yielded a +2.1% improvement (35.2%).
 - Tracking only robot points yielded a +10.7% improvement (43.8%).
 - **Conclusion:** Tracking both scene and robot points (world tracking) yielded the largest gain (+13.2%), suggesting that modeling robot-environment interaction is key.

**C. Ablations on Design Choices:**
- **Effect of P_t input:**
 - Appending the current point set P_t to the backbone (requiring 3D observations at test time) showed mixed results, improving some suites but degrading others (e.g., Pick-and-Place by -7.8%), attributed to perturbing pretrained VLM representations.
 - Adding point track supervision on top of P_t input to backbone still showed PnP degradation.
 - **Conclusion:** Pri4R's design of conditioning only the point track head on P_t (without P_t input to backbone or at test time) yielded consistent gains across all suites (+13.2% average on RoboCasa) and preserved PnP performance. This confirms the value of keeping the test-time interface clean.
- **Ablations on Embedding Module (for π series):**
 - Variants like a point expert or various learnable query tokens performed worse than the proposed embedding module.
 - **Conclusion:** The specific design of the lightweight transformer embedding module, taking learnable query tokens and cross-attending over backbone features to produce z_t, is important for effective learning in expert-style VLAs.
- **Ablations on Point Track Head Architecture:**
 - Testing alternatives for the point embedding module (PointNet, Point Transformer) and fusion module (Transformer) showed that heavier architectures sometimes underperformed.
 - **Conclusion:** The lightweight MLP design for both the point embedding and fusion modules within Pri4R achieved the best performance.
- **Ablation on ω_pt (tracking loss weight):** ω_pt = 1.0 was found to perform best among tested values (0.1, 1.0, 10.0), indicating minimal tuning is required.
- **Ablation on N_p (number of tracked points):** Using too few points (e.g., 256 or 512) degraded performance compared to the default 1024 points, suggesting a moderate point density is necessary.

**D. Real World Evaluation:**
Pri4R was evaluated on four real-world tasks requiring world-dynamics understanding using the OMY-F3M robot.
- **Tasks:** Pick-and-place over an obstacle, Pick-and-place into a bin (seen/unseen object placements), Pick the farthest object (randomized target), and Pick a moving object.
- **Results:** Pri4R consistently outperformed OpenVLA-OFT and π0.5 baselines across all real-world tasks.
 - For OpenVLA-OFT, Pri4R improved success rates by **+13.4%** on average (e.g., "Height" tasks: 96.7% vs 83.3%, "Spatial" tasks: 80.0% vs 60.0% for unseen, "Depth" tasks: 50.0% vs 0.0% for unseen-b, "Tracking" tasks: 66.7% vs 41.7% for unseen, 66.7% vs 50.0% for OOD).
 - For π0.5, Pri4R showed improvements, particularly in "Spatial" unseen (+29.1%), "Depth" seen (+29.1%), and "Tracking" OOD (+16.2%).
 - **Qualitative Observations:** The baseline models often exhibited collisions, incorrect grasp locations, and failures to adapt to dynamic changes. Pri4R, in contrast, demonstrated stronger spatiotemporal awareness, avoiding collisions, re-localizing targets, grasping objects from unseen locations, and adapting grasp plans to moving objects.
 - **Conclusion:** Pri4R effectively transfers spatiotemporal understanding learned through privileged supervision to real-world settings, improving robustness under distribution shifts in object placement and dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Pri4R represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in enhancing the physical intelligence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The primary significance lies in its ability to instill an *implicit understanding of world dynamics* within VLA models, a crucial capability for robust and generalizable robotic control that has often been a challenge for imitation learning-based approaches.

**Key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** Pri4R consistently improves task success rates across a diverse range of challenging manipulation tasks in both simulation (LIBERO, RoboCasa) and real-world environments. This enhanced performance, particularly in scenarios involving contact, obstacles, randomized object configurations, and dynamic targets, suggests that the model learns more robust and generalizable policies. The substantial gains on complex benchmarks like RoboCasa and real-world tasks requiring dynamic awareness highlight its practical utility.
2. **Implicit World Dynamics Learning with Zero Inference Overhead:** A core strength of Pri4R is that it achieves improved physical understanding *without altering the VLA's inference-time architecture, inputs, or computational cost*. This "privileged learning" paradigm means that the benefits of 4D geometric supervision are internalized into the VLM's shared representation during training, allowing the deployed policy to run with the same efficiency as the original VLA while demonstrating superior performance. This characteristic is critical for real-world applications where computational efficiency and latency are major concerns.
3. **Validation of 3D Point Tracks as Supervisory Signal:** The research systematically demonstrates that 3D point tracks are an effective and uniquely suitable supervisory signal for learning action-world dynamics. Ablation studies clearly show that the temporality, metric geometry, and focus on both robot and scene points in 3D tracks lead to superior performance compared to other forms of supervision (e.g., goal-only prediction, 2D tracks, dense depth maps). This insight can guide future research in designing effective supervisory signals for physically-aware robot learning.
4. **Broad Applicability to VLA Architectures:** Pri4R's framework is designed to be compatible with different state-of-the-art VLA architectures, as demonstrated by its successful integration with both backbone-centric (OpenVLA-OFT) and expert-style (π series) models. This broad applicability increases its potential for widespread adoption and integration into existing and future VLA pipelines.
5. **Accelerated Training Convergence:** The observation that Pri4R can reach the baseline's peak performance significantly faster (e.g., 2.7x faster in some cases) suggests potential benefits in terms of training efficiency, reducing the computational resources and time required to achieve high-performing policies.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Capable Robots:** By providing robots with a better understanding of how their actions affect the environment, Pri4R contributes to the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems that can operate reliably in complex, interactive scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes 3D point track supervision as a strong paradigm for learning world dynamics. This could inspire further research into other forms of privileged geometric supervision, more sophisticated point tracking mechanisms, or incorporating similar principles into large-scale pretraining of foundational robot models.
- **Bridging Simulation-to-Real Gap:** The successful transfer of learned spatiotemporal understanding from simulation to real-world tasks, even with pseudo-labeled real-world tracks, indicates that this approach can contribute to overcoming the simulation-to-real domain gap in robotics.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations and areas for future research:
- **Inference-Time Invariance:** While a strength for efficiency, the lack of any changes at inference time might limit ultimate performance. Future work could explore incorporating explicit geometric inputs or additional computation at test time if performance gains outweigh efficiency costs.
- **Fine-tuning Focus:** Pri4R was primarily evaluated in a fine-tuning setting on demonstration benchmarks. The authors hypothesize that integrating 3D point-track supervision during *large-scale pretraining regimes* (e.g., Embodiment X) could yield an even greater impact on learning action-world dynamics.
- **Scalability of 4D Data Generation:** While off-the-shelf 3D point trackers can provide real-world supervision, scaling the generation of high-quality 4D point track data for massive datasets remains an area of practical consideration.

Overall, Pri4R presents a principled and effective method for enhancing VLA models with critical physical awareness, paving the way for more robust, generalizable, and efficient robotic manipulation systems.
