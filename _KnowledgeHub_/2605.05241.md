# DexSim2Real: Foundation Model-Guided Sim-to-Real Transfer for Generalizable Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DexSim2Real is a zero-shot sim-to-real framework for dexterous manipulation that couples three foundation-model-driven stages: a VLM realism critic scoring rendered images to optimize domain-randomization distributions via CMA-ES, a bidirectional cross-attention visuo-tactile policy trained with PPO, and an LLM-decomposed progressive skill curriculum. Across six real tasks it reaches 78.2% average success with an 8.3% sim-to-real gap.

## Problem

- Domain randomization ranges are set manually or by costly trial-and-error, and uniform sampling wastes training on physically implausible configurations that degrade final policy quality.
- Prior LLM-guided sim-to-real work reasons only over text, never inspecting rendered frames against real reference images, so visual fidelity of the simulator goes unchecked.
- Foundation-model randomization, visuo-tactile fusion, and automatic curricula existed only in isolation; none were integrated into one zero-shot dexterous transfer pipeline.

## Method

- **FM-DR**: a ==VLM== scores rendered-versus-real image realism 1-10, and ==CMA-ES== optimizes a ==Gaussian-mixture== parameter distribution under an ==entropy bonus== that preserves randomization diversity.
- **TVCAP**: ==DINOv2== visual, ==1D-CNN== tactile, and ==MLP== proprioceptive encoders fused by ==bidirectional residual cross-attention==, feeding a ==transformer action-chunking== head trained with ==PPO==.
- **PSC**: an ==LLM== decomposes the language task into ordered sub-skills, a step-size difficulty scheduler promotes on an 0.8 success threshold, then sub-skills chain end-to-end.

## Results

- **78.2%** average real success over **six** tasks and **50** trials each, beating the best demonstration-trained baseline by **13.1** points and DeXtreme by **19.0** points, with zero real demonstrations.
- Sim-to-real gap averages **8.3%** versus **28.5%** for vanilla randomization and **19.2%** for active randomization; realism scores rise **4.2** to **7.8** with only **18%** entropy loss.
- Ablations: removing FM-DR costs **12.4** points, random scores under equal CMA-ES budget cost **11.1**, dropping tactile **8.1**, concatenation instead of cross-attention **5.8**, no curriculum **9.3**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Foundation models pay off as critics of the training environment itself, not only as policy backbones; optimizing the simulator is orthogonal to architecture choice.
> - The random-score control is the decisive ablation: it isolates genuine VLM signal from generic search, a check any model-in-the-loop optimization should report.
> - An explicit entropy term is what keeps realism optimization from collapsing randomization diversity, retaining robustness while discarding implausible parameter regions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous robotic manipulation, which involves complex interaction with objects using multi-fingered hands, is a persistent challenge in robotics. Reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in acquiring such skills within simulated environments. However, transferring these learned policies to physical robots is often hindered by the "sim-to-real gap," which refers to discrepancies in visual appearance, physical dynamics, and sensor characteristics between simulated and real-world environments.

Historically, **Domain Randomization (DR)** has been a prevalent paradigm for sim-to-real transfer. DR methods randomize simulation parameters during training to generate policies robust to domain shifts. However, these methods often face limitations, including the manual or trial-and-error-based tuning of randomization ranges and the potential for reduced policy performance due to training on an excessive number of unrealistic configurations. Active Domain Randomization (ADR) and approaches like DeXtreme have sought to address some of these limitations, with DeXtreme employing large-scale manual DR.

The recent emergence of **vision-language foundation models (VLMs)** and large language models (LLMs) has opened new avenues for robot learning. These models possess extensive semantic and visual priors. Prior research, such as Eureka, has shown LLMs can automate reward function design, and DrEureka extended this to LLM-guided generation of domain randomization distributions. Other works have explored LLM-based curriculum design (e.g., CurricuLLM, Plan-Seq-Learn) and cross-modal visuo-tactile fusion (e.g., Visuo-Tactile Transformers, 3D-ViTac, ViTacFormer). Some approaches, like RT-2 and π0, leverage foundation models as direct policy backbones for control.

This work contributes to the landscape by integrating these advancements. While previous research has explored foundation model applications for specific aspects of robot learning, the authors identify a gap in unifying foundation-model-guided domain randomization, multi-modal policy learning, and automatic curriculum generation into a single, integrated sim-to-real pipeline specifically for dexterous manipulation. The proposed system aims to provide complementary visual feedback for DR, adapt multi-modal policies for zero-shot sim-to-real without real demonstrations, and design curricula tailored to contact-rich tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the sim-to-real gap for dexterous manipulation by developing an integrated framework that leverages vision-language foundation models across multiple stages of the learning process. The motivation stems from several identified limitations in existing approaches:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Traditional Domain Randomization**: Existing DR methods often require extensive manual tuning of randomization ranges, which is time-consuming and expertise-dependent. Furthermore, randomizing over a broad, untargeted parameter space can lead to training on visually or physically unrealistic scenarios, potentially degrading the performance of the learned policies in the real world. The authors propose to overcome this by introducing a mechanism for automated, visually informed optimization of simulation parameters.
2. **Leveraging Foundation Models as Realism Critics**: The research hypothesizes that vision-language models (VLMs) can serve as effective "visual realism critics." By comparing rendered simulation images with real-world reference images, a VLM can quantitatively assess visual fidelity. This direct visual feedback, guided by a closed-loop optimization process, is intended to complement and potentially improve upon text-based LLM approaches for generating domain randomization parameters, which may lack direct visual grounding.
3. **Enhancing Multi-Modal Policy Learning for Zero-Shot Transfer**: Dexterous manipulation tasks are inherently contact-rich, making multi-modal sensing (visual and tactile) crucial. While previous work has explored visuo-tactile fusion, adapting these methods for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without requiring real-world demonstrations remains a challenge. The objective is to develop a policy architecture that effectively integrates visual and tactile information through attention mechanisms, enabling robust transfer from simulation to reality without real-world data collection.
4. **Automating Curriculum Generation for Complex Tasks**: Learning complex manipulation skills from scratch using RL can be inefficient. Curriculum learning strategies have been shown to improve training efficiency. The motivation here is to automate the decomposition of complex tasks into manageable sub-skills and to progressively schedule their difficulty. Leveraging LLMs for task decomposition and developing a tailored difficulty scheduler is intended to optimize the learning process for contact-rich dexterous tasks.
5. **Developing a Unified and Integrated System**: The overarching motivation is to integrate these individual components—foundation model-guided domain randomization, multi-modal policy learning, and automated curriculum generation—into a cohesive, end-to-end framework. The aim is to demonstrate that their combined application can lead to improved generalizability and significantly reduce the sim-to-real performance gap for dexterous manipulation tasks, without relying on real-world demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DexSim2Real framework is structured into three main components: Foundation Model-Guided Domain Randomization (FM-DR), a Tactile-Visual Cross-Attention Policy (TVCAP), and a Progressive Skill Curriculum (PSC). The system operates by taking a natural language task description and using these components to train a policy in simulation for zero-shot transfer to a real robot.

#### 3.1. Foundation Model-Guided Domain Randomization (FM-DR)

FM-DR aims to optimize simulation parameters to reduce the visual and physical gap with the real world.
- **Parameterization**: Simulation parameters ξ (e.g., lighting, texture, friction, mass, camera pose) are represented. Unlike traditional DR, which often uses uniform distributions, FM-DR learns an optimized distribution p\*(ξ).
- **Realism Scoring**: A Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4V (gpt-4-vision-preview), is employed as a visual realism critic.
 - Given a simulated image I\_s(ξ) generated with parameters ξ and a set of real-world reference images I\_r, the VLM provides a realism score s(ξ).
 - The VLM is prompted to rate visual similarity on a scale of 1–10 across dimensions such as lighting, texture, geometry, and physical plausibility. Prompt robustness was verified, showing minimal variation in downstream success rates and high Spearman ρ for VLM score rankings.
- **Distribution Optimization**:
 - The distribution p(ξ; θ) is parameterized as a mixture of Gaussians.
 - These parameters θ are optimized using CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) to maximize an objective function: E\_\[ξ~p(·;θ)\] \[s(ξ) + λ · H(p(·; θ))\] .
 - The term s(ξ) represents the VLM realism score. The entropy term H(p(·; θ)) encourages diversity in the parameter distribution, preventing it from collapsing into a single, highly realistic but narrow configuration. The trade-off parameter λ was set to 0.3 after a grid search.
 - Optimized parameters include friction (range 0.3–1.2), mass scaling (0.8–1.5), three lighting parameters, texture noise amplitude, and camera pose noise.
- **Iterative Refinement**: FM-DR proceeds in rounds. In each round, a set of M parameter configurations are sampled, corresponding images are rendered, the VLM provides realism scores, and CMA-ES updates the parameter distribution. The process typically converges within 10–15 rounds, each using 20 samples, requiring approximately 200–300 VLM queries per task.

#### 3.2. Tactile-Visual Cross-Attention Policy (TVCAP)

TVCAP is designed to process multi-modal observations and generate actions, adapted for zero-shot sim-to-real RL without requiring real-world demonstrations.
- **Observation Modalities**: The policy processes RGB images (o\_v), tactile readings (o\_t) from fingertip sensors, and proprioceptive state (o\_p) (joint angles, velocities, torques).
- **Modality Encoders**:
 - Each modality is encoded independently into a shared embedding dimension 'd'.
 - Visual encoder (f\_v): A ViT-based architecture initialized from a pre-trained DINOv2 backbone, outputting N\_v patch tokens (z\_v).
 - Tactile encoder (f\_t): A 1D-CNN, producing N\_t tokens (z\_t).
 - Proprioceptive encoder (f\_p): An MLP, producing a single token (z\_p).
- **Cross-Attention Fusion**: Instead of simple concatenation, bidirectional cross-attention with residual connections is used for dynamic information exchange.
 - The visual stream attends to tactile and proprioceptive signals: ˜z\_v = CrossAttn(z\_v, \[z\_t; z\_p\]) + z\_v.
 - The tactile stream attends to visual and proprioceptive signals: ˜z\_t = CrossAttn(z\_t, \[z\_v; z\_p\]) + z\_t.
 - This mechanism allows modalities to dynamically weight relevant information, particularly tactile signals during contact-rich phases.
- **Action Head**: The fused representation (z = \[˜z\_v; ˜z\_t; z\_p\]) is passed to a transformer decoder, which outputs a sequence of T future actions (a\_1:T), following an action chunking paradigm.
- **Training**: The policy is trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.

#### 3.3. Progressive Skill Curriculum (PSC)

PSC addresses the challenge of learning complex manipulation tasks by automatically decomposing them and scheduling training difficulty.
- **LLM-Based Task Decomposition**:
 - A large language model is prompted with a natural language description of the target manipulation task.
 - The LLM generates an ordered sequence of sub-skills (S = {s\_1..., s\_L}) along with their associated success criteria. For instance, "pour water from cup A to cup B" might be decomposed into grasping, lifting, positioning, tilting, and returning upright.
- **Automatic Difficulty Scheduling**:
 - For each sub-skill s\_l, a difficulty parameter δ\_l ∈ \[0, 1\] is defined. This parameter controls factors like the initial state distribution and tolerance thresholds, starting at δ\_l = 0 (easiest).
 - The difficulty progresses based on the sub-skill's success rate (SR). The parameter is updated as: δ\_l^(t+1) = min(1, δ\_l^(t) + α · I(SR\_l^(t) > γ)), where γ = 0.8 is the promotion threshold, and α = 0.1 is the step size.
- **Skill Chaining**: Once individual sub-skills achieve a high proficiency (SR > 0.9), they are chained together. This involves initializing subsequent sub-skills from the terminal state distribution of their predecessors. The entire sequence is then fine-tuned end-to-end using a composite reward function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DexSim2Real was evaluated through extensive real-world experiments on six dexterous manipulation tasks, with comparisons against various baselines and detailed ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Environment**: NVIDIA Isaac Sim with PhysX 5, operating at 120 Hz. Training was parallelized across 4096 environments using 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Real-World Hardware**: A Franka Emika Panda arm with an Allegro Hand (16-DoF), equipped with XELA tactile sensors (15 taxels per fingertip, four fingers). Visual observations were provided by two Intel RealSense D435 cameras (front and wrist-mounted).
- **Manipulation Tasks**: Six tasks of increasing complexity: Pick-Place, Stacking, Peg Insertion, In-Hand Rotation, Tool Use, and Pouring.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: Each task involved 50 real-world trials with randomized object poses, repeated across 3 random seeds. Success was independently judged by two human evaluators, blinded to the method identity, achieving an inter-rater κ of 0.94. Re-grasps were permitted within a 30-second time limit. Task-specific success criteria were defined (e.g., object within target zone for Pick-Place, ≥70% mass transferred for Pouring).
- **Baselines**:
 - **Zero-shot Sim-to-Real**: Vanilla DR, Active Domain Randomization (ADR), RAPS, DeXtreme, and DrEureka.
 - **Demonstration-based**: PerAct, RVT, and Act3D (each trained with 100 real demonstrations).
 - All sim-to-real methods used the same simulation environment, PPO training, and a 48 GPU-hour budget.

#### 4.2. Main Results

- **Overall Performance**: DexSim2Real achieved an average real-world success rate of 78.2% across the six tasks (Table 1). This result surpassed all baseline methods.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - DexSim2Real outperformed the best demonstration-based baseline (Act3D, 65.1%) by 13.1 percentage points, despite not requiring any real-world demonstrations.
 - It significantly outperformed zero-shot sim-to-real baselines, including DrEureka (65.1% average), by 13.1 percentage points. The improvements were notably pronounced in contact-rich tasks, such as In-Hand Rotation (+9.4% over DrEureka) and Tool Use (+14.4% over DrEureka).
 - The automated FM-DR approach demonstrated substantially better transfer performance than DeXtreme, which relies on massive-scale manual DR, showing an average improvement of 19.0%.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Gap Analysis

- DexSim2Real achieved an average sim-to-real gap of 8.3% (Table 2), defined as the absolute difference between simulation and real-world success rates.
- This gap was considerably smaller compared to Vanilla DR (28.5%) and ADR (19.2%), indicating that FM-DR effectively aligned the simulation distribution with real-world conditions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study (Table 3) revealed the contribution of each component:
- **FM-DR's Critical Role**: Replacing FM-DR with Vanilla DR resulted in the largest performance drop (−12.4%), indicating FM-DR as the most impactful component. Even replacing it with ADR led to an 8.9% performance decrease. Using random scores for VLM feedback with CMA-ES yielded only 67.1% success, confirming that the VLM’s visual realism signal provides meaningful guidance beyond a generic search.
- **Importance of Tactile Sensing**: Removing tactile input reduced average performance by 8.1%, with the most significant drops in contact-rich tasks like In-Hand Rotation (−14.3%) and Peg Insertion (−11.7%).
- **Cross-Attention vs. Concatenation**: Replacing cross-attention fusion with simple concatenation reduced performance by 5.8%, validating the benefit of dynamic modality weighting.
- **PSC's Impact on Learning**: Training without PSC resulted in a 9.3% lower final performance and slower convergence. Using a manual curriculum instead of LLM-based decomposition was less effective (−4.1%).
- **Entropy Term in FM-DR**: Removing the entropy term from the FM-DR objective led to a 6.6% drop in performance, highlighting its role in maintaining sufficient diversity.

#### 4.5. Training Efficiency

- Training curves (Figure 2) showed that DexSim2Real with PSC converged approximately 2 times faster than without PSC and 3 times faster than Vanilla DR, while achieving higher asymptotic performance.
- The FM-DR optimization introduced an overhead of approximately 2 GPU-hours, which was considered negligible compared to the 48 GPU-hours allocated for RL training.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Analysis of FM-DR

- Analysis of optimized parameter distributions indicated that FM-DR concentrated lighting parameters within realistic indoor ranges, adjusted friction coefficients to align more closely with real-world values (mean error reduced from 0.35 to 0.08), and selected texture distributions better representing real object materials.
- VLM realism scores improved from an average of 4.2/10 (initial uniform DR) to 7.8/10 after FM-DR optimization, while maintaining sufficient diversity (only an 18% reduction in entropy from uniform).

#### 4.7. Generalization to Unseen Objects

- DexSim2Real demonstrated strong generalization to 10 novel YCB objects not seen during training (Table 4). Pick-Place and In-Hand Rotation policies maintained 85.4% and 63.8% success rates, respectively, with modest performance drops from seen objects.
- In contrast, Vanilla DR exhibited a significantly larger generalization gap, suggesting that FM-DR contributes to more transferable representations.

#### 4.8. Computational Cost

- The FM-DR optimization added a one-time cost of 2 GPU-hours.
- The total pipeline (FM-DR + TVCAP training with PSC) required 50 GPU-hours on 4x A100, which is comparable to ADR (45 GPU-hours) and substantially less than methods requiring real-world data collection (Table 5).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DexSim2Real, an integrated framework that addresses the sim-to-real gap for dexterous manipulation by systematically leveraging foundation models.

**Significance:**

- **Integrated Approach to Sim-to-Real**: The work unifies three distinct components—foundation model-guided domain randomization, multi-modal policy learning, and automatic curriculum generation—into a coherent, zero-shot sim-to-real pipeline. This integration provides a comprehensive solution for transferring complex manipulation skills from simulation to reality without requiring real-world demonstrations.
- **Novelty in Domain Randomization**: The proposed Foundation Model-Guided Domain Randomization (FM-DR) advances the state of domain randomization by utilizing a Vision-Language Model as a direct visual realism critic. This provides a complementary approach to text-based LLM-guided DR methods (e.g., DrEureka) by offering direct visual feedback for optimizing simulation parameters, leading to more visually plausible and effective training environments.
- **Effective Multi-Modal Policy Learning**: The Tactile-Visual Cross-Attention Policy (TVCAP) demonstrates an effective method for dynamic fusion of visual and tactile information using cross-attention, adapted for zero-shot sim-to-real reinforcement learning. This is particularly significant for contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks where both modalities are crucial.
- **Automated Curriculum for Complex Skills**: The Progressive Skill Curriculum (PSC), which combines LLM-based task decomposition with a δ-based difficulty scheduler, streamlines the learning of complex, long-horizon tasks. This automation reduces the manual effort typically involved in designing effective curricula for reinforcement learning.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: The framework achieves a 78.2% average real-world success rate and significantly narrows the sim-to-real performance gap to 8.3%, outperforming existing zero-shot sim-to-real methods and even those requiring real-world demonstrations on challenging dexterous manipulation tasks.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Robot Deployment**: By substantially reducing the sim-to-real gap and the reliance on manual tuning or real-world data collection, DexSim2Real could accelerate the development and deployment of dexterous manipulation policies on physical robots. This has implications for industries requiring automated assembly, logistics, or advanced robotics.
- **Enhanced Generalizability**: The foundation model-guided approach to domain randomization, along with multi-modal sensing, contributes to policies that generalize more effectively to unseen objects and varied real-world conditions, making robots more adaptable.
- **Broader Role of Foundation Models in Robotics**: This research highlights the versatile utility of foundation models, not just as direct controllers or policy backbones, but also as intelligent critics and curriculum designers within the broader robot learning pipeline. This could inspire further exploration of foundation models for optimizing simulation, sensor fusion, and learning strategies.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning**: By automating aspects of domain randomization and curriculum design, the framework could lower the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners in developing and deploying advanced robotic manipulation skills.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations. The current FM-DR primarily optimizes visual and physics parameters, with tactile sim-to-real transfer still relying on noise randomization rather than VLM-guided optimization. This is identified as a key area for future work, suggesting the development of tactile-aware critics. The framework is currently restricted to rigid objects, and extending it to deformable objects and fluids would require new simulation capabilities. The VLM realism scoring, while effective, may be subject to biases from its training data and incurs computational cost from frequent queries, though the authors deem this cost negligible relative to RL training. Future work could explore strategies to reduce VLM query costs, such as caching scores or using smaller distilled models. Lastly, further research directions include investigating multi-task generalization across unrelated dexterous skills and extending TVCAP to bimanual manipulation scenarios.
