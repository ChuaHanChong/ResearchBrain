# RLBench: The Robot Learning Benchmark & Learning Environment

> [!summary] Summary
> RLBench, developed by the Dyson Robotics Lab at Imperial College London, is a large-scale, simulation-based benchmark providing 100 visually-guided manipulation tasks and infinite expert demonstrations. This platform aims to standardize robot learning research, accelerate progress in few-shot learning, and provide a unified environment for comparing diverse robotic algorithms.

## Problem

- The robot learning field lacks standardized, diverse, and scalable benchmarks, making cross-study comparisons and reproducibility difficult.
- Existing reinforcement learning benchmarks often feature 'toy tasks' that do not translate well to complex, real-world robotic manipulation problems.
- There is a scarcity of benchmarks specifically designed for few-shot learning in robotics that encourage truly generalizable algorithms.

## Method

- **RLBench** is a simulation-based environment built on ==V-REP (CoppeliaSim)== and ==PyRep==, featuring a Franka Emika Panda robot arm.
- It organizes tasks into a hierarchical structure of ==Task==, ==Variation==, and ==Episode== to facilitate diverse problem instances and generalization studies.
- The platform offers an open-source task building tool that enables users to easily create and validate new tasks, ensuring extensibility.

## Results

- **RLBench** successfully implements **100** unique, hand-designed visually-guided manipulation tasks of varying complexity.
- The system can generate an 'infinite' supply of expert demonstrations for all tasks and their variations using ==waypoint-based motion planning==.
- It provides a comprehensive suite of observations, including RGB, depth, segmentation masks from multiple cameras, and detailed proprioceptive data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automated, waypoint-based motion planning enables the generation of an infinite supply of expert demonstrations, overcoming limitations of human data collection.
> - A hierarchical task structure (Task, Variation, Episode) allows for nuanced evaluation of generalization capabilities in robot learning.
> - Designing a 'one-size-fits-all' benchmark can effectively compare both traditional modular robotics methods and modern end-to-end deep learning approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of RLBench is positioned as a direct response to several critical shortcomings in the existing robot learning research landscape. The authors argue that while deep learning and large-scale data have revolutionized fields like computer vision, their application to robotics, particularly visually-guided manipulation, is hampered by a lack of standardized, diverse, and scalable benchmarks.

Existing continuous-control reinforcement learning (RL) benchmarks, such as OpenAI Gym and DeepMind Control Suite, primarily feature "toy tasks" that, while useful for algorithm development, often fail to translate to complex, real-world robotic problems. Researchers are frequently forced to create custom tasks for evaluation, leading to difficulties in reproducibility and comparison across different studies. This ad-hoc task creation can also unintentionally bias results by tailoring tasks to specific algorithms.

Furthermore, the paper highlights a significant gap in benchmarks for specific areas like few-shot learning in robotics. Previous works in this domain often define "tasks" too narrowly (e.g., "place a peach into a red bowl" being a different task from "placing an apple in to a green bowl"), preventing the development of truly generalizable algorithms that can leverage knowledge across diverse scenarios. There's also a recognized need for benchmarks that can accommodate both traditional, modular robotics approaches (e.g., explicit perception, planning) and modern end-to-end deep learning methods on a level playing field.

Manipulation-focused benchmarks also have their limitations. Many existing benchmarks, like the Amazon Robotics Challenge (ARC) or ACRV Picking Benchmark, focus on specific sub-problems (e.g., picking and stowing) or are difficult to reproduce. Other imitation learning datasets, such as RoboTurk or Simitate, are limited in task diversity or scalability due to reliance on crowd-sourcing or real-world data collection, which is time-consuming. Similarly, benchmarks for sub-problems like perception (e.g., ImageNet, COCO) or grasping (OpenGrasp, VisGraB) do not evaluate the complete robotic pipeline.

RLBench aims to fill these voids by providing a comprehensive, scalable, and reproducible environment that unifies various robot learning paradigms. By offering 100 unique tasks, an "infinite" supply of demonstrations, diverse sensor modalities, and an easy-to-use task creation tool, it seeks to become a standard platform for evaluating algorithms across reinforcement learning, imitation learning, multi-task learning, few-shot learning, geometric computer vision (including SLAM), and even sim-to-real transfer. This positions RLBench as a crucial infrastructure for advancing the field towards more general, adaptable, and intelligent robotic systems capable of performing complex tasks in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RLBench stems from the recognized need for a "one-size-fits-all" benchmark that can support the burgeoning field of data-driven robot learning while also allowing comparison with classical robotics methods. The authors pinpoint several problems in the current landscape: the lack of standardized comparisons for manipulation methods, the "toy" nature of existing RL benchmarks that don't scale to real-world problems, and the limited diversity of tasks in few-shot learning research.

To address these challenges, RLBench articulates three key objectives:

1. **Provide a benchmark and learning environment for both 'robot learning' and 'traditional' methods:** This objective aims to create a neutral ground where algorithms from different paradigms—ranging from fully end-to-end deep learning to modular systems incorporating object recognition, state estimation, and planning—can be rigorously compared. The diversity of tasks and available observation modalities (both visual and proprioceptive) are designed to facilitate this broad applicability.
2. **Provide a large-scale few-shot challenge:** Recognizing the human ability to quickly adapt to new tasks from minimal demonstrations, the authors propose a dedicated challenge. This involves training systems on a large set of known tasks and then evaluating their ability to perform *unseen* tasks given only a small number (K) of demonstrations. This objective directly addresses the current limitations of narrow task distributions in existing few-shot robotics research, pushing for truly general algorithms that can leverage knowledge from prior experiences.
3. **Provide a set of tools to allow easy task creation:** To ensure the long-term growth and relevance of the benchmark, RLBench offers open-source tools that simplify the process of designing and integrating new tasks. This extensibility is crucial for the benchmark to evolve with research needs and remain challenging over time, encouraging community contributions to expand its scope.

In essence, RLBench is motivated by the desire to accelerate progress in robot manipulation by providing a diverse, reproducible, scalable, and extensible platform that pushes the boundaries of generalization, particularly in the challenging few-shot learning paradigm, bridging the gap between simulated research and real-world applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLBench is meticulously designed as an ambitious, large-scale benchmark and learning environment built around a **V-REP** (now CoppeliaSim) and **PyRep** interface. PyRep, a toolkit for robot learning research developed by some of the same authors, enhances V-REP's capabilities for speed, rendering, and flexible API for robot control.

**A. Scene and Observations:**
The core simulation scene remains constant across all tasks, featuring a Franka Emika Panda 7 DoF robotic arm affixed to a wooden table, under controlled lighting. The environment provides a rich set of observations:
- **Visual Observations:** From an over-the-shoulder stereo camera and an eye-in-hand monocular camera, providing RGB, depth, and segmentation masks. This multi-view and pixel-level information is crucial for vision-guided manipulation.
- **Proprioceptive Observations:** Including joint angles, velocities, torques, and end-effector pose, offering detailed robot state information.
All tasks begin with the assumption that no objects are pre-held, forcing agents to first grasp necessary tools or objects, increasing task realism and complexity.

**B. Task Structure: Tasks, Variations & Episodes:**
RLBench introduces a hierarchical structure to organize its content:
- **Task:** The highest level, representing a broad objective (e.g., "stack blocks").
- **Variation:** Each task can have multiple variations. For instance, "pick up the apple" and "pick up the banana" might be variations of a single "pick up X" task. Variations usually change target objects, colors, or minor task parameters, aiding in developing algorithms that can generalize within a task family. Each variation comes with textual descriptions, useful for NLP or HRI research.
- **Episode:** An infinite number of episodes can be drawn from each variation. Episodes involve changing the positions and configurations of objects within a variation, providing diverse instances of the same problem.
This structure facilitates the generation of vast amounts of unique data while maintaining logical groupings of similar problems.

**C. Environment API:**
Users interact with RLBench through an `Environment` class, which spawns `TaskEnvironment` instances. The API mirrors typical RL setups, providing `reset` and `step` functions. A crucial design choice is the **sparse reward of +1** given only upon task completion, pushing algorithms to learn complex behaviors without dense supervision. A wide array of action spaces is supported, including absolute or delta joint velocities, positions, torques, and end-effector velocities or poses, offering flexibility for different control strategies.

**D. Demonstration Generation:**
A unique and significant feature of RLBench is its ability to provide an **infinite supply of expert demonstrations**. This is achieved not through human teleoperation or crowd-sourcing, but by leveraging waypoint-based motion planning. During task creation, a series of waypoints are defined for successful task completion. The **Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL)** is then used to generate collision-free trajectories (expert algorithm $\pi^*$) that connect these waypoints, producing high-quality, reproducible demonstrations for each task and variation. This automated generation scales efficiently, unlike human-provided data.

**E. Task Builder Tool:**
To ensure extensibility and allow community contributions, RLBench provides a **task building tool**. This interface allows users to easily create new tasks by defining a V-REP model file (.ttm) for scene elements and a Python (.py) file for wiring scene objects, applying variations, defining success criteria, and implementing complex task behaviors. The Python interface uses PyRep for scene manipulation and allows registering graspable objects and success conditions. A **task validation tool** is also provided to ensure that newly designed tasks are solvable by the motion planner and generate demonstrations reliably, before being submitted to the public repository. This streamlined process encourages the continuous expansion of the benchmark's task repertoire.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a benchmark and environment paper, RLBench's "findings" are the successful creation and characterization of its features and capabilities, rather than experimental results *using* the benchmark. The key outcomes and results presented are:

- **100 Completely Unique, Hand-Designed Tasks:** The paper successfully presents a large-scale collection of 100 distinct visually-guided manipulation tasks. These tasks span a wide range of difficulty, from simple target reaching and door opening to complex multi-stage operations like emptying a dishwasher (opening, sliding tray, grasping plate, lifting). This unprecedented diversity aims to push the boundaries of robotic generalization.
- **Infinite Supply of Demonstrations:** A cornerstone achievement is the ability to generate an "infinite" number of expert demonstrations for each task and its variations. This is facilitated by the waypoint-based motion planning approach, circumventing the limitations and costs associated with human data collection or real-world experimentation. These demonstrations are invaluable for imitation learning, reinforcement learning with demonstrations, and meta-learning approaches.
- **Rich Sensor Modalities:** RLBench provides a comprehensive suite of observations, including RGB, depth, and segmentation masks from both an over-the-shoulder stereo camera and an eye-in-hand monocular camera, alongside proprioceptive robot data (joint angles, velocities, torques, end-effector pose). This rich data stream supports research requiring diverse sensory inputs and allows for exploration of different observation spaces (e.g., partial observability).
- **Structured Task Hierarchy:** The introduction of "Task," "Variation," and "Episode" concepts provides a clear and flexible framework for organizing the diverse set of problems. This hierarchy enables researchers to study different levels of generalization, from solving new instances of the same problem (new episodes) to adapting to closely related problems (new variations) or entirely novel tasks.
- **Scalable and Extensible Task Creation Tools:** The provision of open-source tools built on V-REP and PyRep simplifies the development and validation of new tasks. This ease of extensibility is a critical feature, as it allows the community to continuously expand the benchmark's size and scope, ensuring its long-term relevance and challenge.
- **Proposed Large-Scale Few-Shot Challenge (v1.0):** The paper formally proposes the "RLBench Few-Shot Challenge," addressing a significant gap in the literature. This challenge defines a clear protocol for evaluating few-shot learning methods in robotics: systems are given K demonstrations of N *unseen* tasks and evaluated on their ability to perform these tasks in new configurations. This provides a standardized benchmark for an increasingly important research area.
- **Characterization of Task Complexity:** The paper provides insights into the tasks, such as the frequency of words in variation descriptions (indicating diversity in objectives) and the average length of demonstrations (showing tasks ranging from 100 to 1000 timesteps, indicative of long-horizon tasks).

In essence, RLBench's main "results" are the successful implementation of a robust, diverse, and scalable simulated environment that directly addresses the current limitations in robot learning benchmarks, setting the stage for significant future research advancements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RLBench represents a significant contribution to the robot learning community, with the potential to profoundly impact several research areas:

- **Standardization and Reproducibility:** By offering a common set of 100 diverse and reproducible tasks in a simulated environment, RLBench provides a much-needed standard for comparing different robot manipulation algorithms. This will reduce the current fragmentation where researchers often create bespoke tasks, making cross-study comparisons difficult. This standardization will foster more rigorous evaluation and accelerate progress.

- **Accelerating Few-Shot Learning Research:** The paper's explicit proposal of the "RLBench Few-Shot Challenge" is particularly impactful. By providing a diverse set of tasks and a clear evaluation protocol for the K-shot setting on unseen tasks, it directly addresses a critical gap. This challenge will push the development of truly generalizable robot learning algorithms that can learn new skills rapidly from minimal demonstrations, mirroring human learning capabilities. This is vital for practical robot deployment in varied environments.

- **Driving Progress in Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RLBench's visually-guided, real-world inspired tasks offer a more challenging and realistic alternative to existing toy RL benchmarks. The availability of infinite demonstrations can facilitate research in bootstrapping RL policies, reducing sample complexity, and exploring new RL algorithms for long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks. The eye-in-hand camera also opens avenues for research in partial observability.

- **Advancing Imitation Learning (IL):** With its infinite, motion-planned expert demonstrations, RLBench provides an unparalleled dataset for imitation learning algorithms. Researchers can generate as much data as needed, allowing for the exploration of data-hungry deep imitation learning methods and comparison across a broad range of tasks, unlike datasets constrained by human data collection.

- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While RLBench is a simulated environment, its design prioritizes features relevant for sim-to-real transfer research, such as a realistic robot model, quality graphics with lighting and shadows, and a domain randomization rendering option. The ability to easily swap out the robot model and create new tasks with the task builder allows researchers to rigorously test and compare sim-to-real methods on a standard set of problems.

- **Enabling Multi-task Learning:** The breadth of 100 unique tasks makes RLBench an ideal platform for multi-task learning, where agents learn to perform multiple tasks simultaneously. This can provide valuable insights into developing unified policies and understanding how knowledge can be shared across diverse robotic skills.

- **Bridging SLAM and Manipulation:** The paper highlights the potential for RLBench to foster research at the intersection of SLAM and manipulation. By providing realistic visual observations, it can serve as a testbed for developing "task-based SLAM" systems, exploring how robotic agents can use environmental maps to achieve manipulation goals, and investigating optimal map representations and accuracy requirements for interactive tasks.

In summary, RLBench is positioned as a foundational infrastructure that can unite diverse research strands in robot learning. Its scale, diversity, reproducibility, and extensibility aim to accelerate the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and general-purpose robotic systems, ultimately pushing towards robots that can effectively operate in complex, real-world environments. The proposed challenges, particularly in few-shot learning, are poised to become a key benchmark for the community, defining the cutting edge of what robots can learn to do.
