# MotionDirector: Motion Customization of Text-to-Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionDirector enables motion customization in text-to-video diffusion models by decoupling motion and appearance learning. This method allows users to teach models specific motion concepts from reference videos and then generate diverse videos where various subjects perform that learned motion, demonstrated by superior motion fidelity and appearance diversity compared to prior approaches.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-video diffusion models struggle to generate videos with specific motion patterns while maintaining appearance diversity, as motion and appearance are entangled during training.
- Traditional fine-tuning methods for video models couple motion patterns with visual characteristics, limiting generalization to new subjects (e.g., a monkey performing a golf swing learned from human videos).
- Current controllable generation methods often rely on signals like depth maps or pose sequences, which inherently link motion to appearance constraints.

## Method

- **MotionDirector** introduces a dual-path **LoRA** architecture, separating appearance learning (Spatial Path with spatial **LoRAs** on single frames) from motion learning (Temporal Path with temporal **LoRAs** on multiple frames).
- The architecture crucialy shares the exact same spatial **LoRA** weights between the two paths, forcing temporal **LoRAs** to focus purely on motion by handling appearance through shared spatial components.
- An appearance-debiased temporal loss function is introduced to remove appearance-driven shifts from latent space during motion learning, ensuring a stronger focus on temporal relationships.

## Results

- MotionDirector significantly outperforms baselines in human preference for motion fidelity (75% preferred over base ModelScope, 76.47% over base ZeroScope).
- The method preserves or slightly improves appearance diversity (54.84% preference over base ModelScope) compared to coupled tuning methods that severely degrade diversity (23.08% preference).
- It achieves high efficiency, requiring only 21 million trainable parameters (vs. 1.8 billion in foundation models) with short training times (8-20 minutes) and low VRAM (14GB).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Motion and appearance information in video generation can be effectively decoupled by using distinct learning paths and specialized loss functions.
> - Training spatial adapters on single frames prevents motion information capture, while sharing these adapters with temporal paths forces temporal learning to focus purely on motion dynamics.
> - An appearance-debiased temporal loss, which centers noise vectors relative to an anchor, further enhances decoupling by removing appearance-driven shifts in latent space.
