# MotionDirector: Motion Customization of Text-to-Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionDirector enables motion customization in text-to-video diffusion models by decoupling motion and appearance learning. This method allows users to teach models specific motion concepts from reference videos and then generate diverse videos where various subjects perform that learned motion, demonstrated by superior motion fidelity and appearance diversity compared to prior approaches.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-video diffusion models struggle to generate videos with specific motion patterns while maintaining appearance diversity, as motion and appearance are entangled during training.
- Traditional fine-tuning methods for video models couple motion patterns with visual characteristics, limiting generalization to new subjects (e.g., a monkey performing a golf swing learned from human videos).
- Current controllable generation methods often rely on signals like depth maps or pose sequences, which inherently link motion to appearance constraints.

## Method

- **MotionDirector** introduces a dual-path **LoRA** architecture, separating appearance learning (Spatial Path with spatial **LoRAs** on single frames) from motion learning (Temporal Path with temporal **LoRAs** on multiple frames).
- The architecture crucialy shares the exact same spatial **LoRA** weights between the two paths, forcing temporal **LoRAs** to focus purely on motion by handling appearance through shared spatial components.
- An appearance-debiased temporal loss function is introduced to remove appearance-driven shifts from latent space during motion learning, ensuring a stronger focus on temporal relationships.

## Results

- MotionDirector significantly outperforms baselines in human preference for motion fidelity (75% preferred over base ModelScope, 76.47% over base ZeroScope).
- The method preserves or slightly improves appearance diversity (54.84% preference over base ModelScope) compared to coupled tuning methods that severely degrade diversity (23.08% preference).
- It achieves high efficiency, requiring only 21 million trainable parameters (vs. 1.8 billion in foundation models) with short training times (8-20 minutes) and low VRAM (14GB).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Motion and appearance information in video generation can be effectively decoupled by using distinct learning paths and specialized loss functions.
> - Training spatial adapters on single frames prevents motion information capture, while sharing these adapters with temporal paths forces temporal learning to focus purely on motion dynamics.
> - An appearance-debiased temporal loss, which centers noise vectors relative to an anchor, further enhances decoupling by removing appearance-driven shifts in latent space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Text-to-video generation has emerged as one of the most exciting frontiers in generative AI, with diffusion models achieving remarkable progress in creating high-quality videos from textual descriptions. However, while current models excel at generating diverse content, they struggle with a fundamental limitation: users cannot easily teach these models to generate videos with specific motion patterns while maintaining appearance diversity.

MotionDirector addresses this gap by introducing a method for motion customization in text-to-video diffusion models. The key innovation lies in decoupling motion learning from appearance, allowing users to teach a model a specific motion concept (such as "a bear lifting weights" or "a car turning left") and then generate videos where any subject performs that motion in diverse contexts.

### 2. Problem Definition and Motivation

The core challenge in video generation lies in the entanglement of motion and appearance information. When existing methods attempt to customize video models using traditional fine-tuning approaches, they inevitably learn both the motion patterns and the visual characteristics of the training data together. This coupling severely limits generalization - a model trained on videos of humans playing golf cannot easily generate a monkey playing golf with the same motion dynamics.

Current approaches fall into three main categories, each with significant limitations:

1. **Appearance Customization Methods**: Techniques like DreamBooth and LoRA adaptations focus on customizing visual elements but don't address motion-specific control.

2. **Controllable Generation Methods**: These use control signals like depth maps or pose sequences to guide motion, but these signals inherently couple motion with appearance constraints - a depth map dictates both movement and object shape.

3. **Direct Fine-tuning Approaches**: Methods like Tune-A-Video can learn specific video patterns but suffer from the motion-appearance coupling problem, limiting their ability to generalize learned motions to new subjects.

MotionDirector formally defines the **Motion Customization** task: adapting pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models to generate videos exhibiting specific motion concepts while preserving the model's ability to generate diverse appearances. This represents a new paradigm that separates "how something moves" from "what it looks like."

### 3. Methodology

MotionDirector's approach centers on a dual-path architecture that explicitly separates appearance and motion learning through carefully designed Low-Rank Adaptations (LoRAs) and a specialized loss function.

#### 3.1. Dual-Path LoRA Architecture

The method builds upon standard video diffusion models that use 3D U-Nets for denoising latent codes guided by text prompts. The architecture introduces two distinct learning paths:

**Spatial Path (Appearance Learning)**:
- Injects unique spatial LoRAs into spatial self-attention and feed-forward layers
- Trains exclusively on single randomly sampled frames from videos
- Explicitly prevents motion information capture by avoiding temporal relationships
- Uses standard noise prediction loss: $L_{spatial} = \mathbb{E}_{z_0, \epsilon, t}[\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(z_t, t, \tau_\theta(y))\|^2]$

**Temporal Path (Motion Learning)**:
- Injects temporal LoRAs into temporal transformer layers
- Crucially shares the exact same spatial LoRA weights learned from the spatial path
- Forces temporal LoRAs to focus purely on motion by handling appearance through shared spatial components
- Trains on multiple frames to capture temporal dynamics

#### 3.2. Appearance-Debiased Temporal Loss

To further enhance motion-appearance decoupling, the authors introduce an innovative loss function that removes appearance bias from temporal learning:

The key insight comes from analyzing latent codes: motion affects the connectivity structure between frames, while appearance influences the distances between latent code sets. The debiasing function:

$$\phi(\epsilon_i) = \sqrt{\beta^2+1}\epsilon_i - \beta\epsilon_{anchor}$$

This operation centers noise vectors relative to a randomly sampled anchor, effectively removing appearance-driven shifts and allowing the loss to focus on temporal relationships. The final temporal loss combines standard and debiased components:

$$L_{temporal} = L_{org-temp} + L_{ad-temp}$$

### 4. Experimental Setup and Results

The effectiveness of MotionDirector is demonstrated through comprehensive experiments on two distinct benchmarks with both automatic metrics and human evaluations.

#### 4.1. Benchmarks and Datasets

**UCF Sports Action Dataset (Motion Customization on Multiple Videos)**:
- 95 videos covering 12 human motion types (golf, weightlifting, etc.)
- 72 diverse text prompts testing appearance generalization
- Example: Learning "playing golf" from human videos, then generating "a monkey is playing golf"

**LOVEU-TGVE-2023 Benchmark (Motion Customization on Single Video)**:
- 76 videos with original and significantly modified prompts
- Tests single-video motion learning and generalization

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

The results demonstrate MotionDirector's superior performance across key metrics:

**Motion Fidelity**: MotionDirector significantly outperforms all baselines in human preference evaluations. For ModelScope, it's preferred 75% vs. 25% over the base model; for ZeroScope, 76.47% vs. 23.53%.

**Appearance Diversity**: While coupled tuning methods severely degrade appearance diversity (only 23.08% preference vs. base model), MotionDirector preserves or slightly improves diversity (54.84% vs. 45.16% preference over base ModelScope).

**Efficiency**: The method requires only 21 million total parameters (9M spatial + 12M temporal LoRAs) compared to 1.8 billion in the foundation model, with training times of just 8-20 minutes and 14GB VRAM requirements.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparisons

The visual results clearly demonstrate MotionDirector's advantages:
- Tune-A-Video fails to generate diverse appearances with learned motions
- Controllable generation methods struggle with appearance generalization
- MotionDirector successfully maintains motion fidelity while enabling appearance diversity

### 5. Applications and Capabilities

MotionDirector enables several powerful applications that were previously difficult or impossible:

#### 5.1. Video Mixing

The decoupled architecture allows for creative combinations:
- Motion from one video + Appearance from another video
- Custom motion patterns applied to any subject described in text
- Animation of static images using learned motion concepts

#### 5.2. Motion Concept Learning

The method can learn abstract motion concepts like:
- Complex camera movements (surround shots, drone flyovers)
- Specific action patterns (weightlifting, golf swings)
- Object-specific motions (car turning patterns)

#### 5.3. Creative Content Generation

MotionDirector opens new possibilities for content creators:
- **Film and Animation**: Generate specific camera movements or character actions
- **Advertising**: Create consistent brand-specific motion patterns across different products
- **Education**: Demonstrate concepts through motion while varying visual context
- **Entertainment**: Generate imaginative scenarios with realistic motion patterns

### 6. Significance and Impact

MotionDirector represents a significant advancement in video generation technology with several important implications:

**Technical Innovation**: The dual-path architecture and appearance-debiased loss represent genuine innovations in disentangling complex video representations. This work establishes new architectural principles for handling multi-modal temporal data.

**Practical Utility**: By making motion customization computationally efficient and accessible, MotionDirector democratizes advanced video generation capabilities. The low resource requirements enable widespread adoption.

**Research Direction**: The formal definition of Motion Customization as a distinct task opens a new research area. The work provides clear baselines and evaluation frameworks for future investigations.

**Creative Applications**: The ability to separate motion from appearance fundamentally changes how users can interact with video generation systems, enabling more nuanced creative control.

**Limitations and Future Work**: The authors acknowledge current limitations with complex multi-agent motions, pointing toward clear directions for future research in hierarchical motion control and multi-subject coordination.

The success of MotionDirector in achieving true motion-appearance decoupling marks a crucial step toward more controllable and versatile video generation systems, with implications extending beyond entertainment to education, simulation, and scientific visualization applications.
