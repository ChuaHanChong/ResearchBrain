# VT-Refine: Learning Bimanual Assembly with Visuo-Tactile Feedback via Simulation Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel real-to-sim-to-real framework named VT-Refine enables robots to perform precise, contact-rich bimanual assembly tasks by integrating visuo-tactile feedback and simulation fine-tuning. This approach, which uses a custom piezoresistive tactile sensor and point-cloud representations, improves real-world success rates for visuo-tactile policies by approximately 40% over vision-only methods, facilitating nuanced adjustments crucial for tight-fit insertions.

## Problem

- Behavioral cloning from human demonstrations struggles with the precision and exploratory behaviors required for complex, contact-rich bimanual assembly tasks.
- Integrating tactile feedback for robotic manipulation faces significant challenges in accurate simulation and robust transfer from simulation to the real world.
- Scaling robot learning for dexterous manipulation is hindered by the high cost and time investment for collecting sufficient real-world data.

## Method

- A real-to-sim-to-real framework that pre-trains diffusion policies on limited real-world demonstrations and then fine-tunes them using reinforcement learning in a parallelized simulation environment.
- Development of FlexiTac, a custom piezoresistive tactile sensor designed for sim-to-real transfer, providing normal force readings, paired with a GPU-accelerated viscoelastic tactile simulation model (TacSL in Isaac Gym).
- A unified point-cloud representation that integrates visual, tactile (sensor unit positions and forces), and proprioceptive data for robust multimodal policy learning and transfer.

## Results

- RL fine-tuning drastically improved real-world success rates, with visuo-tactile policies showing approximately a 40% increase compared to vision-only policies, demonstrating the learned precision.
- Visuo-tactile policies consistently achieved higher performance and precision after fine-tuning (e.g., up to 0.98 success rate in simulation) compared to vision-only approaches across various bimanual assembly tasks.
- The framework successfully demonstrated robust real-to-sim-to-real transfer, with policies trained from limited real data and refined in simulation exhibiting strong performance on physical robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning fine-tuning in simulation is critical for acquiring the precise micro-adjustments and exploratory 'wiggle-and-dock' behaviors necessary for tight-fit assembly, which are often absent in human demonstrations.
> - Effective integration of tactile feedback with vision is indispensable for high-precision, contact-rich tasks, enabling robots to make fine adjustments even when visual cues are occluded or insufficient.
> - Designing tactile sensors and their simulation models to focus on easily transferable modalities (e.g., normal forces only) with robust representations (e.g., point clouds) significantly reduces the sim-to-real domain gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, driven by breakthroughs in machine learning, particularly deep learning and reinforcement learning. A significant thrust in this area is enabling robots to perform complex, dexterous manipulation tasks that resemble human capabilities. Among these, bimanual assembly tasks, such as fitting a plug into a socket or assembling mechanical connectors, represent a pinnacle of robotic dexterity, requiring precise coordination, fine motor skills, and robust perception.

Historically, learning such tasks has relied heavily on human demonstrations (imitation learning or behavioral cloning). However, as highlighted by the authors, this approach faces two major limitations for high-precision, contact-rich bimanual assembly:
1. **Data Scarcity and Suboptimality:** Collecting a sufficient quantity and diversity of human demonstrations for precise, contact-rich tasks is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. Human demonstrations often lack the exploratory behaviors (e.g., iterative adjustments) critical for overcoming subtle misalignments or unexpected contact forces.
2. **Lack of Tactile Feedback:** Many existing teleoperation interfaces for data collection do not provide haptic or tactile feedback to the human operator, leading to demonstrations that underutilize tactile information crucial for fine manipulation, especially when visual cues are occluded or insufficient.

Simultaneously, the "sim-to-real" paradigm has emerged as a promising avenue to scale robot learning, where policies are trained in simulation and then transferred to the real world. While successful for visual-only tasks or those with limited reliance on physical contact, extending sim-to-real to tactile-rich tasks, especially bimanual ones, remains a significant challenge. Vision-based tactile sensors (e.g., GelSight) offer high-resolution data but are difficult to simulate accurately and transfer robustly due to their reliance on internal illumination and complex deformation patterns.

This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a novel "real-to-sim-to-real" framework that specifically tackles precise, contact-rich bimanual assembly using visuo-tactile feedback. It leverages recent advancements in diffusion policies for robust imitation learning from limited data and combines it with large-scale reinforcement learning in a meticulously designed tactile simulation environment. By focusing on a "sim-to-real friendly" piezoresistive tactile sensor and a point-cloud representation, VT-Refine attempts to bridge the critical domain gap that has hindered prior efforts in tactile-enabled sim-to-real transfer for complex bimanual tasks. The work integrates high-fidelity simulation and learning architectures, positioning it at the forefront of robust, generalizable robotic assembly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the VT-Refine project is to enable robots to perform precise, contact-rich bimanual assembly tasks effectively by leveraging both visual and tactile feedback. The authors are motivated by the current limitations in robotic capabilities for such tasks, particularly in contrast to human dexterity.

The key specific objectives and motivations are:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Behavioral Cloning for Precision Tasks:** While diffusion policies show promise in learning from limited demonstrations, they often fall short for high-precision, contact-rich tasks due to the suboptimality and lack of exploratory behaviors in human demonstrations. The authors aim to go beyond mere imitation by refining these policies.
2. **Integrate and Scale Visuo-Tactile Learning:** Precise assembly heavily relies on tactile feedback, especially when visual cues are occluded or lack the necessary resolution. The motivation is to effectively incorporate tactile sensing into bimanual policies and scale their learning process, which has been difficult due to the challenges of simulating tactile feedback accurately and transferring it to the real world.
3. **Enable Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer for Tactile Sensing:** Current tactile sensors, especially vision-based ones, pose significant hurdles for accurate simulation and sim-to-real transfer, leading to a large domain gap. A core motivation is to develop a tactile sensing and simulation approach that minimizes this gap, allowing for reliable transfer of policies trained in simulation to real robots.
4. **Develop a Cost-Effective and Scalable Policy Learning Framework:** Real-world data collection for complex tasks is expensive. The authors aim to create a framework that can learn robust policies with minimal real-world data by leveraging the scalability and safety of simulation, thereby reducing the cost and effort of robot training.
5. **Achieve Bimanual Coordination with Multimodal Feedback:** Bimanual tasks require sophisticated coordination. The objective is to learn policies that can orchestrate both robotic arms, adapt to visual and tactile cues, and perform fine adjustments necessary for successful assembly.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of robotic assembly by addressing the critical challenges of data efficiency, multimodal sensing, and robust sim-to-real transfer within the context of complex bimanual manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VT-Refine proposes a novel real-to-sim-to-real policy learning framework for precise bimanual assembly, structured in two main stages: real-world pre-training and simulation fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Visuo-Tactile System and Tactile Simulation

- **Tactile Sensor Hardware (FlexiTac):** The authors developed FlexiTac, a piezoresistive, matrix-based flexible tactile sensor. Each robotic finger is equipped with a 12x32 sensing unit pad (2mm resolution). The sensor uses a triple-layer structure (piezoresistive layer between two flexible printed circuit (FPC) layers) for spatial consistency and resolution. This choice is crucial due to its **compatibility** with various end-effectors and, most importantly, its **sim-to-real transferability**, as it primarily provides normal force signals, which are easier to simulate accurately than vision-based tactile sensors.
- **Tactile Simulation:** The tactile input is simulated using TacSL, a GPU-based tactile simulation library integrated with Isaac Gym. For soft-contact interactions between the deformable tactile sensors and rigid objects, a **Kelvin-Voigt viscoelastic model** is employed. This model simulates the interaction of each tactile point (sensing unit) with the object using a spring-damper system, computing normal forces based on interpenetration depth and relative velocity. The simulation is GPU-parallelized, enabling large-scale reinforcement learning.
- **Real-to-Sim-to-Real for Tactile:** The choice of normal-force-only piezoresistive sensors simplifies calibration and reduces the sim-to-real gap compared to optical sensors. The GPU-accelerated simulation efficiently approximates sensor softness without computationally expensive Finite Element Methods (FEM), supporting scalable training and robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. A detailed calibration procedure, matching simulated and real sensor reading distributions, is critical for this alignment.

#### 3.2. Visuo-Tactile Policy Optimization

The framework aims to learn a policy π: O → A mapping multimodal observations (O) to robot actions (A).

- **Visuo-Tactile Representation:** A point cloud-based representation is adopted for its robust sim-to-real transferability. Observations comprise three modalities:
 1. **Visual:** A colorless point cloud from an ego-centric camera.
 2. **Tactile:** A point cloud representing 3D positions of sensing units and their continuous normal force readings.
 3. **Proprioception:** Joint positions from the robot arms and grippers.
 These are merged into a unified visuo-tactile point cloud, with tactile sensor positions transformed into the camera's frame to preserve spatial relationships. A PointNet encoder processes the merged point cloud, and its output is concatenated with proprioceptive features to condition a denoising diffusion network.

- **Stage 1: Real-World Pre-training:**
 - A small dataset (e.g., 30 episodes) of human teleoperated demonstrations is collected. The human operator controls both arms to pick up and assemble parts, while visual, tactile, robot joint states, and action commands are recorded.
 - A diffusion policy is pre-trained using behavioral cloning on this limited real-world data. The goal is to learn a policy that can sporadically succeed, providing a strong initialization for subsequent fine-tuning.

- **Stage 2: Simulation Fine-tuning:**
 - The pre-trained diffusion policy is transferred to a digital twin of the scene in a parallelized simulation environment.
 - The policy is then fine-tuned end-to-end using Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO), which optimizes a diffusion policy with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
 - An asymmetric actor-critic strategy is used, where the pre-trained diffusion policy initializes the actor network, and the critic is randomly initialized, receiving a low-dimensional environment state.
 - **Reward Function:** A sparse reward is used: 1 for successful assembly, 0 otherwise. The pre-trained policy provides a sufficient prior to guide RL exploration, simplifying reward engineering.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- The system is evaluated on five challenging bimanual plug-and-socket assembly tasks with approximately 2mm clearance.
- Two robot setups are used: a tabletop bimanual setup (two 6-DoF WidowX arms with fin-shaped soft grippers) and a semi-humanoid setup (two 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 arms with Robotiq grippers). Both have four tactile sensor pads and an ego-centric camera.
- Initial object poses are randomized within a 3cm range to increase task variability and test policy robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the effectiveness of the VT-Refine framework across various bimanual assembly tasks and robot configurations.

- **Fine-tuning Significantly Improves Precise Manipulation:**
 - RL fine-tuning dramatically boosts performance, with success rates improving by approximately 20% for vision-only policies and **40% for visuo-tactile policies** in real-world experiments (Table 1).
 - Behavioral cloning alone struggles with the "small, repeated adjustments" (e.g., "wiggle-and-dock" maneuvers) necessary for tight-fit insertions, which are efficiently learned through RL fine-tuning in simulation. This is attributed to the simulation enabling diverse data generation and exploration.
 - Tactile feedback is critical for these micro-adjustments, as it provides clear signals of alignment changes that vision alone often misses due to occlusion (Figure 8).

- **Tactile Feedback Enhances Policy Fine-tuning and Performance:**
 - Visuo-tactile policies consistently start from a stronger baseline after pre-training and achieve higher final precision after fine-tuning compared to vision-only policies (Figure 6, Table 1, Table 2).
 - In simulation, visuo-tactile policies achieve success rates like 0.94-0.98 after fine-tuning, significantly outperforming vision-only policies (e.g., 0.72-0.84). A common failure for vision-only is stalling at small gaps, while visuo-tactile policies continue adjusting.

- **Robust Representation Transfer Across Domains (Real-to-Sim-to-Real):**
 - The chosen low-gap tactile modality (piezoresistive normal force) and point-based representation facilitate effective real-to-sim-to-real transfer.
 - While a slight performance drop (5-10%) is observed when transferring from real to simulation due to domain mismatch, this loss is acceptable and outweighed by the significant gains (over 30% improvement) from RL fine-tuning in simulation (Figure 7). Sim-to-real transfer shows a smaller, sometimes negligible, drop.
 - Tactile simulation calibration is critical, as uncalibrated simulation leads to an initial drop in success rate during fine-tuning due to a shifted observation distribution (Figure 10, Supplementary).

- **Effect of Pre-training Data Quantity:**
 - An ablation study (Table 3) shows that a moderate amount of pre-training data (e.g., 30 demonstrations) provides a strong enough prior for RL fine-tuning to achieve near-perfect success rates.
 - Policies trained with very few demonstrations (10) perform poorly initially but still benefit substantially from RL fine-tuning.
 - Increasing demonstrations from 30 to 50 yields minimal additional benefit to the base policy, as the bottleneck remains the acquisition of fine, exploratory micro-motions that are hard to capture through modest increases in human demos but are efficiently learned via RL.

In summary, the VT-Refine framework effectively combines the strengths of real-world demonstrations and large-scale simulation. The careful design of the tactile sensing system and its simulation, coupled with point-cloud representations, enables robust sim-to-real transfer. Reinforcement learning fine-tuning, guided by a pre-trained diffusion policy, proves crucial for acquiring the nuanced, contact-rich behaviors essential for high-precision bimanual assembly, demonstrating a significant advancement over imitation learning alone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VT-Refine framework represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for complex, contact-rich bimanual assembly tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Precise, Contact-Rich Bimanual Assembly:** This work directly addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: achieving human-like dexterity in bimanual assembly that requires fine-grained contact understanding. By effectively integrating visuo-tactile feedback and learning subtle exploratory behaviors, VT-Refine demonstrates a robust solution for tasks that were previously difficult for robots to master. This could unlock automation for a wider range of manufacturing and domestic assembly applications.

2. **Pioneering Scalable Visuo-Tactile Sim-to-Real Learning:** VT-Refine is presented as the first work to demonstrate successful reinforcement learning with large-scale simulation and sim-to-real transfer for bimanual visuo-tactile policies. This is a crucial contribution, as it overcomes a major hurdle in deploying tactile-enabled robots beyond research labs. The careful design of the FlexiTac sensor and its GPU-accelerated simulation offers a practical blueprint for future research aiming to leverage tactile feedback in simulation-trained policies.

3. **Data Efficiency and Robust Policy Learning:** The framework's ability to achieve high performance with a small set of real-world demonstrations (e.g., 30 episodes) is highly impactful. By using these demonstrations for pre-training and then leveraging large-scale simulated reinforcement learning for refinement, VT-Refine offers a data-efficient approach. This reduces the prohibitive cost and effort associated with collecting massive real-world datasets, accelerating the development and deployment of robot skills.

4. **Advancing Multimodal Perception in Robotics:** The work underscores the critical importance of multimodal perception, particularly the synergy between vision and touch, for complex manipulation. It shows that tactile feedback not only complements vision but is indispensable for micro-adjustments and overcoming visual occlusions during contact-rich phases, leading to more robust and precise behaviors.

5. **Practical Toolchain for Robotics Research:** By releasing detailed tutorials for hardware design and fabrication, the authors are contributing to the democratization of advanced tactile sensing. The use of established platforms like Isaac Gym, TacSL, and DPPO, combined with their point-based representation, provides a robust and replicable methodology for other researchers to build upon.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to future research avenues:
- **Manual Calibration:** The real-to-sim alignment for tactile and visual modalities still requires manual calibration, which could be automated for a truly plug-and-play system.
- **Simulator Constraints:** Current simulators like Isaac Gym and TacSL lack robust support for deformable objects and direct shear force sensing, which limits the applicability to tasks involving highly deformable materials or requiring complex in-hand manipulation with shear forces. Enhancing sim-to-real fidelity, particularly for deformable objects, is a key area.
- **CAD Model Dependency:** The current approach relies on object CAD models for simulation, limiting its immediate extension to novel, arbitrary everyday objects. Future work could explore CAD-free or perception-based object modeling.
- **Longer-Horizon Tasks:** While effective for assembly, longer-horizon and more complex tasks would require significantly more training time.

Despite these limitations, VT-Refine establishes a powerful methodology for learning complex bimanual manipulation skills. Its success in integrating tactile sensing into a scalable real-to-sim-to-real pipeline provides a strong foundation for developing truly dexterous and adaptable robots capable of operating in unstructured environments.
