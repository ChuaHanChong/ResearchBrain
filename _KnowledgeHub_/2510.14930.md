# VT-Refine: Learning Bimanual Assembly with Visuo-Tactile Feedback via Simulation Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel real-to-sim-to-real framework named VT-Refine enables robots to perform precise, contact-rich bimanual assembly tasks by integrating visuo-tactile feedback and simulation fine-tuning. This approach, which uses a custom piezoresistive tactile sensor and point-cloud representations, improves real-world success rates for visuo-tactile policies by approximately 40% over vision-only methods, facilitating nuanced adjustments crucial for tight-fit insertions.

## Problem

- Behavioral cloning from human demonstrations struggles with the precision and exploratory behaviors required for complex, contact-rich bimanual assembly tasks.
- Integrating tactile feedback for robotic manipulation faces significant challenges in accurate simulation and robust transfer from simulation to the real world.
- Scaling robot learning for dexterous manipulation is hindered by the high cost and time investment for collecting sufficient real-world data.

## Method

- A real-to-sim-to-real framework that pre-trains diffusion policies on limited real-world demonstrations and then fine-tunes them using reinforcement learning in a parallelized simulation environment.
- Development of FlexiTac, a custom piezoresistive tactile sensor designed for sim-to-real transfer, providing normal force readings, paired with a GPU-accelerated viscoelastic tactile simulation model (TacSL in Isaac Gym).
- A unified point-cloud representation that integrates visual, tactile (sensor unit positions and forces), and proprioceptive data for robust multimodal policy learning and transfer.

## Results

- RL fine-tuning drastically improved real-world success rates, with visuo-tactile policies showing approximately a 40% increase compared to vision-only policies, demonstrating the learned precision.
- Visuo-tactile policies consistently achieved higher performance and precision after fine-tuning (e.g., up to 0.98 success rate in simulation) compared to vision-only approaches across various bimanual assembly tasks.
- The framework successfully demonstrated robust real-to-sim-to-real transfer, with policies trained from limited real data and refined in simulation exhibiting strong performance on physical robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning fine-tuning in simulation is critical for acquiring the precise micro-adjustments and exploratory 'wiggle-and-dock' behaviors necessary for tight-fit assembly, which are often absent in human demonstrations.
> - Effective integration of tactile feedback with vision is indispensable for high-precision, contact-rich tasks, enabling robots to make fine adjustments even when visual cues are occluded or insufficient.
> - Designing tactile sensors and their simulation models to focus on easily transferable modalities (e.g., normal forces only) with robust representations (e.g., point clouds) significantly reduces the sim-to-real domain gap.
