# ActSWM: Action-Sensitive World Models for Long-Horizon Planning in Open-World Games

> [!summary] Summary
> ActSWM introduces an action-sensitive latent world model to overcome "Context Collapse" in open-world game planning. By enforcing action sensitivity at both transition and rollout levels, it maintains distinguishable latent futures under different action sequences, achieving up to a 12.9x improvement in action-conditioned steerability and higher success rates in Minecraft tasks compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing latent world models often suffer from "Context Collapse," where predicted future states become insensitive to input actions despite high prediction accuracy, hindering effective long-horizon planning.
- Planners struggle to reliably compare the downstream consequences of different action sequences if the latent dynamics model fails to produce clearly distinguishable futures for varying actions.
- Prior action-relevant information approaches primarily operated on observed local transitions, without explicitly constraining predicted rollouts under alternative future action sequences over extended horizons.

## Method

- **ActSWM** extends a ==Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)==-based latent prediction backbone with explicit mechanisms for action sensitivity at both rollout and transition levels.
- It incorporates an ==action-contrastive hinge loss== to penalize high cosine similarity between rollouts conditioned on recorded actions versus all-zero actions, enforcing a separation of latent futures.
- A ==frozen, randomly initialized action readout== (`q_ϕ0`) is utilized to enforce transition-level action recoverability; its fixed parameters ensure gradients propagate to the encoder and predictor, making latent transitions discriminative of actions.

## Results

- ActSWM achieved a **380x** larger separation (action gap of **0.760**) between recorded-action and zero-action futures compared to the strongest baseline (LeWM), effectively mitigating Context Collapse.
- In closed-loop Minecraft planning tasks, ActSWM achieved higher success rates than LeWM across all tasks, including **100%** for torch placement, **95%** for stone mining (a **90.0%** improvement), and **85%** for pillar building (a **54.5%** improvement).
- ActSWM demonstrated improved action-conditioned steerability in offline action recovery, with up to a **16.5x** improvement in action gap over LeWM in games like Grand Theft Auto V and Apex Legends.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective model-based planning requires not just prediction accuracy, but also explicit action sensitivity in latent rollouts to prevent "Context Collapse" and enable reliable counterfactual comparisons.
> - Enforcing action discriminability via a *frozen* action readout ensures the latent space adapts to represent action-dependent differences, rather than the readout simply fitting a collapsed representation.
> - The proposed "transition-separation principle" (maintaining distinguishable futures and recoverable actions) serves as a robust design principle for planning-useful latent dynamics models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the broader objective of developing general-purpose autonomous agents capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting in open-world environments, a central pursuit in artificial general intelligence (AGI). Open-world games, characterized by high-dimensional visual perception, low-level control, and extended task structures, serve as a testing ground for such agents.

Prior advancements in game agents include behavior-cloning approaches like Video Pretraining (VPT) and STEVE-1, which learn policies from large-scale gameplay videos. Multimodal agent systems, such as the JARVIS family and Game-TARS, integrate visual perception, language-based reasoning, and low-level control for open-world decision-making. Lumine has demonstrated extended task completion in complex 3D environments. Despite these developments, existing methods often face limitations regarding sample efficiency, deployment cost, and sustained long-horizon decision-making, particularly in open-ended tasks like navigation, resource gathering, manipulation, and construction.

World-model-based planning is identified as a promising direction to address these challenges. By learning environment dynamics and optimizing control sequences in a latent space, agents can evaluate action consequences with reduced real-world interactions. Recent Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) methods, including LeWorldModel (LeWM), have shown potential in learning efficient and abstract latent dynamics from raw pixels. These latent world models necessitate action sensitivity, meaning distinct action sequences should induce separable latent consequences. However, models primarily optimized for future-state similarity do not inherently guarantee this property. The World-Action Model (WAM) augmented latent dynamics models with inverse-dynamics heads to retain action-relevant information, but these approaches primarily operate on observed local transitions rather than explicitly constraining predicted rollouts under alternative future action sequences.

This paper identifies a specific failure mode termed "Context Collapse," where autoregressive latent predictors maintain high similarity to future states but produce nearly indistinguishable futures under different action sequences. This issue is distinct from standard accumulated prediction error in long-horizon models; it concerns the degradation of action conditioning itself. Context Collapse prevents planners from reliably comparing the downstream consequences of different action sequences, hindering effective long-horizon decision-making. ActSWM aims to explicitly address this gap by enforcing action-sensitive dynamics at both the transition and rollout levels, ensuring that predicted futures preserve action-dependent differences over extended horizons.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an action-sensitive latent world model, named ActSWM, that can support stable long-horizon planning in open-world games by addressing a specific failure mode called "Context Collapse."

The primary motivation stems from the limitations of existing latent world models. While these models can achieve high fidelity in predicting future states, they often suffer from a critical flaw: their predicted futures can become insensitive to the specific actions provided as input. This "Context Collapse" occurs when the latent context becomes sufficiently informative for the predictor to extrapolate state progression from visual history alone, rendering it unresponsive to the conditioning action sequence. Consequently, even if a model accurately predicts a plausible future, it may produce nearly identical future predictions for distinct action sequences (e.g., recorded actions versus all-zero actions).

For planning-oriented world models, Context Collapse is detrimental. Effective planning requires an agent to anticipate how current actions will cascade into different future states over many time steps and to reliably compare the consequences of alternative action sequences. If distinct actions fail to induce distinguishable latent futures, a planner cannot effectively differentiate between potential outcomes, undermining its ability to optimize control sequences.

To overcome this, the authors propose ActSWM, grounded in a "transition-separation principle." This principle posits that a planning-useful latent dynamics model should achieve two related goals:
1. **Distinguishable Futures:** It should maintain alternative-action futures as clearly separable in the latent space.
2. **Recoverable Actions:** It should ensure that the action associated with each local transition remains recoverable from the latent state change.

The motivation for ActSWM is therefore to explicitly enforce action sensitivity as a constraint on latent rollouts, rather than treating action prediction merely as an auxiliary task. By doing so, the model is encouraged to encode action-dependent state changes in its predictions, preserving these differences over long horizons and enabling reliable counterfactual comparisons by downstream planners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ActSWM extends a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)-based latent prediction backbone with mechanisms designed to enforce action sensitivity at both rollout and transition levels.

**Preliminaries:**
The model operates on offline trajectories of observations `o_t` and actions `a_t`. A visual encoder `f_θ` maps observations to latent states `z_t`, and an action encoder `g_θ` maps actions to action features `e_t`. A predictor `p_θ` takes a window of encoded observations and action features (`z_{t-H+1:t}`, `e_{t-H+1:t}`) to predict the next latent state (`hat{z}_{t+1}`). For multi-step prediction, the predictor is applied recursively.

**Modeling Action Sensitivity:**
ActSWM conceptualizes action sensitivity at two levels:
1. **Rollout Level:** For any given horizon `K`, different future action sequences originating from the same context (`a^{(1)}_{t:t+K-1}` and `a^{(2)}_{t:t+K-1}`) should yield separable predicted latent futures (`hat{z}^{(1)}_{t+1:t+K}` and `hat{z}^{(2)}_{t+1:t+K}`) if these sequences imply different consequences. During training and evaluation, the recorded future action sequence and an all-zero sequence are used for this contrast.
2. **Transition Level:** The input action `a_t` should be recoverable from the change between adjacent latent states, represented as a transition `u_t = [z_t, z_{t+1}]`. This is related to inverse-dynamics and bidirectional action prediction.

**Action-Readout Separation Principle:**
The transition-level criterion is implemented using an action readout `q_ϕ0` that maps latent transitions to actions. A critical aspect is that the parameters `ϕ0` of this readout are **randomly initialized and remain frozen** during the world-model training. This fixed readout acts as a stable constraint: if `q_ϕ0` can accurately recover different actions from different latent transitions, then those transitions must be distinguishable in latent space. As per Lipschitz continuity, if different actions (`a_i`, `a_j`) are accurately recovered from transitions (`u_i`, `u_j`) by a fixed `L`-Lipschitz readout, then `||u_i - u_j||` must be greater than or equal to `(||a_i - a_j|| - ϵ_i - ϵ_j) / L`. This ensures that the latent space adapts to represent action-dependent differences rather than the readout adapting to a collapsed representation.

**ActSWM Architecture (Figure 2):**
ActSWM comprises the JEPA-based latent prediction backbone and two action-sensitivity mechanisms:
- **Rollout Branch:** This branch is used for the action-contrastive hinge loss, comparing rollouts from recorded and alternative (zero) actions.
- **Readout Branch:** This branch incorporates the frozen action readout `q_ϕ0`.

**Training Objective (Equation 9):**
The full training objective `L` combines several terms:
- `L_pred` (Multi-step Prediction Loss): Minimizes the L2 distance between the model's K-step latent rollout conditioned on recorded future actions (`hat{z}^{gt}_{t+k}`) and the encoded ground-truth future states (`z_{t+k}`). This ensures prediction fidelity.
- `L_hinge` (Action-Contrastive Hinge Loss): Applies a hinge loss `max(0, cos(hat{z}^{gt}_{t+k}, hat{z}^{0}_{t+k}) - (1 - m))` to penalize high cosine similarity between the rollout conditioned on recorded actions (`hat{z}^{gt}`) and a counterfactual rollout conditioned on all-zero actions (`hat{z}^{0}`). This term enforces rollout-level separation, encouraging distinct futures under different actions. `m` is the hinge margin hyperparameter.
- `L_readout` (Action Readout Loss): This loss applies the frozen readout `q_ϕ0` to both encoded (`u^{enc}_{t+k}`) and rollout-predicted (`u^{pred}_{t+k}`) transitions, measuring their ability to recover the corresponding ground-truth action `a_{t+k}` using an L2 loss (`ℓ^{enc}_k` and `ℓ^{pred}_k`). Since `ϕ0` is fixed, gradients from this loss propagate through its latent inputs, updating the encoder and predictor to produce action-discriminative latent transitions.
- `L_sig` (SigReg Loss): Adopted from LeWM, this term regularizes the scale and distribution of prediction-space features for stability.

The final objective `L = L_pred + λ_sig L_sig + λ_hinge L_hinge + λ_readout L_readout` combines these terms with weighting coefficients `λ`. Training is conducted using AdamW for 100k steps with a batch size of 32, a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁴, and mixed 16-bit precision. The context length `H` is 32, and the training rollout horizon `K` is 12.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented three main experiments to evaluate ActSWM: a diagnostic of Context Collapse, closed-loop planning performance, and discriminative action representations.

**Q1: Diagnosing Context Collapse**
- **Methodology:** Offline step-drift analysis on Minecraft VPT trajectories. The evaluation compared two K-step latent rollouts from the same initial context: one conditioned on recorded future actions (`hat{z}^{gt}`) and another on all-zero actions (`hat{z}^{0}`). Metrics included `s^{gt}_k` (cosine similarity to ground-truth under recorded actions), `s^{0}_k` (cosine similarity to ground-truth under zero actions), and `Δ_k = s^{gt}_k - s^{0}_k` (the action gap). Context Collapse is indicated by high `s^{gt}_k` but a small `Δ_k`.
- **Results:**
 - Baseline LeWM variants (with longer context, higher resolution, and multi-step rollout training) showed high `s^{gt}_k` (e.g., 0.972 at step 31) but a negligible action gap `Δ_k` (e.g., 0.002), confirming Context Collapse where predicted futures are nearly identical despite different action inputs.
 - A LeWM variant with a *jointly trained readout* increased the action gap to 0.592 but at the cost of reduced `s^{gt}_k` (0.698), indicating a trade-off.
 - ActSWM maintained high `s^{gt}_k` (0.923) while substantially reducing `s^{0}_k` (0.163 compared to 0.970 for the best LeWM baseline), resulting in the largest action gap `Δ_k` of 0.760. This represents approximately a 380x larger separation between recorded-action and zero-action futures compared to the strongest LeWM baseline, demonstrating that ActSWM's rollouts remain responsive to action inputs.

**Q2: Closed-Loop Model-Based Planning**
- **Methodology:** Evaluation on three Minecraft tasks in MineStudio: torch placement, stone mining, and pillar building. The planner used Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to minimize the average latent distance between the world model's K-step rollout and a task-specific reference latent trajectory (`J(a)`). LeWM served as the matched backbone baseline.
- **Results:**
 - ActSWM consistently outperformed LeWM in success rate across all tasks.
 - Torch Placement: ActSWM achieved a 100% success rate (20/20 trials) compared to LeWM's 95% (19/20), an improvement of 5.3%.
 - Stone Mining: ActSWM achieved a 95% success rate (19/20) compared to LeWM's 50% (10/20), representing a 90.0% improvement.
 - Pillar Building: ActSWM achieved an 85% success rate (17/20) compared to LeWM's 55% (11/20), an improvement of 54.5%.
 - The larger improvements observed in sustained and sequential tasks indicate that action-sensitive rollouts contribute to more reliable planning, particularly when success hinges on distinguishing long action sequences.

**Q3: Discriminative Action Representations**
- **Methodology:** Evaluation of local action recovery from offline gameplay videos across three diverse domains: Counter-Strike 2 (CS), Grand Theft Auto V (GTA), and Apex Legends (Apex). CEM was used to infer an action sequence that would generate a world model rollout approaching a target future frame. Performance was compared against direct inverse-dynamics prediction (G-IDM) and LeWM. Metrics included Action Gap (`J_rand - J_cem`), Key Accuracy (per-frame binary key accuracy), and Active Accuracy (recall on frames where ground-truth keys are active).
- **Results:**
 - **Action Gap:** ActSWM consistently improved the action gap across all three games, demonstrating stronger action-conditioned steerability. It achieved 60.02 for CS (vs. 54.86 for LeWM), 252.38 for GTA (vs. 19.57 for LeWM, a 12.9x improvement), and 172.01 for Apex (vs. 10.44 for LeWM, a 16.5x improvement).
 - **Active Accuracy:** ActSWM showed significant gains in active accuracy for CS (0.389 vs 0.363 for LeWM) and GTA (0.761 vs 0.050 for LeWM, an absolute improvement of 0.711). For Apex, ActSWM's active accuracy (0.269) was lower than LeWM's (0.576).
 - **Key Accuracy:** ActSWM maintained competitive overall key accuracy across all domains (CS: 0.743, GTA: 0.662, Apex: 0.721).
 - G-IDM, while showing moderate overall key accuracy, exhibited zero active accuracy, suggesting it primarily predicts the dominant inactive class.
 - The consistent action-gap gains confirm that ActSWM's latent space responds meaningfully to candidate actions, enabling CEM to effectively rank alternatives.

In summary, the results indicate that ActSWM effectively mitigates Context Collapse by enforcing a larger separation between rollouts conditioned on different actions, leading to improved performance in closed-loop planning tasks and more discriminative action representations for offline action recovery.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on ActSWM addresses a fundamental challenge in the development of planning-oriented latent world models: the phenomenon of Context Collapse. By explicitly recognizing and addressing this issue, the research highlights that prediction accuracy alone is insufficient for effective model-based planning; the model's sensitivity to action inputs is equally critical.

The significance of ActSWM and its underlying principles lies in several areas:

1. **Enhanced Planning Reliability:** By ensuring that distinct action sequences lead to distinguishable future states in the latent space, ActSWM enables planning algorithms like CEM to more reliably evaluate and compare alternative actions. This directly translates to improved performance in complex, long-horizon interactive tasks, as demonstrated in the Minecraft planning experiments. This capability is crucial for agents operating in open-ended environments where tasks require extended sequences of interdependent actions.

2. **More Discriminative Latent Representations:** The enforced action sensitivity leads to latent representations that better encode the effects of actions. This is evident in the strong action gap observed in diagnostic tests and the improved action recovery capabilities across diverse game environments. Such discriminative representations are valuable not just for planning but also for understanding environment dynamics and for tasks like offline action inference.

3. **Foundation for Learning from Unlabeled Data:** The ability to recover actions from offline gameplay videos, even in varied game settings, suggests a potential for leveraging large amounts of unlabeled video data for agent training. By inferring the actions that explain observed transitions, world models could contribute to scalable action labeling or pre-training, reducing reliance on costly human-annotated datasets or extensive real-world interaction.

4. **Advancement in World Model Design:** ActSWM establishes action sensitivity not merely as an auxiliary property but as a core requirement and design principle for planning-oriented latent world models. This shifts the focus beyond just maximizing future-state accuracy to include the critical aspect of counterfactual consistency and action-dependent state changes. The proposed transition-separation principle and the use of a frozen action readout offer a novel methodological contribution to how such sensitivity can be effectively enforced during training.

The potential impact extends to the broader field of embodied AI and reinforcement learning, particularly for agents designed to operate in complex, dynamic, and open-ended environments. By fostering world models that truly understand the consequences of actions over long horizons, ActSWM contributes to building more robust, intelligent, and general-purpose autonomous agents.
