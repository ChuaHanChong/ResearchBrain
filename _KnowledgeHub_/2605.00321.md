# Embodied Interpretability: Linking Causal Understanding to Generalization in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A causal interpretability framework quantifies Vision-Language-Action model reliance on task-relevant versus spurious visual features, demonstrating that increased attribution to nuisance regions negatively correlates with generalization. The Interventional Significance Score (ISS) provides more faithful and robust explanations compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit misdirected attention, activating on visually prominent but task-irrelevant regions.
- Current VLA models demonstrate resilience of output actions even when significant visual inputs are masked, suggesting a lack of direct causal dependency.
- This reliance on spurious correlations hinders generalization performance, particularly under out-of-distribution shifts.

## Method

- Visual-action attribution is formalized as an interventional estimation problem to rigorously quantify the influence of visual regions on action predictions.
- The Interventional Significance Score (ISS) is introduced to measure the causal influence of specific visual regions on VLA model action decisions via expected Kullback-Leibler divergence.
- The Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR) is developed as a scalar metric to quantify the extent to which a VLA policy's decision-making is attributed to task-irrelevant visual features, diagnosing causal misalignment.

## Results

- The Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR) at top-k (specifically `k=10`) showed a maximum negative Pearson correlation of -**0.77** with task success rate, effectively predicting generalization performance.
- Interventional Significance Score (ISS) demonstrated superior robustness and fidelity compared to attention scores and token norms under both soft and hard visual interventions.
- The framework's optimal configuration for ISS (N=100, p=**0.3**) minimized action perturbation, with a computational latency of **5.18** seconds per state, making it suitable for offline diagnosis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A higher Nuisance Mass Ratio, indicating greater reliance on task-irrelevant visual features, strongly predicts lower generalization performance for VLA policies.
> - Interventional methods, like ISS, provide more faithful, consistent, and informative explanations of VLA model behavior compared to correlation-based interpretability techniques.
> - VLA models can exhibit 'causal hallucination' by over-relying on spurious correlations from nuisance visual regions, leading to reduced robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a notable area of advancement in embodied intelligence, integrating visual perception, natural language understanding, and robotic control. Despite their capabilities, the authors identify two empirical anomalies in current mainstream VLA research: a tendency for attention mechanisms to activate on task-irrelevant visual regions (e.g., background elements) and a resilience of output actions even when visual inputs are substantially masked. These observations suggest that VLA models may not be learning the underlying causal mechanisms of tasks but rather memorizing statistical mappings or relying on spurious correlations. This reliance, the authors hypothesize, is a primary contributor to the models' limited generalization performance, particularly under out-of-distribution (OOD) shifts.

The broader research landscape of interpretability in embodied intelligence has explored several avenues to understand model behavior. Earlier efforts focused on system-level transparency, employing modular designs or explicit reasoning mechanisms like those seen in SayCan, Code-as-Policies, and Chain-of-Thought approaches (e.g., CoT-VLA, PhysiAgent). While these methods shed light on the high-level planning or intermediate outputs, they often do not fully reveal the feature-level operating mechanisms of the models.

More recent work has shifted towards mechanistic interpretability, categorizing into:
1. **Attention Analysis:** Methods like "Robotic Steering" and DTP framework analyze attention weights to identify focal regions. However, a limitation of these approaches is their inability to establish whether these focal regions are causally necessary for the resulting actions. The quantitative assessment of reliance on task-relevant versus nuisance features remains an open challenge.
2. **Latent State Probing:** Techniques that decipher the semantic content of hidden representations (e.g., in OpenVLA for symbolic states or internal world models). A fundamental limitation noted by the authors is their passive nature; proving that information exists within hidden layers does not confirm its active use by the model for decision-making, leading to a causal disconnect.
3. **Feature Disentanglement:** Approaches that decompose dense neural activations into sparse, interpretable features, allowing for semantic steering or identification of unsafe behaviors. These methods, however, can struggle with the complexity of compositional embodied concepts and incur substantial computational overhead for large-scale autoencoders.

This paper positions itself to address the identified gap in the existing interpretability landscape. It proposes an approach that integrates mechanistic interpretability with interventional causality, aiming to quantify the extent to which VLA policies depend on causally relevant visual evidence versus nuisance features and, crucially, to assess if this dependence predicts generalization performance. By focusing on causal understanding through intervention, the work seeks to move beyond correlational insights offered by many existing interpretability methods, providing a more robust diagnostic for generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from observed limitations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically their susceptibility to distribution shifts and their apparent reliance on spurious correlations. The authors highlight two critical anomalies:
1. **Misdirected Attention:** VLA models' attention scores frequently activate on visually prominent yet task-irrelevant regions, such as background elements, textures, or shadows, rather than on components directly implicated in task execution.
2. **Robustness to Visual Ablation:** The models often produce similar actions even when significant portions of the visual input are masked, suggesting a lack of direct causal dependency on the visual information for decision-making.

These phenomena collectively suggest that VLA models might be learning superficial statistical associations between tasks and actions, rather than acquiring a deeper understanding of the causal mechanisms governing successful task completion. This reliance on spurious correlations is hypothesized to be a primary determinant of their limited out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization capabilities, as illustrated by performance drops in novel environments (Figure 1).

Against this background, the paper formulates a fundamental research question: "How can we quantify whether a VLA policy relies on causally relevant visual evidence rather than spurious correlations, and does this reliance predict generalization performance?"

To address this question, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Formulate Visual-Action Attribution as an Interventional Estimation Problem:** To move beyond correlational interpretability, the authors aim to define how the influence of visual regions on action predictions can be rigorously quantified through causal interventions.
2. **Introduce Interventional Significance Score (ISS):** To develop a quantitative measure that estimates the causal influence of specific visual regions or tokens on the VLA model's action decisions.
3. **Introduce Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR):** To provide a scalar metric that quantifies the extent to which a VLA policy's decision-making is attributed to task-irrelevant visual features, thereby serving as an indicator of causal misalignment.
4. **Provide Theoretical Analysis:** To establish a principled basis for the proposed measures by analyzing the statistical properties of ISS, demonstrating its unbiased estimability, and characterizing the conditions under which action prediction error can serve as a valid proxy for distributional divergence.
5. **Empirically Validate NMR's Predictive Power:** To demonstrate that the Nuisance Mass Ratio correlates with and predicts the generalization behavior of VLA policies under distribution shifts across diverse manipulation tasks.
6. **Empirically Demonstrate ISS's Superiority:** To show that ISS yields more faithful, consistent, and informative explanations compared to existing interpretability methods, thereby offering a more reliable diagnostic tool.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a diagnostic framework for identifying and quantifying causal misalignment in embodied policies, ultimately contributing to the development of more robust and generalizable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a causal analysis framework to quantify the causal influence of visual regions on actions and diagnose causal misalignment in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The approach is grounded in interventional causality and structured around two primary measures: the Interventional Significance Score (ISS) and the Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR).

#### 3.1. Problem Formalization

The authors define VLA models as learned policies $\pi_\theta$ mapping multimodal observations $X_t = [v_1, \ldots, v_{n_1}, l_1, \ldots, l_{n_2}]$ (visual patch embeddings and linguistic instruction tokens) to actions $a_t$. To formalize causal dependencies, they leverage the concept of conditional independence and Markov Blankets, aiming to identify the minimal set of visual regions necessary to infer the expert action.

#### 3.2. Causality Quantification within Latent Space

This section details how causal effects are quantified through interventions and counterfactual reasoning.

- **Causal Intervention:** To measure the causal contribution of an individual input token $x_i$, a counterfactual input $\tilde{X}^{(i)}_t$ is constructed. Instead of zero-ablation, which can introduce out-of-distribution (OOD) artifacts, the $i$-th token is replaced with a static baseline $\mu_i$. This $\mu_i$ is the marginal mean embedding conditioned on the token's modality (visual mean for visual tokens, textual mean for linguistic tokens), derived from the training distribution. This approach aims to maintain distributional validity within the respective semantic subspace.

- **Interventional Significance Score (ISS):** The ISS quantifies the causal significance of token $i$ by measuring the expected cumulative Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the original policy's action distribution $\pi_\theta(\cdot | X_t)$ and the perturbed policy's distribution $\pi_\theta(\cdot | \tilde{X}^{(i)}_t)$. This divergence is evaluated under "teacher forcing," conditioning both distributions on the ground-truth action to isolate the causal effect on the current decision step.
 - **Estimation Strategy:** Direct computation of the cumulative KL divergence is intractable. The authors employ a Monte Carlo estimation strategy using randomized binary masks (Algorithm 1). This stochastic method is theoretically shown to be a consistent and unbiased estimator of coalitional causal effects (Appendix A.1).
 - **Action MSE as Proxy:** Under the common assumption of a fixed isotropic Gaussian policy in VLA training, the KL divergence is proportional to the squared difference between the predicted action means. Therefore, Action Mean Squared Error (MSE) is used as a computationally efficient proxy for distributional divergence (Appendix A.2). The saliency map $S_t$ is constructed by accumulating the squared action differences $\delta_k$ for each mask $m_k$, weighted by $(1-m_k)$ to attribute the discrepancy to the masked regions.

#### 3.3. Causal Misalignment in Learned Subspaces

This part introduces a spatial causal attribution framework and a metric to quantify causal misalignment.

- **Causal Spatial Partition:** The visual token space $\Omega$ is partitioned into three disjoint sets relative to the task instruction and ideal expert policy $\pi^*$:
 1. **Action-Critical Regions ($\Omega_{act}$):** Tokens representing the robot's manipulator arm and end-effector.
 2. **Environmental Support Regions ($\Omega_{sup}$):** Tokens representing task-relevant objects and essential physical support structures (e.g., table surfaces).
 3. **Visual Nuisance Regions ($\Omega_{nuis}$):** Tokens representing elements that do not physically interact with the task, such as background walls, lighting reflections, or distractor objects.
 This partition is applied to the latent token space, where semantic entities are spatially disentangled. The authors propose that the union of action-critical and support regions ($\Omega_{act} \cup \Omega_{sup}$) constitutes the Causal Markov Blanket for the action variable, implying that any learned dependence on $\Omega_{nuis}$ indicates a "causal hallucination" or reliance on spurious correlations.

- **Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR):** Building on the spatial partition, the NMR metric (specifically `nmr@k`) quantifies causal misalignment. It is defined as the Regional Mass Ratio for the nuisance region $\Omega_{nuis}$, using ISS as the importance scoring function $\phi$.
 - $\rho^{(k)}_{ISS}(\Omega_{nuis}) = E_X \left[ \frac{|H^{(k)}_{ISS}(X) \cap \Omega_{nuis}|}{|H^{(k)}_{ISS}(X)|} \right]$, where $H^{(k)}_{ISS}(X)$ is the set of tokens accounting for the top $k\%$ of cumulative ISS mass.
 - This metric measures the proportion of causally significant tokens (identified by ISS) that belong to the irrelevant background. A high NMR indicates that the policy is overfitting to spurious correlations. An ideal policy should exhibit an NMR approaching zero.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted on the AGNOSTOS benchmark using a VLA policy $\pi_{0.5}$. The policy is fine-tuned on 3600 episodes from "seen" tasks (S) and evaluated on 575 episodes from "unseen" tasks (U), divided into two levels of generalization (U1 with partial semantic overlap, U2 with novel scenarios). The evaluation protocol employs offline interventional methods to decouple causal mechanisms from simulator artifacts. The VLA policy uses SigLIP as a visual encoder and PaliGemma as a multimodal language model backbone, with actions represented as continuous 8-DoF control signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation of the proposed framework involved experiments on the AGNOSTOS benchmark using a VLA policy $\pi_{0.5}$. The results address the relationship between the Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR) and generalization, the robustness and fidelity of the Interventional Significance Score (ISS), and its computational characteristics.

#### 4.1. Task Success Rate and NMR at Top-k

The relationship between NMR and task generalization performance was investigated by computing the Pearson correlation coefficient (r) between `nmr@k` and the task success rate of $\pi_{0.5}$. Across 41 tasks (seen and unseen) and 5 random seeds, each with 25 trials per task, the analysis revealed a consistent negative correlation.
- **Finding:** The optimal `nmr@k` was identified at `k=10`, yielding a maximal negative correlation of **-0.77**. This indicates that a higher attribution of policy decisions to nuisance visual features (i.e., higher NMR) is associated with a lower task success rate, thereby predicting reduced generalization performance.

#### 4.2. Robustness and Fidelity of ISS

The robustness and fidelity of ISS were compared against two baseline interpretability methods: attention score (ATT) and token norm (NORM).

- **Robustness Study (Soft Interventions):** Gaussian noise was added to the nuisance regions of visual inputs, assessing how the saliency map and action predictions change. The study analyzed 200 episodes with minimal action Mean Squared Error (MSE). Saliency stability was measured by cosine similarity ($\Delta$S) and action deviation by MSE ($\Delta$A).
 - **Finding:** In a Pareto plot (MSE vs. Cosine Similarity), ISS was positioned at (0.002, 0.995). This indicates that ISS simultaneously minimizes action deviation ($\Delta$A) and maximizes saliency consistency ($\Delta$S) under soft interventions, outperforming ATT (0.002, 0.959) and NORM (0.011, 0.999). This suggests ISS is more robust to task-irrelevant visual perturbations.

- **Fidelity Study (Hard Interventions):** Three types of structured perturbations (textural, geometric, and patch-based) were applied to nuisance regions across 1,000 sampled episodes. The correlation between saliency map changes (cosine similarity) and action MSE ($\Delta$A) was examined.
 - **Finding:** ISS consistently exhibited stronger linear alignment and higher Pearson correlation coefficients than ATT and NORM across all perturbation types. For instance, ISS achieved coefficients of 0.78 (texture), 0.64 (geometric), and 0.72 (patch), while ATT yielded 0.64, 0.49, 0.56, and NORM yielded 0.47, 0.33, 0.40. These results suggest that ISS more faithfully captures the policy's underlying reliance on specific visual cues.

#### 4.3. Hyperparameter of ISS

The sensitivity of ISS to the number of Monte Carlo interventions (N) and masking probability (p) was evaluated by measuring the average Action MSE across 500 episodes.
- **Finding:** The configuration of **N = 100 and p = 0.3** yielded the lowest average Action MSE (3.7 $\times 10^{-3} \pm 0.1 \times 10^{-3}$), indicating this combination minimized action perturbation during the interventional process and was selected for other experiments.

#### 4.4. Additional Results

- **Computation Efficiency:** While more computationally expensive than single-pass heuristics like attention weights, the Monte Carlo strategy for ISS (at N=100, p=0.3) resulted in a total latency of 5.18s (0.19Hz) per state, representing a slowdown factor of 65.8x compared to a single inference. This makes it suitable for offline safety verification and post-hoc interpretability, rather than real-time control.
- **Visualization:** Qualitative visualization of saliency maps for the "close jar" task under various perturbations (noise, texture, geometric, patch) showed that ISS more effectively concentrates saliency on manipulation-relevant components, particularly in the wrist view, maintaining low nuisance attribution (e.g., $\rho_{nuis}$ values between 0.02 and 0.15). In contrast, baselines like NORM and ATT exhibited higher dispersion over task-irrelevant background regions, with higher $\rho_{nuis}$ values (e.g., 0.76 for NORM in the front view), highlighting ISS's superior localization of causal entities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces a diagnostic framework for embodied policies that addresses the critical issue of causal understanding and its link to generalization. By formulating visual-action attribution as an interventional estimation problem, the authors provide a method to move beyond correlational interpretability.

### 6. Significance of the Findings

- **Quantification of Causal Influence:** The Interventional Significance Score (ISS) offers a principled method to quantify the causal influence of specific visual regions on a VLA policy's action decisions. This represents a methodological advancement over prior interpretability techniques that may not establish causal necessity.
- **Prediction of Generalization:** The Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR) demonstrates a strong negative correlation with task success rates, particularly under out-of-distribution conditions. This establishes NMR as a predictive metric for generalization behavior, suggesting that policies less reliant on spurious visual cues are more likely to generalize effectively.
- **Enhanced Explanations:** Empirical results indicate that ISS provides more faithful and robust explanations compared to established interpretability methods like attention scores and token norms. This increased fidelity can lead to a more accurate understanding of how VLA models make decisions.
- **Diagnostic Tool for Misalignment:** The framework provides a "simple diagnostic approach" to identify causal misalignment in embodied policies, allowing developers to pinpoint where a model might be relying on task-irrelevant features.

**Potential Impact:**
The primary potential impact of this work lies in facilitating the development of more reliable, robust, and generalizable embodied systems, including robotics and autonomous agents. By enabling the identification of reliance on spurious correlations and causal misalignment, the framework can inform model development and refinement, particularly in high-stakes applications where safety and performance under varied conditions are paramount. Understanding these causal dependencies could lead to targeted interventions during training or fine-tuning, steering models towards learning more robust, causal relationships.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- **Unimodal Decision Assumption:** The current ISS formulation implicitly assumes a unimodal action distribution. In scenarios with multimodal action distributions or ambiguous long-horizon planning, Action MSE might not accurately capture mode-specific causal effects, potentially invalidating attribution. Future work aims to extend ISS to multimodal settings.
- **Computational Overhead:** While more efficient than exhaustive causal interventions, ISS still requires multiple forward passes, limiting it to offline diagnosis rather than real-time online application. Future research plans to improve efficiency through low-variance estimators, local approximations, or learned gating mechanisms.
- **Dependence on Nuisance Masks:** The accuracy of NMR relies heavily on the quality of ground-truth nuisance masks. In real-world deployments, this dependency on instance segmentation models introduces data collection costs and sensitivity to segmentation errors. Future work proposes exploring self-supervised or weakly supervised nuisance discovery to infer causal partitions directly.
- **Scope of Evaluation:** The current evaluation is conducted on a single VLA model family and benchmark. Broader large-scale evaluations across diverse architectures and benchmarks are needed to assess the generality and scalability of ISS and NMR.

**Ethical Considerations and Risks (from Impact Statement):**
The authors acknowledge that interpretability tools like ISS and NMR carry risks if over-trusted or misinterpreted as formal guarantees of correctness. Attribution scores are approximations and should not be used to justify opaque decision-making or in high-stakes domains without appropriate human oversight. The computational cost also presents a practical limitation. They advocate for using ISS and NMR as diagnostic tools alongside complementary evaluation methods, uncertainty analyses, and human judgment, with careful consideration of the context of their application.
