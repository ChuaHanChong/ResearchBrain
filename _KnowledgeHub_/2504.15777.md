# Tina: Tiny Reasoning Models via LoRA

> [!summary] Summary
> Tina, a research initiative from the University of Southern California, demonstrates a highly cost-effective approach to instill strong reasoning abilities in language models. It shows that combining a tiny 1.5B base model with LoRA-based reinforcement learning can match or surpass the reasoning performance of state-of-the-art RL models built on the same base, achieving this at post-training costs as low as $9 USD for a single model.

## Problem

- Achieving robust, multi-step reasoning in large language models remains a significant challenge.
- Current methods for enhancing LLM reasoning, such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, are resource-intensive and often require substantial computational power and large models.
- The high cost and complexity of RL pipelines limit accessibility and widespread research into RL-based reasoning for language models.

## Method

- Leverage a small 1.5 billion parameter base model, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, known for its initial reasoning aptitude.
- Apply ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== for ==parameter-efficient reinforcement learning== using a GRPO-style algorithm, updating only a small fraction of model parameters.
- Utilize a minimalist training setup on two NVIDIA L40S GPUs and publicly available reasoning datasets to ensure cost-efficiency and direct comparability with existing models.

## Results

- Tina models achieved an average performance of **50.60%** across six challenging reasoning benchmarks, surpassing SOTA RL models built on the same 1.5B base model (e.g., Open-RS2 at **41.60%**).
- The best Tina model obtained a **>20%** reasoning performance increase and **43.33%** Pass@1 accuracy on AIME24, with a post-training and evaluation cost of approximately **$9 USD**, representing a **~260x** cost reduction.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that concise, high-quality datasets (**7k** examples) yielded superior performance compared to significantly larger ones, and low LoRA ranks (e.g., **16** or **32**) proved highly effective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LoRA primarily adapts models to the structural format of reasoning (e.g., step-by-step thinking) rewarded by RL, while largely preserving the base model Hovering around 50%, an impressive gain that could be achieved with minimal resources.
> - A "less compute can yield more performance" phenomenon indicates that increasing training FLOPs with LoRA can sometimes inversely affect reasoning performance, suggesting efficiency in format adaptation.
> - A distinct "phase transition" point in format-related metrics during training correlates with the best-performing checkpoints, implying an optimal stopping point for LoRA-based RL.
