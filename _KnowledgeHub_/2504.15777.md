# Tina: Tiny Reasoning Models via LoRA

> [!summary] Summary
> Tina, a research initiative from the University of Southern California, demonstrates a highly cost-effective approach to instill strong reasoning abilities in language models. It shows that combining a tiny 1.5B base model with LoRA-based reinforcement learning can match or surpass the reasoning performance of state-of-the-art RL models built on the same base, achieving this at post-training costs as low as $9 USD for a single model.

## Problem

- Achieving robust, multi-step reasoning in large language models remains a significant challenge.
- Current methods for enhancing LLM reasoning, such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, are resource-intensive and often require substantial computational power and large models.
- The high cost and complexity of RL pipelines limit accessibility and widespread research into RL-based reasoning for language models.

## Method

- Leverage a small 1.5 billion parameter base model, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, known for its initial reasoning aptitude.
- Apply ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== for ==parameter-efficient reinforcement learning== using a GRPO-style algorithm, updating only a small fraction of model parameters.
- Utilize a minimalist training setup on two NVIDIA L40S GPUs and publicly available reasoning datasets to ensure cost-efficiency and direct comparability with existing models.

## Results

- Tina models achieved an average performance of **50.60%** across six challenging reasoning benchmarks, surpassing SOTA RL models built on the same 1.5B base model (e.g., Open-RS2 at **41.60%**).
- The best Tina model obtained a **>20%** reasoning performance increase and **43.33%** Pass@1 accuracy on AIME24, with a post-training and evaluation cost of approximately **$9 USD**, representing a **~260x** cost reduction.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that concise, high-quality datasets (**7k** examples) yielded superior performance compared to significantly larger ones, and low LoRA ranks (e.g., **16** or **32**) proved highly effective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LoRA primarily adapts models to the structural format of reasoning (e.g., step-by-step thinking) rewarded by RL, while largely preserving the base model Hovering around 50%, an impressive gain that could be achieved with minimal resources.
> - A "less compute can yield more performance" phenomenon indicates that increasing training FLOPs with LoRA can sometimes inversely affect reasoning performance, suggesting efficiency in format adaptation.
> - A distinct "phase transition" point in format-related metrics during training correlates with the best-performing checkpoints, implying an optimal stopping point for LoRA-based RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of language models has seen remarkable progress, yet achieving robust, multi-step reasoning remains a significant challenge. This paper operates at the cutting edge of current LLM research, which is increasingly focused on enhancing complex reasoning abilities, especially in a cost-effective manner.

Traditionally, improving reasoning in LLMs has largely relied on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), often involving distillation where smaller models learn from reasoning traces generated by larger, more capable models (e.g., Min et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024). While effective, this approach is limited by the cost and availability of high-quality expert demonstrations and can foster a shallow, imitative form of learning.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) presents an attractive alternative, enabling models to learn directly from verifiable reward signals, potentially leading to more robust and explorative reasoning paths (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; Lambert et al., 2025). However, RL pipelines are notoriously complex and resource-intensive, typically requiring substantial computational power and large models (tens of billions of parameters).

"Tina" directly challenges this high-resource paradigm. It fits into a burgeoning sub-field known as "open-source reasoning replicas," which aims to reproduce or surpass the performance of proprietary, advanced reasoning models (like OpenAI's o1-preview) using open-source resources and more efficient methods. The paper explicitly places Tina alongside contemporary efforts such as STILL, Sky-T1, SimpleRL, PRIME, DeepScaleR, s1, L1, OREAL, OpenThinker, Open Reasoner Zero, and Open-RS, all released around late 2024 or early 2025. These projects collectively explore various strategies, including imitation learning, scaling laws, lightweight RL, process supervision, and self-correction, often targeting smaller model sizes or more accessible training budgets.

Tina distinguishes itself within this landscape by pushing the boundaries of cost-efficiency and minimalism. While many of the aforementioned "open-source reasoning replicas" still rely on full-parameter training (even if on smaller models or curated data), Tina specifically investigates the synergistic combination of a *tiny* base model (1.5B parameters) with *parameter-efficient* reinforcement learning via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). This focus on extreme efficiency, particularly regarding the post-training phase, is a crucial differentiator and a direct response to the prevailing high-cost nature of RL for LLMs. By demonstrating that strong reasoning can be achieved with minimal computational and financial investment, Tina aims to democratize RL-based reasoning research, making it accessible to a much wider community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is encapsulated by the fundamental question posed by the authors: "How cost-effectively can strong reasoning abilities be achieved in language models?" This question directly addresses the primary barrier to wider adoption and research in RL for LLMs: its prohibitive cost and resource demands.

Driven by this core question, the key objectives and motivations behind Tina are:

- **Achieve High Cost-Efficiency:** The central aim is to develop language models with substantial reasoning performance using only minimal resources. This involves leveraging parameter-efficient updates during RL.
- **Demonstrate Surprising Effectiveness:** To show that a minimalist approach, combining a tiny base model (1.5B parameters) with LoRA-based RL, can yield reasoning performance competitive with, and sometimes superior to, state-of-the-art (SOTA) RL reasoning models built upon the same base model, but at a "tiny fraction" of the computational post-training cost.
- **Democratize RL Reasoning:** By providing a reproducible and highly cost-effective approach (e.g., $9 USD post-training and evaluation cost for the best model), the authors aim to lower the entry barrier for researchers and practitioners, enabling wider participation in the exploration of RL techniques for language models without requiring extensive computational resources.
- **Investigate Rapid Reasoning Format Adaptation:** To propose and partially validate a hypothesis that LoRA's effectiveness and efficiency stem from its ability to rapidly adapt the model to the structural format of reasoning rewarded by RL, while largely preserving the base model’s underlying knowledge. This suggests a more compute-efficient process than deep knowledge integration achieved via full-parameter training.
- **Promote Open Research:** In service of accessibility and open science, a critical motivation is to fully open-source all code, training logs, and model weights & checkpoints, facilitating reproducibility and further advancements by the community.

In essence, Tina seeks to prove that "less is more" in the context of RL-driven reasoning, challenging the notion that only massive models with extensive training can achieve high-level reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Tina is meticulously designed around the principle of **minimalism and efficiency**.

**Base Model Selection:**
The authors chose `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B` as their base model. This 1.5 billion parameter model is intentionally "tiny" compared to typical LLMs used for reasoning tasks (which often have tens of billions of parameters). The choice is strategic:
- Its lineage (DeepSeek/Qwen) and distillation process suggest it already possesses a stronger initial reasoning aptitude than a generic pre-trained model of similar size. This allows the researchers to isolate and measure the incremental reasoning enhancements from RL.
- Its compact size dramatically lowers the computational and financial threshold for experimentation.

**Parameter-Efficient Post-Training with LoRA:**
The core of Tina's efficiency lies in applying **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** during the Reinforcement Learning phase. LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique that modifies a model's behavior by training only an exceptionally small number of new, low-rank parameters (matrices A and B in the formulation). This means that instead of updating all parameters of the 1.5B model, only a small fraction is trained, drastically reducing computational cost and memory footprint. The default LoRA implementation from the PEFT library was used, with LoRA modules applied to `query`, `key`, `value`, and `dense` layers, using a rank of 32, alpha of 128, and dropout of 0.05.

**Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:**
The RL component of the training pipeline primarily employed a **GRPO-style algorithm** (Group Relative Policy Optimization). GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), a variant of PPO, removes the need for a separate value network by using a group-based baseline for advantage estimation, thereby improving training efficiency and reward alignment. For ablation studies, Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), a modification of GRPO, was also explored.

**Training Pipeline & Datasets:**
To ensure fair comparisons and enable precise ablations, Tina models were post-trained using datasets and setups from publicly available reasoning models, ensuring direct comparability:
- **STILL-3-1.5B-preview (33k traces):** Focused on slow-thinking reasoning through iterative RL on mathematics competition problems.
- **DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (40k problem-answer pairs):** Focused on long-context mathematical reasoning.
- **Open-RS1/2/3 (small, high-quality datasets):** Curated from s1 and DeepScaleR, specifically for 1.5B models trained via RL. The `Tina-Open-RS` models replicated these setups, including data splits and reward scaffolding.
- Ablation studies also included `OpenR1` (93.7k samples), `OpenThoughts` (66.1k samples), and `LIMR` (1.39k samples) datasets to evaluate the impact of data size and quality.

**Training Setup: Infrastructure and Budget:**
- **Codebase:** The implementation built upon `OpenR1` (a fully open reproduction of DeepSeek-R1), which integrates `Accelerate` and `Trl` libraries with DeepSpeed ZeRO optimization. This inherited scaffolding and utilities for RL methods.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key parameters were replicated from `OpenR1` and `OpenRS`. The authors deliberately avoided costly hyperparameter search, largely fixing settings across runs and only varying specific factors for ablation studies. This strategy reinforces the "tiny cost" ethos.
- **Hardware:** A minimal setup of only **two NVIDIA L40S GPUs** was used. To achieve this, the RL training process and the vLLM inference engine (for sample generation) were co-located on the same GPUs by constraining vLLM's memory usage. This "constrained" approach significantly reduced hardware requirements, even if it might increase wall-clock training time compared to dedicated inference GPUs.
- **Budget:** This minimalist approach resulted in remarkably low costs:
 - **$9 USD** for reproducing the best Tina checkpoint (including post-training and evaluation).
 - **$526 USD** for reproducing *all* experiments presented in the paper from scratch.
 These figures represent a drastic reduction in computational costs compared to typical LLM training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several compelling findings that underscore the efficacy and efficiency of the Tina approach:

**a. Surprising Effectiveness of Efficient RL Reasoning:**
- Tina models achieved reasoning performance that is **competitive with, and in some cases, superior to SOTA RL reasoning models built upon the same 1.5B base model**, despite using only LoRA for parameter-efficient updates.
- For instance, the best Tina model (Tina-Open-RS2) achieved an average performance of 50.60% across six challenging reasoning benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC23, MATH500, GPQA, Minerva), surpassing its corresponding baseline (Open-RS2 at 41.60%).
- Notably, the best Tina model achieved a **>20% reasoning performance increase and 43.33% Pass@1 accuracy on AIME24**, at an estimated **$9 USD** post-training and evaluation cost. This represents a staggering **~260x cost reduction** compared to existing SOTA models.
- The models achieved strong results with remarkably limited training durations, typically using only 19% to 57% of a full training epoch, highlighting rapid adaptation.

**b. Rapid Reasoning Format Adaptation Hypothesis & "Less is More" Phenomenon:**
- The authors hypothesize that LoRA's effectiveness stems from its ability to **rapidly adapt the model to the structural format of reasoning rewarded by RL** (e.g., step-by-step thinking), while largely preserving the base model’s existing knowledge.
- Empirically, they observe a "less compute can yield more performance" phenomenon: plotting reasoning performance against approximate training FLOPs shows that Tina models achieve comparable or superior scores to fully fine-tuned baselines with orders of magnitude fewer FLOPs. Counter-intuitively, in LoRA models, *increased training compute sometimes inversely affects performance*, unlike full-parameter models. This supports the idea that LoRA efficiently teaches the model *how to format* its knowledge rather than relearning it.

**c. Phase Transition in LoRA-based RL:**
- Analysis of training logs reveals a distinct "phase transition" or "turning point" in format-related metrics (format reward, completion length) across most Tina models. Around this point, format reward often peaks or destabilizes, and completion length frequently reaches a minimum.
- Crucially, the **best-performing checkpoint consistently occurred just prior to or around this observed phase transition point** in the format metrics. This suggests that while learning the correct output format is essential and efficiently achieved by LoRA, pushing further on format-centric optimization alone does not necessarily improve reasoning accuracy and may even be detrimental.

**d. Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Impact of Training Dataset:** Quality over quantity. The Tina-Open-RS model, trained on a concise 7k example dataset, outperformed models trained on significantly larger datasets (e.g., 93.7k samples), achieving the highest average score (50.60%). This reinforces the value of curated, high-quality data.
- **Sensitivity to Learning Rate:** Optimal performance was found at a learning rate of 1x10^-6, though results across tested values were robust, indicating that effective results can be obtained without extensive tuning.
- **Effect of LoRA Rank:** Highly parameter-efficient configurations (low ranks like 16 or 32) proved effective, with rank 16 achieving peak performance (48.92%) in its ablation set. This validates that minimal trainable parameters are sufficient.
- **Comparison of RL Algorithms:** Both GRPO and Dr.GRPO yielded similar peak performances. However, **Dr.GRPO reached its best checkpoint significantly earlier** in the training process (17% vs. 57% of an epoch), suggesting potential advantages in sample efficiency and faster convergence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Tina" project carries significant implications for the future of LLM research, particularly in the domain of reasoning and accessibility:

1. **Democratization of RL Reasoning Research:** The most direct and impactful contribution is the drastic reduction in computational and financial barriers for exploring RL techniques in language models. By demonstrating strong reasoning capabilities with a post-training cost as low as $9 USD for a single model and $526 USD for all experiments, Tina makes advanced RL research accessible to smaller research groups, individual researchers, and educational institutions without massive budgets or specialized hardware. This fosters wider participation, accelerates innovation, and promotes more diverse research perspectives.

2. **Validation of Parameter-Efficient RL:** Tina provides strong empirical evidence that Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) can be highly effective for instilling complex reasoning abilities via RL, even in relatively small base models. This validates the potential of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods beyond supervised learning scenarios, suggesting they can be a key strategy for scaling RL applications.

3. **Insights into Model Learning Dynamics:** The "Rapid Reasoning Format Adaptation Hypothesis" and the observation of "Phase Transition in LoRA-based RL" offer crucial theoretical insights into how LLMs learn reasoning. The idea that LoRA primarily teaches the model *how to format* its existing knowledge, rather than deeply integrating new knowledge, provides a valuable framework for understanding efficiency gains. This could guide future research towards more targeted and efficient training strategies for specific capabilities.

4. **Challenging the "Bigger is Better" Paradigm:** Tina directly challenges the prevalent assumption that only increasingly large models trained with enormous resources can achieve SOTA reasoning. It showcases that intelligent application of efficient techniques to smaller models can yield competitive or even superior results in certain reasoning tasks, leading to more sustainable and responsible AI development.

5. **Foundation for Future Accessible Research:** By fully open-sourcing all code, training logs, and model weights & checkpoints, the authors provide an invaluable resource for the community. This commitment to open science ensures reproducibility, allows for direct comparisons, and provides a robust foundation upon which others can build, iterate, and further optimize RL-driven reasoning techniques.

Despite its encouraging results, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the inherent ceiling of a 1.5B parameter model compared to much larger counterparts for complex multi-step problems, and the focused scope on mathematical/formal logic reasoning. However, these limitations do not diminish the core message: Tina offers a powerful and accessible pathway to advancing reasoning capabilities in language models, shifting the focus towards efficiency and broad participation in the research ecosystem.
