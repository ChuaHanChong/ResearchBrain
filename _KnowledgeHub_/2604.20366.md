# Mitigating Hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models without Performance Degradation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Science and Technology of China and the National University of Singapore developed MPD, a framework that effectively mitigates hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models. This method achieved a 23.4% reduction in object hallucinations and maintained 97.4% of general generative capability across various benchmarks like CHAIR and LLaVA-Bench.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently produce hallucinations (fabricating non-existent objects, misattributing properties), compromising their reliability for practical applications.
- Existing representation intervention methods for hallucination mitigation often degrade LVLMs' general generative capabilities due to incomplete disentanglement of hallucinatory and general semantic components.
- Prior methods use non-selective and extensive parameter updates, disrupting original model distributions and leading to issues like semantic incoherence or lexical repetition.

## Method

- Introduces **MPD**, a dual-stage framework that uses ==contrastive vision-language pairs== to identify and address hallucinations without performance degradation.
- Employs an ==orthogonal projection== method to accurately disentangle 'pure' hallucination components from general semantic components within the model's hidden representations.
- Selectively updates only a small, relevant subset of model parameters using a ==projection operator== to suppress hallucination-inducing capacity while minimally impacting other model behaviors.

## Results

- MPD significantly reduced object hallucinations, achieving CHAIR_S scores of **12.80** and CHAIR_I of **4.20** on LLaVA-1.5-7B, outperforming previous baselines like Nullu.
- The method preserved or improved general generative capacity, with LLaVA-Bench accuracy increasing from **5.59** to **6.39** and detailedness from **4.72** to **5.52** for LLaVA-1.5-7B.
- It demonstrated robust performance across multiple LVLMs (MiniGPT-4 V2, LLaVA-1.5-7B, mPLUG-Owl2) and various benchmarks (POPE, HallusionBench, MME), with minimal inference latency (**3.7s** per query).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucinations stem from subtle deviations in hidden representations, requiring precise disentanglement from intertwined general semantic components.
> - A projection-based residual approach provides a more accurate estimation of disentangled hallucination signals compared to naive difference-based methods.
> - Targeted and selective parameter modifications are crucial for mitigating hallucinations effectively without compromising the LVLM's overall generative quality or introducing widespread disruption.
