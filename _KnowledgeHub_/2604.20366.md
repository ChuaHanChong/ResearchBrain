# Mitigating Hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models without Performance Degradation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Science and Technology of China and the National University of Singapore developed MPD, a framework that effectively mitigates hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models. This method achieved a 23.4% reduction in object hallucinations and maintained 97.4% of general generative capability across various benchmarks like CHAIR and LLaVA-Bench.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently produce hallucinations (fabricating non-existent objects, misattributing properties), compromising their reliability for practical applications.
- Existing representation intervention methods for hallucination mitigation often degrade LVLMs' general generative capabilities due to incomplete disentanglement of hallucinatory and general semantic components.
- Prior methods use non-selective and extensive parameter updates, disrupting original model distributions and leading to issues like semantic incoherence or lexical repetition.

## Method

- Introduces **MPD**, a dual-stage framework that uses ==contrastive vision-language pairs== to identify and address hallucinations without performance degradation.
- Employs an ==orthogonal projection== method to accurately disentangle 'pure' hallucination components from general semantic components within the model's hidden representations.
- Selectively updates only a small, relevant subset of model parameters using a ==projection operator== to suppress hallucination-inducing capacity while minimally impacting other model behaviors.

## Results

- MPD significantly reduced object hallucinations, achieving CHAIR_S scores of **12.80** and CHAIR_I of **4.20** on LLaVA-1.5-7B, outperforming previous baselines like Nullu.
- The method preserved or improved general generative capacity, with LLaVA-Bench accuracy increasing from **5.59** to **6.39** and detailedness from **4.72** to **5.52** for LLaVA-1.5-7B.
- It demonstrated robust performance across multiple LVLMs (MiniGPT-4 V2, LLaVA-1.5-7B, mPLUG-Owl2) and various benchmarks (POPE, HallusionBench, MME), with minimal inference latency (**3.7s** per query).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucinations stem from subtle deviations in hidden representations, requiring precise disentanglement from intertwined general semantic components.
> - A projection-based residual approach provides a more accurate estimation of disentangled hallucination signals compared to naive difference-based methods.
> - Targeted and selective parameter modifications are crucial for mitigating hallucinations effectively without compromising the LVLM's overall generative quality or introducing widespread disruption.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in cross-modal understanding and generation. However, a significant limitation of these models is the frequent occurrence of "hallucination phenomena," where generated textual descriptions misrepresent visual content. These hallucinations manifest as fabricating non-existent objects, misattributing visual properties, or describing erroneous spatial relationships. Such errors can compromise the reliability of LVLM outputs, hinder practical applications requiring accurate vision-language alignment, and pose risks related to misinformation and safety-critical deployments.

Initially, approaches to mitigate hallucination primarily involved fine-tuning LVLMs on manually annotated datasets that were curated to be devoid of hallucinatory content. While effective, this method is computationally expensive and time-consuming, limiting its scalability.

More recent research has shifted towards "representation intervention" methods. This paradigm typically involves a two-stage process:
1. **Component Extraction:** Identifying and extracting hallucinatory components from the model's hidden representations by analyzing internal activations when the model produces both hallucinated and faithful outputs.
2. **Parameter Update:** Subsequently updating model parameters to reduce the influence of these identified hallucinatory components.

This representation intervention approach offers the advantage of eliminating the need for extensive dataset reconstruction and enables real-time hallucination mitigation. However, the authors of the current paper observe that existing representation intervention methods often lead to a degradation of the LVLMs' general generative capabilities. They attribute this degradation to two primary issues:
- **Incomplete Component Disentanglement:** Hallucinatory components are frequently coupled with general semantic components. Current methods tend to overlook these shared elements, leading to the unintentional removal of general semantic information alongside the hallucinatory content. This can cause the internal representations of post-intervention LVLMs to diverge significantly from their original distributions.
- **Non-Selective Parameter Updates:** Existing methods often apply substantial perturbations to all weights within targeted layers, modifying a large number of parameters. Such widespread updates can induce overfitting and disrupt the model's original parameter distributions, thereby compromising its broader generative performance, potentially leading to semantic incoherence and lexical repetition.

This work, therefore, positions itself as an advancement in the field of hallucination mitigation within LVLMs, specifically addressing the observed trade-off between hallucination suppression and the preservation of general generative performance in existing representation intervention methods. It aims to develop a framework that effectively reduces hallucinations while maintaining the model's overall generative quality and efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, termed MPD (Mitigating Hallucinations without Performance Degradation), that effectively mitigates hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) without concurrently degrading their general generative capabilities.

The motivation for this objective stems from critical limitations observed in prior hallucination mitigation techniques, particularly representation-based intervention methods:

1. **Observed Performance Degradation in Existing Methods:** The authors empirically note that current representation intervention methods, while efficient in reducing hallucinations, tend to diminish the general generative capacity of LVLMs. This manifests as increased semantic incoherence and lexical repetition in the generated outputs following intervention, compared to the original models. This degradation is considered a significant drawback for practical applications where both factual accuracy and high-quality generation are required.

2. **Incomplete Extraction of Hallucination Components:** A key issue identified is that hallucinatory components are often deeply intertwined with general semantic components within the model's hidden representations. Existing methods, according to the authors, do not fully disentangle these components. This incomplete separation leads to the unintended removal of valuable general semantics when attempting to suppress hallucinations. The consequence is a noticeable shift in the distribution of hidden representations in post-intervened models, indicating a disruption of the model's learned knowledge structure. The authors illustrate this by showing that the distribution of representations in LVLMs after prior interventions drifts significantly from that of the original model.

3. **Non-Selective and Extensive Parameter Updates:** Another motivational factor is the coarse-grained nature of parameter updates in existing intervention methods. These methods frequently modify a substantial number of parameters across entire layers of the model. For instance, modifying hundreds of millions of parameters through global weight adjustments can disrupt the model's finely tuned parameter distributions, potentially inducing overfitting and thereby impairing its overall generative versatility. The authors present evidence that prior methods involve much larger-scale parameter perturbations compared to their proposed approach.

To address these identified limitations, the MPD framework is motivated by the need for a dual-stage approach that incorporates two essential factors:
- **Semantic-aware Component Disentanglement:** This aims to precisely extract "pure" hallucination components from hidden representations, preventing the accidental removal of general semantics.
- **Interpretable and Selective Parameter Updates:** This focuses on modifying only those parameters that are most directly relevant to the identified hallucinatory components, thereby minimizing widespread disruption to the model's existing knowledge and preserving its general generative capacity.

By addressing these specific shortcomings, the research seeks to bridge the gap between efficiency and effectiveness in hallucination mitigation, offering a solution that is both performant and practical for real-world LVLM deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, MPD (Mitigating Hallucinations without Performance Degradation), is a dual-stage framework designed to address the challenges of incomplete hallucination component extraction and non-selective parameter updates observed in previous representation intervention techniques.

The framework operates on a typical LVLM architecture, which comprises a vision encoder, a text encoder, and a large language model (LLM) as the decoder. Given an image and a textual input (e.g., a question), the vision encoder extracts visual features, which are then projected into a shared embedding space with the text and concatenated for auto-regressive decoding.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Contrastive Pair Construction

To facilitate the disentanglement of hallucinated and faithful components, MPD constructs contrastive vision-language pairs. Each pair shares the same image but contains two different textual prompts:
- **Hallucination-inducing prompt (x−i):** Designed to elicit hallucinatory content from the LVLM.
- **Semantically equivalent non-hallucinatory prompt (x+i):** Provides a faithful visual description grounded in the image, aiming for an output devoid of hallucinations.

This dataset, D = {(x+i, x−i)}, is constructed offline using an auxiliary large language model (LLM), such as GPT-3.5 or GPT-5.1, following a protocol from prior work.

#### 3.2. Hallucination Component Extraction

This is the first stage of MPD, focusing on obtaining "pure" hallucination components. Hallucinations are hypothesized to arise from subtle deviations in hidden representations. The process involves:
1. **Token-level Feature Extraction:** For each image-caption pair, token embeddings are extracted from specified transformer layers (ℓ ∈ {L0..., L}) of the base LLM for both faithful (x+i,ℓ) and hallucinated (x−i,ℓ) descriptions.
2. **Compact Representation:** The token embeddings are averaged across the sequence to obtain compact vector representations, resulting in matrices X+ℓ and X−ℓ, where N is the number of samples and D is the feature dimension.
3. **Modeling Hallucinated Representation:** The hallucinated representation X−ℓ is conceptually decomposed as:
 X−ℓ = Xrealℓ + Xhallℓ + ϵ−
 where Xrealℓ represents grounded semantics aligned with the image, Xhallℓ captures the hallucinatory semantics, and ϵ− is residual noise.
4. **Disentanglement via Orthogonal Projection:**
 - The row space of X+ℓ (faithful descriptions) is considered to approximate the "grounded semantic subspace."
 - Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is performed on X+ℓ: X+ℓ = UℓΣℓV⊤ℓ. The top-C left singular vectors, Uℓ ∈ R N×C, are used.
 - A projection matrix Pℓ is formed from Uℓ (Pℓ = UℓU⊤ℓ ∈ R N×N). This projection matrix is then implicitly or explicitly used to project features in the D-dimensional space. Assuming `I` is a DxD identity matrix, the hallucination component is derived by projecting the hallucinatory representations onto the orthogonal complement of the faithful subspace.
 - The grounded component from X−ℓ is estimated as ˆX−ℓ = PℓX−ℓ.
 - The pure hallucinatory component (˜Xℓ) is then extracted as the residual, lying orthogonal to the faithful subspace:
 ˜Xℓ = X−ℓ − ˆX−ℓ = (I − Pℓ)X−ℓ (where I is an identity matrix in the feature space).

#### 3.3. Parameters Updating

The second stage of MPD focuses on selectively updating model parameters to suppress the influence of the extracted hallucination components.
1. **Parameter Selection:**
 - Given the extracted hallucination component ˜Xℓ at layer ℓ, the objective is to identify weight parameters (Wℓ ∈ R L×D, where L is the number of neurons) most responsible for hallucinated generation.
 - For each weight vector w(i)ℓ (a row in Wℓ), its average cosine similarity (s_i) with the sample-wise hallucination vectors ˜xℓ,j (rows of ˜Xℓ) is computed.
 - The top-K weight vectors with the highest similarity scores s_i are selected. Their indices are denoted Ihallℓ. These are considered the target parameters for editing.
2. **Parameter Editing:**
 - A projection operator ˜Qℓ is constructed. This operator projects vectors onto the orthogonal complement (null space) of the subspace spanned by ˜Xℓ. The formula for ˜Qℓ is:
 ˜Qℓ = I − ˜X⊤ℓ ( ˜Xℓ ˜X⊤ℓ )−1 ˜Xℓ
 This projection matrix (I in R D×D, ˜Xℓ implicitly treated to define a subspace in R D) effectively removes directions associated with the hallucinatory component.
 - For each selected weight vector w(i)ℓ (where i ∈ Ihallℓ), the model parameter is updated by projecting it using ˜Qℓ:
 w(i)ℓ ← ˜Qℓw(i)ℓ
 This targeted projection aims to suppress hallucination-inducing capacity with minimal impact on unrelated behaviors, as only a subset of weights is modified.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical justification for using the projection-based residual (˜Xℓ) for hallucination extraction, comparing it against a naive difference-based estimate (˜Xdiffℓ = X−ℓ − X+ℓ). Proposition 1 asserts that ˜Xℓ provides a more accurate estimation of the disentangled hallucination signal (Xhall,⊥ℓ) than ˜Xdiffℓ, in terms of expected squared error. The proof demonstrates that the projection method effectively isolates unique hallucinated concepts by removing shared semantic components, leading to a smaller error term.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details (from Appendix)

- **Decoding:** Beam search with 3 beams.
- **Editing Layers:** Transformer layers ℓ from 16 to 32 were selected.
- **Retained Principal Components (C):** 2500.
- **Number of Selected Weights (K):** 6000.
- **Hardware:** A single A40 GPU (40G) for experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments across three representative LVLMs (MiniGPT-4 V2, LLaVA-1.5-7B, and mPLUG-Owl2) and various benchmarks to evaluate MPD's efficacy in mitigating object hallucinations and preserving generative capacity.

#### 4.1. Performance on Object Hallucinations (RQ1)

- **CHAIR Benchmark:** MPD exhibited a reduction in both sentence-level (CHAIR_S) and instance-level (CHAIR_I) hallucinations. For instance, on LLaVA-1.5-7B, MPD achieved a CHAIR_S score of 12.80 and CHAIR_I of 4.20, outperforming baselines like Nullu (15.20 CHAIR_S, 5.30 CHAIR_I) and HALC (16.90 CHAIR_S, 5.72 CHAIR_I). The BLEU scores, which indicate caption quality, remained competitive, suggesting that hallucination reduction did not significantly compromise generation quality.
- **POPE Benchmark:** MPD consistently achieved high F1 scores across all three query sampling strategies (random, popular, and adversarial) for object presence detection. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, MPD obtained F1 scores of 88.20 (random), 85.52 (popular), and 80.18 (adversarial), surpassing the original models and Nullu baselines in each category. This indicates MPD's robustness and generalizability in suppressing hallucinations under varied conditions, including complex visual scenes.
- **HallusionBench Dataset:** MPD demonstrated improvements across metrics (fACC, qACC, easyA, hardA, aACC) on this benchmark, which assesses fine-grained hallucinations beyond mere object existence. For LLaVA-1.5-7B, MPD achieved an aACC of 44.3, compared to 41.5 for the original model and 44.2 for Nullu. This suggests the method's ability to generalize beyond object-centric hallucinations.
- **Additional Benchmarks (OPOPE, AMBER, MMHalBench - Appendix C.1):** Further evaluations confirmed MPD's performance. On OPOPE, MPD showed high accuracy and F1 scores. On AMBER, it achieved a favorable trade-off between object coverage and hallucination rate, indicating improved grounding without sacrificing content completeness. On MMHalBench, MPD resulted in higher GPT-4-evaluated response scores and lower hallucination rates, suggesting more faithful and informative responses.

#### 4.2. Performance on Generative Capacity (RQ2)

- **LLaVA-Bench:** MPD consistently improved both the Accuracy and Detailedness scores as evaluated by GPT-4V. For LLaVA-1.5-7B, accuracy increased from 5.59 to 6.39, and detailedness from 4.72 to 5.52. This suggests that MPD enhanced factual alignment and produced richer responses without negatively impacting fluency.
- **MME Benchmark:** MPD achieved strong performance on hallucination-sensitive subsets of MME. It recorded the highest scores on "Existence" and "Count" (which measure object hallucination) and remained competitive on "Position" and "Color." This indicates an enhancement in grounding accuracy for perception-oriented tasks.
- **Analysis of Hidden Representations:** A qualitative analysis using PCA revealed that MPD induced smaller shifts in the distribution of hidden features compared to Nullu. This closer alignment with the original model's representations indicates that MPD preserves the internal structure and general capabilities of the LVLM more effectively.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (RQ3)

- **Impact of Editing Layers:** Experiments showed that editing deeper transformer layers (e.g., layers 16-32) yielded more effective hallucination suppression, with the specified range producing the best CHAIR scores. Narrower or shallower editing ranges resulted in weaker performance, suggesting the need to address hallucinatory components that manifest at deeper layers.
- **Impact of Sample Size:** Increasing the number of editing samples (from 500 to 5000) for contrastive pair construction consistently improved CHAIR_S scores. However, the method demonstrated efficacy even with a smaller number of samples (e.g., 500), indicating data efficiency.
- **Effect of Selected Weights (K):** Performance improved as the number of selectively updated weights (K) increased, up to a certain point (approximately 5x10^3). Beyond this, gains were marginal, suggesting that hallucination generation might be concentrated in a limited subset of parameters and that overly broad edits offer diminishing returns.
- **Effect of Auxiliary LLM Choice:** Using different auxiliary LLMs (GPT-3.5 vs. GPT-5.1) for generating contrastive pairs showed consistent hallucination reductions and comparable BLEU scores, indicating the robustness of MPD to the choice of the auxiliary model.
- **Generalization to Non-Object Hallucinations (V* Bench, MMMU, MathVision):** MPD also showed improvements in attribute, spatial, and overall scores on V* Bench and yielded consistent gains on multimodal reasoning benchmarks like MMMU and MathVision. This suggests that the editing strategy does not harm broader multimodal reasoning abilities and can even offer slight benefits.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Comparison (Appendix D)

- MPD introduced minimal inference latency increase (3.7 seconds per query on LLaVA-1.5 vs. 3.1 seconds for greedy decoding) and did not require additional modules during the decoding phase. This indicates an efficient approach that does not add significant computational overhead during actual inference.

In summary, the main findings indicate that MPD effectively mitigates hallucinations in LVLMs across various evaluation paradigms, including object-centric and more fine-grained hallucinations, while simultaneously preserving or even improving the models' general generative capacity. This is achieved through a targeted, dual-stage approach that disentangles hallucination components and selectively updates relevant parameters, leading to both high performance and computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MPD presents several significant implications for the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and their applications:

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness:** Hallucinations are a major impediment to the widespread adoption of LVLMs, particularly in high-stakes domains where factual accuracy is paramount (e.g., medical imaging analysis, legal document interpretation, or autonomous systems). By demonstrating a substantial reduction in hallucinations (quantified as 23.4%) while preserving generative capabilities, MPD contributes to making LVLMs more reliable and trustworthy. This directly addresses the "critical divergence where generated textual descriptions systematically misrepresent visual content," which has been a primary concern.

2. **Preservation of Generative Quality:** A key innovation of MPD is its ability to mitigate hallucinations "without performance degradation," maintaining 97.4% of general generative capability. This differentiates it from prior representation intervention methods that reportedly compromised the model's fluency, coherence, and overall descriptive quality. This ensures that improved factual grounding does not come at the cost of less useful or repetitive outputs, making LVLMs more practically valuable for open-ended captioning, reasoning, and conversational tasks.

3. **Computational Efficiency for Deployment:** MPD introduces no additional computational cost during inference compared to the original models. This efficiency is critical for real-world deployment, especially in applications requiring low latency or operating on resource-constrained devices. The pre-inference parameter editing approach minimizes runtime overhead, making it a scalable solution for hallucination mitigation.

4. **Improved Model Interpretability and Control:** The dual-stage framework, with its emphasis on "semantic-aware component disentanglement" and "interpretable parameter updates," offers a more fine-grained approach to understanding and controlling LVLM behavior. By identifying and selectively modifying parameters most relevant to hallucinations, the method provides insights into where and how hallucinatory tendencies are encoded within the model. This could inform future research into targeted model editing, debiasing techniques, and enhancing the safety alignment of generative AI.

5. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The method's consistent performance across multiple LVLM architectures (MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl2, LLaVA-1.5) and diverse evaluation benchmarks (CHAIR, POPE, MME, LLaVA-Bench, HallusionBench, V* Bench, MMMU, MathVision) suggests a high degree of robustness and generalizability. This indicates that MPD is not a model-specific fix but a broadly applicable technique for improving LVLM behavior. Its effectiveness in non-object-centric hallucination benchmarks further extends its potential impact.

6. **Theoretical Justification:** The inclusion of a theoretical analysis supporting the projection-based residual for hallucination extraction provides a stronger foundation for the methodology. This rigorous approach contributes to the scientific understanding of why the proposed disentanglement method is more accurate in isolating hallucination-specific components.

The acknowledged limitations, such as not addressing hallucinations from broader data biases or prompt limitations, and the need for further evaluation under highly ambiguous visual inputs, suggest avenues for future research. However, the current contribution represents a step towards developing more reliable and practical LVLMs by addressing a critical challenge without sacrificing the models' inherent generative strengths.
