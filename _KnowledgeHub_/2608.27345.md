# PAWBench: How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling?

> [!summary] Summary
> PAWBench is a diagnostic benchmark testing whether video generators used as world models are probabilistically aligned: under a fixed image and action, repeated rollouts should recover both which futures are possible and how likely each is. It pairs 50 scenarios (PAW-Calibration with reference probabilities, PAW-Coverage with enumerable supports) with PAWEval, a rubric judge converting rollouts into outcome distributions. Across eleven systems, none matches reference probabilities while covering valid futures.

## Problem

- Existing video-generation benchmarks score each rollout in isolation for visual quality, coherence, text alignment or physical plausibility, never testing the induced distribution over futures.
- Stochastic physical processes admit several valid futures, yet single-sample evaluation cannot distinguish a model collapsing onto one mode from one allocating probability mass correctly.
- World-model evaluations judge action-conditioned prediction, physical-law violations, or downstream task success, leaving calibration and support recovery under a fixed observation-action pair untested.

## Method

- **PAWBench**: 50 curated scenarios over eight mechanism groups, split into ==PAW-Calibration== (analytic or symmetry-derived reference distributions) and ==PAW-Coverage== (enumerable valid outcomes, unspecifiable probabilities).
- **PAWEval**: a ==rubric-based Gemini 3.5 Flash judge== maps each of K=50 rollouts to a terminal outcome or a readout failure, scoring ==total variation distance==, ==support coverage==, and a ==Scene Pass Rate== gate.
- Three intervention probes: GPT-5.5 prompt engineering plus an ==Oracle-target== variant, ==Couple-to-Control repulsive Gaussian noise coupling== across rollouts, and ==LoRA fine-tuning== on mixtures with varied outcome ratios.

## Results

- No system leads jointly: Cosmos 3 Super I2V has the best Calibration TVD **20.5** at **80%** pass rate, LTX-2.3 the best Coverage **71.7%** at only **72%** pass rate; average observed TVD is **31.2** versus a **9.22** finite-sampling ceiling.
- Prompt engineering worsens calibration for every generator (Cosmos **20.5** to **31.6**); Oracle targets help (MiniMax H3 to **10.8** TVD, **82.9%** coverage) yet generators realize only **37.6-58.1%** of requested outcomes.
- Coupled C2C noise lowers TVD and raises coverage for all three tested models (LTX-2.3 **30.1** to **19.9** TVD, **71.7** to **74.8%** coverage); PAWEval agrees with decisive human labels on **722 of 888** videos (**81.3%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diversity and calibration are separable axes: covering every valid outcome does not imply correct probability mass, so benchmarks should report support recovery and calibration separately.
> - Models underreact to physically causal interventions while overreacting to non-causal textual or visual distractors, indicating their sampled distributions do not track the underlying physical transition.
> - Global training-mixture rebalancing gives only coarse control, improving one scene while degrading another; alignment demands distributions conditioned on each scene's initial physical state.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a specific limitation in the evaluation of contemporary video generation models, particularly as they are increasingly conceptualized as "world models." Recent advancements in video generation have led to systems capable of producing visually plausible, temporally coherent, and instruction-following video continuations from initial observations and prompts. This capability has fostered the view that these models function not merely as content creation tools but as representations of dynamic environments, or "world models."

However, the existing research landscape for evaluating these models primarily focuses on metrics that assess individual video quality, such as visual fidelity, temporal consistency, text-to-video alignment, and physical plausibility. While these criteria are important, they do not comprehensively evaluate a crucial aspect of stochastic physical processes: the ability to accurately represent the distribution of possible future outcomes under identical initial conditions and actions. Many real-world physical events are stochastic, meaning a single observation and action can lead to multiple valid future trajectories with varying probabilities. Current evaluation paradigms, by assessing only individual, plausible rollouts, fail to determine if a model captures this underlying stochasticity. A model might generate plausible individual videos yet exhibit a biased or incomplete understanding of the full range and likelihood of potential futures.

This paper positions itself to bridge this gap by introducing a new framework for evaluating "probabilistic alignment." It draws upon established concepts in world modeling, reinforcement learning, and generative model evaluation (e.g., calibration and coverage metrics) but applies them specifically to the context of video generation as world modeling. By formalizing probabilistic alignment and developing a dedicated benchmark, the research provides a diagnostic tool to assess whether video generators can not only predict *a* plausible future but also the *distribution* of all plausible futures, a requirement crucial for applications in planning, interaction, and intelligent agency. The work complements existing benchmarks by focusing on the distributional properties of generated futures rather than solely on the quality of single samples.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to quantify the extent to which current video generation models, when interpreted as world models, achieve **probabilistic alignment**. This concept is defined as the model's ability to reproduce not only a plausible trajectory but also the correct distribution of possible behaviors given the same initial observation and action. The authors motivate this objective by highlighting several key points:

- **Stochasticity of Real-World Processes:** Many physical processes are inherently stochastic, meaning the same initial conditions can lead to multiple valid outcomes. A comprehensive world model must therefore account for this variability, capturing both the range of physically possible futures and their relative likelihoods.
- **Limitations of Current Evaluation Metrics:** Existing benchmarks predominantly evaluate video generators based on individual video attributes such as visual quality, temporal coherence, text alignment, and single-sample physical plausibility. These evaluations do not assess whether repeated generations from the same input accurately recover the underlying probability distribution of future events. A model might produce individually plausible videos while either collapsing to a narrow subset of valid outcomes or misallocating probability mass among them.
- **Importance for Downstream Tasks:** For world models to be useful in interactive and planning contexts, accurately predicting the distribution of outcomes is critical. Decisions often depend on understanding the full spectrum of consequences of an action and their associated probabilities, not just one possible outcome. Misaligned probabilistic predictions can lead to suboptimal or erroneous decisions.
- **Formalizing a Missing Criterion:** The paper aims to formalize "probabilistic alignment" as a measurable, distributional criterion for world models. This formalization differentiates between *support alignment* (capturing all possible outcomes) and *probability-mass alignment* (assigning correct relative likelihoods to these outcomes).
- **Developing a Diagnostic Benchmark:** To operationalize this criterion, the authors are motivated to introduce a dedicated benchmark, PAWBench, designed to diagnose this specific capability gap. This benchmark is intended to serve as a foundation for future efforts to improve probabilistic alignment in video world models.
- **Investigating Improvement Pathways:** Beyond identifying the gap, the research is motivated to explore potential avenues for improving probabilistic alignment, ranging from inference-time interventions like language prompting and noise sampling to changes in model training.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond judging individual video plausibility to rigorously evaluating the distributional fidelity of video generators as stochastic world models, recognizing this as a fundamental requirement for their reliable deployment in advanced AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology developed in this paper revolves around defining, measuring, and probing "probabilistic alignment" in video generation models.

**Definition of Probabilistic Alignment:**
The authors define a video world model M as representing a conditional distribution $P_M(\tau | x, a)$ over future trajectories $\tau$, given an initial observation $x$ and an action $a$. This trajectory distribution induces an outcome distribution $p_M(y | x, a)$ over a finite set of terminal outcomes $Y$, where $g(\tau) = y$ maps a trajectory to its realized outcome.
- **Support Alignment:** Requires that the model's induced distribution covers all physically possible outcomes, i.e., $supp(p_M(\cdot | x, a)) = Y$.
- **Probability-Mass Alignment:** A stronger requirement that demands the model's outcome distribution matches a reference distribution $q$, i.e., $p_M(\cdot | x, a) = q$.

**PAWBench Design:**
PAWBench is a diagnostic benchmark consisting of 50 scenarios, divided into two complementary suites:
- **PAW-Calibration (25 scenarios):** Features scenarios with analytically specified or symmetry-derived reference distributions ($q$), such as coin tosses or spinners. This suite assesses probability-mass alignment.
- **PAW-Coverage (25 scenarios):** Includes scenarios where valid terminal outcomes can be enumerated but relative probabilities are difficult to specify (e.g., bowling, bottle flips). This suite primarily tests support alignment.
For each scenario, a fixed source image ($x$) and action prompt ($a$) are used across repeated rollouts.

**Scenario Curation:**
Scenarios are curated to meet three requirements for meaningful repeated-rollout statistics:
1. **Visible Stochasticity:** Stochasticity must originate from a visible physical mechanism, not hidden conditions or ambiguous prompts.
2. **Atomic Action:** The action prompt must specify a single, complete intervention.
3. **Finite, Distinguishable Outcomes:** Terminal outcomes must form a finite, visually distinct set, allowing consistent mapping from video to outcome.
Source images are generated (using models like Google DeepMind Nano banana pro, OpenAI GPT Image 2) and manually selected. Action prompts are carefully written, and valid terminal outcomes, along with reference distributions (for PAW-Calibration) and outcome-readout criteria, are defined and reviewed before model evaluation.

**PAWEval Protocol:**
PAWEval is an outcome-level evaluation protocol for repeated rollouts.
1. **Video Generation:** For each model and scenario, $K=50$ video rollouts are generated using the fixed $(x, a)$ pair.
2. **Outcome Readout:** Gemini 3.5 Flash (a VLM) applies a scene-specific rubric to each generated video. This rubric maps readable, in-schema rollouts to a terminal outcome label ($y \in Y$) or identifies them as an "outcome-readout failure" (⊥).
3. **Scene Pass Rate (SPR):** A scene passes the outcome-readout gate if no more than 30 of its 50 rollouts are readout failures (i.e., at least 20 yield readable outcomes).
4. **Metric Calculation (for passing scenes):**
 - An empirical outcome distribution $\hat{p}_M$ is derived from the readable, in-schema outcomes.
 - **PAW-Calibration:** Total Variation Distance (TVD) between $\hat{p}_M$ and the reference distribution $q$ is computed ($1/2 \sum | \hat{p}_M(y) - q(y) |$). Lower TVD indicates better probability-mass alignment.
 - **PAW-Coverage:** Valid-support recovery is measured as the fraction of valid outcomes in $Y$ observed across the repeated rollouts ($|\{y \in Y : \hat{p}_M(y) > 0\}| / |Y|$). Higher coverage indicates broader support recovery.

**Evaluation Setup:**
Eleven diverse video generation models (proprietary and open-source) are benchmarked using $K=50$ rollouts per scenario.

**Intervention Experiments:**
The paper probes probabilistic alignment through three types of interventions:
1. **Prompt Engineering:**
 - **VLM Future Sampling:** Five VLMs are repeatedly queried (K=50) to predict outcomes from the fixed $(x, a)$ pair, with predictions mapped to PAWBench outcomes.
 - **GPT-5.5 Prompt Engineering (PE):** GPT-5.5 predicts an outcome for each rollout and generates a prompt requesting that outcome from the video generator.
 - **Oracle Prompt Engineering (Oracle PE):** Target outcomes, following the reference distribution, are directly specified in generator prompts.
2. **Initial Noise Sampling:** Couple to Control (C2C), a repulsive Gaussian coupling scheme, is applied to the initial noise samples across $K=50$ rollouts for selected models, to enhance exploration of the model's latent space without changing the underlying distribution.
3. **Updating Model Parameters:** LoRA-adapted Wan2.2 models are trained on datasets with varying ratios of left- and right-falling pencil videos (0/100 to 100/0), then evaluated on upright and left-leaning pencil scenes to assess how training data composition shapes outcome distributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation across PAWBench revealed several key findings:

- **Significant Gap in Probabilistic Alignment:** No evaluated video generation model consistently achieved well-aligned probability mass, broad support recovery, and reliable performance across all scenarios. Cosmos 3 Super I2V showed the lowest Calibration TVD, but with a lower Scene Pass Rate (80%), while LTX-2.3 achieved the highest Coverage average, but with only 72% of its Coverage scenes passing the gate. This indicates a general shortfall in probabilistically aligned world modeling.
- **Distinct Failures: Calibration vs. Coverage:** The models demonstrated that recovering a broad range of valid futures (coverage) does not equate to assigning them the correct probabilities (calibration). Models capable of exposing many plausible outcomes often misallocated probability mass, and vice versa. This highlights that PAW-Calibration and PAW-Coverage assess complementary limitations.
- **Insensitivity to Causal Changes, Over-sensitivity to Non-Causal Cues:** Video generators and direct VLM future samplers frequently underreacted to physically causal interventions (where the physical transition and reference distribution change, requiring a shift in model distribution). Conversely, they often overreacted to non-causal cues (e.g., distractor text), shifting probability mass even when the underlying physical process and reference distribution remained unchanged. This suggests models do not reliably distinguish between causal and spurious correlations.
- **Robustness of Evaluation Protocol:**
 - **Rollout Budget:** Increasing the rollout budget from $K=50$ to $K=100$ generally increased support coverage but did not substantially improve calibration. This indicates that the observed calibration gap is not primarily a finite-sampling artifact. A Monte Carlo analysis showed that observed TVD (average 31.2) was significantly higher than the TVD from matched samples drawn from reference distributions (99% of simulations below 9.22), further confirming a genuine calibration issue.
 - **Human Agreement:** PAWEval demonstrated substantial agreement with human judgments, matching decisive human labels in 81.3% of comparable videos. This suggests PAWEval's outcome readout is generally reliable for clear outcomes.
- **Limited Efficacy of Inference-Time Interventions:**
 - **VLM Future Sampling:** Direct predictions from VLMs were found to be misaligned with reference distributions even before video generation, indicating an inherent challenge in generating distribution-aware outcomes via language.
 - **Prompt Engineering (PE):** While PE (using GPT-5.5 to predict and prompt specific outcomes) improved Scene Pass Rate for generators, it generally increased Calibration TVD and only sometimes improved Coverage. Oracle PE, which supplied target outcomes directly, lowered TVD and raised Coverage more effectively. However, even with Oracle PE, generators only realized 37.6-58.1% of requested outcomes, indicating limitations in following precise instructions. Language-based control could shift individual rollouts but not reliably align the aggregate distribution.
 - **Initial Noise Sampling (C2C):** Coupled noise sampling (C2C) successfully broadened the exploration of a model's latent possibilities within a finite budget, leading to modest improvements in both Calibration TVD and Coverage for the same generator. This demonstrated that C2C helps uncover more of the model's *existing* possibilities rather than altering its *learned* underlying distribution.
- **Impact of Training Data Composition:** Experiments with LoRA-adapted models of Wan2.2 showed that altering the relative frequencies of outcomes (e.g., left-fall vs. right-fall pencil videos) in the training data could reshape the generated outcome distribution. Increasing the proportion of left-falling videos in training led to a higher frequency of left-falls in generated videos for both upright and initially left-leaning pencil scenes. However, this shift was global: improving the match for one scene often worsened the other, indicating that models learn global outcome frequencies but struggle to learn scene-conditioned probabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the development and evaluation of advanced AI systems, particularly those that aim to function as world models capable of interaction and planning.

**Establishing a Critical Evaluation Gap:** The primary significance of PAWBench is its clear demonstration of a fundamental gap in current video generators: their inability to achieve probabilistic alignment. This gap is hidden by existing evaluation methods that prioritize individual video quality or single-sample plausibility. By rigorously quantifying this deficiency, the paper highlights that "plausible, diverse, or controllable rollouts do not by themselves establish probabilistically aligned world modeling across different physical states." This reframes a key requirement for next-generation world models.

**Novel Diagnostic Tool:** PAWBench and PAWEval provide a novel and systematic diagnostic framework for assessing probabilistic alignment. The dual-track approach (PAW-Calibration for probability-mass alignment and PAW-Coverage for support alignment) offers a granular understanding of model failures. This benchmark can serve as a standardized tool for researchers and developers to evaluate their models, identify specific weaknesses in capturing stochastic dynamics, and track progress in this area.

**Informing Future Research Directions:** The findings directly inform future research priorities:
- **Model Architectures and Training Objectives:** The consistent miscalibration and poor coverage suggest a need for new model architectures and training objectives that explicitly learn and enforce calibrated distributions over possible futures, rather than just generating plausible single samples.
- **Robust Causal Reasoning:** The observation that models struggle to distinguish causal from non-causal changes points to a need for integrating stronger causal reasoning capabilities into world models.
- **Beyond Terminal Outcomes:** While PAWBench focuses on terminal outcomes for tractability, the authors suggest future work could extend probabilistic alignment evaluation to richer, trajectory-level dynamics, providing a more comprehensive understanding of intermediate physical processes.
- **Efficient Estimation:** The challenge of finite sampling budgets in estimating distributions for large models indicates a need for more efficient and reliable rollout-based estimators.
- **Scaling to Complex Environments:** Extending the diagnostic to longer-horizon, interactive, and embodied environments is a natural next step, crucial for real-world applications.

**Impact on AI Planning and Interaction:** For AI agents to effectively plan and interact in complex, uncertain environments, they must accurately predict not just what *might* happen, but also how *likely* different outcomes are. The demonstrated lack of probabilistic alignment in current models implies that their utility for robust planning, risk assessment, and decision-making in stochastic settings is currently limited. Addressing this gap through the insights provided by PAWBench could lead to more reliable, predictable, and trustworthy AI systems capable of navigating the inherent uncertainties of the physical world.
