# T-JEPA: A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Trajectory Similarity Computation

> [!summary] Summary
> T-JEPA applies the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture to trajectory similarity computation, automating data augmentation and predicting high-level semantic information in the representation space. It consistently outperforms or remains competitive with existing self-supervised methods on five diverse trajectory datasets, demonstrating enhanced robustness to data quality issues and stronger generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised trajectory similarity methods rely heavily on manually defined data augmentation schemes, which are labor-intensive and limit the diversity of learned features.
- Current trajectory data transformations operate at a low 2D Euclidean level, failing to capture intricate high-level semantic variations.
- Real-world trajectory data often suffers from noise, discontinuity, and irregular or low sampling rates, posing challenges for robust representation learning.

## Method

- T-JEPA adapts the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to learn ==trajectory representations== by predicting sampled target representations from corresponding contexts.
- The method automates data augmentation by sampling trajectory information directly in the embedding space, eliminating the need for manual, domain-specific augmentation strategies.
- An `AdjFuse` module is introduced to enrich contextual information by aggregating features from adjacent geographical cells, enhancing robustness to noisy or sparsely sampled data.

## Results

- T-JEPA demonstrated superior or competitive performance in most similar trajectory search tasks, especially outperforming baselines on sparser Foursquare check-in datasets.
- The method exhibited enhanced robustness to down-sampling and distortion, consistently outperforming baselines across most datasets for various noise levels, with the `AdjFuse` module proving critical.
- It showed stronger generalization capabilities, achieving higher Hit Ratios and Recall when approximating various heuristic similarity measures with a frozen encoder, indicating more adaptable learned representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting missing trajectory components at a high semantic level in the representation space, rather than raw data reconstruction, leads to more generalized and semantically rich embeddings.
> - Automating data augmentation through representation space sampling effectively overcomes the limitations of manual, low-level augmentations prevalent in prior contrastive learning approaches.
> - Integrating local spatial context via the `AdjFuse` module significantly improves robustness for real-world trajectory data, particularly those with low or irregular sampling rates.
