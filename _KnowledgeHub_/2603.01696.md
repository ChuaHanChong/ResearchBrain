# Cross-modal Identity Mapping: Minimizing Information Loss in Modality Conversion via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba and the University of Hong Kong developed Cross-modal Identity Mapping (CIM), an annotation-free reinforcement learning framework that enhances Large Vision-Language Models to generate more detailed image captions. CIM quantifies information loss by assessing the consistency and relevance of images retrieved using generated captions, leading to substantial improvements in capturing fine-grained visual details.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently omit or misrepresent fine-grained visual details when generating image captions.
- Quantifying information loss between an image and its textual description is inherently challenging due to the fundamental semantic and structural differences between modalities.
- Existing solutions are limited by costly human annotation for supervised fine-tuning, susceptibility to 'reward hacking' in reinforcement learning, or computational overhead in training-free methods.

## Method

- The Cross-modal Identity Mapping (**CIM**) framework uses an ==annotation-free== reinforcement learning approach to optimize image captioning in LVLMs.
- It introduces two novel metrics derived from information retrieval: Gallery Representation Consistency (GRC) quantifies detail richness by measuring visual consistency among retrieved images, and Query-gallery Image Relevance (QIR) evaluates caption precision by assessing relevance to the source image.
- LVLMs are optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a combined reward signal from GRC and QIR, encouraging the generation of captions that maximize both descriptive granularity and factual correctness.

## Results

- Base LVLMs demonstrated significant information loss for fine-grained details, achieving high species classification accuracy (e.g., **100%**) but substantially lower breed accuracy (e.g., **40.54%**) from generated captions.
- CIM-trained models showed an average improvement of **3.7%** in the overall CAPTURE metric, with notable gains of **12.3%** in Attributes F1 and **20.4%** in Relations QA on COCO-LN500 for Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**.
- **7B**/**8B** parameter models fine-tuned with CIM surpassed much larger models (e.g., **72B**/**76B**/**78B**) in several metrics (BLEU-4, METEOR, CAPTURE, Objects F1, Attributes F1, Relations QA), averaging improvements of **2.3%** to **5.1%** over their larger counterparts, and demonstrated robustness to diverse retrieval model choices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of an image caption, particularly its detail and accuracy, can be effectively inferred by analyzing the visual consistency and relevance of images retrieved using that caption as a query.
> - Information loss in LVLM-generated captions is most pronounced in fine-grained details (e.g., object attributes, relationships) rather than coarse-grained concepts (e.g., main objects).
> - An annotation-free reinforcement learning framework, guided by retrieval-based metrics, can successfully push LVLMs towards an 'identity mapping' from visual input to textual output, even enabling smaller models to outperform much larger baselines.
