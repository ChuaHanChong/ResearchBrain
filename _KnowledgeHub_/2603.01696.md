# Cross-modal Identity Mapping: Minimizing Information Loss in Modality Conversion via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba and the University of Hong Kong developed Cross-modal Identity Mapping (CIM), an annotation-free reinforcement learning framework that enhances Large Vision-Language Models to generate more detailed image captions. CIM quantifies information loss by assessing the consistency and relevance of images retrieved using generated captions, leading to substantial improvements in capturing fine-grained visual details.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently omit or misrepresent fine-grained visual details when generating image captions.
- Quantifying information loss between an image and its textual description is inherently challenging due to the fundamental semantic and structural differences between modalities.
- Existing solutions are limited by costly human annotation for supervised fine-tuning, susceptibility to 'reward hacking' in reinforcement learning, or computational overhead in training-free methods.

## Method

- The Cross-modal Identity Mapping (**CIM**) framework uses an ==annotation-free== reinforcement learning approach to optimize image captioning in LVLMs.
- It introduces two novel metrics derived from information retrieval: Gallery Representation Consistency (GRC) quantifies detail richness by measuring visual consistency among retrieved images, and Query-gallery Image Relevance (QIR) evaluates caption precision by assessing relevance to the source image.
- LVLMs are optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a combined reward signal from GRC and QIR, encouraging the generation of captions that maximize both descriptive granularity and factual correctness.

## Results

- Base LVLMs demonstrated significant information loss for fine-grained details, achieving high species classification accuracy (e.g., **100%**) but substantially lower breed accuracy (e.g., **40.54%**) from generated captions.
- CIM-trained models showed an average improvement of **3.7%** in the overall CAPTURE metric, with notable gains of **12.3%** in Attributes F1 and **20.4%** in Relations QA on COCO-LN500 for Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**.
- **7B**/**8B** parameter models fine-tuned with CIM surpassed much larger models (e.g., **72B**/**76B**/**78B**) in several metrics (BLEU-4, METEOR, CAPTURE, Objects F1, Attributes F1, Relations QA), averaging improvements of **2.3%** to **5.1%** over their larger counterparts, and demonstrated robustness to diverse retrieval model choices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of an image caption, particularly its detail and accuracy, can be effectively inferred by analyzing the visual consistency and relevance of images retrieved using that caption as a query.
> - Information loss in LVLM-generated captions is most pronounced in fine-grained details (e.g., object attributes, relationships) rather than coarse-grained concepts (e.g., main objects).
> - An annotation-free reinforcement learning framework, guided by retrieval-based metrics, can successfully push LVLMs towards an 'identity mapping' from visual input to textual output, even enabling smaller models to outperform much larger baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal understanding, particularly image captioning, has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). These models are capable of generating textual descriptions for visual inputs, which is crucial for applications such as visual question answering, image-text retrieval, and compositional reasoning. However, a persistent challenge in LVLM-based image captioning is the tendency to omit or misrepresent fine-grained visual details, leading to information loss between the image and its generated caption. This limitation affects the precision and richness of the textual output.

Existing approaches to improve image captioning in LVLMs generally fall into three categories:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This involves training models on large datasets of meticulously annotated image-caption pairs. While effective, the creation of such detailed datasets is resource-intensive and time-consuming, particularly for fine-grained descriptions.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Methods in this category use task-specific reward signals to optimize LVLMs. Examples include using CLIP-based image-text similarity, enforcing cycle consistency (reconstructing an image from its caption), or employing self-retrieval rewards (rewarding captions that retrieve the source image). A noted limitation of some VLM-based metrics in RL is their potential for "reward hacking" due to limited compositional reasoning abilities.
- **Training-Free Methods:** These approaches guide sampling during inference using models like CLIP or employ contrastive decoding to enhance caption quality without additional training. However, they often introduce computational overhead during inference, which can hinder practical deployment.

This paper addresses the challenge of information loss in image captioning by proposing an annotation-free reinforcement learning framework. It seeks to bridge the gap in quantitatively evaluating caption quality without relying on costly human annotations, thereby offering an alternative to current SFT-based methods and aiming to mitigate the limitations of existing RL reward functions. The work positions itself as a novel approach to guide LVLMs toward generating more detailed and precise captions by effectively quantifying the information loss during modality conversion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to minimize information loss in image-to-text modality conversion performed by Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) during image captioning. The authors aim to compel LVLMs to focus on fine-grained image details, thereby generating more precise and comprehensive descriptions.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed deficiencies in current LVLMs:
- **Information Omission and Misrepresentation:** Despite advancements, LVLMs frequently omit or misrepresent critical visual content when generating image captions. This leads to captions that are either too generic or factually inaccurate regarding specific details.
- **Challenge of Quantifying Information Loss:** Measuring the degree of information loss during modality conversion from a visual input to a textual output is inherently difficult due to the "modal gap" between image content and text. Traditional metrics or human evaluations often fail to capture the nuances of fine-grained detail preservation.
- **Cost of Annotation:** Existing methods that aim to improve fine-grained captioning often rely on constructing large, labor-intensive datasets with detailed human annotations. This process is costly, time-consuming, and presents a significant barrier to scalability.
- **Limitations of Current RL Reward Functions:** Reinforcement learning approaches, while promising, can be limited by the quality of their reward functions. Some VLM-based rewards may suffer from "reward hacking" due to the compositional reasoning limitations of the underlying VLMs, making it difficult to accurately assess information completeness or precision.

To address these motivations, the authors propose an insight: the quality of an image caption is positively correlated with the similarity among images retrieved through a text search using that caption. Specifically, they hypothesize that:
1. A more detailed caption will lead to higher representation consistency among the images retrieved using that caption.
2. A more accurate caption will result in higher visual similarity (relevance) between the retrieved images and the source image.

This insight forms the foundation for developing a quantitative, annotation-free method to evaluate image caption quality and subsequently guide LVLMs to produce more faithful and detailed descriptions. The ultimate goal is for LVLMs to achieve an "identity mapping" from images to captions, where the textual output fully and accurately preserves the information from the visual input.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Cross-modal Identity Mapping (CIM), is an annotation-free reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance image captioning by minimizing information loss. The methodology consists of three main stages: verifying information loss, quantitatively evaluating information loss using novel metrics, and employing these metrics as a reward function within a reinforcement learning framework.

#### 3.1. Verifying the Existence of Information Loss

To empirically confirm that LVLMs struggle with fine-grained details, the authors conducted an experiment using the Oxford-IIIT Pet dataset. For each image, an LVLM generates a detailed caption. A Large Language Model (LLM) then attempts to classify the animal's species and breed based *only* on the generated caption. The results showed high species accuracy but significantly lower breed accuracy across various LVLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B and InternVL3-8B). This indicated that LVLMs tend to describe coarse concepts accurately but often omit or misrepresent fine-grained details, confirming information loss during image-to-text conversion.

#### 3.2. Quantitative Evaluation of Information Loss in Modality Conversion

Based on their central insight, the authors propose a retrieval-based metric to quantify information loss without human annotations. This metric evaluates image caption quality along two dimensions:

- **Gallery Representation Consistency (GRC):** This metric assesses the detail richness of an image caption. Given a caption `c` generated by an LVLM for a source image `v`, the method performs text-to-image retrieval on a dataset `D` to obtain the top `K` most relevant image-text pairs, forming a support set `A(c) = {x_{i_1}..., x_{i_K}}`. GRC quantifies the consistency among the retrieved images' visual representations. It is calculated as the squared L2-normalized mean resultant length of the L2-normalized embeddings of the retrieved images:
 `GRC(c) = (1/K * Σ_{r=1}^K ˜v(x_{i_r}))^2`
 where `˜v(x_j)` is the L2-normalized embedding of image `x_j` extracted by a vision representation model. A low GRC value suggests the caption is too coarse to retrieve visually consistent images.

- **Query-gallery Image Relevance (QIR):** This metric quantifies the precision of an LVLM-generated caption. It measures the visual similarity between the source image `v` and the images `x_i` in the retrieved support set `A(c)`. QIR is calculated as a weighted sum of cosine similarities between the source image embedding and the embeddings of the retrieved images, with an exponential decay function `λ(r)` applied based on retrieval rank:
 `QIR(v, c) = Σ_{r=1}^K λ(r) R(v, x_{i_r})`
 where `R(v, x_i) = Cos(˜v(v), ˜v(x_i))` and `λ(r) = 1/2^(r-1)`. A high QIR value indicates that the query caption accurately describes the visual content of the source image, leading to the retrieval of visually relevant images.

**Correlation Verification:** To validate these metrics, the authors binned LVLM-generated captions by GRC and QIR values on the Oxford-IIIT Pet dataset and correlated them with breed classification accuracy (a proxy for information loss). A positive correlation was observed across various LVLMs, indicating that higher GRC and QIR values indeed correspond to captions that better preserve fine-grained information.

#### 3.3. Cross-modal Identity Mapping (CIM) Reinforcement Learning Framework

CIM integrates GRC and QIR into a reinforcement learning framework to optimize LVLMs.
- **Reward Function:** A combined reward function `Υ(v, c)` is defined as a weighted sum of GRC and QIR:
 `Υ(v, c) = GRC(c) + β * QIR(v, c)`
 where `β` is a hyperparameter (set to 1 in experiments) to balance the contribution of detail richness and precision.
- **Optimization:** The LVLM is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). For an input image, the LVLM generates a group of `G` (e.g., 5) distinct captions. The advantage `A_z` for each caption `c_z` in the group is calculated by normalizing its reward `Υ_z` against the mean and standard deviation of rewards from the entire group. This encourages the model to generate captions with higher combined GRC and QIR scores. This process iteratively refines the LVLM to minimize information loss and achieve an "identity mapping" from visual inputs to textual descriptions.

#### 3.4. Experimental Settings

- **Training Data:** Reinforcement learning was performed on 6.5K images from the COCO training split, part of the RefinedCaps dataset.
- **Retrieval Corpus:** The retrieval corpus used for GRC and QIR calculation was created by augmenting RefinedCaps with DenseFusion-1M. `K` was set to 5 for retrieving top candidates.
- **Encoders:** SBERT with an MPNet-base backbone was used as the text encoder for retrieval. OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 served as the image encoder for visual representation.
- **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer was used, with training for two epochs, a batch size of 256, and a learning rate of 1x10^-6.
- **Models Evaluated:** The method was tested on architecturally distinct LVLMs including LLaVA (1.5-7B), Qwen-VL (2-VL-7B, 2.5-VL-7B), and InternVL (2-8B, 2.5-8B, 3-8B), as well as their SFT versions.
- **Benchmarks:** DOCCI500 and COCO-LN500 were used to evaluate image captioning performance.
- **Metrics:** CAPTURE was used for overall caption quality, parsing captions into Objects, Attributes, and Relations, and computing F1 scores and QA scores (for relations). BLEU-4 and METEOR were also reported for broader comparisons in supplementary material.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed Cross-modal Identity Mapping (CIM) framework consistently enhances the performance of various LVLMs in image captioning, particularly in generating fine-grained and precise descriptions.

#### 4.1. Reinforcement Learning on Base Models

- **Consistent Gains:** Applying CIM directly to pre-trained base LVLMs (without supervised fine-tuning) resulted in consistent performance improvements across all evaluated models and benchmarks (COCO-LN500 and DOCCI500).
- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Details:** CIM achieved substantial gains on challenging tasks such as Attributes and Relations. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed improvements of 12.3% in Attributes F1 score and 20.4% in Relations QA score on COCO-LN500.
- **Overall Caption Quality:** The CAPTURE metric showed an average improvement of 3.7% over base models across the two benchmarks.
- **Precision Trade-off:** While there were significant improvements in detailed descriptions, a slight reduction in Objects Precision was observed for some models (QwenVL and InternVL). The authors attribute this to GRPO prioritizing more challenging tasks like Attributes and Relations to gain higher reward scores, suggesting a focus on areas with greater potential for improvement.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning on SFT Models

- **Further Performance Enhancement:** When CIM was applied to LVLMs that had already undergone Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), it further boosted their performance, surpassing both the SFT models and a strong baseline (SC-Captioner).
- **Superiority over SC-Captioner:** On the Relations metric, CIM achieved an average improvement of 3.2% over SC-Captioner. For Qwen2-VL-7B, CIM outperformed SC-Captioner by 1.1% on CAPTURE for COCO-LN500 and 1.0% for DOCCI500.
- **Model Scalability Impact:** The improvements were more significant for more powerful LVLMs (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B compared to LLaVA1.5-7B), indicating that stronger base models can benefit more from the reinforcement learning process by efficiently sampling high-quality outputs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Effectiveness of GRC and QIR:** Separate evaluation of GRC and QIR as reward functions demonstrated that each metric individually contributed to performance improvements compared to the base model.
- **Complementary Nature:** Combining GRC and QIR within the CIM framework yielded the best overall performance, indicating their complementary roles in optimizing caption precision and detail richness. For example, on COCO-LN500 using Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the combined CIM showed improvements of 4.8% on CAPTURE, 12.3% on Attributes F1, and 20.4% on Relations QA, which were superior to using GRC or QIR alone.

#### 4.4. Scalability of the Retrieval Corpus

- **Performance Scaling with Corpus Size:** Experiments with different retrieval corpus sizes (RefinedCaps to RefinedCaps augmented with DenseFusion-1M) showed that increasing the corpus size consistently led to performance improvements in Objects F1, Attributes F1, and Relations QA. This supports the argument that larger corpora necessitate more detailed and precise captions for consistent and relevant image retrieval.

#### 4.5. Robustness across Diverse Retrieval Models

- **Encoder-Agnostic Robustness:** CIM exhibited minimal performance variance when tested with various combinations of image and text encoders (e.g., DINOv3 or OpenCLIP for image, MiniLM or MPNet for text). CAPTURE, Objects F1, and Attributes F1 scores varied by less than 1.2%, indicating that the method's effectiveness is largely insensitive to the choice of underlying encoders.

#### 4.6. Comparisons with Larger Models (Supplementary)

- CIM-trained 7B/8B models consistently outperformed much larger models (72B/76B/78B) across multiple benchmarks and metrics, suggesting that CIM effectively unlocks the potential of smaller models to generate more detailed and precise captions.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Examples (Supplementary)

- Visualizations confirmed that CIM-trained models generate more comprehensive and precise descriptions, capturing fine-grained details that are often overlooked by base or SFT models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel approach to address a fundamental challenge in large vision-language models: the loss of fine-grained information during image-to-text conversion. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Annotation-Free Evaluation of Caption Quality:** The most significant contribution is the development of a quantitative, annotation-free method for evaluating the precision and detail richness of image captions. By converting the problem of caption quality assessment into a measure of image-to-image similarity through retrieval, the authors bypass the costly and time-consuming process of human annotation. This provides a scalable and efficient alternative to current evaluation paradigms.

- **Effective Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The Cross-modal Identity Mapping (CIM) framework leverages these novel metrics (Gallery Representation Consistency and Query-gallery Image Relevance) as a reward signal within a reinforcement learning setup. This mechanism effectively guides LVLMs to generate captions that are both more detailed and more accurate, moving closer to an "identity mapping" where textual outputs faithfully represent visual inputs.

- **Improved Fine-Grained Captioning Capabilities:** The experimental results demonstrate consistent and substantial improvements in LVLMs' ability to describe objects, attributes, and particularly, relations. This is crucial for applications requiring a deep understanding of visual scenes, such as advanced visual question answering, complex image retrieval, and compositional reasoning.

- **Enhanced Model Performance and Efficiency:** The method shows improvements across various LVLMs, including those already fine-tuned on supervised datasets. Notably, it enables smaller 7B/8B models to surpass the performance of significantly larger 70B+ models in certain metrics. This suggests that CIM can unlock the potential of existing models more effectively, potentially reducing the need for excessively large models or extensive supervised fine-tuning, thus improving computational efficiency.

- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The demonstrated robustness of CIM across different retrieval corpus sizes and diverse image/text encoders indicates its broad applicability and insensitivity to specific architectural choices of underlying components. This enhances its practical deployability in various multimodal systems.

- **New Research Direction:** This work provides a new perspective for improving image captioning by focusing on the consistency and relevance of retrieved images as a proxy for caption quality. This paradigm could inspire further research into self-supervised or weakly supervised methods for multimodal understanding, reducing reliance on costly explicit labels.

In summary, the Cross-modal Identity Mapping framework offers a practical and effective solution to a critical problem in LVLMs, enhancing their ability to generate high-quality, information-preserving captions without requiring additional human annotations. This has implications for the development of more capable and efficient multimodal AI systems.
