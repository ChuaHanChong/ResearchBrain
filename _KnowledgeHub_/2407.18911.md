# HRP: Human Affordances for Robotic Pre-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> HRP mines hand-object affordances (contact points, future hand pose, active-object boxes) from Ego4D human videos using off-the-shelf detectors, then fine-tunes only LayerNorm params of a pretrained encoder via L2 regression on these labels. Boosts 6 SOTA visual representations by 15%+ on 5 real robot tasks, 3 morphologies, 3 camera views.

## Problem

- Self-supervised encoders pretrained on human video (MAE, contrastive) fail to reliably beat a plain ImageNet baseline on real robot manipulation, per independent evaluations.
- Standard reconstruction/contrastive objectives applied to human-object interaction video do not force encoders to key on object identity or hand-object spatial relationships.
- Prior robotic representations are typically effective only for specific camera views and robot setups, limiting transfer across morphologies and viewpoints.

## Method

- **HRP** pipeline: automatically mines 3 affordance label types (contact heatmaps via ==GMM==-fit hand-object intersections, future 2D wrist pose via ==FrankMocap==, active-object bounding boxes) from 150K Ego4D clips using off-the-shelf detectors.
- ==Affordance distillation== via 3 small regression heads (contact, hand, object) trained with weighted ==L2 losses== on top of a frozen pretrained encoder, injecting hand-object priors without full self-supervised retraining.
- ==LayerNorm-only fine-tuning==: only the encoder's LayerNorm gamma/beta parameters are updated during affordance distillation, preserving the base pretraining distribution's learned features.

## Results

- Across **6** baselines (ImageNet MAE, Ego4D MAE, CLIP, DINO, MVP, VC-1) and **3000+** real robot trials, HRP improves every baseline on toasting/pouring/stacking, a minimum **15%** gain and up to **20%** over SOTA.
- LayerNorm-only fine-tuning beats full-weight fine-tuning consistently across all **6** base models on the ablation comparison (Fig. 7).
- Ablating object or hand loss uniformly degrades performance; ablating contact loss only hurts the Ego4D base model; HRP+ImageNet raises diffusion policy performance from **56%** to **76%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit hand-object affordance supervision extracts information from human video that self-supervised objectives alone cannot recover, even from identical source images.
> - Freezing most pretrained weights and adapting only normalization statistics is a stronger fine-tuning strategy than full fine-tuning when the auxiliary dataset is far smaller than the original pretraining corpus.
> - Distilling affordance labels into a representation outperforms directly using the label-generating detector network as the representation, so distillation adds value beyond the teacher's own features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is grappling with the "data challenge": the immense difficulty and cost of collecting sufficient, diverse real-world robot interaction data to train generalist agents. Traditional approaches often rely on extensive robot-specific data collection (e.g., "arm farms" or tele-operation) or highly curated simulations, neither of which fully capture the complexity and diversity of real-world environments.

One promising direction is **representation learning from out-of-domain (OOD) data**, particularly large datasets of human videos (e.g., Ego4D). The rationale is that human videos inherently contain rich information about object manipulation, interaction, and environmental affordances that are highly relevant to robots. Prior work has explored **self-supervised learning (SSL)** techniques (like Masked Auto-Encoders (MAE) and Temporal Contrastive Networks (TCN)) on these human video datasets to pre-train visual encoders. These pre-trained encoders are then fine-tuned for downstream robotic tasks, aiming to reduce the robot-specific data requirement.

However, as highlighted by the authors and supported by independent evaluations, these existing OOD pre-training methods often struggle to consistently and substantially outperform simpler baselines, such as representations pre-trained on ImageNet. A key limitation identified is that generic self-supervised objectives, while effective for general vision tasks, might not explicitly extract the crucial object-level and agent-level interaction information (i.e., "affordances") that robots need for manipulation.

HRP addresses this gap by proposing a **semi-supervised pre-training paradigm** that explicitly injects human-centric affordance information into visual encoders. Instead of solely relying on self-supervision, HRP leverages readily available, off-the-shelf computer vision models to automatically extract "affordance labels" (contact points, hand poses, active objects) from human videos. This approach moves beyond generic visual features to imbue the representation with a deeper understanding of *how* to interact with objects, thereby bridging a critical gap between high-level visual understanding and actionable robotic control.

The paper thus builds upon and critically evaluates the current state of representation learning in robotics, offering a concrete and empirically validated method to enhance the utility of OOD human video data for robot manipulation. It stands as a significant contribution to the ongoing effort to enable more capable and generalist robotic agents in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop **suitable visual representations (vision networks) for robotic agents that enable them to predict optimal actions given high-dimensional vision inputs and generalize to various tasks in the wild.**

The core motivation stems from the limitations of current approaches to robotic learning:

- **Prohibitive Cost of Robot Data:** Learning generalist agents requires an extreme amount of diverse training data. Collecting this data on real robots is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, making it infeasible for most task settings.
- **Inadequacy of Current OOD Pre-training:** While using internet-scale human videos for pre-training is a promising solution, existing self-supervised methods (e.g., MAE or TCN on Ego4D) often fail to consistently and significantly improve performance over simpler baselines like ImageNet pre-training. They also tend to be less robust to changes in camera views or robot setups.
- **Missing Interaction-Centric Information:** The authors hypothesize that traditional self-supervised learning objectives, though applied to human video data rich in agent-environment interactions, are inherently unable to extract explicit object attributes, spatial relationships between end-effectors and objects, and interaction cues (e.g., where to grasp a mug). These are precisely the "affordances" crucial for robotic manipulation.

Therefore, the **key insight and motivation** behind HRP is that instead of merely *observing* human interactions, the system should *explicitly learn to predict* these interactions. By abandoning pure self-supervision for a semi-supervised approach, and leveraging off-the-shelf computer vision modules to *automatically* generate these "affordance labels" from human videos, robots can distil this crucial information into a pre-trained representation. This approach aims to create representations that are not only visually rich but also inherently "actionable" for robotic control, leading to stronger downstream robotic performance and better generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, **Human affordances for Robotic Pre-training (HRP)**, is a semi-supervised pipeline designed to learn effective robotic representations from human video data. It operates in two main stages:

**Stage 1: Automatic Affordance Label Extraction from Human Videos**
HRP curates a human affordance dataset (D_H) by processing human-object interaction videos from the Ego4D dataset (150K clips). This process is automated using off-the-shelf computer vision modules, eliminating the need for manual annotation. The extracted affordance labels capture agent-centric (how to move and interact) and environment-centric (where to interact) properties.

- **Contact Locations:** For a given frame I_t (where no object contact is yet present), the system identifies a future frame I_j (j > t) where contact with an object *begins*, using a hand-object interaction detection model (e.g., 100DoH). The active object O_j and hand mask M_j are used to define contact points as the intersection of M_j and O_j. These contact points are then projected back to I_t using a homography matrix (computed via SIFT features), indicating *where* in the current frame contact will occur. To standardize the output, a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with k=5 modes is fitted to these points, and their means (c_t) serve as the uniform contact descriptor.
- **Future Hand Poses:** This label captures anticipated human hand motion. For I_t, the 2D wrist position (h_t+k) in a future frame I_t+k (typically k=30 frames ahead) is detected using a hand detector like Frank Mocap. These future wrist points are then back-projected to I_t (using homography) to create the "future wrist label" (h_t), indicating where the hand will be in the near future relative to the current frame.
- **Active Object Labels:** Similar to contact extraction, the hand-object interaction model identifies the frame I_c where contact begins and extracts the bounding box coordinates (b_c) of the "active object" being interacted with. These coordinates are then projected across all frames in the video clip (using homography) to provide an active bounding box (b_t) for each image.

**Stage 2: Affordance Distillation (HRP Fine-tuning)**
A pre-existing visual encoder network f_θ (e.g., a ViT-B pre-trained with ImageNet MAE) is fine-tuned using the automatically generated D_H.

- **Affordance Modules and Losses:** Three small, dedicated affordance modules (p_c, p_h, p_b) are added on top of the frozen f_θ. These modules are trained via L2 regression to predict the respective affordances (contact points c_t, hand poses h_t, and object bounding boxes b_t) from the latent vector f_θ(I_t). A combined loss function (L) sums these three L2 losses, with mask variables to handle frames where some affordances might be missing, and hyperparameters (λs) to control their relative weights.
- **Parameter Tuning Strategy:** Crucially, to preserve the rich features learned from the initial large-scale pre-training (e.g., ImageNet), HRP fine-tunes *only* the **LayerNormalization parameters (γ and β)** within the pre-trained encoder, while keeping the rest of the network weights fixed. This strategy has been shown to be effective in preserving pre-trained knowledge while adapting to new tasks. The Adam optimizer is used for this fine-tuning.

**Downstream Task Evaluation:**
After HRP fine-tuning, the enhanced representations are evaluated on diverse real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

- **Policy Learning:** The HRP-enhanced visual encoder is used as part of a visuomotor policy (π) trained via **Behavior Cloning (BC)**. A small 2-layer MLP (p) is placed on top of the HRP representation (p(f(o_t))) to predict robot actions (end-effector velocities + gripper commands). The policy and encoder are fine-tuned end-to-end on 50 expert demonstrations per task.
- **Experimental Setup:** The evaluation covers 5 unique manipulation tasks (Stacking, Pouring, Toasting, Pot on Stove, Hand Lift Cup) across 3 distinct robot morphologies (Franka, xArm, a custom dexterous hand) and 3 camera viewpoints (front, ego, side). Six state-of-the-art baseline representations (ImageNet MAE, Ego4D MAE, CLIP, DINO, MVP, VC-1) are used for comparison, both in their original form and after HRP fine-tuning. Experiments involve 3000+ robot trials with A/B testing for statistical significance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling findings that strongly support the efficacy of HRP:

- **Universal Performance Boost:** HRP consistently and substantially boosts the downstream robotic performance of all six state-of-the-art baseline representations. For every baseline considered, `baseline + HRP` consistently outperforms `baseline` across diverse tasks. The paper claims a minimum 15% performance boost, often exceeding 20% compared to SOTA baselines.
- **Superiority of ImageNet + HRP:** The `ImageNet MAE + HRP` combination emerges as the strongest representation, delivering state-of-the-art performance across multiple tasks and camera views. This highlights that HRP can unlock the latent potential of general-purpose visual representations for robotics.
- **Robustness to Camera View Shifts:** HRP representations demonstrate increased robustness to different camera viewpoints (front vs. ego camera). For instance, `ImageNet + HRP` performs similarly across both views, whereas the vanilla `ImageNet` baseline shows a preference for the front camera. This suggests HRP learns more invariant and generalizable visual features for interaction.
- **Generalization Across Robot Morphologies:** The benefits of HRP extend to radically different robotic setups, including a 6-DOF xArm and a high-degree-of-freedom dexterous hand. This indicates that the learned affordance-centric representations are not tied to specific hardware but capture fundamental interaction principles.
- **Distillation Outperforms Direct Use:** Explicitly distilling affordance labels into a pre-trained encoder via HRP significantly outperforms directly using the "teacher" networks (e.g., the 100DoH ResNet-101 detector) as encoders for policy learning. This validates the distillation approach and the quality of the HRP representation.
- **Affordance-Specific Supervision is Crucial:** An ablation study comparing HRP's affordance losses against a semantic classification loss (using Detic labels from the same Ego4D clips) reveals that HRP's affordance-based supervision is vastly superior for robotic tasks. This confirms the hypothesis that explicitly learning interaction-centric features is more beneficial than generic object semantics.
- **Importance of Design Decisions:**
 - **LayerNorm-Only Fine-tuning:** Ablations demonstrate that fine-tuning *only* the LayerNormalization parameters (γ and β) is critical and consistently superior to fine-tuning all weights of the encoder. This approach effectively adapts the representation without catastrophic forgetting of large-scale pre-training knowledge.
 - **Loss Contribution:** The object and hand pose losses are found to be critical for HRP's performance, resulting in significant degradation when removed. The contact loss provides a marginal boost, particularly for the Ego4D base model, but is less universally impactful, possibly due to the noisiness of contact point extraction.
- **Robustness to Novel Distractors:** HRP representations improve robustness against out-of-distribution distractor objects during test time, especially for representations initially trained on less diverse data like Ego4D.
- **Compatibility with Diverse IL Frameworks:** HRP representations are shown to significantly boost performance when integrated with a fundamentally different imitation learning framework, Diffusion Policy, indicating their broad applicability beyond standard Behavior Cloning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HRP represents a significant advancement in robot learning, with several profound implications:

- **Addressing the Robotics Data Bottleneck:** The primary impact of HRP is its scalable solution to the critical data challenge in robotics. By demonstrating an effective way to leverage readily available, internet-scale human video data, HRP dramatically reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming robot-specific data collection. This democratizes robot learning, making advanced manipulation capabilities more accessible to researchers and practitioners without access to massive robot farms.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** HRP creates more robust and generalizable visual representations. The consistent performance boosts across diverse tasks, robot morphologies, camera views, and even the improved handling of out-of-distribution distractors indicate that HRP helps robots learn fundamental interaction principles rather than task- or setup-specific correlations. This is a crucial step towards truly generalist robotic agents.
- **Bridging Computer Vision and Robotics:** The work effectively bridges the fields of computer vision and robotics by showing how sophisticated, human-centric visual cues (affordances) can be explicitly extracted and distilled into representations optimized for robotic control. It moves beyond generic visual features to imbue representations with actionable knowledge, highlighting a promising avenue for interdisciplinary research.
- **New Paradigm for Pre-training:** HRP introduces a powerful semi-supervised pre-training paradigm. It effectively combines the strengths of large-scale self-supervised pre-training (for rich general features) with targeted, automatically generated supervision from human videos (for interaction-specific knowledge). This hybrid approach offers a blueprint for future representation learning strategies in embodied AI.
- **Practical Implications for Robot Deployment:** For real-world robot deployment, the ability of HRP-trained policies to perform well with novel objects and in varying environmental conditions (like different lighting or camera views) is invaluable. It promises more reliable and adaptable robotic systems that require less fine-tuning for new scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays a strong foundation for future research. The authors explicitly state their intention to scale HRP to more tasks and robot morphologies, and crucially, to integrate it with other robot learning paradigms like reinforcement learning and model-based control. This could lead to even more capable and autonomous learning agents that leverage human knowledge to navigate and interact with the world.
- **Open-Sourcing Initiative:** The commitment to open-source the code, weights, and data will foster rapid adoption and further development within the research community, accelerating progress in robot learning.

In summary, HRP offers a compelling and empirically validated method to create more powerful and generalizable visual representations for robotic manipulation, fundamentally pushing the boundaries of what is possible with data-driven robot learning.
