# CounterScene: Counterfactual Causal Reasoning in Generative World Models for Safety-Critical Closed-Loop Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> CounterScene integrates counterfactual causal reasoning into generative world models for autonomous driving, enabling the creation of safety-critical scenarios that are both highly adversarial and realistic. The framework achieves superior collision rates while maintaining behavioral plausibility and demonstrates robust generalization to unseen datasets without retraining.

## Problem

- Evaluating autonomous driving systems requires realistic safety-critical scenarios, which are rare in naturalistic driving data and challenging for simulations to generate meaningfully.
- Existing generative world models, especially diffusion-based ones, lack explicit causal understanding, limiting their ability to reason about *why* a scenario is safe or *how* to induce controlled risk.
- Current safety-critical scenario generation methods often face a realism–adversarial trade-off, where inducing dangerous situations compromises behavioral plausibility.

## Method

- CounterScene uses a conditional diffusion world model, augmented with a Causal Interaction Graph (CIG), to explicitly model dynamic inter-agent dependencies for coherent multi-agent rollouts.
- It implements a causal adversarial agent identification method that pinpoints the agent most responsible for maintaining safety, based on conflict type and a danger scoring mechanism.
- A stage-adaptive counterfactual guidance module applies minimal, targeted spatial and temporal interventions to the identified critical agent's trajectory, transitioning the scene from safe to dangerous while preserving overall realism.

## Results

- CounterScene achieved the lowest Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE) while yielding the highest Collision Rate (CR) across all prediction horizons on nuScenes, e.g., 22.7% CR at 8-10s with 1.877 ADE.
- Its causal adversarial agent selection strategy produced the best realism (ADE 0.721) and highest CR (11.0%) on average over 3s and 7s horizons, outperforming random and proximity-based selection methods.
- The framework demonstrated strong zero-shot transferability to the unseen nuPlan dataset, maintaining superior realism (lowest ADE/FDE) and achieving high Hard Braking Rates (HBR) and Collision Rates (CR) (e.g., 40.2% CR at 7s).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit causal reasoning into generative world models allows for the generation of safety-critical scenarios that overcome the realism–adversarial trade-off, achieving both high adversarial intensity and realistic behavior.
> - Precisely identifying the causally critical agent for intervention is crucial; perturbing irrelevant agents degrades scenario realism more significantly than it impacts the ability to force a collision.
> - Minimal and targeted interventions on an agent's spatiotemporal safety margins, guided by a progressive, stage-adaptive schedule, are more effective and realistic than brute-force or unstructured perturbations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving (AD) critically relies on robust safety evaluation. A key prerequisite for effective safety evaluation is the generation of realistic safety-critical driving scenarios, which are inherently rare in naturalistic driving data. This rarity renders real-world testing insufficient on its own. Simulation offers a scalable alternative, and recent advancements in generative world models have enabled the learning of traffic dynamics directly from data, leading to the creation of realistic and diverse scenarios.

Within this landscape, a significant challenge exists for current generative world models, particularly diffusion-based models operating on bird's-eye-view (BEV) representations that support closed-loop multi-agent rollouts. While these models can simulate complex interactions, they typically lack an understanding of the causal structure underlying these interactions. This limitation means they can simulate *what* happens but cannot effectively reason about *why* a scenario remains safe, *which* agent is causally responsible for maintaining safety, or *what would change* if that agent behaved differently.

Existing approaches to safety-critical scenario generation often exhibit a realism–adversarial trade-off. They commonly rely on heuristic methods for selecting adversarial agents (e.g., manual specification or distance-based proximity) and unstructured perturbation strategies to induce collisions. This often leads to either implausible dynamics if perturbations are aggressive or a low rate of meaningful risk generation if they are conservative. Such methods struggle to model how behavioral changes propagate through a multi-agent system, leading to a compromise between generating dangerous scenarios and maintaining behavioral realism.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by introducing structured causal reasoning into generative BEV world models. It seeks to move beyond statistical correlations to actively construct controllable, high-risk interactions. By focusing on counterfactual interventions grounded in the causal interaction structure, CounterScene aims to generate safety-critical scenarios that are both highly adversarial and realistic, thereby resolving the realism–adversarial trade-off that characterizes much of the existing research in this domain. This represents a shift from merely forcing collisions to understanding and intervening on the causal mechanisms that lead to dangerous outcomes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged insufficiency of naturalistic data for thoroughly evaluating the safety of autonomous driving systems. Safety-critical events, such as collisions and near-misses, are rare. While generative world models have advanced the ability to simulate diverse scenarios, they often fall short in generating *meaningful* safety-critical scenarios because they lack an explicit understanding of the causal relationships governing multi-agent interactions.

The key objectives of the CounterScene framework are:

1. **Introduce Structured Counterfactual Causal Reasoning:** The core objective is to integrate explicit causal reasoning into generative BEV world models. This involves endowing these models with the ability to perform counterfactual interventions, specifically by asking: "Given a safe scene, what if the causally critical agent had behaved differently?" This allows for targeted and interpretable scenario modifications.

2. **Overcome the Realism–Adversarial Trade-off:** Current methods often face a dilemma: aggressive perturbations designed to force collisions tend to yield implausible or unrealistic agent behaviors, while conservative perturbations rarely generate genuinely dangerous situations. CounterScene aims to generate scenarios that are simultaneously highly adversarial (high collision/hard braking rates) and realistic (low trajectory deviation and off-road rates). The motivation is that collision risk arises from structured interaction dependencies, not isolated behaviors, and interventions should leverage this structure.

3. **Identify Causally Critical Agents:** A significant objective is to precisely identify the agent whose behavior is most responsible for maintaining the current safe outcome. The motivation is that perturbing an irrelevant or weakly coupled agent leads to unnatural behavior and unrealistic scenarios. By accurately pinpointing the "critical variable," interventions can be minimal and effective.

4. **Model Dynamic Inter-Agent Dependencies Explicitly:** The work aims to develop an interaction model that explicitly structures how behavioral changes propagate through a multi-agent system. This is motivated by the observation that existing models often rely on correlation-based interaction modeling, which limits their ability to support structured causal reasoning and control the emergence of high-risk interactions. The goal is to ensure multi-agent coherence even when one agent's behavior is modified.

5. **Develop Minimal, Targeted Interventions:** The framework seeks to implement intervention strategies that remove spatial and temporal safety margins of the identified critical agent with minimal modification, allowing risk to emerge naturally through interaction propagation rather than brute-force manipulation. This is motivated by the desire to produce "minimal counterfactuals" where a single, targeted behavioral change transforms a safe scene into a dangerous one while preserving overall scene realism.

In summary, CounterScene is motivated by the need for scalable, realistic, and diagnostically informative safety evaluation for autonomous driving, aiming to address the limitations of existing generative models by incorporating a principled causal framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CounterScene is a framework designed to integrate structured counterfactual reasoning into closed-loop generative BEV world models for safety-critical scenario generation. It operates by identifying a causally critical agent in a safe scene and applying minimal, targeted interventions to its behavior to induce a dangerous outcome. The framework comprises a diffusion-based interactive world model augmented with three tightly coupled components: causal adversarial agent identification, conflict-aware interaction modeling via a Causal Interaction Graph (CIG), and stage-adaptive counterfactual guidance.

#### 3.1. Interactive World Model

The generative foundation of CounterScene is a conditional diffusion world model.
- **Scene Representation:** A scene is defined by `M` agents with states `s_t^(i)` observed over `H` historical timesteps. The model predicts future trajectories `X = {x_1:T^(1)..., x_1:T^(M)}` over a horizon `T`.
- **Diffusion Generation:** Trajectories are generated through a denoising diffusion process. A forward process adds Gaussian noise to clean trajectories `x_0` to produce noisy `x_k`. A reverse process learns a denoiser `ϵ_θ(x_k, k, c, G)` conditioned on scene context `c` and an interaction graph `G`, trained with a standard L2 objective.
- **SceneTransformer Denoiser:** The denoising network employs a SceneTransformer architecture. It encodes historical trajectories and uses a diffusion decoder to reconstruct future trajectories from noisy inputs. This decoder incorporates scene context using cross-temporal attention to history, causal temporal attention across future timesteps, and map cross-attention for road geometry.
- **Conflict-Aware Interaction Modeling:** To explicitly capture inter-agent dependencies, the SceneTransformer is augmented with a **Causal Interaction Graph (CIG)**. This is a learned directed graph `G = (V, E)` where nodes `v_i` represent agents and directed edges `(i, j)` signify potential interaction dependencies. Edge features `e_t^ij` are computed for each agent pair, including relative position, velocity, time-to-collision indicators, and interaction properties near predicted conflict regions. These features are encoded via a multilayer perceptron and integrated into the transformer's attention mechanism, allowing behavioral changes to propagate along conflict-coupled pathways.
- **Closed-Loop Simulation:** The world model supports closed-loop rollouts. Predicted trajectories over horizon `T` are used to update the simulated scene state, which then serves as input for the next prediction step. This ensures that when an intervention is applied to one agent, others react dynamically and realistically.

#### 3.2. Causal Adversarial Agent Identification

This component identifies the specific agent whose behavior is causally responsible for maintaining safety, making it the target for intervention.
- **Conflict Analysis and Candidate Filtering:** Ego-agent interactions are categorized into two fundamental conflict types: intersection conflicts (agents approaching a shared crossing region) and following conflicts (agents interacting longitudinally). Interactions without stable conflict semantics are discarded. Intersection conflicts are prioritized over following conflicts, which are ranked by longitudinal risk indicators (e.g., closing speed, minimum distance). Formal criteria involve analyzing coarse travel directions and spatiotemporal encounter characteristics.
- **Danger Scoring:** A danger score is calculated for each candidate agent to quantify the latent risk suppressed by its current safe behavior. For intersection conflicts, the score `s_conflict = v_rel / (∆t + 0.5)` emphasizes relative speed and minimal arrival-time difference at the conflict point. For following conflicts, `s_conflict = v_close / (d_min + ϵ)` emphasizes closing speed and minimum inter-agent distance. A higher score indicates greater suppressed risk. The agent with the highest score is identified as the adversarial target, and a proportional guidance weight is assigned.

#### 3.3. Counterfactual Guidance

This module modifies the identified critical agent's trajectory to transition the scene from safe to dangerous.
- **Denoising-Stage-Adaptive Guidance:** Intervention strength varies based on the denoising progress `p ∈`. A three-stage schedule applies weak guidance early in denoising (when trajectory structure is forming), progressively stronger guidance mid-stage, and strongest guidance late-stage (for precise spatial-temporal control).
- **Spatial Guidance:** This objective redirects the adversarial agent toward a designated conflict point `p_c`, often anchored by the ego vehicle's expected state. The loss `L_sp = w_s (∥x_e(t_e) - p_c∥^2 + ∥x_a(t_a) - p_c∥^2)` removes the adversarial agent's spatial avoidance behavior.
- **Temporal Guidance:** This mechanism removes the temporal safety margin. An arrival-time compression schedule, activated after `p ≥ 0.5`, progressively narrows the target arrival-time gap `∆t'` toward zero as denoising completes. A synchronization objective `L_tm = w_t ∥x_e(t_e) - x_a(t_a)∥^2` encourages the adversarial agent to match this compressed timing relative to the ego vehicle. The model is not prescribed a collision trajectory; it finds a realistic path consistent with the compressed timing.
- **Overall Objective:** The complete guidance objective is `L_total = m(p)(L_sp + L_tm) + L_jerk`, where `L_jerk` is a jerk regularization term to maintain physical plausibility. During sampling, gradients from `L_total` only modify the adversarial agent's trajectory, allowing other agents to adapt via the world model's learned dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across multiple experimental setups, demonstrating CounterScene's effectiveness in generating realistic safety-critical scenarios.

#### 4.1. Main Results Across Prediction Horizons (nuScenes)

Experiments on the nuScenes dataset evaluated realism (ADE, FDE, ORR) and adversarial effectiveness (HBR, CR) over short (1-4s), mid (5-7s), and long (8-10s) horizons.
- **Superior Realism and Adversarial Effectiveness:** CounterScene consistently achieved the lowest Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE) while simultaneously yielding the highest Collision Rate (CR) across all horizons. For instance, at 8-10 seconds, CounterScene recorded a CR of 22.7% (compared to 12.3% for the strongest baseline, CCDiff) while maintaining a lower ADE (1.877 vs. 2.092). This indicates that CounterScene effectively generates dangerous scenarios without significant distortion of naturalistic behaviors.
- **Widening Advantage at Longer Horizons:** The performance gap, particularly in collision rate, increased with longer prediction horizons. This suggests that CounterScene's approach encourages interactions that naturally evolve into critical situations over time, rather than forcing immediate, potentially unrealistic, collisions.
- **Avoidance of Realism–Adversarial Trade-off:** Baselines often demonstrated a trade-off: conservative methods (CTG, CTG++) maintained realism but rarely generated collisions, while perturbation-based methods (STRIVE, VAE) achieved moderate CR at the cost of significantly degraded realism. CounterScene circumvented this by using targeted, single-agent interventions, preserving multi-agent coherence.

#### 4.2. Adversarial Agent Selection Analysis

To validate the importance of identifying the correct adversarial agent, CounterScene's causal selection strategy was compared against random, CCDiff (TTC-based), and SafeSim (distance-based) selection methods, while keeping the world model and guidance function fixed.
- **Causal Selection's Impact:** CounterScene's causal selection yielded the best realism (ADE 0.721) and the highest CR (11.0%) on average over 3s and 7s horizons.
- **Degradation from Incorrect Selection:** Notably, CCDiff's TTC-based selection produced the lowest CR (8.0%), performing worse than random sampling (10.0%). This suggested that proximity-based criteria could misidentify the true critical agent, leading to less effective and less realistic adversarial scenarios.
- **Realism vs. Adversarial Gap:** The realism metrics (ADE) showed a larger spread across selection strategies (44% spread) compared to the adversarial effectiveness (CR, 8% to 11% spread). This finding highlights that selecting the wrong agent degrades scenario realism more substantially than it compromises the ability to force a collision, as more unnatural behavior is required from an irrelevant participant.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study systematically evaluated the contribution of each guidance component: progressive scheduling, conflict-aware weighting, adaptive arrival-time compression, and jerk regularization.
- **Temporal Guidance Drives Adversarial Effectiveness:** Removing adaptive arrival-time compression (Variant 5) caused the largest drop in CR (from 11.0% to 7.5%) with minimal impact on realism (ADE 0.753 vs. 0.747). This indicated that dynamically closing the temporal margin is the primary mechanism for collision generation.
- **Realism Preservation:** Removing jerk regularization or progressive scheduling slightly increased ADE and off-road rates. This implies these components are crucial for ensuring the physical plausibility and smoothness of modified trajectories.
- **Synergistic Contributions:** The minimal variant (retaining only basic spatial and temporal objectives) performed poorly across all metrics, confirming that the full structured design is necessary for achieving both high adversarial effectiveness and realism simultaneously.

#### 4.4. Cross-Dataset Zero-Shot Transfer (nuPlan)

CounterScene's generalization capability was assessed through zero-shot transfer to the nuPlan dataset, without retraining or fine-tuning.
- **Strong Realism Generalization:** CounterScene achieved the lowest ADE and FDE across all three horizons (3s, 5s, 7s) on the unseen nuPlan dataset. This consistency suggested that the targeted single-agent intervention limits distribution shift, allowing the world model to maintain realism for other agents.
- **Near-Misses Evolve into Collisions:** At short horizons (3s), CounterScene showed the highest Hard Braking Rate (HBR, 17.6%) but a modest CR (5.4%). As the horizon extended, CR significantly increased (to 40.2% at 7s), matching CCDiff while maintaining superior realism. This suggests CounterScene generates credible threats that force planner reactions, which then naturally evolve into collisions over time.
- **HBR as Scenario Quality Indicator:** The sustained high HBR across horizons indicated that CounterScene consistently produced scenarios that genuinely challenge the ego planner, reflecting a higher quality of "stress-test" scenarios beyond mere collision counts.
- **Causal Invariance for Generalization:** The superior zero-shot performance was attributed to CounterScene's focus on intervening on invariant physics of conflict (spatiotemporal margins) rather than dataset-specific statistical correlations. This decoupling allows the intervention to remain valid across different driving domains.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

Visualizations confirmed that CounterScene successfully induced safety-critical events, such as merging, rear-end, and head-on collisions (Figures 3, 4, 5, 6, 7), while preserving natural multi-agent dynamics. In contrast, baselines either failed to generate meaningful risk or produced physically implausible trajectory deviations, reinforcing the quantitative findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CounterScene framework introduces structured counterfactual causal reasoning into generative world models for safety-critical scenario generation, representing a notable advancement in the evaluation of autonomous driving systems. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Resolution of the Realism–Adversarial Trade-off:** A core contribution is the empirical demonstration that safety-critical scenarios can be constructed with both high adversarial intensity and realistic behavior. By precisely identifying the causally critical agent and minimally intervening on its safety-maintaining behaviors, CounterScene navigates the long-standing challenge of generating scenarios that are simultaneously dangerous and plausible. This is crucial for developing robust and trustworthy autonomous systems, as unrealistic scenarios offer limited diagnostic value.

2. **Enhanced Interpretability and Diagnostic Value:** The causal structure of CounterScene's interventions provides a degree of interpretability. By identifying *which* specific behavior prevents danger and *how* its spatial and temporal margins maintain safety, the framework offers insights into the vulnerabilities of the autonomous driving policy. The counterfactual trace directly indicates where and when the ego planner experiences spatiotemporal pressure, which can inform debugging, policy refinement, and safety certification. This moves beyond merely identifying failures to understanding the underlying causes.

3. **Improved Generalization through Causal Invariance:** The strong zero-shot transfer performance to the unseen nuPlan dataset is a significant finding. This suggests that by focusing on universal physical constraints and causal mechanisms of conflict (e.g., spatiotemporal co-occurrence), CounterScene's interventions are more invariant to dataset-specific driving styles or environmental priors. This capability is critical for scalable safety evaluation across diverse operational design domains without requiring extensive re-training or fine-tuning.

4. **Foundation for Advanced Scenario Generation:** CounterScene proposes a new paradigm where realism is anchored at the scene level, while vulnerability is exposed through agent-level causal intervention. This forms a foundation for future work, including:
 - **Unified Scene-Agent Formulation:** A potential direction is to jointly model scene-level constraint preservation (road topology, traffic rules) and agent-level causal interaction control. This could lead to more expressive and diagnostically informative scenario generation.
 - **Composable Interaction Grammar:** The decomposition of safety into spatial and temporal separation suggests a "counterfactual interaction grammar" of composable primitives. This could enable the generation of a wider diversity of nuanced interactions and support more sophisticated style-aware evaluation.
 - **Extension to 3D Multi-Agent Environments:** As world models advance towards general physical simulation in 3D, extending this causal grammar to more complex environments promises interpretable and controllable interaction generation beyond 2D BEV representations.

In conclusion, CounterScene's introduction of structured counterfactual causal reasoning offers a principled and effective method for safety-critical scenario generation. Its ability to simultaneously achieve realism and adversarial effectiveness, coupled with its interpretability and generalization capabilities, has the potential to significantly improve the methodology for evaluating and validating autonomous driving technologies.
