# VoMP: Predicting Volumetric Mechanical Property Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> A feed-forward model named VOMP, developed by NVIDIA and the University of Toronto, predicts volumetric mechanical property fields for 3D objects, enabling realistic elastodynamic simulations and achieving a 5-100x inference speedup compared to optimization-based methods.

## Problem

- Manually assigning spatially-varying mechanical properties (Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, density) to 3D objects is a time-intensive bottleneck in physics simulation workflows.
- Existing data-driven methods for inferring physical properties from object appearance are often computationally expensive (relying on per-object optimization) or lack volumetric fidelity.
- The scarcity of large-scale, accurately annotated 3D datasets with volumetric material properties limits the training of robust learning models.

## Method

- **VoMP** employs a ==Geometry Transformer== that processes voxelized 3D geometry and aggregates multi-view ==DINOv2== image features to predict ==per-voxel material latent codes==.
- A learned ==material latent space (MatVAE)==, trained on real-world material triplets, ensures that decoded ==Young’s modulus==, ==Poisson’s ratio==, and density values are physically plausible and smoothly interpolable.
- A novel ==automatic data annotation pipeline==, leveraging part-segmented 3D assets, material databases, and ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)==, generates large-scale volumetric material property ground truth for training.

## Results

- VoMP achieved substantially lower errors on a new benchmark dataset of **166** objects, with an Average Log Relative Error (ALRE) for Young’s Modulus of **0.0409**, outperforming prior methods like NeRF2Physics (**0.1346**).
- The method demonstrated a significant speedup, with an average inference time of **3.59 seconds** per object, representing a **5-100x** speedup compared to optimization-based baselines.
- Qualitative evaluations confirmed VOMP's ability to predict realistic, simulation-ready volumetric mechanical fields for various 3D representations, enabling accurate elastodynamic simulations without manual tuning and inferring properties for internal object structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a compact, constrained material latent space (MatVAE) using normalizing flows and disentanglement penalties guarantees physically valid and coherent material property predictions.
> - Aggregating DINOv2 features from multiple rendered views allows the model to generalize across diverse 3D representations (meshes, NeRFs, Gaussian Splats) while capturing fine visual cues for material inference.
> - Combining high-quality part-segmented assets with VLM-guided material assignment and real-world property ranges provides an effective strategy for overcoming the challenge of data scarcity in volumetric material annotation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate physics simulation is a foundational component in fields such as digital twins, Real-2-Sim, and Sim-2-Real workflows. A significant challenge in establishing reliable simulations is the necessity of providing accurate, spatially-varying mechanical properties, specifically Young’s modulus (E), Poisson’s ratio (ν), and density (ρ), throughout the volume of 3D objects. Conventional 3D capture techniques and digital asset repositories typically lack these detailed annotations, often forcing manual, subjective, and time-intensive material assignment by artists and engineers. This limitation hinders the integration of realistic physics into digital pipelines.

Existing research has explored methods for inferring physical properties from object appearance, but these approaches often exhibit several limitations:
- **Computational Cost:** Many prior methods, such as NeRF2Physics (Zhai et al., 2024) and PUGS (Shuai et al., 2025), rely on per-object optimization of feature fields, leading to slow processing times. VoMP aims to provide a feed-forward solution.
- **Representation Specificity:** Several techniques are tailored to particular 3D representations (e.g., Gaussian Splats, specific real-time simulation implementations), restricting their applicability across diverse asset types. VoMP seeks to generalize across any geometry that can be voxelized and rendered.
- **Simulator-Specific Parameters:** Some pipelines are designed for fast, approximate simulators, producing material parameters that may not be physically accurate or transferable between different simulation frameworks. VoMP focuses on predicting true, real-world mechanical properties compatible with high-fidelity simulators like Finite Element Methods.
- **Limited Volumetric Fidelity:** Many approaches primarily focus on surface properties or struggle to predict accurate material values within the object's interior due to a lack of meaningful internal features in their representations. VoMP is explicitly designed to assign materials throughout the object volume.
- **Coarse or Noisy Annotations:** Datasets for training learning-based methods are often small, contain noisy labels, use simulator-specific parameters, or provide only coarse material categories that require manual mapping to continuous physical parameters. VoMP proposes a novel, automatic annotation pipeline to address this data scarcity and quality.
- **Runtime Aggregation of Large Models:** Methods that directly aggregate Vision-Language Model (VLM) outputs or video model supervision at runtime (e.g., Lin et al., 2025a) can be brittle and computationally intensive. VoMP leverages VLMs for *training data generation* to enable a feed-forward inference model.
- **Generative Models:** Some related works (e.g., SOPHY, PhysX-3D) are 3D generative models that produce new shapes with physical attributes but are not designed to augment existing 3D assets, which is a key objective of VoMP.
- **Concurrent Work:** Concurrent efforts like Pixie (Le et al., 2025) are also feed-forward but may rely on surface-biased segmentations or may not prioritize the prediction of physically plausible, real-world material properties.

VoMP addresses these limitations by proposing a feed-forward model that predicts physically valid, continuous volumetric mechanical property fields across various 3D representations. It introduces a novel material latent space and a data annotation pipeline to enable this capability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automated, data-driven method for predicting spatially-varying mechanical properties (Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, and density) throughout the internal volume of arbitrary 3D objects. The predicted properties are intended to be "simulation-ready," meaning they are physically accurate and directly compatible with high-fidelity physics simulators.

The motivation for pursuing this objective is multifaceted:
- **Automating Simulation Setup:** The manual assignment of mechanical properties in 3D models is a significant bottleneck in engineering and design workflows. Automating this process would substantially reduce the labor and expertise required to prepare assets for physics simulations.
- **Enhancing Simulation Realism and Accuracy:** Hand-crafted or generalized material assignments can lead to inaccurate or unrealistic simulation outcomes. Predicting fine-grained, volumetric, and physically plausible properties is crucial for achieving high-fidelity simulations necessary for applications like digital twins, where virtual replicas must behave identically to their real-world counterparts.
- **Bridging the Gap between 3D Assets and Physics:** Common 3D asset creation and capture pipelines do not typically yield mechanical property data. This work aims to augment existing 3D models with the necessary physical information, making them directly usable in physics engines without further manual intervention.
- **Ensuring Physical Plausibility:** Many existing learning-based methods may produce material parameters that yield visually convincing simulations in specific approximate engines but lack real-world physical validity, limiting their transferability and general utility. A core motivation for VoMP is to ensure its predicted properties correspond to measurable, real-world material values.
- **Generalizing Across Diverse 3D Representations:** To be widely applicable, a solution must operate on various common 3D data formats, including meshes, signed distance functions (SDFs), neural radiance fields (NeRFs), and 3D Gaussian Splats.
- **Efficient Inference:** For practical adoption, the prediction process should be fast and efficient, avoiding slow optimization loops or extensive runtime processing per object. A feed-forward architecture is sought to achieve this.
- **Addressing Data Challenges:** The scarcity of large-scale, accurately annotated 3D datasets with volumetric material properties necessitates the development of new data generation methodologies to train robust learning models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VoMP is a feed-forward method that processes 3D geometry to predict volumetric mechanical property fields. Its methodology is structured around three main components: a learned material latent space (MatVAE), a feature aggregation mechanism, and a Geometry Transformer.

#### 3.1. Mechanical Properties Latent Space (MatVAE)

- **Purpose:** To learn a compact, 2-dimensional latent space (z ∈ R²) that represents physically valid triplets of Young’s modulus (E), Poisson’s ratio (ν), and density (ρ). This latent space ensures that all decoded material properties are within a physically plausible range, even during interpolation or generation.
- **Data (Material Triplet Dataset - MTD):** MatVAE is trained on a collected dataset of 100,562 real-world (E, ν, ρ) triplets, sourced from various online material databases. E and ρ values are first log-transformed (log₁₀(E), log₁₀(ρ)) and then normalized to, while ν is directly normalized to.
- **Architecture:** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) forms the core, with several modifications:
 - **Normalizing Flow:** The encoder's output is passed through a radial Normalizing Flow. This allows the variational distribution q_ϕ(z|m) to capture complex, non-Gaussian posteriors (e.g., heavy-tailed distributions for E and ρ, or boundary-concentrated values for ν), improving the approximation of the true material distribution.
 - **Total Correlation (TC) Penalty:** The KL-divergence term in the VAE's Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) is decomposed to include a direct penalty on the total correlation TC(z). This encourages disentanglement within the latent dimensions, preventing redundancy where multiple dimensions encode the same material property.
 - **Capacity Constraint (Free Nats):** A dimension-wise capacity constraint, `max(δ, KL(q_ϕ(z_j) || p(z_j)))`, is applied to the KL-divergence term for each latent dimension. This "free nats" constraint (with δ=0.1) prevents posterior collapse, ensuring that both latent dimensions are actively utilized to encode distinct aspects of the material properties.
- **Loss Function:** The MatVAE is trained with a combined loss (Equation 2) comprising a mean-squared error (MSE) for reconstruction (L_Recon) and regularization terms for the latent space, including mutual information (MI), total correlation (TC), and dimension-wise KL divergence with a capacity constraint.

#### 3.2. Aggregating Features

- **Input Flexibility:** VoMP accepts any 3D representation that can be voxelized and rendered from multiple turnaround views (e.g., meshes, SDFs, NeRFs, 3D Gaussian Splats).
- **Voxelization:** The input 3D geometry is voxelized to create a discrete volumetric representation. Crucially, this process includes the object's interior, enabling the prediction of internal material compositions. A novel three-phase voxelizer is introduced for 3D Gaussian Splats.
- **Multi-view Feature Lifting:** DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) image features are extracted from multiple rendered 2D views of the 3D object. These 2D features are then lifted to 3D by projecting each voxel's center into all rendered views and averaging the retrieved image features to obtain a 1024-dimensional feature vector (f_i) for each active voxel (Equation 3).

#### 3.3. Geometry Transformer

- **Architecture:** The core model is a Transformer-based network, adapted from the TRELLIS (Xiang et al., 2025) encoder/decoder architecture, with its backbone layers initialized with TRELLIS weights. It processes a variable-length set of active voxels, represented by their 3D positions and aggregated DINOv2 features.
- **Spatial Awareness:** Sinusoidal positional encodings derived from each voxel's 3D coordinates are injected to provide spatial context. A 3D shifted window attention mechanism is employed.
- **Scalability:** For objects with more voxels than a predefined maximum sequence length (L_N = 32768), a stochastic sampling strategy is used during training, selecting a random subset of voxels in each epoch to ensure diverse exposure.
- **Output:** The Transformer (F) predicts per-voxel material latent codes. These latents are then passed to the frozen decoder of the pre-trained MatVAE, which decodes them into the final (E, ν, ρ) triplets for each voxel.
- **Loss Function:** The Geometry Transformer is trained using mean squared error (Equation 4) between the decoded (normalized) material properties from the predicted latents and the ground-truth material properties for the sampled voxels.
- **Transfer to Original Representation:** Predicted per-voxel material properties are transferred back to the original geometry (e.g., splat means, FEM tets) using nearest-neighbor interpolation, preserving sharp material interfaces.

#### 3.4. Training Data Generation (Geometry with Volumetric Materials - GVM Dataset)

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** An automatic annotation pipeline is developed to overcome the limited availability of volumetric material datasets.
- **Multi-Source Knowledge Integration:** This pipeline combines knowledge from:
 - **Part-segmented 3D assets:** High-quality 3D meshes with component-wise part segmentations, English material names, and realistic PBR textures (from NVIDIA datasets).
 - **Material databases:** The MTD (§5.1) provides ranges of real-world material properties.
 - **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Qwen 2.5 VL-72B is used. The VLM receives renderings of the full object, detailed renderings of the part's visual material on a sphere, the material's name, and the property ranges of the three closest real-world materials from MTD.
- **Annotation Process:** The VLM outputs material triplets for each part, which are then mapped to all volumetric voxels within that part. This process generated 37 million annotated voxels, providing robust training data. The guidance of the VLM with real-world material ranges helps mitigate inaccuracies and implausible predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates VoMP qualitatively through realistic simulations and quantitatively using dedicated benchmarks.

#### 4.1. End-to-End Qualitative Evaluation

- **Realistic Simulations:** VoMP successfully predicts volumetric mechanical fields for various 3D representations (meshes, 3D Gaussian Splats, SDFs, NeRFs). These predictions enable realistic elastodynamic simulations (e.g., falling oranges, objects navigating a pachinko machine, an armchair reacting to a bowling ball, a bulldozer interacting with a forest of ficus splats) without any manual tuning of parameters (Fig. 5, 8). This suggests that VoMP directly outputs simulation-ready material parameters.
- **Internal Property Inference:** The method demonstrates the ability to infer material properties for internal object structures, even when these are not visible from external renders (e.g., dirt inside a pot, or the internal composition of a complex mesh).
- **Fine Detail Capture:** VoMP is observed to capture fine material details, such as the stem of an orange or the veins of leaves, assigning distinct properties to these small regions (Fig. 9, 10).
- **Representation Agnostic:** The system's ability to operate across meshes, SDFs, NeRFs, and Gaussian Splats is qualitatively demonstrated (Fig. 8a).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

- **Mechanical Property Estimation:** VoMP significantly outperforms prior art—NeRF2Physics (Zhai et al., 2024), PUGS (Shuai et al., 2025), and Phys4DGen\* (Lin et al., 2025a)—on a newly contributed, larger benchmark dataset of 166 objects with 4.9 million point annotations.
 - VoMP achieves substantially lower errors across all evaluated properties (Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, and density) and metrics (Average Log Displacement Error (ALDE), Average Log Relative Error (ALRE), Average Displacement Error (ADE), and Average Relative Error (ARE)) (Fig. 6b, Tb. 2, Tb. 4).
 - For instance, VoMP's ALRE for Young’s Modulus is 0.0409 (±0.04), compared to 0.1346 (±0.05) for NeRF2Physics, 0.1688 (±0.10) for PUGS, and 0.2227 (±0.14) for Phys4DGen\*.
 - Qualitative comparisons further indicate that baselines produce noisier, less accurate, and occasionally mislabeled volumetric values (Fig. 6a).
- **Run-Time Performance:** VoMP demonstrates a considerable speed advantage, with an average inference time of 3.59 seconds per object, representing a 5-100x speedup over prior methods that involve per-object optimization (e.g., NeRF2Physics: 1454.55s, PUGS: 1058.33s). Even compared to other feed-forward approaches like Pixie (Le et al., 2025), VoMP is faster due to lighter preprocessing (Tb. 1). The most time-consuming steps in VoMP are rendering and DINOv2 feature computation, which could be further optimized.
- **Mass Estimation:** On the ABO-500 dataset (Collins et al., 2022) for object mass estimation, VoMP achieves comparable or superior performance across most metrics (Fig. 6c), providing a proxy validation for its density predictions.
- **Material Validity:** VoMP is designed to predict realistic materials. When compared against the MTD dataset of real materials, VoMP consistently outputs values that are closer to physically measured material ranges, exhibiting much lower relative errors than baseline methods (Fig. 6d). This confirms the effectiveness of the MatVAE and annotation pipeline in ensuring physically plausible outputs.

#### 4.3. MatVAE Performance

- **Reconstruction Accuracy:** MatVAE demonstrates low reconstruction errors on the MTD test set for E, ν, and ρ, as well as derived quantities like specific modulus (E/ρ), shear modulus (G), and bulk modulus (K) (Fig. 7a).
- **Latent Space Properties:** The learned 2D latent space exhibits desirable properties:
 - Samples drawn from the latent space decode into plausible, real-world material ranges (Fig. 7b).
 - Real material properties encoded into the latent space vary smoothly, indicating a meaningful organization (Fig. 7c).
 - Interpolation within the latent space generates valid intermediate material properties, unlike naive linear interpolation in (E, ν, ρ) space, which can produce physically impossible values (Fig. 7d, 12).
- **Ablation Study:** Ablations confirm the importance of MatVAE's specific design choices (Tb. 7). MatVAE significantly outperforms a vanilla VAE, which tends to collapse information into a single property. The normalizing flow, total correlation penalty, and free nats capacity constraint are all shown to contribute to improved reconstruction and better-behaved latent space.

#### 4.4. VoMP Ablation Study

- **Image Features:** DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) and CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) features yield comparable performance when the Geometry Transformer starts with random weights, significantly outperforming raw RGB colors as input (Tb. 8).
- **MatVAE's Role:** Directly predicting R³ material vectors without the MatVAE latent space results in substantially worse performance for Young’s Modulus and Poisson’s Ratio, highlighting MatVAE's role in guiding predictions toward physically valid values.
- **Normalization:** The chosen normalization scheme (log-transform for E and ρ, min-max scaling for all) is crucial; alternatives like Z-score normalization or removing log-transforms severely degrade accuracy (Tb. 8).
- **Loss Function:** Using an L2 (MSE) loss for the Geometry Transformer's training yields superior results compared to an L1 loss, with errors increasing by a factor of 2-3x for most properties with L1 (Tb. 8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VoMP represents an advancement in the automated assignment of physical properties to 3D models. By introducing a feed-forward model that generalizes across diverse 3D representations, predicts volumetric material fields, and guarantees physical plausibility through a learned latent space, the research significantly lowers the barrier for integrating accurate physics into digital workflows.

The potential impact of this work is broad:
- **Digital Twins:** It could facilitate the creation of more accurate and realistic digital twins by enabling virtual assets to intrinsically possess correct mechanical behaviors.
- **Robotics:** In simulation-to-reality (Sim-2-Real) and reality-to-simulation (Real-2-Sim) pipelines for robotics, VoMP can accelerate the process of endowing simulated objects with realistic physical properties, thus improving the transferability of learned policies.
- **Design and Engineering:** Engineers and designers can more readily perform material-aware simulations during product development, leading to better-informed design decisions and reduced prototyping cycles.
- **Content Creation:** For 3D content creators, VoMP streamlines the asset preparation phase for physics-enabled interactive experiences or animations, removing a significant manual burden.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The introduction of MatVAE and the automatic data annotation pipeline, along with the release of the new benchmark, provides valuable resources that can support future research in learning and predicting physical properties.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its advancements, the method has identified limitations. The fixed-grid voxelization can result in oversmoothing in highly heterogeneous regions and potential approximation errors when transferring properties to highly detailed input geometries. The current annotation pipeline assumes part-level isotropic materials, which may not hold for all real-world materials (e.g., wood). Future work could explore higher-resolution or adaptive voxelization schemes, extend the method to anisotropic material properties, and predict a broader range of physical parameters such as yield strength, shear modulus, or thermal expansion coefficients. Additionally, research into adapting the physically accurate outputs of VoMP to simulator-specific scales for faster, approximate simulation algorithms could further broaden its utility.
