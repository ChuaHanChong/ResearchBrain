# VoMP: Predicting Volumetric Mechanical Property Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> A feed-forward model named VOMP, developed by NVIDIA and the University of Toronto, predicts volumetric mechanical property fields for 3D objects, enabling realistic elastodynamic simulations and achieving a 5-100x inference speedup compared to optimization-based methods.

## Problem

- Manually assigning spatially-varying mechanical properties (Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, density) to 3D objects is a time-intensive bottleneck in physics simulation workflows.
- Existing data-driven methods for inferring physical properties from object appearance are often computationally expensive (relying on per-object optimization) or lack volumetric fidelity.
- The scarcity of large-scale, accurately annotated 3D datasets with volumetric material properties limits the training of robust learning models.

## Method

- **VoMP** employs a ==Geometry Transformer== that processes voxelized 3D geometry and aggregates multi-view ==DINOv2== image features to predict ==per-voxel material latent codes==.
- A learned ==material latent space (MatVAE)==, trained on real-world material triplets, ensures that decoded ==Young’s modulus==, ==Poisson’s ratio==, and density values are physically plausible and smoothly interpolable.
- A novel ==automatic data annotation pipeline==, leveraging part-segmented 3D assets, material databases, and ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)==, generates large-scale volumetric material property ground truth for training.

## Results

- VoMP achieved substantially lower errors on a new benchmark dataset of **166** objects, with an Average Log Relative Error (ALRE) for Young’s Modulus of **0.0409**, outperforming prior methods like NeRF2Physics (**0.1346**).
- The method demonstrated a significant speedup, with an average inference time of **3.59 seconds** per object, representing a **5-100x** speedup compared to optimization-based baselines.
- Qualitative evaluations confirmed VOMP's ability to predict realistic, simulation-ready volumetric mechanical fields for various 3D representations, enabling accurate elastodynamic simulations without manual tuning and inferring properties for internal object structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a compact, constrained material latent space (MatVAE) using normalizing flows and disentanglement penalties guarantees physically valid and coherent material property predictions.
> - Aggregating DINOv2 features from multiple rendered views allows the model to generalize across diverse 3D representations (meshes, NeRFs, Gaussian Splats) while capturing fine visual cues for material inference.
> - Combining high-quality part-segmented assets with VLM-guided material assignment and real-world property ranges provides an effective strategy for overcoming the challenge of data scarcity in volumetric material annotation.
