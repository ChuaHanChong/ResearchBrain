# ImageNet-trained CNNs are not biased towards texture: Revisiting feature reliance through controlled suppression

> [!summary] Summary
> This research re-evaluates feature reliance in ImageNet-trained CNNs, demonstrating that these models primarily depend on local shape rather than texture, contrary to a prevailing hypothesis. It introduces a systematic suppression framework to quantify feature reliance, revealing domain-specific feature preferences such as color in medical imaging and texture in remote sensing.

## Problem

- The widely accepted "texture bias hypothesis" suggested ImageNet-trained CNNs primarily rely on texture over global shape, leading to a simplified understanding of model perception.
- Traditional "cue-conflict" experiments, used to establish this hypothesis, suffered from methodological issues like entangled features and confounding factors.
- A lack of a controlled, domain-agnostic framework prevented a precise quantification of deep learning models' actual feature reliance, beyond mere preference in conflict.

## Method

- Developed a domain-agnostic framework that quantifies ==feature reliance== by systematically suppressing specific visual cues (shape, texture, color).
- Utilized complementary transformations, such as Patch Shuffle for shape, Bilateral Filtering for texture, and Grayscale for color, each validated to selectively impact the target feature.
- Evaluated human and various CNN/Transformer models' performance under these controlled suppression conditions across multiple visual domains.

## Results

- CNNs exhibited low reliance on texture (retaining **80%** accuracy) but showed significant performance degradation (down to **28%** for ResNet50) under local shape suppression.
- Modern training strategies and architectures (e.g., ConvNeXtV2) improved retained accuracy under local shape suppression to 62-**65%**.
- Cross-domain analysis revealed distinct feature reliance patterns: medical imaging models emphasized color, while remote sensing models showed strong texture reliance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ImageNet-trained CNNs are not inherently texture-biased; instead, they show pronounced reliance on local shape features.
> - Feature reliance is flexible and adapts to the semantic properties of different visual domains, rather than being a fixed characteristic of CNNs.
> - Modern training strategies, advanced architectures, and vision-language pretraining can mitigate local shape reliance and encourage more human-aligned feature use.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly engages with and challenges one of the most influential claims in the field of deep learning interpretability: the **"texture bias hypothesis."** This hypothesis, popularized by Geirhos et al. (2019), posits that ImageNet-trained CNNs are inherently biased towards texture features, unlike humans who predominantly rely on global shape for object recognition. This claim has significantly shaped the discourse on understanding human–machine perceptual differences and has motivated numerous follow-up studies.

Prior to Geirhos et al. (2019), early interpretations of CNNs often assumed a hierarchical build-up of representations from low-level edges to complex shape recognition, similar to human visual processing. However, subsequent work increasingly suggested that CNNs might rely disproportionately on local texture over global shape. The cue-conflict experiment by Geirhos et al. formalized this by presenting models and humans with hybrid images (shape of one object, texture of another) and observing CNNs' preference for the texture cue.

The broader research landscape then branched into several directions:
- **Identifying factors influencing bias:** Studies explored how training objectives, data augmentations (e.g., blurring, cropping), and architectural changes (e.g., Transformers) affect shape and texture bias. Hermann et al. (2020) showed that training strategies could mitigate texture bias. Vision Transformers (ViTs) and vision-language models (like CLIP) were also investigated, with some showing better alignment with human-like shape use.
- **Enforcing shape bias for robustness:** Many methods were developed to encourage shape bias or suppress texture, often with the goal of improving model robustness to adversarial examples or out-of-distribution shifts. Techniques included anisotropic filtering, edge encoding, style disentanglement, and shape-focused augmentations.
- **Human-model alignment:** Beyond just shape bias, a larger goal has been to achieve human-model alignment in terms of error patterns and conceptual structures. This includes work by Geirhos et al. (2018, 2021) on generalization and Muttenthaler et al. (2023) on conceptual alignment.

Burgert et al.'s work intervenes in this landscape by critically re-evaluating the foundational methodology (the cue-conflict experiment) of the texture bias hypothesis. By proposing an alternative, more controlled framework, they aim to provide a clearer and more generalizable understanding of feature reliance, rather than just preference in conflict. This directly addresses the limitations of previous methods and aims to refine our understanding of CNNs' internal representations, moving beyond a potentially oversimplified "texture-or-shape" dichotomy. Their extension to diverse domains also positions the work within the growing interest in understanding how model biases and feature use adapt to different task characteristics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to **revisit and challenge the widely accepted hypothesis that ImageNet-trained CNNs are inherently biased towards texture.** The authors argue for a fundamental re-evaluation of feature reliance in deep neural networks.

The core motivations for this re-examination stem from identified limitations in the traditional "cue-conflict" experiment, particularly as formalized by Geirhos et al. (2019):
- **Conceptual Simplification:** The cue-conflict experiment reduces feature reliance to a binary choice between shape and texture, potentially overlooking other important cues like color. It also conflates "preference" (what a model chooses in a conflict) with "reliance" (what a model genuinely depends on for its decisions in natural settings). A feature might be preferred due to salience, not necessarily its overall importance.
- **Methodological Confounding:**
 - **Lack of Feature Isolation:** The stylized stimuli generated by neural style transfer (e.g., texture cues) often entangle multiple features, including local shape structures and color, making it difficult to attribute decisions solely to "texture."
 - **Overloaded Texture Class Signals:** Texture cues were often inserted into both the object and background regions, giving the "texture class" a broader spatial distribution and potentially biasing CNNs due to signal dominance, not inherent preference.
 - **Human Interface Bias:** The human experiments used shape-based icons for responses, which could inadvertently guide participants towards shape decisions, especially in ambiguous cases.

To overcome these limitations, the authors aim to **introduce a new domain-agnostic evaluation framework** that more cleanly quantifies feature reliance by systematically suppressing individual feature types (shape, texture, and color) and measuring the resulting performance degradation. This framework is designed to provide a more reliable and generalizable method for interpreting model decisions and comparing representational strategies across different models, humans, and visual domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology is a **domain-agnostic framework for quantifying feature reliance through systematic suppression** of specific visual cues. This contrasts with the "forced-choice conflict" approach of previous work.

#### 3.1. Feature Suppression Transformations

The framework defines three primary feature types: shape, texture, and color. For each, two complementary transformations are chosen to selectively suppress the target feature while minimally affecting others:
- **Shape Suppression:**
 - **Patch Shuffle:** Randomizes the spatial positions of non-overlapping image patches, disrupting global and local shape (depending on grid size).
 - **Patch Rotation:** Preserves patch locality but breaks edge continuity by rotating patches.
- **Texture Suppression:**
 - **Bilateral Filtering:** Smooths high-frequency details (texture) while preserving edges (shape).
 - **Gaussian Blur:** Reduces high-frequency details more uniformly, also affecting shape.
- **Color Suppression:**
 - **Grayscale:** Removes all chromatic information.
 - **Channel Shuffle:** Disrupts color correlations without altering intensity.

#### 3.2. Quantitative Validation of Transformations

The effectiveness of these transformations is empirically justified using four quantitative metrics:
- **Texture Suppression Metrics:** Local Variance (LV) and High-Frequency Energy (HFE) are used to assess the reduction of texture. Lower values indicate stronger suppression.
- **Shape Preservation Metrics:** Edge-SSIM (ESSIM) and Gradient Correlation (GC) are used to measure the preservation of edge and structural information. Higher values indicate better preservation.
- A harmonic mean is computed for texture and shape scores. The validation confirms that transformations like bilateral filtering provide a good trade-off between texture reduction and shape preservation, while patch shuffle/rotation effectively disrupt shape while largely preserving texture.

#### 3.3. Experimental Design

The methodology involves two main experiments:

**Experiment I: Human vs. CNNs Feature Reliance**
- **Dataset:** A curated ImageNet16-like dataset with 50 representative images for each of 16 entry-level categories.
- **Conditions:** Humans and models are evaluated under five conditions: original, global shape suppression (Patch Shuffle, grid=3), local shape suppression (Patch Shuffle, grid=6), texture suppression (bilateral filtering), and color suppression (grayscale).
- **Human Protocol:** Twenty participants, each seeing only one randomized version of an image per condition to avoid learning effects. Responses collected via a 4x4 grid of category names.
- **Model Evaluation:** Various CNN architectures (ResNet50-standard, ResNet50-sota, MobileNetV3, EfficientNet, ConvMixer, ConvNeXt) and Transformer-based models (ViT, DeiT, SwinTransformer, CLIP ViT) are tested. Accuracy under suppression is reported relative to baseline accuracy on original images.

**Experiment II: Domain-specific Feature Reliance**
- **Domains:** Computer Vision (CV), Medical Imaging (MI), and Remote Sensing (RS).
- **Datasets:** Five datasets for each domain (e.g., ImageNet, Caltech101 for CV; MedMNIST-v2 datasets for MI; UCMerced, AID for RS).
- **Models:** ResNet50 architecture, trained from scratch or fine-tuned from ImageNet pretraining, with standard augmentations (Random Resized Crop, Horizontal Flip).
- **Suppression:** Suppression strength is treated as a continuous hyperparameter and systematically varied (e.g., increasing grid size for Patch Shuffle, increasing `d` for Bilateral Filter) to generate suppression curves.
- **Comparability:** Performance is standardized by mapping chance-level accuracy to 0 and baseline accuracy to 1, allowing direct cross-domain comparison.
- **Additional Analysis:** Class-wise analysis for ImageNet, effects of pretraining on MI/RS, alternative suppression transformations, and joint suppression of multiple features are included in the supplementary material.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings that significantly revise the understanding of feature reliance in CNNs:

#### 4.1. Experiment I: Human vs. CNNs Feature Reliance

- **Challenging the Texture Bias Hypothesis:** The results directly contradict the claim that CNNs are inherently texture-biased. CNNs (e.g., ResNet50-standard) retained high accuracy (around 80%) under texture suppression, indicating low reliance on texture.
- **Dominant Reliance on Local Shape:** The most significant performance degradation in CNNs was observed under *local shape suppression* (e.g., ResNet50-standard accuracy dropped to 28%), suggesting a pronounced sensitivity and reliance on local shape features rather than texture. Humans also showed highest vulnerability to local shape suppression but retained higher accuracy (76%).
- **Mitigation through Modern Training and Architectures:** Modern training strategies (e.g., ResNet50-sota) and architectures (e.g., ConvNeXtV2) substantially mitigated the reliance on local shape, achieving higher retained accuracy (62-65%) under local shape suppression. This suggests that this reliance is not an inherent architectural bias but can be alleviated.
- **Vision-Language Models Align Closest to Humans:** Among all tested models, the CLIP ViT most closely matched human performance across all feature suppression conditions, implying that vision-language supervision encourages representations more aligned with human perception.
- **Consistency of Transformers:** Vision Transformers (ViT) exhibited feature reliance profiles similar to well-trained CNNs (ResNet50-sota), challenging the notion that transformers are inherently more shape-oriented.

#### 4.2. Experiment II: Domain-specific Feature Reliance

- **Computer Vision (CV) Models Prioritize Shape:** For standard CV benchmarks, fine-tuned CNNs primarily relied on local shape, showing the strongest performance degradation under shape suppression. They were largely robust to texture and color suppression. Models trained from scratch on CV datasets showed increased sensitivity to texture and color compared to pretrained models, but shape remained dominant.
- **Medical Imaging (MI) Models Emphasize Color:** Feature reliance in MI was more heterogeneous across datasets, but a common trend was a substantial dependence on color information. Color suppression induced significant accuracy decline for most MI datasets, reflecting the diagnostic importance of chromatic cues in medical imagery. Shape and texture reliance varied but were generally less pronounced than color.
- **Remote Sensing (RS) Models Exhibit Strong Texture Reliance:** RS models showed pronounced performance degradation under texture suppression, indicating that fine-grained surface patterns are critical for classification in this domain. Color suppression also resulted in notable performance drops, likely due to correlations between chromatic cues and semantic land cover. Reliance on local shape was comparatively lower than in CV.
- **Cross-Domain Differences:** Domain-averaged suppression curves clearly illustrated these systematic differences: CV models (especially from scratch) were most reliant on local shape, MI models on color, and RS models on texture. This highlights that feature reliance is not fixed but is shaped by data properties and domain-specific semantics, alongside architecture and training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper makes significant contributions to the fields of deep learning interpretability, computer vision, and cognitive science by fundamentally challenging a widely held belief about CNNs and proposing a robust alternative methodology.

#### 5.1. Re-evaluation of the Texture Bias Hypothesis

The most immediate impact is the **refutation of the "inherent texture bias" claim** in ImageNet-trained CNNs. By demonstrating that CNNs primarily rely on local shape, and that this reliance can be mitigated by modern training strategies, the paper shifts the understanding of CNNs' perceptual strategies. This challenges a dominant narrative that has informed countless research directions and model designs, paving the way for a more nuanced understanding of how these models "see" the world.

#### 5.2. Novel and Robust Methodological Framework

The proposed "targeted suppression" framework offers a **more controlled, domain-agnostic, and less confounded method** for quantifying feature reliance compared to the cue-conflict experiment. This methodology provides a valuable tool for future research aiming to understand model behavior, biases, and representational strategies in a cleaner, more interpretable way. Its quantitative validation further enhances its credibility.

#### 5.3. Insights into Human-Model Alignment

The finding that modern training strategies and architectures (especially vision-language models like CLIP ViT) can **encourage more human-aligned feature reliance** is crucial. This suggests that achieving human-like perception in AI is not solely about architectural inductive biases but also heavily dependent on optimization objectives and the richness of training signals. This opens new avenues for designing models that are not only high-performing but also more interpretable and aligned with human cognitive processes.

#### 5.4. Domain-Specific Understanding of Feature Reliance

The extensive cross-domain analysis is highly significant. It demonstrates that feature reliance is **flexible and adapts to the statistical structure and semantic properties of different visual domains.** This finding has direct implications for:
- **Model Design and Training:** Tailoring model architectures and training protocols to emphasize (or de-emphasize) specific feature types based on domain characteristics can lead to more robust and effective models (e.g., enhancing texture processing for remote sensing, or color sensitivity for medical imaging).
- **Interpretability:** Understanding domain-specific feature reliance improves the interpretability of models in specialized applications, where reliance on particular cues might be critical for trust and deployment (e.g., explaining why a medical model uses color vs. shape for diagnosis).
- **Generalization and Robustness:** A deeper understanding of feature reliance can inform strategies to improve out-of-distribution generalization and robustness, as models might need to flexibly adapt their feature use to novel environments.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The paper explicitly points to future work, such as systematically evaluating the relative contributions of architectural components and training strategies to these reliance patterns. The framework also opens doors for exploring feature reliance in other modalities (e.g., video, 3D data) and for investigating the interaction between different features more deeply.

In conclusion, this research is impactful for its rigorous re-examination of a foundational claim, its development of a robust new methodology, and its provision of detailed, actionable insights into how deep learning models leverage visual features across diverse domains, ultimately fostering the development of more human-aligned and contextually intelligent AI systems.
