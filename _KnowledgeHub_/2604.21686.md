# WorldMark: A Unified Benchmark Suite for Interactive Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldMark is a unified benchmark suite designed for interactive Image-to-Video (I2V) world models, providing a standardized framework to evaluate disparate models on shared scenes and action sequences. The suite standardizes evaluation by translating common actions into each model's native control format, and its automated metrics achieved a strong correlation (ρ > 0.9) with human perceptual judgments.

## Problem

- Evaluation of interactive video world models was fragmented due to each model using proprietary datasets, custom scenes, and specific action trajectories, preventing fair cross-model comparison.
- A lack of standardized test conditions meant that even common metrics yielded incomparable numerical values across models with diverse control interfaces.
- Existing public benchmarks focused on general video generation or 3D consistency but did not jointly evaluate action controllability, visual quality, and long-horizon world consistency under standardized interactive conditions.

## Method

- The **WorldMark** benchmark provides a ==unified action-mapping layer== that translates a common WASD-style action vocabulary into the specific control formats required by different interactive world models.
- Curates a ==hierarchical test suite of 500 evaluation cases==, covering various perspectives, visual styles, and three difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard).
- A modular evaluation toolkit measures performance across ==eight metrics in three dimensions==: Visual Quality (Aesthetic, Imaging), Control Alignment (Translation, Rotation), and World Consistency (Reprojection, State, Content, Style).

## Results

- Closed-source **Genie 3** demonstrated superior world consistency, while open-source models (e.g., **YUME 1.5**) often led in visual quality metrics in first-person scenarios.
- Models like **HY-Game** showed strong control alignment with low translation/rotation errors, but this did not always correspond to leading performance in visual quality or world consistency.
- WorldMark's automated evaluation scores exhibited Spearman's rank correlation **ρ > 0.9** with human perceptual judgments, validating alignment with human preferences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual quality often showed an uncorrelation with long-horizon logical world consistency; models excelling in one often lacked in the other.
> - Achieving precise control alignment did not necessarily translate to high overall generation quality, sometimes at the expense of visual fidelity or global coherence.
> - Third-person interactive video generation presented significant challenges for several models, revealing difficulties in maintaining stable camera control and character interaction.
> - Domain-specific models exhibited poor generalizability, struggling to perform adequately when tested on scenes outside their training domains.
