# WorldMark: A Unified Benchmark Suite for Interactive Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldMark is a unified benchmark suite designed for interactive Image-to-Video (I2V) world models, providing a standardized framework to evaluate disparate models on shared scenes and action sequences. The suite standardizes evaluation by translating common actions into each model's native control format, and its automated metrics achieved a strong correlation (ρ > 0.9) with human perceptual judgments.

## Problem

- Evaluation of interactive video world models was fragmented due to each model using proprietary datasets, custom scenes, and specific action trajectories, preventing fair cross-model comparison.
- A lack of standardized test conditions meant that even common metrics yielded incomparable numerical values across models with diverse control interfaces.
- Existing public benchmarks focused on general video generation or 3D consistency but did not jointly evaluate action controllability, visual quality, and long-horizon world consistency under standardized interactive conditions.

## Method

- The **WorldMark** benchmark provides a ==unified action-mapping layer== that translates a common WASD-style action vocabulary into the specific control formats required by different interactive world models.
- Curates a ==hierarchical test suite of 500 evaluation cases==, covering various perspectives, visual styles, and three difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard).
- A modular evaluation toolkit measures performance across ==eight metrics in three dimensions==: Visual Quality (Aesthetic, Imaging), Control Alignment (Translation, Rotation), and World Consistency (Reprojection, State, Content, Style).

## Results

- Closed-source **Genie 3** demonstrated superior world consistency, while open-source models (e.g., **YUME 1.5**) often led in visual quality metrics in first-person scenarios.
- Models like **HY-Game** showed strong control alignment with low translation/rotation errors, but this did not always correspond to leading performance in visual quality or world consistency.
- WorldMark's automated evaluation scores exhibited Spearman's rank correlation **ρ > 0.9** with human perceptual judgments, validating alignment with human preferences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual quality often showed an uncorrelation with long-horizon logical world consistency; models excelling in one often lacked in the other.
> - Achieving precise control alignment did not necessarily translate to high overall generation quality, sometimes at the expense of visual fidelity or global coherence.
> - Third-person interactive video generation presented significant challenges for several models, revealing difficulties in maintaining stable camera control and character interaction.
> - Domain-specific models exhibited poor generalizability, struggling to perform adequately when tested on scenes outside their training domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of interactive video generation models, often referred to as "world models," is experiencing rapid advancements, with notable systems such as Genie, YUME, HY-World, and Matrix-Game emerging. These models aim to create interactive environments that respond to user actions and camera controls while maintaining long-term scene memory. However, the evaluation of these models has historically been fragmented. Each model is typically benchmarked against its own private datasets, custom scenes, and specific action trajectories, which impedes direct and fair cross-model comparison. This practice makes it challenging to accurately assess the incremental progress of individual models or to identify leading approaches under controlled conditions.

Existing public benchmarks address various aspects of video generation but do not fully bridge this gap for interactive world models. For instance, VBench and VBench++ focus on the general quality of text-to-video and image-to-video generation, respectively, without incorporating action control. WorldScore introduces 3D-aware consistency metrics but uses passive camera paths that do not reflect action-driven interaction. The concurrent MIND benchmark targets interactive generation but relies on ground-truth camera trajectories, which many keyboard-controlled models cannot utilize. Other efforts like PhyGenBench, WorldModelBench, and 4DWorldBench concentrate on physical plausibility or 3D/4D consistency but similarly lack standardized interactive action sequences.

A critical disconnect in the current landscape is the absence of a benchmark that jointly evaluates action controllability, visual quality, and long-horizon world consistency under standardized interactive conditions. This results in scenarios where models might appear to excel under their proprietary test settings, while their performance under common, semantically equivalent inputs remains obscured. WorldMark positions itself to address this fundamental limitation by providing a unified and standardized evaluation framework. It aims to establish a common ground for comparison, allowing researchers to evaluate interactive world models using identical scenes, action sequences, and a transparent translation layer for heterogeneous control interfaces, thereby enabling meaningful and reproducible comparisons across diverse architectures and input modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WorldMark is to introduce the first standardized benchmark purpose-built for interactive Image-to-Video (I2V) world models. This initiative is motivated by the recognized fragmentation in the evaluation practices for these rapidly evolving models. Currently, interactive world models are developed with disparate control interfaces—ranging from natural language captions and structured pose commands to gamepad-style inputs and custom action functions. Each model is then evaluated using its own unique scenes, action sequences, and evaluation protocols. This bespoke benchmarking approach makes it nearly impossible to conduct an "apples-to-apples" comparison and reliably determine which models genuinely advance the state of world modeling versus those that merely perform well under specific, possibly favorable, test conditions.

The authors argue that the core issue is not a deficiency in available metrics—as metrics for trajectory error, aesthetic quality, and VLM-based judgments already exist—but rather the critical absence of standardized test conditions. When models are evaluated on distinct scenes, trajectories, and action definitions, even shared metrics yield incomparable numerical values. Therefore, the central motivation behind WorldMark is to establish a common playing field characterized by identical scenes, identical action sequences, and a transparent translation layer that feeds semantically equivalent inputs to each model through its native control interface.

To achieve this, WorldMark sets several key objectives:
1. **Unified Action-Mapping Layer:** Develop a standardized action vocabulary (WASD-style) and construct an action-mapping layer that translates these common actions into the specific control format required by each diverse model (e.g., caption prompts for YUME, pose parameters for HY-World, gamepad controls for Genie 3). This layer is designed to enable direct and fair comparisons across multiple prominent models on shared scenes and trajectories.
2. **Hierarchical Test Suite:** Curate a comprehensive test suite comprising 500 evaluation cases. This suite is designed to offer broad coverage by including various perspectives (first-person and third-person), visual styles (photorealistic and stylized scenes), and distinct difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard) based on action sequence duration and complexity (20–60 seconds).
3. **Modular Evaluation Toolkit:** Provide a ready-to-use toolkit for evaluating generated videos across three critical dimensions: Visual Quality, Control Alignment, and World Consistency. This toolkit is modular, allowing researchers to leverage the standardized inputs provided by WorldMark while integrating their own metrics as the field progresses.
4. **Resource Dissemination:** Commit to releasing all collected data, evaluation code, and outputs from the benchmarked models to foster transparency, reproducibility, and further research within the community.
5. **Online Engagement Platform:** Launch an online platform, World Model Arena (warena.ai), to enable live, side-by-side comparisons of leading world models, complete with a dynamic leaderboard to encourage competitive development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldMark is designed as a standardized benchmarking toolkit structured around five core components: an Evaluation Dimension Suite, an Image Suite, an Action Suite, a Unified Action Interface, and an Evaluation Workflow.

**Image Suite:** A diverse set of 50 reference images was curated from the WorldScore dataset. Duplicate and near-duplicate scenes were removed to enhance visual diversity. These initial images were designated as first-person views. To enable cross-viewpoint evaluation, a corresponding third-person view was synthesized for each original image using an image generation model (Nano-Banana-2), resulting in a total of 100 test images. The Image Suite encompasses three scene categories (Nature, City, Indoor), two visual styles (Real, Stylized), and two viewpoints (first-person, third-person), with the stylized subset including various aesthetics such as oil painting, Ukiyo-e, cyberpunk, and Minecraft.

**Action Suite:** Fifteen standardized action sequences were defined, varying in complexity. These actions are expressed using a shared vocabulary of six discrete primitives: forward (W), backward (S), strafe-left (A), strafe-right (D), yaw-left (L), and yaw-right (R). Actions are organized into three difficulty tiers: Easy (single-segment, 20s), Medium (two-segment, 40s), and Hard (three-segment, 60s). This tiered approach evaluates basic compliance, smooth transitions, and sustained consistency over longer horizons, respectively. A Vision-Language Model (VLM), Gemini-3.1-Pro, is employed to analyze each reference image and select contextually plausible action sequences from this library, ensuring that physical constraints of the scene are respected. This process generates approximately 500 standardized evaluation cases.

**Unified Action Interface:** A critical component is the unified action interface, which addresses the heterogeneity of control formats across different interactive world models. Models like YUME 1.5 interpret actions from natural-language captions, HY-World 1.5 and HY-GameCraft use 6-DoF pose parameters, Genie 3 accepts gamepad-style controls, Matrix-Game 2.0 exposes custom action functions, and Open-Oasis uses 25-dimensional continuous action vectors. WorldMark implements a specific action-mapping adapter for each supported model. These adapters translate the shared WASD+L/R vocabulary into the model's native input format. The adapters also calibrate model-specific parameters, such as step size and yaw rate, to ensure that the intended semantic action is faithfully reproduced, thereby enabling semantically identical instructions across models.

**Evaluation Dimension Suite:** WorldMark evaluates generated videos across three complementary dimensions using eight metrics:
- **Visual Quality:** Assesses per-frame fidelity.
 - *Aesthetic Quality:* Measured using the LAION aesthetic predictor, trained on human preference data.
 - *Imaging Quality:* Quantified by MUSIQ, which detects low-level distortions like over-exposure, noise, and blur.
- **Control Alignment:** Measures the faithfulness of the model's response to input actions by reconstructing 3D camera poses.
 - *Translation Error:* Scale-invariant Euclidean distance between DROID-SLAM-reconstructed camera positions and ground-truth trajectories.
 - *Rotation Error:* Geodesic angular deviation between estimated and ground-truth rotation matrices, primarily capturing yaw-axis deviations.
- **World Consistency:** Evaluates the model’s ability to maintain a coherent world over time, combining geometric reconstruction and VLM-based judgment.
 - *Reprojection Error:* Assesses 3D spatial coherence using DROID-SLAM with Dense Bundle Adjustment, detecting scene structure collapse or unnatural morphing.
 - *State Consistency:* A VLM tracks primary subjects across frames to detect abrupt mutations or jerky movements.
 - *Content Consistency:* A VLM compares scene elements across keyframes to penalize sudden appearance/disappearance of objects (e.g., object hallucination, popping effect).
 - *Style Consistency:* A VLM analyzes global visual characteristics (aesthetic tone, lighting, color palette) across the video to detect stylistic degradation.

**Evaluation Workflow:** This pipeline integrates the components:
1. **Image Selection:** Users select images by viewpoint, scene category, visual style, or provide custom references for VLM-based action inference.
2. **Action Mapping:** Per-model adapters translate the shared actions into the target model’s native control format.
3. **Video Generation:** The target model generates video outputs under standardized conditions.
4. **Metric Evaluation:** The generated outputs are scored using the eight-metric suite. The modular design supports the integration of custom or third-party metrics.

**Experiment Setup:** Six representative models were benchmarked: YUME 1.5, Matrix-Game 2.0, HY-World 1.5, HY-GameCraft, Open-Oasis (all open-source), and Google's Genie 3 (closed-source). All six models were evaluated in first-person scenarios. For third-person evaluations, only models with native support (Matrix-Game 2.0, HY-World 1.5, Genie 3) were included. The VLM Gemini-3.1-Pro was used for both action selection and video evaluation. Experiments were conducted across four main scenarios: First-Person Real, First-Person Stylized, Third-Person Real, and Third-Person Stylized.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation conducted using WorldMark yielded several quantitative and qualitative findings regarding the performance of interactive video world models.

**Quantitative Evaluation Results:**

- **First-Person View (Real and Stylized Scenarios):**
 - **Visual Quality:** YUME 1.5 and HY-World 1.5 generally demonstrated superior performance. YUME 1.5 achieved the highest Aesthetic Quality (56.94) and Imaging Quality (74.36) in the Real scenario. HY-World 1.5 led in Aesthetic Quality (58.50) in the Stylized scenario.
 - **Control Alignment:** HY-Game showed strong control, exhibiting the lowest Translation Error in both Real (0.159) and Stylized (0.116) scenarios, and the lowest Rotation Error (0.932) in Stylized settings. Matrix-Game 2.0 also displayed strong rotational alignment in the Real scenario (1.324).
 - **World Consistency:** Genie 3, a closed-source model, generally outperformed open-source baselines across most consistency metrics. It consistently achieved the best or second-best results in Reprojection Error, State Consistency, Content Consistency, and Style Consistency in both Real and Stylized first-person scenarios, demonstrating robust spatial-temporal coherence.

- **Third-Person View (Real and Stylized Scenarios):**
 - Evaluations were restricted to Matrix-Game 2.0, HY-World 1.5, and Genie 3 due to native support for third-person viewpoints.
 - **Visual Quality:** HY-World 1.5 established a clear advantage, showing superior Aesthetic and Imaging Quality in both Real and Stylized third-person domains.
 - **Control Alignment:** HY-World 1.5 also exhibited strong control, particularly in the Real scenario, with the lowest Translation Error (0.206) and Rotation Error (2.137).
 - **World Consistency:** Genie 3 maintained its dominance in World Consistency, achieving the highest scores in State, Content, and Style Consistency in both Real and Stylized settings. This indicates its strength in preserving complex world dynamics and temporal consistency even in third-person interactive video generation.

- **Real vs. Stylized Comparison:** Stylized scenes sometimes resulted in improved aesthetic scores for certain models (e.g., HY-World 1.5 Aesthetic Quality increasing from 54.79 to 58.50). However, they often led to a degradation in control alignment (e.g., HY-World 1.5 Rotation Error increasing from 2.08° to 4.32°), suggesting that non-photorealistic textures might introduce challenges for control or that models struggle with out-of-distribution visual styles.

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Uncorrelated Visual Quality and World Consistency:** Models capable of generating aesthetically pleasing frames (e.g., YUME 1.5) often exhibited a lack of long-horizon logical coherence in their generated worlds. Conversely, models maintaining strong world consistency (e.g., Genie 3) sometimes presented only moderate frame-level visual fidelity.
2. **Control Alignment vs. Overall Quality:** Achieving precise control alignment (e.g., HY-Game) did not necessarily translate to high overall generation quality, sometimes coming at the expense of visual fidelity. Models like Genie 3, despite showing higher trajectory error in some cases, preserved a more globally coherent world.
3. **Third-Person Generation Challenges:** The transition from first-person to third-person generation exposed significant weaknesses in several models. For instance, Matrix-Game 2.0's rotation error increased by approximately 20 times, highlighting the difficulty in maintaining stable camera control around a visible character.
4. **Domain-Specific Training Limitations:** Open-Oasis, a model specifically trained on Minecraft, performed poorly across all evaluation metrics when tested on real-world and stylized scenes, indicating a lack of generalizability beyond its training domain.

**Qualitative Evaluation and Human Preference Alignment:**
Qualitative comparisons visually demonstrated both successful video generation (high visual fidelity, correct action execution, consistent character/background/style) and common failure modes (visual degradation, incorrect translations, stylistic shifts, object hallucination). A human preference alignment study involving 20 volunteers, who ranked videos, showed a strong Spearman's rank correlation (ρ > 0.9) with the automated WorldMark Evaluation scores. This strong alignment indicates that the automated metrics developed within WorldMark closely correspond to human perceptual judgments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldMark represents a significant contribution to the field of interactive video world modeling by addressing a fundamental gap in evaluation practices. Its primary significance lies in providing the first standardized benchmark suite for Interactive Image-to-Video (I2V) world models. This directly resolves the long-standing issue of fragmented evaluations, where disparate control interfaces and private datasets rendered cross-model comparisons infeasible.

The **unified action interface** is a key innovation, enabling "apples-to-apples" comparison across diverse models with heterogeneous input requirements. This capability allows researchers to truly gauge the progress of various world modeling approaches under identical conditions, differentiating genuine advancements from performance optimized for specific proprietary benchmarks. This newfound comparability is crucial for fostering fair competition and accelerating research by providing a clear, common standard for progress.

The **hierarchical test suite** offers comprehensive coverage across various viewpoints, visual styles, and difficulty levels, ensuring a thorough evaluation of model capabilities in diverse scenarios relevant to real-world applications. The inclusion of three difficulty tiers allows for granular assessment of model robustness and long-horizon consistency, which are critical for interactive environments.

The **modular evaluation toolkit** is designed for extensibility, allowing the research community to integrate new metrics as the field evolves while still utilizing WorldMark's standardized inputs. This forward-looking design ensures the benchmark's longevity and adaptability to future research trends.

The **main findings** of the study have direct implications for future model development:
- The observed uncorrelation between visual quality and world consistency highlights a critical trade-off that current models face, guiding future research toward developing architectures that can achieve both high visual fidelity and long-term coherence simultaneously.
- The distinction between precise control alignment and overall generation quality indicates that models need to prioritize not just obeying commands but also maintaining a visually and logically consistent world.
- The pronounced challenges in third-person generation identify a specific failure mode and an open research problem, suggesting areas where focused development is needed to enhance camera control and character interaction.
- The poor transferability of domain-specific training emphasizes the need for more generalizable world models that can operate effectively across a wider range of scenes and styles.

Furthermore, the commitment to **releasing all data, evaluation code, and model outputs** promotes transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative research within the community. The launch of the **World Model Arena** online platform fosters community engagement, allowing for live comparisons and contributing to a dynamic and competitive research environment. Overall, WorldMark has the potential to become a foundational tool, providing essential insights and a common framework to guide and accelerate the development of more robust, consistent, and truly interactive video world models.
