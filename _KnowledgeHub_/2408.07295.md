# Learning Multi-Modal Whole-Body Control for Real-World Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Oregon State University developed the Masked Humanoid Controller (MHC), a learned whole-body controller for real-world humanoid robots, which utilizes a unified interface of masked target trajectories to interpret and execute diverse behaviors. This controller demonstrated robust execution across locomotion, mimicry, and loco-manipulation tasks, achieving successful sim-to-real transfer with a 5% real-world failure rate on a Digit V3 robot.

## Problem

- Traditional humanoid controllers are often specialized for individual skills, leading to limited versatility, difficulty in extension, and poor reusability across different applications.
- A unified input representation for diverse control modalities (e.g., velocity commands, end-effector targets, motion capture) is lacking, complicating application development and multi-modal input handling.
- Integrating full whole-body capabilities, including seamless transitions between locomotion modes and manipulation, remains a challenge, often requiring complex switching mechanisms between specialized controllers.

## Method

- Developed the **Masked Humanoid Controller (MHC)**, a ==reinforcement learning== policy trained via ==PPO== in simulation, which processes motion directives as future pose trajectories with optional binary masks.
- The MHC receives the robot's current proprioceptive state and a ==masked motion directive==, then outputs PD setpoints for all robot motors, allowing it to "fill in the blanks" for unspecified joints.
- Implemented a ==three-stage curriculum== (Locomotion, Stability, Whole-Body Directives) and extensive ==domain randomization== during training to foster robustness and enable effective sim-to-real transfer across various behaviors.

## Results

- MHC demonstrated competitive locomotion performance compared to the state-of-the-art SaW controller, achieving lower root drift in walking tasks (e.g., **0.413m** for 1m/s x-velocity vs. SaW's **0.803m**).
- For combined locomotion and upper-body mimicry, MHC significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a **0.450%** failure rate (vs. **49.213%** for OffsetBlind) and a lower mean end-effector positional error of **0.098m**.
- Successfully transferred complex, multi-modal behaviors to a real-world Digit V3 robot, executing joystick locomotion, boxing jab mimicry, and loco-manipulation with a **5%** real-world failure rate and a **0.117m** mean end-effector positional error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing target behaviors as future pose trajectories with optional masking provides a unified and expressive interface, enabling a single learned controller to handle diverse inputs from locomotion to full-body imitation.
> - A progressive, three-stage training curriculum significantly improves the controller's ability to learn complex tasks, resulting in enhanced walking, mimicry, and stability compared to training without a structured curriculum.
> - The framework's performance on various tasks shows a trend of improvement with larger and more diverse training datasets, indicating its scalability and potential for continuous repertoire expansion.
