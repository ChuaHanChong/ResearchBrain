# CycleResearcher: Improving Automated Research via Automated Review

> [!summary] Summary
> CycleResearcher is an open-source framework that automates the research-review-refinement loop by post-training LLMs. A policy model (CycleResearcher) writes papers, and a reward model (CycleReviewer) simulates peer review, giving preference pairs for iterative SimPO training. CycleReviewer cuts score-prediction MAE by 26.89% versus individual human reviewers. CycleResearcher papers reach a simulated review score of 5.36, above the 5.24 average for human preprints.

## Problem

- Existing automated-research agents depend on commercial LLMs, so they cannot be treated as trainable policies and cannot be improved with reinforcement learning from review feedback.
- Prior work handles isolated stages such as idea generation or experiment assistance, and rarely covers the full cycle from literature review through writing, review and refinement.
- Self-correction and preference-optimization methods have been applied to reasoning, not to paper writing, which needs multi-perspective evaluation and lacks large open datasets of papers and structured peer reviews.

## Method

- ==Iterative preference-training== framework: **CycleResearcher** (policy, 12B to 123B on Mistral and Qwen2.5) drafts papers and **CycleReviewer** (==generative reward model==) scores them, yielding win/lose pairs optimized with ==iterative SimPO== plus an ==NLL term==.
- Two new datasets: **Review-5k** (4,991 ICLR 2024 papers, over 16,000 reviews with soundness, presentation, contribution and rating) and **Research-14k** (14,911 accepted papers with structured outlines, segmented text and cited abstracts).
- **CycleResearcher** plans from a ==literature-informed outline== and generates the full paper, with experiment results fabricated in training for speed and execution left to code models. ==Rejection sampling== over generated candidates raises quality further.

## Results

- CycleReviewer reduces Proxy MSE by **48.77%** and Proxy MAE by **26.89%** versus individual human reviewers, with **74.24%** decision accuracy, competitive with closed-source systems.
- CycleResearcher-12B scores **5.36** in simulated review versus **5.24** for human preprints and **5.69** for accepted papers. Its acceptance rate is **35.13%**, while AI Scientist scores **4.31** with **0%** acceptance.
- Ablations on the 12B model: removing RL drops the score from **5.36** to **5.12**, removing iteration to **5.21**, and removing the NLL loss to **4.91** with acceptance falling from **35.14%** to **12.03%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Open, trainable models let a simulated review loop serve as an RL reward, so research quality can be optimized directly instead of relying on prompt-engineered API agents.
> - Regularizing preference optimization with an NLL term is critical, since dropping it caused the largest ablation drop. Preference-only training on generated long documents is unstable.
> - Because the policy and reward model are not updated together, the policy can hack the reviewer. Reported gains are simulated-review scores on fabricated results, not real research quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The automation of scientific discovery has been a long-standing ambition, dating back to the early days of artificial intelligence in the late 1970s and 1980s. Historically, efforts focused on symbolic AI systems for tasks like hypothesis generation and data interpretation. The recent surge in large language models (LLMs) has dramatically revitalized this field, opening unprecedented possibilities for automating complex intellectual tasks.

Current research leveraging LLMs for scientific endeavors primarily falls into several categories:
- **Research Assistance/Idea Generation:** Many works explore LLMs as tools to propose novel research ideas, summarize literature, or assist researchers in conceptualizing problems. These often rely on commercial, closed-source LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) due to their advanced capabilities.
- **Automated Experimentation/Code Generation:** Some initiatives focus on using LLMs to design experiments, generate code for simulations, or even interpret experimental results, effectively acting as "AI scientists" in specific domains.
- **Partial Publication Generation:** While LLMs have been shown to generate segments of scientific papers (e.g., introductions, related work), automating the *entire* manuscript preparation process that meets peer-review standards remains a significant challenge.

This paper distinguishes itself by addressing several critical gaps in the existing landscape:
- **Full-Cycle Automation with Open-Source LLMs:** Unlike many current efforts that rely on commercial, black-box LLMs and focus on isolated aspects of research, "CYCLERESEARCHER" specifically explores the feasibility of automating the *entire* research-review-refinement cycle using *open-source*, post-trained LLMs. This is crucial for transparency, reproducibility, and enabling reinforcement learning-based policy optimization.
- **Integration of Iterative Feedback (Automated Peer Review):** A fundamental missing piece in most automated research pipelines is the iterative refinement driven by peer feedback. The paper introduces CycleReviewer, a generative reward model, to simulate this crucial peer review process. This integration mirrors the real-world scientific process, where feedback from reviewers plays a vital role in refining and improving research quality. This moves beyond simple self-correction mechanisms in LLMs by introducing a multi-perspective, evaluative component specific to academic standards.
- **Addressing Academic Integrity Concerns:** As LLMs become more capable, concerns about their misuse in academic writing (e.g., generating plagiarized or low-quality papers) have grown. This work proactively addresses these ethical considerations by integrating AI-generated content detection and proposing disclosure frameworks, positioning itself within the broader discussion on responsible AI in academia.

In essence, "CYCLERESEARCHER" pushes the boundaries from LLMs as mere research *assistants* to LLMs as *autonomous agents* capable of engaging in the core cyclical process of scientific inquiry, from ideation to refinement, all within an open-source and iteratively trainable framework. It aims to bridge the gap between LLM capabilities and the rigorous demands of peer-reviewed scientific publications, while simultaneously laying groundwork for ethical AI integration in research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to accelerate scientific knowledge creation by exploring the frontier of automated scientific discovery. The authors posit that while large language models (LLMs) have shown promise in assisting various research tasks, the possibility of automating the entire research process, especially with open-source LLMs, remains largely unexplored.

The key objectives and motivations driving this work can be broken down as follows:

1. **Automate the Full Research Lifecycle with Open-Source LLMs:** The primary goal is to determine the feasibility of using open-source, post-trained LLMs to perform the complete cycle of automated research and review. This includes crucial stages such as literature review, idea generation, experimental design, manuscript preparation, peer review, and subsequent paper refinement. The emphasis on *open-source* models is a deliberate choice, aiming to overcome the limitations of proprietary LLMs which cannot be effectively modeled for policy optimization using reinforcement learning.

2. **Integrate Iterative Feedback for Quality Improvement:** Inspired by the human scientific process where peer review is central to maintaining quality and integrity, the authors aim to integrate an iterative feedback mechanism. By simulating the peer review process, the system can provide continuous feedback, allowing the research-generating LLM to refine its outputs. This is motivated by the understanding that current LLMs often struggle to adapt and improve across the full spectrum of research stages without structured, iterative guidance. The central research question encapsulates this: "How can we automate the Research-Review-Refinement process by post-training LLMs?"

3. **Develop Specialized Models and Datasets:** To achieve the above objectives, the authors recognize the need for purpose-built components. They aim to develop:
 - **CycleResearcher:** A "policy model" that acts as the scientific thinker and manuscript generator.
 - **CycleReviewer:** A "reward model" that simulates the peer review process to provide evaluative feedback.
 - **New Datasets:** The creation of Review-5k and Research-14k datasets is motivated by the need for high-quality, large-scale data specifically reflecting real-world machine learning research and peer review dynamics to effectively train these specialized models.

4. **Advance AI-Driven Research Capabilities:** Beyond the immediate practical application, a core motivation is to represent a significant step toward fully automated scientific inquiry, pushing the boundaries of what AI can achieve in complex, creative domains like research.

5. **Establish Ethical Safeguards:** Recognizing the potential for misuse of LLMs in academic contexts, a crucial motivation is to proactively implement ethical safeguards. This includes developing detection tools for AI-generated content and proposing licensing frameworks with clear disclosure requirements, aiming to protect academic integrity and ensure responsible deployment of such powerful tools.

In essence, the project is motivated by a grand vision of accelerated scientific discovery, grounded in a practical approach that leverages open-source AI, iterative learning, and a strong commitment to ethical considerations in the evolving landscape of AI-assisted research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of "CYCLERESEARCHER" revolves around an innovative **iterative preference training framework** that mirrors the real-world Research-Review-Refinement cycle, built upon the Simple Preference Optimization (SimPO) method. This framework consists of two main components: CycleResearcher (the policy model) and CycleReviewer (the reward model).

#### 3.1. Datasets Construction

Two large-scale, publicly available datasets were meticulously constructed:

- **Review-5k:** Designed for training the CycleReviewer. It comprises 4,991 papers from ICLR 2024, accompanied by over 16,000 reviewer comments. Each entry includes the paper's title, abstract, and content (converted from LaTeX/PDF), along with comprehensive review components: summary, strengths, weaknesses, clarification questions, numerical scores (soundness, presentation, contribution), and an overall rating. The dataset is split into 4,189 training and 782 testing samples.

- **Research-14k:** Created for training the CycleResearcher. This dataset contains 14,911 accepted papers (12,696 training, 802 test samples after filtering) from major machine learning conferences (ICLR, NeurIPS, ICML, ACL, EMNLP, CVPR, ICCV) from 2022-2024. The data construction involves:
 - Compiling accepted papers with LaTeX-format files from ArXiv.
 - Pre-processing the main text to remove irrelevant content.
 - Retrieving cited works' abstracts via Semantic Scholar API to enrich the background knowledge.
 - Organizing the main body into structured outlines (motivation, methods, experimental setup, results) and corresponding sections using Mistral-Large-2, enabling the LLM to plan research before generation.
 This dataset is characterized by long outputs (average 28K tokens), crucial for comprehensive paper generation.

#### 3.2. Iterative Training Framework

The framework operates in a cyclical manner (Figure 2 in the paper):

- **Initialization:** A baseline language model (Mistral-Nemo-12B, Qwen2.5-Instruct-72B, or Mistral-Large-2 123B) is fine-tuned for academic writing to become the initial **CycleResearcher**. Concurrently, an LLM (Mistral-Large-2) is specialized in evaluating research papers to become the initial **CycleReviewer**.

- **Phase 1: Research Generation (CycleResearcher):**
 - CycleResearcher, starting with a literature review from a given reference (bib) file, formulates research ideas.
 - It then proceeds to manuscript preparation, alternating between generating high-level outlines and detailed main text sections (title, abstract, introduction, methods, experimental setup, results, discussion, conclusion).
 - Crucially, for accelerating training in the virtual RL environment, the "experimental results" generated by CycleResearcher are *fabricated* rather than based on actual experiments. The paper acknowledges this limitation and suggests delegation to code models or human experimenters for real-world scenarios.
 - The generated sections are compiled into a complete paper, typically in LaTeX format.

- **Phase 2: Automated Peer Review (CycleReviewer):**
 - The complete paper generated by CycleResearcher is fed into **CycleReviewer**.
 - CycleReviewer simulates a multi-reviewer process, producing sequential feedback and scores (Strengths, Weaknesses, Soundness, Presentation, Contribution, Overall Score on a 1-10 scale). It simulates diverse perspectives by generating feedback from the lowest-rated to highest-rated reviewer, then a senior reviewer synthesizes the feedback.
 - The final output is the average of these simulated reviewer scores, representing the paper's overall evaluation.

- **Phase 3: Refinement and Policy Optimization (Iterative SimPO):**
 - From a knowledge base (e.g., recent arXiv papers' reference sections), CycleResearcher samples multiple paper drafts (e.g., M1, M2, M3).
 - CycleReviewer evaluates each draft, assigning an average score (r_i).
 - A preference pair (x, y_w, y_l) is constructed, where y_w is the output with the highest reward value (best score) and y_l is the one with the lowest.
 - **SimPO (Simple Preference Optimization):** Instead of DPO, SimPO is adopted for computational efficiency. Its objective function combines a length-normalized reward function (log-likelihood of winning vs. losing responses) with a target reward margin.
 - **Loss Function:** The SimPO loss is combined with a Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss to stabilize training, preventing issues like repetitive text or significant errors.
 - **Iterative Update:** The CycleResearcher (policy model) parameters are updated using this combined loss. This process is repeated across iterations, enabling CycleResearcher to continuously adapt and improve its paper generation capabilities based on the feedback (rewards) from CycleReviewer.

#### 3.3. Ethical Safeguards

A critical part of the methodology is ensuring responsible use:

- **Detection Tool:** Fast-DetectGPT (using Llama-3-8B) is integrated to classify AI-generated content based on conditional probability curvature, aiming for high accuracy.
- **Mandatory Disclosure:** All AI-assisted papers are required to include a clear disclosure statement.
- **Licensing and Monitoring:** A comprehensive system is implemented, requiring institutional affiliation disclosure, agreement not to misuse models for official reviews/submissions without disclosure, and allowing publishers to verify model access in case of concerns, balancing transparency with user privacy.
- **Red-teaming:** Exercises were conducted to identify potential misuse scenarios (e.g., cyber-attacks, harmful content generation). SafetyLock (Zhu et al., 2024) is used to enhance safety before open-sourcing.

In summary, the methodology is a sophisticated closed-loop system where AI learns to research and refine its output by simulating the academic peer review process, all while emphasizing transparency and ethical considerations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results demonstrating the potential of LLMs to automate significant parts of the scientific research and review process.

#### 4.1. CycleReviewer Performance (Automated Paper Review Generation)

- **Superior Scoring Consistency:** CycleReviewer (123B model) significantly outperforms individual human reviewers and various leading closed-source LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, GLM-4, Gemini-1.5-pro, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GPT-4o) in terms of review score consistency. It achieved a 26.89% reduction in Proxy Mean Absolute Error (MAE) (0.92 vs. 1.16 for individual experts) and a 48.77% reduction in Proxy MSE (1.43 vs. 2.34) compared to individual human reviewers.
- **Competitive Decision Accuracy:** With a decision accuracy of 74.24%, CycleReviewer's performance is competitive with human experts (75.40%) and superior to closed-source systems, indicating its ability to provide reliable evaluation scores.
- **Nuanced Score Distribution:** Analysis of score distributions (minimum, maximum, and average scores) shows that CycleReviewer's patterns closely mirror human reviewers, demonstrating its capacity for nuanced quality assessments beyond just median-centric scoring.

#### 4.2. CycleResearcher Performance (Automated Paper Generation)

- **Quality Approaching Human Preprints:** CycleResearcher models (particularly 12B and 72B variants) generated papers that achieved an average simulated peer review score of 5.36 (CycleResearcher-12B), which is competitive with human-written preprint papers (5.24 average score from human experts) and notably higher than papers generated by the "AI Scientist" baseline (4.31).
- **Higher Acceptance Rates:** CycleResearcher-12B achieved a simulated acceptance rate of 35.13%, substantially higher than AI Scientist's 0% and even slightly above human preprint papers (29.63%), showcasing its superior ability to produce review-acceptable research outputs.
- **Improved Quality Across Criteria:** CycleResearcher consistently scored higher than AI Scientist across soundness, presentation, and contribution metrics. For instance, CycleResearcher-12B's average soundness score was 2.71 (vs. AI Scientist's 2.48), and its average presentation score was 2.70 (vs. 2.69).
- **Effectiveness of Rejection Sampling:** Generating multiple drafts (rejection sampling) significantly improved the quality of CycleResearcher's outputs. Increasing the number of generated papers from 1 to 100 boosted the average score from ~5.36 to 7.02, surpassing even the average score of human-accepted papers (5.69). This suggests that generating and selecting from a pool of ideas/drafts is highly effective.
- **Ablation Study Insights:** The ablation study confirmed the critical roles of Reinforcement Learning (RL), iterative training, and Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss. Removing RL resulted in a 0.24 point drop in average score and a 5.34% decrease in acceptance rate. Removing iterative training also led to a drop. Most significantly, removing NLL loss caused a sharp drop to 4.91 average score and 12.03% acceptance rate, highlighting its importance for stability and quality.
- **Enhanced Reference Inclusion:** CycleResearcher-12B included an average of 37.8 references per paper, which is over four times more than the AI Scientist baseline (8.2 references). This indicates improved grounding in academic literature.
- **Robustness against Reward Exploitation:** While a slight performance drop was observed when evaluated with an independently trained reward model (∆=0.14 in average score), the minor difference suggests that CycleResearcher learns genuine research capabilities rather than merely exploiting the training reward model's patterns.

#### 4.3. Human Evaluation

- **Outperformance of Baseline:** Human experts rated CycleResearcher's papers (average overall score 4.8) higher than AI Scientist's (3.6).
- **Room for Improvement:** Despite improvements, CycleResearcher's performance in human evaluations still lagged behind actual ICLR 2024 submissions (5.54) and accepted papers (6.44), indicating that fully human-level research generation is still a future goal.

#### 4.4. Ethical Safeguard Performance

- **High Detection Accuracy:** The Fast-DetectGPT tool demonstrated high accuracy in identifying AI-generated content: 95.14% for review content and nearly 99% for paper text, providing a robust mechanism for academic integrity.

In summary, the findings indicate that the "CYCLERESEARCHER" framework, through its iterative research-review cycle and specialized models, represents a substantial leap forward in automated scientific inquiry, generating research outputs that approach human preprint quality and demonstrating highly consistent automated review capabilities, all while integrating robust ethical safeguards.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "CYCLERESEARCHER" framework represents a significant stride toward the long-standing goal of automating scientific discovery, holding profound implications for the future of research and academic publishing.

#### 5.1. Accelerating Scientific Discovery and Knowledge Creation

- **Automation of Routine Tasks:** By automating tasks such as literature review, experimental design (at a planning level), and manuscript preparation, CycleResearcher can free up human researchers to focus on higher-level creative thinking, critical analysis, and real-world experimentation.
- **Rapid Hypothesis Validation:** The ability to quickly generate research ideas, simulate experiments (even with fabricated results for rapid iteration), and draft papers could significantly accelerate the pace of hypothesis generation and preliminary validation, allowing for more rapid exploration of research avenues.
- **Benefits for Resource-Constrained Researchers:** The framework could democratize access to sophisticated research assistance, potentially benefiting researchers in less-resourced environments by providing tools for generating high-quality research proposals and initial drafts.

#### 5.2. Enhancing the Peer Review Process

- **Consistency and Efficiency in Evaluation:** CycleReviewer's demonstrated ability to provide consistent and nuanced review scores, surpassing individual human reviewers in some consistency metrics, suggests its potential to improve the efficiency and objectivity of the peer review process.
- **Flagging Discrepancies and Recruiting Reviewers:** The model could be integrated into conference systems to identify submissions with significant score variations or low confidence, helping area chairs focus human attention where it's most needed and aiding in the recruitment of emergency reviewers.
- **Supporting Award Selection:** By providing a standardized evaluation metric, CycleReviewer could complement human judgment in processes like best paper selection, flagging papers where human and AI evaluations diverge significantly.

#### 5.3. Contributions to the AI Community

- **Open-Source Revolution in Scientific AI:** By building upon and releasing open-source models (Mistral, Qwen) and datasets (Review-5k, Research-14k), the work promotes transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative development in the field of AI for science. This is crucial for wider adoption and scrutiny.
- **Advancing LLM Capabilities for Complex Reasoning:** The iterative preference training framework, particularly the novel application of SimPO combined with NLL loss for paper writing, pushes the boundaries of LLMs' ability to engage in complex, multi-step reasoning and creative generation while maintaining coherence and academic standards.

#### 5.4. Proactive Ethical Framework for AI in Academia

- **Safeguarding Academic Integrity:** The integrated detection tool (Fast-DetectGPT) and the proposed licensing and disclosure framework are vital steps toward responsibly integrating AI into academic workflows. These measures aim to prevent misuse, ensure transparency regarding AI involvement, and maintain trust in scientific publishing. The explicit warning against LLMs for paper writing underscores a commitment to ethical use.
- **Augmenting vs. Replacing Human Researchers:** The authors advocate for AI as a tool to *augment* human capabilities rather than replace them, envisioning CycleResearcher as an intelligent assistant that empowers researchers to focus on their unique creative and critical thinking skills. This sets a responsible precedent for the integration of powerful AI.

#### 5.5. Future Directions and Challenges

- **Integration with Real-World Experimentation:** The current limitation of fabricated experimental results highlights a clear future direction: integrating CycleResearcher with real-world experimentation modules or human experimenters to achieve truly verifiable scientific discovery. The Appendix provides an example of this synergistic integration.
- **Domain Generalization:** The current focus on machine learning research, while practical for data availability, points to the challenge of expanding the framework to other scientific domains, which would require specialized datasets and evaluation criteria.
- **Addressing Reward Hacking:** The recognition of potential reward exploitation by the policy model is an important self-critique, paving the way for future research into more robust alignment techniques like synchronized model updates or adversarial training.
- **Knowledge Enhancement:** The plan to integrate RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) or other knowledge-enhancement techniques will be crucial for CycleReviewer to assess novelty and provide accurate feedback on newer papers, addressing the limitation of outdated knowledge.

In conclusion, "CYCLERESEARCHER" is a pioneering work that not only demonstrates significant technical advancements in automated scientific research and review but also sets a thoughtful example for integrating powerful AI tools into the academic ecosystem with a strong emphasis on ethics and responsible innovation. Its impact could be transformative for how scientific inquiry is conducted, accelerating discovery while striving to uphold academic rigor.
