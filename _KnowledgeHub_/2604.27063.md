# Learning to Forget: Continual Learning with Adaptive Weight Decay

> [!summary] Summary
> Forgetting through Adaptive DEcay (FADE) automates the management of knowledge retention in neural networks by dynamically adjusting individual parameter decay rates, demonstrating enhanced learning performance and stability in online continual learning scenarios.

## Problem

- Continual learning models struggle with 'catastrophic forgetting,' where acquiring new knowledge leads to the loss of previously learned information due to finite capacity.
- The 'stability-plasticity trade-off' is difficult to manage, requiring a balance between retaining old knowledge and adapting to new tasks.
- Conventional weight decay uses a single, fixed rate uniformly across all parameters, which is inefficient for selectively forgetting irrelevant or outdated information in non-stationary environments.

## Method

- **FADE** implements ==per-parameter adaptive weight decay==.
- Each weight has its own decay rate, dynamically updated online via ==meta-gradient descent==, derived for linear models and applied to the final layer of neural networks.
- Combinable with adaptive optimizers like ==IDBD== or ==Adam==, enabling simultaneous adaptation of learning and forgetting rates.

## Results

- **FADE** achieved up to **50% lower** prediction error in non-linear tracking and **over 30% higher** online accuracy on streaming image classification.
- Enhanced robustness to hyperparameter initialization — recovers strong performance even from sub-optimal initial decay rates.
- Learned distinct per-parameter decay rates, rapidly forgetting irrelevant features while retaining relevant ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Controlled and adaptive forgetting, rather than just retention, is essential in continual learning to free up model capacity and effectively manage the stability-plasticity trade-off.
> - Per-parameter adaptive weight decay allows a model to automatically differentiate between relevant and irrelevant information, selectively retaining stable knowledge while discarding outdated parameters.
> - Adaptive decay rates complement adaptive learning rates, suggesting that optimally managing both how fast new information is integrated and how much old information is retained can lead to better continual learning performance.
