# HumanScale: Egocentric Human Video Can Outperform Real-Robot Data for Embodied Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> A study empirically compares egocentric human video and real-robot data for embodied pretraining, demonstrating that human video yields superior generalization to unseen tasks and enhanced robustness in real-world robot deployment, even with pseudo-action labels. The research finds that egocentric data scales predictably and fosters a more effective "open-world prior" for robotic policies.

## Problem

- Embodied foundation models face a data bottleneck; teleoperated real-robot trajectories are costly and limited in scale and diversity.
- Current robot data acquisition methods restrict the ability of embodied models to learn robust and generalizable representations for diverse physical world interactions.
- A controlled, empirical comparison between egocentric human video and real-robot data for pretraining efficacy, particularly concerning generalization, was absent.

## Method

- An autoregressive ==World-Action Model (WAM)== built on a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture was employed.
- Two pretraining datasets of matched scale (5,000 hours each) were curated: one from ==egocentric human video with pseudo-action labels==, and another from aggregated real-robot trajectories.
- A ==controlled experimental design== ensured consistent model architecture, post-training data, compute budget, and evaluation protocols to isolate the impact of pretraining data source.

## Results

- Egocentric pretraining demonstrated a **20%** lower action loss on unseen tasks compared to real-robot pretraining when both were scaled to **5,000 hours**.
- Real-world robot experiments showed that the egocentric-pretrained model achieved a **90.0%** success rate on out-of-distribution objects, experiencing only a **2.5%** drop from in-distribution performance.
- The baseline model, without egocentric pretraining, saw its success rate plummet from **40.0%** to **0%** on out-of-distribution tasks, highlighting its inability to generalize.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric human video pretraining exhibits predictable, log-linear scaling with data volume, continuously reducing downstream task loss, particularly on unseen tasks.
> - The superior out-of-distribution generalization of egocentric data stems from its inherent diversity and information density, outweighing the precise alignment of more limited real-robot data.
> - An "open-world prior" learned from egocentric human video is critical for maintaining robust performance under distribution shifts in real-world robot deployment, preventing overfitting to narrow training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed significant advancements through foundation models in areas like natural language processing and computer vision. These models typically benefit from "data scaling," where increasing the volume and diversity of training data, alongside model size and computational resources, leads to improved performance and generalization. This work situates itself within the emerging domain of "embodied foundation models," which aim to apply a similar scaling paradigm to robotic systems, enabling them to learn broad, transferable representations for interacting with the physical world.

Currently, the dominant data source for pretraining embodied foundation models consists of teleoperated real-robot trajectories. While these data offer precise action supervision and direct embodiment alignment, their scalability is inherently limited by high collection costs, technical difficulties in acquisition, and restricted diversity in behaviors and environments. This limitation creates a "data bottleneck" for embodied AI, hindering the development of models that can generalize across a wide range of tasks and novel scenarios.

To address this bottleneck, researchers have explored alternative data sources, with egocentric human video emerging as a promising candidate. Egocentric video, captured from a first-person perspective during everyday human activities, offers a potential solution due to its accessibility, lower collection cost, and inherent diversity in interactions, objects, and environments. Previous research has begun to leverage egocentric video for various aspects of embodied learning, including visual representation learning, human-to-robot motion retargeting, cross-embodiment prior distillation, and downstream manipulation policy learning.

However, a critical gap in the existing literature has been a systematic, controlled comparison of egocentric human video against teleoperated real-robot data as pretraining sources. While egocentric data's advantages in scale and diversity are acknowledged, its effectiveness compared to the kinematic alignment and precise action labels of real-robot data remained underexplored, particularly under matched-scale pretraining conditions and controlled post-training and evaluation protocols. This paper directly addresses this gap by providing an empirical comparison, aiming to determine whether the coverage benefits of egocentric data can indeed outweigh the alignment benefits of robot data for generalization in downstream robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic and controlled study to compare the effectiveness of egocentric human video against teleoperated real-robot trajectories as pretraining data sources for embodied foundation models. The authors aim to empirically determine which data source yields superior generalization capabilities in downstream robotic tasks, specifically when evaluated under fixed post-training and validation protocols.

The motivation for this study stems from several critical challenges and opportunities in the field of embodied AI:

1. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck in Embodied Learning:** Unlike language and vision domains where internet-scale data is readily available, embodied foundation models face a severe data bottleneck. The current reliance on teleoperated real-robot data, while providing high-quality action supervision and embodiment alignment, is inherently limited in scale due to its high collection cost, complex acquisition process (requiring robots, operators, and staged environments), and resulting narrow diversity of behaviors and environments. This scarcity restricts the ability of embodied models to learn robust and generalizable representations.

2. **Exploring Scalable Alternatives:** Egocentric human video presents a compelling alternative data source. It is substantially lower in collection cost, readily available (passive capture or web harvesting), and inherently more diverse, exposing models to a vast array of contact-rich hand-object interactions, tool use, object state changes, and long-horizon behaviors in open-world settings. The authors are motivated to investigate if these advantages can translate into superior pretraining efficacy.

3. **Evaluating the "Coverage vs. Alignment" Trade-off:** Embodied pretraining aims to learn broadly transferable representations, which benefits from wide data coverage (diversity in scenes, objects, interactions). Post-training, in contrast, requires alignment (embodiment-matched observations and actions) to adapt the model to a specific robot and task. The core tension is whether the broad coverage offered by egocentric data, despite lacking direct robot actions and exact embodiment alignment (requiring pseudo-labels and retargeting), can be more beneficial for pretraining than the precise alignment but limited diversity of real-robot data. The study is motivated to empirically resolve this trade-off under controlled conditions.

4. **Informing Future Data Collection Strategies:** The high cost of real-robot data collection necessitates careful planning. By systematically comparing data sources, the research aims to provide guidance for data quality assessment and resource allocation. If egocentric data proves superior for pretraining, it would encourage a paradigm shift, enabling labs to focus on leveraging abundant human video before investing heavily in costly robot data.

5. **Unlocking Generalization for Embodied Foundation Models:** Ultimately, the motivation is to accelerate the development of embodied foundation models that exhibit strong generalization, particularly to unseen tasks and out-of-distribution scenarios. The study investigates whether egocentric pretraining, by exposing models to open-world diversity, can foster a more robust "open-world prior" that better transfers to novel manipulation scenarios compared to priors learned from constrained robot data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a controlled, matched-scale comparative methodology to assess the effectiveness of egocentric human video versus real-robot data for embodied pretraining.

**Model Architecture:**
The core model utilized is an autoregressive World-Action Model (WAM) built on a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture. This architecture is designed to predict future video observations and subsequent actions jointly. Specifically, the video expert component of the MoT is initialized from Wan 2.2, a large-scale video generative model, while the action expert is initialized via interpolation. This WAM design allows for learning dense representations of world dynamics alongside action prediction, where video generation provides a strong self-supervised signal.

**Data Curation and Pretraining (Stage 1):**
Two distinct pretraining datasets, each matched at approximately 5,000 hours of video, were constructed:
1. **Egocentric Human Video Dataset:** This dataset was curated from the egocentric portion of HumanNet, a large-scale human activity video corpus comprising one million hours. For this dataset, pseudo-action labels (end-effector poses and gripper states) were estimated by retargeting human hand-pose signals. This process aligns the action space with that of robot data, enabling cross-embodiment pretraining. The dataset prioritizes open-world coverage, including diverse backgrounds, objects, and interaction skills found in everyday human activities.
2. **Real-Robot Data Dataset:** This dataset consists of multi-embodiment robot trajectories aggregated from various publicly available real-robot datasets. It provides precise, kinematically aligned end-effector poses and gripper states. While offering direct embodiment alignment, its environmental diversity and task scenarios are acknowledged to be more bounded compared to the egocentric counterpart, reflecting the constraints of laboratory-based robot data collection.

**Post-training Data (Stage 2):**
To adapt the pretrained models to specific robot tasks, a real-robot dataset was curated from AgiBot World. This dataset comprised 15 manipulation tasks, with 100 expert demonstrations per task, totaling 1,500 trajectories. This post-training set was intentionally designed to be disjoint from the real-robot pretraining data and to contain relatively more diverse backgrounds and object instances to provide richer downstream manipulation scenarios for policy adaptation.

**Experimental Design and Protocol:**
The experimental design focused on isolating the effect of the pretraining data source. Therefore, the following elements were rigorously kept constant across all comparisons:
- Model architecture (WAM based on MoT).
- Pretraining data scale (5,000 hours for both egocentric and real-robot data).
- Post-training data (the AgiBot World dataset).
- Compute budget for training.
- Evaluation protocol.

The pretraining data (egocentric and real-robot) and post-training data (AgiBot World) were kept disjoint to ensure that the measured transfer reflects genuine generalization rather than data leakage.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
Models were evaluated based on their validation flow-matching action loss on held-out robot data from Stage 2. The evaluation utilized two distinct splits to assess different aspects of generalization:
1. **Seen Split (In-Distribution):** This split comprised held-out trajectories from the 15 tasks used during post-training. While the task semantics were observed during post-training, the evaluation trajectories featured unseen object instances and variations. This split measured the model's robustness within the distribution of post-training tasks.
2. **Unseen Split (Out-of-Distribution):** This split consisted of 25 tasks that were entirely distinct and not included in the Stage-2 post-training data. This served as the primary measure of out-of-distribution generalization, testing the model's ability to transfer learned priors to novel manipulation scenarios.

**Baselines:**
Two baselines were used for comparison:
1. **Wan2.2 (No Pretraining):** This model represents the performance without any embodied pretraining, relying solely on its initial video backbone and subsequent post-training.
2. **LingBot-VA:** This baseline involved fine-tuning Wan2.2 on 20,000 hours of real-robot data, serving as a strong reference for embodied pretraining using a substantial amount of robot data.

**Real-World Robot Experiments:**
To validate the findings beyond validation loss, real-world rollouts were conducted on an AgiBot bimanual platform. Three manipulation tasks (placing a cup, sorting fruits/vegetables, stamping) were used, each evaluated under an in-distribution setting (objects seen during post-training) and an out-of-distribution setting (previously unseen object instances). The egocentric-pretrained model was compared against the Wan 2.2 baseline under identical post-training conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical studies conducted around the scalability and comparative performance of egocentric and real-robot pretraining yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. Egocentric Pretraining Scales with Data

- **Monotonic Loss Reduction:** As the amount of egocentric pretraining data increased from 100 to 5,000 hours, the best post-training action loss consistently decreased in both evaluation settings (seen and unseen tasks).
- **Significant Improvement over Baseline:** The loss dropped from 0.0080 to 0.0067 on seen tasks and from 0.0234 to 0.0204 on unseen tasks, representing reductions of 35% and 24% respectively, compared to the Wan2.2 baseline without pretraining.
- **Log-Linear Scaling Law:** The trend was observed to follow a log-linear scaling law (L = a - b ln(D)), with high R² values (0.86 for seen tasks and 0.94 for unseen tasks). This indicates a predictable and consistent benefit from increased egocentric data.
- **No Saturation at 5,000 Hours:** The fitted slope remained clearly negative up to 5,000 hours, suggesting that the benefits of egocentric pretraining had not yet saturated, implying potential for further gains with larger datasets.
- **Attribution to Diversity:** This scaling behavior is attributed to the inherent diversity of the egocentric data. Increasing hours introduced a wider range of manipulated objects, manipulation skills, and physical environments, rather than merely repeating redundant trajectories. This growing diversity improved both in-distribution robustness (by encouraging a more general representation) and out-of-distribution generalization (by broadening observed behavioral space).

#### 4.2. Egocentric Pretraining Generalizes Better than Robot Pretraining

- **Comparable on Seen Tasks:** On seen tasks, real-robot pretraining improved steadily with data scale and exhibited performance comparable to egocentric pretraining, achieving a loss of 0.0071 at 5,000 hours, only slightly higher than egocentric pretraining's 0.0067.
- **Superior on Unseen Tasks:** On unseen tasks, a significant divergence was observed. Scaling real-robot data yielded no consistent improvement, with its loss remaining near 0.025 across all scales and reaching 0.0254 at 5,000 hours. In contrast, egocentric pretraining continued to improve, achieving 0.0204 at 5,000 hours, representing approximately a 20% lower loss than real-robot pretraining at the same scale. This indicates egocentric data's superior ability to foster out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Reasons for Disparity:** The difference is attributed to two primary factors:
 - **Diversity and Information Density:** Egocentric data is inherently more diverse and covers a wider range of tasks, objects, and backgrounds than teleoperated robot data, which is constrained by laboratory setups. Furthermore, egocentric video is more information-dense; for instance, a 100-hour egocentric dataset contained approximately 45,000 trajectories, compared to about 8,000 from real-robot data, due to less idle time and smoother human motion.
 - **Limited Generalization of Robot Data:** Despite keeping real-robot pretraining tasks disjoint from evaluation, scaling the robot corpus did not yield substantial generalization to genuinely unseen tasks, suggesting that manipulation data collected in constrained laboratory settings transfers poorly beyond its specific distribution.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robot Experiments

- **Post-training Loss Advantage:** On the fruit-and-vegetable sorting task, the egocentric-pretrained model started with a substantially lower loss during post-training and converged to an action loss approximately 2.4 times lower than the no-pretraining baseline, indicating that the pretrained prior facilitates faster and better learning of downstream tasks.
- **Robustness to Distribution Shift:** On real-robot rollouts, the egocentric-pretrained model achieved a 92.5% success rate in in-distribution settings and maintained a 90.0% success rate under distribution shift to unseen objects, experiencing only a 2.5-point degradation.
- **Baseline Collapse:** In contrast, the Wan 2.2 baseline attained a 40.0% success rate in-distribution but completely degraded to 0% on out-of-distribution trials, suffering a 40-point collapse.
- **Validation of Open-World Prior:** These results indicate that the open-world prior learned from egocentric human video effectively transfers to real-world robot tasks and enables robust generalization under distribution shifts. Conversely, policies without this diverse prior tend to overfit to the narrow visual distribution of their post-training data and fail when confronted with novelty.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI by empirically demonstrating the superior effectiveness of egocentric human video for pretraining embodied foundation models compared to traditional real-robot data, particularly in fostering generalization. The findings have several important significances and potential impacts:

1. **Validation of a Scalable Pretraining Paradigm:** The most significant impact is the verification of a new, scalable paradigm for embodied foundation models. The study suggests that models can first be pretrained on massive, diverse egocentric human video to learn broad world representations, and then efficiently adapted with a smaller, focused amount of labeled real-robot data for precise action-space alignment. This approach addresses the current data bottleneck in embodied learning by leveraging a far more abundant and diverse data source.

2. **Challenge to Conventional Wisdom:** The "surprising" finding that egocentric data can *outperform* real-robot data challenges the long-held assumption that direct kinematic alignment from robot demonstrations is intrinsically superior for pretraining. It highlights that the breadth and diversity of observed interactions, objects, and environments, even with pseudo-action labels, can be more critical for generalization than exact action supervision during the initial pretraining phase.

3. **Guidance for Data Strategy and Resource Allocation:** The research provides critical guidance for roboticists and AI labs on how to strategically collect and utilize data. It suggests that significant resources currently allocated to costly, large-scale teleoperated robot data collection for pretraining might be better spent on curating and processing egocentric human video, which offers higher information density and diversity per hour at a lower marginal cost. This could lead to more efficient development cycles for robot learning.

4. **Acceleration of Embodied AI Development:** By demonstrating a more scalable and effective pretraining method, this work has the potential to accelerate the development of embodied foundation models. Models trained with this paradigm are shown to generalize better to unseen tasks and exhibit greater robustness to object and environmental shifts in real-world scenarios, which are crucial capabilities for deploying generalist robots.

5. **Encouragement for Broader Exploration of Egocentric Data:** The study explicitly encourages the robotics and computer vision communities to explore egocentric data more broadly and deeply. It lays a foundation for future work on advanced filtering, pseudo-labeling, and cross-embodiment retargeting techniques to further unlock the potential of human-centric video for robot learning.

6. **Insights into Generalization Mechanisms:** The clear distinction in generalization performance between egocentric and real-robot pretraining, particularly for out-of-distribution tasks, provides valuable insights into what constitutes a truly generalizable prior for embodied agents. It emphasizes that exposure to "open-world diversity" is key to preventing overfitting and enabling robust transfer to novel situations.

In summary, "HumanScale" offers an empirical basis for re-evaluating data strategies in embodied AI, proposing a path towards more generalizable and capable robotic systems by effectively leveraging the vast, diverse, and cost-effective resource of egocentric human video.
