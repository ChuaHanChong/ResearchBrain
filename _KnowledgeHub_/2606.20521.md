# HumanScale: Egocentric Human Video Can Outperform Real-Robot Data for Embodied Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> A study empirically compares egocentric human video and real-robot data for embodied pretraining, demonstrating that human video yields superior generalization to unseen tasks and enhanced robustness in real-world robot deployment, even with pseudo-action labels. The research finds that egocentric data scales predictably and fosters a more effective "open-world prior" for robotic policies.

## Problem

- Embodied foundation models face a data bottleneck; teleoperated real-robot trajectories are costly and limited in scale and diversity.
- Current robot data acquisition methods restrict the ability of embodied models to learn robust and generalizable representations for diverse physical world interactions.
- A controlled, empirical comparison between egocentric human video and real-robot data for pretraining efficacy, particularly concerning generalization, was absent.

## Method

- An autoregressive ==World-Action Model (WAM)== built on a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture was employed.
- Two pretraining datasets of matched scale (5,000 hours each) were curated: one from ==egocentric human video with pseudo-action labels==, and another from aggregated real-robot trajectories.
- A ==controlled experimental design== ensured consistent model architecture, post-training data, compute budget, and evaluation protocols to isolate the impact of pretraining data source.

## Results

- Egocentric pretraining demonstrated a **20%** lower action loss on unseen tasks compared to real-robot pretraining when both were scaled to **5,000 hours**.
- Real-world robot experiments showed that the egocentric-pretrained model achieved a **90.0%** success rate on out-of-distribution objects, experiencing only a **2.5%** drop from in-distribution performance.
- The baseline model, without egocentric pretraining, saw its success rate plummet from **40.0%** to **0%** on out-of-distribution tasks, highlighting its inability to generalize.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric human video pretraining exhibits predictable, log-linear scaling with data volume, continuously reducing downstream task loss, particularly on unseen tasks.
> - The superior out-of-distribution generalization of egocentric data stems from its inherent diversity and information density, outweighing the precise alignment of more limited real-robot data.
> - An "open-world prior" learned from egocentric human video is critical for maintaining robust performance under distribution shifts in real-world robot deployment, preventing overfitting to narrow training data.
