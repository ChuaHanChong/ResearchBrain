# $N_0$-TWAM: Scaling Tactile-Native World-Action Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> N0-TWAM, developed by the NeoteAI and Fudan TEAI Teams, introduces a tactile-native world-action model that jointly predicts future vision and touch for contact-rich robotic manipulation. This approach achieved an 84.5% success rate on the UniVTAC benchmark, surpassing prior vision-language-action models by approximately 17 percentage points, and demonstrated superior robustness to visual perturbations across diverse real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation in contact-rich scenarios (e.g., seating a screw) is challenging, often relying on granular fingertip interactions that standard visual sensors cannot fully capture.
- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack explicit future event models and native tactile integration, while Video World-Action Models (WAMs) primarily overlook contact dynamics in their predictions.
- Prior attempts to integrate tactile sensing were partial, either not predicting future tactile states, using separate and frozen tactile predictors, or treating touch as a distinct, often gated, input.

## Method

- **N0-TWAM** is a world-action model built on a ==video-diffusion transformer==, designed to jointly predict future video, tactile, and action streams using a unified ==conditional flow-matching objective==.
- It employs a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== backbone with dedicated, parameter-efficient experts for vision, action, and tactile modalities, interacting via shared self-attention.
- The model integrates touch via two pathways: predicting future tactile signals (==foresight==) and conditioning action generation on observed current tactile inputs (==reactive feedback==), with a staged training approach.
- A ==tactile-aware sub-task system== uses predicted and observed contact events to segment and orchestrate long-horizon manipulation sequences during inference, enabling robust task staging.

## Results

- N0-TWAM achieved an **84.5%** average success rate on the UniVTAC simulation benchmark, outperforming the best VLA baseline by approximately **17** percentage points.
- On an 8-task real-robot suite, the model demonstrated a macro average success rate of **46.3%**, significantly higher than competitive baselines (e.g., **30.0%** for ", π0.5), with the largest gains in tasks relying on hidden contact states.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both predicted tactile foresight and observed tactile conditioning are critical for performance, and large-scale pre-training data is indispensable for the model's efficacy.
- The model exhibited superior robustness to visual perturbations (**45%** success vs. **25%** for ", π0.5), demonstrating the benefit of tactile feedback when visual input is compromised, and qualitatively, predicted future tactile maps closely matched ground truth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling and predicting future tactile and visual states within a unified world-action model significantly enhances robot performance in contact-rich tasks, overcoming the limitations of vision-only or partially tactile-aware systems.
> - A dual-pathway approach—anticipating future contact and reacting to current contact—provides both proactive and reactive capabilities crucial for fine-grained manipulation, particularly where visual information is ambiguous or insufficient.
> - Scaling a tactile-native world model with a large dataset of real-robot teleoperation and synchronized per-finger tactile streams is essential for learning precise contact dynamics and achieving strong empirical results across diverse tasks and embodiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

General manipulation policies, particularly in contact-rich scenarios such as seating a screw or peeling cups, present a challenge for robotic systems. These tasks often depend on granular interactions at the fingertips, information that standard visual sensors frequently cannot fully resolve. The broader research landscape has developed two primary lines of work to address aspects of this challenge:

- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** These models typically attach an action head to large, pretrained vision-language models, translating observations into actions. While they benefit from broad semantic priors, they generally lack an explicit model of future events and do not natively incorporate tactile information.
- **Video World Models and World-Action Models (WAMs):** These models learn to predict future observations, and WAMs couple this prediction with action generation, grounding control in an anticipated future. However, the predicted future in most existing WAMs is predominantly visual, often overlooking contact dynamics.

The integration of tactile sensing into robotic policies has followed several partial paths:
- **Tactile Policies:** These approaches utilize touch as an input channel but do not predict future tactile states.
- **Separate Tactile Predictors:** Some systems forecast touch using a distinct, often frozen, tactile predictor, operating independently of the main policy network.
- **Touch-Aware World-Action Models:** While these models admit touch, they often treat it distinctly, for example, by gating or masking tactile tokens to prevent interference with the visual stream. Consequently, in these methods, touch is typically not an intrinsic part of what the model predicts as a future observation.

This paper addresses a gap in these existing approaches by developing a model that jointly predicts touch and vision under a unified objective and causal step. The work distinguishes itself by scaling a tactile world-action model to a large dataset, aiming to bridge the divide between anticipating future visual states and predicting precise contact interactions. By making tactile prediction a native component of the world-action model, this research seeks to enhance fine-grained manipulation capabilities in complex, contact-rich environments beyond what purely visuomotor or vision-language-action models typically provide.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for N0-TWAM is to overcome the inherent difficulties of learning general manipulation policies in scenarios characterized by significant physical contact. In such tasks, subtle interactions at the robot's fingertips—such as force application, incipient slip, or precise alignment—are critical for success. Conventional camera-based systems often cannot capture these nuanced details, leading to policies that may lack the precision and robustness required for contact-rich operations. The core objectives stem from this identified limitation:

1. **Develop a Native Tactile World-Action Model:** The central objective is to create a world-action model where touch is treated as a fundamental, native modality, predicted jointly with future vision. This involves designing a model capable of anticipating both the future visual scene and future contact events under a single unified objective. The authors explicitly state their aim to develop the first tactile world-action model trained at a large scale.

2. **Implement a Dual-Pathway Predictive Tactile Design:** To fully leverage tactile information, the model is designed to incorporate touch via two distinct pathways:
 - **Predicted Tactile Stream (Foresight Target):** The model should generate future tactile signals as part of its predictive output, allowing the policy to anticipate how contact will evolve.
 - **Observed Tactile Stream (Action Conditioning):** The policy should also directly read the current tactile input to provide reactive feedback for immediate action adjustments. This pathway is intended to provide physically grounded contact signals, often in force-based representations, to condition action generation.

3. **Introduce a Tactile-Aware Sub-task System for Long-Horizon Manipulation:** Long-horizon tasks often involve multiple stages delimited by specific interaction events. A key objective is to use tactile contact events (e.g., grasp onset, release) to segment long-horizon demonstrations during training and to orchestrate execution stages during inference. The predicted tactile signal is intended to trigger advances to the next sub-task, while observed contact events confirm these transitions, thereby enhancing the model's ability to manage multi-stage manipulation sequences.

4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Empirical Results:** A foundational objective is to demonstrate the practical efficacy of N0-TWAM by achieving high performance on diverse contact-rich tasks in both simulated and real-robot environments. This includes outperforming existing vision-language-action (VLA) and vision-only world-action models (WAMs), with particular emphasis on tasks where tactile feedback is crucial. The work also aims to justify the benefits of large-scale data pre-training for precise tactile and action prediction.

These objectives collectively aim to imbue robotic manipulation policies with advanced predictive and reactive capabilities concerning contact, thereby laying a foundation for robust and fine-grained control in a wider array of real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

N0-TWAM (Tactile World-Action Model) is a world-action policy constructed upon a video-diffusion transformer backbone. Its methodology integrates vision, tactile, and action prediction into a unified generative model.

**Model Architecture (Section 2.1):**
N0-TWAM is designed to predict future video ($x_v$), tactile ($x_t$), and action ($x_a$) streams, conditioned on a language instruction ($c$) and observation history ($X_{<k}$). The model operates autoregressively over chunks, modeling their joint distribution.
- **Conditional Flow-Matching Objective:** The model utilizes flow matching to regress the velocity of a straight path from a clean target to Gaussian noise, applied at latent-frame granularity. This objective is employed for video, action, and tactile modalities, with scalar noise levels shared across spatial tokens within a frame or action steps aligned with a frame.
- **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Backbone:** The core of N0-TWAM is an MoT architecture comprising three dedicated experts—one for each modality (video, action, tactile). These experts maintain separate weights and capacity, interacting through a single shared self-attention mechanism at each layer. Modality-specific queries, keys, and values are concatenated and processed through a masked self-attention.
- **Parameter-Efficient Experts:** To manage computational costs, an asymmetric design is adopted. The video expert retains its full width (3072 dimensions) and is warm-started from a pre-trained vision-only world-action model (LingBot-VA). In contrast, the action and tactile experts are slimmed (1024 dimensions) and trained from scratch, benefiting from the visual prior indirectly through shared attention. This design approximately halves the parameter count compared to an all-full-width approach.
- **Diffusion-Forcing Cascade:** A causal mask governs the shared attention, implementing a "predict-then-act" sequence within a single forward pass. This mask defines dependencies such that noisy video and tactile tokens co-generate within a chunk, and the noisy action token is conditioned on the just-predicted clean video and tactile. During training, the model is teacher-forced on ground-truth past history, with clean video and tactile history lightly re-noised for robustness.
- **Action Representation:** Actions are represented as 10-dimensional per-frame end-effector deltas (3D position, 6D orientation, 1D gripper command), relative to the chunk-start pose. This delta representation maintains small, centered targets and enables cross-embodiment generalization.

**Modeling Touch (Section 2.2):**
Touch is integrated into N0-TWAM via two pathways:
- **Predicting Future Touch:** Tactile streams are VAE-encoded and patchified, sharing a latent space with video. A dedicated tactile expert, which does not cross-attend to text, predicts future touch in a residual form (change from an initial frame), optimized jointly with video. This co-denoising ensures consistency between predicted visual and contact states.
- **Conditioning on Observed Touch:** This reactive pathway provides current tactile readings to the action expert. For real robots, a frozen estimator maps raw tactile images to a three-axis surface force map. A NeoForce tactile encoder, pre-trained on real visuo-tactile data and then fine-tuned, converts this map into representation tokens. These tokens are cross-attended into the action stream immediately before the action head, with the cross-attention initialized to zero for minimal perturbation during fine-tuning. For simulation, a lightweight encoder trained from scratch on current-frame tactile latents is used.
- **Staged Training:** During pre-training, only the predicted tactile stream is active, enabling the model to learn large-scale contact dynamics. The observed tactile pathway is activated only during post-training (task adaptation) to provide real-time reactive feedback without shortcutting the foresight learning.

**Tactile-Aware Execution (Section 2.3):**
At inference, N0-TWAM executes autoregressively:
- **Real-time Inference:** The system leverages a rolling key/value cache for previous chunks and an asynchronous pipeline. After the video and tactile predictions are complete, their keys/values are cached, allowing only the lightweight action expert to re-run for each action denoising step. This, combined with independent denoising schedules for prediction and action, and reduced precision (FP8/NVFP4) for linear layers, facilitates real-time operation.
- **Long-Horizon Execution via Tactile Punctuation:** For long-horizon tasks, tactile contact events (e.g., contact onset, release) are used to segment demonstrations into sub-task clips. During inference, a scheduler maintains a queue of sub-tasks. The predicted tactile signal triggers the advance to the next sub-task, which is then confirmed by the observed tactile stream, providing a robust mechanism for task staging.

**Data (Section 3):**
N0-TWAM is pre-trained on NeoData, a large-scale corpus exceeding 30,000 hours, featuring six embodiments and 450 contact-rich tasks. A significant portion of this data includes synchronized per-finger tactile streams.
- **Data Processing:** Raw episodes are converted into latent tokens offline. Multi-view RGB and tactile streams are encoded by a single frozen causal video VAE, and language instructions by a frozen umT5 text encoder. Episodes are chunked into 33 latent-frame windows with overlap. Actions are canonized into a unified 20-dimensional end-effector space, normalized per robot. Contact events segment demonstrations for long-horizon task training.

**Training Details (Section 4.1):**
Pre-training involves a two-stage curriculum on real-robot teleoperation data, initially to convergence, then mixed with UMI data. The flow-matching loss for video, touch, and action is equally weighted. Fine-tuning for downstream tasks adapts the pre-trained checkpoint, enabling the observed tactile pathway and fine-tuning all transformer parameters. Condition dropout for language and tactile is employed to enhance robustness, with tactile dropout implemented as token absence for scenarios with genuinely tactile-less data or sensor failure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of N0-TWAM was conducted across a range of contact-rich tasks in both simulation and real-robot environments, comparing it against existing vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action model (WAM) baselines.

**Performance on Simulation Benchmarks:**
- **UniVTAC Benchmark (Table 2):** N0-TWAM achieved an average success rate of 84.5%, outperforming the strongest VLA baseline (InternVLA-A1 at 67.1%) by approximately 17 percentage points. Vision-only WAMs (LingBot-VA at 31.4% and FastWAM at 48.0%) exhibited lower performance, indicating that tactile integration is a substantial factor for tasks involving complex contact.
- **NeoSim Benchmark (Table 3):** On this in-house simulation suite, N0-TWAM demonstrated the highest overall average success rate at 49.4%, surpassing the next best baseline, $\pi_{0.5}$ (45.8%). The model showed a notable advantage in contact-intensive tasks such as "Insert USB," "Bowl Unstack," "Plate Stack," and "Bowl Stack." The authors noted that the tactile advantage was comparatively smaller here, attributed to the simulated tactile not being the specific sensor N0-TWAM was pre-trained on.

**Performance on Real-World Robotics Tasks:**
- **Eight-Task Real-Robot Suite (Figure 7):** N0-TWAM demonstrated the highest macro average success rate of 46.3% across eight contact-rich tasks, compared to $\pi_{0.5}$ (30.0%), LingBot-VA (21.9%), and FastWAM (14.4%). The performance margin for N0-TWAM was most pronounced on tasks where success relied on resolving hidden contact states that external cameras could not capture, such as "Bottle Standing" (70% vs. 20% for best baseline), "Socket Plugging" (70% vs. 60%), "Fruit Collection" (60% vs. 50%), and "Board Wiping" (55% vs. 40%). For tasks dominated by visually guided placement, N0-TWAM performed comparably to or slightly behind $\pi_{0.5}$ (e.g., "Cup Stacking," "Bag Packing").

**Mechanism of Advantage:**
The superior performance of N0-TWAM is attributed to its dual tactile pathways:
- **Predicted Tactile Foresight:** The model's ability to co-denoise future contact with future video enables the action expert to anticipate contact events, potentially avoiding actions that appear visually plausible but lead to unstable contact.
- **Observed Tactile Conditioning:** The integration of current force-space tactile representation provides a low-latency feedback signal for immediate adjustments to issues like slip, shear, or excessive pressure.

Design elements such as private expert weights for tactile, staged training, zero-initialized cross-attention, and tactile-condition dropout contributed to preserving the pre-trained visual policy while effectively integrating contact sensitivity.

**Generalization Capabilities (Table 4):**
- **Unseen Object:** $\pi_{0.5}$ showed stronger generalization (80%) to novel objects, likely due to its broad vision-language pre-training, while N0-TWAM achieved 65%.
- **Unseen Position:** N0-TWAM matched $\pi_{0.5}$ (both 45%) in generalizing to out-of-distribution initial placements.
- **Visual Perturbation:** N0-TWAM demonstrated superior robustness to changes in lighting and background (45% vs. $\pi_{0.5}$ at 25% and LingBot-VA at 30%), indicating that tactile feedback provides a reliable, vision-independent signal when visual input is compromised. Overall, N0-TWAM exhibited the highest average generalization (51.7%), demonstrating resilience where vision is unreliable.

**Ablation Studies (Figure 9, Tables 7, 8):**
- **Pre-training Scale:** Reducing pre-training data to 20% resulted in a significant drop in UniVTAC success (from 84.5% to 65.4%), indicating the critical role of large-scale pre-training.
- **Tactile Pathways:** Removing either the predicted tactile foresight or the observed tactile conditioning consistently degraded performance across UniVTAC and NeoSim. Specifically, removing predicted foresight lowered UniVTAC success to 71.8% and NeoSim to 41.1%; removing observed conditioning lowered UniVTAC to 70.5% and NeoSim to 29.6%. This confirms that both predictive and reactive tactile pathways contribute to the model's overall performance.

**Analysis of Action Representation and Tactile Realism:**
- **Delta vs. Absolute Actions (Table 5):** While delta end-effector parameterization is generally suitable for generalization across embodiments, absolute pose was found to outperform delta on alignment-sensitive tasks (82.5% vs. 50.0% average for 4 tasks) due to avoiding error accumulation.
- **Realism of Simulated Tactile (Table 6):** N0-TWAM trained on a more realistic gel-rendered tactile image (mimicking real vision-based sensors) performed comparably to training on the simulator's clean output (82.4% vs. 84.5% on UniVTAC), demonstrating minimal accuracy cost for visual domain alignment.
- **Pre-trained Force Encoder with Gel-rendered Tactile:** Utilizing the NeoForce pre-trained force encoder with the gel-rendered images further *improved* success (88.1% vs. 82.4% on UniVTAC), especially for tasks requiring weight-bearing or shear cues, suggesting a positive transfer from real-world force representation knowledge to visually realistic simulated tactile data.
- **Qualitative Tactile Foresight (Figure 11):** Qualitative examples of predicted future tactile images and contact-flow maps closely matched ground truth, confirming the model's ability to anticipate contact formation and evolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

N0-TWAM represents a notable advancement in robot manipulation by introducing a tactile-native world-model policy that integrates touch prediction directly into the anticipated future of a video world-action model. This work addresses a fundamental limitation of previous models that primarily relied on visual foresight or treated tactile information as a secondary input.

The significance of N0-TWAM is multi-faceted:

- **Pioneering Large-Scale Tactile World Modeling:** To the authors' knowledge, N0-TWAM is the first tactile world-action model trained at large scale, leveraging a substantial dataset of real-robot demonstrations with synchronized tactile information. This approach demonstrates that tactile intelligence can be integrated natively and scaled effectively, moving beyond task-specific or limited-scale tactile applications.
- **Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Tasks:** By jointly predicting vision and touch, N0-TWAM achieves state-of-the-art results on challenging contact-rich benchmarks in both simulation and real-world scenarios. It consistently outperforms leading vision-language-action models and vision-only world-action models, particularly in tasks demanding fine-grained contact understanding, such as precise insertions, stable grasps, and object manipulation under load. This directly addresses the performance gap identified in current robotic systems for such tasks.
- **Robustness to Visual Ambiguity:** The dual-pathway tactile design, incorporating both predicted foresight and observed real-time feedback, enhances the policy's robustness. The ablations and generalization studies confirm that N0-TWAM degrades more gracefully under visual perturbations (e.g., changes in lighting or background) compared to vision-only baselines, providing a critical fall-back mechanism where visual information is unreliable.
- **Efficient and Scalable Architecture:** The asymmetric Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture allows for the integration of dedicated tactile and action experts with reduced computational cost while retaining a powerful visual prior. This design facilitates real-time streaming inference by caching predicted video and tactile information, making the large-scale model practical for deployment.
- **Framework for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The tactile-punctuated execution mechanism offers a structured approach to managing complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks. By segmenting demonstrations and dynamically advancing sub-tasks based on predicted and observed contact events, the model can navigate long-horizon scenarios more effectively, reducing the likelihood of task failure due to unmanaged transitions.
- **Foundation for Fine-Grained Manipulation:** N0-TWAM builds a solid foundation for developing more general and capable robotic systems that can perform delicate and precise manipulations in open-ended, unstructured environments. The public release of the codebase and model checkpoints is intended to foster further research and development in tactile-enabled robotic manipulation, accelerating progress in this area.

Potential future impacts include the development of robots capable of more dexterous assembly, handling of delicate objects, and robust interaction with novel environments, ultimately contributing to more versatile and intelligent embodied AI systems.
