# RoboHarness: Memory-Driven Orchestration of Heterogeneous Robot Policies for Long-Horizon Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The RoboHarness framework orchestrates diverse robot policies, including VLAs, RL, and TAMP, to perform complex long-horizon tasks, achieving a 98.7% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrating improved robustness to perturbations compared to individual policies.

## Problem

- Executing long-horizon robotic tasks is difficult due to the diverse capabilities required, which no single existing policy (VLA, RL, TAMP) can fully address.
- Independently developed heterogeneous policies suffer from unclear, context-dependent capability boundaries and input-output distribution mismatches, hindering robust coordination.
- Traditional long-horizon planning methods typically assume homogeneous skills with fixed applicability, overlooking the complexities of integrating diverse policy types.

## Method

- Develops **RoboHarness**, an ==agentic framework== where a coding agent orchestrates heterogeneous robot policies (==VLAs==, ==RL==, ==TAMP==) wrapped as callable skills.
- Integrates ==Understanding==, ==Memory==, and ==Self-Evolution== auxiliary skills to interpret inputs, manage execution history, characterize policy capabilities, and adapt coordination strategies online.
- Implements a ==Memory Bridge== that uses ==multimodal memory retrieval== and ==spatial distribution learning== to generate bridge trajectories, ensuring stable and in-distribution handoffs between policies.

## Results

- Achieved a **98.7%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and a **95.2%** success rate on the long-horizon LIBERO-LoHo benchmark, outperforming all individual policies and baselines.
- Demonstrated enhanced robustness with a **93.2%** average success rate across seven perturbation categories on LIBERO-Plus, significantly improving resilience to out-of-distribution conditions.
- Real-robot experiments (**135 tasks**) validated the framework's practical efficacy, showing consistent performance in complex assembly tasks and resilience to disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging complementary strengths of heterogeneous robot policies through capability-aware selection and composition leads to superior performance on complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Explicitly managing cross-policy distribution mismatches at handoffs using memory-derived spatial guidance is critical for maintaining consistency and preventing failures in sequential task execution.
> - Online adaptation and refinement of policy orchestration strategies based on execution experience allow the system to continuously improve its robustness and capability understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of executing complex, long-horizon tasks is a significant challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics. Such tasks often require a diverse set of capabilities, including semantic understanding, fine-grained manipulation, logical reasoning, and robust adaptation to environmental variations. Existing robotic policies, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies, and Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems, each possess distinct strengths and limitations. VLAs offer semantic grounding and open-vocabulary instruction following but may lack long-horizon consistency or geometric precision. RL policies can generate closed-loop behaviors within their training distributions but struggle with distribution shifts. TAMP systems provide structured logical and geometric reasoning but are limited by predefined abstractions and state representations. Consequently, no single policy provides a universal solution for long-horizon tasks that demand these varied strengths.

This work addresses the problem of coordinating these heterogeneous policies, a challenge largely overlooked by conventional long-horizon planning methods. Traditional approaches typically assume a homogeneous set of predefined skills with fixed applicability, simplifying task decomposition and skill selection. However, independently developed heterogeneous policies differ in architecture, input-output requirements, underlying assumptions, and execution histories, leading to unclear and context-dependent capability boundaries. Furthermore, direct composition of such policies is challenging because the terminal state of one policy may not fall within the feasible execution region or input distribution of the subsequent policy, potentially causing cascading failures during handoffs.

The paper positions RoboHarness as an evolution of "harness systems" that originated in software and coding agents, where large language models interact with tools, memory, and feedback. While these systems have been extended to robotics, existing robotic agent systems primarily organize skills from the same policy family, assuming compatible representations and in-distribution handoffs. RoboHarness distinguishes itself by specifically tackling the orchestration of *heterogeneous* robot policies, explicitly addressing their uncertain capability boundaries and the distribution mismatch problem during policy transitions. It also aims to move beyond training-side solutions for policy chaining, offering an online, plug-and-play coordination mechanism that does not require additional joint training or policy regularization. By focusing on capability-aware planning and stable inter-policy handoffs for diverse policy types, RoboHarness seeks to bridge a critical gap in enabling robust, zero-shot long-horizon robotic task execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of individual robotic policies when confronted with long-horizon tasks that demand a wide spectrum of capabilities. While recent advancements have been made in VLAs, RL, and TAMP systems, each excels in specific aspects but falters in others. For instance:
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** offer strong semantic grounding and open-vocabulary instruction following, but their ability to maintain long-horizon consistency and achieve high geometric precision is often constrained.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies** can generate robust closed-loop behaviors within their training distribution but tend to degrade significantly under distribution shifts or when reward functions are ill-defined.
- **Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems** provide robust logical and geometric reasoning, but their generalization is often limited by reliance on predefined abstractions, skills, and state representations.

No single policy provides a comprehensive solution for long-horizon tasks that frequently involve subtasks requiring these distinct strengths. This disparity motivates the formulation of long-horizon execution as a coordination problem for heterogeneous policies, where their complementary strengths can be leveraged.

The key challenges identified in coordinating such diverse policies, which existing planning methods largely overlook, are:
1. **Uncertain Capability Boundaries:** Heterogeneous policies differ significantly in their architectures, input-output requirements, assumptions, and execution histories. Their applicability often overlaps but varies with context (e.g., object configurations, visual observations, language instructions), making it difficult to determine which policy is most suitable for a given subtask.
2. **Cross-Policy Distribution Mismatch:** Independently trained or designed policies are not inherently composable. The terminal state produced by one policy may not lie within the feasible execution region or input distribution of the subsequent policy, leading to instability and cascading failures during policy handoffs.

Given these motivations and challenges, the objectives of this work are articulated through the proposal of RoboHarness:

1. **Introduce a Unified Framework:** To develop RoboHarness as a unified framework that orchestrates heterogeneous robot policies to solve zero-shot long-horizon robotic tasks. "Zero-shot" here implies solving previously unseen task compositions without task-specific joint training of the constituent policies or their orchestration mechanisms.
2. **Characterize Policy Capabilities and Support Orchestration:** To design and integrate three categories of auxiliary skills – Understanding, Memory, and Self-Evolution – that work in conjunction with the underlying control policies. These skills aim to characterize policy capability boundaries, support capability-aware task decomposition, facilitate policy routing, and enable adaptive coordination.
3. **Ensure Stable Policy Handoffs:** To create the Memory Bridge, a plug-and-play inter-policy chaining technique. This component is designed to combine multimodal memory retrieval with spatial distribution learning to preserve spatial consistency and enable stable policy handoffs between heterogeneous policies without requiring additional joint retraining.
4. **Demonstrate Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** To conduct extensive evaluations across multiple benchmarks, including customized tasks and real-robot experiments, to demonstrate improvements in long-horizon consistency, out-of-distribution resilience, and zero-shot robustness achievable through capability-aware planning and stable orchestration of heterogeneous policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboHarness is designed as an agentic orchestration framework where a coding agent serves as the high-level planner and router for heterogeneous robot policies. This agent dynamically invokes three types of auxiliary skills: Understanding, Memory, and Self-Evolution skills. These auxiliary skills process multimodal observations and execution histories to extract information necessary for task decomposition, policy assignment, and inter-policy handoff coordination. A core principle of RoboHarness is to preserve the native interfaces of existing policies, allowing their independent integration without requiring joint retraining or a shared action representation. The framework wraps each policy as a callable agentic skill, augmented by an associated "policy card" that records its type, capabilities, interface requirements, constraints, assumptions, training tasks, and historical execution statistics.

The three primary categories of auxiliary skills are:

#### 3.1. Understanding Skills

These skills interpret raw inputs (task instructions, visual observations, object poses, robot states, execution results) to extract decision-relevant information about the relationship between the current task state and the capability boundaries of available policies. RoboHarness includes five types:
- **Uncertainty Assessment:** Computes mean and variance of estimated object poses to gauge temporal stability.
- **Visual-Context Assessment:** Projects observations into a latent space and compares them with visual embeddings of policy-specific training trajectories using frozen visual encoders (e.g., SigLIP2, DINOv2).
- **Semantic-Context Assessment:** Compares encoded task instructions and candidate subtasks with language descriptions associated with policies using a frozen language encoder (e.g., BGE).
- **State-Policy Compatibility Assessment:** Utilizes the spatial distribution reconstructed by the Memory Bridge to score the current end-effector pose and joint configuration, indicating if the robot state is within the next policy's in-distribution region.
- **Input-Quality Assessment:** Evaluates image sharpness, exposure, noise, and task-relevant object visibility using conventional image processing algorithms to determine observation reliability.
These skills provide structured evidence that enables the coding agent to reason over outputs for task decomposition and policy assignment.

#### 3.2. Memory Skills and Memory Bridge

Memory skills consist of two components: multimodal memory management and memory-based inter-policy bridging.

#### 3.3. Memory Management

The system maintains a memory bank (`M`) as a collection of linked-node trajectories. Each node (`n`) in a trajectory stores a subtask instruction, an observation, a robot-state vector, and their corresponding text and visual embeddings. A hierarchical retrieval process (`Retrieve_M(g, o)`) is used to query the memory bank: it first retrieves nodes semantically relevant to a query subtask `g` (based on text embeddings), then refines this set by retrieving nodes visually similar to a query observation `o` (based on visual embeddings). Global historical statistics summarizing policy execution outcomes are also maintained and continuously updated after each rollout, providing empirical evidence for capability estimation.

#### 3.4. Memory Bridge

The Memory Bridge addresses distribution mismatch when transferring control between policies. When the terminal observation of policy `π_k_t` (`o_t`) is not within the input space of the next policy `π_k_t+1` (`O_k_t+1`), the Memory Bridge constructs a local progress function and generates a bridge trajectory. This process involves three stages:
1. **Spatial Distribution Construction:** Relevant anchor nodes are retrieved from memory. Each anchor node's linked trajectory (forward and backward) is expanded, generating robot-state-score pairs (state `s_i,j`, temporal offset score `y_i,j`). These pairs train a lightweight regression model (`f_score,t`) that predicts local progress. A local support region (`R_conf,t`) is defined around the expanded states, restricting `f_score,t` to states supported by retrieved memory.
2. **Spatial State Scoring:** `f_score,t(s)` evaluates candidate robot states within `R_conf,t`, with a positive sign indicating progress. `R_conf,t` defines admissible handoff states.
3. **Bridge Trajectory Generation:** A target robot state (`s*_t`) is selected by maximizing `f_score,t(s)` while penalizing motion cost from the current state (`s_t`), subject to `s` being within `R_conf,t` and `f_score,t(s)` being non-negative. An off-the-shelf motion planner then generates a bridge trajectory (`b_t`) connecting `s_t` to `s*_t`, guiding the robot to an in-distribution state for `π_k_t+1`. Handoffs are stored in memory for future reuse.

#### 3.5. Self-Evolution Skills

These skills enable RoboHarness to adapt using online execution evidence through four update types:
- **Policy Adaptation:** Incorporates methods (e.g., SIMPACT for grasp adjustment, PDDLLM for learning logical representations) to extend individual policy capabilities.
- **Harness Refinement:** The coding agent (e.g., Codex powered by GPT-5.5) modifies orchestration structure, task-routing strategies, and skill-invocation rules based on execution traces and errors.
- **Parameter Tuning:** Adjusts hyperparameters (e.g., memory-retrieval settings, state-scoring thresholds) through grid search, with the coding agent proposing direction changes.
- **Metadata Update:** Revises policy capability metadata (e.g., observed successes/failures, relevant contexts) to refine capability boundaries. Updates are triggered by persistent failures and validated across episodes, allowing continuous adaptation.

The experimental setup utilized three heterogeneous policies: an open-source `π_0.5` VLA checkpoint, an open-source OpenVLA-OFT checkpoint post-trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using RLinf, and a TAMP planner designed for geometrically constrained pick-and-place tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RoboHarness was conducted across three public benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-LoHo), 500 custom-designed simulated tasks, and 135 real-robot experiments. The system was compared against diverse baselines, including various VLA models, world-action models, and hierarchical planning approaches.

**RQ1: Coordination of Heterogeneous Policies (LIBERO Benchmark)**
On the original LIBERO benchmark, which assesses general language-conditioned manipulation, RoboHarness achieved a success rate of 98.7%. This performance surpassed all individual baselines, including its constituent policies, `π_0.5` (96.9%) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.6%), by 1.8 and 1.1 percentage points, respectively. This outcome suggests that RoboHarness effectively exploits the complementary capabilities of its underlying policies through capability-aware selection and composition.

**RQ2: Zero-Shot Long-Horizon Generalization (LIBERO-LoHo Benchmark)**
For LIBERO-LoHo, which features tasks with significantly longer horizons and requires the integration of complementary capabilities, RoboHarness achieved an average progress score of 97.5% and an average success rate of 95.2%. These results were superior to all baselines on every task. The underlying policies, `π_0.5` and OpenVLA-OFT, exhibited non-zero progress scores, indicating individual subtask completion ability, but their overall success rates for long-horizon tasks were substantially lower. High-level guidance improved `π_0.5`'s performance but remained below RoboHarness. This finding indicates that task decomposition alone is insufficient, and RoboHarness's capability-aware routing and reliable inter-policy handoffs are critical for completing tasks beyond the scope of any single policy.

**RQ3: Robustness to Perturbations (LIBERO-Plus Benchmark)**
RoboHarness achieved the highest average success rate of 93.2% across seven perturbation categories in LIBERO-Plus, ranking first in six categories. It consistently outperformed its underlying policies, `π_0.5` and OpenVLA-OFT, demonstrating an improvement in out-of-distribution task-execution robustness rather than simply inheriting individual policy capabilities. A particularly large improvement was observed under robot-state perturbations, which the authors attribute to the Memory Bridge's ability to guide out-of-distribution robot states back toward a VLA's training distribution. Improvements under language perturbations were linked to Understanding Skills' ability to interpret ambiguous instructions, while gains under camera-viewpoint changes suggested dynamic policy routing based on robustness. Smaller improvements under sensor noise and layout perturbations were noted due to direct input degradation or inherent task impossibility within the robot's workspace, respectively.

**RQ4: Capability-Aware Policy Routing (LIBERO-Plus Analysis)**
An additional experiment on LIBERO-Plus, using only `π_0.5` and a TAMP system, investigated RoboHarness's ability to characterize context-dependent policy capability boundaries. RoboHarness dynamically adjusted policy usage ratios across perturbation categories (Figure 3a). It invoked `π_0.5` more frequently when the policy exhibited strong standalone robustness (e.g., under lighting, background, and layout changes) and relied more on TAMP when `π_0.5` performed less reliably (e.g., under robot-state, camera-viewpoint, and language-instruction perturbations). A positive correlation was observed between the VLA invocation ratio and its standalone success rate (Figure 3b), suggesting that RoboHarness infers how policy reliability changes with execution context and routes subtasks accordingly. Sensor noise was an outlier, where degraded visual observations limited reliable information for routing.

**RQ5: Contribution of System Components (Ablation Study on 500 Custom Tasks)**
An ablation study on 500 custom-designed long-horizon manipulation tasks highlighted the distinct contributions of each auxiliary module:
- **Understanding Skills:** Removal caused the most significant degradation in performance, indicating their fundamental role in task and scene interpretation, decomposition, and policy assignment.
- **Evolution Skills:** Without these, RoboHarness struggled with repeated routing errors due to an inability to refine policy capability estimates from execution experience.
- **Memory Bridge:** Its removal maintained strong task progress but frequently prevented full task completion, demonstrating its role in stabilizing inter-policy handoffs by ensuring compatibility between consecutive policies.
- **Single-Policy Variants:** These could only solve task segments aligned with their individual capabilities, confirming that long-horizon performance requires both complementary policies and the auxiliary modules for coordination and adaptation.

**Real Robot Experiments**
The 135 real-robot experiments across five challenging task classes and four disturbance types further validated RoboHarness. The system performed consistently across various construction tasks (Figure 5b). When evaluated under disturbances on a "Bridge" task (Figure 5c), re-hiding blocks caused the largest degradation (from 86.7% to 66.7% success), primarily due to increased exploration and replanning exceeding time limits. RoboHarness retained 80.0% success after a partially completed structure was dismantled, indicating online reactivity. It achieved 73.3% success under 5%–10% random errors in object-pose estimates, demonstrating tolerance to moderate perception errors. Distracting objects had minimal effect, with success remaining at 86.7%, suggesting robust identification of task-relevant objects. These results confirm the practical benefits of RoboHarness in real-world scenarios requiring coordinated use of TAMP and VLA policies for cabinet exploration and assembly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboHarness presents a framework for integrating and orchestrating heterogeneous robot policies, addressing critical challenges in long-horizon robotic task execution. Its significance lies in several key aspects:

Firstly, the framework demonstrates a method for **leveraging the complementary strengths of diverse robot policies** (e.g., VLAs, RL, TAMP) that traditionally operate in isolation. By encapsulating these as reusable agentic skills and providing mechanisms for capability-aware planning, RoboHarness enables the composition of specialized intelligences to tackle tasks that exceed the capabilities of any single policy. This approach allows for a modular and extensible system where new policies can be integrated without needing to retrain the entire system or enforce a common action representation.

Secondly, RoboHarness explicitly addresses the problem of **uncertain policy capability boundaries and cross-policy distribution mismatch**, which are prevalent issues when combining independently developed systems. The **Understanding Skills** provide context-dependent insights into policy applicability, while the **Memory Bridge** offers a novel, plug-and-play solution for stabilizing policy handoffs. By retrieving relevant execution memories and guiding the robot towards in-distribution states, the Memory Bridge mitigates cascading failures, a frequent bottleneck in long-horizon sequential execution. This functionality removes a significant barrier to the robust integration of diverse control systems.

Thirdly, the inclusion of **Self-Evolution Skills** allows RoboHarness to **adapt and refine its orchestration strategies online** based on execution evidence. This adaptive capability, encompassing policy adaptation, harness refinement, parameter tuning, and metadata updates, enables the system to progressively improve its understanding of policy capabilities and its coordination logic. This continuous learning from experience contributes to enhanced robustness and resilience to novel conditions, addressing a common limitation of static planning systems.

The experimental results across various simulated benchmarks and real-robot scenarios demonstrate substantial improvements in zero-shot long-horizon planning, out-of-distribution robustness, and overall task success rates compared to existing methods and individual policies. This indicates that the proposed memory-driven orchestration approach effectively translates the potential of heterogeneous policies into reliable task accomplishment.

The potential impact of RoboHarness includes:
- **Enabling more complex and generalizable robotic applications:** By facilitating the coordination of specialized robot intelligence, RoboHarness could pave the way for robots capable of performing increasingly intricate and varied tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Accelerating robotic development:** The modular nature of RoboHarness allows researchers and engineers to develop and integrate new policies more efficiently, without needing to consider joint training with other policies. This could foster innovation in specialized robotic capabilities.
- **Improving robustness and reliability:** The explicit handling of policy handoffs and adaptive orchestration mechanisms enhances the system's ability to cope with uncertainties and perturbations in real-world deployments.

However, the framework does have limitations, as acknowledged by the authors. Its performance is ultimately constrained by the collective capabilities of the underlying policy library; it cannot solve subtasks for which no policy is competent. Furthermore, capability characterization and Memory Bridge construction rely on accumulated execution evidence, which may be unreliable when experience is sparse or a policy is newly introduced. Future work involves expanding the policy library to include more diverse systems (e.g., navigation, MPC), automating the integration of new policies, enabling online training for unsupported scenarios, and incorporating additional sensory modalities like tactile feedback to enhance capability characterization. Despite these limitations, RoboHarness represents a notable advancement toward developing adaptive and general-purpose long-horizon robotic systems by effectively integrating and orchestrating heterogeneous AI components.
