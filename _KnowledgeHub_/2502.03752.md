# Self-Improving Skill Learning for Robust Skill-based Meta-Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-Improving Skill Learning (SISL) framework enhances meta-reinforcement learning by enabling robust skill acquisition from noisy offline demonstration data, dynamically refining a skill library and prioritizing high-quality trajectories. It consistently achieves superior performance and adaptability in diverse long-horizon tasks, even under significant data corruption.

## Problem

- Existing skill-based Meta-RL methods are highly susceptible to noise present in offline demonstration datasets, which are common in real-world scenarios.
- Noise in offline data corrupts the learned skill library, leading to unstable training and degraded performance, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks where errors accumulate.
- Current approaches typically assume clean offline demonstrations, limiting their practical applicability when data quality is suboptimal.

## Method

- **SISL** introduces a decoupled ==skill self-improvement== mechanism, using a skill-improvement policy to perturb trajectories near the offline data distribution and discover higher-quality behaviors, guided by intrinsic motivation and self-supervision.
- The framework employs skill prioritization via maximum return relabeling, training a reward model on high-quality online trajectories to re-label and prioritize offline samples based on their potential maximum return.
- A dynamic mixing coefficient adaptively balances contributions from the diverse but noisy offline dataset and the progressively cleaner online buffer during the skill learning process.

## Results

- SISL demonstrated superior robustness across all evaluated environments (Kitchen, Office, Maze2D, AntMaze) and noise levels, consistently outperforming baseline skill-based Meta-RL methods which showed sharp performance declines with increased noise.
- The framework maintained strong performance even under significant noise (e.g., Kitchen σ=**0.3**\sigma=0.3σ=**0.3** with an average return of 3.48 ±\pm± **0.07**, compared to SiMPL's **0.81** ±\pm± **0.25**), effectively refining useful skills and achieving higher task completion rates.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each core component of SISL (decoupled skill self-improvement, skill prioritization, dynamic mixing coefficient, and exploration mechanism) is critical for achieving robust skill acquisition and overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling skill refinement from high-level policy learning allows for continuous discovery and improvement of effective behaviors, mitigating the negative impact of initial noisy data.
> - Prioritizing offline samples based on their estimated potential maximum return helps filter out noisy or irrelevant trajectories, leading to a cleaner and more task-relevant skill library.
> - Dynamically balancing the influence of offline and online data enables the system to continuously adapt and refine its understanding of skills as it gains more online experience, improving robustness and generalization.
