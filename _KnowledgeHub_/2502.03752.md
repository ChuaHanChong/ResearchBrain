# Self-Improving Skill Learning for Robust Skill-based Meta-Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-Improving Skill Learning (SISL) framework enhances meta-reinforcement learning by enabling robust skill acquisition from noisy offline demonstration data, dynamically refining a skill library and prioritizing high-quality trajectories. It consistently achieves superior performance and adaptability in diverse long-horizon tasks, even under significant data corruption.

## Problem

- Existing skill-based Meta-RL methods are highly susceptible to noise present in offline demonstration datasets, which are common in real-world scenarios.
- Noise in offline data corrupts the learned skill library, leading to unstable training and degraded performance, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks where errors accumulate.
- Current approaches typically assume clean offline demonstrations, limiting their practical applicability when data quality is suboptimal.

## Method

- **SISL** introduces a decoupled ==skill self-improvement== mechanism, using a skill-improvement policy to perturb trajectories near the offline data distribution and discover higher-quality behaviors, guided by intrinsic motivation and self-supervision.
- The framework employs skill prioritization via maximum return relabeling, training a reward model on high-quality online trajectories to re-label and prioritize offline samples based on their potential maximum return.
- A dynamic mixing coefficient adaptively balances contributions from the diverse but noisy offline dataset and the progressively cleaner online buffer during the skill learning process.

## Results

- SISL demonstrated superior robustness across all evaluated environments (Kitchen, Office, Maze2D, AntMaze) and noise levels, consistently outperforming baseline skill-based Meta-RL methods which showed sharp performance declines with increased noise.
- The framework maintained strong performance even under significant noise (e.g., Kitchen σ=**0.3**σ=0.3σ=**0.3** with an average return of 3.48 ±±± **0.07**, compared to SiMPL's **0.81** ±±± **0.25**), effectively refining useful skills and achieving higher task completion rates.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each core component of SISL (decoupled skill self-improvement, skill prioritization, dynamic mixing coefficient, and exploration mechanism) is critical for achieving robust skill acquisition and overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling skill refinement from high-level policy learning allows for continuous discovery and improvement of effective behaviors, mitigating the negative impact of initial noisy data.
> - Prioritizing offline samples based on their estimated potential maximum return helps filter out noisy or irrelevant trajectories, leading to a cleaner and more task-relevant skill library.
> - Dynamically balancing the influence of offline and online data enables the system to continuously adapt and refine its understanding of skills as it gains more online experience, improving robustness and generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated success in various domains, but traditional RL methods exhibit limitations in adapting quickly to novel tasks and achieving sample efficiency. Meta-reinforcement learning (Meta-RL) addresses these issues by enabling agents to learn how to learn, facilitating rapid adaptation to unseen tasks. Context-based Meta-RL methods, such as PEARL, represent tasks with contextual information to expedite adaptation through off-policy learning.

Despite advancements, existing Meta-RL approaches encounter difficulties in long-horizon environments due to challenges in extracting meaningful task context. Skill-based approaches are employed to mitigate this by decomposing complex state-action sequences into reusable skills, which enables hierarchical decision-making. SPiRL introduced skills as temporal abstractions for low-level policies within a hierarchical framework, while SiMPL extended this concept to Meta-RL by training skills from offline expert data and employing a context-based high-level policy for skill selection.

A significant challenge in current skill-based Meta-RL methods is their susceptibility to noise present in offline demonstration datasets. Real-world data often contains noise originating from various sources, such as sensor errors, infrastructure degradation, or environmental perturbations. This noise can lead to unstable skill learning and degraded performance, especially in long-horizon and precise manipulation tasks where errors can accumulate. While some research has explored handling noisy demonstrations in other RL contexts, this issue has largely been unaddressed within skill-based Meta-RL. The identified gap is that existing methods typically assume clean offline demonstrations, and when data is suboptimal, the learned skill library becomes corrupted, adversely affecting the high-level policy's adaptation capabilities. This work positions itself to address this specific vulnerability by proposing a framework robust to noisy offline demonstrations in skill-based Meta-RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust skill-based Meta-Reinforcement Learning framework that can effectively learn and adapt to long-horizon tasks, even when utilizing noisy and suboptimal offline demonstration datasets. The authors identify a critical failure mode in existing skill-based Meta-RL methods: the degradation of the skill library when offline data is corrupted, which subsequently harms the performance of the high-level policy and overall adaptation.

The motivation for proposing Self-Improving Skill Learning (SISL) stems from this identified vulnerability. Specifically, the paper highlights that:
- **Real-world data is inherently noisy:** Unlike idealized experimental settings, practical applications of RL often rely on demonstrations that are not perfectly clean due to factors such as sensor limitations, environmental disturbances, or human error during data collection.
- **Existing skill-based Meta-RL methods assume clean data:** Current approaches, while effective with expert data, treat all offline trajectories equally, leading to skill corruption when low-quality samples are present. This results in unstable training and reduced reliability, particularly in long-horizon tasks where errors can compound.
- **Accumulation of errors in long-horizon tasks:** In complex, multi-step tasks, the impact of noisy skills can be magnified, preventing agents from completing subtasks or overall objectives. The provided example of the Kitchen microwave-opening task demonstrates that skills learned from noisy data fail to grasp the handle, leading to task failure.
- **Need for dynamic adaptation and refinement:** Unlike static offline training, which is highly susceptible to noise, online training offers opportunities for continuous adaptation. The goal is to leverage this principle to progressively denoise and improve the skill library through online interaction.

To address these motivations, SISL aims to achieve two key contributions:
1. **Decoupled skill self-improvement:** To mitigate the detrimental effects of noise, the framework aims to introduce an independent mechanism for skill refinement. This mechanism should actively discover higher-quality behaviors by perturbing trajectories near the offline data distribution and using these improved behaviors to self-supervise skill updates.
2. **Skill prioritization for effective learning:** To prevent low-quality or irrelevant samples from dominating skill learning, the framework seeks to implement a method for prioritizing trajectories. This prioritization should focus skill updates on the most beneficial samples, thereby accelerating convergence and ensuring a progressively cleaner and more task-relevant skill library.

Overall, the core motivation is to enhance the practical applicability and reliability of skill-based Meta-RL in real-world scenarios where perfectly clean demonstration data is rarely available.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Self-Improving Skill Learning (SISL) framework integrates a two-pronged approach to achieve robust skill learning under noisy offline demonstrations: decoupled skill self-improvement and skill prioritization via maximum return relabeling. SISL builds upon the standard skill-based meta-RL setup (e.g., SiMPL), where a skill encoder $q(z|\tilde{\tau})$ extracts a latent skill $z$ from trajectory segments, a high-level policy $\pi_h(z|s, e_T)$ selects $z$ periodically, and a low-level skill policy $\pi_l(a|s, z)$ executes the chosen skill.

#### 3.1. Decoupled Skill Self-Improvement

SISL decouples the training process into two complementary policies:
1. **High-level policy ($\pi_h$):** This policy, parameterized by $\theta$, primarily exploits the current skill library to maximize task returns. It selects skill latents $z$ every $H_s$ steps, and a task encoder $q_e$ infers a task latent $e_T$ from high-level trajectories (Eq. B.4). The high-level policy and its value function $Q_h$ are trained using a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework, optimizing for cumulative rewards while regularizing skill selection towards the skill prior.
2. **Skill-improvement policy ($\pi_{imp}$):** Parameterized by $\psi$, this policy is designed to independently perturb trajectories near the offline data distribution to discover higher-quality behaviors. It aims to expand the skill distribution and find task-relevant actions. Perturbations are guided by intrinsic motivation signals, such as Random Network Distillation (RND), which calculates intrinsic rewards based on prediction errors (Eq. B.2). The skill-improvement policy and its value function $Q_{imp}$ are also trained using SAC, with a loss that includes both extrinsic task rewards and intrinsic exploration rewards. Crucially, it also includes a KL divergence term that encourages $\pi_{imp}$'s action distribution to match successful samples from an online buffer $B_{on}$ (Eq. B.3), providing a self-supervised guidance mechanism.

Two additional buffers facilitate this self-improvement:
- **Improvement buffer ($B_{imp}$):** Stores all trajectories generated by $\pi_{imp}$.
- **Prioritized online buffer ($B_{on}$):** Selectively retains only the highest-return trajectories generated by $\pi_{imp}$ and $\pi_h$. This buffer is initialized with the offline dataset $B_{off}$ and progressively populates with improved trajectories. It serves as a progressively cleaner dataset to supervise $\pi_{imp}$ and supply high-quality samples for refining the core skill components.

Skill refinement occurs in dedicated phases every $K_{iter}$ iterations. During these phases, the skill encoder $q_{\phi}$, skill prior $p_{\phi}$, and low-level policy $\pi_{l,\phi}$ (all parameterized by $\phi$) are re-trained on a combination of $B_{off}$ and $B_{on}$ using the SPiRL objective (Eq. B.1). Following this refinement, the high-level policy $\pi_h$ is reinitialized to leverage the updated skill library effectively.

#### 3.2. Skill Prioritization via Maximum Return Relabeling

To address the trade-off between the diverse but potentially noisy offline dataset $B_{off}$ and the high-quality but potentially narrowly distributed online buffer $B_{on}$, SISL introduces a skill prioritization mechanism.
1. **Reward Model Training:** A reward model $\hat{R}_{\zeta}(s_t, a_t, i)$ (parameterized by $\zeta$) is trained for each task $T_i$ using a regression loss (Eq. B.5). The target rewards $r_t$ for this model come from improved trajectories sampled from $B_{imp} \cup B_{on}$, ensuring the model learns from empirically successful behaviors. This online training process for the reward model is stable as it uses actual environment rewards.
2. **Maximum Return Relabeling:** For each offline trajectory $\tilde{\tau} \in B_{off}$, a hypothetical maximum return $\hat{G}(\tilde{\tau})$ is computed. This is done by estimating the cumulative reward of $\tilde{\tau}$ across all training tasks using the learned reward model and taking the maximum (Eq. 5). This $\hat{G}(\tilde{\tau})$ represents the trajectory's potential contribution to task success.
3. **Softmax Prioritization:** Offline trajectories are then sampled from $B_{off}$ according to a softmax distribution $P_{B_{off}}(\tilde{\tau}) = \text{Softmax}(\hat{G}(\tilde{\tau})/T)$, where $T$ is a temperature parameter. This biases sampling towards promising trajectories while suppressing noisy or irrelevant ones, providing a cleaner training signal for skill learning.
4. **Dynamic Mixing Coefficient ($\beta$):** The skill learning objective (Eq. B.6) combines samples from $P_{B_{off}}$ and $B_{on}$ using a mixing coefficient $\beta$. This coefficient is dynamically computed based on the average returns in $B_{off}$ and $B_{on}$ (Eq. 7), adaptively balancing their contributions. As training progresses and $B_{on}$ fills with higher-quality trajectories, $\beta$ tends to increase, shifting the focus towards online-discovered skills.

During the meta-test phase, the low-level policy $\pi_l$, skill prior $p$, and task encoder $q_e$ are frozen, and only the high-level policy $\pi_h$ is fine-tuned for a limited number of steps on unseen tasks, leveraging the now refined skill library.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SISL focused on its robustness to noisy demonstrations and its ability to enhance performance in diverse long-horizon environments: Kitchen, Office, Maze2D, and AntMaze. Noise levels for offline datasets were introduced by adding Gaussian noise to expert actions, with increasing standard deviations.

**Performance Comparison:**
- **Superior Robustness:** SISL consistently demonstrated superior robustness across all evaluated environments and noise levels compared to baselines. While non-meta RL algorithms (SAC, SAC+RND) and PEARL-based meta-RL methods performed poorly on long-horizon tasks, skill-based methods (SPiRL, SiMPL, SISL) generally achieved better performance.
- **Degradation in Baselines:** SPiRL and SiMPL, which rely on static offline datasets without refinement, showed sharp performance declines as dataset noise increased. SiMPL, in particular, sometimes performed worse than SPiRL under noisy conditions due to instability in its task encoder.
- **SISL's Consistent Outperformance:** SISL maintained strong performance even under significant noise (e.g., Kitchen $\sigma=0.3$, Maze2D $\sigma=1.5$), effectively refining useful skills. For instance, in Kitchen, SISL sustained task completion rates (average return of 3.48 $\pm$ 0.07 at $\sigma=0.3$) while SiMPL dropped to 0.81 $\pm$ 0.25. In Maze2D, SISL achieved near-perfect task completion (0.99 $\pm$ 0.02 at $\sigma=1.5$) where other methods struggled.
- **Improvement on Expert Data:** SISL also achieved superior test performance even with expert (noise-free) data, suggesting that its refinement process can discover more effective skills than those initially present in the expert demonstrations alone.
- **Mild Noise Benefits Baselines (Occasionally):** Interestingly, mild noise sometimes boosted the performance of SPiRL and SiMPL, particularly in Maze2D and AntMaze. This was attributed to increased behavioral diversity in the dataset, leading to more flexible skill learning, but significant noise always led to degradation.

**In-Depth Analysis of Skill Refinement Process:**
- **Dynamic Mixing Coefficient ($\beta$):** Visualizations showed that the mixing coefficient $\beta$ (balancing offline and online data contributions) started low, reflecting reliance on initial offline datasets, especially in high-return environments. As training progressed and online data quality improved, $\beta$ gradually increased, shifting focus towards the refined online samples. For higher offline noise levels, $\beta$ converged to higher values, indicating a greater reliance on self-improved online data. This dynamic balancing prevented abrupt distribution shifts and ensured stable skill evolution.
- **Skill Evolution and Refinement:** Visualizations in Kitchen (microwave-opening subtask) demonstrated that initial noisy skills failed to complete subtasks (e.g., not grasping the handle). After training, SISL agents learned to successfully open the microwave and manipulate objects. In Maze2D, initial skills led to noisy or failed trajectories. As training iterations increased, agents using refined skills explored more diverse trajectories, eventually solving all training tasks with reduced deviations and shorter paths during meta-test.
- **Improved Task Representation:** t-SNE visualizations of task embeddings in Kitchen (noisy data) showed that skill refinement in SISL enabled the task encoder to form more distinct and task-specific representations. This improved task discrimination, aiding the high-level policy in selecting optimal skills. In contrast, fixed skills from noisy data resulted in less structured task representations.
- **Reward Model Stability:** The reward model used for maximum return relabeling was trained exclusively on online, clean trajectories. Ablation studies confirmed that its Mean Squared Error (MSE) remained consistently low across various offline noise levels, indicating its stability and accuracy were unaffected by the noise in the initial offline dataset.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Component Importance:** Removing any of SISL's core components led to significant performance drops:
 - **Without $B_{off}$:** Relying solely on $B_{on}$ degraded performance, highlighting the importance of the diverse offline dataset for generalization.
 - **Without $P_{B_{off}}$ (uniform sampling):** Applying uniform sampling to $B_{off}$ instead of maximum return relabeling reduced performance, underscoring the value of prioritization.
 - **Without $B_{on}$:** Using only $B_{off}$ for skill refinement led to performance deterioration, emphasizing the contribution of high-quality samples discovered online.
 - **Without $\pi_{imp}$:** Excluding the skill-improvement policy notably degraded performance, confirming its critical role in discovering improved behaviors near the skill distribution.
 - **Without RND:** Removing RND from $\pi_{imp}$ training reduced exploration capability, especially under high noise, leading to performance degradation.
 - **Without Re-initialization:** Not re-initializing the high-level policy $\pi_h$ after skill updates caused instability and significant performance drops due to non-stationarity.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
 - **Prioritization Temperature $T$:** Both very low ($T=0.1$, over-prioritization) and very high ($T=2.0$, uniform sampling) values of $T$ degraded performance, confirming the need for proper tuning, often proportional to the environment's maximum achievable return.
 - **KLD Coefficient $\lambda_{kld_{imp}}$:** Setting $\lambda_{kld_{imp}}=0$ (removing the KL term guiding $\pi_{imp}$ towards high-return samples) caused significant performance degradation, demonstrating the necessity of this self-supervised guidance. Performance was otherwise stable across a range of positive values.
 - **Skill Refinement Interval $K_{iter}$:** Very small intervals led to poor performance because $\pi_h$ lacked sufficient adaptation time. Very large intervals reduced the frequency of skill improvement. Optimal performance was achieved with an interval that allowed $\pi_h$ to converge and exploit skills before re-refinement.

**Computational Complexity:**
- SISL's meta-train time increased by approximately 16% compared to SiMPL. This overhead primarily resulted from continuous skill refinement and reward model training, rather than additional policy learning. The meta-test computational cost remained similar to SiMPL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Self-Improving Skill Learning (SISL) framework addresses a critical and previously underexplored challenge in skill-based Meta-Reinforcement Learning: the susceptibility of skill learning to noisy and suboptimal offline demonstrations, particularly in long-horizon tasks. By introducing decoupled skill self-improvement and skill prioritization via maximum return relabeling, SISL enhances the robustness and reliability of Meta-RL systems.

### 6. Significance

- **Addresses a Real-World Problem:** The work acknowledges and provides a solution for the common challenge of imperfect data quality in real-world applications, where collecting perfectly clean expert demonstrations can be difficult, expensive, or impossible. This increases the practical applicability of Meta-RL in complex domains such as robotic manipulation and autonomous navigation.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** SISL's ability to refine skills and prioritize relevant trajectories from noisy data leads to a progressively cleaner and more effective skill library. This results in agents that are more robust to data corruption and can generalize better to unseen tasks, a key objective of Meta-RL.
- **Enhanced Skill and Task Representations:** The methodology not only improves the low-level skill execution but also contributes to more distinct and effective task representations, enabling the high-level policy to make better decisions and adapt more efficiently.
- **Dynamic Adaptation:** The dynamic mixing coefficient $\beta$ and periodic skill refinement introduce a continuous learning and adaptation mechanism, moving beyond static offline skill learning. This allows the system to evolve its understanding of skills as it gathers more online experience.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Deployment of Meta-RL:** By mitigating a major barrier to entry (the need for pristine offline data), SISL could facilitate the adoption of Meta-RL in a wider range of real-world scenarios where data is inherently noisy.
- **Cost Reduction in Data Collection:** The framework's robustness to suboptimal data may reduce the stringent requirements for expert data collection, potentially lowering the cost and effort associated with bootstrapping RL systems.
- **Advancements in Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** In domains like robotics, where precise manipulation and navigation over long horizons are crucial, SISL's ability to learn robust skills from noisy demonstrations could lead to more reliable and adaptable robotic agents.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concepts of decoupled self-improvement and dynamic prioritization for skill refinement could inspire further research into online adaptation of offline learned behaviors, especially in hierarchical and compositional RL settings.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge certain limitations, including a 16% increase in computational time during the meta-training phase compared to baselines, primarily due to the continuous skill model training and reward model updates. Additionally, SISL currently requires fine-tuning during the meta-test phase for optimal performance. Future work could focus on reducing computational overhead and exploring zero-shot skill adaptation to new tasks, which would further enhance its practicality in real-world, dynamic environments.

Overall, SISL represents a step towards making skill-based Meta-RL more robust and practical, addressing a fundamental challenge that currently limits its deployment in real-world applications with imperfect data.
