# DeVA: Decoupled Video-Action Model with physical guidance for robot policy learning

> [!summary] Summary
> DeVA introduces a decoupled Video-Action Model for robot policy learning that separates visual dynamics modeling from action prediction, while integrating multi-level feature transfer and physically salient guidance. The model achieved an average success rate of 72.0% on RoboCasa and 99.0% on LIBERO, demonstrating enhanced data efficiency and robustness in simulation and real-world bimanual manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, despite strong semantic priors, lack explicit supervision for physical dynamics and temporal causality, requiring extensive data for acquiring control-relevant knowledge.
- Existing Video-Action Models (VAMs) either couple visual generation and action prediction within a single feature space, limiting modality-specific learning, or under-utilize multi-level video information and implicitly acquire control-relevant features.
- Prior VAMs typically acquire control-relevant features implicitly, without explicitly integrating task-relevant signals like scene geometry (depth) and interaction regions (affordance).

## Method

- **DeVA** employs a ==decoupled Video-Action architecture== with specialized video and action experts, each maintaining separate parameters and token representations for visual dynamics modeling and action prediction.
- A ==multi-level feature interaction== mechanism routes intermediate representations from various video backbone layers to the action expert via ==layer-wise cross-attention== and ==learnable bridge tokens==.
- Physically salient guidance is integrated by attaching lightweight decoders to the video backbone to predict task-conditioned ==affordance== and ==relative-depth maps==, which directly condition the action expert.

## Results

- DeVA achieved an average success rate of **72.0%** on RoboCasa and **99.0%** across 40 LIBERO tasks, outperforming VLA and VAM baselines, and demonstrating up to **22.0** percentage points improvement under the same data budget.
- The model exhibited improved robustness on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark with an **80.8%** average success rate, surpassing OpenVLA-OFT by **11.2** percentage points against controlled perturbations.
- In real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, DeVA attained an average **74%** success rate with limited demonstrations, substantially outperforming GR00T-N1.6 (**48%**) and Cosmos-Policy (**34%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling video dynamics modeling from action prediction into specialized experts improves optimization tractability and preserves modality-specific capacity, leading to faster convergence and higher success rates.
> - Leveraging multi-level intermediate representations from pretrained video diffusion models, along with learnable bridge tokens, effectively transfers rich spatiotemporal priors for more informed action prediction.
> - Explicitly incorporating physical guidance like affordance and relative-depth maps provides direct, task-relevant cues that make action predictions more grounded and precise, complementing predictive visual dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is advancing towards generalizable policies that can interpret natural language instructions and adapt to dynamic visual scenes. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent paradigm in this area, leveraging large-scale vision-language backbones pretrained on web-scale data. These models exhibit strong semantic priors for instruction following and scene understanding. However, their pretraining objectives are predominantly static, providing limited supervision for physical dynamics, temporal causality, and the evolution of scenes resulting from robot actions. Consequently, much of the control-relevant knowledge often needs to be acquired during downstream imitation learning, which can be data-intensive and computationally demanding for policy optimization.

Video generative models offer a complementary approach by learning to predict future observations, thereby encoding rich spatiotemporal priors related to how scenes, objects, and interactions change over time. This capability has led to the development of Video-Action Models (VAMs), which aim to adapt pretrained video backbones to jointly learn future visual dynamics and robot actions. Despite the potential of VAMs, effectively transferring video dynamics to action prediction presents architectural challenges, particularly in balancing rich cross-modal interaction with tractable policy optimization.

Existing VAMs typically follow two main architectural directions:
1. **Unified formulations:** These models, such as Cosmos-Policy and UWM, embed both visual futures and robot actions within a shared backbone or latent representation. While this design allows for direct cross-modal information exchange, it requires a single feature space to accommodate both visual generation and control prediction, potentially limiting the capacity to learn modality-specific features essential for each task.
2. **Modular formulations:** These approaches connect separate video and action networks, often through predicted features or attention mechanisms. However, some earlier methods may repurpose pretrained video models primarily as predictive representation encoders, potentially under-utilizing the full spectrum of video information. Concurrent dual-DiT architectures allow action transformers to attend to intermediate video features, but these interfaces often rely on representations from a single backbone layer or fixed denoising stage, overlooking complementary abstractions distributed throughout the video backbone. Furthermore, prior VAM work has primarily acquired control-relevant features implicitly through video and action objectives, without explicitly integrating task-relevant signals like scene geometry and interaction regions.

DeVA addresses these limitations by introducing a decoupled Video-Action Model that separates video dynamics modeling from action prediction. It aims to overcome the constraints of shared backbones by employing specialized experts while facilitating rich, multi-level feature transfer and explicitly incorporating physically salient guidance (affordance and depth). This positions DeVA as an effort to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of transferring predictive video priors for robust and generalizable robot policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robot manipulation policy capable of anticipating visual scene evolution in response to language instructions, thereby improving generalizability and data efficiency in robot learning.

The motivation for DeVA stems from identified limitations in current robot policy learning paradigms:
1. **Limitations of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** While VLAs benefit from large-scale pretraining, their training objectives are primarily static (image-text based). This provides limited supervision for physical dynamics, temporal causality, and the effects of robot actions, which are crucial for real-world manipulation. Consequently, VLAs often require substantial task-specific demonstrations and extensive policy optimization to acquire control-relevant knowledge.
2. **Challenges in existing Video-Action Models (VAMs):** VAMs, which leverage video generative models to predict future observations, offer a promising foundation for encoding spatiotemporal priors. However, existing VAM architectures face specific trade-offs:
 - **Coupled Architectures:** Models that couple video and action prediction within a shared backbone or latent space often struggle because a single feature space must simultaneously support both visual generation (high-dimensional, detailed) and control prediction (action-centric, focused). This can restrict the ability to learn modality-specific features, making policy adaptation harder to optimize.
 - **Under-utilization of Video Information:** Modular VAMs, which use separate networks, sometimes under-utilize the rich spatiotemporal information available in video. They might rely on representations extracted from a single backbone layer or fixed denoising stage, failing to fully explore the complementary abstractions distributed across multiple layers of the video backbone.
 - **Implicit Feature Learning:** Prior VAMs largely acquire control-relevant features implicitly through video and action objectives, without explicit integration of task-relevant signals such as scene geometry (e.g., depth) and interaction regions (e.g., affordances). These explicit cues could provide more direct and efficient guidance for action learning.

To address these motivations, DeVA sets the following key objectives:
- **Decouple Video and Action Prediction:** To maintain specialized capacity for both visual generation and control prediction, thereby making downstream policy optimization more tractable.
- **Implement Multi-Level Feature Transfer:** To enable rich information exchange between the decoupled video and action experts by transferring intermediate representations from multiple levels of the video backbone. This aims to leverage the complementary abstractions encoded across different layers of diffusion models.
- **Integrate Physically Salient Guidance:** To explicitly guide the action expert with task-conditioned affordance and relative-depth supervision. This encourages the video backbone to encode control-relevant interaction regions and scene geometry, making this information directly available to the action prediction stream.
- **Achieve Efficient Learning:** To demonstrate strong performance with limited task-specific data, faster convergence compared to unified architectures, and improved robustness in diverse manipulation scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeVA is designed as a decoupled Video-Action Model that processes visual observations and language instructions to predict both future observations and corresponding robot actions. This is achieved through specialized video and action experts, multi-level feature transfer, and physically salient guidance.

#### 3.1. Decoupled Video-Action Architecture with Multi-Level Feature Interaction

DeVA’s core architectural principle is the separation of visual dynamics modeling and action prediction into distinct transformer branches.

- **Decoupled Architecture:**
 - **Video Expert:** This component is responsible for predicting future observations. It is initialized from Cosmos-Predict2, a pretrained latent video diffusion transformer. It utilizes a spatiotemporal VAE to encode video observations into compact latent tokens, and a video transformer models their evolution conditioned on the current frame and language instruction. The video stream is conditioned visually on the initial observation frame, with subsequent frames serving as future prediction targets. Multi-view observations are spatially tiled into a single composite frame for processing efficiency. The video backbone consists of 28 DiT blocks and approximately 2 billion parameters.
 - **Action Expert:** This component predicts the action sequence {A_t..., A_t+h-1} aligned with the predicted future. It follows a DiT-style architecture, similar to the action head of GR00T-N1.5. It receives conditioning from intermediate video representations, T5 language embeddings, and physical guidance, avoiding a redundant visual encoder. It contains 16 layers and approximately 500 million parameters.
 - **Separation Rationale:** By assigning video generation and action prediction to dedicated branches, DeVA maintains separate parameters and token representations for each objective. This avoids forcing visual dynamics and robot actions to share a single feature space, preserving modality-specific capacity and rendering downstream policy optimization more tractable.

- **Multi-Level Feature Interaction:**
 - To maintain connectivity between the decoupled experts, DeVA routes intermediate representations from multiple levels of the video backbone to corresponding action blocks. This transfer is implemented using layer-wise cross-attention.
 - **Learnable Bridge Tokens:** In addition to cross-attention, 12 learnable bridge tokens are introduced as input to the action expert. These tokens aggregate video context and are incorporated into the action stream through self-attention, providing a compact interface for global information flow.
 - **Rationale:** Diffusion models encode distinct abstractions across different network layers and denoising stages. This multi-level transfer mechanism exposes the action expert to a comprehensive range of spatiotemporal representations distributed across the video backbone, enriching the information available for action prediction while preserving the action expert's specialized control capacity.

#### 3.2. Physically Salient Guidance

DeVA integrates explicit physical guidance to further emphasize control-relevant information in the video representations and directly inform action prediction.

- **Affordance & Depth Decoding:**
 - Intermediate video features from the video backbone are repurposed as dense representations for manipulation perception. Lightweight DPT-style decoders, augmented with interleaved temporal-attention layers, are attached to the video backbone. These decoders predict two complementary physical signals: task-conditioned affordance maps and relative-depth maps.
 - **Affordance Maps:** Defined as the probability of an end-effector location (u,v) being an interaction point given the observation and instruction. Language features are incorporated via FiLM layers. Targets are constructed from ground-truth contact points (simulation) or pseudo-labels from an off-the-shelf affordance model (real-world).
 - **Relative-Depth Maps:** Monocular depth estimations. Targets are obtained from an offline depth prediction model.
 - **Feature Sampling:** Features are uniformly sampled from eight video backbone layers (four for affordance, four for depth) to distribute supervision across its depth. Each decoder uses a four-level feature pyramid.

- **Physical Guidance Injection:**
 - Features from the third stage of both affordance and depth decoders are extracted, resampled to the spatiotemporal resolution of the video tokens, and flattened into tokens.
 - These physical guidance tokens are then concatenated with the multi-level video representations along the channel dimension. The fused features are projected to the hidden dimension of the text tokens and appended to them.
 - This augmented conditioning sequence provides additional keys and values for the cross-attention layers in the action expert, allowing it to attend to both predictive video dynamics and explicit cues regarding interaction regions and scene geometry.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

DeVA employs a two-stage training procedure and a joint inference scheme.

- **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1: Video and Decoder Warmup:** The video expert is warmed up using the standard EDM denoising objective (L_video) in latent space. Concurrently, the affordance and depth decoders are supervised using a noise-weighted pixel-wise binary cross-entropy loss (L_aff) for affordances and a noise-weighted masked L1 reconstruction loss with an image-gradient loss (L_depth) for depth. The overall objective for Stage 1 is L_v = L_video + λ_aff * L_aff + λ_depth * L_depth. This stage encourages intermediate video features to encode physically salient structures.
 - **Stage 2: Joint Video-Action Training:** The action expert is introduced and jointly optimized with the video expert. The physical decoders are frozen, but their predictions continue to supervise video features (via gradient flow) and provide direct guidance to the action expert. The action expert is trained using a flow-matching objective (L_act) conditioned on the current observation, language instruction, multi-level video representations, and decoded physical guidance. The complete Stage 2 objective is L = L_v + L_act. Training is performed on eight GPUs using FusedAdamW with a piecewise learning-rate schedule and bfloat16 mixed precision.

- **Inference:**
 - During inference, future video latents and action trajectories are initialized from Gaussian noise.
 - The video expert and action expert jointly sample these through their respective generative processes. The video expert produces a future visual rollout, and the action expert generates the corresponding action chunk.
 - The lightweight physical decoders remain active to provide affordance- and depth-aware guidance during this process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DeVA encompassed both simulation benchmarks and real-world robot deployment, demonstrating its performance, data efficiency, and the benefits of its architectural design and physical guidance.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **RoboCasa:** DeVA was evaluated on 24 kitchen manipulation tasks, achieving a success rate of 72.0%. This result outperformed various VLA and VAM baselines, including those trained with significantly more data (e.g., 300–3,000 demonstrations per task, compared to DeVA's 50 demonstrations per task). Under the same data budget, DeVA improved performance by up to 22.0 points, indicating strong data efficiency.
- **LIBERO:** Across the four standard LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), comprising 40 tasks, DeVA achieved an overall average success rate of 99.0%, which was the highest among the compared baselines. DeVA maintained consistently high performance across tasks with varying spatial layouts, object identities, goal specifications, and task horizons.
- **LIBERO-Plus (Robustness):** On this benchmark, which includes 10,030 variants with controlled perturbations across seven dimensions (e.g., object layout, camera viewpoint, robot initial state), DeVA achieved an average success rate of 80.8%. This exceeded the strongest baseline, OpenVLA-OFT, by 11.2 percentage points, demonstrating improved robustness. However, an 18.2-point drop from its performance on standard LIBERO tasks also highlighted a remaining gap between in-distribution and robustness performance.

#### 4.2. Real-World Deployment

- DeVA was deployed on an I2RT bimanual manipulation platform for three tasks: Handover Marker, Lift Pot, and Pick Up Diverse Bottles. With 30 expert demonstrations per task, DeVA achieved an average success rate of 74%. This compared to 48% for GR00T-N1.6 and 34% for Cosmos-Policy, indicating a substantial performance gain in real-world bimanual manipulation scenarios.
- Qualitative stress tests on Handover Marker indicated that DeVA remained effective under changes in object appearance (color, thickness, category), could identify instructed targets despite distractors, and performed successfully with substantially shifted object layouts, generalizing from limited demonstrations.

#### 4.3. Learning and Data Efficiency

- **Optimization Convergence:** A comparison between DeVA's decoupled architecture and a unified counterpart (under matched optimization budget) showed that decoupled variants converged faster (within approximately 40-50K steps) and achieved higher success rates (66-71% for decoupled vs. 34% for unified). This suggests that specialized experts with structured feature transfer improve optimization tractability.
- **Data Budget:** DeVA exhibited higher success rates at every shared data budget on RoboCasa compared to VLA baselines like π0.5 and GR00T-N1.6. It also processed significantly fewer training examples (up to 20x fewer than Cosmos-Policy), indicating superior data efficiency.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of different DeVA components:

- **Backbone Design (Predictive Visual Modeling):**
 - Action-only model: 19.8% success rate.
 - Adding goal-image prediction: 25.8%.
 - Adding future-video prediction: 36.8%.
 - Incorporating the decoupled architecture with multi-level feature transfer: 66.0%. This progression highlighted the increasing importance of predictive video priors and their effective transfer.
- **Physical Guidance:**
 - Base model (without physical guidance): 66.0%.
 - Adding affordance guidance: 70.2%.
 - Adding depth guidance: 71.3%.
 - Combining both affordance and depth guidance: 72.0%. These results indicate that both affordance and relative-depth guidance provide complementary contributions to physically grounded action prediction.
- **Feature-Interaction Mechanism:** Comparisons of various interaction mechanisms (e.g., direct self-attention, direct cross-attention) showed comparable performance, suggesting that direct transfer of predictive video representations is effective.
- **Conditioning Sources:** Explicitly adding initial-frame features as a separate conditioning source to the action expert provided only a marginal improvement, suggesting that the initial observation's visual context is already sufficiently captured within the video backbone's intermediate representations.
- **Denoising-Step Conditioning:** Conditioning the action expert on features aggregated across multiple video denoising steps improved performance (53.2%) compared to using features from only the final denoising step (49.6%). Freezing the video backbone under final-step conditioning significantly degraded performance (22.4%), underscoring the importance of joint adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DeVA offers several areas of significance and potential impact within the field of robot learning and manipulation:

- **Addressing Architectural Trade-offs:** DeVA presents a framework that addresses the architectural challenge of effectively transferring video dynamics to action prediction. By decoupling video dynamics modeling from action prediction into specialized experts, it mitigates the limitations associated with unified architectures that require a single feature space to support both visual generation and control. This approach maintains modality-specific capacity, which can lead to more tractable policy optimization.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Convergence:** The research demonstrates that DeVA achieves strong performance with limited task-specific demonstrations and exhibits faster convergence compared to unified architectures. This has significant practical implications, as it can reduce the substantial data collection efforts and computational resources typically required for training robot policies, making advanced robot learning more accessible.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** DeVA's superior performance on the LIBERO-Plus robustness benchmark and its qualitative generalization capabilities in real-world scenarios suggest an increased ability to handle variations in object appearance, distractors, and out-of-distribution layouts. This is crucial for deploying robots in complex and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Integration of Physical Guidance:** The explicit incorporation of physically salient guidance, such as affordance and relative-depth maps, highlights the value of injecting task-relevant geometric and interaction information into the learning process. This mechanism shapes intermediate video representations to emphasize control-relevant structures and directly conditions the action expert, potentially leading to more grounded and precise action predictions.
- **Advancing Video Generative Priors for Control:** DeVA underscores the promise of leveraging powerful video generative models as a foundation for robot policy learning. By effectively transferring predictive video representations, the model capitalizes on the rich spatiotemporal priors encoded in video dynamics, which provides a complementary source of knowledge beyond static image-text pretraining.
- **Modular Design for Future Development:** The decoupled nature of DeVA's architecture may facilitate future research. Advances in video generative models or action prediction methodologies could potentially be integrated or refined independently within their respective expert branches, promoting modular development and specialized improvements.

In summary, DeVA's contributions in architectural design, data efficiency, robustness, and the integration of explicit physical cues represent a step towards more capable and deployable generalizable robot manipulation policies, particularly for tasks requiring an understanding of visual dynamics and physical interaction.
