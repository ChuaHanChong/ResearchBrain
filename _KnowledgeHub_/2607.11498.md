# See like a Robot: Robot-Centric Pointmaps for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> KAIST AI researchers introduce robot-centric pointmaps, a method to provide dense, robot-frame 3D geometry to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in an image-compatible format. This approach improved average success rates by 7.6 percentage points on the "0.5 backbone in RoboCasa simulation and enhanced generalization to unseen camera viewpoints by 11.7 percentage points in real-world Franka robot experiments, fostering greater robustness to diverse camera setups.

## Problem

- Robotic actions are defined in the robot's 3D coordinate frame, while visual observations are typically in the camera's frame, leading to a "frame mismatch" that VLA policies must infer.
- Large-scale VLA datasets aggregate data from various camera setups, requiring policies to generalize observation-to-action mappings across diverse viewpoints, which is a complex learning challenge.
- Existing methods for injecting 3D geometry into VLAs often sacrifice the regular image structure, require dedicated 3D encoders, or introduce incompatible modules, hindering seamless integration with pretrained 2D VLA backbones.

## Method

- A **robot-centric pointmap** is constructed by converting ==per-pixel depth== data to 3D points in the camera frame, transforming these points to the robot's ==base frame==, and then centering them relative to the current end-effector position, preserving an H x W image grid.
- An independent ==pointmap encoder==, initialized with weights from the VLA's RGB encoder, processes the ==end-effector-centered pointmap==; its output tokens are then added ==element-wise== to the corresponding RGB tokens.
- The fused visual representation, combining RGB and robot-centric 3D geometry, serves as the comprehensive input for the VLA, allowing for minimal architectural modifications and reuse of existing pretrained 2D visual weights.

## Results

- In RoboCasa simulation, the addition of robot-centric pointmaps improved the average success rate of the "0.5 VLA backbone by **7.6 percentage points** (from **55.3%** to **62.9%**) and SmolVLA by **4.2 percentage points** (from **37.2%** to **41.4%**) across **24** tasks.
- On a real Franka robot, the method improved task success rates by **5.0 percentage points** (from **73.3%** to **78.3%**) under seen camera viewpoints and by **11.7 percentage points** (from **55.0%** to **66.7%**) when generalizing to unseen camera viewpoints, compared to an RGB-only policy.
- The proposed RGB + Pointmap method demonstrated stable performance (**35.8%** SR) as training-time camera viewpoint variation increased, while the RGB-only policy experienced a notable decline in success rate (from **34.5%** to **24.9%** SR) under the same conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly providing pre-computed robot-frame 3D geometry via pointmaps is more effective than requiring the VLA policy to infer it from raw camera parameters like depth, intrinsics, and extrinsics.
> - Preserving the H x W image grid structure for 3D pointmaps and using element-wise fusion with RGB tokens is beneficial, enabling the seamless integration and leverage of existing 2D VLA backbones.
> - Centering the pointmap coordinates relative to the end-effector significantly enhances viewpoint robustness by providing a consistent local geometric representation for interactions, regardless of the camera's absolute position.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. VLA models aim to enable robots to predict actions based on visual observations and natural language instructions, leveraging large-scale datasets. A fundamental challenge in this domain is the "frame mismatch": robot actions are typically defined in the robot's own 3D coordinate frame (e.g., end-effector commands in the robot base frame), whereas visual observations are often acquired in the camera's coordinate frame (e.g., RGB images or depth maps).

The significance of this frame mismatch is amplified by the increasing scale and diversity of robot learning datasets, which aggregate demonstrations from various institutions using different camera setups. Under fixed camera viewpoints, VLA policies might memorize a direct mapping from camera-frame observations to robot-frame actions. However, with diverse viewpoints, policies must learn to generalize this mapping across different observational perspectives, a more complex task.

Prior work has explored several approaches to address camera viewpoint variation and inject 3D geometry into VLAs:
- **Handling Camera Viewpoint Variation:** Some methods provide explicit camera or 3D positional cues to the policy or reparameterize action labels into the camera frame. These approaches typically retain camera-view images as the primary visual input, leaving the scene's representation tied to the camera frame. Another line of research involves synthesizing views to expand training data coverage or canonicalizing test-time views, which may rely on generative models whose robustness can be limited for viewpoints significantly different from the training distribution. A third approach involves building shared 3D scene representations, but these often sacrifice the regular image structure that many pretrained 2D VLAs expect.
- **Injecting 3D Geometry:** Efforts to augment VLAs with explicit 3D geometry include attaching dedicated 3D encoders or geometric experts, which may introduce new modules incompatible with existing VLA pretrained weights. Other methods re-render geometry as 2D views or predict 3D scenes with generative models, introducing an additional inference stage. Some leverage geometric foundation models, but these might not provide metric 3D input during policy deployment.

This work addresses the limitations of these approaches by proposing "robot-centric pointmaps." This representation aims to provide direct robot-frame 3D geometry while simultaneously preserving the dense H x W image grid structure. This design choice is intended to allow for minimal architectural modifications when integrating into existing pretrained 2D VLAs and to facilitate the reuse of their pretrained visual weights, thereby offering a more integrated and efficient solution to the frame mismatch problem under viewpoint variation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a fundamental challenge in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the disparity between the coordinate frame in which visual observations are acquired (camera frame) and the coordinate frame in which robot actions are defined (robot frame). This discrepancy, termed "frame mismatch," necessitates that VLA policies infer 3D spatial relationships relative to the robot's operational space from camera-centric inputs.

The problem is exacerbated by the trend towards large-scale VLA datasets, which often aggregate demonstrations collected across diverse camera setups and institutions. In such scenarios, policies must generalize their understanding of observation-to-action mappings across a wide range of viewpoints, rather than simply memorizing a single, consistent mapping applicable under a fixed camera. This generalization requirement imposes a significant learning burden on VLA models.

The key objectives are motivated by the limitations of existing approaches to address this frame mismatch:
1. **Direct 3D Spatial Information:** To provide the VLA policy with explicit 3D spatial information that is directly relevant for manipulation tasks, moving beyond implicit inference from 2D images or depth maps.
2. **Robot-centric Coordinate Frame:** To express this 3D information directly in the robot's own coordinate frame. This aims to align the observational input with the action space, ensuring that physical points in the environment consistently map to the same coordinates relative to the robot, regardless of the camera's perspective.
3. **Preservation of Image-form Structure:** To achieve the above objectives while preserving the dense H x W grid format characteristic of traditional images. This is motivated by the desire to leverage and seamlessly integrate with existing pretrained 2D VLA backbones, avoiding the need for specialized 3D encoders or alternative data structures (like sparse point clouds) that might disrupt the existing visual processing pathways and prevent the reuse of learned features. Point clouds, for instance, discard the image grid and density, making integration challenging and potentially losing fine-grained details.

In summary, the research aims to develop a novel input representation that concurrently provides dense, robot-centric 3D geometry in an image-compatible format, thereby simplifying the learning of observation-to-action mappings, improving generalization across diverse camera viewpoints, and integrating efficiently with current VLA architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology introduces robot-centric pointmaps as a primary input to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, alongside traditional RGB images. The approach involves a specific pipeline for pointmap construction and a fusion mechanism to integrate pointmap features with RGB features within the VLA's visual processing stream.

**Robot-Centric Pointmap Construction:**
The process begins with an RGB-D observation from a camera *c*, characterized by its resolution (H x W), intrinsic parameters (*K_c*), and its rotation (*R_c*) and translation (*t_c*) relative to the robot base frame. Per-pixel depth information (*D_c*) is also utilized.
1. **Lifting to Camera Frame 3D:** Each pixel (u,v) in the image is converted into a 3D point *P_cam_c(u,v)* in the camera's local coordinate frame. This is achieved using the pixel coordinates, its corresponding depth value *D_c(u,v)*, and the inverse of the camera's intrinsic matrix *K_c*. The formula used is *P_cam_c(u,v) = D_c(u,v)K_c^-1[u, v, 1]^T*.
2. **Transforming to Robot Base Frame 3D:** The camera-frame 3D point *P_cam_c(u,v)* is then transformed into the robot's base coordinate frame, yielding *P_R_c(u,v)*. This transformation uses the known camera-to-robot rotation *R_c* and translation *t_c*: *P_R_c(u,v) = R_c P_cam_c(u,v) + t_c*. The resulting *P_R_c* maintains the original H x W grid, with each pixel now storing the 3D coordinates (x, y, z) of the corresponding scene point in the robot's base frame. This ensures that a given physical point in the environment is assigned the same robot-frame coordinate regardless of the camera's viewpoint.
3. **End-Effector Centering:** To further align the observation space with the action space, the robot-frame pointmap *P_R_c(u,v)* is re-centered relative to the current end-effector (EE) position *t_EE*. This results in an end-effector-centered pointmap *P_EE_c(u,v) = P_R_c(u,v) - t_EE*. This centering expresses scene geometry relative to the end-effector, ensuring that interactions requiring similar gripper motions have consistent local geometric representations.

**Pointmap-RGB Fusion:**
The pointmap preserves the H x W image grid, enabling its integration into 2D VLAs.
1. **Separate Encoding:** An independent "pointmap encoder" *g_phi* processes the end-effector-centered pointmap *P_EE_c*. This encoder is designed with the same architecture as the VLA's existing RGB encoder *f_theta* and is initialized using the weights from *f_theta*.
2. **Element-wise Fusion:** The tokens generated by the pointmap encoder are then added element-wise to the corresponding RGB tokens produced by the RGB encoder *f_theta(I_c)*, where *I_c* is the RGB image. This additive fusion creates a combined visual representation *z_c = f_theta(I_c) + g_phi(P_EE_c)*.
3. **VLA Input:** These fused tokens *z_c* subsequently serve as the comprehensive visual input for the VLA. This mechanism integrates robot-centric 3D geometry into the VLA's visual stream without requiring a specialized point cloud encoder, voxelization module, or introducing an additional sequence of tokens.

**Design Choices and Controlled Studies (Section 4):**
The research conducted controlled studies on the RoboCasa platform to systematically evaluate the design choices underlying the robot-centric pointmap:
- **Robot-frame geometry vs. Learning from camera information (RQ1):** Comparison showed that pre-computing robot-frame geometry (pointmap) yielded better performance than expecting the policy to infer it from depth, intrinsics, and extrinsics.
- **Image-form pointmaps vs. Point clouds (RQ2):** Experiments demonstrated that preserving the H x W image grid and fusing pointmap tokens element-wise with RGB tokens was more effective than using point clouds, even with advanced point cloud encoders. This highlighted the importance of spatial correspondence for fusion.
- **Coordinate origin (End-effector centering) (RQ3):** End-effector centering was found to enhance viewpoint robustness. By expressing points relative to the end effector, it creates a more consistent local geometry for interactions, reducing the policy's dependence on absolute workspace location.
- **Robustness to increasing training-time viewpoint variation (RQ4):** Evaluations showed that the proposed method (RGB + Pointmap) maintained stable performance as the level of camera viewpoint randomization during training increased, whereas RGB-only policies experienced performance degradation.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Simulation (RoboCasa):** Policies were trained on 50 human demonstrations across 24 atomic tasks. Camera viewpoints were randomized across demonstrations during training, and evaluation was performed under both fixed and randomized viewpoints. Two pretrained VLA backbones, π_0.5 and SmolVLA, were fine-tuned.
- **Real-world (Franka Robot):** A Franka Research 3 robot equipped with a wrist-mounted and an external RealSense camera was used. Data was collected across three external camera placements, with 15 demonstrations per task for four manipulation tasks (total 180 demonstrations). Evaluation involved both a "seen" camera placement (one of the training placements) and a "held-out unseen" placement to assess generalization.
- **Baselines:** In simulation, comparisons included camera-aware VLAs (OC-VLA, KYC), 3D-augmented VLAs (GeoVLA, PointVLA) re-implemented on the π_0.5 backbone, and a standalone point cloud policy (FP3). In real-world experiments, comparisons were made against an RGB-only π_0.5 policy and a point cloud diffusion policy (DP3).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings from both controlled studies and experimental evaluations in simulation and on a real robot.

**Controlled Studies on Design Choices (Section 4):**
- **Pre-computed Robot-Frame Geometry:** Providing pre-computed robot-centric 3D geometry through a pointmap (34.7% success rate, SR) was more effective than having the policy learn to convert camera information (depth, intrinsics, extrinsics) into robot-frame coordinates (RGB + Plücker + Depth: 31.6% SR). This indicated that direct provision of robot-frame geometry is beneficial.
- **Image-form Pointmaps vs. Point Clouds:** The preservation of the H x W image grid and the use of element-wise addition for fusing pointmap and RGB tokens (34.7% SR) outperformed approaches that treated 3D geometry as a sparse point cloud (e.g., RGB + Point cloud with PTv3 encoder: 32.8% SR). This suggested that image-form representation and direct spatial correspondence in fusion are advantageous for pretrained VLAs.
- **End-Effector Centering:** Centering the pointmap coordinates on the end-effector (EE) position significantly improved robustness to viewpoint variation. The performance drop from a fixed to a randomized evaluation viewpoint was only 0.3 points (from 36.9% to 36.6% SR) for EE-centered pointmaps, compared to a 2.0-point drop for robot-base-centered pointmaps. This indicated that aligning the observation origin with the action space origin fosters consistency.
- **Robustness to Training-Time Viewpoint Variation:** The RGB + Pointmap method demonstrated consistent performance even as the training-time camera viewpoint variation increased (from 37.6% SR under "No variation" to 35.8% SR under "High variation"). In contrast, the RGB-only policy experienced a notable decline in success rate (from 34.5% to 24.9% SR) under the same conditions. This supports the hypothesis that robot-frame geometry enhances robustness to diverse training viewpoints.

**Simulation Experiments on RoboCasa (Section 5.1):**
- **Improvement on Pretrained Backbones:** The addition of robot-centric pointmaps consistently improved the performance of two distinct pretrained VLA backbones:
 - For π_0.5, the average success rate across 24 tasks increased from 55.3% to 62.9% (a +7.6 percentage point gain). Improvements were observed across all five task categories (Doors, Drawers, Coffee, Pick-and-place, Turn objects).
 - For SmolVLA, the average success rate increased from 37.2% to 41.4% (a +4.2 percentage point gain).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** The proposed method (π_0.5 + pointmap, 62.9% SR) outperformed existing camera-aware baselines (e.g., KYC, 59.1% SR) and 3D-augmented baselines (e.g., PointVLA, 57.3% SR) that share the π_0.5 backbone. This suggests that the approach of expressing geometry in the robot frame and fusing it on the RGB grid is more effective than conditioning on camera parameters or using separate 3D modules. The standalone point cloud policy, FP3, achieved 42.8% SR, trailing all π_0.5-based methods.
- **Randomized Evaluation Viewpoints (Appendix D):** The performance gains with pointmaps persisted even when evaluation viewpoints were randomized, with π_0.5 + pointmap achieving 60.3% SR compared to 54.2% for RGB-only π_0.5.

**Real-World Experiments (Section 5.2):**
- **Performance under Seen Viewpoints:** On a Franka robot, with an external camera placed at a configuration seen during training, adding pointmaps improved π_0.5 from 73.3% to 78.3% SR (a +5.0 point margin over RGB-only). Both VLA variants outperformed the point cloud policy DP3 (63.3% SR).
- **Enhanced Generalization to Unseen Viewpoints:** The advantage of pointmaps was more pronounced when the external camera was moved to an unseen placement. The RGB-only π_0.5 policy experienced an 18.3-point drop in success rate (from 73.3% to 55.0%). In contrast, π_0.5 with pointmaps showed a smaller 11.6-point drop (from 78.3% to 66.7%), widening its margin over RGB-only to +11.7 points. This indicated that pointmaps contributed to greater robustness against camera viewpoint changes in real-world deployment. DP3, while showing better resistance to viewpoint shift than RGB-only, still achieved a lower success rate (48.3%) than π_0.5 + pointmap (66.7%) in the unseen scenario.

These results collectively indicate that robot-centric pointmaps provide a robust and effective method for integrating 3D geometry into VLAs, particularly in scenarios involving diverse camera viewpoints, leading to improved performance and generalization in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces robot-centric pointmaps as a method for augmenting Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, demonstrating an approach to address the observation-to-action frame mismatch that is inherent in robotic manipulation tasks. The work is significant for several reasons:

First, it proposes a **straightforward and integrated mechanism for injecting robot-frame 3D geometry** into existing pretrained 2D VLAs. By preserving the dense H x W image grid structure, pointmaps allow for element-wise fusion with RGB tokens, utilizing the VLA's existing visual pathway and reusing pretrained weights. This contrasts with approaches that require dedicated 3D encoders, separate modules, or discard the image structure, potentially making integration more complex and less efficient. This architectural compatibility lowers the barrier for adopting 3D awareness in many existing VLA frameworks.

Second, the findings consistently demonstrate **improved robustness to camera viewpoint variation**. Both controlled studies and full experimental evaluations in simulation (RoboCasa) and on a real robot (Franka Research 3) indicate that policies augmented with robot-centric pointmaps maintain performance levels more effectively when exposed to diverse camera setups during training and generalize better to unseen camera placements during deployment. This capability is crucial for scaling VLA models, as large-scale datasets increasingly aggregate demonstrations from heterogeneous camera configurations. By providing a consistent robot-frame coordinate for physical points regardless of viewpoint, pointmaps allow policies to focus on learning manipulation skills rather than complex viewpoint-dependent mappings.

Third, the research shows **quantitative improvements in task success rates** across a range of manipulation tasks. The gains observed on both π_0.5 and SmolVLA backbones in simulation, and on the π_0.5 backbone in real-world experiments, indicate that the proposed input representation contributes to more reliable policy execution. The outperformance of various camera-aware and 3D-augmented baselines further underscores the efficacy of the robot-centric pointmap design.

Finally, the work articulates a **general principle for developing 3D-aware VLAs**: when camera calibration information is available, observations should be expressed in the robot's action coordinate system before being processed by the policy. Robot-centric pointmaps serve as a concrete implementation of this principle, demonstrating its practical benefits.

The potential impact of this work is in advancing the development of more generalizable and robust robotic manipulation policies. By simplifying the integration of 3D geometry and enhancing resilience to diverse observational viewpoints, robot-centric pointmaps could facilitate the creation of VLA models that are more adaptable to varied deployment environments and capable of learning from larger, more diverse datasets. This could accelerate progress in real-world robot learning, enabling robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured or dynamically changing settings with greater reliability.
