# Distillation-PPO: A Novel Two-Stage Reinforcement Learning Framework for Humanoid Robot Perceptive Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics and The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology introduce Distillation-PPO (D-PPO), a two-stage reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to achieve robust perceptive locomotion in complex, irregular real-world environments. The framework enhances training efficiency and stability while demonstrating superior noise robustness on the "Tien Kung" humanoid robot, successfully navigating diverse terrains including stairs and slopes.

## Problem

- Traditional two-stage reinforcement learning methods, which distill knowledge from a teacher policy, often limit the student's potential and struggle to adapt to noisy, partially observable real-world conditions.
- End-to-end reinforcement learning in partially observable environments (POMDPs) faces significant hurdles with training instability, low sample efficiency, and difficulty in achieving high-quality policies.
- Humanoid robots require advanced control frameworks to achieve adaptive and robust locomotion in complex, unstructured real-world environments, a challenge not fully met by existing methods.

## Method

- A novel two-stage **Distillation-PPO** (D-PPO) framework is proposed, combining a teacher policy trained in a fully observable simulated MDP with a student policy trained in a partially observable simulated POMDP.
- The student network is initialized with the teacher's parameters and trained using a composite loss function that includes both a distillation loss (L2-norm) for regularization and a standard PPO loss for continuous reinforcement learning.
- The system incorporates a robust perceptive framework, utilizing ==LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO)== and a depth camera for accurate terrain reconstruction, along with a comprehensive reward system designed for stable and adaptive bipedal locomotion.

## Results

- The "Tien Kung" humanoid robot, controlled by the D-PPO framework, successfully demonstrated robust locomotion across various complex real-world terrains, including navigating platforms, slopes, and stairs with adaptive posture adjustments.
- Comparative analysis showed D-PPO achieved "Good" control performance and "Good" noise robustness, surpassing methods based solely on distillation (Worse noise robustness) or pure PPO (Medium noise robustness).
- The integrated LIO system provided high-precision terrain reconstruction and accurate pose estimation, crucial for the robot's informed decision-making and stable navigation in diverse environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a teacher's supervisory regularization with a student's intrinsic reinforcement learning capabilities provides a more stable, efficient, and robust training approach for complex robotic tasks.
> - Initializing the student policy with pre-trained teacher network parameters significantly reduces training difficulty and accelerates convergence compared to end-to-end reinforcement learning from scratch.
> - Incorporating intentional noise in perceptual inputs during student training in simulation, alongside a sophisticated perception system, is critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap and achieving high real-world noise robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of robust and adaptive locomotion in complex environments is a long-standing grand challenge in robotics. Historically, optimal control methods, relying on precise dynamic models and manual feature engineering, dominated this field. However, these traditional approaches struggle with dynamic and unpredictable real-world scenarios due to their inherent difficulty in adapting to novel conditions.

The advent of reinforcement learning (RL) and deep learning has revolutionized robot control, offering a data-driven paradigm where robots can learn complex behaviors through trial and error in simulated environments, then transfer these learned policies to physical hardware. This has enabled robots, including humanoids and quadrupeds, to perform increasingly agile and versatile locomotion tasks, from basic walking and running to advanced parkour and stair traversal.

Within the domain of perceptive locomotion for legged robots—where robots use sensor data (e.g., LiDAR, depth cameras) to perceive and react to their environment—existing RL-based methods generally fall into two categories:

1. **Two-stage methods:** These approaches typically involve training a "teacher" policy in a fully observable simulated environment (Markov Decision Process, MDP) that benefits from privileged information (e.g., perfect terrain height maps). The knowledge from this teacher is then transferred or "distilled" to a "student" policy, which operates in a partially observable environment (Partially Observable Markov Decision Process, POMDP) using real-world sensor inputs. The advantage is that the teacher's expertise provides a strong supervisory signal, helping the student learn efficiently. However, a significant drawback is that the student policy might be limited by the teacher's capabilities, struggling to surpass it or adapt to unforeseen scenarios where the teacher's guidance might be inaccurate or incomplete. This also introduces a potential "sim-to-real" gap, as privileged information is often only available in simulation. Examples of this approach are cited for quadrupedal robots in challenging terrains.
2. **End-to-end methods:** These directly train a policy in a POMDP using RL, foregoing explicit teacher supervision or privileged information. This allows for potentially greater flexibility and avoids the limitations of a teacher policy. However, learning directly from partial observations in complex environments is inherently more challenging, often leading to unstable training, lower sample efficiency, and difficulties in achieving high-quality policies.

This paper critically addresses the limitations of both paradigms by proposing a novel framework, Distillation-PPO (D-PPO), that seeks to combine their respective strengths. It positions itself as an innovative hybrid approach that leverages the robust regularization and guidance of a teacher policy (characteristic of two-stage methods) while simultaneously enabling the student policy to continually learn and improve through intrinsic reinforcement learning in a POMDP (addressing the upper-bound limitation of traditional distillation and the training instability of pure end-to-end RL). By doing so, the authors aim to achieve superior performance in terms of training efficiency, stability, robustness, and generalization for humanoid robot perceptive locomotion, particularly in real-world, irregular terrains. This work advances the state-of-the-art by offering a more robust and adaptable training methodology for complex robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more effective and robust reinforcement learning framework for enabling humanoid robots to achieve perceptive locomotion in complex and irregular real-world environments. The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations observed in existing approaches to perceptive locomotion:

1. **Limitations of Traditional Two-Stage Methods:** While these methods offer valuable supervision from a teacher policy trained with privileged information in simulation, they suffer from several drawbacks. The teacher's policy, being trained in an ideal MDP, may not accurately reflect real-world complexities and noise, leading to potentially erroneous guidance. Crucially, the student policy, heavily reliant on the teacher, may be limited in its potential, unable to surpass the teacher's performance or adapt effectively to novel, unseen scenarios where the teacher might fail. This contradicts the very essence of reinforcement learning, which emphasizes continuous learning and improvement through interaction.
2. **Challenges of End-to-End Reinforcement Learning:** Conversely, end-to-end methods, which directly train policies in partially observable environments, circumvent the teacher's limitations but face significant hurdles. The absence of complete environmental information makes the learning process inherently more difficult, often leading to instability and reduced performance. Without the guiding supervision of a teacher, the student policy may struggle to converge to high-quality solutions, resulting in less stable and efficient locomotion.

Recognizing these trade-offs, the authors pose a fundamental question: Is it possible to synthesize the advantages of both approaches to enhance the locomotion capabilities of humanoid robots? Their motivation stems from the belief that the teacher policy's supervision can act as an excellent regularization signal, guiding the student towards correct behaviors, while simultaneously allowing the student to leverage the inherent exploration and improvement capabilities of reinforcement learning within a partially observable context.

Thus, the core objective is to propose a novel two-stage framework that:
- Combines the stabilizing and guiding influence of a teacher policy learned in a fully observable MDP.
- Integrates the continuous learning and performance-enhancing characteristics of reinforcement learning for the student policy in a POMDP.
- Ultimately, aims to achieve higher training efficiency, improved stability in simulations, and superior robustness and generalization capabilities when deployed on real humanoid robots navigating complex terrains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is centered around **Distillation-PPO (D-PPO)**, an innovative two-stage reinforcement learning framework designed to achieve robust perceptive locomotion in humanoid robots.

**A. Two-Stage Reinforcement Learning with Teacher Distillation**

1. **Teacher Training Stage:**
 - **Environment:** A fully observable Markov Decision Process (MDP) within a simulation environment (Isaac Gym), ensuring access to clean and precise environmental information.
 - **Perception:** The teacher directly receives accurate "scan dots" (compressed elevation map) from the simulator.
 - **Network Architecture:** The teacher network takes as input compressed scan vectors (via Conv1D into a 32-dimensional latent space) and a 50-frame history state (via an external encoder into a 32-dimensional latent space). These latents are then processed by a combined Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
 - **Gait Control:** A periodic system, as described in prior work, is used to structure the locomotion, though the authors acknowledge potential conflicts between fixed periodic parameters and dynamic terrain navigation.
 - **Purpose:** This stage establishes a highly performant baseline policy with optimal perception, providing a strong reference for the subsequent student training.

2. **Student Training Stage:**
 - **Environment:** A partially observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) in simulation. To bridge the sim-to-real gap, Gaussian noise is intentionally added to the perceptual input (scan dots), mimicking real-world sensor inaccuracies (domain randomization).
 - **Initialization:** Crucially, the student network inherits the structure and parameters directly from the trained teacher network. This acts as a form of "self-distillation" or strong pre-training, giving the student an excellent starting point.
 - **Learning Mechanism:** The student policy is trained using a composite loss function that combines two key components:
 - **Distillation Loss ($L_{distillation}$):** This is an L2-norm (Mean Squared Error) between the actions output by the teacher policy ($\pi_{teacher}(s_t)$) and the student policy ($\pi_{student}(s_t)$). It acts as a regularization term, guiding the student towards the teacher's effective behaviors and ensuring training stability.
 - **PPO Loss ($L_{PPO}$):** This is the standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) loss, which optimizes the policy to maximize cumulative reinforcement learning rewards. This component enables the student to continuously explore the action space, learn from its own interactions, and potentially surpass the teacher's performance by adapting to the noisy POMDP environment. It explicitly seeks to improve the model's upper bound.
 - **Total Loss ($L_{total}$):** The overall loss function is a weighted sum: $L_{total} = \alpha L_{distillation} + \beta L_{PPO}$. In experiments, $\alpha = 0.5$ and $\beta = 0.5$ were chosen to balance the guidance from distillation with the exploration from PPO.
 - **Advantage:** This combined approach allows the student policy to benefit from the teacher's robust actions as a supervisory signal while simultaneously refining its policy through direct reinforcement learning in a more realistic (noisy) environment. Both the policy and the history state encoder are trained using gradients from both DAgger (implied by the distillation loss) and PPO.

**B. Perceptual System and Reward Design**

1. **Perception Framework:**
 - **Elevation Maps & Scan Dots:** The system utilizes 2.5D elevation maps to represent complex terrain geometry, suitable for uneven surfaces. This map is then compressed into a 441-dimensional "scan dots" vector, serving as the policy's environmental input.
 - **Real-world Sensor Integration:**
 - **LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO):** A Fast-LIO2 system (using Livox MID-360 LiDAR and Xsence IMU) is implemented for accurate robot pose estimation and terrain mapping, crucial for humanoid stability.
 - **Depth Camera:** An Orbbec 355L depth camera provides depth images, which are converted into point clouds.
 - **Occlusion Mitigation:** To counter depth map occlusions by the robot's legs, joint angles and a forward kinematic model are used to estimate the robot's body bounding box, allowing occluded regions to be excluded from depth image processing.
 - **Elevation Map Update:** A one-dimensional Kalman filter (Equations 5 and 6) is employed to update the height of each grid cell in the elevation map, incorporating sensor noise models for robustness.

2. **Reward System:** A comprehensive reward system is designed to achieve stable and adaptive locomotion:
 - **Periodic Rewards ($r_{per}$):** Encourages a natural bipedal gait by rewarding specific foot forces during the stance phase and foot velocities during the swing phase, defined based on a cyclic phase indicator (following,).
 - **Regularization Rewards (Table I):** These are critical for promoting safe and smooth robot movements, essential for real-world deployment. They include penalties for:
 - Action differentials (smoothness).
 - Joint DoF limits (physical safety).
 - DoF velocity and acceleration (movement quality).
 - Arm DoF movements (minimizing unnecessary arm motion).
 - Orientation differentials (roll, pitch) and torso yaw (balance and upright posture).
 - Joint torques (energy efficiency and hardware longevity).
 - **Command Reward ($r_{com}$):** Guides the robot towards a desired velocity in x, y, and yaw directions, enabling navigation tasks.

**C. Training and Implementation Details**

- **Parallel Training:** Both teacher and student policies are trained on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU, leveraging 4096 parallel Isaac Gym environments for high-throughput data collection and efficient learning.
- **Observation & Action Space:** Actions are 18-dimensional joint desired positions. Observations include proprioceptive data (velocities, orientation, joint states, previous action), command signals, periodic gait signals, and the processed scan dots from the perception system.
- **Noise Handling:** The overall noise signal settings for training are adopted from prior work to accurately reflect real-world robot interactions, enhancing sim-to-real transferability.

This meticulously designed methodology aims to create a highly effective, stable, and generalizable framework for humanoid robot perceptive locomotion, addressing the shortcomings of previous approaches by strategically combining their strengths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that the Distillation-PPO (D-PPO) framework significantly enhances the perceptive locomotion capabilities of humanoid robots, particularly in challenging real-world scenarios.

1. **Real-world Locomotion Performance on "Tien Kung" Humanoid Robot:**
 - **Diverse Terrains:** The humanoid robot "Tien Kung," trained with D-PPO, successfully navigates a variety of complex terrains. Figure 1 visually illustrates its ability to walk on platforms nearly as high as its shins, cross sloped surfaces, and traverse small ditches. This highlights the robot's adaptability to varying ground profiles.
 - **Adaptive Posture:** The robot demonstrates intelligent posture adjustments according to the terrain. For instance, it maintains a relatively straight-knee posture on flat ground for a broader field of view, while bending its knees and swinging its arms to reduce impact and maintain balance when overcoming obstacles.
 - **Stair and Slope Traversal:** Figure 5 provides detailed visualizations of the "Tien Kung" robot accurately performing locomotion tasks on classic stair and slope terrains, including ascending and descending both. These images clearly show the precise joint adjustments and center-of-gravity shifts required for such maneuvers, confirming the robot's enhanced stability and adaptability.

2. **High-Precision Perception and Terrain Reconstruction:**
 - **LIO System Effectiveness:** The integrated LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) module is highly effective in achieving accurate terrain reconstruction. Figure 4 showcases the high fidelity of the reconstruction results across various terrains, including flat ground, slopes, steps, and irregular surfaces. This accurate perception is critical for the robot's informed decision-making and robust navigation.
 - **Pose Estimation:** Figure 3 further details the visual perception system's performance in estimating posture on elevated terrains and during motion, demonstrating its accuracy in reconstructing the environment, which is vital for selecting precise scan points for the policy network.

3. **Comparative Analysis of Training Methods (Table II):**
 - The paper provides a concise comparative analysis of D-PPO against traditional methods that rely solely on distillation or solely on reinforcement learning (PPO) in terms of training difficulty, control performance, and noise robustness.
 - **Only Distillation:** This method is characterized by "Easy" training difficulty and "Normal" control performance, but critically, it exhibits "Worse" noise robustness. This validates the authors' motivation that pure distillation methods struggle when the teacher's knowledge is applied to noisy, partially observable real-world conditions or when the teacher encounters its limitations.
 - **Only RL (PPO):** This approach shows "Hard" training difficulty and "Normal" control performance, with "Medium" noise robustness. This supports the claim that end-to-end RL in POMDPs is challenging and can be unstable, even if it offers some level of noise tolerance due to its direct learning in a noisy environment.
 - **D-PPO (Ours):** D-PPO clearly outperforms both alternatives. It achieves "Easy" training difficulty (benefiting from teacher initialization and guidance), "Good" control performance (surpassing the "Normal" of other methods), and significantly "Good" noise robustness.

These findings strongly support the authors' central hypothesis: by combining the supervisory regularization of distillation with the adaptive learning capabilities of PPO in a two-stage framework, D-PPO offers a superior solution that balances training efficiency, stability, and crucial real-world robustness for humanoid robot perceptive locomotion. The practical demonstrations on the "Tien Kung" robot provide compelling evidence of the framework's effectiveness in bridging the sim-to-real gap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Distillation-PPO (D-PPO) framework proposed in this paper represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robot locomotion control, particularly for navigation in complex and unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Novel Integration of Learning Paradigms:** The core contribution lies in its innovative combination of two powerful yet often conflicting learning paradigms: behavior distillation for supervision and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for continuous reinforcement learning. By designing a two-stage framework where teacher policies provide regularization and initial guidance, while PPO allows for ongoing exploration and performance enhancement in partially observable environments, the authors successfully mitigate the respective drawbacks of purely distillation-based or purely end-to-end RL approaches. This hybrid strategy offers a blueprint for more effective policy learning in complex robotic systems.

2. **Enhanced Real-world Robustness and Generalization:** A critical challenge in robot learning is the "sim-to-real" gap and the ability to operate reliably in the face of sensor noise, environmental variability, and unforeseen conditions. D-PPO explicitly addresses this by incorporating noisy perceptual inputs during student training and by allowing the student to adapt beyond the teacher's limitations through RL. The experimental results, particularly the "Good" noise robustness shown in Table II and the successful deployment on the physical "Tien Kung" robot traversing diverse terrains, confirm its superior practical performance. This enhanced robustness is crucial for widespread adoption of humanoid robots in real-world applications.

3. **Improved Training Efficiency and Stability:** The framework provides a more stable and efficient training process. By inheriting weights and structure from the teacher, the student policy benefits from a strong initialization, accelerating convergence and reducing the "hard training" associated with pure end-to-end RL in POMDPs. This efficiency is vital for complex tasks like humanoid locomotion, which typically demand extensive computational resources and long training times.

4. **Enabling Advanced Humanoid Capabilities:** The ability of "Tien Kung" to perform intricate maneuvers like climbing/descending stairs and navigating sloped and irregular terrains, demonstrated through high-precision terrain reconstruction and adaptive posture, showcases the potential of D-PPO to unlock new levels of agility and autonomy for humanoid robots. This pushes humanoids closer to operating effectively in human-centric environments, which are inherently complex and dynamic.

5. **Broad Applicability Beyond Humanoids:** The authors explicitly state that "our method can be easily extended and transferred to other legged robots." This suggests that the D-PPO framework is not confined to humanoids but offers a generalizable solution for any legged robot requiring perceptive locomotion, including quadrupeds and other bipedal platforms. This broadens its potential impact across the field of mobile robotics.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** This paper provides new insights and a validated method for addressing the complexities of perceptive locomotion. It opens avenues for future research into adaptive learning architectures, dynamic weighting of distillation and RL losses, and more sophisticated methods for bridging the sim-to-real gap. The work suggests that a judicious blend of structured supervision and adaptive learning is key to developing truly intelligent and resilient robotic systems.

In summary, D-PPO offers a powerful and practical solution to a long-standing problem in robotics. Its successful real-world validation on a humanoid robot, coupled with its demonstrable improvements in robustness and training efficiency, positions it as a significant contribution that will likely influence the development of next-generation autonomous legged robots capable of navigating the complexities of our world.
