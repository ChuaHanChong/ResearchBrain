# RL Grokking Recipe: How Does RL Unlock and Transfer New Algorithms in LLMs?

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforcement learning, when combined with a staged reward approach, enables large language models to acquire and transfer new algorithmic reasoning strategies for previously unsolvable problems, demonstrating a distinct "grokking" phase transition. The work introduces DELTA, a controlled benchmark for evaluating learnability and generalization in LLMs, revealing that while compositional generalization is strong, transformative generalization remains challenging.

## Problem

- A central debate exists on whether Reinforcement Learning (RL) can truly enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to acquire genuinely new reasoning strategies or merely refine existing latent skills.
- Existing open benchmarks lack sufficient control over train-test splits and problem family composition, making it difficult to definitively assess learnability and systematic generalization.
- LLMs struggle with hard, sparse-reward tasks where they initially achieve zero success, as the lack of positive feedback hinders effective RL training.

## Method

- Introduced DELTA (==Distributional Evaluation== of Learnability and Transferrability in Algorithmic Coding), a novel, controlled benchmark with diverse programming and mathematical problem families, including a custom Out-of-Distribution (OOD) domain called Manufactoria.
- Developed a staged RL training regime for LLMs, beginning with a warm-up phase using fine-grained per-test pass rates as dense rewards, followed by a transition to binary full-pass rewards for convergence.
- Systematically investigated the effects of experience replay, feedback-in-the-loop, and curriculum learning on accelerating the observed grokking phenomenon during RL training.

## Results

- RL-trained models using the staged reward regime achieved a **100%** full pass rate on the challenging Manufactoria-HAS family, starting from an initial `pass@K=0` for the base Qwen3-**4B**-Instruct-2507 model.
- Models demonstrated high full-pass rates (60-**70%**) on unseen compositional skill combinations in BouncingSim, showing effective integration of learned modules.
- Exploratory generalization performance decreased significantly with increasing OOD difficulty, and transformative generalization tasks requiring entirely new schemas remained largely unsolved.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL can induce a "grokking" phase transition, allowing LLMs to discover and master algorithmic solutions for tasks where the base model initially exhibits zero success, indicating genuine acquisition of new capabilities.
> - The use of staged rewards, particularly a dense per-test pass rate warm-up, is critical for providing sufficient gradient signal to enable learning in initially unsolvable, sparse-reward tasks.
> - LLMs exhibit strong compositional generalization in coding tasks (integrating unseen combinations of skills) but struggle with transformative generalization that demands the discovery of qualitatively novel solution schemas.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a central and ongoing debate within the field of large language models (LLMs) and reinforcement learning (RL): whether RL merely refines and sharpens existing latent skills within an LLM (learned during pre-training or post-training) or if it can enable LLMs to genuinely acquire and generalize novel reasoning strategies.

Prior work has presented conflicting views. Some, like Yue et al. (2025) and Wu et al. (2025), suggest a skeptical stance, arguing that RL's influence might be bounded by the LLM's pre-existing representational limits, with apparent improvements often being mere refinements that become negligible at larger `pass@K` evaluations. Conversely, optimistic views, such as Liu et al. (2025b), propose that RL can indeed push reasoning boundaries, demonstrating emergent problem-solving in specific tasks.

The authors position their work as an attempt to make this debate "testable" through two clear criteria:
1. **Learnability:** Can RL enable an LLM to solve problem families where even highly-sampled pretrained models (e.g., `pass@K=0`) fail entirely?
2. **Generalization (or Transferability):** If such new skills are learned, can they systematically transfer to diverse Out-of-Distribution (OOD) test sets, going beyond mere memorization?

The paper critiques existing open benchmarks in math and coding (e.g., Numina-Math, DeepMath, OpenCodeReasoning) for their lack of precise control over train-test splits and problem family composition. This heterogeneity, the authors argue, blurs the distinction between sharpening existing capabilities and acquiring truly novel ones. In response, they introduce **DELTA (Distributional Evaluation of Learnability and Transferrability in Algorithmic Coding)**, a new benchmark specifically designed to overcome these limitations.

DELTA extends the lineage of controlled datasets like OMEGA (Sun et al., 2025), which focused on mathematical reasoning. However, DELTA shifts to programming, offering several key improvements:
- **Novel OOD problem families:** DELTA includes "Manufactoria," a completely novel programming domain with bespoke syntax and puzzle rules, designed to be genuinely out-of-distribution and thus unlikely to be present in pretraining corpora. This is a crucial distinction from math tasks, which, even when complex, often remain within familiar mathematical domains.
- **Harder to shortcut with tools:** Unlike some synthetic math problems that can be solved by external tool invocation (e.g., Python interpreters for computation), DELTA's tasks require synthesizing the *program itself*, demanding genuine algorithmic understanding.
- **Rich reward signal:** Programming environments naturally provide fine-grained feedback via test cases, which DELTA leverages for graded rewards – a critical enabler for RL in sparse reward environments.

Furthermore, the paper investigates the phenomenon of **grokking** (Power et al., 2022) within an RL context. Grokking, traditionally observed in supervised learning on small algorithmic datasets, describes a delayed generalization where a model first memorizes the training data and only much later, after prolonged training, suddenly generalizes to unseen data. DELTA explores if and how this phase transition occurs during RL fine-tuning of LLMs on difficult reasoning tasks, a largely unexplored area.

By providing a controlled testbed and demonstrating that RL can induce grokking and unlock previously unsolvable tasks, this work makes a significant contribution to understanding the true potential of RL for pushing the boundaries of LLM capabilities, moving beyond simple capability refinement towards genuine algorithmic discovery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to rigorously investigate whether Reinforcement Learning (RL) can empower Large Language Models (LLMs) to acquire and transfer genuinely new reasoning strategies, rather than merely refining existing ones. This objective is broken down into two core questions, which DELTA is specifically designed to probe:

1. **Learnability:** Can LLMs, through RL, solve problem families that pretrained models, even with extensive attempts (`pass@K=0`), demonstrably fail to solve? This question directly challenges the skeptical view that RL cannot instill capabilities beyond what is already latent in the base model. The motivation here is to ascertain if RL can act as a catalyst for *discovery* of new algorithms or problem-solving procedures.
2. **Transferability (or Generalization):** If learnability is achieved, can these newly acquired skills systematically transfer to out-of-distribution (OOD) test sets? This probes whether the learned strategies are robust and generalizable, or merely memorized patterns specific to the training distribution. The motivation is to understand the scope and limits of RL-driven skill acquisition and to ensure that any "new" capability is genuinely algorithmic and transferable.

The authors are motivated by several critical gaps in existing research and benchmarks:
- **Lack of Controlled Environments:** Current open-ended benchmarks often mix diverse problem types and difficulties, making it difficult to isolate specific reasoning skills or definitively attribute performance gains to novel acquisition versus mere sharpening of existing skills.
- **Difficulty in Probing True Novelty:** Without truly OOD problems that demand *new* strategies (not just tool invocation or variations of pre-existing patterns), it's hard to discern if LLMs are learning genuinely new algorithms.
- **Sparse Rewards in Hard Tasks:** For very hard problems where LLMs consistently fail, standard RL with binary (pass/fail) rewards struggles due to an absence of positive learning signals, making it difficult for the model to initiate learning.
- **Understanding "Grokking":** The phenomenon of grokking, where generalization occurs abruptly after prolonged training, has been observed in supervised learning but is under-explored in the context of RL on complex reasoning tasks, prompting a need to investigate if and how it manifests.

By introducing DELTA, a benchmark with templated problem generators, custom syntax for OOD tasks (Manufactoria), and richly graded reward signals, the authors aim to provide a "clean testbed" to address these motivations. This allows for precise control over problem distributions, systematic testing of generalization axes, and the ability to detect phase transitions in learning, ultimately seeking to clarify the debate on RL's role in advancing LLM reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around the design and application of DELTA, a novel, controlled benchmark, coupled with specific RL training strategies.

#### 3.1. DELTA Benchmark Design

DELTA consists of synthetic programming problem families designed to isolate reasoning skills and allow for precise control over difficulty and distribution. It includes four main scopes:

- **Manufactoria (Out-of-Distribution Problems for Learnability Study):**
 - **Nature:** Re-formalization of a classic Flash game into a custom programmatic syntax, akin to constructing finite-state automata.
 - **Justified OOD-ness:** The syntax is novel, game solutions were previously images (not text), problems are newly synthesized, and puzzle strategies differ qualitatively from conventional programming.
 - **Scalable Difficulty:** 14 families grouped into BASIC, EASY, MEDIUM, HARD tiers, allowing for studies across different model scales (e.g., `HAS` family, where Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 initially achieves 0% pass@128).
- **BouncingSim (2D Simulation Programming Tasks for Generalization Study):**
 - **Nature:** Requires synthesizing a program to simulate elastic collisions of polygonal objects in polygonal containers and predict their state.
 - **Task Design:** Verifiable (scored against an oracle), synthesizable (generated from configurable parameters), composable (single-skill families like `ROT OBJ` and `ROT BOX` can be combined), and difficulty-controlled (BASIC to EXTREME tiers by varying geometry, speeds, gravity, object count).
 - **Generalization Axes:** Designed to explicitly align with Boden's creativity typology:
 - **Exploratory:** Extend known skills within a family (e.g., harder container shapes/dynamics).
 - **Compositional:** Combine previously separate skills (e.g., `ROT BOX` + `ROT OBJ`).
 - **Transformative:** Discover unconventional solutions or handle qualitatively new dynamics (e.g., perfectly periodic bouncing trajectories based on specific initial conditions).
- **Competition Coding Problem Families:**
 - **Nature:** Algorithmic problems sharing a core algorithm (e.g., Mo’s algorithm, Segment Tree Decomposition, CDQ Divide-and-Conquer, Meet-in-the-Middle, Square Root Decomposition).
 - **Synthesis Pipeline:** Gathers seed tasks, perturbs contexts (LLM-based narrative surface changes), and uses expert-provided solution strategies and brute-force verification to ensure consistency. Includes background rewrites for OOD samples while preserving core logic.
- **LEAN (Formalized Mathematics):**
 - **Nature:** Four families (algebra, number theory, inequality, geometry) sourced from existing Lean formalizations.
 - **Variant Generation:** Uses algebraic, compositional, and functional transformations to create variants that preserve the core reasoning skill but diversify surface forms, ensuring models rely on substantive reasoning.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Approach

The paper uses GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization, a variant of PPO) within the Open-Instruct framework for fine-tuning LLMs. The key methodological innovation in RL training for "pass@K=0" tasks is the **staged reward regime**:

- **Addressing Sparse Rewards:** For tasks where initial success is zero, direct optimization with a binary (full-pass) reward fails due to a lack of gradient signal.
- **Staged Warm-up with Dense Rewards:**
 - **Phase 1 (Warm-up):** The model is initially trained using a fine-grained **per-test pass rate** as the reward signal (a continuous value in). This provides dense feedback, guiding exploration towards regions where partial solutions are possible.
 - **Phase 2 (Convergence):** After the warm-up, training transitions to a binary **full-pass reward** (1 for perfect solution, 0 otherwise). This sharpens the learned partial strategies into exact, verifiable solutions.
- **Accelerating Grokking:** The authors explored additional strategies:
 - **Experience Replay:** Logging and re-inserting successful reasoning traces into future rollouts to alleviate reward sparsity.
 - **Feedback-in-the-loop:** Directly injecting explicit failure feedback (e.g., failed test cases) into the generation process to guide corrections.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Investigating if warm-up effects can be achieved by training on structurally related easier problem families before moving to harder target tasks.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

Evaluation involves measuring the full pass rate (fraction of correctly synthesized programs) for RL-trained models on various test sets (in-distribution, explorative OOD, compositional OOD, transformative OOD). Each score is averaged over multiple runs (4 runs) to ensure robustness. The reference model used is Qwen3-4B-Instruct.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a systematic and controlled investigation into the fundamental capabilities of LLMs under RL, specifically targeting the acquisition and generalization of novel algorithmic reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, most notably the demonstration of an RL-driven "grokking" phase transition and a nuanced understanding of generalization across different axes.

#### 4.1. RL-Driven Grokking and Learnability

- **Grokking Phase Transition:** The most striking finding is the observation of a "grokking phase transition" in RL training. On hard problems (e.g., Manufactoria-HAS) where the reference model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) initially achieved `pass@K=0` (zero success even with 128 attempts), standard RL with binary rewards failed to learn. However, with the **staged training regime** (warm-up with per-test pass rate, then switch to binary full-pass reward), the models exhibited a clear pattern:
 1. **Extended Exploration Phase:** A long period (e.g., ~450 steps) with near-zero full-pass reward.
 2. **Abrupt Grokking Moment:** A sudden, rapid climb to near-perfect accuracy (e.g., 100% full pass rate on Manufactoria-HAS).
 3. **Convergence Phase:** The model then consistently reinforces and sharpens the newly discovered strategy.
- **Discovery Beyond Base Model Limits:** This grokking phenomenon provides strong evidence that RL is not limited to merely sharpening existing abilities. It *can* enable LLMs to discover genuinely new procedural strategies, unlocking capabilities that were absent in the base model and previously unsolvable. This was consistently observed across different model families (Qwen3-4B-Instruct, Nemotron-14B) and problem scopes (Manufactoria, BouncingSim).
- **Role of Warm-up:** The per-test pass rate warm-up phase was crucial. It provided initial learning signals to guide exploration out of the "all-zero" reward region, pushing the model towards states where full solutions became reachable. Even for tasks with a small non-zero `pass@K` (ϵ > 0), warm-up improved stability and speed of convergence.
- **Limitations of Warm-up:** Not all hard problems were unlocked by warm-up. For instance, on the harder Manufactoria-PREPEND family, the model remained stuck at near-zero full-pass rate even with per-test rewards, indicating limits to its universal applicability.

#### 4.2. Accelerating Grokking

- **Experience Replay:** Helped models grok at an earlier stage by re-inserting successful traces, though convergence speed was sometimes slower than the baseline GRPO.
- **Feedback-in-the-loop:** Expedited the grokking moment but introduced training instability due to off-policy injection of feedback tokens, often leading to models persisting in incorrect solutions despite explicit feedback.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Demonstrated mixed results. A curriculum with structurally compatible tasks (e.g., REGEX to HAS in Manufactoria) led to successful transfer and mastery. However, a curriculum with structurally incompatible tasks (e.g., COMPR to HAS) failed to transfer, plateauing at low performance. This highlights that effective curricula require structural alignment, whereas dense reward warm-up is more broadly applicable.

#### 4.3. Generalization Study (on BouncingSim)

RL-trained models showed varied transferability across the three generalization axes:
- **Exploratory Generalization:** Performance was strong on in-distribution (Basic level, 70-85%) and carried over well to moderately harder OOD variants (Easy: 50-75%). However, gains diminished significantly with increasing difficulty (Medium: 15-50%, Hard: single digits), indicating limits to extending known skills.
- **Compositional Generalization:** Models showed surprising skill integration. Unseen combinations of skills (e.g., ROT BOX + MOV BOX, MOV BOX + GRAVITY, MULTI BOX + MULTI OBJ) achieved high full-pass rates (60-70%), a contrast to weaker compositional transfer reported in previous math reasoning studies. This suggests that coding tasks might be more amenable to structural composition (merging simulation modules) than symbolic math tasks which require inventing new reasoning steps.
- **Transformative Generalization:** This proved to be the most challenging. Models remained near zero on qualitatively novel dynamics (e.g., perfectly periodic or degenerate trajectories), which demand the discovery of new invariants or fundamentally different solution schemas. This aligns with persistent difficulties in transformative math generalization.

**In summary:** RL, when appropriately configured with staged rewards, can induce grokking and enable LLMs to discover new algorithmic strategies for previously unsolvable problems. These learned skills generalize robustly to parametric shifts and recomposed tasks but struggle significantly with tasks requiring qualitatively novel solution schemas or "schema creation."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "RL Grokking Recipe" paper makes several significant contributions and carries substantial potential impact for the future of LLM research and development:

#### 5.1. Resolving the RL-on-LLM Debate

The most profound impact is in providing clear, empirical evidence that RL can indeed enable LLMs to **discover genuinely new reasoning strategies**, moving beyond merely sharpening existing, latent skills. The demonstration of grokking, where an LLM transitions from `pass@K=0` to near-perfect mastery on previously unsolvable tasks, fundamentally challenges the skeptical view of RL's capabilities. This finding suggests that RL can act as a true "unlocking" mechanism for LLM intelligence, rather than just a fine-tuning tool.

#### 5.2. Introducing DELTA as a Controlled Research Testbed

The creation of DELTA—with its unique OOD Manufactoria domain, verifiable BouncingSim, and systematically perturbed Competitive Coding and LEAN families—is a major contribution. DELTA provides a meticulously controlled environment for studying learnability and generalization. This allows researchers to:
- **Isolate Reasoning Skills:** Attribute performance gains to specific skill acquisition rather than confounding factors.
- **Systematically Probe Generalization:** Test different axes of generalization (exploratory, compositional, transformative) with precise OOD controls.
- **Benchmark Novelty:** Use Manufactoria as a truly novel, un-pretrained domain for "apples-to-apples" comparisons of LLM capabilities on tasks requiring fresh algorithmic insight.
This benchmark will be invaluable for future research in understanding the limits and potential of LLMs.

#### 5.3. Highlighting the Importance of Training Recipes

The research underscores that "how we train is as important as what we train." The staged reward mechanism (dense to binary rewards) is a critical discovery for training LLMs on hard, sparse-reward tasks. This insight has immediate practical implications for designing more effective RL fine-tuning pipelines. The investigation into experience replay, feedback-in-the-loop, and curriculum learning further elucidates the nuances of accelerating and stabilizing learning in these challenging regimes.

#### 5.4. Guiding Future Research Directions

The paper explicitly advocates for:
- **Focusing on the "Hard Frontier":** Encouraging future benchmarks to explicitly isolate and track truly hard instances (`pass@K=0`) rather than obscuring progress with aggregate metrics over mixed-difficulty pools. This ensures that genuine advancements in novel reasoning are properly recognized.
- **Extending Fine-Grained Feedback:** The success of fine-grained, verifiable feedback in coding suggests that similar principles can be applied to other reasoning-heavy domains like math, science, and formal logic. Developing rubric-based scoring, stepwise checkers, or theorem-prover verification could unlock similar learnability gains in these areas.
- **Understanding Transformative Generalization:** While compositional generalization shows promise in coding, transformative generalization remains a significant challenge. This points to a crucial frontier for AI research: enabling models to discover qualitatively new solution schemas, which is a hallmark of genuine creativity.

In conclusion, "RL Grokking Recipe" shifts the narrative regarding RL's power in LLMs. It not only demonstrates a path to unlocking previously inaccessible algorithmic skills but also provides the tools and insights necessary for the community to systematically explore and extend these capabilities, moving closer to LLMs that can truly discover and generalize novel intelligence.
