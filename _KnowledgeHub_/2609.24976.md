# DexTacWAM: A Visuo-Tactile World-Action Model for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DexTacWAM is a visuo-tactile world-action model for dexterous hands. It encodes each fingertip's tactile map with a frozen pretrained visual VAE, fuses five fingers into one hand latent via a finger- and pose-aware compressor, and co-denoises tactile and visual latents in a video diffusion world model feeding an action expert. On six real bimanual tasks it averages 70.6 versus 38.0 for the best baseline.

## Problem

- World-action models and VLA policies are largely vision-centric, modeling visible scene evolution but not hidden contact, slip, and grasp-stability states that decide contact-rich dexterous success.
- Existing visuo-tactile world models such as VTAM, VT-WM, and OmniVTA mostly target parallel grippers with localized tactile signals, leaving distributed, pose-dependent multi-finger contact dynamics unmodeled.
- Tactile policies like ViTacFormer and RDP treat touch as a policy input or auxiliary forecast rather than jointly predicted world state, and scarce tactile data makes tactile pretraining costly.

## Method

- **Finger- and pose-aware tactile compressor**: fingertip maps pass a 1x1 grayscale-to-RGB adapter and frozen visual ==VAE==; ==self-attention pooling== with finger embeddings, pose-conditioned hand query, and ==space-time attention== fuses five fingers into one hand latent.
- **Visuo-bimanual tactile world modeling** via continual vision-to-touch learning: hand tactile latents are appended as extra views in an unchanged video ==DiT==, finetuned on roughly 100 demonstrations with a modality-split ==flow-matching== loss, no tactile midtraining.
- **Per-modality K/V RMS-normalized action expert**: parameter-free ==RMSNorm== separately normalizes visual and tactile keys/values before shared cross-attention; a single ==flow-matching== loss supervises per-finger force, relative end-effector pose, hand targets, and proprioceptive state.

## Results

- Best on all six real-robot tasks (20 trials each), averaging **70.6** versus **38.0** for RDP; Tongs **60** versus at most **10** for every baseline; pooled binary success **68.8%** versus **30.0%**, p=**1.6e-6**.
- Replacing tactile world modeling with direct tactile conditioning, keeping the same encoder, action expert, and data, drops the four-task mean from **74.7** to **26.6**; removing per-modality K/V normalization prevents convergence.
- Compressor retains **89.4%** of pre-fusion contact recall versus **62.9%** for cross-attention pooling; compression yields **2.26x** faster training, **1.29x** faster inference; tactile forecasts hit **0.561x** copy-last-frame error; visual PSNR within **0.5 dB** of vision-only.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting touch as part of the world state beats feeding touch to the policy: identical tactile signals help far less when consumed as input than when forecast.
> - Image-like tactile maps can pass through a frozen pretrained visual VAE and pretrained video backbone, so abundant vision priors bootstrap touch from hours of data without hurting visual prediction.
> - Dense visual and sparse tactile tokens need separate K/V normalization before shared cross-attention; without this parameter-free fix the action expert never converged, a caution for any multimodal policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. When sight cannot tell whether the grasp is secure

A bimanual robot closing tongs around a cherry tomato faces a problem that cameras alone cannot resolve: the tool hides the decisive grasp state, and too little force slips while too much can crush the tomato. DexTacWAM addresses this by letting touch become part of the robot's predicted future rather than merely another current input—each fingertip's evolving contact state enters a joint visuo-tactile world model, and its predictive features condition a separate action expert. Across six contact-rich tasks on a 22-DoF bimanual platform, this approach averages **70.6%** task score versus **38.0%** for the strongest baseline that receives the same camera and touch signals.

### 2. From ten fingertips to a usable predicted state

Each fingertip supplies a single-channel image of a deformable marker grid; a learned grayscale-to-RGB adapter feeds a frozen pretrained visual VAE shared across fingers. Finger-specific embeddings preserve identity. For each hand, self-attention pooling combines five finger latents into one hand latent, while hand-pose injection disambiguates contact under different configurations and divided space-time attention models transitions such as slip, regrasping, and sustained wiping. Those two hand-level tactile views and the visual stream enter the diffusion world model together; its predictive latents pass in one forward pass to the action expert.

Because visual and tactile features have different scales, the action expert uses per-modality K/V RMS normalization; without it, training did not converge. Compressing ten fingertip streams into two hand latents retains **89.4%** of pre-fusion Recall@9, reduces training iteration time **2.26×** (3.87 to 1.71 s/iteration), and reduces measured inference latency **1.29×** (363.0 to 281.6 ms per chunk).

Five fingertip latent streams per hand receive finger-specific positional embeddings; a hand query plus pose embedding drives 5:1 self-attention pooling; TimeSformer-style temporal refinement produces one fused latent per hand; pre- and post-fusion tactile-flow decoders supervise contact retention. The lower section shows visual and tactile encoders feeding the visuo-tactile world model, whose predictive features feed the action expert. Force enters supervision during training; the deployed action model acts from predictive tactile representations, not direct force input.

### 3. Prediction matters, not just access

The nearest sensor-access comparison is RDP, which receives the same ten fingertip 6-D wrench signals but consumes touch as a downstream policy input rather than modeling contact evolution. RDP reaches **38.0%** on average; DexTacWAM reaches **70.6%**. The Tongs task sharpens the gap most: **60.0% versus 10.0%**, where every camera-driven baseline scores at or below 10%.

To isolate the contribution of predictive modeling—holding the tactile encoder, observations, action expert, action space, training data, and architecture fixed—an ablation replaces tactile prediction with direct conditioning on encoded tactile features. This lowers the four-task mean (Cube Place, Handover, Wipe, and Tongs) from **74.7% to 26.6%**.

A held-out prediction check supports the same direction: on tactile futures, latent cosine similarity is 0.967, NMSE is 0.063, and flow-space precision/recall/F1 are 0.749/0.725/0.737. Relative to copying the last tactile frame, the model's error is 0.561×, supporting prediction of contact evolution rather than merely exploiting temporal smoothness.

Six time columns (15.0–38.2 s) show four rows: visual ground truth, visual prediction, tactile ground truth, tactile prediction. The wiping scene progresses through approach, contact, wiping, and release; the predicted tactile deformation emerges, shifts, and fades in step with the visual interaction instead of remaining a static copy. The tactile panels show right-index shear magnitude on a shared color scale.

### 4. Predict contact, then act

The robot does not need sight alone to guess whether a hidden grasp is stable: it learns a compact, finger-aware tactile state and predicts how that state changes. These results are demonstrated on one Sharpa sensing platform with success-only task data; transfer to other sensing principles, embodiments, or failure-recovery settings is untested.
