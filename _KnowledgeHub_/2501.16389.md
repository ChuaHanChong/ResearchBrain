# Bridging the Sim2Real Gap: Vision Encoder Pre-Training for Visuomotor Policy Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> An offline framework quantifies the suitability of pre-trained vision encoders for Sim2Real visuomotor policy transfer using Domain Invariance and Action Scores. The evaluation of 23 encoders revealed that models pre-trained on manipulation-specific data and CNN architectures generally achieve higher performance in bridging the simulation-to-reality gap.

## Problem

- The "Sim2Real gap," a distribution shift between simulated and real data, hinders the effective deployment of simulation-trained policies in physical environments.
- Acquiring large-scale robotic datasets from physical robots is often impractical and poses safety concerns, making simulation a necessary but challenging alternative.
- A lack of reliable, offline methods to evaluate the quality of visual representations from pre-trained vision encoders before costly real-world robot experiments.

## Method

- Developed an offline framework to evaluate pre-trained vision encoders for Sim2Real transfer, introducing two quantitative metrics: ==Domain Invariance Score (DIS)== and ==Action Score (AS)==.
- **DIS** measures how well an encoder's embeddings align between simulated and real images, while **AS** quantifies its ability to extract task-relevant information for linear action inference.
- Employed ==Grad-CAM== saliency maps as a qualitative tool to visually validate if high-performing encoders attend to task-relevant features consistently across both simulated and real domains.

## Results

- MCR (encoder 8), a CNN pre-trained on robotic manipulation data, achieved the highest performance across both Domain Invariance Score and Action Score.
- Encoders pre-trained on manipulation-relevant datasets consistently demonstrated higher Action Scores and often higher Domain Invariance Scores compared to those pre-trained on generic datasets.
- Qualitative analysis with Grad-CAM showed high-performing encoders like MCR consistently focusing on task-relevant elements (e.g., end-effector, objects) in both domains, unlike low-performing encoders like DinoV2-B.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The relevance of pre-training data is paramount for effective Sim2Real transfer, with manipulation-specific datasets consistently yielding better performance than general-purpose ones.
> - CNN architectures generally exhibit stronger domain invariance and robustness to Sim2Real distribution shifts compared to Vision Transformers, indicating architectural biases can play a crucial role.
> - Model capacity (parameter count) does not directly correlate with improved Sim2Real performance, suggesting that the quality and relevance of pre-training are more important than sheer scale.
