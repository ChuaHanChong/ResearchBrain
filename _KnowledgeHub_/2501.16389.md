# Bridging the Sim2Real Gap: Vision Encoder Pre-Training for Visuomotor Policy Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> An offline framework quantifies the suitability of pre-trained vision encoders for Sim2Real visuomotor policy transfer using Domain Invariance and Action Scores. The evaluation of 23 encoders revealed that models pre-trained on manipulation-specific data and CNN architectures generally achieve higher performance in bridging the simulation-to-reality gap.

## Problem

- The "Sim2Real gap," a distribution shift between simulated and real data, hinders the effective deployment of simulation-trained policies in physical environments.
- Acquiring large-scale robotic datasets from physical robots is often impractical and poses safety concerns, making simulation a necessary but challenging alternative.
- A lack of reliable, offline methods to evaluate the quality of visual representations from pre-trained vision encoders before costly real-world robot experiments.

## Method

- Developed an offline framework to evaluate pre-trained vision encoders for Sim2Real transfer, introducing two quantitative metrics: ==Domain Invariance Score (DIS)== and ==Action Score (AS)==.
- **DIS** measures how well an encoder's embeddings align between simulated and real images, while **AS** quantifies its ability to extract task-relevant information for linear action inference.
- Employed ==Grad-CAM== saliency maps as a qualitative tool to visually validate if high-performing encoders attend to task-relevant features consistently across both simulated and real domains.

## Results

- MCR (encoder 8), a CNN pre-trained on robotic manipulation data, achieved the highest performance across both Domain Invariance Score and Action Score.
- Encoders pre-trained on manipulation-relevant datasets consistently demonstrated higher Action Scores and often higher Domain Invariance Scores compared to those pre-trained on generic datasets.
- Qualitative analysis with Grad-CAM showed high-performing encoders like MCR consistently focusing on task-relevant elements (e.g., end-effector, objects) in both domains, unlike low-performing encoders like DinoV2-B.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The relevance of pre-training data is paramount for effective Sim2Real transfer, with manipulation-specific datasets consistently yielding better performance than general-purpose ones.
> - CNN architectures generally exhibit stronger domain invariance and robustness to Sim2Real distribution shifts compared to Vision Transformers, indicating architectural biases can play a crucial role.
> - Model capacity (parameter count) does not directly correlate with improved Sim2Real performance, suggesting that the quality and relevance of pre-training are more important than sheer scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of visuomotor robotic policies, which enable robots to perform tasks using only visual information, is a critical area in robotics. Neural networks and machine learning have advanced this field, but the acquisition of large-scale datasets from physical robots is often impractical and presents safety concerns. Simulation offers a scalable alternative, allowing for rapid generation of extensive training data.

However, a fundamental challenge arises when policies trained in simulation are deployed in real-world environments. This challenge, known as the "Sim2Real gap," is a distribution shift between simulated and real data, which can lead to poor performance of simulation-trained policies in physical settings. Prior research has indicated that pre-trained vision encoders can mitigate this Sim2Real gap by extracting task-relevant features from images. A central outstanding challenge in this domain is the ability to evaluate the quality of these visual representations offline, without the need for expensive and time-consuming physical robot experiments.

Existing efforts in the broader landscape include comprehensive investigations into pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) for downstream robotic tasks. For instance, Silwal et al. demonstrated that PVRs improve Sim2Real transfer in specific navigation tasks. Burns et al. further explored PVRs in robotic manipulation, identifying that models pre-trained on manipulation-relevant data and exhibiting emergent segmentation capabilities tend to generalize better under distribution shifts, which are directly pertinent to the Sim2Real problem. Beyond PVRs, custom frameworks like Maniwhere have been developed to enhance generalization across visual disturbances, using multi-view representation learning and reinforcement learning to improve Sim2Real transfer in manipulation tasks.

This current work positions itself by directly addressing the need for an offline evaluation framework. It builds upon the understanding that pre-trained encoders can aid Sim2Real transfer by proposing a systematic method to quantify an encoder's suitability for this purpose. The paper contributes by developing quantitative metrics and applying them to a diverse and extensive collection of 23 vision encoders, which is noted as a larger number than typically examined in this specific field, thus offering a broad comparative analysis that informs the selection and development of visual representations for robust Sim2Real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent Sim2Real gap, which hinders the deployment of simulation-trained visuomotor policies in real-world robotics. While simulation offers significant advantages in data generation and training efficiency, the inherent differences between simulated and real environments create a distribution shift that reduces the effectiveness of policies when transferred. A critical challenge identified is the lack of a reliable method to evaluate the quality of visual representations generated by pre-trained vision encoders offline, prior to resource-intensive real-world robot rollouts.

Against this background, the key objectives of this study are multifaceted:

1. **Develop an Offline Evaluation Framework:** The core objective is to propose and implement a systematic framework for evaluating the performance of pre-trained vision encoders in the context of Sim2Real transfer for visuomotor policies. This framework is designed to assess an encoder's ability to bridge the Sim2Real gap without requiring direct deployment on physical robots.

2. **Define and Implement Quantitative Metrics:** To operationalize the evaluation, the study aims to introduce two specific quantitative metrics:
 - **Domain Invariance Score (DIS):** This metric is designed to measure how well the embeddings generated by an encoder align between simulated and real-world images. The motivation is that an encoder effective in bridging the Sim2Real gap should produce similar representations for semantically equivalent scenes, regardless of whether they originate from simulation or reality, thereby exhibiting domain invariance.
 - **Action Score (AS):** This metric evaluates the degree to which an encoder's embeddings capture task-relevant information necessary for robot control. The underlying motivation is that effective encoders must extract features pertinent to inferring robot actions (e.g., object positions, end-effector states) to facilitate successful policy transfer. The AS uses linear probing to assess the linear recoverability of actions from the learned representations.

3. **Incorporate Qualitative Validation:** The research aims to complement the quantitative metrics with a qualitative validation tool, specifically Grad-CAM saliency maps. The motivation here is to provide visual interpretability, allowing researchers to observe if high-performing encoders attend to task-relevant features consistently across both simulated and real domains, thereby corroborating the insights from DIS and AS.

4. **Conduct a Comprehensive Encoder Evaluation:** A significant objective is to assess a diverse collection of 23 pre-trained vision encoders, encompassing various architectures (Convolutional Neural Networks and Vision Transformers), training methodologies (supervised, self-supervised), and pre-training datasets (manipulation-specific and general-purpose). The motivation is to uncover patterns and relationships between encoder attributes (architecture, pre-training data, parameter scale) and their performance in bridging the Sim2Real gap, as measured by DIS and AS.

The overarching hypothesis motivating these objectives is that effective vision encoders for Sim2Real transfer should simultaneously excel at extracting features critical for inferring actions while remaining robust and invariant to task-irrelevant variations commonly found between simulated and real environments (e.g., differences in lighting, textures, or background clutter).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research develops an offline framework to evaluate pre-trained vision encoders for their utility in Sim2Real visuomotor policy transfer. The methodology is structured around quantifying two key properties: domain invariance and action inference capability, using a comprehensive dataset and a diverse set of encoders.

**A. Dataset**
The study utilized a dataset comprising over 50,000 frames of a robot arm performing an assembly task (constructing a small table). This dataset includes images captured from both simulated and real-world environments, with corresponding action and domain labels. This extensive collection allows for assessing encoder generalization across domain shifts, which encompass variations in illumination and object layouts.

**B. Vision Encoders**
A total of 23 pre-trained vision encoders were selected for analysis, representing a range of contemporary visual representation learning approaches. These encoders were categorized based on:
- **Architecture:** Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs).
- **Training Type:** Supervised and Self-supervised learning.
- **Pre-Training Data:** Datasets specific to robotic manipulation (e.g., Ego4D, R3M) and general-purpose datasets (e.g., ImageNet).
Key attributes for each encoder, including embedding dimension, number of parameters, training type, and pre-training data, were documented (Table I in the paper). This diverse selection aimed to provide a broad and representative sampling for comparative analysis.

**C. Generating Embeddings**
Each selected encoder, denoted as a function $f_{\theta}: \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$, processes raw images ($x_i$) of size $240 \times 320$ pixels with three RGB channels, mapping them to a 1-dimensional vector $z_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$. These vectors, termed embeddings, serve as compact, information-rich representations of the image content. To ensure consistency for subsequent analyses, all generated embeddings were normalized across models.

**D. Domain Invariance Score (DIS)**
The DIS quantitatively measures an encoder's domain invariance, reflecting the alignment between embeddings of simulated and real images. The process involves:
1. **Embedding Concatenation and Standardization:** All generated embeddings (from both simulated and real images) are concatenated into a matrix $E \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$. Features are then standardized.
2. **Dimensionality Reduction:** The standardized embeddings are projected to a fixed target dimension $d^*$ using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), yielding $\tilde{E}$. This step addresses varying output dimensions across different encoders, allowing for comparison in a common space.
3. **Min-Max Normalization:** The projected embeddings $\tilde{E}$ undergo per-feature min-max normalization, scaling their components to the range $$, resulting in $\hat{E}$.
4. **Centroid Calculation:** The normalized embeddings $\hat{E}$ are partitioned into simulated ($\hat{E}_{sim}$) and real ($\hat{E}_{real}$) subsets. Centroids ($\mu_{sim}$ and $\mu_{real}$) are then computed for each subset.
5. **Score Calculation:** The DIS is defined as $max(0, 1 - \frac{\|\mu_{real} - \mu_{sim}\|_2}{\sqrt{d^*}})$. A higher DIS indicates a smaller Euclidean distance between the simulated and real embedding centroids, signifying stronger sim-real alignment and thus greater domain invariance.

**E. Action Score (AS)**
The AS quantifies an encoder's ability to extract task-relevant information for action inference. This is achieved using a linear probing technique:
1. **Linear Probe Training:** Given the frozen encoder embeddings ($z_i$) and corresponding ground-truth robot actions ($a_i$), a linear probe $g_{\phi}(z) = Wz + b$ is trained. The probe learns to predict actions from the embeddings by minimizing the mean-squared error on a training split of the dataset.
2. **Validation and Score Calculation:** The trained linear probe is evaluated on a held-out validation split. The AS is calculated as $max(0, 1 - \frac{1}{n_{val} d_a} \sum_{i \in D_{val}} \|a_i - g_{\phi}(z_i)\|_2^2)$, where $n_{val}$ is the number of validation samples and $d_a$ is the action dimension. A higher AS indicates that robot actions are more accurately and linearly recoverable from the encoder's representations, implying greater task informativeness.

**F. Qualitative Understanding with Grad-CAM Saliency Maps**
Grad-CAM saliency maps were employed as a qualitative tool to visualize the regions of an image that each encoder attends to. This served as a supporting check to ensure that the quantitative metrics (DIS and AS) corresponded to interpretable visual behaviors. The expectation was that encoders with high DIS would exhibit similar attention patterns across simulated and real images for task-relevant regions, while encoders with high AS would focus sharply on manipulators and objects crucial for the task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated 23 diverse pre-trained vision encoders using the proposed Domain Invariance Score (DIS) and Action Score (AS). The joint results, presented in a scatter plot (Fig. 4), illustrate each encoder's performance, with DIS on the x-axis and AS on the y-axis. Encoders positioned towards the top-left of the plot indicate a combination of strong domain invariance and high action informativeness. Various visual cues in the plot (marker color for architecture, marker shape for pre-training type, and marker size for parameter count) facilitated the identification of patterns.

**A. Patterns Across Encoders:**

1. **Influence of Pre-training Data:** The pre-training dataset exerted a substantial influence on encoder performance. Encoders pre-trained on manipulation-relevant datasets consistently achieved higher Action Scores. This trend was evident when comparing ResNet baselines, typically trained on general datasets like ImageNet, with their R3M counterparts, which are trained with manipulation-focused self-supervision. R3M models generally demonstrated higher AS and, in most cases, higher DIS values as well, suggesting that specialized pre-training data is beneficial for both action inference and domain invariance. Encoders trained on generic datasets exhibited a wider variance in AS.

2. **Parameter Count:** The raw capacity of the model, as indicated by the parameter count, did not correlate directly with improved performance. Larger encoders did not consistently outperform smaller ones across either the DIS or AS metrics. This suggests that the relevance of the pre-training task to the downstream robotic manipulation problem is a more significant factor than the sheer number of model parameters.

3. **Encoder Type (Architecture):** The type of encoder architecture played a role in performance. Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based models generally achieved higher DIS compared to Vision Transformers (ViTs). This observation implies that traditional convolutional structures may offer greater robustness to the Sim2Real domain shift, potentially due to their inductive biases in processing local features.

**B. Top and Bottom Performing Encoders:**

- **Best-Performing Encoder:** The analysis identified MCR (encoder 8), a CNN pre-trained on robotic manipulation data, as the highest-performing encoder according to the framework. It was located near the top-left corner of the performance plot, indicating both strong alignment of simulated and real embeddings (high DIS) and effective linear action decoding (high AS). This suggests that a combination of a robust architecture (CNN) and task-specific pre-training is effective.
- **Contrast with Low-Performing Encoder:** In contrast, models such as DinoV2-B (encoder 4) scored low on both DIS and AS, representing the lower end of performance in this evaluation.

**C. Qualitative Validation with Grad-CAM:**

Qualitative analysis using Grad-CAM saliency maps supported the quantitative findings:
- **DinoV2-B (Low Performance):** Saliency maps for DinoV2-B showed a lack of clear focus across images from both domains (Fig. 2). This diffuse attention pattern aligned with its low Action Score, indicating that it did not effectively identify task-relevant features.
- **MCR (High Performance):** MCR's saliency maps (Fig. 3) exhibited a sharp and consistent focus on key task elements such as the robot's end-effector and the objects being manipulated, in both simulated and real images. This visual evidence corresponded well with its high scores in both AS and DIS, demonstrating that its representations are both informative for actions and invariant to domain shifts.

These findings collectively underscore DIS and AS as complementary metrics that offer quantitative and interpretable benchmarks for evaluating the quality of vision encoders for Sim2Real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for the offline evaluation of vision encoders, which addresses a significant challenge in Sim2Real robotics: the need to assess representation quality without costly and time-consuming real-world robot experiments. The introduction of the Domain Invariance Score (DIS) and Action Score (AS) provides standardized, quantitative metrics that allow for objective comparison of different encoder models.

The empirical findings from evaluating 23 encoders offer several impactful insights:
- **Importance of Task-Specific Pre-training:** The consistent superior performance of encoders pre-trained on manipulation-relevant datasets highlights the critical role of pre-training data in bridging the Sim2Real gap. This suggests that investment in creating and utilizing specialized robotic datasets for pre-training vision models may yield greater benefits than relying solely on general image datasets or increasing model complexity.
- **Architectural Implications:** The observation that CNN-based models generally demonstrate stronger domain invariance than Vision Transformers provides valuable guidance for model selection in Sim2Real applications, particularly where robustness to domain shift is paramount.
- **Efficiency in Model Selection:** The finding that parameter count does not directly correlate with improved performance suggests that researchers and practitioners might not need to default to the largest available models, potentially enabling the use of more computationally efficient encoders without compromising transferability.

The framework's ability to offer a quantitative, interpretable benchmark of Sim2Real encoder quality has several potential impacts:
- **Accelerated Research and Development:** By providing an offline evaluation method, the framework can significantly accelerate the iterative process of designing, training, and selecting vision encoders for robotic tasks. This could reduce the dependence on physical robot experiments at early stages of research.
- **Informed Encoder Selection:** Practitioners can use DIS and AS to make informed decisions about which pre-trained encoders are most suitable for specific Sim2Real robotic manipulation tasks, potentially improving the success rate of policy transfer to the real world.
- **Foundation for Benchmarking:** The metrics could form the basis of an open-access benchmark or online database, fostering community-wide comparisons of architectures, training strategies, and datasets. Such a resource would streamline progress toward developing more robust visual encoders for embodied intelligence.
- **Development of Integrated Tools:** The potential for incorporating these metrics into a reusable software library could allow for the computation of a standardized "Sim2Real score" for any encoder, further democratizing the evaluation process.
- **Guidance for Future Encoder Design:** The identified patterns provide concrete directions for the development of new vision encoder architectures and pre-training strategies that are inherently more robust to the Sim2Real distribution shift.

Ultimately, by bridging the analytical gap between simulation-trained policies and real-world deployment, this work contributes to the broader goal of enabling robots to reliably function in unstructured environments from visual input alone, which has the potential to transform various industries and aspects of daily life. The next step of integrating these offline scores with downstream policy transfer studies on real robots will be crucial to further validate and calibrate the predictive power of this framework.
