# FUDOKI: Discrete Flow-based Unified Understanding and Generation via Kinetic-Optimal Velocities

> [!summary] Summary
> FUDOKI, developed by The University of Hong Kong and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab, introduces a unified multimodal model based on discrete flow matching with kinetic optimal velocities, enabling continuous self-correction and richer bidirectional context for both visual understanding and generation. The model achieves competitive performance on various benchmarks, demonstrating improved fidelity in image generation and more robust reasoning in understanding tasks compared to prior autoregressive and mask-based approaches.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), primarily based on autoregressive (AR) architectures, suffer from rigid raster-scan generation order, unidirectional context, and difficulties in self-correction or complex reasoning.
- Discrete generative models, while promising, often rely on masking-based corruption processes that prevent modification of tokens once unmasked, hindering iterative refinement.
- Training large, novel MLLM architectures from scratch is computationally expensive and resource-intensive.

## Method

- Introduces **FUDOKI**, a unified multimodal model built purely on ==discrete flow matching== (DFM) that learns a continuous transformation from a source to target distribution in discrete space.
- Leverages metric-induced probability paths and kinetic optimal velocities to define a semantically meaningful corruption process that enables iterative refinement and bidirectional context integration.
- Employs a two-stage adaptive transfer strategy, initializing from pre-trained autoregressive MLLMs (Janus-1.5B) to efficiently adapt the model to the DFM paradigm.

## Results

- FUDOKI achieved a competitive **0.77** overall score on the GenEval benchmark for visual generation and strong performance on DPG-Bench, demonstrating superior text-image alignment and visual fidelity compared to AR and mask-based baselines.
- The model scored 1485.4 on MME-P and 73.9 on MMB, showing robust multimodal understanding capabilities competitive with or surpassing AR-based MLLMs of similar or larger scale.
- FUDOKI qualitatively demonstrated self-correction in reasoning processes and improved multi-step task completion, with test-time inference scaling further boosting GenEval to **0.88** and MM-Vet to 55.5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete Flow Matching with kinetic optimal velocities enables iterative refinement and continuous self-correction in MLLMs, addressing limitations of autoregressive and masking-based models.
> - Pre-training from autoregressive MLLMs can efficiently initialize and accelerate the training of discrete flow-based models, making large-scale development more feasible.
> - Explicit time embedding layers are not always necessary, as the model can implicitly infer timesteps from metric-induced corrupted inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed a paradigm shift with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs), leading to remarkable progress in natural language understanding and generation. A natural progression has been the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate capabilities across different modalities, most notably vision and language. These MLLMs aim to achieve a unified framework for both visual understanding (image-to-text) and image generation (text-to-image), moving closer to the vision of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

Historically, the majority of MLLMs, such as Chameleon, Emu3, LLaVA, InstructBLIP, and the Janus series (Janus, Janus-Pro, Illume), have adopted an **autoregressive (AR) architecture**. This involves processing multimodal tokens sequentially, typically from left to right. While effective for many tasks, AR models inherently face limitations:
- **Raster-scan order:** Imposes a rigid, sequential generation process for images, which might not be optimal for capturing global visual coherence.
- **Restricted reasoning:** Challenges in complex reasoning, future planning, and self-correction, which are crucial for advanced AI applications like embodied AI and autonomous agents.
- **Unidirectional Context:** The causal masking limits the model to only use past tokens, hindering bidirectional information flow.

In parallel, **discrete-space generative models**, including discrete diffusion models and discrete flow models, have emerged as a promising alternative. These models, successful in text generation, protein design, and image synthesis, operate by iteratively refining a fully corrupted sequence in parallel. This allows for richer bidirectional context integration, potentially enhancing prolonged reasoning, and offers flexibility and controllability through their iterative refinement process. Recent works like LLaDA and Dream have shown the scalability of discrete diffusion models.

Within the discrete generative landscape, earlier unified multimodal models like D-DiT and UniDisc primarily focused on **masking-based corruption processes**. While a step away from AR, these models still have limitations, particularly the inability to modify tokens once they are "unmasked," which hinders self-correction.

FUDOKI positions itself at the forefront of this evolving landscape by introducing a **unified multimodal model purely based on discrete flow matching**. This approach represents a significant departure from both the dominant AR paradigm and the more common masking-based discrete diffusion. By leveraging *metric-induced probability paths with kinetic optimal velocities*, FUDOKI aims to overcome the "irreversible unmasking" problem, enabling continuous self-correction and a more semantically meaningful corruption/denoising process. The work also addresses the practical challenge of training large discrete flow models from scratch by proposing an adaptive transition from pre-trained AR-based MLLMs (specifically Janus-1.5B), making it a pragmatic yet innovative contribution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind FUDOKI are multifaceted, driven by a critical assessment of the limitations of prevailing MLLM architectures:

1. **Challenge Autoregressive Dominance:** The primary motivation is to fundamentally challenge the widespread adoption of autoregressive (AR) architectures in MLLMs. The authors contend that AR models, despite their success, impose inherent limitations on future development, particularly in areas requiring complex reasoning, future planning, and self-correction, which are crucial for advanced AGI applications like embodied AI.

2. **Introduce Discrete Flow Matching as a Unified Paradigm:** The paper aims to present Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) as a robust and superior alternative to conventional AR and masking-based discrete diffusion models for unified multimodal understanding and generation. The goal is to demonstrate that DFM can provide a single, coherent framework for both vision and language tasks.

3. **Enhance Generative Process with Kinetic-Optimal Velocities:** A specific objective is to move beyond the limitations of previous masking-based corruption processes (common in discrete diffusion models). FUDOKI leverages "metric-induced probability paths with kinetic optimal velocities." This sophisticated mathematical framework is motivated by the desire to enable:
 - **Iterative Refinement with Self-Correction:** Allowing the model to continuously adjust and correct its outputs during the generation process, analogous to human self-correction.
 - **Richer Bidirectional Context Integration:** Facilitating the flow of information in both directions during generation, which is hypothesized to lead to more coherent and contextually aware outputs.

4. **Mitigate High Training Costs:** Training large-scale multimodal models from scratch, especially with novel architectures, is prohibitively expensive. A practical objective is to develop an efficient training strategy. FUDOKI addresses this by initializing from pre-trained AR-based MLLMs (specifically Janus-1.5B) and adaptively transitioning them to the discrete flow matching paradigm, thereby leveraging existing computational investment.

5. **Achieve Competitive Performance Across Modalities:** The authors are motivated to demonstrate that FUDOKI can achieve performance comparable to, or even surpass, state-of-the-art AR-based MLLMs on both visual understanding and image generation tasks, validating the efficacy of the DFM approach in a unified multimodal setting.

6. **Lay Foundation for Next-Generation MLLMs:** Ultimately, the work aims to position FUDOKI as a foundational model for future unified multimodal systems. Its inherent properties, such as iterative refinement and bidirectional context modeling, are presented as highly suitable for scenarios demanding advanced reasoning and controllability, potentially opening avenues for further enhancement through techniques like reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FUDOKI's methodology hinges on the novel application of Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) to unify visual understanding and generation. The approach can be broken down into three main components: theoretical foundation (DFM), architectural design, and training/inference strategies.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation: Discrete Flow Matching (DFM)

Unlike diffusion models that typically corrupt data with noise and then learn to reverse this process, DFM learns a continuous transformation or "flow" from a simple source distribution to a complex target data distribution in a discrete space.

- **Probability Paths:** FUDOKI defines a family of time-indexed probability distributions, $p_t(x)$, that smoothly transform from a source distribution ($p_0$) to the target data distribution ($p_1$). Crucially, FUDOKI employs **metric-induced probability paths** (Equation 4), which differ from common mask-based mixture paths. These paths are defined using a distance function $d(x_i, x_i^1)$ (e.g., L2-distances between normalized token embeddings). This means that as the flow progresses, tokens semantically similar to the ground-truth token $x_i^1$ are given higher probability, leading to a more meaningful corruption process than simple masking.
- **Kinetic Optimal Velocities:** To simulate the generative process along these paths, FUDOKI utilizes "probability velocities" ($u_t$) that describe the rate of probability change of each token. These velocities are derived by minimizing the kinetic energy of the flow, which ensures a smooth transformation. The key insight is that this kinetic optimal velocity (Equation 5) ensures that probability mass flows from state $z_i$ to $x_i$ only when $x_i$ is semantically closer to the target $x_i^1$ than $z_i$. This directly supports the iterative refinement and self-correction capabilities.

#### 3.2. Architecture Overview

FUDOKI builds upon the existing **Janus-1.5B** architecture, an AR-based MLLM, with key modifications to align with the DFM paradigm:

- **Bidirectional Transformer:** Unlike AR models that use causal masks, FUDOKI adopts a full attention mask, allowing all tokens to attend to each other. This enables the bidirectional context integration inherent to DFM.
- **Output Logit Shifting:** A shifting operation is applied to output logits to inherit the next-token prediction capabilities from the AR-based initialization, facilitating the transfer.
- **Implicit Timestep Inference:** FUDOKI *does not* incorporate explicit time embedding layers. The authors found that the model can implicitly infer timesteps from the corrupted input along the metric-induced probability path, simplifying the architecture and accelerating adaptation.
- **Modality-Specific Processing:**
 - **Visual Understanding (Img2Text):** Uses a semantic encoder (SigLIP) to extract high-dimensional image features, which are then mapped into the LLM input space via an adaptor.
 - **Image Generation (Text2Img):** Employs a pixel encoder and decoder (following LlamaGen) to convert images into discrete tokens. These image token embeddings are transformed into input features via a generation adaptor.
- **Unified Output Heads:** The model has two output heads (text and image) to generate tokens for the respective target modality during inference.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

Training FUDOKI involves a two-stage adaptive transfer process, leveraging the pre-trained Janus-1.5B weights:

1. **Stage 1: Adaptive Relearning (Transformer Fine-tuning):** The primary goal is to quickly adapt the AR-based LLM to the DFM paradigm. Only the transformer parameters are fine-tuned, while other components (semantic encoders, adaptors) are frozen. This stabilizes and accelerates convergence.
2. **Stage 2: Full Model Fine-tuning:** After the initial adaptation, the entire model (including vision components and adaptors) is fine-tuned to enhance overall multimodal understanding and generation performance.

- **Dataset:** Training uses approximately 13M supervised fine-tuning data, split between 9M in-house text-to-image generation data and 4M public image-to-text understanding data (covering OCR, document, chart, math, etc.). This dataset is noted to be smaller than those used by some larger MLLMs (e.g., Chameleon, LWM).
- **Loss Function:** The training objective is the expected cross-entropy loss between the ground-truth target sequence ($x_1$) and the model's predicted categorical distribution ($p_\theta^{1|t}(\cdot | x_t)$). At each step, a time $t$ is sampled, and a noised sequence $x_t$ is generated according to the metric-induced probability path based on $x_1$.

#### 3.4. Inference Procedure

FUDOKI's inference process simulates the continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) using an Euler solver, enabling iterative refinement:

1. **Predict Final State:** The model first predicts the final ground-truth state ($x_i^1$) based on the current noisy state ($x_t$).
2. **Compute Transition Rate:** It then computes the total conditional transition rate ($\lambda_i$) based on the kinetic optimal velocity (Equation 5). This rate indicates the intensity of probability mass flow from the current token $x_i^t$ to other states.
3. **Probabilistic State Update:** A uniform random variable determines whether a token "jumps" to a new state. If a jump occurs, the new token $x_i^{t+h}$ is sampled from states that are semantically closer to the model's predicted final state $x_i^1$, according to the derived velocities.

This iterative process allows FUDOKI to continuously refine and self-correct its predictions. Unlike mask-based discrete diffusion where unmasked tokens are fixed, FUDOKI's tokens can be adjusted at each step, making errors reversible and enhancing the reasoning process, as qualitatively demonstrated in the paper (Fig. 3 and 7).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FUDOKI demonstrates strong performance across both visual understanding and generation tasks, providing compelling evidence for the efficacy of discrete flow matching as a unified multimodal paradigm.

#### 4.1. Visual Generation Performance

- **GenEval Benchmark:** FUDOKI achieved a competitive overall score of **0.77** on the GenEval benchmark, matching or even surpassing state-of-the-art AR-based and diffusion-only models in categories for both generation-only and unified understanding-and-generation. This indicates strong capabilities in accurate multi-object understanding and attribute binding, which are critical for complex visual generation. The authors attribute this to the bidirectional information integration facilitated by discrete flow matching, leading to better layout design in generated images.
- **DPG-Bench:** On the Dense Prompt Graph Benchmark (DPG-Bench), designed for lengthy and complex prompts, FUDOKI showed competitive performance (Overall 83.63), demonstrating its robustness in handling detailed semantic alignments.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons (Figure 6) illustrate that FUDOKI consistently produced images with superior text-image alignment and higher visual fidelity compared to Janus (AR) and D-DiT (mask-based discrete diffusion).

#### 4.2. Multimodal Understanding Performance

- **Diverse Benchmarks:** FUDOKI (1.5B parameters) was evaluated on a comprehensive suite of multimodal understanding benchmarks including POPE, MME-P, SEED, MMB, GQA, MMMU, and MM-Vet.
- **Competitive Results:** The model achieved highly competitive results, often on par with or surpassing several AR-based MLLMs of similar or even larger scale (e.g., Janus-Pro-1B, MobileVLM, LLaVA-Phi). For instance, FUDOKI scored **1485.4 on MME-P** and **73.9 on MMB**, showing robust understanding capabilities.
- **Bidirectional Reasoning:** The strong performance in understanding is attributed to the inherent bidirectional reasoning property of discrete flow matching, which allows for deeper contextual comprehension.
- **Self-Correction in Reasoning:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 7) visually demonstrate FUDOKI's ability to self-correct its reasoning process during answer generation, leading to more coherent and accurate responses, a significant advantage over AR models (which propagate errors) and mask-based diffusion models (which cannot revise unmasked tokens). This is further highlighted by its ability to complete multi-step maze navigation tasks (Figure 9, 10).

#### 4.3. Inference Scaling

- **Significant Performance Gains:** By applying test-time inference scaling techniques (leveraging a judge model to select the best output from multiple candidates), FUDOKI achieved substantial improvements. For generation, its GenEval score increased from 0.77 to **0.88**. For understanding, its MM-Vet score jumped from 38.0 to **55.5**. These results underscore FUDOKI's potential for further enhancement through techniques like reinforcement learning from human feedback.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **AR Initialization:** Initialization from pre-trained AR weights (Janus-1.5B) provided a significant advantage, leading to consistently lower training loss and faster convergence compared to training from scratch. This validates the adaptive transfer strategy.
- **Time-embedding Layers:** The study showed that FUDOKI performed slightly better without explicit time embedding layers, suggesting that the discrete generative model can implicitly infer timesteps from the corrupted input, reducing model complexity.
- **Quality-Speed Trade-off:** FUDOKI demonstrates a favorable trade-off between generation speed (images per minute) and quality (GenEval score) with varying inference timesteps. At optimal timesteps, FUDOKI *outperformed* the AR baseline (Janus-Pro-1B) in both speed and quality. This efficiency is attributed to fewer function evaluations (NFE) and richer bidirectional context modeling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FUDOKI represents a significant stride in the development of unified multimodal AI, offering a compelling alternative to the prevailing autoregressive paradigm. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Paradigm Shift for MLLMs:** FUDOKI challenges the long-dominant autoregressive architecture by demonstrating that discrete flow matching is a highly competitive, if not superior, foundation for unified multimodal models. This work could inspire a broader shift in how next-generation MLLMs are designed, moving towards models that inherently support bidirectional context and iterative refinement.

2. **Enhanced Reasoning and Self-Correction:** The core innovation of using metric-induced probability paths with kinetic optimal velocities directly enables FUDOKI's unique ability for continuous self-correction and richer bidirectional context integration during generation and understanding. This property is crucial for tasks requiring complex reasoning, planning, and robustness to errors, which are often shortcomings of AR models. Its ability to revise tokens during inference, unlike mask-based diffusion, makes it more akin to human thought processes where errors can be rectified.

3. **Improved Generative Quality and Coherence:** By allowing information flow in both directions for visual information and leveraging semantically meaningful transformations, FUDOKI achieves strong performance in visual generation, particularly in handling multi-object understanding and attribute binding for complex prompts. This leads to more accurate and aesthetically pleasing image outputs.

4. **Robust Multimodal Understanding:** FUDOKI's strong performance across diverse visual understanding benchmarks, even with a relatively smaller parameter count (1.5B) compared to some larger AR models, highlights the effectiveness of discrete flow matching for multimodal reasoning. This suggests that the architectural choice itself contributes significantly to performance, not just scale.

5. **Practical Scalability and Efficiency:** The adaptive initialization strategy from pre-trained AR models addresses a major practical hurdle—the high cost of training large new architectures from scratch. This makes the DFM paradigm more accessible for large-scale deployment. Furthermore, the demonstrated favorable speed-quality trade-off during inference (fewer NFEs) suggests greater computational efficiency than AR models for certain applications.

6. **Foundation for Future AI Research:** The paper explicitly positions FUDOKI as a foundation for next-generation unified multimodal models, especially those integrated with reinforcement learning (RL). The significant performance gains observed with test-time inference scaling, inspired by RL techniques, underscore this potential. This opens new avenues for training agents that can reason, plan, and interact with the world in a more robust and self-correcting manner, which is essential for embodied AI, autonomous systems, and advanced AGI.

7. **Broader Applicability:** The unified nature of FUDOKI across understanding and generation, combined with its iterative refinement, could have implications for various critical applications beyond traditional text-to-image/image-to-text, such as interactive design, educational tools, medical diagnostics requiring visual interpretation and generative outputs, and sophisticated human-AI interaction systems.

In conclusion, FUDOKI offers a fresh perspective on unified multimodal modeling, demonstrating that discrete flow matching provides a powerful and efficient framework that addresses key limitations of current AR-based approaches. Its success paves the way for a new generation of multimodal AI systems with enhanced reasoning, controllability, and practical applicability. As with all powerful generative AI, ethical considerations regarding bias, misuse, and content safety will require careful attention as this technology evolves.
