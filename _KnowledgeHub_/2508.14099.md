# Task and Motion Planning for Humanoid Loco-manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an optimization-based Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) framework for humanoid robots that unifies locomotion and manipulation. It generates long-horizon, dynamically feasible loco-manipulation plans by integrating full whole-body dynamics and joint actuation limits, allowing for flexible, acyclic contact planning across all end-effectors.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid loco-manipulation planning methods often produce kinematically possible but dynamically infeasible trajectories due to simplified dynamics or omitted actuation limits.
- Prior Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) frameworks for mobile manipulators or quadrupeds lacked full whole-body dynamics and actuation constraints for humanoids, often relying on predefined locomotion cycles or short-horizon optimizations.
- Treating locomotion and manipulation as separate problems or asymmetrically in planning frameworks limits the robot's ability to perform complex, integrated tasks requiring intricate coordination.

## Method

- A TAMP framework combining symbolic contact planning with a contact-explicit, gradient-based trajectory optimization (TO) solver was developed.
- A unified symbolic representation of contacts for all robot end-effectors (hands, feet) and objects enables flexible, acyclic planning of contact sequences.
- The TO solver integrates a second-order kinematic model, whole-body centroidal momentum dynamics, and joint torque actuation limits to ensure dynamically consistent and executable trajectories.

## Results

- The framework successfully generated complex, long-horizon plans for humanoid robots in tasks like platform climbing and two-box pick-and-place, demonstrating coordinated loco-manipulation.
- Solutions consistently leveraged task-specific contact exploration, such as using a free end-effector for stabilization, showing improved versatility.
- The inclusion of joint actuation limits was shown to be crucial, preventing the generation of dynamically infeasible 'jumping' behaviors and producing realistic, executable motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified, contact-centric representation for symbolic planning enables truly integrated reasoning over both locomotion and manipulation actions.
> - Explicitly incorporating full whole-body dynamics and joint actuation limits in trajectory optimization is essential for generating physically realistic and executable behaviors for unstable humanoid robots.
> - Acyclic planning of contact sequences, rather than relying on predefined cycles, enables the discovery of diverse, opportunistic, and dynamically optimal loco-manipulation strategies.
