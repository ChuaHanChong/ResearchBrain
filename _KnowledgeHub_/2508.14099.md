# Task and Motion Planning for Humanoid Loco-manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an optimization-based Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) framework for humanoid robots that unifies locomotion and manipulation. It generates long-horizon, dynamically feasible loco-manipulation plans by integrating full whole-body dynamics and joint actuation limits, allowing for flexible, acyclic contact planning across all end-effectors.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid loco-manipulation planning methods often produce kinematically possible but dynamically infeasible trajectories due to simplified dynamics or omitted actuation limits.
- Prior Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) frameworks for mobile manipulators or quadrupeds lacked full whole-body dynamics and actuation constraints for humanoids, often relying on predefined locomotion cycles or short-horizon optimizations.
- Treating locomotion and manipulation as separate problems or asymmetrically in planning frameworks limits the robot's ability to perform complex, integrated tasks requiring intricate coordination.

## Method

- A TAMP framework combining symbolic contact planning with a contact-explicit, gradient-based trajectory optimization (TO) solver was developed.
- A unified symbolic representation of contacts for all robot end-effectors (hands, feet) and objects enables flexible, acyclic planning of contact sequences.
- The TO solver integrates a second-order kinematic model, whole-body centroidal momentum dynamics, and joint torque actuation limits to ensure dynamically consistent and executable trajectories.

## Results

- The framework successfully generated complex, long-horizon plans for humanoid robots in tasks like platform climbing and two-box pick-and-place, demonstrating coordinated loco-manipulation.
- Solutions consistently leveraged task-specific contact exploration, such as using a free end-effector for stabilization, showing improved versatility.
- The inclusion of joint actuation limits was shown to be crucial, preventing the generation of dynamically infeasible 'jumping' behaviors and producing realistic, executable motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified, contact-centric representation for symbolic planning enables truly integrated reasoning over both locomotion and manipulation actions.
> - Explicitly incorporating full whole-body dynamics and joint actuation limits in trajectory optimization is essential for generating physically realistic and executable behaviors for unstable humanoid robots.
> - Acyclic planning of contact sequences, rather than relying on predefined cycles, enables the discovery of diverse, opportunistic, and dynamically optimal loco-manipulation strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards creating autonomous agents capable of performing complex tasks in human-centric environments. A critical challenge in this endeavor is "loco-manipulation," the ability of a robot to simultaneously move its base (locomotion) and manipulate objects (manipulation). This problem is particularly acute for humanoid robots, which are inherently unstable and possess many degrees of freedom, making coordinated whole-body movements extremely difficult to plan and control. This paper directly addresses this grand challenge, aiming to bridge significant gaps in existing task and motion planning (TAMP) frameworks for humanoids.

Current approaches to loco-manipulation planning face several limitations:
- **Optimal Control-based Methods:** While capable of generating complex behaviors, these often rely on relaxing complementarity constraints for contacts, leading to non-physical behaviors (e.g., "force at a distance") and susceptibility to poor local minima due to non-convexity. They also struggle with multiple objects and sparse contact points.
- **Classical Approaches:** These often treat manipulation as a disturbance to a predefined locomotion system, failing to consider the two concurrently in a truly integrated manner necessary for complex tasks (e.g., moving a table to stand on it).
- **Learning-based Methods (DRL, Imitation Learning):** Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) is limited to simple tasks and struggles with long-horizon reasoning. Imitation learning requires extensive and costly demonstrations, particularly challenging for unstable systems like humanoids.
- **Logic-Geometric Programming (LGP):** This optimization-based TAMP framework has shown promise for sequential manipulation tasks, particularly for stationary or mobile manipulators. However, a key limitation is the omission of robot dynamics and dynamic patch planning, restricting its use to statically stable scenarios.
- **Quadruped Loco-manipulation:** Recent work extended LGP to quadrupeds, incorporating centroidal dynamics. While impressive, this approach still relied on predefined locomotion cycles, a fixed number of ground contacts, and short-horizon optimizations for tree expansion. Crucially, it did not incorporate actuation limits, using a first-order kinematic model, which limits the diversity and dynamic realism of generated behaviors.

This paper positions itself by tackling these specific shortcomings, particularly for the more challenging humanoid platform. It directly compares its approach to, highlighting key advancements: using a second-order kinematic model with whole-body centroidal momentum dynamics (enabling actuation limits), solving full-horizon trajectory optimization at each tree expansion, unifying locomotion and manipulation symbols for more complex reasoning, and addressing the problem for humanoids (two arms) rather than quadrupeds (single arm). By integrating these elements, this work seeks to provide a more holistic, dynamically consistent, and versatile TAMP solution for humanoid robots, advancing the field towards truly autonomous and capable human-like agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a novel optimization-based Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) framework that unifies planning for both locomotion and manipulation in humanoid robots. The core idea is to achieve this unification through a shared, contact-centric representation, allowing for simultaneous decision-making over task-level goals, contact sequences, and continuous robot motions, all while adhering to the complex physical constraints of whole-body dynamics and robot actuation.

More specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Generate Physically Consistent Loco-Manipulation Behaviors:** The primary motivation stems from the limitations of existing methods that often produce non-physical or dynamically infeasible trajectories. The goal is to generate a broad range of behaviors that are physically sound and executable by a real humanoid robot over long action sequences, requiring complex reasoning.
- **Integrate Whole-Body Dynamics and Actuation Constraints:** Humanoid robots are inherently unstable and require precise control over their entire body. Previous TAMP frameworks often simplified or omitted full robot dynamics and crucial actuation limits. This work aims to incorporate a second-order kinematic model with whole-body centroidal momentum dynamics and joint torque constraints, which is essential for stable and performant dynamic motions on humanoids.
- **Enable Fully Acyclic Planning for All End-Effectors:** Unlike approaches that rely on predefined locomotion cycles or fixed contact patterns, a central objective is to allow for flexible, "acyclic" planning of all end-effector contacts (feet and hands). This increased flexibility is critical for discovering novel, more efficient, or more stable contact sequences, especially in complex, multi-contact scenarios.
- **Unify Locomotion and Manipulation through Shared Contact Representation:** The paper aims to move beyond treating locomotion and manipulation as separate problems or considering manipulation as a disturbance. By defining symbolic actions as "contact mode changes" and grounding high-level planning in low-level contact states, the framework seeks to reason over complex loco-manipulation skills in a truly integrated manner.
- **Solve Integrated TAMP Formulation:** The ultimate goal is to resolve an integrated TAMP formulation for humanoid loco-manipulation that, to the best of their knowledge, is the first to combine fully acyclic planning, whole-body dynamics, and actuation constraints simultaneously. This holistic approach is motivated by the need for humanoids to perform real-world tasks that necessitate intricate coordination between movement and object interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is an optimization-based Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) framework that effectively combines symbolic graph search with continuous, gradient-based trajectory optimization. This iterative approach allows the system to jointly optimize both discrete symbolic variables (task steps, contact modes) and continuous physical variables (robot/object states and controls).

The method operates in two main, iteratively connected components:

**A. Symbolic Contact Planner:**
1. **Unified Symbolic State and Actions:** Unlike prior work that restricted symbols to manipulation, this framework models discrete variables at the level of *contacts*, encompassing both manipulation and non-manipulation contact switches.
 - **Symbolic State (s):** Defined as the set of all active contact pairs between interfaces (robot-environment, robot-object, object-environment, object-object) and their respective contact types (e.g., unilateral point-surface, bilateral patch-to-patch). Interfaces can be robot end-effectors (hands, feet), object surfaces, or static environment patches.
 - **Symbolic Actions (a):** Represent transitions between contact states, involving making new contacts or breaking existing ones. This unification enables reasoning over complex loco-manipulation skills.
2. **Symbolic Planning Search:**
 - Given an initial symbolic state and a goal condition (which can also involve general contact configurations or even be empty for objective maximization), a search graph `G` of possible state transitions is built.
 - The search uses Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to explore this graph, guided by object-centric manipulation logic. Symbolic plans that result in low trajectory cost and constraint violation are rewarded.
 - The planner outputs a candidate sequence of symbolic states and actions (`s_1:K`, `a_1:K-1`).

**B. Contact-Explicit Trajectory Optimization (TO) Solver:**
1. **Optimization Formulation:** For each candidate symbolic plan, the TO solver formulates a large-scale nonlinear program (NLP) to optimize continuous trajectories.
 - **Decision Variables:**
 - **Continuous States (x):** Includes robot states (configuration `q_r`, velocity `v_r`, centroidal momentum `h_r`) and object states (pose `q_o`, spatial velocities `V_o`).
 - **Continuous Controls (u):** Includes robot controls (generalized accelerations `d_v_r`, end-effector contact wrenches `λ_r_ee`) and object controls (spatial accelerations `d_V_o`, object-environment wrenches `W_o_env`).
 - **Symbolic Phase Durations (T_k):** Crucially, the time duration of each contact phase is also a decision variable, optimized alongside the continuous trajectories.
 - **Objective Function:** Minimizes a sum of stage costs (`ϕ`) and a terminal cost (`ϕ_N`), plus a weighted sum of phase durations (`w_T * Σ T_k`).
 - **Constraints:**
 - **Robot Dynamics:** Enforces a second-order kinematic model, centroidal momentum dynamics (`d_h = ...`), and critically, *joint torque actuation limits* (`τ_min ≤ M_a d_v + b_a - Σ J_a^T_c,i λ_i ≤ τ_max`). This is a key differentiator, especially for unstable humanoids.
 - **Object Dynamics:** Includes state integration and twist-wrench dynamics for all manipulated objects.
 - **Contact Constraints:** These are mode-dependent, changing with the symbolic state `s_k`.
 - **Point-to-Patch:** For end-effectors modeled as points (e.g., fingertips), enforces sticking (position/velocity equality), allows in-plane position optimization, prevents limb-to-patch collision, and applies unilateral force constraints (friction cone, non-negative normal force, zero moment).
 - **Patch-to-Patch:** For larger contact surfaces (e.g., feet, object faces), enforces sticking (position/velocity), aligns patch orientations, includes bounded patch constraints, and applies unilateral force/moment constraints (friction cone, torsional friction, normal force/moment limits).
 - **Collision Penalties:** Soft constraints using sigmoid activation functions are introduced to penalize penetration between robot limbs, objects, and the environment.

**C. Solving TAMP Optimization:**
- The overall process repeats for a fixed number of iterations (`N_it`), retaining the trajectory with the lowest cost and constraint violation.
- The continuous optimization uses the Pinocchio library for robot dynamics, CasADi for symbolic differentiation and NLP formulation, and IPOPT as the NLP solver. MuJoCo is used for visualization.

**D. Symbolic Search Pruning:**
To manage the combinatorial explosion of the symbolic search space, several pruning strategies are employed:
- **Infeasible States:** Eliminates trivial or physically impossible states (e.g., self-contact, redundant symmetric contact pairs, static-static contact).
- **Infeasible Transitions:** Prunes transitions where an interface changes its contact partner without first breaking the initial contact.
- **Manipulation Skeletons:** Object-centric manipulation actions (grasp, lift, place, release) are defined as high-level sequences. These "skeletons," often generated by a logic-based planner like PDDL, constrain the symbolic search, pruning transitions that do not conform to these fundamental manipulation patterns. The planner first uses these skeletons to constrain the main manipulation sequence, then fills in details for undecided contacts (e.g., foot placements). This guides the search efficiently while allowing flexibility for non-manipulation contacts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed TAMP framework was demonstrated on two challenging long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks using a humanoid robot model, highlighting its key contributions.

**A. Task Demonstrations:**
1. **Platform Climbing:** The objective was to maximize the robot's base height, with no predefined goal configuration or symbolic state. This task emphasizes the need for full dynamics and actuation constraints, as simple kinematic solutions might suggest unrealistic jumping behaviors. The robot could choose various end-effector contact sequences and manipulation strategies for a 1 kg red box.
2. **Two Box Pick and Place:** The goal was to move a red box (1 kg, bilateral handle grasp) and a green box (1 kg, unilateral patch grasp, bimanual required) from an initial configuration to an empty table. This task tested the framework's ability to sequence complex manipulation actions, select appropriate foot contacts, and handle different grasping modalities.

**B. Performance and Solution Characteristics:**
- **Two Box Pick and Place:** The framework demonstrated efficient problem-solving, expanding the search tree 200 times with an average solve time of 52.3 seconds per expansion. A feasible solution satisfying the goal was found rapidly within 30 iterations, showcasing the effectiveness of the combined symbolic and continuous optimization. Solutions consistently leveraged task-specific contact exploration, for instance, using a "free" end-effector for stabilization during reaching motions (Fig. 4, panel 7).
- **Platform Climbing:** This task proved more challenging due to its continuous objective and the high number of dynamically infeasible paths. However, the embedded whole-body dynamics and actuation constraints allowed the tree search to find dynamically feasible, high-reward sequences. Notably, all high-reward solutions involved the robot actively using the box to climb onto the platform (Fig. 5), demonstrating that the framework effectively explores the solution space for dynamically complex tasks without resorting to unrealistic shortcuts.

**C. Impact of Actuation Constraints:**
- A critical finding was the profound impact of incorporating actuation limits. When the platform climbing task was re-run *without* enforcing torque limits, the solver produced "jumping" behaviors onto the table (Fig. 6). Analysis of torque profiles (Fig. 7, right) revealed significant and infeasible torque violations in such solutions.
- In contrast, solutions generated *with* actuation limits (Fig. 5, Fig. 7 left) were dynamically consistent, realistic, and representative of what a physical humanoid could achieve, highlighting that these constraints are crucial for distinguishing between kinematically possible and dynamically feasible motions.

**D. Acyclic Planning and Generality:**
- The framework explicitly avoids introducing cyclic heuristics for locomotion, resulting in genuinely acyclic, multi-patch contact mode sequences for both the feet and hands (e.g., Fig. 8 for platform climbing). This design choice increases the diversity of achievable behaviors and expands the potential regions for stability, which is particularly vital for inherently unstable humanoid platforms.
- By enforcing whole-body dynamics and actuation limits, the framework eliminates the need for over-constraining heuristics (like quasi-static assumptions, prescribed gait cycles, or restricted environmental regions), enabling a broader range of adaptable behaviors.

In summary, the results validate the paper's main contributions, demonstrating the framework's ability to generate complex, dynamically feasible, and long-horizon loco-manipulation plans for humanoid robots by integrating whole-body dynamics, actuation constraints, and a unified, acyclic contact planning approach. The claim of being the first to achieve this integrated resolution for humanoid loco-manipulation with these specific features appears well-supported by the empirical findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics and motion planning, with profound implications for the capabilities and autonomy of complex robotic systems.

**A. Advancing Humanoid Robot Autonomy:**
By successfully unifying task-level planning, contact sequence generation, and continuous motion optimization under rigorous whole-body dynamic and actuation constraints, this work pushes humanoid robots closer to performing truly complex, multi-stage tasks in unstructured environments. The ability to generate dynamically consistent, long-horizon plans for inherently unstable platforms like humanoids is a critical step towards realizing their potential in real-world applications.

**B. Enhancing Dynamic Feasibility and Realism:**
The explicit incorporation of whole-body dynamics and, crucially, joint actuation limits, dramatically improves the physical realism and executability of the generated trajectories. This directly addresses a major shortcoming of previous TAMP methods, which often produced kinematically possible but dynamically infeasible plans. By ensuring dynamic consistency from the outset, the framework greatly increases the likelihood that these complex behaviors can be successfully transferred and executed on physical humanoid hardware, reducing the gap between simulation and reality.

**C. Increased Versatility and Adaptability:**
The unified symbolic representation of locomotion and manipulation, combined with acyclic contact planning, allows for greater flexibility and versatility in robot behavior. Unlike methods that rely on predefined gait cycles or fixed manipulation strategies, this framework can discover novel, opportunistic, and context-dependent loco-manipulation sequences. This adaptability is vital for robots operating in dynamic, unpredictable environments where pre-programmed behaviors are insufficient. The ability to leverage "extra hand support" for stabilization during reaching, as demonstrated in the pick-and-place task, exemplifies this increased versatility.

**D. Reducing Manual Engineering and Heuristics:**
By providing a holistic, optimization-based framework, the research reduces the reliance on extensive manual engineering of task-specific heuristics, stability criteria, or simplified dynamic models. This makes the system more scalable to new tasks and environments, as it can automatically derive complex behaviors from fundamental physical principles and task goals, rather than requiring human experts to pre-define every intricate interaction.

**E. Foundation for Future Research and Deployment:**
This work lays a robust foundation for several promising future research directions. The authors explicitly state their plan to realize these behaviors in the real world, which would be a compelling validation of the framework's practical utility. Further developments could include:
- **Real-time execution:** Optimizing the solver for faster computation to enable online adaptation or reactive planning.
- **Robustness to uncertainty:** Incorporating perception and feedback control to handle environmental uncertainties and execution errors.
- **Learning from experience:** Integrating learning techniques to further prune the symbolic search space or refine cost functions based on past performance.
- **Human-robot collaboration:** Extending the framework to plan for tasks involving interaction with humans, considering human intent and safety.

In conclusion, this paper provides a significant methodological and practical contribution to humanoid robotics. By enabling the integrated resolution of TAMP with fully acyclic planning, whole-body dynamics, and actuation constraints, it moves the field closer to deploying truly intelligent and agile humanoid robots capable of performing complex, dynamically challenging tasks in diverse real-world scenarios.
