# Scalable and General Whole-Body Control for Cross-Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> XHugWBC is a framework that enables a single whole-body control policy to generalize across diverse humanoid robot embodiments without requiring robot-specific retraining. The policy achieved a 100% survival rate and robust command-tracking across 19 unseen simulated and 7 real-world humanoids, performing at about 85% of specialized controllers' return.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based whole-body controllers require costly, robot-specific retraining due to unique morphology and dynamics, hindering scalability.
- Humanoid robots exhibit high morphological diversity and complex whole-body dynamics, making cross-embodiment generalization challenging.
- Traditional domain randomization often leads to physically inconsistent robot models, causing simulation instability and policy optimization issues.

## Method

- **XHugWBC** introduced ==physics-consistent morphological randomization== that reparameterizes inertial and joint spaces to generate diverse, yet physically plausible, robot embodiments.
- Developed a ==universal cross-embodiment representation== by semantically aligning joint spaces to a fixed dimension and using ==graph-based morphology descriptions== for heterogeneous robots.
- Employed reinforcement learning with specialized policy architectures, specifically ==Transformer-based GCNs==, to capture local kinematic dependencies and global coordination across diverse humanoid designs.

## Results

- The XHugWBC policy demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization with a **100%** survival rate and consistent command-tracking on **19** unseen simulated and **7** real-world humanoid platforms, achieving approximately **85%** of specialist policy performance.
- Fine-tuning the generalist policy significantly improved sample efficiency and accelerated convergence, leading to up to **10%** higher peak performance compared to training specialist controllers from scratch.
- The framework enabled successful zero-shot deployment on seven distinct real-world humanoid robots for robust locomotion and complex loco-manipulation tasks, including object manipulation and door opening, with near-perfect success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-consistent randomization is essential for creating a meaningful distribution of diverse robot embodiments, preventing simulation instability and enabling robust policy learning for complex humanoids.
> - A universal representation combining semantically aligned joint spaces and graph-based encoding allows a single control policy to effectively generalize across humanoids with varied degrees of freedom and kinematic structures.
> - Graph Convolutional Network and Transformer architectures effectively exploit the robot's embodied kinematic topology, leading to more sample-efficient learning and improved generalization compared to simpler neural network models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics, particularly in the domain of humanoid locomotion, has witnessed significant advancements through learning-based whole-body control (WBC). These controllers have enabled legged robots to exhibit agile and robust behaviors that often surpass the capabilities of traditional model-based approaches. However, a prevalent limitation in this area is that most existing learning-based controllers are developed for a specific robot embodiment. This necessitates extensive and costly retraining whenever a new humanoid platform is introduced, as each robot possesses unique morphological structures, kinematic chains, degrees of freedom (DoFs), and dynamic characteristics. This robot-specific training paradigm significantly hinders scalability and efficiency in humanoid robot development and deployment.

Prior research has explored cross-embodiment learning to generalize control policies across different robot designs. Efforts in areas like animation and quadrupedal locomotion have leveraged shared morphological priors, unified state-action representations, or similar dynamics to achieve limited transfer. However, these assumptions frequently break down for humanoid robots, which exhibit substantially greater heterogeneity in kinematic structures, joint orderings, DoFs, and physical properties. Simplifications that have facilitated transfer in quadrupeds, such as reduced dynamic models or relatively homogeneous body morphologies, are not directly applicable to the more complex and diverse whole-body dynamics of humanoids.

This paper addresses the gap of developing a single, robust learned controller capable of generalizing across a wide range of diverse humanoid embodiments without requiring robot-specific retraining. It aims to overcome the challenges posed by highly dynamic contacts, strict stability requirements, and significant morphological variations inherent in humanoid robots. The work positions itself as a step towards more scalable and versatile humanoid control systems, moving beyond the current paradigm of specialized controllers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of current learning-based whole-body controllers for humanoid robots. While these controllers have achieved notable successes in enabling agile and robust behaviors, they typically require dedicated training for each specific robot embodiment. This per-robot training paradigm is characterized by high computational costs, extensive data collection, and significant time investment, making it inefficient and impractical for scaling humanoid robot deployments across diverse platforms. The authors identify this as a critical bottleneck limiting the widespread adoption and advancement of humanoid robotics.

The challenges in achieving cross-embodiment generalization for humanoids are multifaceted:
1. **High Morphological Diversity:** Humanoids vary significantly in their kinematic structures, number of degrees of freedom (DoFs), joint arrangements, and physical dimensions.
2. **Complex Dynamics:** The whole-body dynamics involve intricate interactions, highly dynamic contacts, and strict stability requirements, which are sensitive to morphological changes.
3. **Physical Consistency:** Randomizing robot parameters without ensuring physical consistency can lead to unrealizable models, simulation instability, and degenerate policy optimization, ultimately hindering real-world transfer.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of the XHugWBC framework are:
1. **Develop a Generalist Policy:** To create a single learned control policy capable of robustly generalizing across a wide range of humanoid robot designs after a single training phase, eliminating the need for robot-specific retraining.
2. **Ensure Physics-Consistent Randomization:** To introduce a method for generating diverse and physically meaningful humanoid embodiments during training, ensuring that randomized models remain physically plausible and avoid simulation instability or optimization issues.
3. **Establish Universal Representation:** To design semantically aligned observation and action spaces that can consistently represent diverse humanoid robots, irrespective of their specific kinematic structures or number of actuated joints. This involves mapping robot-specific states into a global joint space and constructing an embodiment graph to encode morphological structure.
4. **Implement Effective Policy Architectures:** To integrate policy architectures that can effectively model and leverage the morphological and dynamical properties of diverse humanoids, capturing both local kinematic dependencies and global coordination requirements.
5. **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Transfer and Robustness:** To empirically validate the framework's ability to achieve robust zero-shot generalization to previously unseen robots in both extensive simulation environments and real-world humanoid platforms.
6. **Enhance Training Efficiency:** To investigate whether the generalist policy can serve as a strong initialization for subsequent fine-tuning on specific robots, thereby improving sample efficiency and accelerating convergence compared to training from scratch.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to enable scalable, general-purpose humanoid control, thereby facilitating rapid transfer and iteration across new embodiments and significantly reducing development overheads in humanoid robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

XHugWBC is a cross-embodiment training framework designed to learn a generalist whole-body controller for diverse humanoid robots. The methodology comprises three core components: physics-consistent morphological randomization, a universal cross-embodiment representation, and a learning strategy based on reinforcement learning with specialized policy architectures.

#### 3.1. Physics-Consistent Morphological Randomization

The framework introduces a method to generate diverse yet physically plausible robot embodiments. Traditional domain randomization often perturbs inertial parameters in ways that can lead to physically inconsistent models, especially for complex humanoids, causing simulation instability and policy optimization issues.

- **Parameterizing a Template Robot:** A template humanoid model captures common structures, defined by a parameter vector κ encoding kinematic layout, link geometries, inertial properties, and joint configurations. κ includes `κ_link` (10 parameters per rigid body: mass, first mass moments, rotational inertia) and `κ_joint` (13 parameters per joint: position, orientation, motion axis, range of motion, max velocity, max torque).
- **Reparameterizing the Link Space:** To ensure physical consistency, the pseudo-inertia matrix `J` (a 4x4 matrix combining mass, CoM, and inertia) is utilized. A rigid body's inertial parameters are physically consistent if `J` is symmetric positive definite.
 - **Cholesky-Level Parameterization:** Any physically consistent `J` can be uniquely factorized as `J = LLᵀ`, where `L` is an upper-triangular matrix with positive diagonal entries. This allows perturbations to be applied to `L` (`L' = L + ε`), ensuring `J'` remains positive definite.
 - **Affine Transformation of Pseudo-Inertia:** `J` also transforms as `J' = UJUᵀ` under affine deformations (`E`) and mass density scaling (`β`) of the rigid body, where `U = βE`. The unique upper-triangular factorization of `U` with positive diagonal entries can be bijectively mapped to a 10-dimensional real vector `θ_inert`. This reparameterization allows physics-consistent randomization of inertial parameters through unconstrained perturbations in `R¹⁰`, preserving physical feasibility and smoothness.
- **Reparameterizing the Joint Space:** Joint randomization focuses on parameters constrained by the robot's physical design.
 - **Rotational Axes:** The orientation of hip joint rotational axes (`a`) is randomized to synthesize diverse kinematic hip configurations.
 - **Joint Position:** Joint positions (`p`) are randomized relative to the parent link's frame, within a bounded magnitude.
 - **Joint Actuation:** Proportional-derivative (PD) gains and torque limits (`τ_max`) are linearly scaled with the robot's total mass. Actuation type (revolute or fixed) is randomized for selected joints (waist, arms, head), allowing the controller to operate on robots with 12 to 32 active joints.

#### 3.2. Universal Cross-Embodiment Representation

To facilitate a unified control policy across humanoids with varied kinematics and DoFs, a universal representation is developed.

- **Joint Space Semantic Alignment:** Robot-specific joint states are mapped into a global, canonical joint space of fixed dimension `N_max = 32`. Each index in this space corresponds to a semantically aligned joint. For a robot with `N_r` joints, its configuration `q_r` is mapped to `q_global` ∈ `R^N_max`, where unactuated or missing joints are zero-padded. This provides a consistent, fixed-dimensional input for the policy.
- **Graph-based Morphology Description:** Each `q_global` representation supports the construction of a directed kinematic graph `G = (V, E)`. Vertices `V` represent joints, and edges `E` capture rigid-body connections. An adjacency matrix `A` (N_max x N_max) encodes connectivity. Parallel-linkage mechanisms are handled by collapsing nodes and connecting them to the preceding joint. The resulting graph is a connected, acyclic kinematic tree encoding diverse kinematic and actuation patterns.

#### 3.3. Cross-Humanoid Learning

The problem is formulated as a reinforcement learning (RL) task over a family of morphologically diverse robots. A single policy `π` is optimized to maximize expected return across embodiments.

- **Observation:** The policy receives a five-step history of proprioceptive observations (`o_P`: base angular velocity, gravity, joint positions `q_t` and velocities `˙q_t`, previous action `a_t-1`), a joint controllability indicator `I(t)`, and a whole-body command vector `c_t`. `c_t` includes target base velocities, height, pelvis angle, waist rotations, and gait parameters. An upper-body intervention scheme is used for loco-manipulation, where `I(t)` indicates if an external controller drives the upper body, allowing the policy to adapt lower-body balance.
- **Policy Architectures:**
 - **GCN Policy Encoder:** A Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) processes node features `X` (linearly projected canonical joint states) and the structural mask `M = I + A` (adjacency matrix with self-loops). Multiple GCN layers aggregate information from local kinematic neighborhoods to produce structure-aware node features.
 - **Transformer Policy Encoder:** Node embeddings `X` are augmented with learned positional encodings `W_pos`. A hybrid-mask strategy is employed: the first layer uses masked attention based on the graph structure (MAB), while subsequent layers use unmasked self-attention (SAB) for global information exchange, enabling integration of both local kinematic structure and global coordination.
- **State Estimator:** A separate state estimator is concurrently trained via supervised regression to reconstruct privileged information (e.g., base linear velocity, base height) that is available in simulation but not directly on real robots. Its outputs are used by the actor detokenizer.
- **Action Prediction:** Encoded node features `Z` from the GCN or Transformer are concatenated with a global context vector (`o_g`) and the reconstructed privileged information (`P`). Linear layers then generate per-node joint actions, which are aggregated into a global action vector `a_global` and mapped back to the robot's physical joints via an embodiment-specific inverse mapping function `inv(ϕ_r)`.
- **Critic Structure:** The critic network mirrors the actor but omits the state estimator. During training, it receives additional privileged observations (e.g., pelvis/torso linear velocities, link collision pairs, morphological parameters) to ensure stable and accurate value estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of XHugWBC involved extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, addressing five key research questions.

**RQ1. Generalization to Previously Unseen Embodiments (Zero-Shot)**
The generalist policy learned by XHugWBC demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization. Across twelve simulated humanoids and seven real-world robots, all of which were excluded from training, the policy achieved a 100% survival rate. Command-tracking accuracy remained consistently high, without apparent bias towards any specific system. While specialist policies (trained specifically for each robot) generally represented an upper bound, XHugWBC achieved comparable performance, reaching approximately 85% of the specialist return on average (Table 1 and 7). This indicates that the policy successfully internalized a broad distribution of morphological and dynamical characteristics, acquiring a structural bias for unseen robots.
Qualitative analysis using t-SNE visualization of transformer latent representations revealed that robot structure influenced the embedded latent distribution. Robots with similar hip-joint arrangements clustered together, and the number of waist DoFs further modulated these embeddings, suggesting that the model effectively learned to encode structural information (Figure 4).

**RQ2. Generalist Policy as Initialization for Fine-Tuning**
Fine-tuning experiments indicated that the generalist policy served as an effective initialization. Reusing the learned weights for per-robot fine-tuning significantly improved sample efficiency and led to substantially faster convergence compared to training specialist policies from scratch (Figure 3). At convergence, fine-tuned generalist policies (Generalist-FT) achieved up to a 10% improvement in return compared to specialist policies. This suggests that the learned representations within the generalist policy are highly adaptable, providing a strong foundation for efficient specialization while retaining the potential for high-performance control.

**RQ3. Comparison with Cross-Embodiment Baselines**
XHugWBC was compared against two cross-embodiment baselines, MetaMorph and MorAL, as well as a "Naive Random" approach using simple morphological randomization. In zero-shot embodiment generalization, XHugWBC outperformed MetaMorph and MorAL in terms of survival rate (Figure 5). Naive Randomization, which approximates rigid bodies as simple primitives, generalized to only a small subset of embodiments and showed significant performance degradation on others. When fine-tuned, MetaMorph-FT and MorAL-FT also benefited, but their convergence was slower, and their peak performance was comparable to specialists but consistently lower than that achieved by Generalist-FT (Figure 3). These results highlight the efficacy of XHugWBC's physics-consistent morphological randomization and its universal representation approach.

**RQ4. Effectiveness of Policy Architectures**
An ablation study on policy network architectures (MLP, GCN, and Transformer) evaluated training convergence and generalization. Both GCN and Transformer architectures significantly outperformed the MLP baseline in terms of mean episodic return and episode length (Figure 6). This performance gain was attributed to their ability to exploit the robot's embodied kinematic topology and dependency structure, leading to more sample-efficient learning. The MLP, despite receiving explicit morphological information, struggled to capture inter-joint dependencies due to its flattened state representation. Based on these findings, the Transformer architecture was adopted for subsequent experiments.

**RQ5. Sim-to-Real Performance Gap**
XHugWBC was zero-shot deployed on seven distinct real-world humanoid robot platforms, demonstrating reliable transfer and achieving a 100% task success rate despite substantial hardware design, physical property, and kinematic topology variations (Figure 1).
A quantitative evaluation of the sim-to-real gap, measured by average forward velocity tracking error, revealed performance degradation in the real world for all platforms, but the magnitude varied. Smaller humanoids like Booster T1 and Unitree G1 exhibited superior transferability with relatively smaller gaps (27.45% to 45.31%). In contrast, full-size humanoids such as Dobot Atom and Unitree H1-2 showed more pronounced discrepancies (80.00% to 107.93%), attributed to hardware factors like larger gear reduction ratios and increased joint clearances. Fourier N1 and Agibot X2 also exhibited larger gaps, suggesting that sim-to-real performance is influenced by platform-specific actuation and mechanical characteristics beyond just scale (Table 2).

**Loco-Manipulation Real-World Experiment**
Beyond basic locomotion, the generalist policy enabled robust and accurate full-body teleoperation and long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks on robots like Unitree G1 and H1-2 (Figure 7). Tasks included picking up a plush toy, opening a door, traversing, squatting, and placing the toy. The policy achieved near-perfect success rates, demonstrating coordinated whole-body behaviors across diverse embodiments for complex interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents XHugWBC, a framework that addresses a fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics: the development of a single whole-body controller capable of zero-shot generalization across morphologically diverse platforms.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Pioneering Generalist Control:** To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first generalist controller to demonstrate robust zero-shot whole-body control across seven real-world humanoid robots with substantial diversity in DoFs, dynamics, and kinematic structures. This capability represents a significant advancement over existing robot-specific or limited-transfer approaches.
- **Physics-Consistent Randomization:** The introduction of physics-consistent morphological randomization is a key technical contribution. By reparameterizing inertial and joint spaces to ensure physical plausibility, the framework generates diverse and meaningful embodiments, overcoming stability and degeneracy issues common in naive randomization.
- **Universal Embodiment Representation:** The development of a universal representation through semantically aligned joint spaces and graph-based morphology descriptions allows a single policy to handle heterogeneous robot designs by abstracting their unique characteristics into a consistent input format.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Performance:** The generalist policy, while showing strong zero-shot performance, also serves as an effective pre-training initialization. Fine-tuning the generalist policy leads to significantly higher sample efficiency and faster convergence, ultimately surpassing specialist controllers in peak performance. This offers a practical pathway for rapidly deploying high-performing controllers on new hardware.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** Extensive validation in real-world scenarios, including robust locomotion and complex loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., object manipulation, door opening), confirms the framework's practical utility and transferability.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Development:** By eliminating the need for extensive per-robot training, XHugWBC can significantly reduce the development time and cost associated with deploying new humanoid robots or adapting existing ones to new tasks. This could accelerate research and commercial applications in humanoid robotics.
- **Scalable Robot Fleets:** The ability to use a single controller across diverse humanoid designs facilitates the creation and management of scalable robot fleets, where heterogeneous robots can perform similar tasks with minimal customization effort.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research in generalist robotics. It opens avenues for exploring more expressive, morphology-aware control beyond unified command interfaces, such as motion tracking that dynamically adapts to robot morphology.
- **Increased Accessibility:** The availability of generalized controllers could make advanced humanoid capabilities more accessible to researchers and developers without extensive expertise in robot-specific control design.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that XHugWBC currently relies on a unified command interface where all robots are driven by control signals with shared semantics. While simplifying the learning process, this approach may limit applicability to highly expressive control settings where morphology-aware adjustments are crucial (e.g., motion tracking requiring embodiment-specific retargeting). Future work aims to extend cross-embodiment learning to support such expressive, morphology-aware control, allowing for a deeper integration of robot-specific characteristics into the control policy while maintaining generalization. Additionally, the physics-consistent randomization, while effective, still requires embodiment-specific parameter ranges, as different humanoids exhibit distinct physical properties.
