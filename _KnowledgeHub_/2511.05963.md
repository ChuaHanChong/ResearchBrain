# Next-Latent Prediction Transformers Learn Compact World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Next-Latent Prediction (NextLat) framework introduces a self-supervised objective for transformers, guiding them to learn compact world models with coherent latent dynamics. This method enables superior generalization and efficient variable-length self-speculative decoding by encouraging transformer hidden states to act as belief states.

## Problem

- Modern transformer architectures lack an inherent mechanism to compress historical information into compact, fixed-dimensional latent states with consistent transition rules.
- Without explicit compression incentives, transformers often learn complex, data-fitting shortcuts that generalize poorly rather than forming principled 'world models' or belief states.
- The standard next-token prediction objective can be 'myopic,' limiting transformer capabilities in tasks requiring long-range planning, reasoning, and consistent internal state dynamics.

## Method

- **NextLat** augments standard transformer training with auxiliary self-supervised objectives that enforce predictions in the latent space, theoretically shaping hidden states into belief states.
- It jointly trains a transformer with a lightweight, three-layer MLP ==latent dynamics model== (`p_ψ`) that predicts the transformer's next hidden state based on the current state and next token.
- The full objective combines the standard next-token loss with a Smooth L1 loss for multi-step latent state prediction and a KL divergence loss to align the token-prediction semantics of predicted latent states.

## Results

- NextLat achieved **98.7%** valid trajectories and **0.71** sequence compression in world modeling, producing a **3x** more compact representation (lowest effective latent rank of **52.7**) than baselines.
- In mathematical reasoning (Countdown), NextLat reached **58.7%** accuracy, outperforming all baselines including MTP (**57.3%**), JTP (**55.0%**), BST (**42.3%**), and GPT (**33.1%**).
- It demonstrated up to **3.3x** inference speedups via variable-length self-speculative decoding and showed that its learned latent dynamics model generalized to sequence lengths far beyond the transformer's training context in algorithmic reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Theoretically, joint optimization for next-token consistency and latent state transition consistency ensures that transformer hidden states converge to belief states, acting as sufficient statistics of the past.
> - NextLat provides a denser learning signal by supervising in the latent space (representing full predictive distributions) compared to sparse one-hot token targets, enhancing data efficiency.
> - The learned latent dynamics model facilitates 'variable-length self-speculative decoding,' allowing recursive prediction of an arbitrary number of future tokens for significant inference acceleration beyond fixed-horizon methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a perceived limitation in modern transformer architectures within the field of sequence modeling and representation learning. Transformers, while excelling in parallelization and predictive power by replacing recurrence with a scalable memory and self-attention, typically lack an inherent mechanism to compress historical information into compact latent states with consistent transition rules. This can lead to learned solutions that are specific to training data and generalize less effectively, in contrast to the principle that simpler, more compact explanations often generalize better.

The paper positions Next-Latent Prediction (NextLat) as a method to reintroduce a recurrent inductive bias into transformers, encouraging them to form compact "world models" that channel future prediction through compressed representations of the past. This approach is informed by several areas of research:

- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** NextLat falls under self-predictive representation learning, which involves jointly learning latent representations and a transition function that models their evolution over a sequence. This contrasts with some other SSL methods for language, such as LLM-JEPA, which depend on manually curated paired data, whereas NextLat is fully self-supervised.
- **Belief States:** In both sequence modeling and reinforcement learning (RL), belief states are defined as sufficient statistics of past observations for predicting future outcomes. While recurrent neural networks naturally encourage such compression, transformers do not, as their memory grows linearly with sequence length. Previous work, like Belief State Transformers (BST), has aimed to extend the concept of belief states to transformers, but NextLat seeks a more computationally efficient realization.
- **World Models:** The concept of an internal predictive model of the world is explored across various scientific disciplines. In RL, successful approaches like MuZero and Dreamer utilize latent dynamics models to predict future latent states given current states and actions. NextLat extends this principle to transformer-based language modeling, a domain where explicit learning of latent dynamics has been less explored.
- **Beyond Next-Token Prediction:** Existing research has highlighted the "myopic" nature of the standard next-token prediction objective, which can limit capabilities in tasks requiring long-range planning and reasoning. Some recent methods augment transformers with multi-token prediction objectives in the *token space*. NextLat distinguishes itself by shifting prediction into the *latent space*, aiming to enforce coherent dynamics over the model’s internal representations rather than its token outputs directly.
- **Speculative Decoding:** This inference acceleration technique uses a lightweight draft model to propose multiple tokens, which are then verified in parallel by a target model. Self-speculative decoding allows a single model to act as both draft and verifier, typically through multi-token prediction (MTP) with a fixed speculative horizon. NextLat's recursively composable latent dynamics model enables variable-length self-speculative decoding, potentially offering greater flexibility and efficiency.

In summary, NextLat contributes to the landscape by offering a novel, theoretically grounded, and computationally efficient method to imbue transformers with properties previously associated with recurrent models and world-modeling agents, specifically the ability to learn compact, predictive belief states and coherent latent dynamics, without altering their core architecture or parallel training advantages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from an observation about the inductive biases of modern transformers. While transformers have demonstrated remarkable performance in various sequence-based tasks, their architectural design, which replaces recurrence with attention-based lookups over an ever-growing memory, lacks an intrinsic drive to compress historical information into parsimonious, fixed-dimensional latent states that follow consistent transition rules. This absence of an explicit compression incentive can lead to:

1. **Learning of Complex, Task-Specific Shortcuts:** Transformers may develop intricate, data-fitting strategies that perform well on training data but generalize poorly to new, out-of-distribution scenarios. This phenomenon is analogous to Ptolemy's convoluted geocentric model, which accurately predicted observations but lacked the structural simplicity and generalizability of Copernicus' heliocentric model. In learning theory, simpler explanations are often associated with better generalization.
2. **Lack of Coherent Internal World Models:** The ability to form a compact, predictive internal representation of the environment, often termed a "world model," is considered crucial for advanced reasoning, planning, and generalization. While recurrent models inherently promote such compression, standard transformers do not necessarily learn coherent world models or "belief states"—sufficient statistics of the past for predicting the future.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of the NextLat framework are:

- **To Inject a Recurrent Inductive Bias:** The central goal is to encourage transformers to learn compact world models with coherent belief states and transition dynamics, akin to recurrent neural networks, but without sacrificing the parallel training efficiency and architectural flexibility of transformers.
- **To Develop a Theoretically Grounded Mechanism for Belief State Learning:** The paper aims to provide a formal argument that NextLat's objective function can provably shape transformer's latent representations into belief states—representations that encapsulate all necessary information from the past to predict future observations.
- **To Enhance Representation Quality:** By enforcing self-supervised predictions in the latent space, NextLat seeks to guide the transformer to learn richer, more predictive internal representations. This is hypothesized to improve data efficiency by augmenting sparse one-hot token targets with dense, distribution-level latent-state supervision.
- **To Improve Generalization and Downstream Performance:** The learned compact world models are expected to yield better generalization across a range of tasks, including world modeling, reasoning, and planning, where anticipating future states and maintaining consistency over long horizons are critical.
- **To Facilitate Efficient Inference:** Beyond training benefits, the learned latent dynamics model is intended to enable a new form of "variable-length self-speculative decoding." This objective is to accelerate inference by allowing the model to recursively predict multiple future latent states and their corresponding tokens, thereby reducing the number of costly full-transformer passes.

In essence, the motivation is to address a fundamental architectural gap in transformers by providing an explicit incentive for internal state compression and temporal consistency, thereby fostering the development of more principled, generalizable, and efficient artificial intelligence systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NextLat extends the standard next-token prediction objective with self-supervised predictions in the latent space of a transformer. The methodology involves the joint training of a transformer and a separate, lightweight latent dynamics model.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework

The paper establishes a theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.2) asserting that if a system jointly optimizes for two conditions, the transformer's hidden states (`h_t`) will converge to belief states. The conditions are:

1. **Next-Token Consistency (Equation 1):** The transformer's output head `p_θ` accurately predicts the next token `X_t+1` given the current hidden state `h_t`. This is the standard cross-entropy loss in autoregressive models: `p_θ(X_t+1 | h_t) = P(X_t+1 | X_1:t)`.
2. **Transition Consistency (Equation 2):** A latent dynamics model `p_ψ` accurately predicts the transformer's next hidden state `h_t+1` given the current hidden state `h_t` and the next token `X_t+1`: `p_ψ(h_t+1 | h_t, X_t+1) = P(h_t+1 | X_1:t+1)`.

The theorem posits that optimizing these two objectives ensures that `h_t` becomes a belief state for the sequence `X_1:t`. This means `h_t` is a sufficient statistic for the past history `X_1:t` to predict the future `X_t+1:T`. The proof involves a backward induction, demonstrating that if `h_k+1` is a belief state, then `h_k` must also be one, given the recursive predictability enforced by `p_θ` and `p_ψ`. A key insight is that standard next-token prediction alone does not guarantee belief state formation because self-attention allows ad-hoc retrieval of past information, obviating the need for compact compression at each step.

NextLat's method also offers improved data efficiency by providing a denser learning signal. Unlike token-space supervision which is typically sparse (one-hot labels), NextLat supervises in latent space, predicting `h_t+1` which parameterizes the full predictive distribution over `X_t+2`. This implicit chain of prediction propagates information about future tokens into earlier representations.

#### 3.2. Practical Implementation of NextLat

NextLat augments the standard transformer training objective with auxiliary losses on the latent representations. The core components are:

- **Transformer and Next-Token Loss (`L_next-token`):** A standard decoder-only transformer is used, optimized with the cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction: `L_next-token(θ) = E_t<T [ -log p_θ(X_t+1 | h_t) ]`. Here, `h_t` refers to the final-layer output (pre-logit activations) of the transformer at time step `t`.
- **Latent Dynamics Model (`p_ψ`):** This is a lightweight, three-layer MLP with GELU activations. It takes the layer-normalized concatenation of the current hidden state `h_t` and the embedding of the next token `X_t+1` as input. Its output is added residually to `h_t` to predict the next hidden state `hat_h_t+1`: `hat_h_t+1 = f_ψ(h_t, X_t+1) + h_t`. The model `f_ψ(·)` predicts the delta update to `h_t`.
- **Next-Hidden Loss (`L_next-h`):** This loss enforces the transition consistency of the hidden states. It uses a Smooth L1 loss to supervise multi-step rollouts (`d` steps) of the latent dynamics model:
 `L_next-h(θ, ψ; d) = E_t [ (1/d) * sum_{i=1 to d} SmoothL1Loss(sg[h_t+i], hat_h_t+i) ]`.
 The `sg[·]` (stop-gradient) operator is applied to the target `h_t+i` to prevent representational collapse and ensure `h_t+i` acts as a stable target derived from the transformer's main computation. Even `d=1` is sufficient for theoretical belief-state convergence, while larger `d` provides a richer learning signal.
- **KL Loss (`L_KL`):** A complementary objective that aligns the token-prediction semantics of the recursively predicted states (`hat_h`) with the true states. It computes the KL divergence between the output head's distribution given the actual future hidden state and its distribution given the predicted future hidden state:
 `L_KL(θ, ψ; d) = E_t [ (1/d) * sum_{i=1 to d} D_KL(p_sg_θ(· | sg[h_t+i]) || p_sg_θ(· | hat_h_t+i)) ]`.
 The output head `p_sg_θ(·)` is frozen for this loss, directing gradients only through `p_ψ`. This acts as a form of knowledge distillation, encouraging `hat_h_t+i` to reproduce the distribution over future tokens.
- **Overall Objective:** The final loss function is a weighted sum:
 `L_NextLat(θ, ψ; d, λ_next-h, λ_KL) = L_next-token(θ) + λ_next-h * L_next-h(θ, ψ; d) + λ_KL * L_KL(θ, ψ; d)`.
 `λ_next-h` and `λ_KL` are scalar coefficients.
- **Inference:** Crucially, during standard inference, the learned transformer operates independently, and `p_ψ` is not required; its role is solely to shape the transformer's representations during training.

#### 3.3. Variable-Length Self-Speculative Decoding

A practical advantage of NextLat's methodology is its application to inference acceleration. By learning a latent dynamics model (`p_ψ`) that can recursively predict future hidden states, NextLat enables a flexible form of self-speculative decoding. The model can generate a token `X_t+1` from `h_t`, then use `h_t` and `X_t+1` to predict `h_t+1` via `p_ψ`, and so on. This recursive composition of predictions allows NextLat to draft an arbitrary number of future tokens, even beyond the multi-step training horizon `d`. This capability is distinct from Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) methods, which are typically constrained to a fixed speculative horizon determined during training. NextLat can therefore reduce the number of draft-verification cycles and achieve higher inference speedups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research empirically evaluated NextLat across diverse domains including world modeling, reasoning, planning, and language modeling, comparing it against standard next-token prediction (GPT) and other baselines like Multi-Token Prediction (MTP), Joint Multi-Token Prediction (JTP), and Belief State Transformer (BST).

#### 4.1. World Modeling (Manhattan Taxi Rides)

NextLat demonstrates superior performance in learning interpretable and compact world models.
- **Qualitative:** Reconstructed internal maps from NextLat-trained transformers show higher consistency with the true Manhattan street map, exhibiting fewer and more localized inconsistencies compared to baselines.
- **Quantitative:** NextLat significantly outperforms baselines across several metrics:
 - **Valid Trajectories:** Achieved 98.7% valid traversals for out-of-distribution (OOD) pickup–dropoff pairs, higher than GPT (97.0%), MTP (98.1%), and JTP (97.1%).
 - **Sequence Compression:** Achieved the highest compression (0.71), indicating greater consistency in generating identical continuations for trajectories ending in the same state.
 - **Effective Latent Rank:** Produced the lowest effective latent rank (52.7), implying a more compact representation (over 3x smaller than GPT's 160.1). Lower rank signifies better compression and an internal model closer to the true world model's dimensionality.
 - **Detour Robustness:** Maintained the strongest generalization to OOD pairs even with random detours (95.0%).

#### 4.2. Reasoning (Countdown)

NextLat consistently outperforms all baselines in the Countdown mathematical reasoning task.
- **Accuracy:** Achieved 58.7% accuracy with `d=8` and 54.8% even with `d=1`, substantially surpassing MTP (`d=8`: 57.3%) and JTP (`d=8`: 55.0%), and significantly outperforming BST (42.3%) and GPT (33.1%).
- **Equation Validity:** Demonstrated stronger lookahead planning, with a substantially higher mean validity in the final equation (54.8% for `d=1`) compared to the next best baseline (42.3%). This suggests NextLat helps models anticipate long-range dependencies and form globally consistent plans, reducing myopic errors.

#### 4.3. Planning (Path-Star Graph and TinyStories)

NextLat shows improved planning capabilities and long-range predictive power in its latent representations.
- **Path-Star Graph:** NextLat maintained a solve rate close to 100% across all tested graph configurations (G_2,10, G_5,5, G_7,7). Baselines like BST showed degradation at larger graphs (e.g., G_7,7), and MTP/JTP also struggled, indicating NextLat's ability to avoid "Clever Hans cheat" (exploiting local shortcuts) and learn more generalizable solutions, especially in data-constrained settings.
- **TinyStories (Latent Predictive Capability):** When training linear probes on frozen hidden states to predict future tokens, NextLat matched GPT's next-token performance and exhibited the strongest long-range predictive capability (up to 20 tokens ahead) for both `d=1` and `d=8`. In contrast, other multi-token prediction baselines often degraded next-token performance and showed sharply declining predictive power over longer offsets, suggesting their representations encoded only short-horizon information.

#### 4.4. Language Modeling (FineWeb-Edu)

NextLat demonstrates competitive next-token prediction and significant inference acceleration.
- **Perplexity and Downstream Accuracy:** NextLat (d=2) showed a modest gain in average accuracy across multiple-choice benchmarks (59.21% vs. GPT's 58.82%) and better preserved next-token perplexity compared to MTP and JTP across various datasets.
- **Inference Acceleration (Self-Speculative Decoding):** NextLat achieved substantially higher inference speedups, up to 3.3x, compared to MTP and JTP across Wikipedia, Books, Code, and Math domains. The average accepted tokens per drafting step for NextLat far exceeded its training horizon `d`, indicating that the learned latent dynamics remain coherent over extended recursive rollouts. This allows for variable-length speculative decoding, drafting more tokens than trained for, leading to greater acceleration.

#### 4.5. NextLat vs. Recurrent Neural Networks (A5 Word Problem)

NextLat demonstrates an intriguing ability to co-train a highly generalizable recurrent model.
- On the A5 word problem (a state-tracking task), a 2-layer NextLat-trained transformer (d=1) showed better state-tracking than GPT within its 12-token training horizon.
- Notably, while the transformer itself failed to generalize beyond the 12-token horizon (remaining within TC0 circuit complexity), the *learned latent dynamics model (RNN)* successfully generalized to 36-token sequences (>95% accuracy). This suggests that the transformer can implicitly train an RNN that can generalize far beyond the transformer's own expressivity limits and training context, effectively learning reusable computational circuits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Next-Latent Prediction (NextLat) framework presents a notable contribution to the field of large language models and representation learning, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Theoretical Grounding for Representation Learning:** NextLat provides a theoretical argument for shaping transformer latent representations into "belief states." This is a significant advancement because, unlike recurrent models, standard transformers do not inherently form such compact, sufficient statistics of the past for predicting the future. This theoretical guarantee offers a more principled approach to representation learning in transformers.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** By encouraging transformers to learn compact internal world models with coherent recurrent-like dynamics, NextLat addresses the issue of transformers learning task-specific shortcuts. The empirical results across world modeling, reasoning, and planning benchmarks suggest that NextLat leads to more robust and generalizable solutions, better at anticipating long-range dependencies and handling out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Training:** NextLat introduces a recurrent inductive bias without requiring architectural changes to the transformer, maintaining its parallel training efficiency. The auxiliary latent dynamics model is lightweight, making the approach computationally efficient compared to methods like BST which incur significantly higher training costs (e.g., O(T^2) gradient computation).
 - **Inference:** The ability to perform variable-length self-speculative decoding allows NextLat to achieve substantial inference speedups (up to 3.3x) compared to fixed-horizon multi-token prediction methods. This could lead to more efficient deployment of large language models.
- **Richer Learning Signals:** By augmenting sparse, one-hot token targets with dense, distribution-level latent-state supervision, NextLat provides richer learning signals. This potentially enhances data efficiency, enabling stronger performance in scenarios with limited training data.
- **Bridging Architectural Paradigms:** NextLat effectively unifies the inherent bias towards compact and temporally consistent representations characteristic of recurrent models with the scalability and parallelism advantages of transformers. This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of both paradigms without their respective drawbacks (e.g., sequential bottleneck of RNNs).
- **Implications for Algorithmic Reasoning and RL:** The observation that a transformer trained with NextLat can co-train a lightweight recurrent neural network (latent dynamics model) that generalizes to sequence lengths far beyond the transformer's own capacity is particularly impactful. This suggests a potential mechanism for transformers to "bootstrap" or "distill" more abstract, generalizable computational circuits, potentially offering a path towards improving algorithmic reasoning capabilities and overcoming some limitations imposed by fixed-depth transformer architectures. Such learned recurrent structures could also be beneficial for reinforcement learning applications, where Bellman-like recursive value estimation is crucial.
- **Broad Applicability and Future Directions:** As NextLat is an auxiliary training objective that requires no fundamental architectural changes, it is broadly compatible with diverse sequence-modeling architectures. Future work could explore its application as a post-hoc fine-tuning objective for existing pretrained models, integrate richer latent dynamics models, or investigate hierarchical belief states to further enhance reasoning and planning.

In conclusion, NextLat offers a simple yet effective paradigm that enhances transformers' ability to learn compact, predictive, and generalizable representations, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with current transformer architectures and opening new avenues for research into more robust and efficient artificial intelligence.
