# Next-Latent Prediction Transformers Learn Compact World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Next-Latent Prediction (NextLat) framework introduces a self-supervised objective for transformers, guiding them to learn compact world models with coherent latent dynamics. This method enables superior generalization and efficient variable-length self-speculative decoding by encouraging transformer hidden states to act as belief states.

## Problem

- Modern transformer architectures lack an inherent mechanism to compress historical information into compact, fixed-dimensional latent states with consistent transition rules.
- Without explicit compression incentives, transformers often learn complex, data-fitting shortcuts that generalize poorly rather than forming principled 'world models' or belief states.
- The standard next-token prediction objective can be 'myopic,' limiting transformer capabilities in tasks requiring long-range planning, reasoning, and consistent internal state dynamics.

## Method

- **NextLat** augments standard transformer training with auxiliary self-supervised objectives that enforce predictions in the latent space, theoretically shaping hidden states into belief states.
- It jointly trains a transformer with a lightweight, three-layer MLP ==latent dynamics model== (`p_ψ`) that predicts the transformer's next hidden state based on the current state and next token.
- The full objective combines the standard next-token loss with a Smooth L1 loss for multi-step latent state prediction and a KL divergence loss to align the token-prediction semantics of predicted latent states.

## Results

- NextLat achieved **98.7%** valid trajectories and **0.71** sequence compression in world modeling, producing a **3x** more compact representation (lowest effective latent rank of **52.7**) than baselines.
- In mathematical reasoning (Countdown), NextLat reached **58.7%** accuracy, outperforming all baselines including MTP (**57.3%**), JTP (**55.0%**), BST (**42.3%**), and GPT (**33.1%**).
- It demonstrated up to **3.3x** inference speedups via variable-length self-speculative decoding and showed that its learned latent dynamics model generalized to sequence lengths far beyond the transformer's training context in algorithmic reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Theoretically, joint optimization for next-token consistency and latent state transition consistency ensures that transformer hidden states converge to belief states, acting as sufficient statistics of the past.
> - NextLat provides a denser learning signal by supervising in the latent space (representing full predictive distributions) compared to sparse one-hot token targets, enhancing data efficiency.
> - The learned latent dynamics model facilitates 'variable-length self-speculative decoding,' allowing recursive prediction of an arbitrary number of future tokens for significant inference acceleration beyond fixed-horizon methods.
