# OpenMMReasoner: Pushing the Frontiers for Multimodal Reasoning with an Open and General Recipe

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenMMReasoner introduces a transparent, two-stage Supervised Fine-tuning and Reinforcement Learning recipe for multimodal reasoning, establishing a new state-of-the-art for open-source models across nine diverse benchmarks. This approach improves token efficiency and boosts cross-domain textual reasoning capabilities, with all components released as open source.

## Problem

- Existing Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often lack transparency and reproducibility in their training pipelines, especially for Reinforcement Learning (RL) components.
- Detailed insights into data curation, training strategies, and comprehensive ablation analyses for LMMs are frequently not provided, hindering community understanding and further research.
- A gap exists between the advanced capabilities of LMMs and the practical, replicable knowledge required to reproduce and build upon these models.

## Method

- A fully transparent, ==two-stage training recipe== combining Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) is developed for Large Multimodal Models, starting with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.
- The SFT stage employs a meticulous data curation pipeline, including distillation from a powerful teacher model (Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct), diverse answer sampling (×8), and cross-domain data mixing, without aggressive filtering.
- The RL stage leverages Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) with a composite reward function emphasizing accuracy and an overlength penalty to ensure efficient, concise reasoning.

## Results

- OpenMMReasoner achieved an average accuracy of **54.3%** across nine multimodal reasoning benchmarks, setting a new state-of-the-art for open-source models, with specific scores of **79.5%** on MathVista and **63.8%** on MathVerse.
- The model demonstrated enhanced token efficiency, generating accurate responses with significantly fewer tokens compared to prior methods, such as using approximately 9,893 tokens on MathVision where another model used 17,876.
- Reinforcement Learning training led to a notable improvement in textual reasoning, with the model's average textual reasoning score reaching **29.4%**, a substantial increase from the **15.1%** baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Answer diversity in Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) data, achieved through methods like multi-sampling and cross-domain mixing, critically enhances a model's overall reasoning performance, while aggressive filtering can be detrimental.
> - Distilling SFT data from a powerful teacher model (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct) significantly improves reasoning capabilities and data efficiency.
> - The Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) algorithm offers superior stability and faster convergence in multimodal Reinforcement Learning, and training with RL can lead to beneficial cross-domain transfer of textual reasoning abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in large reasoning models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) proving effective in enhancing structured reasoning across domains like mathematics, programming, and science. Models such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI's o3 have demonstrated impressive textual reasoning capabilities through RLVR.

This paper extends these advancements to Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), which integrate reasoning across various modalities (e.g., vision and language). While previous research has shown the potential of RL in strengthening fine-grained visual understanding and cross-modal problem-solving in LMMs (e.g., ThinkLite-VL, MM-Eureka, Open Vision Reasoner), a critical gap remains: the **lack of transparency and reproducibility** in training pipelines. Many existing studies often do not provide detailed insights into their data curation processes, training strategies, or comprehensive ablation analyses, hindering community-wide understanding and further scalable research.

OpenMMReasoner directly addresses this limitation by introducing a "fully transparent two-stage recipe" for multimodal reasoning. It differentiates itself from prior works by:
- **Providing a unified and scalable recipe:** Unlike efforts that focus on specific stages (SFT or RL) or offer limited generalizability, OpenMMReasoner presents a cohesive framework spanning both Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **Emphasizing data quality and transparency:** The work meticulously details data curation, distillation, and mixing strategies, a level of openness often missing in state-of-the-art model releases.
- **Benchmarking across diverse tasks:** The evaluation covers a wide array of multimodal reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, MathVerse, MMMU), demonstrating generalizability across different types of reasoning (mathematical, logical, visual QA).
- **Open-sourcing all components:** This commitment to open science sets a new standard, providing the research community with a reproducible foundation for future LMM development, thereby democratizing access to powerful multimodal reasoning models.

In essence, OpenMMReasoner positions itself as a critical step towards more understandable, reproducible, and robust LMM development, bridging the gap between impressive model capabilities and the practical knowledge required to replicate and advance them.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind OpenMMReasoner are multifaceted, driven by the desire to foster more transparent, reproducible, and effective research in large multimodal reasoning models:

1. **Address the Transparency and Reproducibility Gap:** The core motivation stems from the observation that despite significant progress in multimodal reasoning, the underlying data curation and training strategies (especially for RL-enhanced LMMs) are often opaque. The authors aim to demystify these processes by providing a "fully transparent two-stage recipe."
2. **Develop a Scalable and General Recipe:** The goal is to create a robust and unified training methodology that can effectively equip open-source LMMs with strong reasoning capabilities, applicable across diverse tasks and modalities, and scalable to larger datasets and models. This involves developing simple yet effective data pipelines for both SFT and RL.
3. **Establish a Strong SFT Foundation:** The researchers aim to construct a high-quality SFT dataset and recipe that provides a robust "cold-start" for reasoning abilities, recognizing that a strong initial foundation is crucial for subsequent RL enhancement.
4. **Enhance Reasoning Capabilities via RL:** The objective is to leverage reinforcement learning to further "sharpen and stabilize" the multimodal reasoning abilities acquired during SFT, optimizing for robustness and efficiency. This includes a systematic comparison of various RL algorithms.
5. **Provide Comprehensive Data Curation Insights:** The paper seeks to conduct a systematic study on what factors (e.g., data diversity, teacher model selection, filtering strategies) most effectively contribute to high-quality SFT and RL data for multimodal reasoning.
6. **Open-Source a Complete Workflow:** A major objective is to open-source all codes, pipelines, and data, offering a comprehensive and reproducible reference for the research community, thereby facilitating further advancements and empirical validation.
7. **Balance Reasoning Depth with Efficiency:** The authors are motivated to explore how models can achieve strong reasoning performance without resorting to excessively long and computationally expensive response generation, aiming for a favorable trade-off between accuracy and efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenMMReasoner employs a comprehensive, transparent, two-stage training recipe: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL). The base model used throughout the experiments is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

#### 3.1. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Recipe

The SFT stage focuses on building a strong reasoning foundation using meticulously curated data.

**4.1.1. Data Curation Pipeline:** The SFT dataset evolves through three stages:
1. **Data Sourcing and Formatting:**
 - Initial collection of approximately 103k raw question-answer pairs from public datasets like LLaVA-CoT, OpenVLThinker, and WeMath2.0, primarily covering general VQA and reasoning tasks.
 - Additional sources (MMR1 for image-based math reasoning, MiroMind-M1 for text-based math reasoning) are incorporated in later stages.
 - All data is standardized to a unified reasoning format to ensure consistency in step-wise outputs.
2. **Data Distillation and Scaling:**
 - **Teacher Model Selection:** To improve data quality and efficiency, a stronger teacher model is used for distillation. Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct was selected after comparing candidates, based on its superior performance in generating verified reasoning traces (passing rule-based and LLM-as-judge checks). This produced about 59k verified traces initially.
 - **Scaling Data with Verifiable Answers:** Each question is enriched with multiple verified reasoning traces by repeatedly sampling answers from the selected teacher. Evaluating scaling factors (×1, ×2, ×4, ×8), the ×8 configuration was chosen, increasing the dataset to approximately 583k verified general-reasoning samples, balancing performance and computational cost.
3. **Length and Difficulty Filtering:** Common filtering strategies (difficulty-based and length-based) were evaluated but ultimately discarded. The study found that over-filtering reduced answer diversity and consequently model performance. A no-filtering policy was adopted to preserve the full 583k general-reasoning set.
4. **Domain Mixing:** To enhance generalization, the 583k distilled dataset was mixed with data from additional reasoning domains, specifically MMR1 (image-based math) and MiroMind-M1 (text-based math). This yielded the final 874k mixed SFT dataset.

##### 3.1.1. Experimental Setup (SFT)

- Online packing and Liger-Kernel were used for training efficiency.
- The LMMs-Engine served as the training framework and LMMs-Eval for evaluation.
- Training was conducted until convergence, starting from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Recipe

The RL stage further refines and stabilizes the reasoning capabilities established during SFT.

##### 3.2.1. Dataset Curation (RL)

- **Data Sourcing:** Diverse samples were collected from MMEureka, ViRL, TQA, We-Math, PuzzleVQA, AlgoPuzzleVQA, and ThinkLiteVL, covering science, mathematics, charts, and puzzles.
- **Data Cleaning:** Final answers were extracted and verified, and duplicates were removed based on image and text similarities. This resulted in approximately 74k samples for the RL stage.

##### 3.2.2. Experimental Setup (RL)

- The `verl` and `vllm` frameworks were used to accelerate training and inference, respectively.
- Evaluation protocol was consistent with SFT, using LMMs-Eval.
- The top-performing SFT checkpoint (874k mixed SFT dataset with ×8 sampling) was used as the starting point.
- A global batch size of 128 was employed, with dynamic batch-size for log probability computation. Max length for generation was set to 28,696 new tokens, with a prompt length cap of 4,096 tokens.

##### 3.2.3. Training Recipe (RL)

1. **RL Algorithm Selection:**
 - Three algorithms were compared: Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - GSPO demonstrated faster convergence, higher rewards, and more stable behavior, effectively balancing exploration and stability. It was chosen for the final recipe.
2. **Reward Function:** A composite reward function was adopted, similar to those in DeepSeek-R1. It combined task accuracy ($R_{acc}$) and output formatting ($R_{fmt}$) with a weighted manner: $R = (1 - \lambda_{fmt}) \cdot R_{acc} + \lambda_{fmt} \cdot R_{fmt}$. A $\lambda_{fmt}$ of 0.1 was found to yield the best performance, indicating a stronger emphasis on accuracy.
3. **Sampling Strategies:** Curriculum learning, which progressively transitions from easier to harder tasks, was explored but found not to outperform the original mixed sampling approach. Thus, a no-sampling strategy was adopted.
4. **Balancing Reasoning Capability and Efficiency:** An overlength penalty strategy, similar to DAPO, was implemented to mitigate "overthinking" behavior, ensuring that the model achieves strong performance without generating excessively long (and thus inefficient) responses. This contrasts with models like OpenVisionReasoner, which generate very long responses.
5. **Factors for Stable Training Dynamics:** Rollout temperature (moderate, not too high) and rollout count (×16 rollout configuration performing better than ×8 for stability, especially with DAPO) were identified as critical for RL training stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OpenMMReasoner demonstrates significant advancements in multimodal reasoning, supported by extensive evaluations and detailed ablations.

#### 4.1. SFT Stage Findings: Building a Strong Reasoning Foundation

- **Performance Improvement:** The SFT recipe, specifically OMR-7B-ColdStart, significantly outperforms other SFT-based methods and baselines. For instance, it achieves an average score of 49.4% across nine benchmarks, notably improving upon the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline. On MathVista, it reaches 74.8%, surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-7B (69.2%) and LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B (69.6%).
- **Key Insights from SFT Data Curation:**
 1. **Answer Diversity Enhances Reasoning:** Increasing the diversity of generated answers (e.g., from ×1 to ×8 sampling, raising average performance from 50.5 to 55.2) consistently improved the model's overall reasoning performance, highlighting the importance of varied solutions.
 2. **Crucial Teacher Model Selection:** Distilling from a stronger teacher model (Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct) substantially boosted reasoning ability and data efficiency, providing an average gain of at least 4.5 points over baselines.
 3. **Over-filtering is Detrimental:** Applying length-based or difficulty-based filtering strategies reduced performance, suggesting that maintaining greater answer diversity is more beneficial than imposing strict filters.
 4. **Cross-domain Knowledge Improves Generalization:** Incorporating supervised data from additional domains (e.g., image-based and text-based mathematical reasoning) consistently enhanced the model’s overall reasoning capabilities across tasks, with "Img+TxtMath Mix" yielding the best SFT results (56.3% average).

#### 4.2. RL Stage Findings: Sharpening and Stabilizing Reasoning

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The full OpenMMReasoner (OMR-7B) model, after the RL stage, achieves state-of-the-art results among open-source models, outperforming strong baselines and other RL-based methods across nine multimodal reasoning benchmarks. It secured an average accuracy of 54.3% with GSPO + ×16 rollout, an 11.6% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline. Notable scores include 79.5% on MathVista, 63.8% on MathVerse, and 79.0% on WeMath.
- **RL Algorithm Superiority:** GSPO demonstrated superior stability, faster convergence, and higher rewards compared to DAPO and GRPO in multimodal RL training, making it the preferred algorithm for the final recipe.
- **Token Efficiency:** OpenMMReasoner achieved better accuracy with significantly fewer tokens compared to OpenVisionReasoner (OVR). For instance, on MathVision and MathVerse, OpenMMReasoner used approximately 9,893 and 3,714 tokens on average, respectively, while OVR used 17,876 and 10,304 tokens, demonstrating a more efficient reasoning process.
- **Cross-Domain Reasoning Transfer:** RL training not only improved multimodal reasoning but also led to the gradual emergence and strengthening of textual reasoning capabilities. Validation performance on textual benchmarks like AIME24, AIME25, and AMC23 steadily increased throughout RL training, suggesting strong cross-domain generalization. The model trained with RL showed an average textual reasoning score of 29.4%, a significant improvement over the baseline (15.1%) and the SFT-only model (22.2%).
- **Stable Training Dynamics:** Rollout temperature and rollout count were identified as critical factors for RL stability. A moderate temperature and a higher rollout count (×16) consistently yielded higher rewards and smoother training dynamics, preventing issues like entropy collapse observed with fewer rollouts (e.g., DAPO ×8).
- **Reflection Word Analysis:** An interesting observation from rollout logs showed an increasing proportion of reflection-related words (e.g., "let," "wait," "think") in the model's outputs as rewards increased during RL training, indicating that the recipe effectively encourages more explicit reasoning.
- **Reward Function Sensitivity:** An ablation study on the reward coefficient ($\lambda_{fmt}$) revealed that a lower format-reward weight (0.1) yielded the best performance, emphasizing that task accuracy is more critical than strict format adherence for optimal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OpenMMReasoner paper makes several significant contributions and has a substantial potential impact on the field of large multimodal model research:

1. **Enhanced Transparency and Reproducibility:** By open-sourcing all its code, pipelines, and data, OpenMMReasoner directly tackles a major challenge in LMM research. This transparency enables other researchers to understand, verify, and build upon the presented methodology, fostering a more collaborative and accountable scientific community. It sets a new benchmark for open-source releases in this complex domain.
2. **A Robust and Generalizable Training Recipe:** The two-stage SFT and RL recipe, carefully constructed and extensively validated, offers a practical and effective framework for training high-performing multimodal reasoning models. The detailed insights into data curation, teacher selection, and algorithm choices provide valuable guidance for practitioners and researchers.
3. **Advancements in Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** OpenMMReasoner achieves state-of-the-art performance across a diverse set of multimodal reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in complex problem-solving that requires integrating information from both visual and linguistic modalities. The significant performance gains over strong baselines highlight the efficacy of the proposed transparent pipeline.
4. **Efficiency in Reasoning:** The focus on token efficiency, by achieving high accuracy with shorter, more concise reasoning trajectories, addresses a critical practical concern. Efficient reasoning is crucial for deploying LMMs in real-world applications where computational resources and inference latency are important constraints.
5. **Demonstration of Cross-Domain Generalization:** The finding that improvements in multimodal reasoning capabilities through RL also enhance textual reasoning abilities suggests a deeper, shared cognitive foundation for reasoning across modalities. This insight is important for understanding how LMMs acquire and transfer knowledge, potentially simplifying future training efforts for generalized AI.
6. **Empirical Foundation for Future Research:** OpenMMReasoner serves as a solid empirical foundation for future large-scale multimodal reasoning research. Its detailed ablation studies on data scaling, filtering, and RL algorithm choices provide valuable insights into the design principles for building robust LMMs, guiding subsequent investigations into even larger models and more complex reasoning tasks.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that the current work primarily focuses on a single model family (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct) and predominantly within the image domain. This limits its immediate applicability to other modalities like video or audio. Future work aims to:
- Extend the methodology to a wider range of domains, including video, audio, and richer temporal-stream modalities.
- Explore generation capabilities across multiple modalities simultaneously, enabling more coherent multimodal content synthesis.
- Broaden the model families used for evaluation and training to further validate the generality and robustness of the findings across different architectures.

In conclusion, OpenMMReasoner represents a significant leap forward in the development of transparent, reproducible, and highly capable large multimodal reasoning models. Its open-source nature and comprehensive empirical analysis are poised to accelerate progress and foster innovation in the burgeoning field of multimodal AI.
