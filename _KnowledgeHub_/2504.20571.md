# Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Large Language Models with One Training Example

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Washington and Microsoft introduced 1-shot Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (1-shot RLVR), demonstrating that training Large Language Models (LLMs) with a single example can achieve mathematical reasoning performance comparable to training with thousands of examples. This approach boosted mathematical reasoning accuracy on benchmarks like MATH500 by over 8% and improved average performance across six mathematical benchmarks from 17.6% to 35.7% on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuning via Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) conventionally assumes the necessity of extensive datasets, leading to high computational and data curation costs.
- The data-centric aspects of RLVR, specifically the minimum data quantity required for effective reasoning enhancement, remained largely unexplored despite advancements in RL algorithms.
- A clear understanding of whether RLVR primarily teaches new skills or unlocks existing latent capabilities within LLMs, particularly when trained with sparse data, was lacking.

## Method

- The core solution involves ==1-shot Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (1-shot RLVR)==, which fine-tunes LLMs using just one or a few carefully selected examples, rather than large datasets.
- The methodology employs ==GRPO (Group-normalized Proximal Policy Optimization)== as the default RL algorithm, utilizing a binary (0-1) outcome reward for mathematical problems.
- A ==Historical Variance Score (HVS)== was proposed to select high-impact single or few-shot examples for training, and the approach was validated through extensive experiments on multiple LLMs and evaluation benchmarks.

## Results

- 1-shot RLVR with a single example on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B boosted MATH500 accuracy from a base of **36.0%** to **73.6%** and improved average performance across six mathematical benchmarks from **17.6%** to **35.7%**.
- This performance was comparable to, or even slightly exceeded, results obtained using much larger datasets (e.g., a **1.2k** DeepScaleR subset or the **7.5k** MATH train set), demonstrating remarkable data efficiency.
- The improvements generalized across different mathematical categories and were robust across various base LLMs (including Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama3.2-3B-Instruct) and different RL algorithms (GRPO and PPO).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs possess significant latent reasoning capabilities that 1-shot RLVR can effectively activate with extremely sparse data, suggesting RLVR primarily acts as a catalyst for existing skills rather than teaching entirely new ones.
> - The phenomenon of "post-saturation generalization" was identified, where test performance continues to improve long after training accuracy saturates and even when the model overfits, distinguishing it from traditional "grokking."
> - Policy gradient loss is the primary driver of reasoning improvements, while entropy loss plays a critical role in promoting diverse exploration and enhancing sustained generalization across tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and increasingly scrutinized aspect of large language model development: data efficiency in post-training. Recent advancements in LLM reasoning, particularly in complex mathematical tasks, have often relied on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR). RLVR typically employs rule-based outcome rewards (e.g., binary correctness for a math problem) to fine-tune LLMs. While significant effort has been invested in refining RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, GRPO) to enhance RLVR performance and stability, the data-centric side—specifically, the quantity and quality of data required—has remained relatively underexplored.

The prevailing assumption in LLM training is "more data is better," with many efforts focused on curating large, high-quality mathematical reasoning datasets. This paper directly challenges this assumption by investigating the aggressive reduction of the RLVR training dataset. It builds upon earlier observations that base models already possess substantial latent reasoning capabilities that RLVR can "stimulate" or "enhance," sometimes leading to cognitive behaviors like self-reflection and improved generalization. Previous work like LIMR (Li et al., 2025) reduced training examples by sixfold while maintaining performance, but did not explore the extreme limits.

By demonstrating that even a single example can yield comparable performance to datasets with thousands of examples, this research makes a profound statement about the underlying mechanisms of RLVR and the nature of LLM reasoning. It suggests that RLVR might not primarily be about teaching new reasoning steps but rather about effectively activating pre-existing, dormant capabilities within the base model. This work contributes to a growing body of literature (e.g., Liu et al., 2025; Yue et al., 2025; Shah et al., 2025) that questions the necessity of massive datasets for certain LLM fine-tuning tasks and highlights the power of targeted, efficient interventions. It also offers a fresh perspective on phenomena like "grokking" by distinguishing its observed "post-saturation generalization" from the regularization-dependent nature of grokking.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is explicitly stated in the paper: **"To what extent can we reduce the training dataset for RLVR while maintaining comparable performance compared to using the full dataset?"**

This primary question is motivated by several key considerations:

- **Underexplored Data-Centric Aspects:** While algorithmic advancements in RLVR are abundant, the role of data quantity and quality remains less understood. The authors aim to fill this gap.
- **Efficiency in LLM Training:** Reducing the data requirements for effective RLVR training could lead to significant reductions in computational cost, data curation efforts, and overall resource consumption, making advanced LLM fine-tuning more accessible and sustainable.
- **Understanding LLM Mechanisms:** By probing the minimal data requirements, the researchers seek to gain deeper insights into how RLVR truly influences LLMs. They want to understand if LLMs are "learning new skills" or primarily "unlocking existing capabilities" when fine-tuned with RL.
- **Relationship to Empirical Phenomena:** The study aims to connect data quantity/quality with observed empirical phenomena in RLVR, such as the emergence of self-reflection and robust generalization across tasks, to better understand their underlying triggers.
- **Challenging Conventional Wisdom:** The work directly challenges the implicit assumption that large, diverse datasets are always indispensable for achieving high performance in complex reasoning tasks via RL fine-tuning.

In essence, the paper strives to demonstrate that extremely sparse data can still be highly effective in RLVR, thereby paving the way for more efficient and interpretable methods for improving LLM reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a rigorous experimental methodology to investigate the feasibility and mechanisms of 1-shot RLVR:

- **Core Approach: 1-shot RLVR:** The central idea involves training LLMs using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) but with an extremely small training dataset, specifically one or a few examples.
- **Base Models:** Experiments are primarily conducted on **Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B**, a relatively small yet capable mathematical reasoning model. To validate the generalizability of their findings, they also test on **Qwen2.5-Math-7B**, **Llama3.2-3B-Instruct**, and **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B**.
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** **GRPO (Group-normalized Proximal Policy Optimization)** is the default RL algorithm used, chosen for its effectiveness in RLVR settings. PPO is also used to verify findings on an alternative, widely used RL algorithm.
- **Loss Function Components:** The GRPO loss function comprises three main components:
 - **Policy Gradient Loss:** Encourages the model to generate responses with higher rewards, based on group-normalized advantages (better-than-average solutions are reinforced).
 - **KL Loss:** Maintains language quality by preventing drastic divergence from the reference model.
 - **Entropy Loss:** Applied with a negative coefficient, it promotes higher per-token entropy to encourage exploration and diverse reasoning paths.
- **Reward Mechanism:** A simple **binary (0-1) outcome reward** is used for mathematical problems, where a reward of 1 is given only if the model's final answer correctly matches the ground truth. A "format reward" baseline is also used, which grants a reward if the final answer can be parsed, regardless of correctness, to isolate the impact of format correction.
- **Data Selection for Few-Shot Examples:** For selecting the "one example" or "few examples," the authors propose a **Historical Variance Score (HVS)**. This involves initially training on a full dataset for a few epochs, calculating the historical variance of training accuracy for each example, and then selecting examples with high variance. This criterion is acknowledged as not necessarily optimal but is shown to be effective. For 1-shot training, the selected single example is duplicated to meet the minimum batch size requirement of the training framework.
- **Datasets for Comparison:**
 - **DSR-sub (DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset subset):** A subset of 1,209 examples from a high-quality mathematical reasoning dataset (DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset) is used for "full-set" RLVR comparisons.
 - **MATH Training Set:** The 7,500-instance MATH training set is used as another large-scale benchmark for full-set comparisons.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** Six widely-used and challenging benchmarks are employed: MATH500, AIME 2024, AMC 2023, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, and AIME 2025. Evaluation reports Pass@1, with small datasets (AIME, AMC) averaged over 8 repetitions (avg@8) for stability.
 - **Non-Mathematical Reasoning:** ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge are included to assess cross-domain generalization.
- **Ablation Studies:** Extensive ablation studies are conducted to dissect the contributions of individual components of the loss function (policy gradient, weight decay, KL loss, entropy loss) and to investigate the impact of label correctness and prompt modifications. This helps distinguish 1-shot RLVR's mechanism from phenomena like "grokking."
- **Qualitative Analysis:** The study includes qualitative observations such as changes in response length, frequency of self-reflection terms (e.g., "rethink," "recheck") in model outputs, and detailed examination of specific example solutions.

This comprehensive approach allows the authors to not only demonstrate the surprising effectiveness of 1-shot RLVR but also to provide initial insights into *why* it works and *what* underlying capabilities it activates in LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several striking findings that challenge conventional wisdom in LLM fine-tuning:

- **Remarkable Effectiveness of 1-shot RLVR:**
 - Using a single, well-chosen example (e.g., $\pi_{13}$ or $\pi_1$) for RLVR training on **Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B** significantly boosts performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks. For instance, MATH500 accuracy jumped from a base of 36.0% to 73.6% (an 8.6% improvement beyond format correction), and the average performance across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks improved from 17.6% to 35.7% (a 7.0% non-format gain).
 - This performance *matches* that obtained using the much larger 1.2k DeepScaleR subset (DSR-sub) and is comparable to training with the 7.5k MATH train set.
 - Training with just **two examples** ($\pi_1, \pi_{13}$) even slightly *exceeds* the performance of the 1.2k DSR-sub and matches the 7.5k MATH train set.
- **Robustness Across Models and Algorithms:** Similar substantial improvements are observed across various base models (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) and different RL algorithms (GRPO and PPO), confirming the broad applicability of the phenomenon.
- **Post-Saturation Generalization:** A novel phenomenon termed "post-saturation generalization" is identified. The training accuracy on the single example rapidly reaches near 100%, yet the model's test accuracy continues to improve significantly for many subsequent training steps. Furthermore, even after the model exhibits severe overfitting on the training example (producing "unintelligible multilingual gibberish"), its test performance remains strong, and its reasoning outputs for test examples remain human-interpretable. This occurs relatively late in training, after millions of rollouts.
- **Cross-Category Generalization:** Training on a single example from one mathematical category (e.g., Geometry) often enhances performance across diverse categories (e.g., Algebra, Number Theory) in the evaluation benchmarks. Counterintuitively, the improvements are not necessarily strongest in the same category as the training example.
- **Increased Self-Reflection:** As 1-shot RLVR training progresses, both the response length for the training example and the frequency of self-reflective terms (e.g., "rethink," "recheck," "recalculate") in outputs for downstream tasks increase. This suggests that RLVR stimulates more deliberate and complex reasoning processes.
- **Key Role of Policy Gradient Loss and Exploration:**
 - Ablation studies reveal that **policy gradient loss is the primary driver** of the observed improvements, distinguishing 1-shot RLVR from "grokking" which heavily relies on regularization methods like weight decay.
 - **Entropy loss** plays a critical role in promoting diverse exploration in model outputs, significantly enhancing post-saturation generalization. Even training with *only* entropy loss (without outcome reward) yields a performance boost, albeit weaker than reward-based training, suggesting it contributes to format correction.
- **Not-So-Difficult Examples:** The highly effective single examples ($\pi_1$, $\pi_{13}$) are problems that the base model can already solve the key steps for with high probability. RL fine-tuning appears to help the model consistently arrive at the correct final answer and format it appropriately.
- **Label Robustness and Prompt Sensitivity:** Slight inaccuracies in labels do not severely impact performance, but significantly incorrect yet "guessable" labels can be more detrimental than completely unguessable ones. Prompt complexity also matters; a simplified version of $\pi_1$ focusing only on the hardest arithmetic step yielded much worse results than the full problem statement, highlighting the importance of rich CoT (Chain-of-Thought) patterns.
- **Format Correction:** While 1-shot RLVR definitely improves output formatting (e.g., using `\boxed{}`), the non-format gain beyond simple format correction is substantial, indicating genuine reasoning improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research delivers highly significant findings with substantial potential impact across several dimensions of LLM development and understanding:

- **Paradigm Shift in Data Efficiency:** The most impactful finding is the demonstration that RLVR can be effective with an unprecedentedly small training dataset. This fundamentally challenges the "more data is better" paradigm in LLM fine-tuning and opens new avenues for highly data-efficient RL-based instruction following and reasoning improvements. It implies that for certain tasks, the bottleneck might not be data volume but rather the *quality* and *targetedness* of a few key examples.
- **Unlocking Latent Capabilities:** The results strongly support the hypothesis that powerful base LLMs already possess significant, albeit latent, reasoning capabilities. RLVR, even with minimal data, appears to act as a powerful catalyst to "ignite" or activate these dormant skills, rather than teaching them from scratch. This understanding can guide future pre-training and fine-tuning strategies to maximize inherent model potential.
- **Reduced Resource Demands:** If complex reasoning can be enhanced with one or two examples, it could dramatically reduce the computational costs, energy consumption, and human effort associated with data collection, annotation, and RL fine-tuning. This would make advanced LLM capabilities more accessible to researchers and developers with limited resources.
- **Deeper Understanding of RLVR:** The discovery of "post-saturation generalization" provides a novel empirical phenomenon distinct from "grokking." Its connection to policy gradient loss and entropy loss offers crucial insights into the mechanisms by which RL fine-tuning impacts generalization. This could inspire theoretical work to rigorously model and explain how sparse, high-reward signals propagate through a large neural network to induce such broad improvements.
- **Enhanced Data Selection and Curation:** The observation that specific examples are far more effective than others in 1-shot RLVR highlights the critical importance of data selection and curation. Future research can focus on developing advanced metrics or methods to identify "high-impact" examples that efficiently unlock reasoning capabilities, leading to smarter and more efficient dataset design for RLVR.
- **Inspiring New RL Algorithms:** The emphasis on the importance of exploration (via entropy loss) for sustained generalization, even in a 1-shot setting, could spur the development of RL algorithms specifically designed to promote beneficial exploration without sacrificing stability, particularly in resource-constrained environments.
- **Re-examination of LLM Evaluation:** The nuanced findings regarding format correction, cross-category generalization, and the subtle interplay of various loss components suggest a need for more sophisticated evaluation metrics that can disentangle different types of reasoning improvements and generalizability.

In conclusion, this paper presents a compelling case for the surprising power of minimal data in RLVR, offering both practical implications for efficiency and profound theoretical insights into the learning dynamics of large language models.
