# VPTwin: Real-Sim-Real Video Prediction for Robotic Manipulation Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> VPTwin is a Real-Sim-Real video prediction framework that anchors real-world future frames with synchronized simulation twins. A VLM builds an Isaac Sim digital twin from one demonstration. Ten randomized-physics rollouts of the candidate actions then condition a 3D U-Net predictor, which is co-trained on synthetic failures. The predictor checks VLM-proposed actions before execution. PSNR rises about 3 dB and real-robot drawer success reaches 5/5.

## Problem

- Purely data-driven action-conditioned video predictors accumulate compounding errors over long rollouts, hallucinating object interpenetration, floating artifacts, and spontaneous motion that mislead downstream action planning.
- Planning entirely inside simulators or digital twins inherits sim-to-real gaps in geometry, friction, and contact, so simulation-only critics falsely accept actions that fail on real hardware.
- Teleoperation datasets contain almost only successes, so video predictors grow over-optimistic and hallucinate success for flawed actions, while visual history alone cannot resolve unobserved physical parameters.

## Method

- ==Real-to-Sim== twin authoring: a frontier VLM parses demonstration keyframes, writes USD assets and parameter ranges, and filters rollouts by visual success criteria, then Isaac Sim replays actions across ten randomized pose/mass/friction seeds.
- **VPTwin-Core**: a two-stage latent video predictor whose ==3D U-Net== channel-concatenates ten time-aligned simulation rollout latents with noised observation history, ==FiLM==-conditioned on 14-D action windows, predicting frames autoregressively.
- ==Failure-aware co-training== plus staged planning loop: perturbed actions yield simulated pseudo-failure targets. At plan time a VLM proposes, IK converts, sim and VPTwin-Core jointly judge, and failures trigger up to three refinements.

## Results

- Full-episode prediction beats the IWS baseline on every metric: PSNR +**2.99 dB** on **548** measured-catalog episodes and +**3.32 dB** on **277** VLM-authored episodes, with LPIPS dropping from **0.310** to **0.259**.
- On real bimanual ALOHA hardware VPTwin succeeds **3**/**7** on Grasp Cup and **5**/**5** on Open and Close Drawer versus SIMPACT's **2**/**7** and **2**/**5**, with critic precision **1.00** versus **0.33**/**0.67**.
- Synthetic failure co-training adds **0.86-0.97 dB** full-rollout PSNR on drawer failure episodes. Training with ten rollouts beats training with three at an equal three-rollout test budget, **28.69** versus **27.82 dB**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulation works best as a multi-hypothesis conditioning prior for a learned predictor, not as ground truth, because randomized rollouts sidestep ill-posed identification of unobserved physical parameters.
> - Even outcome-mismatched failed simulation rollouts still supply useful geometric and articulation cues for rigid tasks, though they can hurt deformable-object prediction, so sim context degrades gracefully.
> - Verifiers trained on success-only data are optimistic. Cheap simulated counterfactual failures plus joint sim-and-video checking remove the false acceptances that simulation-only critics let through.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Predicting what a robot's action will look like before it acts is useful when the robot is handling a cup or a drawer. Two approaches each have a weakness. Long-horizon video predictions can drift into physically implausible states. Simulation-only plans can still fail on real contacts and friction. VPTwin runs the same action through several plausible simulated versions of the scene. It then uses those simulated futures as physics-informed references for predicting how the real scene will look. A planner checks a proposed action both in simulation and in that predicted real-looking future before committing to it.

Full-episode video prediction for six tasks. Each comparison shows ground truth, baseline, and VPTwin at 0%, 50%, and 100% of the timeline. Baseline examples include an absent robot arm in Wipe Table, lost drawer geometry in Open and Close Drawer, and a red cube rendered blue in Place Cube into Drawer. VPTwin's corresponding rows visibly retain these elements more closely. These are selected predictions, not the full test set.

### 2. Many plausible futures, one real prediction

The pipeline starts from a real demonstration. A vision-language model (VLM) reads the demonstration and authors an executable digital scene. Visual observations cannot uniquely reveal friction, mass, or other hidden dynamics. For that reason, Isaac Sim runs the same recorded 14-dimensional robot action sequence across randomized object placements and physical parameters. This produces randomized alternatives rather than one supposedly exact twin.

The video model, VPTwin-Core, combines these simulated clips with the real observation history and actions. The clips are time-aligned: the simulation and real-video clips correspond to the same moments and actions. VPTwin-Core uses a 3D spatiotemporal model to predict a real-domain future. It then feeds its prediction back autoregressively for the next step.

The decisive comparison is against IWS, an action-only baseline. The test is the measured-catalog video-prediction setting, and the metric is PSNR, a pixel-fidelity score where higher is better. VPTwin's full-episode PSNR was 28.57 ± 1.92 dB, compared with 25.58 ± 3.00 dB for IWS. These results are family-averaged across six task families in a 548-episode setting. Each family's scores were first averaged over five training seeds, and the mean and standard deviation were then calculated across families.

This is an aggregate comparison, not a claim that every individual task improved on every metric. The trend also holds in the four-family, 277-episode VLM-authored setting.

Overview of VPTwin. (A) Demonstrations pass through VLM twin authoring, Isaac Sim rollout, success filtering, and a randomized context bank. (B) Observation history and actions combine with time-aligned simulation contexts to predict real-domain future frames. (C) Staged VLM proposals, action-to-joint conversion, simulation and video checks, refinement, commitment, and deployment.

### 3. Predict, check, then commit

The planner works stage by stage:

1. The VLM proposes one symbolic action for a substage.
1. Inverse kinematics converts that action into a 14-D trajectory.
1. Isaac Sim runs candidate rollouts.
1. VPTwin-Core predicts the corresponding real-domain future.
1. The VLM judge accepts only if both the simulation and the video prediction meet the local success criterion. Otherwise, the VLM can refine the action up to three times.

Hardware execution happens after all six substages pass.

The comparison baseline is SIMPACT, a planner that checks proposed actions in simulation. On the Open and Close Drawer task, VPTwin succeeded in 5/5 trials versus 2/5 for SIMPACT. VPTwin also outperformed SIMPACT on Grasp Cup.

The authors also measured how well each planner's critic judged plans. Precision is the share of accepted plans that succeed physically. Recall is the share of physically successful plans that were accepted. Rejected plans were separately executed to measure critic agreement. SIMPACT's critic had five false acceptances: four on grasping and one on drawer articulation. VPTwin reported precision and F1 of 1.00 on both tested tasks.

These are small hardware evaluations, with five drawer trials and seven cup trials. They support the value of the whole VPTwin planning system. They do not isolate the video verifier as the sole cause. VPTwin also plans through six demonstration-informed substages, whereas SIMPACT tests whole-skill plans and lacks VPTwin's teleoperation guidance and video prediction.

### 4. Closing

VPTwin asks neither learned video nor an approximate simulator to stand alone. Simulated alternatives supply physical cues, and the video model predicts the real scene from them. The boundary is set by the authors' stated limitations: twin authoring assumes calibrated camera extrinsics and task-specific reconstruction, and synthetic failures do not span all unmodeled real dynamics.
