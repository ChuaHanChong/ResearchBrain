# CorrectionPlanner: Self-Correction Planner with Reinforcement Learning in Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Johns Hopkins University and XPENG Motors developed CorrectionPlanner, an autonomous driving system that integrates an explicit self-correction mechanism inspired by LLM reasoning. This system reduced collision rates by over 20% in Waymax reactive simulations and achieved state-of-the-art planning scores on the nuPlan dataset by iteratively refining unsafe motion proposals.

## Problem

- Learning-based autonomous driving planners often lack an inherent mechanism to evaluate and correct potentially unsafe actions before they are executed.
- Once an unsafe action is proposed, existing systems typically lack a pathway for internal revision, which can lead to the execution of hazardous trajectories.
- Imitation learning alone, based on expert data, is insufficient to teach planners how to handle unseen collision scenarios or effectively revise unsafe actions.

## Method

- Introduced **CorrectionPlanner**, an ==autoregressive self-correction mechanism== for autonomous driving planning that operates at the ==motion token level==.
- Developed a =="propose, evaluate, and correct" loop== where a learned ==critic== identifies unsafe motion proposals, which are then added to a =="correction trace"== to inform subsequent revised actions.
- Employed a ==two-stage training scheme== combining initial ==imitation learning== with ==model-based reinforcement learning==, utilizing a ==reactive multi-agent world model== for realistic rollouts.

## Results

- Reduced collision rates by over **20%** compared to the best baseline (SMART) in Waymax reactive simulations (from **2.36%** to **1.68%**), a significant safety improvement.
- Achieved consistently better overall planning scores across most nuPlan splits, outperforming various baselines and demonstrating state-of-the-art performance while maintaining competitive progression rates.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the explicit self-correction loop, particularly the use of the correction trace, was responsible for the primary safety gains, surpassing the performance of rejection sampling and candidate selection methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An explicit self-correction mechanism, adapted from LLM reasoning to the motion-token space, can significantly enhance safety by allowing autonomous driving planners to revise unsafe actions pre-execution.
> - Leveraging a "correction trace" (a history of rejected unsafe tokens) enables the policy to learn from past failures and shift its subsequent proposals away from unsafe regions more effectively than simple rejection sampling.
> - Combining imitation learning with model-based reinforcement learning and a reactive world model is crucial for teaching proactive self-correction and exploring counterfactual unsafe scenarios not present in expert data.
