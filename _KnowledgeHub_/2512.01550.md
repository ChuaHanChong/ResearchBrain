# NavForesee: A Unified Vision-Language World Model for Hierarchical Planning and Dual-Horizon Navigation Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> NavForesee, developed by Amap, Alibaba Group, introduces a unified Vision-Language Model that integrates hierarchical language planning with dual-horizon predictive world modeling for embodied navigation. The model achieved a 66.2% Success Rate and 78.4% Oracle Success Rate on the R2R-CE Val-Unseen benchmark, demonstrating enhanced long-term planning and environmental foresight.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation agents struggle with long-horizon tasks due to limited context windows and planning capabilities, frequently leading to disorientation.
- Existing navigation models are predominantly reactive, lacking the predictive foresight to anticipate future environmental states or visual milestones.
- A disconnection exists between VLM-centric planning (prone to semantic hallucinations) and world model-based prediction (prone to semantic drift) in current approaches.

## Method

- **NavForesee** is a unified VLM (based on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) combining VLM-driven ==hierarchical language planning== and world model-based ==dual-horizon== visual prediction.
- A specialized dataset was created using Gemini 2.5 Pro to train hierarchical VLM planning, enabling instruction decomposition into sub-goals and identification of keyframe visual milestones.
- The architecture integrates learnable 'dream queries' for both short-term (fixed 5-step horizon) and long-term (adaptive, milestone-based) depth and semantic feature prediction.

## Results

- NavForesee achieved a **66.2%** Success Rate (SR) and **78.4%** Oracle Success Rate (OSR) on R2R-CE Val-Unseen, representing improvements of **1.1%** SR and **10.9%** OSR over previous methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that hierarchical VLM planning, long-term prediction, and short-term prediction modules each contribute significantly to performance, with VLM planning causing the largest drop (SR to **48.8%**) when removed.
- The model demonstrated strong predictive capabilities, outperforming identity baselines in predicting environmental depth up to T+2 and high-level semantics up to T+3 (Cosine Similarity **0.62**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying VLM-driven hierarchical planning with a world model's dual-horizon prediction creates a robust perception-planning/prediction-action loop, mitigating issues like semantic hallucinations and drift.
> - Explicit decomposition of long instructions into sub-goals and tracking mission progress through visual milestones significantly improves an agent's ability to maintain long-term task understanding.
> - Anticipating both immediate environmental dynamics (short-term) and key visual features of future milestones (long-term) is crucial for proactive decision-making and coherent, goal-oriented navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation, particularly for long-horizon tasks guided by complex natural language instructions, remains a significant challenge in artificial intelligence. Recent advancements have been driven by Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which allow agents to perceive environments and interpret instructions. However, existing VLM-based agents frequently encounter limitations in long-term planning and memory, leading to an inability to maintain course or consistently make correct decisions over extended periods. This is often due to limited context windows and planning capabilities, causing agents to become disoriented. Furthermore, current models are predominantly reactive, lacking the predictive foresight to anticipate future environmental states proactively.

The broader research landscape has attempted to address these issues through two main avenues: enhancing VLM reasoning (e.g., via curated datasets or Chain-of-Thought prompting) and developing world models to predict future states for action planning. A notable gap in this landscape is the disconnection between these two paradigms. VLM-centric agents can suffer from semantic hallucinations, where their plans diverge from visual reality, while world models without linguistic guidance can experience semantic drift, where predictions lose coherence with the overall instructional goal. This paper introduces NavForesee to bridge this gap by unifying VLM planning and predictive world modeling within a single framework. It aims to integrate high-level language understanding and structured planning with the ability to "imagine" future environmental states, thereby offering a more coherent and robust approach to embodied navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a unified Vision-Language Model (VLM) capable of concurrently performing hierarchical language planning and predictive world model imagination for long-horizon embodied navigation tasks. The motivation stems from addressing two core limitations observed in existing navigation agents:

1. **Planning and Memory Deficit:** Current deployable VLMs often possess restricted context windows and planning capabilities. This limitation makes it difficult for agents to maintain a long-term understanding of complex natural language instructions and their progress within an unseen environment, frequently leading to task failures where the agent gets "lost." NavForesee aims to overcome this by enabling the VLM to explicitly decompose full instructions into manageable sub-goals and track mission progress.
2. **Lack of Predictive Foresight:** Many existing navigation models are reactive, making decisions based solely on current and historical observations. They lack the ability to anticipate future environmental dynamics or the visual characteristics of future milestones, which is crucial for proactive decision-making, obstacle avoidance, and robust long-term navigation. NavForesee addresses this by integrating a generative world model that provides dual-horizon predictive foresight: short-term environmental dynamics and long-term navigation milestones.

Inspired by human navigation, which is often hierarchical and milestone-based rather than a continuous, low-level process, the authors hypothesize that an artificial agent should operate similarly. By tightly coupling explicit language planning with implicit spatiotemporal prediction within a single VLM, NavForesee seeks to create an internal feedback loop of perception-planning/prediction-action, generating coherent, goal-oriented actions informed by both strategic milestones and immediate surroundings. This unification is posited to mitigate issues such as semantic hallucinations in VLM-centric agents and semantic drift in language-agnostic world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NavForesee is built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct backbone and incorporates two complementary modules: VLM-driven hierarchical planning and a world model-based dual-horizon visual prediction.

**A. VLM-driven Hierarchical Planning Dataset Generation:**
To train the hierarchical planning component, a specialized dataset was constructed from public Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) benchmarks, specifically R2R-CE (10k episodes) and RxR-CE (20k episodes). Each episode, comprising natural language instructions and corresponding image observation sequences, was processed using Gemini 2.5 Pro. A custom prompt template guided Gemini 2.5 Pro to:
- Decompose long instructions into sequential sub-instructions.
- Identify a dense visual chain of keyframe milestones, including intermediate waypoints for visual continuity, even for single sub-instructions or during extended travel/sharp turns.
- Standardize the output format to include the milestone frame index, a textual description of the completed sub-instruction, and the upcoming planned instruction.
Post-processing involved filtering incomplete annotations, correcting logical inconsistencies, and converting each episode into multiple navigation segments. Waypoints were sampled along each trajectory, with each waypoint serving as the endpoint of a segment between milestones. Each sampled waypoint was assigned a planning label consisting of a navigation summary (completed sub-instruction), a future plan (next instruction), and a language action (forward, left, right, stop). This process yielded approximately 1.3M training samples from RxR-CE and 0.2M from R2R-CE. Over-represented straight-motion cases were down-sampled, and stop-action examples were augmented to balance the training data.

**B. Model Architecture and Training:**
The Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model serves as the backbone, utilizing its original multimodal encoders and text decoder for hierarchical planning. For world model prediction and action policy learning, additional components are integrated:
- **Position Encoder:** Encodes the agent’s relative position and orientation from image observations.
- **Dream Queries:** Two sets of learnable dream queries, short-term ($Q_S$) and long-term ($Q_L$), are introduced. Each set comprises depth and semantics (DINOv2 and SAM features) subqueries, specialized for prediction at distinct horizons. These queries, along with textual instruction embeddings and visual observation sequences, are processed by the Qwen2.5-VL backbone.
- **Action Query:** A learnable action query ($Q_a$) is introduced to enhance task-relevant context encoding for action prediction.
- **Structured Attention Mask:** A specific attention mask is designed to ensure a clear separation between short- and long-horizon predictions and prevent cross-type contamination. Long-horizon predictions are conditioned on short-horizon predictions for temporal coherence, and mutual attention between depth and semantics queries is masked. The action query attends to all available information (past context and both horizons of dream queries) for globally consistent navigation predictions.
- **Lightweight Decoders:** These decoders interpret the dream query embeddings into predicted depth ($d_p$) and high-level semantics ($c_p$). A 2-layer ViT-based decoder is used, and a VQ-VAE decoder renders depth features into depth maps.
- **Action Prediction Head:** An MLP with two linear layers and a ReLU activation processes the action embedding, extracted through the Qwen2.5-VL backbone, to generate continuous waypoints and binary flags for stopping. Action predictions are primarily conditioned on the dual-horizon predictive features.

**C. Dual-horizon World Model Prediction:**
- **Short-term Prediction:** Forecasts environmental features (depth and semantics) for a fixed horizon of $k=5$ steps ahead.
- **Long-term Prediction:** Targets navigation milestones, with an adaptive horizon ($M_t$) determined by progress toward the next milestone. This allows the model to anticipate key visual features at the completion of the current sub-instruction.
The model learns to align the long-term predictions with sub-goal intent through interleaved training of planning and prediction tasks.

**D. Close the Planning/Prediction and Action Loop:**
Training involves optimizing hierarchical planning and world model objectives in a unified manner by interleaving their respective training data.
- **Hierarchical Planning Training:** The model is finetuned auto-regressively on the constructed planning dataset using Qwen2.5-VL's original components.
- **World Model Training:** The training tasks include depth prediction, semantic feature prediction, and action prediction. The overall loss function ($L$) is a weighted sum of:
 - Depth prediction error ($L_d$): Measured using Scale-invariant Logarithmic Loss (SiLogLoss) at the pixel level.
 - Semantic feature prediction error ($L_c$): Computed using Mean Squared Error (MSE).
 - Action error ($L_a$): Computed using MSE.
 $L = \alpha L_d + \beta L_c + L_a$, where $\alpha=0.25$ and $\beta=0.3$ are weighting hyperparameters. The model is trained for 3 epochs on 64 NVIDIA H20 GPUs, with ViT parameters frozen.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods:**
NavForesee was evaluated on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks, specifically on their Val-Unseen splits.
- **R2R-CE Val-Unseen:** NavForesee achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 66.2%, an Oracle Success Rate (OSR) of 78.4%, a Navigation Error (NE) of 3.94 meters, and a Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 59.7%. This represents an improvement in SR by 1.1%, OSR by 10.9%, and a reduction in NE by 0.3 meters compared to previously published methods (e.g., CorrectNav, Stream VLN, NaVILA).
- **RxR-CE Val-Unseen:** NavForesee achieved an SR of 66.3%, OSR of 53.2%, NE of 4.20 meters, and SPL of 53.2%. While showing competitive performance, it performs slightly below the highest SR achieved by some state-of-the-art methods on this dataset (e.g., CorrectNav). The authors attribute this difference to NavForesee being trained solely on R2R-CE and RxR-CE, whereas other methods often leverage diverse and large-scale datasets for generalization. Notably, NavForesee achieved the highest OSR across both datasets, suggesting its underlying ability to find successful paths when given optimal actions.

**B. Ablation Study:**
- **Contribution of Key Modules (Table II):** Removing any of the three core modules—hierarchical VLM planning, long-term prediction, or short-term prediction—resulted in performance degradation.
 - Full NavForesee model: SR 66.2%, OSR 78.4%, NE 3.94, SPL 59.7%.
 - Without VLM planning: SR dropped to 48.8%, and SPL decreased by over 16 points, indicating the importance of explicit instruction decomposition.
 - Without long-term prediction: SR dropped to 58.6%, highlighting the role of milestone foresight.
 - Without short-term prediction: SR dropped to 47.8%, further emphasizing the need for immediate environmental awareness.
 - Removing all three modules led to the most significant deterioration.
- **Short-term Horizon (Table III):** Varying the short-term prediction horizon ($k$) from 3 to 5 (waypoint length) showed that matching the horizon to the action space ($k=5$) yielded optimal performance (SR 66.2%, SPL 59.7%).
- **Depth and Semantics Predictions (Table V):** Both depth and semantics predictions contributed to navigation performance.
 - Without depth prediction: SR dropped to 61.8% and SPL decreased by 4.8 points.
 - Without semantics predictions: SR dropped to 60.0% and SPL decreased by 6.8 points.
 - The results suggest that high-quality semantic foresight is more critical for navigation performance than short-term geometric precision, as indicated by a larger drop in SR when semantics are removed.

**C. Quantitative Prediction Quality (Table IV):**
Evaluation of prediction quality (target at T+5) on R2R Val-Unseen against identity baselines (copying GT at T+i as prediction for T+5) showed:
- **Depth:** NavForesee's depth predictions outperformed identity baselines up to T+2, indicating the model's ability to capture short-term geometric dynamics.
- **Semantics (DINOv2):** NavForesee achieved a Cosine Similarity (CosSim) of 0.62, significantly outperforming identity baselines up to T+3. This suggests a strong capability in predicting high-level semantic evolution further into the future.

**D. Qualitative Analysis (Figures 4, 5, 6, 7):**
- **Short-term Predictions (Figure 4):** The model generated depth and semantic feature predictions for up to five future steps. Although depth maps appeared coarse, they preserved global geometry and spatial layout, capturing movements like ascending stairs or entering rooms. Semantic predictions, visualized via a pretrained segmentation head, aligned with ground truth and reflected dynamic environmental changes.
- **Geometric-Semantic Feature Imagination (Figure 5):** NavForesee demonstrated an ability to anticipate semantic features across various motion patterns. It accurately predicted environmental dynamics in straight motion, generalized effectively to turning scenarios, and could infer detailed object geometry and depth distribution from minimal visual input, such as a brief glance into a room, suggesting an capacity to reason about unseen spatial regions.
- **Hierarchical Planning (Figure 6):** The model accurately identified milestones, summarized completed sub-instructions, and formulated subsequent sub-instructions in alignment with the overall context, confirming its multimodal understanding and task decomposition abilities.
- **Long-term Depth Predictions (Figure 7):** Long-term depth predictions, while potentially less precise than short-term ones due to the adaptive and longer horizon, maintained overall scene layout and depth distribution, providing strategic guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NavForesee introduces a unified framework that tightly integrates hierarchical language planning with dual-horizon predictive world modeling within a single Vision-Language Model. This approach addresses a recognized gap in embodied navigation research, where VLM-centric planning and world model-based prediction have often been treated as separate concerns. By forming an implicit perception–planning and prediction–action loop, NavForesee enables agents to leverage both high-level linguistic understanding for strategic guidance and foresight into future environmental states for robust execution.

The quantitative results, particularly the competitive Success Rate and the highest Oracle Success Rate across R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks, indicate the efficacy of this unified design. The ablation studies further reinforce the individual and synergistic contributions of VLM planning, short-term prediction, and long-term prediction. Qualitatively, the model's ability to anticipate future depth and semantic features, even under complex motion patterns or limited initial observation, demonstrates a capacity for environmental imagination that could enhance an agent's adaptability and resilience in dynamic and unseen environments.

The significance of this work lies in its potential to advance the development of more intelligent and capable embodied agents. By equipping agents with the ability to "foresee" future states, NavForesee paves the way for systems that can navigate complex, long-horizon tasks with greater autonomy, reduce failure rates due to planning deficits or reactive behaviors, and improve generalization to novel environments. This integration of explicit planning with implicit spatiotemporal prediction represents a step towards embodied AI that can reason, predict, and act in a more human-like, proactive manner.
