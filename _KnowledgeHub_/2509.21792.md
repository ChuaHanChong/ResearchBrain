# FastGRPO: Accelerating Policy Optimization via Concurrency-aware Speculative Decoding and Online Draft Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> FastGRPO introduces a framework that accelerates Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training for Large Language Models via concurrency-aware speculative decoding and online draft learning. The approach achieves end-to-end speedups of 2.35x to 2.72x and generation phase speedups of 2.62x to 3.04x across diverse models and datasets.

## Problem

- Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training for LLMs suffers from excessive latency, with the autoregressive generation phase consuming 91% to 98% of total training time.
- Direct application of conventional speculative decoding to GRPO's high-concurrency, high-latency training conditions provides limited speedup and can degrade performance.
- Distributional drift between an evolving target LLM and a fixed draft model causes a progressive decline in token acceptance rates and acceleration gains for speculative decoding during continuous RL training.

## Method

- **FastGRPO** proposes a concurrency-aware ==speculative decoding== framework that dynamically adjusts decoding parameters (e.g., number of verified tokens, draft tree size) based on real-time batch concurrency and hardware operational intensity.
- An online draft learning mechanism continuously updates the draft model using "free" supervision signals (hidden states and logits) cached from the target model's generation process.
- The framework integrates these components into an iterative training loop, where the target model generates responses, and both the draft and target models are updated to maintain alignment and efficiency.

## Results

- FastGRPO achieved end-to-end speedups ranging from **2.35x** to **2.72x** and generation phase speedups of **2.62x** to **3.04x** across various LLMs and mathematical reasoning datasets.
- The framework substantially outperformed existing speculative decoding methods, which typically yielded only **1.09x** to **1.31x** end-to-end speedups when directly applied to GRPO training.
- Online draft learning increased generation speedup by approximately **0.7x** to **0.9x** by sustaining high token acceptance rates and demonstrated that initial draft model pretraining might be unnecessary due to rapid online adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Speculative decoding parameters require dynamic adjustment based on real-time batch concurrency and hardware operational intensity (C_peak) to achieve optimal acceleration in GRPO's high-latency, variable-length generation phase.
> - Continuously updating the draft model online with feedback from the evolving target model is critical to mitigate distributional drift and sustain high token acceptance rates throughout RL training.
> - Supervision signals for the online draft model learning can be derived from the target model's own output (hidden states, logits) with minimal additional computational overhead, making the adaptation efficient.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant challenge in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuning, specifically within the domain of reinforcement learning (RL) for enhancing reasoning capabilities. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is presented as a promising framework that improves LLM reasoning by utilizing group-level feedback signals during training. However, the computational intensity of GRPO, particularly its reliance on autoregressive generation of multiple responses per query, renders its training process excessively slow. This latency bottleneck limits the practical deployment and empirical exploration of GRPO.

Concurrently, speculative decoding has emerged as an efficient technique for accelerating autoregressive inference in LLMs. This method typically involves a smaller "draft model" to propose candidate token sequences, which are then verified in parallel by the larger "target model," reducing the number of full forward passes required by the target model. While effective in low-concurrency, low-latency scenarios, the paper identifies that its direct application to the high-concurrency, high-latency conditions of GRPO training yields limited benefits and can even degrade performance. This limitation arises because speculative decoding, despite alleviating memory bandwidth pressure, introduces computational overhead that can shift the system from a memory-bound to a compute-bound regime under high concurrency.

Furthermore, existing speculative decoding approaches often face issues of distributional drift. As the target model continuously updates its parameters during RL training, a fixed draft model becomes progressively misaligned, leading to decreased token acceptance rates and diminishing acceleration gains over time.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical potential of GRPO and its practical efficiency by tailoring speculative decoding specifically for the dynamic, high-concurrency environment of GRPO training. It also addresses the issue of draft model obsolescence by integrating an online adaptation mechanism. By tackling these issues, the work contributes to making advanced RL fine-tuning techniques for LLMs more viable for deployment and research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly accelerate the training process of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for Large Language Models (LLMs) without compromising model performance. This overarching objective is driven by several specific motivations and challenges identified within the current LLM fine-tuning landscape:

- **Addressing the GRPO Training Bottleneck:** GRPO has demonstrated efficacy in improving LLM reasoning, but its practical adoption is severely hampered by its low training throughput. Empirical analysis shows that the "generation phase," where the LLM autoregressively generates multiple responses per query, consumes 91% to 98% of the total training time across various models and datasets. This makes the generation phase the principal performance bottleneck, necessitating optimization to make GRPO a more practical framework. The cost of generation scales with output sequence length, and as models mature, they tend to generate longer, more complex outputs, further exacerbating this issue. Additionally, the prevalence of low-reward-variance rollouts in GRPO, which are discarded, reduces data efficiency and increases the effective inference cost.

- **Overcoming Limitations of Direct Speculative Decoding Application:** Speculative decoding is a promising technique for accelerating autoregressive inference. However, its direct application to GRPO's generation phase yields limited speedup compared to its performance in other contexts, and in some cases, can even lead to performance degradation. This is attributed to a mismatch between the typical evaluation settings for speculative decoding (low-concurrency, low-latency) and the operational conditions during GRPO training (high-concurrency, high-latency). Under high concurrency, the computational overhead introduced by speculative decoding can shift the system from a memory-bound state to a compute-bound state, thereby neutralizing anticipated performance gains.

- **Managing Intra-Batch Variance in Response Lengths:** The group-based relative advantage mechanism within GRPO encourages variability in response lengths generated within a single batch. Analysis reveals significant differences in sequence lengths across a batch (e.g., maximum length 3 to 5 times the minimum), leading to a dynamic shift from high to low effective concurrency as sequences complete at different times. This dynamic environment renders fixed speculative decoding parameters suboptimal, as an optimal configuration for high initial concurrency may be inefficient for later low-concurrency stages within the same batch.

- **Mitigating Distributional Drift of the Draft Model:** During GRPO training, the target LLM undergoes continuous parameter updates, causing its output token distribution to evolve. If a fixed draft model is used for speculative decoding throughout this process, its alignment with the evolving target model deteriorates over time. This "distributional drift" results in a progressive reduction of the average token acceptance length during speculative decoding, consequently diminishing the acceleration gains and eventually undermining the efficiency benefits.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes a dual strategy: a **concurrency-aware speculative decoding framework** to adapt to the dynamic concurrency levels and optimize generation speed, and an **online draft learning mechanism** to continuously align the draft model with the evolving target model, thereby sustaining high token acceptance rates and acceleration throughout training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FastGRPO framework integrates two main mechanisms to accelerate GRPO training: a concurrency-aware speculative decoding strategy and an online draft learning mechanism. The overall procedure involves an iterative loop where the target model generates responses using the accelerated decoding, and then both the draft and target models are updated.

#### 3.1. Concurrency-Aware Speculative Decoding for GRPO Training

The motivation for this component stems from the observation that speculative decoding's performance is sensitive to concurrency levels, and GRPO training involves dynamically changing batch concurrency due to variable response lengths.

- **Operational Intensity Analysis:** The method begins with an analysis of operational intensity, defined as the ratio of computation (FLOPs) to memory traffic (bytes). For General Matrix Multiplication (GEMM), a core operation in Transformers, operational intensity scales approximately linearly with batch size (B). The authors introduce `C_peak`, an empirically determined, hardware- and architecture-dependent constant representing the batch size at which a GPU reaches its practical peak operational intensity, transitioning from memory-bound to compute-bound. This `C_peak` is derived through profiling forward pass latency across varying batch sizes.

- **Adaptive Parameter Adjustment:** To maintain optimal hardware utilization by keeping the system at the compute-memory balance point, the strategy dynamically adjusts key speculative decoding parameters based on the current effective batch size (`B_cur`).
 - **Number of Verified Tokens (`N_verify`):** The number of tokens verified by the target model per sequence is set as:
 `N_verify = C_peak / B_cur`
 This ensures that the effective batch size for GEMM operations during the verification stage (`N_verify × B_cur`) remains approximately constant at `C_peak`, thus maximizing hardware utilization.
 - **Draft Tree Size (`K_draft`):** `K_draft` represents the number of candidate tokens proposed by the draft model per forward pass (branching factor). It is configured as:
 `K_draft = min(N_verify - 1, K_max_draft)`
 The `N_verify - 1` term accounts for the fact that one token (the root of the draft tree) is generated by the target model in the previous step. The `K_max_draft` upper bound prevents excessive branching, which could lead to wasted computation on unverified candidates and increased overhead.
 - **Drafting Steps (`L_draft`):** `L_draft` is the number of sequential drafting steps, influencing the depth of the draft tree. It is proposed to be positively correlated with `log2(N_verify)`:
 `L_draft = min(log2(N_verify / alpha), L_max_draft)`
 Here, `alpha` is a hyperparameter reflecting the draft model's approximation quality (a stronger draft model has a smaller `alpha`, allowing deeper speculation). `L_max_draft` is an upper bound to prevent diminishing returns and increased overhead from excessively deep trees where prediction fidelity degrades.

#### 3.2. Draft Model Learning with Online Target Feedback

This component addresses the problem of distributional drift between the evolving target model and a fixed draft model during GRPO training.

- **Online Adaptation:** At each GRPO iteration, the draft model (`π_d`) is updated online using feedback signals derived from the current target model (`π_θ`). This continuous adaptation allows the draft model to maintain alignment with the evolving target policy.
- **Supervision Signal:** The responses and corresponding hidden states generated by the target model during the generation phase are naturally produced and can be cached. These serve as "free" supervision signals for training the draft model, incurring negligible additional computational overhead (approximately 2% to 3% of total training cost). This contrasts with conventional pre-training on static, offline datasets.
- **Draft Model Architecture and Verification:** The draft model architecture is adapted from EAGLE Li et al. (2024b), which uses feature sampling and partial alignment distillation to train a standalone model that autoregressively generates hidden states. These hidden states are then decoded into tokens using the target LLM's language model head. For verification, the adaptive token tree verification strategy from EAGLE-2 Li et al. (2024a) is adopted, where the draft model expands a candidate token tree, and the target model evaluates branches in parallel to accept the longest matching path.

#### 3.3. FastGRPO Framework (Algorithm 1)

The overall FastGRPO framework integrates these components:
1. **Batch Sampling and Prefill:** A batch of prompts is sampled, and the target model prefills the batch.
2. **Concurrency-Aware Speculative Decoding Loop:** This loop continues until all sequences in the batch have generated an End-Of-Sequence (EOS) token.
 - At each step, `N_verify`, `K_draft`, and `L_draft` are dynamically adjusted based on the current effective batch size (`B_cur`).
 - The draft model expands a candidate token tree (`L_draft` steps, `K_draft` paths).
 - `N_verify` tokens are selected from the candidates, reranked by confidence, and verified in parallel by the target model using tree-structured attention.
 - Accepted tokens and hidden states are extracted, and generated sequences are updated. `B_cur` is adjusted as sequences complete.
3. **Online Draft Model Updating:** The draft model (`π_d`) is updated using the `S_generated` sequences and their corresponding hidden states obtained from the target model's verification phase.
4. **Policy Training Phase:** Rewards are computed for the generated sequences (`S_generated`), and the target policy model (`π_θ`) is updated using standard GRPO policy optimization.

This alternating training allows the draft model to continuously adapt to the evolving target model while the target model learns from group-level feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of FastGRPO was conducted on various LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-I) across three mathematical reasoning datasets of varying difficulty (GSM8K, SimpleRL-Abel-Level3to5, DAPO-Math-17K). The key findings are summarized below:

- **Overall End-to-End Speedup:** FastGRPO consistently achieved substantial end-to-end speedups ranging from **2.35x to 2.72x** across different models and datasets. This indicates a significant improvement in the overall efficiency of the GRPO training pipeline.

- **Generation Phase Speedup:** The method demonstrated generation speedup ratios (Gen SR) ranging from **2.62x to 3.04x**, significantly accelerating the most time-consuming part of GRPO training.

- **Comparison to Baseline Speculative Decoding Methods:**
 - Standard autoregressive decoding served as a baseline with a 1.00x speedup ratio.
 - Existing speculative decoding methods (EAGLE-2, HASS, EAGLE-3), when directly applied, yielded modest end-to-end speedups typically ranging from 1.09x to 1.31x. FastGRPO substantially outperformed these baselines, highlighting the limitations of direct application in GRPO's high-concurrency environment. For example, on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, FastGRPO achieved an average E2E SR of 2.49x, while EAGLE-3 achieved 1.20x.

- **Ablation Study: Online Draft Learning:**
 - The online draft learning strategy was found to significantly enhance computational efficiency, increasing the generation speedup ratio by approximately **0.7x to 0.9x** across different experimental settings.
 - This mechanism led to a steadily increasing average accepted length of speculative tokens over training epochs, contrasting with the performance decay observed when a static draft model was used.
 - The computational overhead of online draft learning was minimal, contributing only 2% to 3% of the total training cost, largely because supervision signals (hidden states and logits) were "free" due to being cached during the target model's generation phase.
 - Furthermore, experiments showed that online draft learning maintained comparable performance to pre-trained draft models on general-purpose tasks (MT-bench, HumanEval) while achieving substantial in-domain gains (e.g., a 0.34 increase in speedup ratio on GSM8K), indicating improved alignment without compromising versatility.

- **Ablation Study: Concurrency-Aware Speculative Decoding:**
 - Vanilla autoregressive generation with early termination provided moderate generation speedups (1.22x to 1.68x). However, it could not fully exploit computational headroom as batch concurrency reduced.
 - FastGRPO without the concurrency-aware strategy (i.e., using fixed speculative decoding parameters) achieved lower speedups (e.g., 2.27x Gen SR on GSM8K) compared to the full FastGRPO (2.66x Gen SR on GSM8K).
 - The dynamic adjustment of decoding parameters based on real-time concurrency consistently delivered higher speedup ratios across all evaluated tasks, validating the efficacy of the concurrency-aware mechanism.

- **Ablation Study: Need for Draft Model Pretraining:**
 - Experiments indicated that initial pretraining of the draft model might be unnecessary in many cases for GRPO acceleration. Without pretraining, the initial token acceptance length was lower but rapidly increased and converged to levels comparable to those of a pretrained draft model within 1-2 epochs due to online draft learning.

- **Transfer Experiments on GRPO Variants:**
 - The acceleration framework demonstrated its generality by achieving significant speedups (over 2x end-to-end) when applied to GRPO variants such as DAPO and GPG. This indicates broad applicability beyond the standard GRPO framework.

In summary, the research presents quantitative evidence that the proposed concurrency-aware speculative decoding and online draft learning mechanisms collectively provide substantial and consistent acceleration to GRPO training across a range of LLMs and mathematical reasoning tasks, significantly outperforming existing speculative decoding approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, FastGRPO, that addresses critical efficiency challenges in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a method for enhancing Large Language Models' (LLMs) reasoning capabilities. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Practical Deployment of GRPO:** The most direct impact is the significant acceleration of GRPO training, which was previously impeded by excessive computational costs. By achieving end-to-end speedups of 2.35x to 2.72x, FastGRPO makes GRPO a more practical and accessible method for fine-tuning LLMs for complex reasoning tasks. This can lead to broader adoption of GRPO in research and development, as the time and resource costs become more manageable.

- **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** GRPO is a specific RL-based fine-tuning technique. The acceleration provided by FastGRPO can catalyze further research into RL applications for LLMs. Faster training iterations allow researchers to conduct more experiments, explore larger model architectures, and develop more sophisticated RL algorithms without prohibitive computational overhead. This contributes to the broader field of alignment and capability enhancement of LLMs through reinforcement learning.

- **Extending Speculative Decoding to Dynamic and High-Concurrency Scenarios:** The work successfully adapts speculative decoding, traditionally optimized for low-concurrency inference, to the complex and dynamic environment of high-concurrency, variable-length batch processing characteristic of GRPO training. The introduction of concurrency-aware dynamic parameter adjustment for speculative decoding is a methodological advancement that can be relevant beyond GRPO, potentially benefiting other batch-inference scenarios where dynamic concurrency is present.

- **Mitigating Model Drift in Fine-tuning:** The online draft learning mechanism effectively addresses the problem of distributional drift between an evolving target model and a static draft model. This mechanism maintains the draft model's predictive accuracy and, consequently, high token acceptance rates throughout the entire training process. This approach is a generalizable solution for ensuring the continued effectiveness of auxiliary models (like draft models) in iterative training pipelines where the primary model undergoes continuous updates.

- **Improving Resource Utilization:** By dynamically adjusting speculative decoding parameters based on real-time concurrency and aligning them with the `C_peak` operational intensity, FastGRPO optimizes the utilization of GPU resources. This leads to more efficient use of computational and memory bandwidth, which is critical for expensive LLM training workloads.

- **Broad Applicability:** The demonstration of consistent speedups across multiple LLM families, diverse mathematical reasoning datasets, and different GRPO variants (DAPO, GPG) indicates the robustness and generalizability of the FastGRPO framework. This suggests that the proposed techniques are not narrowly tailored to a specific setup but have wider applicability within the LLM ecosystem.

In summary, FastGRPO removes a significant barrier to the widespread use of GRPO, fostering innovation in LLM reasoning capabilities. It contributes novel solutions for adapting speculative decoding to complex training environments and maintaining auxiliary model alignment during iterative fine-tuning, thereby impacting both the efficiency of current LLM practices and the trajectory of future research in reinforcement learning for language models.
