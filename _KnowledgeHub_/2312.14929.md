# MACS: Mass Conditioned 3D Hand and Object Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> MACS is the first mass-conditioned 3D hand-object motion synthesis method. Cascaded denoising diffusion models generate an object trajectory from a mass value and action label, then two-hand joint motion plus per-vertex contact probabilities, followed by fitting optimization. A RatioNet module re-times user-drawn trajectories by mass. It beats VAE and VAEGAN baselines in diversity, physical plausibility, and user-study realism, and generalizes to unseen masses.

## Problem

- Static grasp synthesis methods produce single frames only, lacking the scene dynamics and temporal continuity that AR/VR, avatar, and character animation applications require.
- Sequential manipulation methods ignore object mass entirely, so grasps and trajectories never adapt to weight, unlike real manipulation where heavy objects recruit the whole palm.
- Physics-simulation grasping approaches depend on a predetermined target grasp pose and deterministic outputs, giving no explicit mass control and limited motion diversity.

## Method

- **TrajDiff**, a ==diffusion model== conditioned on mass and a one-hot action label, synthesizes object trajectories; rotations are emitted as six reference-vertex 3D positions, ==Procrustes-aligned== to templates to avoid jitter.
- **HandDiff** synthesizes two-hand joints from trajectory and mass under position, velocity, acceleration, and bone-length losses, while **ConNet**, a 1D CNN, predicts per-vertex contacts feeding a ==GHUM== fitting optimization with touch, collision, and pose-prior terms.
- **RatioNet**, an MLP, re-times user-drawn trajectories: it resamples them to 720 points and predicts per-step travel ratios from mass and total path length, yielding a normalized target trajectory.

## Results

- On a captured **110k**-frame dataset of five equal-size spheres spanning **0.175-4.9 kg**, MACS reaches **97.84%** non-collision, **0.041 mm** penetration, **1.97%** non-touching, beating VAE and VAEGAN.
- Diversity and multimodality reach **9.606/7.07** for hands and **11.01/8.05** for trajectories, above both baselines; **42** users rated realism **6.01** versus **5.10** and **4.54**, with ground truth **7.10**.
- Ablations: removing the contact loss raises non-touching to **63.3%**, removing the collision loss drops non-collision to **38.41%**; mass conditioning cuts acceleration Wasserstein distance roughly sevenfold, and **92.8%** of users identified the heavier object.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning a generative motion model on a single physical scalar such as mass induces qualitatively different contact strategies: fingertips for light objects, whole palm for heavy ones.
> - Predicting contact probabilities as an intermediate representation, then optimizing against them, is what buys shape generalization; contacts transfer to object shapes never seen in training.
> - Synthesizing rotation as Cartesian reference-vertex positions instead of rotation parameters removes jitter, a reusable trick when one model must output mixed translation-rotation representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of 3D motion synthesis, particularly concerning human-object interaction, is an active area of research driven by its applications in augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), character animation, and robotics. Prior work has addressed various aspects of hand-object interaction, including static grasp synthesis, dynamic object manipulation sequences, 3D reconstruction of interactions, grasp refinement, and contact prediction.

Traditional approaches to grasp synthesis often focus on static frames or specific object manipulations, but they generally do not account for the physical properties of the object beyond its geometry. Recent advancements have seen the application of denoising diffusion models in general motion synthesis for 3D characters, offering improvements in quality and diversity compared to earlier generative models like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs).

This work identifies a specific gap in the existing literature: the explicit consideration of an object's mass in synthesizing 3D hand-object interactions. While real-world manipulation is significantly influenced by mass (e.g., using fingertips for light objects versus the entire palm for heavy objects), prior state-of-the-art methods for generating continuous interaction sequences have not incorporated this physical property. MACS positions itself as the first learning-based approach to address this omission, integrating mass as a conditioning factor for generating more physically plausible and natural 3D hand-object motions.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize natural and physically plausible 3D hand-object motions that dynamically adjust based on the object's mass and the type of interaction. This aims to overcome the limitations of previous methods that neglect object physics, which often results in unrealistic or unnatural animations.

The motivation for pursuing this objective is multifaceted:

- **Enhancing Realism in Applications**: Physically plausible interactions are crucial for achieving realism in graphics applications such as AR/VR, avatar communication, and character animation for computer games. Current methods often produce motions that lack the nuanced adjustments humans make when interacting with objects of different weights, leading to visual artifacts like "floating objects" or improper contact.
- **Addressing a Neglected Aspect of Interaction**: The authors highlight that object mass substantially influences how humans manipulate objects, affecting hand posture, contact area, and overall motion dynamics. For example, grasping a heavy object typically involves a larger contact area and more supportive hand poses compared to a light object. This fundamental aspect of human interaction has not been explicitly modeled in prior 3D motion synthesis research.
- **Reducing Manual Animation Effort**: Manually creating animations that accurately reflect varying object masses is a tedious and skill-intensive process. A method capable of generating such mass-conditioned interactions automatically can significantly streamline animation workflows for artists and developers.
- **Providing Flexible Control**: The research also aims to offer flexibility in motion generation. Beyond purely synthesizing motions, MACS is designed to accept user-provided 3D object trajectories, allowing animators tighter control while still conditioning the synthesized hand motions on object mass. This capability supports diverse downstream applications, including generating synthetic training data for machine learning tasks, rapid prototyping of animations, and creating interactive experiences.
- **Generalization Capabilities**: A key objective is to demonstrate that the proposed method can generalize effectively. This includes generating plausible interactions for object mass values interpolated or extrapolated from the training data, as well as showing moderate generalization to objects with unseen shapes, leveraging mass-conditioned contact predictions.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The MACS framework is a learning-based approach centered on cascaded denoising diffusion models (DDMs) to synthesize 3D hand and object motion sequences conditioned on object mass.

#### 3.1. Assumptions and Modeling

- **Objects**: Represented as meshes.
- **Hands**: Represented using the GHUM (Generative 3D Human Shape and Articulated Pose Models) parametric model, which provides a consistent topology for 3D hand meshes, vertices, and joints. Two GHUM models are used for left and right hands.
- **Poses**: Object pose is defined by 3D translation and 6D rotation. Hand translations are relative to the object center, and rotations are relative to a fixed world frame. The output is a sequence of hand vertices/joints and object poses.

#### 3.2. Denoising Diffusion Model (DDM)

- MACS employs DDPMs, a generative model known for its training simplicity and ability to generate high-quality, diverse samples.
- The process involves a forward diffusion (adding Gaussian noise over T steps) and a reverse denoising process (learning to remove noise to reconstruct the original data).
- To enhance plausibility in motion synthesis, geometric loss terms are integrated into the DDM training, penalizing artifacts like joint jitters and varying bone lengths by applying them to the approximated original data (`ˆX^(0)`).

#### 3.3. Overall Framework (Cascaded Diffusion Models)

The framework consists of two main stages: object trajectory synthesis (TrajDiff) and hand motion synthesis (HandDiff), along with a contact synthesis component (ConNet) and a final fitting optimization step.

##### 3.3.1. Object Trajectory Generation

The object trajectory, Φ, can be obtained in two ways:

- **Object Trajectory Synthesis (TrajDiff)**:
 - **Purpose**: Generates a 3D object trajectory conditioned on a mass value `m` and a one-hot encoded action label `a`.
 - **Representation**: To avoid rotation jitter artifacts, rotations are indirectly synthesized. Instead of 6D rotation, TrajDiff synthesizes reference vertex positions (`P_ref`) on the object surface, which are then aligned to predefined template vertex positions (`P_temp`) using Procrustes alignment to derive object rotations.
 - **Architecture**: A DDM based on a Unet architecture with 2D convolutional residual blocks.
 - **Loss Function (`L_T`)**: Combines `L_simple` (DDM noise prediction) with geometric losses (`L_geo`), including reconstruction (`L_rec`), velocity (`L_vel`), acceleration (`L_acc`), and a reference vertex loss (`L_ref`) that penalizes errors in reference vertex positions and their relative distances.
 - **Behavior**: Synthesized trajectories reflect mass, showing larger motion ranges/velocities for lighter objects and slower, more regulated motions for heavier objects.

- **User-Provided Object Trajectory with Dynamic Adjustment (RatioNet)**:
 - **Purpose**: Allows users to input a custom 3D trajectory while dynamically adjusting it to reflect object mass and provide plausible dynamics.
 - **Process**:
 1. **Uniform Input Trajectory Re-sampling**: A user-provided trajectory (`N_user` points) is uniformly re-sampled to a fixed length (`N_fix` = 720 points) path, Φ_fix. The total path length (`d_user`) is calculated.
 2. **Intermediate Ratio Updates Estimation (RatioNet)**: An MLP-based network, RatioNet, takes the residual of Φ_fix, the mass `m`, and `d_user` as input. It outputs a vector `r` representing updates to the object's location ratio along the path for each time step.
 3. **Normalized Target Trajectory (NTT) Calculation**: Cumulative ratios (`r_cuml`) are computed from `r`, which then determine the object's position on Φ_fix for each time step, yielding the final NTT, Φ_NTT.
 - **Loss Function (`L_ratio`)**: Penalizes errors in the ratio updates, their velocities, and accelerations, and ensures the sum of ratio updates across the sequence equals 1.0.

##### 3.3.2. Hand 3D Motion Synthesis (HandDiff)

- **Purpose**: Synthesizes 3D hand motions and per-vertex hand contact probabilities conditioned on the object trajectory (from TrajDiff or user-provided), mass, and action label.
- **Architecture**: A DDM based on a Unet architecture with 2D convolutional residual blocks.
- **Inputs**: Object trajectory (Φ), mass `m`, action label `a`.
- **Output**: Synthesized 3D hand joints (`J*`).
- **ConNet**: A 1D convolution-based network that estimates per-vertex hand contact probabilities (`b`) from the synthesized hand joints, object poses, mass, and action label. It is trained using a binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss against ground truth contact labels. These probabilities are crucial for the subsequent fitting optimization.
- **Loss Function (`L_H`)**: Combines `L_simple` with `L_geo`, which includes reconstruction (`L_rec`), velocity (`L_vel`), acceleration (`L_acc`), and bone length (`L_blen`) losses for the hand joints.

##### 3.3.3. Fitting Optimization

- **Purpose**: After `J*` is synthesized, a final optimization step fits GHUM hand models to the synthesized joints, ensuring plausible interaction with the object.
- **Objectives**:
 - **`L_data`**: Minimizes the Euclidean distance between GHUM-generated hand joints and `J*`.
 - **`L_touch`**: Composed of two terms: reducing distances between contact hand vertices (identified by `b > 0.5`) and their nearest object vertices, and aligning the normals of these contact surfaces.
 - **`L_col`**: Minimizes penetration distances between hand and object meshes.
 - **`L_prior`**: Encourages plausible GHUM hand poses by minimizing the pose vector `θ`.
- **Smoothing**: For non-spherical objects not in the training set, Gaussian smoothing is applied post-optimization for smoother motion.

#### 3.4. Dataset

Since no public dataset exists for mass-conditioned 3D hand-object interaction, the authors created a custom dataset.
- **Capture**: 8 synchronized 4K-resolution cameras (Z-CAM E2) at 50 fps.
- **Objects**: Five plastic spheres of the same radius (0.1m) filled with different materials to achieve varying masses (0.175, 2.0, 3.6, 3.9, 4.9 kg).
- **Actions**: Five subjects performed five actions (vertical throw/catch, passing, lifting, moving horizontally, drawing a circle) using both hands.
- **Reconstruction**:
 - **Hand Motion**: 2D hand keypoints from MediaPipe are used to fit GHUM hand models through a 2D reprojection-based optimization, incorporating collision and pose prior losses.
 - **Object Trajectory**: ArUco markers on the spheres are tracked using OpenCV, and 3D object positions are reconstructed via multi-view 2D reprojection optimization.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments demonstrate that MACS produces physically plausible and diverse 3D hand-object manipulations, reflecting the conditioned object mass. Comparisons were made against VAE and VAEGAN baselines, and qualitatively against ManipNet.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Diversity and Multimodality**: MACS consistently generated more diversified motions for both hand synthesis and object trajectory synthesis compared to VAE and VAEGAN. Notably, MACS's trajectory synthesis sometimes exhibited higher diversity than the ground truth data, indicating its generative capacity.
 - Hand synthesis: MACS (9.606 diversity, 7.07 multimodality) outperformed VAE (8.350, 6.0465) and VAEGAN (7.821, 5.887).
 - Trajectory synthesis: MACS (11.01 diversity, 8.05 multimodality) outperformed VAE (9.584, 7.696) and VAEGAN (8.428, 6.285).
- **Physical Plausibility**: MACS achieved superior performance across all physical metrics:
 - **Non-collision ratio (`m_col`)**: MACS 97.84% (highest), indicating minimal hand-object interpenetration.
 - **Collision distance (`m_dist`)**: MACS 0.041 mm (lowest), showing low magnitude of any remaining penetrations.
 - **Non-touching ratio (`m_touch`)**: MACS 1.97% (lowest), indicating few "floating object" artifacts (unintended absence of contact).
 - In contrast, VAE showed a higher `m_touch` (2.80%), and VAEGAN underperformed in collision metrics.
- **Ablation Study (Loss Terms)**:
 - Removing `L_touch` from the fitting optimization drastically increased `m_touch` (63.3%), confirming its role in ensuring contact.
 - Removing `L_col` led to a significant drop in `m_col` (38.41%) and increase in `m_dist` (0.296 mm), validating its importance for collision avoidance.
 - The combination of `L_vel` and `L_acc` in network training resulted in the most physically plausible acceleration distributions (lowest Wasserstein distance to ground truth).
 - RatioNet improved the physical plausibility of dynamics for user-provided trajectories, yielding lower Wasserstein distances for acceleration distributions compared to simple uniform interpolation.
- **Plausibility of Mass Conditioning**: The network trained with mass conditioning synthesized motions with more physically plausible accelerations for each mass value compared to a network trained without mass conditioning, as measured by Wasserstein distance of acceleration distributions.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Hand-Object Interaction Synthesis**: The synthesized motions exhibited realistic and dynamic interactions, with high diversity across generated samples. The motions plausibly adjusted based on the conditioned mass value.
- **Grasp Synthesis**: MACS produced diverse grasps that reflected mass. For light objects (e.g., 0.05 kg), hands often used fingertips, while for heavy objects (e.g., 10.0 kg), the entire palm supported the object. This behavior was observed even for interpolated (2.5 kg) and extrapolated (0.05 kg, 10.0 kg) mass values.
- **Mass-Conditioned Contacts**: Visualizations showed that with heavier masses (e.g., 4.9 kg), contact regions spanned the entire palm, while lighter masses (e.g., 0.18 kg) resulted in contacts concentrated around the fingertips, consistent with real-world observations.
- **User-Provided Trajectories**: When given user-defined trajectories (e.g., S-curve, infinity curve), RatioNet effectively adjusted the object's manipulation speed to reflect the conditioned mass, moving lighter objects faster and heavier objects slower.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects**: MACS demonstrated moderate adaptation to novel object shapes not present in the training dataset (e.g., cone, Stanford bunny, cube). This adaptability was attributed to the mass-conditioned hand contact labels synthesized by ConNet, which guided plausible interactions even with unfamiliar geometries.

#### 4.3. User Study

- **Perceptual Motion Quality**: An online user study (42 participants, 26 questions) rated the realism of synthesized motions on a scale of 1-10. MACS achieved the highest average "reality score" (6.01 ± 2.08) compared to VAE (5.10 ± 2.24) and VAEGAN (4.54 ± 2.39), indicating its superior perceptual quality.
- **Mass Sensitivity**: Participants were asked to identify which of two simultaneous videos (conditioned on 1.0 kg and 5.0 kg) depicted a heavier object manipulation. They correctly identified the heavier object in 92.8% of cases, confirming that MACS plausibly reflects mass in its synthesized motions.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of 3D motion synthesis by introducing the first approach to generate 3D object manipulations explicitly conditioned on object mass. This addresses a previously neglected aspect of human-object interaction, which has implications for enhancing the realism and naturalness of synthesized animations.

The potential impacts include:

- **Increased Realism in Digital Content**: By integrating the physical property of mass, MACS can generate more convincing and realistic hand-object interactions for applications such as AR/VR, virtual avatars, and character animation in films and video games. This can lead to more immersive and believable digital experiences.
- **Streamlined Animation Workflows**: The ability to automatically synthesize nuanced mass-conditioned interactions can reduce the time and artistic skill required for manual animation, accelerating content creation pipelines in the entertainment and graphics industries.
- **Enabling New Machine Learning Applications**: MACS's capacity to generate diverse and plausible synthetic data, including variations based on object mass, can be valuable for training machine learning models for tasks like grasp prediction, robotic manipulation, or physical property estimation in vision.
- **Flexibility for Designers and Developers**: The dual capability of generating object trajectories automatically or adjusting user-provided trajectories based on mass offers practical flexibility, allowing creators to balance automated generation with specific creative control.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work establishes a precedent for incorporating physical properties into motion synthesis. It opens avenues for future research into the effects of other physical factors (e.g., friction, non-uniform mass distribution, dynamic mass changes like a liquid in a bottle, or individual muscle strength) on human-object interaction. It also points towards inverse problems, such as inferring an object's weight from observed manipulation, which could have applications in monitoring and supervision systems.

While currently focused on a single object shape with uniform static mass distribution, the demonstrated generalization capabilities to unseen masses and moderate adaptation to unseen object shapes indicate the robustness and potential for broader applicability of the approach.
