# Algorithmic Guarantees for Distilling Supervised and Offline RL Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> Algorithms are presented for dataset distillation in supervised linear regression and offline reinforcement learning, offering the first theoretical performance guarantees for such methods without explicit model training. The approach matches loss values against random models, achieving a 	ilde{O}(d^2) upper bound for sampled regressors in linear regression and providing the first provable guarantees for offline RL by matching Bellman loss, including an improved 	ilde{O}(d^2) bound for decomposable feature maps.

## Problem

- Large datasets in machine learning create significant challenges regarding storage, management, and computational costs for training and hyperparameter tuning.
- Supervised learning dataset distillation methods are largely heuristic and lack rigorous theoretical performance guarantees, especially for approaches that do not require explicit model training.
- Existing offline reinforcement learning dataset distillation often relies on behavioral cloning, underutilizing reward and next-state information, limiting effectiveness to expert policies, and lacking theoretical underpinnings.

## Method

- The paper proposes ==dataset distillation== algorithms for supervised regression and offline RL that operate by matching loss values between the original and synthetic datasets, with respect to a fixed, randomly sampled collection of models.
- For supervised linear regression, a convex objective is optimized to match Mean Squared Error (MSE) losses for randomly sampled linear regressors without training any models.
- For offline RL, the method optimizes to match a modified Bellman loss, which explicitly incorporates reward and next-state information, using randomly sampled linear Q-value predictors.

## Results

- For supervised linear regression, the proposed algorithm achieves a 	ilde{O}(d^2) upper bound on the number of sampled regressors needed for 	riangle-approximation, matching a proven 	extOmega(d^2) lower bound; empirical tests showed comparable performance to full datasets and superiority over random subsampling.
- For offline RL, the method provides the first theoretical performance guarantees for dataset distillation without policy optimization, with a worst-case predictor sample complexity of 	extexp(O(d 	extlog d)) and an improved 	ilde{O}(d^2) for decomposable feature maps.
- Empirical evaluations on Cartpole, Mountain Car, and Acrobot environments demonstrated that policies trained on the distilled offline RL datasets significantly outperformed those trained on randomly subsampled data, sometimes matching or exceeding performance from the full dataset, even when using non-linear Q-value predictors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rigorous performance guarantees for dataset distillation can be achieved without explicit model training by optimizing synthetic data to match losses across a diverse set of randomly sampled models.
> - Anti-concentration bounds for Gaussian polynomials and conditioning arguments are crucial for deriving theoretical guarantees for both supervised and offline RL loss matching objectives.
> - Matching the Bellman loss in offline RL allows for full utilization of reward and next-state information, making distillation robust even for data from sub-optimal policies, unlike behavioral cloning approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of dataset distillation (DD), a technique aimed at compressing large training datasets into smaller, synthetic ones such that models trained on the synthetic data achieve comparable performance to those trained on the original dataset. Dataset distillation is motivated by the growing scale of machine learning datasets, which present challenges related to storage, management, and computational expense for training and hyperparameter tuning.

In **supervised learning**, dataset distillation has been a subject of extensive research. Existing methods primarily focus on optimizing synthetic datasets by matching various properties of the original data, such as loss gradients, feature-embeddings, or training trajectories of models. These optimizations are often conducted either through bi-level optimization (where models are simultaneously trained) or with respect to a fixed collection of sampled networks. While these techniques have demonstrated empirical success in reducing dataset size and preserving quality, many of them are heuristic in nature. A notable limitation in this area has been the sparsity of rigorous theoretical performance guarantees, particularly for methods that do not require explicit model training. Previous theoretical contributions, such as those by, focused on the convergence of synthetic feature-vectors given labels, and by, required access to an optimally trained model for linear ridge regression. The development of provably efficient DD algorithms without model training for supervised learning has largely remained an open area.

For **offline reinforcement learning (RL)**, the use of large datasets is increasingly prevalent. Offline RL, which trains policies on pre-collected data without online environment interaction, offers scalability and the ability to leverage diverse data. However, this also exacerbates the challenges associated with large datasets. Recent attempts at offline RL dataset distillation have largely centered on behavioral cloning (BC) objectives. Methods like optimize synthetic datasets using action-value weighted BC loss, requiring a pre-trained action-value predictor, while extends loss gradient matching to BC in offline RL. A key limitation of these BC-based approaches is their reliance solely on state-action pairs, neglecting the reward and next-state information typically available in offline RL datasets. This often restricts their effectiveness to data generated by expert policies. Furthermore, theoretical performance guarantees for offline RL dataset distillation methods are largely absent.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by providing theoretical foundations and efficient algorithms for dataset distillation in both supervised learning and offline RL settings, specifically focusing on approaches that do not necessitate extensive model training during the distillation process and, for offline RL, fully leverage the available data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop efficient dataset distillation algorithms for supervised regression and offline reinforcement learning that come with rigorous theoretical performance guarantees, while notably operating *without requiring explicit model training* during the distillation process.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several critical gaps and challenges in the existing landscape:

1. **Addressing Computational and Resource Challenges:** The increasing size of datasets in modern machine learning, particularly in deep learning and reinforcement learning, creates significant challenges. These include increased requirements for data storage, complex data management, and substantial computational costs associated with training numerous models (e.g., during hyperparameter tuning or architectural searches) on massive datasets. Dataset distillation offers a method to mitigate these issues by creating smaller, more manageable synthetic datasets.

2. **Bridging the Theoretical Gap in Supervised Learning DD:** While supervised dataset distillation has seen empirical successes, a notable portion of existing methods are heuristic. There has been a demand for algorithms with provable efficiency and performance guarantees that do not rely on strong assumptions, such as the availability of a pre-trained optimal model or the convergence of synthetic features for given labels. This work aims to establish such guarantees for linear regression, thereby moving beyond empirical observations to a more theoretically grounded approach.

3. **Enhancing Offline RL Dataset Distillation:** Current offline RL dataset distillation methods are nascent and primarily rely on behavioral cloning (BC). This approach suffers from two main limitations:
 - **Underutilization of Data:** BC objectives often only leverage state-action pairs, neglecting the valuable reward and next-state information present in offline RL datasets. This limits the quality of the distilled data, especially when policies are not expert-generated.
 - **Lack of Theoretical Guarantees:** Similar to supervised learning, there is a scarcity of theoretical underpinnings for offline RL dataset distillation, making it difficult to predict or assure performance.
 The research aims to overcome these by proposing an algorithm that directly matches the Bellman loss, thus incorporating rewards and next-state information, and provides the first known performance guarantees for offline RL dataset distillation without model training.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors seek to provide more robust, theoretically sound, and practically effective methods for dataset distillation across different machine learning paradigms, addressing fundamental limitations in current practices.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology presented in this paper involves distilling datasets by matching loss values between the original training dataset and a synthetic dataset, with respect to a fixed, randomly sampled collection of models, crucially without requiring any explicit model training during the distillation process.

**Supervised Learning Dataset Distillation (Algorithm 1):**

1. **Problem Formulation:** The task is regression in $\mathbb{R}^d$. The objective is to find a synthetic dataset $D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{sup}}$ such that the squared difference of the Mean Squared Error (MSE) losses, $L_{\text{err}}^{\text{mse}}(D_{\text{train}}^{\text{sup}}, D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{sup}}, f) = (L_{\text{mse}}(D_{\text{train}}^{\text{sup}}, f) - L_{\text{mse}}(D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{sup}}, f))^2$, is small for all linear regressors $f$ in a bounded class $F_0$.
2. **Loss Matching Objective:** The algorithm minimizes the sum of these loss differences over a fixed set of $k$ randomly sampled regressors. The underlying concept is that if a synthetic dataset performs similarly to the training dataset on a sufficiently diverse set of random models, it will generalize well to other models in the class.
3. **Regressor Sampling:** Homogeneous linear regressors $g_j(x, y) = r_j^T \zeta(x, y)$ are sampled, where $r_j$ are drawn from a standard Gaussian distribution (scaled by $1/(d+1)$). This specific sampling strategy is critical for the theoretical guarantees derived using anti-concentration bounds.
4. **Optimization:** The summation $\sum_{j=1}^k L_{\text{err}}^{\text{mse}}(D_{\text{train}}^{\text{sup}}, D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{sup}}, g_j)$ is shown to be a convex function with respect to the points of the synthetic dataset $D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{sup}}$. This convexity implies that the minimization problem is tractable and can be solved efficiently (e.g., using standard convex optimization techniques).
5. **"Model Training Free" Aspect:** Unlike bi-level optimization approaches, this method does not train a model (regressor) on either the original or synthetic data during the distillation phase. Instead, it directly optimizes the synthetic data points to match the observed losses of fixed, random models.

**Offline RL Dataset Distillation (Algorithm 2):**

1. **Problem Formulation:** For an MDP, the goal is to find a synthetic dataset $D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{orl}}$ such that the squared difference of the Bellman losses, $L_{\text{err}}^{\text{Bell}}(D_{\text{train}}^{\text{orl}}, D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{orl}}, f) = (L_{\text{Bell}}(D_{\text{train}}^{\text{orl}}, f) - L_{\text{Bell}}(D_{\text{syn}}^{\text{orl}}, f))^2$, is small for all linear action-value predictors $f$ in a bounded class $Q_0$. The Bellman loss $L_{\text{Bell}}(D, f) = E_{(s,a,r,s') \leftarrow D} [(f(s,a) - r - \gamma \max_{a'} f(s',a'))^2]$ explicitly utilizes reward and next-state information.
2. **Modified Bellman Loss:** A modified Bellman loss $\hat{L}_{\text{Bell}}(D, f, \lambda)$ is introduced, incorporating a scale factor $\lambda$ for the reward. The objective is to match this modified loss.
3. **Q-value Predictor Sampling:** Pairs of (predictor, scalar) $(f_j, \lambda_j)$ are sampled from a distribution $H$. Predictors $f_j(s, a) = r_j^T \phi(s, a)$ are defined by Gaussian-sampled vectors $r_j$, and $\lambda_j$ is also sampled from a Gaussian distribution.
4. **Optimization:** Similar to supervised learning, the algorithm minimizes the sum of the modified Bellman loss differences for the sampled predictors. The optimization is again over the points of the synthetic dataset.
5. **Decomposable Feature-Maps (Theorem 4.4):** A specific, common case is considered where the feature-map $\phi(s, a)$ is decomposable into a sum of state and action-specific feature maps, $\phi(s, a) = \phi_1(s) + \phi_2(a)$. In this scenario, the "max" term in the Bellman loss simplifies, leading to a structure analogous to linear regression. An additional constraint (Equation 11) requiring the mean of certain feature combinations to be matched between training and synthetic datasets is imposed. When $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ are linear, this problem becomes a convex optimization with linear constraints.

**Theoretical Foundations:**
The theoretical guarantees rely on:
- **Anti-concentration Bounds:** For supervised learning, the Carbery-Wright anti-concentration bound for Gaussian polynomials is used to show that individual sampled regressors are likely to exhibit a significant loss difference if the overall error is large.
- **Conditioning Argument:** For the general offline RL case, due to the non-smoothness introduced by the "max" operator in the Bellman loss, a conditioning argument is employed instead of polynomial anti-concentration.
- **Epsilon-Nets and Union Bounds:** To generalize the probabilistic guarantees from individual samples to the entire class of regressors/predictors and synthetic datasets, the authors construct fine-grained epsilon-nets over the function classes and the space of possible synthetic datasets. Union bounds over these nets allow the extension of single-sample guarantees to uniform guarantees.
- **Lower Bound Analysis:** For supervised learning, a lower bound on the number of sampled regressors is derived by leveraging properties of symmetric matrices and low-rank approximations, demonstrating the tightness of the upper bound.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents algorithmic contributions alongside rigorous theoretical guarantees and empirical validations for dataset distillation in both supervised regression and offline reinforcement learning.

**Supervised Learning Dataset Distillation:**

- **Algorithmic Guarantee (Theorem 4.1):** For linear regression in $\mathbb{R}^d$, the proposed Algorithm 1, which optimizes a convex objective by matching MSE losses with respect to a fixed set of randomly sampled regressors, requires $\tilde{O}(d^2 \log(d(B+b)/\Delta) \log(1/\delta))$ sampled regressors. This guarantees that, with high probability ($1-\delta$), the MSE loss of any bounded linear model on the synthetic dataset is approximately the same (within $\Delta$) as its MSE loss on the original training dataset. Consequently, a model optimized on the synthetic data achieves a near-minimum loss on the training data, performing comparably to a model optimized on the original data. The method operates without requiring any model training during distillation.
- **Matching Lower Bound (Theorem 4.2):** The analysis of Theorem 4.1 is shown to be tight (up to logarithmic factors) by proving a $\Omega(d^2)$ lower bound on the number of sampled regressors necessary to achieve such guarantees.
- **Synthetic Dataset Size:** A lower bound of $O(d)$ is also established for the size of the synthetic dataset required.
- **Sample Complexity Advantage:** The dependence of the sample complexity on $\log(1/\Delta)$ for the error $\Delta$ is noted to be quantitatively better than the $1/\Delta$ dependence often found in matrix sketching techniques.

**Offline RL Dataset Distillation:**

- **Algorithmic Guarantee (Theorem 4.3):** For offline RL, Algorithm 2 is introduced, which distills a dataset by matching the Bellman loss (inclusive of rewards and next-state information) with respect to randomly sampled linear Q-value predictors. This is presented as the first method of its kind without requiring policy model optimization. The theorem guarantees that minimizing the difference of Bellman losses for a collection of $k = (\text{constant}/\nu)^{O(d \log d)}$ randomly sampled Q-value predictors generates a synthetic dataset. This synthetic dataset's Bellman loss for any linear bounded Q-value predictor is shown to be a close approximation (within $\Delta$) to that on the training dataset. As a result, a policy derived from an action-value predictor optimized on this synthetic data performs nearly as well as one from an optimizer on the original training data. The worst-case sample complexity for predictors is $\exp(O(d \log d))$.
- **Decomposable Feature-Maps (Theorem 4.4):** A more efficient guarantee is provided for a common and natural setting where the state-action feature-map $\phi(s,a)$ is decomposable, i.e., $\phi(s,a) = \phi_1(s) + \phi_2(a)$. Under this assumption and an additional linear constraint (Equation 11) requiring the mean of certain features to be matched, the algorithm requires only $\tilde{O}(d^2)$ sampled linear Q-value predictors. Furthermore, if $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ are linear maps, the optimization problem for finding the synthetic dataset is convex and tractable.

**Empirical Evaluations:**

Extensive experiments were conducted to validate the theoretical findings:

- **Supervised Regression:** On the Wine Quality (red and white) and Boston Housing datasets, models trained on the synthetic datasets generated by Algorithm 1 performed comparably to models trained on the full original dataset. They consistently outperformed models trained on randomly subsampled datasets and showed competitive or superior performance compared to leverage score subsampling, a conventional data reduction technique for linear regression. The effectiveness was observed with few sampled models.
- **Offline RL:** Experiments on Cartpole, Mountain Car, and Acrobot environments (using 2-layer neural networks for Q-value predictors, despite theoretical guarantees being for linear models) demonstrated performance gains. Policies trained on synthetic datasets generated by Algorithm 2 significantly outperformed those trained on randomly subsampled datasets. For Cartpole and Acrobot, increasing the synthetic dataset size (N_syn) resulted in performance on par with or even exceeding that of policies trained on the full training dataset. These results suggest the practical applicability of the algorithms even with non-linear models and a limited number of sampled predictors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents significant advancements in the theoretical understanding and practical application of dataset distillation, particularly for supervised regression and offline reinforcement learning.

**Theoretical Significance:**
The primary contribution is the establishment of rigorous, provable performance and efficiency guarantees for dataset distillation algorithms that operate *without the need for explicit model training* during the distillation process.
- For **supervised linear regression**, the $\tilde{O}(d^2)$ upper bound on the number of sampled regressors, complemented by a matching $\Omega(d^2)$ lower bound, clarifies the fundamental complexity of the problem and demonstrates the tightness of the proposed algorithm's analysis. This moves the field beyond largely heuristic approaches towards a more theoretically grounded understanding.
- For **offline RL**, this work introduces the first dataset distillation algorithm with rigorous performance bounds. By matching the Bellman loss, the method effectively leverages all available data (states, actions, rewards, and next states), addressing a key limitation of prior behavioral cloning-based methods that often underutilized reward information and performed best with expert policies. The improved $\tilde{O}(d^2)$ guarantee for decomposable feature-maps highlights the practical relevance and efficiency for a commonly encountered scenario in RL.

**Practical Impact:**
The proposed algorithms offer several practical benefits for real-world machine learning systems:
- **Resource Efficiency:** By enabling the creation of substantially smaller synthetic datasets (with size as low as $O(d)$), the methods directly alleviate challenges associated with storing and managing large-scale datasets. This can lead to reduced infrastructure costs and simpler data pipelines.
- **Computational Savings:** Training and evaluating models, especially deep neural networks, on massive datasets is computationally intensive. Distilled datasets allow for faster experimentation, hyperparameter tuning, and model development cycles, reducing computational time and energy consumption.
- **Enhanced Offline RL Utility:** The Bellman loss matching approach for offline RL allows for the distillation of datasets that are robust even if collected from sub-optimal policies, as it fully incorporates the feedback loop (rewards and next states) crucial for value estimation. This broadens the applicability of offline RL techniques.
- **Empirical Validation for Non-linear Models:** While the theoretical guarantees are primarily for linear models, the empirical results demonstrate that the proposed algorithms perform effectively with non-linear neural networks in practice. This suggests that the underlying principles of loss matching with random models generalize beyond the linear setting, providing a strong basis for real-world deployments.

**Future Directions:**
This work lays a foundation for future research. Key areas for development include extending the theoretical guarantees to general non-linear models, particularly neural networks, in both supervised and offline RL contexts. Further investigation into specific geometric properties of MDPs could also lead to more efficient offline RL dataset distillation algorithms for more complex state-action embeddings. Overall, this research advances the state of dataset distillation by providing principled, provably efficient methods that address critical challenges in modern machine learning.
