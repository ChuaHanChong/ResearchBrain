# Real-to-Sim for Highly Cluttered Environments via Physics-Consistent Inter-Object Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A physics-constrained Real2Sim pipeline reconstructs dynamically consistent 3D scenes of highly cluttered tabletops from a single RGB-D image. It builds an explicit contact graph of support and proximity relations, then runs two-stage optimization: SDF-based geometry refinement followed by hierarchical differentiable rigid-body simulation that jointly tunes pose, mass, friction, and center of mass. Stability ratio reaches 85.7% on GSO and 89.3% on YCB, roughly 38% above baselines.

## Problem

- Appearance-driven reconstruction pipelines ignore physical constraints, producing floating objects and interpenetration that make downstream simulation of contact-rich manipulation unreliable.
- Physics-aware methods mostly stabilize single objects against the ground, and geometric heuristics fix local penetration without guaranteeing long-horizon dynamic stability in multi-object clutter.
- Sampling-based stability search is computationally expensive, sample-inefficient, and typically needs multi-view video; differentiable simulators stay confined to single-object or single-agent settings.

## Method

- ==Contact graph== construction: a directed parse tree of support edges scored by a support-gap and support-polygon likelihood, plus undirected proximity edges from overlapping axis-aligned bounding boxes.
- Two-stage optimization: geometry-aware stage minimizes an ==SDF== non-penetration plus top-K multi-point contact loss with visual regularization anchoring poses to the ==ICP==-aligned initialization.
- ==Hierarchical physics== stage walks the parse tree root-to-leaf, minimizing time-integrated squared velocity through ==DiffSDFSim== to jointly refine pose, mass, friction, and center of mass; ==DIB-R== refines texture.

## Results

- Stability ratio reaches **85.7%** (GSO) and **89.3%** (YCB) versus roughly **51%** and **49%** for SAM3D+ICP and HoloScene, with settling time dropping to **0.73s** and **0.43s**.
- Runtime averages **22** minutes per scene versus **840** minutes for the video-based sampling baseline; **5** minutes for **4-6** objects, **20** minutes for **14-15** objects.
- Ablation: geometry constraint alone lifts GSO stability **51.4%** to **58.3%**; adding hierarchical physics reaches **85.7%**. Real-world stability **71.6-73.3%** versus **0-15%**, decluttering success **70-80%** versus **0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Static geometric proxies like non-penetration and minimal contact are necessary but insufficient; only simulating forward dynamics certifies long-horizon stability under gravity.
> - Physical plausibility trades against geometric metrics: Chamfer Distance degrades as objects move off the geometry optimum, but stable scenes matter more since geometry error is fixable by domain randomization.
> - Decomposing coupled multi-body contact into a support hierarchy makes differentiable physics tractable at scene scale, where joint optimization gives unstable gradients.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: bridging the gap between visual perception and robotic control, particularly in highly cluttered environments. Real-to-Simulation (Real2Sim) approaches aim to replicate real-world dynamics in simulation, enabling robust planning and policy learning through real-sim-real transfer. However, existing methods encounter limitations when confronted with scenes requiring precise contact reasoning and accurate modeling of inter-object physical dependencies.

Traditional perception pipelines often prioritize geometric fidelity but overlook physical constraints, leading to reconstructed scenes that are physically invalid (e.g., objects floating or severely inter-penetrating). Such invalid states render downstream simulations unreliable for contact-rich manipulation tasks common in industrial and domestic settings.

Previous attempts to incorporate physics into object reconstruction can be categorized:
- **Object-ground interaction methods** integrate differentiable physics into regression pipelines to stabilize individual objects on a surface. While effective for basic stability, these methods generally do not model hierarchical inter-object dependencies prevalent in cluttered scenes.
- **Geometry-based pose refinement approaches** (e.g., CAST,) enforce non-penetration and minimal contact using geometric heuristics. These methods can resolve local interpenetration but typically do not guarantee long-horizon dynamic stability under complex contact interactions.
- **Sampling-based methods** (e.g., HoloScene,) search for stable configurations via techniques like Monte Carlo Tree Search. While capable, these approaches are often computationally expensive, sample-inefficient, and frequently require multi-view observations, which may not always be available.
- **Differentiable physics simulators** offer efficient and deterministic mechanisms for inferring dynamic properties by minimizing discrepancies between simulated and observed trajectories. However, existing differentiable physics systems primarily operate in single-object or single-agent scenarios, facing challenges in scaling to multi-object scenes due to the rapidly increasing nonlinearity of coupled contact dynamics.

This research aims to bridge the gap by proposing a physics-constrained Real2Sim pipeline that specifically addresses the joint reasoning of geometric and dynamic properties in highly cluttered, multi-object scenes. It differentiates itself by integrating a contact graph for explicit spatial dependency modeling and leveraging hierarchical differentiable rigid-body simulation to enforce long-horizon dynamic consistency from a single RGB-D observation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to reconstruct physically consistent 3D scenes from single-view RGB-D observations, thereby generating a dynamically consistent digital twin suitable for robotic control in highly cluttered environments. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives:

- **Develop a Real2Sim framework:** The framework aims to reconstruct a digital representation of a cluttered environment that accurately reflects real-world physical interactions.
- **Explicitly model inter-object relationships:** A key objective is to design and incorporate a contact graph that captures hierarchical support and contact relationships between objects, enabling effective organization and resolution of complex coupled constraints.
- **Jointly optimize geometric and physical properties:** The research seeks to refine not only object poses but also critical physical attributes such as mass, friction coefficients, and center of mass. This joint optimization is intended to ensure the reconstructed scene behaves realistically under physical simulation.
- **Enforce physical plausibility via differentiable physics:** The method aims to directly minimize stability violations using differentiable rigid-body simulation, moving beyond static geometric proxies employed in prior work. This is intended to guarantee long-horizon dynamic stability.
- **Demonstrate utility in robotic tasks:** A final objective is to validate that the reconstructed scenes can faithfully replicate real-world contact dynamics, thereby enabling stable and reliable contact-rich manipulation tasks for robots.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the limitations of current Real2Sim pipelines in handling cluttered environments. Robotic manipulation in such settings requires reasoning about complex inter-object dependencies (e.g., stacking, leaning, frictional contact), which are highly sensitive to accurate physical modeling. Standard perception outputs, which often suffer from floating objects or inter-penetration, yield unreliable simulations for robot planning and learning. The authors are motivated to provide a solution that generates dynamically consistent scene reconstructions from limited single-view data, thereby enhancing the robustness and applicability of Real2Sim for real-world robotic challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed physics-constrained Real2Sim pipeline operates in four main stages to reconstruct a dynamically consistent and photorealistic digital twin scene from a single RGB-D observation and instance masks.

**A. Initial Guess of Geometry and Appearance**
The process begins by obtaining an initial estimation of object geometry and appearance. The method utilizes SAM3D-objects, a foundation reconstruction model, to predict the geometry, texture, and layout from a single RGB-D image. To improve the accuracy of initial object transforms, which may deviate from reality, an Iterative Closest Point (ICP) pose refinement algorithm, implemented in Open3D, is applied. This stage provides a preliminary but robust approximation of complete mesh geometry, physically meaningful initial object poses, and consistent texture estimates, serving as a basis for subsequent optimization.

**B. Contact Graph Construction**
Following the initial geometric and appearance estimation, a contact graph, `cg = (pt, E)`, is constructed to infer inter-object relationships. This graph comprises a directed parse tree (`pt`) representing hierarchical vertical support structures and an undirected edge set (`E`) encoding proximal relationships.
- **Scene Entity Nodes:** The node set `V` includes a ground node as the global root and individual object nodes `v_i`. Each object node encapsulates properties such as the instance label (`o_i`), its triangular mesh (`M_i`), an oriented bounding box (`B_i`), a set of upward-facing supporting planes (`Π_i`) derived from mesh faces with normals opposite to gravity, and a support polygon (`F_p`) defined as the convex hull of the mesh projected onto the XY plane.
- **Supporting Relations:** To determine the parent (supporting object) for each non-root object `v_c`, a two-stage procedure is used.
 1. **Broad-phase candidate selection:** Identifies potential parents `v_p` that lie vertically below `v_c` and whose projected OBBs overlap in the XY plane.
 2. **Narrow-phase support likelihood:** For candidate parents, a support likelihood `H(v_c, v_p, π^k_p)` is calculated. This combines a normalized support gap (`d_gap`), representing the minimum distance between the child's bottom surface and the parent's supporting plane, with a measure of consistency between the child's projected center of mass (`c_c`) and the parent's support polygon (`F_p`). The parent and plane yielding the highest likelihood define the directed support edge.
- **Proximity Edges:** Undirected edges `E` are added between any pair of objects whose Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs) overlap, indicating potential contact interactions that will be evaluated later using Signed Distance Functions (SDFs).

**C. Two-Stage Physics-Constrained Optimization**
The core of the methodology is a two-stage optimization process designed to resolve geometric inconsistencies and enforce dynamic stability.

1. **Geometry-Aware Constraint:**
 The first stage globally optimizes all object poses (`q`) to achieve a penetration-free configuration with coherent contact support. This is formulated as minimizing `L_geom(q)`, an objective function combining SDF-based contact constraints (`L_con`) and visual regularization (`L_vis`).
 - `L_con` (SDF-based contact constraint): For supporting pairs in the parse tree, it penalizes penetration between object surfaces and encourages multi-point contact by ensuring a minimum number of sampled points lie close to the contact surface. For proximal pairs, only the non-penetration term is applied. The SDF is computed using the GPU-accelerated Kaolin library.
 - `L_vis` (Visual regularization): This term anchors the optimized poses to the initial ICP-aligned estimates, preventing excessive deviation.

2. **Hierarchical Physics Constraint:**
 Building upon the geometry-aware initialization, the second stage refines object-level initial poses and physical parameters (`θ_j = {q_j, c_j, m_j, f^j_c}`) to achieve long-horizon physical stability. Due to the high nonlinearity of rigid-body contact dynamics, a hierarchical approach is adopted. Objects are optimized sequentially, following the support tree (`pt`) from the root (e.g., the table) outwards to leaf objects. During the optimization of a child object, its parent objects are treated as locally fixed.
 - The optimization problem for an object `j` minimizes `L_phy(θ_j)`, which comprises a simulation stability loss (`L_sim`), contact constraints (`L_con`), and visual regularization (`L_vis`).
 - `L_sim` (Simulation stability loss): This term leverages DiffSDFSim, a fully differentiable rigid-body simulator that computes analytical gradients through contact dynamics formulated as a Linear Complementarity Problem (LCP). The objective is to drive the object's simulated velocity (`v_i`) to zero over a simulation horizon `T` under gravity, thereby achieving a stable static equilibrium. This allows for joint optimization of the initial pose, mass, friction coefficients, and center of mass (`c_j`).
 - To enable gradient backpropagation with respect to the center of mass, contact and friction Jacobians are corrected by expressing contact point positions relative to the COM. Collision detection is performed efficiently using the Frank-Wolfe algorithm operating directly between SDF and mesh, with each object represented as a voxelized SDF.

**D. Photometric Refinement**
As a final post-process, object textures (`T`) are refined to enhance photometric fidelity. A differentiable renderer, DIB-R, is employed to optimize each object's texture while keeping geometric parameters fixed. Given that only a single view is provided, an isotropic Total Variation (TV) regularizer is incorporated to propagate photometric gradients across the texture map, ensuring consistency. The objective aligns the rendered image with the observed image and applies the TV regularization to the texture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed method was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art scene reconstruction methods.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
Experiments were conducted using the PyBullet physics engine with object assets from the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset and the YCB object set. Twenty highly cluttered tabletop scenes, each containing 4 to 15 objects with diverse inter-object relationships, were constructed for each dataset.
- **Physical Stability:** The reconstructed scenes were re-simulated under gravity, and physical properties were automatically estimated by the optimization process. The proposed method demonstrated superior physical stability. For the GSO dataset, it achieved an 85.7% stability ratio, and for the YCB dataset, 89.3%. This represented a substantial improvement (average 38% increase) over SAM3D+ICP (GSO: 51.4%, YCB: 48.8%) and HoloScene (GSO: 51.8%, YCB: 49.2%), both of which lack physics-based reasoning for long-horizon stability. The method consistently reduced average translational velocity (V_t) and average rotational velocity (Ω_t) to near zero, indicating successful convergence to stable static equilibrium.
- **Geometry and Rendering Quality:** While achieving high physical stability, the method maintained competitive geometry (Chamfer Distance, F-Score, IoU) and rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). It was observed that baselines like SAM3D+ICP, which do not incorporate physical reasoning, might achieve slightly better geometric metrics by overfitting to imperfect meshes, but this often resulted in interpenetration and floating artifacts, leading to unstable simulations. The proposed method demonstrated a trade-off where physical stability was prioritized, sometimes leading to a slight degradation in geometric accuracy but ensuring dynamically feasible configurations.
- **Efficiency:** The proposed method had an average runtime of 5 minutes for scenes with 4-6 objects and up to 20 minutes for scenes with 14-15 objects. This was significantly more efficient than the video-based HoloScene, which required 840 minutes, making the proposed method more practical for real-time robotic applications.

**B. Ablation Study**
An ablation study analyzed the contribution of geometric (Geo.) and physics (Phy.) constraints.
- **Geometric Constraint Only (✓ Geo, ✗ Phy):** Adding only the geometry-aware constraint yielded a moderate improvement in physical stability (e.g., GSO: 58.3%) and reduced translational and rotational velocities compared to no constraints. This indicated that geometric constraints effectively resolved interpenetration but were insufficient to guarantee dynamic feasibility.
- **Full Method (✓ Geo, ✓ Phy):** Incorporating the hierarchical physics constraint on top of geometry-aware initialization led to a substantial gain in stability (e.g., GSO: 85.7%) and faster settling times. This confirmed the critical role of differentiable physics in achieving long-horizon dynamic stability. The study highlighted that prioritizing physical stability, even if it meant slight deviations from initial geometric alignment, was beneficial for downstream robotic tasks.

**C. Physical Experiments**
Real-world experiments utilized a Franka robotic arm and an Azure Kinect DK to capture single RGB-D observations of cluttered tabletop scenes, using 3D-printed objects from GSO and Toy4K datasets.
- **Real-world Generalization:** The method achieved approximately 72% stability (71.6% for GSO-like objects, 73.3% for Toy4K-like objects) with an average initial unilateral Chamfer Distance (UCD) of about 40 mm. This stability, though slightly lower than in simulation, was attributed to real-world sensor noise and depth inaccuracies.
- **Interaction Outcome Prediction:** The reconstructed scenes enabled successful prediction of scene evolution following pushing actions by a real robot. In contrast, SAM3D+ICP often failed at initialization due to severe object interpenetration, preventing meaningful interaction prediction. This demonstrated the method's ability to create simulation-ready scenes for predicting real-world dynamics.
- **Real2Sim2Real Decluttering Task:** The method was successfully applied in a decluttering task. The reconstructed, physically stable scene, coupled with an inferred contact graph, allowed for simulation-guided grasping sequence selection. This strategy was then executed on a real robot, successfully removing clutter. This outcome provided empirical evidence that physically consistent scene reconstruction serves as an effective interface between perception and robot action.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of Real-to-Sim scene reconstruction, particularly for robotic applications in cluttered environments.
- **Enhanced Physical Fidelity for Robotics:** The primary significance lies in its ability to generate dynamically consistent digital twins from single-view RGB-D data. By explicitly modeling inter-object relationships through a contact graph and leveraging hierarchical differentiable rigid-body simulation, the method produces scenes with high physical fidelity that accurately replicate real-world contact dynamics. This addresses a critical limitation of previous methods, which often yield physically invalid or unstable reconstructions.
- **Improved Reliability for Robotic Manipulation:** The reconstructed scenes enable more reliable simulation of contact-rich interactions, which is crucial for tasks like object retrieval, pushing, and rearrangement in cluttered settings. This improved reliability facilitates more robust planning, policy learning, and real-sim-real transfer for robotic systems, potentially reducing the need for extensive real-world trials.
- **Advancement in Differentiable Physics:** The proposed hierarchical optimization strategy demonstrates a scalable approach to applying differentiable physics to complex multi-object scenes. This extends the applicability of differentiable simulators beyond single-object or single-agent scenarios, addressing the challenges posed by highly coupled and nonlinear contact dynamics.
- **Practical Utility for Digital Twins:** The work contributes to the creation of high-fidelity digital twins that not only look realistic but also behave physically plausibly. Such digital twins are invaluable for virtual prototyping, testing, and validation of robotic solutions in a controlled simulated environment before deployment in the real world.

The potential impact of this work extends across several domains:
- **Industrial Automation:** In warehouses and factories, where cluttered environments are common, robots equipped with this scene understanding capability could perform manipulation tasks with greater autonomy and precision.
- **Service Robotics:** Enabling service robots to interact safely and effectively with household items in unstructured, cluttered home environments.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Providing a robust method for generating physically accurate synthetic data for training machine learning models, thereby mitigating the costs and challenges associated with collecting real-world data.
- **Computer Graphics and Virtual Reality:** Contributing to more realistic and interactive virtual environments by providing tools for reconstructing physically plausible 3D scenes from real-world observations.

Despite its contributions, the method has acknowledged limitations, including sensitivity to severe occlusions and single-view ambiguity, which can lead to physically stable but visually implausible solutions or entrapment in local minima. Additionally, it does not currently consider advanced appearance models, complex lighting, or shadows under single-view supervision. Future work might focus on addressing these limitations to further enhance the realism and robustness of scene reconstruction.
