# DSI-Bench: A Benchmark for Dynamic Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, DSI-Bench, assesses dynamic spatial intelligence in Vision-Language Models and 3D expertise models by evaluating their comprehension of simultaneous motion between observers and objects in 3D environments. Evaluations reveal that current state-of-the-art models significantly struggle with dynamic tasks, exhibit limited robustness, and often suffer from biases like forward prediction or conflating rotation with translation, even as model scale increases.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and benchmarks are primarily limited to static or semi-dynamic 3D environments, neglecting scenarios where both observer and observed objects are in motion.
- Current models demonstrate significant accuracy degradation, hallucinations, and biases when faced with complex, dynamic 3D scenes requiring simultaneous understanding of multiple moving entities.
- The absence of a comprehensive benchmark for "Dynamic Spatial Intelligence" prevents rigorous evaluation and targeted development of AI systems for real-world dynamic interactions.

## Method

- ==DSI-Bench== was introduced, a novel benchmark comprising nearly **1,000** dynamic scene videos and over **1,700** manually annotated Vision-Question-Answering (VQA) pairs covering diverse motion patterns.
- A ==task taxonomy for dynamic spatial intelligence== was developed, categorizing questions into object-scene, observer-scene, and observer-object relationships.
- ==Spatio-temporal flip augmentation== with a group-wise evaluation strategy was implemented to rigorously assess model robustness and mitigate biases from 3D motion patterns.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLMs consistently showed a decline in accuracy when tested on dynamic scenarios compared to static ones, with performance degradation sometimes exceeding **10%** on specific tasks.
- VLMs exhibited a substantial drop in performance (e.g., up to **20-30%** on some tasks) when evaluated group-wise (requiring consistency across flipped variants) compared to sample-wise accuracy, indicating a lack of robustness.
- Models frequently displayed a "forward bias" (disproportionately selecting "forward" options) and confusion between rotational and translational movements, leading to hallucinations where objects were perceived as moving even when fixed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs lack true dynamic spatial intelligence, demonstrating significant performance drops and limited robustness when both observer and objects are in simultaneous motion.
> - Simply increasing VLM scale improves sample-wise accuracy but does not consistently enhance, and can sometimes decrease, robustness against spatio-temporal transformations and inherent biases.
> - Language-based free-form reasoning (FFR) provides only marginal and inconsistent benefits for dynamic spatial tasks, suggesting a fundamental limitation in visual perception rather than purely reasoning capabilities.
