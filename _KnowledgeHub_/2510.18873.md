# DSI-Bench: A Benchmark for Dynamic Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, DSI-Bench, assesses dynamic spatial intelligence in Vision-Language Models and 3D expertise models by evaluating their comprehension of simultaneous motion between observers and objects in 3D environments. Evaluations reveal that current state-of-the-art models significantly struggle with dynamic tasks, exhibit limited robustness, and often suffer from biases like forward prediction or conflating rotation with translation, even as model scale increases.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and benchmarks are primarily limited to static or semi-dynamic 3D environments, neglecting scenarios where both observer and observed objects are in motion.
- Current models demonstrate significant accuracy degradation, hallucinations, and biases when faced with complex, dynamic 3D scenes requiring simultaneous understanding of multiple moving entities.
- The absence of a comprehensive benchmark for "Dynamic Spatial Intelligence" prevents rigorous evaluation and targeted development of AI systems for real-world dynamic interactions.

## Method

- ==DSI-Bench== was introduced, a novel benchmark comprising nearly **1,000** dynamic scene videos and over **1,700** manually annotated Vision-Question-Answering (VQA) pairs covering diverse motion patterns.
- A ==task taxonomy for dynamic spatial intelligence== was developed, categorizing questions into object-scene, observer-scene, and observer-object relationships.
- ==Spatio-temporal flip augmentation== with a group-wise evaluation strategy was implemented to rigorously assess model robustness and mitigate biases from 3D motion patterns.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLMs consistently showed a decline in accuracy when tested on dynamic scenarios compared to static ones, with performance degradation sometimes exceeding **10%** on specific tasks.
- VLMs exhibited a substantial drop in performance (e.g., up to **20-30%** on some tasks) when evaluated group-wise (requiring consistency across flipped variants) compared to sample-wise accuracy, indicating a lack of robustness.
- Models frequently displayed a "forward bias" (disproportionately selecting "forward" options) and confusion between rotational and translational movements, leading to hallucinations where objects were perceived as moving even when fixed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs lack true dynamic spatial intelligence, demonstrating significant performance drops and limited robustness when both observer and objects are in simultaneous motion.
> - Simply increasing VLM scale improves sample-wise accuracy but does not consistently enhance, and can sometimes decrease, robustness against spatio-temporal transformations and inherent biases.
> - Language-based free-form reasoning (FFR) provides only marginal and inconsistent benefits for dynamic spatial tasks, suggesting a fundamental limitation in visual perception rather than purely reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and largely unfulfilled gap in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their ability to operate in dynamic 3D environments.

**Current State of Research:**
- **VLMs and Visual Expertise Models:** Significant strides have been made in visual perception and understanding. VLMs have demonstrated impressive cross-modal semantic alignment and reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art performance in 2D computer vision tasks (e.g., detection, VQA) and showing some understanding of temporal actions and *static* spatial relations. Examples include OpenAI's GPT-4o/GPT-5, Google's Gemini, ByteDance's Seed, and open-source models like Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL-3.5.
- **Spatial Perception Expertise Models:** Parallel advancements have occurred in specialized "expertise models" (often built upon visual foundation models like DINOv2), which exhibit strong zero-shot capabilities in spatial perception. These models, such as Dust3R for 3D vision, VGGT for 3D scene reconstruction and pose estimation, and CoTracker/SpatialTracker for pixel-level motion understanding, excel at specific tasks. However, many classical geometric constraints underpinning these models are primarily designed for static scenes.

**The Identified Gap:**
Despite these advancements, the authors highlight a crucial limitation: most existing methods and benchmarks are restricted to scenarios where *either* the observer *or* the observed objects remain static. This creates a significant disconnect from real-world dynamic environments where both observer and objects are continuously in motion. This limitation prevents current models from fully grasping complex, evolving spatial relationships, leading to issues like hallucinations and biases.

**Evolution of Spatial Intelligence Benchmarks:**
The paper contextualizes DSI-Bench within a lineage of benchmarks aimed at evaluating spatial intelligence in VLMs:
- **VSI-Bench (Yang et al., 2025a):** Systematically measures performance across eight categories of spatial reasoning and explores cognitive maps.
- **MMSI-Bench (Yang et al., 2025b):** Focuses on multi-image dependency, addressing problems not solvable from a single viewpoint.
- **VLM4D (Zhou et al., 2025):** Extends to video as a 4D modality, exploring spatiotemporal correlations.
- **3DSR-Bench (Ma et al., 2024) and Ori-Bench (Wang etal., 2024c):** Introduced strategies like Evalflip and queries from uncommon perspectives to analyze hallucination and orientation biases in more diverse (but still largely static) settings.

**DSI-Bench's Unique Contribution to the Landscape:**
DSI-Bench advances this research landscape by explicitly and comprehensively targeting "Dynamic Spatial Intelligence." It moves beyond previous benchmarks by:
1. **Focusing on True Dynamics:** Systematically exploring scenarios where *both* the observer and observed objects are in motion, a critical real-world condition largely neglected before.
2. **Decoupled Motion Analysis:** Disentangling the motion patterns of the observer and the observed object, offering a granular understanding of how models perceive these distinct yet interacting dynamics.
3. **Robustness Evaluation:** Introducing spatio-temporal symmetry-based augmentation and group-wise evaluation to rigorously assess model biases, hallucinations, and overall robustness in dynamic settings, moving beyond simple accuracy metrics.
4. **Challenging Assumptions:** Revealing that current methods, including those leveraging classical 3D constraints, introduce instability in dynamic scenes, thereby questioning the direct applicability of static-scene assumptions to dynamic understanding.

In essence, DSI-Bench positions itself as a crucial next step, pushing the frontier of AI research from static or semi-dynamic spatial understanding towards a more complete and ecologically valid "dynamic spatial intelligence" essential for general-purpose intelligent agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind DSI-Bench stems from the recognition that real-world interaction and navigation fundamentally involve dynamic spatial reasoning, an ability humans possess intuitively but current AI models largely lack. Whether it's a primitive hunter adjusting to prey movements or a modern autonomous vehicle navigating traffic, understanding how objects and the observer's own position change simultaneously in 3D space is paramount.

The authors identify that while Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and specialized visual expertise models have excelled in static environments or tasks limited to 2D projections, their capabilities significantly diminish when confronted with complex, dynamic 3D scenarios where both the camera (observer) and the objects within the scene are in motion. This limitation results in models exhibiting "hallucinations and biases in dynamic spatial reasoning due to semantic misleadness and coupled motion understanding."

Given this crucial gap, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Define and Introduce Dynamic Spatial Intelligence (DSI):** To formally conceptualize and articulate the problem of dynamic spatial intelligence, which involves the perception and reasoning of spatio-temporal relationships where positions, distances, and orientations of observers and objects evolve over time.
2. **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark (DSI-Bench):** To create a novel benchmark specifically designed for the systematic evaluation of models' dynamic spatial reasoning abilities. This benchmark aims to be realistic, diverse, and robust against inherent data biases.
3. **Systematically Evaluate State-of-the-Art Models:** To conduct a thorough evaluation of leading general VLMs and specialized 3D expertise models on DSI-Bench, thereby exposing their strengths and, more importantly, their limitations in dynamic scenarios.
4. **Decouple and Analyze Motion Understanding:** To conduct a detailed, decoupled analysis of models' ability to understand distinct forms of motion, separating the observer's motion from the observed objects' motion.
5. **Identify and Characterize Model Biases and Hallucinations:** To leverage novel evaluation strategies, particularly spatio-temporal flip augmentation, to uncover and analyze specific biases (e.g., forward bias, confusion between rotation and translation) and hallucination tendencies in dynamic spatial reasoning.
6. **Provide Insights for Future Development:** To derive valuable findings and insights from the evaluation and error analysis, guiding the future development of more robust and intelligent multimodal AI systems capable of truly understanding and interacting with dynamic 3D environments.

Ultimately, the motivation is to bridge the gap between current AI capabilities and the intuitive dynamic spatial intelligence that humans exhibit, paving the way for more reliable and capable AI agents in complex, real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing DSI-Bench and evaluating models is robust and multi-faceted, designed to rigorously assess dynamic spatial intelligence while mitigating data biases.

#### 3.1. Concept of Dynamic Spatial Intelligence (DSI)

The authors define DSI as the perception and reasoning of spatio-temporal relationships among the observer, observed objects, and the scene, particularly when all entities are in motion. This extends traditional static spatial reasoning to include temporal variations in position, distance, and orientation.

#### 3.2. Task Taxonomy

DSI tasks are categorized based on three fundamental 3D entities:
- **Object–Scene tasks:** Examine spatial relationships between objects and the scene, differentiating between moving and stationary observer conditions.
- **Observer–Scene tasks:** Evaluate tracking changes in the observer’s 3D pose under dynamic and static conditions.
- **Observer–Object tasks:** Focus on relative relationships between the observer and the observed object, including distance and orientation changes.
This taxonomy structures the VQA pairs within the benchmark.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Construction Process

DSI-Bench comprises nearly 1,000 dynamic scene videos and over 1,700 manually annotated VQA questions. The construction pipeline involves:

- **Data Collection and Standardization:**
 - **Diverse Sources:** Videos were sampled from CameraBench (camera motion), Kinetics-700 (object motion), and SynFMC (synthetic motion-control), supplemented by LLaVA-178K and online sources for greater diversity.
 - **Motion Patterns:** The dataset covers nine decoupled motion patterns for both observer and observed objects, including translation (e.g., forward, upward), rotation (clockwise, counterclockwise), and combinations.
 - **Preprocessing:** PySceneDetect was used for scene segmentation. SpatialTrackerV2 filtered out irregular observer motion. Human experts performed final selection and clip definition. All videos were standardized to 480p resolution and a minimum duration of 3 seconds.

- **Question-Answer Generation:**
 - **Manual Annotation:** Human annotators provided motion patterns for both observer and objects in 3D scenes, as well as relative distance changes and object orientation information.
 - **Template-Based VQA:** Cam-Scene, Obj-Scene, relative-distance, and relative-orientation VQA pairs were generated using templates based on these annotations.
 - **Reference Point Consistency:** To avoid ambiguity in dynamic scenes, a fixed 3D reference point (initial pose of observer or object) was maintained.
 - **Expert Review:** All VQA pairs underwent expert review, filtering, and refinement to ensure clarity and eliminate ambiguity.

- **Spatio-Temporal Flip Augmentation:**
 - This critical step mitigates 3D motion pattern biases and assesses model robustness. Each original video generates four variants:
 1. Standard
 2. Horizontal flip
 3. Reverse (temporal reversal)
 4. Reverse + Horizontal flip
 - **Answer Adjustment:** Questions remain unchanged, but answer options are symmetrically adjusted rule-based (e.g., "forward" becomes "backward" after reversal; "clockwise" becomes "counterclockwise" after flip). Manual correction handles cases where rule-based substitution is insufficient (e.g., due to reference frame shifts).

#### 3.4. Evaluation Setup

- **Benchmark Models:**
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** A wide array of state-of-the-art proprietary (Nova-pro-v1, GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Seed-1.6, Seed-1.6-vision) and open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL series, InternVL-3.5 series) were evaluated.
 - **3D Expertise Models:** Two representative models, VGGT and SpatialTrackerV2, were included for comparison.

- **VLM Evaluation Details:**
 - **Accuracy Metric:** Direct comparison of model output to ground truth.
 - **Standardized Settings:** Temperature of zero, max output length 2,048 tokens, 5 fps video sampling rate for consistency.
 - **Reasoning Settings (MindCube Protocol):**
 - **RAWQA (Direct Answering):** Models output only the final answer.
 - **FFR (Free-Form Reasoning):** Models articulate their reasoning process before providing an answer, to investigate reasoning impact.

- **3D Expertise Model Evaluation Details:**
 - Manual calibration of observed object orientations and refinement of Segment Anything masks were performed.
 - Predicted trajectories of observer and objects were extracted and mapped to answer choices via rule-based procedures.
 - Orientation reasoning tasks were removed for these models as their design primarily focuses on geometric tracking rather than semantic-level orientation inference.

- **Spatio-Temporal Flip Evaluation Strategies:**
 - **Sample-wise:** Each of the four spatio-temporal flip variants is treated as an independent sample, evaluating individual performance.
 - **Group-wise:** The four variants originating from a single video are grouped. An instance is considered correct only if at least three of the four augmented answers are correct. This strategy directly measures robustness against symmetry transformations and bias.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that DSI-Bench provides a rigorous and fair assessment of current models' dynamic spatial intelligence, while also offering diagnostic insights into their limitations and biases.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 14 VLMs and expertise models on DSI-Bench yielded several critical insights into the current state of dynamic spatial intelligence:

1. **Current Models Struggle Significantly with Dynamic Tasks:**
 - A consistent and notable decline in accuracy was observed for most models when transitioning from static to dynamic scenarios, regardless of whether the observer or the observed object was in motion (Figure 5).
 - This performance degradation was particularly severe in models with more advanced spatial reasoning capabilities (e.g., Seed-1.6-vision's accuracy dropped by 2.67% for object dynamics with a moving observer and 11.55% for observer motion under dynamic conditions).
 - The group-wise evaluation, which assesses robustness against spatio-temporal flips, further amplified this struggle, showing reduced robustness in dynamic settings across the board for VLMs.

2. **Free-Form Reasoning (FFR) Offers Only Marginal and Unstable Benefits:**
 - For most VLMs, explicitly requesting free-form reasoning before answering led to only minor and inconsistent improvements in overall accuracy (Table 2).
 - Some top models, such as Gemini-2.5-Pro and InternVL3.5-241B, even showed a slight *decrease* in accuracy in FFR mode.
 - This suggests that current VLMs' language-based reasoning component often fails to compensate for fundamental inaccuracies in their visual perception, sometimes even introducing new biases from common-sense knowledge. Models were also observed to generate incoherent text or fail to terminate their reasoning.

3. **VLMs Exhibit Limited Robustness:**
 - A substantial performance drop was observed across all task categories for VLMs when comparing sample-wise accuracy (treating each augmented video independently) with group-wise accuracy (requiring consistency across flipped variants) (Table 1).
 - In stark contrast, 3D expertise models like SpatialTrackerV2 showed minimal degradation in group-wise evaluation, indicating their superior geometric robustness. This highlights that VLMs often rely on superficial cues or biases rather than a deep, robust understanding of dynamic spatial information.

4. **Scaling Model Size Enhances Accuracy but Not Robustness:**
 - Larger models within the same VLM architecture generally achieved higher *sample-wise* accuracy on DSI-Bench (e.g., QwenVL2.5-72B outperformed its 32B counterpart by 2.8%). This suggests that increased parameters improve perceptual granularity.
 - However, in *group-wise* evaluation (emphasizing robustness), larger models often underperformed their smaller counterparts (e.g., QwenVL2.5-72B and InternVL3.5-38B). This critical finding implies that simply scaling up model size does not eliminate inherent biases in spatial perception and reasoning patterns, suggesting that architectural or training methodology improvements are needed for true dynamic spatial intelligence.

**Error Analysis (Detailed Observations):**

- **Forward Bias:** VLMs demonstrated a strong "forward bias," disproportionately selecting "forward" options, far exceeding its actual prevalence in the ground truth (Figure 4). This bias can lead to hallucinations, where even fixed objects are perceived as moving forward (Figure 6), likely due to imbalances in training datasets where forward motion is common.
- **Confusion Between Rotation and Translation:** VLMs often conflated observer's in-place rotations with translational movements (Figure 7). They struggled to distinguish whether visual changes were due to the observer rotating or physically moving through space. 3D expertise models, leveraging classical geometric constraints, performed better in these specific tasks.
- **Coupled Motion Reasoning:** VLMs exhibited difficulty in independently inferring the motion of the observer and the observed object. They frequently conflated these motions, mistakenly substituting one for the other or generalizing relative motion to the scene's reference frame (Figure 8), leading to significant hallucinations.
- **Instability in 3D Expertise Models:** While robust in camera pose estimation, 3D expertise models showed instability in estimating observer-object relative distances, especially under viewpoint changes and foreground occlusions. This instability can indirectly impact the accuracy of object motion estimation.

In summary, current state-of-the-art models, especially VLMs, are far from achieving human-like dynamic spatial intelligence. They are plagued by biases, lack robustness in dynamic settings, and struggle to accurately decouple and reason about simultaneous motions, even with explicit reasoning prompts or increased model scale.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of DSI-Bench marks a significant contribution to the field of artificial intelligence, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Defining a New Frontier:** By formally introducing and benchmarking "Dynamic Spatial Intelligence," this work carves out a new, critical area for research. It shifts the focus from static or simplified dynamic scenarios to the more complex and realistic challenge of understanding simultaneous motion of both observer and objects in 3D space.

2. **Elevating Real-World Relevance:** DSI-Bench is invaluable for developing AI systems that can function effectively in real-world applications. Industries such as autonomous driving (interpreting complex traffic dynamics), robotics (navigating and interacting with moving environments), augmented/virtual reality (anchoring virtual objects in a dynamically changing real world), and surveillance (tracking multiple moving entities) critically rely on robust dynamic spatial intelligence. This benchmark provides a testbed directly relevant to these high-stakes applications.

3. **Comprehensive and Rigorous Evaluation:** The benchmark's construction methodology, including diverse video sources, meticulous manual annotation, and especially the innovative spatio-temporal flip augmentation with group-wise evaluation, sets a new standard for robustness assessment. It moves beyond superficial accuracy to reveal deeper, systemic biases and inconsistencies in model behavior.

4. **Diagnostic Insights for Model Development:** The detailed error analysis (forward bias, confusion between rotation and translation, coupled motion reasoning) provides invaluable diagnostic information. This pinpointing of specific failure modes offers clear directions for future research and development, guiding researchers to focus on:
 - Architectural designs that better decouple observer and object motion reasoning.
 - Training strategies that explicitly address and mitigate common semantic and data biases.
 - Novel ways to integrate robust 3D geometric principles into VLMs for dynamic scenes, moving beyond the limitations of classical constraints.
 - Improvements in stability for relative distance and motion estimation in 3D expertise models.

5. **Challenging the "Scaling Hypothesis":** The finding that larger VLMs, while improving overall accuracy, do not necessarily enhance robustness (and sometimes even decrease it in group-wise evaluation) is a profound insight. It challenges the prevailing "scaling hypothesis" by suggesting that simply increasing model size is not sufficient for achieving true dynamic spatial intelligence. Instead, fundamental breakthroughs in model architecture, inductive biases, or training paradigms are likely required.

6. **Fostering Reproducibility and Collaboration:** The commitment to releasing the dataset and evaluation code ensures reproducibility and encourages broad participation from the research community. This open access will accelerate progress by allowing researchers worldwide to test their models against a shared, challenging standard and build upon the insights provided.

In conclusion, DSI-Bench is more than just a dataset; it is a critical diagnostic tool and a roadmap for the next generation of multimodal AI. By highlighting the current deficiencies in dynamic spatial reasoning, it compels the research community to pursue more sophisticated and ecologically valid approaches, ultimately moving closer to building truly intelligent agents capable of navigating and understanding our dynamic 3D world.
