# MAPS: Preserving Vision-Language Representations via Module-Wise Proximity Scheduling for Better Vision-Language-Action Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called MAPS improves the generalization of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by employing module-wise proximity scheduling during fine-tuning, preserving pre-trained vision-language representations. This approach enhances out-of-distribution performance on various robotic benchmarks and in real-world robot experiments without adding computational overhead.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned on limited robotics data, losing broad generalization and rich pre-trained priors.
- Existing fine-tuning methods, such as freezing modules or applying uniform regularization, are either too restrictive, computationally expensive, or fail to account for the distinct roles of different VLM components.
- The challenge lies in balancing necessary action adaptation with the preservation of the VLM's extensive world knowledge and linguistic understanding.

## Method

- Introduced ==Module-Wise Proximity Scheduling== (**MAPS**), which extends ==Selective Projection Decay== (SPD) by applying a layer-wise proximity schedule during fine-tuning.
- MAPS assigns a distinct regularization strength to each VLM submodule, decreasing linearly from early visual layers (high regularization) to late language layers (low regularization), based on empirically observed importance.
- This "plug-and-play" method integrates into existing VLA architectures without new parameters, auxiliary networks, or additional data, ensuring efficient and practical deployment.

## Results

- MAPS improved out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization in simulations, boosting average OOD performance by +**26.9%** on SimplerEnv for MiniVLA-OFT, often surpassing larger models.
- Real-world robot experiments on a Franka arm showed substantial gains, with average in-distribution (ID) performance increasing from **40.0%** to **72.5%** and average OOD success from **22.5%** to **52.5%**.
- The framework proved architecture-agnostic, consistently delivering OOD performance improvements (average gain of 1–**5%** on LIBERO) across four state-of-the-art VLA backbones without adding parameters or data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic analysis revealed an empirical hierarchy for VLM component preservation, where early visual features (e.g., DINOv2) are more critical for out-of-distribution generalization than higher-level language layers.
> - Fine-tuning requires differential regularization across VLM modules, as a uniform approach fails to balance task adaptation with the preservation of pre-trained knowledge effectively.
> - Integrating this module-wise hierarchy with a linear proximity scheduling mechanism is crucial for enabling robust VLA generalization without sacrificing adaptability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning has seen rapid advancements with the emergence of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. These models represent a significant step towards unified embodied intelligence, allowing robots to understand natural language instructions, perceive complex environments, and execute actions end-to-end. Prominent examples include RT-1, PaLM-E, RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0, which have demonstrated impressive capabilities by leveraging the rich visual and semantic priors embedded in pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like DINOv2, SigLIP, and LLaMA.

However, a fundamental challenge in VLA research is the **scarcity of large-scale, diverse robotics data**. Unlike the vast web-scale datasets used for VLM pre-training, robotics datasets are typically limited, sparse, and task-specific. This disparity leads to a critical problem: naive fine-tuning of pre-trained VLMs on robotics data often results in **catastrophic forgetting**, where the model loses its broad generalization capabilities, spatial reasoning, world knowledge, and linguistic grounding. The models become highly susceptible to overfitting to the specific fine-tuning data, leading to a dramatic degradation in performance when faced with novel tasks, objects, or environments (out-of-distribution, OOD scenarios).

The research community has proposed several strategies to mitigate this loss of generalization while enabling adaptation to the action space:
- **Freezing modules:** Keeping parts of the VLM (e.g., visual encoders) frozen during fine-tuning to preserve their pre-trained representations. While simple, this can overconstrain adaptation to robotics-specific cues.
- **Dual-encoder designs:** Employing separate frozen and trainable visual pathways, which offers flexibility but at the cost of doubled memory and computational resources.
- **Weight interpolation techniques:** Gradually reverting visual weights to their pre-trained state during fine-tuning or after, often requiring multiple training stages and increased complexity.
- **Regularization methods:** Applying various forms of regularization to prevent excessive deviation from pre-trained weights. Existing methods, however, typically apply uniform regularization strength across all model components, failing to account for their distinct roles and varying sensitivities to fine-tuning.

This paper positions MAPS as a significant advancement within this landscape. It directly addresses the limitations of existing approaches by introducing an **empirically guided, module-wise proximity scheduling** strategy. Unlike uniform regularization, MAPS recognizes that different VLM components contribute differently to generalization (e.g., early visual layers for geometric priors, late language layers for action grounding) and thus require differential treatment during fine-tuning. By leveraging insights from robust fine-tuning (specifically Selective Projection Decay, SPD) and enhancing it with architectural awareness, MAPS offers a **lightweight, plug-and-play solution** that achieves superior generalization without introducing additional parameters, auxiliary networks, or data. This work represents a crucial step towards developing truly generalizable and robust VLA models for real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient fine-tuning framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that effectively **balances action adaptation with the preservation of pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) generalization capabilities**. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting:** VLA models, when fine-tuned on limited robotics data, suffer from catastrophic forgetting. This leads to the erosion of rich visual and semantic priors learned during large-scale VLM pre-training, resulting in poor generalization to new tasks, objects, or environments. The primary motivation is to counteract this phenomenon.
2. **Limitations of Existing Solutions:**
 - **Model Freezing:** While simple, freezing certain modules (e.g., visual encoders) can be too restrictive, limiting the model's ability to adapt to the specific action space and introducing task-specific inductive biases that don't generalize universally. Freezing also often overlooks the importance of language module adaptation.
 - **Complex Architectural Modifications:** Approaches like dual-encoders or weight interpolation improve flexibility but come at the cost of increased computational overhead, memory requirements, and architectural complexity, making them less practical for widespread adoption.
 - **Uniform Regularization:** Existing robust fine-tuning techniques, while valuable, typically apply a single, uniform regularization strength across the entire VLM. This ignores the fact that different modules (e.g., early visual layers vs. late language layers) play distinct roles and require different levels of flexibility during adaptation. Early visual layers might need to stay close to their pre-trained state to preserve geometric priors, while language layers might need more freedom to adapt to action-specific semantics.
3. **Uncovering Empirical Hierarchies:** The authors were motivated to systematically investigate how different VLM components contribute to generalization when subjected to various fine-tuning strategies. This led to the empirical discovery of a "hierarchy of importance" among modules, where visual components (like DINOv2) are more critical to preserve than higher-level language layers for maintaining broad generalization.
4. **Developing a Lightweight and Seamless Solution:** A core motivation was to create a fine-tuning framework that could be easily integrated into existing VLA architectures without introducing new parameters, auxiliary models, or requiring additional data. This ensures high practicality and broad applicability across diverse VLA backbones.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond rigid or uniformly applied constraints by proposing a more nuanced, **module-wise and scheduled approach** to VLM preservation. This approach is designed to selectively guide the fine-tuning process, allowing necessary adaptation while safeguarding the foundational knowledge encoded in the pre-trained VLM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a systematic analysis of VLA component behavior during fine-tuning, followed by the development and validation of the Module-Wise Proximity Scheduling (MAPS) framework.

#### 3.1. Systematic Analysis of Model Freezing

The authors begin by revisiting the common practice of model freezing to understand its impact on VLA generalization. They decompose typical VLA architectures (like OpenVLA, which integrates DINOv2 and SigLIP) into four key components: DINOv2, SigLIP, early language layers, and late language layers. By systematically freezing different combinations of these modules, they evaluate their impact on both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) performance across benchmarks like SimplerEnv and LIBERO.
This analysis reveals a crucial empirical finding:
- **Language Adaptation is Essential:** Freezing the entire language backbone severely cripples performance.
- **Vision Encoder Preservation is Beneficial:** Freezing the visual encoder generally improves performance, especially OOD.
- **DINOv2 over SigLIP:** Preserving DINOv2 (for geometric priors) is more impactful for OOD generalization than preserving SigLIP (for vision-language alignment).
- **Late Language Layers for Task Performance:** Fine-tuning late language layers is crucial for task-specific adaptation.
- **Hierarchy of Importance:** The findings suggest an empirical hierarchy for preserving generalization: DINOv2 > SigLIP > Early Language Layers > Late Language Layers.
However, the analysis also concludes that freezing alone is insufficient, as it introduces strong, task-dependent inductive biases that can be brittle.

#### 3.2. Foundation in Robust Fine-Tuning (RFT) and Selective Projection Decay (SPD)

Recognizing the limitations of hard freezing, the authors turn to soft regularization techniques, specifically robust fine-tuning (RFT) methods. They build upon the Selective Projection Decay (SPD) framework, which itself is an extension of L2-SP and TPGM.
- **L2-SP and TPGM:** These methods penalize or constrain the distance between fine-tuned parameters ($\theta_t$) and pre-trained initialization ($\theta_0$), ensuring the model stays close to its original state. TPGM specifically uses a Projected Gradient Method to keep weights within an $l_2$ ball around $\theta_0$.
- **SPD:** SPD refines this by dynamically determining *when* and *how much* to project weights back. It triggers a projection if the gradient-displacement correlation (`c_t`) is negative (indicating movement away from $\theta_0$ that increases task loss), and the projection strength is controlled by a single hyperparameter `λ` and a deviation ratio `r_t`.
The authors observe that directly applying RFT/SPD with a *uniform* `λ` across the entire VLM yields only modest improvements, as it fails to account for the differential roles of VLA components.

#### 3.3. Introducing Module-Wise Proximity Scheduling (MAPS)

MAPS is the core innovation, designed to bridge the gap between the modularity of freezing and the flexibility of soft regularization by adapting SPD.
- **Layer-wise Proximity Schedule:** Instead of a single global `λ`, MAPS assigns a distinct proximity weight `λ_k` to each ordered submodule `k` of the VLM.
- **Linear Decay:** A linear schedule is adopted for `λ_k`: `λ_k = λ_max * (1 - (k-1)/(|L|-1))`. This monotonically decreases the regularization strength from the earliest visual layers (e.g., DINOv2, where `λ_1 = λ_max`) to the latest language layers (where `λ_|L| = 0`). This design directly encodes the empirically observed hierarchy of importance.
- **Differential Treatment for Non-Pretrained Modules:** Modules initialized from scratch, such as the action head, proprioceptive-state projector, or other non-VLM components, are assigned `λ_k = 0`, meaning they undergo full fine-tuning without any proximity constraint.
- **Integration with Adam Optimization:** During each optimization step (using Adam), MAPS first computes an unconstrained update (`~θ_t`). It then calculates the gradient-displacement correlation (`c_t = -g_t^T (θ_{t-1} - θ_0)`). If `c_t < 0` (indicating a problematic deviation from $\theta_0$), a projection step is applied: `θ_t ← ~θ_t - λ_k * r_t * (~θ_t - θ_0)`. Otherwise, the unconstrained update is accepted.

#### 3.4. Experimental Validation

MAPS is validated extensively across four state-of-the-art VLA backbones (MiniVLA-VQ, MiniVLA-OFT, VLA-Adapter, OpenVLA-OFT) and diverse benchmarks:
- **SimplerEnv:** Lightweight simulation for ID and OOD generalization (visual, novel object, novel category shifts).
- **CALVIN:** Long-horizon tabletop manipulation in a real-to-sim setting, focusing on OOD generalization (ABC→D protocol).
- **LIBERO:** Procedurally generated household tasks testing generalization across task configurations, object appearances, and spatial layouts.
- **Franka Emika Panda Robot:** Real-world tabletop manipulation experiments with custom ID and OOD tasks, assessing practical transferability.

The experimental setup emphasizes fine-tuning VLM weights directly from scratch using MAPS, without external pre-training on large robotic datasets unless specified for baselines. Ablation studies are also conducted to validate the choice of the linear scheduler and `λ_max` values.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several compelling findings demonstrating the effectiveness and practicality of the MAPS framework:

1. **Empirical Hierarchy for VLM Preservation:** The initial systematic analysis of freezing different VLM components revealed a consistent hierarchy of importance for maintaining generalization: DINOv2 (early visual features) > SigLIP (vision-language alignment) > Early Language Layers > Late Language Layers. This empirical insight directly informed the design of MAPS.
2. **Significant OOD Generalization Boost (Simulation):**
 - **SimplerEnv:** MAPS consistently improved OOD generalization across MiniVLA-VQ, MiniVLA-OFT, and OpenVLA-OFT. For MiniVLA-OFT, MAPS boosted average OOD performance by an impressive +26.9%, often matching or exceeding the OOD generalizability of much larger, extensively pre-trained baselines (like RT-1-X, Octo, and π0) despite using substantially smaller pre-training corpora.
 - **CALVIN:** MAPS led to notable gains in long-horizon task success rates. For MiniVLA-OFT, it improved average sequence length by +0.7 and boosted success rates for completing 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-task sequences by roughly 15% each. Modest but consistent improvements were also observed for OpenVLA-OFT.
 - **LIBERO:** MAPS delivered consistent OOD performance improvements (average gain of 1–5%) across all four evaluated backbones. Notably, it enabled the base MiniVLA model, which initially showed no OOD capability, to achieve 8% and 10% success on LIBERO-Goal and LIBERO-Long, respectively. It also provided moderate ID performance boosts for some models.
3. **Enhanced Real-World Performance and Robustness:**
 - **Franka Emika Panda:** MAPS demonstrated substantial gains on a real-world robotic arm, increasing average ID performance from 40.0% to 72.5% and average OOD success from 22.5% to 52.5%. This highlights MAPS's ability to facilitate stronger policy learning from limited demonstrations and improve generalization under real-world distribution shifts.
 - **Fine-grained Analysis:** Detailed analysis of subtasks on the Franka robot showed that MAPS improved critical intermediate steps, such as reliable grasps, accurate object localization, and stable multi-step execution, even under challenging "Hard" OOD conditions. This suggests a more robust and careful control policy.
4. **Architecture Agnosticism:** The consistent performance gains across a diverse set of VLA backbones (MiniVLA-VQ, MiniVLA-OFT, OpenVLA-OFT, VLA-Adapter) and action tokenization schemes (vector-quantized VAE, continuous regression) confirm that MAPS is a flexible and architecture-agnostic framework.
5. **Validation of Module-Wise Scheduling:** Ablation studies comparing different schedulers (constant, cosine, linear) for the projection strength `λ` on LIBERO showed that the linear scheduler, as implemented in MAPS, provided the most stable ID performance and the greatest OOD gains, with `λ_max=0.5` being an optimal configuration. This empirically validates the core design choice of structured, layer-wise proximity control.
6. **Efficiency:** MAPS achieves its strong performance without introducing any additional parameters, auxiliary networks, or data. This makes it highly computationally efficient and "plug-and-play," outperforming more complex and resource-intensive methods like dual-encoders while maintaining a significantly smaller model footprint.

In summary, MAPS proves to be a powerful, lightweight, and versatile fine-tuning framework that effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting in VLA models, leading to robust generalization across a wide range of simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MAPS carries significant implications for the field of Vision-Language-Action models and robotics:

1. **Enabling Robust VLM-to-VLA Transfer:** MAPS provides a critical solution to the widespread problem of catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning powerful pre-trained VLMs for robotics. By intelligently preserving foundational VLM knowledge while allowing necessary adaptation, it unlocks the full potential of these large models for embodied AI, making them more resilient to the distribution shifts inherent in real-world environments.
2. **Highly Practical and Accessible:** One of MAPS's most significant contributions is its "plug-and-play" nature. It introduces no additional parameters, requires no auxiliary networks, and uses no extra data beyond the fine-tuning demonstrations. This low overhead makes it incredibly easy to integrate into existing and future VLA architectures, democratizing access to robust fine-tuning for a broader range of researchers and practitioners, regardless of their computational resources.
3. **Improved Generalization in Robotics:** The consistent and substantial improvements in out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across multiple benchmarks (SimplerEnv, CALVIN, LIBERO) and, critically, on a real-world Franka Emika Panda robot, represent a major step forward for robotic learning. This enhanced robustness means robots can more reliably perform tasks in novel environments, with unseen objects, or under varying conditions, moving closer to the goal of truly generalist robot policies.
4. **Guidance for Future VLM and VLA Design:** The empirical hierarchy uncovered by the authors (DINOv2 > SigLIP > Early Language > Late Language in terms of preservation importance) provides invaluable insights. This knowledge can guide the design of future VLMs, prompting developers to consider how different components contribute to downstream robotics tasks and where preservation efforts should be prioritized during pre-training or architectural development.
5. **Efficiency and Sustainability:** By achieving superior performance with minimal computational overhead compared to alternative methods (e.g., dual encoders), MAPS contributes to more sustainable and efficient development of embodied AI systems. This is particularly important as model sizes continue to grow.
6. **Foundational Principle for Adaptation:** The core idea of "empirically guided proximity to pretrained VLMs" as a principle for VLM-to-VLA transfer is a powerful new paradigm. It moves beyond generic regularization or rigid freezing, offering a more nuanced and effective approach to balancing stability and flexibility during adaptation. This principle could inspire further research into dynamic, architecture-aware fine-tuning strategies across various domains.

In conclusion, MAPS offers a simple yet profoundly impactful solution for better VLM-to-VLA transfer. Its effectiveness, practicality, and the fundamental insights it provides are set to significantly advance the capabilities and deployment of intelligent robotic systems.
