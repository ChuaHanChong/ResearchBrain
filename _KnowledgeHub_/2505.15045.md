# Diffusion vs. Autoregressive Language Models: A Text Embedding Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University, Yale University, and NYU Shanghai demonstrate that diffusion language models outperform autoregressive LLMs for text embedding tasks, with their DIFFEMBED approach achieving 20% improvement on long-document retrieval and 8% improvement on reasoning-intensive retrieval by leveraging the inherent bidirectional attention architecture of diffusion models, while also introducing REASONAUG, a new training dataset for logical reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Autoregressive language models have unidirectional attention that limits their ability to capture global context in text embedding tasks
- Existing LLM-based embedding models struggle with long documents and reasoning-intensive retrieval scenarios
- Current training datasets lack sufficient reasoning-intensive examples for developing specialized embedding capabilities

## Method

- Propose ==DIFFEMBED==, which uses diffusion language models (DREAM-7B) as the backbone for text embeddings instead of autoregressive LLMs
- Leverage the ==bidirectional attention== inherent in diffusion models to better capture global document context
- Create ==REASONAUG== dataset with logically related question-solution pairs and hard negatives for training reasoning capabilities
- Apply contrastive learning to fine-tune the diffusion model for embedding tasks

## Results

- **20%** improvement over LLM-based models on LONGEMBED long-document retrieval benchmark
- **8%** improvement on BRIGHT reasoning-intensive retrieval, with **16.4%** improvement on TheoremQA specifically when using REASONAUG training
- Competitive performance on instruction-following retrieval and traditional MTEB text embedding benchmarks
- Ablation studies confirm bidirectional attention's importance for global context understanding

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional attention is crucial for encoding long and complex documents, with reverse attention having greater impact than in LLM-based models
> - Generalist embedding models underperform on reasoning tasks because they aren't trained to recognize theorems or use them as clustering signals
> - Diffusion models' architecture naturally suits text embedding tasks better than the unidirectional nature of autoregressive models
