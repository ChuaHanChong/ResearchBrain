# Diffusion vs. Autoregressive Language Models: A Text Embedding Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University, Yale University, and NYU Shanghai demonstrate that diffusion language models outperform autoregressive LLMs for text embedding tasks, with their DIFFEMBED approach achieving 20% improvement on long-document retrieval and 8% improvement on reasoning-intensive retrieval by leveraging the inherent bidirectional attention architecture of diffusion models, while also introducing REASONAUG, a new training dataset for logical reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Autoregressive language models have unidirectional attention that limits their ability to capture global context in text embedding tasks
- Existing LLM-based embedding models struggle with long documents and reasoning-intensive retrieval scenarios
- Current training datasets lack sufficient reasoning-intensive examples for developing specialized embedding capabilities

## Method

- Propose ==DIFFEMBED==, which uses diffusion language models (DREAM-7B) as the backbone for text embeddings instead of autoregressive LLMs, leveraging their inherent ==bidirectional attention== to better capture global document context
- Create ==REASONAUG== dataset with logically related question-solution pairs and hard negatives for training reasoning capabilities
- Apply contrastive learning to fine-tune the diffusion model for embedding tasks

## Results

- **20%** improvement over LLM-based models on LONGEMBED long-document retrieval, with competitive performance on instruction-following retrieval and traditional MTEB text embedding benchmarks
- **8%** improvement on BRIGHT reasoning-intensive retrieval, with **16.4%** improvement on TheoremQA specifically when using REASONAUG training
- Ablation studies confirm bidirectional attention's importance for global context understanding

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional attention is crucial for encoding long and complex documents, with reverse attention having greater impact than in LLM-based models
> - Generalist embedding models underperform on reasoning tasks because they aren't trained to recognize theorems or use them as clustering signals
> - Diffusion models' architecture naturally suits text embedding tasks better than the unidirectional nature of autoregressive models

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of text embeddings: the limitations of adapting autoregressive language models (LLMs) for tasks that require bidirectional context understanding. The research fits into the broader landscape by:

- **Addressing a Known Issue:** Previous research has highlighted the unidirectional nature of attention in autoregressive LLMs as a bottleneck for text embedding tasks, particularly in scenarios requiring a holistic view of the input text, such as long-document retrieval and logical reasoning.
- **Exploring a Novel Approach:** Instead of focusing on modifying existing LLMs, the paper proposes leveraging diffusion language models, which inherently possess bidirectional attention, as a more natural fit for text embedding.
- **Building upon Recent Advancements:** The work is motivated by the recent success of diffusion LMs in matching or surpassing LLMs on certain reasoning tasks, indicating their potential for capturing complex semantic relationships.
- **Expanding the Scope of Evaluation:** The paper goes beyond traditional text embedding benchmarks and evaluates the proposed approach on tasks that require long-document encoding and logical reasoning capabilities, providing a more comprehensive assessment of its strengths and weaknesses.
- **Contributing a New Dataset:** The introduction of REASONAUG, a dataset specifically designed for training models on reasoning-intensive retrieval, fills a gap in existing resources and enables targeted evaluation of logical reasoning abilities.
- **Competing with Existing Solutions:** The paper directly competes with existing methods, such as LLM2Vec and Echo Embeddings, which attempt to address the limitations of autoregressive LLMs for text embedding. By comparing against these methods, the paper demonstrates the potential advantages of the diffusion LM approach.
- This work builds off other work such as DREAM (Ye et al., 2025), LLM2Vec (BehnamGhader et al., 2024), Echo embeddings, MTEB (Muennighoff et al., 2023), and various other baselines (BERT, T5, E5, etc.).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of the paper are:

- To propose and evaluate a novel approach to text embedding using diffusion language models (DIFFEMBED) due to their inherent bidirectional architecture.
- To systematically compare the performance of DIFFEMBED with LLM-based embedding models across a wide range of tasks, including long-document retrieval, reasoning-intensive retrieval, instruction-following retrieval, and general text embedding tasks.
- To investigate the impact of bidirectional attention on text embedding performance and determine its importance for encoding global context in long and complex text.
- To develop REASONAUG, a new training dataset for reasoning-intensive retrieval, and demonstrate its effectiveness in improving the performance of embedding models on logical reasoning tasks.

The motivation behind this research stems from the desire to overcome the limitations of LLM-based embedding models, which struggle to capture global context due to their unidirectional attention structure. The authors hypothesize that diffusion embeddings, trained at scale with a bidirectional architecture, are better suited for capturing global context and achieving superior performance on long and complex documents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper can be summarized as follows:

- **Model Development:** The authors propose DIFFEMBED, which extracts contextualized token representations from a backbone diffusion LM (DREAM-7B) and aggregates them using mean pooling. Contrastive learning is then used to fine-tune the model for embedding tasks.
- **Dataset Creation:** They create REASONAUG, a new dataset containing pairs of logically related positives and unrelated hard negatives. The dataset is constructed using LLMs to generate questions and solutions covering a range of domains, from mathematics and physics theorems to code. The dataset construction process involves concept generation, question-solution generation, quality checks, and hard negative mining.
- **Experimental Setup:** The authors conduct experiments to compare DIFFEMBED with LLM-based embedding models on four main tasks:
 - Long-document retrieval (using LONGEMBED benchmark)
 - Reasoning-intensive retrieval (using BRIGHT benchmark and REASONAUG dataset)
 - Instruction-following retrieval (using FOLLOWIR benchmark)
 - Traditional text embedding tasks (using MTEB benchmark)
- **Ablation Study:** They perform an ablation study to assess the importance of bidirectional attention by comparing the performance of models with full bidirectional attention versus unidirectional (causal only) attention.
- **Analysis and Case Studies:** The authors conduct in-depth analysis of the experimental results, including case studies, to understand the strengths and weaknesses of DIFFEMBED and LLM-based embedding models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the paper are:

- DIFFEMBED outperforms LLM-based embedding models by 20% on the long-document retrieval benchmark LONGEMBED.
- DIFFEMBED outperforms LLM-based embedding models by 8% on the reasoning-intensive retrieval benchmark BRIGHT. Notably, with REASONAUG, DIFFEMBED surpasses the state-of-the-art performance on TheoremQA tasks in BRIGHT by 16.4%.
- DIFFEMBED achieves competitive performance on instruction-following retrieval and traditional text embedding benchmarks.
- The ablation study confirms that bidirectional attention is crucial for encoding long and complex documents, with the reverse attention having a greater impact in DIFFEMBED than LLM-based models.
- Analysis reveals that generalist embedding models underperform in reasoning-intensive retrieval because they are not trained to recognize theorems or use them as clustering signals.
- Analysis of the Leetcode benchmark shows high levels of noise due to inconsistent query relevance and gold annotations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Advances Text Embedding Technology:** The paper demonstrates the potential of diffusion language models as a viable alternative to LLMs for text embedding, opening up new avenues for research and development in this area.
- **Improves Long-Document Retrieval:** The superior performance of DIFFEMBED on long-document retrieval suggests that it could be used to build more effective search engines and information retrieval systems for large collections of text.
- **Enhances Reasoning Capabilities:** The improved performance on reasoning-intensive retrieval tasks indicates that DIFFEMBED can be used to develop systems that can better understand and reason about complex semantic relationships in text.
- **Informs Model Design:** The ablation study highlights the importance of bidirectional attention for encoding global context, providing valuable insights for the design of future text embedding models.
- **Provides a Valuable Resource:** The REASONAUG dataset can be used by other researchers to train and evaluate models on reasoning-intensive retrieval tasks, fostering further advancements in this area.
- **Challenges Existing Paradigms:** The paper challenges the prevailing focus on autoregressive LLMs for text embedding and encourages researchers to explore alternative approaches, such as diffusion LMs.
- **Spurs Further Research:** This work can inspire further research into the application of diffusion LMs in various NLP tasks, including text generation, summarization, and question answering.

In conclusion, this paper makes a significant contribution to the field of text embeddings by proposing and evaluating a novel approach based on diffusion language models. The findings of the paper have the potential to advance text embedding technology, improve long-document retrieval, enhance reasoning capabilities, and inform the design of future models. The introduction of the REASONAUG dataset and the analysis of noisy benchmarks provide valuable resources and insights for the research community.
