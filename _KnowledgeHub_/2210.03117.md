# MaPLe: Multi-modal Prompt Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MaPLe introduces a multi-modal prompt learning framework that adapts pre-trained Vision-Language (V-L) models like CLIP by simultaneously optimizing learnable prompts in both vision and language branches. This approach improved average novel class accuracy by 3.45% and overall harmonic mean by 2.72% compared to Co-CoOp across 11 datasets, while enhancing cross-dataset and domain generalization.

## Problem

- Existing prompt learning methods for V-L models predominantly adapt only a single modality (either vision or language), which is suboptimal for intrinsically multi-modal architectures like CLIP.
- Current prompt-based adaptation strategies for V-L models often face limitations in consistently generalizing to novel classes, entirely new datasets, or unseen domain shifts.
- Adapting large pre-trained V-L models through full fine-tuning is computationally prohibitive and can lead to catastrophic forgetting or overfitting in low-data scenarios.

## Method

- **MaPLe** introduces learnable ==context prompts== into both the vision and language branches of the pre-trained CLIP model, enabling simultaneous ==multi-modal adaptation==.
- It employs a ==deep prompting== strategy, introducing new learnable tokens in multiple early transformer blocks of both the vision and language encoders to capture hierarchical context.
- A novel ==V-L coupling function== explicitly conditions the vision prompts on the language prompts, fostering mutual synergy and ensuring gradient propagation between the two modalities.

## Results

- MaPLe achieved an absolute average gain of **3.45%** on novel classes and **2.72%** on the overall harmonic mean (HM) across **11 diverse image recognition datasets** compared to the state-of-the-art Co-CoOp method.
- It demonstrated superior cross-dataset generalization, outperforming CoOp on **9 out of 10** and Co-CoOp on **8 out of 10** target datasets, achieving the highest averaged accuracy of **66.30%** across these transfer tasks.
- The framework maintained computational efficiency, incurring only a marginal **0.1%** increase in Floating Point Operations (FLOPS) compared to prior methods, while exhibiting faster convergence and inference speeds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly coupling vision and language prompts leads to synergistic interaction and superior performance compared to independently learning prompts for each modality.
> - Introducing prompts at multiple stages ('deep prompting') within transformer encoders allows for richer context learning and greater flexibility in aligning representations at various abstraction levels.
> - The multi-modal coupled prompting approach is particularly beneficial for datasets with larger distribution shifts from pre-training data or for visual concepts that are rare or less generic.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The work presented in "MaPLe: Multi-modal Prompt Learning" operates within the domain of adapting large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language (V-L) models, such as CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining), for various downstream tasks. These foundational V-L models have demonstrated considerable generalization capabilities due to their training on extensive web-scale datasets, enabling them to comprehend open-vocabulary visual concepts through natural language supervision.

However, adapting these massive models to specific downstream tasks presents several challenges. Full fine-tuning is often computationally prohibitive, can lead to catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge, and risks overfitting, especially in few-shot learning scenarios where training data is scarce. To mitigate these issues, prompt learning emerged as an efficient adaptation strategy, initially in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and subsequently extended to V-L models.

Early adaptations of prompt learning for V-L models, such as CoOp (Context Optimization) and Co-CoOp (Conditional Context Optimization), primarily focused on optimizing continuous prompt vectors within the language branch of the V-L model. These methods aim to reduce the need for manual prompt engineering and provide a mechanism to adapt the model while keeping its core weights frozen. While Co-CoOp addressed certain generalization limitations of CoOp, particularly for novel classes, these approaches generally remain "uni-modal," meaning they adapt only one branch (either language or vision) of the inherently multi-modal V-L architecture.

MaPLe positions itself within this landscape by identifying and addressing this uni-modal limitation. The paper argues that restricting prompt learning to a single modality is suboptimal for V-L models, as it prevents the flexible, dynamic adjustment of both vision and language representation spaces. MaPLe proposes a multi-modal prompt learning approach that aims to achieve a more "complete" adaptation by simultaneously tuning prompts in both the vision and language branches of the V-L model, thereby fostering a stronger alignment between the two modalities for downstream tasks. This represents an evolution from prior uni-modal prompt learning strategies for V-L models.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a novel multi-modal prompt learning framework, termed MaPLe (Multi-modal Prompt Learning), for efficiently adapting pre-trained Vision-Language (V-L) models like CLIP to diverse downstream tasks. This framework aims to improve the alignment between vision and language representations by simultaneously learning prompts in both the vision and language branches of the V-L model.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding existing V-L model adaptation strategies:

1. **Suboptimality of Uni-modal Prompting:** Current prompt learning methods for V-L models predominantly adapt only one branch—either the language encoder (e.g., CoOp, Co-CoOp) or, less commonly, the vision encoder. The authors contend that this uni-modal adaptation is suboptimal for intrinsically multi-modal models like CLIP, where both vision and language encoders contribute equally to forming aligned representations. Adapting only one branch limits the model's flexibility to dynamically adjust both representation spaces to the nuances of a new downstream task.

2. **Need for Complete Adaptation:** To fully leverage the multi-modal nature of V-L models, the authors argue for a "complete" prompting approach that can simultaneously fine-tune representations in both the text and image encoders. This comprehensive adaptation is hypothesized to achieve a more optimal alignment between the visual and linguistic modalities for specific tasks.

3. **Enhancing Generalization:** While V-L models exhibit strong generalization, prompt-based adaptation methods still face challenges in consistently generalizing to novel classes, entirely new target datasets, and unseen domain shifts. For instance, CoOp showed limitations in novel class generalization, which Co-CoOp sought to address by conditioning prompts on image instances. MaPLe is motivated to further enhance this generalization ability across these challenging settings.

4. **Promoting Mutual Synergy:** A simple combination of independent uni-modal prompts may not be sufficient. The authors recognize the importance of establishing a strong coupling between the vision and language prompts. They hypothesize that explicitly conditioning the vision prompts on their language counterparts would ensure mutual synergy and facilitate gradient propagation between the two modalities, preventing the learning of isolated, less effective uni-modal solutions.

5. **Capturing Hierarchical Context:** Transformer-based V-L models process information hierarchically across multiple layers. The motivation includes progressively modeling stage-wise feature relationships by introducing learnable prompts at different early stages (i.e., multiple transformer blocks) in both branches. This "deep prompting" strategy aims to allow richer context learning and provide greater flexibility in aligning representations at various levels of abstraction.

6. **Efficiency Considerations:** The motivation also includes developing an efficient architecture that avoids significant computational overhead during training and inference. Methods like Co-CoOp, which condition prompts on image instances, can introduce efficiency challenges. MaPLe seeks to provide a streamlined design that offers improved performance without substantial computational burden.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The MaPLe (Multi-modal Prompt Learning) framework is designed to efficiently fine-tune pre-trained Vision-Language (V-L) models, specifically CLIP, for improved generalization to downstream tasks through context optimization. The approach deviates from prior uni-modal prompt learning by introducing learnable prompts into both the vision and language branches of CLIP, alongside a mechanism to couple these prompts.

#### 3.1. Foundation: Revisiting CLIP Architecture

MaPLe builds upon a pre-trained CLIP model, specifically employing a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture for the image encoder and a transformer-based text encoder.
- **Image Encoder:** Takes an image `I` and splits it into `M` fixed-size patches, which are projected into patch embeddings. These embeddings, along with a learnable class (CLS) token, are processed through `K` transformer layers. The final image representation `x` is derived from the last layer's CLS token via `ImageProj`.
- **Text Encoder:** Tokenizes text descriptions into word embeddings. These embeddings are processed through `K` transformer layers. The final text representation `z` is obtained by projecting the embedding of the last token from the last transformer block via `TextProj`.
- **Zero-shot Classification:** Predictions are made by calculating the cosine similarity between the image embedding `x` and class-specific text embeddings `z_y`, generated from hand-crafted prompts like "a photo of a <category>."

#### 3.2. MaPLe: Multi-modal Prompt Learning Design

MaPLe introduces several key components to achieve multi-modal prompt learning:

- **Joint Prompting:** Unlike methods that learn prompts only in the language branch, MaPLe simultaneously learns context prompts in both the vision and language branches of CLIP.

- **Deep Language Prompting:**
 - Learnable context tokens `{P^i}` are introduced into the language branch.
 - These tokens are appended to the input word embeddings `W^0`, forming `[P^1, ..., P^b, W^0]`.
 - Crucially, new learnable tokens are introduced in *each* transformer block of the language encoder (`L_i`) up to a specified depth `J`. This means prompts are not just at the input layer but are integrated throughout early transformer stages.
 - For layers beyond `J`, the prompts learned in previous stages are propagated.
 - Only these context prompts are learned during fine-tuning, while the original CLIP text encoder weights remain frozen.

- **Deep Vision Prompting:**
 - Analogous to language prompting, learnable tokens `{~P^i}` are introduced into the vision branch.
 - These tokens are incorporated alongside the input image tokens and the CLS token.
 - New learnable tokens are introduced in deeper transformer layers of the image encoder (`V_i`) up to depth `J`.
 - The authors observe that sharing prompts across stages is more effective than independent prompts, implying that later stages benefit from the contextual learning of earlier stages.
 - Only these vision prompts are learned, with the original CLIP image encoder weights frozen.

- **Vision Language Prompt Coupling (Central Innovation):**
 - To foster interaction and mutual synergy between the two modalities, MaPLe explicitly conditions the vision prompts `{~P}` on the language prompts `P`.
 - This is achieved through a "V-L coupling function" `F(.)`. Specifically, `~P_k = F_k(P_k)` where `F_k` is a linear layer mapping the dimension of language prompts (`d_l`) to the dimension of vision prompts (`d_v`) at each layer `k`.
 - This coupling function is learned during fine-tuning alongside the context prompts. It acts as a bridge, enabling mutual propagation of gradients and encouraging prompts to learn in a shared, aligned embedding space. This contrasts with a hypothetical "Independent V-L Prompting" where both sets of prompts are learned separately without direct interaction.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Few-shot Setting:** All experiments utilize a 16-shot training strategy, where 16 random samples per class are used.
- **Frozen Weights:** During fine-tuning, only the newly introduced context prompts and the V-L coupling function are learned; the weights of the pre-trained CLIP vision and text encoders are frozen.
- **Optimizer and Hyperparameters:** SGD optimizer, trained for 5 epochs (for base-to-novel generalization) or 2 epochs (for cross-dataset evaluation). Learning rates vary (0.0035 for base-to-novel, 0.0026 for ImageNet source).
- **Prompt Initialization:** Language prompts for the first layer (`P^0`) are initialized using pre-trained CLIP word embeddings from a template (e.g., 'a photo of a <category>'), while prompts for subsequent layers are randomly initialized from a normal distribution.
- **Prompt Depth and Length:** Prompt depth `J` is typically set to 9 layers (for base-to-novel) or 3 layers (for ImageNet source), and prompt length (number of tokens) is 2 for both vision and language branches.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research investigated MaPLe across three generalization tasks: base-to-novel class generalization, cross-dataset evaluation, and domain generalization. Ablation studies were also conducted to understand the contribution of different design choices.

#### 4.1. Prompting Design Comparisons (Ablations)

- **Effectiveness of Multi-modal Coupled Prompting:** Ablation studies comparing different prompt designs demonstrated that MaPLe, with its coupled multi-modal prompting (where vision prompts are conditioned on language prompts), achieved the highest harmonic mean (HM) of 78.55% averaged over 11 datasets. This surpassed "Independent V-L Prompting" (HM 77.90%), deep language prompting (HM 77.56%), and deep vision prompting (HM 76.68%), indicating that the synergistic interaction between modalities, rather than just combining them, is beneficial.
- **Prompt Depth:** Performance generally improved as prompt depth (J) increased, with MaPLe achieving its peak performance at J=9 layers. The study noted increased performance sensitivity when randomly initialized prompts were inserted into deeper layers of the frozen model.
- **Prompt Length:** Increasing prompt length tended to maintain base class accuracy but decreased novel class accuracy, suggesting a risk of overfitting with longer prompts. The optimal length was found to be 2.
- **Prompt Initialization:** Initializing first-layer language prompts with a pre-trained word embedding template ('a photo of a <category>') and subsequent layers randomly yielded the best performance compared to fully random or uniform template initialization.
- **Direction of Prompt Coupling:** Conditioning vision prompts on language prompts (P → ˜P) via the coupling function proved more effective than the reverse (˜P → P), possibly due to the higher dimensionality of vision prompts (`d_v > d_l`), suggesting lower information loss during projection.

#### 4.2. Base-to-Novel Generalization

- MaPLe demonstrated consistent improvements across 11 diverse image recognition datasets compared to CLIP zero-shot, CoOp, and Co-CoOp.
- It achieved an absolute average gain of 3.45% on novel classes and 2.72% on overall harmonic mean (HM) compared to the state-of-the-art method Co-CoOp.
- Specifically, MaPLe improved novel class accuracy over CLIP zero-shot on 6 out of 11 datasets, whereas Co-CoOp only improved on 4. The average novel accuracy for MaPLe was 75.14%, compared to Co-CoOp's 71.69% and CLIP's 74.22%.
- On base classes, MaPLe improved over CoOp on 6 out of 11 datasets, maintaining an average base accuracy of 82.28%, while Co-CoOp saw a slight average drop compared to CoOp.
- When compared to CoOp† (CoOp trained under Co-CoOp's settings), MaPLe still showed an improvement of 1.43% in average base accuracy and substantial gains in novel accuracy and HM.

#### 4.3. Cross-Dataset Evaluation

- When trained on all 1000 ImageNet classes and directly transferred to 10 other datasets, MaPLe achieved a competitive performance on the ImageNet source.
- It exhibited stronger generalization to target datasets, outperforming CoOp on 9/10 datasets and Co-CoOp on 8/10 datasets.
- Overall, MaPLe achieved the highest averaged accuracy of 66.30% across these transfer tasks, indicating superior cross-dataset generalization.

#### 4.4. Domain Generalization

- MaPLe consistently improved performance on out-of-distribution datasets, including ImageNetV2, ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, and ImageNet-R, when transferring an ImageNet-trained model. This suggests enhanced robustness to various types of domain shifts.

#### 4.5. Understanding Multi-modal Prompts

- Per-class analysis revealed that MaPLe's performance gains over Co-CoOp were more pronounced on datasets with larger distribution shifts from CLIP's pre-training data (e.g., EuroSAT, DTD – satellite imagery, textures) compared to datasets with smaller shifts (e.g., ImageNet, Caltech101 – generic objects).
- MaPLe showed significant benefits for visual concepts that are typically rare or less generic in the pre-training dataset (e.g., specific satellite image categories, unique actions), while performing competitively on frequent and generic categories.
- Quantitative analysis of text embeddings indicated that MaPLe resulted in better separability among categories, as evidenced by higher average L2 distance and lower average cosine similarity.

#### 4.6. Prompting Complexity

- MaPLe incurred only a marginal increase in Floating Point Operations (FLOPS) (0.1%) compared to CoOp and Co-CoOp.
- In terms of inference speed (FPS), MaPLe provided significantly better performance than Co-CoOp, which suffers from overhead due to its image-instance-conditioned design.
- MaPLe also exhibited faster convergence, requiring approximately half the training epochs of Co-CoOp.
- Even MaPLe†, a version with significantly fewer parameters (approx. 9x less than full MaPLe), still demonstrated improvements over existing methods, suggesting that the architectural design of multi-modal prompting, rather than merely parameter count, contributes to the gains.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The MaPLe framework introduces multi-modal prompt learning as an approach to adapt pre-trained Vision-Language (V-L) models like CLIP, offering several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Comprehensive Model Adaptation:** The core contribution of MaPLe lies in extending prompt learning beyond a single modality (language or vision) to simultaneously adapt both branches of a V-L model. This addresses a perceived limitation of prior methods and proposes a more holistic way to fine-tune these inherently multi-modal architectures. By enabling dynamic adjustment in both vision and language representation spaces, MaPLe aims for a "complete" model adaptation that can better align modalities for specific downstream tasks.

2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across diverse generalization tasks – including generalization to novel classes, cross-dataset transfer, and robustness to domain shifts – are significant. This suggests that the multi-modal coupled prompting approach effectively mitigates issues like catastrophic forgetting and overfitting often encountered when adapting large pre-trained models. Improved generalization is critical for deploying V-L models in real-world applications where data distributions can vary substantially from pre-training.

3. **Deeper Understanding of V-L Model Behavior:** The detailed ablation studies, particularly on prompt depth, prompt coupling direction, and the varying effectiveness across different types of datasets, contribute to a deeper understanding of how V-L models learn and adapt. The finding that MaPLe offers greater benefits for datasets with larger distribution shifts or for rare visual concepts provides insight into which scenarios multi-modal prompting is most impactful and why. This could guide future research in tailoring adaptation strategies for specific data characteristics.

4. **Computational Efficiency:** Achieving performance gains with minimal computational overhead (marginal increase in FLOPS) and improved inference/training speeds (compared to instance-conditioned methods like Co-CoOp) underscores the practical utility of MaPLe. This efficiency is crucial for large-scale V-L models, making the approach scalable and accessible for practitioners and researchers with limited computational resources. The ability to converge faster also reduces training time and resource consumption.

5. **Foundation for Future V-L Research:** The introduction of a robust multi-modal prompting paradigm could serve as a foundational method for various downstream V-L tasks beyond image classification, such as object detection, semantic segmentation, or video understanding. Future work could explore how this coupling mechanism could be extended or refined for even more complex multi-modal reasoning and generation tasks, potentially leading to V-L models that are more agile and adaptable across a broader spectrum of applications.
